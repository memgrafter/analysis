## Overall conclusions

### The corpus supports 3 swarm eras
1. **2023–2024:** classical swarm optimization, especially PSO, mostly for applied ML/statistics
2. **2024–2025:** swarm robotics / MARL / decentralized task allocation grows
3. **2025–2026:** swarm ideas migrate into **LLM multi-agent systems**

### Swarm is growing, but still niche
Even in 2025–2026, strict swarm papers are under **8 per 10k** corpus files.

### PSO dominates classical swarm work
If you mean “swarm algorithms” in the traditional optimization sense, the corpus is overwhelmingly about:
- PSO first
- ACO a distant second
- firefly / bees much smaller

### The freshest frontier is not classical SI
The most active newer direction is:
- **agentic / LLM swarms**
- **pheromone-like routing**
- **swarm-inspired collaborative inference/search**

## Appendix 4 — Detailed PSO Analysis

Using filename/title-level precision, I count **19 explicit PSO papers** in the swarm candidate set: 5 (2023), 2 (2024), 11 (2025), 1 (2026). This is the clearest classical swarm subfield in the corpus.

### 1) What PSO is doing in this corpus

PSO appears in three recurring roles:

1. **Applied optimizer for downstream ML/statistics problems.**     Examples: latent factor analysis, forecasting, counterfactual generation, statistical estimation, feature selection, EEG/emotion recognition, and image-detection rejection mechanisms.

2. **Hybrid optimizer augmented by another controller or prior.**     Examples: RL-controlled adaptive PSO, LLM-guided PSO, quantum-PSO hybrids, PSO principles injected into genetic algorithms.

3. **PSO as an object of analysis or redesign.**     Examples: communication topology studies, exact-dynamics formulations, modular PSO-X analysis, and PSO-based model merging.

This corpus therefore does **not** show PSO disappearing. It shows PSO being repurposed: less as a standalone flagship metaheuristic, more as a reliable search primitive that is easy to hybridize.

### 2) PSO timeline in the corpus

- **2023:** 5 explicit PSO papers. The emphasis is on classical optimizer design and applied use: dynamic-neighbor PSO, Adam-enhanced PSO, PAO, PSO-optimized forecasting, PSO counterfactuals.
- **2024:** 2 explicit PSO papers. Fewer in count, but conceptually important: one frames PSO as a robust statistical estimator; one studies PSO + fuzzy XGBoost in EEG classification.
- **2025:** 11 explicit PSO papers. This is the expansion year. PSO is combined with RL, LLMs, explainability/XAI, model merging, streaming feature selection, quantum circuits, and application-specific decision systems.
- **2026:** 1 explicit PSO paper in the candidate set, but it is meta-methodological: PSO-X analyzes which PSO modules matter most across 1,424 variants.

The key story is not raw growth alone; it is **broadening of PSO’s attachment points** in 2025.

### 4) The main PSO mechanisms that recur

- **Search diversification via neighborhood structure or topology.**    Example: dynamic-neighbor PSO shares neighbor velocities; topology work studies ring/star/Von Neumann information flow.
- **Adaptive control of exploration vs exploitation.**    Example: inertia schedules, RL-tuned PSO parameters, and modular PSO-X choices.
- **Constraint-friendly, derivative-free optimization.**    Example: maximum-likelihood estimation, log-binomial regression, counterfactual generation, and feature selection.
- **PSO as a wrapper around expensive evaluators.**    Example: model merging, hyperparameter tuning, and quantum circuit training, where each fitness query is costly.

The mechanism-level commonality is that PSO remains attractive whenever authors want a search loop that is **simple, population-based, gradient-free, and easy to graft new guidance onto**.

### 5) What changed in 2025

2025 is the inflection point where PSO stops looking like just a classical optimizer paper and starts looking like **an integration substrate**. In this corpus, PSO gets attached to:

- RL controllers (`2501.15203`)
- LLM priors or search hints (`2504.14126`)
- XAI / communication-topology analysis (`2504.12803`)
- model merging for large models (`2508.19839`)
- online sparse streaming feature selection (`2508.20123`)
- domain-heavy systems such as antenna design, quantum circuits, and medical imaging/detection

That is the clearest sign that PSO remains useful: researchers keep reusing it where they need a **robust outer-loop search procedure**.

### 6) Strengths of PSO, as reflected by this corpus

- **Versatility:** it ports across statistics, recommender-style factor models, explanation generation, AutoML-like tuning, and model merging.
- **Low differentiability requirements:** many papers exploit the fact that PSO does not need gradients or Hessians.
- **Hybridizability:** RL, LLMs, sparsification, and topology control can all be bolted onto the swarm.
- **Interpretability at the structural level:** topology studies and PSO-X style analysis make PSO easier to reason about than many opaque optimizers.

### 7) Weaknesses / recurring caveats

- **Benchmark-heavy evaluation:** many PSO papers still rely on standard suites or narrow domain setups.
- **Parameter sensitivity:** inertia schedules, topology, swarm size, and update rules often matter, but sensitivity analysis is uneven.
- **Limited head-to-head comparison against stronger modern optimizers:** several papers compare mainly to older heuristics or small baseline sets.
- **Scalability evidence is often incomplete:** papers claim good convergence or efficiency without full wall-clock or scaling curves.

### 8) Bottom line on PSO in this corpus

PSO is the **dominant classical swarm algorithm** in the corpus, but its role has evolved. The center of gravity is no longer “plain PSO beats baseline X on benchmark Y.” Instead, the corpus increasingly treats PSO as a **modular search scaffold**:

- a search core to be adaptively controlled by RL,
- a search loop to be biased by LLM suggestions,
- a configurable architecture whose topology/modules can be studied, or
- a gradient-free outer optimizer wrapped around new ML tasks.

So the right summary is: **PSO persists by becoming hybrid infrastructure.**
