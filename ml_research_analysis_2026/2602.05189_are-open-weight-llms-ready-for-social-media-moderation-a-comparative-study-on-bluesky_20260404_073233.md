# Are Open-Weight LLMs Ready for Social Media Moderation? A Comparative Study on Bluesky

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05189
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05189
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Open-weight LLMs achieve comparable zero-shot sensitivity and specificity to proprietary models for detecting rude, intolerant, and threatening content on Bluesky, suggesting viability for privacy-preserving, on-device moderation.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates whether open-weight large language models can perform zero-shot social media moderation as effectively as proprietary models. Using real-world posts from Bluesky with labels from its official moderation service and human annotations, seven state-of-the-art LLMs were tested on detecting three harm categories: rudeness, intolerance, and threats. Results show that open-weight models achieve statistically overlapping performance in sensitivity (81%–97% vs. 72%–98%) and specificity (91%–100% vs. 93%–99%) with proprietary models, without any fine-tuning. The agreement between LLMs and human annotators matches human-human agreement, indicating that open-weight models could be deployed for on-device moderation that preserves user privacy. However, the evaluation is confined to a single platform and a limited set of harm types, raising questions about broader generalizability.

## Method Summary
The researchers assembled a dataset of public posts from Bluesky, leveraging the platform's official moderation labels as one source of ground truth. A smaller subset was additionally annotated by human judges to cross-validate labels. Seven LLMs—three open-weight (e.g., Llama 2) and four proprietary (e.g., GPT-4)—were evaluated in a zero-shot setting, meaning they received no task-specific training or examples. Each model was prompted to classify posts as containing rude, intolerant, or threatening content. Performance was measured using sensitivity (recall of harmful content) and specificity (correct identification of safe content), and inter-rater agreement was calculated between models and humans.

## Key Results
- Open-weight models achieve sensitivity (81%–97%) and specificity (91%–100%) that statistically overlaps with proprietary models (72%–98% sensitivity, 93%–99% specificity).
- Performance varies by harm category: rudeness detection prefers higher specificity, while intolerance and threat detection exhibit higher sensitivity.
- Inter-rater agreement between LLMs and human annotators is on par with human-human agreement, suggesting models can replicate human judgment for these harm types.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Zero-shot learning allows pre-trained LLMs to apply their broad linguistic and moral knowledge to new classification tasks without fine-tuning. The models' exposure to diverse internet text during pre-training likely includes patterns associated with the defined harm categories. Open-weight models, with transparent architectures and weights, can run locally, enabling moderation without sending user data to remote servers—a key privacy advantage. The study's focus on clear, defined harm types (rude, intolerant, threatening) aligns well with models' capabilities in lexical and semantic toxicity detection.

## Foundational Learning
- **Zero-Shot Classification:** Why needed? To assess models' inherent ability to generalize to moderation without task-specific training. Quick check: Validate on a small, obvious set of examples from each harm category (e.g., explicitly rude posts).
- **Harm Taxonomy Definition:** Why needed? Consistent, precise labels (rude, intolerant, threatening) are essential for reliable evaluation. Quick check: Review annotation guidelines to ensure categories are mutually exclusive and well-defined.
- **Model Calibration:** Why needed? Well-calibrated models output confidence scores that reflect true likelihood of harm, crucial for threshold-based moderation. Quick check: Compare predicted confidence against accuracy on a held-out validation subset.
- **Social Media Context Awareness:** Why needed? Platform-specific slang, norms, and sarcasm affect content interpretation. Quick check: Analyze misclassified posts for contextual misunderstandings unique to Bluesky.
- **Privacy-Preserving Deployment:** Why needed? On-device moderation eliminates data transmission, reducing privacy risks. Quick check: Estimate model size and computational requirements for typical mobile/edge hardware.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Raw Post -> Zero-Shot Prompt Template -> LLM Inference -> Label Prediction  
**Critical Path:** Data collection (Bluesky posts + labels) -> Prompt design & validation -> Zero-shot inference across all models -> Metric computation (sensitivity, specificity) -> Statistical comparison of open-weight vs. proprietary performance.  
**Design Tradeoffs:** Zero-shot simplicity vs. potential accuracy loss; open-weight transparency vs. possible performance gap; high specificity reduces false positives but may increase false negatives for subtle harms.  
**Failure Signatures:** Sharp performance drop for a specific harm category (e.g., intolerance); large variance across models; poor agreement with humans on nuanced posts; high computational cost preventing on-device use.  
**First Experiments:** 1) Re-run the zero-shot pipeline on a labeled dataset from a different platform (e.g., Reddit) to test cross-platform generalizability. 2) Extend evaluation to additional harm categories (e.g., misinformation, harassment) using a standardized taxonomy. 3) Measure inference latency and memory footprint of top open-weight models on representative edge devices.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions beyond the implications of its limitations. However, the findings naturally raise questions about performance on other platforms, additional harm types, and under varying demographic and linguistic conditions.

## Limitations
- **Platform-Specificity:** Results are based solely on Bluesky; generalizability to other social media platforms with different norms and user bases is uncertain.
- **Label Source Bias:** Ground truth relies on Bluesky's moderation labels and a limited human set; potential biases or inconsistencies in these labels may affect reported metrics.
- **Harm Scope:** Evaluation covers only three harm categories; performance on other critical harms (e.g., misinformation, sexual exploitation) remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Performance parity (open-weight vs. proprietary):** Medium confidence—strong overlapping results for tested categories/platform, but narrow scope limits universal claims.
- **Viability for on-device moderation:** Medium confidence—high sensitivity/specificity suggests potential, but without resource usage measurements, feasibility is partly theoretical.
- **Agreement with human judgment:** Low-Medium confidence—promising match to human-human agreement, but based on a single, non-detailed annotation process; replication needed.

## Next Checks
1. **Multi-Platform Replication:** Execute the identical zero-shot evaluation on a large, labeled dataset from at least two other major platforms (e.g., X/Twitter, Reddit) to test generalizability.
2. **Expanded Harm Taxonomy & Dataset Audit:** Evaluate models on a comprehensive harm taxonomy (e.g., 10+ categories) using a large, publicly documented dataset with explicit demographic and linguistic metadata to uncover category-specific failures and bias risks.
3. **Resource Efficiency Benchmarking:** Measure and compare wall-clock inference time, memory usage, and energy consumption of top-performing open-weight vs. proprietary models on representative edge/mobile hardware to validate on-device deployment claims.
