# A Detection-Gated Pipeline for Robust Glottal Area Waveform Extraction and Clinical Pathology Assessment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.02087
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02087
- **Reference count**: 31
- **Primary result**: The pipeline achieves state-of-the-art segmentation (DSC=0.81 on GIRAFE, DSC=0.85 on BAGLS), robust cross-dataset performance (DSC=0.77), and significant clinical discrimination (cv p=0.006), while operating at ~35 frames/s on consumer hardware with public code release.

## Executive Summary
The authors propose a detection-gated pipeline for robust extraction of glottal area waveforms (GAW) from high-speed videoendoscopy. The system combines a YOLOv8 object detector with a U-Net segmenter, and uses a temporal consistency gate to suppress spurious segmentation outputs during non-glottal frames (e.g., glottal closure or occlusion). The detector provides a tight region of interest that normalizes geometric and illumination variations, enabling the segmenter—trained on just 600 frames from the GIRAFE dataset—to generalize to new datasets without fine-tuning. The pipeline achieves state-of-the-art segmentation accuracy (DSC up to 0.85) and runs in real-time (~35 fps) on consumer hardware. Downstream analysis shows the coefficient of variation of the GAW is a significant discriminator between healthy and pathological voices, suggesting clinical utility.

## Method Summary
The pipeline consists of three core components: (1) a YOLOv8 detector that localizes the glottis with a bounding box per frame, (2) a U-Net segmenter that processes either the full frame or a cropped region around the detection, and (3) a temporal gate that zeros the segmentation output if the detector fails to provide a valid box for four consecutive frames (∼1 ms at 4000 fps). The segmenter is trained on a small, manually annotated subset of GIRAFE (600 frames) using a combined BCE and Dice loss, with augmentations to improve generalization. The detector is independently fine-tuned for each dataset (e.g., 2 epochs for BAGLS, 100 for GIRAFE). During inference, the detector’s output is thresholded (τ varies per dataset) and optionally drift-clamped to ensure temporal smoothness. The final GAW is obtained by summing the foreground pixels of the gated mask over time. The cropping operation is key to cross-dataset generalization: it normalizes scale and position, allowing the segmenter to learn anatomy-specific boundaries without being confounded by background variations. At ∼35 frames/s, the system meets real-time clinical requirements.

## Key Results
- **Segmentation accuracy**: In-distribution test DSC: 0.81 on GIRAFE (60 videos), 0.85 on BAGLS (55,750 frames). Cross-dataset (GIRAFE-trained model applied to BAGLS) DSC: 0.77.
- **Clinical validation**: Coefficient of variation (cv) of the GAW significantly differs between healthy and pathological female voices (Mann–Whitney U, p=0.006).
- **Efficiency & openness**: Processing speed ∼35 frames/s on consumer hardware; code and weights released publicly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The temporal consistency gate suppresses spurious segmentation outputs during non-glottal frames (e.g., closure, occlusion, scope motion), ensuring the derived glottal area waveform (GAW) is artifact-free.
- **Mechanism:** The YOLOv8 localizer acts as a binary detector (Bt ∈ {0,1}) per frame. A short-term memory (4-frame, ~1 ms at 4000 fps) holds the last valid bounding box. If no detection occurs in the last 4 frames, the segmenter output is zeroed. This logic gate removes transient false positives without requiring a recurrent segmenter.
- **Core assumption:** Glottal visibility changes are temporally contiguous at the millisecond scale; a brief absence of detection reliably indicates a non-glottal state.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “temporal consistency wrapper ensures robustness by suppressing false positives during glottal closure and occlusion.”
  - [section 3.3] Formal definition of the gating rule (Equation 1) and hold duration analysis in Figure 2.
  - [corpus] Weak direct corpus evidence; adjacent work on temporal gating in video segmentation (e.g., corpus neighbor on feature discovery in ICP monitoring) uses similar stability principles but not for glottal imaging.
- **Break condition:** If the glottis is occluded for more than 4 consecutive frames (e.g., prolonged coughing, camera withdrawal), the GAW will have a zero gap, potentially losing valid cycles. The 4-frame window is tuned to the physiological closed phase duration.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Decoupling localization (localizer) from segmentation (segmenter) and cropping to a tight ROI removes geometric and illumination variabilities across datasets, enabling cross-dataset generalization without fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism:** The localizer provides an anatomy-constrained bounding box. The segmenter processes only this cropped region (or is masked to it), eliminating irrelevant background (endoscope rim, throat anatomy, lighting gradients). The crop operation normalizes scale and position, presenting a consistent input distribution to the segmenter regardless of camera FOV or patient anatomy.
- **Core assumption:** Glottal morphology is invariant across institutions; the observed domain shift stems from background/geometry variations, not fundamental changes in the glottis appearance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “The in-distribution localizer provides a tight region of interest (ROI), removing geometric anatomical variations and enabling cross-dataset generalization without fine-tuning.”
  - [section 5.3] Component-swap analysis: GIRAFE-trained segmenter performance is nearly identical when paired with BAGLS localizer vs. GIRAFE localizer (Figure 5), proving the segmenter learns generic anatomy. The performance jump from 0.588 to 0.745 DSC is attributable solely to improved localization recall.
  - [corpus] The GIRAFE dataset paper (corpus neighbor) highlights the challenge of cross-dataset generalization, providing the benchmark where this mechanism is validated.
- **Break condition:** If the localizer fails to enclose the entire glottis (box clipping), pixels are lost, reducing DSC (observed in GIRAFE in-distribution for Localizer-Crop+Segmenter). This is mitigated by padding (+8px) and is less severe when the glottis is smaller relative to frame size (as in BAGLS cross-dataset).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Training a lightweight U-Net segmenter on a small, dataset-specific subset (600 frames) is sufficient for high-accuracy segmentation because the localizer’s ROI reduces the effective problem complexity and the training recipe (grayscale, combined loss, AdamW+annealing) optimizes for limited data.
- **Mechanism:** Cropping focuses the segmenter on a high-resolution, standardized 256×256 patch containing only the glottis and immediate surroundings. This reduces the number of confounding features the network must learn to ignore. The training recipe (1-channel input, BCE+DSC loss, aggressive learning rate) prevents overfitting on small datasets by simplifying input dimensionality and stabilizing gradient flow.
- **Core assumption:** The glottal boundary is a local, high-contrast feature that can be learned from limited examples when irrelevant global context is removed.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “The segmenter was trained on a limited subset of the GIRAFE dataset (600 frames)... enabling cross-dataset generalization.”
  - [section 3.4] Description of training configurations (grayscale, loss, optimizer) and data augmentation.
  - [section 5.1.1] Segmenter-only achieves DSC 0.81 on GIRAFE test, beating prior baselines (0.64, 0.62, 0.71) despite using only 600 training frames.
  - [corpus] Weak; adjacent work on small-dataset medical segmentation (e.g., fetal lung segmentation) uses similar data-efficient recipes but not for glottal imaging.
- **Break condition:** If the localizer’s ROI is consistently misaligned (systematic bias), the segmenter will learn an incorrect spatial mapping. Also, if the glottis occupies a very small fraction of the frame even after cropping (extreme scale variation), effective resolution may be insufficient.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Localizer -> Segmenter -> Temporal Gate -> GAW Extractor. Specifically: YOLOv8n detects bounding box; U-Net processes cropped/full frame; gate applies 4-frame hold; summing yields GAW.

- **Critical path:**
  1. For input frame t: run localizer → get box Rt and confidence.
  2. Apply threshold τ and drift-clamp (±30px/frame) to Rt.
  3. If detection valid (Bt=1): feed frame (full or crop) to segmenter → get raw mask Mt.
  4. Apply temporal gate: if no detection in frames t-3 to t, set cMt=0; else output Mt|Rt (or pasted crop).
  5. Accumulate glottal area from cMt; repeat for all frames.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Crop vs. Full-frame:** Crop improves cross-dataset generalization by normalizing scale/position but may clip glottis pixels and loses global context. Full-frame achieves higher in-distribution DSC by using context (e.g., endoscope rim) but fails on domain shift.
  - **Hold duration:** 4 frames (~1 ms) balances suppressing closed-glottis artifacts vs. missing fast opening phases. Longer holds risk losing valid cycles; shorter holds leave spurious detections (Figure 2).
  - **Localizer threshold τ:** Low τ → high recall but more false-positive boxes (noisy GAW). High τ → precise boxes but many missed frames (zeroed GAW). Optimal τ is dataset-dependent (0.25 for GIRAFE, 0.35 for BAGLS in-distribution; cross-dataset requires much lower τ, e.g., 0.02).

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Localizer recall collapse:** On cross-dataset, GIRAFE localizer recall on BAGLS drops to ~0.69 (τ=0.25), zeroing 31% of frames → GAW has gaps, features like open_quotient become unreliable.
  - **Bounding box clipping:** In GIRAFE, tight boxes occasionally exclude peripheral glottal pixels → DSC penalty for Localizer-Crop+Segmenter vs. Localizer+Segmenter (0.70 vs. 0.75).
  - **Drift-clamp violation:** If glottis moves >30px/frame (extreme motion), detector output may be rejected → temporary zeroing.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. In-distribution segmentation on GIRAFE: Validate segmenter-only (DSC 0.81) and gated pipelines against published baselines; tune hold duration (Figure 2).
  2. In-distribution segmentation on BAGLS: Show pipeline reaches near-SOTA (DSC 0.856) with BAGLS-trained weights; confirm detection gate adds +0.010 DSC over ungated.
  3. Cross-dataset generalization (GIRAFE→BAGLS): Apply GIRAFE-trained models to BAGLS test set. Demonstrate Localizer-Crop+Segmenter (DSC 0.61) outperforms ungated segmenter (0.59). Perform component swap (Table 5) to isolate localization as the primary bottleneck.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can knowledge distillation from SAM improve segmentation accuracy beyond current state-of-the-art while maintaining real-time throughput (~35 frames/s) on consumer hardware?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The discussion states: "Exploring SAM-based distillation to further improve segmenter accuracy without sacrificing throughput is an interesting direction for future work."
- **Why unresolved:** SAM's ViT-H backbone (636M parameters) is ~80× larger than the current U-Net (7.76M) and runs at ~150 ms/frame on GPU, making it impractical for clinical workflows. The proposed pipeline trades some accuracy for speed; it is unknown whether KD can recover that accuracy without sacrificing speed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement standard KD (e.g., response-based or feature-based) from SAM or SAM-Med to the lightweight U-Net. Evaluate mean DSC on GIRAFE and BAGLS test sets, and benchmark inference speed on Apple M-series hardware. A successful outcome would show ≥0.01 absolute DSC improvement while sustaining ≥30 frames/s processing.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the minimal annotation burden (number of bounding-box annotations) required to adapt the YOLOv8n localizer to a new clinical institution and achieve cross-dataset segmentation DSC > 0.70?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The component-swap analysis reveals that cross-dataset performance degradation is primarily due to localizer recall. The authors suggest that only the localizer needs fine-tuning for new sites, but provide no data efficiency analysis.
- **Why unresolved:** Clinical adoption hinges on reducing annotation effort. While bounding boxes are cheaper than pixel masks, the required quantity for effective adaptation remains unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Collect incremental annotation sets (e.g., 50, 100, 500, 1000 frames) from a held-out hospital not represented in GIRAFE/BAGLS. Fine-tune the pre-trained localizer on each set, then run the full detection-gated pipeline on that hospital's data. Plot segmentation DSC versus number of annotated frames to identify the point of diminishing returns.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Is the coefficient of variation (cv) of the glottal area waveform a robust biomarker for distinguishing healthy from pathological voices after multiple-comparison correction and with a sufficiently powered, sex-balanced cohort?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Table 6 shows cv is significant at p=0.006 in the female subgroup (12 Healthy, 11 Pathological), but the male Healthy group is very small (n=3). The authors explicitly call the analysis exploratory and note the lack of multiple-testing correction and sex imbalance.
- **Why unresolved:** The small, imbalanced sample risks false positives. Clinical utility requires validation in larger, balanced cohorts and accounting for multiple tested features.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a prospective multi-center study with ≥50 healthy and ≥50 pathological speakers per sex group. Apply the same automated pipeline, extract all 7 kinematic features, and test cv (and others) using FDR or Bonferroni correction. Report effect sizes (Cohen's d) and 95% confidence intervals to establish reliability.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the optimal temporal hold duration depend on HSV frame rate, and would a fixed time window (e.g., 1-2 ms) rather than fixed frame count improve robustness across different acquisition speeds?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The 4-frame (1 ms at 4000 fps) hold was tuned on GIRAFE and the authors note "the optimal hold duration is coupled to the video frame rate," but only the 4000 fps case was systematically evaluated.
- **Why unresolved:** Clinical HSV systems operate at various frame rates (2000–10000 fps). A frame-count hold may suppress artifacts too aggressively at low fps or too weakly at high fps, affecting GAW fidelity.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Repeat the hold duration sensitivity analysis (Figure 2) on datasets captured at 2000 fps, 6000 fps, and 8000 fps. Compare performance using fixed-frame holds (e.g., 4, 8, 16 frames) versus fixed-time holds (e.g., 1 ms, 2 ms). Measure segmentation DSC and artifact metrics (proportion of non-zero area during expected closed phases).

## Limitations
- **Temporal gate**: The 4-frame hold is tuned to ∼1 ms at 4000 fps; prolonged glottal occlusions (>4 frames) generate zero-gaps in the GAW and may discard valid vibratory cycles.
- **ROI cropping**: The tight bounding box can clip peripheral glottal pixels, particularly when the glottis occupies a large portion of the frame, leading to reduced segmentation accuracy.
- **Cross-dataset threshold sensitivity**: The localizer's confidence threshold (τ) critically affects detection recall and thus segmentation DSC; optimal τ varies widely across datasets and is not automatically determined by the paper, hindering out-of-the-box use on new data.

## Confidence
- **Temporal gate effectiveness**: Medium confidence. The ablation (Figure 2) supports the 4-frame hold, but generalizability to other frame rates and occlusion patterns is untested.
- **Decoupled localization for cross-dataset generalization**: High confidence for the claim that ROI cropping removes geometric variations; Medium confidence for robustness across arbitrary datasets because box clipping offsets gains.
- **Small-data segmenter sufficiency**: Medium confidence. Strong in-distribution performance (DSC 0.81 with 600 frames) but contingent on accurate localizer; no stress test on severe misalignment.
- **Clinical utility of cv biomarker**: Medium confidence. Significant p-value in a limited female cohort; lacks multiple-testing correction and sex-balanced validation.
