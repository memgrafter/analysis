# CARE Drive A Framework for Evaluating Reason-Responsiveness of Vision Language Models in Automated Driving

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15645
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15645
- **Reference count:** 8
- **Primary result:** Injecting human-centered reasons into driving scenarios significantly shifts VLM decisions toward expert-recommended behavior, with time-to-collision showing the strongest effect.

## Executive Summary
CARE-Drive is a framework designed to evaluate whether vision-language models (VLMs) for automated driving make decisions that genuinely respond to human-centered reasons, rather than merely generating post-hoc rationalizations. It employs a two-stage process: prompt calibration to ensure stable VLM outputs, followed by systematic contextual perturbation where normative reasons (e.g., safety margins, social pressure) are explicitly injected into scenarios. Tested on a cyclist-overtaking task, the framework demonstrates that VLMs like gpt-4.1 with Tree-of-Thought adjust their decisions in response to these reasons, with key factors like time-to-collision and passenger urgency exerting strong, statistically significant influences. This provides a method to assess the "tracking" condition for Meaningful Human Control without model retraining.

## Method Summary
The framework begins with prompt calibration to minimize output variability from the VLM. Then, it systematically perturbs contextual factors (e.g., time-to-collision with oncoming traffic, presence of a following vehicle) while introducing explicit human reasons. For each perturbed scenario, the VLM produces a driving decision (e.g., overtake or not). These decisions are recorded and analyzed using logistic regression to quantify the effect size and significance of each factor. This isolates reason-responsiveness by measuring how sensitively the VLM's choices track the injected normative reasons.

## Key Results
- Time-to-collision with oncoming traffic dramatically increased overtaking likelihood (β=3.015, p<0.001, odds ratio ~20.4).
- The presence of a vehicle behind increased overtaking (β=1.330, p<0.001, odds ratio ~3.8).
- Passenger urgency decreased overtaking (β=-0.872, p<0.001).
- Constraints on explanation length drastically reduced overtaking (β=-4.184, p<0.001).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CARE-Drive works by decoupling stable model behavior from reason-sensitivity. Prompt calibration establishes a consistent decision baseline. Systematic perturbation then allows the framework to attribute changes in the VLM's decisions directly to the injected reasons, rather than to stochastic output variations. By measuring the magnitude and direction of shifts for each factor, it quantifies reason-responsiveness as a proxy for alignment with human-centered driving norms, enabling evaluation without retraining.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Reason-responsiveness:** The degree to which a model's decisions change when provided with human reasons. *Why needed:* To assess if the VLM tracks human intentions, a prerequisite for meaningful control. *Quick check:* Inject reason, observe statistically significant decision shift.
2. **Meaningful Human Control:** Ethical principle requiring automated systems to remain responsive to human reasons. *Why needed:* Ensures AI behavior aligns with human values. *Quick check:* Compare VLM decisions to established human normative baselines.
3. **Prompt calibration:** Tuning prompts to produce stable VLM outputs. *Why needed:* Reduces noise to isolate reason-sensitivity. *Quick check:* Measure output consistency across multiple identical runs.
4. **Contextual perturbation:** Controlled variation of scenario parameters. *Why needed:* Isolates causal effect of specific reasons. *Quick check:* Regression coefficients significant for targeted factors.
5. **Logistic regression:** Statistical model for binary outcomes. *Why needed:* Quantifies effect sizes and significance of each factor. *Quick check:* Significant p-values and interpretable odds ratios.
6. **Tree-of-Thought:** Reasoning framework for step-by-step VLM inference. *Why needed:* Improves decision quality in complex scenarios. *Quick check:* Better calibration stability vs. baseline prompting.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Scenario Generator -> Prompt Calibrator -> VLM (with Tree-of-Thought) -> Perturbation Engine -> Decision Recorder -> Analysis Module (Logistic Regression)  
**Critical path:** Generate base scenario -> Calibrate prompt -> Apply reason-based perturbations -> Query VLM for decisions -> Record outputs -> Fit regression to measure factor effects.  
**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Simplicity vs. coverage:* Linear factor perturbation is tractable but may miss interactions.  
- *Prompt engineering dependency:* High sensitivity to calibration prompts may limit generalizability.  
- *Single-scenario focus:* Depth in overtaking scenario vs. breadth across driving contexts.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- Non-significant regression coefficients for all factors.  
- High variance in VLM decisions post-calibration.  
- Inconsistent effect directions across repeated runs.  
**First experiments:**  
1. Replicate study on LLaVA and Claude 3 using identical prompts and perturbations.  
2. Apply framework to unprotected left turn and pedestrian crossing scenarios.  
3. Conduct human simulator study with identical reason conditions for baseline comparison.

## Limitations
- Evaluation confined to a single overtaking scenario; generalizability to diverse driving contexts is unknown.  
- Heavy reliance on prompt calibration and a single VLM; sensitivity to prompt engineering and model architecture is unclear.  
- No human-driver benchmark; alignment with actual human normative behavior is inferred, not measured.

## Confidence
- **High:** Framework successfully detects systematic decision shifts when normative reasons are injected.  
- **High:** Specific factor effects (e.g., TTC with oncoming traffic, passenger urgency) show strong, statistically significant influence.  
- **Medium:** Claims that reason-responsiveness proxies Meaningful Human Control; assumes injected reasons map directly to human values without empirical validation.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-model replication:** Apply CARE-Drive to at least two open-source VLMs (e.g., LLaVA, Claude 3) with identical prompts and perturbations.  
2. **Scenario expansion:** Re-run on at least three new driving contexts (e.g., unprotected left turn, pedestrian crossing) to test generalizability.  
3. **Human baseline study:** Conduct a simulator experiment with human drivers under identical perturbed reason conditions to directly compare VLM decisions.
