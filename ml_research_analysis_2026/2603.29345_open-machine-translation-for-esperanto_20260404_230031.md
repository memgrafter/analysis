# Open Machine Translation for Esperanto

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29345
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29345
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** NLLB models achieve highest translation quality, but a compact 60M-parameter Transformer matches a fine-tuned 8B Llama in most directions despite being 500× smaller.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the scarcity of systematic evaluations and optimized open-source machine translation systems for Esperanto. It benchmarks rule-based (Apertium), neural (NLLB family), and large language model-based (Llama, Tower) approaches across six translation directions involving English, Spanish, Catalan, and Esperanto using automatic metrics and human evaluation. The core contribution is training and evaluating compact bilingual Transformers (17M–60M parameters) from scratch and fine-tuning Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Results show the NLLB family delivers the best overall performance, while the authors’ small, dedicated Transformer models provide a highly efficient alternative that competes with much larger fine-tuned LLMs. The best models and code are released publicly.

## Method Summary
The study uses the FLORES+ benchmark for evaluation. Training data comes from the Tatoeba Challenge (OPUS), filtered via OpusFilter (length, ratio, HTML, language ID, alphabet restrictions). A 32k SentencePiece vocabulary is trained on a balanced multilingual sample. The authors evaluate existing systems out-of-the-box: Apertium (RBMT), NLLB-200 (600M–3.3B), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (zero-shot), and Tower (MT-tuned LLMs). They train compact encoder-decoder models (Marian Transformer-base/tiny) from scratch on filtered parallel data (up to 5M sentence pairs). Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is fine-tuned with LoRA (r=16, α=32) for one epoch using instruction-style prompts. Evaluation employs ChrF++ (primary), BLEU, COMET, and MetricX, complemented by human pairwise ranking on 100 es↔eo samples. Attempts to fine-tune NLLB did not improve base performance.

## Key Results
- NLLB-200-3.3B achieves the highest ChrF++ scores across all six directions (e.g., 67.27 for eo→en, 56.19 for eo→es) and is preferred in ~50% of human comparisons.
- The authors’ compact Transformer-base (60.6M) matches or exceeds fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct in 4 of 6 directions despite being 500× smaller.
- MT-specialized LLMs (Tower family) severely underperform, showing extremely low scores and malformed output.
- Fine-tuning NLLB or Llama yields negligible gains over their base versions for this language pair.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Compact Encoder-Decoders Achieve Parameter Efficiency in Low-Resource MT
- **Claim:** Training a small, dedicated Transformer model (60.6M parameters) from scratch can match or exceed the performance of a much larger fine-tuned general-purpose LLM (Llama-3.1-8B) for Esperanto translation, despite a 500× size difference.
- **Mechanism:** Task-specific architectural specialization and data efficiency. The compact Transformer is trained end-to-end on parallel data exclusively for translation, with a vocabulary jointly optimized for the involved languages. Fine-tuning a generalist LLM must alter a vast, pre-existing parameter space designed for broad capabilities, spreading task-specific knowledge thin across billions of parameters.
- **Core assumption:** The available parallel training data (up to 100k sentence pairs per direction) is sufficient to train a high-quality, specialized translation model but insufficient to significantly change the ingrained capabilities of a large pre-trained LLM.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Notably, the authors’ compact Transformer-base (60.6M) matches or exceeds fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct in 4/6 directions despite being 500× smaller." [section 5.2] "Despite its substantially smaller size, the Transformer-base model performs comparably to Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct-FT across most directions, surpassing it in 4 out of 6 pairs."
- **Break condition:** This advantage would diminish with significantly more high-quality, in-domain parallel data for LLM fine-tuning, or if the task required extensive world knowledge or complex reasoning beyond direct translation.

### Mechanism 2: MT-Specific Fine-Tuning of LLMs Degrades Performance on Diverse Language Pairs
- **Claim:** LLMs specifically fine-tuned for translation (Tower family) underperform both their general-purpose base models (Llama) and dedicated neural MT models (NLLB) in a low-resource, multilingual setting like Esperanto.
- **Mechanism:** Fine-tuning data and objectives for models like TowerInstruct are heavily skewed toward a small set of high-resource languages and translation "templates," causing overfitting or negative transfer. The model learns rigid patterns that fail to generalize or loses broader multilingual representational capacity present in the base model, impairing ability to handle Esperanto's morphological regularity.
- **Core assumption:** The fine-tuning corpora for MT-tuned LLMs lack sufficient diversity (language pairs, domains, morphological typology) to maintain general translation competence when adding new, structurally different languages like Esperanto.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 5.1] "Both Tower variants lag considerably behind... TowerInstruct-7B-v0.2 exhibits extremely low scores... frequently mixing languages or producing malformed output." [section 7.2] "general-purpose LLMs appear to be a more reliable choice than translation-specialized LLMs, since language-specific fine-tuning may reduce their general translation abilities."
- **Break condition:** This negative effect would be mitigated if the MT fine-tuning dataset were massively multilingual and morphologically diverse, explicitly including languages with Esperanto's agglutinative characteristics, or if techniques like mixture-of-adapters were used.

### Mechanism 3: Highly Multilingual Encoder-Decoder Pre-training Provides Robust Transfer for Low-Resource Languages
- **Claim:** The NLLB family, pre-trained on a vast multilingual corpus (200+ languages) with a translation objective, provides a robust, high-quality baseline that is difficult to surpass for a language like Esperanto.
- **Mechanism:** The model develops a shared, language-agnostic "interlingua" representation space during massive pre-training, allowing transfer learning from high-resource to low-resource languages. Its encoder-decoder architecture is inherently suited for sequence-to-sequence mapping, and the explicit translation training objective directly optimizes for bilingual semantic fidelity. Fine-tuning on limited Esperanto data does not improve because the model has already effectively learned to map Esperanto's structures into this shared space.
- **Core assumption:** The pre-training data contained sufficient Esperanto text (evidence: presence in Common Crawl, MADLAD-400, HPLT) to seed a meaningful representation, and the shared space learned for other languages is sufficiently universal.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "The NLLB family (600M–3.3B) achieves the highest ChrF++ scores in all directions... Human evaluation confirms NLLB as preferred in ~50% of the comparisons." [section 7.1] "NLLB remains the strongest overall system... due to NLLB’s highly multilingual training, which allows it to benefit from transfer learning, as well as its explicit training for translation." [section 4.1] "Esperanto ranks 75th in language presence in Common Crawl... included in MADLAD-400 and HPLT."
- **Break condition:** This dominance would be challenged by a model with a more optimal architecture for low-resource scenarios, or one trained with vastly superior quality/quantity of Esperanto-inclusive parallel data, or if Esperanto's uniqueness made it truly "out-of-distribution."

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Agglutinative Morphology and Lexical Compositionality**
  - **Why needed:** Esperanto builds words by combining roots with fixed prefixes/suffixes. A single word can carry phrase-like meaning. MT systems must handle long, rare tokens or decompose/recompose elements. Errors in stem/affix attachment create meaning-altering mistakes.
  - **Quick check:** Given root `vort-` (word) and suffix `-ist-` (person who does), what is `vortisto`, and how would a morphology-blind subword tokenizer likely split it?

- **Concept: FLORES+ Benchmark and its Biases**
  - **Why needed:** FLORES+ is the only standardized MT benchmark for Esperanto; all quantitative conclusions depend on it. It was created by translating *from* English, introducing source-side artifacts and domain biases (e.g., formal, written style), affecting generalizability for *→eo directions.
  - **Quick check:** If a test set is created by translating from Language A into Language B, what inherent bias does this introduce for evaluating systems translating *into* Language B?

- **Concept: Metric Calibration for Unseen Languages**
  - **Why needed:** The paper uses COMET and MetricX, not fine-tuned on Esperanto. Their strong correlation with human judgments suggests effective zero-shot transfer, but numeric scores (e.g., COMET ~85, MetricX ~3–5) are on scales calibrated for other languages. Interpreting "what score is good?" requires understanding metric behavior on languages with similar typology to their training data (largely Romance/Germanic).
  - **Quick check:** Why might a learned metric like MetricX, trained on WMT data not containing Esperanto, still correlate well with human judgments for Esperanto translation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Data Ingestion & Filtering (Tatoeba → OpusFilter) → Vocabulary Construction (SentencePiece/Llama tokenizer) → Model Families (Apertium/NLLB/Marian/Llama/Tower) → Evaluation (FLORES+ + Metrics + Human) → Failure Replication (NLLB fine-tuning).

- **Critical path:**
  1. Acquire and filter Tatoeba Challenge data for en|es|ca↔eo pairs.
  2. Train SentencePiece vocabulary (32k) on balanced 50k-sample multilingual subset.
  3. Train Marian Transformer-base/tiny models using filtered data and hyperparameters (Adam, LR 3e-4, early stopping on perplexity).
  4. Fine-tune Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with LoRA (r=16, α=32) on 100k instruction prompts for 1 epoch.
  5. Evaluate all models on FLORES+ with ChrF++, BLEU, COMET, MetricX.
  6. For reproduction: Run NLLB fine-tuning experiments (Appendix B) to confirm they do not yield gains.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Training Cost vs. Performance:** Training a 60M model from scratch (hours on one GPU) yields performance close to an 8B LoRA-finetuned LLM (more expensive) and approaches a 3.3B NLLB. The compact model wins on inference efficiency (CPU-deployable).
  - **Generalization vs. Specialization:** NLLB's massive multilingual pre-training provides robustness and best absolute scores. A dedicated model trades robustness for efficiency and potentially better handling of Esperanto-specific patterns if trained on more in-domain data.
  - **Architectural Bias:** Encoder-decoder models (NLLB, Marian) are *trained* for translation. Decoder-only models (Llama) *perform* translation via instruction tuning, which is less sample-efficient and more prone to hallucination.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Tower models:** Extremely low ChrF++ (e.g., eo→ca: 8.22), frequent language mixing, malformed output. Indicates catastrophic failure of MT-specific fine-tuning.
  - **NLLB fine-tuning:** No improvement over base model scores. Suggests base model is already saturated; fine-tuning data not novel enough.
  - **Llama fine-tuning:** Modest gains only for eo→* directions. Suggests base model has stronger en representations; fine-tuning helps most for low-resource target generation.
  - **Compact model vs. NLLB gap:** Persistent gap in eo→es/ca (~3–4 ChrF++ points). Suggests NLLB's multilingual transfer provides a reserve of quality hard to match with bilingual data alone.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate Benchmark: Run inference with all listed benchmark models on FLORES+ es↔eo. Compute metrics. Confirm ranking: NLLB-3.3B > Llama-Instruct > Transformer-base > others.
  2. Train Tiny Model: Implement Marian Transformer-tiny (17.4M) setup. Train on 100k es↔eo filtered pairs. Expect ChrF++ ~45 (es→eo) and ~54 (eo→es), competitive with early NLLB distilled models.
  3. LoRA Fine-tune Llama: Use open-instruct with given hyperparameters. Train on 100k es↔eo instruction pairs. Validate improvement over zero-shot is minimal (<2 ChrF++), and it still lags behind NLLB and the tiny Transformer for es→eo.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** Does Esperanto's regular morphosyntactic structure make it inherently easier to model for MT compared to natural low-resource languages?
  - **Basis:** Conclusion: "For future work, we are interested in studying whether Esperanto is inherently easier to model than natural languages due to its regular morphological and syntactic structure, following work by Ploeger et al. (2025)."
  - **Why unresolved:** No systematic cross-linguistic comparison controls for resource level and typological features; existing evidence is preliminary and does not isolate Esperanto's constructed nature.
- **Question:** Can Esperanto effectively serve as an interlingua in pivot-based MT for language pairs with limited direct resources?
  - **Basis:** Conclusion: "Another important direction would be to revisit the original idea of Esperanto as an interlingua for pivot-based MT."
  - **Why unresolved:** Modern multilingual systems use direct translation or English-centric pivots; no recent empirical evaluation tests Esperanto's pivot viability across diverse low-resource pairs.
- **Question:** How can rule-based MT resources like Apertium be effectively integrated with neural MT to improve Esperanto translation?
  - **Basis:** Conclusion: "given the availability of rule-based resources in Apertium, future work could explore hybrid approaches that leverage RBMT knowledge."
  - **Why unresolved:** Apertium underperforms neural systems, but its hand-crafted linguistic knowledge may address neural weaknesses in compositional morphology; no systematic hybrid method has been proposed or tested.
- **Question:** Why does fine-tuning large pre-trained models (NLLB, Llama) fail to improve over base models for low-resource Esperanto MT despite available training data?
  - **Basis:** Appendix B: "fine-tuning did not improve over the base NLLB models"; Section 5.2 notes Llama fine-tuning gave only modest gains.
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper does not diagnose whether failure stems from limited data (100k pairs), hyperparameters, model capacity, or inherent limitations of base representations; without analysis, the cause remains speculative.
- **Question:** Why do machine translation–specialized LLMs (Tower) underperform general-purpose LLMs (Llama) for Esperanto translation, and does this generalize to other low-resource languages?
  - **Basis:** Section 7.2: "MT-tuned LLMs... consistently underperform the general-purpose LLM baseline"; Section 5.1 notes TowerInstruct often failed to generate Esperanto/Catalan properly.
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper hypothesizes language-specific fine-tuning may reduce general abilities but offers no analysis of training data distribution, tokenization, or internal representations; generalizability beyond Esperanto is unknown.

## Limitations
- FLORES+ is English‑centric; source‑side bias may skew *→eo results.
- COMET/MetricX lack Esperanto calibration; score interpretation is uncertain.
- Human evaluation covers only es↔eo; other directions remain unchecked.
- Insufficient methodological details for filtering/prompting hinder exact replication.
