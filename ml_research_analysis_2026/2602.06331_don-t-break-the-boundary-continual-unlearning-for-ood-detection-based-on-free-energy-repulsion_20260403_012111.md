# Don't Break the Boundary: Continual Unlearning for OOD Detection Based on Free Energy Repulsion
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.06331
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06331
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: TFER achieves low Forget FPR95 (~41.5%) while preserving high Retain Accuracy (~76.1%) with 98.9% fewer parameters and 20× speedup vs. retraining.

## Executive Summary
Traditional machine learning unlearning disrupts the geometric manifold essential for out-of-distribution (OOD) detection, creating a conflict. This paper reformulates class unlearning as “forget-as-OOD”: forgotten classes must be transformed into OOD samples. The proposed TFER framework employs a Push-Pull mechanism—a Free Energy Repulsion loss to push forgotten samples into high-energy OOD regions and a Protection loss to pull retained classes into low-energy in-distribution regions—using parameter-efficient LoRA fine-tuning. On CIFAR-100, TFER significantly outperforms baselines by achieving a superior trade-off between effective forgetting and retention, with exceptional computational efficiency and demonstrated capability for continual unlearning.

## Method Summary
TFER instantiates forget-as-OOD on a hyperspherical OOD detection framework where class prototypes lie on a hypersphere. It employs LoRA adapters inserted into a frozen backbone for parameter-efficient tuning. The Push Force (Free Energy Repulsion loss) increases the free energy (negative logits) of forgotten class samples, expelling them from the low-energy in-distribution manifold. The Pull Force (Protection loss) minimizes the free energy of retained class samples, anchoring them within the in-distribution region. For continual unlearning, orthogonal LoRA adapters are trained sequentially for each forgetting task to prevent interference with previously unlearned classes.

## Key Results
- Achieves Forget FPR95 of ~41.5% at 50 epochs on CIFAR-100, substantially lower than baselines like gradient ascent.
- Maintains high Retain Accuracy of ~76.1%, preserving most retained class performance.
- Demonstrates 98.9% reduction in tunable parameters and 20× speedup compared to full retraining.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The push-pull mechanism directly engineers the free energy landscape. The Push Force increases the free energy of forgotten samples, moving them into regions where the model’s average logit is high (typical OOD behavior), while the Pull Force decreases free energy for retained samples, keeping them in low-energy in-distribution zones. This geometric manipulation aligns with the hyperspherical prototype structure, where logit magnitude encodes OODness. LoRA’s low-rank updates enable precise boundary adjustments with minimal parameter changes, avoiding global disruption of the in-distribution manifold.

## Foundational Learning
- **Hyperspherical OOD Detection**: Why needed: provides a geometric manifold where ID classes form a compact cluster via normalized logits; OOD samples have smaller logit magnitudes. Quick check: normalize penultimate layer features and examine class prototype separation.
- **Free Energy**: Why needed: scalar OOD score; lower values indicate ID. Quick check: compute -log(sum(exp(logits))) per sample; ID should have lower average than OOD.
- **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**: Why needed: enables efficient fine-tuning by updating only low-rank matrices, preserving backbone knowledge. Quick check: decompose weight update ΔW into BA (rank r); total params ≈ 2*r*d_model.
- **Prototype-based Logits**: Why needed: class means on hypersphere define decision boundaries; forget-as-OOD exploits this geometry. Quick check: compute class centroids from training features; logit = feature·prototype.
- **Continual Unlearning**: Why needed: real-world scenarios require sequential forgetting without catastrophic recall. Quick check: after each unlearning step, evaluate FPR on all previously forgotten classes.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input -> Frozen Backbone (ResNet) -> LoRA Adapters (per task, orthogonal) -> Logits -> Free Energy -> Push-Pull Losses -> Adapter Updates. Critical path: During unlearning, mini-batch split into forgotten/retained; free energy computed; push loss applied to forgotten, pull loss to retained; gradients update only the active LoRA adapter. Design tradeoffs: Orthogonal adapters prevent interference but may limit expressivity for many tasks; rank choice balances parameter cost and modeling capacity. Failure signatures: (1) High Forget FPR95 indicates push force ineffective or insufficient adaptation; (2) Drop in Retain Accuracy suggests pull force too weak or unlearning leakage; (3) Small singular values in adapters may signal subspace exhaustion in continual setting. First three experiments: (1) Vary number of forgotten classes (1, 5, 10) on CIFAR-100 to measure scalability; (2) Sweep LoRA ranks (1, 2, 4, 8) for a single forgetting task to assess rank sensitivity; (3) Transfer TFER to a different backbone (e.g., ViT) to check architectural robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Is the Forget-as-OOD concept generally valid for all OOD detection paradigms, or confined to hyperspherical prototype methods?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state, "We instantiate TFER on a hyperspherical OOD detection framework" (Sec. 3.1) and only evaluate this variant.
- Why unresolved: The equivalence relies on prototype-based logits; alternative OOD scorings (density, energy) may not push forgotten samples into the OOD region via Lunlearn.
- What evidence would resolve it: Apply the same push-pull losses to at least two non-hyperspherical detectors (e.g., energy-based, Mahalanobis), measure Forget FPR95 and Retain Accuracy, and compare to the reported results.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the orthogonal LoRA strategy remain effective for continuous streams of many (>10) unlearning tasks, or does it eventually fail due to subspace exhaustion?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Sec. 4.5 tests only two sequential tasks (10+10 classes) and claims parametric isolation prevents catastrophic recall, but longer sequences are not examined.
- Why unresolved: Enforcing pairwise orthogonality across many adapters may become too restrictive, limiting the model's ability to encode new unlearning directions without interfering with past ones.
- What evidence would resolve it: Run continual unlearning with 5, 10, and 20 tasks on CIFAR-100, track FPR95 for all previously forgotten classes after each step, and analyze orthogonality metrics of adapter matrices.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the rank of the LoRA adaptation affect the trade-off between unlearning efficacy (Forget FPR95), retained accuracy, and computational cost?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] TFER employs LoRA but all experiments use a fixed, unreported rank; the paper provides no analysis of rank sensitivity.
- Why unresolved: Low rank may insufficiently capture boundary adjustments, while high rank increases parameters and might overfit retained classes; the optimal rank for different forgetting scenarios is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Sweep LoRA ranks (e.g., 1, 2, 4, 8, 16) for varying numbers of forgotten classes, reporting the full suite of metrics (Forget FPR95, Retain-Acc, OOD Avg AUC) along with training time and parameter count.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to CIFAR-100; generalizability to larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet) and diverse OOD benchmarks is unknown.
- Forget FPR95 remains ~41.5%, leaving many false positives; trade-offs across decision thresholds are not fully explored.
- No thorough hyperparameter sensitivity analysis (Push/Pull strengths, LoRA rank), which may hinder practical deployment.

## Confidence
- Geometric reformulation (forget-as-OOD): Medium
- Empirical effectiveness (low Forget FPR95, high Retain Accuracy): High
- Parameter and time efficiency (98.9% fewer params, 20× speedup vs. retraining): High
- Structural stability during unlearning: Medium
- Continual unlearning capability: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Scale to large datasets: Apply TFER on ImageNet and multiple OOD benchmarks (e.g., Textures, iNaturalist) to assess generalizability and robustness.
2. Ablation of push-pull forces: Conduct systematic sweeps over Push strength (λ), Pull strength (γ), and LoRA ranks to quantify individual contributions and optimal balance.
3. Adversarial boundary test: Generate OOD samples via adversarial perturbations of forgotten class images to stress the separation guarantee and measure failure modes.
