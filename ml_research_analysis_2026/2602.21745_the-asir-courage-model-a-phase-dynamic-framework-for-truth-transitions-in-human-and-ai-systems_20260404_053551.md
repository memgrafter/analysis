# The ASIR Courage Model: A Phase-Dynamic Framework for Truth Transitions in Human and AI Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21745  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21745  
- **Reference count:** 2  
- **Primary result:** The paper proposes the ASIR Courage Model—a phase-dynamic framework that defines courage as a state transition from suppression (S0) to expression (S1) when λ(1+γ)+ψ > θ+ϕ, with feedback loops that generate path‑dependent dynamics and testable predictions such as a gamma band effect and accumulation‑driven disclosures.

## Executive Summary
The ASIR Courage Model fills a gap by providing the first mathematical formulation of truth‑telling under risk for both humans and AI systems. It formalizes courage as a stochastic threshold crossing in a continuous variable space: when the combined facilitative forces (openness λ, relationally‑amplified openness via γ, and accumulated internal pressure ψ) exceed inhibitory forces (truth threshold θ and transition cost ϕ), the system shifts from suppression (S0) to expression (S1). A feedback mechanism updates all parameters based on the outcome of each transition, recursively reshaping the system’s future state. Simulations reveal three robust signatures: (1) a sigmoidal increase in transition probability as relational gravity γ grows (the gamma band effect), (2) geometric growth of internal pressure ψ during prolonged suppression, eventually forcing disclosure, and (3) divergence into long‑term attractors—virtuous cycles versus trauma spirals—driven by early successes or failures. The model’s qualitative invariance across wide parameter sweeps yields a set of dynamical predictions ready for empirical validation.

## Method Summary
The model operates in discrete episodes, each defined by five time‑varying parameters: baseline openness (λ), relational gravity (γ), internal pressure (ψ), truth threshold (θ), and transition cost (ϕ). The transition condition is a linear inequality: λ(1+γ) + ψ > θ + ϕ. If satisfied, the state becomes expressed (S1=1); otherwise it remains suppressed (S0). After each outcome, deterministic update rules adjust the parameters: λ and γ increase with success and decrease with failure; ψ multiplies by (β+δ) if the transition fails (S1=0) or by β if it succeeds; θ and ϕ decrease with success. The authors conduct three simulation experiments: (1) sweeping γ to verify a sigmoid probability curve; (2) comparing ψ trajectories under forced suppression versus eventual success to confirm geometric buildup; and (3) forcing divergent outcome sequences (mostly success vs. mostly failure) to demonstrate path‑dependent separation in the λ‑ψ phase plane. Sensitivity analyses across parameter grids confirm that the core signatures persist qualitatively.

## Key Results
- The ASIR model introduces a mathematically precise, phase‑dynamic definition of courage as a state transition (S0→S1) governed by λ(1+γ)+ψ > θ+ϕ.  
- Relational gravity (γ) acts as a multiplicative accelerator of openness, producing a sigmoidal (gamma band) effect on transition probability.  
- Internal pressure (ψ) grows geometrically under suppression when β+δ > 1, eventually forcing disclosure regardless of other factors.  
- Early transition outcomes, via parameter updates, drive the system into distinct long‑term regimes: virtuous cycles (increasing λ,γ; decreasing ψ) or trauma spirals (decreasing λ,γ; explosive ψ).  
- The model’s dynamics are qualitatively invariant across broad parameter ranges, yielding testable predictions for empirical work.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Transition as a facilitative‑inhibitory balance**  
The inequality λ(1+γ)+ψ > θ+ϕ aggregates facilitative components (λ scaled by γ, and ψ) against inhibitory thresholds (θ, ϕ). Its linear form, combined with stochastic noise, can generate nonlinear behaviors (e.g., sigmoid curves) without requiring a nonlinear core. The model breaks if empirical transition probabilities do not monotonically increase with the composite facilitative score.

**Mechanism 2: Relational gravity as a multiplicative accelerator**  
The (1+γ) factor embeds relational gravity as a direct scale on λ, distinguishing it from mere reduction of costs or thresholds. γ>0 amplifies effective openness; γ<0 diminishes it. This structure preserves functionality when γ=0 and makes relational resonance an explicit, dynamic multiplier. The mechanism fails if relational context primarily moderates θ or ϕ, or if γ’s effect is better captured additively.

**Mechanism 3: Feedback‑driven path dependence**  
Post‑transition updates recursively alter all parameters. Success raises λ and γ while lowering ψ, θ, ϕ; failure does the opposite. Crucially, ψ compounds geometrically under failure when β+δ>1, eventually forcing a disclosure. This creates two emergent attractors (virtuous cycle vs. trauma spiral). The mechanism breaks if longitudinal data show no lasting impact of early outcomes or if ψ accumulation is linear or bounded (β+δ ≤ 1).

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:
Initial parameters (λ₀, γ₀, ψ₀, θ₀, ϕ₀) and feedback coefficients (α, β, δ, κ, θ_adj, ϕ_adj) → Episode loop: compute LHS = λ(1+γ) + ψ; RHS = θ + ϕ → if LHS > RHS set S1=1 else S1=0 → record outcome → apply updates: λ += α(2S1−1); ψ *= β + (1−S1)δ; γ += κ(S1−0.5); θ -= θ_adj(2S1−1); ϕ -= ϕ_adj(2S1−1) → next episode.

Critical path:
1. Initialize all variables and feedback parameters.
2. For each episode until termination:
   - Compute LHS and RHS.
   - Determine state S1 using the inequality.
   - Record outcome (S1=1 or 0).
   - Apply the five update equations.
3. Stop after a fixed number of episodes or when a variable exceeds a preset limit.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Linearity of transition:** The core inequality is linear for parsimony, but may miss sharp behavioral thresholds; a future extension could replace it with a sigmoidal activation.
- **Parameter regime:** The claim that ψ compounds under suppression hinges on β+δ > 1; choosing β,δ ≤ 1 would fundamentally alter the model’s central prediction.
- **Measurement of γ:** γ is a latent, domain‑specific construct; its operationalization (e.g., linguistic style matching, reciprocity metrics) is a major source of flexibility and vulnerability.

Failure signatures:
- **No sigmoid γ curve:** Experiment 1 yields a flat or linear P(S1) vs. γ, suggesting excessive noise or incorrect scaling.
- **ψ does not compound:** Experiment 2 shows ψ stable or decaying under perpetual suppression (β+δ ≤ 1).
- **No path divergence:** Experiment 3 fails to separate trajectories in the λ‑ψ phase plane, indicating weak feedback (e.g., α=0, κ=0) or mis‑applied updates.

First 3 experiments:
1. **γ sweep (Exp1):** Fix all other variables, sweep γ across a range, run n=2000 Monte‑Carlo trials per γ, and plot P(S1) vs. γ. Expect a sigmoid curve, confirming the nonlinear acceleration effect.
2. **ψ accumulation (Exp2):** Initialize with given parameters. Path A: force S1=0 forever → ψ should multiply repeatedly by (β+δ). Path B: allow a successful transition → ψ resets to β thereafter. Plot ψ over time.
3. **Feedback divergence (Exp3):** Start two agents from identical initial state (λ=3, ψ=1, γ=1, θ=5, ϕ=4). Impose a sequence with 12 successes/3 failures for one agent and the opposite for the other over 15 episodes. Using update parameters (α=0.3, β=0.5, δ=1.0, κ=0.4, θ_adj=ϕ_adj=0.2), plot trajectories of λ, γ, ψ and the transition gap (LHS−RHS). Expect clear separation into virtuous vs. trauma zones.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Empirical validation:** The model is purely theoretical; its testable signatures (sigmoid γ effect, ψ‑driven tipping points, attractor divergence) need validation using human data (e.g., longitudinal diaries, controlled experiments) or AI interaction logs.
- **Operationalizing relational gravity (γ):** γ is a central latent variable without a universal operational definition. Domain‑specific proxies (linguistic style matching, depth of self‑disclosure, temporal reciprocity) must be ethically developed and validated.
- **Alternative pressure regimes:** Systems with β+δ ≤ 1 (where suppression does not compound internal pressure) may exist. Identifying such contexts and understanding their dynamics is a critical extension.
- **Cultural calibration of θ and ϕ:** Truth sensitivity and transition cost likely vary across cultures and institutions. Cross‑cultural calibration is necessary to generalize predictions such as the location of the gamma band.

## Limitations
- **Lack of empirical validation:** The model is supported only by simulations; no real‑world data confirm its core mechanisms.
- **Construct operationalization:** Relational gravity (γ) remains underspecified, making measurement and comparison across domains challenging.
- **Simplified dynamics:** The linear inequality and deterministic feedback rules may oversimplify complex psychological or sociological processes, and the assumption β+δ > 1 remains untested.

## Confidence
