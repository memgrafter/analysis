# Systematicity between Forms and Meanings across Languages Supports Efficient Communication

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17181  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17181  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Attested linguistic paradigms with systematic form-meaning mappings achieve better accuracy-complexity trade-offs than counterfactual permutations, as measured by a learnability-based complexity metric (CETL), confirming communicative efficiency.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether systematic relationships between linguistic forms and meanings—such as consistent use of prefixes for grammatical person—support efficient communication. It introduces CETL, a learnability-based complexity measure computed via neural sequence-to-sequence models that encode forms as character sequences, capturing fine-grained regularities. Evaluated on verb and pronoun paradigms across hundreds of languages, CETL reveals that attested paradigms occupy near-optimal regions of the accuracy-complexity trade-off space and strongly correlate with paradigm naturalness, whereas the classic Information Bottleneck model fails to capture these effects. The results suggest that systematicity in form-meaning mappings reduces learnability complexity, thereby enhancing communicative efficiency.

## Method Summary
The study compares attested linguistic paradigms to counterfactual permutations using two efficiency metrics: complexity (CETL) and accuracy. CETL is derived by training an LSTM seq2seq model to map categorical feature vectors (meanings) to character sequences (forms); complexity is the need-weighted average cross-entropy loss over 50 training epochs, with lower values indicating higher learnability. Accuracy measures how well a Bayesian listener recovers meanings from forms given semantic similarity and need distributions. Counterfactuals include *structural permutations* (altering syncretism patterns) and *form-only permutations* (preserving syncretism but scrambling surface forms). The method is applied to hundreds of languages’ verb and pronoun paradigms from typological databases, with need distributions estimated from corpus frequencies.

## Key Results
- Attested paradigms achieve significantly better accuracy-complexity trade-offs than both structural and form-only counterfactuals across hundreds of languages.
- CETL correlates strongly with paradigm naturalness (Pearson’s r up to 0.88), whereas the Information Bottleneck model shows no correlation.
- CETL correctly identifies 65–90% of form-only permutations as less efficient, while the IB model fails entirely (0% identification).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1**  
**Claim:** CETL captures systematic form-meaning covariation (e.g., consistent prefixes/suffixes) by encoding forms as character sequences, reducing learnability complexity compared to atomic-form models.  
**Mechanism:** An LSTM seq2seq is trained to predict character sequences from feature vectors. Faster cross-entropy decay (lower CETL) indicates that systematic regularities (e.g., shared morphemes across paradigm cells) make the paradigm easier to learn.  
**Core assumption:** Learnability, measured via training loss trajectory, is a valid proxy for cognitive complexity in language use.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] CETL shows attested systems achieve better trade-offs than counterfactuals and captures fine-grained regularities.  
- [section 3.1] The encoder exploits systematic regularities across forms; training converges more rapidly for more learnable paradigms.  
**Break condition:** If CETL fails to discriminate form-only permutations that preserve syncretism but break internal regularities (e.g., disrupting a prefix pattern), the mechanism fails. The paper shows CETL succeeds where IB fails (65–90% vs. 0%), supporting the mechanism.

**Mechanism 2**  
**Claim:** Attested language paradigms occupy a near-optimal region of the accuracy-complexity trade-off space due to competing pressures for simplicity and accuracy.  
**Mechanism:** For each paradigm, complexity (CETL or IB) is computed from need-weighted training loss. Accuracy is computed via Bayesian decoding with semantic similarity. Attested paradigms should show lower complexity and higher accuracy than most counterfactuals.  
**Core assumption:** Natural languages are shaped by selection for communicative efficiency, so existing systems outperform random alternatives on this trade-off.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] Attested systems achieve better trade-offs than counterfactual permutations.  
- [section 4.1] Counterfactuals are generated by permuting existing paradigms; attested paradigms are compared to their source as baseline.  
- [section 5.1] Figure 4 shows attested paradigms consistently dominate counterfactuals in complexity-accuracy space; statistical tests confirm significance.  
**Break condition:** If attested paradigms are not systematically more efficient than counterfactuals (e.g., many counterfactuals show better trade-offs), the efficiency claim fails. The paper finds significance across domains, supporting the mechanism.

**Mechanism 3**  
**Claim:** Paradigms with more “natural” syncretism patterns (syncretic cells share similar grammatical features) have lower CETL complexity, but IB shows no such correlation.  
**Mechanism:** Naturalness is quantified by an “unnaturalness” score counting feature value differences within syncretic classes. CETL correlates because character-level learning benefits when shared form implies shared meaning (e.g., second person consistently marked by *ta-*). IB, treating forms atomically, cannot capture this.  
**Core assumption:** Syncretism is more learnable when it groups semantically similar meanings; this shapes paradigms.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] CETL correlates strongly with naturalness (r up to 0.88), whereas IB shows no correlation.  
- [section 4.2] Defines unnaturalness score based on feature variance within syncretism classes.  
- [section 5.1] Figure 5 shows strong positive correlation for CETL but not IB; per-language correlations: 0.82–0.88 for CETL vs. near-zero for IB.  
**Break condition:** If CETL fails to correlate with naturalness across domains or shows similar correlation to IB, the claim fails. The paper shows robust correlations for verbs and pronouns.

## Foundational Learning
- **Information Bottleneck (IB) framework**  
  *Why needed:* CETL is positioned as an extension of IB; understanding IB’s complexity (mutual information between meaning and atomic form) and accuracy (Bayesian decoding) is essential to see what CETL changes (form representation) and why.  
  *Quick check:* How does the IB model define complexity, and why is it insensitive to systematic form-meaning covariation within paradigms?

- **Sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) modeling for morphology**  
  *Why needed:* CETL’s core innovation is using an LSTM seq2seq to predict character sequences from features. Grasping how seq2seqs model structured output and how training loss reflects learnability is key.  
  *Quick check:* Why might a seq2seq model that outputs characters (vs. atomic tokens) learn paradigms faster when they exhibit systematic prefixes/suffixes?

- **Morphological paradigms and syncretism**  
  *Why needed:* The paper evaluates on verb and pronoun paradigms. Understanding paradigm structure (cells indexed by feature combinations), syncretism (identical forms for distinct cells), and “naturalness” (syncretic cells sharing features) is necessary to interpret permutations and results.  
  *Quick check:* What is the difference between a structural permutation (alters syncretism) and a form-only permutation (preserves syncretism but changes surface forms)?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input encoder (embeds feature vectors) -> Seq2seq core (LSTM encoder-decoder generating character sequences) -> Need distribution (pcog(t)) -> CETL calculator (averages pcog-weighted cross-entropy loss)  
Parallel: Same seq2seq training -> Accuracy calculator (Bayesian decoding with semantic similarity)  
Separate: Counterfactual generator (creates structural/form-only permutations) -> Naturalness scorer (computes unnaturalness score)

**Critical path:**  
1. **Data preparation:** Extract paradigm tables mapping feature combinations to character-string forms; encode features as categorical vectors; estimate pcog(t) from corpus frequencies.  
2. **Model training:** For each paradigm variant, train a fresh LSTM seq2seq (2 layers, dropout 0.5, batch 1) for Tmax=50 epochs; track per-epoch, pcog-weighted loss.  
3. **Metric computation:** Compute CETL (average loss) and accuracy (Bayesian listener posterior quality).  
4. **Comparison:** For each counterfactual, compare (CETL, accuracy) to attested baseline; classify as “Correct” if worse in both or one+equal, else “Incorrect”; compute correlation with unnaturalness.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Character vs. subword/atomic:** Character-level captures fine-grained regularities but increases sequence length; atomic forms are simpler but miss internal structure.  
- **Tmax=50:** Fixed for comparability, but may be insufficient for some complex counterfactuals to converge; could affect CETL comparability.  
- **Need distribution estimation:** Using corpus frequencies introduces language-specific biases; uniform/random alternatives are robust but less ecologically valid.  
- **Paradigm isolation:** Modeling each paradigm separately controls inter-paradigm variability but ignores lexicon-wide effects.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **CETL does not discriminate form-only permutations:** If CETL performance matches IB (0% correct) on form-only swaps, the encoder fails to learn systematic regularities. Check training loss curves: attested and permuted converge similarly.  
- **No correlation with naturalness:** If CETL-unnaturalness correlation is low or negative, the model may not encode feature-based similarity in a learnable way. Inspect learned representations.  
- **High variance in CETL across random seeds:** Unstable training could mask true differences; increase training runs or adjust hyperparameters.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Implement the seq2seq as described; train on a single attested paradigm (e.g., Classical Arabic verbs); verify near-zero training loss; compute CETL and compare to a uniform random counterfactual (should be higher).  
2. **Discrimination test:** For 2–3 languages, generate all structural and form-only permutations; compute CETL and accuracy; verify attested paradigms show better trade-offs than >50% of structural permutations and that CETL correctly classifies >50% of form-only permutations as less efficient, while IB classifies 0%.  
3. **Naturalness correlation check:** For one domain (e.g., PRON), compute unnaturalness scores for all permutations; plot CETL vs. unnaturalness; verify strong positive correlation (r > 0.7) for attested and structural permutations, and that CETL correlation exceeds IB’s.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Adaptation to continuous semantic domains:** Can CETL be applied to domains like color terms lacking discrete categorical structure? The current seq2seq architecture requires discrete features as input/output.  
- **CETL vs. IB complementarity:** Under what conditions does CETL outperform IB, and when are they complementary? No systematic comparison across diverse linguistic domains exists.  
- **Characterizing the Pareto frontier:** Can the set of maximally efficient paradigms be characterized without exhaustive enumeration of all form combinations? Exhaustive sampling is computationally intractable.  
- **Generalizability beyond verbs and pronouns:** Does CETL’s superiority over IB extend to other structured semantic domains (e.g., kinship, spatial terms)? Evaluation was restricted to two domains.

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters and training stability are unspecified; Tmax=50 may be insufficient for some paradigms, potentially biasing CETL.
- Need distribution (pcog(t)) estimation details are incomplete; merging procedures for pronoun and verb frequencies could affect results.
- Additional concerns: reliance on constructed counterfactuals rather than natural usage; character-level encoding may not optimally handle non-concatenative morphology; high computational cost limits replication scale.

## Confidence
- **CETL captures systematic form-meaning covariation:** Medium confidence (depends on seq2seq learning stability; representation uncertainties).
- **Attested paradigms show better accuracy-complexity trade-offs:** High confidence (consistent across hundreds of languages, significant statistical support).
- **CETL outperforms IB (correlation and discrimination):** High confidence (IB fails on form-only permutations, CETL shows robust naturalness correlation).

## Next Checks
1. **Verify training convergence across languages:** Extend Tmax beyond 50 epochs for a subset of paradigms and monitor loss curves to ensure CETL stability and comparability.
2. **Test robustness to need distribution variations:** Recompute CETL and accuracy using uniform or alternative frequency-based pcog(t) to rule out bias from current estimation.
3. **Audit character-level representations for non-concatenative languages:** Inspect learned embeddings and generated forms for languages with root-template morphology (e.g., Semitic languages) to confirm systematic patterns are captured.
