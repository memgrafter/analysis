# Minimal Computational Preconditions for Subjective Perspective in Artificial Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02902
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02902
- **Reference count:** 5
- **Primary result:** A slow global latent g_t exhibits directional hysteresis during environmental regime switches, while policy entropy does not, providing a measurable signature of perspective-like subjectivity.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the computational preconditions for instilling a subjective perspective in artificial agents. The authors propose an architecture where a slow global latent state g_t modulates a fast reactive policy, all trained without reward via prediction error minimization in a grid-world with three zones of varying observation noise. When the environment undergoes regime switches (noise inversion between zones), g_t shows asymmetric adaptation trajectories—directional hysteresis—whereas the policy’s entropy responds reactively and symmetrically. This hysteresis, measured via switch-aligned interquartile bands, suggests that g_t encodes a history-dependent internal state that shapes perception and action, akin to phenomenological subjectivity. The work highlights the importance of timescale separation and gradient blocking in instilling a primitive form of perspective.

## Method Summary
The experiment uses a 3‑zone grid‑world (each 5×9) with distinct Gaussian observation noise levels. Regime A assigns noise `[0.6, 0.3, 0.05]` to zones Z0, Z1, Z2; Regime B inverts them. The agent receives an 8‑channel observation `x_t` and a one‑hot efference copy of its last action `p_{t-1}`. It learns purely by minimizing one‑step prediction error, with no external reward. The agent’s core is a slow‑fast separation. A fast encoder transforms `(x_t, p_{t-1})` into a perceptual latent `z_t`. A slow global latent `g_t` is updated by a GRU cell that ingests `(z_t, p_{t-1})` and applies damping (`g_t ← (1−d)g_{t-1} + d h_t`) plus a smoothness penalty on successive `g_t` values. The state `s_t = concat(z_t, p_{t-1}, g_t)` is passed through a stop‑gradient before the policy head, which outputs an action distribution `π(a_t|s_t)`. A decoder `D_ψ(g_t, a)` predicts the next observation for each action; the mixture of these predictions weighted by a detached version of `π` yields the agent’s world model prediction `x̂_{t+1}`. Losses combine prediction error, smoothness on `g_t`, an actor term using prediction error of the chosen action as cost, and entropy regularization. After 48 000 steps of training in Regime A, the agent enters a testing phase where regimes switch periodically to probe the internal dynamics of `g_t` and the policy.

## Key Results
- g_t demonstrates clear directional hysteresis during regime transitions (A→B vs B→A), while policy entropy is reactive and direction‑insensitive.
- Post‑training zone occupancy shifts strongly toward the low‑noise zone, indicating successful learning of environmental regularities.
- Switch‑aligned interquartile bands of g‑score (projection of g_t onto a reference direction) diverge asymmetrically for forward vs. backward switches, evidencing history‑dependent internal dynamics proposed as a signature of perspective‑like subjectivity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A slow, globally modulating latent `g_t` can operationalize a history‑dependent, perspective‑like internal state.
- **Mechanism:** The architecture separates a fast reactive policy (`z_t`‑conditioned) from a slowly evolving global latent `g_t` (via GRU damping and smoothness regularization). `g_t` accumulates statistical regularities of the environment over time, creating an internal model of "what kind of world I am in." This slow variable then biases the fast policy's perception‑action loop without being directly optimized for immediate outcomes.
- **Core assumption:** Phenomenological perspective functions as a temporally persistent, global constraint on cognition, not a moment‑to‑moment belief or reward signal.
- **Evidence anchors:**

  [section "A Phenomenologically Coherent Agent Architecture"] "The global latent g is designed to evolve gradually and to encode habitual, slowly varying regularities"  
  [corpus] Weak direct corpus support; neighboring papers focus on latent structures for control or prediction, not specifically on hysteresis as a signature of perspective. Assumption: the proposed slow‑fast separation is novel in its explicit link to phenomenological structure.
- **Break condition:** If `g_t` updates become as fast and reactive as policy entropy during regime shifts (i.e., no temporal dissociation), the mechanism fails to instantiate a persistent "perspective."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Gradient blocking between `g_t` and policy updates (`stopgrad` in Eq. 2, 3) enforces the pre‑reflective, non‑metacognitive character of perspective.
- **Mechanism:** By preventing direct policy‑driven optimization of `g_t`, the latent evolves solely via prediction error signals and smoothness constraints. This prevents `g_t` from becoming an instrumental tool for task performance and instead lets it encode the sedimented history of sensory regularities, analogous to a pre‑thematic experiential orientation.
- **Core assumption:** Perspective is structurally prior to explicit representation and optimization; making it an optimization target would alter its functional role.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section "Learning Dynamics and Objectives"] "strict stop‑gradient separation between policy and perspective updates"  
  [section "Phenomenological Foundations"] "perspective operates in a pre‑reflective manner... shaping processing without being directly optimized"  
  [corpus] Neighbors like HiVAE use hierarchical latents for explicit inference (theory of mind). This design's deliberate non‑optimization of `g_t` is atypical. Assumption: this architectural choice is necessary for the required functional decoupling.
- **Break condition:** If removing `stopgrad` causes `g_t` to track immediate prediction error or action values, the latent becomes a reflexively accessible state rather than a pre‑reflective conditioner.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Direction‑dependent hysteresis in `g_t`'s projection score during regime inversions is a measurable signature of perspective‑like subjectivity.
- **Mechanism:** Because `g_t` evolves slowly and carries momentum from past states, transitions between environmental regimes (A→B vs B→A) produce asymmetric adaptation trajectories. The slow latent's state at switch onset depends on the preceding regime, creating history‑sensitive hysteresis. In contrast, fast policy entropy shows reactive, direction‑insensitive fluctuations.
- **Core assumption:** Hysteresis indicates an internal state with temporal persistence and path‑dependence, mirroring how lived history shapes future experience.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "g_t showed clear directional hysteresis... while policy entropy was reactive and direction‑insensitive"  
  [section "Results and Analysis"] "the global latent exhibits pronounced directional hysteresis... policy entropy shows high short‑term variability but minimal directional asymmetry"  
  [corpus] Neighbors like "Situation Graph Prediction" model evolving perspective states but do not measure hysteresis. "Complexity as Advantage" frames observer‑dependent regret; hysteresis may be one instantiation of such observer‑relative dynamics. Assumption: hysteresis is a necessary signature; other dynamics could potentially satisfy the phenomenological criteria.
- **Break condition:** If `g_t`'s projection trajectories for A→B and B→A are symmetric once aligned on switch time, the latent encodes only the current regime, not a history‑dependent perspective.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Observations `x_t` and previous action `p_{t-1}` feed into an encoder producing perceptual latent `z_t`. `z_t` and `p_{t-1}` also update the slow global latent `g_t` via a damped GRU. The concatenated state `s_t = [z_t, p_{t-1}, g_t]` passes through a stop‑gradient before the policy head to yield action distribution `π(a_t|s_t)`. A decoder `D_ψ(g_t, a)` predicts the next observation for each action; the mixture of these predictions weighted by a detached `π` produces the model's prediction `x̂_{t+1}`. Losses: `L_pred` (MSE between `x̂_{t+1}` and actual `x_{t+1}`), `L_smooth` (MSE between `g_t` and `stopgrad(g_{t-1})`), `L_actor` (using prediction error of executed action as cost), and entropy regularization.

### Critical Path
For each timestep:
1. Encode `(x_t, p_{t-1})` → `z_t`.
2. Update `g_t` via damped GRU using `(z_t, p_{t-1})`.
3. Form `s_t = concat(z_t, p_{t-1}, g_t)`.
4. Apply stopgrad to `s_t`, pass through policy head → `π(a|s_t)`.
5. Sample action `a_t` from `π`.
6. Execute `a_t`, observe `x_{t+1}`.
7. Compute predicted `x̂_{t+1} = Σ_a π̃(a) * D_ψ(g_t, a)` where `π̃ = stopgrad(π)`.
8. Compute losses: `L_pred = MSE(x̂_{t+1}, x_{t+1})`; `L_smooth = MSE(g_t, stopgrad(g_{t-1}))`; `c_t = MSE(D_ψ(g_t, a_t), x_{t+1})`; `L_actor = -(c_t - b_t) * log π(a_t)` (b_t is baseline); plus entropy term.
9. Backpropagate total loss `L = Σ_t [L_pred + λ_smooth L_smooth + λ_actor L_actor - λ_ent H(π)]` with gradient clip 1.0.
After 48k steps, enter regime‑switching test phase.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Reward‑free vs. task performance:** The absence of extrinsic reward isolates internal perspective dynamics but may limit behavioral goals; introducing reward could interfere with hysteresis.
- **Slow g_t timescale:** Damping factor `d` and smoothness weight `λ_smooth` must balance persistence and adaptability. Too slow → g_t never adapts; too fast → loses hysteresis.
- **Gradient blocking:** Stop‑gradient on `s_t` before policy head is crucial to keep g_t pre‑reflective, but it also prevents g_t from directly optimizing prediction, relying on indirect signals.

### Failure Signatures
- **Loss spikes** after a regime switch indicate world model or g_t misalignment.
- **Entropy collapse** (policy becomes deterministic) suggests g_t is not providing stabilizing bias; check `λ_ent` or `L_actor` weighting.
- **Symmetrical g‑score trajectories** (no directional hysteresis) imply g_t is not sufficiently slow or stopgrad is absent; inspect `d` and `λ_smooth`.
- **Unbounded g_t norm growth** signals insufficient `L_smooth` regularization.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline training:** Train in Regime A for 48k steps. Verify zone occupancy shifts to low‑noise zone (Z2) by late episodes and g_t IQR remains low.
2. **Single switch test:** After a 150‑step warm‑up in Regime A, switch to Regime B. Plot g‑score and policy entropy for 200 steps post‑switch; expect gradual g‑score change and a spike in entropy that settles.
3. **Quantile‑aligned hysteresis:** Run multiple episodes with periodic regime switches (period `P=40`). For each switch event, extract g‑score trajectory aligned to switch time. Compute median trajectory across switches and seeds, then compare IQR bands for A→B vs B→A; significant divergence confirms directional hysteresis.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Causal intervention on g_t:** Would artificially clamping g_t to values from opposite regimes alter adaptation trajectories? The paper proposes future work with `do(g)` but provides no evidence.
- **Generalization to LLM agents:** Can the slow global latent and hysteresis signature be implemented in large language model agents operating in linguistic environments? This remains untested.
- **Sufficiency of hysteresis:** Is directional hysteresis unique to the proposed architecture, or could other slow‑adaptation mechanisms (e.g., slow RL, slow world models) produce similar patterns? No control conditions.
- **Robustness to richer environments:** Does the mechanism hold under partial observability, multi‑agent interactions, or naturalistic stimuli? Experiments only used simple noise‑based regime shifts.
- **Comparison to standard world models:** How does g_t differ from latents in architectures like Dreamer’s RSSM in encoding history‑dependent structure? Empirical comparison lacking.

## Limitations
- Key hyperparameters (damping factor `d`, smoothing weight `λ_smooth`, latent dimensions, baseline implementation) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication and sensitivity analysis.
- The claim that hysteresis uniquely indicates perspective‑like subjectivity lacks direct controls; alternative slow‑varying mechanisms could produce similar asymmetries.
- The reward‑free setting isolates internal dynamics but may not generalize to agents with extrinsic goals; effect of rewards on hysteresis is unknown.

## Confidence
- Overall claim (slow global latent operationalizes subjective perspective): **Medium**
- Mechanism 1 (slow‑fast separation as temporal persistence): **Medium**
- Mechanism 2 (stopgrad enforces pre‑reflectivity): **Medium‑Low**
- Mechanism 3 (directional hysteresis as measurable signature): **Medium**

## Next Checks
- **Hyperparameter sweep:** Vary damping factor `d` and smoothing weight `λ_smooth` across orders of magnitude; quantify hysteresis (IQR divergence between A→B and B→A trajectories) to identify robustness and minimal slow‑fast separation.
- **Stop‑gradient ablation:** Remove `stopgrad` from policy updates, keep all other constraints; compare hysteresis and g‑score dynamics to the full model. Significant reduction would confirm the role of gradient blocking.
- **Reward perturbation:** Introduce a small extrinsic reward (e.g., +1 for entering the low‑noise zone) while preserving g_t modulation. Test whether hysteresis persists or diminishes, probing reward‑independence.
