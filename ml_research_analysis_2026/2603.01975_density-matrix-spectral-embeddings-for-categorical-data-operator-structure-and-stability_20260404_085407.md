# Density-Matrix Spectral Embeddings for Categorical Data: Operator Structure and Stability

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.01975
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01975
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: A supervised dimensionality reduction method for categorical data using a density‑matrix operator that yields low‑dimensional spectral embeddings with bounded rank and demonstrates high accuracy and robustness.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces a novel supervised dimensionality reduction technique for categorical data based on a density‑matrix operator derived from class‑conditional frequencies. The operator is positive semidefinite, unit‑trace, and has rank at most the number of classes, enabling low‑dimensional spectral embeddings. Classification is performed by kernel density estimation in the embedded space. Theoretical analysis provides Davis‑Kahan subspace stability and perturbation bounds under multinomial sampling. Empirical evaluations on synthetic data demonstrate high accuracy (up to 1.0), robustness to sparsity, noise, and class imbalance, with the maximum‑likelihood decision rule preserving balanced accuracy under severe imbalance while MAP degrades.

## Method Summary
Given a labeled dataset with categorical features encoded as one‑hot vectors, the method first computes class‑conditional frequency vectors by summing one‑hot vectors per class. These frequencies are entrywise square‑root transformed to obtain an amplitude matrix. The density operator ρ is formed as ρ = X Xᵀ / tr(X Xᵀ), which is PSD and unit‑trace. Its spectral decomposition is obtained efficiently via the Gram matrix G = Xᵀ X (size k × k), avoiding the high‑dimensional d × d matrix. The top‑r eigenvectors U_r define the spectral embedding: for a sample x, the coordinate is π_r(x) = U_rᵀ (x/√q). In the latent space, a separate kernel density estimator is fitted for each class, and classification is done by the maximum a posteriori rule using these densities.

## Key Results
- The density‑matrix operator has intrinsic rank bounded by the number of classes, guaranteeing low‑dimensional embeddings.
- Theoretical guarantees include Davis‑Kahan subspace stability and high‑probability perturbation bounds under multinomial sampling, assuming a spectral gap and positive class‑conditional probabilities.
- Synthetic experiments show perfect or near‑perfect accuracy across a range of sparsity, noise, and cardinality settings; the maximum‑likelihood rule remains robust under severe class imbalance while MAP accuracy collapses.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Square‑root lift and Bhattacharyya geometry**  
Claim: The entrywise square‑root lifting `X = √F` induces a Hellinger/Bhattacharyya geometry on class‑conditional profiles, making inner products in the lifted space equal to Bhattacharyya affinities.  
Mechanism: For class profiles `p̂_y = f_y / ‖f_y‖₁`, the amplitude vectors `ψ̂_y = √p̂_y` lie on the unit sphere. Their inner product `⟨ψ̂_y, ψ̂_y'⟩ = Σ_i √(p̂_y(i) p̂_y'(i))` is exactly the Bhattacharyya coefficient, a bounded similarity measure.  
Core assumption: Class profiles are strictly positive to ensure the square root is Lipschitz on a uniform interval (Assumption 5.12). Without positivity, the lift may be discontinuous at zeros.  
Evidence: Abstract mentions "Hellinger/Bhattacharyya geometry" and "amplitude transform"; Section 5.2, Proposition 5.6 derives the affinity Gram matrix.  
Break condition: If any two class profiles have disjoint supports (some features present in one class but never in the other), Bhattacharyya affinity drops to zero for those features, potentially collapsing discriminative signal.

**Mechanism 2: Intrinsic rank bound**  
Claim: The density operator ρ_D has intrinsic rank at most the number of classes k, guaranteeing that the spectral embedding dimension is bounded by k independently of the ambient one‑hot dimension d.  
Mechanism: The amplitude matrix X (from √F) satisfies ρ_D ∝ X Xᵀ. Since rank(X) ≤ k, rank(ρ_D) ≤ k. All nonzero eigenvalues of ρ_D are recoverable from the k × k Gram matrix G = Xᵀ X (Proposition 7.1).  
Core assumption: None beyond the construction; holds deterministically for any finite dataset.  
Evidence: Abstract states "intrinsic rank bound controlled by the number of classes"; Section 3, Proposition 3.6 proves rank(ρ_D) ≤ k; Section 7.2 shows eigenvector computation via G.  
Break condition: When k is large (many classes), the bound still holds but the embedding dimension may not reduce meaningfully if k is comparable to d.

**Mechanism 3: Davis‑Kahan stability under sampling**  
Claim: Under a spectral gap δ_r = λ_r(ρ^⋆) - λ_{r+1}(ρ^⋆) > 0, the dominant r‑dimensional invariant subspace of the empirical operator ρ̂^⋆ is stable under sampling perturbations, as quantified by a Davis‑Kahan sin Θ bound.  
Mechanism: Theorem 5.8 gives ‖sin Θ(Ū_{⋆,r}, U_{⋆,r})‖₂ ≤ ‖ρ̂^⋆ - ρ^⋆‖₂ / δ_r. Theorem 5.14 provides a high‑probability bound on ‖ρ̂^⋆ - ρ^⋆‖₂ under multinomial sampling, assuming class profiles are positive (Assumption 5.12).  
Core assumption: A non‑zero spectral gap exists (Assumption 5.9) and empirical class frequencies concentrate (Assumption 5.12). Without a gap, the bound degenerates.  
Evidence: Abstract highlights "Davis‑Kahan subspace stability and perturbation bounds"; Section 5.3, Theorem 5.11 states the stability bound; Section 5.4, Theorem 5.14 gives the high‑probability perturbation estimate.  
Break condition: If the top r eigenvalues of ρ^⋆ are nearly equal (δ_r ≈ 0), even tiny perturbations can cause large rotations in the estimated subspace.

## Foundational Learning
- **Positive semidefinite (PSD) operators**  
  Why needed: ρ_D must be PSD to admit a real eigendecomposition and interpretable spectral embedding; the unit‑trace property further aligns with density‑matrix analogies.  
  Quick check: What would fail if ρ_D had a negative eigenvalue?  
  *Answer: The spectral embedding would be undefined or complex; geometric interpretation breaks.*

- **Spectral gap condition**  
  Why needed: Guarantees that the dominant eigenspace is well‑separated and stable to perturbations (Davis‑Kahan). Without a gap, the embedding may be highly sensitive to sampling noise.  
  Quick check: If λ₂(ρ^⋆) is very close to λ₃(ρ^⋆), what happens to the 2‑dimensional embedding?  
  *Answer: The subspace spanned by the top two eigenvectors may rotate significantly under small data perturbations.*

- **Entrywise square‑root transform**  
  Why needed: Maps categorical probability vectors to the unit sphere, turning the Bhattacharyya affinity into an inner product. This geometry is central to the operator’s construction and stability.  
  Quick check: Why not use raw class‑conditional frequencies f_y directly?  
  *Answer: Raw frequencies lack normalization; inner products would not correspond to a bounded similarity measure and would scale with class sizes.*

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
1. Input encoding: One‑hot survey vectors x ∈ {0,1}^d and labels y ∈ {e₁,…,e_k}.  
2. Frequency accumulation: Build F = [f₁|…|f_k] by summing x per class.  
3. Amplitude lifting: X = √F (entrywise).  
4. Density operator: ρ_D = X Xᵀ / tr(X Xᵀ).  
5. Spectral truncation: Extract top‑r eigenvectors U_r ∈ ℝ^{d × r} via low‑rank route (Gram matrix G = Xᵀ X).  
6. Embedding: π_r(x) = U_rᵀ (x/√q).  
7. Latent KDE: For each class y, fit kernel density on Π_y = {π_r(x^{(j)}) : y^{(j)} = y}.  
8. Prediction: ℓ_D(x) = argmax_y π_y · f_h(π_r(x) | y) (with optional priors).

**Critical path:** The most compute‑intensive step is the eigendecomposition of the k × k Gram matrix G = Xᵀ X (complexity O(d k² + k³)). This avoids forming the d × d operator explicitly. The embedding for a new point is cheap (O(q r)) due to one‑hot sparsity.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Count‑based ρ_D vs class‑normalized ρ_CN: ρ_D weights classes by empirical sizes; ρ_CN uses uniform weights. Imbalance can distort ρ_D unless priors are adjusted (Section 5.6, Experiment S4).  
- Embedding dimension r: Must be ≤ k. Smaller r reduces KDE variance but may increase bias; choose via spectral decay or validation.  
- Kernel/bandwidth: Bandwidth h in KDE heavily influences latent density estimates; cross‑validation recommended (Section 6.5, Remark 6.5).

**Failure signatures:**  
- MAP rule collapse under severe class imbalance: Table 4 (Experiment S4) shows MAP balanced accuracy drops to ~0.5 at 95/5 imbalance, while ML remains ~0.997.  
- Spectral instability: If empirical ρ̂ deviates from ρ^⋆ and the gap δ_r is small, subspace rotation sin Θ blows up (Corollary 5.16).  
- Zero Bhattacharyya affinities: If class profiles have disjoint supports on all features, G becomes diagonal, embedding loses discriminative power.

**First 3 experiments:**  
- **S1 (Controlled separation):** Varies separation δ between informative class profiles. DMM+KDE shows strong performance at moderate δ (e.g., 0.586 accuracy at δ=0.2) and perfect at δ=1. Demonstrates sensitivity to signal strength.  
- **S2 (High cardinality/sparsity):** Scales ambient dimension d by increasing block modalities. Accuracy remains ~0.98 across d=75 to 250, confirming robustness to sparsity (intrinsic rank bound).  
- **S3 (Irrelevant variables):** Adds purely noise blocks (α = q_noise/q). Performance stable up to α=2.0, showing invariance to class‑independent categorical noise.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Alternative monotone transforms:** The paper uses entrywise square‑root lifting; exploring other monotone transforms could yield different operator properties. Unresolved because no systematic comparison exists. Resolution would require experiments and theoretical analysis linking transforms to induced distances.  
2. **Kernel bandwidth selection in latent KDE:** Bandwidth critically affects classification, especially under imbalance or multimodal distributions, but no tailored method is provided. Resolution would involve cross‑validation or plug‑in studies specific to the bounded latent space.  
3. **Incorporating pairwise/higher‑order co‑occurrences:** The current construction uses only class‑conditional frequencies, ignoring interactions. The paper notes this as a direction. Resolution would involve defining augmented operators with cross‑products and evaluating on data where interactions matter.  
4. **Bayes‑consistency and finite‑sample bounds:** The paper lacks theoretical guarantees under explicit generative models. Resolution would require proofs under assumptions like blockwise multinomial and derivation of excess risk bounds.  
5. **Impact of violating strict positivity:** The assumption that class‑conditional probabilities are bounded away from zero may not hold for sparse categorical data. The robustness of the embedding to zeros is not studied. Resolution would need simulations with zero probabilities and analysis of smoothed variants.

## Limitations
- **Replication details missing:** Synthetic experiments (S1–S4) lack exact hyperparameters (sample sizes, modality counts, separation values, noise levels) and KDE bandwidth selection, preventing exact reproduction.
- **Strict positivity assumption may be unrealistic:** For sparse categorical data, many class‑conditional probabilities can be zero, violating Assumption 5.12 and potentially breaking the Lipschitz continuity needed for perturbation bounds.
- **Rank bound ceiling:** While rank ≤ k, when the number of classes k is large the embedding dimension may not be sufficiently reduced; the choice of r remains heuristic.

## Confidence
- **Intrinsic rank bound (≤ k):** High confidence (deterministic proof, independent of assumptions).
- **Square‑root lift yields Bhattacharyya geometry:** Medium confidence (relies on strictly positive probabilities; zero probabilities cause discontinuities).
- **Spectral stability under sampling (Davis‑Kahan):** Medium confidence (requires a non‑zero spectral gap and concentration of empirical frequencies, which may not always hold).
- **Empirical robustness (accuracy, sparsity, noise, imbalance):** Medium confidence (results are promising but full hyperparameter details are missing, limiting independent verification).
