# Multi-Task Learning with Additive U-Net for Image Denoising and Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12649
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12649
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** Additive U-Net (AddUNet) achieves stable multi-task training for denoising and classification, with minimal PSNR drop (0.44 dB) and 89.4% classification accuracy, while learned skip gates enable post-training control.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces AddUNet, a variant of the U-Net architecture that replaces concatenative skip connections with additive fusion modulated by scalar gates. The design constrains feature dimensionality to stabilize joint optimization in multi-task learning (MTL) settings, specifically combining image denoising with an auxiliary classification task. Experiments show that AddUNet maintains competitive denoising performance (e.g., 29.21 dB PSNR at σ=25 on Kodak-17) while achieving high classification accuracy (89.4% on EMNIST). The learned gates redistribute information flow: shallow skips dominate reconstruction, deeper skips feed classification, implying implicit task decoupling. Furthermore, these gates provide a stable interface for post-training modulation of the trade-off between tasks without performance collapse.

## Method Summary
AddUNet modifies the standard U-Net by making skip connections additive rather than concatenative. Each decoder stage receives an upsampled signal from the previous decoder layer plus a scaled copy of the corresponding encoder feature map: `decoder_input = upsample(prev) + α * encoder_features`, where α is a learned nonnegative scalar. This enforces fixed channel dimensionality throughout the network. For multi-task learning, a lightweight classification head is attached to the encoder bottleneck. Training uses a weighted sum of a Charbonnier denoising loss and a cross-entropy classification loss, with the classification weight λ ramped up during early training to stabilize optimization.

## Key Results
- Competitive single-task denoising performance (e.g., 29.21 dB PSNR on Kodak-17 with σ=25) despite fixed channel capacity.
- In multi-task denoising-classification on EMNIST, reconstruction degrades by only 0.44 dB PSNR while classification reaches 89.4% accuracy.
- Learned scalar gates show systematic redistribution: shallow skips have high weights for reconstruction, deep skips are suppressed, supporting implicit task decoupling.
- Additive regularization yields more stable joint training and better generalization, reducing train-test accuracy gaps (e.g., 1.94% on EMNIST mixed noise vs. >2% in baselines).
- Gates allow smooth post-training modulation of skip contributions, enabling controlled trade-offs between tasks without retraining.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Architectural Regularization via Additive Fusion
Additive skip fusion acts as an architectural regularizer by limiting shortcut capacity. Replacing concatenation with addition enforces fixed feature dimensionality across encoder-decoder stages, preventing the implicit channel expansion that can occur with concatenation. This constraint is argued to improve multi-task optimization stability and reduce overfitting by discouraging over-reliance on shallow features and promoting disciplined feature reuse. Evidence includes the paper's claim that this "stabilizes joint optimization" and yields a "lightweight architecture with stable optimization behavior." A break condition would be tasks requiring highly divergent feature scales or dimensionality, where the fixed-channel constraint could be excessively restrictive.

### Mechanism 2: Implicit Task Decoupling via Learned Gating
Under multi-task pressure, the learned scalar gates induce an implicit redistribution of information flow: shallow skips (capturing high-frequency details) remain strong for reconstruction, while deeper skips (carrying semantic abstractions) are suppressed and diverted to the classification head. This specialization arises from the optimization dynamics and the additive gating mechanism. Evidence includes direct measurements of gate values (Table II): in MTL vs. denoise-only, weights shift from a relatively even distribution `[0.188,0.071,0.057,0.079,0.022]` to a more shallow-biased one, with deep weights drastically reduced. The break condition would be if the auxiliary task also demanded fine spatial details (e.g., segmentation), which might harm both tasks.

### Mechanism 3: Stable Post-Training Modulation Interface
The scalar gates provide a stable knob for inference-time control. Because the decoder's computation depends linearly on each gated skip's contribution (due to additive fusion), adjusting an individual gate α_i scales its effect smoothly. The paper shows experimentally (Figure 5) that varying one gate (e.g., α₃) around its trained value produces a smooth, unimodal response in PSNR/SSIM without abrupt collapse. The break condition is that gates pushed far from their trained values (especially near zero) can cause rapid degradation due to insufficient shortcut information; smoothness is only local.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Noisy Input -> Encoder (with residual blocks) -> Bottleneck -> [Path A: Decoder (additive gated skips) -> Denoised Output]; [Path B: Lightweight CNN Classification Head -> Class Label]. Gates: one scalar α per encoder-decoder depth level applied as additive scaling.

**Critical path:** Data flows from input through the encoder, caching features at each depth. The deepest features branch to the classification head. The decoder upsamples and sequentially adds gated encoder features from corresponding depths. Gradients from both denoising (Charbonnier) and classification (cross-entropy) losses backpropagate through the shared encoder and decoder.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Additive vs. Concatenative Skips:* Additive limits capacity (fixed channels) but improves stability and interpretability; concatenative increases capacity and may yield higher peak PSNR but risks interference in MTL.
- *Fixed Channel Dimensionality:* Simplifies analysis and regularization but may constrain representational power compared to expanding U-Nets.
- *Scalar Gate Simplicity:* Extremely lightweight modulation (1 param per skip) vs. more complex attention/FiLM; trade-off is reduced flexibility for intuitive control.

**Failure signatures:**
- *Training instability in MTL:* If λ (classification loss weight) is not ramped or is too high, classification loss may dominate, causing reconstruction collapse.
- *Gate collapse:* Learned α values may approach zero for all skips, effectively removing skip connections and degrading reconstruction.
- *Poor generalization:* Without additive regularization, the model may overfit to noise patterns, showing large train-test accuracy gaps on classification.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline single-task denoising:** Train AddUNet on Kodak-17 with σ=25. Verify PSNR ~29.2 dB (as in Table I) and confirm it is competitive with pseudo-additive and autoencoder baselines.
2. **Visualize MTL skip weight redistribution:** Train AddUNet on EMNIST with MTL (noise σ=0.2). Extract final gate values α_i. Confirm pattern: shallow weights larger, deep weights suppressed (as in Table II). Also verify classification accuracy >89% and PSNR drop <0.5 dB vs. denoise-only.
3. **Test post-training gate modulation:** Take a trained MTL model from (2). Fix all weights, vary one gate (e.g., α₃) across [0,1]. Plot PSNR/SSIM. Confirm smooth, unimodal response peaking near trained value (as in Figure 5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** To what extent does the observed task-aware redistribution of skip weights generalize beyond the denoising-classification pair to other MTL formulations, such as segmentation-denoising or depth-estimation-classification?
- **Open Question 2:** How does the capacity of the auxiliary classification head quantitatively affect the trade-off between denoising degradation and classification accuracy?
- **Open Question 3:** What is the causal mechanism by which additive (vs. concatenative) skip fusion induces the emergent hierarchical frequency organization in encoder filters?
- **Open Question 4:** What is the fundamental trade-off between aggressive intensity-level correction (favored by pseudo-additive/unconstrained skips) and structural/feature regularization (favored by gated additive skips) across different noise models?
- **Open Question 5:** How do multiple scalar skip gates interact during post-training modulation? Can they be adjusted independently without performance collapse?

## Limitations
- Datasets are limited to grayscale (Kodak-17, EMNIST); generalizability to color images and diverse real-world tasks is unknown.
- Mechanistic claims (e.g., skip weight redistribution causing decoupling) are inferential; no causal ablation or intervention experiments are provided.
- Critical hyperparameters (λ schedule, channel width, learning rate details) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication and sensitivity analysis.
- Fixed-channel additive fusion may restrict representational capacity for tasks requiring flexible multi-scale feature recombination.
- Classification head is intentionally lightweight; results may not extend to stronger backbones or more complex auxiliary tasks.

## Confidence
- **Regularization & stability:** Medium – Reduced train-test gaps and stable MTL training are shown, but contributions from other design choices (loss weighting, head architecture) are not fully isolated.
- **Skip weight redistribution:** Medium-High – Direct α measurements confirm strong redistribution patterns; causality for task decoupling remains inferred.
- **Post‑training modulation:** High – Figure 5 demonstrates smooth variation when varying a single gate, confirming a stable local interface.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-dataset generalization:** Train AddUNet on color denoising (e.g., DIV2K) and multi-task with a stronger classifier (e.g., ResNet-18) on a diverse dataset (e.g., CIFAR-100). Evaluate reconstruction metrics, classification accuracy, and skip weight patterns.
2. **Causal test of redistribution:** Freeze a jointly trained MTL model's weights. Manually set all skip gates to (i) uniformly high values (denoise-only style) or (ii) the model's learned MTL distribution. Measure both tasks' performance. Expect classification drop in (i) and reconstruction drop in (ii) if redistribution is critical.
3. **Multi-gate modulation surface:** Sweep two gate values (e.g., α₁ and α₃) over a grid while reconstructing. Plot PSNR and classification accuracy to detect nonlinear interactions or abrupt performance cliffs beyond single-gate smoothness.
