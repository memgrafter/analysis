# Incremental, inconsistency-resilient reasoning over Description Logic Abox streams

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.01799  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01799  
- **Reference count:** 27  
- **Primary result:** Novel timestamp-based preferred repair semantics for sliding-window ABox streams enable incremental materialization maintenance in OWL2 RL, where newer assertions override older ones during inconsistency resolution and derived facts are attributed to the oldest supporting assertion, allowing deletions by simply dropping expired interpretations.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a theoretical framework for incremental reasoning over streams of Description Logic ABoxes under a sliding window. It introduces a canonical window model that assigns each derived fact to the timestamp of the oldest ABox assertion used in its derivation. This attribution supports efficient updates: when the window slides, discarding an expired ABox’s entire interpretation automatically removes all facts derived solely from it, eliminating backward reasoning. Inconsistencies are handled via a “new is always better” policy: conflicts are resolved by preferentially deleting older assertions according to a timestamp-based preorder. For OWL2 RL, semi‑naive algorithms are provided for adding new ABoxes to a window materialization and for sliding the window, along with a conflict-resolution procedure for non‑recursive negative inclusions. The approach guarantees deterministic, guess‑free repair and correctness via formal proofs. The work is purely theoretical; no implementation or experiments are provided.

## Method Summary
The method reasons over a stream of finite, timestamped ABoxes and a fixed OWL2 RL TBox. The **canonical window model** constructs a separate interpretation `I_t` for each ABox timestamp `t`. During forward chaining (Algorithm 1), when a rule body is satisfied by facts from multiple interpretations, the head fact is added to the interpretation corresponding to the oldest timestamp among those sources. This creates a dependency on the oldest supporting assertion. The **window order** (Definition 6) defines a preorder on subsets of the window ABox where newer timestamps dominate, and **window repairs** (Definition 7) are consistent subsets maximal under this preorder. **Inconsistency resolution** (Algorithm 3) for non‑recursive negative inclusions `B ⊑ ⊥` identifies minimal conflict sets `S` where `S ⊨ B`, then computes deletion sets `D` (oldest singletons) and `N` (minimal older sets) to resolve the most recent conflicts first. **Sliding** (Algorithm 2) discards interpretations for timestamps exiting the window and adds new ABoxes via Algorithm 1. Correctness is proven: dropping an ABox removes its interpretation and all facts depending solely on it (Proposition 2), and repairs are inherited incrementally (Lemma 2).

## Key Results
- Canonical window model with per‑timestamp interpretations enables incremental materialization by attributing derived facts to the oldest supporting assertion.
- Timestamp‑based preferred repair semantics (“new is always better”) provides deterministic inconsistency resolution for non‑recursive negative inclusions.
- Semi‑naive algorithms for adding ABoxes and sliding windows avoid full recomputation; deletions are handled by simply dropping expired interpretations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Incremental window materialization is achieved by assigning derived facts to the interpretation of the oldest ABox assertion used in their derivation.
- **Mechanism:** The canonical window model (Definition 8) constructs an interpretation `I_t` for each ABox timestamp `t`. When a rule body is satisfied across multiple interpretations, the head is added to `I_t` where `t` is the minimum timestamp among the used ABoxes. This directly links facts to their oldest source.
- **Core assumption:** Dropping an ABox from the window invalidates its entire interpretation `I_t`, automatically removing all facts whose oldest supporting assertion was in that ABox (Proposition 2). No backward reasoning is needed.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] “incrementally computing the materialization of the window based on the materialization of the previous window”  
  - [section 5.1] “the head must be added to the oldest interpretation used in its derivation”  
  - [corpus] Analogous to incremental view maintenance (e.g., DRed, FBF) but with timestamp attribution; indirect support via known incremental principles.
- **Break condition:** If a fact has independent derivations involving both old and new ABoxes, it may survive the drop of the oldest ABox because another derivation remains. The mechanism still holds because the fact’s oldest timestamp is not necessarily the dropped one.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Inconsistency resolution under volatility follows a deterministic “new is always better” policy via timestamp‑based preference levels.
- **Mechanism:** The window order (Definition 6) is a preorder on ABox subsets where `A ≤_w A'` if every assertion in `A` is no newer than some in `A'`. Window repairs are maximal consistent subsets under `≤_w`. Algorithm 3 resolves each negative inclusion `B ⊑ ⊥` by: (1) finding minimal conflict sets `S` where `S ⊨ B`; (2) sorting `S` by recency (max of min timestamp); (3) iteratively building deletion sets `D` (oldest singleton from most recent conflict) and `N` (minimal older sets from remaining conflicts) until all conflicts are broken. This ensures the newest conflicting facts are preserved.
- **Core assumption:** In a streaming setting, newer data is more reliable than older data; thus conflicts should be resolved by trusting the newest information and discarding the oldest contradictory facts.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] “timestamp‑based preferred repair semantics, where newer assertions are prioritized over older ones”  
  - [section 4] “New is always better.” and Example 3 demonstrating timestamp‑based deletion.  
  - [corpus] Preferred repair semantics exist in static DL literature [6]; timestamp‑driven application to streams is novel.
- **Break condition:** The assumption fails if newer data is less trustworthy (e.g., sensor drift) or if background knowledge should override temporal priority. The semantics would then need a different preorder.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Semi‑naive incremental algorithms avoid recomputation by reusing prior window materialization and applying targeted changes during slides.
- **Mechanism:** Algorithm 1 adds a new ABox `A_t` to a window model `{I_s}`: initialize `Δ = I_t` (canonical model of `A_t`). Iterate: for each rule, if body facts come from `Δ` and some `I_s`, add head to the oldest involved interpretation, accumulating new facts in `Δ`. Repeat until `Δ` empty. Algorithm 2 slides: drop interpretations `I_s` for `s < t_start`; for each new ABox in the extended window, call Algorithm 1. The reuse is explicit: line 1 of Algorithm 2 captures `{I_t}_{t∈I∩I'}` from the prior model.
- **Core assumption:** Consecutive windows overlap heavily; thus most ABoxes and their interpretations persist. The oldest‑timestamp attribution ensures that dropping an ABox’s interpretation removes all facts depending solely on it, so only additions need processing.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] “semi‑naive algorithms… for incremental materialization maintenance”  
  - [section 5.2] Algorithm 2 explicitly reuses `{I_t}_{t∈I∩I'}`.  
  - [corpus] IMaRS [13] and LASER [4] use timestamp horizons for deletion; this adapts the idea to DL with repair.
- **Break condition:** High churn (small slide relative to window) reduces overlap, diminishing incremental gains. Non‑monotonic rules or recursive inconsistencies could break the attribution guarantee.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Stream Ingest -> Window Manager -> Materialization Engine & Inconsistency Resolver -> Window Model Store
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Ingest new ABox `A_t`.  
  2. Resolve inconsistencies between `A_t` and current window ABox union via Algorithm 3 → obtain cleaned `(A_W ∪ A_t) \ D`.  
  3. Compute canonical model for `A_t` (line 1 of Algorithm 1).  
  4. Merge into window model via Algorithm 1’s semi‑naive delta iteration.  
  5. On window slide: drop interpretations for earliest timestamps; for each new ABox in extended interval, repeat steps 2–4.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Non‑recursive inconsistencies only: Limitation; remediable by depth‑limited NI rewriting at cost of more rule checks (Section 7). Tradeoff: completeness vs. runtime.  
  - No background knowledge: Extensible by treating background as infinite‑timestamp ABox always included; ensures it’s never deleted but adds to conflict checks.  
  - OWL2 RL specificity: Other OWL profiles lack canonical models; adaptation requires analogous “minimal” model construction, which may not exist finitely.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Unhandled recursive negative inclusion: Algorithm 3’s NI rewriting fails → unresolved inconsistency → materialization halts or incorrect.  
  - Timestamp disorder: Stream violates minimum δ assumption → window order not a valid preorder → repair semantics undefined.  
  - Stale facts after slide: Derived facts from dropped ABox remain → check against recomputed scratch model; indicates attribution tracking bug.  
  - Conflict set detection error: Algorithm 3 removes wrong facts → verify minimality and entailment of conflict sets.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Incremental slide efficiency:** Compare wall‑clock time of Algorithm 2 vs. full recompute for sliding windows with 50%–90% overlap on synthetic OWL2 RL stream; measure scaling with window size.  
  2. **Conflict resolution correctness:** For streams with injected contradictory ABoxes, verify materialization after each slide respects “new is better” by checking that oldest contradictory assertions are absent; cross‑check against brute‑force repair enumeration for small windows.  
  3. **Recursive NI handling depth:** Extend Algorithm 3 with depth‑limited NI rewriting (e.g., unfold `A ⊓ ∃R.B ⊑ ⊥` to depth 2). Measure false positive/negative inconsistency detections vs. ground truth as depth varies.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the actual performance (latency, throughput, memory usage) of the proposed incremental algorithms (Algorithms 1–3) under realistic streaming conditions with varying window sizes, stream velocities, and inconsistency rates?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper explicitly states in the abstract and conclusion that “no experimental evaluation” was performed and that it is a “core goal of our future work.”
- **Why unresolved:** The algorithms are theoretically correct and designed for efficiency, but their practical scalability, overhead from inconsistency resolution (Algorithm 3), and comparison to non‑incremental or other incremental approaches (e.g., IMaRS, LASER) remain unknown. Real‑world streams may introduce patterns (e.g., bursty updates, complex TBoxes) that affect performance unpredictably.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comprehensive empirical evaluation using benchmark streaming datasets (synthetic and real‑world, e.g., sensor networks, social media) with controlled parameters (window size, slide interval, TBox complexity, inconsistency frequency). Metrics should include end‑to‑end latency per window slide, throughput (facts processed/time), memory footprint, and comparison against baseline incremental and non‑incremental reasoners.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the proposed approach for handling non‑recursive inconsistencies be effectively extended to handle recursive negative inclusions via depth‑limited rule rewriting, and what is the trade‑off between the rewrite depth and the correctness/performance of the materialization?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper acknowledges the limitation to non‑recursive inconsistencies in Section 7 and suggests “including rewritten version of recursive negative inclusions up to some predetermined depth” as a remedy, but notes it “would likely impact performance” and is untested.
- **Why unresolved:** The depth‑limited rewriting introduces an unbounded number of new rules. It is unclear how to compute the necessary rewrite depth for a given TBox and stream, whether the resulting system remains sound and complete for the intended “new is always better” repair semantics, and how the combinatorial explosion of rules affects the incremental algorithms’ efficiency.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A formal analysis of the rewriting procedure for recursive negative inclusions in the context of the window order semantics, proving soundness/completeness relative to a depth bound. An empirical study measuring the change in materialization size, number of conflict sets (Algorithm 3), and runtime as a function of rewrite depth on TBoxes with known recursive inconsistencies.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can the incremental, inconsistency‑resilient semantics be adapted to the OWL2 EL and QL profiles, which lack a finite canonical model akin to OWL2 RL, and what alternative minimal model constructions or approximation techniques are necessary to support incremental maintenance?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion identifies adaptation to other OWL2 profiles as a “major challenge” because they “lack a general finite ‘minimal’ model like the canonical model of RL knowledge bases,” though the authors believe “similar properties” exist.
- **Why unresolved:** The core incremental algorithm (Definition 8, Algorithm 1) relies critically on the existence and properties of the canonical model for RL (e.g., unique minimal model, forward chaining termination). EL and QL have different reasoning characteristics (e.g., EL uses hypertableau, QL uses query rewriting); it is unclear how to define a “minimal” window model that supports the same incremental drop‑and‑add properties (Proposition 2) without a canonical model.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of a suitable model construction (e.g., a “canonical” structure for EL under the window semantics, or an partitioning‑based approach for QL) that satisfies the required incremental properties. Formal proofs that this construction allows for incremental updates analogous to Proposition 2. A prototype implementation for a fragment of EL/QL demonstrating the incremental maintenance procedure.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What are the precise formal semantics and algorithmic modifications required to integrate a static, high‑priority background ABox (with timestamp +∞) into the window‑based stream reasoning framework, ensuring background facts are never removed during inconsistency repair?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 7 briefly mentions that semantics “could be modified to this scenario… by having a background ABox that is the starting ABox of every window and is of the highest priority in the repair order, i.e. it has timestamp +∞,” but no details are provided.
- **Why unresolved:** The current window order (Definition 6) and window repair definition (Definition 7) assume all ABoxes in a window come from the stream timeline with finite timestamps. Integrating a permanently present background ABox changes the preference structure and the incremental repair construction (Algorithm 3). It is unclear how conflicts involving both background and stream facts are resolved, how the “incremental repair” property (Lemma 2) interacts with the background, and whether the canonical window model construction (Definition 8) needs adjustment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A formal extension of the window order to include a special highest‑priority level (e.g., timestamps > all stream times). A revised definition of window repair that treats the background ABox as immutable and always included. Proofs that the incremental addition/removal algorithms (Algorithms 1–2) remain correct with this modification. An example‑driven illustration showing how background knowledge influences conflict resolution across window slides.

## Limitations
- Handles only non‑recursive negative inclusions; recursive cases are not supported.
- Conflict set enumeration is not optimized; brute‑force may not scale to large ABoxes.
- No experimental evaluation; practical performance and scalability are unknown.
- Requires discrete timestamps with minimum delta and finite ABoxes; violations can break the preorder.

## Confidence
- **Incremental materialization (oldest‑timestamp attribution):** High – formal definitions and proofs (Proposition 2, Lemma 2) ensure correctness.
- **Timestamp‑based repair for non‑recursive inconsistencies:** Medium – clear logic but limited to non‑recursive case.
- **Semi‑naive algorithms:** High for correctness; Medium for efficiency (no experiments, conflict resolution overhead).
- **Extension to other OWL profiles:** Low – acknowledged as a major challenge with no concrete plan.
- **Handling recursive inconsistencies via depth‑limited rewriting:** Low – suggested but untested and potentially costly.
