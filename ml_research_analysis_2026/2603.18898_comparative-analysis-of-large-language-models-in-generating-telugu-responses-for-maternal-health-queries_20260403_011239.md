# Comparative Analysis of Large Language Models in Generating Telugu Responses for Maternal Health Queries

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18898
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18898
- Reference count: 7
- Primary result: GeminiAI achieved the highest expert-rated accuracy, fluency, relevance, coherence, and completeness; Perplexity AI's quality significantly improved when prompted in Telugu; ChatGPT-4o performed moderately with lower accuracy and completeness; BERT Score showed high semantic alignment for Perplexity (English prompt) but diverged from expert medical assessments, highlighting critical roles of model selection and prompt language.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates the capability of three leading large language models (ChatGPT-4o, GeminiAI, Perplexity AI) to generate accurate and coherent maternal health information in Telugu, a low-resource language. The researchers generated responses to bilingual (English/Telugu) pregnancy queries and evaluated them using both automated BERT Score and ratings from ten Telugu-speaking gynecologists. Key findings indicate GeminiAI consistently produced the most medically accurate and coherent responses regardless of prompt language, while Perplexity AI's performance improved markedly with Telugu-language prompts. The study reveals a discrepancy between automated semantic similarity scores and expert medical judgment, underscoring that both model architecture and prompt engineering are pivotal for reliable AI-generated healthcare advice in regional languages.

## Method Summary
The evaluation used a dataset of common maternal health questions in English and their Telugu translations, with reference answers authored by a Telugu-speaking gynecologist. Each model was prompted to respond in Telugu to both the English and Telugu versions of each query. Outputs were assessed automatically via multilingual BERT Score (precision, recall, F1) against the expert references. Concurrently, ten gynecologists blind to model identity rated each response on five dimensions: accuracy, fluency, relevance, coherence, and completeness (1–10 Likert scale). The analysis compared average scores across models (ChatGPT-4o, GeminiAI, Perplexity AI) and prompt languages (English, Telugu) to determine performance differences and the impact of prompt language.

## Key Results
- GeminiAI received the highest expert scores across all five metrics for both English and Telugu prompts, demonstrating robust medical accuracy and coherence.
- Perplexity AI showed substantially higher fluency and relevance scores when prompted in Telugu versus English, indicating strong prompt-language sensitivity.
- ChatGPT-4o produced moderately scored responses, with notably lower accuracy and completeness compared to GeminiAI.
- BERT Score was highest for Perplexity with English prompts (F1=0.704), but expert ratings did not rank it highest, demonstrating that surface-level semantic similarity does not guarantee medical correctness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Prompt Language as a Pathway Selector**  
The language of the input prompt activates distinct pathways within the LLM's multilingual representations. A Telugu prompt directly engages Telugu-specific medical vocabulary and syntactic patterns, improving domain relevance and fluency for models like Perplexity. An English prompt may induce an English-knowledge retrieval followed by translation/paraphrase, risking loss of medical nuance.  
*Evidence anchors*: Perplexity's improvement with Telugu prompts ( abstract, Section 4C); statement that both model and prompt language are crucial (abstract).  
*Break condition*: Model is monolingual or trained primarily on code-switched/translated text where knowledge is not language-modality bound.

**Mechanism 2: Training Data Drives Baseline Capability**  
Models exposed to extensive, high-quality Telugu medical text during pre-training develop richer internal embeddings for terminology, cultural context, and syntax, enabling accurate fact retrieval and coherent generation. Gemini's consistently high expert scores suggest superior Telugu medical data exposure.  
*Evidence anchors*: Gemini excelling in accuracy/coherence (abstract); top expert metric scores (Section 4B, Table 2).  
*Break condition*: Training data lacks substantive Telugu medical text or tokenization poorly handles Telugu script, losing semantic granularity.

**Mechanism 3: Automated Metrics Miss Medical Nuance**  
BERT Score measures embedding-level similarity but does not encode medical knowledge, safety, or completeness. A response can be semantically close to a reference yet contain factual inaccuracies or omissions that experts detect, explaining Perplexity's high BERT Score but not top expert ratings.  
*Evidence anchors*: Direct discrepancy between BERT Score and expert assessments (abstract, Sections 4A vs 4B).  
*Break condition*: A perfectly exhaustive reference set exists and BERT Score correlates perfectly with all dimensions of medical accuracy—unlikely in open-ended domains.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input Processor & Tokenizer -> Multilingual Encoder -> Domain Knowledge Subspace (implied) -> Response Generator -> Output Post-Processor.

**Critical path**:  
1. Receive query (English or Telugu text).  
2. Tokenize → Embeddings.  
3. Encoder processes embeddings; attention heads may specialize by language/domain.  
4. Decoder generates Telugu tokens autoregressively.  
5. Post-process for script validity.  
*Key Branch*: Prompt language influences which language-specific pathways in encoder/decoder are predominantly activated.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Semantic Fluency vs. Factual Accuracy: Optimizing for BERT Score may trade off with precise medical details.  
- Prompt Language Flexibility vs. Consistency: Models robust to both prompts (e.g., Gemini) vs. those requiring L1 prompts (e.g., Perplexity).  
- General Capability vs. Domain Specialization: Base LLMs need integration of verified medical knowledge for high-stakes accuracy.

**Failure signatures**:  
- High BERT Score (>0.70) but expert accuracy <7.0: Semantically plausible but medically unsafe.  
- Large performance gap (>1.5 points) between English and Telugu prompts: Fragile language-specific knowledge.  
- Low fluency (<7.0) despite high accuracy: Tokenizer or generation issues with Telugu script.  
- Low completeness that omits key maternal health warnings: Critical safety failure.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Prompt Language Ablation**: For a fixed set of 20 maternal health queries, generate responses from each model using both English and Telugu prompts. Calculate the delta in expert accuracy/fluency scores per model. Goal: Quantify each model's "prompt language sensitivity."
2. **Metric Correlation Analysis**: For all generated responses, plot BERT Score (F1) vs. Expert Accuracy score. Compute Pearson correlation. Goal: Validate or refute the paper's finding of low correlation for certain models/configurations.
3. **Error Taxonomy Analysis**: Manually analyze 50 "failure" cases (low expert accuracy) from each model. Categorize errors: factual incorrectness, incompleteness, irrelevance, poor fluency/coherence. Goal: Determine if failures stem primarily from knowledge gaps or language generation issues.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What architectural or training data characteristics cause Gemini to maintain high performance regardless of prompt language, while Perplexity's performance significantly improves with Telugu prompts?**  
   *Basis*: Explicit finding that Gemini is robust to prompt language while Perplexity shows marked Telugu-prompt improvement; discussion notes this is "likely due to its architecture and training data" without analysis.  
   *Why unresolved*: No investigation into model-specific factors (e.g., multilingual training strategies, tokenization, fine-tuning data).  
   *What evidence would resolve it*: Comparative ablation of training data sources, multilingual alignment techniques, and internal representations when processing English vs. Telugu medical queries.

2. **How can LLMs be effectively fine-tuned for maternal health queries in Telugu given the scarcity of high-quality, domain-specific Telugu medical training data?**  
   *Basis*: Future work proposes fine-tuning; introduction implies lack of existing benchmark datasets for this domain and language.  
   *Why unresolved*: No methodology proposed or tested for adapting models with limited local-language medical resources.  
   *What evidence would resolve it*: Development and evaluation of fine-tuning approaches (synthetic data, cross-lingual transfer, expert-guided augmentation) on a held-out Telugu maternal health test set, measuring gains in accuracy/completeness.

3. **Can an automated evaluation metric be developed that aligns more closely with expert gynecologists' multi-dimensional judgments (accuracy, fluency, relevance, coherence, completeness) for low-resource language healthcare responses?**  
   *Basis*: Paper identifies a "discrepancy" and "gap" between BERT Score and expert assessments, calling for combined methods but proposing no alternative.  
   *Why unresolved*: BERT Score is shown inadequate for medical factuality/completeness; no new or supplementary metric is validated.  
   *What evidence would resolve it*: Creation and validation of a new metric or suite (e.g., integrating clinical knowledge graphs, linguistic estimators) showing higher correlation with expert ratings on the paper's dataset.

4. **What are the key determinants of user trust and perceived reliability in AI-generated maternal health advice in Telugu, and how do they interact with the objective quality metrics reported?**  
   *Basis*: Future work mentions understanding trust; study focuses on expert evaluation, omitting end-user (e.g., pregnant women's) perceptions.  
   *Why unresolved*: High expert-rated accuracy does not guarantee adoption; cultural appropriateness, empathy, transparency unexplored.  
   *What evidence would resolve it*: User study with Telugu-speaking expectant mothers measuring trust and intention to follow advice, correlating perceptions with expert scores and response characteristics.

## Limitations
- Exact evaluation corpus (questions and gynecologist references) not public, risking dataset shift in reproductions.
- Prompting and inference parameters (templates, system messages, temperature, max tokens) unreported, preventing exact replication.
- Model versions/checkpoints and BERT Score implementation unspecified, affecting comparability of absolute scores and correlations.
- Expert evaluation protocol (number of questions, rater calibration, inter-rater reliability) opaque, leaving human assessment noise unquantified.

## Confidence
- Prompt language causally influences output quality: **High** (explicit within-paper ablation shows Perplexity's improvement).
- Gemini achieves highest expert scores across metrics: **Medium** (based on reported averages but unknown evaluation setup and rater variability).
- BERT Score diverges from expert medical accuracy: **High** (direct comparative data clearly shows discrepancy for Perplexity).
