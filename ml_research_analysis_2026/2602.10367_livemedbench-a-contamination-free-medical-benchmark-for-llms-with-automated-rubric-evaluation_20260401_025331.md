# LiveMedBench: A Contamination-Free Medical Benchmark for LLMs with Automated Rubric Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.10367
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10367
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Top LLM (GPT-5.2) scores 39.2%; 84% of models show significant post-cutoff degradation, indicating widespread contamination.

## Executive Summary
LiveMedBench addresses critical issues of data contamination and temporal misalignment in medical LLM evaluation. It introduces a weekly-updated benchmark harvested from verified online medical communities, ensuring temporal separation from training corpora. A Multi-Agent Clinical Curation Framework validates cases against evidence-based guidelines, while an Automated Rubric Framework generates fine-grained, case-specific criteria for evaluation. Assessment of 38 LLMs reveals low absolute performance (peak 39.2%) and widespread contamination: 84% of models degrade significantly on post-cutoff cases. The rubric aligns strongly with physician consensus (Pearson ρ=0.54), outperforming LLM-as-a-scribe approaches, and error analysis identifies contextual application—not factual knowledge—as the primary failure mode.

## Method Summary
LiveMedBench constructs its benchmark by weekly harvesting clinical Q&A threads from four verified online medical platforms (iCliniq, Student Doctor Network, DXY, Medlive). Raw threads are filtered by date (≥2023-01-01), clinical tagging, text-only format, and verified physician responses. A three-agent curation pipeline processes each thread: the Screener extracts structured SOAP components (Narrative, Query, Advice); the Validator assesses query validity, narrative sufficiency, and evidence alignment using MedCPT retrieval against authoritative medical guidelines, applying thresholds (S_inf>0.5, S_align>0.5); the Controller audits for hallucinations and synthesizes the final triplet. Cases failing any check are discarded. For each curated case, an Automated Rubric Generator (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) creates 6–19 bipolar criteria organized by thematic filters and weighted by clinical criticality. Evaluation employs a Rubric Grader (gpt-4.1-2025-04-14) to judge each criterion against model outputs, with final scores computed as a normalized weighted sum. This end-to-end pipeline yields a dynamic, contamination-resistant benchmark with clinically aligned, granular assessment.

## Key Results
- Top model (GPT-5.2) achieves a rubric-based score of 39.2% across 38 evaluated LLMs.
- 84% of models exhibit statistically significant performance drops on cases posted after their knowledge cutoff, confirming widespread training data contamination.
- The automated rubric correlates with physician consensus at Pearson ρ=0.54, substantially higher than LLM-as-a-scribe (ρ=0.26).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Multi-Agent Clinical Curation Framework
The framework produces clinically valid, contamination-free cases from noisy online discussions. Three agents operate hierarchically: the Screener extracts SOAP components; the Validator checks clinical validity via evidence retrieval and scoring against guidelines; the Controller verifies no hallucinations and synthesizes the final triplet. This pipeline enforces evidence-based alignment and prevents synthetic contamination. The core assumption is that authoritative medical evidence exists in retrievable form and can validate advice plausibility. Break conditions include retrieval failure for niche specialties or contradictory guidelines, which may cause incorrect passes or rejections.

### Mechanism 2: Automated Rubric Framework
The framework achieves physician alignment by decomposing verified physician advice into case-specific, weighted criteria. The Rubric Generator assigns a thematic category, extracts facts, and formulates bipolar criteria with clinical criticality weights. The Rubric Grader independently evaluates model outputs against these criteria. The core assumption is that such decomposition captures necessary and sufficient aspects of clinical correctness. If the initial physician advice is incomplete, the rubric may miss critical criteria, leading to false passes.

### Mechanism 3: Weekly Harvesting for Temporal Separation
Cases are sourced from verified, high-activity platforms with posting dates strictly after model knowledge cutoffs. Continuous weekly updates introduce novel cases unseen during training. Performance degradation on post-cutoff cases indicates contamination or knowledge obsolescence. The mechanism assumes these communities generate novel cases faster than they are ingested into training corpora. If model training pipelines begin scraping these communities in near-real-time, the temporal separation collapses and contamination reappears.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Source Layer (4 online medical communities) → Curation Layer (Screener → Validator → Controller agents) → Rubric Generation Layer (Theme classifier → fact extractor → criterion formulator) → Evaluation Layer (Rubric Grader + scoring normalizer) → Human QA Layer.
- **Critical path:** Raw thread → Screener (extract L_N, Q, L_A) → Validator (check S_cc, S_inf, S_align) → Controller (audit + synthesize) → Rubric Generator (theme → criteria) → Rubric Grader (check criteria) → final score.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Automation vs. Clinical Rigor:* Multi-agent automation enables scalability but risks false positives/negatives; mitigated by thresholds and human QA.
  - *Granularity vs. Noise:* Many criteria capture nuance but require careful weighting to avoid over-penalizing stylistic differences; addressed by weight clipping.
  - *Live updates vs. Reproducibility:* Weekly updates combat contamination but threaten reproducibility; solved via frozen snapshots with checksums and pinned grader versions.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High validator rejection rate (>70%): source platform quality issue or retrieval failure.
  - Low physician validation AC1 (<0.8): rubric criteria are clinically invalid or ambiguous.
  - Low correlation between rubric scores and LLM-as-Judge (<0.3): rubric too strict or misaligned with clinical utility.
  - Model performance drop >50% on post-cutoff: severe contamination in prior evaluation or rapid knowledge evolution.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate human validation metrics: Sample 50 cases, have two physicians rate Narrative/Query, Advice, and Criteria validity; compute Gwet’s AC1 to confirm >0.8 agreement.
  2. Calibrate validator thresholds: Vary τ_inf and τ_align on a held set to balance case retention (~30% pass rate) with clinical safety (zero contradicted advice).
  3. Test rubric grader reliability: Compare Macro F1 of rubric grader vs. human graders on 50 cases; target MF1 ≥0.75 to proceed to full evaluation.

## Limitations
- Retrieval dependency: Validator’s evidence alignment assumes MedCPT reliably retrieves guidelines; niche specialties may have sparse coverage, risking false passes/rejections.
- Rubric completeness: Criteria depend entirely on initial physician advice; incomplete/atypical advice yields narrow rubrics, potentially missing critical clinical dimensions.
- Temporal arms race: Weekly harvesting assumes LLM training pipelines do not ingest these communities in near-real-time; if they do, contamination re-emerges and benchmark novelty decays.

## Confidence
- Contamination-free case generation: Medium (pipeline well-specified but retrieval quality and rare-specialty validation uncertain)
- Rubric-physician alignment: High for reported correlation (ρ=0.54), Medium for generalizability (alignment measured on sampled cases)
- Temporal contamination detection: High for demonstrating post-cutoff drops, Medium for long-term novelty (no guarantee sources remain uncontaminated)

## Next Checks
1. **Specialty stress-test:** Run Validator on 100 cases from low-resource specialties (e.g., rare genetic disorders); have clinicians judge S_align validity. Target: ≥80% clinician validation rate.
2. **Rubric completeness audit:** For 50 cases, have independent clinicians expand the generated rubric with missing criteria; measure criterion coverage gap. Target: ≤15% added criteria per case.
3. **Source ingestion monitor:** Track training data release logs (e.g., Common Crawl, proprietary corpora) for mentions of the 4 source platforms. Set alert if ≥5% of weekly-harvested cases appear in training data post-benchmark release.
