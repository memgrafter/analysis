# 2Mamba2Furious: Linear in Complexity, Competitive in Accuracy

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.17363
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17363
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: 2Mamba achieves test loss comparable to softmax attention on FineWeb with 300M and 700M parameter models across sequence lengths up to 8192, and becomes more memory-efficient than softmax attention beyond sequence length 1058 for head dimension 64.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the accuracy gap between linear and softmax attention by simplifying Mamba-2 and introducing a second-order hidden state. Through ablations, the authors identify two critical components for Mamba-2's accuracy: a softplus-based A-mask and a small input convolution. Combining these yields Mamba-2S. They then square the query-key inner product, effectively incorporating the second-order term of the softmax Taylor expansion while switching to online softmax normalization, resulting in 2Mamba. On FineWeb, 2Mamba matches softmax attention's test loss up to 8192 tokens with 300M and 700M models. For head dimension 64, it becomes more memory-efficient than softmax beyond sequence length 1058 and slightly outperforms softmax on Needle-in-a-Haystack.

## Method Summary
The authors start from Mamba-2, a linear attention variant with a time-varying mask and associative scan. They systematically ablate its components to isolate what improves accuracy over vanilla linear attention. The top contributors are a softplus A-mask (replacing the original exponential mask tied to a discretization parameter) and a size-2 causal input convolution. Their combination forms Mamba-2S. To further close the gap with softmax, they increase the hidden state order: the inner product QKᵀ is squared before aggregation, capturing second-order interactions. This requires switching to softmax normalization and removing residual connections and RMS norm. For 700M models, they also omit time discretization to avoid training instability. The final architecture, 2Mamba, thus achieves softmax-competitive accuracy while maintaining linear memory complexity during inference.

## Key Results
- 2Mamba (300M/700M) matches softmax attention test loss on FineWeb across sequence lengths up to 8192.
- For head dimension 64, 2Mamba consumes less memory than softmax attention when sequence length exceeds 1058.
- On Needle in a Haystack, 2Mamba slightly outperforms softmax attention. An exponentiated variant, 2Mamba-E, surpasses softmax accuracy and relates to forgetting transformer designs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The softplus A-mask (negative softplus applied to a learned parameter) significantly improves accuracy over the original Mamba-2's exponential A-mask tied to the dt discretization.
- **Mechanism:** Replacing the original A-mask `A = -exp(A_log) ⊙ dt` with `A = -softplus(h·W_A)` removes the coupling with the dt parameter and provides a smoother, strictly negative decay factor that is learned directly. This stabilizes and sharpens the exponential decay mask applied during the associative scan.
- **Core assumption:** The original A-mask's dependency on the time-discretization parameter dt is not critical for expressiveness; a directly learned, strictly negative mask via softplus is more effective and stable.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract]: "...identify a softplus A-mask and input convolution as critical components, forming Mamba-2S."
  - [section 3, Table 2]: The "softplus A-masking" variant achieves test loss 2.88 vs. 2.98 for "original A-masking".
  - [section 5]: Removing dt discretization stabilizes training for the medium model (700M).
  - [corpus]: Neighbor papers (e.g., Hedgehog & Porcupine, Log-Linear Attention) explore similar mask modifications, but direct verification for this specific change is weak in the provided corpus.
- **Break condition:** If the softplus non-linearity introduces gradient saturation or if the performance gain vanishes when scaling model size or dataset diversity.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A small input convolution (window size 2) provides a crucial positional bias that boosts accuracy for linear attention.
- **Mechanism:** Applying a causal 1D convolution to the projected Q, K, V matrices before the linear attention operation injects a limited form of local positional awareness. This allows the model to condition current token representations on the immediate past (1-token lookback), which vanilla linear attention lacks.
- **Core assumption:** A very short convolutional receptive field (size 2) is sufficient to capture the essential local context that improves next-token prediction, and larger windows offer diminishing returns.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract]: "...input convolution as critical components..."
  - [section 3, Table 1]: Adding a convolution with window size 2 drops test loss from 3.13 to 2.93. Larger sizes (3,4) show marginal improvement.
  - [section 4]: The model "Mamba-2S" retains a convolution of size 2 as a non-negotiable component.
  - [corpus]: CAPS (neighbor) uses clock-weighted aggregation, suggesting positional structure helps, but does not directly anchor the specific convolution window size finding.
- **Break condition:** If tasks requiring longer-range positional dependencies (e.g., code, structured data) do not benefit, or if the convolution's memory overhead (3d per window increase) outweighs accuracy gains for larger head dimensions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Squaring the QK inner product (`(QK^T)^2`) creates a second-order hidden state that approximates the second-order term of the softmax Taylor expansion, achieving near-softmax accuracy while maintaining linear memory for long sequences.
- **Mechanism:** Softmax can be expressed via its Taylor series: `exp(x) ≈ 1 + x + x^2/2 + ...`. Standard linear attention uses a first-order RNN (`x`). Squaring the inner product effectively incorporates the `x^2` term into the hidden state dynamics. This expands the hidden state to size `d_h(d_h+1)/2` per head, capturing pairwise feature interactions and enabling a form of "softmax mimicry" without the exponential kernel's quadratic cost.
- **Core assumption:** The dominant accuracy gap between linear and softmax attention is bridged by the second-order term; higher orders (cubic, etc.) yield diminishing returns and are not cost-effective.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract]: "...increase the order of the hidden state, resulting in a method, which we call 2Mamba, that is nearly as accurate as softmax attention."
  - [section 2.4]: Directly references Mongaras & Larson 2025 showing higher-order RNNs approach softmax accuracy.
  - [section 5.1]: Derives the reduced hidden state size `d(d+1)/2` and the sequence length break-even point (>1058 for d=64) where memory drops below softmax's KV cache.
  - [section 5.2, Figure 8 & 9]: 2Mamba matches softmax test loss on FineWeb and performs comparably/slightly better on Needle-in-a-Haystack.
  - [corpus]: The Hedgehog & Porcupine paper (neighbor) also uses polynomial activations to mimic softmax, supporting the mechanism of higher-order expansions.
- **Break condition:** If the accuracy gap re-emerges on tasks requiring complex non-linear interactions beyond pairwise (e.g., certain reasoning tasks), or if the memory savings are negated by implementation overhead for very small context lengths.

- **Concept:** Hidden State Order in RNNs & Taylor Expansion of Softmax
  - **Why needed here:** The key innovation "2Mamba" explicitly increases the order of the recurrent state from 1 to 2. This is motivated by viewing softmax attention as an infinite-order sum. Understanding how `(QK^T)^2` maps to a second-order state is central to the mechanism.
  - **Quick check question:** How does squaring the QK inner product in a linear attention loop change the size and nature of the recurrent hidden state, and why does this make it more expressive?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Mamba-2 (Baseline): Input → Conv1d → QKV_proj → [dt, A_log] → Associative Scan (with decay & causal mask) → Output_norm (RMS) → Output_proj. 2Mamba: Input → Conv1d (w=2, no act) → QKV_proj → [A_param (softplus only, no dt)] → Associative Scan (with decay & causal mask) → ((QK^T)^2 ⊙ mask) → Softmax Norm (online) → Output_proj (removed: dt discretization, RMS norm, SiLU activation post-conv, D_residual, Z_gate).
- **Critical path:**
  1. Replace Mamba-2 with vanilla linear attention + output norm (baseline).
  2. Ablate and identify top components: softplus A-mask and convolution (size 2) (Section 3).
  3. Combine top components → Mamba-2S (Algorithm 4).
  4. Increase hidden state order: square QK inner product, switch to softmax norm → 2Mamba (Algorithm 5).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Memory vs. Accuracy:** Squared inner product increases hidden state to `~d_h^2/2` vs. softmax's `2*N*d_h`. Break-even at N>~1058 (d_h=64). Gains accuracy but loses efficiency for short contexts.
  - **Stability vs. Simplicity:** Removing dt discretization (used in Mamba-2S for small models) improves numerical stability for medium models but may lose a minor accuracy boost.
  - **Implementation complexity:** Squared kernel requires custom Triton for unique second-order terms (Eq 5) vs. standard matmul.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Numerical divergence:** Training loss spikes/NaNs, especially in medium-sized models with dt discretization enabled (Section 5, Fig 5b). *Action: Remove dt or force FP32 compute.*
  - **Ablation mismatch:** If isolated component gains (e.g., Z_gate) do not translate to full model buildup (Section 4, Fig 4b), indicates interactions or redundancy.
  - **Softmax norm instability:** If online softmax normalization fails with the A-mask exponents, gradients may explode. *Action: Use softmax on (QK^T)^2 where values are strictly positive.*
  - **Memory measurement error:** Incorrect hidden state size calculation leads to wrong break-even threshold. *Action: Compute per-head memory exactly: for 2Mamba, `d(d+1)/2 + 3d + d(d+1)/2` vs `2*N*d` for softmax; verify with profiling.*
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation study:** Take Llama-2 model, replace all attention blocks with Mamba-2S variations (vary: A-mask type, conv size, norm type). Train 300M model on FineWeb for 90k steps, compare final test loss. *Goal: Isolate softplus A-mask and conv(w=2).*
  2. **Sequence length scaling:** Train 700M model with 2Mamba for max_seq_len ∈ {2048, 4096, 8192}. Plot test loss vs. steps and final loss. *Goal: Verify accuracy parity with softmax attention at longer contexts.*
  3. **Memory benchmark & NIAH:** Profile hidden state memory of 2Mamba vs. softmax (d_h=64) across N∈[512,8192]. Run Nanotron Needle-in-a-Haystack at N=16384 after 400k steps training on 8192 context. *Goal: Confirm memory advantage past N=1058 and contextual retrieval capability.*

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** What is the minimal set of architectural components from Mamba-2 required to maintain its accuracy advantage over naive linear attention?
**Why unresolved:** The authors performed component-wise ablations and a sequential buildup, but interactions between components may not be fully captured, and the final simplified model (Mamba-2S) still includes three components (softplus A-mask, convolution, discretization), leaving open whether all are essential.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Exhaustive ablation of all possible component combinations, or systematic search (e.g., Bayesian optimization) to find minimal configurations that match or exceed original Mamba-2 accuracy.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How does the dimensionality of the second-order hidden state (generated by squaring the query-key inner product) trade off between accuracy and memory consumption compared to alternative expansions or projections?
**Why unresolved:** The paper fixed the hidden state size based on mathematical derivation and did not compare to varying the order or using learnable projections to control dimension.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Train models with polynomial expansions of different orders (e.g., second, third) and also models with learnable linear projections that compress the second-order terms, measuring accuracy and memory on diverse sequence lengths.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Does combining the DeltaNet update rule with 2Mamba's squared inner product yield further improvements in accuracy or efficiency?
**Why unresolved:** No experiments were conducted to merge these two architectures; the potential synergy remains theoretical.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Implement a hybrid model incorporating DeltaNet's delta rule (e.g., gradient-based update) into 2Mamba's recurrence and evaluate on language modeling tasks with long contexts.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** How can the numerical instability caused by time discretization in 2Mamba be mitigated for large models without sacrificing training speed?
**Why unresolved:** The instability limits the scalability of the discretized variant; the root cause and robust solutions are not identified.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze the sensitivity of discretization parameters in mixed precision; develop stable kernel implementations or alternative parameterizations that retain the benefits of discretization under low precision.

## Limitations
- The paper's reproducibility is hindered by critical implementation details (e.g., Triton kernels for squared inner product) being only in the GitHub repository, and the final evaluation intentionally skips the 100k-step checkpoint due to a noted bug.
- Results are validated solely on FineWeb (English web text) and Needle-in-a-Haystack; generalizability to other domains (code, mathematics, structured data) remains unproven.
- The reported stability benefits of removing time discretization for 700M models rely on specific mixed-precision settings; the root cause of instability and its mitigation for larger models are not fully understood.

- **Input convolution (w=2) as critical positional bias**: High confidence.
