# Parametric Knowledge and Retrieval Behavior in RAG Fine-Tuning for Electronic Design Automation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23047
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23047
- **Reference count:** 24
- **Primary result:** Fine-tuned 7B models exceed a 72B baseline on reference-backed F1 and PKP; PA-RAG generalizes best across retrieval conditions.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates fine-tuning of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems for long-form text generation in electronic design automation (EDA). The authors find that conventional NLP metrics like ROUGE and BERTScore fail to capture factual errors in generated outputs. They introduce TRIFEX, a pipeline that extracts factual triples from responses and attributes them to source (reference, context, query), and propose Parametric Knowledge Precision (PKP) to isolate correct internal knowledge. Experiments with a 7B model fine-tuned under five retrieval conditions show that TRIFEX detects differences missed by standard metrics. Decomposing Self-Knowledge reveals that cross-retrieval variance is primarily driven by Parametric Rate rather than PKP. Notably, fine-tuned 7B models outperform a 72B baseline on reference-backed F1 and PKP, with PA-RAG generalizing best. The work demonstrates that smaller models can be cost-effectively adapted for domain-specific RAG and underscores the limitations of traditional evaluation metrics.

## Method Summary
The study uses a synthetic EDA dataset of ~12K entries, each comprising a user query, a reference response, and a context chunk. A 7B parameter model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) is fine-tuned via LoRA under five conditions: without context, with relevant context, RAFT, PA-RAG, and with all contexts. A separate test set of 4K prompts (1K prompts × 4 context conditions: n/a, relevant, irrelevant, noisy) is used for evaluation. The proposed TRIFEX pipeline extracts triples from generated responses, references, contexts, and queries using a large LLM (Qwen3-Next-80B), normalizes them, retrieves top-k candidate sources via embedding similarity, and attributes each triple to its supporting source using an LLM judge. Metrics include standard ROUGE/BERTScore, reference-backed precision/recall, Context Utilization (CU), User Utilization (UU), Self-Knowledge (SK), Parametric Knowledge Precision (PKP), and Parametric Rate (PR).

## Key Results
- TRIFEX detects factual differences that ROUGE and BERTScore miss, highlighting the limitations of standard metrics.
- Fine-tuned 7B models outperform a 72B baseline on reference-backed F1 and PKP, showing that smaller models can be effectively adapted for domain-specific RAG.
- PA-RAG generalizes most robustly across retrieval conditions, achieving the highest Context Utilization and PKP.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates three key mechanisms:

**Mechanism 1: Mixed-context training improves robustness.** Fine-tuning on retrieval-conditioned data (including irrelevant/noisy contexts) forces the model to distinguish signal from noise, shifting its reliance between parametric knowledge and retrieved context. Evidence: PA-RAG achieves highest CU, PKP, and F1ref across conditions and outperforms larger baselines. Break condition: If fine-tuning only increases context utilization without improving stability under poor retrieval, mechanism fails.

**Mechanism 2: PKP isolates true internalized knowledge.** PKP filters out any triples also present in the user query or context, counting only those supported solely by the reference. This removes "leakage" and measures genuine parametric recall. Evidence: PKP shows lower coefficient of variation than SK; 75% of cross-retrieval SK variance is attributable to PR. Break condition: If many correct triples are also in query/context, PKP undercounts.

**Mechanism 3: Standard metrics obscure factual errors.** ROUGE/BERTScore rely on lexical/semantic overlap, which can be high even when generated text contains unsupported claims (e.g., wrong values). Evidence: ROUGE/BERTScore fail to detect large drops in Rec ref (-39.51) and F1ref (-27.5) under irrelevant context. Break condition: If ROUGE/BERTScore correlate strongly with human factuality, mechanism invalid.

## Foundational Learning
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**  
  - *Why needed:* The study evaluates fine-tuning within a RAG pipeline; understanding the roles of retriever and generator is crucial.  
  - *Quick check:* In a RAG system, what is the functional difference between the retrieval and generation components, and why might fine-tuning affect their interaction?
- **Triple-based Fact Extraction and Attribution**  
  - *Why needed:* TRIFEX converts outputs into (subject, predicate, object) triples and attributes them to source, enabling disentanglement of knowledge sources.  
  - *Quick check:* If a generated triple matches both the reference and the user query, how would TRIFEX label it, and why does this matter for PKP?
- **Decomposition of Self-Knowledge (SK = PKP × PR)**  
  - *Why needed:* Decomposing SK reveals that most cross-retrieval variation is driven by PR, not PKP, reshaping interpretation of model behavior.  
  - *Quick check:* If Model A has high PKP but low PR, and Model B has moderate PKP but very high PR, which would have higher SK and what does that imply?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Data Pipeline → Fine-Tuning Engine → Inference → TRIFEX Evaluator → Metric Calculator.

**Critical path:** Synthetic dataset creation (12K entries) → Model training via LoRA on Qwen2.5-7B (5 variants) → Inference on test set (4K prompts × 4 context conditions) → Triple extraction & normalization using Qwen3-Next-80B → Top-k candidate retrieval via embeddings → Attribution via Qwen3-Coder-30B → Metric aggregation (Precref, Rec ref, PKP, PR, SK, UU, CU).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Extraction fidelity vs. cost: 80B LLM ensures ~80% accuracy but consumes ~100 H100 GPU-hours for 512 samples; smaller judges reduce cost but increase noise.
- Candidate set size (k): Larger k improves recall of supporting evidence but linearly increases LLM judgment cost; k=7 is chosen as a compromise.
- Evaluation subset: Full test set (4K) is prohibitively expensive for TRIFEX; a 512-sample subsample introduces sampling variance but makes the study feasible.

**Failure signatures:**
- High `NONE` attribution rate across models → extraction too strict or references/context insufficient.
- PKP and PR moving together across conditions → normalization failure or leakage not properly filtered.
- ROUGE/BERTScore showing no variation while F1ref varies → expected metric blindness, but confirms need for triple-based evaluation.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replicate baseline metrics: Compute ROUGE-1, ROUGE-L, and BERTScore F1 for the base model and a `w/ relevant` fine-tuned variant across all four test context conditions. Verify minimal variation compared to F1ref trends.
2. Run TRIFEX on a tiny subset: Process 10 test examples through the full pipeline; manually verify triples and attribution labels to ensure quality (target >75% agreement with paper’s attribution accuracy).
3. Ablation on mixed-context exposure: Train a model only on `w/ relevant` data, evaluate on `relevant` and `irrelevant` test contexts. Observe collapse in PKP/F1ref on `irrelevant` and high CU, confirming need for mixed-context training.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can LLM-based triple attribution accuracy be improved beyond the current ~80% to reduce evaluation noise? (Basis: reported 80.13% accuracy; requires larger human-annotated data or better judges.)
2. Does limiting candidate evidence to a fixed top-k set omit valid supporting triples and bias metrics like PKP? (Basis: limitation noted; could cause false negatives; needs ablation varying k or full comparison.)
3. What specific properties of PA-RAG (synthetic Q&A generation with diverse context) give it superior cross-retrieval robustness compared to RAFT or standard fine-tuning? (Inferred from results; understanding could inform better protocols; requires controlled component isolation.)
4. Why do PA-RAG and RAFT show opposite patterns in Parametric Rate (PR) versus User Utilization (UU) under irrelevant context? (Inferred; suggests different strategies for handling misleading retrieval; needs fine-grained error analysis or attention probing.)
5. To what extent are the findings on metric limitations, PKP decomposition, and smaller-model success transferable to other specialized domains beyond EDA? (Inferred; based on single-domain study; requires replication in other technical domains.)

## Limitations
- The synthetic EDA dataset and its generation details (GPT-4o prompts, normalization rules, test sampling) are unreleased, impeding exact replication.
- TRIFEX relies on LLM-based extraction/attribution with ~80% accuracy, introducing noise that may obscure subtle metric differences.
- The study is confined to one domain (EDA) with no cross-domain validation; generalizability of conclusions remains uncertain.

## Next Checks
1. **Metric divergence replication:** Compute ROUGE-1, ROUGE-L, and BERTScore F1 for the base model and a `w/ relevant` fine-tuned variant across all four test context conditions. Expect ROUGE/BERTScore to remain relatively constant while reference-backed F1 (F1ref) drops sharply on `irrelevant` and `noisy` contexts.
2. **TRIFEX sanity check:** Run the full TRIFEX pipeline on 10 randomly selected test examples. Manually verify the quality of extracted triples and the correctness of attribution labels (e.g., triples from the reference should be labeled `R`). The goal is to achieve >75% agreement with the paper’s reported attribution accuracy of 80.13%.
3. **Ablation on mixed-context exposure:** Train a minimal model using only the `w/ relevant` training data. Evaluate this model on the `relevant` and `irrelevant` test contexts. Predict a high Context Utilization (CU) on `irrelevant` and a collapse in PKP and F1ref, confirming the necessity of mixed-context training for robustness.
