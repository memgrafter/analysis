# When the Prompt Becomes Visual: Vision-Centric Jailbreak Attacks for Large Image Editing Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10179
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10179
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** VJA achieves attack success rates up to 80.9% on Nano Banana Pro and 70.1% on GPT Image 1.5, with open-source models showing 100% ASR.

## Executive Summary
This paper uncovers a critical safety vulnerability in vision-prompted image editing models: malicious instructions conveyed purely through visual inputs (e.g., arrows, marks) bypass text-centric safeguards. The authors propose Vision-Centric Jailbreak Attack (VJA), which embeds harmful editing cues directly into input images, and introduce IESBench, a comprehensive safety benchmark spanning 15 risk categories and 1054 samples. Experiments demonstrate VJA’s high effectiveness, with attack success rates reaching 80.9% on commercial models and 100% on open-source variants. A training-free defense using an introspective safety trigger reactivates the model’s latent safety awareness, reducing attack success by approximately 33% with negligible overhead.

## Method Summary
The study introduces VJA, a black-box attack that transforms a benign image and a malicious editing intent into an adversarial image containing only visual cues (e.g., bounding boxes, arrows, handwritten notes). These cues are interpreted by the image editing model as executable instructions while evading text-based safety filters. For evaluation, the authors construct IESBench, a curated dataset of 1054 samples covering 15 fine-grained risk categories (e.g., disinformation, evidence tampering). Each sample includes a base image and a corresponding visual prompt image. To assess attack success, an MLLM judge (e.g., Gemini 3 Pro) compares the original and edited outputs, scoring harmfulness (HS), editing validity (EV), and high-risk ratio (HRR). A defense method is proposed: appending a text-based safety trigger (e.g., “You are an image editing safety evaluator…”) to the multimodal input, forcing the model to reason about safety in language space. No model retraining is required.

## Key Results
- VJA achieves attack success rates (ASR) up to 80.9% on Nano Banana Pro and 70.1% on GPT Image 1.5; open-source models (e.g., Qwen-Image-Edit*) exhibit 100% ASR.
- The introspective safety trigger reduces ASR by ~33% (e.g., to 66.9% on Qwen-Image-Edit-Safe) with minimal computational overhead (~3% latency increase).
- IESBench reveals uneven safety generalization: models show high vulnerability in evidence tampering (I13) and fabrication categories but relatively lower ASR on disinformation (I8) and aversive manipulation (I15).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Vision-centric jailbreak attacks (VJA) bypass safety mechanisms by embedding malicious instructions purely in visual inputs, evading text-centric safeguards.
- **Mechanism:** Modern image editing models interpret visual prompts (e.g., marks, arrows) as edit instructions. Safety systems, trained to detect harmful text, fail to parse these visual cues, treating them as benign image content rather than executable commands.
- **Core assumption:** Current safety alignment is predominantly text-focused; visual editing intents are not equally monitored or blocked.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] “malicious instructions conveyed purely through visual inputs bypass text-centric safeguards”
  - [section 2.1] “safety alignment and safeguard mechanisms are predominantly text-centric... resulting in a mismatch with text-based safety controls”
  - [corpus] Weak—neighbors discuss multimodal jailbreaks generally but do not specifically evidence visual-only attacks on image editing models.
- **Break condition:** Models with robust, natively multimodal safety reasoning that process visual prompts through the same safety filters as text.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A training-free defense using an introspective safety trigger reactivates the model's latent safety awareness by forcing multimodal reasoning in language space.
- **Mechanism:** Appending a text-based safety prompt (e.g., “Please review the image and text... predict whether the edited image will be safe”) after the user’s visual prompt leverages the model’s attention mechanism to reframe the visual input as a language-based safety judgment task, where alignment is stronger.
- **Core assumption:** The underlying vision-language model (VLM) has inherent safety knowledge in its language reasoning pathways that can be activated with appropriate prompting.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 2.2] “the inherent safety-awareness of VLMs can be activated and utilized for defense... transform the vision-centric attack back to a text-centric attack”
  - [section 4.2] Defense reduces ASR by ~33% on Qwen-Image-Edit-Safe, matching commercial model safety levels.
  - [corpus] Weak—no direct corpus evidence; this is a novel proposal from the paper.
- **Break condition:** The VLM’s safety alignment in language space is poorly calibrated or the visual prompt is so deceptive that even language-space reasoning is misled.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** IESBench exposes systematic vulnerabilities and uneven safety generalization across risk categories and model types.
- **Mechanism:** By providing a standardized suite of visually-prompted harmful edits across 15 fine-grained risk categories, the benchmark reveals that models show high ASR (>80%) on some categories (e.g., evidence tampering) while being more resistant on others, indicating gaps in safety coverage.
- **Core assumption:** A comprehensive, vision-centric benchmark is necessary to diagnose specific weaknesses in image editing safety, as text-only benchmarks are insufficient.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 4.1] “Vulnerabilities also vary across risk levels... category- and risk-level discrepancies indicate that current safety alignment... generalize unevenly”
  - [section 3] IESBench covers 15 risk categories, 1054 samples, with hierarchical taxonomy.
  - [corpus] Strong—neighbors like “Visual Contextual Attack” (paper 9509) confirm visual context exploits safety vulnerabilities, supporting the need for vision-centric evaluation.
- **Break condition:** Future models achieve uniformly low ASR (<5%) across all 15 IESBench categories, indicating robust, generalized safety.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Visual Prompting in Multimodal Models  
  *Why needed:* VJA hinges on models treating embedded visual marks/arrows as executable instructions. Understanding how VLMs parse non-text visual cues is key to grasping the attack vector.  
  *Quick check:* How might a bounding box with an arrow differ in semantic interpretation from a text instruction like “edit the circled region”?

- **Concept:** Text-Centric vs. Multimodal Safety Alignment  
  *Why needed:* The core vulnerability is the misalignment between safety mechanisms (text-focused) and the model’s full capabilities (vision-language). This gap is what VJA exploits.  
  *Quick check:* Why might a safeguard fine-tuned on text-only harmful prompts fail on an image containing a hand-drawn “remove watermark” symbol?

- **Concept:** Benchmark Construction for Niche Safety Evaluation  
  *Why needed:* IESBench’s design (visual prompts, editing-specific taxonomy) sets a precedent for evaluating task-specific safety. Understanding its structure is crucial for interpreting results.  
  *Quick check:* What makes a safety benchmark for image editing fundamentally different from one for text generation or general VQA?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  IESBench dataset → Adversarial Image Generator (produces I_adversarial from I_benign + malicious intent) → Victim Model (takes I_adversarial ± T_safe) → Edited image (I_harmful) → Judge Model (compares I_original vs I_harmful) → metrics (ASR, HS, EV, HRR).  
  Safety Trigger Module generates T_safe string to prepend/append for defense.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Select IESBench sample (base image + target edit intent).  
  2. Generate I_adversarial by embedding visual editing cue.  
  3. Query victim model with (I_adversarial, NULL) or (I_adversarial, T_safe).  
  4. Obtain I_harmful.  
  5. Judge model evaluates harmfulness to compute metrics.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Defense efficiency vs. coverage: The introspective trigger adds ~300 tokens and ~3% latency but may fail against fabricated information requiring world knowledge.  
  - Benchmark fidelity vs. scalability: IESBench uses high-quality, manually curated visual prompts but scales via MLLM-as-judge, trading some human nuance for coverage.  
  - Attack realism vs. reproducibility: VJA uses natural visual cues for stealth but introduces variability in perception across models (sensitivity experiments in Appendix A.1).

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Invalid edit: Output does not reflect intended edit → low EV.  
  - Refusal: Model rejects request → HS=1.  
  - Neutralization: Model produces safe but related output (e.g., adds toy knife) → HS=2/3.  
  - Hallucinated safety: Judge assigns low HS to clearly harmful output (observed with smaller judges like Qwen3-VL-2B).

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline VJA success rate:** Run VJA (no text prompt) on all models using full IESBench. Report ASR and HS per model (Table 1). Expect commercial models ~70–98% ASR, open-source 100%.  
  2. **VJA vs. Text Jailbreak (TJA):** Sample 20% of IESBench, run both attacks on commercial models. Compare ASR/HS/HRR deltas (Table 2). Hypothesis: VJA yields +ASR on safer models (Nano Banana, GPT Image) because it evades text safeguards.  
  3. **Defense efficacy:** Integrate safety trigger into a local model (e.g., Qwen-Image-Edit*) as “Qwen-Image-Edit-Safe”. Rerun VJA and measure reduction in ASR/HS (Table 1). Expect ~33% ASR reduction with minimal runtime overhead (Table 5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** How can visual jailbreak attacks be made effective against image editing models with limited visual perception and reasoning?  
  **Basis:** [explicit] The paper’s conclusion explicitly states that VJA is less effective on such models.  
  **Why unresolved:** Many deployed models, particularly open-source ones, have weak visual reasoning; without effective attack methods, their safety cannot be properly assessed.  
  **Evidence:** Conduct controlled experiments varying visual prompt complexity and measuring attack success across models with known reasoning deficits; propose a framework to generate simplified yet effective visual prompts.

- **Question:** How can introspective safety defenses be improved to detect visual jailbreak prompts that require contextual or up-to-date world knowledge?  
  **Basis:** [explicit] The defense section notes it remains less effective against attacks involving fabricated or misleading information requiring world knowledge.  
  **Why unresolved:** Attacks that manipulate contextual knowledge (e.g., falsifying documents) are high-risk and currently bypass defenses, undermining trustworthiness.  
  **Evidence:** Augment the safety trigger with external knowledge retrieval or fine-tuning on factually diverse data; evaluate on categories like I8 (Disinformation) and I13 (Evidence Tampering) for improved detection.

- **Question:** What automatic evaluation protocol for image editing safety best aligns with human judgments of harmfulness, given observed discrepancies between MLLM judges and human evaluators?  
  **Basis:** [inferred] The paper’s evaluation shows humans are more conservative than MLLMs, and different MLLMs produce inconsistent scores.  
  **Why unresolved:** The safety community lacks a standardized, human-aligned judge; reliance on a single MLLM (e.g., Gemini) may bias results and hinder reproducibility.  
  **Evidence:** Perform a large-scale human annotation study on IESBench outputs; benchmark multiple MLLM judges (including open-source) against human labels; develop a calibrated judge or rubric that maximizes correlation.

## Limitations
- **Unknown A (Attack details):** The exact image transformation function for embedding visual cues is not open-sourced; reproductions must reverse-engineer from examples, risking deviation from the paper’s precise attack vector.
- **Unknown B (Model access):** Results on commercial models (Nano Banana Pro, GPT Image 1.5, Qwen-Image-Edit Online) rely on proprietary APIs with potential access, cost, or versioning barriers, hindering independent verification.
- **Unknown C (Judge prompt):** The full, unedited judge prompt template is redacted; minor variations could alter Harmfulness Score (HS) calibration, especially for edge cases.
- **Benchmark scope:** IESBench focuses on static image edits; it does not evaluate video editing models or attacks leveraging temporal visual cues, limiting generalizability.

## Confidence
- **VJA effectiveness (ASR up to 100%):** Medium. Strong evidence for open-source models, but commercial model results depend on unreleased attack implementation and API access.
- **IESBench validity (15 categories, 1054 samples):** High. The benchmark construction is thoroughly detailed, and neighbor corpus confirms the need for vision-centric evaluation.
- **Defense efficacy (33% ASR reduction):** Medium. The training-free trigger shows promise in the paper’s setup, but its transferability to all commercial models and robustness to advanced visual deception remain unproven.
