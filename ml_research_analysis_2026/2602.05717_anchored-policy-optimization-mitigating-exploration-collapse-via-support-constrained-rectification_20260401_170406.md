# Anchored Policy Optimization: Mitigating Exploration Collapse Via Support-Constrained Rectification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05717
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05717
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: Anchored Policy Optimization (APO) achieves state-of-the-art Pass@1 (50.67%) and Pass@K (78.07%) on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, breaking the accuracy-diversity trade-off.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) often suffers from Recursive Space Contraction (RSC), where the policy collapses by discarding valid reasoning paths due to aggressive sharpening and blind negative squeezing. Standard KL regularization conflicts with necessary sharpening and fails to recover lost support. Anchored Policy Optimization (APO) resolves this by shifting from shape matching to support coverage: it defines a Safe Manifold (top-K high-confidence tokens from the reference) and applies a dual-force ratio rectification—pushing down errors while pulling up probability mass on the safe manifold only during negative updates. On math reasoning tasks with Qwen2.5-Math-7B, APO achieves state-of-the-art Pass@1 (50.67%) and Pass@K (78.07%), demonstrating a broken accuracy-diversity trade-off.

## Method Summary
Anchored Policy Optimization (APO) modifies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) by introducing a support constraint on a sparse safe manifold. For each token with negative advantage, it constructs a rectified importance sampling ratio: ˜r_APO = λ·(πθ(y_t)/π_old(y_t)) − β·r_anchor, where r_anchor is the normalized total probability of the safe manifold (Top-K tokens from the reference, excluding the current token). The hyperparameters are λ=1.05, β=0.1, K=8, and clipping ϵ=0.2. The push term (λ) suppresses errors, while the pull term (β) actively restores diversity by increasing mass on the safe manifold. APO operates only on negative-advantage samples, allowing aggressive sharpening on correct paths. The method is computationally efficient, requiring only a sparse Top-K computation per token.

## Key Results
- Achieves SOTA Pass@1 (50.67%) and high Pass@K (78.07%) on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, breaking the accuracy-diversity trade-off.
- Outperforms GRPO and KL-regularized baselines on mathematical benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Recursive Space Contraction (RSC) as Irreversible Collapse
**Claim:** Recursive Space Contraction (RSC) is an irreversible collapse of the policy's valid reasoning support caused by the combined dynamics of positive sharpening and negative squeezing.
**Mechanism:** Positive updates (rewarding a chosen path) passively suppress unsampled tokens via normalization, while negative updates (penalizing errors) redistribute probability mass proportional to existing token probability ("Rich-get-Richer"). This permanently discards low-probability but valid reasoning branches because their sampling probability and recovery gradient vanish.
**Core assumption:** The policy's update dynamics under Softmax parameterization create an absorbing state where once a valid token's probability nears zero, on-policy sampling and gradient signals cannot recover it.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "Recursive Space Contraction (RSC), an irreversible collapse driven by the combined dynamics of positive sharpening and negative squeezing, where the sampling probability of valid alternatives vanishes."
- [section 3.1] "theSqueezing Effectblindly redistributes the released mass to already dominant tokens rather than re-inflating the suppressed tail... this results in an irreversible collapse."
- [section B.2] Provides formal proof: "Once the policy enters a region where valid paths are unsampled and their logits are suppressed, it cannot escape via standard policy gradients."
**Break condition:** If the policy's initial distribution or the reward signal is structured such that all valid paths remain within the high-probability mass (Top-K) throughout training, the squeezing effect may not push any valid branch into the absorbing tail. A sufficiently diverse initial prior or a reward shaping that prevents aggressive early sharpening could interrupt the recursive loop.

### Mechanism 2: KL Regularization Imposes Rigid Shape Matching
**Claim:** Standard KL regularization imposes a rigid "Shape Matching" constraint that conflicts with the sharpening required for Pass@1 efficiency and fails to provide a proactive recovery mechanism.
**Mechanism:** KL divergence penalizes any deviation from the reference model's full probability density, forcing the policy to maintain uncertainty even on correct paths. This creates a gradient conflict where the KL penalty gradient competes with the reward gradient, potentially pushing updates outside the trust region and not addressing the topological issue of lost support.
**Core assumption:** The reference model's full distribution contains both valid knowledge support and noise; mimicking the entire shape is unnecessary and harmful for extracting correct reasoning paths.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [section 2.1] "Forcing the policy model to mimic the reference model’s full distribution, including its noise, creating a fundamental Gradient Conflict."
- [section 3.3] "Minimizing this divergence forces the policy πθ to align its entire probability density function with πref... This constraint is agnostic to the correctness of the reasoning path; it penalizes the RL agent for becoming confident (sharpening) even on correct answers."
- [section B.4] "Even if ∇θLCLIP is zero due to the ratio hitting the clipping boundary... the KL gradient ∇θLKL remains non-zero... can move the importance sampling ratio... outside the[1−ϵ,1 +ϵ]window."
**Break condition:** If the reference model's distribution is nearly deterministic (low entropy) on correct reasoning steps, Shape Matching may not conflict with sharpening. Alternatively, if the reward signal is weak or the trust region is large enough to absorb the conflicting gradients without instability, the practical impact may be muted.

### Mechanism 3: APO Enables Elastic Recovery via Dual-Force Ratio Rectification
**Claim:** Anchored Policy Optimization (APO) enables "Elastic Recovery" by shifting from Shape Matching to Support Coverage via Ratio Rectification with a dual-force mechanism (Push/Pull).
**Mechanism:** On error samples (negative advantage), APO constructs a rectified ratio ˜r_APO = λ·(πθ(y_t)/π_old(y_t)) - β·(∑_{k∈S_anchor} πθ(k) / Z_ref). The "Push" term suppresses the error token. The "Pull" term actively maximizes the total probability mass on a "Safe Manifold" (Top-K reference tokens excluding the error), which is mathematically collinear with the Support Coverage objective J_support. This restorative force is activated only during negative updates, permitting aggressive sharpening on correct paths.
**Core assumption:** A sparse, high-confidence subset of the reference model's support (Safe Manifold) contains nearly all valid reasoning paths, and restoring mass to this subset is sufficient for diversity recovery without hallucination.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "By defining a Safe Manifold based on the reference model’s high-confidence support, APO permits aggressive sharpening for efficiency while selectively invoking a restorative force during error correction to prevent collapse."
- [section 4] "APO employs a dual-force mechanism: a Push Force to suppress specific errors, and a corresponding Pull Force that actively re-inflates the Safe Manifold."
- [section B.1] "∇θLpull = C · ∇θJ_support... the gradient pushes strictly in the direction of increasing the total probability mass of the Safe Manifold."
- [section 3.2, Table 1] Empirical justification: Top-8 covers 97.47% of valid tokens, while Top-1 loses 16.16%.
**Break condition:** If the correct reasoning path consistently lies outside the defined Safe Manifold (e.g., the reference model is highly uncertain or incorrect on the true solution), then anchoring to its Top-K will fail to recover validity. The method assumes the reference's high-confidence support is a reliable proxy for latent correct solutions.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**
- **Why needed here:** APO operates within the RLVR paradigm where rewards are computed per whole trajectory (e.g., problem solved correctly). Understanding the tree pruning hypothesis—that RL optimizes by amplifying latent correct paths and suppressing incorrect ones—is central to diagnosing RSC.
- **Quick check question:** If an RL algorithm finds a correct reasoning path in the reference model's distribution, why might it still fail to generalize to other correct paths?

**Concept: Policy Gradient Mechanics & the Squeezing Effect**
- **Why needed here:** The core pathology (RSC) arises from the gradient update dynamics under Softmax. Deriving ∇_z_k log π(y_t) = δ_{k,y_t} - π(k) shows how positive/negative updates redistribute mass. The "Rich-get-Richer" effect (Δz_path ∝ π_path for negative updates) explains irreversible collapse.
- **Quick check question:** For a Softmax policy, why does penalizing an error token provide a smaller recovery gradient to a low-probability valid token than to a high-probability one?

**Concept: KL Divergence as a Shape Constraint**
- **Why needed here:** Standard RLVR uses D_KL(π_θ || π_ref) for regularization. Framing it as a "Shape Matching" constraint—mimicking the entire reference density—highlights its conflict with sharpening and its passive nature. Contrasting this with the desired "Support Coverage" objective is key to APO's innovation.
- **Quick check question:** How does minimizing D_KL(π_θ || π_ref) differ in effect from maximizing ∑_{y∈M_safe} π_θ(y)?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
1. Reference Model (`π_ref`): Frozen prior providing logits. Used to compute Safe Manifold (`Top-K`) and normalization constant `Z_ref`.
2. Safe Manifold (`M_safe`): Set of Top-K tokens from `π_ref` (per token prediction), *excluding* the current error token `y_t`. The anchor set `S_anchor`.
3. Virtual Anchor Ratio (`r_anchor`): `(1/Z_ref) * ∑_{k∈S_anchor} π_θ(k)`. Serves as differentiable proxy for total safe manifold mass.
4. Rectified Ratio (`˜r_APO`): `λ * (π_θ(y_t)/π_old(y_t)) - β * r_anchor`, used *only* for negative-advantage samples `(A_t < 0)`.
5. Clipping Operator: Applied to `˜r_APO` identical to standard PPO's `clip(r, 1-ϵ, 1+ϵ)` to maintain trust region.

**Critical path:** For a sampled token `y_t` with `A_t < 0`:
1. Detect error: `A_t` negative indicates a penalized trajectory/step.
2. Compute anchor set: Fetch `π_ref(·|context)`, take Top-K tokens, remove `y_t` to form `S_anchor`.
3. Compute anchor mass: `r_anchor = (1/Z_ref) * sum(π_θ(k) for k in S_anchor)`.
4. Rectify ratio: Construct `˜r_APO = λ*(π_θ(y_t)/π_old(y_t)) - β*r_anchor`.
5. Compute loss: `L_APO = min(˜r_APO * A_t, clip(˜r_APO,1-ϵ,1+ϵ)*A_t)`. Since `A_t < 0`, this becomes `max(˜r_APO * A_t, (1-ϵ)*A_t, (1+ϵ)*A_t)` (minimizing a negative value).
6. Backprop: Gradient flows through `˜r_APO` to `π_θ`. The `-β*r_anchor` term provides the "Pull" gradient aligned with increasing safe mass.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Anchor Size (K): Small K (e.g., 4) risks excluding valid paths (under-coverage). Large K (e.g., 16) introduces noise/dilution. K=8 empirically balances coverage (97.47%) and sparsity.
- Pull Coefficient (β): β=0 removes recovery (vanilla PG). Too high (≥0.2) may over-push, potentially flattening the distribution or conflicting with the Push term. Optimal β (~0.1) provides sufficient restorative force without harming sharpening.
- Push Coefficient (λ): λ=1.0 is standard rejection. λ>1.0 (e.g., 1.05) amplifies error suppression. Too high (≥1.1) causes "Over-pruning" where recoverable paths are excessively penalized.

**Failure signatures:**
1. Vanishing recovery: If `r_anchor` is too small (policy mass leaked from `M_safe`), the `-β*r_anchor` term's magnitude becomes negligible compared to the Push term, failing to restore mass.
2. Gradient conflict (if misapplied): Using APO's Pull term on positive-advantage samples (`A_t >= 0`) would conflict with sharpening. APO is *conditional* on negative advantage.
3. Anchor invalidity: If the true correct token `y*` is not in `M_safe` (K too small or reference model poor), Pull term cannot recover it. Check oracle coverage rate on reference model.
4. Clipping dominance: If `˜r_APO` consistently hits the lower clip bound `(1-ϵ)`, gradients vanish. This can indicate either successful strong correction (desired) or that β is so high that the anchor force is clipped before it can provide a meaningful gradient.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Oracle Coverage Validation: On a held set, compute the recall rate of ground-truth tokens within the Top-K support of the reference model. This empirically justifies the Safe Manifold scope (K). If recall < 95% for K=8, APO's anchor may not contain sufficient validity.
2. Gradient Alignment Probe: Isolate a batch of negative-advantage samples. Compute two gradient directions: (a) `∇_θ ∑_{k∈M_safe} π_θ(k)` (true Support Coverage gradient), (b) `∇_θ ( -β * r_anchor )` (APO Pull gradient). Measure cosine similarity. Values near 1.0 confirm alignment.
3. Entropy Trajectory: Track average token entropy on a validation set during training. Compare GRPO (should collapse, entropy ↓) vs. APO (should recover, entropy ↑ or stable). Visualize as in Figure 6 to confirm "Elastic Recovery."

## Limitations

- Safe Manifold anchored on reference model’s Top‑K may miss valid paths if reference is poor.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (λ, β, K) not fully explored; values chosen on limited benchmarks.
