# SynthSAEBench: Evaluating Sparse Autoencoders on Scalable Realistic Synthetic Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14687
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14687
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces SynthSAEBench, a synthetic benchmark with ground-truth features, revealing that Matching Pursuit SAEs can overfit to superposition noise to improve reconstruction without learning true features, and that no evaluated architecture achieves perfect feature recovery.

## Executive Summary
SynthSAEBench addresses the lack of precise benchmarks for sparse autoencoders (SAEs) by providing a toolkit that generates large-scale synthetic activations with configurable realistic properties—correlation, hierarchy, and superposition—along with a standardized 16k-feature benchmark (SynthSAEBench-16k) that includes ground-truth feature directions and activations. This controlled environment enables low-noise evaluation of SAE performance on feature recovery, reconstruction, and sparsity. The benchmark successfully reproduces several known SAE phenomena, including a disconnect between reconstruction quality and latent interpretability, poor probing performance, and an L0-mediated precision-recall trade-off. Additionally, it uncovers a new failure mode: Matching Pursuit SAEs exploit superposition noise to boost reconstruction metrics without actually learning the true feature directions. Across five evaluated architectures (BatchTopK, Matryoshka, Matching Pursuit, L1, JumpReLU), none achieve perfect feature recovery, establishing a clear target for future architectural improvements.

## Method Summary
The synthetic data generator constructs activations by first sampling binary firing indicators via a Gaussian copula with a configurable low-rank correlation matrix Σ. Hierarchical dependencies are enforced through a forest of trees, applying parental gating and mutual exclusion rules. Continuous coefficients are then drawn from a rectified Gaussian and multiplied with sampled feature directions (unit vectors) to produce the final activation as a linear sum plus bias. This process yields ground-truth feature firings and dictionary directions.

SAEs are trained on these activations using various architectures that modify the encoder, decoder, or loss to control sparsity. Training follows standard Adam optimization with a fixed learning rate schedule and batch size. Evaluation leverages the known ground truth: the Mean Correlation Coefficient (MCC) matches decoder columns to true dictionary entries via the Hungarian algorithm; probing F1-score measures how well a linear classifier can predict true firings from latent activations; additional metrics include variance explained, L0 sparsity, dead latent count, and uniqueness. An autotuner dynamically adjusts sparsity coefficients for L1 and JumpReLU to hit target L0 values.

## Key Results
- SynthSAEBench-16k provides a scalable, configurable synthetic benchmark with ground-truth features, enabling precise, low-noise SAE evaluation.
- The benchmark reproduces known SAE phenomena (reconstruction/latent quality disconnect, probing issues, L0-mediated precision-recall trade-off) and reveals a new failure mode: Matching Pursuit SAEs overfit superposition noise, improving reconstruction while harming feature recovery.
- None of the five evaluated SAE architectures achieve perfect feature recovery; Matryoshka BatchTopK performs best on feature recovery (MCC, probing F1), while Matching Pursuit excels at reconstruction (variance explained) but fails on recovery metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Controlled synthetic generation isolates failure modes.** The generator independently tunes correlation, hierarchy, and superposition, mapping real LLM SAE challenges onto controllable axes. This rests on the Linear Representation Hypothesis that features are linear directions. Evidence: the paper defines each component (low-rank Σ for correlation, forest for hierarchy, random vectors for superposition) and shows varying them affects SAE outcomes. Break condition: if LLM representations involve non-linear manifolds not captured by linear features, insights may not transfer.

**Mechanism 2: Ground-truth evaluation removes ambiguity.** Access to true feature directions and activations allows direct computation of alignment metrics (MCC, probing F1), eliminating noise from proxy tasks used in LLM benchmarks. Evidence: logistic probes achieve very high F1 (~0.974), confirming the benchmark can distinguish strong performance. Break condition: if synthetic features are not semantically coherent, high alignment may not imply interpretable features.

**Mechanism 3: More expressive encoders can overfit to noise.** Matching Pursuit’s iterative, adaptive encoder can exploit spurious correlations from overlapping features (superposition noise) to improve reconstruction without learning true directions. By varying superposition strength, the paper shows MP’s variance explained rises while MCC/F1 fall, demonstrating that reconstruction gains can be illusory. Evidence: Figure 7 shows divergent trends across superposition levels. Break condition: if real LLM superposition is structured differently, this overfitting pattern might not appear.

   - *Why needed:* The entire synthetic model assumes features are linear directions; understanding LRH is necessary to interpret the construction `a = D⊤c + b`.

   - *Why needed:* The paper evaluates various SAEs (L1, BatchTopK, Matryoshka, Matching Pursuit, JumpReLU), each modifying the encoder/decoder or loss to control sparsity. Knowing the underlying equations is key to understanding performance differences.

3. **Sparsity metrics (L0) and the precision-recall trade-off**  
   - *Why needed:* Results are analyzed by target L0; the paper highlights a precision-recall trade-off mediated by L0, a core phenomenon in SAE probing.  
   - *Quick check:* Why would increasing a SAE’s L0 typically increase recall at the cost of precision for detecting ground-truth features?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Synthetic Data Generator → SAE Trainer → Evaluator, with a Controller (Autotuner) adjusting sparsity coefficients for L1/JumpReLU.

**Critical path:**
1. Instantiate the SynthSAEBench-16k generator with fixed parameters (N=16,384 features, hidden dim 768, etc.).
2. Generate 200M training samples.
3. Train a SAE (width 4096) with the chosen architecture and target L0 (using autotuner if needed), using Adam (lr=3e-4), batch size 1024, and linear LR decay.
4. Evaluate on a held-out set using ground-truth metrics (variance explained, MCC, probing F1, etc.).
5. Repeat across an L0 sweep (15–45) and for model variants (e.g., varying superposition rank).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Reconstruction vs. Feature Recovery: Matryoshka trades lower variance explained for higher MCC/F1.
- Sparsity (L0) vs. Recall: Higher L0 increases recall but lowers precision.
- Encoder expressivity vs. Overfitting: Matching Pursuit improves reconstruction but overfits superposition noise.
- Auxiliary loss design: Matryoshka requires per-prefix auxiliary loss to reduce dead latents at low L0.

**Failure signatures:**
- Dead latents, especially at low L0 for BatchTopK/Matryoshka.
- High reconstruction with low MCC/F1 (Matching Pursuit).
- Disconnect between reconstruction and probing (e.g., Matryoshka’s low variance explained but high probing).
- Autotuner instability for L1/JumpReLU.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline replication: Train all five architectures (BatchTopK, Matryoshka BatchTopK, Matching Pursuit, Standard L1, JumpReLU) on SynthSAEBench-16k at L0=25. Verify that Matryoshka leads in MCC/F1 while MP leads in variance explained (reproducing Figure 5).
2. Superposition ablation: Vary the hidden dimension D of the synthetic model to change superposition strength (keep N=16k fixed). Retrain all SAEs at L0=25. Confirm that MP’s variance explained increases with superposition while MCC/F1 decrease (Figure 7).
3. Supervised probe upper bound: Train batched logistic regression probes on the same activations. Verify probes achieve F1 ~0.97, while the best SAE achieves ~0.88, establishing the performance gap.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does feature capacity scale with hidden dimension?**  
   - **Basis:** Appendix J experimentally explores scaling but over a limited range of dimensions and feature counts.  
   - **Why unresolved:** The scaling law likely depends on feature distributions (e.g., Zipfian firing, correlation structure), and a theoretical foundation is missing.  
   - **What would resolve:** Systematic experiments across wider ranges of dimensions and feature numbers, combined with theoretical analysis (e.g., random matrix theory), to derive a precise formula linking capacity to hidden dimension and other parameters.

2. **What causes dead latents in JumpReLU and their sensitivity?**  
   - **Basis:** Appendix G reports that JumpReLU is “quite finicky” on SynthSAEBench-16k, with high incidence of dead latents, and that tweaking initialization thresholds and decoder norms helps.  
   - **Why unresolved:** The root cause—whether due to optimization dynamics, feature interference, or the JumpReLU gating mechanism—remains unknown.  
   - **What would resolve:** Ablation studies varying superposition, hierarchy, and correlation, along with diagnostic monitoring (gradients, activation distributions) during training, to pinpoint failure modes.

3. **Extending the framework to non-linear representations.**  
   - **Basis:** Section 8 states future work to extend to Minkowski representations and feature manifolds.  
   - **Why unresolved:** The current linear model may not capture more plausible representations (polytopes, manifolds); it is unclear whether SAEs can recover such structures or need architectural changes.  
   - **What would resolve:** Designing generative models that produce activations with known manifold or polytope structures, and devising evaluation metrics beyond linear matching (e.g., subspace alignment, manifold coverage). Success would be SAEs that accurately recover these ground-truth non-linear structures.

## Limitations
- **Transfer to real LLMs:** The synthetic model assumes exact linear feature directions and simple superposition (random unit vectors). If LLM representations involve non-linear manifolds or structured superposition, the identified failure modes may differ.
- **Semantic validity of features:** Ground-truth features are linear directions with engineered statistics; even perfect metric alignment may not guarantee semantically coherent, human-interpretable features.
- **Implementation fidelity:** Minor details (autotuning hyperparameters, Matryoshka auxiliary loss formulation, MP encoder iterations) could impact results and require careful replication.

## Confidence
- **Benchmark setup and metrics (MCC, probing F1):** High – fully specified and reproducible from code.
- **Claim that MP-SAEs exploit superposition noise:** Medium – strong evidence in synthetic setting but uncertain transfer to LLMs.
- **External corpus support for synthetic realism:** Low – neighboring papers apply SAEs to LLMs but do not validate the synthetic design’s realism.

## Next Checks
1. Reproduce the overfitting trend: Train Matching Pursuit SAEs across varying superposition levels (different hidden dimensions D of the synthetic model) and confirm that variance explained increases while MCC/F1 decrease.
2. Test on real SAE: Apply SAEs trained on SynthSAEBench-16k to an existing LLM SAE checkpoint (e.g., from Anthropic) to see if the reconstruction/MCC discrepancy persists.
3. Human evaluation of synthetic features: Sample top-activating tokens for ground-truth features and assess whether they form coherent semantic concepts (e.g., via crowdsourced labeling).
