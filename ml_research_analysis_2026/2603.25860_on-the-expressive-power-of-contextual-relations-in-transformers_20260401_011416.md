# On the Expressive Power of Contextual Relations in Transformers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25860
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25860
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Sinkhorn Transformers can universally approximate any continuous contextual semantic relation between texts via entropy-regularized optimal transport.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of mathematically characterizing Transformer expressiveness for modeling contextual semantic relations between texts. It introduces a measure-theoretic framework where texts are probability measures over a compact semantic space, and contextual relations are coupling measures. The proposed Sinkhorn Transformer appends a Sinkhorn operator (entropy-regularized optimal transport) to standard Transformer encoders. A universal approximation theorem proves that for any continuous mapping from pairs of probability measures to joint couplings, this architecture can uniformly approximate it in Wasserstein-1 distance, formally showing that attention-based models can represent arbitrary continuous systems of contextual relations.

## Method Summary
Each text is converted to an empirical probability measure (uniform over token embeddings) in a compact semantic space. Two separate Transformer encoders process these measures to produce query and key functions per token. A cost matrix is defined as the negative inner product of queries and keys. The Sinkhorn operator is applied to this cost to yield a doubly-stochastic coupling matrix, interpreted as the contextual relation. This combines the universality of Transformers for measure-to-measure functions with the density of Sinkhorn operators in the space of coupling systems.

## Key Results
- Universal approximation theorem: Sinkhorn Transformers can approximate any continuous coupling system arbitrarily well.
- The family of Sinkhorn operators with continuous costs is dense in the space of continuous coupling systems.
- Standard Transformer encoders suffice to parameterize the required cost functions via query/key outputs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Measure Representation for Texts
- **Claim:** Representing texts as probability measures over a compact semantic space enables handling variable-length inputs and provides a stable domain for functional analysis.
- **Mechanism:** Texts are mapped to empirical measures (uniform over token embeddings), converting sequences into points in the compact space \(\mathcal{P}(X)\), where continuous operators can be defined.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic embedding space \(X\) is compact; token embeddings are bounded.
- **Break condition:** If \(X\) is not compact, \(\mathcal{P}(X)\) may not be compact, breaking uniform approximation.

### Mechanism 2: Density of Sinkhorn Operators
- **Claim:** Sinkhorn operators (with continuous costs) form a dense family in the space of continuous coupling systems.
- **Mechanism:** By varying the continuous cost \(c\), the Sinkhorn plan \(S_c\) can approximate any coupling \(F\) with correct marginals.
- **Core assumption:** Target coupling \(F\) is continuous in the weak topology.
- **Break condition:** If \(F\) is discontinuous or \(c\) cannot be made continuous, density fails.

### Mechanism 3: Transformer Encoders for Cost Parameterization
- **Claim:** Transformer encoders can approximate the necessary query and key functions to parameterize the cost.
- **Mechanism:** Encoders process input measures to output \(Q(\mu,x)\) and \(K(\nu,y)\); cost \(c = -\langle Q, K \rangle\). Transformer universality for measure-valued functions justifies approximating arbitrary continuous \(G, H\).
- **Core assumption:** Encoders can uniformly approximate continuous functions on \(\mathcal{P}(X) \times X\) and \(\mathcal{P}(Y) \times Y\).
- **Break condition:** If required functions exceed the approximation class of finite Transformers, approximation may fail.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Probability Measures on Compact Spaces**
   - **Why needed:** The framework treats texts as elements of \(\mathcal{P}(X)\); compactness and weak topology are essential for function space analysis.
   - **Quick check:** Why weak topology over total variation? How does compactness of \(X\) imply compactness of \(\mathcal{P}(X)\)? (Answer: Prokhorov's theorem.)
2. **Entropic Optimal Transport and Sinkhorn Operator**
   - **Why needed:** Sinkhorn operator \(S_c\) is the core building block for coupling approximation; its stability and convergence are key.
   - **Quick check:** Role of entropy regularization parameter \(\varepsilon\)? Why fix \(\varepsilon = 1\) without loss? (Answer: Scaling invariance; can absorb into cost.)
3. **Transformer Context Operators and Universal Approximation**
   - **Why needed:** Encoders are modeled as context operators; their universality (from prior work) justifies approximating arbitrary continuous functions on measures.
   - **Quick check:** How does measure-valued attention extend classical softmax? (Answer: Integrates over empirical measure instead of summing over tokens directly.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input texts -> token embeddings -> uniform empirical measures \(\mu, \nu\) -> two Transformer encoders -> query/key vectors \(Q(\mu,x), K(\nu,y)\) -> cost matrix \(c(x,y) = -\langle Q, K \rangle\) -> Sinkhorn layer -> coupling measure \(T(\mu,\nu)\)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Ensure embedding space is bounded (e.g., via LayerNorm) for compactness.
  2. Implement encoders as standard Transformers operating on measures (via integration over empirical measure).
  3. Compute pairwise \(QK^\top\) scores, treat negative log as cost, run Sinkhorn iterations.
  4. Output normalized matrix as coupling.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Sinkhorn only at final layer vs. all layers: Final layer reduces cost and preserves early expressivity; replacing all may over-regularize.
  - Number of Sinkhorn iterations: Tradeoff between approximation precision and latency; typically 5-10 iterations suffice.
  - Cost function design: Negative inner product generalizes softmax; other costs possible but inner product is simplest.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Non-convergence of Sinkhorn due to extreme cost values; mitigate with gradient clipping or cost offset.
  - Marginal mismatch: Output coupling marginals deviate from input measures; check \(\pi \mathbf{1} \approx \mu\), \(\pi^\top \mathbf{1} \approx \nu\).
  - Break of continuity assumption: Discontinuous target couplings may cause high approximation error near discontinuities.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Synthetic coupling approximation: Train on random continuous couplings; compare Sinkhorn Transformer vs. softmax attention in Wasserstein-1 error.
  2. Ablation on Sinkhorn placement: Compare final-layer-only vs. Sinkhorn at every attention layer on a balanced coupling task like word alignment.
  3. Expressivity vs. standard Transformer: Test on a task requiring doubly-stochastic output; standard Transformer should fail, Sinkhorn should succeed.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can Sinkhorn Transformers efficiently learn coupling systems from finite data, and what are their generalization guarantees?
- **Open Question 2:** What are the quantitative approximation rates (e.g., in terms of network width/depth) for Sinkhorn Transformers approximating arbitrary continuous coupling systems?
- **Open Question 3:** Can the measure-theoretic framework and universality results be extended to non-compact semantic spaces or to sequential/dynamical settings (e.g., autoregressive generation)?
- **Open Question 4:** Does the reliance on entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn) limit the class of representable contextual relations compared to other regularization schemes or primal-dual formulations?
- **Open Question 5:** Can transformer architectures without the Sinkhorn final layer (i.e., standard softmax attention) also approximate arbitrary coupling systems, or is the doubly-stochastic normalization essential?

## Limitations
- Compactness requirement: Semantic space must be compact, which may restrict natural embeddings.
- Continuity assumption: Target coupling must be continuous; real-world relations can be discontinuous.
- Encoder capacity: Depends on Transformers approximating arbitrary functions, which may require infinite width/depth in theory.

## Confidence
- **Measure-theoretic framework:** Medium (validation depends on enforcing compactness in practice).
- **Density of Sinkhorn operators:** High (rigorous theorem under continuity assumptions).
- **Universal approximation:** Medium (hinges on optimistic assumptions about Transformer encoder expressivity).
