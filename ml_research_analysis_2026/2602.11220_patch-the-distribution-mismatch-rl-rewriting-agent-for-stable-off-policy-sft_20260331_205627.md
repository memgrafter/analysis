# Patch the Distribution Mismatch: RL Rewriting Agent for Stable Off-Policy SFT

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11220
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11220
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** Proposes an RL-based data-rewriting agent that reduces catastrophic forgetting in LLM fine-tuning by an average of 12.34% relative drop on general-domain benchmarks while achieving math accuracy gains comparable to standard SFT.

## Executive Summary
Catastrophic forgetting during supervised fine-tuning (SFT) often stems from a distribution mismatch between fixed expert demonstrations and the model’s current generation distribution. This paper introduces a reinforcement learning (RL) agent that learns a policy to rewrite downstream training targets. The agent, trained via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with LoRA patches on a frozen backbone, generates task-consistent rewrites that are better aligned with the model’s native QA-style distribution and encourages diversity. A hard gate ensures only correct solutions are considered for alignment and diversity rewards. The resulting rewritten dataset enables downstream SFT that achieves similar in-domain gains as standard SFT but with significantly reduced forgetting on general-purpose benchmarks, demonstrating a better trade-off.

## Method Summary
The method operates in two stages. **Stage I** trains a rewriting policy \( R_\phi \) (implemented as LoRA adapters on a frozen backbone \( \pi_0 \)) using GRPO. For each expert demonstration \((x, y^*)\), the agent samples a group of \(K\) rewrites \(\tilde{y}\). Rewards consist of: a binary task-consistency gate \(r_{task}\) (rule-based answer check plus an LLM judge for reasoning validity); a distributional alignment reward \(r_{dist}\) (negative log-likelihood under \(\pi_0(\cdot|x)\), normalized within the group); and a diversity reward \(r_{div}\) (semantic pairwise distance among feasible rewrites). Alignment and diversity rewards are only applied if \(r_{task}=1\) (hard gate). **Stage II** constructs the final dataset \(D_R\): for each \((x, y^*)\), one rewrite is sampled; if it passes \(r_{task}\), it is kept; otherwise the original \(y^*\) is used. Standard SFT is then performed on \(D_R\) using the full model initialized from \(\pi_0\).

## Key Results
- The approach reduces average relative performance drop on general-domain benchmarks by **12.34%** compared to standard SFT, while achieving similar math accuracy gains.
- On **Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct**, math accuracy increases by **11.75%** with only a **4.51%** drop in general performance, versus a **17.58%** drop under standard SFT.
- Cross-backbone experiments (Llama-3.2-1B/3B, Mistral-7B) consistently show improved gain-retention trade-off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Rewriting supervision targets to be more generatable under the backbone's QA-style distribution \( \pi_0(\cdot|x) \) reduces the implicit importance-weighting amplification of low-likelihood tokens during downstream SFT, stabilizing optimization and mitigating forgetting.
- **Mechanism:** Standard SFT minimizes \(-\log \pi_\theta(y^*|x)\). If \(\pi_\theta\) assigns very low probability to \(y^*\), the gradient update is implicitly scaled by \(1/\pi_\theta(y^*|x)\), causing high-variance, large-magnitude updates that over-shift \(\pi_\theta\) toward the downstream distribution. The rewriting policy \(R_\phi\) produces task-consistent \(\tilde{y}\) with higher likelihood under \(\pi_0(\cdot|x)\), so the downstream SFT loss \(-\log \pi_\theta(\tilde{y}|x)\) starts lower and involves fewer low-probability tokens, leading to smaller, more stable updates and less catastrophic forgetting.
- **Core assumption:** The backbone's QA-style distribution \( \pi_0(\cdot|x) \) is a better proxy for the model's natural behavior during downstream SFT than the prompt-conditional distribution used in fixed-template rewriting.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] notes that fixed-template rewriting "can only partially narrow the effective distribution gap"; [section 3.4.2] defines alignment reward \(r_{dist}\) as normalized NLL under \(\pi_0(\cdot|x)\); experiments show lower initial SFT loss (Fig. 2) and reduced forgetting.
- **Break condition:** If \(\pi_0\)'s QA-style distribution itself is highly mismatched with the downstream task or has lost general capabilities, aligning rewrites to it may not improve downstream stability.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Using a hard gate for task consistency (\(r_{task} \in \{0,1\}\)) to condition auxiliary rewards prevents noisy reward signals from corrupting policy learning for \(R_\phi\), leading to higher-quality feasible rewrites.
- **Mechanism:** The total reward \(r = r_{task} + r_{task} \cdot (\lambda_{dist} r_{dist} + \lambda_{div} r_{div})\) masks alignment and diversity rewards for infeasible rewrites (\(r_{task}=0\)). This avoids applying positive shaping rewards to incorrect solutions, which could incentivize plausible-but-wrong outputs that are in-distribution. The hard gate ensures \(R_\phi\) only optimizes for alignment/diversity on solutions that first satisfy correctness.
- **Core assumption:** Task consistency is a non-negotiable prerequisite; any improvement in distributional alignment or diversity is meaningless without a correct solution.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions "under a hard task-consistency gate"; [section 3.4] explicitly describes the gating mechanism to prevent misleading shaping signals; [section 4.4] ablation shows soft shaping reduces pass rate and math performance.
- **Break condition:** If the task-consistency verifier is too strict or noisy, it may reject many potentially correct rewrites, reducing the effective training signal for alignment/diversity and limiting diversity of feasible rewrites.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Parameter-efficient LoRA patching on a frozen backbone \(\pi_0\) learns a localized "correction" to the rewriting distribution, avoiding full-policy drift and ensuring rewrites remain semantically close to the model's original capabilities.
- **Mechanism:** \(R_\phi\) is implemented as LoRA adapters (low-rank updates) applied to the frozen \(\pi_0\). This limits the capacity of the learned rewriting policy, biasing it toward making minimal, targeted adjustments to the base model's output distribution under the rewriting prompt. It prevents \(R_\phi\) from learning a divergent generation style that would transfer poorly to downstream SFT.
- **Core assumption:** The gap between \(\pi_0(\cdot|x, y^*, x_{prompt})\) and the desired \(\pi_0(\cdot|x)\)-aligned distribution is a low-dimensional "patch" capturable by low-rank adapters.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] states that freezing the backbone and using LoRA provides a capacity-limited "patch" over \(\pi_0\), empirically reducing unnecessary drift; [section 4.3] shows high task-consistency yield, suggesting the adapted policy stays within the base model's competence.
- **Break condition:** If the required shift from prompt-conditional to QA-style distribution is highly complex and non-linear, a low-rank LoRA patch may be insufficient, leading to suboptimal alignment.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Off-policy Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
  - **Why needed here:** Standard SFT on fixed expert demonstrations is framed as an off-policy learning problem where the mismatch between the expert distribution and the model's current distribution causes instability via implicit importance-weighting effects.
  - **Quick check:** Explain why training on fixed demonstrations \(y^*\) can lead to high-variance updates if the current model \(\pi_\theta\) assigns very low probability to \(y^*\).

- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning for Data Generation (Policy Learning)**
  - **Why needed here:** The core method treats rewriting as a policy learning task, using RL (GRPO) to optimize non-differentiable or complex rewards (task consistency via verifier, distributional alignment via base model likelihood, diversity). This moves beyond heuristic prompting or MLE.
  - **Quick check:** Why is standard maximum likelihood fine-tuning (MLE) of a rewriting model unsuitable for directly optimizing the multi-faceted objectives (correctness, alignment, diversity)? What does RL provide?

- **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)**
  - **Why needed here:** The rewriting agent \(R_\phi\) uses LoRA to avoid full-model training, preserve base model capabilities, and act as a constrained "patch." This design affects stability and generalization.
  - **Quick check:** What are the theoretical and practical benefits of using LoRA instead of full fine-tuning for learning the rewriting policy in this context?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Frozen backbone \(\pi_0\) -> Rewriting agent \(R_\phi\) (LoRA on \(\pi_0\)) -> GRPO optimizer -> Rewards (\(r_{task}\), \(r_{dist}\), \(r_{div}\)) -> Dataset construction (GENERATE, VERIFY, FALLBACK) -> Downstream SFT on \(\pi_\theta\) (from \(\pi_0\))

- **Critical path:**
  1. **Stage I (RL Training):** For each \((x, y^*)\) in agent training set, sample group \(S_x\) of \(K\) rewrites from \(R_\phi\). Compute \(r_{task}\) for each. For feasible ones (\(S_x^+\)), compute \(r_{dist}\) (NLL under \(\pi_0(\cdot|x)\)) and \(r_{div}\) (semantic diversity within \(S_x^+\)). Update LoRA parameters \(\phi\) via GRPO to maximize expected reward.
  2. **Stage II (Dataset Construction):** For each \((x, y^*)\) in SFT set, generate one rewrite; apply \(r_{task}\) check; keep rewrite if pass, else fallback to \(y^*\). Form \(D_R\).
  3. **Stage III (Downstream SFT):** Train full model \(\pi_\theta\) (initialized from \(\pi_0\)) on \(D_R\) via standard MLE.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Compute vs. Stability:** RL training of \(R_\phi\) adds significant compute but aims to reduce downstream SFT instability and forgetting.
  - **Diversity vs. Consistency:** \(r_{div}\) encourages semantic diversity but may conflict with \(r_{dist}\) (which may favor templated outputs). The hard gate mediates by only applying \(r_{div}\) to correct rewrites.
  - **LoRA rank (\(r\)):** Lower rank reduces capacity for alignment but limits drift; higher rank may learn more flexible rewrites but risks overfitting or deviating from \(\pi_0\)'s space.
  - **Candidate group size (\(K\)):** Larger \(K\) improves reward estimation (diversity, normalization) but increases RL batch compute; diminishing returns observed around \(K=10\).

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Low task-consistency yield (<50%):** \(R_\phi\) fails to generate many correct rewrites, leading to high fallback rate and minimal dataset modification.
  - **High training loss variance in downstream SFT:** Rewritten data still contains many low-likelihood tokens or inconsistent styles.
  - **Diversity collapse:** All feasible rewrites for an \(x\) are near-identical (high cosine similarity), indicating \(r_{div}\) ineffective.
  - **Reward hacking:** \(R_\phi\) learns to satisfy \(r_{task}\) via superficial tricks (e.g., correct final answer but invalid reasoning) that bypass the LLM judge. Monitor verification component pass rates.
  - **Poor generalization retention despite rewriting:** Indicates \(r_{dist}\) is insufficient or mis-specified; perhaps \(\pi_0(\cdot|x)\) itself has drifted.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation of reward components:** Remove \(r_{dist}\), \(r_{div}\), or both, and measure impact on math gains and general retention to validate necessity of each signal.
  2. **Sweep candidate group size \(K\) in GRPO:** Determine the sweet spot for stable reward estimation vs. compute cost.
  3. **Fallback necessity test:** Train downstream SFT on success-only subset (no fallback) vs. GENERATE-VERIFY-FALLBACK. Quantify cost of rewriting failures (coverage vs. quality).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Scale to >7B models?** Limitations: only up to 7B tested; LoRA/GRPO stability at scale unknown.
- **Generalize to other domains (code, dialogue)?** Limitations: math-only evaluation may not generalize; other tasks have different distribution shifts.
- **Improve reward/verifier design?** Limitations: automatic verifier may be imperfect and bias rewrites; hard gate and LLM judge might miss subtle errors.
- **Update alignment target during SFT?** Inferred: static alignment to \(\pi_0\); policy \(\pi_\theta\) changes ignored during rewriting, potentially suboptimal.

## Limitations
- Reproducibility is hindered by missing details: LLM judge model/prompt, GRPO hyperparameters (learning rate, epochs, KL coefficient), reward weights (\(\lambda_{dist}\), \(\lambda_{div}\)), LoRA layer scope, and dataset split seed.
- Core assumption that \(\pi_0\)’s QA distribution better predicts SFT dynamics may fail if \(\pi_0\)’s general knowledge erodes.
- Hard gating could cause low rewrite coverage if the verifier is too strict; LoRA patches might be too limited for complex distribution shifts.

## Confidence
- **Alignment via rewriting stabilizes SFT:** Medium
- **Hard gate necessity:** High (ablation confirms)
- **LoRA patches preserve capabilities:** Medium
- **Overall gain-retention improvement:** High (cross-backbone consistency)
