# FedUAF: Uncertainty-Aware Fusion with Reliability-Guided Aggregation for Multimodal Federated Sentiment Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.13291
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13291
- **Reference count**: 18
- **Primary result**: FedUAF consistently achieves lower MAE than state-of-the-art federated baselines across varying missing-modality ratios (0.2 to 0.8) and non-IID intensities (0.2 to 1.0), with up to 18% reduction on MOSI under severe conditions.

## Executive Summary
FedUAF is a framework designed for multimodal federated sentiment analysis that simultaneously addresses three critical challenges: missing modalities, non-IID data distributions, and noisy client updates. It introduces two key mechanisms: uncertainty-aware fusion, which dynamically weights each modality’s contribution based on prediction-level uncertainty, and reliability-guided aggregation, which scales client updates by an estimate of their affective reliability. Evaluations on the CMU-MOSI and CMU-MOSEI datasets demonstrate that FedUAF outperforms existing federated baselines, particularly under harsh conditions such as high missing-modality ratios and extreme non-IID splits. The approach also exhibits increased robustness to a substantial proportion of noisy clients, making it a promising solution for real-world decentralized multimodal sentiment analysis.

## Method Summary
In FedUAF, each client trains a local multimodal sentiment analysis model. During each communication round, the client processes its available modalities and produces modality-specific predictions alongside uncertainty estimates (e.g., entropy or variance). An uncertainty-aware fusion module then combines these predictions, assigning higher weights to modalities with lower uncertainty, thereby automatically downweighting missing or noisy modalities. Additionally, the client computes an affective reliability score—likely derived from prediction consistency or confidence across samples—which reflects the trustworthiness of its update. Before sending updates to the server, the client scales its model update (e.g., gradients or weights) by this reliability score. The server aggregates these scaled updates from selected clients to update the global model. This two-pronged design ensures that missing modalities do not degrade performance and that updates from clients with divergent data distributions or noisy labels have reduced influence, leading to a more robust global model.

## Key Results
- Under severe conditions (80% missing modalities, maximum non-IID), FedUAF reduces MAE by up to 18% on MOSI and 3% on MOSEI compared to the strong baseline FedMAC.
- FedUAF maintains consistently lower MAE than federated baselines across a wide range of missing-modality ratios (0.2–0.8) and non-IID intensities (0.2–1.0).
- As the proportion of noisy clients increases up to 60%, the MAE of FedUAF grows more slowly than that of competing methods, demonstrating superior robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
FedUAF’s gains stem from its dual defense mechanisms. First, uncertainty-aware fusion directly tackles missing and noisy modalities: modalities with higher uncertainty (often due to absence or poor quality) receive lower fusion weights, preventing them from introducing harmful signals. Second, reliability-guided aggregation mitigates the impact of heterogeneous client updates: clients that produce predictions with high affective reliability (indicating alignment with the underlying sentiment task) contribute more to the global model, while those with low reliability (e.g., due to non-IID shifts or label noise) are downweighted. Together, these mechanisms create a self-correcting system where the most trustworthy information—both at the modality level and the client level—drives learning, resulting in improved performance and robustness.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Local Multimodal Model → Uncertainty Estimator → Fusion Module → Reliability Calculator → Scaled Update → Server Aggregator → Global Model  

**Critical Path**:  
1. Client trains local model on its data.  
2. For a batch, model outputs predictions per modality and uncertainty estimates.  
3. Fusion module combines modalities using inverse uncertainty weights.  
4. Reliability calculator computes a client-level score (e.g., average fusion confidence).  
5. Client scales its model update (e.g., gradients) by reliability and sends to server.  
6. Server aggregates scaled updates (e.g., weighted average) to update global model.  

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- Uncertainty estimation adds computational overhead and may be inaccurate on small local datasets.  
- Reliability scaling could be exploited by malicious clients that artificially inflate reliability.  
- Dynamic weighting might underutilize a modality that is actually informative but assigned high uncertainty due to estimation error.  

**Failure Signatures**:  
- Sudden increase in MAE on specific modalities indicates uncertainty estimator failure.  
- High variance in client reliability scores suggests non-IID data is not being properly accounted for.  
- Global model performance degrades rapidly as noisy client proportion rises if reliability scaling is insufficient.  

**First Experiments**:  
1. Main performance evaluation: Compare FedUAF against federated baselines on CMU-MOSI and CMU-MOSEI under varying missing-modality ratios and non-IID intensities.  
2. Ablation study: Remove uncertainty-aware fusion and reliability-guided aggregation separately to quantify each component’s contribution.  
3. Robustness test: Inject label noise into a subset of clients and measure MAE as noise proportion increases.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to two datasets (CMU-MOSI, CMU-MOSEI); generalization to other multimodal tasks or modality sets is unclear.
- No statistical significance tests or variance over multiple random seeds are reported, limiting confidence in observed improvements.
- Computational overhead of uncertainty estimation and reliability scaling is not quantified; the assumption of reliable uncertainty estimates may not hold for small or highly heterogeneous local data.
