# Linguistic Indicators of Early Cognitive Decline in the DementiaBank Pitt Corpus: A Statistical and Machine Learning Study

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11028
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11028
- **Reference Count:** 25
- **Primary Result:** Syntactic and grammatical features (POS-only) achieve stable macro F1-scores of ~0.71–0.75 under conservative subject-level evaluation, outperforming raw lexical text and providing interpretable markers of early dementia.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates linguistic markers of early cognitive decline using spontaneous speech transcripts from the DementiaBank Pitt Corpus. By comparing three feature representations—raw text, POS-enhanced (lexical + syntactic), and POS-only (syntactic)—under both transcript-level and subject-level cross-validation, the authors demonstrate that grammatically abstracted features retain strong discriminative power without lexical content. Key findings include increased use of pronouns, adverbs, and punctuation, along with decreased auxiliary verbs, determiners, and nouns in dementia speech, accompanied by reduced lexical diversity and shorter sentences. The results support the use of interpretable syntactic features as robust, transparent signals for language-based screening tools.

## Method Summary
The analysis uses 500 transcripts (control: 243, dementia: 257) from the Cookie Theft picture description task. For each transcript, three feature sets are extracted: (1) raw lexical/structural statistics (e.g., TTR, sentence length), (2) POS-enhanced (content words retained lexically, function words as POS tags), and (3) POS-only (all tokens replaced by POS tags, content-word tags excluded). Models include L2-regularized logistic regression and random forests (200 trees), trained with class weighting. Evaluation employs two protocols: a transcript-level 80/20 split and a subject-level 5-fold GroupKFold (preventing speaker leakage). Feature importance is derived from model coefficients (LR) or mean decrease impurity (RF). Statistical validation uses Mann-Whitney U tests and Cliff’s delta effect sizes, with p-values adjusted via Benjamini-Hochberg.

## Key Results
- POS-only and POS-enhanced representations achieve macro F1-scores of 0.71–0.75 under subject-level cross-validation, surpassing raw text performance.
- Increased proportions of pronouns (δ = -0.39), adverbs (δ = -0.41), and punctuation, and decreased auxiliaries (δ = +0.35), determiners (δ = +0.30), and nouns (δ = +0.37) are robust univariate markers (all p_adj < 1e-8).
- Discourse-level degradation is indicated by reduced semantic coherence (δ = -0.14), lower lexical diversity (TTR δ = +0.18), and shorter sentences (δ = +0.18).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Syntactic abstraction (POS-only features) provides robust, speaker-generalizable markers by removing lexical variability that may obscure grammatical degradation.
- **Mechanism:** Cognitive decline impairs syntactic planning and working memory, leading to measurable changes in part-of-speech distributions (e.g., more pronouns, fewer auxiliaries) independent of specific word choices. Abstracting to POS tags isolates these structural patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Early dementia affects grammatical processing systems in ways consistent across individuals, even when lexical content varies.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "syntactic and grammatical features retain strong discriminative power even without lexical content"; [section V.B.3] Subject-level POS-only LR F1 = 0.71±0.07, RF F1 = 0.65±0.07; [corpus] DementiaBank Pitt enables subject-level CV via multiple transcripts per subject.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Dysregulation of functional word usage (increased pronouns/adverbs, decreased auxiliaries/determiners/nouns) is a core, statistically robust linguistic signature of early cognitive decline.
- **Mechanism:** Declining executive function and working memory reduce ability to plan complex syntax and retrieve content words, leading to over-reliance on simple, high-frequency function words and under-use of grammatically complex markers and content-bearing nouns.
- **Core assumption:** Shifts in functional vs. content word proportions reflect a compensatory or degraded language production strategy tied to cognitive load.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "increased use of pronouns, adverbs, and punctuation; decreased use of auxiliary verbs, determiners, and nouns"; [section V.A.7] Cliff’s δ: PRON = -0.39, ADV = -0.41, AUX = +0.35, DET = +0.30, NOUN = +0.37 (all p_adj < 1e-8); [section V.B.4] Subject-level AUX δ = +0.357, ADV δ = -0.308, PRON δ = -0.274.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Degradation in discourse-level coherence and lexical diversity provides an independent, scalable signal that complements syntactic markers and remains detectable under conservative evaluation.
- **Mechanism:** Cognitive decline impairs ability to maintain narrative theme and access broad vocabulary, leading to repetitive, shorter, semantically disjointed speech. These macro-level features are less sensitive to local parsing errors.
- **Core assumption:** Discourse organization and lexical retrieval rely on higher-order cognitive systems (episodic memory, executive function) compromised early in dementia.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "reduced lexical diversity and shorter sentences"; [section V.A.7] semantic coherence δ = -0.14 (p_adj=0.009), TTR δ = +0.18 (p_adj=0.0012), mean sentence length δ = +0.18 (p_adj=0.00078); [section V.B.4] Subject-level semantic coherence δ = -0.143 (p_adj=0.00937), num types δ = +0.164 (p_adj=0.00265).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Subject-level vs. transcript-level cross-validation**
  - *Why needed here:* Prevents data leakage when multiple transcripts come from the same participant, which inflates performance and fails to test generalization to new speakers—a critical requirement for clinical screening.
  - *Quick check:* What is the methodological risk if you treat all 500 transcripts as independent samples when 100 participants each contributed 5 transcripts? How does GroupKFold mitigate this?
- **Concept: Non-parametric effect sizes (Cliff’s delta) for heterogeneous clinical data**
  - *Why needed here:* Linguistic features are rarely normally distributed in real-world speech data, especially with small samples and heterogeneous populations. Cliff’s delta quantifies group separation without distributional assumptions.
  - *Quick check:* Why might Cohen’s d be misleading here, and what does a Cliff’s delta of -0.4 actually tell you about the probability that a random dementia transcript has higher adverb proportion than a random control transcript?
- **Concept: Feature representation hierarchy (raw → POS-enhanced → POS-only)**
  - *Why needed here:* Systematically stripping away lexical, then content-word information reveals which linguistic levels carry the diagnostic signal, disentangling whether differences are due to vocabulary, grammar, or both.
  - *Quick check:* If POS-only performance matches POS-enhanced, what does that imply about the relative importance of semantics vs. syntax in early dementia detection?

## Architecture Onboarding
Data Ingestion -> Preprocessing & Feature Extraction -> Modeling -> Evaluation Protocol -> Interpretation -> Validation
- **Critical path:** Preprocessing → Feature extraction (especially correct POS tagging) → Subject-level CV → Feature importance aggregation → Statistical validation. Subject-level CV loop is most computationally heavy and must be finalized before claims about generalization.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Interpretability vs. Accuracy: Use LR/RF instead of transformers for coefficient-based explanations, accepting lower ceiling accuracy.
  - Lexical vs. Syntactic abstraction: POS-only removes content entirely, risking loss of semantic signal but gaining speaker invariance; POS-enhanced balances both.
  - Conservative evaluation: Subject-level CV is mandatory for realistic clinical claims, even if it lowers reported F1-scores.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Speaker leakage: Subject-level F1 drops drastically (<0.55) while transcript-level F1 is high (>0.80), indicating features capture speaker idiosyncrasies.
  - POS-only collapse: POS-only performance falls to random chance (~0.50), suggesting grammatical features alone are insufficient or POS tagging is error-prone on disfluent speech.
  - Stat-ML divergence: Top ML features show no significant Mann-Whitney differences (high p_adj), flagging that predictive power comes from multivariate interactions, not univariate markers.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Baseline transcript-level LR on raw text: Establish reference performance (expect ~0.68 macro F1) and top lexical features (TTR, sentence length).
  2. Subject-level CV on POS-only features: Test conservative generalization using only syntax. Compare macro F1 to raw text subject-level results.
  3. Convergence check: For POS-only, plot top 10 features from RF importance vs. their Cliff’s delta ranks. High correlation (Spearman >0.7) confirms robust markers; low correlation suggests model-specific artifacts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Longitudinal trajectories:** How do linguistically interpretable markers evolve over time within individuals, and can they predict longitudinal progression? (Inferred from Section C; unresolved due to lack of evenly sampled longitudinal data in corpus.)
- **Generalizability:** Do the identified markers generalize to other speech tasks, languages, and diverse demographic groups? (Explicitly noted in Section C; experiments limited to Cookie Theft task and DementiaBank Pitt.)
- **Multimodal integration:** Can integrating acoustic-prosodic features with linguistic features improve early detection while preserving interpretability? (Mentioned in Section C; acoustic information was excluded.)
- **Clinician perception:** How do clinicians perceive and utilize the provided linguistic explanations for screening decisions, and do they enhance trust? (Noted in Section C; no clinician feedback collected.)

## Limitations
- Key feature definitions are incompletely specified: "semantic coherence" computation is undefined; POS tagset and content/function word split criteria are not enumerated.
- Evaluation is limited to a single corpus (DementiaBank Pitt) and one task (Cookie Theft), raising questions about task- and corpus-generalizability.
- Cross-sectional design cannot confirm that linguistic changes track individual progression; no within-subject longitudinal analysis.

## Confidence
- **Syntactic abstraction robustness (Mechanism 1):** Medium – Subject-level F1s (0.71–0.75) support POS-only signal, but unknown feature definitions and potential tagging errors on disfluent speech limit certainty.
- **Function-word dysregulation (Mechanism 2):** Medium – Large, consistent Cliff’s δ values with adjusted p-values provide strong univariate evidence, but effect could reflect general speech reduction rather than specific grammatical impairment without longitudinal data.
- **Discourse degradation (Mechanism 3):** Medium – Moderate effect sizes for coherence/diversity hold under subject-level CV, but task-specific elicitation and undefined coherence metric reduce confidence in broader applicability.

## Next Checks
1. **Longitudinal within-subject analysis:** Track the same participants over time (if multiple sessions exist) to verify that identified features (e.g., pronoun increase, auxiliary decrease) change progressively with cognitive decline, not just between groups.
2. **Coherence metric robustness study:** Implement and compare alternative semantic coherence measures (e.g., entity-grid, utterance embedding similarity) to determine which definitions yield highest feature importance and effect sizes, revealing the paper’s likely operationalization.
3. **Cross-corpus generalization test:** Replicate the POS-only feature pipeline on an independent dementia speech corpus (e.g., ADReSS, CCC) using a similar narrative task; stable macro F1 >0.65 under subject-level CV would confirm generalizability beyond DementiaBank Pitt.
