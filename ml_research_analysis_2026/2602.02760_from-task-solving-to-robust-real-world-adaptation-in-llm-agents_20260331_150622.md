# From Task Solving to Robust Real-World Adaptation in LLM Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.02760  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02760  
- **Reference count**: 21  
- **Primary result**: LLM agent robustness degrades sharply under real-world stressors (partial observability, noise, non-stationarity), with unstable model rankings and evidence of implicit multi-objective inference despite simple goal instructions.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical gap between LLM agent performance in controlled benchmarks and real-world deployment robustness. It introduces a grid-based benchmark that systematically incorporates four real-world stressors: partial observability (latent rules/cells), dynamic environments (hazard spread, teleports), noisy signals (corrupted observations, action slip), and agent-state drift. Evaluating five state-of-the-art LLM agents across grid sizes reveals large performance drops under full stressors, unstable model rankings (weaker models can outperform stronger ones in specific regimes), and evidence that agents implicitly infer and trade off multiple objectives (completion, efficiency, penalty avoidance) without explicit instruction. Ablations show strongly non-monotonic, model-specific sensitivities to individual stressors, and feature attributions identify consistent failure drivers like goal distance and hazard spread. The work demonstrates that robust deployment requires adaptive strategy selection, not just nominal task-solving capability.

## Method Summary
The authors design a text-based gridworld environment where agents navigate to collect keys while encountering hidden rule tiles, spreading hazards, teleportation events, observation noise, and occasional action slips. The agent’s observation is a local window around its position, and the state includes energy, keys, score, and orientation. Five LLMs (GPT-5.2, GPT-5 mini, Gemini-3 Pro, Gemini-3 Flash, Qwen3-235B-A22B) interact via prompts that describe the current view and recent events, outputting actions like MOVE, SCAN, MEASURE, INTERACT. The evaluation varies grid sizes (6×6, 8×8, 10×10) and stressor intensities, using 50 random seeds per condition. Metrics include success rate (accuracy), score, and steps taken on successful episodes. Ablations isolate each stressor, and feature attribution correlates performance with normalized environment parameters.

## Key Results
- Performance degrades significantly with grid scale when all stressors are active, with success rates dropping sharply for larger grids.
- Model rankings are unstable across stressor regimes; a model’s relative success depends on whether its exploration and sensing strategy matches the specific combination and intensity of stressors.
- Agents implicitly balance competing pressures (minimizing steps, avoiding penalties, conserving energy) despite receiving only a simple goal instruction, indicating partial objective inference from environmental feedback.
- Single-stressor ablations yield non-monotonic sensitivity curves that vary by model, confirming that no single strategy dominates all uncertainty configurations.
- Feature attribution highlights hazard spread and goal distance as consistent failure drivers, underscoring the need for adaptive policies that account for dynamic risks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Agents operate under a suite of deployment stressors that force trade-offs between immediate costs and long-term robustness. First, they implicitly infer auxiliary objectives (efficiency, penalty avoidance) from the environment’s implicit cost structure, developing heuristics that balance step minimization against hazard exposure and energy preservation. This emerges because poor trade-offs lead to failure within episodes, allowing learning from repeated interaction. Second, no single exploration-sensing strategy is universally optimal; different LLMs adopt distinct approaches (e.g., aggressive early probing vs. conservative movement-based inference) that interact non-monotonically with stressors. For instance, frequent teleports can reset local context beneficially for some models while disrupting planned routes for others. Third, adaptive information gathering—front-loaded sensing via SCAN/MEASURE—reduces long-horizon uncertainty from latent structures and noise, but over-probing wastes resources while under-probing yields brittle beliefs. The optimal investment level depends on latent density, noise, and the evolving belief state, which agents approximate through pre-deployment heuristics.

## Foundational Learning
- **Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs)**  
  *Why needed here*: The grid game’s local view and latent tiles create partial observability; success requires belief tracking over hidden states and acting on expected information gain.  
  *Quick check*: How would you compute the expected value of a MEASURE action given a prior over latent cell types and a cost per probe?

- **Implicit Reward Shaping / Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL)**  
  *Why needed here*: Agents infer auxiliary rewards (efficiency, penalty avoidance) from environmental feedback without explicit programming, akin to cooperative IRL where the reward is hidden.  
  *Quick check*: What behavioral patterns would indicate an agent is optimizing an implicit penalty-avoidance objective versus purely maximizing completion?

- **Non-Stationary Reinforcement Learning**  
  *Why needed here*: The environment shifts (hazard spread, teleports) and the agent’s capabilities drift, requiring online adaptation and change detection—a classic non-stationary RL setting.  
  *Quick check*: What sensor or outcome patterns might trigger an agent to infer a “drift event” and replan?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Environment Simulator -> Observation Generator (local window, noise) -> LLM Agent (prompt-based policy with history) -> Action Selection -> Environment Step (stochastic transitions, stressors) -> Outcome Log -> (repeat).
- **Critical path**: The agent receives a text observation, performs an LLM forward pass (optionally with internal reasoning), selects an action token, the environment updates its state (including stressors), and the outcome is logged for the next step.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - *Action cost vs. information gain*: SCAN/MEASURE cost energy but reduce uncertainty; overuse risks depletion.  
  - *Exploration vs. exploitation*: Early probing builds robust beliefs but increases steps; late focus on goal risks unseen hazards.  
  - *Robustness to drift*: Policies that adapt quickly to shifts avoid collapse but may be less efficient in static phases.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - *Hypothesis fixation*: Agent commits to a wrong rule-tile outcome after one interaction and repeatedly triggers hazards.  
  - *Sensing neglect*: Low SCAN/MEASURE usage in high-latent or high-noise settings, leading to surprise failures.  
  - *Drift blindness*: No behavioral change after teleport or agent-drift events (e.g., continued expensive actions despite reduced energy).
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Baseline stress test**: Run all modifiers at default across grid sizes to reproduce the main performance gap and ranking instability.  
  2. **Single-stressor sweep**: Isolate each stressor (Noise, Latent, Hazard-Spread, Teleport) while holding others neutral; plot accuracy vs. stressor level to identify non-monotonic sensitivities per model.  
  3. **Action profile analysis**: Aggregate trajectories to compute non-movement action probabilities over time; check for “sense-then-act” pattern and quantify early vs. late probing to link strategy to performance trade-offs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How can LLM agents learn to trigger cost-effective verification actions (e.g., SCAN, MEASURE) based on uncertainty estimates to improve long-term robustness under partial observability?  
  *Basis*: The authors explicitly state the need for "uncertainty- and cost-aware verification policies that activate probing only when beneficial" (Conclusion).  
  *Why unresolved*: The paper identifies differences in information-gathering strategies but does not provide a method for teaching agents to optimally balance exploration costs against uncertainty reduction.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Developing and evaluating agents with built-in uncertainty estimation (e.g., via self-consistency, confidence scores) or auxiliary losses that penalize unnecessary probing, then measuring success and efficiency across the gridworld’s stressor conditions.

- **Open Question 2**: Can LLM agents detect sudden changes in the environment (e.g., hazard spread, teleports) or in their own capabilities (drift) and dynamically replan to recover performance?  
  *Basis*: The authors call for "online change detection with rapid replanning/reset under shifts and capability drift" (Conclusion).  
  *Why unresolved*: The paper shows performance degradation due to non-stationarity but does not investigate or propose mechanisms for change detection or adaptive replanning.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Incorporate explicit change detection modules (e.g., monitoring prediction errors) or train agents with frequent environment shifts, measuring detection accuracy and recovery speed in the gridworld.

- **Open Question 3**: How should LLM agents be trained and evaluated to properly balance multiple competing objectives (completion, efficiency, safety) rather than a single success signal?  
  *Basis*: The conclusion highlights the need for "multi-objective training and evaluation that explicitly balances completion, efficiency, and safety costs."  
  *Why unresolved*: Current evaluations focus on success rate, yet agents show implicit trade-offs; it remains unclear how to shape agent behavior to optimize robustness across multiple criteria without manual reward tuning.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Design training with vector-valued rewards or constrained objectives, and evaluation with composite metrics; compare performance on the gridworld across stressor regimes.

- **Open Question 4**: Under what conditions do LLM agents infer implicit objectives (e.g., step minimization, penalty avoidance) from environment feedback and structure, and how does this inference influence their robustness?  
  *Basis*: The abstract motivates "objective inference under partial observability, noise, and non-stationarity," and results show agents trade off completion, efficiency, and penalty avoidance without explicit instruction.  
  *Why unresolved*: The paper observes partial objective inference but does not explain the mechanisms or when it helps or harms adaptation.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Ablate the presence of score feedback or modify its correlation with implicit goals; analyze policy gradients or attention patterns to see if agents encode efficiency/risk; test whether prompting to consider efficiency changes inference.

## Limitations
- Exact numeric values for game mechanics (energy costs, hazard spread probabilities, rule tile effects) are unspecified, threatening faithful reproduction.
- The tile placement algorithm and wall density parameters are missing, introducing uncontrolled layout variance across runs.
- Model-specific inference settings (temperature, thinking budget, token limits) are not standardized, confounding strategy comparisons.

## Confidence
- **Implicit multi-objective inference**: Medium – based on indirect behavioral patterns (step counts, scores); break condition: explicit multi-objective rewards remove the effect.
- **Strategy-dependent ranking instability**: High – directly observed across grid sizes and stressor regimes; break condition: convergence to a universal optimal policy.
- **Adaptive sensing-timing trade-off**: Medium – supported by action profile correlations; break condition: negligible information costs or full observability.

## Next Checks
1. **Mechanics verification**: Simulate deterministic edge cases (e.g., rule tile triggers, hazard spread) to confirm environmental dynamics match paper descriptions.
2. **Map seed control**: Use fixed random seeds or the official generator to produce identical maps for all agents, eliminating layout confounds.
3. **Inference parameter sweep**: Ablate temperature and max tokens per model to ensure reported rankings and action profiles are robust to hyperparameter variations.
