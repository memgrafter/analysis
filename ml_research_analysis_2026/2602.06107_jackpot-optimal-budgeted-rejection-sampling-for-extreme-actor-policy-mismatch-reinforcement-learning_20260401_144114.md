# Jackpot: Optimal Budgeted Rejection Sampling for Extreme Actor-Policy Mismatch Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06107
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06107
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Primary result:** Jackpot stabilizes decoupled RL training under extreme actor-policy mismatch (e.g., Qwen3-1.7B rollouts for Qwen3-8B policy), achieving comparable performance to on-policy RL (82.65% vs. 79.50% MATH-500 Mean@4) while maintaining high acceptance rates (~90%).

## Executive Summary
Decoupling rollout generation from policy optimization in reinforcement learning for LLMs—using a separate, cheaper actor model to generate trajectories—introduces severe distribution mismatch between the rollout and target policy. This mismatch destabilizes standard off-policy corrections like Truncated Importance Sampling (TIS), causing training collapse. Jackpot addresses this with Optimal Budgeted Rejection Sampling (OBRS), which provably reduces the KL divergence between rollout and policy distributions by strategically rejecting and reweighting sampled tokens. Combined with a unified training objective that jointly updates both the policy and the rollout model (via PPO and distillation) and an efficient top-k/bias-corrected implementation, Jackpot enables stable, high-performance training even under extreme scale gaps.

## Method Summary
Jackpot's core is OBRS, which for each token `x` proposed by the rollout model `p_inf` computes an acceptance probability `a(x) = min(1, p_target(x) / (λ * p_inf(x)))`, where `p_target` is the current policy distribution and `λ` controls the acceptance budget. Accepted tokens form a new distribution `p'_inf` that is theoretically guaranteed to be closer to `p_target` than `p_inf`. The training objective `L_Jackpot` has three parts: (1) an OBRS-adjusted PPO loss for the policy `θ`, using importance weights derived from the post-rejection distribution; (2) a standard PPO loss for the rollout model `ω`; and (3) a forward KL distillation loss pulling `ω` toward the updated policy `p_θnew`. To avoid expensive full-vocabulary operations, the normalization constant `Z` is approximated using the union of top-k tokens from `p_inf` and `p_θnew`, with a batch-level bias-correction factor `κ` derived from the empirical acceptance rate.

## Key Results
- **Stability under extreme mismatch:** On Qwen3-8B policy trained with Qwen3-1.7B rollouts (64× batch staleness), TIS collapses while Jackpot learns stably over 300 steps.
- **On-policy parity:** Jackpot reaches 82.65% MATH-500 Mean@4, comparable to an on-policy baseline (79.50%) and significantly above the collapsed TIS run.
- **High acceptance:** Even with large initial KL divergence between actor and policy, OBRS maintains >90% acceptance rate, ensuring computational efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: OBRS Provably Aligns Distributions
- **Claim:** Optimal Budgeted Rejection Sampling (OBRS) reduces the KL divergence `D_KL(p_target || p'_inf)` between the target policy and the post-rejection rollout distribution, guaranteeing `p'_inf` is strictly closer to `p_target` than the original `p_inf`.
- **Mechanism:** For a token `x` from `p_inf`, accept it with probability `a(x) = min(1, p_target(x) / (λ * p_inf(x)))`. The resulting accepted distribution, reweighted by `p_inf(x)*a(x)/Z`, satisfies `D_KL(p_target || p'_inf) ≤ D_KL(p_target || p_inf)` for any `λ` where acceptance is non-trivial.
- **Core assumption:** The target distribution `p_target` is accessible (e.g., policy logits can be computed for the same batch), and a `λ` exists such that `λ < max_x p_target(x)/p_inf(x)`.
- **Evidence:** Theorem 3.3/3.4 (formal guarantee); synthetic experiments (Fig 2) show KL reduction and high acceptance across large KL ranges.

### Mechanism 2: Unified Stabilizing Objective
- **Claim:** Jointly optimizing the policy with OBRS-adjusted PPO, the rollout model with standard PPO, and the rollout model with on-policy distillation prevents the actor from drifting away, maintaining a usable proposal distribution.
- **Mechanism:** The loss `L_Jackpot(θ,ω) = L_PPO-OBRS(θ) + L_PPO(ω) + λ_distill * L_distill(ω)`. The distillation term `L_distill(ω) = KL(p_θnew || p_ω)` pulls the actor `ω` toward the latest policy `θ`, counteracting staleness.
- **Core assumption:** The actor `ω` can be updated on the same trajectories used for policy updates without generating new rollouts; the forward KL is an effective regularizer.
- **Evidence:** Ablation (Table 3) shows the full triplet is necessary for long-term stability; masking-only or missing distillation leads to eventual collapse.

### Mechanism 3: Efficient Implementation via Top-k and Debiasing
- **Claim:** Approximating the normalization constant `Z` via a union of top-k tokens and applying a batch-level bias correction makes OBRS computationally feasible for large-vocabulary LLMs without sacrificing alignment quality.
- **Mechanism:** `Z = Σ min(p_inf(x), p_target(x)/λ)` is estimated by summing over the union set `V_k` of top-20 tokens from `p_inf` and `p_target`. A calibration factor `κ = (empirical acceptance rate) / mean(Z_approx)` scales the estimate to be unbiased at batch level.
- **Core assumption:** LLM output probability mass is concentrated in a small top-k set; the empirical acceptance rate is a low-variance, unbiased estimator of the true `Z`.
- **Evidence:** Appendix C.3 shows `k=20` captures >99.98% of `Z` after warm-up; performance stable across `k∈{10,20,40}`.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Actor-Policy Distribution Mismatch**
- *Why needed:* The divergence between the rollout actor's distribution `p_inf` and the target policy `p_target` causes inaccurate advantage estimates and PPO instability when using decoupled rollouts.
- *Quick check:* If `p_inf` and `p_target` differ by KL = 0.5, what is a primary risk when using importance sampling without correction?

**Concept: Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence**
- *Why needed:* The key metric for quantifying actor-policy mismatch. OBRS's theoretical guarantee is a monotonic reduction in `D_KL(p_target || p'_inf)`.
- *Quick check:* Does `D_KL(p || q)=0` imply `p=q`? What about `D_KL(q || p)=0`?

**Concept: Importance Sampling (IS) and Truncated IS (TIS)**
- *Why needed:* Standard off-policy correction that reweights returns by `p_target/p_inf`. TIS clips these ratios to reduce variance but fails under extreme mismatch, motivating OBRS.
- *Quick check:* In the PPO off-policy objective `E[ (p_target/p_inf) * clip(r_θ) * A]`, what role does `p_target/p_inf` play, and what is its main failure mode?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Rollout Engine (generates trajectories with `p_inf`) -> OBRS Reweighting Module (computes acceptance mask and `ρ_jackpot` using `p_target`) -> Multi-Objective Trainer (computes `L_Jackpot` and updates `θ` and `ω`) -> Top-k & Bias Correction Module (approximates `Z` and computes `κ`).

**Critical path:** Rollout data collection → compute `p_target` logits for batch tokens → construct `V_k` (top-k union) → compute `Z_approx` and empirical acceptance rate → compute `κ` → calculate per-token `ρ_jackpot` → apply to PPO loss → backpropagate `L_Jackpot` to update both policy `θ` and actor `ω`.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Acceptance budget (`λ`) vs. alignment:** Lower `λ` increases acceptance rate but loosens alignment; higher `λ` improves alignment but risks low throughput.
- **Top-k size (`k`) vs. memory/compute:** Larger `k` improves `Z` estimate accuracy but increases memory for storing extra logits and compute for union/sum.
- **Distillation weight (`λ_distill`) vs. RL signal:** Balances keeping actor on-policy vs. allowing it to learn from its own PPO signal; poor choice causes collapse or stifled learning.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Pre-collapse:** Actor-policy KL rises monotonically in baseline; acceptance rate drops violently.
- **Silent degradation:** `Z_approx` consistently underestimates true `Z` by >5% (indicating `k` too small), causing systematically scaled gradients.
- **Actor `ω` divergence:** `L_distill` → 0 while `L_PPO(ω)` vanishes, indicating `ω` has stopped updating.
- **Memory overflow:** Storing `seq_len × batch × k` logits exhausts GPU memory.
- **Numerical mismatch:** `p_inf` log-probs from rollout engine differ from those recomputed at training time (e.g., due to FP8 KV cache).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **OBRS Simulation Validation:** Use synthetic Dirichlet distributions to confirm (a) acceptance rate >90% across wide KL range and (b) `D_KL(p_target || p'_inf) << D_KL(p_target || p_inf)`.
2. **Extreme Mismatch Stability Test:** Train Qwen3-8B with Qwen3-1.7B rollouts (batch size 64, 300 steps). Compare Jackpot vs. TIS baseline; expect TIS collapse and Jackpot stable learning matching on-policy performance.
3. **Component Ablation:** Test "Masking-only" (acceptance mask but no reweighting) vs. "Masking & Reweighting" vs. full Jackpot. Expect masking-only delays collapse but fails long-term; full Jackpot stable.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can OBRS fully eliminate distribution mismatch in decoupled RL training, or is there a fundamental limit? The paper shows improved stability but not indefinite convergence; theoretical lower bounds on achievable KL are lacking.
- **Open Question 2:** What precise threshold of actor-policy KL divergence distinguishes regimes where Jackpot substantially outperforms TIS? The paper identifies "large" vs. "small" shifts qualitatively but does not quantify the break-even point.
- **Open Question 3:** What is the optimal choice of `p_target` (reference policy `p_ref` vs. updated policy `p_θnew`) under different staleness conditions? The paper recommends `p_θnew` for large-batch/stale settings but provides no principled decision rule.
- **Open Question 4:** Do the top-k approximation and batch-level bias correction preserve the theoretical KL reduction guarantees of exact OBRS? The guarantees assume exact `Z`; the heuristic approximation's theoretical properties are unproven.

## Limitations
- **Replication-blocking unknowns:** Exact `p_target` selection logic for extreme staleness, top-k storage/union implementation details, timing of bias-correction factor `κ` computation, and key hyperparameters (`λ_distill`, PPO clip `ϵ`) are unspecified.
- **Robustness unvalidated:** Performance beyond Qwen models and math tasks (e.g., other architectures, broader domains) is not demonstrated.
- **Theoretical gaps:** Provable properties of top-k approximated OBRS (e.g., monotonic KL reduction) are not established; empirical validation suggests stability but lacks formal bounds.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (OBRS KL Reduction):** High. Formal theorem and synthetic validation strongly support the claim.
- **Mechanism 2 (Unified Objective Stability):** Medium. Ablations demonstrate benefit, but reliance on untuned hyperparameters (`λ_distill`) and weak corpus evidence reduce certainty.
- **Mechanism 3 (Efficient Implementation):** Medium. Empirical validation of top-k accuracy exists, but no theoretical bounds on approximation error; memory/compute tradeoffs not stress-tested.
