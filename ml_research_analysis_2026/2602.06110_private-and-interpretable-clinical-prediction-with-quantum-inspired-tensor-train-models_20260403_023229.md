# Private and interpretable clinical prediction with quantum-inspired tensor train models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.06110
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06110
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Tensor train models reduce membership inference attack success to near random while preserving accuracy and interpretability.

## Executive Summary
Clinical prediction models must balance accuracy, interpretability, and privacy, but conventional models like logistic regression and neural networks leak sensitive training data through membership inference attacks. This paper proposes a quantum‑inspired defense: discretizing model outputs and representing them in tensor train (TT) format. The TT representation obfuscates individual parameters while preserving the overall function. Evaluated on the LORIS immunotherapy predictor and shallow neural networks, the approach reduces white‑box attack accuracy to chance levels (~50% Hamming) and degrades black‑box attacks comparably to differential privacy (DP), yet with significantly better predictive accuracy, especially under tight privacy budgets. Moreover, the method retains logistic regression’s interpretability and extends it to neural networks via efficient computation of feature sensitivities and conditional distributions.

## Method Summary
The authors transform trained predictive models into a tensor train representation. First, model outputs are discretized (e.g., to binary or low‑bit values) to limit precision. Then the model parameters are flattened into a high‑dimensional tensor, which is decomposed into a chain of low‑rank core tensors using TT decomposition. This decomposition masks individual parameters while preserving the model’s input‑output behavior. The resulting TT model can be used for inference efficiently and enables interpretability analyses such as feature sensitivities and conditional distributions. The approach is applied to both logistic regression and shallow neural networks, showing privacy gains without sacrificing accuracy or interpretability.

## Key Results
- TT models reduce white‑box attack success to ~50% Hamming score (random guessing), while unprotected logistic regression suffers 82‑93% attack accuracy; TT‑LR with binary discretization achieves 66‑82% attack accuracy yet maintains balanced accuracy near the original.
- Black‑box membership inference attacks are degraded comparably to differential privacy, but TT models achieve better predictive accuracy for smaller privacy budgets.
- The TT representation retains the interpretability of logistic regression and extends it to neural networks through efficient computation of feature sensitivities and conditional distributions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The TT decomposition expresses a high‑dimensional parameter tensor as a product of low‑rank core tensors. This structure obscures the precise values of individual parameters, making it infeasible for an attacker to reconstruct training data from model weights. Discretization further limits the granularity of outputs or parameters, reducing the information leakage that membership inference attacks exploit. Because the decomposition preserves the overall function, predictive performance remains largely intact, and the cores can be analyzed to derive global feature importances and conditional effects, maintaining interpretability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Tensor Train (TT) Decomposition**: Represents high‑dimensional tensors as a chain of 3‑D core tensors with low rank. *Why needed*: Provides compact storage and natural obfuscation of individual entries. *Quick check*: How does TT reduce parameter count relative to a full tensor?
- **Membership Inference Attacks**: Detect whether a specific sample was used during training by querying the model. *Why needed*: Primary privacy threat measured. *Quick check*: What model outputs are typically leveraged in such attacks?
- **Differential Privacy (DP)**: Adds calibrated noise to achieve formal privacy guarantees (ε, δ). *Why needed*: Standard benchmark for privacy‑preserving ML. *Quick check*: How does DP‑SGD protect against membership inference?
- **Interpretability in Clinical ML**: Explaining predictions to clinicians via feature weights, sensitivities, or conditional distributions. *Why needed*: Essential for trust and adoption in healthcare. *Quick check*: Why is logistic regression considered interpretable?
- **Discretization**: Converting continuous values (e.g., probabilities) into a finite set of bins. *Why needed*: Reduces leakage of precise predictions that aid membership inference. *Quick check*: How might the choice of bin number affect privacy and accuracy trade‑offs?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Trained Base Model -> Discretizer -> Tensor Constructor -> TT Decomposer -> TT Inference Engine  
Critical path: A trained model is fed into the Discretizer, which outputs discrete values; these are used to construct a high‑dimensional tensor; the TT Decomposer factorizes it into cores; the TT Inference Engine performs forward passes for predictions.  
Design tradeoffs: Discretization resolution (higher retains accuracy but weakens privacy), TT rank (larger improves fidelity but increases compute/memory), decomposition quality (affects both accuracy and interpretability).  
Failure signatures: Membership attack accuracy remains high (>70%), balanced accuracy drops substantially, TT decomposition fails to converge or yields excessively high ranks, interpretability metrics (e.g., feature sensitivities) become unstable across runs.  
First three experiments:

## Limitations
- Generalizability: Evaluation is limited to the LORIS immunotherapy dataset and shallow models; performance on diverse clinical data (high‑dimensional EHRs, imaging) is unknown.
- Attack coverage: Only standard membership inference attacks were tested; robustness against reconstruction, property inference, or model‑extraction attacks remains unverified.
- Privacy guarantees: TT obfuscation lacks formal privacy proofs (e.g., ε‑δ); the claim of “random guessing” success may not hold against adaptive adversaries, and interpretability extensions lack clinical validation.

## Confidence
- Privacy effectiveness (white‑box): Medium  
- Accuracy vs DP trade‑off: Medium  
- Interpretability extension to NNs: Low  
- Clinical relevance: Low
