# ShrimpXNet: A Transfer Learning Framework for Shrimp Disease Classification with Augmented Regularization, Adversarial Training, and Explainable AI

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.00832
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00832
- **Reference count**: 19
- **Primary result**: Achieved 96.88% test accuracy on four-disease shrimp classification using ConvNeXt-Tiny with adversarial robustness.

## Executive Summary
ShrimpXNet addresses automated classification of four shrimp diseases using a transfer learning framework on 1,149 preprocessed images with background removal. Six pretrained CNNs were evaluated; ConvNeXt-Tiny achieved the highest test accuracy of 96.88%. The framework incorporates robustness via Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) adversarial training and augmented regularization with MixUp/CutMix. Explainability is provided through Grad-CAM variants. The model demonstrates strong performance with macro-averaged precision, recall, and F1-scores around 97%, and maintains 88.61% accuracy under adversarial perturbation (ε=0.14). The work highlights transfer learning's efficacy for small aquatic pathology datasets but notes limitations in dataset diversity and robustness evaluation.

## Method Summary
ShrimpXNet employs a two-stage transfer learning pipeline on a preprocessed dataset of 1,149 shrimp images (background removed via `rembg`, split 70/15/15 train/val/test). A pretrained CNN backbone (e.g., ConvNeXt-Tiny) is first frozen while a custom 4-layer CNN classifier (GlobalAvgPool → Dense → Dropout → Dense → Softmax) is trained on extracted features. The backbone is then partially unfrozen (via grid search) for joint fine-tuning. Regularization and robustness are explored via MixUp/CutMix data augmentation and FGSM adversarial training, though the best-performing model used no augmentation (α=0). Training uses Adam optimizer, sparse categorical crossentropy loss, StepLR scheduler, 30 epochs, batch size 128, and early stopping (patience=5). Evaluation includes accuracy, confusion matrix, per-class metrics, ROC-AUC, and Grad-CAM-based explainability.

## Key Results
- ConvNeXt-Tiny achieved 96.88% test accuracy (99% CI [0.953, 0.971]) with a confusion matrix error rate of 3.125% (5/160).
- Under adversarial perturbation (ε=0.14), accuracy remained 88.61%, demonstrating robustness.
- Macro-averaged precision, recall, and F1-scores were approximately 97%; ROC-AUC values ranged from 0.99 to 1.00.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Transfer Learning with Model-Specific Fine-Tuning
**Claim**: Fine-tuning a pretrained CNN backbone (ConvNeXt-Tiny) on the shrimp disease dataset yields high classification accuracy by leveraging generic visual features learned on large-scale datasets.
**Mechanism**: Pretrained early layers capture universal features (edges, textures); later layers are fine-tuned to specialize on shrimp pathology patterns (e.g., White Spot lesions). A lightweight custom CNN classifier maps extracted features to the four disease classes.
**Core assumption**: Visual patterns of shrimp diseases share sufficient low/mid-level feature similarity with natural images in the pretraining corpus.
**Evidence anchors**:
- [abstract]: "Six pretrained CNNs were evaluated; ConvNeXt-Tiny achieved the highest test accuracy of 96.88%."
- [section III.B]: Details the six pretrained models and custom classifier architecture.
**Break condition**: If shrimp disease imagery contains textures/structures absent from natural image distributions (e.g., ultrasound artifacts), feature transfer may degrade, requiring pretraining on aquatic/medical corpora.

### Mechanism 2: Adversarial Training via FGSM for Robustness
**Claim**: Incorporating FGSM-generated adversarial examples during training improves model resilience to small input perturbations (e.g., image noise, compression artifacts) while preserving accuracy on clean images.
**Mechanism**: FGSM adds a signed gradient perturbation (ε) to inputs, forcing the model to learn locally linear decision boundaries less sensitive to worst-case distortions, acting as regularization.
**Core assumption**: Real-world shrimp images may contain noise (camera sensor, lighting, water turbidity) modelable as small adversarial perturbations.
**Evidence anchors**:
- [section III.E]: Describes FGSM formula and use for robustness.
- [section IV.D, Table IV]: Shows accuracy at ε=0.14 remains 88.61%.
**Break condition**: If perturbation ε exceeds a model-specific threshold (ε>0.18 causes >30% accuracy drop), robustness gains vanish and clean accuracy may degrade.

### Mechanism 3: Augmented Regularization via MixUp/CutMix
**Claim**: MixUp and CutMix mitigate overfitting on the small dataset (1,149 images) by encouraging smoother decision boundaries and reducing reliance on spurious features.
**Mechanism**: MixUp blends two images linearly; CutMix pastes a patch from one onto another. Both force learning from ambiguous, interpolated samples, discouraging overconfidence on single-image features.
**Core assumption**: Disease-discriminative features are globally consistent (e.g., lesion patches appear in various contexts), so mixed samples retain label semantics.
**Evidence anchors**:
- [section III.D]: Defines MixUp/CutMix equations and implementation.
- [section IV.C, Table III]: Shows accuracy remains >88% across α variations, but α=0 (no augmentation) yields highest (96.88%).
**Break condition**: If α is too high (α>0.35), synthetic samples may distort label semantics, harming performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Transfer Learning**
  - *Why needed*: Small dataset (1,149 images) would overfit a deep CNN from scratch; pretrained weights provide a strong feature prior.
  - *Quick check*: Do the pretrained model's first-layer filters align with textures in shrimp images? (Visualize filters.)
- **Adversarial Robustness**
  - *Why needed*: Field-deployed shrimp imaging may have variable quality (blur, low light); adversarial training hardens against distortions.
  - *Quick check*: Does FGSM training improve accuracy on *naturally* noisy images (e.g., blurred test samples), not just synthetic perturbations?
- **MixUp/CutMix Regularization**
  - *Why needed*: Prevents memorizing background artifacts or camera-specific signatures instead of pathological features.
  - *Quick check*: After MixUp/CutMix, does Grad-CAM show attention on *both* source image regions (e.g., lesion from image A and healthy tissue from image B)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input images -> Pretrained CNN backbone (e.g., ConvNeXt-Tiny) -> Custom 4-layer CNN classifier (GlobalAvgPool -> Dense(ReLU) -> Dropout -> Dense(Softmax)) -> Output probabilities. Robustness modules: FGSM generator (on-the-fly during training) and MixUp/CutMix augmenters (optional). XAI: Grad-CAM/++/XGrad-CAM (post-hoc).
- **Critical path**: Data preprocessing -> Backbone feature extraction -> Head classification -> (optional) adversarial augmentation during training -> XAI visualization.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Accuracy vs. Speed*: ConvNeXt-Tiny (96.88% acc) vs. MobileNetV3 (55.21% acc). MobileNet's aggressive downsampling may lose fine disease textures.
  - *Robustness vs. Clean accuracy*: MixUp/CutMix and FGSM reduce clean accuracy slightly but improve worst-case robustness.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - *Overfitting*: Training accuracy >> validation accuracy without augmentation.
  - *Poor transfer*: Frozen backbone yields low accuracy (<80%) – indicates feature mismatch.
  - *Breakdown under augmentation*: Accuracy drops >5% with moderate α (0.2–0.3) or ε (>0.14), suggesting insufficient model capacity.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Baseline transfer: Freeze all backbone layers, train only custom head. Expected: ~70–80% accuracy (feature mismatch).
  2. Grid search unfreezing: Unfreeze last 10%, 30%, 50% of backbone layers. Expected: Best accuracy at ~30–50% unfrozen for ConvNeXt.
  3. Add robustness sequentially: Start with MixUp (α=0.2), then CutMix (α=0.3), then FGSM (ε=0.1). Expected: Clean accuracy drops slightly, adversarial accuracy improves.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1**: Can ShrimpXNet be extended to real-time video monitoring for continuous health assessment in aquaculture ponds?
  - *Basis*: Conclusion mentions future "real-time video monitoring."
  - *Why unresolved*: Current framework processes static images; video introduces temporal dynamics, motion artifacts, and computational demands not addressed.
  - *Needed evidence*: Develop a video pipeline (frame sampling + temporal modeling like 3D CNNs/LSTMs) and evaluate accuracy, latency, resource usage on farm video streams.
- **Question 2**: How robust is the background removal preprocessing step to imperfect segmentation across diverse shrimp species, water turbidity, and farmer-captured image qualities?
  - *Basis*: Background removal via `rembg` is critical but its sensitivity to segmentation errors is unexplored.
  - *Why unresolved*: Real-world images may have partial backgrounds, occlusions, or poor contrast; failure here could cascade into classification errors.
  - *Needed evidence*: Systematically corrupt preprocessed images with simulated segmentation errors (incomplete removal, background residues) and measure accuracy degradation; test on raw, unprocessed field images.
- **Question 3**: Does the model generalize to shrimp populations from other geographic regions or different shrimp species not in the ShrimpDiseaseBD dataset?
  - *Basis*: Study uses a single Bangladesh dataset; disease manifestations may vary with genetics, environment, farming practices.
  - *Why unresolved*: Performance claims are dataset-specific; unknown if domain shift (e.g., Thai/Indian strains) degrades accuracy.
  - *Needed evidence*: Test on independent datasets from other regions/species, report performance gaps, perform domain adaptation experiments (fine-tuning on small target datasets).
- **Question 4**: Would end-to-end fine-tuning of the entire pretrained CNN (instead of a separate frozen model with custom classifier) yield better performance or efficiency?
  - *Basis*: Methodology freezes pretrained layers initially and uses a fixed custom classifier; no ablation compares to full fine-tuning.
  - *Why unresolved*: Custom classifier might create a feature bottleneck; allowing all layers to adapt could improve feature extraction but risks overfitting.
  - *Needed evidence*: Controlled experiments comparing current approach versus fine-tuning all layers, measuring accuracy, training time, and generalization.
- **Question 5**: How does the model's explainability via Grad-CAM variants align with veterinary/biological ground truth regarding disease indicators (e.g., specific lesions, gill discoloration)?
  - *Basis*: Grad-CAM heatmaps are presented as interpretable, but no domain expert validation confirms alignment with pathological features.
  - *Why unresolved*: Visual plausibility ≠ clinical relevance; experts might prioritize different anatomical markers.
  - *Needed evidence*: User study with aquaculture pathologists rating heatmaps for relevance to disease symptoms; correlate activation regions with annotated medical features.

## Limitations
- Small single-source dataset (1,149 images) risks overfitting and limits generalizability.
- Background removal preprocessing may introduce artifacts inconsistent across real-world conditions.
- Critical FGSM training parameters (ε, sampling rate) are missing, hindering replication of robustness claims.
- Explainability via Grad-CAM is qualitative and unvalidated by domain experts.

## Confidence
- **Transfer learning accuracy (96.88%)**: Medium – strong within-dataset metrics but no external validation.
- **Adversarial robustness (88.61% at ε=0.14)**: Medium – clear synthetic results but training details omitted and natural noise untested.
- **Augmented regularization benefit**: Low – best model uses α=0; no demonstrated robustness improvement.
- **Grad-CAM explainability**: Low – qualitative only; no expert or quantitative validation.

## Next Checks
1. *External validation*: Test ShrimpXNet on an independent shrimp disease dataset from a different source to assess real-world performance.
2. *Natural corruption robustness*: Evaluate models (with/without FGSM training) on images degraded by blur, compression, and lighting changes to test transfer of adversarial robustness.
3. *Expert-driven XAI validation*: Have aquaculture pathologists rate Grad-CAM heatmaps for alignment with disease lesions; compute agreement metrics to quantify explanation reliability.
