# A Dual-Path Generative Framework for Zero-Day Fraud Detection in Banking Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.13237
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13237
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Achieves sub-50ms real-time zero-day fraud detection by combining a VAE-based anomaly detector with an asynchronous WGAN-GP synthesizer, selectively triggering SHAP explanations for high-uncertainty transactions, and adapting via human-in-the-loop feedback.

## Executive Summary
Detecting zero-day fraud in high-frequency banking systems demands reconciliation of extreme class imbalance (0.17% fraud), GDPR-mandated explainability, and sub-50ms inference latency. This paper presents a dual-path generative framework that decouples real-time anomaly detection from offline adversarial synthesis. A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) learns a manifold of legitimate transactions and flags transactions with high reconstruction error. An asynchronous Wasserstein GAN with Gradient Penalty (WGAN‑GP) generates diverse synthetic fraud samples to stress‑test the VAE boundaries and address class imbalance. Discrete features (e.g., Merchant Category Codes) are handled via a Gumbel‑Softmax estimator, enabling differentiable processing. SHAP explainability is selectively activated only for high‑uncertainty transactions (typically <1% of traffic), thereby decoupling computational XAI cost from baseline throughput. A human‑in‑the‑loop (HITL) mechanism incorporates analyst feedback to recalibrate the system and counter concept drift. The architecture maintains real‑time latency under 50 ms while providing adaptive, explainable detection for novel fraud patterns.

## Method Summary
The system processes streaming transaction data through a preprocessing and embedding layer that applies entity embeddings to high‑dimensional sparse categoricals. Its dual‑path core runs a synchronous anomaly detection path—a VAE trained solely on legitimate transactions to learn a probabilistic latent manifold (optimizing the ELBO with KL‑divergence annealing)—and an asynchronous generative augmentation path—a WGAN‑GP with gradient penalty that synthesizes high‑entropy fraud samples, using a Gumbel‑Softmax module to handle discrete outputs. At inference, reconstruction error `E(x)` from the VAE is compared against a dynamic threshold `τ`; only when `E(x) > τ` is SHAP triggered, and the transaction is routed for HITL review. Confirmed fraud labels feed an adversarial buffer that updates the WGAN‑GP during off‑peak hours; refined synthetic samples then fine‑tune the VAE manifold, enabling adaptation to emerging fraud tactics without impacting online latency.

## Key Results
- Real‑time inference latency consistently below 50 milliseconds.
- Explainability cost decoupled from throughput via trigger‑based SHAP activation (covers <1% of transactions).
- Adaptive model recalibration through human‑in‑the‑loop feedback effectively counters concept drift.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The VAE‑based detection path identifies zero‑day fraud by flagging transactions with high reconstruction error from a learned "legitimate manifold."
- **Mechanism:** The VAE is trained exclusively on legitimate transactions to minimize the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), creating a compressed probabilistic latent representation. At inference, a transaction's reconstruction error `E(x) = ||x - x̂||²` is computed; transactions reconstructing poorly indicate deviation from normal behavior.
- **Core assumption:** Legitimate transactions occupy a coherent, low‑dimensional manifold in feature space, while novel fraud lies outside it.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "employs a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to establish a legitimate transaction manifold based on reconstruction error"
  - [section] "The Anomaly Sensor Path (VAE) component functions as the primary sensor... By optimizing the ELBO... a high `E(x)` indicates a transaction that deviates from the learned normal manifold"
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence. Neighbor papers discuss VAE for anomaly detection but not this specific dual‑path deployment.
- **Break condition:** If the legitimate manifold becomes contaminated with undetected fraud during training (e.g., poor initial data quality), the VAE may learn a corrupted manifold, causing high false negatives as zero‑day fraud appears "normal."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The asynchronous WGAN‑GP path synthesizes diverse, high‑entropy fraudulent samples to expand the VAE's detection boundaries for unseen attack patterns.
- **Mechanism:** The WGAN‑GP generator, trained with a gradient penalty for stability, creates synthetic fraud samples. These are fed back to fine‑tune the VAE offline, effectively "stress‑testing" its decision boundary. The Gumbel‑Softmax estimator enables differentiable sampling of discrete features (e.g., MCC codes).
- **Core assumption:** Manifold extrapolation via generative synthesis is more effective than linear interpolation (e.g., SMOTE) for modeling complex, non‑linear fraud strategies.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "an asynchronous Wasserstein GAN with Gradient Penalty (WGAN‑GP) synthesizes high‑entropy fraudulent scenarios to stress‑test the detection boundaries... integrates a Gumbel‑Softmax estimator"
  - [section] "WGAN‑GP path operates asynchronously to synthesize high‑entropy fraudulent scenarios... fed back into the VAE to stress‑test the detection boundaries"
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers [4, 5, 6] discuss GANs for fraud augmentation but not in this coupled, asynchronous architecture.
- **Break condition:** Mode collapse in the WGAN‑GP, where the generator produces limited variations, would fail to expose the VAE to the full spectrum of potential zero‑day fraud vectors.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Selective activation of SHAP explainability only for high‑uncertainty transactions reconciles GDPR's "right to explanation" with sub‑50ms latency constraints.
- **Mechanism:** SHAP is computationally triggered only when a transaction's VAE reconstruction error `E(x)` exceeds a dynamic threshold `τ`. The system blocks low‑latency explanation costs to the <1% of flagged anomalies, routing them for human‑in‑the‑loop review.
- **Core assumption:** The vast majority of legitimate and clearly fraudulent transactions do not require post‑hoc feature attribution to satisfy regulatory or operational needs.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "introduce a trigger‑based explainability mechanism where SHAP is activated only for high‑uncertainty transactions, reconciling the computational cost of XAI with real‑time throughput requirements"
  - [section] "To maintain a latency of <50 ms, the SHAP‑based interpretability module is not activated for every transaction. It is selectively triggered only when `E(x) > τ`"
  - [corpus] Neighbor paper [3] discusses XAI in fraud detection but not a latency‑triggered mechanism.
- **Break condition:** Poor calibration of threshold `τ` could either bottleneck the system (if `τ` is too low, triggering SHAP too often) or violate compliance (if `τ` is too high, missing explanations for truly ambiguous, high‑risk fraud).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)**
  - **Why needed here:** Forms the core real‑time anomaly detector by learning a probabilistic latent space of normal transactions.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the purpose of the KL‑divergence term in the VAE's ELBO loss, and how does it prevent overfitting?
- **Concept: Wasserstein GAN with Gradient Penalty (WGAN‑GP)**
  - **Why needed here:** Provides a stable framework for generating diverse, high‑quality synthetic fraud samples to augment the minority class without mode collapse.
  - **Quick check question:** Why does the gradient penalty in WGAN‑GP help more than weight clipping in the original WGAN for training stability?
- **Concept: Gumbel‑Softmax Estimator**
  - **Why needed here:** Allows differentiable sampling from a discrete categorical distribution (e.g., Merchant Category Codes), enabling end‑to‑end gradient flow through the WGAN‑GP generator.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the temperature parameter `τ` in the Gumbel‑Softmax trade off between sample discreteness and gradient smoothness?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  1. **Preprocessing & Embedding Layer:** Handles high‑dimensional sparse categoricals (e.g., MCC) via entity embeddings.
  2. **Dual‑Path Core:** Synchronous VAE path (real‑time inference) and asynchronous WGAN‑GP path (offline synthesis).
  3. **Gumbel‑Softmax Module:** Integrated into WGAN‑GP generator for discrete output.
  4. **Trigger Logic:** Compares VAE reconstruction error `E(x)` against dynamic threshold `τ`.
  5. **Selective SHAP Layer:** Activated only on high‑uncertainty transactions.
  6. **Human‑in‑the‑Loop (HITL) Interface:** Analyst validation dashboard feeding labels back to the Adversarial Buffer.
  7. **Asynchronous Retraining Pipeline:** Uses confirmed fraud from HITL to update WGAN‑GP, which then generates new samples to fine‑tune the VAE during off‑peak hours.
- **Critical path:** Live transaction → Preprocessing → VAE inference → `E(x)` calculation → Threshold check (`E(x) > τ`?) → [NO: Approve] or [YES: Trigger SHAP → HITL Review → Block/Adaptive Refinement].
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Latency vs. Explainability:* Mitigated by conditional SHAP execution.
  - *Online vs. Offline Load:* WGAN‑GP training is fully asynchronous to avoid impacting inference.
  - *Generative Diversity vs. Stability:* WGAN‑GP (vs. vanilla GAN) chosen for training stability at the cost of slightly more complex hyperparameter tuning (λ for gradient penalty).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **VAE Manifold Collapse:** Sustained high false‑negative rate on confirmed zero‑day fraud; check reconstruction error distributions on recent confirmed fraud.
  - **WGAN‑GP Mode Collapse:** Low diversity in synthetic samples (measured via cluster entropy); generator produces repetitive patterns.
  - **SHAP Trigger Misconfiguration:** Either (a) SHAP queue backlog and rising latency, or (b) regulatory flags for missing explanations on blocked transactions.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **VAE Reconstruction Calibration:** On a held‑out set of recent *confirmed* fraud samples, measure the false‑negative rate (FNR) at different `τ` percentiles to set an initial operating threshold that captures >95% of known zero‑day patterns.
  2. **WGAN‑GP Synthetic Validation:** Use a domain expert to score a sample of generated transactions for plausibility and diversity. Also, compute the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) between synthetic fraud and a validation set of real fraud.
  3. **Triggered‑XAI Load Test:** Simulate peak transaction volume (e.g., 10,000 TPS) with a realistic fraud rate (0.17%). Measure the 99th percentile end‑to‑end latency, ensuring SHAP‑triggered cases complete within a separate SLAs without affecting the main approval path.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the dual‑path framework be effectively extended into a Federated Learning environment while preserving the sub‑50ms real‑time latency constraint and the privacy guarantees required for cross‑institutional collaboration?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The "Future Research Avenues" section explicitly proposes extending the architecture to a Federated Learning setting for multiple financial institutions to collaboratively train the WGAN‑GP without exposing raw data.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not address the significant computational and communication overhead introduced by federated averaging and model aggregation, which could violate the stringent <50ms inference latency requirement and may require novel privacy‑preserving techniques compatible with the VAE and WGAN‑GP components.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experimental results demonstrating the framework's end‑to‑end latency and detection performance in a simulated multi‑institution federated setup, comparing centralized vs. federated WGAN‑GP training and analyzing the trade‑off between privacy (e.g., differential privacy noise) and model utility.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What specific Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) agent architecture and state‑action‑reward design can optimally and autonomously adjust the anomaly threshold τ to dynamically balance detection recall against false positives under fluctuating real‑time transaction volumes and fraud attack velocities?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The "Future Research Avenues" section states an intent to investigate DRL agents for adaptive thresholding in response to network congestion and fraud velocities.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper uses a static or manually calibrated dynamic threshold τ for triggering SHAP but provides no mechanism for its autonomous optimization. The design space for the DRL state (e.g., current traffic load, recent reconstruction error distribution, system latency) and reward function (e.g., maximizing detection while penalizing false positives and latency violations) is entirely unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A simulation study or production trial comparing a DRL‑driven τ against the paper's baseline dynamic threshold, measuring key metrics (precision, recall, FPR, latency) across diverse synthetic attack scenarios and traffic patterns to validate improved adaptability and reduced manual tuning.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent does the learned "Legitimate Manifold" from the VAE component generalize across different banking institutions with disparate customer bases, product suites, and geographical regulatory environments, or is the model fundamentally domain‑specific?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The "Future Research Avenues" section lists evaluating the framework's resilience and the universality of the learned manifold across diverse banking datasets as a planned research direction.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper's evaluation, while indicating strong results, is implicitly conducted on a single institution's dataset. The underlying distributions of "normal" transaction behavior (e.g., spending habits, common MCCs, geographic patterns) vary significantly between banks, which could cause the VAE's reconstruction error threshold to become ineffective or require complete retraining.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A rigorous cross‑validation study where a VAE trained on Bank A's data is tested on Bank B's held‑out legitimate transactions (without further training), measuring the reconstruction error distribution shift and its impact on false positive rates. This would establish the transferability limits of the manifold.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Beyond simple reconstruction error, what composite uncertainty quantification metric (e.g., combining reconstruction probability, latent space density estimation, or predictive entropy) provides the most reliable and calibrated signal for triggering the expensive SHAP explainability module, thereby minimizing the risk of missing subtle zero‑day fraud while keeping SHAP invocation below 1%?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The framework triggers SHAP only for "high‑uncertainty" transactions based on a reconstruction error threshold τ. However, reconstruction error alone may be an insufficient or uncalibrated uncertainty measure for complex, high‑dimensional transaction data, potentially leading to either missed anomalies or unnecessary SHAP calls.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper asserts that the trigger mechanism reconciles latency and explainability but does not validate or compare the sufficiency of reconstruction error as the sole uncertainty metric. It assumes a direct correlation between high reconstruction error and high model uncertainty, which may not hold for all fraud patterns (e.g., adversarial attacks designed to have low reconstruction error).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study comparing different uncertainty metrics (reconstruction error, Monte Carlo dropout variance, Mahalanobis distance in latent space) as the trigger, evaluating their ability to rank fraudulent transactions higher than legitimate ones (AUROC of the uncertainty score itself) and their impact on the final fraud detection F1‑score and SHAP invocation rate.

## Limitations
- **Architectural details missing:** Neural network architectures, latent dimensions, and hyperparameters (e.g., learning rates, batch sizes, Gumbel‑Softmax temperature schedule) are not provided, hindering exact replication.
- **Implementation specifics lacking:** The "Domain Constant Validator" and "Differential Privacy Layer" components, including their parameters (e.g., ε), are undefined.
- **Weak external validation:** Evaluation relies on a single institution's data with no cross‑institution benchmarks, and citation metrics indicate limited independent replication.
