# Pervasive Annotation Errors Break Text-to-SQL Benchmarks and Leaderboards

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.08778
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08778
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: Annotation errors affect >52% of examples in BIRD Mini-Dev and >62% in Spider 2.0-Snow, causing significant ranking distortions in text-to-SQL leaderboards.

## Executive Summary
Text-to-SQL benchmarks are fundamental for evaluating natural language to SQL translation, yet their reliability is compromised by widespread annotation errors. This paper uncovers the severity of this issue and introduces SAR-Agent, an AI assistant that incrementally verifies annotations by probing the underlying database, and SAPAR, a human-in-the-loop correction pipeline. Audits of BIRD Mini-Dev and Spider 2.0-Snow reveal error rates of 52.8% and 62.8%, respectively. After manually correcting a 100-example subset of BIRD Dev and re-evaluating 16 agents, the authors observe large fluctuations in execution accuracy and ranking positions, with a weak Spearman correlation (ρ=0.32, p=0.23) between original and corrected leaderboards. SAR-Agent achieves high precision (83–89%) and identifies 41.6% more errors than a previous human audit, demonstrating both the scale of the problem and a viable solution for building more trustworthy benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The study develops SAR-Agent, an LLM-based verifier that, given an annotation (question, external knowledge, gold SQL) and a database schema, iteratively proposes targeted SQL queries to probe for inconsistencies between the annotation and the actual database content. After up to 30 query executions, SAR-Agent synthesizes a diagnostic report flagging potential errors. This agent is embedded in the SAPAR correction pipeline: (1) SAR-Agent generates reports for each example; (2) human SQL experts adjudicate the flagged cases, assigning correctness labels; (3) human annotators revise the annotations (question, knowledge, SQL, or schema) iteratively until accepted. To quantify leaderboard distortion, the authors manually corrected a random 100-example subset of BIRD Dev using SAPAR, then re-evaluated 16 open-source text-to-SQL agents on both the original and corrected subsets, comparing execution accuracy and rank correlations.

## Key Results
- Annotation error rates: 52.8% in BIRD Mini-Dev, 62.8% in Spider 2.0-Snow.
- Re-evaluation on corrected BIRD Dev subset: relative execution accuracy changes from -7% to +31%; rank shifts from -9 to +9 positions.
- Rankings based on original vs. corrected subsets show weak correlation (Spearman ρ=0.32, p=0.23).
- SAR-Agent precision: 83-89%; it identified 41.6% more errors than a prior human audit (Arcwise).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Incremental database probing**: SAR-Agent incrementally verifies annotations by formulating verification queries that expose contradictions between the gold SQL and the database content, executing them, retaining results, and synthesizing a report. This simulates careful exploratory data analysis and leverages the fact that annotation errors often manifest as data inconsistencies. *Break condition*: Errors that are purely logical without data contradiction may be missed; restricted database access can also hinder probing.

**Human-in-the-loop correction (SAPAR)**: The pipeline combines SAR-Agent’s scalable pre-screening with expert human judgment. SAR-Agent flags likely errors, experts adjudicate and provide correction guidance, and annotators revise accordingly. This reduces manual effort while maintaining accuracy. *Break condition*: If experts frequently disagree with SAR-Agent’s reasoning, efficiency drops; cost may outweigh benefits if too many iterations are needed.

**Benchmark re-evaluation**: By manually correcting a representative subset and re-running agents, the study isolates the impact of annotation errors on performance metrics. Significant changes in accuracy and ranking instability directly link distortions to erroneous annotations. *Break condition*: If the corrected subset is not representative or other factors (e.g., randomness) drive changes, the inference may not generalize.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Annotation + Schema → SAR-Agent (LLM + `execute_query`) → Diagnostic Report → Human SQL Expert → Error Label / Correction Guidance → Human Annotator → Revised Annotation → (loop back if not accepted).  
(Note: SAR-Agent internally iterates: formulate query → execute → update memory, up to 30 steps.)

**Critical path**:
1. SAR-Agent receives an annotation and its schema.
2. It iteratively proposes and executes verification queries (up to 30 times) to probe the annotation.
3. After termination, it outputs a diagnostic report.
4. A human expert reviews the report to confirm errors and guide correction.
5. A human annotator revises the annotation; if the revision passes SAR-Agent verification, the example is accepted; otherwise, it loops.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Automation vs. human effort*: SAR-Agent reduces manual review time but still requires expert verification; precision is high (83–89%) but not perfect.
- *Cost vs. thoroughness*: More verification steps improve detection but increase per-example cost ($0.44–$1.11) and time.
- *Schema complexity*: For Spider 2.0-Snow, the authors restricted to gold tables to manage complexity, possibly missing errors involving non-gold tables.
- *Correction scope*: Full correction (question, knowledge, SQL, schema, and sometimes database) is expensive; they sampled only 100 examples.

**Failure signatures**:
- Low precision: Many false positives overwhelm human reviewers.
- Low recall: Missing many errors (not directly measured but inferred from comparison with Arcwise).
- High variance: Small corrected subset leads to unstable ranking changes.
- Disagreement: If experts often override SAR-Agent, its utility diminishes.
- Cost overrun: Average steps exceed limit or per-example cost spikes.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Precision Check**: Run SAR-Agent on 20 randomly selected examples from BIRD Mini-Dev; have a human expert verify each diagnostic report; compute precision (expected ~83%).
2. **Hit Rate against Known Errors**: Use 161 errors identified by the Arcwise audit as ground truth; run SAR-Agent on that subset and compute hit rate (expected ~85.7%).
3. **Rank Correlation Replication**: Evaluate a small set of agents (top and bottom from BIRD leaderboard) on a corrected subset of 30 examples; compute Spearman correlation with original rankings (expect weak correlation ~0.32).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Error rates in other benchmarks**: What are the annotation error rates in benchmarks like Spider 1.0, full BIRD Dev/Test? The study only audited BIRD Mini-Dev and Spider 2.0-Snow and manually corrected a small BIRD Dev sample. Systematic audits across major benchmarks using SAR-Agent with expert verification are needed.
2. **Fully automated detection**: Can we eliminate the need for human expert verification while maintaining high precision? Manual verification is a scalability bottleneck. A fully automated method with comparable precision/recall would greatly improve efficiency.
3. **Hidden test sets**: How should the community handle annotation errors in hidden test sets that cannot be corrected post-release? Test errors continue to distort research. Solutions could include periodic re-evaluation by benchmark custodians or evaluation designs robust to annotation noise.

## Limitations
- Error audits restricted to BIRD Mini-Dev and Spider 2.0-Snow; manual correction applied to only 100 BIRD Dev examples, limiting generalizability.
- SAR-Agent may miss errors that lack data inconsistencies (e.g., purely logical bugs).
- Statistical evidence of ranking distortion is weak (Spearman ρ=0.32, p=0.23) due to the small corrected subset.

## Next Checks
1. Scale the SAPAR correction to a random subset of ≥500 BIRD Dev examples, then re-evaluate all 16 agents and compute Spearman correlations with 95% confidence intervals to improve statistical power.
2. Measure inter-rater reliability: have two independent SQL experts separately verify SAR‑Agent’s diagnostic reports on a shared set of 50–100 examples and compute Cohen’s κ.
3. Test ranking stability on corrected sets with a broader agent pool, including state-of-the-art closed-source models (e.g., GPT‑4, Claude 3), to determine if distortion persists beyond open‑source agents.
