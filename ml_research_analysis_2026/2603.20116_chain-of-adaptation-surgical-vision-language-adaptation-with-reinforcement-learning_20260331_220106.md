# Chain-of-Adaptation: Surgical Vision-Language Adaptation with Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.20116
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20116
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Chain-of-Adaptation (CoA) substantially outperforms supervised fine-tuning on surgical object recognition (e.g., F1-score 83.7 vs. 65.7 on EndoVis2018) and generalizes better to unseen data while preserving general multimodal performance.

## Executive Summary
Fine-tuning large vision-language models (VLMs) for surgical domains often suffers from catastrophic forgetting and poor generalization. The proposed Chain-of-Adaptation (CoA) framework addresses these issues by introducing a structured four-stage reasoning format—<general description>, <evidence>, <thought>, <answer>—and a two-stage training pipeline. First, a cold-start phase uses pseudo-labeled data from a teacher model (Gemini-Flash-2.5) to instill the reasoning format via supervised fine-tuning. Second, reinforcement learning with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) optimizes the model against verifiable task rewards. Evaluated on surgical object recognition benchmarks EndoVis2018 and CholecT50, CoA significantly surpasses supervised fine-tuning (e.g., F1-score 83.7 vs. 65.7 on EndoVis2018, per-class macro F1 58.0 vs. 43.2). The approach also generalizes to an unseen dataset (GraSP) and maintains performance on general multimodal benchmarks, demonstrating robust domain specialization.

## Method Summary
CoA consists of two main phases. In the cold-start phase, a powerful teacher model (Gemini-Flash-2.5) generates responses in the four-stage reasoning format for surgical images and queries. These pseudo-labeled pairs are used to supervisedly fine-tune a pretrained VLM (e.g., LLaVA) so it learns to produce the structured output. In the reinforcement learning phase, the VLM acts as a policy that generates multiple CoA responses for each training example. A verifiable reward (e.g., exact match or F1 between the predicted answer and ground truth) is computed based solely on the final answer component. GRPO then updates the VLM parameters to maximize expected reward, encouraging accurate answers while retaining the reasoning structure. This design avoids overwriting the VLM’s multimodal competence and mitigates catastrophic forgetting.

## Key Results
- On EndoVis2018, CoA achieves an F1-score of 83.7 compared to 65.7 for supervised fine-tuning, and improves per-class macro F1 from 43.2 to 58.0.
- CoA generalizes better to an unseen dataset (GraSP) and preserves performance on general multimodal benchmarks, indicating reduced overfitting.
- Substantial gains are also observed on the CholecT50 benchmark, demonstrating consistent improvement across surgical object recognition tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CoA combats catastrophic forgetting by preserving the VLM’s pretrained multimodal reasoning abilities through a structured output format that encourages step-by-step thinking. The cold-start phase provides a high-quality initialization into the desired format without direct task rewards, preventing early overfitting to limited annotations. Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards then fine-tunes the model for accuracy while the format constraint maintains interpretability and alignment. The four-stage decomposition separates general context, evidence grounding, reasoning, and final answer, which helps the model generalize by focusing on domain-relevant features and avoiding shortcut learning. Using GRPO, which optimizes a relative policy, stabilizes training and reduces the need for hand-crafted reward shaping.

## Foundational Learning
- **Catastrophic forgetting**: When fine-tuning large models, performance on previously learned tasks degrades. *Why needed*: To maintain general VLM capabilities while adapting to surgery. *Quick check*: Evaluate on a general VQA benchmark after adaptation.
- **Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR)**: Optimizes a policy using automatically computable rewards. *Why needed*: Scalable domain adaptation without extensive human annotation. *Quick check*: Compare RLVR performance against supervised fine-tuning with same data budget.
- **Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning**: Step-by-step textual reasoning preceding the final answer. *Why needed*: Improves interpretability and accuracy on complex tasks. *Quick check*: Ablate reasoning steps and measure drop in accuracy.
- **Pseudo-labeling with teacher models**: Using a stronger model to generate training labels. *Why needed*: To create structured reasoning data when human annotations lack that format. *Quick check*: Compute agreement between pseudo-labels and human expert labels on a subset.
- **Domain adaptation for vision-language models**: Transferring general pretrained VLMs to specialized domains. *Why needed*: To leverage massive pretraining while excelling in niche areas like surgery. *Quick check*: Zero-shot performance on target domain before adaptation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Teacher (Gemini) -> Pseudo-labels -> Cold-start SFT -> Initial VLM -> RL loop: VLM -> CoA Response -> Verifiable Reward -> GRPO -> Updated VLM

**Critical path**: 
1. Collect surgical image-query pairs.
2. Use teacher to generate CoA pseudo-labels (cold-start).
3. Supervised fine-tune VLM on pseudo-labels (cold-start SFT).
4. For RL: For each batch, sample multiple responses from VLM.
5. Compute reward based on answer correctness (verifiable).
6. Apply GRPO update to VLM parameters.
7. Repeat steps 4-6 until convergence.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Cold-start pseudo-label quality vs. data quantity: Noisy teacher labels may misalign VLM, but more data helps format acquisition.
- Structured reasoning adds computational overhead but improves interpretability and generalization.
- Verifiable rewards require ground-truth answers; applicable only to tasks with automatic evaluation metrics.

**Failure signatures**:
- Degenerate reasoning: VLM ignores reasoning stages and outputs only final answer.
- Reward hacking: VLM finds loopholes in reward function (e.g., copying answer without reasoning) without improving true performance.
- Training instability: GRPO may diverge if batch size or KL penalty misconfigured.
- Poor generalization: Cold-start overfits to teacher’s style; RL fails to correct.

**First three experiments**:
1. **Replication**: Reproduce EndoVis2018 and CholecT50 results with multiple random seeds; report mean ± std and disclose train/test splits.
2. **Cold-start ablation**: Train RL from scratch without cold-start; compare performance and convergence to full CoA.
3. **Format ablation**: Remove <general description> stage and retrain; measure impact on in-distribution accuracy and out-of-distribution (GraSP) generalization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: To what extent does Chain-of-Adaptation generalize to other medical imaging domains (e.g., radiology, pathology) and complex surgical tasks beyond object recognition, such as phase classification or workflow analysis?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors explicitly pose: "Can we adapt pretrained VLMs to express visual content in clinically accurate ways while leveraging their existing multimodal competence, rather than overwriting it?" Their evaluation is limited to two surgical object recognition benchmarks.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper demonstrates success only on instrument recognition; it remains unknown whether CoA's benefits hold for tasks with different linguistic and reasoning demands or in entirely new medical specialties.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic replication of the CoA framework on a diverse set of surgical tasks (e.g., surgical phase recognition, action triplet detection) and other medical domains (e.g., radiology VQA, pathology report generation), measuring both domain-specific performance and retention of general multimodal abilities.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What is the individual necessity of each stage in the CoA reasoning format (<general description>, <evidence>, <thought>, <answer>) for achieving improved generalization and reduced catastrophic forgetting?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The design rationale assigns specific roles to each stage, and ablations show CoA outperforms vanilla CoT. However, the paper does not isolate the contribution of individual stages.
- **Why unresolved**: It is unclear whether all four stages are essential or if a simplified format (e.g., omitting <general description>) could yield similar benefits, which would impact the framework's efficiency and applicability.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Controlled experiments that sequentially remove or modify each reasoning stage, evaluating impacts on in-distribution accuracy, out-of-distribution generalization, and metrics of forgetting (e.g., performance on general VQA benchmarks, output diversity).

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How sensitive is the cold start phase to the quality and bias of pseudo-labels generated by the teacher model (Gemini-Flash-2.5), and can CoA be effectively implemented with weaker or alternative pseudo-labeling sources?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The cold start relies on Gemini-generated pseudo-labels with the assumption that "correctness is secondary," but no analysis of label quality or teacher dependency is provided.
- **Why unresolved**: Noisy or biased pseudo-labels could misalign the model before RL optimization, potentially harming final performance. The method's robustness to teacher model errors is unknown, especially in resource-constrained settings where powerful teachers may be unavailable.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Quantify agreement between pseudo-labels and ground-truth annotations on a held-out subset; conduct experiments using teachers of varying strengths (e.g., smaller open-source models) and measure resulting CoA performance; explore alternative pseudo-labeling strategies (e.g., self-supervision).

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: What is the sample efficiency and data scaling behavior of RLVR compared to supervised fine-tuning for surgical VLM adaptation?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The results show RLVR outperforms SFT with a fixed small training set (~2K annotated frames), but the paper does not investigate how performance changes with varying amounts of annotated data.
- **Why unresolved**: In practice, the cost of annotating surgical data varies widely; it is unclear whether RLVR's advantage persists with larger datasets, whether it achieves higher sample efficiency, or if there are diminishing returns.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Train both SFT and RLVR with systematically varied amounts of annotated surgical data (e.g., 500, 1k, 5k, 10k samples), plot learning curves comparing final performance and convergence speed, and analyze the point where additional data yields minimal gains for each method.

## Limitations
- **Incomplete methodological details**: The summary lacks specifics on pseudo-label quality assessment, GRPO hyperparameters, and exact reward design, hindering reproducibility.
- **Potential benchmark overfitting and hidden costs**: Evaluation restricted to two surgical datasets raises risk of overfitting; computational overhead of RL and reasoning format not reported.
- **Limited community validation**: The paper has zero citations and modest author metrics, suggesting minimal independent scrutiny so far.

## Confidence
- Generalization improvement via CoA structure: **Medium**
- Quantitative gains on EndoVis2018/CholecT50: **Medium**
