# Does Your Reasoning Model Implicitly Know When to Stop Thinking?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08354
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08354
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SAGE-RL improves token efficiency by 70–97% while increasing accuracy on math benchmarks by extracting concise, effective reasoning chains from models that otherwise overthink.

## Executive Summary
Large reasoning models (LRMs) often generate long, redundant chains of thought (CoTs) that harm efficiency without improving accuracy. This paper reveals that LRMs implicitly possess knowledge about when to stop reasoning, but standard sampling methods obscure this signal. The authors propose SAGE (Self-Confidence Aware Generation) sampling, which uses the model's own token-level confidence to identify concise, high-quality reasoning traces. Integrating SAGE into reinforcement learning (SAGE-RL) further teaches models precise thinking patterns. Evaluated on six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, SAGE-RL consistently boosts both accuracy and token efficiency, demonstrating that models can learn to reason more effectively when guided by their implicit stopping knowledge.

## Method Summary
SAGE sampling operates by first generating multiple candidate reasoning chains via standard decoding. Each token's likelihood is used to compute a confidence score, and chains are ranked by a combination of confidence and length. The highest-ranked, most confident chains—which tend to be shorter and more direct—are selected as positive examples. In SAGE-RL, these selected chains serve as superior demonstrations for reinforcement learning, encouraging the model to produce similarly concise and accurate reasoning patterns. This contrasts with typical best-of-N or majority voting approaches that favor longer, redundant traces.

## Key Results
- LRMs systematically overthink, producing verbose CoTs that reduce token efficiency without commensurate accuracy gains.
- SAGE sampling extracts concise, correct reasoning chains hidden within the model’s own outputs, leveraging implicit stopping knowledge.
- SAGE-RL improves both accuracy and token efficiency across six math benchmarks; e.g., on DS-1.5B, token usage drops 70–97% while pass@1 increases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
LRMs generate reasoning traces where correct solutions are often associated with higher token-level confidence and shorter lengths, while incorrect or meandering thoughts exhibit lower confidence and redundancy. Standard sampling (e.g., greedy, beam search, or temperature sampling) typically selects longer chains because they dominate the probability mass, masking the model’s internal signal for when to stop. SAGE explicitly surfaces this signal by scoring chains on confidence, thereby identifying the model’s own “good” reasoning patterns. When these patterns are used for RL fine-tuning, the model learns to reproduce concise, high-confidence reasoning, leading to efficiency gains without sacrificing accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Confidence Scorer -> SAGE Sampler -> RL Trainer -> Fine-tuned Model

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate multiple candidate CoTs from base model.  
2. Compute confidence score for each token (e.g., negative log-likelihood).  
3. Aggregate token confidences to score full chains (e.g., average confidence, penalizing length).  
4. Select top-k highest-scoring chains as positive examples.  
5. Run RL (e.g., GRPO) with rewards based on correctness and SAGE-derived preferences.  
6. Update model policy to favor concise, confident reasoning.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Confidence metric choice (e.g., entropy vs. likelihood) affects sensitivity to overconfidence.  
- Length penalty strength balances conciseness against potential premature stopping.  
- RL reward design must avoid overfitting to SAGE’s selection bias.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Model becomes underconfident, producing overly terse or incomplete reasoning.  
- SAGE selects chains that are short but incorrect due to mis-calibrated confidence.  
- RL diverges if reward signal (correctness + conciseness) is mis-weighted.

**First three experiments:**  
1. Sweep SAGE’s confidence threshold and length penalty on a validation set to locate optimal operating point.  
2. Apply SAGE-RL to a model with known overthinking issues (e.g., Llama-3-8B-Instruct) and measure changes in chain length distribution.  
3. Ablate the confidence component: run SAGE-RL with random chain selection to isolate the contribution of confidence-based filtering.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to six mathematical benchmarks; domain generalizability is unknown.
- Reliance on model self-confidence assumes adequate calibration, which may not hold across scales or tasks.
- Baselines may not include latest adaptive reasoning methods (e.g., dynamic halting tokens), potentially overstating gains.
- No statistical significance testing or confidence intervals reported, making effect reliability uncertain.

## Confidence
- *LRMs overthink and possess implicit stopping knowledge*: **Medium** – inferred from analysis but direct psychometric evidence sparse.
- *SAGE sampling extracts concise, effective reasoning chains*: **Medium** – benchmark-supported but risk of selection bias.
- *SAGE-RL improves both accuracy and token efficiency*: **Medium** – empirical gains shown, yet independent replication lacking.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain test**: Apply SAGE-RL to at least three non-math reasoning tasks (commonsense QA, code generation, logical deduction) to gauge generality.
2. **Confidence calibration study**: Quantify correlation between model’s self-confidence scores and actual correctness across diverse samples and model scales.
3. **Ablation on sampling hyperparameters**: Sweep confidence thresholds and maximum token budgets to measure robustness and trade-off surfaces.
