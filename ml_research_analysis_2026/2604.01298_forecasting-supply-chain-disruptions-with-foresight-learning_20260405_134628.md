# Forecasting Supply Chain Disruptions with Foresight Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.01298
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01298
- Reference count: 3
- Primary result: End-to-end Foresight Learning framework achieves Brier score 0.0791, beating GPT-5 (0.1203) on rare supply chain disruption forecasting from news.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles forecasting rare supply chain disruptions from unstructured news. The authors introduce Foresight Learning, an end-to-end framework that trains LLMs on temporally aligned news-outcome pairs, optimizing for calibrated probabilistic forecasts via a log-score reward. Their fine-tuned model substantially outperforms strong baselines, including GPT-5, achieving a Brier score of 0.0791 and Expected Calibration Error of 0.0525. Training also induces emergent structured reasoning behaviors (e.g., base-rate anchoring, statistical modeling), increasing their frequency dramatically. The work demonstrates that task-specific reinforcement learning can transform general-purpose LLMs into reliable forecasters for high-stakes, low-frequency events.

## Method Summary
The framework constructs forecasting examples where the input is an entity-specific prompt containing the current disruption index, recent news context (strictly prior to the prediction date), and a clear event definition, while the label is a binary outcome indicating whether a one-month-ahead disruption exceeds a threshold. The base LLM (GPT-OSS-120B) is kept frozen, and a LoRA module (rank 32) is trained using a reinforcement learning objective akin to Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). At each step, the model outputs a probability, which is rewarded with the log score—the log likelihood of the true binary outcome. This proper scoring rule directly supervises calibrated probability estimation. The training pipeline enforces strict temporal separation to avoid look-ahead bias. Evaluation uses standard calibration metrics (Brier, ECE) and an automated LLM judge to assess reasoning patterns.

## Key Results
- **Calibration:** Brier score 0.0791 (vs. GPT-5 0.1203; untrained base 0.1433); Expected Calibration Error 0.0525 (vs. GPT-5 0.1304).
- **Precision:** Precision@10% of 0.3478 (vs. GPT-5 0.0870), indicating high confidence forecasts are well-calibrated.
- **Emergent reasoning:** Base-rate anchoring rises from 9% to 50%; statistical modeling from 48% to 100% after training (evaluated via LLM judge).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** End-to-end training on temporally aligned news-outcome pairs teaches the model to extract predictive signals from unstructured text for forward-looking probability estimation.
- **Mechanism:** The framework constructs forecasting examples with inputs (news up to time *t*, current index) strictly separated from labels (disruption at *t+1*). The model is optimized via reinforcement learning using the log score reward, which directly supervises probability outputs against realized binary outcomes. This forces the model to learn mappings from textual patterns to calibrated likelihoods rather than to descriptive summaries.
- **Core assumption:** Relevant supply chain precursors appear in news streams with sufficient frequency and lead time to be predictive.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "trains LLMs to produce calibrated probabilistic forecasts using realized disruption outcomes as supervision." Sections 3.1–3.5 detail the forward-looking construction. Corpus evidence weak.
- **Break condition:** If news contains no persistent actionable signal preceding disruptions, the mapping would be spurious.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Optimizing for the log score reward induces emergent, structured probabilistic reasoning patterns (e.g., base-rate anchoring, iterative refinement) without explicit prompting.
- **Mechanism:** The log score is a strictly proper scoring rule that incentivizes honest probability reporting. Through repeated exposure to temporally aligned examples, the model internalizes strategies: anchoring to unconditional event rate, adjusting conditionally, using simple stochastic models, and refining iteratively. These emerge as effective heuristics to minimize expected loss.
- **Core assumption:** The model’s pretrained knowledge contains latent reasoning patterns that can be activated and shaped by the reward signal.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "training induces more structured and reliable probabilistic reasoning without explicit prompting." Section 6.2 Table 4 shows increases in base-rate anchoring (9%→50%), statistical modeling (48%→100%), and uncertainty refinement (33%→100%).
- **Break condition:** If the model lacks capacity to discover in-context strategies or reward is too noisy, structured reasoning may not emerge.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Task-specific adaptation via parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) allows a general-purpose LLM to overcome its default descriptive mode and specialize for calibrated probabilistic prediction.
- **Mechanism:** The base model (GPT-OSS-120B) is frozen, and a low-rank adaptation matrix is trained with the RL objective. This retains broad linguistic knowledge while learning to map the specific prompt structure, news context format, and outcome supervision into a calibrated probability output. LoRA+GRPO adapts decision boundaries without catastrophic forgetting.
- **Core assumption:** The base model possesses extractable textual understanding and basic numerical reasoning that can be repurposed for forecasting.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states fine-tuned model "substantially outperforms strong baselines—including GPT-5." Section 5.3 Table 2 shows fine-tuned (Brier 0.0791) outperforms untrained base (0.1433) and GPT-5 (0.1203). Section 4.2 specifies LoRA rank 32 and frozen base.
- **Break condition:** If the base model’s pretraining distribution is too far from the forecasting task, fine-tuning may not yield meaningful specialization.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Temporal Information Constraints
- **Why needed here:** To prevent look-ahead bias; the model must only use information available at prediction time *t*. The dataset construction strictly segregates news (≤*t*) and outcome (*t+1*).
- **Quick check:** Can you construct a single forecasting example from the dataset that would be invalid due to leakage? (Answer: No—labels are computed from future index values not knowable at *t*.)

### Concept: Proper Scoring Rules for Calibration
- **Why needed here:** The log score reward is a strictly proper scoring rule that uniquely incentivizes truthful, well-calibrated probability reporting. Maximizing expected log score minimizes KL divergence between predicted and true distributions.
- **Quick check:** Does maximizing log score guarantee perfect calibration? (Answer: No—it incentivizes truthful reporting given the model’s information, but calibration depends on learning the true data-generating process.)

### Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)
- **Why needed here:** To adapt a 120B-parameter model with limited task-specific data without full-parameter overfitting or catastrophic forgetting. LoRA updates only low-rank matrices, preserving broad capabilities while specializing.
- **Quick check:** If LoRA rank is too low (e.g., 1), what is the most likely failure mode? (Answer: Insufficient capacity to learn the forecasting mapping, leading to minimal improvement over the base model.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data pipeline (timestamped news + disruption index) -> Forecasting question generator -> Prompt template -> Base model (GPT-OSS-120B, frozen) + LoRA modules -> Training loop (GRPO with log-score reward) -> Trained model -> Evaluation (Brier, ECE, Precision@10%, LLM judge)

**Critical path:**  
1. Verify temporal integrity of dataset (no news after *t*, label from *t+1*).  
2. Ensure prompt template cleanly separates context, question, and output format.  
3. Initialize LoRA; confirm base model outputs probabilities in `<answer>p</answer>` format.  
4. Implement log score reward and policy gradient; monitor for reward hacking (e.g., always predicting base rate).  
5. Evaluate calibration reliability diagram post-training.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Context window vs. comprehensiveness: Sliding-window truncation may omit distant relevant news; they chose concise, relevance-filtered news.  
- LoRA rank vs. adaptation: Higher rank improves fit but risks overfitting; rank 32 chosen empirically.  
- Binary event vs. continuous outcome: Binary shocks simplify but discard severity; continuous prediction is future work.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Calibration collapse: ECE remains high (>0.15) → reward signal weak or capacity insufficient.  
- Reasoning degradation: Automated rubric scores decrease → fine-tuning overwrote useful reasoning.  
- Precision@10% near random: Model fails to concentrate high-confidence forecasts on true events → poor discrimination.  
- Leakage in eval: Test performance exceeds train → invalid temporal split.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Sanity check: Train for 1 gradient step; ensure loss decreases and outputs differ from base rate.  
2. Baseline replication: Reproduce historical baseline (constant rate) and untrained model prompts; confirm they underperform trained model on Brier.  
3. Ablation - LoRA rank: Train with ranks {8, 32, 64}; measure ECE and reasoning rubric to validate chosen rank.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Improving noisy news-outcome mapping:** How to increase theoretical upper bound on predictive performance given incomplete reporting and non-deterministic translation to disruptions? Evidence needed: curated dataset of missed signals, ablation studies, label threshold sensitivity.
2. **Multi-horizon and continuous forecasts:** Can Foresight Learning extend to longer horizons or continuous disruption magnitudes? Evidence needed: adaptation for 3/6-month horizons and continuous index changes, evaluated with proper multi-horizon scoring rules.
3. **Robustness to regime shifts:** How do forecasts and reasoning behaviors hold up under structural breaks (e.g., pre-COVID vs. post-2022)? Evidence needed: stress-test on pre-2022 data or simulated regime changes; identify brittle reasoning patterns.

## Limitations
- **Unreleased base model:** `GPT-OSS-120B` is not publicly identified or available, preventing direct replication.
- **Opaque data pipeline:** News retrieval, filtering, and summarization methods are unspecified, creating potential leakage and bias risks.
- **Incomplete training specification:** GRPO implementation details and hyperparameters are omitted, hindering faithful reproduction.
- **Proprietary reasoning evaluator:** The LLM judge setup and prompts are not released, making emergent reasoning claims hard to verify.

## Confidence
- End-to-end training yields calibrated forecasts: **Medium**
- Log-score reward induces structured reasoning: **Low-Medium**
- LoRA adaptation specializes a general LLM: **Medium**
