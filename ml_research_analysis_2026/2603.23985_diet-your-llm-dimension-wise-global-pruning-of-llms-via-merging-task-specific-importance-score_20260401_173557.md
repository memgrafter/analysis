# Diet Your LLM: Dimension-wise Global Pruning of LLMs via Merging Task-specific Importance Score

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23985
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23985
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** DIET achieves state-of-the-art structured pruning accuracy (e.g., 45.0% avg at 20% sparsity on Gemma-2 2B), a >10-point absolute gain over prior methods, with minimal hardware-aware post-processing.

## Executive Summary
DIET introduces a training-free, dimension-wise structured pruning framework for large language models. It estimates the importance of each hidden dimension by profiling activation magnitudes on a small set of calibration samples per task, then aggregates these task-specific "votes" via majority voting to construct a single global pruning mask. This mask is applied uniformly across all residual-connected linear layers, enabling hardware-friendly sparsity without per-task routers or fine-tuning. Applied to models like Gemma-2 2B and 9B on seven zero-shot benchmarks, DIET substantially outperforms prior structured pruning methods at equal sparsity, especially at aggressive sparsity levels (10%-30%). Simple variance rescaling after hard pruning further yields real reductions in FLOPs, memory, and latency while preserving accuracy.

## Method Summary
DIET operates in three phases: profiling, voting, and application. First, for each target task, the method profiles the absolute magnitude of MLP outputs (post-MLP, pre-layernorm) across all transformer blocks using only 100 calibration samples. It averages these magnitudes across tokens, samples, and blocks to compute a stable per-dimension importance score for that task. Second, for each task, the dimensions with the lowest scores are marked for pruning, forming a binary vote vector. All task vote vectors are stacked; a global prune set is selected by taking the dimensions with the highest vote counts (majority voting). Finally, the global binary mask is applied to the model: for layers where the masked dimension is an input (e.g., embeddings, Q/K/V projections), corresponding columns are zeroed; for output dimensions (e.g., attention out, MLP out), rows are zeroed. Optionally, the model can be *hard-pruned* (weights physically sliced) and the weights of layers with reduced *input* dimension are rescaled by α = sqrt(d/d') to correct for variance drops, enabling actual efficiency gains.

## Key Results
- At **20% sparsity** on Gemma-2 2B, DIET attains **45.0%** average zero-shot accuracy across seven benchmarks, a **10-point absolute improvement** over SliceGPT (35.0%) and PuDDing (35.8%).
- At **10% sparsity**, DIET reaches **56.3%** average accuracy, improving by over **21 points** versus prior structured methods.
- Similar strong gains hold on larger models (Gemma-2 9B) and other architectures (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B: 60.9% vs. 38.6%).
- Hard pruning with variance rescaling yields consistent reductions in FLOPs, memory footprint, and inference latency while maintaining accuracy close to the zero-mask evaluation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Activation Magnitude as a Training-Free Importance Proxy
- **Claim:** Task-specific dimension importance can be effectively estimated from short activation profiles.
- **Mechanism:** DIET hooks the MLP layer output (before post-MLP layernorm and residual add) in every block. For a task's calibration samples, it computes the mean absolute activation per hidden dimension, first averaging across blocks (cross-layer consistency), then across tokens and samples (stable score). Low average activation indicates a dimension that contributes little to the forward pass for that task's data distribution.
- **Core assumption:** Activation magnitude is a reliable, training-free proxy for a dimension's contribution to model performance on a given task.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states profiling uses "only 100 samples per task." Section 2.2 (Eqs. 3-4) defines the averaging procedure; Table 3 and Figure 3 show ~100 samples saturates performance. Related work (e.g., Fluctuation-based Adaptive Structured Pruning) also uses activation statistics, supporting the premise.
- **Break condition:** May fail if important dimensions have consistently low activations due to cancellation/saturation effects, or if the proxy is architecture- or task-sensitive.

### Mechanism 2: Majority Voting for a Safe Global Mask
- **Claim:** A single global pruning mask can be constructed by aggregating task-specific pruning votes via majority voting.
- **Mechanism:** Each task independently marks its bottom s% dimensions (by importance) as "prune" (binary vote). These task masks are stacked into a vote matrix. A dimension is pruned globally if it receives a vote from a majority of tasks (i.e., is in the top-k by vote count for target sparsity s). Consensus identifies dimensions widely considered uninformative.
- **Core assumption:** Dimensions consistently ranked low across diverse tasks are safe to remove globally without disproportionately harming any single task.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract mentions "applies majority voting to construct a single global mask." Section 2.2 notes dimensions flagged by multiple tasks are "likely safe to remove globally." Section 3.3 (Table 5) shows the global mask outperforms masks from single tasks; Figure 2 shows vote concentration (e.g., ~10% of dimensions get 6-7 votes out of 7 tasks), enabling 10% sparsity with strong agreement.
- **Break condition:** Assumes tasks have overlapping but not conflicting importance signals. Could break if tasks have strongly opposing dimension utilities (one task's critical dimension is another's redundant), leading to a mask that harms multiple tasks.

### Mechanism 3: Hard Pruning with Variance Rescaling
- **Claim:** Hard pruning (physically removing dimensions) with variance rescaling preserves accuracy close to zero-masking evaluation.
- **Mechanism:** After selecting the global mask, DIET can zero-mask (evaluative) or hard-prune (deployable). Hard pruning reduces residual width d to d'. This reduces expected input variance to subsequent linear layers by factor d'/d. DIET compensates by rescaling the weights of layers with reduced *input* dimension by α = sqrt(d/d'), correcting the scale.
- **Core assumption:** The primary distribution shift from hard pruning is a scale change in layer inputs due to reduced dimensionality, counteracted by simple linear weight rescaling.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.3 describes the rescaling factor α. Section 3.2 (Table 4) compares zero-mask (45.0% avg) vs. hard-prune (45.4% avg) on Gemma-2 2B, showing near-identical accuracy, validating the rescaling. The approach is standard in network surgery contexts.
- **Break condition:** Assumes independent, equally scaled input features and that variance is the sole cause of distribution shift. May fail if pruning induces complex changes (e.g., broken feature rituals, non-linear layer instability).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Structured Pruning**
  - *Why needed here:* DIET's core contribution is a *structured* (dimension-wise) pruning method. Understanding the difference between structured (removing entire channels/heads/layers) and unstructured pruning (individual weights) is crucial to grasp DIET's goal: creating a hardware-efficient sparsity pattern.
  - *Quick check:* Why is unstructured pruning generally incompatible with efficient sparse matrix operations on modern hardware?

- **Concept: Activation-based Importance Scoring**
  - *Why needed here:* DIET's entire importance estimation relies on measuring activation magnitudes. This is a classic, training-free heuristic. One must understand its trade-offs (simplicity vs. potential crudeness) versus gradient-based or retraining-based methods.
  - *Quick check:* What is a key limitation of using average absolute activation as a proxy for a neuron's/dimension's importance?

- **Concept: Majority Voting / Aggregation of Subjective Judgments**
  - *Why needed here:* The core innovation of DIET is merging multiple task-specific "opinions" (binary masks) into one global decision. This is a specific form of ensemble/pluralistic decision-making. Understanding the rationale (robustness through consensus) and its assumptions (tasks provide independent, reasonably aligned signals) is key.
  - *Quick check:* In DIET's voting, what might happen if all tasks strongly agree on the *wrong* set of dimensions to prune? What does this imply about the calibration data?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Activation Profiler -> Task-wise Vote Generator -> Consensus Builder -> Mask Applier -> (Optional) Hard Pruner
- **Critical path:** Profiling (per-task, on dense model) → Voting (aggregation) → Mask Application (global). The profiling must be done on a *dense* model; the mask is then applied to create a sparse model.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Granularity vs. Simplicity:** Dimension-wise is finer than block-wise (like PuDDing) but requires a uniform mask across all layers (no per-layer allocation).
  - **Task-Awareness vs. Training Cost:** Task-aware via calibration data vs. task-agnostic (SliceGPT) or per-task router (PuDDing). DIET aims for the former without training.
  - **Hardware Compatibility:** Global mask ensures structured sparsity compatible with speedup kernels (unlike non-uniform masks from some router methods). Hard pruning with proper rescaling is needed for real FLOP/latency gains.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High vote dispersion in Figure 2: If vote counts are spread evenly (no consensus), the global mask is arbitrary and likely to harm performance.
  - Large performance gap between `N=100` and full-data profiling in Figure 3: Indicates profiling data is insufficient/not representative.
  - Hard-prune accuracy << zero-mask accuracy in Table 4: Suggests variance rescaling is insufficient or other distribution shifts occur.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate profiling stability: On Gemma-2 2B, run DIET's profiling with `N=100` random samples per task 5 times with different seeds. Compute variance of resulting global masks (Jaccard similarity). Expected: High agreement (>90% overlap).
  2. Validate voting vs. continuous merging: Implement continuous score merging (normalize per-task activation scores, average, rank). Compare accuracy to binary voting (Table 6). Expected: Binary voting slightly better or comparable.
  3. Test hard-prune variance correction: Ablate the rescaling factor α. Apply hard pruning with and without α at 20% sparsity. Measure accuracy drop. Expected: Without α, significant drop (>5% abs); with α, minimal drop (<0.5%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does DIET generalize to multilingual, safety-critical, long-context, and tool-use tasks beyond the current English zero-shot benchmarks? The evaluation focuses on seven English-centric zero-shot benchmarks; broader coverage remains unexplored.
- Why does DIET degrade significantly at high sparsity on non-Gemma architectures (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B, Phi-4-mini), and can adaptive sparsity or per-layer allocation mitigate this? At high sparsity levels on non-Gemma architectures, notable degradation is observed, suggesting the need for adaptive strategies, but this is untested.
- Does task-weighted voting, where tasks are weighted by difficulty or relevance, improve the global mask's robustness over uniform majority voting? Task-weighted or distribution-aware voting mechanisms may improve robustness, but uniform voting is currently used.
- Can smarter calibration policies, like difficulty-aware or uncertainty-driven sampling, reduce the profiling sample size (currently 100 per task) while maintaining accuracy? DIET saturates around 100 samples, but strategic selection might lower cost; this is unexplored.

## Limitations
- **Activation magnitude as importance proxy:** May fail if critical dimensions exhibit low activations due to feature cancellation or saturation effects.
- **Majority voting assumption:** Requires task importance signals to be reasonably aligned; conflicting task preferences could degrade the global mask.
- **Variance rescaling adequacy:** Compensates only for scale changes; may not address complex distribution shifts from structural pruning (e.g., disrupted feature rituals).
