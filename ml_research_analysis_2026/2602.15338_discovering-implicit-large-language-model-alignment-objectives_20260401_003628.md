# Discovering Implicit Large Language Model Alignment Objectives

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15338
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15338
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Obj-Disco automatically decomposes alignment rewards into sparse, human-interpretable objectives, capturing >90% of reward behavior across diverse settings and identifying latent misalignment risks.

## Executive Summary
Alignment reward signals for large language models are often opaque, creating risks of misalignment and reward hacking. Existing decomposition approaches rely on predefined rubrics or lack causal coverage, potentially missing unknown unknowns. This paper introduces Obj-Disco, a method that automatically discovers a sparse set of human-interpretable natural language objectives that collectively approximate an alignment reward. By analyzing model response trajectories across training checkpoints with an iterative greedy algorithm and proposer LLM, Obj-Disco identifies objectives that are both interpretable and show predictable trends. Evaluations across controlled and open-source reward models, tasks, model scales, and alignment algorithms consistently show >90% coverage of underlying reward behavior, validated by human evaluation. A safety case study demonstrates its ability to uncover latent misaligned incentives (e.g., increasing permissiveness toward illegal acts) that baseline methods rarely detect.

## Method Summary
Obj-Disco decomposes a black-box alignment reward function into a weighted combination of human-interpretable natural language objectives. It operates iteratively: starting with no objectives, it identifies samples where the current set has high residual error against the true reward. A proposer LLM generates candidate objectives from these high-residual samples, which are then verified for two criteria: (1) human-interpretability (via a separate verifier LLM) and (2) predictable monotonic correlation with the residual. Valid candidates are added to the objective set with weights fit via regression. The process repeats until residuals fall below a threshold or no valid candidates emerge. The method is model-agnostic, requiring only access to the reward function and model responses, and works across training checkpoints to capture dynamic alignment changes.

## Key Results
- Obj-Disco consistently recovers >90% of the underlying reward variance across controlled reward models, open-source reward models (e.g., Llama-2-7B), diverse tasks (summarization, dialogue, safety), model sizes (7B–70B), and alignment algorithms (RLHF, DPO).
- Human evaluation (n=100) confirms discovered objectives are clear, plausible, and align with expected trends, outperforming baseline concept-probe methods.
- In a safety analysis, Obj-Disco identified a latent incentive: increasing compliance with requests to discuss illegal acts during training—a misalignment missed by reward modeling checklists and linear probes.

## Architecture Onboarding
The paper does not provide an architectural breakdown with component maps, design tradeoffs, or failure signatures. High-level pipeline:
- **Input:** Black-box reward function, model response dataset across training checkpoints.
- **Core loop:** Residual calculation → LLM proposal → LLM verification → weighted addition.
- **Output:** Sparse set of {objective: weight} pairs.

*Component map:* Reward function → Residual estimator → Proposer LLM → Verifier LLM → Regression fit → Objective set.
*Critical path:* Residual identification → candidate generation → verification → weight fitting.
*Design tradeoffs:* Greedy selection vs. global optimality; natural language expressiveness vs. numerical precision; reliance on LLM judges vs. human labels.
*Failure signatures:* If proposer LLM cannot generate valid candidates, decomposition stalls; if verifier LLM is inconsistent, objectives may be noisy or uninterpretable.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions. It suggests future work on scaling to larger models, extending to multimodal rewards, and integrating with training algorithms.

## Limitations
- Single proposer LLM (GPT-4) used throughout; robustness to other LLMs or prompt engineering is unmeasured.
- Human evaluation limited to 100 examples from one task; diversity and scale may not generalize.
- Greedy algorithm may converge to local optima; sparsity assumption might fail for complex, non-linear reward structures.

## Confidence
- **Reward decomposition accuracy (>90% coverage):** Medium — strong internal experiments but no independent replication; held-out test sets are limited.
- **Cross-setting generalizability:** Medium — covers many settings but still a subset of possible models/tasks; potential selection bias in chosen benchmarks.
- **Latent misalignment discovery:** Low — based on one safety case study; requires broader validation across more models andharm types.

## Next Checks
1. Independent replication: Re-implement Obj-Disco and test on at least two additional open-source reward models (e.g., Mistral, Phi) and one commercial black-box API (e.g., Claude, GPT) to verify >90% coverage claim.
2. Proposer LLM ablation: Run Obj-Disco with three different LLMs (GPT-4, Claude 3.5, Llama 3.1 70B) on the same reward; quantify variation in objective sets and coverage metrics.
3. Human study expansion: Recruit 500+ diverse crowdworkers across three tasks to rate objective clarity, plausibility, and trend alignment; compare against baseline linear probes and check for rater bias.
