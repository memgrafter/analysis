# Attention-guided Evidence Grounding for Spoken Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.16292
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16292
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: AEG with LFE improves evidence grounding F1 by up to 36.53 points and answer EM by up to 2.73%, while reducing inference latency by 62% compared to cascaded systems.

## Executive Summary
Spoken question answering (Spoken QA) systems often suffer from hallucinations and poor interpretability due to ineffective cross-modal alignment between speech and text. This paper introduces Attention-guided Evidence Grounding (AEG), an end-to-end framework that leverages the internal cross-modal attention of Speech Large Language Models (SpeechLLMs) to automatically locate and ground key evidence in the provided textual context. To overcome the diffuseness of pre-trained attention, the authors propose Learning to Focus on Evidence (LFE), a supervised fine-tuning paradigm that calibrates the model to distinguish query-relevant segments. Evaluated on SQuAD, HotpotQA, and MuSiQue, AEG with LFE yields substantial gains in evidence grounding (e.g., +36.53 F1 on SQuAD) and answer accuracy, while also reducing inference latency by approximately 62% relative to strong cascaded baselines.

## Method Summary
AEG operates within a SpeechLLM (e.g., Qwen2-Audio) by extracting and aggregating cross-modal attention weights during the prefill phase to compute a relevance score for each context segment. Segments with scores above a threshold τ are marked with <EVIDENCE> tags, constraining the decoder to generate answers primarily from these segments. The Learning to Focus on Evidence (LFE) technique fine-tunes the SpeechLLM to generate only the ground-truth evidence passages (selected from a set of N candidate contexts) via standard autoregressive loss. This supervised objective shapes the model’s internal attention distribution to become sharper and more discriminative, addressing the innate diffuseness of pre-trained multimodal attention. At inference, the fine-tuned model produces focused attention maps, enabling reliable evidence grounding without external retrievers or rerankers.

## Key Results
- Evidence grounding F1 improves by up to 36.53 points on SQuAD, reaching 80.02% with LFE.
- Answer Exact Match increases by up to 2.73% on MuSiQue compared to strong baselines.
- AEG reduces inference latency by ~62% (to 238ms) while outperforming a cascaded Whisper-Large-v3 + reranker system (F1 79.14%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** Internal cross-modal attention weights from a SpeechLLM can be extracted and thresholded to locate key evidence segments, enabling explicit grounding. During prefill, attention across layers and heads is aggregated to compute a relevance score for each context segment; segments exceeding τ are marked with <EVIDENCE> tags, constraining the decoder. *Break condition:* If pre-trained attention is too diffuse or misaligned, raw attention fails to distinguish relevant segments.

**Mechanism 2:** Supervised fine-tuning via LFE calibrates the attention distribution to concentrate on ground-truth evidence. By training the model to generate only the true evidence passages (from N candidates), the autoregressive loss penalizes attention to non-evidence tokens, implicitly sharpening the attention map. *Break condition:* If training data lacks clear evidence boundaries or negatives are not challenging, the model may not learn a robustly sharpened distribution.

**Mechanism 3:** The end-to-end SpeechLLM architecture with integrated evidence grounding avoids cascaded ASR error propagation and reuses prefill computations, reducing latency. Direct speech-to-evidence alignment in a shared latent space eliminates transcription errors and sequential model invocation delays. *Break condition:* If the base SpeechLLM’s audio encoder is poorly pre-trained, the end-to-end approach may underperform a cascade with a dedicated ASR system.

## Foundational Learning
- **Cross-modal Self-Attention:** Needed to understand how a SpeechLLM’s transformer layers attend across audio and text streams; the attention score theoretically represents relevance between tokens. *Quick check:* In a multimodal transformer, what does the attention score α between an audio query token and a text context token represent?
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Structured Output:** LFE trains the model to output a specific structured sequence (concatenated evidence passages), using autoregressive loss to shape internal representations beyond standard answer generation. *Quick check:* How does training a model to generate only the ground-truth evidence influence its internal attention mechanism?
- **Evidence Grounding Metrics (Hit-Rate, Precision, Recall, F1):** Required to interpret improvements in evidence selection, not just answer accuracy. *Quick check:* If a model selects many context passages (high recall) but most are irrelevant (low precision), what will its F1 score be relative to a model with balanced precision and recall?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Audio Encoder → Text Tokenizer → Sequence Constructor → Evidence Grounding Model → Grounding Marker Inserter → LLM Decoder

**Critical path:** Input (Audio QA + Text CT) → Sequence Construction → Prefill (Compute Attention) → Attention Extraction & Thresholding → Insert Tags → Generate Answer

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Threshold τ: Higher τ increases precision but lowers recall; requires validation tuning.
- Layer range (L_start–L_end): Aggregating only middle-to-upper layers (e.g., 10–28) is optimal; using all layers dilutes the signal.
- Candidate passage count (N): LFE uses N=10 (1 positive + 9 negatives); more negatives may improve discrimination but increase training cost.

**Failure signatures:**
- Diffuse attention: Without LFE, attention weights are uniformly low across segments, leading to poor grounding.
- Threshold sensitivity: Baseline models show wild performance swings with τ; LFE stabilizes the curve.
- Positional bias persistence: Model may still favor beginning/end segments if LFE data lacks uniform evidence distribution.
- Layer mis-selection: Using wrong layer range (e.g., all layers) yields irrelevant attention scores.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Ablation (LFE): Train Qwen2-Audio-7B with and without LFE on SQuAD/HotpotQA/MuSiQue; measure evidence grounding metrics to confirm LFE’s role.
2. Layer Selection Analysis: For a fixed checkpoint, compute evidence F1 while varying L_start and L_end; validate optimal contiguous block (e.g., 10–28).
3. Threshold Sweep: For baseline vs. LFE model, vary τ from 0 to 1 and plot Precision, Recall, Hit-Rate, F1; confirm LFE’s higher and broader F1 peak.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Performance on real acoustic variability:** Paper uses synthesized audio (Higgs Audio); how does AEG handle natural speech with noise, accents, or disfluencies? Need evaluation on spoken QA benchmarks with real recordings.
2. **Eliminating supervised LFE:** LFE requires explicit evidence annotations; can self-supervised signals (e.g., pseudo-labels from attention or reinforcement learning) achieve similar grounding without manual evidence labels?
3. **Generalization to other modalities:** Is the attention-guided grounding effective for video QA or image-text grounding? Need adaptation to multimodal tasks beyond speech-text.
4. **Layer range cross-model generalization:** Optimal layer range (10–28) found for Qwen2-Audio; does it hold for other SpeechLLMs (e.g., GPT-4o Audio, Qwen3-Omni)? Requires per-model analysis.

## Limitations
- Optimal threshold τ is dataset-dependent and not disclosed; must be tuned per validation set.
- Negative sampling strategy for LFE training (N=10) is unspecified, affecting robustness and generalizability.
- Evaluation uses synthetic audio queries; performance on natural, noisy speech remains unverified.
