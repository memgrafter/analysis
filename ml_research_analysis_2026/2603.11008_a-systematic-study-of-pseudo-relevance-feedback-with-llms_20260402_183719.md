# A Systematic Study of Pseudo-Relevance Feedback with LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11008
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11008
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** LLM-generated feedback effectiveness hinges on the feedback model (Rocchio > average) for dense retrievers; LLM-only feedback is most cost-effective; corpus-based feedback requires a strong initial retriever; combining sources helps dense retrievers but not BM25.

## Executive Summary
Pseudo-relevance feedback (PRF) is a long-standing technique to improve retrieval by expanding the query with terms from presumed relevant documents. With the advent of large language models (LLMs), new possibilities arise for generating feedback, either by leveraging the corpus itself or by synthesizing hypothetical documents. This paper systematically investigates two fundamental design choices in LLM-based PRF: the source of feedback (corpus documents, LLM-generated text, or both) and the feedback model (Rocchio, RM3, average vector). Using 13 BEIR datasets and three retrievers (BM25, Contriever, Contriever-MSM), the authors find that the feedback model is crucial for LLM-generated feedback, particularly for dense retrievers, with Rocchio consistently outperforming simple averaging. LLM-only feedback (HyDE) emerges as the most cost-effective approach, while corpus-based feedback (PRF-Umbrela) only yields gains when paired with a strong initial retriever. Combining both sources benefits dense retrievers but not BM25. Latency measurements further show that LLM-only feedback is fastest, whereas corpus-based methods scale poorly with the number of candidate documents. These results offer clear guidelines for deploying LLM-based PRF in practice.

Dense retrievers, despite their higher base effectiveness, gain less from PRF than sparse retrievers, highlighting an area for future research. Overall, this work disentangles key factors in LLM-based PRF and provides an empirical foundation for further innovations in query expansion.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct a comprehensive evaluation of LLM-based pseudo-relevance feedback across 13 BEIR benchmark datasets. They experiment with three first-stage retrievers: BM25 (sparse), Contriever, and Contriever-MSM (dense). For feedback, they consider three sources: (1) corpus-only (PRF-Umbrela), which uses an LLM to assess the relevance of top‑k documents from the initial retriever and selects the most relevant ones; (2) LLM-only (HyDE), which prompts the LLM to generate multiple hypothetical answer documents; and (3) combined sources, either by concatenating feedback from both (Umbrela‑HyDE) or by including corpus examples in the HyDE prompt (PRF‑HyDE). Feedback models differ by retriever type: for sparse retrieval, Rocchio (α=1, β=0.75) and RM3 (λ=0.5); for dense retrieval, simple average vector and Rocchio vector (α=0.4, β=0.6). The number of feedback documents is capped at 8. Effectiveness is measured by nDCG@20, and LLM inference latency is recorded. All LLM calls use the Qwen3-14B model. The experimental design isolates the impact of each dimension by controlling variables and comparing across retrievers and datasets.

## Key Results
- For LLM-generated feedback, the feedback model (Rocchio vs. average) significantly impacts effectiveness, especially for dense retrievers.
- LLM-only feedback (HyDE) is the most cost-effective; corpus-based feedback (PRF-Umbrela) requires a strong initial retriever; combining sources helps dense retrievers but not BM25.
- LLM-only feedback has the lowest latency, while corpus-based methods scale poorly with candidate document count.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
The choice of feedback model (e.g., Rocchio vs. average vector) is critical for LLM-generated feedback, especially with dense retrievers. LLM-generated hypothetical documents (like HyDE) have different term/vector distributions than corpus documents; simple averaging may misalign with the dense retriever's similarity function. Rocchio adaptively reweights feedback vectors, balancing query and feedback to improve expansion. This assumes dense retrievers benefit from balanced integration. Evidence: The abstract notes the critical role of feedback model; Section 5 shows HyDE with Rocchio yields +4.4 nDCG over average vector for Contriever. Rocchio's reweighting can cause topic drift if feedback is already very similar to the query, and gains are smaller for sparse retrievers.

### Mechanism 2
Corpus-derived feedback (PRF-Umbrela) is highly sensitive to the quality of initial candidate documents. It uses an LLM to assess the relevance of top‑k documents; if these candidates are truly relevant, the expansion is useful, but a weak initial retriever introduces noise. This relies on the pseudo‑relevance assumption that top‑ranked docs are relevant. Evidence: The abstract states feedback from the corpus is most beneficial with a strong first‑stage retriever; Table 5 shows PRF-Umbrela improves when switching from BM25 to stronger retrievers; Figure 3 shows more assessed candidates boost effectiveness. However, with a weak initial retriever, corpus feedback may underperform LLM-only, and assessing many documents adds latency.

### Mechanism 3
Combining feedback sources (corpus + LLM) consistently benefits dense retrievers but not sparse retrievers like BM25. Dense retrievers in continuous vector space can fuse multiple vector representations, gaining complementary semantic signals from both sources. BM25, being term‑centric, already gets strong expansion from LLM‑generated terms; adding corpus terms yields diminishing returns and may introduce noise. Evidence: The abstract notes the consistent benefit for dense retrievers; Section 7 shows Umbrela‑HyDE improves over PRF‑HyDE by 2.9 points for Contriever, while for BM25 combination helps only with strong corpus feedback and no clear advantage. However, if LLM feedback already covers the semantic space for dense retrievers, combination may not help; for BM25, corpus feedback might help when LLM feedback is low‑quality.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  1. Initial Retriever (BM25, Contriever, Contriever-MSM) → top‑k candidate documents  
  2. Feedback Source Module: Umbrela (assesses relevance), HyDE (generates hypothetical docs), Combined (Umbrela‑HyDE or PRF‑HyDE)  
  3. Feedback Model Module: For sparse: Rocchio or RM3; for dense: average vector or Rocchio vector  
  4. Query Updater: Constructs expanded query (string for sparse, vector for dense)  
  5. Downstream Retriever: Second‑pass retrieval with updated query  

- **Critical path:** query → initial retriever → feedback source → feedback model → query updater → downstream retriever → results. For combined sources, either merge feedback sets before the feedback model (Umbrela‑HyDE) or incorporate corpus into LLM prompt (PRF‑HyDE).

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Latency vs. effectiveness:** LLM-only (HyDE) is fastest; corpus-based methods slower due to document processing and LLM assessment, scaling with candidate count.  
  - **Feedback source selection:** LLM-only is cost‑effective; corpus-based requires strong initial retriever to justify added latency.  
  - **Feedback model selection:** Rocchio generally superior for LLM‑generated feedback, especially dense; for corpus‑derived feedback, model choice less critical.  
  - **Combination strategy:** For dense, explicit concatenation (Umbrela‑HyDE) preferred; for sparse, combination only helps with high‑quality corpus feedback and choice does not matter much.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Performance degradation when initial retriever is weak (topic drift), particularly for corpus‑based feedback.  
  - Dense retrievers underperforming BM25 with PRF despite higher base effectiveness, indicating limited exploitation of feedback.  
  - Unexpectedly high latency when assessing many candidate documents or long documents.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **RQ1:** Fix feedback source (e.g., HyDE) and compare feedback models (average vs. Rocchio) across retrievers; measure nDCG@20.  
  2. **RQ2:** Compare LLM‑only (HyDE) vs. corpus‑only (PRF‑Umbrela) under identical retriever and feedback model; also vary initial retriever strength to test robustness.  
  3. **RQ3:** Compare combination methods (Umbrela‑HyDE, PRF‑HyDE) against single sources for dense and sparse retrievers; evaluate whether combination yields consistent gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Can the observed advantage of using BM25 to exploit feedback from dense retrievers be generalized to other dense retriever architectures?  
- *Basis in paper:* Explicitly stated (Page 7) as future work.  
- *Why unresolved:* Only Contriever and Contriever-MSM were evaluated.  
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic experiments with modern dense retrievers (e.g., BGE, Sentence-T5) across multiple datasets.

**Open Question 2:** Why do dense retrievers exhibit significantly smaller improvements from PRF compared to sparse retrievers like BM25, despite higher base effectiveness?  
- *Basis in paper:* Inferred from results showing BM25 PRF outperforms Contriever-MSM PRF by up to 8.5% despite lower base performance.  
- *Why unresolved:* Underlying causes (matching granularity, vector averaging limitations) not investigated.  
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Ablation studies isolating factors like term semantics preservation, feedback document count, and failure analysis; possibly designing modified dense update rules.

**Open Question 3:** To what extent do the paper's conclusions about feedback source (corpus vs. LLM-only vs. combined) depend on the choice of backbone LLM (e.g., size, architecture, instruction tuning)?  
- *Basis in paper:* Inferred; only Qwen3-14B used.  
- *Why unresolved:* Different LLMs may produce qualitatively different hypothetical documents, affecting PRF effectiveness.  
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Repeating key experiments with a diverse set of LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Llama-3, Claude) and comparing the relative performance of feedback sources and models.

**Open Question 4:** How sensitive are LLM-based PRF results to hyperparameter settings (e.g., number of feedback documents, Rocchio α/β, RM3 λ), and do optimal values vary across datasets and retrievers?  
- *Basis in paper:* Inferred; hyperparameters fixed without tuning.  
- *Why unresolved:* Fixed parameters might not be optimal; sensitivity could affect reported trends, especially when comparing methods.  
- *What evidence would resolve it:* A comprehensive hyperparameter sensitivity analysis sweeping key parameters per retriever/dataset, reporting performance ranges and identifying robust settings.

## Limitations
- Exact prompt templates for UMBRELA and HyDE are not provided; reproduction must approximate from prior work, risking subtle mismatches.
- The study uses fixed hyperparameters (α, β, λ, feedback limits) without validation tuning; these may not generalize to all datasets or retrievers.
- Latency analysis isolates only LLM inference time; total system cost (retrieval, candidate processing) is omitted, potentially overstating LLM-only efficiency.

## Confidence
- **Feedback model choice critical for LLM-generated feedback (especially dense):** High  
- **Corpus-derived feedback sensitive to initial retriever quality:** Medium  
- **Combining sources benefits dense but not sparse retrievers:** Medium
