# Transporting Task Vectors across Different Architectures without Training

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.12952
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12952
- **Reference count:** 32
- **Key result:** Enables transferring task-specific updates across mismatched transformer architectures (width and depth) without training, achieving +10.3% avg accuracy on vision tasks and +22.0% on language probing.

## Executive Summary
Task vectors—the difference between a fine-tuned and pre-trained model—cannot be directly applied to models with different architectures due to mismatched parameter dimensions. THESEUS introduces a training-free framework for transporting such updates across models of varying widths (and depth). Instead of matching parameters directly, it characterizes a task update by its functional effect on intermediate activations. Using a small calibration set, THESEUS aligns the source and target models’ representation spaces via orthogonal Procrustes analysis, yielding embeddings that map the source’s activation subspaces into the target’s spaces while preserving norms and angles. The task update is then transported via a closed-form rule: τ_B = T_out τ_A T_in^⊤. Experiments on CLIP vision transformers and T5 language models show consistent gains over zero-shot baselines and even match gradient-based methods on identical architectures, all without additional training.

## Method Summary
THESEUS operates on task vectors τ = θ^ft – θ, where θ are base parameters and θ^ft are fine-tuned ones. For a linear or attention projection layer, the functional effect of τ on a calibration input x is approximated as y_out ≈ H_out θ + H_in τ, where H_in and H_out are Jacobians (activation matrices) collected from the base model over a calibration set D. The update τ_A from source model A is characterized by the bilinear form G_A = H_in,A τ_A^⊤ H_out,A^⊤. To transfer τ_A to a target model B with different parameter dimensions, THESEUS seeks τ_B such that G_B ≈ G_A. Because H_in,B and H_out,B have different shapes, the authors align the source and target representation spaces via orthogonal Procrustes: compute cross-covariances C_in = H_in,A^⊤ H_in,B and C_out = H_out,A^⊤ H_out,B, take their SVDs, and set T_in = U_in V_in^⊤, T_out = U_out V_out^⊤. These orthogonal embeddings satisfy H_in,B ≈ H_in,A T_in and H_out,B ≈ H_out,A T_out on the dominant shared subspaces. Substituting into the matching condition yields a unique closed-form solution τ_B = T_out τ_A T_in^⊤ under mild rank conditions. For mismatched sequence lengths (e.g., ViTs), tokens are reshaped to a 2D grid and bilinearly interpolated to the target grid size. Finally, τ_B is scaled by α (tuned on validation) and added to the target’s base parameters. The entire process requires no gradients or training beyond collecting activations on D.

## Key Results
- **Width-mismatched vision:** Transporting from ViT-B to ViT-B+ using 20 calibration batches improves average accuracy by +10.3% over zero-shot on eight vision tasks (EuroSAT, SVHN, GTSRB, RESISC45, DTD, CARS, MNIST, SUN397).
- **Language models:** Transferring encoder updates from T5-3B to T5-Large boosts linear probing accuracy on GLUE tasks by +22.0%.
- **Broad applicability:** THESEUS matches gradient-based baselines on identical architectures and extends to simultaneous width/depth scaling, all while remaining training-free.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Task identity is defined by functional effect on activations
- **Claim:** A task update τ is characterized by its bilinear influence on input-output activations (G = H_in τ^⊤ H_out^⊤), not by raw parameter values.
- **Mechanism:** G aggregates how τ couples input and output activations across a calibration set. Transporting τ requires finding τ_B in the target that induces a matching G_B.
- **Core assumption:** The “task” is captured by this input-output interaction structure; preserving it maintains task identity even across architectures.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “Rather than matching parameters directly, we characterize a task update by the functional effect it induces on intermediate representations.” Section 3: “We formalize task-vector transport as a functional matching problem on observed activations… GA = H_in,A τ_A^⊤ H_out,A^⊤.”
- **Break condition:** Calibration data must activate task-relevant features in both models; otherwise, estimated G_A and G_B misrepresent the true functional effect.

### Mechanism 2: Representation alignment via orthogonal Procrustes resolves underdetermined transport
- **Claim:** Aligning source and target activation subspaces using orthogonal maps (T_in, T_out) restricts τ_B to a shared, isometric subspace.
- **Mechanism:** Compute cross-covariances C_in = H_in,A^⊤ H_in,B and C_out = H_out,A^⊤ H_out,B; SVD gives T_in = U_in V_in^⊤, T_out = U_out V_out^⊤. These embed source activations into target spaces, preserving norms and angles, and naturally accommodate mismatched widths.
- **Core assumption:** Dominant, task-relevant subspaces of the two models are related by an approximate orthogonal transformation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “…after aligning representation spaces via orthogonal Procrustes analysis, it admits a stable closed-form solution…” Section 3: “We address this challenge… by aligning the representation spaces of the two models via orthogonal Procrustes analysis…”
- **Break condition:** If source and target models learn dissimilar features on calibration data, cross-covariances have low nuclear norm and embeddings become poor approximations.

### Mechanism 3: Closed-form transport follows from injectivity of the alignment
- **Claim:** Under the Procrustes alignment, the transport problem simplifies to τ_B = T_out τ_A T_in^⊤, which is stable and training-free.
- **Mechanism:** The alignment imposes H_in,B ≈ H_in,A T_in and H_out,B ≈ H_out,A T_out on shared subspaces. Substituting into min ||H_in,A (τ_A^⊤ – T_in τ_B^⊤ T_out^⊤) H_out,A^⊤||_F^2 yields zero error when τ_A^⊤ = T_in τ_B^⊤ T_out^⊤, thus τ_B = T_out τ_A T_in^⊤. This holds if activation matrices have full column rank on the calibration set (ensuring injectivity).
- **Core assumption:** The linear map E → H_in,A E H_out,A^⊤ is injective on the aligned subspace, which requires H_in,A and H_out,A to have full column rank on the calibration data.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3: “Substituting Eq. (8) into Eq. (4)… the minimum is attained when E=0… yielding the closed-form solution: τ_B = T_out τ_A T_in^⊤.” Appendix: injectivity proof via Kronecker product.
- **Break condition:** Rank-deficient activation matrices (e.g., from too small or homogeneous calibration set) break injectivity, leading to non-unique or unstable solutions.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Calibration dataset → ActivationExtractor (collects H_in^A, H_out^A and H_in^B, H_out^B per layer) → ProcrustesSolver (computes T_in, T_out via SVD) → TransportRule (τ_B = T_out τ_A T_in^⊤ per layer) → UpdateScaler (θ_B ← θ_B + α τ_B) → Evaluation

**Critical path:** Sequential per layer: calibration activations → alignment → transport → scaling addition. Alignment must precede transport.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Calibration batch size:* More batches improve alignment but increase compute; gains taper around 20 batches.
- *Sequence alignment (vision):* For token length mismatches, 2D bilinear interpolation on a token grid preserves spatial topology better than 1D or pooling.
- *Direction:* Transport is symmetric; both wide→narrow and narrow→wide supported. Performance depends on target’s pretraining quality.
- *Scope:* Applied per-layer to linear/attention submodules only; excludes LayerNorm, biases. Assumes layer correspondence; depth scaling uses naive interpolation.

**Failure signatures:**
- *Degenerate alignment:* T_in/T_out have small singular values; check Σ norms from SVD.
- *Rank deficiency:* Activation matrices rank-deficient due to small/homogeneous calibration set; check Gram matrix ranks.
- *Catastrophic forgetting:* Overwriting pretrained knowledge if scaling α is too large; tune α on validation.
- *Architectural mismatch beyond width:* Convolutions, different normalization or activations break linear effect model.

**First 3 experiments to reproduce:**
1. **Width-mismatched ViTs:** Transport from ViT-B/16 to ViT-B+ using 20 calibration batches; 2D sequence interpolation; evaluate on 8 vision tasks. Expect >5% avg accuracy gain over zero-shot.
2. **Pseudo-inverse baseline comparison:** Run τ_pinv and τ_pinv-Tikh on same setup; verify THESEUS yields higher accuracy and lower variance (Table 1).
3. **Linear probing (language):** Transport T5-3B encoder update to T5-Large; freeze encoder, train linear head on GLUE tasks. Expect significant improvement over target zero-shot (Table 2).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Layer correspondence for depth mismatch:** How to align layers when source and target have different depths? Current naive interpolation is preliminary; a more principled treatment is needed.
2. **Calibration sample complexity:** What is the minimum number of calibration samples required for reliable Procrustes alignment and effective transport? The paper uses 1–20 batches but lacks analysis.
3. **Generalization beyond transformers:** Does THESEUS work for non-transformer architectures (e.g., CNNs)? Applicability to convolutions or other layers is untested.
4. **Limitations of bilinear matching:** Under what conditions does matching the bilinear form G fail to capture the true functional effect of a task update? This could occur for highly nonlinear layers or certain tasks.

## Limitations
- Assumptions (linear layer effect, full-rank activations) may be violated with too few or unrepresentative calibration examples.
- Scope limited to linear and attention projection layers; residual connections, LayerNorm, biases, and attention-specific parameters are not transported, limiting completeness.
- Depth scaling uses a naive interpolation scheme that may not correctly align layers when depths differ substantially.
- Evaluation restricted to transformer families (ViT, T5); cross-architecture transfer (e.g., CNN → Transformer) remains unvalidated.

## Confidence
- **High:** Functional matching theory and orthogonal Procrustes alignment are mathematically rigorous under stated assumptions.
- **High:** Procrustes alignment is a standard, stable technique for isometric embedding.
- **Medium:** Empirical gains are shown on vision and language tasks within limited model families and datasets; broader generalizability is not yet proven.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-family generality test:** Apply THESEUS to non-transformer architectures (e.g., ResNet, VGG) and to cross-architecture transfers (e.g., CNN → Transformer) to evaluate transfer success rates.
2. **Calibration sensitivity sweep:** Systematically vary calibration set size (1–100 batches) and distribution (in-task vs random) across multiple tasks; correlate alignment quality (e.g., subspace cosine similarity) with downstream accuracy.
3. **Multi-task interference assessment:** Transport and combine multiple task vectors (e.g., from different GLUE tasks) into a single target model; compare performance against individual transports to quantify interference and robustness.
