# Grounding Arabic LLMs in the Doha Historical Dictionary: Retrieval-Augmented Understanding of Quran and Hadith

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.23972  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23972  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Proposed RAG framework boosts Arabic-native LLMs to >85% accuracy on Quran/Hadith queries, nearly matching Gemini.

## Executive Summary
Arabic language models face difficulties with historical religious texts due to archaic vocabulary and semantic shifts. This paper presents a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system anchored in the Doha Historical Dictionary of Arabic. The system employs hybrid retrieval combining BM25 and dense embeddings, an intent-based router, and a fine-tuned cross-encoder for re-ranking. Applied to Fanar and ALLaM, the method yields over 85% accuracy on Quran and Hadith question-answering, significantly closing the performance gap with Gemini. Automated evaluation using Gemini indicates near-perfect alignment with human judgments (κ=0.87). Error analysis reveals diacritics and compound phrases as primary failure modes. Limitations include reliance on Gemini for evaluation and lack of human validation.

## Method Summary
The method integrates the Doha Historical Dictionary into a RAG pipeline to address lexical and semantic challenges in classical Arabic. Queries first pass through an intent classifier that selects between sparse (BM25) and dense (embedding-based) retrieval from a corpus of religious texts. Both retrieval results are merged and then re-ranked by a cross-encoder model fine-tuned for relevance. The top context passages are fed to an Arabic LLM (Fanar or ALLaM) to generate answers. This hybrid approach aims to capture exact term matches for archaic words while also retrieving semantically related passages, thereby improving comprehension of historical texts.

## Key Results
- Arabic-native LLMs (Fanar, ALLaM) achieve >85% accuracy on Quran/Hadith queries after RAG augmentation.
- The performance gap with Gemini is substantially reduced.
- Automated evaluation via Gemini shows κ=0.87 agreement with human judgments.
- Diacritics and compound phrases are identified as the main sources of errors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Doha Historical Dictionary provides historical meanings and usage contexts for Arabic terms, bridging the gap between modern LLMs and archaic language. Hybrid retrieval leverages BM25’s strength in exact term matching (critical for rare historical words) and dense embeddings’ ability to capture semantic similarity despite surface form differences. Intent-based routing ensures that the retrieval strategy matches the query’s nature, while cross-encoder re-ranking adds a layer of fine-grained relevance assessment. This multi-stage pipeline enriches the context presented to the LLM, enabling more accurate generation on religious texts.

## Foundational Learning
- **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)**: *Why needed* – Incorporates external knowledge without full model retraining. *Quick check* – Compare generation quality with and without retrieved context.
- **Hybrid Retrieval (BM25 + Dense Embeddings)**: *Why needed* – BM25 captures lexical matches for archaic terms; dense embeddings handle semantic variation. *Quick check* – Measure recall on queries containing historical vocabulary.
- **Cross-encoder Re-ranking**: *Why needed* – Provides deeper interaction between query and document for precise ranking. *Quick check* – Evaluate NDCG@k before and after re-ranking.
- **Doha Historical Dictionary**: *Why needed* – Supplies specialized lexicon for historical Arabic, resolving semantic shifts. *Quick check* – Verify presence of key religious terms and their archaic definitions.
- **Intent-based Routing**: *Why needed* – Dynamically selects optimal retrieval strategy per query, improving efficiency and accuracy. *Quick check* – Classify a sample of queries and check routing decisions against manual labels.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Query -> Intent Router -> BM25 Retriever & Dense Retriever -> Cross-encoder Re-ranker -> Arabic LLM -> Answer
- **Critical Path**: The query first goes through intent classification, which activates one or both retrieval components. Retrieved documents are combined and re-ranked; the top result is passed to the LLM for answer generation. The dictionary informs retrieval by potentially expanding or normalizing query terms.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: 
  - BM25 vs. dense embeddings: BM25 is fast and precise for lexical matches but misses semantic equivalence; dense embeddings capture semantics but may suffer from vocabulary mismatch.
  - Cross-encoder re-ranking improves relevance at the cost of additional latency.
  - Intent routing adds complexity but can tailor retrieval to query type.
- **Failure Signatures**:
  - Diacritic mismatches causing BM25 misses.
  - Compound phrases not properly handled by either retriever.
  - Misclassification of intent leading to suboptimal retrieval strategy.
- **First Experiments**:
  1. Baseline comparison: Run the pipeline without the Doha Historical Dictionary to quantify its impact.
  2. Ablate intent routing: Use only BM25 or only dense retrieval for all queries to assess routing benefit.
  3. Diacritic sensitivity test: Augment queries with random diacritics and measure accuracy degradation.

## Limitations
- Evaluation circularity: Gemini serves as both judge and performance benchmark.
- Insufficient details on Doha Historical Dictionary coverage and domain specificity hinder reproducibility.
- Absence of human validation; κ=0.87 agreement relies on an unverified automated metric.

## Confidence
- Accuracy improvement (≥85% on Quran/Hadith): **Medium**  
- Gap closure with Gemini: **Medium**  
- Gemini-human agreement (κ=0.87): **Low**  

## Next Checks
1. Independent human evaluation: Engage classical Arabic scholars to label 200 sampled responses; compare with Gemini scores to assess true agreement.
2. Ablation on error types: Build test sets focusing on diacritic variants and compound phrases; measure exact accuracy drops for each factor.
3. Cross-corpus generalization: Apply the RAG system to a held-out classical Arabic corpus (e.g., Al-Muwatta) without retuning; report accuracy change.
