# ViCLSR: A Supervised Contrastive Learning Framework with Natural Language Inference for Natural Language Understanding Tasks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21084
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21084
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** ViCLSR substantially outperforms PhoBERT Large and XLM-R Large across five Vietnamese NLU benchmarks, with gains up to +9.02% F1 and achieving 82.62% top-1 retrieval accuracy.

## Executive Summary
Vietnamese natural language understanding (NLU) is constrained by limited annotated data, which hampers the performance of pre-trained models like PhoBERT. ViCLSR introduces a supervised contrastive learning framework that repurposes Vietnamese Natural Language Inference (NLI) datasets (ViNLI, XNLI-Vi) to generate positive (entailment) and negative (contradiction) sentence pairs. Built on XLM-R, the model is trained with a contrastive loss to optimize sentence embeddings, followed by task-specific fine-tuning. This approach yields significant improvements on five diverse Vietnamese NLU tasks—NLI, fact-checking, constructive speech detection, and multiple-choice reading comprehension—demonstrating effective semantic discrimination for low-resource languages without requiring large amounts of task-specific labeled data.

## Method Summary
ViCLSR first converts Vietnamese NLI training data into triplet contrastive samples: (premise, entailment-hypothesis) as positive pairs and (premise, contradiction-hypothesis) as negative pairs, excluding neutral pairs. This yields 8,594 triplets from ViNLI and XNLI-Vi. The framework uses a shared XLM-R Large encoder to embed each sentence, extracts the [CLS] token representation as the sentence vector, and applies a supervised contrastive loss that minimizes cosine distance for positives and maximizes it for negatives within a batch, scaled by temperature τ=0.05. After contrastive pre-training (10 epochs, lr=1e-5), a task-specific classification head is added and the entire model is fine-tuned (7 epochs, lr=1e-5) on each downstream benchmark with early stopping. Key design choices include [CLS] pooling (superior to mean pooling for classification) and omitting auxiliary MLM (which degrades most tasks).

## Key Results
- vs. PhoBERT Large: +6.97% F1 (ViNLI), +4.97% F1 (ViWikiFC), +9.02% F1 (ViFactCheck), +5.36% F1 (UIT-ViCTSD), +4.33% Acc (ViMMRC2.0)
- vs. XLM-R Large: +1.50% Acc (ViNLI)
- Superior embedding quality: higher alignment (positive pairs closer) and uniformity (even coverage) scores; 82.62% top-1 retrieval accuracy on ViWikiFC.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Supervised contrastive learning with NLI pairs structures embedding space
**Claim:** Using entailment as positive and contradiction as negative pairs directly shapes the embedding space to reflect semantic similarity and difference.
**Mechanism:** The contrastive loss (Eq. 1) treats an anchor premise *x* and its entailment hypothesis *x⁺* as a positive pair, while the premise and its contradiction *x⁻* form a negative pair. Within a batch, cosine similarities are scaled by temperature *τ* and optimized via softmax to pull positives together and push negatives apart.
**Core assumption:** NLI labels (entailment/contradiction) provide reliable, high-quality semantic relationships for alignment.
**Evidence anchors:** 
- [abstract] "...leverages existing natural language inference (NLI) datasets to create positive (entailment) and negative (contradiction) sentence pairs."
- [section 3.2] "Positive pairs are derived from entailment instances... Negative pairs are derived from contradiction instances."
**Break condition:** Noisy or ambiguous NLI labels (e.g., mislabeled contradictions) degrade the contrastive signal.

### Mechanism 2: [CLS] token pooling leverages pre-trained aggregation capability
**Claim:** Using the final layer's [CLS] token as sentence embedding yields better downstream performance than mean or other pooling strategies.
**Mechanism:** During pre-training, the [CLS] token is optimized for sequence-level classification, making it a natural fixed-length summary. Contrastive training further refines this aggregated representation.
**Core assumption:** The [CLS] representation inherently captures whole-sequence semantics better than uniform token averaging.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [section 5.1] "[CLS] Pooling method consistently achieves the highest accuracy... Mean Pooling... results in a slight performance drop, likely due to feature dilution."
- [table 7] [CLS] pooling outperforms Mean, First-Last, and Top-2 pooling across all five downstream tasks.
**Break condition:** Tasks heavily reliant on local token matching (e.g., exact retrieval) may benefit more from mean pooling.

### Mechanism 3: Temperature *τ* balances contrastive loss sharpness
**Claim:** A temperature parameter *τ* ≈ 0.05 optimally balances the loss's gradient signal, preventing embedding collapse or excessive scatter.
**Mechanism:** *τ* scales cosine similarities before softmax. Lower *τ* focuses on hard negatives/positives but risks instability; higher *τ* flattens the distribution, reducing driving force for separation.
**Core assumption:** An intermediate *τ* maintains sufficient gradient for both alignment and uniformity.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [section 5.1] "The best overall performance is observed at τ=0.05... Deviating from this optimal value leads to performance degradation."
- [table 5] τ=0.05 yields highest dev accuracy on 4/5 tasks; τ=0.001 works best only on ViFactCheck.
**Break condition:** Fine-grained tasks (e.g., ViFactCheck) may require lower *τ* (e.g., 0.001) for sharper discrimination.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Supervised Contrastive Loss**
  - *Why needed:* Replaces standard fine-tuning with a pre-training phase that directly optimizes sentence embeddings using labeled positive/negative pairs.
  - *Quick check:* What happens to the gradient for a positive pair if all other batch examples are semantically similar to the anchor? (Answer: gradient diminishes, reducing learning signal.)
- **Concept: Natural Language Inference (NLI) Task Structure**
  - *Why needed:* ViCLSR repurposes NLI's entailment/contradiction labels as direct contrastive signals; understanding the 3-class (entailment, contradiction, neutral) formulation explains why neutral pairs are excluded.
  - *Quick check:* Why might including neutral pairs as "soft negatives" introduce noise? (Answer: Neutral pairs are not strictly negative and may reduce alignment pressure.)
- **Concept: Vietnamese Linguistic Challenges**
  - *Why needed:* Vietnamese's complex syntax, tonal nature, and analytic morphology make tokenization and semantic modeling difficult, justifying specialized frameworks even for multilingual models like XLM-R.
  - *Quick check:* How might tonal variations affect subword tokenization in XLM-R? (Answer: Tone marks may be split into separate tokens, increasing sparsity and losing phonological cues.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (premise, entailment, contradiction) -> Shared Encoder (XLM-R) -> [CLS] Pooling -> Sentence Embeddings -> Supervised Contrastive Loss -> (Pre-trained checkpoint) -> Add Task-specific Classification Head -> Downstream Fine-tuning -> Predictions
- **Critical path:** Data Prep (NLI→Contrastive Triplets) → Contrastive Pre-train (Update XLM-R with loss) → Add Head → Task-specific Fine-tune.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Multilingual vs. monolingual backbone:* XLM-R provides cross-lingual transfer and capacity but may dilute Vietnamese-specific knowledge; ViCLSR fine-tunes XLM-R on Vietnamese NLI to specialize.
  - *Auxiliary MLM objective:* Adding MLM during contrastive pre-training (λ>0) harms most tasks (Table 6), suggesting token-level reconstruction conflicts with sentence-level alignment.
  - *Pooling strategy:* [CLS] is optimal for classification tasks (Table 7), but mean pooling sometimes better for information retrieval (Table 8), indicating task dependence.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Poor alignment/uniformity:* High ℓ_align (positives not close) or low ℓ_uniform (embeddings clumped) in analysis (Fig. 6). Causes: incorrect *τ*, noisy NLI pairs, insufficient training.
  - *Overfitting on small datasets:* Diverging training/dev loss curves (e.g., UIT-ViCTSD, Fig. 9d). Mitigate with early stopping.
  - *Fine-grained retrieval failures:* Even ViCLSR retrieves incorrect evidence for approximate numerical or conceptual equivalence (e.g., "around 30°C" vs "28°C") (Table 9), indicating limitations in capturing soft equality.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Sweep temperature *τ* ∈ {0.001, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0} on ViNLI dev set; expect peak at 0.05.
  2. Ablate pooling strategies ([CLS], mean, first-last, top-2) on ViWikiFC dev; expect [CLS] to dominate.
  3. Vary NLI training data size (25%, 50%, 75%, 100%) and measure downstream accuracy; expect steady gains with diminishing returns beyond 75%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** Why does incorporating auxiliary MLM degrade performance on most tasks but improve ViFactCheck?
  - *Basis:* Table 6 shows excluding MLM yields best results on 4/5 tasks; small λ=0.05 helps ViFactCheck.
  - *Why unresolved:* No analysis of task characteristics that make ViFactCheck benefit from token-level context.
  - *Resolving evidence:* Probing experiments comparing token representations with/without MLM on ViFactCheck vs. other tasks; failure analysis on ViFactCheck examples where MLM helps.
- **Question 2:** What causes optimal *τ* to vary across tasks (e.g., 0.05 vs. 0.001 for ViFactCheck)?
  - *Basis:* Table 5 shows τ=0.001 best for ViFactCheck only.
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper hypothesizes finer-grained discrimination needs sharper distributions but does not link task metadata (e.g., negative hardness, lexical diversity) to *τ*.
  - *Resolving evidence:* Correlate task-level statistics with optimal *τ*; test adaptive temperature schedules to see if dynamic adjustment improves consistency.
- **Question 3:** How does contrastive learning modify transformer attention mechanisms to produce more semantically focused patterns?
  - *Basis:* Figure 8 shows ViCLSR attends to semantically tied keywords (e.g., "guitar"–"instrument") while XLM-R distributes attention broadly.
  - *Why unresolved:* Visualizations are qualitative; no quantitative analysis (e.g., attention entropy, head specialization) establishes causality.
  - *Resolving evidence:* Measure attention metrics across layers/heads for models trained with/without contrastive loss; ablate the objective and track attention shifts.

## Limitations
- Random seeds and exact data splits unreported, hindering reproducibility.
- Batch construction for in-batch negatives ambiguous: unclear whether anchors contrast against all batch examples or only their own contradiction.
- Single-run evaluation; no variance estimates reported.
- Sensitivity to label noise in NLI datasets not assessed.
- Neutral NLI pairs excluded; potential loss of useful “soft negative” signal.

## Confidence
- *Effectiveness:* Medium (large gains but single-run, no variance)
- *Mechanism (contrastive structuring):* Medium (alignment/uniformity metrics support, but causality not fully isolated)
- *Pooling strategy:* Medium (ablation-based, single-run)
- *Temperature choice:* Medium (4/5 tasks consistent, but task-specific variations unexplained)
- *Embedding quality metrics:* High (direct measurements of alignment/uniformity)

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sweep:** Systematically vary τ and pooling on all five tasks using a fixed pretrained checkpoint; confirm τ=0.05 and [CLS] as best defaults, document deviations.
2. **Loss implementation audit:** Reimplement Eq. 1 with explicit in-batch negative handling; compare performance when negatives restricted to triplet-internal contradictions vs. entire batch to verify intended formulation.
3. **NLI robustness test:** Inject label noise (5–10%) and include neutral pairs as soft negatives; measure downstream accuracy to quantify sensitivity and potential gains.
