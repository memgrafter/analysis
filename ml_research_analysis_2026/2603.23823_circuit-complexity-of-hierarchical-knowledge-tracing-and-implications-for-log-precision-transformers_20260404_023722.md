# Circuit Complexity of Hierarchical Knowledge Tracing and Implications for Log-Precision Transformers
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23823  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23823  
- **Reference count:** 34  
- **Primary result:** Hierarchical prerequisite propagation (recursive majority) lies in NC¹ but is not known to be in TC⁰; transformers with root-only supervision learn permutation-invariant shortcuts (≈70–80% accuracy), while auxiliary supervision on intermediate subtrees elicits hierarchical computation (≈99% accuracy on depth 3–4) but fails at depth 6.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether transformer models can correctly perform hierarchical prerequisite propagation for knowledge tracing, using circuit complexity to frame theoretical limits. The authors model the task as evaluating recursive majority trees. Theoretically, this function is in NC¹ (logarithmic depth) but separating it from TC⁰ is an open problem, suggesting constant-depth log-precision transformers may be inherently limited. Empirically, transformers trained only on root labels converge to permutation-invariant shortcuts based on aggregate leaf statistics, matching a simple sum-only baseline and ignoring tree structure. However, adding auxiliary supervision on intermediate subtree values—combined with explicit structural encoding—steers the same architecture toward hierarchical computation, achieving near-perfect accuracy on moderate depths, though performance degrades for deeper trees (depth 6). The work highlights a gap between theoretical expressivity and practical optimization, and suggests iterative computation as a direction for deeper hierarchies.

## Method Summary
The study uses synthetic hierarchical knowledge tracing tasks based on balanced ternary trees of depth d (3–6), where each internal node outputs the majority of its three children (MAJ₃). Data consists of binary leaf bits sampled i.i.d. from Bernoulli(0.5), with deterministic root labels computed bottom‑up. Two model families are compared: (1) a 4‑layer transformer encoder taking a flat sequence of leaf bits plus a [CLS] token, trained with root‑only cross‑entropy loss; and (2) a 3‑layer MLP baseline that consumes only the normalized leaf sum. A scaffolded transformer variant inserts level‑tagged separator tokens marking subtree boundaries and uses an auxiliary classifier at each separator to predict the closed subtree’s majority value, trained with a combined root + auxiliary loss. Models are trained via AdamW (lr 3e‑4, weight decay 0.01) with early stopping, using 20k/5k/5k train/val/test splits per depth. Key diagnostics include test accuracy, permutation sensitivity (evaluating on shuffled leaf orders), and auxiliary prediction accuracy.

## Key Results
- Recursive majority KT lies in NC¹ via O(log n)-depth bounded-fanin circuits; separating it from uniform TC⁰ would require major circuit lower-bound breakthroughs.
- Transformers with root‑only supervision achieve 70–80% root accuracy, matching a permutation‑invariant sum‑only MLP baseline and showing no sensitivity to leaf permutation.
- Adding explicit structure and auxiliary supervision yields near‑perfect accuracy (99.36–99.96%) on depths 3–4 and makes predictions permutation‑sensitive, but performance collapses at depth 6 (auxiliary accuracy ≈53%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Theoretical depth requirement for hierarchical prerequisite propagation
Hierarchical prerequisite propagation on balanced trees with fixed‑arity majority requires logarithmic circuit depth (Θ(log n)), placing it in NC¹ but not known to be in TC⁰. Thus, constant‑depth transformers (which fall in TC⁰) may not compute it exactly without a separation breakthrough. The recursive majority function corresponds to a Boolean formula of depth d = logₖ n with majority gates; each gate has a constant‑fan‑in bounded subcircuit, yielding overall O(log n) depth.

*Evidence:* Abstract and Section 4.2 (Theorem 1) establish KT_MAJ ∈ NC¹; the open separation is explicitly stated.

### Mechanism 2: Shortcut learning under root‑only supervision
When trained only on root labels, transformers converge to permutation‑invariant shortcuts using aggregate statistics (e.g., leaf sum), which correlates with the root under i.i.d. Bernoulli(0.5) leaves. Gradient descent prefers this simple, permutation‑invariant predictor over the more complex hierarchical computation, as the loss does not force intermediate structure.

*Evidence:* Abstract and Section 5.2 show transformer accuracy matches the sum baseline and is unchanged under leaf permutation.

### Mechanism 3: Eliciting hierarchical computation via auxiliary supervision
Auxiliary supervision on intermediate subtree values, combined with explicit level‑tagged separators, forces the model to align leaf positions with structural boundaries and produce correct intermediate states. This prevents collapse to global aggregates and induces permutation‑sensitive, hierarchical computation, achieving near‑perfect accuracy on moderate depths.

*Evidence:* Abstract and Section 5.3 report 99%+ root accuracy with Struct+Aux, large permutation‑induced drops, and high separator‑prediction accuracy at depths 3–5.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input tokens (leaf bits + [CLS]) with sinusoidal positional encodings → 4 self‑attention layers (hidden 128, 4 heads, FFN 512) → [CLS] representation → linear classifier (root prediction). Scaffolded variant: insert level‑tagged separator tokens (]k) at subtree boundaries; shared auxiliary classifier applied to each separator token’s representation → predict subtree majority.
- **Critical path:** Without auxiliary loss, the encoder may aggregate leaf information early (e.g., via mean‑like attention) and ignore positional structure, leading to a permutation‑invariant shortcut. With auxiliary supervision, the encoder must produce correct intermediate values at separators, forcing hierarchical state formation across layers.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Depth vs. performance: deeper hierarchies (depth 6) cause accuracy collapse even with aux supervision, suggesting capacity or algorithmic limits. Structure encoding alone is insufficient; auxiliary loss is essential to incentivize hierarchical use. Permutation sensitivity is a diagnostic of structure use but may trade off robustness.
- **Failure signatures:** (1) Transformer root accuracy ≈ sum‑only MLP baseline. (2) No accuracy drop under random leaf permutation. (3) Root accuracy degrades as depth increases (∼80% at d=3 to ∼68% at d=6). (4) For Struct+Aux, auxiliary accuracy plummets at depth 6 (≈53%) and permutation sensitivity vanishes. (5) Training converges quickly without improvement beyond aggregate baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
Is the recursive‑majority knowledge tracing function (KT_MAJ) not computable by logspace‑uniform TC⁰ circuits?
*Basis in paper:* The abstract and Section 4.2 state that separating KT_MAJ from uniform TC⁰ is open and would require major circuit lower‑bound breakthroughs.
*Why unresolved:* Proving general TC⁰ lower bounds, especially separating NC¹ from TC⁰, is a longstanding open problem.
*What evidence would resolve it:* A proof that KT_MAJ ∉ TC⁰, or alternatively a breakthrough showing TC⁰ = NC¹.

### Open Question 2
What causes the empirical failure at depth 6 even with structural scaffolding and auxiliary supervision?
*Basis in paper:* Section 5.3 reports that for depth 6 (729 leaves), the Struct+Aux model shows no accuracy improvement over the baseline and auxiliary prediction accuracy drops to 53%, with no permutation sensitivity.
*Why unresolved:* The paper attributes this to unknown factors—optimization difficulty, insufficient model capacity, or inadequate inductive bias—under a fixed training budget and architecture.
*What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic experiments varying model scale, training duration, and architectural inductive biases (e.g., iterative processing) on depths ≥6.

### Open Question 3
Are permutation‑invariant shortcuts an inevitable learned solution for standard transformers on hierarchical KT tasks, or can they be avoided purely through training objectives without explicit intermediate supervision?
*Basis in paper:* Sections 5.2 and 5.3 show transformers converge to permutation‑invariant predictors under root‑only supervision, and that explicit structure alone does not prevent this; only auxiliary supervision on subtrees elicits sensitivity.
*Why unresolved:* The experiments use a specific transformer encoder; it is unclear whether the shortcut phenomenon is universal across model families or training schemes, or if alternative objectives could elicit hierarchical computation implicitly.
*What evidence would resolve it:* Training a diverse set of architectures (e.g., GNNs, state‑space models) on deep recursive‑majority trees using only root supervision, then measuring permutation sensitivity and accuracy to see if any achieve high performance without shortcuts.

### Open Question 4
Can iterative or recurrent mechanisms enable exact prerequisite propagation for hierarchies deeper than those successfully learned with the current scaffolded transformer (depth >4)?
*Basis in paper:* The conclusion proposes "iterative inference, recurrent/looped computation" as a design principle for depth‑adaptive computation on long prerequisite chains, motivated by the observed degradation at depth 6 even with auxiliary supervision.
*Why unresolved:* The paper only evaluates single‑pass feedforward transformers; iterative architectures remain unevaluated on this task and their potential for deeper trees is unknown.
*What evidence would resolve it:* Implementing and training iterative models (e.g., looped transformers, neural Turing machines, or state‑space models with recurrent propagation) on recursive‑majority trees of depths 5–10, measuring whether they achieve near‑perfect accuracy and maintain permutation sensitivity where the single‑pass model fails.

## Limitations
- The theoretical barrier (NC¹ vs. TC⁰ separation) is conjectural, relying on an unproven circuit lower bound.
- Scaffold design details (separator token placement, auxiliary head architecture) are underspecified, risking reproducibility.
- Results may not generalize beyond balanced ternary trees and i.i.d. Bernoulli leaf distributions.
- Depth‑6 failure with auxiliary supervision lacks a clear mechanistic explanation.

## Confidence
- ** KT_MAJ ∈ NC¹ ** : High (trivial circuit construction)
- ** Strict depth hierarchy for monotone threshold circuits ** : Medium (depends on unverified proof)
- ** Root‑only shortcut (70–80%, permutation‑invariant) ** : High (consistently matches sum baseline)
- ** Aux+struct near‑perfect at depth 3–4 ** : High (99%+ accuracy, clear permutation sensitivity)
- ** Log‑precision transformers cannot compute hierarchy exactly ** : Low (hinges on open separation problem)
