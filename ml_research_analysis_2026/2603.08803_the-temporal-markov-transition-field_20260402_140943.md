# The Temporal Markov Transition Field

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.08803
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08803
- Reference count: 5
- One-line primary result: The Temporal Markov Transition Field (TMTF) partitions a time series into K contiguous chunks, estimates a local transition matrix per chunk, and assembles a T×T image with K horizontal bands, each encoding the dynamics of its segment, thereby preserving temporal regime changes while maintaining amplitude invariance and interpretable textures for CNNs.

## Executive Summary
The Markov Transition Field (MTF) encodes time series transition dynamics in a 2D image but uses a single global matrix, which averages out temporal regime changes. The Temporal Markov Transition Field (TMTF) addresses this by partitioning the series into K contiguous chunks, estimating a separate local transition matrix for each chunk, and constructing the image so that each row uses the matrix from its chunk. This produces K horizontal bands, each encoding the dynamics of its temporal segment. TMTF is amplitude-agnostic, degrades gracefully to the global MTF when dynamics are stationary, and includes a bias-variance trade-off analysis that yields a practical guideline (T/K ≥ 5Q²+1) for choosing K. For typical series lengths T∈[200,1000] and quantile bins Q∈{6,10,14}, K=4 balances resolution and estimation reliability. A worked example demonstrates that TMTF captures regime-switching textures invisible to the global MTF, and the interpretable band textures correspond to process properties like persistence, mean reversion, and trends, making TMTF a suitable CNN input for time series characterization.

## Method Summary
Given a univariate time series x of length T, TMTF first applies quantile binning to map x to a discrete state sequence b ∈ {1,…,Q} using Q quantile bins, discarding absolute amplitudes. The time indices are then partitioned into K contiguous, equal-sized chunks C₁,…,C_K. For each chunk Cₖ, a Q×Q empirical transition matrix W⁽ᵏ⁾ is estimated from within-chunk consecutive state pairs only (transitions crossing chunk boundaries are excluded). The T×T TMTF matrix M is assembled such that for each pixel (i,j), M[i,j] = W^(chunk(i))_{b_i, b_j}. This asymmetric design uses the local matrix of the origin row's chunk while the column's state is globally defined. The resulting image has K horizontal bands (rows belonging to the same chunk share the same matrix) and is amplitude-invariant. The method includes a guideline to ensure reliable local matrix estimation: T/K ≥ 5Q²+1, implying for typical T and Q, K=4 is a robust default.

## Key Results
- Formal definition of TMTF with proven band structure (Proposition 4.1) and amplitude agnosticism (Proposition 4.3); the representation gracefully reduces to global MTF if all local matrices are identical.
- Bias-variance trade-off analysis yields a practical rule: to keep local matrix estimation reliable, the average number of transitions per state should be at least 5, leading to T/K ≥ 5Q²+1. For T∈[200,1000] and Q∈{6,10,14}, K=4 is recommended.
- A synthetic example with two distinct dynamical regimes shows TMTF producing two visually distinct bands (one with spread texture, one diagonal) while the global MTF averages them into an ambiguous texture. Band textures are interpretable: diagonal dominance indicates persistence, spread indicates mean reversion, triangular patterns indicate trends, making TMTF a meaningful CNN input.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Partitioning the time series into K contiguous chunks and estimating a separate local transition matrix per chunk preserves information about temporal regime changes that the global MTF averages away.
- **Mechanism:** By assigning each row of the image the transition probabilities from the matrix estimated only from its own temporal segment, the TMTF produces K distinct horizontal bands. Each band's texture encodes the dynamics (e.g., persistence, mean-reversion) active during that segment. A CNN can detect these band boundaries and associate them with regime transitions.
- **Core assumption:** Dynamical regimes are approximately contiguous and stationary within each chunk.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...partitions the series into K contiguous chunks, estimates a separate local transition matrix for each chunk, and assembles the image so that each row reflects the dynamics local to its chunk... The resulting T×T image has K horizontal bands of distinct texture..."
  - [section 4.2] Proposition 4.1 (Band Structure) formally proves the row-texture grouping by chunk.
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers focus on other time series representations or Markov models, not temporal segmentation for MTF.
- **Break condition:** If a true dynamical transition occurs *within* a chunk, the local matrix will average across it, and the band will not reflect the within-chunk change.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The column-asymmetric design (applying `chunk(i)` to the row index only) preserves comparability of destination states across chunks while allowing row textures to vary.
- **Mechanism:** The entry Mᵢⱼ uses the local matrix from the *origin* time i's chunk to score the transition to the *destination* state at time j. This means column j is always interpreted with the same global state bⱼ across all rows. Differences in row i and row i' values for the same column j directly reflect differences in their originating chunks' dynamics, not differences in the destination state's definition.
- **Core assumption:** The relevant dynamics for a transition are governed by the local conditions at the *origin* time step, not the destination time step.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3] "The entry Mᵢⱼ concerns the transition departing from time i. The dynamics relevant to this departure are those prevailing around time i... The column j appearing in a row from C₁ and the same column j appearing in a row from C₂ both refer to the same destination state bⱼ at the same time j."
  - [section 5] In the worked example, comparing row 1 (from chunk 1) and row 7 (from chunk 2) for column j=3 shows different values (0 vs. 0.5) due to different local matrices, even though bⱼ is the same state.
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; this is a novel design choice specific to TMTF.
- **Break condition:** If the dynamics are such that the *destination* time's local regime critically alters the interpretation of the transition probability, this asymmetric encoding may be insufficient.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The bias-variance trade-off inherent in local matrix estimation yields a practical guideline (T/K ≥ 5Q²+1) that balances the need for temporal resolution with the need for reliable transition probability estimates.
- **Mechanism:** Each local matrix W^(k) is estimated from ~T/K transitions instead of T-1. Fewer transitions increase estimation variance. The guideline ensures enough transitions per state (on average) for the empirical probabilities to be stable. This allows K to be chosen to capture non-stationarity (reducing bias) without making local estimates too noisy (controlling variance).
- **Core assumption:** The process generating transitions within a chunk can be reasonably estimated by an empirical frequency count if the number of observed transitions per state is sufficiently large (here, ~5).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.4] "The expected squared error of each entry ˆW^(k)ₗₘ is of order 1/(nₖ·ˆP(bₜ=l))... A practical minimum-transitions rule requires each local matrix to have at least 5Q transitions per row on average, implying nₖ ≥ 5Q²... For the typical range T∈[200,1000] with Q∈{6,10,14}, K=4 is a robust default."
  - [section 7] The multi-resolution extension implicitly validates that different Q values must be chosen compatibly with T and K to maintain estimation reliability.
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; the trade-off analysis is original to this paper.
- **Break condition:** The guideline fails if the true transition dynamics are highly stochastic or sparse within some quantile states, requiring more than 5 transitions per state for stable estimation. It also assumes equal-sized chunks; uneven regime lengths would violate the condition for some chunks.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Quantile Binning and State Sequence**
  - Why needed here: It is the first preprocessing step that discards absolute amplitudes and maps the raw series to a sequence of discrete states (b₁...bₜ). All subsequent MTF/TMTF construction operates on this ordinal sequence. This enables amplitude-agnosticism (Proposition 4.3).
  - Quick check question: If you apply a strictly increasing transformation (e.g., log, scaling) to the time series, what remains unchanged in the TMTF construction pipeline? (Answer: The state sequence `b`, hence all local matrices `W^(k)` and the final image `M`.)

- **Concept: Empirical Transition Matrix Estimation**
  - Why needed here: The core of both global MTF and TMTF is the estimation of a transition probability matrix from a state sequence. Understanding how row counts (occurrences of a state) and transition counts (pairs l→m) are aggregated within a defined window (whole series for global, chunk for local) is fundamental to predicting the image texture.
  - Quick check question: In a local matrix `W^(k)` for a chunk of length |Cₖ|, why does the sum of row totals equal |Cₖ| - 1, not |Cₖ|? (Answer: Because the final observation in the chunk has no *within-chunk* successor; transitions crossing a chunk boundary are excluded by Definition 4.2.)

- **Concept: Geometric Interpretation of Transition Matrices**
  - Why needed here: The "texture" of a TMTF band is a direct visual manifestation of the structure of its local matrix. Interpreting diagonal dominance, spread, triangularity, or uniformity is key to understanding what process property (persistence, mean-reversion, trend, randomness) a band represents.
  - Quick check question: Looking at a band in a TMTF image, what texture pattern would you expect for a segment generated by an AR(1) process with a strong negative coefficient (|ϕ| close to -1)? (Answer: A spread matrix with small diagonals and mass off-diagonal, likely symmetric, indicating rapid oscillation—strong mean reversion.)

## Architecture Onboarding
The pipeline flows as: Preprocessing & Binning → Segmentation → Local Matrix Estimation → Image Assembly → CNN Input.

**Critical path:** Binning → Segmentation → (Parallel) Local Matrix Estimation → Row-wise Image Lookup. The most computationally heavy step in a naive implementation is the O(T²) image assembly, but this can be optimized by precomputing the Q×Q matrices and using vectorized indexing based on `b` and `chunk` arrays.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- `K` (chunk count): Larger K increases sensitivity to short-term regime changes (lower bias) but increases variance of each `W^(k)` and reduces transitions per chunk. Governed by `T/K >= 5Q²+1`.
- `Q` (quantile bins): Larger Q captures finer transition structure but requires more data (larger T) for reliable estimation. Must be chosen compatibly with `T` and target `K`.
- Chunk boundary placement: The paper uses fixed, equal-sized contiguous chunks. This is simple but imposes a hard segmentation. A breakpoint detection pre-step could inform `K` and boundaries, but adds complexity and risk of overfitting.

**Failure signatures:**
- **No visible banding:** The image looks like a global MTF (at most Q distinct rows). This suggests either (a) the process is truly stationary, or (b) `K` is too small to resolve actual changes, or (c) `T/K` is too small so all `W^(k)` are noisy and converge to a similar average.
- **Random, high-variance texture within a band:** The local matrix `W^(k)` has rows with very few observed transitions, leading to extreme probability estimates (0 or 1). This violates the `T/K >= 5Q²` guideline.
- **Abrupt, unrealistic seam between bands:** The contrast between adjacent bands is extreme and not smooth. This could indicate that a regime transition occurred exactly at a chunk boundary, and the two adjacent chunks are modeling two different mechanisms. This is expected for a true step-change, but if the transition is gradual, fixed chunking creates an artificial discontinuity.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Synthetic Regime-Switch Validation:** Generate a long series with a known mid-point regime shift (e.g., from AR(1) with ϕ=-0.8 to AR(1) with ϕ=0.8). Compute TMTF with K=2. Verify: (a) the top band shows a spread texture (mean-reversion), (b) the bottom band shows a diagonal-heavy texture (persistence), (c) a global MTF on the same series shows an averaged, ambiguous texture.
2. **Bias-Variance Guideline Stress Test:** For fixed T=400 and Q=10, vary K from 2 to 8. For each k, compute the average Frobenius norm difference between W^(k) and the true generating matrix for that chunk (if known) or the sample variance of W^(k) entries across multiple synthetic series from the same non-stationary process. Confirm that variance explodes as K exceeds T/(5Q²) ≈ 0.8 (so K>=2 may be marginal, K=4 likely too high).
3. **Downstream Task Utility:** Train a simple CNN to classify time series as "stationary" vs. "single regime-switch" using (a) global MTF images and (b) TMTF images (K=4). Hypothesis: TMTF should yield higher classification accuracy, and Grad-CAM should highlight the band boundary regions as salient.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What are the formal theoretical relationships between the TMTF and other ordinal time series representations (e.g., multi-scale permutation entropy, multi-scale MTF, recurrence plots)? The paper explicitly suggests exploring these relationships as future work.
- **Open Question 2:** Beyond the heuristic T/K ≥ 5Q²+1, what is a principled, data-driven method for selecting the chunk count K and bin count Q for a given time series and downstream task? The paper acknowledges the guideline is a "robust default" but not necessarily optimal.
- **Open Question 3:** Does the deliberate column asymmetry (rows use local matrices, columns use global state sequence) benefit CNN learning compared to a symmetric design where both rows and columns use local matrices (e.g., block-diagonal structure)? The paper justifies asymmetry intuitively but does not compare empirically.
- **Open Question 4:** How does TMTF perform when regime changes are gradual or overlapping rather than abrupt and contiguous, as assumed by the fixed chunk partition? The method assumes sharp transitions; real-world non-stationarity may be smooth.

## Limitations
- Quantile binning edge cases: Unspecified handling when T is not divisible by Q or when ties occur, risking inconsistent state sequences.
- Zero-row handling: No strategy for local matrix rows with zero within-chunk successors (e.g., small chunks or sparse states), potentially leaving undefined probabilities.
- Hyperparameter selection: No data-driven method to choose Q and K beyond the heuristic T/K ≥ 5Q²+1; assumes equal-sized chunks and may fail for uneven regimes or highly stochastic processes.

## Confidence
- High: Band structure (Proposition 4.1) and amplitude agnosticism (Proposition 4.3) are formally proven and logically sound.
- Medium: Column-asymmetric design (rationale in Section 4.3, example in Section 5) and the bias-variance guideline (Section 4.4) are well-argued but lack external validation or empirical stress tests.
- Low: Generalizability of the 5Q²+1 rule to arbitrary real-world series, especially with non-uniform regime lengths or sparse transition patterns.
