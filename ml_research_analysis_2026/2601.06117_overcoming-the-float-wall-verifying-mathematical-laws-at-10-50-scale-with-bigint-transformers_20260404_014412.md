# Overcoming the Float Wall: Verifying Mathematical Laws at $10^{50}$ Scale with BigInt Transformers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.06117
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06117
- **Reference count**: 15
- **Primary result**: Introduced NumberNet, a BigInt-native Siamese Transformer, that successfully extrapolated the Pythagorean theorem to cosmic scales (~10^50), overcoming the IEEE 754 Float Wall at ~10^16 that caused Gradient Boosted Decision Trees to fail even with 10^10 training samples.

## Executive Summary
This work tests whether AI learns universal mathematical laws or statistical heuristics by evaluating models at numerical scales beyond the precision limits of IEEE 754 double-precision (~10^16). It identifies the "Float Wall" where float64 loses integer distinguishability, causing standard models like GBDTs to collapse when extrapolating. To overcome this, the authors develop NumberNet, a BigInt-native Siamese Transformer that processes numbers as symbolic digit sequences via LSB-first tokenization, enabling exact arbitrary-precision reasoning. Experiments show NumberNet, trained on under 10^3 samples, successfully extrapolates the Pythagorean theorem to ~10^50, while GBDTs fail regardless of data size. However, for continuous physical laws, NumberNet identifies correct causal structures but struggles with high-entropy chaotic states and fine perturbations, revealing a fundamental gap between discrete algebraic verification and continuous dynamic simulation.

## Method Summary
The methodology builds mathematically exact datasets using arbitrary-precision arithmetic (Decimal with 80–100 digits) for discrete algebraic identities (e.g., Pythagorean triples via a Stifel generator) and continuous physical laws (20 fundamental laws). Data integrity is enforced via SHA-256 hashing and triple validation to prevent float contamination. NumberNet uses a Siamese architecture: each variable is tokenized into an LSB-first digit sequence (vocabulary: 0–9, sign, PAD), processed by identical Transformer encoders, and combined via attention pooling and an MLP for classification/regression. Models are evaluated on extrapolation to cosmic scales (up to 10^50) and resilience against 98 engineered "Chaos Modes" that target specific failure modes of statistical learners. Baselines include LightGBM with engineered features and raw float64 inputs, both of which collapse at the Float Wall.

## Key Results
- GBDTs, even with 10^10 training samples, fail to generalize beyond their training range and collapse at the Float Wall (~10^16) due to inherent interpolation limits.
- NumberNet, trained on under 10^3 samples, successfully extrapolates the Pythagorean theorem to cosmic scales of ~10^50.
- For continuous physics tasks, NumberNet identifies correct causal structures but struggles with high-entropy chaotic states and fine perturbations (Chaos Modes), indicating a gap between discrete algebraic reasoning and continuous dynamic systems.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Symbolic Digit-Sequence Tokenization**  
Treating numbers as variable-length symbolic strings (digits 0–9, sign, PAD) rather than fixed-width approximate scalars makes the model's internal operations invariant to magnitude. Carry propagation and digit alignment are learned at the token level, so identical weights process `00123` and `...00123` (with leading zeros for scale) the same way. This bypasses the Float Wall where float64 loses integer distinguishability. Core assumption: Physical laws are symbolic relationships, not statistical correlations over magnitude ranges. Evidence: LSB tokenization with `vocab_size=12` and `max_digits=128`; Stifel generator produces exact integer strings. Break condition: If positional encodings scale with absolute digit position, magnitude-dependent heuristics may emerge.

**Mechanism 2: Decision-Tree Interpolation Limits**  
GBDTs construct axis-aligned splits only within training-data quantiles. With training data restricted to a bounded range (e.g., n ∈ [1, 1000]), all split thresholds lie in that interval. For test inputs at cosmic scales (e.g., n=10^50), the float64 representation collapses to the largest finite value, mapping to the same leaf as the largest training examples. No tree can extrapolate because split-finding never considers unseen magnitudes. Core assumption: Generalization to cosmic scales requires algorithmic invariance, not statistical smoothing. Evidence: Formal demonstration in `prove_float_collapse.py` showing identical float64 for c and c+1 at 10^16. Break condition: If GBDTs receive engineered symbolic features (e.g., from a verified engine), apparent extrapolation would rely on feature engineering, not model reasoning.

**Mechanism 3: Discrete vs. Continuous Reasoning Divide**  
Algebraic laws like a² + b² = c² have discrete correctness (exact equality), allowing NumberNet to learn the topological boundary between valid/invalid integer triples. Continuous dynamics involve continuous outputs and tiny perturbations cause diverging trajectories (chaos). NumberNet's quantized digit-token latent space cannot capture infinitesimal changes needed for high-entropy chaotic systems. Core assumption: There is a categorical distinction between verifying an algebraic identity and simulating a chaotic dynamical system. Evidence: Struggles on Chaos Modes Class A (Micro-Perturbations) and Class E (Non-Linear Dimensional Perversions). Break condition: Integrable (low-entropy) continuous systems might be handled within bounded domains.

## Foundational Learning
- **Arbitrary-Precision Arithmetic & Integrity Pipelines**: Needed because cosmic-scale tests require mathematically exact data; a single float64 corruption invalidates results. Integrity checks (SHA-256, triple validation, manual pauses) are non-negotiable. Quick check: If data generator silently switched from `Decimal('1e50')` to `float(1e50)` at chunk #12,345, would validation catch it before training?
- **Siamese Invariance & Variable Separate Processing**: Each variable is tokenized independently and processed through identical `NumberEncoder` weights to prevent position-specific shortcuts (e.g., "first variable is always a"). Concatenation would allow Transformer to cheat via positional features. Quick check: Why would concatenating `[m1, m2, r]` as a single sequence enable positional heuristics?
- **Hypothesis-Driven Negative Sampling (Chaos Modes)**: Random negatives teach nothing; 98 engineered Chaos Modes target specific failure modes (float truncation, operator omission, constant attrition) to separate memorization from reasoning. Quick check: Which Chaos Mode class most directly exposes a model that learned `F ≈ k * q1 * q2 / r^2` but does not understand `k` is a universal constant (Mode 31–40)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Data Generation Engine → PrecisionSafeLoader → NumberNet (NumberEncoder → Attention Pooling → Interaction MLP) → Chaos Matrix Evaluator → Zero-Bias Pipeline (PySR)

**Critical Path**: Data Generation (BigInt/Decimal + SHA-256) → Validation (triple check) → Tokenization (LSB-first) → Siamese Encoding (shared Transformer) → Interaction MLP → Loss (BCE/MSE). Integrity must run before training.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- Tokenization: LSB-first aligns carries locally; MSB-first aligns magnitude globally. Paper chose LSB for lower digit predictability.  
- Siamese vs. Concatenated: Siamese enforces equivariance but increases sequence length (3×). Concatenation is cheaper but allows positional shortcuts.  
- Chaos Mode Coverage: 98 modes comprehensive but expensive; weighted sampling (95% UltraHard/Near-Miss) focuses on hardest boundaries.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- Data-level: PrecisionSafeLoader raises INTEGRITY FAILURE or detects float contamination ('.' or 'e' in integer strings).  
- Model-level: GBDT accuracy drops to 50% at n>10^16; NumberNet fails if tokenization incorrect (e.g., MSB-first).  
- Physics-level: High failure on Chaos Modes 1–10 (micro-perturbations) and 41–50 (non-linear perversions) even if macro law learned.

**First 3 Experiments**:  
1. **Data Integrity Smoke Test**: Generate 1 chunk (100k samples) for Pythagorean triples. Run PrecisionSafeLoader manually; verify SHA-256 and no float contamination.  
2. **LSB Tokenization Sanity Check**: Tokenize 12345; LSB-first should yield ['5','4','3','2','1'] (plus BOS/EOS). Confirm 12345 and padded 0012345 map to same sequence after trimming leading zeros.  
3. **Micro-Extrapolation Probe**: Train NumberNet on n∈[1,100]. Test on n=10^6 (OOD magnitude). Should achieve ~100% accuracy if symbolic learning succeeded; if not, check for positional encoding leakage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**: Why does NumberNet fail on high-entropy chaotic states in continuous physics despite discrete algebraic success?  
*Basis in paper*: Abstract states NumberNet "struggled with high-entropy chaotic states and fine perturbations, indicating a gap between discrete algebraic reasoning and continuous dynamic systems."  
*Why unresolved*: The paper identifies the failure but does not analyze whether it is due to architectural limitations, data insufficiency, or inherent differences.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Ablation studies across all 50 Chaos Modes, especially Class E (Non-Linear Dimensional Perversions), combined with latent space visualizations for chaotic vs. stable states.

**Open Question 2**: Can NumberNet's BigInt tokenization scale to arbitrary mathematical laws and magnitudes beyond 10^50?  
*Basis in paper*: Cosmic Extrapolation Experiment tests only Pythagorean theorem up to ~10^50; claims of scale invariance are unverified for other laws or larger magnitudes.  
*Why unresolved*: Scope limited to one law and magnitude range; assumptions about universal structure preservation and positional encoding effectiveness at extreme scales are untested.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Replication for at least five diverse algebraic laws (e.g., factorial growth, exponential identities) across magnitudes from 10^50 to 10^100, with systematic evaluation of tokenization boundaries.

**Open Question 3**: Will the Zero-Bias Discovery pipeline recover known physical constants (like G) from simulated data without human-defined target functions?  
*Basis in paper*: Section 9 describes Zero-Bias Discovery as ongoing: "The research is ongoing. The GitHub repository will be made public after final submission."  
*Why unresolved*: Design is outlined but no results; unclear if classifier oracle's decision boundary encodes precise constants or if symbolic regression can extract them without overfitting.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Publication of Experiment A (Classifier Oracle) results showing PySR recovering G from Gravitation law data with error margins comparable to human-derived values, including robustness tests.

## Limitations
- Missing critical hyperparameters for NumberNet (dropout, exact LR schedule, training duration) and incomplete physics Chaos Mode implementations.
- Potential magnitude leakage via positional encodings despite LSB tokenization; the break condition is not fully guarded against.
- Active Discoverer symbolic extraction loop and Structural Phase Detector underspecified, hindering full replication of the discovery pipeline.

## Confidence
- Float Wall and GBDT interpolation failure: **High** (rooted in IEEE 754 and empirical collapse).
- NumberNet algebraic extrapolation to 10^50: **High** (clear architecture, described tests).
- Physics causal discovery vs. chaotic breakdown: **Medium** (plausible but underspecified perturbations reduce certainty).
