# Deep Learning for BioImaging: What Are We Learning?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13377
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13377
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Untrained ViTs match pretrained SOTA on RxRx3-core gene retrieval, indicating architectural biases can drive benchmark success without learned biology; structure-only models rival image encoders on tissue, and common metrics fail to distinguish shortcuts.

## Executive Summary
This work comprehensively evaluates what representation learning methods actually acquire in microscopy imaging, focusing on cell culture and tissue data. The authors introduce simple baselines—including untrained networks, pixel statistics, and structure-only cell-graph representations—to calibrate benchmark performance. Their key results show that on the RxRx3-core gene retrieval benchmark, untrained Vision Transformers achieve performance comparable to pretrained state-of-the-art models, demonstrating that architectural biases alone can solve the task without learning biological features. On the HEST-1k-1NN tissue gene prediction benchmark, structure-only models that encode only spatial organization sometimes rival full image-based encoders, highlighting spatial layout as a strong but potentially confounded signal (e.g., via cell count). Moreover, common metrics such as recall@5% and Pearson correlation coefficient often mask these limitations by rewarding any predictive signal, including technical shortcuts. The study concludes that progress demands both stronger models and diagnostic benchmarks that directly measure biological abstraction rather than aggregate performance.

## Method Summary
The evaluation encompasses three main tasks across multiple datasets. For cell-culture imaging, the RxRx3-core dataset (∼1500 genes) is used for gene-gene retrieval, and a JUMP-CP subset for compound retrieval. For tissue, the HEST-1k collection with spatial transcriptomics labels supports gene expression regression, and a 1-nearest-neighbor (1NN) split isolates spatial prediction. Image encoders include ResNet, Vision Transformer (ViT), and foundation models (H-Optimus-1, OpenPhenom, DINOv2). Frozen embeddings—from both pretrained and untrained random-initialized versions—are extracted, along with baselines: pixel statistics (mean, standard deviation, skewness), cell count, and structure-only representations derived from nuclear segmentations (CellViT) rendered as k-nearest-neighbor graphs or point clouds. Gene/compound embeddings are aggregated per target; retrieval is evaluated via cosine similarity using recall@5% or mean average precision. Regression proceeds by PCA-reducing embeddings to 256 dimensions, then training linear ridge models per gene, reporting Pearson correlation coefficients. All experiments use appropriate cross-validation (e.g., patient-stratified splits for tissue) to prevent data leakage. Additional analyses include layer-wise performance, representational dimensionality, and gene-gene similarity matrix correlations.

## Key Results
- **RxRx3-core gene retrieval:** Untrained ViTs and minimal CNNs match pretrained state-of-the-art models, indicating that architectural inductive biases can achieve high performance without learning biology.
- **HEST-1k-1NN tissue regression:** Structure-only cell-graph encoders sometimes rival full image-based encoders, suggesting spatial organization is a strong signal but potentially confounded by simpler proxies like cell count.
- **Metric limitations:** Standard metrics (recall@5%, PCC) do not differentiate biological features from shortcuts; they reward any correlation with the target, masking the need for true abstraction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Architectural Inductive Bias as a Non-Learning Shortcut
- **Claim:** On the RxRx3-core gene retrieval benchmark, untrained ViTs and simple CNN baselines achieve performance comparable to pretrained models, indicating success can arise from architectural biases rather than learned biological features.
- **Mechanism:** Vision transformers and convolutional networks possess inherent inductive biases (e.g., patch tokenization, local receptive fields) that, even with random weights, can capture low-level statistical patterns (e.g., intensity distributions, spatial correlations) present in the dataset. These patterns align with the benchmark's retrieval task due to dataset structure (e.g., batch effects, well artifacts) that is solvable without biological abstraction.
- **Core assumption:** The benchmark's gene pairs are correlated through technical or dataset-specific covariates that are accessible to architectural priors alone.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "On RxRx3-core gene retrieval, untrained ViTs match pretrained state-of-the-art models"; [section 4.1] "Unexpectedly, untrained ViTs perform comparably to pretrained ViTs and foundation models... a minimal untrained SingleConv baseline is competitive"; corpus: Neighbors discuss untrained networks' non-trivial selectivity and pitfalls in biological ML.
- **Break condition:** If the benchmark is rigorously invariant to low-level cues (e.g., via adversarial batch correction or perturbations that break technical correlations), untrained models would not compete.

### Mechanism 2: Spatial Organization as a Confounded Strong Signal
- **Claim:** On the HEST-1k-1NN tissue gene prediction, structure-only models using cell centroids sometimes rival image-based encoders, showing spatial organization is a strong but potentially confounded signal (e.g., with cell count).
- **Mechanism:** Cell-graph representations capture spatial arrangements (e.g., glandular structures, tumor infiltration) that are biologically informative. However, these arrangements may correlate with simpler proxies (e.g., cell density) that are easily extracted, allowing a model to succeed without learning nuanced morphology or texture.
- **Core assumption:** The predictive power of spatial structure is not exclusively due to high-level biological concepts but may be explained by coarse statistics like cell count or local density patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "structure-only models sometimes rival image-based encoders, highlighting spatial organization as a strong but potentially confounded signal"; [section 4.2] "On PRAD and COAD, we observe a surprisingly competitive scores between a structure-only encoder and H-Optimus-1"; [section D.3] Synthetic experiments show that both vision encoders and GNNs can classify graphs beyond cell count, but real-world tissue may still confound.
- **Break condition:** If performance drops when conditioning on cell count (e.g., via matched analysis) or when using synthetic tissues with matched organization but varied morphology, then spatial organization alone would be insufficient.

### Mechanism 3: Metrics That Fail to Distinguish Biology from Shortcuts
- **Claim:** Common metrics (recall@5%, PCC) mask limitations because they reward any predictive signal, including confounders, rather than specifically measuring biological abstraction.
- **Mechanism:** These aggregate metrics do not test for invariance to known shortcuts (e.g., batch, cell count). A model can achieve high scores by exploiting any correlation with the target, including non-biological ones, without developing representations that generalize across tasks or capture high-level semantics.
- **Core assumption:** Meaningful biological representations should be interpretable and robust to technical variation, but current benchmarks lack such diagnostics.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "common metrics (recall@5%, PCC) often mask these limitations, failing to distinguish meaningful features from shortcuts"; [section 5.2] "effective dimensionality is a useful sanity check... but it does not establish biological semantics"; corpus: Neighbors show similar issues in transcriptomics where simple baselines match foundation models.
- **Break condition:** If metrics are supplemented with invariance tests (e.g., evaluate on held-out batches, control for cell count) or with targeted probes for specific biological concepts, they would better reflect abstraction.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Data (microscopy images + labels) -> Models (image encoders: ResNet, ViT, foundation; structure-only encoders: cell-graph renderings, GNNs) & Baselines (untrained nets, pixel stats, cell count, binarized graphs) -> Tasks (gene/compound retrieval, gene regression) -> Evaluation (cosine similarity + recall@5%/mAP; PCA + ridge regression + PCC) -> Splits (cross-validation, patient-stratified)

### Critical Path
1. For each benchmark dataset, load precomputed embeddings (or extract from models using frozen weights).
2. For retrieval: compute pairwise cosine similarities between gene/compound embeddings, then metric.
3. For regression: reduce embeddings via PCA, train ridge per gene, test on held-out, compute PCC.
4. Compare models and baselines, including layer-wise analysis (intermediate stages).
5. Perform correlation analyses (e.g., gene-gene ranking similarity via Spearman) and dimensionality checks (PCA variance).

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Model diversity vs. interpretation:** Including CNNs, ViTs, and foundation models yields generalizable insights but complicates attribution of results to specific inductive biases.
- **Frozen embeddings vs. fine-tuning:** Using frozen embeddings simplifies evaluation and isolates representation quality, but may understate gains from end-to-end training.
- **Structure-only dependency:** Cell-graph baselines require accurate nuclear segmentation (CellViT), introducing potential upstream errors that could affect conclusions.
- **Dataset size vs. coverage:** Larger datasets (e.g., JUMP-CP) may contain more confounders; smaller controlled subsets may better isolate signals but limit generalizability.

### Failure Signatures
- **Untrained models match pretrained:** When untrained ViTs or CNNs achieve similar scores to pretrained ones on a task, the benchmark likely relies on architectural bias or dataset shortcuts.
- **High gene-gene ranking correlation between untrained and pretrained:** Indicates both models capture similar (possibly non-biological) signal structure.
- **Performance decreases in deeper layers for biological tasks (vs. ImageNet increase):** Suggests deeper features are not capturing relevant biology and may even discard useful cues.
- **Structure-only models compete with full-image models:** If spatial-only encoders perform comparably, investigate whether cell count or other simple statistics explain the performance.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Untrained vs. pretrained on RxRx3-core:** Compute recall@5% for gene retrieval across models and their untrained counterparts. Validate that untrained ViTs and SingleConv are competitive.
2. **Gene-gene similarity ranking alignment:** Generate gene-gene similarity matrices from different models, compute pairwise Spearman correlations, and cluster to see if untrained/pretrained ViTs correlate highly.
3. **Structure-only vs. image-based on HEST-1k-1NN:** Evaluate PCC for cell-graph (ResNet on rendered graphs) and image encoders (H-Optimus) on tissue subsets. Identify genes/datasets where structure-only excels.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** How can we design diagnostic benchmarks that directly measure biological abstraction in microscopy representation learning, rather than being solved by architectural biases or dataset shortcuts?
**Basis in paper:** The authors conclude that progress requires "more diagnostic benchmarks that measure what is actually learned" and state that common metrics "often mask these limitations."
**Why unresolved:** Current benchmarks are easily matched by untrained models and pixel statistics, indicating success does not require learning meaningful biological features.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Develop benchmarks with controlled perturbations that isolate specific biological concepts (e.g., gene function, cellular phenotypes), require generalization across experimental batches, and mandate comparison to strong baselines as part of evaluation.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** To what extent do architectural inductive biases (e.g., in ViTs) and confounders (e.g., cell count, spatial organization) explain observed benchmark performance compared to genuine biological learning?
**Basis in paper:** Results show untrained ViTs perform comparably to pretrained models on RxRx3-core gene retrieval, and structure-only models rival image encoders on HEST-1k-1NN, highlighting shortcut susceptibility.
**Why unresolved:** The study reveals these phenomena but cannot disentangle the exact contributions without causal interventions that isolate factors like spatial arrangement from cell morphology.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct experiments where confounders are systematically manipulated (e.g., shuffle cell positions, equalize cell counts) while holding biological variables constant; measure performance changes across architectures and training regimes.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** What interpretability and representation alignment methods can determine whether models rely on shortcuts or form high-level biological abstractions?
**Basis in paper:** The Future Work section invites "further interpretability work" and suggests exploring "representation alignment across in-domain, out-of-domain, and baseline models" to study abstraction formation.
**Why unresolved:** Reliance on downstream prediction metrics does not reveal internal representations or causal features; we lack tools to diagnose what concepts are encoded.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Apply representational similarity analysis, concept activation vectors, and mediation analysis to compare internal representations of models that succeed via shortcuts (untrained) versus those expected to learn biology; validate against known biological annotations and interventions.

## Limitations
- The study is restricted to a small set of benchmarks (RxRx3-core, HEST-1k); its findings may not generalize to other microscopy tasks or modalities (e.g., live-cell time-series).
- The structure-only baseline depends on accurate nuclear segmentation (CellViT), which introduces potential upstream errors that could affect the observed competitiveness.
- The evaluation uses frozen embeddings, so it is unclear whether fine-tuning could overcome architectural biases; causally disentangling confounders remains an open challenge.

## Confidence
- **Architectural bias as a non-learning shortcut (Mechanism 1):** High
- **Spatial organization as a confounded signal (Mechanism 2):** Medium
- **Metrics mask limitations (Mechanism 3):** Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Adversarial confounder removal:** Re-evaluate RxRx3-core after applying batch-effect correction (e.g., adversarial domain adaptation). If untrained model performance drops significantly, the shortcut hypothesis is strengthened.
