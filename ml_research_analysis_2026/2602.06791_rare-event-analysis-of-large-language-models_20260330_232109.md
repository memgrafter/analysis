# Rare Event Analysis of Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06791
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06791
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: LLMs produce rare events that are significant but hard to observe during development. We introduce an end-to-end rare event analysis framework: model LLMs as stochastic processes, use transition path sampling with exponential biasing to efficiently sample rare completions, and apply MBAR with bootstrap error analysis to reconstruct original probabilities. On TinyStories-8M for Automated Readability Index and Log-Probability, we achieve probability density estimates as low as 10^{-11} and 10^{-14}, respectively, far beyond direct sampling limits. Confidence intervals demonstrate reduced relative error in tails. Analysis of extreme ARI completions reveals high repetitiveness (e.g., endless token loops) despite high log-probability, showing model extrapolation to out-of-distribution regimes.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a comprehensive framework for analyzing rare events in large language models (LLMs), addressing the critical challenge of studying completions that occur with vanishingly small probabilities. The approach treats LLMs as stochastic processes and employs transition path sampling (TPS) with exponential biasing to efficiently generate rare completions, then uses multistate Bennett acceptance ratio (MBAR) with bootstrap error analysis to reconstruct unbiased probability estimates. Applied to TinyStories-8M, the method successfully estimates probability densities as low as 10^{-14} for extreme log-probability events and 10^{-11} for high Automated Readability Index completions, with confidence intervals demonstrating reduced relative error in tail regions compared to direct sampling.

## Method Summary
The framework models LLMs as stochastic processes where completions are trajectories through token space. Transition path sampling generates rare events by iteratively modifying existing completions through forward shooting (randomly modifying later tokens) or backward shooting (randomly modifying earlier tokens), then accepting or rejecting these proposals based on an exponential biasing potential applied to a continuous observable proxy (e.g., ARI or log-probability). The biasing parameters determine the target region of interest. After collecting biased samples across multiple TPS chains, MBAR reweights these samples to reconstruct the unbiased probability distribution. Bootstrap resampling provides confidence intervals for these estimates. The entire pipeline is implemented in a Python package called trea (Tools for Rare Event Analysis), supporting extensibility for different models, observables, and sampling algorithms.

## Key Results
- Achieved probability density estimates as low as 10^{-11} for high ARI completions and 10^{-14} for extreme log-probability events
- Demonstrated reduced relative error in tail probability estimates compared to direct sampling, with confidence intervals spanning several orders of magnitude
- Discovered that extreme ARI completions exhibit highly repetitive patterns (endless token loops) despite having high log-probability scores, indicating model extrapolation to out-of-distribution regimes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by combining importance sampling through biasing with reweighting via MBAR. TPS efficiently explores rare regions of completion space by focusing computational effort where it matters most, while exponential biasing ensures proposals are accepted with reasonable probability. MBAR then corrects for the bias, recovering unbiased estimates. Bootstrap resampling provides uncertainty quantification that accounts for both sampling variance and the complexity of the reweighting process. The continuous proxy observables enable smooth biasing landscapes that gradient-based proposals can navigate effectively, unlike discrete binary observables.

## Foundational Learning
**Stochastic processes in LLMs**: Why needed - Understanding LLMs as Markov chains over token sequences enables probabilistic analysis of completions; Quick check - Verify the model satisfies the Markov property in practice by testing n-gram statistics
**Transition path sampling**: Why needed - Provides a way to generate rare trajectories without knowing the exact rare region a priori; Quick check - Confirm acceptance rates remain between 10-30% for efficient sampling
**Exponential biasing**: Why needed - Creates a smooth potential landscape that guides exploration toward rare regions while maintaining reasonable acceptance probabilities; Quick check - Verify that the biasing parameter produces target event frequencies of 0.1-0.5 in biased sampling
**Multistate Bennett acceptance ratio (MBAR)**: Why needed - Enables reweighting from multiple biased distributions to reconstruct the unbiased probability distribution; Quick check - Confirm MBAR convergence by comparing free energy differences across overlapping windows
**Bootstrap resampling**: Why needed - Provides confidence intervals that account for the complex correlations introduced by TPS and MBAR; Quick check - Verify bootstrap distributions are approximately Gaussian for well-sampled regions

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: LLM model -> TPS sampler (forward/backward shooting) -> Exponential biasing -> MBAR reweighting -> Bootstrap analysis -> Probability estimates

**Critical path**: The most time-consuming steps are TPS sampling (dominated by model generations) and MBAR convergence. For estimating probabilities at 10^{-14}, generating millions of completions through TPS is unavoidable, but MBAR reduces the number of required bias levels compared to histogram methods.

**Design tradeoffs**: The framework trades computational cost for statistical accuracy. Direct sampling would require astronomically many samples to observe events at 10^{-14} probability, while TPS+MBAR achieves this with orders of magnitude fewer samples by focusing on relevant regions. The tradeoff is increased algorithmic complexity and dependence on the quality of the proxy observable.

**Failure signatures**: Poor proxy observables lead to MBAR bias and unreliable estimates. Low acceptance rates (>90% rejections) indicate the biasing is too aggressive or the observable landscape is too rugged. MBAR convergence warnings suggest insufficient overlap between biased distributions or inadequate sampling in critical regions.

**First experiments**:
1. Run TPS with exponential biasing on a simple observable (e.g., completion length) to verify the basic pipeline works
2. Compare direct sampling confidence intervals with TPS+MBAR intervals for moderately rare events (10^{-3} probability) to validate the framework
3. Test different biasing parameters on a known distribution to verify MBAR correctly recovers unbiased probabilities

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How effectively does parallel tempering improve convergence and mixing for transition path sampling (TPS) of rare events in LLMs compared to basic annealing?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The conclusion lists parallel tempering (replica exchange) as a potential algorithmic improvement, and Appendix C.7 describes its mechanism for preventing chains from getting stuck.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not implement or evaluate parallel tempering; its benefits and trade-offs for LLMs remain empirically unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Run TPS with and without parallel tempering on TinyStories for the ARI and Log-Probability observables, comparing autocorrelation lengths, effective sample sizes, confidence interval widths, and total computation time.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can using an infilling LLM as the proposal distribution in TPS reduce autocorrelation and increase acceptance rates, especially for modifying early tokens in long completions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Appendix C.6.4 identifies forward shooting's weakness for early-token changes and suggests infilling models as a remedy.
- Why unresolved: The current work employs only forward shooting; the proposed infilling approach is untested and its impact on exploration efficiency is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement TPS with an infilling model (e.g., a decoder-only model trained for fill-in-the-middle) as the proposal and measure changes in acceptance probability, effective sample size, and CI half-widths relative to standard TPS for identical biases and run lengths.

### Open Question 3
- Question: For a binary or sparsely observed target property (e.g., "unsafe" completions), how accurately can a continuous proxy observable reconstruct the true rare event probabilities via MBAR?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Section C.4 notes that biasing directly on a binary observable is ineffective and recommends continuous proxies, but does not quantify the accuracy of resulting estimates or provide guidance on proxy selection.
- Why unresolved: The relationship between proxy quality and final probability error is not established; it is unclear when proxy-based REA yields unbiased estimates.
- What evidence would resolve it: Choose a binary target property and several candidate continuous proxies with varying correlation strengths. For each proxy, perform REA (TPS+MBAR) on a model where the true binary probability can also be measured by exhaustive direct sampling for validation. Compare the estimated binary probabilities and confidence intervals to ground truth across different proxy strengths.

### Open Question 4
- Question: To what extent can the few-prompt rare event probability estimates of this paper be reliably extrapolated across a diverse set of user prompts as suggested by Jones et al. (2025)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The outlook states that extrapolating from a few test prompts to a deployment setting is promising and that "such extrapolation could be combined with the methods presented here," but the combination remains unexplored.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not incorporate the Jones et al. methodology; the accuracy and limits of such an extrapolation under prompt variability are unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: For a set of prompts spanning a range of typicality, obtain REA-based rare event estimates on a small, random subset. Apply the Jones et al. extrapolation technique to predict estimates for the full set, then compare against expensive REA estimates for the full set. Quantify errors and identify factors (prompt distribution, observable) that affect extrapolation accuracy.

## Limitations
- Focuses exclusively on single-metric conditioning rather than compositional or multi-objective rare events
- Validation limited to TinyStories-8M dataset, leaving uncertainty about scalability to larger models
- Extreme event analysis relies on qualitative visual inspection of repetitive patterns rather than quantitative metrics

## Confidence
- High: Core methodology validity for single-metric rare event analysis on tested dataset (supported by successful probability density estimation down to 10^{-14})
- Medium: Framework's general applicability across different models and observables (limited by single dataset and lack of compositional analysis)
- Low: Interpretability of extreme event patterns (observations remain qualitative without systematic characterization)
