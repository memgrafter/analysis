# Curriculum Learning for Efficient Chain-of-Thought Distillation via Structure-Aware Masking and GRPO

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.17686  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17686  
- **Reference count**: 5  
- **Primary result**: BRIDGE improves GSM8K accuracy by 11.29% (76.19% vs 64.90%) and reduces output length by 27.4% (167 vs. 230 tokens) for Qwen2.5-3B-Base.

## Executive Summary
Distilling chain-of-thought reasoning from large teacher models into compact students is hindered by the student's limited capacity to process verbose teacher outputs. The BRIDGE framework tackles this with a three-stage curriculum: structure-aware warmup via masked shuffled reconstruction, GRPO-based compression using hierarchical rewards, and teacher-guided internalization for hard cases. Evaluated on GSM8K with Qwen2.5-3B-Base, BRIDGE achieves 76.19% accuracy—an 11.29% improvement over standard distillation—while cutting output length by 27.4%. It outperforms both standard and RL-based distillation methods and preserves explicit, verifiable reasoning traces.

## Method Summary
The BRIDGE framework introduces a three-stage curriculum to distil chain-of-thought reasoning from large teachers to compact students. The first stage, structure-aware warmup, uses masked shuffled reconstruction: given a teacher’s reasoning steps, a subset of tokens is masked and the remaining steps are shuffled; the student learns to reconstruct the original order and fill masks, thereby capturing the logical skeleton without verbatim imitation. The second stage employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a reinforcement learning algorithm, with a hierarchical reward that combines reasoning quality (e.g., correctness of intermediate steps) and conciseness. This encourages the student to produce accurate yet shorter reasoning traces. The final stage, teacher-guided internalization, identifies samples where the student's confidence is low; for these, the teacher provides explicit reasoning, which the student internalizes via supervised fine-tuning, preventing error accumulation on hard cases.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy**: 76.19% on GSM8K, compared to 64.90% for standard distillation (11.29% relative gain).  
- **Efficiency**: Reasoning output length reduced by 27.4% (167 vs. 230 tokens).  
- **Overall superiority**: Outperforms both standard distillation and RL-based methods while maintaining explicit, verifiable reasoning traces.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The curriculum addresses the capacity mismatch by progressively shifting the student’s learning objective from structure acquisition to compression and finally to robustness on hard cases. Masked shuffled reconstruction forces the student to infer logical dependencies among reasoning steps, establishing a scaffold that later stages can prune without losing coherence. GRPO with hierarchical rewards then balances accuracy and brevity: the reasoning quality reward ensures mathematical correctness, while the conciseness reward discourages verbatim overload. Teacher-guided internalization acts as a safety net for samples where the student’s confidence is low, injecting high-quality reasoning to prevent reinforcement of incorrect shortcuts. Together, these stages yield a student that produces concise, accurate, and interpretable chain-of-thought.

## Foundational Learning
- **Curriculum Learning**: Training proceeds in stages of increasing difficulty to align with the student's capacity. *Why needed*: Prevents overwhelming the small student with full teacher outputs early on. *Quick check*: Does the training schedule gradually increase reasoning complexity?  
- **Masked Shuffled Reconstruction**: A warmup task where the student reconstructs a masked, shuffled version of the teacher's reasoning steps. *Why needed*: Teaches the logical structure without burdening the student with verbatim copying. *Quick check*: Is the reasoning skeleton preserved after reconstruction?  
- **Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (GRPO)**: Uses Group Relative Policy Optimization with a reward composed of multiple objectives (reasoning quality, conciseness). *Why needed*: Balances accuracy and brevity, addressing capacity mismatch. *Quick check*: Is the reward function decomposable into explicit sub-rewards?  
- **Teacher-guided Internalization**: For samples where student confidence is low, the teacher provides explicit reasoning for supervised fine-tuning. *Why needed*: Prevents error propagation on hard cases. *Quick check*: Are hard cases identified reliably before internalization?  

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Teacher Model -> Warmup Module (masked shuffling) -> Student Model -> GRPO Module (hierarchical reward) -> Student Model -> Teacher-guided Internalization -> Final Student Model

**Critical Path**:
1. Generate teacher chain-of-thought for training data.
2. Apply masking and shuffling to create warmup inputs; train student via reconstruction loss.
3. Initialize GRPO: student generates reasoning, reward model computes hierarchical score.
4. Update student policy via GRPO.
5. Periodically evaluate student confidence; collect low-confidence samples.
6. Teacher provides full reasoning for collected samples; perform supervised fine-tuning.
7. Iterate steps 3–6 until convergence.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- Warmup reconstruction is cheap but may not teach conciseness; trade-off with RL cost.
- Hierarchical reward adds interpretability but requires weight tuning; oversimplification may cause reward hacking.
- Teacher internalization ensures correctness on hard cases but increases teacher calls and may overfit to teacher style.
- Curriculum duration: longer warmup improves skeleton learning but delays compression training.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Student output becomes degenerate (e.g., ultra‑short but incorrect) → reward mis‑specification.
- Warmup fails if mask ratio too high → poor structure acquisition.
- Too many hard cases trigger internalization → high teacher dependency and poor generalization.
- No improvement after GRPO → reward model misalignment or insufficient exploration.

**First Experiments**:
1. Baseline comparison: Standard knowledge distillation (student trained on full teacher outputs without masking or RL).
2. Warmup ablation: Train student with only the warmup stage (no GRPO/internalization) to isolate its contribution.
3. Reward sensitivity: Vary weighting of reasoning quality vs. conciseness in the hierarchical reward (e.g., λ_acc ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0}) and measure trade‑offs in accuracy and output length.

## Limitations
- Evaluation confined to GSM8K with Qwen2.5-3B-Base; other datasets and model scales remain untested.  
- Results lack statistical significance (no variance reported) and training compute cost is not measured.  
- The definition of “hard cases” for teacher internalization is vague, raising concerns about transferability.

## Confidence
- Accuracy & length reduction on GSM8K: **Medium**  
- Superiority over standard/RL distillation: **Medium**  
- Maintenance of explicit, verifiable reasoning: **Low**
