# MARS: Harmonizing Multimodal Convergence via Adaptive Rank Search

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00720
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00720
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MARS automates LoRA rank search for multimodal LLM fine-tuning, improving accuracy by up to 12.0% and reducing perplexity by up to 13.2% while cutting total search and fine-tuning time by 11.5×.

## Executive Summary
MARS addresses the critical problem of imbalanced training dynamics in multimodal LLM fine-tuning, where the vision encoder and language model components converge at different rates. By automatically searching for optimal modality-specific LoRA ranks, MARS harmonizes convergence using dual scaling laws. The first law predicts convergence time to prune rank pairs with misaligned dynamics, and the second predicts final task performance to select the best pair. Evaluated on LLaVA-OneVision and Qwen2.5-VL, MARS achieves significant gains on ScienceQA and LLaVA Bench benchmarks while drastically reducing the computational cost of rank search compared to naive grid search.

## Method Summary
MARS operates in two phases. In the calibration phase, lightweight fine-tuning runs are executed for a small set of anchor LoRA rank pairs on a data subset. Metrics (convergence steps, perplexity) are collected at intermediate dataset sizes, and two scaling laws are fitted: Law-C (convergence time as a function of rank and data size) and Law-P (final perplexity as a function of rank and data size). In the guided search phase, Law-C is used to compute a vision encoder rank that aligns convergence for each candidate LLM rank, pruning the 2D search space to a 1D scan. Law-P then predicts performance for these aligned pairs, selecting the one with minimal predicted perplexity. A full fine-tuning run using the selected ranks completes the process.

## Key Results
- Up to 12.0% absolute accuracy improvement on ScienceQA over baselines.
- Up to 13.2% reduction in perplexity on LLaVA Bench.
- 11.5× reduction in total search and fine-tuning time compared to naive grid search.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Modulating LoRA rank per modality directly controls module-specific convergence speed, which heuristic learning rate tuning cannot reliably achieve.
- **Mechanism:** Higher LoRA rank increases adaptation capacity, reducing convergence time (t ∝ r^{-γ}). By independently setting ranks for vision encoder (VE) and LLM, MARS compensates for their disparity in pre-trained knowledge and scale, aligning t_ve ≈ t_llm to prevent overfitting in the faster module.
- **Core assumption:** The relationship between LoRA rank and convergence time follows a smooth power law (Scaling Law-C) across MLLM architectures and dataset scales.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "re-purposing the LoRA rank as a controller for modality-specific convergence speed"; [section 3.2.3] Figure 2b shows inverse relationship; [corpus] Weak neighbor relevance on multimodal convergence.
- **Break condition:** Scaling relationship breaks if rank disparity is extreme or module's pre-trained knowledge is already highly aligned with the task.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** MARS's search space pruning via Scaling Law-C is efficient because the convergence dynamics landscape is monotonic and locally smooth, enabling accurate interpolation from few calibration points.
- **Mechanism:** After fitting t_i(r_i, D_f) for VE and LLM from calibration, MARS solves the balance condition t_ve(r_ve, D_f) ≈ t_llm(r_llm, D_f) for r_ve given r_llm. This reduces the combinatorial search from 2D to a linear scan.
- **Core assumption:** Convergence time as a function of rank and dataset size is monotonic and smooth within the search interval (e.g., {8,16,32,64}), with no pathological discontinuities.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] "training dynamics are smooth and order-preserving"; [section 3.2.3 & appendix F] Figures 2b,9,10 show parallel log-log lines.
- **Break condition:** Landscape becomes non-monotonic or has sharp thresholds for certain architecture-task combinations.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Within the convergence-aligned subset, predicted perplexity (L̂) strongly correlates with final task accuracy, making Scaling Law-P effective for final selection.
- **Mechanism:** Scaling Law-P (L̂ ∝ (r_ve)^{-α_m} (r_llm)^{-α_l} D_f^{-β}) is fitted on calibration data. For aligned pairs, minimizing L̂ maximizes chance of high accuracy, as low perplexity indicates effective knowledge transfer.
- **Core assumption:** For convergence-balanced configurations, perplexity is a reliable monotonic proxy for downstream task accuracy.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.4] Figure 5 shows Pearson/Spearman correlation of -0.97 between LLaVA Bench perplexity and ScienceQA accuracy.
- **Break condition:** Perplexity-accuracy correlation fails for specialized tasks (e.g., dense captioning) or if subtle harmful interference persists in pruned set.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**
  - *Why needed here:* MARS adjusts LoRA rank per modality; understanding that LoRA injects trainable matrices ΔW = BA with rank r is essential to grasp how rank controls capacity.
  - *Quick check question:* If you double the LoRA rank for a module, by what factor does its number of additional trainable parameters increase (assuming a square weight matrix)?

- **Concept: Scaling Laws for Fine-Tuning**
  - *Why needed here:* MARS extends scaling laws from predicting final loss to predicting convergence time; understanding standard form L ~ N^{-α} D^{-β} and how model size N is replaced by rank r in PEFT is crucial.
  - *Quick check question:* According to Scaling Law-P (Eq 5), what happens to predicted perplexity if you quadruple the dataset size D_f?

- **Concept: Imbalanced Training Dynamics in MLLMs**
  - *Why needed here:* This is the problem MARS solves; recognizing that when VE and LLM LoRA adapters converge at different rates, the faster one overfits while the slower under-adapts.
  - *Quick check question:* Describe one empirical symptom (from Figure 1) of when the LLM fine-tunes much slower than the Vision Encoder.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Pretrained Backbone (frozen VE & LLM) -> Projector (MLP with r_ve) -> LoRA Adapters (VE & LLM with r_ve, r_llm) -> Calibration Data Collector -> Dual Law Fitter -> Search Pruner -> Performance Selector -> Final Fine-Tuning.
- **Critical path:** Calibration (collect data) → Fit Scaling Laws → Prune Search Space (solve Eq 3 for all r_llm) → Predict Perplexity (Eq 5) for candidates → Select best → Full Fine-Tuning.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Larger R_options increases optimal pair chance but adds linear scan cost; paper uses {8,16,32,64}.
  - Calibration range up to 2^11 steps sufficient; larger ranges have diminishing returns.
  - Adding more modalities changes to N-dimensional search; MARS anchors one modality and solves N-1 equations.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Complex-valued rank computation from Eq 3 (0.72% of runs); fallback: set r_ve = r_llm.
  - Poor scaling law fit (low R²); remedy: expand calibration ranks.
  - Convergence gap persists after selection; remedy: rerun calibration with more steps or adjust R_options.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate Scaling Law-C validation: run fine-tuning for rank pairs (r_ve, r_llm) ∈ {4,8,16}×{8,16,32,64} on ScienceQA subset; plot convergence time vs D_f log-log.
  2. Test MARS search on LLaVA-OV-7B with LLaVA Bench; compare against grid search for same R_options; verify MARS finds lower perplexity in less time.
  3. Validate convergence-performance link: compute convergence gap |T_ve - T_llm| and final perplexity for 5 random rank pairs; expect strong positive correlation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** How do domain sensitivity across pre-training and fine-tuning tasks and modality-specific learning capacity disparities influence training dynamics and system-level efficiency in multimodal LLM fine-tuning?
  - *Basis in paper:* Explicit future work in Conclusion.
  - *Why unresolved:* Paper does not analyze underlying factors causing capacity and domain disparities.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Experiments measuring domain gap metrics and capacity metrics, correlating with convergence alignment and final performance.

- **Question:** Are the proposed dual scaling laws universally applicable to other multimodal architectures (e.g., audio-vision, video-language) and non-LLM backbones?
  - *Basis in paper:* Inferred; experiments only on vision-language models.
  - *Why unresolved:* Scaling laws are task/architecture-dependent; no validation beyond tested models.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic validation across diverse multimodal models with varied fusion mechanisms, checking fit quality and MARS effectiveness.

- **Question:** Does perplexity reliably serve as a universal proxy for downstream task accuracy across all multimodal benchmarks?
  - *Basis in paper:* Inferred; correlation only on LLaVA Bench ↔ ScienceQA.
  - *Why unresolved:* Perplexity measures language loss; multimodal tasks may involve perceptual reasoning where correlation may be weaker.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Compute perplexity and accuracy across wide array of benchmarks (e.g., MME, POPE, MMStar, GQA) and report correlation statistics.

- **Question:** How robust is MARS to the choice of calibration data range and number of intermediate checkpoints?
  - *Basis in paper:* Inferred; ablation on 2^11 samples but not on distribution or checkpoint count.
  - *Why unresolved:* Calibration relies on few observations; poor choice may lead to suboptimal ranks; robustness unknown.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Vary calibration data range and checkpoint number, measure variance in selected rank pair and final performance across seeds.

## Limitations
- Unclear calibration details: method for constructing effective dataset size subsets (sequential vs. random) is unspecified, possibly affecting scaling law stability.
- Convergence time ambiguity: Whether T is measured in steps or epochs, exact early-stopping configuration, and validation metric are unknown; fitted Law-C coefficients not reported.
- Smooth-landscape assumption: MARS assumes t(r, D) is globally monotonic and separable; this may break for extreme ranks or pre-aligned modules, causing rounding errors or poor interpolation.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (rank controls convergence): Medium-High
- Mechanism 2 (pruning via smooth dynamics): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (perplexity predicts accuracy): Medium-High
