# A Counterfactual Approach for Addressing Individual User Unfairness in Collaborative Recommender System

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.13253  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13253  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** The proposed dual‑stage counterfactual method yields a large improvement in NDCG@20 for both overall users and identified under‑served candidates, outperforming four state‑of‑the‑art fairness baselines on three datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles individual unfairness in collaborative filtering, where users with sparse interactions receive lower‑quality recommendations. The authors introduce a two‑stage counterfactual framework. First, candidate under‑served users are identified by temporarily imputing missing items and measuring the potential performance gain (PGain). Second, for users with positive individual or global PGain, the imputed items are permanently added to the training data, and the model is retrained. Experiments with LightGCN and DualVAE on MovieLens‑100K, Amazon Beauty, and MovieLens‑1M show substantial gains over fairness baselines. Ablation studies confirm the counterfactual stage reduces spurious candidates and improves PGain.

## Method Summary
The method comprises a detection phase and an intervention phase. In detection, each user’s unseen items are imputed using a heuristic (e.g., random or based on popularity) to create a temporary augmented profile. The model is evaluated on this augmented data, and PGain is computed as the difference in recommendation quality (e.g., NDCG@20) compared to the original profile. Users with a PGain above a threshold are marked as candidates. In intervention, the imputed items for selected users are permanently added to the training set, and the recommender model is retrained from scratch. This retrained model then serves all users. The approach is model‑agnostic; the authors test it with LightGCN and DualVAE.

## Key Results
- On MovieLens‑100K, the proposed method achieves NDCG@20 = 0.2139 vs. the best baseline (Antidote) at 0.1022 for all users.
- For identified candidate users on the same dataset, NDCG@20 = 0.2072 vs. 0.1102 for the best baseline.
- Ablation experiments show that removing the counterfactual candidate‑filtering stage increases spurious candidates and reduces average PGain, validating the two‑stage design.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Counterfactual reasoning allows the system to estimate what would happen if a sparse user had interacted with additional items, without permanently altering their profile during identification. PGain quantifies the marginal benefit of such an intervention. Users with high PGain are those who are genuinely under‑served by the current model. By permanently adding only those high‑gain items and retraining, the model adapts its parameterization to better capture these users’ preferences while minimizing distortions to other users. The two‑stage separation prevents spurious candidates (e.g., users who would not benefit long‑term) and focuses resources on the most promising interventions.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Raw user‑item interactions → Imputation module → Temporary augmented profiles → Evaluation engine (computes PGain) → Candidate selector (thresholding) → Permanent data updater → Retraining pipeline → Updated recommendation model  

**Critical path:**  
1. Impute unseen items for all users.  
2. Evaluate model on augmented data, compute PGain.  
3. Select candidates with PGain > τ.  
4. Add imputed items for selected users to the training set.  
5. Retrain the model from scratch.  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Imputation fidelity vs. computational cost: More sophisticated imputation (e.g., MF) is accurate but slower.  
- PGain threshold τ: High τ yields fewer interventions but may miss some under‑served users; low τ risks adding spurious items.  
- Retraining frequency: Full retraining guarantees coherence but is expensive; incremental updates would be faster but may introduce drift.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Spike in number of spurious candidates (low precision) indicating poor PGain estimation.  
- Decline in overall accuracy (e.g., NDCG for non‑candidates) after retraining, suggesting unfair burden shifting.  
- High variance in PGain across different random seeds, pointing to unstable imputation.

**First three experiments:**  
1. Replicate the main result on MovieLens‑100K using LightGCN, verifying the reported NDCG@20 gain.  
2. Ablate the imputation strategy: replace the default with matrix factorization and k‑NN imputation, compare candidate quality and final performance.  
3. Measure the impact of interventions on users with already rich interaction histories to detect any degradation (fairness‑accuracy trade‑off).

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to three public datasets; may not generalize to large‑scale industrial settings.  
- No statistical significance testing; reported improvements might be due to chance.  
- Imputation strategy is heuristic and could introduce selection bias, affecting PGain reliability.  
- Potential negative externalities (e.g., fairness‑accuracy trade‑offs, impact on popular users) are not examined.  
- Reproduction details are insufficient for independent verification.

## Confidence
- **Effectiveness:** Medium – strong empirical gains but limited dataset diversity and lack of significance tests.  
- **Methodology:** Medium – ablation supports design, yet spurious candidate issue remains partly unaddressed.  
- **Generalizability:** Low – tested on only two models and three datasets; broader applicability unproven.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the experiments on additional diverse datasets (e.g., Yelp, Steam) and include newer fairness baselines from the past two years.  
2. Perform rigorous statistical significance testing (paired t‑tests, bootstrap confidence intervals) for all reported metrics.  
3. Conduct a full ablation of the imputation component, comparing multiple strategies (MF, k‑NN, random) and measuring sensitivity of candidate identification and final performance.
