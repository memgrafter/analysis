# SiameseNorm: Breaking the Barrier to Reconciling Pre/Post-Norm
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08064
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08064
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** SiameseNorm enables stable training at learning rates up to 2e-3 where Post-Norm and HybridNorm diverge, achieving test perplexity 10.48 (0.41 absolute gain over Pre-Norm) and arithmetic accuracy 39.6% (41% relative improvement).

## Executive Summary
Pre-Norm (stable but limited depth) and Post-Norm (expressive but unstable) represent a fundamental trade-off in Transformer normalization. SiameseNorm resolves this by introducing a two-stream residual architecture that decouples the optimization dynamics of stability and expressivity. Each layer maintains a Pre-Norm-like stream that preserves an identity gradient path and a Post-Norm-like stream that enforces bounded representations, while both streams share the same residual block parameters. Pre-training 1.3B models on 100B tokens shows SiameseNorm converges at aggressive learning rates (up to 2e-3), where standard Post-Norm and HybridNorm fail. The method yields state-of-the-art results, notably reducing perplexity to 10.48 and boosting arithmetic accuracy to 39.6%, demonstrating the viability of reconciling the two normalization paradigms.

## Method Summary
SiameseNorm modifies the standard Transformer block by duplicating the input into two streams: Y (Pre-Norm-like) and X (HybridNorm-like). At each layer, Y is normalized and then fused with the raw X. The fused representation is normalized again before being processed by a shared, norm-free residual block (F_i) consisting of Attention with QK-Norm and a SwiGLU MLP. The block’s output O_i is split: added directly to Y (no normalization), and added to X after scaling by a depth-dependent factor α_l = 1/√(l+1) and then normalized (RMSNorm). The final output combines X_N with a normalized Y_N. Key design choices are: depth-wise scaling to balance stream contributions as depth increases, and normalizing the fused input to stabilize the shared block. The architecture roughly doubles activation memory but incurs minimal compute overhead beyond extra normalization layers. Training uses AdamW with standard hyperparameters (e.g., weight decay 0.1, gradient clipping 1.0) and a cosine learning rate schedule with warmup.

## Key Results
- Converges at learning rates up to 2e-3, whereas Post-Norm and HybridNorm diverge under the same conditions.
- At LR=2e-3, achieves test perplexity 10.48 vs. Pre-Norm’s 10.89 (0.41 absolute improvement).
- Arithmetic accuracy reaches 39.6%, a 41% relative gain over Pre-Norm’s 28.1%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SiameseNorm stabilizes training while preserving expressivity through two coupled mechanisms.

### Mechanism 1: Decoupled gradient dynamics via two streams
- **Claim:** The architecture preserves an identity gradient path in one stream while bounding representations in the other, thereby reconciling Pre-Norm stability with Post-Norm expressivity.
- **Mechanism:** The Y-stream (Pre-Norm-like) accumulates unnormalized updates, creating a direct gradient highway via skip connections. The X-stream (Post-Norm-like) applies normalization after each residual addition, keeping activations bounded. The Jacobian of the combined block contains an explicit identity term `I` in the bottom-right block (Y-stream) to prevent vanishing gradients, while the top-left block (X-stream) inherits the modulation from normalization that controls scale.
- **Core assumption:** Single-stream designs cannot simultaneously maintain a clean identity gradient pathway and enforce bounded representations due to structural constraints.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3 highlight the decoupling; the Jacobian analysis (Eq. 8) shows the identity term; gradient norm measurements (Figure 5) confirm stable optimization.
- **Break condition:** If the shared block’s Jacobian becomes severely ill-conditioned, both streams may suffer, though the identity path in Y mitigates this.

### Mechanism 2: Normalized input fusion
- **Claim:** Applying normalization to the fused representation before feeding it into the shared residual block is essential for stable optimization.
- **Mechanism:** After computing Z = γ_l * X_i + Y_i' (where Y_i' = RMSNorm(Y_i)), an additional LayerNorm (LN_fused) normalizes Z before it enters the block F_i. This stabilizes the input distribution for subsequent computations, preventing harmful shifts in activation scales.
- **Core assumption:** Even though the two sub-streams are individually normalized, their sum can have a distribution that violates normalization assumptions; re-normalization aligns it with the expected scale.
- **Evidence anchors:** Ablation in Section 4.4 (Table 3 Row 5 vs. 6) shows performance degradation (PPL 10.51 → 10.43) when LN_fused is removed; unstable loss curves observed.
- **Break condition:** Skipping LN_fused leads to divergence, especially at higher learning rates.

### Mechanism 3: Depth-wise scaling
- **Claim:** Scaling the residual update fed into the bounded X-stream by α_l = 1/√(l+1) rebalances stream contributions as depth increases.
- **Mechanism:** Without scaling, the magnitude mismatch between the growing Y-stream (unbounded) and the regulated X-stream becomes more pronounced with depth, either making the update too small to affect Y or too large for X. The depth-dependent factor compensates, maintaining balanced gradient flow.
- **Core assumption:** A fixed scaling would not adapt to the increasing separation in norms; the 1/√(l+1) schedule empirically matches the norm growth of a Pre-Norm stream.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.4 and Table 3 (Row 4 vs. 6) demonstrate improved perplexity (10.51 → 10.43) when depth-wise scaling is used; removal harms convergence in deeper models.
- **Break condition:** Omitting scaling degrades performance and can cause instability in deep networks.

## Foundational Learning
Understanding the following concepts is crucial for reasoning about SiameseNorm.

### Residual connection gradient flow
- **Why needed here:** Skip connections provide an identity mapping that preserves gradient pathways; this is central to the stability of Pre-Norm and the proposed decoupling.
- **Quick check question:** How does the Jacobian of a Pre-Norm block contain an explicit identity term `I`, and why does this help gradient propagation?

### Layer Normalization (LN) Jacobian
- **Why needed here:** The instability of Post-Norm arises from repeated multiplication by the LN Jacobian, which can have sensitive spectral properties.
- **Quick check question:** Why does applying normalization after residual addition introduce a multiplicative factor that can cause gradient explosion or vanishing?

### Effective depth vs. nominal depth
- **Why needed here:** Pre-Norm’s unbounded magnitude growth is said to dilute deeper layers’ contributions, reducing effective depth; SiameseNorm aims to restore full depth utilization.
- **Quick check question:** What is the “dilution problem” in Pre-Norm, and how does bounded representation in Post-Norm attempt to solve it?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Input -> Duplicate into X_0 and Y_0 -> [For each layer l: Y'_l = RMSNorm(Y_l); Z = γ_l * X_l + Y'_l; Z_n = RMSNorm(Z); O_l = F_l(Z_n); X_{l+1} = RMSNorm(X_l + α_l * O_l); Y_{l+1} = Y_l + O_l] -> Final: X_N + RMSNorm(Y_N)

### Critical path
1. Input is duplicated into two streams.
2. Y-stream is normalized (Y'_l) before fusion.
3. Fused representation (γ_l * X_l + Y'_l) is normalized again (Z_n).
4. Shared block F_l processes Z_n to produce O_l.
5. O_l is added directly to Y (unnormalized) and added to X after depth-wise scaling and normalization.
6. After the last layer, the outputs are combined: X_N plus normalized Y_N.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Stability vs. expressivity:** Y-stream provides a stable gradient highway; X-stream enforces bounded representations that improve depth efficiency.
- **Compute overhead:** Slightly increased due to extra normalization ops; memory roughly doubles because two hidden states are stored per layer.
- **Initialization:** All RMSNorm scale parameters and γ_l are initialized to 1.0 to ensure equal initial contributions and test intrinsic stability.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Depth-wise scaling factor α_l and learning rate must be tuned; aggressive LRs (2e-3) are possible with SiameseNorm but may break baselines.

### Failure signatures
- **Divergence at high LR:** Missing depth-wise scaling or normalized input fusion, or incorrect initialization of γ/RMSNorm.
- **Gradient norm spikes (>10):** Indicates unstable training; check scaling and normalization placements.
- **Low arithmetic accuracy (<30%):** Could stem from insufficient training tokens, wrong γ placement, or missing depth-wise scaling.
- **Stream dominance collapse:** Monitor per-layer RMSNorm scales; if one stream’s norms vanish, the network may rely on a single stream.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Gradient norm monitoring under aggressive LR:** Compare SiameseNorm with HybridNorm/Pre-Norm at LR=2e-3; expect stable gradient norms (<0.5 after warmup) for SiameseNorm vs. spikes (>100) for unstable baselines.
2. **Ablation of depth-wise scaling:** Train with and without α_l = 1/√(l+1) at LR=1e-3 and 2e-3; removal should cause divergence or significantly higher perplexity (as in Table 3 Row 4 vs. 6).
3. **Stream contribution analysis:** Apply Logit Lens to final hidden states of X- and Y-streams; expect the X-stream (Post-Norm-like) to dominate final predictions, confirming that expressivity from bounded representations is realized.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Massive activations:** SiameseNorm exhibits more significant “massive activations” compared to baselines. The root causes and mitigation strategies remain unexplored.
2. **Task-specific performance variability:** Gains are large on arithmetic (41% relative improvement) but smaller on other downstream tasks. Why this variation occurs and how to make gains consistent is unknown.
3. **Theoretical foundations:** A formal theoretical framework for two-stream decoupling is lacking; developing such principles could guide future multi-stream residual designs.

## Limitations
- Implementation ambiguities (exact HybridNorm coupling, γ scaling placement and shape, final fusion details) hinder perfect reproduction.
- Scalability has only been demonstrated for 1.3B models trained on 100B tokens; behavior on larger scales is untested.
- The Jacobian-based analysis relies on idealized assumptions; real training dynamics may deviate.
- Memory overhead almost doubles activation storage, potentially limiting batch size.
- Baselines may not be optimally tuned, affecting fairness of comparisons.

## Confidence
- Decoupled stability via two streams: **Medium**
- Normalized input fusion essential: **High**
- Depth-wise scaling rebalances contributions: **High**
- State-of-the-art results on 1.3B model: **Medium**
