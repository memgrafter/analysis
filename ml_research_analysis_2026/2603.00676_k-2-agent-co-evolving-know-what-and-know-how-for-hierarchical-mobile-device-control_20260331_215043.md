# K^2-Agent: Co-Evolving Know-What and Know-How for Hierarchical Mobile Device Control

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.00676
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00676
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Achieves 76.1% success on AndroidWorld using raw screenshots and open‑source backbones, significantly outperforming baselines.

## Executive Summary
K^2-Agent introduces a hierarchical framework for mobile device control that explicitly separates and co‑evolves two types of knowledge: declarative ("know‑what") for high‑level planning and procedural ("know‑how") for low‑level execution. The high‑level planner distills and iteratively refines task‑level knowledge via a Summarize‑Reflect‑Locate‑Revise (SRLR) loop starting from a single demonstration. The low‑level executor is trained using Curriculum‑Guided Group Relative Policy Optimization (C‑GRPO), which combines error‑decoupled experience balancing and dynamic demonstration injection. On the AndroidWorld benchmark, K^2‑Agent achieves a 76.1% success rate while exhibiting strong generalization—declarative knowledge transfers across different vision‑language backbones, and procedural skills transfer to unseen tasks in ScreenSpot‑v2 and Android‑in‑the‑Wild.

## Method Summary
The method comprises a planner (π_H) that generates sub‑goals and an executor (π_L) that performs atomic UI actions. The planner maintains a natural language knowledge base K_G, initially distilled from one expert demonstration via "Summarize". During execution, when failures occur, the SRLR loop Reflects on the trajectory, Locates the first deviation, and Revises K_G using atomic edits (Add/Delete/Update/Highlight). The executor, a trainable VLM, conditions on the current screenshot and a sub‑goal and is optimized with C‑GRPO. C‑GRPO balances experience across error‑decoupled replay buffers (D_con, D_type, D_param) and dynamically injects expert action prefixes of adaptive length to guide the policy gradient (GRPO). The two modules form a closed co‑evolution: executor failures trigger planner revisions, and improved planning yields better training data for the executor.

## Key Results
- 76.1% task success rate on AndroidWorld using only raw screenshots, substantially higher than prior works.
- Dual generalization: declarative knowledge (K_G) transfers across VLM backbones; procedural skills transfer to ScreenSpot‑v2 and Android‑in‑the‑Wild without further training.
- Ablations confirm the necessity of hierarchy, SRLR, and C‑GRPO’s two components.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: SRLR loop for iterative planning improvement**  
The planner refines K_G by analyzing execution failures. For a failed rollout, it Reflects (verifies task logic and step outcomes), Locates the first visual divergence, and Revises K_G with targeted atomic edits. This assumes task logic can be captured in natural language and that selective edits suffice to correct planning errors. Evidence: [abstract], Section 4.2 details the loop and revision operation; experiments show a few iterations often suffice. Termination occurs after max iterations or a success streak, with a fallback for unlocalizable failures.

**Mechanism 2: C‑GRPO for efficient procedural learning from sparse rewards**  
The executor training uses curriculum‑guided Group Relative Policy Optimization. It balances experience via error‑decoupled replay buffers and conditionally prepends expert action prefixes whose length decays with training progress (Eq. 8). This design addresses sparse rewards and sample imbalance, enabling efficient learning of precise UI interactions. Evidence: [abstract] mentions these components; Section 4.3 defines error rates and injection; Figure 4 illustrates the framework. Ablations (Figure 5b) show both components are necessary.

**Mechanism 3: Hierarchical decoupling and co‑evolution yield dual generalization**  
Separating planning (non‑parametric K_G updates via prompt editing) and execution (parametric RL fine‑tuning) lets each module optimize independently: the planner focuses on robust task decomposition and error recovery, while the executor learns precise action grounding. The closed loop—executor failures trigger planner revisions, revised plans generate new sub‑tasks—creates a co‑evolutionary dynamic that yields generalization across backbones (for the planner) and benchmarks (for the executor). Evidence: Dual generalization results in the abstract and Section 5 demonstrate transfer across VLM backbones and to ScreenSpot‑v2/AitW; this contrasts with flat or monolithic hierarchical agents.

## Foundational Learning
**Vision‑Language Model (VLM) Prompting & Fine‑tuning**  
*Why needed*: Both modules are VLMs operating on raw screenshots and text. The planner uses in‑context learning (prompt‑based SRLR); the executor undergoes parameter‑efficient RL fine‑tuning (C‑GRPO).  
*Quick check*: How does the choice of base VLM (e.g., 7B vs. 72B) affect the stability of the SRLR loop’s Reflect and Revise stages?

**Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL)**  
*Why needed*: K^2‑Agent follows a classic HRL structure where a high‑level manager sets sub‑goals and a low‑level controller executes them, addressing credit assignment in long‑horizon tasks.  
*Quick check*: What risks arise if sub‑goals are too ambiguous for the executor, and how does C‑GRPO mitigate them?

**Curriculum Learning & Replay Buffers**  
*Why needed*: C‑GRPO’s core is curriculum via dynamic demonstration injection (annealing expert prefixes) and experience replay balancing by error type, combating sample imbalance and sparse rewards.  
*Quick check*: How would performance change if the error‑decoupled buffers were replaced by a single uniform replay buffer?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Planner (π_H, frozen VLM) → maintains K_G → SRLR loop (Summarize/Reflect/Locate/Revise) → generates sub‑goals → Executor (π_L, trainable VLM) → executes atomic actions → Environment → (screenshot, reward) → feeds back to SRLR on failures. Executor training: C‑GRPO Trainer uses error‑decoupled buffers (D_con, D_type, D_param), computes dynamic injection length l = L·σ(k)·f_gate(d_i), forms augmented context, and updates policy via GRPO objective.

**Critical path**:  
1. **Bootstrap**: For each task category, capture one expert demonstration T_d. Run Summarize(T_d, g) to initialize K_G.  
2. **SRLR Loop**: Execute task with current π_H (using K_G) and π_L. On failure, trigger Reflect→Locate→Revise to update K_G. Repeat until success threshold or max iterations.  
3. **C‑GRPO Training**: Collect successful trajectories’ (s_i, â_i, T_d_i). Initialize buffers by running π_L once to compute η_type, η_param. Alternate SRLR (n=3 loops) with one C‑GRPO epoch, or train π_L after K_G is stable. Sample balanced batches with dynamic prefix injection, then apply GRPO update.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- **Hierarchical vs. Flat**: Hierarchy yields 76.1% vs. flat 35.3% but adds complexity in sub‑goal design and loop synchronization.  
- **Non‑parametric vs. Parametric Planner**: Frozen VLM with prompt editing saves compute but may cap performance; K_G transfer helps weaker models more.  
- **Dynamic Injection vs. Pure RL**: Without injection, learning often diverges; too much injection (high T) stalls learning—requires careful scheduling.  
- **Compute & Data**: Planner needs one demo per task; executor requires 606 expert samples and 8×A100 GPUs.

**Failure signatures**:  
- **SRLR stagnation**: K_G revises but success plateaus below threshold. Likely Reflect cannot find visual divergence or Revise overfits. Check failure localization and prompt fidelity.  
- **C‑GRPO non‑convergence**: Reward curve plateaus low or oscillates. Inspect buffer sampling ratios (β values) and injection temperature T. Adjust thresholds to populate all buffers.  
- **Hierarchical disconnect**: Planner produces sub‑goals executor repeatedly fails (e.g., "click invisible button"). K_G may be too abstract; need Highlight operator to preserve UI specifics.  
- **Generalization gap**: High on AndroidWorld but low on ScreenSpot‑v2 suggests executor overfits to app‑specific layouts; consider more diverse training data.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Ablation: Hierarchy Necessity** Implement a flat agent (no planner; direct task→action) and train the same executor on the same data. Expect ~40% success vs. 76% on AndroidWorld.  
2. **SRLR Diagnostic** Run SRLR with MAX_ITER=10 on a single task and log K_G revisions. Visualize evolution from literal demo transcription to generalized strategy, verifying that revisions target the first failure point.  
3. **C‑GRPO Component Isolation** Train three executor variants on identical data: (a) full C‑GRPO; (b) without dynamic injection; (c) without replay balancing. Expect (b) to fail convergence, (c) to converge slower. Plot reward curves replicating Figure 5b.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **VLM reasoning limits** – To what extent do current VLM constraints (long‑context, complex instruction following) limit SRLR, and how much improvement can be expected from next‑generation VLMs? The paper acknowledges backbone limitations but does not quantify them.
2. **Demonstration efficiency** – How many initial demonstrations are needed for tasks of varying complexity, and can the requirement be reduced via knowledge sharing or self‑supervision? The use of one demo per category is a starting point, but the relationship is unstudied.
3. **Non‑visual failure handling** – How can K^2‑Agent recover from execution failures not observable in screenshots (system freezes, network delays)? The current loop relies on visual divergence; such cases fall back to max‑retry.
4. **Procedural skill transfer to new modalities** – What are the limits of transferring procedural skills to fundamentally different interaction modalities (voice‑only, AR/VR, desktop mouse/keyboard)? Transfer was only evaluated to other 2D GUI benchmarks.
5. **Adaptive co‑evolution** – Can the loop be made more sample‑efficient by adaptively adjusting SRLR revision operators or C‑GRPO curriculum based on real‑time performance, instead of fixed schedules? The paper uses static hyperparameters.

## Limitations
- Replay buffer thresholds for C‑GRPO are unspecified, hindering exact replication and possibly affecting performance.
- Train/test split for executor training is undisclosed, raising potential data leakage concerns.
- The SRLR loop may stagnate on failures that are unlocalizable or when the initial demonstration is of poor quality.
- Dual generalization claims, while promising, are validated only on a limited set of benchmarks; broader transferability remains to be demonstrated.

## Confidence
- **SRLR loop improves planning via reflection**: Medium (internal results positive, external revision quality not vetted).
- **C‑GRPO enables efficient procedural learning**: Medium‑High (ablation supports components, but threshold sensitivity unknown).
- **Hierarchical co‑evolution yields dual generalization**: Medium (results are benchmark‑specific and may not hold universally).

## Next Checks
1. **C‑GRPO threshold sensitivity** – Systematically vary the replay buffer thresholds and measure convergence and final success rates on AndroidWorld.
2. **SRLR revision audit** – Collect K_G updates across tasks and have domain experts rate the logical soundness and plan improvement.
3. **Cross‑backbone planner transfer** – Replace the planner VLM (e.g., with GPT‑4o‑mini) and evaluate the unchanged executor on AndroidWorld to test robustness of knowledge transfer.
