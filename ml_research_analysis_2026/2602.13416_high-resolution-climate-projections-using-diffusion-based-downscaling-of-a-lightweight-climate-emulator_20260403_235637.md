# High-Resolution Climate Projections Using Diffusion-Based Downscaling of a Lightweight Climate Emulator

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13416
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13416
- **Reference Count:** 6
- **Primary Result:** A diffusion-based downscaling framework raises the spatial resolution of a lightweight climate emulator from ~300 km to ~25 km, significantly improving climatological metrics (e.g., temperature RMSE reduced by ~50%) over deterministic baselines while preserving large-scale dynamics and extreme event statistics.

## Executive Summary
Lightweight climate emulators like LUCIE offer efficient global simulations but operate at coarse (~300 km) resolution, limiting their utility for regional impact studies. This study bridges that gap by developing a deep learning super-resolution (SR) framework based on probabilistic diffusion models (Conditional and Posterior Sampling EDM). Trained on high-resolution ERA5 reanalysis data, the SR modules effectively downscale LUCIE's coarse-grained outputs to fine (~25 km) scales. The diffusion-based approach outperforms deterministic SR baselines in capturing fine-scale statistics and high-wavenumber variability, while maintaining computational efficiency suitable for generating large ensemble projections.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework uses a two-stage pipeline. First, the LUCIE emulator generates coarse-resolution (~300 km) climate fields. Second, a pretrained diffusion-based SR model (Conditional EDM or Posterior Sampling) upsamples these fields to high resolution (~25 km). The SR models are trained on paired samples of downsampled (to LUCIE's resolution) and original high-resolution ERA5 data (2000–2009), learning to map coarse inputs to plausible fine-scale outcomes. During inference, the diffusion model performs iterative denoising conditioned on the coarse input, generating a high-resolution sample consistent with the large-scale LUCIE state. This separation allows leveraging LUCIE's fast global integration while adding fine-scale realism post-hoc.

## Key Results
- Diffusion models (Conditional EDM: Temp RMSE 1.237 K; Posterior: 1.127 K) significantly outperform deterministic SR baselines (SFNO-SR, UNet-SR; Temp RMSE ~2.4 K).
- Both diffusion variants better preserve high-wavenumber power spectra and extreme value probability distributions compared to baselines.
- The framework accurately reconstructs major climate modes (e.g., Northern Annular Mode) from empirical orthogonal function analysis.
- Conditional EDM offers a favorable accuracy-throughput trade-off, producing 10 years of high-resolution data in ~3.5 hours on a single GPU.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The diffusion models succeed by explicitly modeling the *conditional distribution* of high-resolution climate states given their coarse-grained counterparts. Unlike deterministic SR, which learns a single mapping, the probabilistic diffusion process captures the inherent uncertainty and multi-modality of sub-grid processes (e.g., convection, orography-driven precipitation). This allows it to generate diverse yet statistically realistic fine-scale structures that align with the large-scale constraints from LUCIE. The training on a diverse decade of ERA5 data imbues the model with generalizable relationships between coarse and fine scales across weather regimes, enabling it to handle LUCIE's somewhat "imperfect" inputs.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**
Input -> LUCIE Emulator -> Coarse Climate Field -> Diffusion SR Model -> High-Resolution Climate Field

**Critical Path**
The core computational path for generating a high-resolution year is: (1) Integrate LUCIE for coarse outputs (very fast), then (2) Run the diffusion SR model iteratively (e.g., 18–39 steps) conditioned on each coarse timestep. SR inference is the primary computational bottleneck, though still tractable.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- **Conditional EDM vs Posterior Sampling:** Conditional EDM uses a simpler, single-stage conditional denoising process, offering higher throughput (~68.5 yr/GPU-day). Posterior sampling uses a two-stage process (predictor + corrector) with an extra guidance scale λg, yielding slightly lower RMSE (1.127 K vs 1.237 K) but at much higher cost (~3.75 yr/GPU-day on 16 GPUs).
- **Diffusion vs Deterministic SR:** Diffusion provides superior statistical fidelity and spectral accuracy but requires many sequential network evaluations. Deterministic SR (SFNO-SR, UNet-SR) is single-pass and faster but introduces spectral artifacts and oversmoothing.
- **Training Data Domain:** Models are trained on ERA5 (2000–2009). Performance relies on LUCIE's biases being within the training distribution's "imperfect input" manifold.

**Failure Signatures**
- **Spectral Artifacts:** Unphysical "wavy oscillations" or spikes in high-wavenumber power spectra, particularly in deterministic SFNO-SR outputs.
- **Dynamic Inconsistency:** SR output may visually look plausible but slowly drift from LUCIE's large-scale state over long integrations, violating conservation.
- **Extreme Event Degradation:** SR output exhibits muted tails in precipitation or wind speed distributions compared to high-res references.
- **Regional Bias Transfer:** If LUCIE has a strong regional bias not represented in ERA5 training data (e.g., specific mountain range precipitation), SR may fail to correct it appropriately.

**First Three Experiments**
1. **Reproduce Core Results:** Run the provided Cond-EDM and Posterior Sampling models on the same LUCIE 2010–2020 test set. Verify reported RMSE values for temperature and zonal wind, and visually inspect power spectra and spatial examples.
2. **Spectral Artifact Diagnosis:** Isolate a case where SFNO-SR shows high-wavenumber oscillations. Analyze its spectral layers and kernel designs. Test if adding a simple spectral regularization term (penalizing energy beyond Nyquist) during SR training reduces artifacts.
3. **Generalization Probe:** Apply the *trained* Cond-EDM model to downscale outputs from a different coarse model (e.g., a low-res CMIP6 GCM like CanESM5, coarse-grained to ~300 km). Quantify the degradation in RMSE and spectral metrics relative to the LUCIE case.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What are the technical and stability challenges in integrating the super-resolution module directly into the LUCIE emulator's architecture, and can joint training preserve long-term climate stability?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state in the Discussion (Page 14): "utilizing a shared operator backbone creates a foundation for potentially embedding the SR model directly within the emulator in future iterations."
- **Why unresolved:** The current framework uses a two-stage approach where SR is a post-processing step. Embedding would require co-design of the emulator and SR, risking instability in long-term integrations due to feedback loops or spectral inconsistencies.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct experiments where the SR model is trained jointly with LUCIE, monitoring metrics like drift, spectral fidelity, and computational cost over multi-decadal simulations. Compare against the two-stage approach.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the posterior sampling diffusion approach achieve comparable accuracy to the conditional EDM with reduced computational cost, and what is the optimal trade-off between guidance strength and sampling efficiency?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 2.6 and Discussion (Page 15) highlight that posterior sampling requires 16 GPUs for 10-year inference (3.75 years/GPU-day) versus conditional EDM's 1 GPU (68.5 years/GPU-day), while posterior sampling achieves slightly lower temperature RMSE (1.127 K vs 1.237 K).
- **Why unresolved:** The high computational cost of posterior sampling limits its practicality for large ensembles. The guidance scale λg and number of sampling steps are not optimized for efficiency, leaving room for improvement.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablation studies varying guidance scale, sampling steps, and solver settings to identify configurations that maintain accuracy (e.g., RMSE within 5% of current) while maximizing throughput. Implement algorithmic optimizations like step distillation or parallelization.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How well do the trained super-resolution models generalize to coarse-grained outputs from climate emulators with different biases or to geographic regions with distinct climatology, given the "imperfect input" challenge?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors test SR models on LUCIE outputs (2010–2020) after training on ERA5 (2000–2009), framing it as an "imperfect input" setting (Page 3). However, they only evaluate on one emulator (LUCIE) and do not test regional hold-out scenarios, despite citing literature on distributional shifts (Harder et al., 2025).
- **Why unresolved:** The robustness to diverse coarse-grid sources or unseen regions is critical for real-world impact assessment but remains unverified. Models may overfit to LUCIE's specific error characteristics or the training period's climatology.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate the trained SR models on outputs from other climate models (e.g., CMIP6 GCMs) or on regional subsets (e.g., tropical vs. polar) not emphasized during training. Metrics should include spatial consistency, extreme event retention, and spectral fidelity.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What causes the high-wavenumber spectral artifacts (e.g., wavy oscillations) in the deterministic SFNO-SR model, and can architectural modifications in spectral neural operators mitigate these while preserving resolution-invariance?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Results (Page 11-12) show SFNO-SR exhibits "physically unrealistic wavy oscillations and periodic spikes" in high-wavenumber power spectra, unlike diffusion models. The authors attribute this to Fourier-based aliasing or Gibbs phenomena but do not investigate remedies.
- **Why unresolved:** Spectral artifacts compromise physical realism for fine-scale turbulence. Understanding their root cause is essential for improving deterministic SR alternatives that may be more efficient than diffusion.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze SFNO-SR's spectral layers and kernel designs; implement modifications like spectral regularization, anti-aliasing filters, or hybrid architectures. Compare power spectra and spatial maps before/after changes.

## Limitations
- **Temporal scope limited:** Evaluation restricted to 2010–2020; lacks coverage of other climate regimes (e.g., volcanic years, strong ENSO) and future conditions.
- **Training data decade:** Model trained on ERA5 2000–2009 only; if LUCIE's biases or future climate states differ, transferability may suffer.
- **Physical realism unchecked:** Diffusion models excel at statistical matching but may violate conservation laws (mass, energy) or generate unphysical fine-scale features; not explicitly tested.
