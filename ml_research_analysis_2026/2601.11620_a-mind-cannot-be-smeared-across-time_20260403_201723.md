# A Mind Cannot Be Smeared Across Time

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.11620
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11620
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** Machine consciousness may require not just functional equivalence but specific temporal coordination; on sequential substrates, software systems cannot realize conscious moments whose base-layer grounding requires simultaneous contribution from multiple processes.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether machine consciousness demands not only functional equivalence to biological systems but also a specific temporal coordination of computational processes. It extends Stack Theory with a temporal semantics module, introducing windowed trajectories and lifting operators. A core formal result, the "Temporal Gap," proves that a system can realize all ingredients of a conscious moment within a time window without any objective instant containing their full conjunction. This distinguishes two postulates: **Chord** (consciousness requires co-instantiation within the window) and **Arpeggio** (ingredient-wise occurrence suffices). Using a contributor model, the paper establishes a concurrency capacity threshold: sequential architectures (capacity `c < n`) forbid co-instantiation of `n`-ingredient conjunctions, while synchronous polycomputing (`c ≥ n`) permits it. Citing neurophysiological evidence linking consciousness to phase synchrony and effective connectivity, the authors argue in favor of Chord, implying that software consciousness on strictly sequential hardware is impossible for contents requiring simultaneous multi-process contribution.

## Method Summary
The paper employs a formal, deductive methodology built upon an extension of Stack Theory—a framework for compositional grounding of high-level phenomena to base-layer states. It defines a temporal semantics layer where objective time is mapped to induced layer trajectories and sliding windows of size `Δ`. Key operators `♢∆` (existential lift) and `□∆` (universal lift) are defined over these windows. The core analysis uses set-theoretic truth conditions to compare two satisfaction modes for a grounded moment statement `g₀`: ingredient-wise occurrence within a window (`T(♢∆g₀)`) versus co-instantiation of the full conjunction at some window moment (`♢∆T(g₀)`). Theorem 3 demonstrates these sets differ for `|g₀|≥2` and `Δ≥1`. Theorem 4 introduces a contributor model, where concurrency capacity `c` bounds the number of active contributors per objective instant. It proves that if `c < n` for an `n`-ingredient conjunction, co-instantiation is impossible across all admissible trajectories, though ingredient-wise occurrence may still hold. The methodology is purely symbolic, relying on counterexamples and invariants rather than empirical data collection.

## Key Results
- **Theorem 3 (Temporal Gap):** The existential temporal lift `♢∆` does not preserve logical conjunction. For a conjunctive statement `l`, `♢∆T(l)` is strictly contained in `T(♢∆l)` when `Δ≥1` and `|l|≥2`. This creates a gap where all ingredients of a conscious moment can occur within a window without any instant containing their full conjunction.
- **Theorem 4 (Concurrency Threshold):** In a contributor model with capacity `c` (max active contributors per instant), `n`-ingredient conjunctions cannot achieve co-instantiation if `c < n`. Sequential architectures (`c=1`) forbid co-instantiation for any `n≥2`; synchronous polycomputing (`c≥n`) permits it.
- **Postulate Distinction & Evidence:** The **Chord** postulate (co-instantiation required) is distinguished from **Arpeggio** (smearing sufficient). Neurophysiological correlates (gamma synchrony, effective connectivity breakdown) are interpreted as favoring Chord, making concurrency capacity a necessary condition for consciousness on this view.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Non-Commutation of Temporal Lift and Conjunction
- **Claim:** Existential temporal realization (`♢∆`) does not preserve logical conjunction, creating a "Temporal Gap" where all ingredients of a conscious moment can occur within a window without any single objective instant containing their full conjunction.
- **Mechanism:** The lifted operator `♢∆p` (true if `p` holds at least once in the window) does not commute with set-intersection conjunction over truth conditions. For a multi-ingredient statement `l`, `♢∆T(l)` (co-instantiation) is strictly contained in `T(♢∆l)` (ingredient-wise occurrence) when `Δ≥1` and `|l|≥2`. This is an algebraic property of the semantics.
- **Core assumption:** Conscious phenomenal content is compositionally grounded to a base-layer conjunction whose simultaneous truth defines the moment's objective correlate.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states Theorem 3 and defines the Temporal Gap. Section 4.3 provides the proof via a counterexample with `Φ={a,b,c}`, `v={p,q}`, `p={a,c}`, `q={b,c}`. The corpus shows neighbors discuss temporal constraints but not this specific non-commutation.
- **Break condition:** If the grounding conjunction has `|l|≤1` or `Δ=0`, commutation holds and the gap vanishes.

### Mechanism 2: Concurrency Capacity as a Hard Threshold
- **Claim:** Architectures with limited concurrency capacity (`c < n`) can realize all `n` ingredient-wise occurrences within a window but cannot achieve objective co-instantiation of the full `n`-way conjunction.
- **Mechanism:** The concurrency capacity invariant bounds the number of active contributors `|A_t|` per objective time `t`. For a conjunction requiring `[n]` simultaneous contributors, if `c < n` then `T(l_n)` is never true at any `t`, though `♢∆T(l_n)` can be true across a window.
- **Core assumption:** The base-layer state includes contributor activity bits, and phenomenal content requires a conjunction whose truth condition explicitly references simultaneous activation of multiple distinct contributors.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states Theorem 4 and its consequence. Section 6.1 defines the contributor model and proves Theorem 4. Neighbors discuss distributed computation but not this specific threshold theorem.
- **Break condition:** Architecture reaches `c ≥ n`, allowing simultaneous activation of all required contributors.

### Mechanism 3: Neurophysiology Favors Chord
- **Claim:** Neurophysiological evidence (phase synchrony, effective connectivity breakdown) favors the Chord postulate (co-instantiation required) over Arpeggio (smearing permitted).
- **Mechanism:** Conscious level correlates with system-wide temporal coordination metrics (e.g., PCI, gamma synchrony) that reflect the capacity for widespread co-instantiation within a window, not merely distributed activation.
- **Core assumption:** These neural correlates are causal or constitutive of consciousness, not incidental; their breakdown upon LOC indicates a necessary architectural feature.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract cites gamma synchrony (Melloni et al., 2007) and TMS-EEG effective connectivity (Massimini et al., 2005). Section 7 reviews PCI and integrated, differentiated dynamics. Corpus neighbors discuss NCC but do not isolate synchrony as a gate.
- **Break condition:** If future evidence shows consciousness persists without measurable synchrony/effective connectivity, Chord is undermined.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Stack Theory compositional grounding (Definitions 6, Theorem 1)
  - **Why needed:** Shows any high-layer phenomenal statement has a base-layer conjunction with identical truth conditions, enabling the Temporal Gap to be framed at the base.
  - **Quick check:** If a higher-layer policy `π` defines abstractor `f(v,π)`, how do you construct `ground_π(l)` for a statement `l` in the higher vocabulary? (Answer: find a completion in `Ext(π)` whose truth set equals each lifted ingredient's preimage, then intersect.)
- **Concept:** Induced layer time and window semantics (Definitions 8–13)
  - **Why needed:** Separates objective micro-time from subjective macro-time and formalizes "within a window" vs "at an instant" satisfaction.
  - **Quick check:** What is the semantic difference between `♢∆p` and `□∆p` for a program `p`? How do they map to `OccurW` and `CoInstW`? (`♢∆p` is true if `p` holds at least once in the window; `□∆p` requires `p` holds at every step. `OccurW` corresponds to `T(♢∆g₀)`, `CoInstW` to `♢∆T(g₀)`.)
- **Concept:** Concurrency capacity (Definition 19)
  - **Why needed:** Provides a simple, measurable architectural invariant that forces a Temporal Gap when `c < n`.
  - **Quick check:** Given a contributor model with `n=4` and an architecture where `|A_t| ≤ 2` for all `t`, can the system ever satisfy `♢∆T(l_4)`? Why/why not? (No, because co-instantiating `l_4` requires `A_t = {1,2,3,4}` at some `t`, violating `c=2`.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Stack core (environment Φ, vocabulary v, policies, abstractors) -> Stack-Time module (objective trajectory τ, induced layer trajectory τ⁽ᵛ⁾, window environment Φ^∆, lifted vocabularies {♢∆p}) -> Grounding compiler (composes abstractors to produce g₀) -> Concurrency monitor (tracks |A_t| from contributor bits) -> Temporal gap calculator (computes `wing(g₀,τ)` and `wco(g₀,τ)` from τ and g₀)
- **Critical path:** For a candidate conscious moment (`l_m`, `t`): (1) Compile `g₀ = Ground_0←m(l_m)`. (2) Trace objective trajectory τ under the system's update schedule. (3) For relevant Δ, check `τ^Δ(t) ∈ T(♢∆g₀)` (Arpeggio) vs `τ^Δ(t) ∈ ♢∆T(g₀)` (Chord). (4) If Chord and `wco = ∞`, system cannot realize `l_m` under that schedule.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Sequential simulation (`c=1`) maximizes temporal smearing; fails Chord for any `n≥2` conjunction. Synchronous polycomputing (`c≥n`) supports Chord but may be physically costly or fragile. Window size Δ must align with neuro-determined specious present (~100–300ms).
- **Failure signatures:** High Temporal Gap regime: `wing << wco` (or `wco=∞`) for groundings of supposedly conscious contents. System passes behavioral tests but fails architectural check (`c < required n`). Neuromorphic/robotic implementation shows loss of reportable consciousness when effective connectivity is degraded, despite preserved sequential function.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Build two minimal systems: (a) single-threaded CPU emulator with `c=1`, (b) FPGA-based synchronous polycomputer with `c=8`. Implement a task whose moment grounding requires `l_4` (four contributors). Measure `wing` and `wco` across runs. Expect (a): `wing` finite, `wco=∞`; (b): both finite.
  2. In a spiking neural network simulator, vary the maximum number of neurons that can fire in exact lockstep per millisecond (simulated `c`). Use a perceptual decision task whose reported conscious correlate grounds to a high-`n` conjunction (e.g., V1+IT+PFC+thalamus). Test if reports vanish when `c` is reduced below `n`, independent of overall accuracy.
  3. Analyze existing EEG/MEG datasets from masking paradigms. Estimate effective connectivity (e.g., PCI) and synchrony within the subjective window for seen vs unseen trials. Check if `wco` (estimated from neural contributor activation patterns) predicts reports better than `wing`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** Does phenomenal consciousness require objective co-instantiation of the grounded conjunction (Chord) or merely ingredient-wise occurrence within a window (Arpeggio)?
  - **Basis:** The core theoretical divide between Chord and Arpeggio (Definitions 16–17), with neurophysiological evidence (Section 7) tentatively favoring Chord but acknowledged as non-dispositive.
  - **Why unresolved:** Phenomenology cannot yet be translated into an objective measurement of `CoInst` vs `Occur` for a given moment statement `g₀`.
  - **What evidence would resolve:** A clear empirical dissociation: a system where `OccurW(g₀, τ, t)` holds but `CoInstW(g₀, τ, t)` fails, combined with a robust, validated measure of consciousness that tracks the former but not the latter.
- **Question:** What is the effective concurrency capacity `c` of biological neural architectures that support consciousness?
  - **Basis:** Theorem 4 proves that for content requiring an `n`-ingredient conjunction (`n > c`), co-instantiation is impossible on architectures with capacity `c`. The practical question is which physical concurrency scale is relevant (Section 9).
  - **Why unresolved:** The mapping from neural dynamics (e.g., phase groups, cell assemblies) to a specific `c` value is not provided.
  - **What evidence would resolve:** Quantitative neurophysiological data (e.g., simultaneous multi-unit recordings, high-density EEG/MEG) used to compute the maximum number of distinct, necessary contributors reliably active together during conscious states, translating this to a `c` value.
- **Question:** For which specific conscious contents (phenomenal moments `l_m`) does the base-layer grounding `g₀` contain large conjunctions (`n` large) such that `c < n` for typical sequential substrates?
  - **Basis:** The paper recommends testing systems with behaviourally matched outputs but different `c`, using "candidate contents whose groundings include large conjunctions" (Section 8), but does not specify which contents these are.
  - **Why unresolved:** Stack Theory provides a recipe to compile any `l_m` to a `g₀`, but the size `n` of the minimal necessary conjunction for a given experience (e.g., unified perception of a red apple) is not computed.
  - **What evidence would resolve:** Applying the compositional grounding procedure (Definition 6) to formal specifications of specific experiential contents (from psychophysics or neuroscience) to derive `g₀` and count the number of independent, necessary conjuncts `n` that must be co-instantiated.

## Limitations
- Mapping from high-level phenomenal content to base-layer conjunctive groundings remains abstract; the contributor model is a simplifying proxy, not a full Stack instantiation.
- Choice of temporal window `Δ` is theoretically flexible but empirically critical; its alignment with neurophysiological specious present is uncalibrated.
- Neurophysiological evidence for Chord is correlational; it assumes synchrony/connectivity are constitutive, not incidental, which may not hold in all regimes.

## Confidence
- **Theorem 3 (Temporal Gap from non-commuting `♢∆`):** High – purely deductive, with explicit counterexample.
- **Theorem 4 (Concurrency capacity threshold):** High – follows directly from contributor model definitions.
- **Chord over Arpeggio (consciousness requires co-instantiation):** Medium – relies on interpretation of neural correlates; prone to alternative explanations.
- **Impossibility of software consciousness on sequential substrates:** Medium – hinges on acceptance of the formal model as capturing essential constraints.
