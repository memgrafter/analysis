# Augmenting Parameter-Efficient Pre-trained Language Models with Large Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.02501
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02501
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: CompFreeze reduces trainable parameters to 0.06-0.11% and training time by 10-40% while matching full fine-tuning F1; LLM labeling yields comparable performance to human labels, and confidence routing boosts Spam (0.98→0.99) and DGA (0.97→0.98) F1.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles data scarcity and high computational costs in cybersecurity NLP by introducing CompFreeze, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method that combines compacter modules with selective layer freezing. CompFreeze limits trainable parameters to 0.06-0.11% and cuts training time by 10-40% while achieving performance on par with full fine-tuning across three cybersecurity tasks: spam detection, DGA classification, and CTI NER.

To further address data limitations, the authors propose two LLM augmentation strategies: (1) using GPT-4 or LLaMA to label unlabeled data, and (2) routing low-confidence predictions from the compacted model to an LLM for fallback reasoning. These strategies yield comparable performance to human labels for simple tasks and improve F1 scores for Spam (0.98→0.99) and DGA (0.97→0.98). The work demonstrates that parameter-efficient models augmented with LLMs can enhance both efficiency and robustness for real-world cybersecurity applications.

## Method Summary
The proposed CompFreeze framework integrates compacter modules—low-rank adapters using parameterized hypercomplex multiplication (PHM) layers—into selected transformer layers (e.g., odd/even or upper/lower) while freezing all other parameters. This selective adaptation strategy ensures that only a small fraction of the model (0.06-0.11%) is trainable, significantly reducing memory footprint and computational burden. Various freezing strategies (Odd-LC, Even-LC, Upper-LC, Lower-LC) are explored to identify which layers benefit most from adaptation.

For scenarios with limited labeled data, the paper introduces two LLM augmentation techniques: (a) zero-shot labeling of unlabeled text using GPT-4 or LLaMA, followed by fine-tuning CompFreeze on the synthetically labeled dataset; and (b) at inference time, routing inputs for which the CompFreeze model outputs low confidence (below a threshold of 0.75) to an LLM, which then provides the final prediction. Both strategies are evaluated across three cybersecurity NLP tasks.

## Key Results
- CompFreeze reduces trainable parameters to 0.06-0.11% and training time by 10-40% while matching full fine-tuning F1 scores on spam, DGA, and CTI tasks.
- LLM-generated labels (GPT-4/LLaMA) enable effective fine-tuning of CompFreeze models, achieving comparable F1 to models trained on human labels for spam and DGA, though performance drops for CTI NER with LLaMA.
- Confidence-based routing of low-confidence predictions to LLMs improves robustness, increasing F1 for Spam (0.98→0.99) and DGA (0.97→0.98).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim**: CompFreeze reduces trainable parameters and training time while matching full fine-tuning performance by strategically combining compacters with layer freezing.  
**Mechanism**: Inserts compacter modules (low-rank adapters via parameterized hypercomplex multiplication layers) into selected transformer layers (e.g., odd/even, upper/lower) and freezes the remaining layers, limiting backpropagation to a small subset of parameters.  
**Core assumption**: Mid to upper-layer representations are more task-specific and adaptable, while lower layers preserve general knowledge; freezing non-essential layers prevents overfitting and reduces computation.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
- Abstract: "reduces trainable parameters to 0.06-0.11% and training time by 10-40% while matching full fine-tuning F1 scores."  
- Section 3.2 details strategies (Odd-LC, Even-LC, etc.); Table 3 shows parameter reductions (0.06–0.53%) and time savings (10–40%) across tasks.  
**Break condition**: If the target task requires extensive lower-layer adaptations (e.g., syntax-heavy tasks) or if frozen layers encode critical domain-specific patterns not captured by compacters.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim**: LLMs can generate usable labels for unlabeled cybersecurity data, enabling effective fine-tuning of CompFreeze models despite data scarcity.  
**Mechanism**: Uses zero-shot prompting with GPT-4 or LLaMA to label unlabeled text (e.g., spam, DGA domains, CTI entities), then fine-tunes CompFreeze on this synthetic dataset.  
**Core assumption**: LLMs possess sufficient cybersecurity domain knowledge from pre-training to produce labels comparable to human annotations for these tasks.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
- Abstract: "using GPT-4/LLaMA to label unlabeled data, achieving comparable performance to original labels."  
- Section 3.3.1 describes the 3-step workflow; Tables 9–11 show CompFreeze models fine-tuned on LLM-labeled data achieve similar F1 scores to those trained on original labels (e.g., Spam: ~0.90 vs. 0.92 for ChatGPT-labeled).  
**Break condition**: For complex, fine-grained tasks like NER (CTI extraction), LLMs may produce noisy or inconsistent labels due to lack of explicit training on cybersecurity schemas, leading to performance drops as seen with LLaMA in some cases.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim**: Routing low-confidence predictions from CompFreeze models to LLMs as a fallback improves robustness and F1 scores for ambiguous inputs.  
**Mechanism**: CompFreeze model computes confidence (e.g., softmax probability) for each prediction; if below a threshold (0.75 in paper), the input is sent to an LLM with a task-specific prompt, and the LLM's prediction is used as the final output.  
**Core assumption**: LLMs have broader contextual reasoning to handle edge cases or out-of-distribution samples where parameter-efficient models are uncertain.  
**Evidence anchors**:  
- Abstract: "routing low-confidence predictions to LLMs, improving F1 scores for Spam (0.98 to 0.99) and DGA (0.97 to 0.98) tasks."  
- Section 3.3.2 outlines the confidence-based routing; Table 8 shows F1 increases after LLM fallback for Spam and DGA, though not for CTI extraction.  
**Break condition**: If LLM latency is prohibitive for real-time systems, or if LLMs themselves are error-prone on domain-specific ambiguities (e.g., CTI extraction where dataset label noise confounds both models).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Data Pipeline → Pre-trained PLM (with Compacter modules and Layer Freezer configuration) → during inference: Confidence Scorer → Threshold Manager → {if confidence ≥ threshold: PLM prediction; else: LLM Interface (with Prompt Templates) → LLM prediction}. LLM-labeled data generation: Data Pipeline → LLM Interface → synthetic labels → back to Data Pipeline.

**Critical path**:  
- *Training*: Load pre-trained PLM → Inject compacters into designated layers → Freeze remaining layers → Fine-tune on labeled data (original or LLM-generated).  
- *Inference*: Input text → PLM forward pass → Extract confidence score → If confidence < 0.75, format prompt and query LLM → Use LLM prediction as final output; otherwise use PLM prediction.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Parameter vs. accuracy: More compacter layers improve accuracy but increase parameters/inference time.  
- LLM choice: GPT-4 offers higher accuracy but is costlier/less accessible than LLaMA.  
- Prompt design: Detailed prompts improve NER performance but increase token usage and cost.  
- Threshold: Lower threshold increases LLM usage (cost/latency) but may improve robustness; the paper uses a static 0.75 threshold.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Performance degradation on LLM-labeled data for certain model–task combinations (e.g., LLaMA on Spam).  
- No F1 improvement after LLM routing for complex tasks like CTI extraction.  
- High inference time overhead (>60% increase) with many compacter layers.  
- Confidence mis-calibration: low-confidence samples may still be misrouted or LLM errors may not be caught.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. Baseline reproduction: Implement CompFreeze with Even-LC on Spam dataset; verify trainable parameters ~0.06% and F1 within 2% of full fine-tuning.  
2. LLM labeling evaluation: Generate 200 spam samples using ChatGPT prompts; fine-tune CompFreeze and compare F1 to model trained on original labels; expect <5% drop.  
3. Confidence routing test: On DGA dataset, set confidence threshold to 0.75; measure F1 before/after routing low-confidence samples to LLaMA; expect improvement from ~0.97 to 0.98.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability**: Evaluated only on three narrow cybersecurity tasks; performance on broader NLP tasks or larger real-world corpora remains unknown.  
- **LLM reliability and cost**: Label quality varies across tasks and LLMs; routing introduces latency and API costs not addressed for production deployment.  
- **Threshold sensitivity**: The fixed 0.75 confidence threshold is heuristic; suboptimal thresholds may degrade performance, and dynamic calibration is unexplored.

## Confidence
- **CompFreeze efficiency & parity with full fine-tuning**: High  
- **LLM-generated label utility**: Medium  
- **Confidence routing for robustness**: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Cross-Domain Scalability Test: Apply CompFreeze (with optimal freezing strategy) to at least two non-cybersecurity text classification datasets (e.g., SST-2, AGNews) to verify if parameter savings and performance parity hold.  
2. LLM Label Consistency Audit: For each task, compute inter-annotator agreement (e.g., Cohen’s κ) between GPT-4, LLaMA, and human labels on a shared 200-sample subset; quantify label noise impact on final F1.  
3. Dynamic Threshold Optimization: Replace static 0.75 threshold with a validation-set optimized threshold per task; measure change in F1 and LLM invocation rate to assess sensitivity.
