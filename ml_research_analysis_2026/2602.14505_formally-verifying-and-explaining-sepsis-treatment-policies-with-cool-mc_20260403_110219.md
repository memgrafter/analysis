# Formally Verifying and Explaining Sepsis Treatment Policies with COOL-MC

## Quick Facts
- ArXiv ID: 2602.14505
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14505
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: COOL-MC formally verifies optimal survival bounds (0.8751 max, 0.633 min) for sepsis treatment and uncovers that a high‑performing RL policy depends excessively on prior IV fluid dosing history.

## Executive Summary
Sepsis treatment policies derived from reinforcement learning (RL) are often opaque and difficult to formally verify. The COOL-MC framework addresses these challenges by constructing only the policy‑induced reachable portion of a discrete‑time Markov chain (DTMC) from a PRISM‑encoded MDP, labeling states with clinically meaningful features, and integrating probabilistic model checking (PCTL) with explainability methods. Applied to the ICU‑Sepsis MDP (717 states), COOL‑MC proved that the theoretical maximum survival probability is 0.8751, and a trained safe RL policy achieved this optimum via post‑shielding. However, a deep dive into feature importance revealed that the policy relied on historical IV fluid dosing (input_4hourly) in 93.4% of states—a hidden weakness not visible through standard aggregate evaluation.

## Method Summary
COOL‑MC takes as input a PRISM model of an MDP and an observation function O that maps integer state IDs to high‑dimensional feature vectors. The process begins with Storm synthesizing an optimal policy π* for a PCTL objective (e.g., Pmax(♢survived)). A dataset of (O(s), π*(s)) pairs is then used for behavioral cloning via a 4×512‑neuron fully connected network. The cloned policy is fine‑tuned with PPO under a post‑shielding scheme: at each step, if the agent’s action is not one of the equally optimal actions for that observation (extracted from π*), it is replaced by a random optimal action. The final policy induces a DTMC built by depth‑first search from the initial state. This DTMC is queried with PCTL and analyzed using feature pruning (zeroing weights from a selected input neuron) and permutation importance (per‑state ranking of feature impact on action).

## Key Results
- Formal survival bounds: maximum 0.8751, minimum 0.633.
- Trained safe RL policy, with shielding, attains the maximum survival probability.
- Explainability analysis: input_4hourly is the most important feature in 93.4% of reachable states, indicating an autoregressive heuristic.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
COOL‑MC avoids the state‑space explosion typical of full MDP model checking by exploring only the subset of states reachable under the learned policy. This reachable‑state construction keeps the DTMC small enough for exhaustive verification while still capturing all relevant behaviors. Post‑shielding enforces safety during RL training by constraining the agent to actions known to be optimal according to the exact model checker, thus guaranteeing that performance improvements remain within the verified safe set. Feature pruning and permutation importance directly measure the policy’s sensitivity to individual inputs by observing changes in the DTMC’s verified properties, thereby exposing non‑generalizable heuristics that would be invisible from reward alone.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (data flow):
PRISM model -> Storm policy synthesis -> Expert dataset (O(s), π*(s)) -> Behavioral cloning -> PPO fine‑tuning (with post‑shielding) -> Trained policy -> Induced DTMC construction (DFS) -> PCTL verification & explainability (feature pruning, permutation) -> Results

Critical path: The entire pipeline hinges on obtaining a correct PRISM model and synthesizing π*; all subsequent steps depend on this foundation.

Design tradeoffs:
- Reachable‑state construction vs. complete model: limits memory use but may miss unreachable counterexamples.
- Neural network capacity: larger networks can approximate more complex policies but increase opacity and risk of overfitting.
- Shielding: enforces safety during training but may restrict exploration and slow convergence.

Failure signatures:
- Memory exhaustion during DFS if reachable set unexpectedly large.
- Trained policy fails to reach the optimal survival probability (0.8751).
- Feature pruning yields no drop for a supposedly critical feature, indicating implementation error.
- State labeling thresholds do not match clinical definitions, leading to invalid PCTL queries.

First three onboarding experiments:
1. Reproduce π*: Run Storm on the provided PRISM model and verify the Pmax(♢survived) bound equals 0.8751.
2. Behavioral cloning: Train the 4×512 network on the expert dataset; validate that it matches π* on all training observations.
3. Induced DTMC and simple query: Construct the DTMC for the trained policy and compute Pmax(♢survived); confirm it matches the bound.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scalability**: How does COOL‑MC perform on larger, more realistic sepsis MDPs? The current case study uses only 717 states, and future work is needed to benchmark resource usage and identify bottlenecks.
2. **Reducing Reliance on Historical Dosing**: What training modifications (e.g., regularization, adversarial debiasing) can diminish the policy’s dependence on input_4hourly while preserving survival performance?
3. **Clinically Meaningful Specifications**: Which nuanced safety properties (e.g., avoiding sustained hypotension) should be formalized in PCTL, and how can labeling be automated to capture them?
4. **Clinical Integration**: How can COOL‑MC be adapted for real‑time decision support in tools like Sepsis Watch, considering latency, EHR integration, and clinician‑friendly explanations?

## Limitations
- Incomplete specification of the ICU‑Sepsis MDP (transition guards, distributions) prevents full independent reconstruction.
- Ambiguities in shielding logic (extraction of equally optimal actions, tie‑breaking) and feature pruning implementation.
- Missing quantitative thresholds for clinical labeling (e.g., exact cutoff for high_sofa).
