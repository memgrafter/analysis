# Establishing Construct Validity in LLM Capability Benchmarks Requires Nomological Networks
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.15121
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15121
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Proposes a nomological network framework from psychometrics to validate LLM capability benchmarks, preventing invalid ascriptions (e.g., conflating reasoning with memorization) by ensuring benchmarks are theoretically coherent and empirically anchored.

## Executive Summary
The paper identifies a critical gap in LLM evaluation: performance on isolated benchmarks is often misinterpreted as evidence of human-like capabilities (e.g., reasoning) without rigorous construct validation. This risks over- or under-attribution of capabilities. To address this, the authors advocate importing the "nomological network" account from psychometrics. In this view, a construct like "reasoning" is defined not by a causal mechanism inside the model but by its systematic relationships to other constructs (e.g., working memory) and the tasks that manifest them. The paper provides a four-step validation framework (define network, design tasks, validate relationships, perform convergent/discriminant analysis) to ensure benchmark scores meaningfully reflect the intended capability and not irrelevant factors.

## Method Summary
The proposed method is a four-step framework for benchmark validation:
1.  **Establish a Nomological Network:** Define a theoretical web (e.g., using the CHC theory of intelligence) that specifies the target construct (e.g., reasoning) and its expected relationships (positive/negative/zero) with other capabilities (Stratum II) and constituent tasks (Stratum I).
2.  **Design Benchmarks for Constituent Tasks:** Create or select task benchmarks (e.g., sequence continuation for inductive reasoning) with strong content validity—items must be representative of the task and free from data contamination that would allow memorization.
3.  **Validate Statistical Relationships:** Check concurrent validity (different benchmarks for the same task correlate) and criterion validity (benchmark scores predict an external outcome).
4.  **Perform Convergent/Discriminant Analysis:** Compute correlations between aggregate capability scores across multiple LLMs. Validate that patterns match the nomological network's predictions (e.g., reasoning correlates positively with working memory but not with language modeling perplexity). Mismatches indicate potential construct underrepresentation, overrepresentation, or network flaws.

## Key Results
*   Provides a structured, theory-driven alternative to evaluating LLMs via isolated benchmark leaderboards.
*   Defines clear validation criteria (content, concurrent, criterion, convergent/discriminant validity) adapted from psychometrics for LLM benchmarks.
*   Argues this nomological approach avoids strong, often indefensible, ontological commitments about internal LLM "cognition" while still enabling meaningful capability ascriptions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Relational Construct Definition
*   **Claim:** Constructs gain meaning through their relational position in a nomological network rather than through identifying causal mechanisms within the model.
*   **Mechanism:** Following logical empiricist holism (Quine's web of belief), the meaning of "reasoning" is derived from its systematic relationships to other defined capabilities (e.g., working memory) and the tasks that operationalize them. This sidesteps unanswerable questions about whether an LLM "truly reasons."
*   **Core Assumption:** LLM capabilities can be characterized relationally without committing to constructs as real, internal entities analogous to human cognition.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** The paper states constructs are "defined by their position in a theoretical web" and contrasts this with causal accounts, noting it's "unclear what it would mean for a model to possess reasoning" in a causal sense.
*   **Break Condition:** Relational patterns fail to stabilize across different theoretical formalizations (e.g., CHC vs. an LLM-specific network) or diverse LLM architectures.

### Mechanism 2: Empirical Coherence Over Isolated Scores
*   **Claim:** Benchmark validity is established through empirical coherence with theoretical expectations, not by high absolute performance on a single test.
*   **Mechanism:** The four-step validation process tests whether the pattern of scores across a suite of tasks aligns with the nomological network's predicted correlation structure. This detects if a benchmark is contaminated (e.g., measures memorization) or incomplete (construct underrepresentation).
*   **Core Assumption:** Human psychometric theories (like CHC) provide a transferable, sufficiently rich relational scaffold for structuring LLM assessment.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Section 3 explicitly outlines the four steps, emphasizing content, concurrent, and criterion validity as prerequisites before interpreting convergent/discriminant patterns.
*   **Break Condition:** Systematic empirical-theoretical mismatches persist even after iterative benchmark refinement based on error analysis.

### Mechanism 3: Ontological Avoidance
*   **Claim:** The nomological account avoids the impractical ontological demands of requiring a causal, internal mechanism for each attributed capability.
*   **Mechanism:** By focusing on the semantic and statistical network of relationships between observable task performances, validation remains a feasible, public, and inter-subjective exercise. It constrains interpretation pragmatically without needing to "open the black box."
*   **Core Assumption:** The pragmatic utility of capability language (e.g., "the model reasons") does not depend on metaphysical realism about a homuncular "reasoning module" inside the model.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** The abstract notes the approach "avoids the strong ontological commitments of the causal account." Section 2.1 critiques the causal account (following Borsboom) as difficult to defend for LLMs.
*   **Break Condition:** If achieving predictive adequacy for a broad range of model behaviors *requires* positing and verifying causal mechanisms within models, the purely relational account becomes insufficient.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Nomological Network**
    *   **Why needed here:** Defines the meaning of a capability (e.g., "reasoning") by its web of theoretical relationships to other capabilities and tasks. Without this, benchmarks are isolated behaviors with ambiguous meaning.
    *   **Quick check:** How would you represent "reasoning" as a node linked to "working memory" and "pattern recognition" with edges indicating the type of expected relationship (positive correlation, causal influence)?
*   **Concept: Construct Validity vs. Reliability**
    *   **Why needed here:** Reliability (consistent scores) is meaningless without validity (measuring the intended construct). A benchmark can reliably measure the wrong thing (e.g., training data memorization instead of reasoning).
    *   **Quick check:** What's the difference between a benchmark that *reliably* sorts models by their ability to continue number sequences and one that *validly* measures inductive reasoning?
*   **Concept: Convergent/Discriminant Validity**
    *   **Why needed here:** These tests verify the benchmark's behavior aligns with theory. Convergent: a "reasoning" score should correlate with other reasoning-adjacent benchmarks. Discriminant: it should *not* correlate with benchmarks for unrelated abilities (e.g., emotional intelligence), signaling construct overrepresentation.
    *   **Quick check:** If your "reasoning" benchmark correlates highly with "vocabulary knowledge" scores, what does this suggest about your task design?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Theory Layer (Nomological Network like CHC) -> Specification Layer (Task Definitions, Item Taxonomies) -> Implementation Layer (Benchmark Code, Datasets) -> Validation Layer (Statistical Checks, Leaderboard Correlation Analysis)
*   **Critical path:** Start with theoretical construct articulation -> derive task priorities and sampling plans -> build minimal viable benchmark datasets -> iterate based on validation feedback (convergent/discriminant results, expert review). Skipping or rushing the theory layer leads to invalid capability ascriptions.
*   **Design tradeoffs:** Using mature human psychometric theories (e.g., CHC) versus developing an LLM-specific nomological network. Human theories offer immediate structure and cross-domain comparability but risk anthropomorphism and may misrepresent LLM-specific capability organization.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    1.  **Construct Underrepresentation:** Benchmark tasks cluster on a single, narrow subtype (e.g., only deductive logic puzzles for "reasoning"), failing to capture the breadth defined by the network.
    2.  **Construct Overrepresentation:** Benchmark scores correlate significantly with theoretically unrelated capabilities (e.g., reasoning score correlates with model size or training data epoch), indicating measurement of extraneous factors like scale or memorization.
    3.  **Low Concurrent Validity:** Different, supposedly equivalent benchmarks for the same task produce divergent scores across models, suggesting unstable operationalization.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Nomological Network Mapping:** For "reasoning," extract relationships from CHC theory. Quantify expected correlations (direction and strength) with 3-5 neighboring capabilities (e.g., fluid intelligence, working memory). Document these theoretical weights.
    2.  **Content Validity Stress Test:** For a task like "sequence continuation," generate 100 test items. Engage two independent experts to judge each item's representativeness of the target task and absence of contamination. Calculate inter-rater agreement; revise or discard low-consensus items.
    3.  **Convergent/Discriminant Pilot:** Run 5 diverse LLMs (e.g., different architectures, scales) on your reasoning benchmark plus 2 benchmarks for related capabilities (e.g., working memory tasks) and 2 for unrelated ones (e.g., sentiment analysis). Compute a Spearman correlation matrix of capability scores. Flag any unexpected positive correlations with unrelated capabilities or missing correlations with related ones.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** To what extent are the structural relationships posited by human-centric nomological networks (e.g., CHC theory) transferable and valid for describing cognitive capabilities in LLMs?
*   **Basis in paper:** The paper explicitly questions this transferability, stating: "Applying it to LLMs presupposes that its structural relations are transferable across radically different substrates—a non-trivial assumption." It notes a LLM-specific network may ultimately diverge.
*   **Why unresolved:** LLMs have fundamentally different architectures, training objectives, and data than humans. Correlations between capabilities (e.g., reasoning and working memory) may manifest differently or not at all.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic studies measuring task correlations mapped to CHC strata across a wide, diverse set of LLMs. A stable pattern matching human predictions supports transferability; systematic deviations refute it.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** How can benchmarks be systematically designed and validated to be robust against data contamination and other forms of test-training overlap that threaten content validity for LLMs?
*   **Basis in paper:** The paper identifies data contamination as a key threat: "LLMs are trained on vast corpora... the test may measure memorization... This phenomenon is known as data contamination." It notes validation practices are underdeveloped.
*   **Why unresolved:** Current contamination detection/mitigation is ad-hoc and task-specific. No standardized framework ensures test items are truly novel to a model at evaluation time, a core psychometric requirement.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Widespread adoption of contamination-proof methods (synthetic data generation, precise provenance tracking, contamination-robust scoring) with empirical proof that scores on "clean" benchmarks correlate with scores on guaranteed-novel held-out tasks as the nomological network predicts.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** What is a principled, systematic methodology for resolving conflicts between a nomological network's theoretical predictions and observed benchmark correlation patterns (the Duhem-Quine problem in this context)?
*   **Basis in paper:** The paper directly raises this: "We are confronted with a version of the Duhem–Quine problem... it remains unclear whether '(i) the measure does not adequately measure the target construct, (ii) the theory requires modification, or (iii) some combination of both.'" It suggests initial doubt falls on benchmarks but offers no formal procedure.
*   **Why unresolved:** No diagnostic toolkit exists to attribute mismatches to flawed benchmark design (under/overrepresentation, contamination) versus a flawed network theory, making iterative improvement ambiguous.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** A framework combining: (a) fine-grained error analysis on items, (b) sensitivity analysis of correlation patterns to individual task benchmarks, (c) probes for construct overrepresentation, and (d) criteria for when to revise the network (e.g., mismatches persist across architectures) versus tasks.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** What should constitute the core set of validation protocols (beyond i.i.d. sampling) specifically mandated for LLM capability benchmarks to satisfy the nomological account's requirements for content, concurrent, and criterion validity?
*   **Basis in paper:** The paper contrasts ML's statistical conditions (i.i.d., test independence) with richer psychometric standards (theoretical justification for tasks, concurrent validity between benchmarks, criterion prediction). It implies current LLM benchmarks lack this suite.
*   **Why unresolved:** While psychometrics defines the targets, operationalizing them for generative, black-box LLMs is under-specified. For example, what is "theoretical justification for task formalization" for a natural language reasoning task? How to establish "concurrent validity" when benchmark creators are often the same team?
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Community-agreed standards and artifacts (validation checklists, curated task taxonomies with rationales, mandatory pilot studies) that are tested and shown to reliably detect underrepresentation and overrepresentation when applied to major benchmarks.

## Limitations
*   **Nomological network weights** (e.g., exact factor loadings from CHC theory for LLM tasks) are not specified, hindering operationalization.
*   **Benchmarks with guaranteed content validity and zero contamination** are not identified; constructing them is a major practical hurdle.
*   **The transferability of human psychometric theories (CHC) to LLMs** is a fundamental, untested assumption.
*   **Expert-driven content validation** is costly, subjective, and may not scale to large benchmark suites.
*   **Relational patterns (correlation structures) may shift** dramatically with new model architectures, training regimes, or data, requiring continuous network re-validation.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1 (Relational Construct Definition):** Medium — Theoretically sound and well-argued, but lacks empirical demonstration of stable cross-LLM correlation networks. Related work shows moderate relevance (avg FMR 0.439).
*   **Mechanism 2 (Four-Step Validation Framework):** Medium — Methodologically coherent and addresses a clear gap, but remains a prescriptive proposal without a worked case study or large-scale validation.
*   **Mechanism 3 (Ontological Avoidance):** High — Strong conceptual argument effectively critiques the causal account's applicability to LLMs; the pragmatic benefits are widely acknowledged in related literature on avoiding anthropomorphism.

## Next Checks
1.  **Map and Test a Nomological Network:** Select a target capability (e.g., reasoning). Adopt CHC theory to define expected correlations with at least three neighboring capabilities. Run 5-10 diverse LLMs on benchmarks for all involved tasks and compute the empirical correlation matrix. Compare to theoretical predictions.
2.  **Audit a Major Benchmark for Validity Threats:** Take a widely used reasoning benchmark (e.g., BBH). Conduct a two-pronged audit: (a) expert review of 50-100 items for content validity (representativeness, contamination hints), and (b) technical check for data contamination (e.g., nearest-neighbor search against known training corpora).
3.  **Pilot Convergent/Discriminant Analysis with Clean Tasks:** Using small, purpose-built, contamination-audited task sets for 2-3 related and 2-3 unrelated capabilities, evaluate 5 models. Perform a formal convergent/discriminant validity analysis (e.g., compare pattern of correlations to a priori hypotheses). Document where the pilot fails and hypothesize why (task design vs. network error).
