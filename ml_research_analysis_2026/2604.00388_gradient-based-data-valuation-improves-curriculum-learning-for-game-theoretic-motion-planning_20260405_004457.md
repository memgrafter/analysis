# Gradient-Based Data Valuation Improves Curriculum Learning for Game-Theoretic Motion Planning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00388
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00388
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** TracIn-weighted curriculum reduces planning ADE to 1.704±0.029 m, significantly beating metadata curriculum (p=0.021) and uniform baseline with lower variance.

## Executive Summary
Game-theoretic motion planners like GameFormer struggle with data imbalance where rare interactive scenarios are under-represented. Metadata-based curriculum learning—using handcrafted scenario features to prioritize training—fails to outperform uniform sampling. This paper introduces a gradient-based data valuation method (TracIn) to score each scenario’s contribution to reducing validation loss. These scores drive a three-phase curriculum that weights samples during training. On the nuPlan benchmark, the TracIn-weighted curriculum achieves a new state-of-the-art mean planning ADE of 1.704 m with remarkably low variance across seeds. Critically, full-data importance weighting succeeds where hard selection (training only on top-scoring scenarios) catastrophically fails, and TracIn scores are nearly orthogonal to metadata, revealing that gradient-based valuation captures hidden learning dynamics missed by heuristics.

## Method Summary
The method computes a per-scenario importance score using TracIn: for each training sample, the dot product between its gradient (from a baseline model) and the mean validation gradient estimates how much that sample points in a direction that reduces validation loss. Scores are min-max normalized to [0,1]. A three-phase curriculum schedule then maps epoch number to a per-sample weight: warm-up (epochs 1‑3, weight=1), ramp-up (epochs 4‑8, linearly increasing weight to 1+(w_max‑1)·score), and focus (epochs 9‑20, holding at max weight). Training uses these weights in the loss, preserving full support over all scenarios while upweighting high-value ones. The approach is evaluated on GameFormer trained on the nuPlan mini dataset (5,148 training, 1,286 validation scenarios) with planning ADE as the primary metric.

## Key Results
- TracIn-weighted curriculum achieves mean ADE 1.704±0.029 m, significantly lower than metadata curriculum (1.822±0.014 m; paired t‑test p=0.021, Cohen’s dz=3.88) and uniform baseline (1.772±0.134 m).
- TracIn scores are nearly orthogonal to metadata (Spearman ρ=-0.014), indicating gradient-based valuation captures different, complementary information about scenario utility.
- Hard selection (training only on top 20% TracIn scenarios) catastrophically degrades performance (ADE 3.687 m), while full-data weighting yields the best results.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Gradient Alignment Improves Optimization Direction
TracIn scores estimate how much a training sample’s gradient aligns with the validation loss gradient. Weighting samples by these scores tilts the aggregated mini‑batch gradient toward directions that reduce validation loss, as formalized in Proposition 1. The three‑phase schedule mediates the bias‑variance trade‑off: warm‑up builds robust representations with uniform sampling, then ramp/focus phases gradually increase the influence of high‑alignment samples. Evidence: TracIn curriculum converges faster by epoch 15 (Fig. 6) and achieves significantly lower ADE (p=0.021). Break condition: unreliable if validation gradient is poorly estimated (few validation samples or high noise).

### Mechanism 2: Metadata Heuristics Are Orthogonal to Learning Dynamics
Metadata scores (e.g., minimum TTC, conflict count) are handcrafted features of scenario geometry/kinematics. The near‑zero Spearman correlation (ρ=-0.014) between metadata and TracIn scores shows these features do not predict which samples are useful for reducing validation loss in GameFormer. A scenario with many nearby agents may be “easy” if interactions are stereotypical, while a simple scenario may be “hard” if it exposes an under‑represented loss region. Evidence: Metadata curriculum underperforms uniform baseline (1.822 m vs. 1.772 m) while TracIn excels. Break condition: if model failure modes are perfectly captured by the chosen metadata (unlikely for complex long‑tailed distributions), metadata could become informative.

### Mechanism 3: Full‑Support Weighting Avoids Distribution Collapse
Selecting only high‑TracIn samples discards all low‑score scenarios, creating a training distribution with zero density on excluded regions. If the validation distribution has support there (likely), KL divergence becomes infinite, harming generalization. Weighting preserves non‑zero probability for all samples, modulating emphasis without altering support. Evidence: Top‑20% TracIn selection yields ADE 3.687 m vs. full‑data weighting 1.704 m (Section IV‑F). Proposition 3 formalizes the KL argument. Break condition: if the high‑score subset already covers the entire validation distribution (rare), hard selection might not fail.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: TracIn (gradient‑based data valuation)** – Computes sample importance as the dot product between its training gradient and a target (e.g., validation gradient). *Why needed*: Assigns model‑specific utility without handcrafted features. *Quick check*: Is TracIn score based on loss value or gradient alignment?  
- **Concept: Curriculum learning schedule** – Gradually shifts training distribution over epochs. *Why needed*: Mediates bias‑variance trade‑off when introducing importance weighting. *Quick check*: What is the purpose of the warm‑up phase (e=1–3) in Eq. 3?  
- **Concept: Full‑support reweighting vs. hard selection** – Importance weighting keeps all data in support; hard selection discards a subset. *Why needed*: Prevents catastrophic distribution mismatch with validation data. *Quick check*: Why does selecting the top 20% of TracIn‑scored scenarios hurt performance despite those being “high‑value” samples?  
- **Concept: Game‑theoretic motion planning** – Models interactions among multiple agents via game theory (e.g., Nash equilibrium). *Why needed*: Addresses interactive scenarios where agents react to each other. *Quick check*: How does GameFormer differ from single‑agent planners?  
- **Concept: nuPlan benchmark** – Large‑scale autonomous driving dataset with long‑tail interactive scenarios. *Why needed*: Standard testbed for motion planning with realistic interaction diversity. *Quick check*: What are the key differences between nuPlan mini and full splits?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Scoring module (computes per‑scenario importance: metadata features, TracIn dot product, or hybrid rank‑average) → Curriculum scheduler (maps epoch `e` to weight `w(z_i,e)` via Eq. 3 with `e_warm=3`, `e_ramp=8`, `w_max=3.0`) → Training loop (supervised with weighted loss; per‑sample loss multiplied by current weight) → Validation evaluator (computes ADE/FDE, saves best checkpoint).

**Critical path**  
1. Pre‑train baseline model `θ₀` for E0 epochs (unspecified, assumed 5).  
2. Compute TracIn scores: one forward‑backward per training sample with `θ₀`, dot with mean validation gradient.  
3. Initialize weights from chosen score (TracIn, metadata, hybrid).  
4. For epoch 1–20: update weights per schedule, train with weighted loss, validate, save best.  
5. Repeat for 3 seeds; report mean ADE ± std.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Compute vs. generality*: TracIn requires one extra forward‑backward per sample (~46 min for 5k scenarios). Metadata is instant. Hybrid incurs both costs.  
- *Signal strength vs. dilution*: Metadata provides no signal (orthogonal), so hybrid attenuates TracIn’s signal by √2 (Prop 2). Pure TracIn optimal if metadata uninformative.  
- *Weighting strength*: `w_max` trades alignment gain vs. variance. Paper uses 3.0 yielding ~18% effective sample reduction (`n_eff/n≈0.82`). Too high increases variance; too low yields negligible gain.

**Failure signatures**  
1. **Hard selection collapse**: ADE spikes (>2× baseline) when training on a subset of high‑score samples.  
2. **Loss‑based SPL instability**: High cross‑seed variance (CV=19.5%) and seed‑specific catastrophes (e.g., ADE 2.555 m).  
3. **Metadata curriculum underperformance**: Mean ADE worse than uniform baseline, indicating scoring provides zero/negative signal.  
4. **Incorrect weight schedule**: Weights not following warm‑up/ramp/focus pattern; check per‑epoch weight plots.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Replicate scoring correlations**: Compute metadata, TracIn, and training loss scores on a small subset; verify Spearman ρ(TracIn, metadata)≈0 and ρ(TracIn, loss)>0.  
2. **Ablation: weighting vs. selection**: Train two models with TracIn: (a) full‑data weighting (Eq. 3), (b) hard selection (top 20% subset). Expect (b) to degrade significantly (~2× ADE).  
3. **Curriculum phase sensitivity**: Sweep `w_max` ({1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 5.0}) with fixed `e_warm=3`, `e_ramp=8`. Expect non‑monotonic effect; reported `w_max=3.0` should be near optimum.

## Limitations
- **Unspecified baseline epoch count** for TracIn scoring (E0) may affect score reliability.
- **Validation gradient aggregation details** (batched vs. full, sum vs. mean) not explicitly described; could introduce subtle biases.
- **Evaluation limited to nuPlan mini**; generalization to full nuPlan or other planners unknown.
- **Schedule hyperparameters** (`e_warm`, `e_ramp`, `w_max`) were fixed; sensitivity not fully explored.

## Confidence
- **Main outcome (ADE reduction)**: High – three seeds, paired t‑test p=0.021, large effect (dz=3.88).  
- **Mechanism 1 (gradient alignment)**: Medium – supported by Proposition 1 and faster convergence, but alignment not directly measured.  
- **Mechanism 2 (metadata orthogonality)**: High – Spearman ρ≈–0.014 and metadata curriculum underperforms uniform.  
- **Mechanism 3 (hard selection failure)**: High – direct ablation: top‑20% TracIn yields ADE 3.687 m vs. full‑data weighting 1.704 m.
