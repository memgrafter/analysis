# When Actions Go Off-Task: Detecting and Correcting Misaligned Actions in Computer-Use Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08995
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08995
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** DEACTION achieves >15% absolute F1 improvement on MisActBench and reduces attack success rate by >90% in adversarial settings while maintaining or improving task success in benign environments.

## Executive Summary
Computer-use agents (CUAs) automate tasks by interacting with graphical interfaces, but they can deviate from user intent due to malicious prompts or internal errors, causing security and reliability issues. Existing approaches often lack both real-time detection and practical remediation that preserve efficiency.

This paper introduces DEACTION, a runtime guardrail that combines a fast conservative filter, a systematic analysis using narrative summaries of interaction history, and an iterative correction loop. DEACTION outperforms prior methods by >15% absolute F1 on the MisActBench benchmark and, in online adversarial tests, cuts attack success by over 90% without harming benign task completion.

## Method Summary
DEACTION operates as a lightweight, run-time monitor for CUAs. It employs a two-stage detection pipeline: a fast, conservative LLM-based check that classifies actions as clearly aligned or ambiguous; ambiguous actions are forwarded to a systematic analysis module that reasons over a compact narrative summary of the interaction history and the current screenshot. This design reduces average latency while maintaining high accuracy.

When misalignment is detected, DEACTION generates structured four-component feedback (injection analysis, action understanding, outcome prediction, misalignment analysis) and sends it back to the CUA. The agent then revises the action, and the new proposal re-enters the pipeline. This iterative correction loop runs up to three times, allowing many misaligned actions to be transformed into aligned ones without halting the task.

## Key Results
- **Detection Performance:** >15% absolute F1 improvement over prior methods on the MisActBench benchmark, with high precision (>85%) and recall.
- **Attack Mitigation:** >90% reduction in attack success rate in online adversarial evaluation, while task success in benign environments remains equal or improves.
- **Correction Effectiveness:** 78% of flagged misaligned actions are successfully corrected via the iterative feedback loop.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Conservative Fast Filtering
- **Claim:** DEACTION reduces false positives while maintaining high recall by routing only ambiguous actions to deeper analysis.
- **Mechanism:** A lightweight LLM prompt assesses whether the proposed action is unambiguously task-serving. If yes, the action is immediately approved; otherwise it proceeds to systematic analysis.
- **Core assumption:** Most actions in a typical CUA execution are clearly aligned and can be verified with minimal cost.
- **Evidence:** The paper describes the fast check as a "conservative screening mechanism" that bypasses further analysis for clearly aligned actions (Sec 4.1). Ablations show it preserves recall while improving precision.
- **Break condition:** If the majority of steps are misaligned, most actions will be sent to systematic analysis, erasing the latency benefit.

### Mechanism 2: Narrative Summarization for Compact Context
- **Claim:** Narrative summaries of past interactions provide a semantically rich, low-token context that improves alignment detection accuracy and reduces latency compared to raw screenshots.
- **Mechanism:** After each step, a summarizer converts (observation, action, next observation) into a concise natural language description. These summaries replace raw screenshot sequences in the guardrail's input, preserving long-horizon context with far fewer tokens.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs can reliably reason about task alignment from natural language summaries without needing raw visual input at every step.
- **Evidence:** Section 4.1 highlights token savings; Figure 4 shows narrative summaries outperforming both "Actions Only" and "Actions + Observations" (raw screenshots) in F1, with lower latency (11s vs 18s).
- **Break condition:** If the summarizer omits critical visual details (e.g., UI element states, injected text), detection accuracy may suffer on visually subtle misalignments.

### Mechanism 3: Iterative Correction with Structured Feedback
- **Claim:** Structured feedback from systematic analysis converts many misaligned actions into aligned ones without blocking, preserving task progress under attack.
- **Mechanism:** Upon flagging a misalignment, DEACTION outputs a JSON with four reasoned components; this feedback is sent to the CUA, which generates a revised action. The new action is re-evaluated, repeating up to three iterations.
- **Core assumption:** The CUA can incorporate detailed feedback to self-correct within a few attempts, and the provided reasoning is sufficiently actionable.
- **Evidence:** Section 5.2 reports that 78% of flagged misaligned actions are corrected through the loop, and utility under attack improves dramatically (e.g., from 44% to 76% for Claude Sonnet 4.5).
- **Break condition:** If the CUA ignores or misinterprets the feedback, the correction loop fails, leading to repeated flags and eventual blocking after the retry limit.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept 1: Action Alignment Definition (Intent-Centric)**  
- *Why needed:* DEACTION requires a precise target: an action is misaligned if it serves injected intent over user intent, causes unauthorized harm, or is task-irrelevant. This definition guides detection.  
- *Quick check:* For user task "export a document to PDF", action "deletes the original document after export" is misaligned because it causes irreversible harm not requested by the user, violating the alignment conditions.

**Concept 2: Narrative History Representation for Long-Horizon Context**  
- *Why needed:* Assessment of alignment often depends on many previous steps. Narrative summaries provide a compact, semantically rich representation that keeps token usage low while preserving context.  
- *Quick check:* A 50-token summary versus 10 screenshots: the summary is far cheaper but may miss fine visual details like exact UI states or hidden text.

**Concept 3: Structured Four-Component Reasoning for Alignment**  
- *Why needed:* Systematic analysis must produce actionable feedback; the four components (Injection Analysis, Action Understanding, Outcome Prediction, Misalignment Analysis) cover all aspects needed for correction.  
- *Quick check:* For an action that clicks a website ad pop-up's close button, each component would analyze whether the action serves injected content, what the agent intended, the expected outcome, and whether it diverges from the user's task.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
- **Fast Check Module:** Lightweight LLM; inputs: user objective, history summary, current action; outputs binary decision + brief thought. Routes to exit or systematic analysis.
- **Narrative Summarizer:** Runs in parallel after each step; inputs: (screenshot_before, action, screenshot_after); outputs concise natural language summary appended to history.
- **Systematic Analysis Module:** Heavyweight LLM; inputs: user objective, history summary, current screenshot, current action; performs four-component reasoning; outputs structured JSON with thought and misalignment conclusion.
- **Iterative Correction Loop:** Controller that, upon misalignment, formats the structured reasoning into feedback for the CUA, receives a revised action, and repeats detection (up to 3 times).

Flow example (critical path):
```
Proposed Action → Fast Check → (aligned? → continue)
                → (not aligned) → Systematic Analysis → (aligned? → continue)
                                 → (misaligned) → Correction Loop (up to 3 iterations) → revised Action → back to Fast Check
```
Narrative Summarizer updates history asynchronously.

### Critical Path
1. Agent proposes an action.
2. Fast Check evaluates; if clearly aligned, action executes and Summarizer updates history.
3. If Fast Check is uncertain, Systematic Analysis re-evaluates using full context.
4. If Systematic Analysis flags misalignment, Correction Loop begins: structured feedback sent to CUA, which returns a revised action.
5. Revised action re-enters Fast Check; loop repeats up to three times before final block.

### Design Trade-offs
- **Latency vs. Accuracy:** Two-stage design lowers average latency by filtering routine steps, but gains shrink when many actions are misaligned.
- **Generality vs. Specificity:** Narrative summaries and a general four-component prompt make the system adaptable across tasks, but may miss domain-specific cues that specialized detectors could catch.
- **Correction vs. Blocking:** The iterative feedback prioritizes task completion over simple blocking, adding complexity and risk that some CUAs cannot act on feedback.

### Failure Signatures
- High false positive rate in Fast Check (too many aligned actions sent to systematic analysis, eroding latency advantage).
- Low correction success rate (<50%), indicating feedback is not actionable or the CUA cannot self-correct.
- Significant performance drop when using narrative summaries compared to raw screenshots, implying the summarizer loses crucial visual details.

### First Experiments
1. **Offline replication on MisActBench:** Implement DEACTION with a chosen LLM backbone (e.g., GPT-4.1). Run the two-stage pipeline on all 2,264 steps from the benchmark. Report precision, recall, accuracy, and F1 to confirm >15% absolute F1 improvement and >80% F1 score.
2. **Ablation 1 – History Representation:** On a fixed subset, compare three conditions: (a) actions only, (b) actions + raw screenshots, (c) actions + narrative summaries. Measure both F1 and per-step latency to validate the trade‑off.
3. **Ablation 2 – Two‑Stage Routing:** On the same subset, compare: (a) Fast Check only, (b) Systematic Analysis only, (c) Fast Check + Systematic (default). Measure F1 and average latency to demonstrate that the default matches systematic-only F1 while reducing latency and improving precision over fast-only.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly identify open questions beyond its scope.

## Limitations
- Under heavy attack (high proportion of misaligned steps), the fast check routes most actions to systematic analysis, diminishing the latency advantage.
- Narrative summaries may omit subtle visual cues needed to detect certain misalignments, potentially reducing accuracy.
- Generalizability to new CUA architectures, domains, or attack types remains an open question, as evaluation was limited to specific model‑task combinations.

## Confidence
- **Two-stage pipeline (high recall, reduced false positives):** High confidence.
- **Narrative history summarization (accuracy & token savings):** High confidence.
- **Iterative correction (effective remediation):** Medium confidence.
- **Overall improvements (≥15% F1 gain on MisActBench, ≥90% attack reduction):** High confidence.

## Next Checks
1. **Fast check routing under high misalignment:** Inject ≥70% misaligned steps into a benchmark task; measure the fraction sent to systematic analysis and resulting average latency.
2. **Summarizer fidelity on visual‑detail attacks:** Design attacks that rely on subtle visual cues (e.g., hidden text, specific UI states); compare detection accuracy when using narrative summaries versus raw screenshots.
3. **Cross‑CUA correction robustness:** Deploy DEACTION with three distinct CUAs (e.g., GPT-4, Claude, an open‑source agent); record correction success rates and analyze failure modes.
