# SPQ: An Ensemble Technique for Large Language Model Compression

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.18420
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18420
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: SPQ reduces weight memory by up to 75% on LLaMA-2-7B while improving perplexity and throughput via an ensemble of SVD, pruning, and quantization.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) are expensive to deploy due to high memory and computational demands. SPQ introduces an ensemble compression technique that combines three complementary methods—variance-retained singular value decomposition (SVD) on attention layers, activation-based structured pruning on MLP layers, and 8-bit linear quantization on all linear layers—applied in a layer-aware manner. On LLaMA-2-7B, SPQ reduces weight memory by up to 75% (to 6.86 GB) while achieving lower perplexity (4.91 vs. 5.47) and comparable downstream task performance. Against strong baselines like GPTQ, SPQ uses less memory and delivers up to 1.9× higher inference throughput. The ensemble consistently outperforms any single or pairwise combination of its components, demonstrating the synergy of the integrated approach.

SPQ's layer-specific targeting ensures that each compression technique is applied where it is most effective, avoiding the accuracy degradation common in uniform compression strategies. The method is training-free aside from optional LoRA fine-tuning, making it practical for rapid deployment of compressed LLMs on resource-constrained devices.

## Method Summary
SPQ employs an ordered pipeline of three compression steps applied sequentially. First, variance-retained SVD is applied exclusively to attention projection layers, decomposing each weight matrix via SVD and truncating singular vectors to retain a user-specified fraction of total variance (ε). This yields low-rank factors that reduce memory and computation for attention. Second, activation-based structured pruning targets MLP layers: neurons are ranked by the mean magnitude of their activations over a calibration set, and per-layer pruning ratios are derived via a normalization function (log-inverse scaling). Entire neurons (rows of the weight matrix and bias) are removed, producing hardware-friendly sparsity. Third, all remaining linear layers undergo symmetric 8-bit linear quantization, with scale factors computed per-tensor or per-channel; hybrid selection rules (e.g., MSH) determine the scaling mode per layer to balance accuracy and throughput. After these steps, optional LoRA fine-tuning can be applied to recover any lost accuracy. The entire process is post-training and requires no gradient computation.

Key design choices include: using a variance threshold ε for SVD to retain a target proportion of spectral energy; calculating neuron importance via calibration-activation magnitudes and normalizing per-layer pruning ratios; and selecting quantization scaling modes (tensor, channel, or hybrids) to balance perplexity and throughput. Empirical results on LLaMA-2-7B show the ensemble outperforms individual components and baselines like GPTQ.

## Key Results
- On LLaMA-2-7B, SPQ reduces weight memory by up to 75% (to 6.86 GB) while improving WikiText-2 perplexity from 5.47 to 4.91.
- Downstream task accuracy on GSM8K and TruthfulQA matches or exceeds the original model.
- Compared to GPTQ, SPQ uses less memory (6.86 GB vs. 7.16 GB) and achieves up to 1.9× higher inference throughput; the ensemble also outperforms any single or pairwise combination of its components.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Variance-Retained SVD on Attention Layers
- **Claim:** Attention layer weight matrices contain low-rank structure that can be truncated via SVD without significant accuracy loss, specifically when retaining singular values that preserve a high fraction of total variance.
- **Mechanism:** For an attention projection matrix \(W\), SVD decomposes it into \(U\Sigma V^\top\). The top-\(k\) singular values are retained such that \(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k} \sigma_i^2}{\sum_{j=1}^{\min(m,n)} \sigma_j^2} \geq \epsilon\). The rank ratio \(r = k / \min(m,n)\) controls compression. This reduces the matrix to two smaller matrices (\(U_k\) and \(\Sigma_k V_k^\top\)), lowering weight memory and compute for that layer.
- **Core assumption:** The variance spectrum of attention weight matrices is such that a substantial portion of the total energy (variance) is captured by a relatively small number of singular vectors, and discarding the rest preserves the essential transformation for attention computation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "SVD reduces attention projections into compact low-rank factors."
  - [section 4.1] Explicit formulation of variance-retained SVD with threshold \(\epsilon\).
  - [section 6.1 & Figure 2] Empirical results showing SVD applied *only* to attention layers yields lower perplexity and higher throughput than applying it to MLP or all layers at matched memory.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., SVD-LLM, ARA) also explore SVD for LLMs, supporting the premise that attention layers are a primary target, though SPQ's specific variance-threshold approach is distinct.
- **Break condition:** Perplexity increases sharply if the variance threshold \(\epsilon\) is set too low (over-aggressive rank reduction), as seen in Figure 7 where SVD-only performance degrades beyond ~15% compression.

### Mechanism 2: Activation-Based Structured Pruning on MLP Layers
- **Claim:** MLP neurons can be pruned in a structured, hardware-friendly manner by ranking their importance using activation statistics on a calibration set, with layer-wise pruning ratios normalized to preserve overall capacity where needed.
- **Mechanism:** For each neuron \(j\) in an MLP layer, compute a magnitude \(m_j^{(l)}\) (L1 or L2 norm of its activation vector over the calibration set). Layer-wise means \(a^{(l)}\) are derived and mapped to per-layer pruning ratios \(r^{(l)}\) via a normalization function (e.g., log-inverse scaling). Entire neurons (rows of weight matrix + bias) are removed based on \(r^{(l)}\).
- **Core assumption:** The average magnitude of a neuron's activation over a representative dataset correlates with its functional importance to the model's output; less active neurons are more redundant and can be removed with minimal impact.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "pruning removes redundant neurons in the feedforward multilayer perceptron (MLP) layers."
  - [section 4.2] Full specification of activation magnitude calculation and three normalization strategies (linear, log, sigmoid) to derive \(r^{(l)}\).
  - [section 6.2 & Figure 3] Empirical comparison showing that pruning *only* MLP layers (vs. attention or all layers) results in lower perplexity and better throughput at similar weight memory.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers on structured pruning (e.g., LLM-Pruner, SlimLLM) validate activation or gradient-based importance, but SPQ's layer-specific and normalization approach is a simplified variant.
- **Break condition:** Perplexity rises notably when the maximum pruning ratio \(r_{\max}\) exceeds ~0.30 or when aggressive pruning is applied to layers with high activation means (low \(n^{(l)}\)), as indicated by the upward trend in Figure 4.

### Mechanism 3: 8-Bit Linear Quantization on All Linear Layers
- **Claim:** Post-training 8-bit symmetric linear quantization can be uniformly applied to all remaining (potentially SVD-compressed and pruned) linear layers with minimal accuracy loss, providing an additional ~25% memory reduction and enabling efficient integer inference.
- **Mechanism:** For each weight element \(W_{i,j}\), compute a per-tensor or per-channel scale \(s = \max(|W|) / (2^{b-1}-1)\) with \(b=8\). Quantize to \(\hat{W}_{i,j} = \text{round}(W_{i,j} / s)\) and dequantize as \(W_{i,j}^{\text{quant}} = \hat{W}_{i,j} \cdot s\). SPQ evaluates hybrid modes (LNH, PBH, MSH) to selectively use per-channel scaling for more sensitive layers.
- **Core assumption:** After structural modifications (SVD, pruning), the weight distributions in the remaining linear layers are well-behaved (not excessively outlier-prone) such that uniform 8-bit linear quantization does not introduce critical error.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "8-bit quantization uniformly compresses all linear layers."
  - [section 4.3] Description of 8-bit symmetric linear quantization and the five scaling modes (tensor, channel, hybrids).
  - [section 6.3 & Figure 5] Results showing perplexity is nearly identical across all 5 quantization modes at 8-bit, with hybrid schemes offering slight perplexity improvements over pure per-tensor.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (GPTQ, AWQ) use more complex quantization (e.g., mixed-precision, calibration), but SPQ's simpler linear approach still works within the ensemble, suggesting the prior compression steps mitigate sensitivity.
- **Break condition:** Performance collapses if bit-width is reduced to 4-bit without additional measures (mentioned in Section 6.5 and Figure 7), or if per-channel scaling is applied indiscriminately without hybrid selection, potentially hurting throughput.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) for low-rank approximation**
  - *Why needed here:* To understand how SPQ reduces attention layer parameters by factorizing weight matrices and retaining only the most significant singular vectors based on variance.
  - *Quick check question:* What does the variance threshold \(\epsilon\) control in SPQ's SVD, and how does it differ from allocating a fixed rank per layer?
- **Concept: Structured pruning vs. unstructured sparsity**
  - *Why needed here:* SPQ prunes entire neurons (rows) in MLP layers, which is hardware-friendly. This contrasts with element-wise pruning that may not yield speedups on standard hardware.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might activation-based pruning (using calibration data activations) be preferred over weight-magnitude pruning for LLMs?
- **Concept: Post-training quantization (PTQ) and scaling strategies**
  - *Why needed here:* To grasp how SPQ's final 8-bit compression step works without retraining, and the trade-offs between per-tensor (faster, slightly less accurate) and per-channel (more accurate, slower) scaling.
  - *Quick check question:* In SPQ's hybrid quantization (e.g., MSH), what metric determines whether a layer gets per-channel or per-tensor scaling?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** The pipeline is: `Original Model` → [1] `Variance-SVD` (applied to *all attention projection layers*) → [2] `Activation-Pruning` (applied to *all MLP layers*) → [3] `8-bit Linear Quantization` (applied to *all remaining linear layers*, including those modified by SVD/pruning) → [4] `LoRA Fine-tuning` (lightweight adaptation on selected layers, all original weights frozen).
- **Critical path:** The order is strictly **SVD → Pruning → Quantization**. Quantization is last because it operates on the final modified weights. Changing the order (e.g., quantizing then pruning) would alter the quantization scale calculations and likely degrade performance.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Layer-aware allocation* (SVD only on attention, pruning only on MLP) vs. uniform application: The paper shows layer-specific targeting yields better perplexity.
  - *Quantization mode*: Per-channel gives lower perplexity but lower throughput; hybrids (MSH, PBH, LNH) attempt to balance this. Per-tensor is fastest.
  - *LoRA budget*: The paper uses a fixed 200 steps; more steps might recover accuracy further but increase compression time.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Perplexity spike** at high compression ratios (e.g., >80%) especially if SVD variance threshold \(\epsilon\) is too low or pruning \(r_{\max}\) is too high.
  - **Throughput drop** if per-channel quantization is overused (every layer) instead of via a hybrid rule.
  - **Model-specific sensitivity**: Small models (e.g., LLaMA-3.2-1B) show perplexity increase where larger models (LLaMA-2-7B) may even improve (Table 4). Applying the same hyperparameters across disparate model families may not be optimal.
- **First 3 experiments (concrete validation steps for a new engineer):**
  1. **Reproduce layer-specific targeting:** Run ablations applying SVD to (a) only attention layers, (b) only MLP layers, (c) all layers. Plot perplexity vs. weight memory as in Figure 2. Expect (a) to dominate.
  2. **Validate pruning normalization:** For MLP pruning, test the three normalization strategies (linear, log, sigmoid) with L1 and L2 activation magnitudes, sweeping \(r_{\max}\). Plot as in Figure 4. Expect log-inverse with L1 to be optimal.
  3. **Test quantization hybrids:** Keep SVD (e.g., \(\epsilon=0.94\)) and pruning (e.g., \(r_{\max}=0.05\)) fixed. Compare the 5 quantization modes (tensor, channel, LNH, PBH, MSH) on perplexity and throughput as in Figure 5. Identify which hybrid (if any) beats pure channel/tensor for your hardware.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: optimal SVD variance threshold ε and pruning ratio r_max for LLaMA-2-7B degrade on smaller models, causing sharp perplexity increases.
- Unclear contribution of LoRA: The reported gains include LoRA fine-tuning; it is unknown how much improvement is due to the ensemble itself.
- Narrow evaluation: Only three datasets (WikiText-2, GSM8K, TruthfulQA) are tested; recent 4-bit baselines like AWQ are omitted, possibly overstating benefits.

## Confidence
- Ensemble superiority over single/pairwise combinations: **Medium**
- Memory reduction with improved perplexity on LLaMA-2-7B: **High**
- Throughput advantage over GPTQ (1.9×): **Medium**
