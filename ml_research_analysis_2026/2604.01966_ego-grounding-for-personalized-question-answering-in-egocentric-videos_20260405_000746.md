# Ego-Grounding for Personalized Question-Answering in Egocentric Videos

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01966
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01966
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** State-of-the-art MLLMs (GPT-5: 46.1%, Qwen3-VL: 36.4%) perform far below humans (84.7%) on personalized egocentric QA, with accuracy decaying sharply for questions about past events not currently visible.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the MyEgo dataset to evaluate "ego-grounding"—the ability to understand first-person references ("me", "my things", "my past") in long-form egocentric videos. Results show leading multimodal LLMs fail at stable, long-range representation of the camera-wearer and personal objects, leading to poor performance on questions requiring memory of past events. Supplying key evidence frames helps but gains decay with temporal distance. Neither model scaling nor chain-of-thought reasoning closes the gap, highlighting fundamental limitations in persistent memory and personalized reasoning for egocentric AI assistants.

## Method Summary
The study conducts zero-shot evaluation of existing multimodal LLMs on the new MyEgo dataset (541 long videos, 5,012 personalized QA pairs). Models process uniformly sampled frames (typically 32 at 1 fps) up to the question timestamp. Inputs use explicit first-person prompting. Evaluation compares open-ended and multiple-choice accuracy against human performance. Controlled experiments vary frame sampling strategies and include oracle key-frame inputs to probe temporal reasoning limits. No model training is performed; the focus is on diagnostic analysis of inherent model capabilities.

## Key Results
- Human performance on personalized QA: 84.7%. Top models (GPT-5: 46.1%, Qwen3-VL: 36.4%) show a massive gap.
- Accuracy drops sharply for "Previous" questions (answer not visible at query moment) versus "Current" questions.
- Providing ground-truth evidence frames boosts performance, but improvement decays rapidly as temporal distance between evidence and question increases.
- Larger model scale and chain-of-thought reasoning yield no consistent improvements.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Models fail to maintain stable representations of the camera-wearer and personal objects over time, causing accuracy to drop sharply for questions about past events.
- **Mechanism:** MLLMs process videos in short, fixed-length frame windows (typically 8–32 frames) and lack an explicit memory component. This forces the model to rely on transient appearance cues present in the current context. When the relevant entity leaves the scene, the representation is discarded and cannot be retrieved later.
- **Core assumption:** Models do not build persistent, entity-centric memory traces across the video timeline; instead, they perform short-horizon perceptual reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 4.2 on improvement with explicit evidence but decay over time; Section 4.3 (Fig. 5) showing accuracy decline with temporal distance; neighboring datasets (EgoToM, EGOILLUSION) reporting similar temporal reasoning bottlenecks.
- **Break condition:** If a model maintains consistent grounding on "previous" questions after long gaps (> 1 minute) without explicit key frames, the memory‑decay mechanism would be invalidated.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Supplying explicit key evidence frames boosts performance, but the gain decays as the temporal distance between the evidence and the question increases.
- **Mechanism:** Providing the ground‑truth frame eliminates the need for the model to retrieve the relevant information from its limited internal memory, thereby improving accuracy. However, as temporal distance grows, the model must still integrate that frame with the surrounding context or other cues from the question moment, which it does poorly. The decay suggests that even with perfect evidence, the model cannot effectively link past and present.
- **Core assumption:** The model’s reasoning is input‑bound; its effective context window is too small or its ability to temporally bind information across non‑contiguous inputs is weak.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Table 3 showing Q&A‑moment input yields larger gains on "Previous" questions than uniform sampling, but improvement still diminishes for questions far from the answer; Section 4.3 showing stability only when question is close to evidence; Long-context models (LongVU, LongVA) not overcoming decay despite more frames.
- **Break condition:** A system that shows no decay in improvement from key‑frame provision regardless of temporal distance would refute this mechanism.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Neither increasing model scale nor employing chain‑of‑thought (CoT) reasoning consistently improves ego‑grounding accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** Scaling parameters and adding explicit reasoning steps primarily enhance general language and visual capabilities but do not address the core deficit of persistent entity memory. The bottleneck is not knowledge or reasoning depth but the inability to maintain and retrieve stable self‑/object representations across time.
- **Core assumption:** The fundamental limitation lies in the architectural lack of long‑range memory rather than in reasoning or capacity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 4.2 noting no consistent improvements for thinking variants; Table 2 showing larger models not uniformly beating smaller ones within the same family; other egocentric benchmarks (EgoThink, VidEgoThink) showing similar scaling inefficacy.
- **Break condition:** If a clear scaling law emerges for ego‑grounding (e.g., larger models consistently achieve human‑level performance) or if CoT produces statistically significant gains across models, this mechanism fails.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input Processor → Frame Sampler → (Optional) Entity Tracker → Memory Module → Retriever → Reasoning Engine (MLLM) → Output Formatter

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Ingest video and preprocess.  
  2. Sample frames up to question timestamp (e.g., 1 fps, fixed count like 32).  
  3. Run entity detection/tracking if using structured memory.  
  4. Index memory with embeddings and temporal metadata.  
  5. At query: locate question timestamp, retrieve top‑k relevant memory entries.  
  6. Fuse retrieved context with recent frames and feed to MLLM.  
  7. Generate answer and evaluate.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Frame sampling vs. full processing: Uniform sampling reduces compute but may miss rare cues; backward sampling improves relevance for recent queries but ignores long history.  
  - Structured vs. implicit memory: Structured memory yields precise entity IDs but adds complexity and error propagation; implicit memory (long-context MLLM) is simpler but currently insufficient.  
  - Memory granularity: Storing every frame vs. key event summaries; finer granularity increases retrieval flexibility but also storage and noise.  
  - Model size vs. efficiency: Larger MLLMs have more capacity but still lack memory; specialized memory‑augmented architectures may offer better performance per FLOP.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Temporal decay: Performance drops markedly when the answer is not visible at the query moment.  
  - Identity confusion: Mixing the camera‑wearer with other people, especially in multi-person scenes.  
  - Object misattribution: Selecting a visually similar distractor instead of the true personal object.  
  - Current-frame bias: Answering based solely on the most salient content in the final frame, ignoring past context.  
  - Inconsistency: Same model yields contradictory answers to semantically identical questions asked at different times.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Baseline replication: Implement a vanilla pipeline (e.g., Qwen‑VL with uniform 32‑frame sampling) on MyEgo’s test split to reproduce reported performance gaps and validate failure modes.  
  2. Temporal sensitivity test: Split the test set by temporal distance (0‑2 s, 2‑20 s, 20‑60 s, 60 s+) and evaluate the baseline. Plot accuracy decay to quantify the gap between “Current” and “Previous” questions.  
  3. Key‑frame augmentation: Modify input to always include the ground‑truth answer moment frames (as an oracle). Measure improvement across temporal bins; expect larger gains on “Previous” questions but still decaying with distance, confirming the retrieval limitation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can MLLMs maintain stable, long-range representations of the camera-wearer and their personal objects over time in egocentric videos?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper states that "models fail to maintain stable representations of the camera-wearer and their objects over time, with accuracy dropping sharply for questions about past events not currently visible." It also highlights the need for "stronger long-range memory" in the conclusion.
- **Why unresolved:** Current MLLMs process only 8–128 frames at a time and rely on short-term appearance cues, unable to integrate information across minutes. They lack mechanisms for persistent identity and object tracking, leading to rapid performance decay as temporal distance increases.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and evaluation of memory-augmented architectures (e.g., external memory banks, recurrent state updates) on MyEgo, demonstrating stable or improved accuracy for "Previous" questions without decay over time.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why do model scaling and chain-of-thought reasoning fail to consistently improve ego-grounding performance, and what alternative approaches could overcome this limitation?
- **Basis in paper:** The abstract notes that "neither model scaling nor chain-of-thought reasoning consistently improves results." Experiments confirm that "thinking" models and larger parameter sizes yield no reliable gains, contrary to trends in general video understanding.
- **Why unresolved:** Ego-grounding may require implicit, integrated spatio-temporal reasoning about first-person references rather than explicit stepwise reasoning. Scaling general video models does not address the core challenges of identity disambiguation and long-term memory.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative studies of models with specialized training objectives (e.g., contrastive learning for ego-identity, memory-pretraining) showing significant improvements on MyEgo where scaling and CoT failed. Ablations isolating the impact of architectural changes versus scale.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What architectural or representational advances are needed to enable MLLMs to reliably distinguish the camera-wearer from other people and track personal objects among visually similar distractors in egocentric scenes?
- **Basis in paper:** The introduction emphasizes that models "must separate the camera-wearer from nearby people, track 'my things' through visually ambiguous scenes." The dataset specifically requires disambiguating ego-person and ego-objects from distractors.
- **Why unresolved:** Current models frequently confuse the camera-wearer with bystanders and misattribute objects, as shown in failure examples. They lack robust spatial reasoning and identity-specific grounding mechanisms tailored to the egocentric perspective.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Introduction of modules that explicitly model egocentric cues (e.g., hand segmentation, gaze direction) and evaluate on MyEgo's disambiguation questions, achieving human-level accuracy in distinguishing self from others and personal from non-personal objects.

## Limitations
- **Dataset specificity:** MyEgo’s long-form, personalized videos may not generalize to all real-world egocentric scenarios.
- **Model version mismatch:** Key results use unreleased models (GPT-5, Qwen3-VL); reproductions use approximations, risking performance gaps.
- **Human benchmark variance:** The human ceiling score's inter-annotator agreement is unreported, affecting confidence in the absolute gap.

## Confidence
- Memory decay as primary bottleneck: **Medium-High**
- Key-frame provision decay: **Medium-High**
- Scaling/CoT inefficacy: **Medium**
