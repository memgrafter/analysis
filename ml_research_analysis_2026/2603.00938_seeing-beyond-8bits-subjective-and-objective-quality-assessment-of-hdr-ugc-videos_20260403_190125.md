# Seeing Beyond 8bits: Subjective and Objective Quality Assessment of HDR-UGC Videos

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00938
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00938
- Reference count: 23
- Primary result: HDR-Q achieves state-of-the-art HDR-VQA performance (PLCC 0.9118, SRCC 0.9206, RMSE 5.1594, KRCC 0.7218) on the new Beyond8Bits dataset and generalizes robustly to LIVE-HDR and SFV+HDR benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
HDR user-generated content (UGC) videos exhibit unique distortions such as near-black crushing and highlight clipping that are not captured by existing SDR-centric video quality assessment (VQA) models. To address this gap, the authors introduce Beyond8Bits, a large-scale HDR-UGC dataset with ~44K videos and over 1.5M crowd-sourced ratings. They propose HDR-Q, the first multimodal large language model (MLLM) for HDR-VQA, which combines an HDR-aware vision encoder (fine-tuned with HDR-SDR contrastive loss) and a novel reinforcement learning fine-tuning scheme called HDR-Aware Policy Optimization (HAPO). HAPO extends GRPO with an HDR-SDR contrastive KL term, dual-entropy regularization, and entropy-weighted token advantages to enforce HDR grounding and stable MOS calibration. On Beyond8Bits, HDR-Q significantly outperforms all SDR, HDR, and MLLM baselines and demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization to LIVE-HDR and SFV+HDR benchmarks, while producing concise, HDR-grounded rationales.

## Method Summary
HDR-Q employs a two-stage training pipeline. First, a SigLIP-2 vision encoder is fine-tuned on 10-bit HDR frames and their SDR tone-mapped counterparts using a combined loss: standard SigLIP alignment to preserve semantics and an HDR-SDR contrastive loss to sensitize the encoder to HDR-specific details. Second, an MLLM (Ovis2.5) is fine-tuned via HDR-Aware Policy Optimization (HAPO), an extension of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). HAPO introduces three key components: (1) an HDR-SDR contrastive KL divergence that penalizes similar token distributions when only SDR visual tokens are provided, thereby preventing modality neglect; (2) dual-entropy regularization applied to both HDR and SDR pathways to maintain stable token distributions; and (3) entropy-weighted token advantages that amplify gradients for high-uncertainty tokens (often those identifying HDR distortions), improving credit assignment and MOS calibration. Rewards combine format compliance, a Gaussian-weighted regression accuracy against MOS, and self-consistency across multiple rollouts.

## Key Results
- HDR-Q achieves PLCC 0.9118, SRCC 0.9206, RMSE 5.1594, KRCC 0.7218 on the Beyond8Bits test set, setting a new state of the art.
- It outperforms all compared SDR models, HDR-specific models, and general-purpose MLLM baselines.
- The model generalizes robustly to external HDR benchmarks (LIVE-HDR, SFV+HDR) without any fine-tuning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** The HDR-aware vision encoder produces embeddings sensitive to HDR-specific distortions (e.g., near-black crushing, highlight clipping) while maintaining semantic alignment.  
**Mechanism:** The encoder (`Eψ`) is fine-tuned on 10-bit HDR frames. For each HDR frame (`x_t`), a synthetic SDR counterpart (`x_t^SDR`) is generated via tone-mapping. A contrastive loss (`L_contrast`) enforces that the HDR embedding is closer to its caption than the SDR embedding, creating perceptual separation, combined with a SigLIP alignment loss (`L_Sigmoid`) to preserve semantics.  
**Core assumption:** HDR and SDR versions share high-level semantics but differ in low-level luminance/color fidelity; explicit supervision allows the model to disentangle these factors.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "HDR-aware vision encoder fine-tuned with HDR-SDR contrastive loss..."; [section 5.1] full training objective (`L_enc = L_Sigmoid + λ_contrast L_contrast`); weak direct corpus support.  
**Break condition:** If contrastive margin (`δ`) is poorly set or captions insufficiently descriptive, embeddings may not separate; detectable via t-SNE/UMAP showing no clustering.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** HDR-Aware Policy Optimization (HAPO) prevents modality neglect by ensuring the policy's token distribution depends on HDR inputs.  
**Mechanism:** HAPO extends GRPO by adding a contrastive KL term (`K_HDR = D_KL(π_θ^HDR ∥ π_θ^SDR)`). During training, two rollouts are sampled per group: one with full HDR tokens (and captions) and one with only SDR tokens (and captions). Maximizing their KL divergence forces the policy to change its output distribution when HDR cues are removed, grounding reasoning in HDR-specific information.  
**Core assumption:** Modality neglect arises because default RL objectives do not penalize ignoring visual cues; distributional divergence between HDR and SDR pathways provides a scalable, unsupervised signal.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "HDR-Aware Policy Optimization (HAPO)... extends GRPO with an HDR-SDR contrastive KL term..."; [section 5.2] formal definition of `K_HDR` and mutual information interpretation; [section 6.3] ablation shows dropping KL causes SRCC drop (0.91 → 0.86).  
**Break condition:** If SDR pathway is not a strong counterfactual (flawed generation or text reliance), KL signal becomes noisy; monitor via token entropy similarity between pathways.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Entropy-weighted advantages (HEW) and dual-entropy regularization refine token-level credit assignment, leading to concise, accurate HDR-grounded rationales and stable MOS calibration.  
**Mechanism:** GRPO assigns the same normalized advantage to all tokens. HEW rescales each token's advantage by its normalized entropy (`w_i,t = clip(1 + λ_HEW * (H_i,t / avg_H), w_min, w_max)`). High-entropy tokens (often identifying HDR distortions) get amplified gradients. Dual-entropy regularization (`H_dual`) adds an entropy penalty for both HDR and SDR pathways to prevent trivial entropy inflation while maintaining distribution sharpness.  
**Core assumption:** Valuable reasoning steps correspond to higher predictive uncertainty; weighting these tokens improves fine-grained MOS alignment and reduces verbose chain-of-thought.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "dual-entropy regularization for stability, and entropy-weighted token advantages for precise MOS calibration."; [section 5.2] equations; [section 6.3] ablation: HEW removal hurts SRCC (0.91 → 0.88) and increases CoT length (137 → 155 tokens).  
**Break condition:** If `λ_HEW` is too high, over-fit to noise; if too low, effect negligible. Mis-scaled Gaussian reward could destabilize MOS prediction. Track CoT length and token entropy distributions.

## Foundational Learning
- **High Dynamic Range (HDR) Video Characteristics**  
  *Why needed:* HDR's 10-bit depth, BT.2020 color gamut, and PQ transfer function create unique distortions (banding, clipping) not captured by SDR models.  
  *Quick check:* What happens if HDR videos are tone-mapped to SDR before encoder input? (Critical HDR details are lost, collapsing embeddings to SDR features.)

- **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) / Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**  
  *Why needed:* HAPO builds directly on GRPO to fine-tune the MLLM's reasoning without a learned critic; understanding group-relative advantages and the clipped PPO objective is prerequisite.  
  *Quick check:* Why does GRPO normalize rewards within a group? (To eliminate the need for a value network and stabilize advantage estimation across diverse responses.)

- **Modality Neglect in Multimodal Models**  
  *Why needed:* HAPO explicitly counters the tendency of MLLMs to ignore visual inputs and rely on text priors, a known failure mode.  
  *Quick check:* How might you detect modality neglect in a VQA MLLM? (Compare performance with vs. without visual input, or analyze attention maps; HAPO uses KL divergence between HDR and SDR pathways as a proxy.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input Pipeline: 10-bit HDR video → uniform frame sampling (T=8) → native PQ encoding.  
HDR-Aware Vision Encoder: SigLIP-2 base + LoRA adapter; processes HDR frames and SDR twins in parallel, outputting two token streams (`E_ψ(HDR)`, `E_ψ(SDR)`).  
Projection & LLM Backbone: Visual tokens projected into Ovis2.5 LLM embedding space; text prompt concatenated.  
HAPO Training Loop: Sample K rollouts per video; compute rewards (format, Gaussian MOS accuracy, self-consistency); compute token-level advantages; apply HEW; generate counterfactual rollout with SDR-only input; compute losses: PPO surrogate, `β D_KL(π_θ^HDR ∥ π_ref)`, `γ K_HDR`, `-H_dual`; update with AdamW.  
Reward Models: Regex format check, Gaussian MOS regression (σ=3), self-consistency (median agreement).

### Critical Path
The flow of HDR-specific information must travel: HDR frames → HDR-aware encoder → HDR visual tokens → policy network → HDR-dependent output distribution. The HDR–SDR KL term (`K_HDR`) is the gatekeeper: if `π_θ^HDR` and `π_θ^SDR` become too similar, the gradient signal disappears, and HDR grounding fails.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Encoder Precision vs. Compute: Native 10-bit PQ preserves fidelity but increases memory; downscaling loses HDR cues.
- KL Weight (`γ`) vs. Entropy: High `γ` forces strong HDR dependence but risks entropy collapse; dual-entropy regularization (`η1`, `η2`) must be tuned.
- Group Size (`K`) vs. Variance: Larger `K` stabilizes advantage normalization but increases sampling cost.
- HEW `λ_HEW` vs. Calibration: Amplifying high-entropy tokens improves reasoning focus but can over-weight noise if `R_sc` is noisy.

### Failure Signatures
- **Modality Neglect:** `K_HDR` near zero, similar token distributions for HDR/SDR pathways; high text-only probe performance but low on HDR videos.
- **Calibration Drift:** MOS predictions biased (systematic over/under-score) despite high correlation; check Gaussian reward scaling (`σ`) and predicted score distribution.
- **Verbose/Unstable CoT:** CoT length increases without accuracy gain, high variance in token entropy across rollouts; indicates insufficient `H_dual` or poorly set `λ_HEW`.
- **Encoder Collapse:** t-SNE of HDR vs. SDR embeddings shows no separation; `L_contrast` loss plateaus near zero.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Encoder Sanity Check:** Train HDR-aware encoder on small HDR/SDR frame–caption subset. Evaluate embedding separation via nearest-neighbor retrieval: HDR frames should retrieve HDR captions more often than SDR captions.
2. **HAPO Component Isolation:** Ablate each HAPO term (HDR–SDR KL, Dual Ent., HEW) on held-out validation. Expected: KL removal hurts SRCC most; Dual Ent. removal increases CoT variance; HEW removal increases CoT length.
3. **Full Model Zero-Shot Test:** After training on Beyond8Bits, evaluate on LIVE-HDR and SFV+HDR without fine-tuning. A sharp drop would indicate poor generalization; HDR-Q expects robust transfer due to physically grounded features.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** Does HDR-Q's HDR-aware encoder generalize to HDR formats like HLG and alternative tone-mapping operators?  
**Basis in paper:** The encoder is trained on PQ-SDR pairs using a specific deterministic tone-mapper; Beyond8Bits and benchmarks only contain PQ content.  
**Why unresolved:** Different HDR formats have distinct luminance/color representations; robustness to format variations is untested.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Construct a test set of HLG-encoded HDR videos with subjective MOS; evaluate HDR-Q and measure performance drop relative to PQ. Ablate the tone-mapping operator used during training.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** Is the HDR-SDR contrastive KL term in HAPO sufficient to eliminate modality neglect, and does it correlate with improved utilization of HDR-specific visual cues?  
**Basis in paper:** HAPO introduces this KL term to "encourage token reliance on HDR inputs" and prevent modality neglect; ablation shows metric gains but no analysis of internal reasoning.  
**Why unresolved:** It is unclear whether the model truly grounds decisions on HDR distortions or exploits shortcuts; no attention or saliency analysis is provided.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Compute token-level attention weights or gradient-based saliency maps for HDR vs. SDR-only inputs; test on synthetic videos where HDR-specific regions are masked; measure drop in performance when HDR cues are removed.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** How accurate and HDR-specific are the generated rationales, and does explicitly optimizing rationale quality lead to further improvements in MOS prediction?  
**Basis in paper:** The paper claims HDR-Q produces "concise, HDR-grounded rationales" but evaluates only MOS prediction; the reward includes a "reasoning quality" term that is not defined or validated.  
**Why unresolved:** Rationales may be plausible but factually incorrect or irrelevant to HDR distortions; the relationship between rationale quality and MOS accuracy is unknown.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a human study where experts rate rationales for correctness, relevance, and HDR-specificity; correlate these scores with MOS prediction accuracy across model checkpoints; modify HAPO to include an explicit rationale quality reward and measure joint improvement.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** To what extent do uncontrolled display calibrations and viewing environments in the Beyond8Bits crowdsourced study affect the reliability of the collected MOS values?  
**Basis in paper:** The subjective study used AMT with a qualification quiz for HDR-capable devices but did not enforce display calibration or control ambient lighting.  
**Why unresolved:** HDR perception is highly sensitive to display peak brightness, black level, and ambient light; unaddressed variations may introduce noise or bias in MOS.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Re-conduct a subset of ratings in a controlled lab with calibrated HDR displays; compare MOS distributions with the crowd-sourced ones. Alternatively, collect detailed display specifications from participants and perform a regression analysis to quantify their impact.

## Limitations
- The Beyond8Bits dataset is not released, preventing independent verification of its quality and coverage.
- Key implementation details (reward weights, caption generation specifics, HAPO hyperparameter tuning) are missing, hindering exact reproduction.
- Generalization claims rely on only two external benchmarks; potential overfitting to the Beyond8Bits distribution cannot be ruled out.

## Confidence
- Dataset existence & scale: **High** (explicitly introduced, though unreleased)
- HDR-aware encoder design: **Medium** (contrastive loss justified, but no direct embedding analysis)
- HAPO mechanism: **Medium** (ablation supports contribution, but causal link to HDR grounding partially inferential)
- Performance & generalization: **Medium** (benchmarks are standard, but independent replication not possible)
