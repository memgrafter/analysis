# Hunt Globally: Wide Search AI Agents for Drug Asset Scouting in Investing, Business Development, and Competitive Intelligence

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15019
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15019
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** Bioptic Agent achieves 79.7% F1 on a novel completeness-first drug asset scouting benchmark, outperforming leading deep research agents by large margins.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of global drug asset scouting, where over 85% of patent filings originate outside the US, often in non-English sources. The authors develop Bioptic Agent, a tree-based, multilingual AI system that employs self-learning exploration with language parallelism and validator-guided refinement to achieve high recall without hallucination. On a custom benchmark derived from regional news, Bioptic Agent attains a 79.7% F1 score, significantly surpassing Claude Opus 4.4 (56.2%) and Gemini 3 Pro Deep Research (50.6%). Performance scales favorably with compute, demonstrating the advantage of a specialized scaffold architecture over generic prolonged search.

## Method Summary
Bioptic Agent is a multi-agent system that iteratively explores a tree of search directives to maximize recall of drug assets while maintaining precision. The core loop selects leaf nodes via Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) balancing exploration and exploitation, then rolls out Investigator Agents in parallel across languages (e.g., English, Chinese) to gather candidate assets from region-specific sources. Each candidate is verified by a Criteria Match Validator that uses evidence-grounded reasoning, and duplicates are merged by a Deduplication Agent. The validator's failure rationales are fed to a Coach Agent, which generates new child directives to cover missed subspaces, expanding the tree. The system is evaluated on a completeness-first benchmark constructed by mining regional news sources across 10 regions, filtering for assets with low English discoverability, and generating investor-style queries. Performance is measured by F1 score, with precision and recall computed using LLM-as-judge graders calibrated to expert audits.

## Key Results
- Bioptic Agent F1 score: 79.7% vs. Claude Opus 4.4 (56.2%) and Gemini 3 Pro Deep Research (50.6%).
- High precision (0.877) and recall (0.730) on the benchmark.
- Performance improves steeply with additional compute, showing favorable scaling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Tree-Based Search with UCB Allocation
The agent maintains a tree of search directives. Each node tracks visit count and cumulative reward. Leaf nodes are selected using UCB: mean reward plus exploration bonus. After each rollout, a reward = precision × number of new unique assets is backpropagated. This balances exploitation of promising branches with exploration of under-visited ones, preventing early saturation. Evidence: Section "Bioptic Agent" defines the UCB rule and reward; Section "Results" shows Bioptic's recall grows steadily while ablations plateau earlier; Figure 1 demonstrates scaling advantage.  
**Break condition:** If reward signal poorly tracks true coverage (e.g., validator inaccuracy or overlapping branches), UCB may misallocate compute, causing stagnation.

### Multilingual Parallel Exploration
Investigator agents operate in parallel across languages (e.g., English, Chinese), querying local news outlets, registries, and filings. This captures assets first disclosed in non-English regional sources, often missed by English-only search. Candidate sets are merged, preserving unique non-English finds. Evidence: Abstract notes >85% patents originate outside US; Section "Language Parallelism" states parallelism increases coverage over regional sources; Section "Results" shows Bioptic outperforms no-tree, lang-free ablation.  
**Break condition:** Poor prompting or weak regional search infrastructure may yield little extra recall and could introduce noise.

### Validator-Guided Refinement with Coach Expansion
The Criteria Match Validator verifies each candidate against query constraints using explicit source citations, outputting match verdicts and failure rationales for mismatches. These rationales are compressed into patterns and fed to the Coach Agent, which generates new child directives targeting missed angles (e.g., missing modality, region). This loop systematically converts recall gaps into focused exploration. Evidence: Section "Criteria Match Validator Agent" details verification; Section "Coach Agent" explains child directive generation; Section "Multi-Agent Debate-Based Precision Grader Tuning" reports validator precision of 88% under expert audit; Results show high precision and recall.  
**Break condition:** If validator accuracy degrades on out-of-domain queries or Coach produces redundant/narrow directives, compute may be wasted or valid assets missed.

## Foundational Learning
- **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) and Upper Confidence Bound (UCB):** Needed to balance exploration vs. exploitation in the directive tree, ensuring compute is allocated efficiently across branches. *Quick check:* How does the UCB formula trade off between a node’s historical reward and its visit count?
- **Cross-lingual Information Retrieval and Entity Normalization:** Needed to retrieve and deduplicate assets appearing in multiple languages and under varying aliases. *Quick check:* What are the risks if deduplication relied only on exact string matching?
- **LLM-as-Judge with Evidence Grounding:** Needed for the validator to make accurate, non-hallucinated decisions by citing sources. *Quick check:* Why is it important that the validator’s decisions are accompanied by verbatim quotes and URLs?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Investigator Agent → Criteria Match Validator Agent → Deduplication Agent → Coach Agent. Global stores: C_global (pre-validation candidates), A_global (validated unique assets), Q_global (queries executed), D_global (visited domains). The tree structure connects root to leaves; each leaf node holds a directive.
- **Critical path:** Initialize tree with root directive. For each epoch: Select *m* leaf nodes via UCB → Rollout: run parallel Investigator Agents per language → Merge candidates → Evaluate: each candidate validated by Validator, then deduplicated against A_global → Compute node reward (precision × new unique assets) → Backpropagate reward to ancestors → Aggregate new assets into A_global → Expand: Coach generates *k* non-overlapping child directives for selected leaves → Repeat until stopping condition (e.g., max epochs or plateau).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Tree depth vs. compute: Deeper trees risk combinatorial explosion; Coach must keep directives non-overlapping yet exhaustive.
  - Validator thoroughness vs. speed: Light deduplication is fast but may miss aliases; heavy is exhaustive but slow.
  - Language coverage vs. noise: More languages may increase recall but lower precision if sources are low-signal.
  - Reward shaping: Using precision-weighted new assets prevents low-quality high-volume branches from dominating but may under-value high-value few assets.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Recall plateau after initial gains: tree not expanding or validator too strict.
  - Precision drop: validator degradation or Investigator over-generation.
  - Duplicate proliferation: deduplication failure.
  - Tree explosion: many branches with few visits; may need adjust UCB constant or child count.
  - Validator overload: heavy validation bottleneck; consider batching or smaller model.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Sanity check: Run on tiny benchmark (2–3 queries) with `--max-epochs 2 --languages en`. Inspect `tree.json` to ensure stores populated and backpropagation updated statistics.
  2. Validator impact: Disable Criteria Match Validator, run 5 epochs. Compare precision and recall to full system; expect precision drop and possible inflated recall.
  3. UCB vs random selection: Replace UCB with random leaf picking, measure F1 over epochs. UCB should yield faster recall growth.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does the non-uniform seed selection in benchmark construction bias evaluation results and what is the magnitude of residual bias?
2. What is the specific contribution of the tree-based exploration scaffold versus other components, and does it hold across query difficulties or asset types?
3. Does performance scaling with compute continue linearly, logarithmically, or plateau asymptotically, and what is the practical compute ceiling before diminishing returns or hallucination increase?
4. How effectively does the system generalize to regions, languages, and therapeutic areas not represented in the curated source list or seed query corpus?
5. To what extent does the Criteria Match Validator's calibration remain stable when applied to queries or asset types significantly different from those in the tuning subset?

## Limitations
- Exact agent prompts and instructions are undisclosed, hindering exact replication.
- Curated regional source lists (URLs, update frequency) are unspecified, affecting benchmark realism.
- Hyperparameters (UCB constant, leaf selections, child count) and stopping criteria lack systematic study; results may be sensitive.
- The benchmark's completeness-first construction relies on LLM judges with unclear external validity.

## Confidence
- **Overall Performance (79.7% F1):** Medium (benchmark novel, lacks external validation, but internal ablations support gain).
- **Tree-Based UCB Search:** High (ablation shows prevention of early saturation; compute scaling demonstrated).
- **Multilingual Parallel Exploration:** Medium (ablation confounds tree and language; only English+Chinese evaluated).
- **Validator-Guided Refinement:** Medium (validator precision audit sparse; ablation mixes tree effects).

## Next Checks
1. Validator ablation: Bypass validation; expect sharp precision drop and possible recall inflation from hallucinations.
2. Directive tree sanity: For a sample query, output the final tree and manually verify child directives are mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive; trace asset attribution.
3. Language-specific recall: Tag assets by discovery language; compute assets found exclusively in non-English streams to quantify multilingual gain.
