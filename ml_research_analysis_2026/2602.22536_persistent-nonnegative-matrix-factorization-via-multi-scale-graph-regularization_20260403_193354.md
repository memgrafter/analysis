# Persistent Nonnegative Matrix Factorization via Multi-Scale Graph Regularization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22536  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22536  
- **Reference count:** 37  
- **Primary result:** pNMF integrates persistent homology to select canonical scales for multi-scale embeddings, consistently outperforming NMF and graph-regularized baselines on scRNA-seq clustering (e.g., ARI 0.835 vs. 0.617 on Dendritic batch1).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces persistent nonnegative matrix factorization (pNMF), a method that enriches standard NMF with multi-scale topological information derived from 0-dimensional persistent homology. By extracting death times from the H0 persistence diagram, pNMF identifies a canonical sequence of scales at which the data’s connectivity structure changes. At each scale, a graph Laplacian regularizes the embedding to preserve local geometry, while additional cross-scale smoothness and anchoring constraints ensure coherent evolution across scales. The authors develop a sequential alternating optimization algorithm with convergence guarantees and demonstrate on synthetic data that embeddings reveal progressive structure (e.g., concentric circles). On five scRNA-seq datasets, pNMF achieves superior clustering performance compared to NMF, GNMF, TNMF, and k-TNMF, with robust results across parameters.

## Method Summary
pNMF begins by computing the 0-dimensional persistent homology (Vietoris–Rips filtration) of the input data to obtain a canonical scale set Λ⋆ consisting of death times from the persistence diagram. For each scale ε ∈ Λ⋆, a weighted adjacency matrix A(ε) is built using a Gaussian kernel with decay α, and the corresponding graph Laplacian L(ε) is derived. The pNMF objective combines a standard NMF reconstruction term for each scale with three regularization components: (1) a scale-specific geometric term λ₁·Tr(HₜLₜHₜ⊤) encouraging manifold preservation; (2) a cross-scale smoothness term λ₂·∑∥Hₜ−Hₜ₋₁∥F²; and (3) an anchoring term λ₃·∑∥Hₜ∥F² for numerical stability. The coupled problem is solved via a sequential alternating optimization: for each scale t, a local subproblem (fixing neighboring embeddings) is minimized using modified multiplicative update rules with safeguards (ν, μ) until a stopping criterion is met. After convergence, a normalization step enforces column sums of Wₜ to unity. Initialization uses nonnegative double SVD with averaging (NNDSVDA).

## Key Results
- **Synthetic data:** pNMF produces coherent multi-scale embeddings where geometric patterns (e.g., concentric circles) emerge progressively as scale decreases, validating the multi-scale capture.
- **scRNA-seq clustering:** pNMF (finest or best scale) consistently outperforms NMF, GNMF, TNMF, and k-TNMF across five datasets; notable ARI improvements: Dendritic batch1 (0.617 → 0.835), GSE75748time (0.644 → 0.722).
- **Ablation:** Removing geometric regularization (λ₁), cross-scale smoothness (λ₂), or anchoring (λ₃) degrades embedding quality, confirming the necessity of each component.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Persistence diagrams provide a canonical, minimal set of scales that capture all essential connectivity transitions.
- **Mechanism:** The 0-dimensional persistent homology tracks connected component merges as the scale parameter increases. Death times in the H0 diagram mark exactly these merge events; using them ensures each graph construction corresponds to a topological connectivity change.
- **Core assumption:** The data’s connectivity evolution is fully captured by 0-dimensional homology; higher-dimensional features (loops, voids) are not required.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “By leveraging persistent homology, we identify a canonical minimal sufficient scale set…”  
  - Section 3.1, Theorem 3.5: death times form the canonical minimal sufficient scale set.  
  - Corpus: neighboring papers focus on NMF variants, not persistence-based scale selection.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Cross-scale smoothness and anchoring constraints, combined with scale-specific Laplacians, produce coherent and stable multi-scale embeddings.
- **Mechanism:** For each persistence-derived scale, a graph Laplacian regularizes the embedding to preserve local manifold structure. An additional penalty (λ₂) penalizes differences between consecutive embeddings, enforcing smooth evolution. An anchoring term (λ₃) controls overall magnitude to improve numerical stability.
- **Core assumption:** Meaningful multi-scale representations should vary continuously with scale; abrupt changes indicate artifacts.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “pNMF couples scale-specific graph Laplacian regularization with cross-scale smoothness and anchoring constraints.”  
  - Section 3.3, objective (3.2): combines per-scale geometric regularization with inter-scale terms.  
  - Section 3.4, Theorem 3.9: bounds embedding increments in terms of Laplacian increments, quantifying smoothness.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A sequential alternating optimization algorithm with guaranteed convergence solves the coupled multi-scale problem.
- **Mechanism:** The full problem is decomposed into s subproblems (one per scale). Each subproblem minimizes a local objective including data fidelity, scale-specific Laplacian term, and coupling terms from neighboring scales (via fixed H±1). Multiplicative update rules with adaptive step sizes (modified to handle zeros) are applied sequentially across scales until convergence.
- **Core assumption:** Nonnegativity constraints can be managed via KKT conditions and multiplicative updates; block-coordinate descent converges despite coupling.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Section 4, Algorithm 4.1: sequential updates and normalization.  
  - Section 5, Theorems 5.1–5.3: non-increasing objective values and convergence to stationary points.  
  - Appendix B.6–B.8: full convergence proofs.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Persistence module → Graph construction module → pNMF model → Optimization engine
- **Critical path:** Persistence diagram extraction → scale selection → graph Laplacians → sequential alternating optimization → final embeddings. The scale sequence Λ⋆ must be fixed before optimization begins.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Scale selection:* Canonical persistence scales vs. heuristic sampling (e.g., uniform). The former is principled but tied to H0; the latter is flexible but may miss topological transitions.  
  *Regularization:* λ₁ controls geometric adherence; λ₂ controls cross-scale smoothness; λ₃ controls magnitude. High λ₁/λ₂ improve structure but risk over-regularization; low values may yield noisy or incoherent scales.  
  *Complexity:* Sequential AO is O(s r n (d p n + d²(p+n) + d n²)). Sparse graphs reduce the d n² term, but memory for storing all {Hₜ} is O(n d n).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  *Meaningless multi-scale output:* If Λ⋆ is poorly chosen (e.g., not minimal), embeddings may not evolve meaningfully.  
  *Non-convergent optimization:* If ν is too small, multiplicative updates may stall or produce NaN due to division by near-zero.  
  *Over-smoothing:* If λ₂ is too large, all Hₜ become nearly identical, losing scale-specific information.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Synthetic concentric circles:** Visualize how pNMF embeddings evolve from coarse (single cluster) to fine (concentric rings), confirming multi-scale capture.  
  2. **Ablation study on regularization terms:** Systematically remove λ₁ (geometric), λ₂ (smoothness), λ₃ (anchor) to validate each component’s role in producing coherent multi-scale embeddings.  
  3. **scRNA-seq clustering benchmark:** Compare pNMF (finest-scale and best-scale) against NMF, GNMF, TNMF, k-TNMF on multiple datasets using ARI/NMI/Purity/Accuracy to demonstrate consistent improvement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Integrate higher-dimensional persistent homology into pNMF.**  
  *Basis:* Conclusion identifies extension to loops/voids as a key challenge: “extending pNMF to incorporate higher-dimensional topological features such as loops or voids remains challenging… Developing principled strategies to integrate higher- or mixed-dimensional persistent homology into multi-scale graph constructions therefore remains an important and challenging direction.”  
  *Why unresolved:* Current method relies on 0-dimensional persistence which provides a canonical ordered set; higher-dimensional features lack a natural total ordering.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* A theoretical framework defining a total order for multi-dimensional persistence and constructing scale-adapted Laplacians with stability guarantees; empirical validation on manifolds like torus/sphere.
- **Develop scalable optimization algorithms for pNMF.**  
  *Basis:* Conclusion: “Exploring alternative optimization strategies, including accelerated methods or hybrid approaches… may further improve scalability and adaptability, particularly in large-scale or streaming data scenarios.”  
  *Why unresolved:* Sequential alternating update has high complexity O(s r n (d p n + d²(p+n) + d n²)) and lacks online updates.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* A new algorithm (e.g., stochastic gradient, block-coordinate descent with warm starts, or neural parameterization) with provable convergence and faster per-iteration cost; runtime comparisons on large-scale datasets (millions of points).
- **Exploit the full multi-scale embedding sequence {Hₜ} for downstream tasks.**  
  *Basis:* Conclusion: “while this work primarily evaluates the learned multi-scale embeddings through clustering performance at selected scales, many downstream tasks could benefit from a more comprehensive use of the entire family of embeddings.”  
  *Why unresolved:* Experiments only use finest or best scale; no methodology exists to aggregate or navigate across scales.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Novel multi-scale approaches (e.g., consensus clustering across scales, trajectory inference using the continuous scale path) evaluated on biologically relevant datasets with improved performance in tasks like cell lineage reconstruction.

## Limitations
- Only 0-dimensional persistent homology (connected components) is used; higher-dimensional topological features (loops, voids) that may drive multi-scale structure are ignored.
- Exact details of Vietoris–Rips filtration construction (e.g., max simplex dimension, max filtration value) are unspecified, which could affect the canonical scale set Λ⋆.
- Hyperparameter choices (λ₁, λ₂, λ₃, α, etc.) are fixed in experiments; their sensitivity across diverse datasets is not thoroughly validated.
