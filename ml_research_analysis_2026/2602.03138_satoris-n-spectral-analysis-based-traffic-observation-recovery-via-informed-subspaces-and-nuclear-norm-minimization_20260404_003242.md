# SATORIS-N: Spectral Analysis based Traffic Observation Recovery via Informed Subspaces and Nuclear-norm minimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.03138
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03138
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: The explicit SDP variant reduces relative root mean square error (RRMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE) by 10–20% compared to ten baselines under high occlusion (75–90% missing).

## Executive Summary
SATORIS-N is a method for imputing missing traffic density data in V2X systems. It exploits the observed stability of singular subspaces across consecutive days. The method proposes two strategies: an implicit meta-algorithm that stacks adjacent-day matrices to align subspaces during imputation, and an explicit convex semidefinite program that injects prior singular subspaces as constraints. Evaluated on Beijing and Shanghai taxi datasets with 10–90% missing entries, the explicit SDP variant (SRESI/SRRSI) achieves 10–20% lower RRMSE and MAE than ten baselines under high missingness (75–90%). The implicit variant performs best at low missing rates. Results confirm that leveraging neighboring-day subspace information substantially improves imputation accuracy, especially when observations are severely incomplete.

## Method Summary
SATORIS-N addresses the problem of completing daily traffic-density matrices (spatial grid × hourly time slots) with missing entries. It assumes the matrices are low-rank and that their dominant singular subspaces are stable across adjacent days. The method uses a neighbor day with near-complete data to estimate the singular subspaces via truncated SVD. Two families of algorithms are proposed: (1) Implicit (SRISI): horizontally or vertically stack the target day's incomplete matrix with the neighbor day's matrix, then apply standard low-rank completion (nuclear norm minimization) to induce shared subspaces; (2) Explicit (SRESI/SRRSI): reformulate nuclear norm minimization as a semidefinite program (SDP) and inject the neighbor's singular subspaces as constraints (hard or soft) to explicitly align the solution. The explicit variants yield a convex optimization problem with a global optimum. For implementation, the explicit method uses CVXPY to solve the SDP, while the implicit method can use any off-the-shelf low-rank completer like SoftImpute. The rank k for the truncated SVD is chosen via grid search on validation data.

## Key Results
- On Beijing and Shanghai taxi datasets (matrices of size 340×24 and 320×24), SATORIS-N's explicit SDP variant (SRESI/SRRSI) reduces RRMSE and MAE by 10–20% relative to ten baseline methods (including SoftImpute, MICE, and deep learning) when missingness is high (75–90%).
- The implicit stacking variant (SRISI) performs best at low missing rates (10–50%), but the explicit variant dominates under severe occlusion.
- Results validate the cross-day stability of singular subspaces, with overlap metrics exceeding 0.75 for consecutive days.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Implicit subspace alignment via stacking
- **Claim**: Stacking the target day's incomplete matrix with a neighbor's near-complete matrix along columns or rows promotes shared singular subspaces during low-rank completion.
- **Mechanism**: The stacked matrix has a larger size; solving a nuclear norm minimization problem on it forces the low-rank solution to explain both days simultaneously, which implicitly aligns the left singular vectors (if horizontal stacking) or right singular vectors (if vertical stacking).
- **Core assumption**: The dominant singular subspaces of adjacent days are sufficiently aligned.
- **Evidence**: The paper's Section III.B describes concatenation to promote shared spatial/temporal subspaces. The abstract mentions the implicit meta-algorithm.
- **Break condition**: If the neighboring day's subspace is poorly aligned (e.g., due to holidays or anomalies), stacking may introduce bias and worsen imputation.

### Mechanism 2: Explicit subspace injection via SDP
- **Claim**: By fixing block matrices in the SDP formulation of nuclear norm minimization to the neighbor's singular subspace estimates, the feasible set is constrained to matrices whose singular vectors align with the prior, yielding a convex problem with global optimum and improved robustness under high missingness.
- **Mechanism**: The nuclear norm is expressed as an SDP (Equation 2). Substituting A = UΣUᵀ and B = VΣVᵀ (from neighbor's SVD) into the constraints explicitly injects the left and right subspaces. This reduces the solution space, making recovery from very sparse observations more reliable.
- **Core assumption**: The neighbor day provides accurate estimates of the dominant singular subspaces for the target day, and these subspaces are stable enough that enforcing their exact use does not introduce significant bias.
- **Evidence**: Section III.C details SDP formulations (3)-(7) for hard and soft injection. The abstract states that the SDP formulation "explicitly informs the reconstruction with prior singular-subspace information."
- **Break condition**: Inaccurate prior subspaces (e.g., from an atypical neighbor or wrong rank k) can lead to poor solutions; soft regularized variants mitigate this at the cost of hyperparameter tuning.

### Mechanism 3: Cross-day subspace stability
- **Claim**: The fundamental property enabling both implicit and explicit prior transfer is the stability of singular subspaces across consecutive days, meaning that the principal angles between rank-k subspaces of adjacent days are small.
- **Mechanism**: Traffic density matrices share common spatial patterns (e.g., downtown vs. residential) and temporal patterns (e.g., rush hour peaks). This manifests as high overlap between singular vector matrices. A neighbor day's subspaces thus provide a good basis for representing the target day, reducing degrees of freedom needed for recovery.
- **Core assumption**: The subspace overlap metric (Equation 8) between adjacent days is large (close to 1).
- **Evidence**: The paper quantifies overlap in Figure 3, showing values much closer to 1 than 0 for both datasets. The abstract and Sections II & IV.C discuss this stability.
- **Break condition**: As the temporal gap increases beyond a few days or during special events (holidays, severe weather), subspace stability may degrade, making the transferred prior less relevant.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Nuclear Norm Minimization**
- *Why needed here*: It is the convex surrogate for matrix rank, promoting low-rank solutions. SATORIS-N builds its explicit SDP on this foundation. Understanding the SDP formulation is key to seeing how subspace priors are injected.
- *Quick check question*: Why is the nuclear norm preferred over rank minimization for matrix completion, and how does its SDP formulation (Equation 2) relate to the SVD of X?

**Concept: Singular Subspace (U, V) and Truncated SVD**
- *Why needed here*: The entire method hinges on extracting and reusing the spatial (U) and temporal (V) singular vectors from a neighbor day as priors. U represents spatial modes (grid locations) and V represents temporal modes (hours).
- *Quick check question*: Given a day's traffic matrix D[t₂] ≈ UΣVᵀ, what do the columns of U and V represent, and why are they expected to be stable across days?

**Concept: Explicit Prior Injection via Convex Constraints**
- *Why needed here*: The key innovation is injecting neighbor-day singular subspaces as constraints into a convex SDP, reducing the feasible set and improving recovery under high missingness compared to unconstrained convex methods (e.g., standard nuclear norm minimization) or heuristic stacking.
- *Quick check question*: How does injecting neighbor day's singular subspaces as constraints in the SDP change the feasible set compared to standard nuclear norm minimization? Why does this help when missingness is very high?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input Handler -> Subspace Estimator -> {Implicit path: Stacking module -> Base Imputer; Explicit path: Subspace Injector -> SDP Builder -> Solver Engine} -> Output Extractor.
- **Critical path**: (1) Obtain reliable neighbor day, (2) Compute its rank-k truncated SVD, (3) Choose implicit or explicit variant, (4) For implicit: stack and apply base imputer; for explicit: build and solve SDP, (5) Return imputed matrix.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Implicit (SRISI)*: Lightweight, modular, fast, best for low missingness (<50%). Aligns only one subspace (spatial or temporal) heuristically.
  - *Explicit (SRESI/SRRSI)*: Robust under high missingness (75–90%), aligns both subspaces optimally via convex SDP. Higher computational cost; requires careful rank-k selection; less flexible if subspaces drift.
  - *Hard vs. Soft injection*: Hard constraints (SRESI) are simpler but brittle; soft regularized variants (SRRSI) add tolerance but introduce hyperparameters (α, β).
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Low subspace overlap between neighbor and target indicates misalignment, leading to poor gains.
  - Rank-k mismatch (too small loses patterns; too large includes noise) causes error to rise.
  - SDP numerical issues (infeasibility, ill-conditioning) due to poor neighbor subspace or mask pattern.
  - Using a non-neighboring or anomalous neighbor day increases error substantially.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Subspace stability quantification**: Compute the overlap metric (Equation 8) for consecutive days in your data; confirm mean >0.75.
  2. **Implicit vs. explicit at high missingness**: At 75–90% missing, compare SRISI, SRESI, and a strong baseline (SoftImpute-h). Expect SRESI/SRRSI to be best.
  3. **Rank‑k sensitivity**: For explicit SDP at 90% missing, sweep k ∈ {1,3,5,10,15,20} and plot error vs. k to find optimal k.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Theoretical extensions**: Can the convex framework incorporate additional structural priors (graph smoothness, temporal periodicity) while maintaining global optimality and recovery guarantees?
2. **Scalable algorithms**: What scalable algorithms (randomized SDP, ADMM) can reduce computational overhead for real-time imputation in large-scale V2X systems?
3. **Online subspace tracking**: How can online subspace tracking (GROUSE, sliding-window PCA) be integrated to adapt to time-varying patterns without full recomputation?
4. **Hybrid convex-deep architectures**: Can unrolled singular value thresholding or neural SDP solvers accelerate inference while preserving global optimality?
5. **Contextual multimodal extensions**: Does incorporating exogenous signals (weather, events) via tensor-based extensions improve robustness under extreme conditions?

## Limitations
- The exact codebase, solver settings, and hyperparameters are not provided; reproduction may diverge from reported results.
- Neighbor-day selection criteria (e.g., missingness threshold, fallback) are unspecified; this could impact subspace quality.
- Baseline implementations lack full detail, raising questions about fairness of comparisons.
- Findings are based on only two Chinese taxi datasets; generalizability to other cities or traffic data sources is unclear.
