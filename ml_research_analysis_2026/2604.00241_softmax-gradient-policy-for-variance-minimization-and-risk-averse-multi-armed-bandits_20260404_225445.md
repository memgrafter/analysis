# Softmax gradient policy for variance minimization and risk-averse multi armed bandits

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00241
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00241
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: A softmax policy gradient method with an unbiased two-draw variance estimator achieves almost sure convergence to the minimal-variance arm under distinct variances and bounded rewards, with empirical regret converging to zero.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles risk-aware multi-armed bandits, where the objective is to identify the arm with minimal variance rather than maximal expected reward. It proposes a softmax-parameterized policy and constructs an unbiased variance estimator using two independent reward draws per step, eliminating the need for stored historical averages. Stochastic gradient descent updates the policy preferences using this estimator. Theoretical analysis guarantees almost sure convergence to a Dirac distribution on the unique optimal arm when arm variances are distinct and rewards are bounded. Numerical experiments show that average regret converges to zero, with optimal arm selection reaching 100% for well-separated variances and around 70% in challenging near-tied cases.

## Method Summary
The algorithm maintains a preference vector \(H_t\) defining a softmax policy \(\Pi_{H_t}(a) = e^{H_t(a)}/\sum_b e^{H_t(b)}\). At each step \(t\), an arm \(A_t\) is sampled from this policy. Two independent rewards are then drawn from the distribution of \(A_t\). The composite reward is \(R_t = \frac{1}{2}(r_{t,1} - r_{t,2})^2\), an unbiased estimate of the arm’s variance. A running baseline \(\bar{R}_t\) is updated online. The gradient estimator is \(g_t(a) = (R_t - \bar{R}_t)(\mathbb{1}_{a=A_t} - \Pi_{H_t}(a))\), and preferences are updated by \(H_{t+1} = H_t - \rho_t g_t\) with constant learning rate \(\rho_t = \rho\). For risk-aware objectives combining mean and variance, a mini-batch of size \(\ell \geq 2\) is used to compute empirical mean and variance, combined via trade-off parameters \(\lambda_\mu, \lambda_\sigma\) into a composite reward.

## Key Results
- Theoretical guarantee: Almost sure convergence to the Dirac distribution on the unique minimal-variance arm, assuming distinct variances and bounded rewards.
- Empirical performance: Average regret converges to zero as the number of steps increases.
- Optimal arm selection: Achieves 100% frequency for arms with well-separated variances; remains around 70% even in challenging cases with closely matched variances.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Unbiased variance estimation without stored averages
- **Claim:** An unbiased estimate of an arm’s variance can be computed online using two independent reward draws, without storing historical averages.
- **Mechanism:** For the selected arm, draw two rewards \(R_t, R'_t \sim \mathbb{P}_a\). Compute \(\hat{\sigma}_t^2 = \frac{1}{2}(R_t - R'_t)^2\). This estimator satisfies \(\mathbb{E}[\hat{\sigma}_t^2] = \sigma_a^2\) because for i.i.d. draws with variance \(\sigma_a^2\) and mean \(\mu_a\), \(\mathbb{E}[(R_t - R'_t)^2] = 2\sigma_a^2\).
- **Core assumption:** Rewards from a given arm are i.i.d. with finite variance.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “constructs an unbiased variance estimator via two independent reward draws per step, avoiding reliance on stored averages”; Section 2.1, Equation 10 defines the composite reward.
- **Break condition:** If reward distributions are non-stationary or draws are dependent, the estimator becomes biased, undermining convergence.

### Mechanism 2: Stochastic gradient descent on softmax policy converges to optimal arm
- **Claim:** Stochastic gradient descent on the objective \(L(H) = \sum_a \Pi_H(a) \sigma_a^2\) with an unbiased gradient estimator converges to the Dirac distribution on the arm with minimal variance.
- **Mechanism:** The update \(H_{t+1} = H_t - \rho_t g_t\) uses \(g_t(a) = (R_t - \bar{R}_t)(\mathbb{1}_{a=A_t} - \Pi_{H_t}(a))\). Lemma 1 proves \(\mathbb{E}[g_t \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = \nabla L(H_t)\), making it unbiased. The softmax parameterization ensures the policy converges to the optimal arm under distinct \(\sigma_a^2\) and bounded rewards.
- **Core assumption:** Arm variances are pairwise distinct (\(\sigma_a^2 \neq \sigma_b^2\) for \(a \neq b\)) and rewards are almost surely bounded.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states “Theoretical analysis proves almost sure convergence … under conditions of distinct variances and bounded rewards”; Section 3.1, Proposition 2 formalizes convergence for Algorithm 1.
- **Break condition:** If multiple arms share the minimal variance, the limit may be a non-Dirac mixture, not a single optimal arm.

### Mechanism 3: Running average baseline reduces gradient variance without bias
- **Claim:** A running average baseline \(\bar{R}_t\) reduces the variance of the gradient estimator while preserving unbiasedness.
- **Mechanism:** The baseline is updated as \(\bar{R}_{t+1} = \bar{R}_t + \frac{R_t - \bar{R}_t}{t+1}\). It is \(\mathcal{F}_t\)-measurable (depends only on past rewards). Lemma 1 shows \(\mathbb{E}[\bar{R}_t(\mathbb{1}_{a=A_t} - \Pi_{H_t}(a)) \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = 0\), so subtracting \(\bar{R}_t\) does not bias the gradient but lowers its variance.
- **Core assumption:** The baseline is computed correctly from historical data only, without leakage of current reward.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.1, Equation 11 gives the update rule; Section 3, Lemma 1 proves the zero-expectation property.
- **Break condition:** If \(\bar{R}_t\) inadvertently includes \(R_t\) or uses future information, the gradient becomes biased and convergence may fail.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Stochastic Approximation (Robbins-Monro)**
  - Why needed: The algorithm is a stochastic iterative process \(H_{t+1} = H_t - \rho_t g_t\). Convergence theory (step-size conditions, Lyapunov functions) underpins the almost-sure convergence claim.
  - Quick check: What conditions on the learning rate schedule \(\rho_t\) are necessary for convergence of a stochastic approximation algorithm with an unbiased gradient estimator? (Answer: \(\sum_t \rho_t = \infty\) and \(\sum_t \rho_t^2 < \infty\), e.g., \(\rho_t = c/t\).)

- **Concept: Softmax Policy Parameterization**
  - Why needed: The softmax maps unbounded preferences \(H\) to a probability simplex. Its gradient structure \(\nabla \Pi_H(a) = \Pi_H(a)(\mathbb{1}_{a} - \Pi_H)\) is used in deriving the unbiased estimator.
  - Quick check: Derive \(\frac{\partial}{\partial H(b)} \Pi_H(a)\) and explain its role in the policy gradient formula.

- **Concept: Unbiased Gradient Estimation in Bandits**
  - Why needed: The objective \(L(H)\) depends on unknown variances. The estimator \(g_t\) must satisfy \(\mathbb{E}[g_t \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = \nabla L(H_t)\) despite this dependence.
  - Quick check: Why is the term \((R_t - \bar{R}_t)\) used instead of just \(R_t\)? How does it maintain unbiasedness? (Answer: The baseline \(\bar{R}_t\) is \(\mathcal{F}_t\)-measurable and has zero correlation with \((\mathbb{1}_{a=A_t} - \Pi_{H_t}(a))\) in expectation.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Policy Module → Sampling & Reward Module → Statistic & Composite Reward Module → Gradient & Update Module

- **Critical path:**  
  Sample arm \(A_t\) from softmax policy → Draw \(\ell\) rewards from arm \(A_t\) → Compute composite reward \(R_t\) and update running baseline \(\bar{R}_{t+1}\) → Compute gradient \(g_t\) using current baseline → Update preferences \(H_{t+1} = H_t - \rho_t g_t\) → Repeat.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Mini-batch size \(\ell\)*: Larger \(\ell\) reduces gradient variance (especially for risk-aware objectives) but increases per-step computation. Variance minimization uses minimal \(\ell=2\) for unbiasedness but suffers high gradient noise.  
  - *Learning rate \(\rho\)*: Constant \(\rho\) (used in theory and simulations) risks oscillations; decaying \(\rho_t \propto 1/t\) aids convergence but slows adaptation.  
  - *Baseline choice*: Running average \(\bar{R}_t\) is simple and unbiased; state-dependent baselines (if available) could further reduce variance but require additional structure.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *Diverging preferences*: \(H_t\) values grow without bound, causing premature collapse to a suboptimal arm (often from too large \(\rho\)).  
  - *Stagnant optimal selection*: Frequency of the optimal arm plateaus well below 100% despite clear variance separation (suggesting insufficient steps or high gradient variance).  
  - *Baseline leakage*: If \(\bar{R}_t\) accidentally incorporates the current \(R_t\), the gradient becomes biased and convergence guarantees break.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Unbiasedness Check:** Fix policy to always select arm \(a\). Run many steps, average computed \(R_t\), and compare to the true \(\sigma_a^2\) (estimated via many samples). Should match within statistical error.  
  2. **Convergence Sanity Check:** For 2 arms with \(\sigma_1^2=1, \sigma_2^2=2\), initialize \(H_1=0\). Track \(\Pi_{H_t}(1)\) over \(t\); it should converge to 1. Test both constant and decaying \(\rho_t\).  
  3. **Baseline Impact Test:** Compare empirical variance of \(g_t\) across steps with \(\bar{R}_t=0\) vs. the running average baseline. Baseline should significantly reduce gradient variance without altering the limiting policy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can the algorithm be extended to contextual, time-dependent, and reinforcement learning settings? The current method assumes stationary i.i.d. rewards without context.
- Can variance reduction techniques improve sample efficiency in high-dimensional action spaces? The two-sample estimator may not scale well with many arms.
- What are the convergence guarantees for variance-reduced softmax policy gradients? The existing proof does not cover variance reduction.
- Does the algorithm converge under heavy-tailed (unbounded) reward distributions? The boundedness assumption may be too restrictive.

## Limitations
- Theoretical assumptions (distinct variances, bounded rewards) may not hold in practical unbounded distributions like Gaussians used in simulations.
- Regret metric definition and risk-aware hyperparameters (\(\lambda_\mu, \lambda_\sigma\), mini-batch size \(\ell\)) are not explicitly specified, hindering exact replication.
- The estimator requires two independent draws per step, doubling sample cost compared to standard bandit algorithms.
- Convergence to a single optimal arm is not guaranteed if variances are tied; the limit may be a mixture over equally good arms.

## Confidence
- Unbiased variance estimator (two-draw): High (straightforward calculation).
- Unbiased gradient estimator: High (Lemma 1 proven under assumptions).
- Convergence to Dirac under distinct variances and bounded rewards: Medium (assumptions are strong; empirical alignment plausible but not verified under all conditions).
- Practical zero regret and high optimal selection: Medium (depends on unspecified hyperparameters and distribution choices; results in paper are promising but details missing).
