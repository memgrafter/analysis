# Rational Synthesizers or Heuristic Followers? Analyzing LLMs in RAG-based Question-Answering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06189  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06189  
- **Reference Count:** 8  
- **Primary Result:** LLMs in RAG settings are vulnerable heuristic followers (repetition, primacy) rather than rational evidence aggregators, with larger models showing greater rigidity.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines how large language models (LLMs) synthesize multiple, often conflicting, evidence documents in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems. The authors introduce GroupQA, a dataset of 1,635 controversial questions with 15,058 stance-annotated documents, and conduct controlled experiments that manipulate evidence quantity, redundancy, order, and conflict. Their results reveal persistent heuristic biases: paraphrased repetitions of a single argument outweigh distinct independent evidence (illusory truth effect), early documents exert disproportionate influence (primacy bias), and larger models exhibit lower belief plasticity—becoming more resistant to updating parametric priors with retrieved evidence. Even when evidence explicitly conflicts, these heuristics remain, challenging the view of LLMs as rational synthesizers and suggesting the need for RAG designs that mitigate shallow textual biases.

## Method Summary
The study uses GroupQA, a dataset constructed by generating binary questions across 95 categories, retrieving web documents via Google Custom Search, and filtering/annotating each document for stance (Yes/No) and strength via dual judge models (GPT-4o-mini and Gemini-2.5-Flash). Controlled experiments are conducted on multiple open- and closed-source LLMs (e.g., Llama-3.1-70B, DeepSeek-R1-8B, Gemini-2.5-FL) by systematically varying the presented document set: number of documents, redundancy (paraphrased copies of one document vs. distinct documents), ordering, and balance of conflicting evidence. Model outputs are analyzed using metrics including belief plasticity (absolute probability shift from prior), answer flip rates, causal document influence via leave-one-out perturbation, and faithfulness of verbalized document importance.

## Key Results
- **Heuristic over rational synthesis:** Paraphrased repetitions of a single argument are more persuasive than multiple distinct independent pieces of evidence (illusory truth effect).
- **Primacy bias:** Models disproportionately rely on early documents in the context window, even when later documents are equally supportive.
- **Scale increases rigidity:** Larger models show lower belief plasticity (e.g., Llama-70B plasticity 0.0074 vs. DeepSeek-R1-8B 0.075), making them less adaptable to retrieved evidence that contradicts strong parametric priors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Illusory Truth Effect
- **Claim:** LLMs are more persuaded by repetition of a single paraphrased argument than by distinct independent evidence.
- **Mechanism:** The model conflates textual frequency with evidential consensus, weighting token-level co-occurrence and surface similarity more heavily than semantic diversity or informational independence.
- **Core assumption:** Aggregation operates on shallow textual heuristics rather than a semantic evaluation of evidence independence.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 4.4 (flipping dynamics tables/figures); neighboring corpus lacks direct study of this repetition effect.
- **Break condition:** Balanced, high-conflict contexts partially attenuate the effect but do not eliminate it.

### Mechanism 2: Primacy Bias
- **Claim:** LLMs exhibit a persistent primacy bias, where earlier documents have disproportionate influence on the final answer.
- **Mechanism:** Early documents establish an anchoring "prior" within the context that subsequent reasoning (even with CoT) fails to adequately override, possibly due to attention "sinks" that accumulate disproportionate weight on initial tokens.
- **Core assumption:** Model processing is order-sensitive in a way that internal reasoning does not correct.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 4.5 (permuted balanced contexts, Figure 7); neighboring "Lost in the Middle" work shows U-shaped curves, but this is a specific primacy effect in multi-document synthesis.
- **Break condition:** Introducing explicit conflict modulates but does not remove the bias.

### Mechanism 3: Scale and Belief Plasticity
- **Claim:** Model scale correlates inversely with belief plasticity; larger models are more resistant to updating parametric priors with retrieved evidence.
- **Mechanism:** Scaling enhances the stability and entrenchment of internal parametric knowledge, so the evidence strength required to override priors increases super-linearly, following a power-law decay in plasticity.
- **Core assumption:** RAG grounding efficacy diminishes with scale when retrieval challenges strong parametric beliefs; larger models are not more rational but more rigid.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract; Section 4.3 (plasticity definition and fitted power-law `y=0.180·x^(-0.097)` across Llama and Qwen families); neighboring corpus focuses on domain-specific RAG, not scaling trends in belief rigidity.
- **Break condition:** May not hold for models trained with explicit objectives to maximize in-context learning or with architectural changes targeting evidence integration.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Belief Plasticity (PL)
- **Why needed:** Primary quantitative metric introduced to measure a model's sensitivity to new evidence; core dependent variable across experiments.
- **Quick check:** If Model A has plasticity 0.05 and Model B has 0.01, which model's beliefs are more easily shifted? What does plasticity 0.0 imply?

### Concept: Causal Document Influence (Leave-One-Out)
- **Why needed:** Self-reported document importance is unfaithful; LOO perturbation provides ground truth for which documents actually change the answer.
- **Quick check:** Why is asking a model "which document was most important?" an unreliable way to measure that document's real impact?

### Concept: Conflicting Context vs. Prior-Only Setting
- **Why needed:** Key manipulation in Section 4.5 moves from a single-sided (prior-challenging) evidence set to a balanced, conflicting one, changing aggregation dynamics.
- **Quick check:** How does the model's "flip threshold" differ when starting with balanced pro/con documents versus only internal prior?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Query Encoder/Retriever (assumed) -> Document Formatter -> LLM Engine -> Output Parser

**Critical path:** Document Set Attributes -> LLM Internal State (Belief) -> Final Output. Experiments permute Document Set Attributes (quantity, redundancy, order, conflict) and measure delta in Final Output; LOO traces influence to individual documents.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Context Window vs. Evidence Quality: stuffing with paraphrased redundancy (illusory truth) is more effective than diverse sources within a fixed context budget.
- Scale vs. Adaptability: larger models improve prior correctness but severely reduce plasticity, making system less responsive to novel retrieval.
- Order Randomization vs. Retrieval Relevance: randomizing document order breaks primacy bias but may disrupt coherence if retrieved documents are topical.

**Failure signatures:**
1. High Unfaithful Attribution: model explanations identify "important" documents with low LOO causal impact.
2. Redundancy Over-Dominance: flip rate/threshold curves for paraphrased evidence dominate those for distinct evidence.
3. Scale-Rigidity Paradox: measured plasticity decays with model size, indicating "grounding" failure on topics with strong priors.
4. Primacy Persistence: answer stability remains high even when supporting evidence is placed at the end of a balanced context.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline Plasticity & Scale Test:** For a random subset of questions, compute PL (Eq. 4) for 2–3 models of different scales (e.g., 8B, 32B, 70B). Expect inverse relationship.
2. **Illusory Truth Test:** For questions where the model's prior opposes the evidence, create two document sets: `[paraphrased_doc x N]` vs. `[distinct_doc_1, …, distinct_doc_N]`. Plot flip rate vs. N (1–5). Expect paraphrased curve above distinct.
3. **Primacy & Conflict Test:** For a balanced set (3 Yes docs + 3 No docs), create two orders: `[Yes,Yes,Yes,No,No,No]` vs. `[No,No,No,Yes,Yes,Yes]`. Measure probability of "Yes" answer. Expect higher "Yes" probability when Yes-first if prior is No.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why do larger language models exhibit lower plasticity (greater resistance to evidence) in RAG-based QA?
2. Does intentionally retrieving dissenting viewpoints (adversarial retrieval) reliably attenuate heuristic biases like the illusory truth effect?
3. How do source metadata (e.g., credibility signals, publication dates) alter LLM aggregation of conflicting evidence?
4. Why does Chain-of-Thought reasoning fail to mitigate heuristic biases in multi-document evidence aggregation?
5. What architectural or training modifications could promote rational synthesis (information-independent aggregation) over heuristic following in RAG systems?

## Limitations
- Dataset construction details (paraphrase generation, similarity thresholds) are under-specified, risking reproducibility.
- Model inference settings (prompt templates, decoding) are not fully documented, potentially affecting output distributions.
- Attribution faithfulness evaluated on only 200 samples, limiting statistical robustness.

## Confidence
- **Illusory truth effect:** Medium – controlled experiments support it, but replication uncertain due to under-specified paraphrase parameters.
- **Primacy bias:** Medium – consistent within-paper evidence, but order effects may interact with document relevance.
- **Scale-rigidity (plasticity decay):** Medium – scaling trend clear across two model families, yet may not generalize beyond tested models.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate dataset construction with explicit paraphrase count (e.g., 5 per doc) and similarity thresholds; re-run illusory truth experiment to confirm flip-rate curves.
2. Test order bias with interleaved pro/con documents (e.g., alternating) vs. blocked; measure if primacy persists when logical coherence is maintained.
3. Evaluate plasticity on models explicitly fine-tuned for RAG to test the break condition that scale alone does not guarantee rigidity.
