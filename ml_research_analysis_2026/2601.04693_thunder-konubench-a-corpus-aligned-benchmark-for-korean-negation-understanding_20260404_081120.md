# Thunder-KoNUBench: A Corpus-Aligned Benchmark for Korean Negation Understanding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04693
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04693
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** LLMs struggle with Korean negation; a corpus-aligned benchmark (Thunder-KoNUBench, 4,784 instances) reveals cloze-style fine-tuning (+34.2%) significantly outperforms symbol-style (+6.4%) for learning negation, with human accuracy at 97.6%.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the lack of dedicated Korean negation benchmarks by introducing Thunder-KoNUBench, a sentence-level test set constructed to mirror the real-world distribution of Korean negation phenomena. Analysis of existing benchmarks (KMMLU, KoBest BoolQ) shows current LLMs perform poorly on Korean negation. The authors evaluate 47 models, finding that larger models handle negation better while instruction tuning has mixed, often negative, effects for non-Korean models in cloze format. Fine-tuning experiments demonstrate that generative, cloze-style supervision—where models produce the negated sentence—yields substantial improvements on the benchmark and better transfer to related tasks compared to multiple-choice symbol-style training. Human performance at 97.6% validates the benchmark's quality and sets a strong upper bound.

## Method Summary
The authors first analyzed a large Korean Wikipedia corpus (~30k sentences) using rule-based negation detection to map the empirical distribution of negation types (e.g., `안`, `못`, `-지말-`) and clause contexts. This distribution directly guided the construction of Thunder-KoNUBench: original sentences were paired with manually crafted "Standard Negation" and "Local Negation" options adhering to Korean syntax, while LLM-assisted generation produced "Contradiction" and "Paraphrase" distractors (all manually reviewed). The resulting 4,784-instance benchmark splits into train/val/test sets. Models are evaluated in two formats: **cloze** (computing likelihood of full negated sentences) and **symbol** (selecting option letters). Fine-tuning uses LoRA on the training split in either format, with transfer evaluated on KoBest BoolQ and other Korean tasks.

## Key Results
- Baseline evaluation of 47 LLMs shows significant performance gaps on Korean negation, with smaller models (<10B params) struggling notably.
- Cloze-style fine-tuning yields a +34.2% absolute gain on Thunder-KoNUBench, far surpassing symbol-style fine-tuning (+6.4%), and demonstrates superior transfer to the symbol format and broader contextual comprehension on KoBest BoolQ.
- Instruction-tuned non-Korean models often underperform their base counterparts in the cloze setting, indicating a "curse of multilinguality" for this format.
- Human accuracy reaches 97.6%, confirming the benchmark's high quality and providing a robust performance ceiling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Corpus-Aligned Benchmark Construction
- **Claim:** Building the benchmark from corpus-derived negation distributions yields ecologically valid evaluation.
- **Mechanism:** Analysis of a large Korean corpus identifies real-world frequencies of negation markers (`안`, `못`, etc.) and clause contexts. Benchmark items are generated to match these frequencies, ensuring models are tested on negation as it naturally occurs.
- **Core assumption:** Performance on a linguistically representative sample better predicts real-world competence than a balanced or artificial set.
- **Evidence anchors:** Tables 2 and 5 show corpus vs. benchmark distributions with small KL divergence (0.007–0.414). The rule-based corpus analysis with manual verification is detailed in Appendix C.
- **Break condition:** If the corpus (Korean Wikipedia) is domain-biased or the mapping to benchmark items introduces distortion, ecological validity fails.

### Mechanism 2: Cloze-Style Supervision for Deeper Learning
- **Claim:** Generative (cloze) supervision forces models to internalize negation syntax, leading to better learning than symbolic multiple-choice.
- **Mechanism:** Producing a full negated sentence requires identifying the correct negative marker and its syntactic integration (scope, morphology). This generative practice promotes deeper rule acquisition versus pattern matching among discrete options in symbol format.
- **Core assumption:** Production (generation) better cultivates syntactic knowledge than recognition (selection).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 5.2 report cloze fine-tuning gains (+34.2%) dwarfing symbol-style (+6.4%), with better transfer to other tasks (Table 8, Figure 4).
- **Break condition:** If model architectures inherently favor discrimination (e.g., encoder-only) or generation introduces instability, the cloze advantage may diminish.

### Mechanism 3: Multilingual Instruction Tuning Degradation
- **Claim:** Instruction tuning on high-resource (often English) data impairs negation understanding in Korean for non-Korean models, especially in cloze format.
- **Mechanism:** Instruction data biases models toward high-resource language patterns and prompt formats. The cloze format (full sentence generation) is underrepresented in such data, causing performance drops when evaluated this way—a "curse of multilinguality."
- **Core assumption:** Implicit format/linguistic biases in instruction corpora transfer negatively to structurally distinct, low-resource evaluations.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes mixed effects and degradation for non-Korean models in cloze; Section 5.2 (Figure 4) shows instruction-tuned models underperforming base models in cloze.
- **Break condition:** More linguistically balanced instruction data or inclusion of Korean cloze tasks could mitigate this effect.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Korean Negation Typology
- **Why needed:** Applying negation correctly in Korean requires selecting the appropriate negative marker (`안`, `못`, `-지말-`, antonym) based on predicate type and sentence structure (e.g., `못` cannot modify stative adjectives).
- **Quick check:** For "그는 빨리 달린다" (He runs quickly), which is valid standard negation? A) 그는 빨리 달리지 않았다. B) 그는 빨리 달리지 못했다. C) 그는 빨리 달리지 말았다.

### Concept: Distributional Alignment Principle
- **Why needed:** The benchmark's generalizability hinges on reflecting real-world negation frequencies; understanding how corpus stats (Table 2) inform benchmark composition (Table 5) is key.
- **Quick check:** If a corpus shows 70% of negation uses `안`-type markers in main clauses, what approximate proportion of `안`-type standard negation items should the benchmark have to maintain alignment?

### Concept: Cloze vs. Symbol MCQA Formats
- **Why needed:** The paper's core finding on format efficacy depends on distinguishing the cognitive demand of generating an answer (cloze) versus selecting a token (symbol), affecting what linguistic knowledge is tested.
- **Quick check:** In cloze, the model computes likelihoods for *complete sentences*. In symbol, it computes likelihoods for *option letters*. What does this imply about the depth of linguistic processing required?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map:
Corpus Analyzer -> Benchmark Constructor -> Evaluator -> Fine-Tuning Module

### Critical path:
Corpus analysis → Distribution statistics → Benchmark item generation → Manual review → Train/val/test split → Model evaluation (cloze/symbol) → Fine-tuning (LoRA) → Transfer evaluation (KoBest BoolQ, etc.)

### Design tradeoffs:
- **Manual vs. Automated Generation:** Manual creation of negation options ensures syntactic accuracy but limits scale; LLM-assisted distractor generation increases efficiency but requires careful review.
- **Format Choice:** Cloze format probes deeper linguistic integration but is computationally heavier; symbol format is efficient but may encourage shallow pattern matching.
- **Multilinguality:** Evaluating non-Korean models tests cross-lingual transfer but introduces confounds from script, syntax, and tokenization differences.

### Failure signatures:
- **Distribution Mismatch:** High KL divergence (>0.5) between corpus and benchmark indicates poor ecological validity.
- **Annotation Inconsistency:** High initial inter-annotator disagreement (e.g., >10%) that does not drop after consensus cycles.
- **Format Overfitting:** Cloze fine-tuning yields minimal symbol transfer (or vice versa), indicating learned format-specific shortcuts rather than negation rules.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting:** Post-fine-tuning performance drops on other Korean tasks (KMMLU, KoBest BoolQ), seen in Tables 14–17.

### First 3 experiments:
1. **Distribution Alignment Replication:** Compute KL divergence between Thunder-KoNUBench and a new, domain-diverse Korean corpus (e.g., news, dialogues). Divergence >0.5 challenges ecological validity claims.
2. **Format Efficacy Replication:** Replicate cloze vs. symbol fine-tuning on 2–3 smaller base models (<10B params) to verify the reported +34.2% vs. +6.4% performance gap.
3. **Cross-Lingual Degradation Test:** Evaluate Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (non-Korean, instruction-tuned) on Thunder-KoNUBench in both formats. Expect significant cloze underperformance vs. its base model, confirming the "curse of multilinguality."

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper implicitly raises several open questions through its limitations and proposed next checks:
- **Domain Generalizability:** Does Thunder-KoNUBench's ecological validity hold across diverse Korean domains (e.g., news, dialogues) beyond Wikipedia?
- **Scale Invariance:** Is the substantial cloze-style fine-tuning advantage (+34.2% vs. +6.4%) consistent across smaller model scales (<10B parameters)?
- **Multilingual Degradation Cause:** What factors (data composition, tokenization, format bias) cause instruction-tuned non-Korean models to underperform in cloze negation tasks?
- **Annotation Automation:** How can manual negation option creation be scaled while preserving syntactic accuracy?
- **Format Bias Separation:** To what extent do cloze vs. symbol performance differences reflect genuine negation understanding versus training data format biases?

## Limitations
- **Corpus representativeness:** Benchmark derived from Korean Wikipedia may not generalize to conversational or literary domains.
- **Manual annotation scalability:** High-quality manual creation of negation options limits benchmark size and may introduce subtle annotator biases.
- **Format confounds:** Cloze vs. symbol performance differences may reflect training data format biases rather than pure negation understanding.
- **Non-Korean model confounds:** Poor cloze performance of instruction-tuned non-Korean models could stem from tokenization or script mismatches, not just instruction data composition.

## Confidence
- **Corpus-aligned benchmark validity:** High (supported by KL divergence metrics and manual verification process).
- **Cloze-style supervision superiority:** Medium (experimental results are strong but causal link to "deeper representations" is inferred).
- **Multilingual instruction tuning degradation:** Medium (effect is clear but attribution to data composition assumes without direct corpus analysis).
