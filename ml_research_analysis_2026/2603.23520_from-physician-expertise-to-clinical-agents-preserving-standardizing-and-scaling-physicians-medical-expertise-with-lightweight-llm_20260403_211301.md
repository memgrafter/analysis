# From Physician Expertise to Clinical Agents: Preserving, Standardizing, and Scaling Physicians' Medical Expertise with Lightweight LLM

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23520
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23520
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** A 1.5B parameter model (Med-Shicheng) trained via a five-stage framework achieves performance comparable to 100B+ scale models in automatic TCM evaluation and is rated by expert physicians as more stylistically faithful to distinguished practitioners than giant models like GPT-5.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical bottleneck of capturing and scaling the individualized clinical expertise of master physicians, which is traditionally transmitted inefficiently via apprenticeship. The authors present **Med-Shicheng**, a framework that trains a lightweight language model (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Base) to internalize the distinct diagnostic and treatment philosophies of five renowned Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) doctors. By progressing through five curated stages—domain pretraining, standard reasoning supervision, reinforcement learning for logic, unique-knowledge fine-tuning with retrieval-augmented context, and final reasoning reinforcement—the model learns to emulate multiple experts within a single parameter-efficient network. Evaluations show Med-Shicheng matches or exceeds state-of-the-art giant models in automated tasks and, crucially, is judged by human experts to better capture the target physicians' unique styles and philosophical consistency. The study also highlights a key limitation: automated LLM judges fail to appreciate these fine-grained, physician-specific distinctions, suggesting a need for domain-adapted evaluation metrics.

## Method Summary
The core is a **five-stage training pipeline** applied to a 1.5B parameter base model. Stage 1 performs continued pretraining on a large TCM corpus (TCMCorpus) to establish a domain foundation. Stage 2 uses supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on standard TCM instruction data to learn basic diagnostic logic with Chain-of-Thought (CoT). Stage 3 applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) reinforcement learning on reasoning-focused data to improve logical coherence. Stage 4 introduces the key innovation: SFT on physician-specific cases where each input is augmented with the top-3 relevant knowledge chunks retrieved from that physician's authored works (a RAG-inspired technique). This conditions the model to apply the correct expert's theoretical lens. Finally, Stage 5 uses Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO) reinforcement learning on unique reasoning data to solidify fidelity to each physician's specific diagnostic patterns. The model is evaluated on seven clinical tasks using both automatic scoring by large proprietary LLMs and a human evaluation rubric administered to the physicians' apprentices.

## Key Results
*   Med-Shicheng (1.5B) achieves automatic evaluation scores comparable to giant models (DeepSeek-R1, GPT-5) across seven TCM clinical tasks, despite a 447× parameter disadvantage.
*   Human expert evaluations rated Med-Shicheng significantly higher than large models on **similarity to the distinguished physicians' diagnostic styles** and **consistency with TCM philosophy**, while large models performed better on generic factual completeness.
*   The framework successfully consolidates five distinct physicians' knowledge systems into a single model without catastrophic interference, enabled by the staged approach and retrieval-augmented context.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Staged, Graduated Knowledge Injection
The five-stage pipeline prevents catastrophic forgetting by first building a robust **in-domain foundation model** via pretraining on TCMCorpus. This creates a stable, generalizable TCM knowledge substrate. Subsequent stages then "stack" increasingly specific capabilities: standard diagnostic logic (Stage 2), flexible reasoning via reinforcement (Stage 3), unique physician knowledge via retrieval-augmented SFT (Stage 4), and finally, the unique reasoning patterns via preference-based RL (Stage 5). This ordered progression minimizes interference between the core TCM system and the multiple, distinct expert "styles."

*   **Break condition:** If Stage 1 is weak, later stages yield generic or incoherent blends of physicians' styles rather than faithful emulation of one.

### Mechanism 2: Reasoning-Chain-of-Thought Supervision and Reinforcement Learning
Stages 3 and 5 explicitly train the model on full diagnostic reasoning traces (etiology → pathogenesis → syndrome → principle → prescription). Reinforcement learning (GRPO in Stage 3, KTO in Stage 5) then rewards outputs that exhibit high-quality, stepwise logical coherence and alignment with target physicians' reasoning preferences. This moves beyond simple imitation of static answers to capturing the *process* of clinical decision-making.

*   **Break condition:** If CoT traces are low-quality or RL rewards are mis-specified (e.g., only matching final prescriptions), the model produces superficially correct outputs without the underlying diagnostic logic, failing human evaluations of "diagnostic philosophy" similarity.

### Mechanism 3: Retrieval-Augmented Context for Multi-Expert Consolidation
During Stage 4, each clinical case is conditioned on the top-3 most relevant 512-token knowledge chunks retrieved from that specific physician's authored books/papers. This creates a direct signal: "solve this case *using these specific theoretical principles*." It forces the model to contextually activate and apply the correct physician's unique knowledge base, preventing a blended, monolithic representation of all experts.

*   **Break condition:** Poor retrieval (irrelevant or generic chunks) dilutes the capture of the physician's distinctive philosophy, leading to low similarity scores in human evaluation.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Domain Adaptation via Continued Pretraining**
    *   **Why needed here:** The base model lacks deep, structured TCM expertise. Stage 1 bridges this gap, providing the essential "TCM lens" required for all subsequent reasoning. Without this, fine-tuning on clinical cases would be ineffective.
    *   **Quick check question:** If you skip domain pretraining and go directly to clinical fine-tuning, what two failure modes would you expect?

*   **Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Preference (KTO/GRPO)**
    *   **Why needed here:** Supervised fine-tuning teaches *what* to output. RL teaches *how to reason well*. Stages 3 and 5 use RL to reward high-quality, coherent reasoning chains and faithful adherence to a physician's diagnostic logic, refining the model beyond imitation.
    *   **Quick check question:** Why might standard SFT on a physician's case notes fail to capture their full diagnostic "philosophy," and how does RL address this?

*   **Concept: Multi-Expert Knowledge Consolidation without Interference**
    *   **Why needed here:** The goal is one model that can emulate five physicians. Naively mixing all data would create a "blended" output. The staged approach and RAG-like context are safeguards to keep each physician's knowledge system distinct and retrievable.
    *   **Quick check question:** What architectural or training components are specifically designed to prevent Physician A's knowledge from corrupting Physician B's style in the single model?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Base Model (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Base) -> Stage 1: TCMCorpus Pretraining (TCM-SC-Base) -> Stage 2: SFT on TCM-General-Instructions (TCM-SC-normal-instruct) -> Stage 3: GRPO RL on TCM-Reasoning (TCM-SC-normal-reasoning) -> Stage 4: SFT on TCM-Unique-Instructions + RAG Context (TCM-SC-unique-instruct) -> Stage 5: KTO RL on TCM-Unique-Reasoning (Med-Shicheng)

*   **Critical path:**
    1.  **Complete Stage 1 (Domain Pretraining):** Non-negotiable foundation. Validate with a general TCM knowledge quiz (>90% accuracy).
    2.  **Integrate Stage 4 (Unique Knowledge SFT with RAG):** Core innovation for multi-physician modeling. Must follow Stages 1-3. Validate by checking if outputs show traces of the target physician's terminology/theories.
    3.  **Finalize with Stage 5 (Unique Reasoning RL):** Refines stylistic match. Human evaluation scores on "Similarity to distinguished physicians" are the key validation metric.

*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Data Volume vs. Specificity:** Performance correlates with a physician's clinical case volume. For sparse data, reliance on published books (knowledge tokens) may yield less accurate style emulation.
    *   **Single Model vs. Multiple Specialists:** Consolidating 5 experts into one 1.5B model simplifies deployment but requires a complex 5-stage schedule and risks knowledge interference, mitigated by RAG and staged RL.
    *   **Automated vs. Human Eval:** Automatic eval (LLM-as-judge) is scalable but fails at "fine-grained, individualized clinical distinctions." The tradeoff is using automated eval for rapid iteration and human eval for final verification of physician alignment.

*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **High auto-eval, low human-eval:** Model outputs are factually correct and logically sound but **generic**, lacking the target physician's distinctive patterns. Indicates Stage 4/5 failure.
    *   **Low scores on one physician:** Check if that physician's case/knowledge volume was disproportionately low.
    *   **HuatuoGPT2-7B anomaly:** Extremely high scores on one physician's prescription but low elsewhere suggests **training data contamination** (specific cases memorized).
    *   **Incoherent reasoning chains:** Correct final prescription but messy etiology/pathogenesis steps. Stage 3 (Reasoning-based RL) was ineffective.

*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Sanity Check - Domain Foundation:** After Stage 1, run a broad TCM knowledge quiz. Target >90% accuracy. If low, increase TCMCorpus quality/quantity or training steps.
    2.  **RAG Context Validation:** For a case from Physician X, inspect the top-3 retrieved knowledge chunks during Stage 4. Are they unambiguously from Physician X's works and directly relevant? If not, fix the embedding model or chunking.
    3.  **Human-like Style Probe (Pre-Final RL):** After Stage 4 (before Stage 5 RL), generate outputs for 10 hidden cases per physician. Perform a blinded check: "Does this sound like Dr. X?" If most are "no," revisit `TCM-unique-instructions` curation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **OQ1:** How can automated evaluation models (LLM-as-a-Judge) be reliably adapted to capture fine-grained, physician-specific diagnostic styles and philosophical nuances that human experts can discern? The paper shows current judges fail at this, requiring domain-adapted judges or direct physician involvement.
*   **OQ2:** What is the precise relationship between the volume of a target physician's curated clinical cases versus their textual knowledge resources in determining the fidelity with which Med-Shicheng internalizes their distinct knowledge system? The paper identifies a correlation but not a causal mechanism or quantitative trade-off.
*   **OQ3:** What are the underlying architectural and training mechanisms that prevent negative interference when integrating multiple, distinct physicians' knowledge systems into a single model? The staged approach, RAG-like context, and RL are implicated but not isolated.
*   **OQ4:** Can the Med-Shicheng framework's five-stage pipeline be directly and effectively translated to model the expertise of modern medicine specialists? A conceptual mapping is provided but lacks empirical validation.
*   **OQ5:** To what extent do the current human evaluation criteria (similarity, consistency, safety, completeness, fluency) fully capture the quality of a clinical reasoning agent? Qualitative feedback suggests missing dimensions like stylistic conciseness or tacit heuristic expression.

## Limitations
*   **Data & Reproducibility:** The original physician materials, exact test cases, and precise training hyperparameters are not released, blocking exact replication. Automated evaluations rely on proprietary, inaccessible LLMs (Deepseek-V3.2, GPT-5).
*   **Evaluation Validity:** Human evaluation details (expert selection, sample size, statistical reporting) are sparse. The discrepancy between automatic and human results is clear, but the robustness of the human finding to a larger, multi-center study is unknown.
*   **Mechanism Attribution:** The paper does not isolate the contribution of individual components (e.g., ablating the RAG context in Stage 4) to the multi-expert consolidation capability, leaving the key mechanism largely inferred.

## Next Checks
1.  **Reproduce Automatic Evaluation with Open-Source Judges:** Re-run the evaluation protocol using accessible judges (e.g., Llama-3.1-70B) and measure correlation with the (limited available) human scores. This tests the robustness of the "LLM-as-judge limitation" finding and establishes a reproducible baseline.
2.  **Ablate the RAG Context Component:** Train a version of Med-Shicheng identical except that Stage 4 SFT is performed **without** the physician-specific knowledge chunk conditioning. Compare human-assessed style similarity and philosophical consistency against the full model. A significant drop would confirm the mechanism's necessity for multi-expert modeling.
3.  **Conduct a Blinded, Multi-Center Human Study:** Perform a new human evaluation with a larger, diverse panel of TCM experts (not just apprentices), using a statistically powered sample size. Report inter-rater reliability (e.g., ICC) and p-values for the superiority claim on "similarity to distinguished physicians." This is the critical validation for the paper's most significant empirical claim.
