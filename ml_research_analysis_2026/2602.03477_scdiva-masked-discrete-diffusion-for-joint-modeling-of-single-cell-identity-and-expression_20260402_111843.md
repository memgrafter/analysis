# ScDiVa: Masked Discrete Diffusion for Joint Modeling of Single-Cell Identity and Expression

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.03477
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03477
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ScDiVa achieves state-of-the-art transfer performance across rank-value reconstruction, multi-batch integration, cell type annotation, and perturbation prediction on single-cell RNA-seq benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
ScDiVa introduces a masked discrete diffusion foundation model for single-cell RNA-seq data, directly addressing the structural mismatch between autoregressive sequence modeling and the unordered, sparse nature of such data. The forward diffusion process mimics biological dropout, enabling the model to jointly recover gene identity and expression magnitude through a bidirectional denoiser and an entropy-normalized serialization scheme. Pre-trained on 59 million cells, scDiVa sets new performance records on a range of downstream tasks, establishing masked diffusion as a biologically coherent and effective alternative to autoregression for single-cell foundation modeling.

## Method Summary
Input single-cell RNA-seq profiles are first log-normalized. Gene entropy is computed across the entire corpus, and each gene’s expression is divided by its entropy plus a small constant to derive a score. The top 1,200 genes per cell, ordered by descending score, are tokenized into discrete IDs using a fixed vocabulary of 41,818 genes, with each position holding a gene ID and a normalized expression value. During training, a continuous-time masking process independently masks each non-special token with probability *t* drawn uniformly from [0,1]. A 12-layer Transformer processes the masked sequence, prepended by a latent anchor token ([LAT]). The model outputs two heads: a classification head for gene identity (cross-entropy) and a regression head for expression (MSE, weighted by λ=10). Loss is computed only on masked positions. At inference, a single denoising step suffices for downstream tasks.

## Key Results
- Rank‑value reconstruction: Spearman 0.970 on Immune (+14.9% over baselines).
- Multi‑batch integration: Avg‑Bio 0.9566 on PBMC12k (vs. 0.9018 for scGPT).
- Cell type annotation & perturbation: Macro‑F1 0.7919 on hPancreas; Pearson 0.837 on Adamson dataset.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Masked discrete diffusion aligns its forward noising process with biological dropout—random zeroing of gene counts—making the corruption process realistic and the denoising objective naturally suited to recover both which genes are present (identity) and how highly they are expressed (magnitude). The bidirectional denoiser leverages global context via the [LAT] token, avoiding the error accumulation inherent in autoregressive generation. Entropy‑normalized serialization ensures gene selection is driven by both expression level and gene specificity, preserving biological signal while reducing ordering bias.

## Foundational Learning
- **Masked diffusion for discrete data**: Extends diffusion to discrete token spaces, enabling joint modeling of categorical and continuous variables. *Why needed*: scRNA‑seq data are sparse and unordered; discrete diffusion captures dropout without imposing artificial order. *Quick check*: Verify forward masking probability matches uniform *t* and that reverse process predicts only masked tokens.
- **Entropy‑normalized gene scoring**: Scores genes by expression divided by gene entropy to select informative genes per cell. *Why needed*: Pure magnitude selection biases toward housekeeping genes; entropy normalization accounts for gene specificity. *Quick check*: Compute gene entropy distribution; ensure selected gene set varies across cell types and that housekeeping genes receive lower scores.
- **Dual denoising objective**: Combines cross‑entropy for gene identity and MSE for expression. *Why needed*: Jointly optimizes rank and value reconstruction, which are distinct modalities. *Quick check*: Monitor separate loss terms during training; both should decrease steadily.
- **Latent anchor token [LAT]**: A special token prepended to the sequence that aggregates global context. *Why needed*: Provides a fixed point for information flow and stabilizes training. *Quick check*: Inspect gradient norms of [LAT] embeddings; they should be non‑zero throughout training.
- **Depth‑invariant sampling**: The masking time *t* is sampled uniformly across the entire sequence depth. *Why needed*: Exposes the model to a variety of masking extents, improving robustness. *Quick check*: Track distribution of number of masked tokens per batch; should approximately follow a binomial with mean 1200·*t*.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Raw counts → Entropy computation → Top‑k gene selection → Tokenization (gene ID + value) → Embedding (gene embedding + value MLP) → Transformer encoder (12 layers) → [LAT] token → Output heads (gene logits, value prediction)

### Critical path
1. Preprocess data: compute gene entropies, score genes, select top 1,200 per cell, map to vocabulary.
2. For each batch, sample *t* ∼ Uniform(0,1) and independently mask each non‑special token with probability *t*.
3. Forward pass through Transformer, using [LAT] to aggregate context.
4. Compute dual loss only on masked positions: L = –log p(gᵢ|xₜ) + λ·(ŷᵢ – yᵢ)².
5. Backpropagate with AdamW; repeat.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Uniform *t* sampling**: Simple and matches dropout randomness, but may cause extreme masking early in training; alternative schedules could ease optimization.
- **λ weighting**: λ=10 emphasizes value reconstruction; too high can dominate loss, too low may neglect magnitude recovery.
- **Fixed 1,200‑gene window**: Simplifies batching but may exclude important genes for specialized datasets; dynamic selection could improve coverage at the cost of consistency.
- **[LAT] vs. pooling**: A dedicated token avoids information loss from mean pooling but introduces extra parameters; pooling is simpler but less expressive.

### Failure signatures
- **Degenerate entropy normalization**: If ε is too large or entropy miscomputed, selection collapses to pure magnitude ordering → low gene diversity. *Check*: Overlap of selected top‑k genes across diverse cells becomes unusually high.
- **Training instability**: Loss diverges, especially L_val. *Check*: Monitor gradient norms and NaN occurrences in value predictions; warm‑up or gradient clipping may help.
- **Integration failure**: After fine‑tuning, batch effects remain (Avg‑Batch low) or biological signal is lost (Avg‑Bio low). *Check*: UMAP visualizations colored by batch and cell type; batch discriminator accuracy should hover near chance.
- **OOV tokens in downstream tasks**: Many evaluation genes missing from pre‑training vocabulary. *Check*: Compute OOV rate; if >5%, extend vocabulary or map symbols appropriately.

### First three experiments
1. **Entropy replication**: On a public dataset (e.g., PBMC), implement entropy calculation with both fixed-width and quantile binning. Compare gene entropy distributions and top‑k overlaps to expected patterns (e.g., housekeeping genes should have lower entropy).
2. **Small‑scale training**: Train a 4‑layer scDiVa on a subset of pre‑training data (e.g., 100k cells). Verify that L_id and L_val both decrease and that [LAT] gradients remain non‑zero. Adjust λ or *t* distribution if L_val dominates.
3. **Reconstruction sanity check**: Hold out a validation set of cells, mask a random 50% of tokens, and compute Spearman correlation between predicted and true expression for masked genes. Expect >0.8 on this small probe.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly identify open questions; it focuses on presenting the scDiVa framework and its empirical success. Potential directions for future work include scaling to larger gene vocabularies, exploring alternative tokenization strategies, and assessing transfer to additional single‑cell modalities.

## Limitations
- Proprietary pre‑training corpus (59M cells) limits comparability and may skew entropy statistics, affecting public replication.
- Critical hyperparameters (AdamW learning rate, entropy binning details, fine‑tuning adversarial weights) are undisclosed, hindering exact reproduction.
- Ambiguity in evaluation alignment (e.g., whether downstream tasks reuse the same 1,200‑gene selection and how out‑of‑vocabulary genes are handled) creates gaps between reported and replicable results.

## Confidence
- Masked diffusion approach effectiveness: **High**
- Pre‑training scale necessity: **Medium**
- SOTA results on public benchmarks: **Low**
- Biological coherence vs. autoregression: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Entropy calculation replication**: Implement both fixed-width and quantile binning on a public scRNA‑seq dataset (e.g., PBMC). Compare gene entropy distributions and top‑k overlap; assess stability of selected gene sets across cell types.
