# Signature-Kernel Based Evaluation Metrics for Robust Probabilistic and Tail-Event Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.10182  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10182  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Proposed Sig-MMD and CSig-MMD metrics capture full inter‑temporal and cross‑variable dependencies while remaining strictly proper; CSig‑MMD focuses on tail events without sacrificing properness.

## Executive Summary
Probabilistic forecasting metrics commonly rely on independence assumptions across time or variables, limiting their ability to assess complex dependencies and tail performance. This paper introduces two novel evaluation metrics based on the signature kernel: Sig‑MMD, which measures the distance between forecast and observed distributions while capturing higher‑order temporal and spatial structure; and CSig‑MMD, which censors the distribution to prioritize tail accuracy yet retains strict properness. Both metrics operate directly on samples, handle missing data robustly, and are validated through synthetic experiments and nine real‑world benchmarks. Results show they yield distinct model rankings compared to standard scores, with CSig‑MMD highlighting linear models for tail forecasting and Sig‑MMD favoring deep learning models for correlation modeling.

## Method Summary
The metrics build on Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) with a characteristic signature kernel. Input paths are augmented with time and base point to ensure translation and scale invariance. Sig‑MMD estimates the squared MMD between the mean embeddings of forecast and observed signature distributions. CSig‑MMD modifies this by censoring: a tail region is defined using Mahalanobis distance on truncated signatures (computed from training data with a robust Minimum Covariance Determinant estimator). Samples outside the tail have their probability mass relocated to a pivot (zero‑path) to preserve properness. The MMD is then computed on these censored distributions, ensuring only tail discrepancies influence the score. Both metrics are sample‑based and can be estimated via Monte Carlo averaging of kernel values, with the signature kernel obtained either by explicit truncation or via a kernel trick solving a Goursat PDE.

## Key Results
- Sig‑MMD rises from 0.0007 to 0.01 when forecasts miss temporal/spatial correlations, while CRPS, ES, VS remain unchanged.
- CSig‑MMD increases only when tail distributions are inaccurate (e.g., from 0.00005 to 0.00024 for jump‑diffusion tails).
- On nine datasets, CSig‑MMD favors linear models for tail forecasting, whereas Sig‑MMD favors Chronos‑2 for correlation modeling; Chronos‑2 ranks worst on CSig‑MMD for extreme weather despite top Sig‑MMD rank.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Signature Kernel Encodes Joint Distribution Geometry
- **Claim**: Sig‑MMD captures complex inter‑variate and inter‑temporal dependencies without assuming independence, by comparing the distributions of signature features.
- **Mechanism**: The signature of a path is a graded sequence of iterated integrals that uniquely characterizes the path’s geometric profile. The signature kernel \(k(x,y)=\langle S(x), S(y) \rangle\) computes the inner product of these signatures. In MMD, this measures the distance between mean embeddings of signature distributions, thus capturing higher‑order moments and temporal structure.
- **Core assumption**: The signature kernel is characteristic, ensuring MMD is strictly proper. Path augmentations (time, base‑point) guarantee translation and scale invariance.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states capture of complex dependencies; Section 3.3 defines signature invariance; Section 4.1 formalizes Sig‑MMD; related work applies signature kernels to spatio‑temporal forecasting.
- **Break condition**: Truncation level \(K\) too low loses high‑order dependencies; high dimensionality without kernel trick becomes computationally prohibitive.

### Mechanism 2: Censoring via Mass Relocation Focuses on Tails
- **Claim**: CSig‑MMD evaluates tail forecasting accuracy by ignoring errors in the body of the distribution, while remaining a strictly proper scoring rule.
- **Mechanism**: A tail region is defined using Mahalanobis distance on truncated signatures. A soft weight \(w(x)\) assigns 1 to tail samples and 0 to body samples. To preserve properness, probability mass from body samples is relocated to a pivot (zero‑path). The MMD is computed on these censored distributions, so only tail discrepancies affect the score.
- **Core assumption**: The pivot lies outside the tail region (ensured by normalized paths and tail definition). The censoring induces a fixed measurable partition. Mahalanobis distance effectively identifies tail samples using an MCD estimator for robustness.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract describes censoring prioritization; Section 4.2 details equations and Proposition 4.1 on properness; Section 5.2 shows CSig increases only for tail inaccuracies; prior work on censoring for properness.
- **Break condition**: Censoring threshold mis‑specified; pivot within tail; MCD estimator fails with high dimensionality or small samples.

### Mechanism 3: Sample‑Based Evaluation with Robustness to Missing Data
- **Claim**: Both metrics operate directly on samples, require no explicit density, and handle missing data due to signature kernel’s invariance to reparameterization.
- **Mechanism**: MMD is estimated via Monte Carlo samples. The signature kernel can be computed via the kernel trick (Goursat PDE) without explicit signatures and can handle paths of varying lengths and missing values. Augmentations ensure robustness to missing observations.
- **Core assumption**: The kernel is characteristic, ensuring strict properness of finite‑sample MMD estimators. Invariance to reparameterization treats missing data as changes in sampling frequency without altering path geometry.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract lists sample‑based, strictly proper, robust; Section 3.2 notes MMD properness with characteristic kernel; Section 3.3 notes signature invariance; Section 5.3.1 mentions PCA for high dimensions.
- **Break condition**: Very few samples increase MMD variance; extremely high‑dimensional data may need truncated signatures losing information; long gaps in missingness could degrade performance if augmentations insufficient.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input (sample paths) -> Augmentation (add time, base point) -> Signature computation (kernel via PDE or truncation) -> [For CSig: truncated signatures -> MCD estimation -> tail weight/pivot] -> MMD estimation (biased/unbiased) -> Output (squared MMD distance).

**Critical path**: The most computationally intensive step is computing the signature kernel matrix for all sample pairs (forecast vs. ground truth, and within each). For large sample sizes, this is \(O(N^2)\) with kernel trick but linear in total path length. For CSig‑MMD, MCD estimation on signatures is \(O(N \cdot d^2)\) but can be reduced by PCA if \(d\) high.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Truncation level \(K\): Higher captures more dependencies but increases cost/overfitting risk.  
- Kernel bandwidth (if using RBF lift): Affects sensitivity; may need cross‑validation.  
- Censoring quantile \(\tau\): Lower focuses on more extreme tails but reduces tail sample size; too high may include body.  
- Logistic steepness \(\beta\): Controls softness of transition; typically fixed.  
- PCA for Mahalanobis: Trades off dimensionality reduction vs. information loss; used when many variates.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Numerical instability in PDE solver (NaNs or huge values) for large path lengths/high dimensions.  
- MCD estimator fails or gives singular covariance if number of samples < number of features after truncation; PCA mitigates.  
- High variance in MMD estimate: confidence intervals overlap, making rankings unreliable.  
- CSig‑MMD score does not change with tail modifications: indicates censoring region not capturing tails or pivot lies in tail.  
- Memory overflow: kernel matrix \(O(N^2)\) too large; need minibatching or approximations.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Dependency Sensitivity**: Generate synthetic Gaussian process with known temporal/spatial correlations. Create forecasts that remove dependencies (independent marginals). Verify Sig‑MMD increases while CRPS/ES/VS remain unchanged.  
2. **Tail Focus Capability**: Generate forecasts with heavy‑tailed (t‑distribution) or uniform distributions vs. ground truth Gaussian. Check that CSig‑MMD increases only for tail‑missing forecasts, while Sig‑MMD increases for all mismatches.  
3. **Ablation on Censoring Quantile**: On a real dataset (e.g., Exchange), vary tail quantile threshold and observe CSig‑MMD scores for models with known tail performance (e.g., Chronos‑2 vs. Moirai). Expect CSig to diverge from Sig as quantile increases, and Chronos‑2 to worsen on CSig at lower quantiles if tail‑weak.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **High‑dimensional censoring scaling**: How can we design a censoring method for CSig‑MMD that scales linearly with dimensionality, overcoming the computational limitation of truncated signatures? *Basis*: Paper notes truncated signatures restrict application to higher‑dimensional scenarios; future work could include kernel‑based tail detection or efficient signature approximations. *Why unresolved*: Truncated signatures scale exponentially with dimension; no alternative provided. *Evidence needed*: Development and empirical validation of a censoring technique operating directly in signature kernel space or an efficient approximation preserving metric properties.
- **Using metrics as loss functions**: Can Sig‑MMD and CSig‑MMD serve as effective, strictly proper loss functions for training probabilistic forecasting models, and how does training with them compare to standard losses? *Basis*: Paper states they were proposed only for evaluation; gradient behaviour and training effects are unexplored. *Why unresolved*: Differentiability, gradient properties, and optimization behaviour of these kernel‑based, censored metrics are unknown. *Evidence needed*: Derivation of analytic or stochastic gradients, implementation in training loops, systematic experiments comparing forecast accuracy and tail performance versus CRPS or quantile loss.
- **Robustness to missing data**: How robust are Sig‑MMD and CSig‑MMD to missing data in forecasts or ground truth, as claimed, and do they require any specific missingness handling? *Basis*: Abstract claims robustness to missing data, but no experimental validation. *Why unresolved*: Missing data could distort signature kernel computations and Mahalanobis distance, potentially biasing scores or violating properness. *Evidence needed*: Controlled experiments introducing MCAR/MAR/MNAR missingness, measuring metric stability and ranking consistency versus complete‑data baselines, and testing if simple imputation suffices.

## Limitations
- Implementation details (kernel choice, truncation order for Mahalanobis, normalization procedure, hyperparameters for censoring) are unspecified, affecting properness guarantees and sensitivity.
- Quadratic scaling of kernel matrix and potential singularities in Mahalanobis estimation limit practical deployment, especially with large sample sizes or high dimensions.
- Claim of robustness to missing data lacks empirical validation under realistic missingness patterns.

## Confidence
- **Sig‑MMD captures full joint distribution geometry**: High (theoretically grounded via characteristic kernel and MMD properness; synthetic results support).
- **CSig‑MMD tail focus while remaining strictly proper**: Medium (rigorous proof exists but relies on correct pivot placement and hyperparameter specification).
- **Robustness to missing data**: Low (invariance property plausible but lacks thorough empirical validation).
