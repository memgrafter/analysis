# ECG-MoE: Mixture-of-Expert Electrocardiogram Foundation Model

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04589
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04589
- **Reference count:** 34
- **Primary result:** Dual-path Mixture-of-Experts architecture with hierarchical fusion achieves state-of-the-art multi-task ECG performance, reducing RR-interval MAE by 46.0% and arrhythmia detection error by 10.6%, while operating 40% faster with lower GPU memory than multi-task baselines.

## Executive Summary
ECG-MoE introduces a novel foundation model for electrocardiogram analysis that addresses the limitations of existing models in capturing signal periodicity and diverse clinical features. The architecture uses a dual-path Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) to separately model beat-level morphology and inter-beat rhythm, combined with hierarchical fusion of multiple pre-trained time-series models and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for parameter efficiency. Evaluated on five public clinical tasks from MIMIC-IV-ECG, the model sets new performance benchmarks while significantly improving computational efficiency. The design is motivated by the quasi-periodic nature of ECGs and the distinct diagnostic information contained within heartbeats versus rhythm patterns.

## Method Summary
ECG-MoE processes raw ECG waveforms by first segmenting them into individual heartbeats via R-peak detection. It then extracts features in two parallel pathways: a **morphology path** uses multiple CNN experts with varying kernel sizes to analyze intra-beat patterns (e.g., QRS complex, ST changes); a **rhythm path** employs dilated CNNs to model inter-beat temporal dynamics (e.g., RR variability). A task-conditioned gating network dynamically weights these experts. Separately, features from five frozen pre-trained time-series foundation models (TimesNet, DLinear, MOMENT, TEMPO, ECG-FM) are projected and pooled. The two feature streams (periodic and multi-model) are fused via another task-conditioned gate. Task-specific prediction heads are built on this fused representation, with LoRA enabling efficient adaptation of the frozen backbones. The model is trained end-to-end with a composite loss including task losses and contrastive regularization.

## Key Results
- Reduced RR-interval estimation mean absolute error (MAE) by **46.0%** compared to prior state-of-the-art.
- Improved arrhythmia detection accuracy by **10.6%** on a 15-class classification task.
- Achieved **40% faster inference** and **35% lower GPU memory** usage than strong multi-task fine-tuning baselines.
- Outperformed individual foundation models in zero-shot and fine-tuned settings across all five tasks (regression: RR, age; binary: sex, potassium; multi-class: arrhythmia).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Separately modeling beat-level morphology and inter-beat rhythm via a dual-path MoE better captures ECG periodicity and phase-localized clinical features than a monolithic backbone.
- **Mechanism:** The input ECG is segmented into individual heartbeats using R-peak detection. Morphological experts (CNNs with varying kernel sizes) process fixed-length beat segments to capture intra-beat features like QRS complex or ST changes. Rhythm experts (dilated CNNs) process sequences of beats to capture inter-beat temporal patterns like RR interval variability. A task-conditioned gating network computes weights for these experts based on a concatenation of a global signal summary and a task embedding, dynamically routing information.
- **Core assumption:** ECG diagnostic information is fundamentally organized by the cardiac cycle, and pathological manifests can be distinct within a beat (morphology) versus across beats (rhythm). A single model path forces a trade-off or misses one type of pattern.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "dual-path Mixture-of-Experts to separately model beat-level morphology and rhythm"; [section 3, "Periodic Expert Network"]: Details segmentation, expert designs, and gating; [corpus]: Related works (e.g., "ECG-SL") note neglect of periodic attribute, supporting the problem.
- **Break condition:** Catastrophic R-peak detection failure on noisy/extreme arrhythmia data corrupts segmentation, causing both paths to process misaligned units and performance collapse.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Hierarchical fusion of heterogeneous multi-model features and period-aware features via task-conditioned gating and LoRA enables parameter-efficient, high-performance multi-task learning without catastrophic interference.
- **Mechanism:** Features from multiple pre-trained time-series foundation models and the periodic expert network are concatenated. A gating network produces scalar weights for each feature stream. These weighted features are summed. Task-specific prediction heads are built upon this fused representation. LoRA injects trainable low-rank matrices into frozen pre-trained backbones, allowing efficient adaptation without full fine-tuning.
- **Core assumption:** Different pre-trained models capture complementary ECG features (morphology, rhythm, long-term trends). Task-specific weighting is necessary because different clinical tasks rely on different feature subsets.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "combined with a hierarchical fusion network using LoRA for efficient inference."; [section 3.3, "Multi-Task Fusion"]: Describes gating weights and fusion; [section 4.2, RQ1 & Table 1]: Zero-shot/fine-tuned performance of individual baselines varies widely by task.
- **Break condition:** Poorly regularized gating or uninformative task embeddings cause degenerate weight distributions or instability, negating ensemble benefits and increasing interference.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The hybrid multi-head attention mechanism within the periodic expert network effectively integrates local, morphology-sensitive context with global, rhythm-sensitive context, producing clinically interpretable attention patterns.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of standard self-attention (global) or cross-attention (local window), a "hybrid" head allows each head to learn to attend either locally or globally, or a mixture, within the same attention matrix. Applied to the sequence of beat features, it models relationships between heartbeats.
- **Core assumption:** Clinical ECG interpretation requires both precise, localized analysis of signal segments and an understanding of overall rhythm pattern and consistency. A single, uniform attention pattern across all heads is suboptimal.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3 & Figure 3]: Attention heatmaps show hybrid pattern with both local blocks and broader contextual links, contrasted with ablations; text states it "effectively integrates local morphology and global rhythm context."
- **Break condition:** If all attention heads converge to similar patterns, the hybrid design provides no benefit over standard attention.

## Foundational Learning
- **ECG Periodicity & Segmentation:** The entire architecture depends on the quasi-periodic nature of ECGs and the heartbeat as the fundamental unit. Misunderstanding this makes the design seem arbitrary. *Quick check:* If you change the assumed cardiac cycle length in segmentation, what happens to the morphology expert's input shape and the rhythm expert's temporal resolution?
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Gating:** The core of the dual-path design is task-conditioned routing. The gate is a lightweight network producing a probability distribution over experts, not a hard selection. *Quick check:* What is the dimension of the gate's input ([ ¯X⊕et ]) and output? How does adding a task embedding (`et`) change gating behavior compared to a data-only gate?
- **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA):** The efficiency claim critically depends on freezing most parameters and only training low-rank updates. *Quick check:* If you set the LoRA rank `r` equal to the original weight matrix dimension, what does LoRA become? What is the trade-off as you decrease `r`?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input & Preprocessing -> Multi-Model Feature Extraction -> Periodic Expert Network (Dual-path: Morphology Path & Rhythm Path with Gating -> Hybrid Multi-Head Attention) -> Multi-Task Fusion (with Gating) -> Task Heads.
- **Critical path:** Raw ECG -> R-peak detection & beat segmentation -> Parallel processing by morphology and rhythm experts -> Expert fusion via gating -> Hybrid attention -> Periodic features -> Concatenation with multi-model features -> Task-conditioned fusion -> Task-specific head -> Prediction.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Expert Count vs. Compute:** More experts increase capacity and specialization potential but linearly increase gating compute and parameter count. The paper uses 5 total experts.
  - **LoRA Rank (`r`) vs. Adaptability:** Lower `r` means fewer trainable parameters and faster training/inference but restricts adaptation ability. The optimal `r` is a key hyperparameter.
  - **Task Embedding (`et`) Design:** Dimensionality and generation method (e.g., one-hot vs. learned) impact gating network size and risk of overfitting to task IDs.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Poor R-peak detection:** Leads to garbage segmentation → both expert paths fail → random predictions. Monitor segmentation quality.
  - **Degenerate gating weights:** If one expert consistently gets >95% weight, MoE collapses to single expert. Check mean/variance of gating weights per task.
  - **Catastrophic interference:** Performance on a previously trained task drops sharply when fine-tuning on a new task. Track per-task validation metrics continuously.
  - **Memory/throughput mismatch:** If GPU memory exceeds 8.2 GB or speed below 14.7 samples/sec, check LoRA application, expert implementation, batch size.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation - Dual-path vs. Single-path:** Replace dual-path MoE with a single, larger expert network (matched parameter count) processing full signal without segmentation. Expect significant drop in periodicity-sensitive tasks (RR, arrhythmia).
  2. **Ablation - Gating Mechanism:** Replace task-conditioned gating with static uniform weights. Expect performance drop, especially on tasks requiring different feature balances.
  3. **Efficiency Stress Test:** Systematically vary LoRA rank and number of experts; measure GPU memory and latency against strong multi-task baseline. Verify claimed 40% speedup and 35% memory reduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Why does potassium abnormality detection remain a challenge, and what architectural modifications could address this? The paper does not investigate root causes (e.g., feature extraction limits, class imbalance) nor propose targeted changes.
- **Open Question 2:** How can electrophysiological priors (e.g., cardiac cycle dynamics, polarity rules) be integrated to improve interpretability and accuracy? Current design is purely data-driven.
- **Open Question 3:** Does the dual-path MoE design separating beat morphology and rhythm provide measurable benefits over alternative fusion strategies? Ablation compares attention mechanisms, not fusion architectures, so the unique value of dual-path decomposition is not isolated.
- **Open Question 4:** How well does ECG-MoE generalize to external ECG datasets with different demographics, devices, and signal qualities? All evaluations use MIMIC-IV-ECG; no cross-dataset generalization tests are presented.

## Limitations
- Missing hyperparameter specifications (feature dimensions, expert layouts, gating MLP, LoRA rank/placement, loss weights) hinder exact reproduction.
- Data processing details vague: sampling rate, R-peak detector, missing label handling, split ratios; these heavily affect outcomes.
- Model relies on correct R-peak detection; errors on noisy/arrhythmic ECGs would mis-segment beats and cause performance collapse.
- Gating networks risk degeneracy without regularization, leading to ineffective MoE routing.
- Efficiency claims need rigorous verification under identical hardware and baseline implementations.
- Hybrid multi-head attention’s benefit is weakly substantiated; if all heads learn similar patterns, the mechanism adds no value.

## Confidence
- **Dual-path MoE separating beat morphology and rhythm:** Medium (architecture plausible but dependent on R-peak accuracy).
- **Hierarchical fusion with task-conditioned gates and LoRA:** Medium (design logical but gating/LoRA not thoroughly validated).
- **Hybrid multi-head attention:** Low (minimal evidence; no quantitative ablation confirms necessity).
- **Overall state-of-the-art performance and efficiency gains:** High (large reported improvements), but tempered by reproducibility uncertainties.
