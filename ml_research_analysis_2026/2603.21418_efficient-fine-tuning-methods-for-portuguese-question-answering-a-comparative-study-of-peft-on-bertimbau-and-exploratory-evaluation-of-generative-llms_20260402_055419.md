# Efficient Fine-Tuning Methods for Portuguese Question Answering: A Comparative Study of PEFT on BERTimbau and Exploratory Evaluation of Generative LLMs
## Quick Facts
- arXiv: 2603.21418
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21418
- Reference count: 7
- Primary result: LoRA on BERTimbau-Large achieves 95.8% of full fine-tuning F1 (81.32 vs 84.86) while reducing training time by 73.5%.

## Executive Summary
This study tackles the computational cost of adapting models for Brazilian Portuguese extractive question answering by systematically evaluating parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) and quantization on BERTimbau. The authors compare 40 configurations of LoRA, DoRA, QLoRA, and QDoRA across Base (110M) and Large (335M) models on the SQuAD-BR dataset. Key findings show that LoRA on BERTimbau-Large reaches 95.8% of full fine-tuning performance with 73.5% less training time, that a higher learning rate (2e-4) is critical for PEFT (improving F1 by up to +19.71 points), and that larger models are twice as resilient to 4-bit quantization. An exploratory evaluation of generative LLMs (Tucano, Sabiá) reveals that while LoRA fine-tuning yields competitive F1 scores, these models require substantially more GPU memory and training time, underscoring the efficiency of encoder-based architectures for this task.

## Method Summary
The research conducts a systematic comparison of four PEFT methods—LoRA, DoRA, QLoRA, and QDoRA—on two variants of BERTimbau (Base and Large) using the SQuAD-BR extractive QA dataset. Each configuration is evaluated with two learning rates (2e-4 and 4.25e-5) and for 2 or 3 epochs. Key hyperparameters include LoRA rank r=16, scaling alpha=32, dropout 0.1, and targeted attention layers (query, key, value, output projections). Quantized setups employ 4-bit NF4 with double quantization and bfloat16 compute dtype. A full fine-tuning baseline serves as reference. Additionally, generative models (Tucano, Sabiá) are fine-tuned via LoRA in a text-to-text format and post-processed to extract answer spans. Performance is measured by F1-score and Exact Match, with secondary metrics of training time and peak GPU memory.

## Key Results
- LoRA on BERTimbau-Large yields 95.8% of full FT performance (F1 81.32 vs 84.86) with 73.5% less training time.
- Learning rate 2e-4 is critical for PEFT, improving F1 by up to +19.71 points over standard 4.25e-5.
- 4-bit quantization on Large loses only 4.83 F1 points vs 9.56 on Base, demonstrating greater quantization resilience in larger models.
- Exploratory: generative LLMs (Sabiá) achieve similar F1 to BERTimbau-Base (78.10 vs 78.01) but require 4.2× more GPU memory and 3× more training time.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning approximates full fine-tuning
**Claim:** LoRA can achieve near-identical performance to full fine-tuning for extractive QA on Brazilian Portuguese while drastically reducing computational cost.
**Mechanism:** LoRA freezes the pre-trained model weights \(W_0\) and injects trainable low-rank matrices \(A\) and \(B\) (with rank \(r \ll \min(d,k)\)) into the attention layers. The update \(\Delta W = \frac{\alpha}{r} BA\) approximates the full fine-tuning update with only 0.1-1% trainable parameters. This works because pre-trained models have low intrinsic dimensionality; effective adaptation lies in a small subspace of the full parameter space.
**Core assumption:** The intrinsic dimensionality of the adaptation space for the SQuAD-BR task is sufficiently low that a rank-\(r\) decomposition (here \(r=16\)) captures the necessary task-specific adjustments without drift from the pre-trained manifold.
**Evidence:** [abstract] LoRA achieves 95.8% of baseline performance on BERTimbau-Large while reducing training time by 73.5% (F1=81.32 vs 84.86). [section 5.1] with lr=2e-4, LoRA and DoRA stand out as best-performing (F1=78.01), closely approaching full FT. [section 5.2] QLoRA on Base gains +19.71 F1 points when LR increased.
**Break condition:** If the task requires divergent representations outside the low-rank subspace or chosen rank too small, performance degrades.

### Mechanism 2: Larger models' resilience to quantization
**Claim:** Larger pre-trained models (BERTimbau-Large, 335M) exhibit greater resilience to aggressive 4-bit quantization than smaller ones (Base, 110M).
**Mechanism:** Quantization to 4-bit NF4 introduces noise into weight representations. Larger models have greater parametric redundancy; the information lost by quantization can be compensated by remaining higher-precision parameters and the adaptable PEFT modules (LoRA matrices in bfloat16).
**Core assumption:** The parametric redundancy in larger encoder models scales such that relative information loss from uniform 4-bit quantization is smaller than in smaller models.
**Evidence:** [abstract] Larger models lose only 4.83 F1 points vs 9.56 for Base under 4-bit quantization. [section 5.2] QLoRA on Large shows degradation of −4.83 F1 points (vs. −9.56 on Base), an approximate 2× difference.
**Break condition:** If quantization noise exceeds the redundancy buffer (e.g., lower-bit quantization or highly sensitive layers), degradation could become severe. Also may not hold for decoder-only architectures.

### Mechanism 3: Higher learning rates for PEFT
**Claim:** PEFT methods require significantly higher learning rates (e.g., 2e-4) than standard full fine-tuning rates (e.g., 4.25e-5) to achieve optimal performance.
**Mechanism:** The low-rank adapters constrain the parameter update to a small subspace. A higher learning rate is needed to generate updates of sufficient magnitude within this constrained space to effectively adapt the model. With a standard lower rate, the updates may be too small to escape poor local minima or capture task-specific features.
**Core assumption:** The optimization landscape for PEFT is such that the effective step size within the low-rank subspace must be comparable to that of full fine-tuning; due to lower dimensionality, a higher base learning rate is necessary.
**Evidence:** [abstract] Higher learning rate (2e-4) is critical, improving PEFT F1 scores by up to +19.71 points over standard rates. [section 5.4] The learning rate emerges as the most decisive factor for PEFT success. [section 5.2] QLoRA improves by +19.71 F1 points in Base when LR increased.
**Break condition:** If LR set too high even for PEFT (beyond 2e-4), instability or divergence may occur; optimal rate is task- and model-size-dependent.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Intrinsic Dimensionality & Low-Rank Adaptation**  
  *Why needed here:* Understanding why updating a tiny fraction of parameters (via LoRA) can match full fine-tuning requires grasping that model adaptation often occurs in a low-dimensional manifold within the full parameter space. This justifies the core mechanism of PEFT.  
  *Quick check question:* If a model has 100M parameters but an intrinsic dimensionality of 10K for a specific task, what percentage of parameters need updating at minimum to capture the adaptation?
- **Concept: Quantization (4-bit NF4) and its Trade-offs**  
  *Why needed here:* QLoRA's memory savings come from 4-bit quantization of base weights. Knowing how quantization works (scale/zero-point) and its impact on numerical precision is key to understanding the memory-performance trade-offs reported.  
  *Quick check question:* In uniform 4-bit quantization, how many distinct values can a weight take, and what is the role of the "scale factor"?
- **Concept: Extractive QA Evaluation Metrics (F1, EM)**  
  *Why needed here:* The paper's outcomes are reported in F1 and Exact Match. These metrics measure token-level span overlap and exact string match, respectively. Misunderstanding them could lead to misinterpreting the claimed performance gaps.  
  *Quick check question:* If a predicted span "New York" matches ground truth "new york" after normalization, does it count as an Exact Match? What about a span "in New York" when ground truth is "New York"?
- **Concept: Encoder vs. Decoder Architectures for QA**  
  *Why needed here:* The paper contrasts BERTimbau (encoder-only) with generative LLMs (decoder-only). The architectural mismatch (span extraction vs. free-text generation) explains why generative models underperform or are less efficient for this specific task.  
  *Quick check question:* Why is an encoder-only model like BERT naturally suited for extractive QA compared to a decoder-only model like GPT?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** BERTimbau encoder -> PEFT adapters (LoRA/DoRA/QLoRA matrices injected into attention layers) -> Span prediction head (linear layer on [CLS] token). For quantized configurations, base model weights are stored in 4-bit NF4 format.

**Critical path:**  
1. Load BERTimbau model (full-precision or 4-bit quantized).  
2. Inject PEFT modules (LoRA/DoRA) into specified attention projections (query, key, value, output).  
3. Freeze base model weights; only adapter parameters and the QA head are trainable.  
4. Forward pass: base model processes input; adapters modify attention outputs.  
5. QA head computes start and end logits for answer span extraction.  
6. Compute loss (cross-entropy for start/end positions); gradients flow only to adapters and head.  
7. Perform AdamW optimizer step with the specified learning rate (typically 2e-4), updating adapter (and head) weights.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Memory vs. Performance:* QLoRA (4-bit) drastically reduces memory (e.g., 81.9% for Large) but incurs an F1 penalty of ~4-10 points. LoRA (bfloat16) retains more performance at higher memory cost.  
- *Rank (r) vs. Efficiency:* Lower rank (r=4) saves memory but may underfit; higher rank (r=64) improves performance but approaches full fine-tuning costs. This study uses r=16.  
- *Learning Rate Sensitivity:* PEFT demands a higher LR (2e-4) than standard BERT fine-tuning (4.25e-5). Using the standard LR causes severe degradation; using high LR on full fine-tuning causes collapse.  
- *Model Size:* BERTimbau-Large offers higher absolute performance and better quantization resilience but increases training time. BERTimbau-Base is faster but more fragile to quantization.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Training collapse:* Full fine-tuning of BERTimbau-Large at lr=2e-4 yields F1 ~3 (complete failure), while PEFT remains stable. Indicates divergence in full parameter space.  
- *Quantization degradation:* QLoRA on Base loses ~9.5 F1 points; if loss exceeds ~10 points, quantization may be too aggressive for that model size.  
- *Memory overflow:* Exceeding GPU VRAM (e.g., full FT on Large uses ~18GB) causes OOM. QLoRA on Large fits in ~3.3GB.  
- *Learning rate mismatch:* Using lr=4.25e-5 with QLoRA leads to severely degraded F1 (e.g., 53.52 on Base) because adapter updates are too small.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Replicate LoRA on BERTimbau-Base:** Use LoRA with r=16, lr=2e-4, 2 epochs. Expect F1 ~78.0, memory ~3.7GB, training time ~0.5h. Validates core PEFT setup.  
2. **Test quantization resilience on Large:** Compare QLoRA (4-bit) vs. LoRA (bfloat16) on BERTimbau-Large at lr=2e-4. Expect QLoRA F1 ~80.0 (loss ~4.8) with memory ~3.3GB vs. LoRA F1 81.3 with memory ~9.0GB. Confirms larger model quantization resilience.  
3. **Learning rate sweep:** Train LoRA on BERTimbau-Base with learning rates {1e-4, 2e-4, 4.25e-5}. Expect peak at 2e-4; 4.25e-5 should show major drop (~6-8 F1 points). Validates higher LR necessity.

## Limitations
- Single dataset (SQuAD-BR) limits generalizability.
- Generative model evaluation lacks prompt/decoding details, hindering reproducibility.
- No statistical tests or confidence intervals; observed differences may be due to chance.

## Confidence
- **PEFT near full FT (95.8% of baseline):** High
- **Larger models more quantization-resilient:** Medium
- **Higher LR needed for PEFT:** Medium
- **Encoder efficiency vs. generative LLMs:** High

## Next Checks
1. Seed variance: Train LoRA/QLoRA on SQuAD-BR with ≥5 seeds, report mean F1±std to assess reliability.
2. Cross-dataset quantization: Apply 4-bit QLoRA to another Portuguese QA dataset (e.g., medical QA) to test if Large model resilience (~4.8 F1 loss) holds.
3. LR ablation: Sweep LR (1e-5–5e-4) for PEFT and full FT across ranks {4,16,64} to map optimal regions and confirm 2e-4 necessity.
