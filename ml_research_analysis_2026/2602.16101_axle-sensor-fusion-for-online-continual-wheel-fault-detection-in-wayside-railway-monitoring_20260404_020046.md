# Axle Sensor Fusion for Online Continual Wheel Fault Detection in Wayside Railway Monitoring

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.16101
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16101
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A semantic-aware continual learning framework fusing VAE embeddings and FBG-derived metadata achieves up to ~94% anomaly detection accuracy and mitigates catastrophic forgetting via loss-based replay.

## Executive Summary
Railway wheel fault detection in wayside monitoring suffers from evolving operational conditions (train type, speed, load, track) and electromagnetic interference. This paper proposes a framework that enhances an accelerometer‑based anomaly detector with semantic metadata extracted from fiber Bragg grating (FBG) sensors. Unsupervised variational autoencoder (VAE) embeddings capture normal wheel‑rail dynamics, while FBG peak detection provides axle counts, wheel indexes, and strain deformations. A lightweight XGBoost classifier fuses these streams, and replay‑based continual learning enables adaptation to new domains without catastrophic forgetting. Simulated experiments show VAE embeddings achieve 95% F1 (vs 75% for manual features), semantic enrichment boosts accuracy by 5–12 points (up to ~94%), and loss‑based replay with buffer size 800 reduces forgetting and yields forward transfer of 0.31.

## Method Summary
The pipeline starts with raw accelerometer and FBG strain signals. The accelerometer is segmented into windows and passed through a pretrained VAE encoder to obtain a 20‑dimensional embedding that encodes normal operational structure. The FBG signal is processed with peak detection (e.g., SciPy’s `find_peaks`) to extract semantic features: number of peaks (axle count), peak indices (wheel indexes), and peak amplitudes (deformations). These semantics are concatenated with the VAE embedding and fed to an XGBoost classifier trained on minimal fault labels. For continual learning, a memory buffer stores past embeddings and soft labels. When a new operational domain arrives, the classifier is updated on its data while replaying buffer samples (e.g., selected by loss‑based prioritization) to retain prior knowledge and transfer to the new domain.

## Key Results
- VAE latent embeddings achieve 95% F1-score, substantially outperforming manual feature engineering (75% F1).
- Semantic enrichment (including train load/speed) improves anomaly detection accuracy by 5–12 percentage points, reaching up to ~94%.
- Loss‑based experience replay with a buffer of 800 samples reduces catastrophic forgetting and yields forward transfer of 0.31; axle counting accuracy via peak detection reaches 93%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Semantic Fusion for Operational Variability**  
FBG‑derived semantics (axle counts, wheel indexes, strain) act as domain anchors, conditioning the classifier on explicit operational context and mitigating performance degradation under speed, load, or train‑type shifts. Evidence: Ablation studies (Table 4) show 5–12% accuracy gains from semantics; FBG’s EMI resistance ensures stable signals.

**Mechanism 2: Unsupervised VAE Embeddings as Robust Representations**  
The VAE learns a smooth latent manifold of normal operational patterns; anomalies deviate as reconstruction errors or low‑density regions. KL divergence regularizes the space, preventing overfitting to specific conditions. Evidence: VAE embeddings yield 95% F1 vs 75% for manual features (Table 3).

**Mechanism 3: Loss-Based Replay for Continual Adaptation**  
Storing high‑loss samples from past domains in a memory buffer and replaying them during new‑domain training preserves decision boundaries and enables forward transfer. Evidence: Buffer size 800 with loss‑based sampling achieves forward transfer 0.31 and lower forgetting than naive replay or reservoir sampling (Table 7).

## Foundational Learning
- **Variational Autoencoder (VAE)**  
  *Why needed*: Generates compact, noise‑robust accelerometer embeddings without fault labels, capturing the manifold of normal wheel‑rail dynamics.  
  *Quick check*: How does the KL divergence term in the VAE loss prevent the latent space from merely memorizing training sequences?

- **Experience Replay (Continual Learning)**  
  *Why needed*: Mitigates catastrophic forgetting when sequentially training on new operational domains without access to all historical data.  
  *Quick check*: Why might loss‑based sampling (prioritizing high‑loss samples) be more effective than random reservoir sampling for a small memory buffer in this fault detection task?

- **Fiber Bragg Grating (FBG) Peak Detection**  
  *Why needed*: Provides EMI‑immune, high‑fidelity semantic features (axle count, timing, strain) that are stable across conditions and inform the classifier about the train’s physical configuration.  
  *Quick check*: What is the core signal processing principle behind detecting wheel axles from FBG strain signals, and why is prominence a better threshold than simple amplitude?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Accelerometer -> VAE encoder -> embedding S; FBG strain -> peak detection -> semantics (axle count, wheel indexes, deformations); embed S + semantics -> XGBoost classifier -> anomaly score; memory buffer (stores past S+soft label) -> loss‑based sampler -> replay batches interleaved with new‑domain data -> XGBoost update.

- **Critical path**: Raw accelerometer and FBG signals are processed in parallel: accelerometer is converted to a VAE embedding; FBG is peak‑detected to extract semantics. The embedding and semantics are concatenated and classified by XGBoost. After each new domain, the replay module updates the classifier using stored samples.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Memory vs Performance*: Larger replay buffer (800 vs 200) reduces forgetting but increases storage/compute.
  - *Embedding Dimensionality*: Latent dim 20 was optimal; higher dim risks overfitting to noise.
  - *Peak Detection Sensitivity*: Threshold trade‑off: lower thresholds risk false axles; higher thresholds miss peaks; balance via anomaly‑detection‑weighted tuning.
  - *Semantic Granularity*: More detailed semantics (raw indexes/deformations) improve accuracy but require reliable peak detection.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - VAE reconstruction loss spikes on new‑domain data → latent manifold mismatch.
  - Axle count accuracy <80% → peak detection parameters need retuning or sensor fault.
  - High negative backward transfer → forgetting severe; increase buffer or use loss‑based sampling.
  - Near‑zero/negative forward transfer → model not leveraging past; check replay diversity.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Validate VAE embedding quality: train on diverse normal passages; visualize t‑SNE colored by known operational variables (speed, load); confirm clusters correspond to domains and anomalies are outliers.
  2. Benchmark peak detection algorithms: run all four (TB, PD, DP, SD) on a labeled FBG dataset; measure axle count accuracy and detection F1; use Friedman/Shaffer tests to confirm SD superiority; tune prominence threshold for specific rail/sensor setup.
  3. Test replay strategy with simulated domain shifts: split dataset into sequential EOVs (by speed or train type); compare naive retraining, reservoir sampling, loss‑based sampling with buffer 200/800; track per‑domain accuracy; compute BWT, FWT; expect loss‑based 800 to minimize forgetting and show positive FWT.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. *Peak detection robustness*: How can peak detection for FBG signals be improved to achieve >99% axle counting reliability under noisy, variable conditions? Current threshold‑based methods degrade with signal complexity; a deep learning pattern matcher is needed.
2. *Thermal drift compensation*: How do temperature variations affect FBG measurements, and what practical compensation (e.g., dual‑wavelength reference) can keep axle‑counting error below 1% across –20°C to +50°C?
3. *Simulation‑to‑reality gap*: Does the framework maintain performance on real‑world railway data, or do unmodeled factors (track defects, extreme weather, sensor failures) introduce unforeseen failure modes? A live trial over 12+ months with ground‑truth inspections is required.
4. *Redundant FBG configuration*: What redundant sensor setup (placement, voting) maximizes availability while meeting railway safety integrity (e.g., SIL 2/3) for continual fault detection? Reliability analysis and hardware‑in‑the‑loop testing needed.

## Limitations
- The original VSI simulation details are proprietary; synthetic signals may differ from real wheel‑rail dynamics, hindering exact replication.
- Feature engineering for variable‑length peak sequences (wheel indexes, deformations) is unspecified, leaving classifier input format ambiguous.
- Experiments are simulation‑only; real‑world EMI, environmental noise, and sensor robustness remain unvalidated.

## Confidence
- **Semantic fusion improves accuracy**: Medium (internal ablation shows 5–12% gains but external validity on real FBG uncertain)
- **VAE embeddings outperform manual features**: High (consistent 95% vs 75% F1 across tests)
- **Loss‑based replay reduces forgetting**: Medium (demonstrated on synthetic domain shifts; long‑term evolving conditions untested)
