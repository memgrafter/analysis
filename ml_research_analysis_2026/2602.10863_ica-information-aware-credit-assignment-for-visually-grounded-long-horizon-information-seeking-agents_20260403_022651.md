# ICA: Information-Aware Credit Assignment for Visually Grounded Long-Horizon Information-Seeking Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10863  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10863  
- **Reference count:** 38  
- **Primary result:** Open-source visual agent with ICA achieves SOTA on multiple long-horizon information-seeking benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Information-seeking agents on the open web suffer from low signal-to-noise feedback: traditional text parsers discard layout semantics and produce noisy observations, while sparse terminal rewards obscure which retrieval actions contributed to success. This paper introduces a visual-native framework that represents webpages as rendered snapshots, preserving layout and non-textual cues. On top of this, they propose Information-Aware Credit Assignment (ICA), a post-hoc method that quantifies each retrieved atomic evidence’s marginal contribution to task success via posterior success association and propagates dense turn-level rewards to the responsible search turns. Combined with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), the approach consistently outperforms text-based baselines on long-horizon benchmarks, setting new state-of-the-art among open-source agents.

## Method Summary
The framework replaces HTML parsing with visual snapshots from the fetch tool, allowing the multimodal agent to consume webpage layouts directly. To address sparse rewards, ICA processes a batch of trajectories after rollout: it identifies atomic evidence (search snippets or fetched snapshots), computes the difference in conditional success probabilities (`Δe = bP(R=1|Ie=1) - bP(R=1|Ie=0)`) across trajectories, aggregates per-turn credits, applies temporal decay to fetch turns, and normalizes. Training uses GRPO with a mixed advantage: `˜A_t = A_n + λ ˆA_t`, where `A_n` is the normalized task-level relative advantage and `ˆA_t` is the normalized turn credit. This decouples global success from local reasoning quality, yielding stable updates in long-horizon settings.

## Key Results
- With Qwen3‑VL‑30B‑A3B, achieves **17.1%** on BrowseComp, **65.0%** on GAIA, **75.0%** on Xbench‑DS, and **27.0%** on Seal‑0.  
- Sets new state-of-the-art among open-source agents on these benchmarks.  
- Outperforms text‑based baselines, demonstrating the benefit of visual snapshots and ICA.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Visual snapshot representation preserves layout semantics and non-textual cues, reducing observation noise and enabling stable evidence identification across trajectories.  
- **Mechanism:** Instead of parsing HTML into linear text, the fetch tool renders the full webpage as a screenshot (optionally sliced). The resulting snapshot retains spatial arrangement, tables, images, and formatting. The agent consumes these visual observations directly, allowing it to localize salient evidence using layout cues and avoid parser artifacts that plague text extraction.  
- **Core assumption:** Visual structure encodes information critical for retrieval tasks (e.g., locating data in tables, distinguishing headings) that is lost or corrupted in text‑only extraction pipelines.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]: “preserves layout and non‑textual cues”  
  - [section 4.2]: “snapshot preserves the full visible content of a webpage, including textual elements, layout structure, tables, and images”  
  - [corpus]: Neighbor papers (e.g., WebExplorer, Hindsight Credit Assignment) focus on other aspects; direct evidence for snapshots is not present in the top related corpus.  
- **Break condition:** If the task relies purely on plain text and layout cues are irrelevant, snapshot overhead (rendering, tokens) may not justify benefits; rendering failures could also corrupt observations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** ICA computes counterfactual contributions of atomic evidence units to produce dense turn-level rewards that overcome sparse terminal supervision.  
- **Mechanism:** For each atomic evidence `e` (a search result snippet or fetched snapshot), estimate its marginal utility via posterior analysis: compute success probability conditioned on acquiring `e` (`bP(R=1|Ie=1)`) and on not acquiring it (`bP(R=1|Ie=0)`) using batch-level statistics. The difference `Δe = bP(R=1|Ie=1) - bP(R=1|Ie=0)` quantifies evidence utility. Aggregate `Δe` over all evidence introduced at a turn to obtain raw turn credit `r_t`. For fetch actions, apply temporal decay `Ω^(T-t-1)` to reflect delayed usage.  
- **Core assumption:** The marginal effect of evidence on success can be approximated from its correlation with outcomes across trajectories; evidence units are sufficiently independent for reliable estimation.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 4.1.2]: Equations 8–11 define conditional success rates, atomic evidence indicator, and per‑turn credit.  
  - [section 4.1.2]: “ICA aggregates atomic‑evidence counterfactual contributions associated with the same turn.”  
  - [abstract]: “quantifies each retrieved atomic evidence’s marginal contribution to task success via posterior success association”  
- **Break condition:** Requires sufficient trajectory diversity; if evidence units are highly correlated (e.g., always fetched together), counterfactual estimates become biased. Also, very fine‑grained evidence may yield noisy `Δe` estimates due to limited samples per unit.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** ICA‑GRPO integrates task‑level and information‑level advantages to decouple global success from local reasoning quality, stabilizing policy updates in long‑horizon settings.  
- **Mechanism:** After collecting a group of trajectories for a query, compute task‑level relative advantage `A_n` from the normalized terminal outcome. Compute local information advantage `ˆA_t` from the normalized turn credit (tool‑wise stats for intermediate turns, batch‑wise for terminal). Mix them: `˜A_t = A_n + λ ˆA_t`. Then optimize the policy with a GRPO‑style objective using decoupled clipping (DAPO‑inspired) and token‑normalization.  
- **Core assumption:** Turn‑level credits from ICA provide a meaningful gradient that correlates with good evidence acquisition; mixing with task advantage yields a balanced learning signal.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [section 4.1.2]: “ICA‑GRPO for Information‑Seeking… decouple global task success from local reasoning quality”  
  - [section 4.1.2]: Equations 15–17 and the hybrid objective.  
  - [corpus]: Neighbor “Hindsight Credit Assignment for Long‑Horizon LLM Agents” identifies similar bottlenecks, independent motivation.  
- **Break condition:** If `λ` is too high, local noise may dominate; too low and ICA’s signal is ignored. Noisy `Δe` estimates or poor normalization can destabilize updates.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map (plain text):**  
  Agent (multimodal LLM) -> Tools (web_search, fetch_url) -> Observations (search snippets, snapshots) -> Trajectory collection -> ICA module (per‑turn credit computation) -> Mixed advantage (˜A_t) -> GRPO optimizer -> Policy update.

- **Critical path (training):** For a query `q`, sample `G` trajectories from current policy `π_θ`. During rollout, the agent interleaves reasoning, tool calls (`web_search`, `fetch_url`), and observation accumulation (snippets/snapshots). After termination, a judge evaluates answer correctness (`R`). ICA processes all trajectories: builds evidence set `E`, computes indicator `I_e` and marginal `Δe` from batch stats, aggregates raw turn credit `r_t`, applies decay `Ω^(T-t-1)` to fetch turns, normalizes advantages (tool‑wise `µ_k,σ_k`; batch‑wise `µ_R,σ_R`), and mixes `˜A_t = A_n + λ ˆA_t`. Finally, perform one gradient step on the policy using a DAPO‑style clipped PPO loss.  
  **Inference:** Deploy trained policy; it follows the same tool‑use loop until `ANSWER` without GRPO overhead.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Snapshot fidelity vs. token budget:* High‑resolution screenshots preserve layout but increase input tokens; pipeline uses slicing (4,480 px slices with overlap) and 0.7× downsampling to manage cost.  
  - *Evidence granularity:* Atomic evidence defined as search snippet or fetched snapshot. Coarser (whole page) may conflate signal; finer (paragraphs) increases noise and correlation; authors chose snapshot level for fetch, snippet for search.  
  - *Decay factor `Ω`:* Balances long‑term influence of fetched content; lower `Ω` credits early fetches less, may under‑reward foundational evidence; set to 0.95 empirically.  
  - *Mixing weight `λ`:* Controls contribution of local ICA signal vs. global task advantage; set to 1 (equal weight), but could be tuned per dataset.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *Tool‑level:* Missing or corrupted snapshots (e.g., rendering timeouts, truncated content) lead to empty evidence units and `|E_t| = 0`; these turns receive no ICA credit.  
  - *Credit estimation:* Near‑zero variance in raw `r_t` across turns suggests ICA fails to differentiate evidence utility, likely due to insufficient trajectory diversity or overly coarse evidence units.  
  - *Optimization:* High gradient variance or sudden policy collapse often indicates mis‑specified `λ` or `Ω`, or noisy `Δe` due to correlated evidence.  
  - *Task mismatch:* Performance drop on short‑horizon benchmarks (e.g., Bamboogle) relative to text baselines may indicate visual overhead or ICA complexity is unnecessary for simpler queries.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Ablation – Observation modality:** Train SFT models with RAG fetch vs. snapshot fetch; snapshot shows consistent gains on layout‑sensitive benchmarks (GAIA, Xbench‑DS).  
  2. **Ablation – Credit assignment:** With snapshot fetch fixed, apply GRPO vs. ICA; ICA outperforms GRPO, demonstrating the value of information‑aware turn rewards.  
  3. **Scaling:** Apply ICA to two model sizes (Qwen3‑VL‑8B and Qwen3‑VL‑30B‑A3B); improvements scale, confirming robustness across capacities.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does ICA's marginal contribution estimate behave when atomic evidence units are correlated?**  
   - *Basis in paper:* ICA’s post‑hoc estimation (Eq. 8–10) implicitly assumes independence among evidence units; the paper does not address potential correlations.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Correlated evidence may cause over‑ or under‑attribution of credit, leading to biased learning signals and suboptimal policy updates.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled experiments with synthetic trajectories where evidence co‑occurrence is manipulated, measuring attribution accuracy and final task performance.

2. **What is the optimal granularity for atomic evidence inside fetched webpage snapshots?**  
   - *Basis in paper:* Atomic evidence is defined as the smallest identifiable unit; for fetch it is the entire snapshot, with no exploration of finer segmentation.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Granularity directly impacts the signal‑to‑noise ratio in credit assignment; too coarse mixes distractors, too fine increases sparsity.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic ablations with different segmentation strategies (e.g., visual blocks, DOM elements, or objects) and evaluating agent success on benchmarks.

3. **How sensitive is ICA performance to the temporal decay factor Ω, and can it be learned instead of being fixed?**  
   - *Basis in paper:* ICA introduces Ω ∈ (0,1] and sets it to 0.95 without justification or analysis of its effect.  
   - *Why unresolved:* The decay schedule may be task‑ and horizon‑dependent; a fixed value might not be optimal and could hinder credit propagation.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* Hyperparameter sensitivity studies across a range of Ω values; extension to a learned, state‑dependent decay function optimized via validation performance.

4. **Are static webpage snapshots sufficient for reliable evidence grounding on dynamic or interactive web content?**  
   - *Basis in paper:* The snapshot representation assumes all relevant information is present upon page load; dynamic content loaded via scripts or user interaction is not discussed.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Modern websites often require scrolling, clicks, or form inputs to reveal critical data; snapshots may miss such evidence, limiting real‑world applicability.  
   - *What evidence would resolve it:* Constructing a benchmark with web tasks that require interaction to access key information and comparing snapshot‑based agents against agents capable of triggering interactions.

## Limitations
- Visual overhead and rendering failures add new failure modes; benefits likely diminish on layout‑irrelevant tasks or very simple queries.  
- ICA’s counterfactual reliability depends on evidence independence; highly correlated evidence (e.g., always fetching the same site) biases `Δe` estimates and degrades the credit signal.  
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (`λ`, `Ω`) may require per‑benchmark tuning; mis‑specification risks destabilizing training or ignoring ICA’s signal.
