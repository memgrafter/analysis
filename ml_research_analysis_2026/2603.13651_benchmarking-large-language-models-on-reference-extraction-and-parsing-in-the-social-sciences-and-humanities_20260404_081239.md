# Benchmarking Large Language Models on Reference Extraction and Parsing in the Social Sciences and Humanities
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.13651
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13651
*   **Reference Count:** 27
*   **Primary Result:** Lightweight LoRA-adapted LLMs demonstrate greater robustness than GROBID on multilingual, footnote-heavy Social Sciences and Humanities (SSH) documents; a hybrid routing system with adaptive segmentation is advocated for end-to-end reliability.

## Executive Summary
This paper confronts a critical gap: existing benchmarks for bibliographic reference processing are ill-suited for the Social Sciences and Humanities (SSH), which feature multilingual footnotes, historical stylistic variation, and complex layouts. The authors construct a unified benchmark from three SSH-focused datasets (CEX, EXCITE, LinkedBooks) to evaluate the classic tool GROBID against several large language models (LLMs). While raw reference *extraction* is largely solved by capable models, the subsequent *parsing* (structured field extraction) and full end-to-end pipelines remain brittle. The key finding is that LLMs, particularly after efficient LoRA fine-tuning, handle linguistic and layout complexity far better than GROBID. Consequently, the paper proposes a pragmatic hybrid deployment strategy: route well-formed English documents to the fast, established GROBID, while directing complex, multilingual, or footnote-dense SSH cases to a task-adapted LLM, supported by smarter segmentation to improve reliability.

## Method Summary
The study synthesizes a benchmark from three existing SSH datasets (CEX, EXCITE, LinkedBooks) to capture domain-specific challenges like multilingualism and footnotes. It evaluates GROBID (a specialized CRF-based tool) against several off-the-shelf and lightly fine-tuned (via LoRA) LLMs on three tasks: extraction (finding reference strings), parsing (extracting structured fields from a reference), and end-to-end (both steps). The core methodological innovation is the analysis of segmentation strategies—adaptively breaking a document's reference section into cleaner sub-units before processing—and the proposal of a hybrid routing logic that assigns documents to either GROBID or an LLM based on predicted complexity.

## Key Results
*   Reference **extraction** performance saturates for capable models (LLMs and GROBID), indicating this sub-problem is largely solved.
*   **Parsing and end-to-end** accuracy remain low overall due to "brittleness" in generating structured outputs, but LoRA-adapted LLMs show a clear robustness advantage on multilingual and footnote-heavy SSH data compared to GROBID.
*   Implementing **adaptive segmentation** (e.g., extract→parse pipelines) substantially improves end-to-end reliability, mitigating some of the structured-output brittleness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed hybrid system works by **decoupling the problem into two paths with complementary strengths**. GROBID provides high-speed, high-accuracy processing for the common case of clean, English-language references where its specialized, deterministic model excels. For the long-tail of complex SSH documents—characterized by non-standard layouts, multiple languages within a footnote, or archaic typography—the system escalates to a LoRA-adapted LLM. The LLM's inherent flexibility and multilingual proficiency, honed on a few hundred examples, allows it to navigate irregularities that break GROBID's feature-based approach. Crucially, a lightweight **routing classifier** and **adaptive segmentation** act as force multipliers: the classifier directs documents to the appropriate path, while segmentation pre-processes messy reference sections into more atomic, parsable units, reducing the burden on any single model's context window and structural understanding.

## Foundational Learning
*   **SSH Bibliographic Conventions:** Understanding that SSH references often include multilingual footnotes, historical punctuation/spelling, and varied journal formats is why a standard English-focused benchmark fails.
    *   *Quick Check:* Review a sample of references from a 19th-century history journal and a modern French sociology article; note non-standard elements.
*   **Structured Output Brittleness:** The challenge of getting LLMs to produce perfectly formatted JSON/XML for parsing, which can fail due to schema complexity or prompt sensitivity.
    *   *Quick Check:* Prompt an LLM to output a fixed JSON schema for a complex reference; repeat and check for consistency.
*   **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA):** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) allows adapting large LLMs for a specific task (SSH parsing) with minimal new parameters, preserving general knowledge while improving domain performance.
    *   *Quick Check:* Compare LoRA vs. full fine-tuning for a small dataset; assess accuracy and training resource differences.
*   **Segmentation as a Pre-processing Strategy:** Breaking a long, noisy reference section into smaller, cleaner chunks (by heuristics or a simple model) before extraction/parsing.
    *   *Quick Check:* Apply a simple rule-based splitter (e.g., on numbers or line breaks) to a messy reference list; measure if individual reference quality improves.
*   **Hybrid System Routing:** The concept of using a lightweight classifier to direct inputs to specialized sub-systems ( GROBID vs. LLM) based on predicted difficulty or features.
    *   *Quick Check:* Train a logistic regression on document features (language ID, footnote density) to predict if a reference list is "complex"; test on held-out data.
*   **CEX/EXCITE/LinkedBooks Datasets:** These provide the grounding SSH data with specific characteristics (multilinguality, historical prints, linked editions) for training and evaluation.
    *   *Quick Check:* Examine the data sheets or sample records from each dataset to understand their unique biases and coverage.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** Raw Document -> Feature Extractor (language, layout metrics) -> Routing Classifier -> Path A: GROBID (Fast, English-Standard) OR Path B: LoRA-LLM (Robust, Complex) -> Aggregator -> Structured Bibliography. Segmentation can occur before Path A/B or within Path B.
*   **Critical Path:** The routing decision and segmentation quality are the critical path. A misrouted complex document to GROBID leads to failure; poor segmentation creates noisy inputs for both paths.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:** **Speed vs. Accuracy:** GROBID path is orders of magnitude faster; LLM path is slower but more accurate on edge cases. **Cost vs. Coverage:** LLM inference has higher monetary cost per page. **Simplicity vs. Flexibility:** GROBID's deterministic rules are simple but brittle; LLMs are flexible but require careful prompt/schema design.
*   **Failure Signatures:** 1) **Routing Failure:** Complex SSH document sent to GROBID yields many parsing errors. 2) **Segmentation Failure:** A single reference is split incorrectly, creating two incomplete fragments. 3) **LLM Output Failure:** The LoRA-LLM generates malformed JSON or hallucinates fields despite correct routing.
*   **First Three Experiments:**
    1.  **Routing Prototype:** Implement a simple logistic regression/router using document-level features (e.g., % non-ASCII characters, average footnote length, layout entropy). Measure its accuracy in distinguishing documents where GROBID fails vs. succeeds on a validation set.
    2.  **Segmentation Efficacy Test:** For a subset of "complex" documents, compare end-to-end parsing accuracy with and without an adaptive segmentation step (e.g., using layout clues to isolate footnotes). Quantify the reduction in LLM parsing errors.
    3.  **Cost-Latency Baseline:** Process a fixed sample (e.g., 1000 reference sections) through GROBID and the LoRA-LLM (with/without segmentation) on the same hardware. Record per-document latency and estimated monetary cost (cloud API or GPU hours) to establish the operational tradeoff.

## Limitations
*   **Dataset Scope:** The unified benchmark relies on only three datasets, which may not capture the full diversity of global SSH publishing traditions, languages, and historical periods.
*   **Generalization Uncertainty:** Performance on completely unseen SSH document styles or languages not represented in the training mixture is unknown; the "brittleness" of LLM structured output is noted but not deeply root-caused.
*   **Operational Unvalidated:** The proposed hybrid system's routing accuracy, segmentation logic, and overall cost-benefit in a real-world, large-scale digital library pipeline are theoretical and not empirically tested at scale.

## Confidence
*   **Claim:** The unified benchmark better captures SSH characteristics than prior general benchmarks. **Confidence:** Medium.
*   **Claim:** LoRA-adapted LLMs show improved robustness on multilingual/footnote-heavy data vs. GROBID. **Confidence:** Medium.
*   **Claim:** Adaptive segmentation strategies improve end-to-end reliability. **Confidence:** Medium.
*   **Claim:** A hybrid deployment (route by complexity) is the optimal operational strategy. **Confidence:** Low.
