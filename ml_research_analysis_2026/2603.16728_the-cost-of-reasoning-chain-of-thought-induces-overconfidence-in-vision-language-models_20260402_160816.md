# The Cost of Reasoning: Chain-of-Thought Induces Overconfidence in Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.16728  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16728  
- Reference count: 37  
- Primary result: Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning degrades token‑likelihood uncertainty estimates while leaving agreement‑based consistency robust; masking answer mentions partially restores token‑likelihood reliability.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines how chain‑of‑thought (CoT) reasoning, despite improving answer accuracy, impacts uncertainty quantification in vision‑language models (VLMs). The authors find that token‑likelihood–based uncertainty estimates (e.g., maximum sequence probability, perplexity, mean token entropy) become systematically less reliable under CoT, as measured by selective‑generation metrics such as Prediction Rejection Ratio (PRR) and Spearman correlation. In contrast, agreement‑based consistency (majority vote over multiple sampled answers) remains stable or even improves under reasoning. The core mechanism identified is *implicit answer conditioning*: reasoning traces converge on a conclusion, inflating answer‑token probabilities regardless of correctness. An intervention that masks explicit answer mentions in the reasoning trace partially recovers the reliability of token‑likelihood scores, confirming the mechanism but indicating that other factors also contribute. The work recommends agreement‑based consistency for uncertainty estimation in reasoning‑enabled VLMs.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates uncertainty‑quantification (UQ) methods on four VQA/multimodal datasets (OK‑VQA, MathVista, MMMU‑Pro‑Vision, Oxford‑IIIT Pet) using several instruction‑tuned VLMs (Gemma3‑4B‑IT, Qwen3‑VL‑8B/32B‑Instruct, Qwen3‑VL‑8B‑Thinking). Two prompting conditions are compared: standard (no‑CoT) and zero‑shot CoT with structured `<think>` and `<answer>` blocks. For each input, the model either generates a single response (for token‑likelihood estimates) or K=10 samples (for consistency). UQ scores include per‑answer token‑likelihood aggregates (MSP, PPL, MTE), self‑report confidence via a meta‑prompt (SRC), and agreement‑based consistency (fraction of samples matching the majority answer). Correctness is determined by comparing extracted answers to ground truth. The quality of each score is assessed via selective‑generation metrics (PRR, Spearman correlation, AUGRC), which measure how well the score ranks correct versus incorrect predictions. A key intervention replaces answer mentions in the reasoning trace with `[MASK]` to test the implicit answer conditioning hypothesis.

## Key Results
- CoT drastically reduces the ranking quality of token‑likelihood uncertainty estimates; for example, mean token entropy PRR on OK‑VQA with Qwen3‑VL‑8B‑Instruct falls from 0.735 (no‑CoT) to 0.205 (CoT).  
- Agreement‑based consistency remains stable or improves under CoT across all models and datasets, achieving higher PRR and Spearman.  
- Masking answer mentions in the reasoning trace partially recovers ATL reliability (e.g., MSP PRR on OK‑VQA increases from 0.141 to 0.475), indicating that implicit answer conditioning is a key factor but not the sole cause.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Implicit Answer Conditioning
- **Claim:** Chain-of-thought reasoning causes the model to progressively commit to a specific answer within its reasoning trace, which artificially inflates answer-token probabilities regardless of the answer's correctness.
- **Mechanism:** Each generated reasoning token builds a context that increasingly suggests a particular conclusion. By the time the answer is produced, the conditional distribution P(answer | reasoning) is strongly peaked, so the tokens of that answer receive high likelihood even if the reasoning or the underlying perception is flawed.
- **Core assumption:** Token predictions are highly sensitive to the immediate linguistic context; a converged reasoning trace acts as a powerful prior that can override perceptual grounding.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "We identify implicit answer conditioning as the primary mechanism: as reasoning traces converge on a conclusion before the final answer is generated, token probabilities increasingly reflect consistency with the model’s own reasoning trace rather than uncertainty about correctness."
  - [section]: The Analysis (Sec. 6) demonstrates that answer frequency in the reasoning trace correlates positively with ATL confidence (Fig. 3) and that masking answer mentions partially restores uncertainty quality (Table 3). The same section also reports a negative correlation between reasoning length and confidence (Fig. 3a), ruling out length as the cause.
  - [corpus]: The corpus includes "Beware of Reasoning Overconfidence", which independently identifies reasoning overconfidence as a pitfall in multi-solution tasks, providing convergent evidence that reasoning can distort confidence.
- **Break condition:** The inflation subsides if the reasoning trace does not reach a strong conclusion (e.g., it remains ambiguous or lists multiple possibilities) or if answer tokens are generated before the reasoning fully converges.

### Mechanism 2: Token-Likelihood Misalignment
- **Claim:** Under CoT, answer-token likelihood ceases to be a reliable proxy for correctness; it instead measures how well the final answer aligns with the model’s self-generated reasoning narrative.
- **Mechanism:** Because the reasoning trace becomes part of the context for the answer, the probability of answer tokens is dominated by internal consistency with that trace. A high likelihood can occur even when the reasoning is incorrect, as long as the answer matches the narrative.
- **Core assumption:** Token probabilities are context-dependent and can be manipulated by the content of the reasoning, which may be detached from the actual visual evidence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "We show that reasoning consistently degrades the quality of most uncertainty estimates, even when it improves task accuracy." This degradation is precisely the misalignment.
  - [section]: Sec. 1 (Introduction) explicitly states: "The issue is not merely that additional conditioning changes token probabilities (which is expected) but that reasoning changes what the answer-token likelihood represents." Furthermore, Fig. 2 (Sec. 6) shows ATL confidence increasing under CoT for samples that become or remain incorrect, confirming correctness-agnostic inflation.
  - [corpus]: Works such as "Decompose and Compare Consistency" highlight the difficulty of relying on token-level signals for VLM reliability, underscoring the broader relevance of this misalignment.
- **Break condition:** If the model were forced to generate the answer without conditioning on the reasoning (e.g., separate modalities or a bottleneck), token likelihood could retain its correlation with correctness.

### Mechanism 3: Robustness of Agreement-Based Consistency
- **Claim:** Agreement-based consistency (majority vote over multiple sampled answers) remains reliable under CoT because it aggregates across diverse reasoning paths, avoiding the single-trace conditioning that harms token-likelihood methods.
- **Mechanism:** Each sampled generation follows a stochastic reasoning trajectory; even if each individual trajectory suffers from implicit answer conditioning, the variability in outcomes across trajectories preserves uncertainty information when answers disagree. Moreover, CoT often improves individual accuracy, so agreement becomes an even stronger signal.
- **Core assumption:** Stochastic sampling yields a distribution of reasoning chains whose final answers reflect genuine predictive uncertainty; diversity is not eliminated by the same conditioning that affects a single trace.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "In contrast, agreement-based consistency remains robust and often improves under reasoning, making it a practical choice for uncertainty estimation in reasoning-enabled VLMs."
  - [section]: Sec. 5 (Main Results) presents Tables 1-2, where Consistency maintains or increases PRR and Spearman under CoT across all datasets, while ATL metrics collapse (e.g., MTE PRR drops from 0.735 to 0.205 on OK-VQA).
  - [corpus]: "Decompose and Compare Consistency" directly uses consistency to measure VLM answer reliability, confirming that these methods are effective in multimodal settings.
- **Break condition:** Consistency would fail to reflect uncertainty if all sampled traces converged to the same answer due to extremely low sampling temperature or a model with near-deterministic outputs; in such cases, agreement would be artificially high even for incorrect predictions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Selective Generation**: Why needed: The paper evaluates uncertainty within the selective generation paradigm, where models abstain when confidence is low. Metrics like Prediction Rejection Ratio (PRR) abstract away overall accuracy and isolate ranking quality. Quick check: What does a higher PRR indicate about the uncertainty estimate?
- **Token-Likelihood Uncertainty Estimates (MSP, PPL, MTE)**: Why needed: These are the white-box methods that degrade under CoT; they rely on token probabilities from a single generation. Quick check: How does mean token entropy differ from perplexity? Which is length-normalized?
- **Agreement-Based Consistency**: Why needed: The paper identifies this black-box approach as robust to CoT, providing a practical alternative that does not require access to token probabilities. Quick check: Why can consistency work even when the model's internal probabilities are miscalibrated?
- **Chain-of-Thought Prompting**: Why needed: CoT is the experimental manipulation that triggers the observed effects; its structure (e.g., `<think>` tags) determines how reasoning is integrated with the answer. Quick check: In standard CoT, in what order are the reasoning trace and final answer generated?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data loader -> Prompt builder -> VLM engine -> Generator -> Answer parser -> (ATL: token logits; Consistency: K sample answers; Self-Report: meta-prompt) -> Evaluator

**Critical path:**  
1. For each input, build prompt according to condition (no‑CoT / CoT).  
2. Invoke generation (single or multiple samples). Capture outputs.  
3. Parse final answer(s) and compute the desired uncertainty score.  
4. Record whether prediction matches ground truth.  
5. Repeat for all dataset examples.  
6. Sort examples by uncertainty (ascending for confidence) and sweep thresholds to compute rejection curves; derive PRR and AUGRC.  
7. Compute Spearman correlation between scores and correctness.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Token‑likelihood (ATL)**:  
  * Pro: fast (single forward pass); yields a continuous confidence score.  
  * Con: fails under CoT; requires white‑box access to logit probabilities.  
- **Agreement‑based Consistency**:  
  * Pro: robust to reasoning; works even with black‑box models if final answers can be parsed.  
  * Con: expensive (K forward passes); answer parsing must be reliable; variance increases if K is small.  
- **Self‑Reported Confidence**:  
  * Pro: cheap (one extra generation); intuitive.  
  * Con: performance is inconsistent; sensitive to meta‑prompt wording.  
- **CoT vs No‑CoT**:  
  * CoT improves accuracy on complex tasks but introduces ATL degradation and longer generation time.  
  * No‑CoT preserves ATL reliability but may sacrifice accuracy.

**Failure signatures:**  
- ATL confidence rises under CoT even on samples that become incorrect.  
- ATL confidence is high while consistency among 10 samples is low (e.g., <0.3) – indicates overcommitment to a single reasoning trace.  
- Masked‑CoT intervention (replacing answer mentions in reasoning with [MASK]) reduces ATL confidence; lack of reduction suggests strong implicit conditioning.  
- Consistency fails only when sampled answers show no diversity (all identical) despite moderate difficulty – may indicate too low temperature or prompt quirk.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline degradation replication**: On OK‑VQA with Qwen3‑VL‑8B‑Instruct, compare no‑CoT vs CoT for MTE and Consistency metrics. Expect MTE PRR drop from ~0.735 to ~0.205 and Consistency PRR rise from ~0.436 to ~0.478 (Table 1). This validates the core claim.  
2. **Answer‑frequency correlation**: For CoT runs, count occurrences of the exact predicted answer string within the `<think>` block. Compute Spearman correlation with MTE confidence. Expect strong positive correlation (positive ρ) for MTE, but near‑zero for Consistency. This directly links implicit answer conditioning to score inflation.  
3. **Masking intervention**: Implement Masked‑CoT by replacing every answer mention in `<think>` with [MASK] while keeping the final answer unchanged. Recompute MTE confidence conditioned on masked reasoning. Compare PRR before vs after masking. Expect substantial increase (e.g., from 0.141 to 0.475 on OK‑VQA; Table 3). Include a random‑masking control to ensure effect is specific to answer mentions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Under what conditions does self‑reported confidence (via P(True) probing) remain reliable or even improve under reasoning, given its mixed empirical behavior?  
2. How can we develop uncertainty estimates that remain reliable under chain‑of‑thought reasoning without requiring multiple samples?  
3. What factors beyond explicit answer mentions in reasoning traces cause implicit answer conditioning, and how can they be mitigated?  
4. Does reasoning‑induced degradation of token‑likelihood uncertainty estimates generalize to other reasoning formats (e.g., tree‑of‑thoughts, few‑shot CoT) and to non‑multimodal large language models?

## Limitations
- The evidence for implicit answer conditioning is correlational; other factors (e.g., reasoning length) may contribute to the observed degradation.  
- The study’s scope is limited to specific VLMs and a small set of benchmarks; it is unclear whether the findings generalize to other models, datasets, or reasoning strategies.  
- The masking intervention relies on exact string matching, which may miss paraphrased answer references and therefore incompletely address implicit conditioning.

## Confidence
- CoT degrades token‑likelihood UQ: High  
- Implicit answer conditioning as primary mechanism: Medium  
- Agreement‑based consistency remains robust: High  
- Masking answer mentions restores ATL reliability: Medium‑High  

## Next Checks
1. Broaden model and dataset coverage – test additional VLMs (e.g., LLaVA, InternVL) and VQA benchmarks (e.g., GQA, VQAv2) to confirm generalizability.  
2. Directly vary reasoning commitment – use prompts that enforce different levels of commitment (e.g., “list all possible answers” vs “commit to one”) to isolate the effect on ATL overconfidence.  
3. Probe consistency under extreme sampling – evaluate consistency with greedy decoding (temperature 0) and small K (2‑3) to determine failure thresholds.
