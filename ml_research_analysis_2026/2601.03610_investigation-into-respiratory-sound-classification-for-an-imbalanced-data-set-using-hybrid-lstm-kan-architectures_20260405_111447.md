# Investigation into respiratory sound classification for an imbalanced data set using hybrid LSTM-KAN architectures

## Quick Facts
- arXiv:2601.03610  
- URL:https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03610  
- References:21  
- Result:94.6% overall accuracy, macro F1=0.703 on ICBHI

## Executive Summary
This study tackles severe class imbalance in multi-class respiratory sound classification using the ICBHI dataset, where COPD accounts for 86.5% of samples. The authors propose a hybrid LSTM-KAN architecture that processes aggregated acoustic features: an LSTM encoder captures temporal patterns, and a KAN classifier with learnable splines makes the final decision. Imbalance mitigation combines focal loss, SMOTE on feature vectors, and class-specific augmentation within a two-stage training strategy. The model achieves 94.6% accuracy and a macro F1-score of 0.703. Minority classes show varied performance (e.g., bronchiectasis 0.84, healthy 0.76, URTI ~0.44, bronchiolitis ~0.45). Inference time is approximately 2.1 ms, indicating real-time feasibility. An ablation study suggests synergy among the imbalance techniques, though performance on the rarest classes remains limited.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with standard acoustic feature extraction (e.g., MFCCs) from raw respiratory sounds, followed by temporal aggregation to produce fixed-length vectors. These are fed into an LSTM encoder to model sequential dependencies. The LSTM output is classified by a Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) that replaces fixed MLP activations with learnable spline functions, offering a flexible non‑linear decision surface. To address the extreme COPD dominance, three complementary strategies are employed: focal loss to down‑weight easy majority examples, SMOTE to generate synthetic minority feature vectors and balance the training distribution, and targeted augmentations (pitch shift, noise injection) applied only to minority classes. A two‑stage training regimen first trains on the full dataset, then refines using oversampled and augmented minority data. This integrated approach aims to boost minority recall without sacrificing majority performance.

## Key Results
- Overall accuracy: 94.6%, macro F1-score: 0.703 on ICBHI test set  
- Minority class F1-scores: bronchiectasis 0.84, healthy 0.76, URTI ~0.44, bronchiolitis ~0.45  
- Inference time: ~2.1 ms per sample, enabling real-time deployment  

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
LSTMs capture the temporal dynamics inherent in respiratory sounds, which are critical for distinguishing pathologies. KANs, with learnable spline activations, adapt to the complex, uneven decision boundaries typical of imbalanced data. Focal loss shifts training focus toward hard and rare examples, preventing the model from being overwhelmed by the abundant COPD samples. SMOTE on feature vectors balances the training set by augmenting minority representations without requiring raw audio synthesis. Class‑specific augmentations increase intra‑class variability for under‑represented conditions, improving generalization. The two‑stage training ensures the model first learns robust features from all data and then fine‑tunes on rebalanced minority data, mitigating catastrophic forgetting of majority patterns.

  Why needed: Respiratory sounds are sequential; LSTMs capture long‑range temporal dependencies.  
  Quick check: Replace LSTM with a feedforward network and compare macro F1 on a validation fold.

- **KAN**  
  Why needed: Standard MLPs may not flexibly model complex boundaries in imbalanced settings; KANs use learnable splines for better adaptability.  
  Quick check: Substitute KAN with an MLP of similar size and measure per‑class F1.

- **SMOTE**  
  Why needed: Extreme COPD dominance biases the model; SMOTE synthesizes minority feature vectors to balance training distribution.  
  Quick check: Train without SMOTE and evaluate recall for URTI and bronchiolitis.

- **Focal Loss**  
  Why needed: Cross‑entropy is dominated by majority examples; focal loss down‑weights easy predictions to focus on hard/rare cases.  
  Quick check: Use cross‑entropy instead and observe change in macro F1.

- **Class‑specific Augmentation**  
  Why needed: Minority classes have limited samples; augmentations (e.g., pitch shift) artificially increase their variability.  
  Quick check: Remove augmentations for a specific minority class and track its F1.

- **Two‑stage Training**  
  Why needed: Prevents loss of majority knowledge when rebalancing; allows stable feature learning before fine‑tuning.  
  Quick check: Train in a single stage with combined rebalancing and compare with the two‑stage approach.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Raw audio → Feature extraction (MFCCs) → Aggregation → LSTM encoder → KAN classifier → Class predictions

**Critical path**  
Inference requires feature extraction, LSTM forward pass, and KAN classification. Imbalance techniques are confined to training.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- SMOTE on features may disrupt temporal coherence but is computationally cheap.  
- KANs add spline parameters, increasing memory versus standard MLPs, but offer flexibility and interpretability.  
- Two‑stage training delays rebalancing, extending training time but preserving majority knowledge.  
- Real‑time inference (2.1 ms) is achieved by limiting model depth; further accuracy gains might increase latency.

**Failure signatures**  
- Very low F1 (<0.5) for URTI/bronchiolitis indicates unresolved imbalance.  
- High training accuracy but low macro F1 suggests overfitting to COPD.  
- Increased inference latency if LSTM size grows.  
- Confusion between URTI and bronchiolitis may point to insufficient discriminative features.

## Limitations
- The extreme class imbalance (COPD 86.5%) makes overall accuracy (94.6%) misleading; macro F1 (0.703) reveals persistent weakness on rare classes (URTI ~0.44, bronchiolitis ~0.45).  
- SMOTE applied to feature vectors may distort temporal relationships inherent in respiratory sounds, questioning its suitability.  
- The novelty of the hybrid LSTM-KAN design is uncertain given similar CNN‑Transformer hybrids in the literature; related work shows low citation impact, suggesting limited field validation.

## Confidence
- Performance metrics: **Medium** – high accuracy plausible but macro F1 shows limitations.  
- Ablation synergy claim: **Medium‑Low** – lacking detailed breakdown of individual contributions.  
- Computational efficiency: **High** – inference time (~2.1 ms) is concrete and reproducible if hardware specs are given.
