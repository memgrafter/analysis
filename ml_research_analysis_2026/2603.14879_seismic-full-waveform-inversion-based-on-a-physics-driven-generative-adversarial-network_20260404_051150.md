# Seismic full-waveform inversion based on a physics-driven generative adversarial network

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14879  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14879  
- **Reference count:** 29  
- **Primary result:** Proposed physics-driven GAN-FWI achieves higher SSIM (0.6652) and SNR (21.42 dB) than baseline FWIGAN (0.6301, 16.71 dB) on noisy Marmousi inversion, with reduced sensitivity to linear initial models.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a hybrid seismic full-waveform inversion (FWI) method that integrates a U‑Net deep neural network with wave‑equation constrained inversion, further stabilized by adversarial discriminator training. The architecture first uses a pre‑trained U‑Net to generate a plausible initial velocity model from observed seismic data, then refines it via traditional FWI to enforce physical consistency, and finally employs a discriminator to ensure the synthetic data from the inverted model matches the distribution of observed data. Evaluated on Marmousi and Overthrust benchmarks under noise and with poor initial models, the method demonstrates superior structural similarity and signal‑to‑noise ratio compared to a prior GAN‑based FWI approach, indicating improved robustness and reduced dependence on initial guesses.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework, termed physics‑driven GAN‑FWI, employs a generator network G that combines a U‑Net with a classical FWI solver. The U‑Net maps observed seismic shot gathers to an initial velocity model \(v_{\text{pre}}\). This model is then iteratively corrected by an FWI process that minimizes data misfit under the acoustic wave equation, yielding \(v_{\text{corr}}\). Synthetic data \(d_{\text{syn}}\) are generated from \(v_{\text{corr}}\) via forward modeling. A discriminator \(D\) is trained to distinguish \(d_{\text{syn}}\) from real observed data \(d_{\text{obs}}\). Adversarial training alternately updates \(D\) (to improve discrimination) and \(G\) (to fool \(D\)), thereby encouraging \(G\) to produce models whose synthetic data are statistically indistinguishable from observations. The U‑Net is first pre‑trained with L2 loss against a smoothed true model to accelerate convergence and provide a physically reasonable starting point.

## Key Results
- On noisy (10 dB AWGN) Marmousi inversion, the proposed method reaches SSIM 0.6652 and SNR 21.42 dB, outperforming baseline FWIGAN (SSIM 0.6301, SNR 16.71 dB).
- On Overthrust models with and without noise, the method consistently yields higher SSIM and SNR than the baseline.
- Starting from a linear initial model (a poor guess), the proposed approach shows markedly better recovery of complex velocity structures and metric improvements, demonstrating reduced sensitivity to the initial model.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The U‑Net component reduces FWI’s dependence on the initial model by generating a structurally plausible velocity model directly from seismic data.
- **Mechanism:** Pre‑training on synthetic (seismic, smoothed true velocity) pairs teaches the U‑Net to map data to a reasonable background model. This avoids the poor local minima that trap classical FWI when starting from linear or random initializations.
- **Core assumption:** The seismic‑to‑velocity mapping is learnable from available training pairs and the smoothed targets are sufficiently close to true models to avoid catastrophic bias.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions integration of deep networks with physical constraints; Section 3.2 describes pre‑training with L2 loss on \(v_{\text{init}}\); corpus literature supports using deep networks for initial model generation.
- **Break condition:** Training data lacking geological diversity leads to biased U‑Net predictions; extremely complex structures absent from training may be misrepresented.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The FWI solver enforces physical consistency by ensuring the final velocity model reproduces observed seismic data through the wave equation.
- **Mechanism:** After U‑Net produces \(v_{\text{pre}}\), a traditional FWI optimization (e.g., adjoint‑state gradient) refines it to \(v_{\text{corr}}\) by minimizing the data misfit under the acoustic wave equation.
- **Core assumption:** The acoustic wave equation adequately models propagation, and gradient descent converges given a sufficiently good initial model from the U‑Net.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract cites physical constraints; Section 2.1 defines the wave equation and misfit; Section 3.2 details the correction step.
- **Break condition:** If \(v_{\text{pre}}\) is too far from truth, cycle‑skipping may occur; heavy noise or extreme sparsity can corrupt gradients.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Adversarial training on seismic data distributions improves robustness to noise and helps recover complex structures by encouraging the generator to produce models whose synthetic data are indistinguishable from real observations.
- **Mechanism:** The discriminator is trained to classify real observed data vs. synthetic data from \(v_{\text{corr}}\). The generator is updated to fool the discriminator, complementing the L2 data misfit with a distribution‑matching objective that may capture higher‑order features.
- **Core assumption:** Distributional differences between real and synthetic data contain useful signals beyond L2 misfit; the discriminator can learn these differences and provide meaningful gradients.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states adversarial training enhances stability; Section 3.1 defines the discriminator’s role; training ratio (6 D steps per 1 G step) is a heuristic to balance stability.
- **Break condition:** Training instability (e.g., vanishing gradients if D becomes too accurate) or discriminator learning noise artifacts instead of structural content.

## Foundational Learning
- **Full‑waveform inversion (FWI):** Needed to understand wave‑equation constrained inversion and adjoint‑state gradient computation. Quick check: How is the gradient of the frequency‑domain misfit computed? (Answer: via zero‑lag cross‑correlation of forward and adjoint wavefields.)
- **Generative adversarial networks (GANs):** Central to the adversarial robustness framework. Quick check: What replaces the random noise vector \(z\) in this physics‑driven GAN? (Answer: Observed seismic data; the generator is deterministic given the data.)
- **U‑Net architecture:** The generator’s neural network component; its encoder‑decoder with skip connections enables high‑resolution mapping from seismic to velocity. Quick check: Why are skip connections used? (Answer: To preserve fine spatial details lost during downsampling, ensuring precise localization.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map (plain text)
Observed seismic data -> U-Net -> v_pre -> FWI solver -> v_corr -> Forward modeling -> d_pred -> Discriminator D (vs. d_obs)

### Critical path (training loop)
1. Sample batch of observed shot gathers \(d_{\text{obs}}\).
2. Compute \(v_{\text{pre}} = \text{U-Net}(d_{\text{obs}})\).
3. Run fixed‑iteration FWI from \(v_{\text{pre}}\) to obtain \(v_{\text{corr}}\) (using \(d_{\text{obs}}\)).
4. Generate \(d_{\text{syn}} = \text{Forward}(v_{\text{corr}})\).
5. Update D: minimize binary cross‑entropy on real \(d_{\text{obs}}\) vs. fake \(d_{\text{syn}}\).
6. Update U‑Net (generator): minimize \(-\log D(d_{\text{syn}})\) (fool D). Training ratio: 6 D updates per 1 G update.

### Design tradeoffs
- **U‑Net capacity vs. data needs:** Deeper U‑Net captures complex mappings but requires more diverse training data; pre‑training mitigates but does not eliminate this.
- **Discriminator on data vs. model:** Discriminating on seismic data directly compares to observations but may be misled by noise; discriminating on velocity models would require defining a prior, which is difficult.
- **FWI iterations per step:** More iterations improve physical correction but increase computational cost; the paper does not specify the exact number used.
- **Training ratio (6:1):** Heuristically chosen; imbalance can cause vanishing gradients (too many D steps) or mode collapse (too few).

### Failure signatures
- Discriminator accuracy reaches near 100% quickly; generator loss plateaus (vanishing gradients).
- Inverted velocity models become overly smooth or exhibit checkerboard artifacts (FWI instability).
- U‑Net pre‑training loss fails to converge, indicating poor mapping capability.
- No SSIM/SNR improvement over baseline despite prolonged training.
- Visual: metrics degrade after certain epochs or with out‑of‑distribution models.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Marmousi benchmark:** Compare proposed method vs. baseline FWIGAN under noise‑free and 10 dB AWGN, using Gaussian‑smoothed initial model; evaluate SSIM and SNR.
2. **Overthrust benchmark:** Same comparison on a more complex overthrust model, with and without noise.
3. **Linear initial model test:** Apply both methods to Marmousi and Overthrust starting from a linearly increasing velocity (poor initial guess) to assess sensitivity reduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the method perform on actual field data, which contains irregular sampling, multiples, and unknown noise patterns?
- What is the computational overhead relative to traditional FWI and other DL‑based FWI approaches?
- Can the framework be effectively extended to 3D seismic inversion, and how does accuracy scale with dimensionality?
- How robust is the method to non‑Gaussian noise and extreme data sparsity common in field acquisitions?
- What specific contribution does the adversarial discriminator make to inversion stability, independent of the U‑Net component? (No ablation study isolates this effect.)

## Limitations
- FWI solver details (iterations, step size, adjoint implementation, boundary conditions) are unspecified, hindering exact reproduction.
- Handling of multiple shot gathers (aggregation, input shape to networks) is not described.
- Pre‑training dataset size and geological diversity are unknown, limiting claims about general robustness.

## Confidence
- U‑Net initial model generation: **Medium confidence** – depends on training data coverage; out‑of‑distribution geology may cause bias.
- FWI solver refinement: **High confidence** – based on well‑established physics, but implementation details could affect stability.
- Adversarial training: **Medium confidence** – heuristic update ratio may cause instability; discriminator might focus on noise artifacts.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation on FWI iterations:** Vary the number of FWI steps within the generator to quantify the trade‑off between physical correctness and computational cost, monitoring SSIM/SNR and training stability.
2. **Out‑of‑distribution geology test:** Evaluate on velocity models with structural features absent from U‑Net training data to test the break condition regarding data diversity.
3. **Discriminator attention visualization:** Use Grad‑CAM or similar techniques on the discriminator to verify it focuses on coherent structural mismatches rather than noise patterns.
