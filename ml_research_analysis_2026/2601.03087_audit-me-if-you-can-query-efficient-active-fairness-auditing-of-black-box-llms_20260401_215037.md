# Audit Me If You Can: Query-Efficient Active Fairness Auditing of Black-Box LLMs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03087
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03087
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: BAFA achieves up to 40× query reduction (e.g., 144 vs 5,956 on CivilComments) to reach ε=0.02 accuracy, with lower AUEC and variance than stratified sampling.

## Executive Summary
BAFA introduces a framework for query-efficient fairness auditing of black-box large language models. It maintains a version space of surrogate models consistent with observed black‑box scores and uses constrained optimization to compute tight lower and upper bounds on the group fairness metric ΔAUC. An active query selector targets inputs where the bounding surrogates disagree most, rapidly shrinking the uncertainty interval. Distributional regularization prevents selection bias, ensuring the audit remains representative of the target population. Evaluated on hate speech detection (CivilComments) and occupation scoring (Bias-in-Bios), BAFA reaches target error thresholds with up to 40× fewer queries, lower area‑under‑error‑curve, and greater stability across random seeds compared to stratified and power sampling baselines.

## Method Summary
BAFA operates in an iterative loop. Starting from a small stratified seed set, it constructs a λ‑approximate version space of surrogate models (e.g., BERT) whose scores lie within λ of the black‑box scores on the queried examples. Two constrained empirical risk minimization problems (minimizing and maximizing the fairness metric) over this space yield a confidence interval for ΔAUC. The next batch of inputs is selected by scoring unqueried candidates with the absolute difference between the upper‑ and lower‑bounding surrogates; high‑disagreement points promise the greatest reduction in interval width. A regularizer reweights selection probabilities to match the group‑label marginals of the audit pool, mitigating bias. The cycle repeats until the bound width falls below a target ε or the query budget is exhausted.

## Key Results
- Query efficiency: BAFA reaches ε=0.02 accuracy with up to 40× fewer queries than stratified sampling (e.g., 144 vs 5,956 on CivilComments).
- Lower AUEC: Over the first 1,000 queries, BAFA attains a smaller area under the error curve than baselines.
- Stability: Across 20 random seeds, BAFA exhibits reduced variance in error at fixed budgets (e.g., 250 queries).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Uncertainty Quantification via Version Space
- **Claim:** BAFA bounds the uncertainty of the fairness metric (ΔAUC) by maintaining a version space of surrogate models consistent with observed black-box scores.
- **Mechanism:** For a queried set *S*, BAFA constructs a λ-approximate version space *Hλ(St)* containing all surrogate hypotheses *h* that approximately match black-box scores: |*h(xi) – h*(xi)*| ≤ λ. It then solves two constrained empirical risk minimization (C-ERM) problems to find the hypotheses *ht_max* and *ht_min* within this space that maximize and minimize the fairness metric μ(h). The resulting interval [*μt_min, μt_max*] certifies that the true ΔAUC lies within these bounds, given the surrogate family and observed data.
- **Core assumption:** The surrogate hypothesis class *H* (e.g., BERT) is expressive enough that, when constrained to match black-box scores on *S*, the resulting version space includes hypotheses whose fairness metrics bracket the true value.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3]: Explicitly defines the version space *Hλ(St)* and the certificate computation via constrained optimization.
  - [section A.2.2]: Details the Cooper-based implementation for solving min/max μ(h) subject to score consistency constraints.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors focus on fairness auditing but not on this specific version-space/constrained-ERM hybrid; evidence is paper-internal.
- **Break condition:** If the surrogate class *H* is too weak to approximate the black-box's input-output behavior under the constraints (e.g., after few queries), the min/max bounds may become vacuous (width ≫ true error) or exclude the true ΔAUC, violating coverage.

### Mechanism 2: Active Query Selection via Disagreement Targeting
- **Claim:** Selecting inputs where the current certificate’s upper- and lower-bound surrogates (*ht_max*, *ht_min*) disagree most in pairwise rankings maximally shrinks the uncertainty interval per query.
- **Mechanism:** BAFA scores each unqueried candidate *x* by the absolute score difference *dist(x) = |ht_max(x) – ht_min(x)|*. High disagreement indicates regions where the fairness metric’s value is most sensitive to the black-box’s unknown behavior on *x*. Querying such points maximally reduces the version space, tightening the μ-interval faster than random or stratified sampling.
- **Core assumption:** Disagreement in surrogate rankings on candidate inputs correlates with the potential reduction in uncertainty width (μ_max – μ_min) after including *x* in *S*.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: “Active query selection targets high-disagreement input regions to maximally shrink these uncertainty intervals.”
  - [section A.2.3]: Formalizes `dist(x)` as the informativeness signal and describes the top-k selection.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like “Active Fourier Auditor” use frequency-domain disagreement; BAFA’s model-disagreement approach is distinct but conceptually aligned.
- **Break condition:** If the surrogates *ht_max* and *ht_min* share spurious correlations (e.g., due to limited *S*), their disagreement may not reflect true fairness-critical regions (e.g., identity tokens), leading to inefficient queries.

### Mechanism 3: Regularization to Prevent Selection Bias
- **Claim:** Distributional regularization during selection prevents drift between the queried set’s (g,y) margins and the audit pool’s, ensuring the ΔAUC estimate remains unbiased for the target population.
- **Mechanism:** BAFA multiplies an informativeness score *st(x)* by a weight *w(g,y)* that inflates selection probability for under-sampled (group, label) strata: if *pT(g,y)* < *pD(g,y)*, *w(g,y) > 1*. This combats the active sampler’s tendency to over-exploit a few informative strata, preserving the pool’s marginal distributions.
- **Core assumption:** The fairness metric ΔAUC is a population parameter over the audit pool *D*; bias in the queried set *S* can invalidate the certificate if *S*’s group-label margins deviate from *D*’s.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section A.2.4]: Describes weight computation based on *pD(g,y)/max(pT(g,y), ε)*.
  - [section 5]: Notes that without regularization, active sampling induces selection bias in fairness estimation.
- **Break condition:** At very low budgets, some strata may have *pT(g,y) = 0*; the weight becomes 1 by default, so under-representation persists until later rounds, potentially introducing temporary bias in the midpoint estimate.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Version Space (Active Learning)**
  - *Why needed here:* Provides a computationally tractable way to represent uncertainty about the black-box’s behavior given limited queries. Instead of a posterior distribution, BAFA uses the set of all surrogates consistent with observed scores.
  - *Quick check question:* If a new candidate *x* is queried and its score lies within [*ht_min(x), ht_max(x)*] for all current version space hypotheses, how does the version space change?
- **Concept: Constrained Empirical Risk Minimization (C-ERM)**
  - *Why needed here:* Allows optimization (min/max) of the non-convex, non-differentiable ΔAUC objective over a large neural surrogate class, subject to hard constraints on black-box score agreement. This yields tight bounds where simple uniform priors would be too loose.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is standard ERM (e.g., minimizing squared error) insufficient for producing useful fairness bounds?
- **Concept: Uncertainty Calibration via Bound Width**
  - *Why needed here:* The width (μ_max – μ_min) is the certificate’s stated uncertainty. Understanding its relationship to actual error (|ΔAUC_est – ΔAUC_true|) is crucial for auditors to know when to stop querying.
  - *Quick check question:* In Figure 5, what does the Pearson correlation *r* between bound width and absolute error tell us about calibration?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Audit Pool (`D`)**: Immutable dataset with (text *x*, group *g*, label *y*, ID). Source of all candidates.
  2. **Black-box Interface**: `h*(x) → s*` (score in [0,1] or [0,100]). Only external dependency.
  3. **Surrogate Model (`H`)**: Pre-trained BERT (frozen or fine-tuned). Used in C-ERM to define version space; also provides embeddings for BO.
  4. **C-ERM Engine (`Cooper`)**: Solves `argmin/max μ(h)` s.t. `|h(x_i) – s*i| ≤ λ` for all `(x_i, s*i) ∈ S`. Returns `ht_min`, `ht_max`.
  5. **Active Selector (`Π`)**: Takes `ht_min`, `ht_max`, candidate pool `U \ S`, and computes scores `st(x)` (disagreement ± BO ± regularization). Returns top-*k* batch.
  6. **Regularizer**: Adjusts `st(x)` by `w(g,y)` to match `pD(g,y)`.
  7. **Logger & Cache**: Tracks queried IDs, scores, surrogate checkpoints, BO state.
- **Critical path:** `Seed → C-ERM (certificate) → Select → Query → Update S → C-ERM`. The loop must maintain ID-based alignment between *S*, cached scores, and selection masks.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Surrogate size vs. feasibility:* BERT-base balances cost and expressiveness; larger models (GPT-2) may improve alignment with GPT-4.1-mini but increase C-ERM cost.
  - *C-ERM frequency vs. overhead:* Running C-ERM every round gives responsive certificates but dominates runtime (~60-70% of time).
  - *BO anchoring:* Using BO as a bounded stabilizer (λ_t ramped) avoids replacing the core disagreement signal with a potentially unreliable GP early on.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Vacuous bounds:** Width ≫ 0.1 after many queries → surrogate cannot satisfy constraints; likely λ too small or *H* mismatched.
  - **High early variance:** Large error spread across seeds at T<100 → active selector might overfit to initial seed; consider larger seed set.
  - **Coverage drop:** ΔAUC_true outside [μ_min, μ_max] frequently → either surrogate mismatch (Bias-in-Bios) or λ too tight; check bound violation diagnostics.
  - **Selection bias:** Marginals `pT(g,y)` diverge from `pD(g,y)` → regularizer α_t not ramped fast enough or strata too sparse.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Sanity check:** Run BAFA-disagreement on a tiny synthetic dataset (N=500) with known ΔAUC and a trivial surrogate (logistic regression). Verify that bound width decreases monotonically and ΔAUC_true stays within [μ_min, μ_max].
  2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Vary `λ` (constraint tolerance) and `k` (batch size) on CivilComments. Expect: smaller `λ` → tighter but potentially infeasible bounds; larger `k` → fewer C-ERM updates → slower convergence.
  3. **Surrogate mismatch stress test:** Use DistilBERT as surrogate for GPT-4.1-mini (Bias-in-Bios). Observe higher early error and lower coverage (as in paper), but confirm eventual convergence. If not, try LoRA fine-tuning of surrogate on early queries.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does using a larger, architecturally matched surrogate model (e.g., GPT-2 or GPT-3 for auditing GPT-4.1-mini) improve query efficiency and uncertainty bound tightness compared to smaller mismatched surrogates like BERT-base?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state: "Future work should especially try out GPT-2 or GPT-3 for the GPT-4.1-mini audit as architectures are the same and, thus, could lead to more accurate results and faster convergence." (Section 9, Limitations)
- **Why unresolved:** Current experiments used BERT-base as a surrogate for both HateBERT and GPT-4.1-mini, showing BAFA works even with mismatch. However, the impact of architectural similarity on version space shrinkage and selection efficacy remains unquantified. Matching architectures might yield tighter constraints and faster convergence in the C-ERM step.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic comparison across multiple black-box models and surrogate families, measuring queries to reach target error, AUEC, and bound calibration, while controlling for model size and task similarity.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How do different active query selection strategies (disagreement vs. Bayesian optimisation) differ in the specific subpopulations, linguistic features, or input regions they prioritise, and how does this affect fairness audit coverage and bias?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors propose: "Future work should build on this to characterise which regions of the input space different selection rules emphasise, for instance, borderline cases, specific linguistic patterns (e.g., AAE or counter-speech (Sap et al., 2019)), identity tokens or particular subpopulations." (Section 6, Discussion)
- **Why unresolved:** The paper evaluates only aggregate performance metrics (error, AUEC). Without analysing selected examples, it is unclear whether active strategies systematically neglect certain groups or over-exploit narrow regions, potentially skewing the fairness estimate.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Post-hoc analysis of selected query batches across strategies, profiling by demographic group, label, and linguistic markers (e.g., using lexica, identity term counts). Compare selection distributions against the audit pool to detect bias, and correlate with changes in fairness estimates.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the BAFA framework be extended to other fairness metrics (e.g., equalized odds, demographic parity at fixed thresholds) and other auditing domains (e.g., privacy leakage, adversarial robustness) while retaining similar query-efficiency gains?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors claim: "Generalisability beyond ROC AUC difference. ... This covers other group metrics (e.g., TPR/FPR gaps at fixed thresholds, equalised odds) and extends to performance, privacy audits, and robustness and safety audits." (Section 6, Discussion)
- **Why unresolved:** The implementation is specialized for ∆AUC using a ranking surrogate. Other metrics may not be well-approximated by smooth surrogates or may require different constraint formulations and selection signals. It is unknown if the active sampling advantage transfers.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop BAFA variants for alternative metrics (e.g., equalized odds difference) by designing appropriate differentiable objectives and selection heuristics. Evaluate on relevant datasets/black-box tasks, comparing query efficiency to passive baselines.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What algorithmic and engineering optimizations (e.g., less frequent C-ERM solves, warm-starting, parallelization) can reduce BAFA's computational overhead without sacrificing query-efficiency advantages?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors note: "A key limitation is that our end-to-end pipeline is resource intensive... However, we think that substantial speedups are likely feasible. Promising directions include engineering improvements (e.g., caching/more efficient data pipelines), algorithmic warm-starting across rounds, more efficient batching strategies, and hybrid protocols..." (Section 9, Limitations)
- **Why unresolved:** The current prototype requires significant GPU time (e.g., 4–6 hours per run), hindering practical use. Proposed speedups are speculative and not implemented, so their effectiveness and trade-offs are unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement candidate optimizations (e.g., run C-ERM every m-th iteration, initialize min/max problems from previous solutions, parallelize solves) and benchmark wall-clock time reductions against baseline BAFA, while monitoring any degradation in query efficiency (e.g., queries to target ε).

## Limitations
- Cooper dependency: The core C-ERM bound computation relies on the external Cooper library, whose API and non-convex optimization behavior are not detailed, making exact reproduction of tight version-space constraints challenging.
- BO feature specification: The "gradient feature" and utility signal for Bayesian optimization are underspecified, so replicating the exact BO-driven selection performance depends on guesswork.
- Surrogate–black-box alignment: The version-space certificate assumes the surrogate family (BERT) can approximate the black-box under constraints. For complex LLMs (e.g., GPT-4.1-mini), this may fail early, requiring ad-hoc tuning not covered in the main evaluation.

## Confidence
- Cooper dependency: Medium
- BO feature specification: Low
- Surrogate–black-box alignment: Medium
