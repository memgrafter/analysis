# TruthStance: An Annotated Dataset of Conversations on Truth Social

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.14406
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14406
*   **Reference count:** 19
*   **Primary result:** Best-performing LLM configuration (Gemini-2.5-Flash with Few-Shot Chain-of-Thought) achieves macro-F1 scores of 0.821 (argument mining) and 0.783 (stance detection) on a human-annotated benchmark.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces TruthStance, the first large-scale dataset of conversational threads from the alt-tech platform Truth Social, preserving full reply-tree structure (24,352 root posts, 523,360 comments). It addresses the lack of resources for argument mining and stance detection on such platforms by creating a human-annotated benchmark (1,500 instances, κ=0.70–0.76) and demonstrating that large language models (LLMs), particularly Gemini-2.5-Flash with Few-Shot Chain-of-Thought prompting, can reliably annotate these tasks on this domain-shifted platform. The authors generate labels for the full dataset and conduct large-scale analyses of stance and argumentation patterns across conversation depth, topics, and user behavior, releasing all data and code publicly.

## Method Summary
The research pipeline begins with scraping full comment threads for Truth Social posts using the Truthbrush framework, followed by preprocessing to remove reposts, low-engagement posts (<3 replies), and duplicates. A human-annotated benchmark (750 posts for argument mining, 750 comments for stance detection) is created to evaluate LLM prompting strategies (Few-Shot, Chain-of-Thought, Few-Shot Chain-of-Thought) across three models (Gemini, DeepSeek, GPT-o3). The best configuration (Gemini-FSCoT) is used to label all filtered posts for argument presence and a large sample of comments for stance relative to their immediate parent. A stance propagation engine then traverses the reply trees to estimate each comment's stance toward the original post, truncating branches at intermediate neutral stances. Finally, descriptive analytics examine the distribution of stances and arguments across depth, topics (clustered from hashtags), and user features.

## Key Results
*   The TruthStance dataset comprises 24,352 posts and 523,360 comments with preserved tree structure, plus a human-annotated benchmark of 1,500 instances (κ=0.70–0.76).
*   Gemini-2.5-Flash with Few-Shot Chain-of-Thought prompting significantly outperforms supervised baselines (e.g., SVM) and other LLM configurations, achieving macro-F1 scores of 0.821 (argument mining) and 0.783 (stance detection) on the test set.
*   Large-scale analysis reveals that the proportion of neutral stances increases with conversation depth, and argumentative posts are associated with higher toxicity and sentiment, though engagement predictors show weaker effects.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Large language models (LLMs) can reliably annotate argument mining and stance detection tasks on Truth Social, a domain-shifted alt-tech platform, despite poor generalization of supervised models trained on mainstream platforms.
*   **Mechanism:** LLMs leverage broad pre-training to adapt in-context to Truth Social's distinct linguistic patterns without task-specific fine-tuning. Their ability to interpret local conversational context is crucial.
*   **Core assumption:** The rhetorical patterns on Truth Social, while unique, are comprehensible to a general-purpose LLM given an appropriate prompt; the platform's ideological homogeneity does not create an entirely novel language.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The human-annotated benchmark (κ=0.70–0.76) provides a reliable ground truth. Table 2 shows LLMs (Gemini, DeepSeek) outperforming the SVM baseline. Abstract states models trained on prior datasets may not transfer reliably to this domain.
*   **Break condition:** If the domain shift were more extreme (e.g., a completely novel argot) or tasks required highly specialized niche knowledge absent from pre-training, the in-context learning advantage would diminish, and performance would converge toward or fall below a supervised model trained on sufficient in-domain data.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Claim-based stance detection in nested conversations can be accurately approximated by recursively inferring the stance of each comment relative to its immediate parent and propagating the relation along the reply tree to compute stance toward the root post.
*   **Mechanism:** Stance is framed as a sentence-pair classification (comment vs. parent comment/post). For a comment `C_n`, its label (FOR/AGAINST/NEUTRAL) is assigned with respect to its direct parent `P`. The inferred stance of `C_n` to the root `R` is the composition of the chain `(C_n vs P) + (P vs its parent) + ...`, provided no intermediate link is NEUTRAL. A NEUTRAL link breaks the chain, making the stance to `R` undefined.
*   **Core assumption:** A comment's primary stance-taking is directed at its immediate conversational context (the parent). Relational stance toward any ancestor can be derived transitively, and a NEUTRAL parent genuinely indicates an absence of a clear stance relation that invalidates transitive inference.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "stance can be framed as a sentence-pair classification problem... relative to the claim... by another user–most commonly, the post to which it directly responds." Section defines stance as relative to the immediate parent and describes the propagation method with NEUTRAL truncation. The LLM labels are explicitly for "stance to parent," enabling this analysis.
*   **Break condition:** This fails if comments frequently express multi-target stances or if replies are not primarily directed at the immediate parent but at another branch or the root. Sarcasm, ambiguity, or topic drift where a reply addresses a different claim than its parent's would break the transitive assumption, causing propagated labels to be inaccurate.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** The full, tree-structured reply data is essential for accurately characterizing conversational dynamics like stance evolution and polarization, as stance expressions change significantly with reply depth.
*   **Mechanism:** Preserving `in_reply_to_id` relationships constructs a Directed Acyclic Graph (tree) for each conversation. This structure allows analysis of how the proportion of FOR/AGAINST/NEUTRAL stances shifts as a function of reply depth and how individual user stances evolve turn-by-turn.
*   **Core assumption:** Reply depth is a meaningful proxy for conversational distance and time, and the position in the tree influences rhetorical strategy and stance expression. Deeper replies represent a different conversational context (e.g., meta-discussion) compared to direct replies to the root.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "preserving full reply-tree structure" and "enabling analysis of stance and argumentation patterns across depth." The "Conversations on Truth Social" and "Descriptive Overview" sections analyze stance by depth. The filtering excludes descendant branches rooted at any NEUTRAL comment from OP-level analyses.
*   **Break condition:** If conversational structure were fundamentally flat (all replies to root) or if replying to a nested comment did not constitute a meaningfully different act than replying to the root, then depth would be a meaningless dimension. The observed patterns (e.g., increasing NEUTRAL with depth) might then be spurious or attributable to other factors.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Claim-Based (Relational) Stance Detection**
    *   **Why needed here:** Stance is not an intrinsic property of a text but a relationship between a text (comment) and a specific claim (from its parent). Misunderstanding this leads to labeling a comment's general opinion rather than its response to the parent.
    *   **Quick check question:** Given the comment "That's a great point!" and its parent comment "We should ban all immigrants," is the stance FOR, AGAINST, or NEUTRAL? Answer: NEUTRAL (agrees with the act of commenting, not necessarily the claim).
*   **Concept: Conversation Tree Structure & Propagation**
    *   **Why needed here:** The analysis hinges on navigating parent-child links to compute stance to the root. Understanding tree vs. flat reply structure is critical for interpreting depth-based analyses and the NEUTRAL truncation rule.
    *   **Quick check question:** In a thread where Post(R) has Comment(A) [FOR], which has Reply(B) [NEUTRAL], which has Reply(C) [AGAINST], what is Comment(C)'s stance toward Post(R)? Answer: Undefined/No label (the NEUTRAL link from B breaks the chain).
*   **Concept: In-Context Learning (Few-Shot & Chain-of-Thought) for LLM Annotation**
    *   **Why needed here:** The large-scale labeling pipeline depends on selecting the right LLM and prompt strategy (FSCoT for Gemini) based on a small human benchmark; this is a core methodological innovation.
    *   **Quick check question:** Why might Chain-of-Thought prompting improve performance over a simple Few-Shot prompt for this task? Answer: CoT forces the model to articulate reasoning steps (e.g., "The comment repeats the claim → FOR"), improving consistency and handling of complex conversational context.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Data Collection -> Preprocessing & Filtering -> Human Benchmark Creation -> LLM Evaluation & Selection -> LLM Bulk Annotation -> Feature Extraction & Analysis -> Stance Propagation Engine -> Descriptive Analytics
*   **Critical path:** The pipeline's backbone is LLM Bulk Annotation -> Stance Propagation Engine. Bulk LLM labels are meaningless for OP-level analysis without the propagation logic. The human benchmark is the prerequisite for trusting the bulk annotation. Missing any of these breaks the core value proposition.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **LLM vs. Human:** Used LLMs for scale vs. full human labeling (cost/time). Tradeoff: LLM noise vs. human consistency. Mitigated by high human κ for benchmark, using best LLM/prompt on that benchmark, and accepting that full corpus is LLM-only.
    *   **Propagation Truncation:** Chose to cut branches at NEUTRAL ancestors. Tradeoff: conservative (reduces false OP-level stance) vs. losing data from substantial NEUTRAL branches. Alternative: infer weak propagation or assign NO_LABEL without dropping branch.
    *   **Topic Modeling via Hashtags:** Used hashtags as topic proxy. Tradeoff: Captures explicit topical signaling vs. missing implicit topic in text-only posts. Could fail for posts without hashtags or with misleading tags.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **LLM prompt drift:** If the best FSCoT prompt on 1,500 examples fails on a different, unseen sub-topic, performance will drop silently in bulk labels. Monitor: spot-check a stratified sample across topics.
    *   **Tree traversal bug:** Incorrect handling of `in_reply_to_id` will create malformed trees, breaking depth calculation and stance propagation. Validate: sanity-check tree depth distributions against known platform norms.
    *   **NEUTRAL over-trigger:** If the LLM labels too many parent comments as NEUTRAL, propagation will truncate excessively, biasing OP-level stance analysis toward shallow threads. Monitor: distribution of NEUTRAL labels by depth (should increase, but not collapse).
    *   **Selection bias leakage:** Filtering for ≥3 replies excludes low-engagement but potentially high-quality argument threads. Any finding about "average" stance evolution may not generalize to low-engagement discourse.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Benchmark Validation Replication:** Re-run the LLM evaluation on a fresh, held-out human-annotated sample (n=200) to confirm `Gemini-FSCoT` still significantly outperforms others. Checks for benchmark overfitting.
    2.  **Propagation Sanity Check:** Manually inspect 50 randomly selected conversation trees. For each, trace the LLM's `stance_to_parent` labels from a deep leaf to the root. Verify that the computed `stance_to_root` matches what a human would infer given the full thread. Quantify error rate at propagation breaks (NEUTRAL ancestors).
    3.  **Depth-Stance Hypothesis Test:** Replicate the pattern in Table 5 (increase in NEUTRAL with depth) on the LLM-labeled corpus. Perform a chi-square test for independence between depth category and stance label. Significant association confirms the core descriptive finding holds at scale. If absent, investigate LLM label quality at deeper levels.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How does the dataset's filtering criterion of excluding posts with fewer than three comments bias the observed stance and argumentation dynamics, and what patterns exist in low-engagement threads that are currently unobserved?
*   **Open Question 2:** To what extent do the LLM-generated stance labels accurately capture nuanced conversational phenomena like sarcasm, implicit agreement, or multi-target disagreement, and how does potential LLM error propagate through the stance-traversal algorithm?
*   **Open Question 3:** How generalizable are the findings on topic-level stance polarization and user-level stance stability to other alt-tech platforms (e.g., Gab, Parler) or even different political moments on Truth Social?
*   **Open Question 4:** What is the causal relationship between argumentative content features (toxicity, sentiment, length) and engagement metrics (likes, replies), and how does this relationship differ for users with varying follower counts?
*   **Open Question 5:** Can the "composition assumption" for stance propagation—that a comment's stance to the root post is the product of its stances along the parent chain—be formally validated, and what alternative models better predict overall thread stance alignment?

## Limitations
*   Small human benchmark (1,500 instances) may not represent full platform diversity; LLM generalization to the full corpus is uncertain.
*   Stance propagation relies on NEUTRAL truncation; inaccurate parent labels or complex conversational moves can bias root stance inference.
*   Topic modeling uses only hashtags, ignoring implicit topics in text-only posts and potentially skewing topic coverage.
*   Undisclosed few-shot examples, comment sampling methodology, and API access details hinder exact replication.

## Confidence
*   **LLM annotation reliability:** Medium (limited human benchmark; missing prompt specifics; performance on full corpus not human-verified).
*   **Stance propagation validity:** Low (transitivity assumption untested; fails on sarcasm/multi-target; truncation discards data).
*   **Depth-based descriptive findings:** Medium (patterns observed; dependent on propagation quality and label accuracy).
