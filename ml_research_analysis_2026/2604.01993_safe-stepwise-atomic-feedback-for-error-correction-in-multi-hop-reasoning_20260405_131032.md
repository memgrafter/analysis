# SAFE: Stepwise Atomic Feedback for Error correction in Multi-hop Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01993
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01993
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SAFE improves multi-hop QA accuracy by 8.4 pp and exact match by 11.2 pp across three benchmarks, while guaranteeing all reasoning trajectories are grounded in knowledge graphs.

## Executive Summary
Multi-hop question answering often exhibits spurious correctness, where models produce correct answers without valid reasoning. SAFE addresses this by enforcing strictly verifiable reasoning through knowledge graph grounding. It introduces a two-phase framework: at training time, a KG-grounded pipeline filters noisy benchmark instances (up to 14% unanswerable); at inference time, a trained feedback model dynamically detects and corrects ungrounded atomic steps. Experiments on 2WikiMultiHopQA, HotpotQA, and MuSiQue show substantial gains over baselines, including self-feedback approaches, and ensure all trajectories are interpretable and verifiable.

## Method Summary
SAFE comprises an offline verification and training phase, followed by an online inference phase. Offline, benchmarks are filtered by extracting atomic knowledge graph triples from gold passages, performing entity resolution, and discovering a continuous logical path from question entities to the answer; instances failing this validation are discarded. A training dataset for the feedback model is constructed by generating ideal reasoning trajectories, synthetically injecting errors covering an atomic taxonomy (Procedural, Attribution, Logical, Final Answer), and augmenting with hard cases derived from analyzing initial model failures. This dataset fine-tunes a Qwen3-8B model via QLoRA. Online, a generator model emits one atomic reasoning step at a time; the feedback model evaluates each step against the question, passages, and prior steps, returning an error type, diagnosis, and guidance. On error, the generator revises the step (up to 3 retries). This loop continues until a final answer is produced, with a maximum of 10 steps and KV caching to reduce overhead.

## Key Results
- Average accuracy improvement of 8.4 percentage points over strong baselines (including Self-Feedback).
- Exact match improvement of 11.2 percentage points.
- All reasoning trajectories are guaranteed to be knowledge graph grounded, eliminating spurious correctness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Filtering Benchmark Noise via KG-Grounded Pipeline
- **Claim:** Removing instances without a valid, continuous KG reasoning path eliminates questions that are unanswerable given the provided evidence, reducing spurious learning.
- **Mechanism:** The pipeline extracts atomic triples from gold passages, performs entity resolution, and explicitly discovers a logical path from question entities to the answer. Instances failing this validation are discarded.
- **Core assumption:** Standard benchmarks contain non-trivial noise where the gold passages cannot support a valid multi-hop derivation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract identifies up to 14% unanswerable; Table 2 reports noise ratios of 3%–14%; related work (Ishii et al., 2024) notes spurious correctness.
- **Break condition:** If the passages lack explicit, connectable facts for required hops (e.g., missing date or location), the instance is flagged.

### Mechanism 2: External Feedback Model for Dynamic Correction
- **Claim:** A dedicated feedback model, trained on verified data, detects and corrects ungrounded steps during inference, preventing error propagation.
- **Mechanism:** At each generation step, the feedback model receives the question, passages, and reasoning prefix. It classifies the latest step using the atomic error taxonomy and outputs a diagnosis and corrective guidance, forcing the generator to revise.
- **Core assumption:** Self-feedback fails because LLMs hallucinate corrections or miss subtle grounding violations; an externally trained model provides reliable guardrails.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes dynamic detection and significant outperformance; Section 4.2 shows +8.4 pp over Self-Feedback; Figure 2 confirms minimal overhead vs. cost explosion.
- **Break condition:** If the feedback model misclassifies errors (e.g., misses contradiction), guidance may be irrelevant or harmful.

### Mechanism 3: Atomic, KG-Grounded Error Taxonomy
- **Claim:** A strictly atomic taxonomy (Procedural, Attribution, Logical, Final Answer) provides fine-grained supervision, making reasoning interpretable and verifiable.
- **Mechanism:** Each step must correspond to exactly one KG triple anchored in passages. Errors are checked hierarchically: procedural integrity before semantic correctness, then logical soundness.
- **Core assumption:** Evaluating entire CoT traces as correct/incorrect is insufficient; breakdowns must be localized for effective feedback.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.1 defines categories and shows procedural errors constitute 64.1% of failures; Figure 4 shows 59% of errors occur in first three steps; Appendices provide qualitative catches missed by self-feedback.
- **Break condition:** If the taxonomy misses a failure mode (e.g., non-KG reasoning error) or stops at first category when multiple apply, nuanced errors may be misclassified.

## Foundational Learning
### Knowledge Graph (KG) Traversal as Reasoning
- **Why needed here:** The framework formalizes multi-hop reasoning as a sequence of atomic KG triple operations; understanding nodes, edges, and path-finding is essential to grasp "grounded".
- **Quick check:** Given passages about "A directed B" and "B starred in C," what is the minimal KG path to answer "Who directed C?"?

### Hierarchical Error Taxonomy (Procedural → Attribution → Logical → Final)
- **Why needed here:** The feedback model's evaluation and training rely on this ordering; a procedural flaw (e.g., off-topic) invalidates the step regardless of factual accuracy.
- **Quick check:** If a step extracts a fact from Passage 2 that contradicts Passage 1, under which category does it fail first—Contradictory (Attribution) or Logical Fallacy?

### Synthetic Error Injection vs. Real Failure Distribution
- **Why needed here:** Training data combines synthetic perturbations (for coverage) with model-specific failure analysis (for distribution alignment); the latter is crucial for hard datasets like MuSiQue.
- **Quick check:** Why might training only on synthetically injected errors lead to a feedback model that fails on real model outputs?

## Architecture Onboarding
Key components: KG-grounded Benchmark Verifier -> Training Data Synthesizer -> Feedback Model -> Reasoning Generator.

**Critical path:**  
1. Offline: Run verification pipeline on benchmark → construct/train feedback model.  
2. Online Inference: Generator proposes step s_t → feedback model evaluates (q, P, s1:t) → if error, generator revises step (up to N=3 times) → proceed to next step until final answer (max K=10 steps).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Verification rigor vs. coverage: Strict KG grounding may filter valid complex queries (false positives).  
- External feedback cost: Adds a model call per step; mitigated by KV caching (~89% token saving) but still slower.  
- Generator compliance: Assumes generator can follow corrective guidance; observed failures when it ignores feedback.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Self-feedback collapse: Accuracy drops with cost explosion; models loop on flawed paths.  
- Feedback misdiagnosis: Missing a premature attribution or hallucinating external knowledge.  
- Generator insubordination: Correct guidance not incorporated in next step.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Reproduce noise filtering: Apply KG pipeline to a benchmark split (e.g., HotpotQA val); verify noise ratio (~3%) matches Table 2; check false positive rate via manual review of 50 samples (target <2%).  
2. Train feedback model ablation: Train on "Synthetic Only" vs. "Refined" data (with 848 hard cases) on MuSiQue; expect ~+3.3 pp gain from refined.  
3. Cost-accuracy profiling: Compare SAFE (N=3, K=10) vs. Self-Feedback; confirm SAFE uses <15 LLM calls per question and plateaus, while Self-Feedback cost explodes without gain.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can SAFE be extended to open-domain multi-hop QA requiring dynamic iterative retrieval when evidence is missing? (Limitations)
- To what extent does the feedback model’s reliability depend on KG quality, and how can verification be robust to KG noise or missing relations? (Inferred from noise filtering and false positive rate)
- What architectural or training modifications improve generator compliance with step-level feedback, especially for smaller or less-aligned models? (Inferred from generator insubordination)
- Can the targeted refinement process (manually analyzing failures to generate hard cases) be automated or scaled to new domains without prohibitive human effort? (Inferred from iterative refinement gains)

## Limitations
- Teacher model (gpt‑oss‑120b) is unavailable; substitutions may degrade pipeline quality.
- Iterative refinement details (848 hard cases) are underspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- No public filtered benchmarks; noise ratios and false positives may differ across implementations.

## Confidence
- **Noise filtering (14% unanswerable):** High – directly supported by abstract and Table 2, but depends on KG pipeline reproducibility.
- **Feedback gains (+8.4 pp):** Medium-high – strong experimental results but self-feedback collapse indicates fragility; relies on feedback model and generator compliance.
- **Atomic taxonomy utility:** Medium – catches 64.1% procedural errors but may miss multi-category or non-KG failures.
