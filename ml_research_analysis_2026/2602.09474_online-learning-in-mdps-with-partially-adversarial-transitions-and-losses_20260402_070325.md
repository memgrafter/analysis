# Online Learning in MDPs with Partially Adversarial Transitions and Losses
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09474
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09474
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces conditioned occupancy measures (COMs) to achieve regret that scales exponentially in the number of adversarial steps Λ rather than the horizon H, with explicit bounds for arbitrary and consecutive adversarial steps.

## Executive Summary
We consider reinforcement learning in finite-horizon MDPs where transitions are stochastic except at a fixed subset of Λ steps per episode, which may be adversarial. The key innovation is the introduction of conditioned occupancy measures (COMs) that remain invariant across episodes despite adversarial transitions. This decomposition allows the learner to optimize a policy using online convex optimization over an episode-invariant polytope. Two algorithms are presented: one for arbitrary adversarial steps with regret Õ(H S^Λ √(K S A^{Λ+1})), and an improved version for consecutive adversarial steps achieving Õ(H √(K S^3 A^{Λ+1})). An EXP3-based reduction removes the need to know which steps are adversarial, incurring an additive K^{2/3} term. For the fully adversarial case (Λ=H−1), almost matching upper and lower bounds are provided, clarifying the role of feedback structure. The results show that when only Λ steps are adversarial, regret scales exponentially in Λ rather than H, fundamentally separating the difficulty caused by adversarial transitions.

## Method Summary
The core method revolves around conditioned occupancy measures (COMs), which factorize the occupancy measure into an episode-invariant component µ and an episode-varying component ϱ_k capturing adversarial transitions. The algorithm maintains a confidence set for the unknown stochastic transitions and constructs a polytope Δ_k of all COMs compatible with that confidence set. At each episode, it computes an optimistic COM u_k by maximizing over Δ_{k-1}, then plays a policy derived from the current COM estimate (via OMD). Losses are estimated by dividing observed losses by u_k+γ (with implicit exploration γ), ensuring unbiasedness. For consecutive adversarial steps, a sub-policy representation reduces the combinatorial explosion in conditions, improving state dependence. When the exact set of adversarial steps is unknown, an outer EXP3 algorithm runs a COM-OMD instance for each candidate set and importance-weights the feedback.

## Key Results
- Regret for arbitrary adversarial steps: Õ(H S^Λ √(K S A^{Λ+1})).
- Regret for consecutive adversarial steps (sub-policy COM): Õ(H √(K S^3 A^{Λ+1})).
- EXP3 reduction for unknown adversarial steps adds an additive K^{2/3} term to the known-Λ regret.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Claim: Conditioned occupancy measures (COMs) decompose the time-varying true occupancy measure into an episode-invariant component (µ) and an episode-varying component (ϱ_k), enabling stable optimization despite adversarial transitions.
Mechanism: For a condition c ∈ C_h capturing outcomes of adversarial steps < h, µ_h(s,a,c) represents the probability of (s_h,a_h,c) under the stochastic dynamics and the policy, independent of the adversarial transition probabilities. The true occupancy q_k^h(s,a) = Σ_{c} µ_h(s,a,c) ϱ_k(c), where ϱ_k(c) is the product of the episode-specific adversarial transition probabilities. The algorithm optimizes over the COM polytope (episode-independent) while using ℓ̂_k as an estimator for ϱ_k(c) ℓ_k(s,a).
Core assumption: The set of adversarial steps Λ is fixed and known; stochastic steps follow a stationary unknown transition p^{stat}. The polytope Δ_k defined by confidence intervals around empirical stochastic transitions contains all true COMs.
Break condition: The decomposition fails if Λ is unknown or if ϱ_k(c) cannot be estimated accurately.

### Mechanism 2
Claim: For consecutive adversarial steps, representing conditions by sub-policies (rather than per-step actions) reduces exponential S^Λ dependence to polynomial in S, because a single sub-policy aggregates feedback across an entire adversarial block.
Mechanism: The first adversarial step h̃_1 selects a deterministic sub-policy σ over the consecutive block {h̃_1, ..., h̃_2-1}. Subsequent steps h ≥ h̃_2 use conditions (s, σ, s') linking s_{h̃_1}, s_{h̃_2} and σ. The loss estimator normalizes by the total optimistic mass Σ_{σ'} u_k, where Σ_k(ã) is the set of sub-policies inducing the observed action sequence ã. This shared feedback across conditions bounds the second moment by KH S^2 A^{Λ+1} instead of KH (SA)^{Λ+1}.
Core assumption: Adversarial steps form a contiguous block; sub-policies are deterministic and confined to that block; stochastic steps are stationary.
Break condition: The block structure is violated; sub-policy enumeration becomes combinatorially expensive.

### Mechanism 3
Claim: An EXP3-based reduction eliminates the need to know Λ at the cost of an additive K^{2/3} regret term, by running one COM-OMD instance per possible Λ-subset and importance-weighting feedback.
Mechanism: The outer algorithm maintains a distribution ν_k over the (H choose Λ) inner algorithms. In episode k, it samples an inner algorithm I_k ~ ν_k and executes its policy. The trajectory/loss feedback is reweighted by 1/ν_k(I_k) to construct unbiased loss estimates for all inner algorithms. An exploration probability ξ ensures bounded second moments, yielding an additive √(K/ξ) term. Setting ξ = K^{-1/3} balances exploration cost against EXP3's √(H^Λ K) switching cost.
Core assumption: Λ is known (though positions are not); inner algorithms' COM polytopes remain valid.
Break condition: Λ itself is unknown; number of candidate subsets exponential in H; K^{2/3} may dominate.

## Foundational Learning
### Occupancy measures
Why needed: Standard occupancy measures q^π(s,a) are inconsistent across episodes under adversarial transitions, breaking policy-optimization methods. COMs generalize occupancy measures by conditioning on fixed adversary outcomes.
Quick check: If a Markov policy π is fixed but transitions at a specific step h are adversarial, how does the standard occupancy measure q_h^π(s,a) vary across episodes?

### Confidence sets for stochastic transitions
Why needed: The COM polytope relies on high-probability confidence intervals P_k containing the true stochastic transition p^{stat}. Violation of the confidence event invalidates containment.
Quick check: What is the form of the confidence radius ε_k^h(s'|s,a) used (e.g., from Jin et al. [2020]) and how does it scale with visit counts N_k(s,a)?

### Online convex optimization (OMD) over a policy-dependent polytope
Why needed: Algorithm 2/3 optimize a COM vector μ in a polytope defined by the confidence set. The regret analysis uses OMD properties (e.g., Reg ≤ (1/η) KL(μ*‖μ̂_1) + (η/2) Σ μ̂ ℓ̂^2).
Quick check: In the OMD analysis for COM-OMD, why does the L1 norm of the COM polytope become S^Λ (action-based) or S (sub-policy)?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Confidence set estimator -> COM polytope builder -> Optimistic COM calculator -> Loss estimator -> OMD updater -> Policy extractor.

Critical path: The confidence set estimated from stochastic transitions defines the COM polytope; from this we compute the optimistic COM u_k; we then form an unbiased loss estimator using u_k; an OMD update projects onto the updated polytope; finally, the policy is extracted by normalizing the COM over actions.

Design tradeoffs:
- Action-based COM: Simple but exponential S^Λ dependence in L1 norm and moment bounds → worse sample complexity.
- Sub-policy COM: Better sample complexity (S^3 dependence) but requires enumerating A^Λ sub-policies, computationally heavy if A^Λ large.
- Unknown Λ reduction: Adds K^{2/3} overhead; runs (H choose Λ) inner algorithms; exploration probability ξ must be tuned.

Failure signatures:
- Confidence set miss: p^{stat} ∉ P_k → COM polytope does not contain true μ* → loss estimator bias.
- Polytope constraint violation: u_k unbounded → division by near-zero in ℓ̂_k → high variance.
- Non-consecutive steps with sub-policy COM: Conditions become incompatible → linear regret.
- Importance weight variance in unknown-step reduction: If ν_k(i) becomes too small, inner algorithm sees biased data.
- Ignoring computational infeasibility: Polytope size exponential in Λ; LP solvers may be too slow.

First 3 experiments:
1. Synthetic MDP with sparse adversarial steps: Set Λ=2 random positions. Compare COM-OMD (action-based) vs. standard occupancy-measure baseline (Zimin & Neu 2013) under stochastic losses. Measure regret vs. K.
2. Consecutive block vs. arbitrary placement: For Λ=3, run both COM variants on identical MDPs. Verify sub-policy COM achieves lower S-dependence (e.g., S^3 vs. S^Λ).
3. Unknown adversarial steps: Test EXP3 reduction with ξ=K^{-1/3}. Vary Λ and check additive K^{2/3} term dominates only when K is large relative to S,A,H^Λ. Validate that knowing Λ recovers the inner algorithm's bound.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
Can the K^{2/3} regret additive term from the EXP3 reduction for unknown adversarial steps be improved to a more natural dependence, such as √K or polylog(K)? Basis: The reduction's K^{2/3} term arises from generic EXP3 and explicit exploration; it is unlikely to be optimal. Evidence for resolution: A new algorithm that adaptively identifies adversarial steps or a lower bound proving K^{2/3} is necessary.

### Open Question 2
Is the exponential dependence on S in the first algorithm's regret (S^Λ) removable for the non-consecutive adversarial steps model, or is it inherent even for arbitrary step patterns? Basis: The action-based COM polytope has a combinatorial explosion of conditions. It is unknown whether a more refined representation or different technique could avoid this exponent. Evidence: A lower bound proving Ω(S^Λ) or a new algorithm achieving polynomial dependence on S.

### Open Question 3
Can a computationally efficient algorithm be designed with regret exponential only in Λ (like the statistical optimum) rather than in H, even under full information? Basis: Current algorithms optimizing directly over occupancy measures are inefficient due to a large policy space. It is unknown if the COM structure can be exploited computationally. Evidence: An efficient algorithm (polynomial in S, H, K) with regret scaling exponentially in Λ but not H, or a lower bound showing such a runtime is impossible without sacrificing regret optimality.

### Open Question 4
How do the results extend to a setting where the number of adversarial steps Λ is unknown or varies across episodes, rather than being fixed and known? Basis: The EXP3 reduction still assumes a fixed Λ and incurs K^{2/3}. It is unclear if a more adaptive method could achieve the same bounds as when Λ is known. Evidence: An algorithm that automatically adapts to Λ without a hyperparameter and achieves the same Õ bounds, or a lower bound showing a fundamental penalty.

### Open Question 5
For the fully adversarial setting (Λ=H−1), what is the precise relationship between feedback structures (bandit vs. full info on losses and transitions) and the achievable regret exponents in H? Can the slight gaps between upper and lower bounds be closed? Basis: The paper tightens previous bounds but still leaves small gaps (e.g., constants/log factors). Evidence: Matching upper and lower bounds up to constant factors for all feedback combinations.

## Limitations
- The exact computation of the optimistic COM u_k (maximum over the polytope) is not detailed; solving the required LP per step may be intractable for larger Λ.
- The subpolicy-based COM requires explicit enumeration of all deterministic subpolicies on the adversarial block, which grows as A^Λ; the paper does not discuss efficient implementation or tie-breaking.
- The confidence radius formula contains unspecified constants; their precise values are needed to preserve high-probability guarantees.
- The Exp3 reduction for unknown Λ adds an additive K^{2/3} term; whether this is optimal or improvable remains open.
- No empirical evaluation beyond tiny synthetic MDPs; scalability to realistic state/action spaces is unproven.

## Confidence
- COM decomposition and invariance: High.
- Regret bounds for Algorithms 2 & 3: Medium.
- Exp3 reduction optimality: Low.

## Next Checks
1. Max-COM verification: For tiny MDPs (S≤3, Λ≤2), implement the COM polytope and solve the max problem via brute-force LP; confirm u_k equals the theoretical supremum.
2. Subpolicy loss estimator sanity: For consecutive adversarial blocks, explicitly build the set of deterministic subpolicies, run the loss estimator, and check that normalization yields an unbiased estimate across all consistent subpolicies.
3. Exp3 reduction scaling: Vary the exploration probability ξ and measure the additive regret term; verify the K^{2/3} dependence and compare against a brute-force meta-learner that knows the adversarial step positions.
