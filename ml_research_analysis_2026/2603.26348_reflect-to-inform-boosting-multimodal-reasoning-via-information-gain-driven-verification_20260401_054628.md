# Reflect to Inform: Boosting Multimodal Reasoning via Information-Gain-Driven Verification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.26348
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26348
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: VRE improves multimodal reasoning by reactivating implicit visual re-examination, achieving up to +7.4% on V*-Bench and consistent gains across math, chart, and OCR benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Visual Re-Examination (VRE), a self-iterative framework that combats visual drift in multimodal LLMs by prompting the model to re-attend to the original image during long reasoning chains, without extra visual inputs or architecture changes. VRE uses homologous reconstruction—generating reflections conditioned on the model's own prior reasoning—and an information-gain filter to ensure reflections provide novel visual evidence. Training combines cold-start SFT, RL with verifiable rewards, and iterative self-distillation. Experiments on Qwen2.5-VL-7B show significant improvements on math and chart reasoning benchmarks, with strong reductions in hallucinations.

## Method Summary
VRE trains a base multimodal LLM through three stages. First, cold-start SFT uses data from the base model's rollouts, filtered by pass rate and information gain, to teach a `<reflection>` format. Reflections are synthesized by re-feeding the initial reasoning and image into the same model (homologous reconstruction). Second, RL with verifiable rewards (GRPO) optimizes a composite reward (format, accuracy, reflection quality) to encourage informative visual re-examination. Third, post-RL self-distillation fine-tunes the RL model on its own high-quality solutions to previously hard queries, stabilizing performance and preventing forgetting.

- MathVista: +3.0% (71.2)
- MathVerse: +3.9% (53.1)

- OCRBench_v2: English +6.3 to 62.6, Chinese +7.5 to 64.7

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Latent capability for late-stage visual verification
MLLMs possess an underutilized ability to re-attend to visual tokens during long reasoning chains. VRE standardizes a `<reflection>` template and trains the model to deliberately invoke this process. Attention analysis shows that while visual attention decays, the capacity for late-stage refocusing exists. **Evidence:** The paper identifies this as "Implicit Visual Re-Examination" (Sec 3). **Break condition:** If the visual encoder lacks fine-grained representation, reactivation won't yield grounded information.

### Mechanism 2: Homologous reconstruction ensures perception-reasoning alignment
Reflections are generated by conditioning on the model's own prior reasoning using the identical visual backbone. This prevents distribution shift and maintains strict alignment between perception and reasoning. **Evidence:** KL divergence analysis shows self-generated SFT data has lower divergence from the base policy than external teacher data (Fig 12). **Break condition:** If the initial reasoning is catastrophically misaligned, conditioning may reinforce errors.

### Mechanism 3: Information-gain filter combats visual drift
Reflections are curated and rewarded based on introducing novel visual evidence. The LLM scorer judges whether a reflection provides meaningful re-examination, penalizing empty verbosity. **Evidence:** VRE reflections have higher conditional entropy (mean 1.08 vs. 0.66) than baselines, indicating less predictable, more informative content (Sec B.3). **Break condition:** If the LLM scorer cannot reliably distinguish genuine gains from hallucinations, the reward signal becomes noisy.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Base MLLM → Cold-Start SFT (using D_cold) → Cold-Start Model → RLVR (GRPO + LLM Judge) → RL Model → Post-RL SFT (using D_new) → Final Policy

**Critical path:**  
Data Curation (Rollouts → Filtering) → Cold-Start SFT → RLVR (Rejection Sampling + GRPO) → Post-RL Self-Distillation → Final Policy

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Self-evolution vs. Teacher Distillation: Using the same model avoids distribution shift but limits gains to base capabilities.
- Reward Complexity: Three reward components improve stability but require two LLM judges, increasing cost.
- Iterative Depth: >150 RL steps cause collapse; post-RL SFT stabilizes but yields smaller absolute gains.

**Failure signatures:**
- Reward Hacking: Long, syntactic `<reflection>` blocks with no new visual information (detect via length increase, low conditional entropy).
- Perceptual Mismatch: Deviating from homologous reconstruction increases KL divergence and reduces gains.
- Catastrophic Forgetting: Extended RL without post-SFT degrades stable-sample accuracy; monitor KL divergence.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Attention Visualization:** Compare base vs. VRE on long-chain problems; compute per-step visual attention entropy. Expect decay in base and resurgence in VRE during `<reflection>`.
2. **Reflection Information Gain Validation:** Compute conditional entropy of reflection tokens given prior reasoning on held-out set; VRE should show significantly higher entropy than baselines.
3. **Boundary Breakthrough Analysis:** Track pass rate distribution (P_pass) on RL training queries before/after VRE; expect decrease in "All Wrong" and increase in "All Correct".

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Computational Efficiency:** Is VRE's claimed efficiency over explicit methods empirically validated, or are hidden costs (longer outputs) overlooked?
2. **Reflection Attribute Predictiveness:** Which fine-grained features of reflection traces (length, lexical diversity, spatial grounding) best predict accuracy gains?
3. **Necessity of Homologous Reconstruction:** Could alternative self-reconstruction strategies (e.g., re-encoding original image tokens) achieve similar benefits?
4. **Scaling to Larger Models:** Do VRE's gains persist for larger base models (e.g., 32B) or other strong architectures?
5. **Correlation with Reasoning Length:** Is the performance improvement specifically tied to longer reasoning chains, or does VRE help uniformly?

## Limitations
- Data curation opacity: Exact filtering criteria for RL datasets and "hard prompt" selection are unspecified, risking distribution shift.
- Judge reliability: The LLM scorer's consistency in detecting genuine information gain is unvalidated; noisy rewards could undermine RL.
- Generalization ceiling: Gains rely on the base model's latent visual capability; if its encoder lacks fine-grained representation, VRE may fail on complex diagrams.
