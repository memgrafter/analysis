# K-Gen: A Multimodal Language-Conditioned Approach for Interpretable Keypoint-Guided Trajectory Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.04868
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04868
- **Reference count**: 28
- **Primary result**: Achieves state-of-the-art trajectory generation with mADE 0.915 on WOMD and 0.591 on nuPlan, and SCR 0.006/0.027, demonstrating superior accuracy, safety, and interpretability.

## Executive Summary
K-Gen introduces a multimodal framework for generating realistic driving trajectories by leveraging rasterized BEV maps and textual scene descriptions through an MLLM. It decomposes trajectory generation into interpretable keypoint prediction and subsequent refinement, enhanced by T-DAPO reinforcement fine-tuning. Evaluations on WOMD and nuPlan benchmarks show significant improvements in accuracy and safety over existing language-guided baselines.

## Method Summary
The method processes scene data into BEV images and serialized text descriptions. An MLLM (InternVL3-8B) generates Chain-of-Thought reasoning and sparse keypoints. These keypoints are linearly interpolated into a coarse trajectory, which is refined by a Transformer-based TrajRefiner module to correct residuals and ensure kinematic consistency. Training proceeds in three stages: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of the MLLM, separate training of TrajRefiner using ground-truth keypoints, and reinforcement fine-tuning with T-DAPO that uses composite rewards (accuracy, CoT length, format) focusing on hard samples.

## Key Results
- Reduces mean average displacement error (mADE) to 0.915 on WOMD and 0.591 on nuPlan.
- Lowers scenario collision rate (SCR) to 0.006 on WOMD and 0.027 on nuPlan.
- Outperforms existing language-guided baselines in accuracy, safety, and interpretability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: BEV + Text Fusion
- **Claim**: Rasterized BEV maps combined with textual scene descriptions enable richer, more flexible scene understanding than vectorized maps for trajectory generation.
- **Mechanism**: A vision encoder processes the BEV image into visual tokens, while a text encoder processes structured scene descriptions. These tokens are fused within the MLLM, allowing the model to leverage both unstructured spatial details (e.g., lane markings, agent placements) and structured semantics (e.g., agent types, velocities) to generate context-aware keypoints and reasoning.
- **Core assumption**: Raw visual representations preserve spatial and contextual nuances that are abstracted away in vectorized maps, leading to more faithful interaction modeling.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and Section II.A note that vectorized encodings abstract away spatial details and contextual semantics essential for modeling complex interactions.
- **Break condition**: If BEV resolution is insufficient or the MLLM's visual grounding is poor, the model fails to extract relevant spatial patterns, undermining the advantage over vectorized inputs.

### Mechanism 2: Decomposition into Keypoints + Refinement
- **Claim**: Decomposing trajectory generation into sparse keypoint prediction followed by residual refinement yields more accurate and stable trajectories than direct full-trajectory generation from an MLLM.
- **Mechanism**: The MLLM generates a small set of semantically meaningful keypoints (e.g., high-curvature points, velocity-change points) guided by CoT reasoning that captures agent intentions. The TrajRefiner module then takes these keypoints, linearly interpolates a coarse trajectory, and predicts a residual correction using a Transformer that ingests historical trajectories and agent states, producing a smooth, kinematically consistent final trajectory.
- **Core assumption**: Separating high-level strategic intent (keypoints) from low-level motion execution (refinement) reduces the complexity of the MLLM's output space and allows a specialized module to enforce physical feasibility.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and Section I explicitly describe the two-step decomposition; Section III.E details TrajRefiner architecture and losses (L_motion, L_KCL, L_FPL) that correct residual errors.
- **Break condition**: If keypoints are too sparse or semantically misaligned (e.g., missing critical maneuvers), TrajRefiner cannot recover the intended path; if keypoints are too dense, the MLLM's generation becomes error-prone and the refinement step may overfit noise.

### Mechanism 3: T-DAPO Reinforcement Fine-Tuning
- **Claim**: Trajectory-aware reinforcement fine-tuning (T-DAPO) with hard example sampling and multi-component rewards enhances keypoint generation quality by aligning the MLLM's outputs with trajectory accuracy, reasoning conciseness, and structural correctness.
- **Mechanism**: T-DAPO selects the top 30% hardest samples (highest mADE/mFDE from post-SFT model) to focus learning on challenging scenarios. It computes a composite reward: accuracy reward (exponential decay on ADE/FDE from TrajRefiner), CoT length reward (penalizing verbosity), and format reward (ensuring correct tags). These signals guide policy updates to produce keypoints that yield better final trajectories when refined.
- **Core assumption**: Standard RL methods like GRPO suffer from gradient oscillation in continuous trajectory spaces; trajectory-centric signals and emphasis on hard samples stabilize training and prevent convergence to simplistic (e.g., straight-line) solutions.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section III.D describes T-DAPO's truncation mechanism to stabilize training on hard samples and defines the composite reward; abstract highlights its trajectory-aware nature.
- **Break condition**: If reward weights (α, β, γ) are poorly tuned, the model may prioritize one aspect (e.g., short CoT) at the expense of others (e.g., trajectory accuracy). The hard sample threshold (30%) must also be calibrated; too high risks overfitting to outliers, too low yields insufficient challenge.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**: Needed to fuse BEV image tokens and text tokens into a unified representation that outputs both reasoning and structured keypoints. Quick check: How do vision and text tokens interact within the MLLM's architecture to produce joint representations?
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning**: Needed to provide interpretable, step-by-step reasoning about scene dynamics and agent intentions, which guides the generation of semantically meaningful keypoints and enhances explainability. Quick check: Why might generating intermediate reasoning improve the quality of sparse keypoint outputs compared to direct keypoint prediction without CoT?
- **Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) Representations**: Needed as the primary visual input, offering a top-down, spatially consistent view of the scene that preserves geometric relationships (e.g., lane topology, agent positions) crucial for trajectory planning. Quick check: What specific spatial information is retained in a BEV map that might be lost in a vectorized representation (e.g., polylines)?
- **Trajectory Evaluation Metrics (mADE, mFDE, SCR)**: Needed to directly feed into the reward function (R_acc) and benchmark performance; mADE/mFDE measure displacement errors, while SCR assesses safety via collision rates. Quick check: How does the Scenario Collision Rate (SCR) differ from point-wise displacement errors in evaluating trajectory quality?
- **Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (e.g., DAPO, GRPO)**: Needed to fine-tune the MLLM with trajectory-aware rewards, moving beyond supervised fine-tuning to improve robustness on hard cases. Quick check: What is the key difference between DAPO and standard PPO/GRPO that makes it more suitable for aligning MLLM outputs with complex reward signals?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Data Preprocessing -> MLLM (InternVL3-8B) -> TrajRefiner -> T-DAPO
- **Critical path**:  
  1. Input scene → BEV image + textual description.  
  2. MLLM generates CoT reasoning and sparse keypoints.  
  3. Linear interpolation fills keypoints into coarse trajectory.  
  4. TrajRefiner refines trajectory using history and states.  
  5. During training, T-DAPO evaluates refined trajectories, computes rewards, and updates MLLM parameters.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - MLLM scale vs. inference speed: Larger models yield better reasoning but increase compute; 8B used as a sweet spot.  
  - Keypoint sparsity vs. refinement burden: Too few keypoints lose maneuver detail; too many increase MLLM generation difficulty. Thresholds (ϵ=0.5m, δv=1.0m/s) balance this.  
  - Reward weighting (α=0.7, β=0.2, γ=0.1): Heavily weighting accuracy prioritizes motion quality but may risk verbose or poorly formatted CoT if β/γ too low.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - MLLM outputs incoherent or irrelevant keypoints despite correct CoT (reasoning-action misalignment).  
  - TrajRefiner produces kinematically invalid trajectories (e.g., sudden jumps, unrealistic curvature) despite L_KCL loss—suggesting residual prediction limitations.  
  - T-DAPO leads to over-short, conservative trajectories due to excessive penalty on collision or over-conciseness in CoT.  
  - Attention heatmaps show diffused focus (no clear safety-critical regions), indicating poor scene grounding.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **SFT warm-up**: Fine-tune MLLM on (BEV, text) → (CoT, keypoints) pairs to establish basic generation capability. Expected: coherent CoT and plausible keypoints but trajectory errors after interpolation.  
  2. **TrajRefiner training**: Train TrajRefiner separately on ground-truth keypoints (from dataset) to predict residuals. Expected: significant reduction in mADE/mFDE and SCR compared to linear interpolation baseline.  
  3. **T-DAPO fine-tuning**: Apply reinforcement tuning to MLLM using trajectory rewards from TrajRefiner on hard samples. Expected: further mADE/SCR reduction, especially in complex scenarios; validate via ablation (Table II row SFT+DAPO+TrajRefiner).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does K-Gen's performance scale with increasing numbers of agents beyond the current limit of 8? The authors restrict to 8 due to MLLM context length limits, but scalability and potential degradation are unknown.
- What is the isolated contribution of using rasterized BEV maps compared to vectorized map representations? No controlled experiment isolates the map input type; the performance gain attributable solely to rasterized inputs remains unclear.
- Are the generated Chain-of-Thought reasoning and sparse keypoints truly interpretable to human experts? Interpretability claims are based on attention visualizations; actual human perception of clarity and intent comprehension is unmeasured.
- How do individual reward components and the hard-example selection rate in T-DAPO affect overall performance? The marginal benefit of each reward term and robustness to the hard-example fraction are unknown, limiting insight into algorithm stability.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed prompt templates for scene serialization and keypoint output formats (e.g., `<num>`, `<point>` tags) risk implementation drift and hinder faithful replication.
- T-DAPO algorithmic modifications (e.g., "decoupled clip", dynamic hard-sample sampling) are underspecified, making independent verification difficult.
- Sensitivity to BEV resolution, reward weighting heuristics, and agent count limits (8) are not stress-tested, leaving robustness unproven.
