# MM-algorithms for traditional and convex NMF with Tweedie and Negative Binomial cost functions and empirical evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09601  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09601  
- **Reference count:** 12  
- **Primary result:** Derives a unified Majorize-Minimisation framework for traditional and convex NMF under Tweedie and Negative Binomial cost functions, showing that correct noise model selection is vital for accurate feature recovery and that convex NMF offers computational efficiency and superior BIC for high-dimensional sparse data.

## Executive Summary
This paper extends Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) beyond Gaussian or Poisson noise assumptions to handle overdispersed data by incorporating Tweedie and Negative Binomial distributions. Using a Majorize-Minimisation (MM) approach, it derives multiplicative update rules for both traditional and convex NMF, including novel updates for convex NMF with Poisson and Negative Binomial costs. An R package (`nmfgenr`) unifies the implementation. Empirical evaluations on liver cancer mutational counts and newsgroup word counts demonstrate that aligning the noise model with the data’s mean-variance relationship significantly improves model fit and feature recovery. Convex NMF is found to be more efficient and robust for sparse high-dimensional data, achieving better Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) with fewer parameters.

## Method Summary
The authors formulate NMF as a maximum likelihood problem under various noise models: Normal, Poisson, Tweedie, and Negative Binomial. For each combination of NMF type (traditional or convex) and distribution, they construct an auxiliary function that majorizes the negative log-likelihood and touches it at the current iterate, yielding multiplicative updates that guarantee non-negativity and monotonic descent. Dispersion parameters (Tweedie’s power *p*, Negative Binomial’s α) are estimated via profile likelihood. Novel convex NMF updates for Poisson and Negative Binomial are introduced, where the factor matrix is constrained to be a convex combination of data columns. All algorithms are implemented in the `nmfgenr` R package. Two case studies validate the approach: in liver cancer data, Negative Binomial and Tweedie models outperform Gaussian/Poisson in residual diagnostics and signature recovery; in text data, convex NMF yields more interpretable topics with lower BIC despite having far fewer parameters.

## Key Results
- Developed a unified MM framework providing convergent multiplicative updates for traditional and convex NMF under Tweedie and Negative Binomial cost functions, with novel convex NB updates.
- Matching the noise model to the data’s empirical mean-variance relationship is critical for accurate feature recovery, as evidenced by improved residual diagnostics and higher cosine similarity to known signatures/topics.
- Convex NMF demonstrates computational efficiency and robustness for high-dimensional sparse data, achieving superior BIC in text analysis while using substantially fewer parameters than traditional NMF.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** The Majorize-Minimisation (MM) framework provides convergent multiplicative update rules for NMF under diverse noise models.  
**Mechanism:** For a cost function *F(θ)*, an auxiliary function *G(θ|θᵗ)* is built that (i) majorizes *F* and (ii) matches *F* at the current iterate *θᵗ*. Minimizing *G* yields a closed-form multiplicative update ensuring *F* does not increase. This is applied to the Tweedie unit deviance and Negative Binomial divergence.  
**Core assumption:** The cost function decomposes into parts where majorization is analytically tractable (e.g., via Jensen’s inequality); non-negativity is preserved if initialization is non-negative.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract states derivation of multiplicative updates for all models via MM.  
- Sections 2.2 and 2.3 detail majorization for Tweedie and NB, including convex cases.  
- Corpus alignment with prior MM-NMF work, though convex NB derivation appears novel.  
**Break condition:** Misidentifying convexity/concavity leads to slow convergence or stagnation; updates assume independent non-negativity constraints—coupling beyond multiplicative form breaks the guarantee.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Matching the NMF noise model to the data’s empirical mean-variance relationship is critical for accurate feature recovery and model fit.  
**Mechanism:** Distributions impose specific mean-variance laws: Poisson (Var=Mean), Tweedie (Var=σ²μᵖ), Negative Binomial (Var=μ+μ²/α). Parameters *p* or α are fitted via profile likelihood. The correct model captures overdispersion (variance > mean) or underdispersion, shaping the reconstructed matrix and extracted features.  
**Core assumption:** The data-generating process approximates one of the specified distributions; residual diagnostics (raw vs. fitted means) reveal mis-specification.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract highlights criticality of correct noise model selection.  
- Section 3.1: Liver cancer residuals show Normal/Poisson fail (heteroscedastic), while Tweedie/NB give random scatter; BIC favors NB/Tweedie.  
- Section 3.2: Text data—Tweedie *p≈1.02* (near-Poisson) fits best; Normal unsuitable due to sparsity; NB also fits well with different mean-variance curve.  
**Break condition:** Zero-inflation or structural zeros beyond NB/Tweedie’s capacity yield poor mean-variance fit and patterned residuals; profile likelihood for *p* or α may be flat, indicating ambiguity.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Convex NMF provides computational efficiency and acts as an implicit regularizer for high-dimensional, sparse data, often achieving comparable or better BIC with fewer parameters.  
**Mechanism:** In convex NMF, features are convex combinations of data columns (*H = VᵀE*), reducing effective degrees of freedom and imposing smoothness/adaptivity to the data manifold. Updates involve *VᵀED* instead of *WH*. For sparse data, this prevents overfitting to noise dimensions.  
**Core assumption:** True latent features lie in (or are well-approximated by) the column space of *V*; sparsity patterns in *V* are informative.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract notes convex NMF’s efficiency, robustness, and superior BIC despite fewer parameters.  
- Section 2.4: Complexity—C-NMF updates are O(MN²K) vs. O(MNK) for traditional, but empirically faster on sparse data.  
- Section 3.2: Newsgroups data—C-NMF models achieve lower BIC with ~6.5k vs. 41.6k parameters; features more interpretable and aligned with document labels.  
- Section 4: Convexity acts as effective regularization in high-dimensional sparse settings.  
**Break condition:** If true features are not convex combinations of data (e.g., orthogonal/exterior), C-NMF is biased and underfits. O(N²) per-iteration cost may make traditional NMF faster per iteration for very large *N* even if not sparse.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
nmfgenr R package (C++ backend via Rcpp) -> functions: `nmf()` for traditional NMF, `cnmf()` for convex NMF -> each takes arguments: `method` ("gaussian","poisson","tweedie","nb"), `rank` (K), optional `p` or `alpha` -> subroutines: `fit_tweedie_p()`, `fit_nb_alpha()` (profile likelihood) -> core kernels: element-wise ops (`^p`, division), matrix mult (`%*%`), element-wise mult (`*`), row/column normalisations.

**Critical path:**  
1. **Preprocessing:** Ensure input `V` is non-negative count matrix (integer for NB/Poisson, real for Gaussian/Tweedie). Handle zeros (NB/Tweedie support zero; Gaussian may need offset).  
2. **Model selection heuristic:** Plot empirical mean-variance of `V`. If Var >> Mean, consider NB or Tweedie (*p* > 1). If Var ≈ Mean, Poisson/Tweedie (*p*≈1). If variance roughly constant, Gaussian.  
3. **Execution:** Call `nmf(V, K, method="nb")` or `cnmf(V, K, method="tweedie")`. Package estimates dispersion if not provided.  
4. **Diagnostics:** Extract `W`,`H` (or `E`,`D`). Compute residuals `R = V - W%*%H` (or `V - t(V %*% E %*% D)`). Plot `R` vs. fitted means; check patterns. Compute BIC provided by package.  
5. **Feature validation:** For signature/topic data, compute cosine similarity between extracted features (`H` or `V%E`) and known references.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Tweedie vs NB:** Tweedie offers continuous *p* but likelihood evaluation for *p*>2 is less standard; NB is discrete overdispersion with single α, easier to fit via Newton-Raphson. NB preferable for integer counts with moderate overdispersion; Tweedie for general mean-variance power laws.  
- **Traditional vs Convex:** Convex has fewer parameters (N*K + N*(K-1) vs. N*K + M*(K-1)), acts as regularizer, often better for high-M sparse data. But updates are O(N²) per iteration due to VᵀE operations; for very large N, traditional may be cheaper per iteration.  
- **Update rule choice:** MM updates are simple but may converge slowly; projected gradient descent is simpler but "shows very slow convergence". Using wrong update (e.g., Poisson for NB data) yields biased factors.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Non-convergence/cycle:** NaNs/Infs from underflow in element-wise ops (e.g., `(V/(WH))^p` for tiny WH). Mitigation: add small ε to denominators or use log-space arithmetic.  
- **Poor residual diagnostics:** Systematic pattern (e.g., funnel) in residual vs. fitted plot indicates wrong noise model; try alternative distribution.  
- **Feature collapse:** All rows of `H` or `V%E` become identical. Could be bad initialization, rank K too high, or cost function not separating sources. Mitigation: multiple random restarts, try lower K.  
- **Convex NMF degenerate features:** If `E` columns become sparse (near one-hot), C-NMF may revert to traditional-like solution. Check column sums of `V%E`; should be near 1 but not sparse.  
- **Parameter estimation failure:** Profile likelihood for *p* or α flat or multimodal. Paper’s method may return boundary (e.g., *p*=1 for Poisson). Consider fixing parameter based on prior knowledge or try method of moments for α.

**First experiments:**  
1. **Reproduce paper's liver cancer analysis:** Use `nmfgenr` on liver counts (260×96). Run all 8 models (T/Po/TW1.5/NB for T and C) with K=5. Replicate Figure 4 residual plots and BIC comparison. Verify NMF/T/NB has lowest BIC and random residuals.  
2. **Synthetic overdispersion test:** Generate NB count matrix *V* (200×500, K=3) with known *W*, *H*, α=20. Run NMF/T/NB, NMF/T/Po, NMF/T/Gauss. Check if NB recovers *W*,*H* with higher cosine similarity (>0.9) than others.  
3. **Sparse data scalability:** Compare runtime and BIC of C-NMF vs. T-NMF on synthetic sparse matrix (M=10k, N=500, sparsity 99%). Vary K. Expect C-NMF to converge faster (fewer iterations) and yield lower BIC for high sparsity, but T-NMF may be faster per iteration for low M. Use `microbenchmark` on updates.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does convex NMF compare to traditional NMF across different applications and cost functions in terms of factor quality, computational efficiency, and model selection? The paper only evaluates two datasets; broader comparison is lacking.  
- What are the identifiability and uniqueness properties of convex NMF solutions under various noise models (e.g., Tweedie, Negative Binomial)? Theoretical analysis is absent.  
- How can the selection of the appropriate variance function (distribution) and structural constraints be automated in NMF? Current practice relies on manual inspection; no unified framework exists.

## Limitations
- Limited validation: only two datasets (mutational counts, newsgroups); no synthetic ground-truth tests to isolate overdispersion effects.
- Dispersion estimation and scalability concerns: profile likelihood for *p*, α may be unstable with sparse data; convex NMF’s O(N²) per-iteration cost could dominate for large *N* (tested only up to moderate sizes).
- Implementation and BIC issues: novel convex NB updates unverified; `nmfgenr` numerical edge cases not addressed; BIC comparisons assume correct likelihood specification.
