# AI-Generated Music Detection in Broadcast Monitoring

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06823  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06823  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** In realistic broadcast scenarios, the best model (SpectTTTra-α) achieved an F1-score of only 61.1%, while a CNN baseline dropped to 27.6%; performance severely degrades under speech masking and short music durations.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces AI-OpenBMAT, a 54.9-hour dataset simulating broadcast audio by mixing human production music with Suno v3.5 AI continuations and speech following real TV statistics. It benchmarks a CNN and several SpectTTTra transformer variants for detecting AI-generated music. Results show that when evaluated in full broadcast scenarios—where music is often short and masked by speech—detection performance collapses, with F1-scores as low as 27.6% for the CNN and 61.1% for the best SpectTTTra model. The study identifies signal-to-noise ratio degradation due to speech masking and insufficient temporal context from short music excerpts as primary failure modes, highlighting a critical gap between current streaming-focused detectors and the realities of broadcast monitoring.

## Method Summary
The authors construct AI-OpenBMAT, a dataset of 3,294 one-minute broadcast-like segments built upon the OpenBMAT framework. Human music (from Epidemic Sound) and AI continuations (from Suno v3.5) are mixed with speech according to real TV loudness statistics. Two model families are evaluated: a 6-layer CNN trained on a private balanced clean dataset (27h human, 27h AI), and three SpectTTTra transformer variants pre-trained on the large SONICS dataset. Models process 5-second log-mel spectrogram windows with a 1-second hop during broadcast evaluation; per-second predictions are aggregated via a moving average. Key experiments measure robustness by synthetically varying SNR (music-speech mixtures) and input music duration (zero-padding).

## Key Results
- AI-OpenBMAT reveals a large performance gap: SpectTTTra-α achieves 61.1% F1 overall in broadcast, while the CNN reaches only 27.6%.
- For background-masked music segments, CNN F1-scores fall to 3% (bgvl class), demonstrating catastrophic failure under speech dominance.
- Detection scores drop below 60% for music durations shorter than 2 seconds, confirming temporal context as a critical limitation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Performance degradation stems from a distribution shift between training (clean, full-length streaming tracks) and deployment (noisy, short broadcast excerpts) conditions.  
**Mechanism:** Models trained on pristine, multi-minute audio learn features (e.g., high-level harmonic structure, long-term timbral consistencies) that are absent or corrupted in broadcast segments where music is short and masked by speech. This creates a covariate shift.  
**Core assumption:** Discriminative cues for AI-generated music are consistent across contexts, but their signal-to-noise ratio and temporal availability vary drastically.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "most existing datasets and detection models have been developed with a streaming-centric view... Broadcast audio... poses a different challenge"  
- [section 2.1] AI-OpenBMAT is built to "mimic the statistical structure of broadcast music" from OpenBMAT, explicitly contrasting with prior streaming-focused datasets like SONICS.  
- [corpus] Neighbors (e.g., 'Segment Transformer') also focus on full-audio detection, indicating the field's streaming bias. Corpus evidence for broadcast-specific failure modes is weak; this paper introduces the first such benchmark.  
- Overall broadcast F1: CNN 27.6%, SpectTTTra-α 61.1% (Key Results).  
- For the bgvl class (background voice-dominant), CNN F1 drops to 3% (Key Results).  
**Break condition:** If models are retrained on data with broadcast-like SNR and duration distributions (e.g., AI-OpenBMAT), the performance gap may close.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Speech masking is a primary failure mode because it obscures the subtle, model-specific artifacts that detectors exploit.  
**Mechanism:** AI music generators leave high-frequency or temporal artifacts (e.g., from neural vocoders or diffusion processes). Dominant speech (high-energy, mid-frequency) masks these cues via auditory scene analysis principles, reducing their salience in the spectro-temporal domain.  
**Core assumption:** Detectors rely on faint, unnatural patterns (e.g., "Fourier explanation of AI-music artifacts" [ref 9]) that are easily drowned out by concurrent speech.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "music appears as short excerpts, often masked by dominant speech... under which existing detectors fail."  
- [section 4.1] Performance drops steeply as SNR decreases, with CNN degrading most steeply. SpectTTTra-α's slower decay suggests some context may help partially mitigate masking.  
- [corpus] Neighbors like 'Double Entendre' and 'Detecting Musical Deepfakes' also note vulnerability to audio transformations/noise, supporting that robustness is a general challenge.  
- CNN F1 for the bgvl class (severe speech masking) is only 3% (Key Results), illustrating catastrophic failure under speech dominance.  
**Break condition:** Development of feature extractors invariant to speech-dominant mixtures (e.g., speech separation pre-processing or attention mechanisms focusing on residual music cues).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Short music durations prevent reliable detection because models require a minimum temporal context to identify AI-generated patterns.  
**Mechanism:** SpectTTTra's tokenization and self-attention are designed for long-range dependencies (e.g., chord progression anomalies, structural inconsistencies). Sub-2-second inputs provide insufficient sequence length for these mechanisms to activate meaningfully. CNNs, with local receptive fields, are slightly more robust but still falter below 1s.  
**Core assumption:** AI-generated music exhibits discernible anomalies only over multi-second windows (e.g., inconsistent voice leading, unnatural timbral evolution).  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "short-duration music... scores dropped below 60%"  
- [section 4.2] SpectTTTra models drop to ~72% at 2s input; CNN remains stable until 0.5s then plummets to 66% at 0.2s.  
- [section 3.3] Experiment directly varies input length via zero-padding, confirming sensitivity.  
- Detection scores drop below 60% for music durations shorter than 2 seconds (Key Results).  
**Break condition:** Architectural modifications that either (a) compress temporal context efficiently (e.g., hierarchical processing) or (b) are trained explicitly on ultra-short excerpts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Broadcast Audio Structure**  
  *Why needed here:* Understanding TV audio's segmented, speech-dominated nature is essential to grasp why AI-OpenBMAT's design (segment durations, loudness relations) is necessary.  
  *Quick check question:* Why does AI-OpenBMAT construct broadcast-like segments by mixing music with speech according to real TV statistics?
- **Concept: AI Music Generation Artifacts**  
  *Why needed here:* The entire detection task hinges on the premise that AI generators (like Suno v3.5) leave detectable, systematic flaws. Without knowing their nature, model design is guesswork.  
  *Quick check question:* What types of artifacts in AI-generated music are mentioned as exploitable by detectors? (e.g., high-frequency or temporal artifacts from neural vocoders or diffusion processes)
- **Concept: Evaluation Under Class/Context Imbalance**  
  *Why needed here:* The dataset and results are dominated by short/no-music segments. F1-score is chosen over accuracy because of this imbalance. Misunderstanding this leads to misinterpreting the "27.6% CNN" result.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (5s mono, 22.05kHz) -> Preprocessing (log-mel spectrogram) -> Models (CNN or SpectTTTra variants) -> Output (binary per window) -> Post-processing (1s hop, moving average) -> Second-level prediction
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Preprocess waveform to spectrogram.  
  2. Feed 5s window to model.  
  3. (Broadcast eval) Aggregate overlapping 1s predictions via moving average.  
  4. Compare to second-level ground truth from AI-OpenBMAT annotations.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *CNN vs. SpectTTTra:* CNN is simpler, more robust to very short inputs (<2s) but less capable of capturing long-range context. SpectTTTra variants aim for better context modeling (β/γ larger patches) but degrade faster on short inputs. α (smallest patches) is most SNR-robust but overall weaker.  
  - *Fixed 5s window vs. variable:* All models use fixed 5s. Zero-padding for shorter segments likely introduces invalid spectral context, harming performance (section 4.2).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Low F1 in bg/bgvl classes:** Indicates failure when music is background-masked (Table 1, e.g., CNN bgvl F1=3%).  
  - **Sharp SNR decay:** Performance drops >40% from clean to -10dB SNR (Fig 2).  
  - **Duration sensitivity:** Sub-1s inputs cause catastrophic failure for SpectTTTra; CNN holds until 0.5s then drops.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline replication:** Train CNN on clean, full-length private data (as in paper). Test on clean 5s music excerpts from same source. Expect >99% accuracy, confirming streaming-scenario competence.  
  2. **Controlled SNR degradation:** Use the paper's synthesized 5s mixtures (music + speech at controlled SNRs). Plot F1 vs. SNR. Expect pattern matching Fig 2 left.  
  3. **Controlled duration degradation:** Use paper's zero-padded short-duration test set. Plot F1 vs. input length. Expect pattern matching Fig 2 right, confirming CNN >2s robustness vs. SpectTTTra fragility.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Will retraining detectors on broadcast-like data (e.g., AI-OpenBMAT) close the performance gap between streaming and broadcast scenarios?
- Can we develop feature extraction methods robust to speech-dominant mixtures (e.g., via speech separation or attention to residual cues)?
- What architectural changes (e.g., hierarchical processing) enable reliable detection from sub-2-second music excerpts?

## Limitations
- Precise CNN/SpectTTTra architectures, hyperparameters, and training data composition are unspecified.  
- Ground-truth labeling for AI-OpenBMAT evaluation (per-second handling of no-music segments) is ambiguous.  
- Models are tested only on Suno v3.5 AI music; generalization to other generators is unknown.  
- No public code or pretrained weights are released, hindering exact replication.  
- Random seeds and post-processing details (e.g., moving-average window size) are not documented.
