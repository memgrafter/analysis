# Thinking in Different Spaces: Domain-Specific Latent Geometry Survives Cross-Architecture Translation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20406
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20406
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** Independently trained language models share domain-specific latent geometries enabling cross-architecture behavior correction via linear projection, but these geometries are not interchangeable across reasoning domains.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether language models with different architectures and training processes develop internally compatible latent representations. The authors learn a linear projection to map a teacher model’s activations into a student’s latent space and inject these during the student’s generation. Across 20 heterogeneous teacher-student pairs, the projection explains significant variance (R² ≈ 0.40–0.50) and yields mean correction rates of 25.2% on TruthfulQA and 25.5% on GSM8K. However, geometric alignment and behavioral correction are virtually uncorrelated (r ≈ -0.07). Critically, projections trained on one reasoning domain catastrophically fail on the other (mean transfer R² = -3.83), establishing that latent geometries are domain-specific and orthogonal.

## Method Summary
The method proceeds in three stages. First, teacher and student models perform forward passes on a shared prompt set; residual stream activations are extracted at chosen layers. Second, a Ridge regression learns an affine projection matrix **W** and bias **b** that maps teacher activations into the student’s coordinate system, using a held-out validation set. Third, during student generation on test prompts, a forward hook replaces (or blends) the student’s residual state at a target layer with the projected teacher vector **W·h_T**, scaled by a coefficient **α**. The coefficient **α** and extraction/injection layers are tuned via grid search to maximize the correction rate **Δ**, defined as the improvement in task-specific accuracy.

## Key Results
- Ridge projection achieves R² ≈ 0.50 on TruthfulQA and ≈0.40 on GSM8K; performance collapses under permutation control (R² ≈ -0.22) and L1 regularization (R² ≈ 0.01), confirming reliance on distributed semantic geometry.
- Interventions yield average correction rates of 25.2% (TruthfulQA) and 25.5% (GSM8K), ranging from 14–50% and 8.5–43.3% across pairings, demonstrating cross-architecture effectiveness.
- Cross-domain projection catastrophically fails in all cases (mean transfer R² = -3.83), proving that reasoning domains occupy geometrically distinct latent subspaces.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Affine Compatibility Across Architectures
- **Claim:** Independently trained language models develop geometrically compatible latent representations such that a linear transformation suffices to align their internal states for the same reasoning domain.
- **Mechanism:** Ridge regression learns an affine projection matrix **W** and bias **b** that maps teacher activations to the student’s coordinate system; **W** captures a global rotation and scaling aligning the teacher’s conceptual manifold with the student’s.
- **Core assumption:** Overlapping linguistic training distributions induce topologically similar latent manifolds; architectural or initialization differences merely rotate/scale these manifolds rather than creating incompatible spaces.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports R² ≈ 0.50 (verbal) and ≈0.40 (mathematical); Section 3.1 formalizes the affine assumption; prior work on model stitching and linear similarity supports this.
- **Break condition:** Permutation control destroys semantic correspondence and collapses R² to ≈ -0.22, proving dependence on structured semantic geometry.

### Mechanism 2: Residual Stream Steering for Behavior Correction
- **Claim:** Injecting a projected teacher state into the student’s residual stream during generation corrects behavior without weight updates by redirecting the reasoning trajectory.
- **Mechanism:** At a chosen layer, the student’s residual **h_student** is replaced or blended with **W·h_T** via **h_final = (1-α)·h_student + α·(W·h_T)**; **α** controls intervention strength.
- **Core assumption:** The residual stream acts as a writable medium where a single aligned vector can steer subsequent computation toward correct outputs.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports mean correction rates ~25%; Section 4.4 details forward-hook implementation with L2-norm rescaling; prior activation steering studies support this.
- **Break condition:** On GSM8K, **α ≥ 0.8** universally collapses correction; **α = 1.0** often underperforms sub-interpolation (**α < 1**), indicating fragility to representational displacement.

### Mechanism 3: Domain-Specific Latent Subspaces
- **Claim:** The geometric structure of latent spaces is domain-specific; a projection trained on one reasoning domain catastrophically misaligns the other, establishing orthogonal subspaces.
- **Mechanism:** Applying a projection **W** learned on domain A to domain B yields actively harmful results (mean transfer R² ≈ -3.83), not merely uninformative ones, indicating the projection points opposite to the correct manifold.
- **Core assumption:** Different cognitive tasks (factual recall vs. arithmetic) are computed in geometrically distinct latent subspaces due to modular specialization during training.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states mean transfer R² = -3.83; Section 5.7 shows double dissociation with negative R² in every cell; no direct prior evidence.
- **Break condition:** All cross-domain applications produce negative R²; mid-network injection (**l_S ≈ 0.50**) worsens failure, suggesting mid-layers are primary sites of domain-specific construction.

## Foundational Learning
### Affine Geometry in Latent Spaces
- **Why needed:** To understand why a simple matrix multiplication (**W**) can map concepts between models, and why this fails across domains.
- **Quick check:** If two models' latent manifolds are topologically compatible but differently oriented, what linear operation aligns them? (Answer: A rotation and uniform scaling, captured by **W**.)

### Regularization as a Diagnostic Tool (L2 vs L1)
- **Why needed:** The collapse of L1-regularized projection (R² ≈ 0.01) vs. success of L2-regularized projection (R² ≈ 0.50) reveals that cross-architecture alignment relies on distributed, not sparse, feature correlations.
- **Quick check:** What does forcing sparsity (**L1**) onto the projection matrix tell us about how semantics are encoded? (Answer: Semantics are not concentrated in a few dimensions; alignment requires the full vector.)

### Hierarchical Processing and Relative Depth
- **Why needed:** Layer selection for extraction/injection must account for differing model depths; absolute layer indices are misleading. Relative depth maps functional stages (syntax → semantics → decoding) across architectures.
- **Quick check:** Why is deeper teacher extraction (**l_T ≈ 0.90**) generally better than shallower? (Answer: Deeper layers hold more resolved, abstract reasoning states ready for injection.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Teacher model -> Student model -> Activation extraction hooks -> Ridge regression module -> Intervention hook -> Evaluation suite

**Critical path:**
1. Data extraction: Teacher and student forward passes on identical prompts, storing residual activations at target layers.
2. Projection learning: Fit Ridge regression (**W**, **b**) on held-out data to map teacher activations to student space.
3. Intervention sweeps: Grid search over injection coefficient **α** and relative layer indices (**l_T**, **l_S**) to maximize correction rate.
4. Correction calculation: Compute improvement in task-specific accuracy (**Δ**) on test prompts.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Linear vs. nonlinear projection: Linear ensures interpretability of geometric alignment; nonlinear adapters could force alignment but obscure true manifold compatibility.
- **α** regime: Sub-interpolation (**α < 1**) is safer and preferred on GSM8K; extrapolation (**α > 1**) sometimes helps on TruthfulQA but risks collapse.
- Layer selection: Relative depth is principled but coarse; finer grids might reveal narrow optimal windows.

**Failure signatures:**
- Projection fails: Permutation control R² > -0.1 or L1 control R² > 0.1.
- Intervention fails: **Δ** ≤ 5% across all **α**, **l** sweeps.
- Domain transfer fails: Cross-domain R² ≥ -1.0 (should be ≤ -1.7 per paper’s mean).
- Quantization artifacts: Large teacher (Llama-70B) underperforms due to 4-bit noise.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Permutation control test:** Shuffle student activation rows; expect R² collapse to ≈ -0.22. Confirms semantic alignment.
2. **L1 regularization test:** Replace Ridge with Lasso; expect R² collapse to ≈ 0.01. Confirms distributed encoding.
3. **Domain transfer test:** Train on TruthfulQA, evaluate on GSM8K; expect negative R² (≤ -1.7). Establishes domain orthogonality.

## Limitations
- Findings are restricted to two reasoning domains (TruthfulQA, GSM8K); domain-generalizability remains untested.
- Behavioral correction exhibits high variance and no correlation with geometric alignment (r ≈ -0.07), making the mechanism uncertain.
- Intervention effectiveness is highly sensitive to hyperparameters (**α**, injection layer) and varies across teacher-student pairs.

## Confidence
- **Cross-architecture linear alignment (same domain):** High
- **Behavioral correction via injection:** Medium
- **Domain-specific geometry (catastrophic transfer):** High

## Next Checks
1. **Domain diversity test:** Extend the pipeline to at least three additional reasoning domains (e.g., MBPP code generation, HellaSwag commonsense, logical deduction puzzles) to verify the universality of domain-specific geometry and catastrophic transfer.
2. **Nonlinear alignment probe:** Replace Ridge regression with a shallow MLP (one hidden layer) as the projection function; compare both R² and correction rates to test if linear projection is insufficient.
3. **Comprehensive hyperparameter stability sweep:** For a diverse subset of 5 teacher-student pairs, perform dense grid searches over **α** ∈ [-1, 2] and injection relative depth **l_S** ∈ [0, 1] with fine increments (e.g., 0.05), including extreme scale mismatches (e.g., 7B → 70B), to map the stability landscape.
