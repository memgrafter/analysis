# Orchestrating Heterogeneous Experts: A Scalable MoE Framework with Anisotropy-Preserving Fusion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.00003
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00003
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: Proposed coarse-grained MoE with concatenation fusion improves AUC by 0.72 percentage points over a dense baseline and increases throughput by 9% on six Southeast Asian e-commerce markets.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) framework for cross-border e-commerce search relevance that leverages heterogeneous large language models (LLMs) without additional pre-training. It introduces a novel Information-Preserving Concatenation Fusion strategy that concatenates expert embeddings instead of averaging them, thereby preserving distinct latent manifolds and avoiding destructive interference. Coupled with hard query-level routing and an asynchronous batch inference pipeline, the method achieves improved AUC (+0.72) and higher throughput (+9%) compared to a dense baseline. Online A/B testing via knowledge distillation further demonstrates reduced bad ratio and higher online AUC.

## Method Summary
The framework freezes multiple pre-trained LLM experts (e.g., Qwen, Gemma) and trains a lightweight router to assign each query to a small subset of experts. Each expert processes the entire query and produces a last-token hidden state. These embeddings are projected to a shared dimension and then concatenated—not averaged—before being fed to a trainable MLP classifier that outputs the relevance score. The router is trained with a load-balancing loss to ensure diverse expert utilization. For deployment, an asynchronous three-stage pipeline (routing → parallel expert execution → late fusion) maximizes throughput by batching queries per expert and overlapping computation across heterogeneous models.

## Key Results
- Offline: +0.72 AUC improvement over a dense baseline with equivalent active parameters on six Southeast Asian markets.
- Throughput: 13.72 queries per second (QPS), a 9% increase over baseline.
- Ablation: Concatenation fusion outperforms weighted averaging by 0.22 AUC.
- Online A/B (via student model distillation): bad ratio reduced from 9.00% to 8.55%; online AUC increased from 90.39% to 91.28%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Concatenation fusion preserves distinct latent manifolds
- **Claim:** Concatenation-based fusion outperforms weighted averaging by preserving the distinct latent manifolds of heterogeneous LLMs, avoiding destructive interference.
- **Mechanism:** Averaging combines misaligned basis vectors from different models, causing feature cancellation; concatenation keeps embeddings in separate subspaces, allowing a downstream MLP to learn a non-linear boundary that selectively uses each expert's signals.
- **Core assumption:** The latent spaces of heterogeneous LLMs are topologically distinct (anisotropic and misaligned), so linear combination is harmful.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.3 emphasize preserving distinct embedding manifolds; corpus does not strongly contradict.
- **Break condition:** If expert latent manifolds are well-aligned (e.g., same architecture), averaging might suffice.

### Mechanism 2: Coarse-grained hard routing with load-balancing
- **Claim:** Query-level hard routing enables effective specialization and stable training without pre-training the experts.
- **Mechanism:** A lightweight router assigns each entire query to a top-*k* subset of frozen experts. Hard selection eliminates train-inference mismatch; load-balancing loss prevents routing collapse, ensuring all experts are utilized and gradients flow.
- **Core assumption:** Queries are homogeneous enough that a single expert (or small set) can handle the entire linguistic and semantic nuance.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.2 highlight hard routing benefits and load balancing.
- **Break condition:** For code-switched or multi-intent queries, query-level routing may be insufficient; token-level might be needed.

### Mechanism 3: Asynchronous batch inference pipeline
- **Claim:** Decoupling routing, parallel expert execution, and late fusion achieves high throughput while supporting heterogeneous experts.
- **Mechanism:** Router first batches queries to compute sparse assignments; then queries assigned to the same expert are batched and run in parallel (across devices); finally, a lightweight fusion step combines outputs. This overlaps computation, exploits GPU parallelism, and compensates for slower experts.
- **Core assumption:** Expert inference is the dominant bottleneck; batching at the expert level maximizes hardware utilization given heterogeneous expert speeds.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports QPS improvement; Section 2.4 describes pipeline stages.
- **Break condition:** If routing becomes bottleneck or expert latencies are too disparate, gains may diminish.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Router (lightweight model) -> Expert pool (frozen LLMs) -> Projection layers -> Fusion classifier (MLP). Async scheduler manages pipeline: Routing stage -> Expert-Specific Batch Inference -> Late-Stage Fusion.

**Critical path:** Input → Router (batch) → Group queries by expert → Parallel expert forward passes → Gather & project → Concatenate → MLP → Output. Router must complete before grouping; fusion waits for all selected experts.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Memory vs. Performance: More experts → higher accuracy but increased VRAM.
- Routing granularity: Query-level simpler/faster but fails on mixed-language; token-level more precise but complex/slower.
- Fusion complexity: Concatenation preserves info but increases classifier input size; averaging simpler but risks interference.

**Failure signatures:**
- Routing collapse: One expert gets >90% of queries (check load-balancing loss and utilization stats).
- Manifold interference: t-SNE of fused embeddings shows class entanglement; concatenation underperforms averaging.
- Pipeline bottleneck: QPS low despite sparsity → check grouping efficiency or slow expert blocking fusion.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Fusion strategy ablation: Compare concatenation vs. weighted averaging AUC/QPS under identical routing (hard, k=2). Expected: Concat wins on AUC.
2. Routing strategy comparison: Test rule-based, pseudo-label, soft, and hard routing with concat fusion. Expected: Hard routing best AUC-balance.
3. Manifold visualization: t-SNE on test embeddings from concat vs. average fusion. Expected: Concat shows clear class separation and "unfolded" manifolds; average shows smearing.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can token-level or chunk-level expert selection be implemented to effectively handle mixed-language queries, and what are the trade-offs in latency and performance compared to query-level routing?
- **Basis in paper:** Future Work: "Fine-grained Routing: Investigating token-level or chunk-level expert selection for mixed-language queries."
- **Why unresolved:** The current query-level routing may fail to capture intra-query linguistic variations common in Southeast Asian markets, where single queries often blend multiple languages.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate a token-chunk routing module on a held-out set of code-switched queries, measuring per-language AUC improvements and QPS overhead against the query-level baseline.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What fusion strategies best integrate multimodal experts (e.g., image encoders) with text-based LLM experts while preserving manifold anisotropy and avoiding destructive interference?
- **Basis in paper:** Future Work: "Multi-modal Experts: Integrating image encoders as experts to address queries where visual information is crucial."
- **Why unresolved:** The proposed concatenation fusion is designed for heterogeneous text experts; its extension to multimodal modalities (text, image) with different representational geometries is unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Construct a multimodal e-commerce dataset, integrate vision experts (e.g., CLIP) with text LLMs, compare concatenation against learned cross-modal attention or projection-based fusion using AUC and t-SNE visualization of joint manifolds.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Which quantization or pruning techniques minimally degrade the anisotropic latent manifolds of heterogeneous LLM experts, thereby preserving the benefits of concatenation fusion?
- **Basis in paper:** Future Work: "Model Compression: Applying quantization techniques to further reduce the deployment cost of the expert pool."
- **Why unresolved:** The framework's memory bottleneck stems from loading multiple LLMs; naive quantization (e.g., INT8) may collapse the high-variance directional signals critical for manifold preservation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply various quantization (e.g., GPTQ, AWQ) and structured pruning schemes to each expert, measure changes in AUC, QPS, and embedding anisotropy (e.g., via eigenvalue spectrum analysis), identifying techniques that maintain concatenation gains.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Under what formal conditions does concatenation fusion provably preserve the discriminative manifold structure of heterogeneous expert embeddings better than scalar mixing?
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred: Section 2.3 and 3.5 posit that concatenation avoids destructive interference by preserving orthogonal subspaces, but this is argued empirically without a general theoretical framework.
- **Why unresolved:** The claim relies on geometric intuition; a formal model linking expert manifold metrics to downstream classification margins is lacking.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop a theoretical analysis (e.g., based on manifold learning or random matrix theory) deriving bounds on classification error for concatenation versus averaging under varying degrees of expert latent space misalignment; validate with synthetic and real expert embedding pairs.

## Limitations
- Dataset & evaluation specifics are unreleased, hindering exact replication.
- Router design details (metadata usage, projection dimension *d*, MLP architectures) are underspecified, requiring assumptions.
- Expert heterogeneity impact is inferred but not directly measured; manifold misalignment remains plausible but untested.
- Online A/B results reflect a distilled student model, not the MoE’s direct deployment—attribution is indirect.
