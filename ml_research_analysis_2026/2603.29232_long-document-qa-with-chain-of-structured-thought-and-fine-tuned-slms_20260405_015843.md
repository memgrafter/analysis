# Long-Document QA with Chain-of-Structured-Thought and Fine-Tuned SLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29232
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29232
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** LiteCoST fine-tunes 3B/7B SLMs to gain +27.58/+17.83 accuracy points on Loong finance QA, surpass GPT-4o-mini and DeepSeek-R1, and run 2–4× faster.

## Executive Summary
Long-document question answering suffers from brittle reasoning over noisy, dispersed evidence. This paper introduces LiteCoST, a framework that first uses a strong LLM to generate auditable, step-wise reasoning traces and structured serialized outputs (SSOs) via a Chain-of-Structured-Thought (CoST) template. These high-quality (trace, SSO) pairs then supervise two-stage fine-tuning of small language models (SLMs): supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to learn format and structure, followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with triple rewards (answer correctness, format compliance, process consistency). On the Loong finance benchmark, LiteCoST-tuned 3B and 7B SLMs achieve dramatic accuracy improvements over their base versions and outperform much larger models like GPT-4o-mini and DeepSeek-R1, while being significantly more efficient at inference.

## Method Summary
LiteCoST operates in two phases. Phase 1 (CoST generation): A powerful LLM (e.g., GPT-4o) processes each (question, document) pair through a fixed four-step template—structure analysis (induce a query-specific schema), trace generation (schema-guided step-by-step extraction yielding a CoST trace and SSO), quality verification (LLM-as-judge binary check), and iterative refinement (recursive regeneration for failures). This produces a curated dataset of (question, document, ground-truth answer, CoST trace, SSO) tuples. Phase 2 (SLM fine-tuning): A base SLM is first fine-tuned via SFT on these tuples to master the structured output format and step discipline. Then GRPO optimizes the policy using group-relative advantages computed from three rewards: an outcome reward (hybrid rule-based and semantic accuracy score), a format reward (enforcing tag and step structure), and a process reward (binary per-step consistency between predicted and ground-truth traces, scaled by a trajectory-level coefficient). The final fine-tuned SLM generates SSOs directly at low latency.

## Key Results
- On Loong (finance), LiteCoST-tuned 3B/7B SLMs gain +27.58 and +17.83 Average Score points and +0.29/+0.22 Perfect Rate over base models.
- The 7B variant exceeds GPT-4o-mini and DeepSeek-R1 (671B) in overall performance while achieving 2–4× lower inference latency.
- Gains generalize to legal and open-domain QA benchmarks, demonstrating both accuracy and efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Claim: CoST improves QA accuracy by generating auditable, schema-aligned traces and structured outputs that consolidate dispersed evidence.
**Mechanism:** A strong LLM follows a CoST template to perform step-wise reasoning—structure analysis, trace generation, quality verification, iterative refinement—producing a serialized structured output (SSO) that normalizes entities, aligns records, and ensures format consistency, yielding machine-checkable artifacts.
**Core Assumption:** High-quality SSO from LLMs provides effective supervision for training SLMs to replicate structure-first reasoning.
**Evidence Anchors:** Abstract describes CoST template; Section 3.1 details the procedure; corpus context shows prior work on SLM efficiency but not CoST-specific mechanisms.
**Break Condition:** LLM-generated SSO suffers hallucinations/omissions due to document noise, degrading supervision quality.

### Claim: Two-phase SLM fine-tuning (SFT then GRPO) effectively distills CoST behavior, balancing accuracy and efficiency.
**Mechanism:** SFT instills initial structure, format, and step discipline using LLM-generated (trace, SSO) pairs; GRPO refines the policy using group-relative rewards combining answer correctness, format compliance, and process consistency, leveraging RL for fine-grained optimization.
**Core Assumption:** Reinforcement learning with dense, multi-level rewards aligns SLM outputs with LLM-quality structured extraction without catastrophic forgetting.
**Evidence Anchors:** Abstract mentions SFT and GRPO; Section 3.2 explains formulations; corpus shows interest in fine-tuned SLMs but lacks direct GRPO-for-structured-tasks evidence.
**Break Condition:** GRPO reward scaling misweights process vs. outcome signals, causing overemphasis on formatting over content accuracy.

### Claim: Triple rewards in GRPO synergistically improve SSO generation by addressing final output quality and intermediate reasoning fidelity.
**Mechanism:** Outcome reward ensures answer accuracy and structural alignment; format reward enforces step-wise tags; process reward uses LLM-judged consistency between predicted and ground-truth reasoning steps, scaled by trajectory-level coefficient γ to stabilize training.
**Core Assumption:** Dense process supervision mitigates sparse outcome signals, promoting faithful step-by-step extraction that generalizes.
**Evidence Anchors:** Section 4.5 ablation: removing process reward hurts (-1.43 AS); removing outcome reward causes larger drop (-4.40 AS); Section 3.2 defines reward composition; corpus lacks strong external evidence on this synergy.
**Break Condition:** Process reward judgments from LLM-as-judge are inconsistent or biased, leading to noisy training signals.

## Foundational Learning
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting**  
  *Why needed:* CoST extends CoT by embedding structured output generation; understanding step decomposition is prerequisite to reducing error propagation in long-document QA.  
  *Quick Check:* How does breaking reasoning into traceable steps reduce error accumulation over long contexts?
- **Structured Data Extraction and Schema Alignment**  
  *Why needed:* Core task involves inducing dynamic schemas and populating them with normalized evidence; requires knowledge of entity linking, unit harmonization, and serialization formats.  
  *Quick Check:* What are the trade-offs between dynamic schema induction versus fixed schemas for query-specific extraction?
- **Reinforcement Learning for Fine-Tuning (e.g., PPO, GRPO)**  
  *Why needed:* GRPO optimizes policy beyond supervised loss using group-relative advantages; essential for reward-based refinement of process consistency.  
  *Quick Check:* How does group-relative advantage calculation in GRPO improve stability compared to standard PPO in sparse-reward settings?
- **Long-Context Modeling**  
  *Why needed:* Documents are long and noisy; models must handle extended sequences without losing local coherence or global evidence integration.  
  *Quick Check:* How do attention mechanisms in SLMs scale with document length, and what mitigations exist for memory/performance degradation?
- **Evaluation with LLM-as-Judge**  
  *Why needed:* Structured outputs (tables, graphs) require nuanced assessment of accuracy, hallucination, completeness; automated rules are insufficient.  
  *Quick Check:* What biases might LLM-as-judge introduce when scoring structured extractions, and how can they be mitigated?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:**  
  CoST Template -> LLM Generator -> Training Data Curator -> SFT Module -> GRPO Module -> Inference Engine
- **Critical Path:**  
  1. Use LLM with CoST template to generate initial traces/SSOs on domain data.  
  2. Verify and refine outputs; curate high-quality dataset.  
  3. Perform SFT on SLM to learn format and step discipline.  
  4. Apply GRPO to optimize for outcome and process rewards.  
  5. Validate on held-out set; iterate if reward misalignment detected.
- **Design Tradeoffs:**  
  - Schema flexibility vs. complexity: Dynamic schemas adapt to queries but increase LLM generation cost and SLM learning burden.  
  - Reward granularity: Dense process rewards improve interpretability but require reliable LLM-as-judge; outcome rewards alone are sparse.  
  - Latency-accuracy: SLMs reduce inference time (~2–4× faster) but may plateau below LLM performance; GRPO narrows gap but adds training compute.
- **Failure Signatures:**  
  - Incomplete SSO: Missing fields or serialization errors; detectable via format reward drop.  
  - Process drift: Step-wise reasoning diverges from evidence; low process reward despite correct answer.  
  - Domain shift: Performance drops on new document types; indicates insufficient training data diversity.
- **First 3 Experiments:**  
  1. CoST Generation Audit: Sample 100 LLM outputs; manually verify trace completeness, schema alignment, and SSO correctness to establish baseline supervision quality.  
  2. SFT Sanity Check: Train SLM on small subset (e.g., 10% data); evaluate if it reproduces structured format and basic extraction; compare to base model zero-shot.  
  3. GRPO Ablation: Run control with SFT-only vs. SFT+GRPO on validation set; measure gains from process reward by analyzing step-level consistency and perfect rate changes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How well does LiteCoST generalize to domains beyond finance, legal, and open-domain QA, such as biomedical or technical manuals?**  
  *Basis:* Limitations state generalization to other distinct domains remains unverified. *Why unresolved:* Only three domains evaluated; datasets scarce. *Resolution evidence:* Systematic evaluation on new benchmarks, adaptation technique analysis, performance variation reporting.
- **How robust and accurate is dynamic schema construction and structure selection in CoST when faced with ambiguous, multi-faceted, or poorly defined questions?**  
  *Basis:* Inferred; schema selection foundational but error cases unanalyzed. *Why unresolved:* Incorrect structure choice cascades to poor extraction, yet reliability unexamined. *Resolution evidence:* Human evaluation of schema choices on ambiguous questions, metrics for schema appropriateness, impact studies on QA accuracy with perturbed schemas.
- **What is the optimal balance and interaction between the three GRPO rewards, and does the current scaling factor γ(Ti) suffice to prevent reward conflicts or overfitting?**  
  *Basis:* Inferred; ablations show each reward contributes, but weights not tuned systematically; γ heuristic. *Why unresolved:* Without understanding trade-offs, training may be suboptimal/unstable on complex/noisy data. *Resolution evidence:* Hyperparameter sweeps over reward weights, conflict detection via gradient analysis, performance comparisons across datasets.
- **Under what conditions does the iterative refinement module (A4) fail to converge or degrade sample quality, and what is the typical iteration count distribution needed for high-quality extraction?**  
  *Basis:* Inferred; iterative structuralizer described recursively but no convergence guarantees, failure case analysis, or iteration statistics provided. *Why unresolved:* Unbounded iterations increase costs; without iteration needs, deployment efficiency/reliability unclear. *Resolution evidence:* Tracking iteration counts per sample, identifying non-convergence patterns (e.g., contradictory evidence), cost-benefit curves for quality vs. iteration budget.
- **What is the total computational and financial cost of generating the CoST training data using a strong LLM (GPT-4o), and how does this amortize over different dataset scales or domains?**  
  *Basis:* Inferred; LLMs create training traces but only final SLM inference latency reported; upfront costs unquantified. *Why unresolved:* Overall pipeline cost depends heavily on LLM-based generation, affecting practicality. *Resolution evidence:* Detailed token usage/API costs per sample, scalability experiments with varying dataset sizes, comparison with cheaper generation alternatives.

## Limitations
- LLM-as-judge used for rewards and evaluation risks bias and hallucinations.
- Key GRPO hyperparameters (ϵ, β) and iterative refinement criteria are unreported, hindering exact replication.
- Evaluation primarily on Loong finance subset; generalization claims lack broad validation.
- No human evaluation of structured outputs; LLM metrics may not capture real utility.
- Training compute and data provenance details are missing, affecting reproducibility.

## Confidence
- CoST produces auditable traces: **Medium** (pipeline described but not independently verified).
- SFT+GRPO effectively distills CoST: **Medium** (ablation shows benefit, but GRPO settings unclear).
- Triple rewards synergy: **Medium** (ablation confirms, but process reward depends on LLM judge).
- Large gains on Loong and surpassing strong baselines: **Medium** (results rely on LLM-as-judge; no statistical tests reported).
- Generalization to legal/open-domain: **Low** (limited evidence, potential domain shift).
