# FedRandom: Sampling Consistent and Accurate Contribution Values in Federated Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05693
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05693
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: FedRandom reduces contribution sample variance by 4–40× and improves accuracy relative to size-based ground truth in >90% of 324 scenarios compared to MSM.

## Executive Summary
Federated learning (FL) often uses Shapley-based contribution metrics to fairly reward clients, but these metrics are highly unstable across different aggregation strategies. FedRandom introduces a simple yet effective approach: instead of fixing one aggregation method per federation run, it randomly selects an aggregation strategy (e.g., FedAvg, FedAdam, FedYogi) at each communication round. This random mixing generates an exponential number of distinct contribution samples (up to |S|^r) from a single training process. Across extensive experiments on four datasets (CIFAR-10/100, MNIST, FMNIST) with various data heterogeneity settings, FedRandom dramatically reduces the standard deviation of contribution estimates (by factors of 4 to 40) and consistently yields contribution means closer to a plausible size-based ground truth, outperforming the multi-strategy mean (MSM) baseline in 92% of scenarios. The method reframes contribution assessment as a statistical estimation problem, leveraging the law of large numbers to achieve more reliable and accurate client valuations.

## Method Summary
FedRandom extends the standard FL training loop with a random strategy selector. At each communication round, it uniformly picks one aggregation algorithm from a predefined set S (e.g., {FedAvg, FedAvgM, FedAdagrad, FedAdam, FedYogi}). The server aggregates client updates using the chosen strategy, broadcasts the new global model, and clients proceed with local training. After the round, per-client Shapley values are computed via a multi-round reconstruction method, treating the current model as the "value" of the participating coalition. Over R rounds, this yields R independent contribution samples per client, vastly outnumbering the |S| samples produced by the MSM baseline (which runs separate federations with each fixed strategy). The final contribution is the arithmetic mean of these samples. This approach achieves exponential sample growth without the linear cost of multiple full federations, exploiting statistical averaging to lower the variance of contribution estimates.

## Key Results
- **Variance reduction**: FedRandom lowered the standard deviation of contribution samples by a factor of 4 to 40 compared to MSM across all tested datasets, local epoch settings, and Dirichlet heterogeneity levels (α).
- **Accuracy improvement**: Compared to a size-based ground truth, FedRandom achieved a lower L2 distance in 85% (275/324) of scenarios and a lower L∞ distance in 82% (267/324). It reduced the L2 distance by more than one-third in half of all scenarios.
- **Overall dominance**: FedRandom outperformed MSM in 92% of all 324 experimental conditions and improved worst-case client divergence (L∞) in every scenario where it beat MSM.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
FedRandom operates on two statistical principles. First, by randomly selecting an aggregation strategy each round, it effectively simulates an exponential number (up to |S|^r) of distinct FL processes, generating far more contribution samples than the MSM baseline's fixed set of |S| runs. Each round’s contribution vector, computed after a random aggregation, is treated as an independent draw from the underlying distribution of true client contributions. Second, the central limit theorem guarantees that the variance of the sample mean shrinks as the number of samples grows, provided the draws are independent and identically distributed. Consequently, the mean of FedRandom’s many samples yields a far more precise estimate of the true contributions than the mean of only eight samples from MSM. This precision translates into lower measured variance across independent runs and, empirically, a smaller distance to a size-based ground truth, which serves as a reasonable fairness proxy. The approach assumes that random strategy selection does not alter the fundamental distribution; if certain strategies systematically bias contributions for specific clients, the independence assumption weakens, but the sheer volume of samples still tends to dominate the variance reduction benefit.

## Foundational Learning
- **Shapley Value**: A cooperative game theory concept that fairly distributes total payoff among players based on marginal contributions. In FL, Shapley values quantify each client's contribution to the global model's performance. Exact computation is combinatorial, so approximations like multi-round reconstruction are used.
  - *Why needed*: FedRandom uses Shapley values as the per-round contribution metric; understanding their fairness and computational cost is essential.
  - *Quick check*: Why is exact Shapley value computation infeasible for many clients in FL?
- **Federated Learning Aggregation Strategies**: Algorithms such as FedAvg, FedProx, and adaptive optimizers (FedAdam, FedYogi) define how the server combines client updates. Each strategy has distinct convergence properties under data heterogeneity.
  - *Why needed*: FedRandom randomly picks from a set of these strategies; knowing their differences explains potential interactions.
  - *Quick check*: How does FedProx differ from FedAvg in handling non-IID data?
- **Statistical Estimation (Sample Mean & Variance)**: The sample mean converges to the true mean as sample size increases, with variance shrinking by 1/n under i.i.d. sampling.
  - *Why needed*: FedRandom's core value proposition is that more samples → lower variance → better contribution estimate.
  - *Quick check*: If you double the number of independent samples from a fixed distribution, how does the standard error of the mean change?
- **Dirichlet Data Partition**: A common method to create non-IID client datasets by sampling class proportions from a Dirichlet distribution controlled by α.
  - *Why needed*: The paper's experiments vary α to control heterogeneity; FedRandom's performance must be understood under different data distributions.
  - *Quick check*: What happens to client data distributions as α decreases in a Dirichlet split?
- **Multi-Round Reconstruction Shapley**: An approximation technique that uses model states after each round to estimate Shapley values without enumerating all subsets, enabling feasible computation in FL.
  - *Why needed*: This is the specific Shapley variant employed in the study; its mechanics affect the contribution samples.
  - *Quick check*: How does multi-round reconstruction use the sequence of global models to compute contributions without testing all client subsets?

## Architecture Onboarding
The FedRandom architecture extends the standard FL server with a random strategy selector. The component flow is:

Strategy Set S -> Random Selector -> FL Training Loop -> Contribution Calculator -> Sample Aggregator

**Critical path**: Initialize model → For each round r: Randomly select s_r → Aggregate updates with s_r → Broadcast new model → Clients train → Compute round contributions using s_r's model → End for → Compute mean(contributions_over_rounds).

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **Accuracy vs. Cost**: More rounds R or larger |S| increase samples and potentially improve estimate quality but linearly increase communication/computation overhead.
- **Stability vs. Convergence**: Randomizing aggregation may degrade primary model convergence (Figure 1 shows FedRandom is not a top convergence performer). The system may need to use a separate fixed strategy for main training and only employ FedRandom in a simulation mode for contribution estimation.
- **Strategy Set Selection**: The set S should contain strategies with similar convergence objectives; mixing fundamentally different strategies (e.g., robust aggregators like Krum with FedAvg) might shift the contribution distribution and violate the i.i.d. sample assumption.

**Failure signatures**:
- **High Variance Persists**: After multiple independent runs (e.g., 30), the standard deviation of final mean contributions across runs remains comparable to MSM, indicating insufficiently independent samples or too small/homogeneous S.
- **Systematic Bias**: FedRandom's mean contributions consistently show large L2/L∞ distance from the size-based ground truth across seeds, suggesting a systemic bias in the chosen strategies.
- **Convergence Collapse**: The global model's test accuracy fails to reach a usable threshold because random aggregation oscillations prevent stable learning.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Variance Reduction Replication**: For a specific scenario (e.g., CIFAR-10, α=10, local epochs=5), run 30 independent seeds of MSM (using the 8 fixed strategies) and FedRandom. Compute per-client contribution standard deviation across the 30 samples. Verify FedRandom's σ is at least 4× lower than MSM's.
2. **Ground Truth Proximity**: Using the same 30 runs, compute the mean contribution vector for each method. Calculate its L2 and Linf distance to the size-based ground truth. Confirm FedRandom's distance is lower than MSM's in >90% of the 30 trials.
3. **Strategy Set Sensitivity**: Run FedRandom with different subsets of S (e.g., {FedAvg, FedAvgM}, {FedAdam, FedYogi}, and all five). Measure changes in σ and L2 distance to ground truth. A significant performance drop with smaller subsets would indicate reliance on strategy diversity and potential issues with the i.i.d. assumption.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How does FedRandom perform with diverse aggregation strategies?** The paper suggests studying the effect of different aggregation strategies and the importance of their types, especially those not derived from FedAvg (e.g., Byzantine-robust or personalized methods).
- **What if strategies have fundamentally different objectives?** The conclusion notes that involved strategies do not necessarily need to share the same objective function and that mixed-objective cases should be tested, as homogeneous objectives may be required for the statistical assumptions to hold.
- **Can FedRandom stabilize other contribution metrics?** Since Shapley values require a validation dataset, the paper proposes exploring FedRandom with alternative contribution methods (e.g., data-based or performance-based valuations) that do not rely on validation data.
- **Does random aggregation enhance security?** Randomization might make it harder for adversaries to fingerprint the aggregation strategy from model updates; this potential security benefit is speculative and unvalidated.

## Limitations
- Details of the multi-round reconstruction Shapley computation (reference [16]) are missing, hindering exact replication.
- Hyperparameters (learning rates, optimizer-specific settings, number of communication rounds) and number of clients are not fully specified, requiring assumptions that may affect results.
- The size-based ground truth is a heuristic; true Shapley values are unknown, so accuracy claims rely on this proxy.
- Random strategy selection could violate the i.i.d. assumption if strategies systematically bias contributions for certain clients, potentially affecting the theoretical variance reduction.
- FedRandom’s random aggregation may degrade model convergence (Figure 1 shows it is not a top convergence performer), limiting its practical adoption if convergence is a primary goal.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (more samples)**: High – clearly described and evidenced by exponential sample growth.
- **Mechanism 2 (variance reduction)**: High – strongly supported by empirical results (σ reduced 4–40×; lower variance in 94% of cases).
- **Mechanism 3 (accuracy improvement)**: Medium – improvements observed but rely on size-based ground truth being a valid proxy; bias-variance trade-off not fully resolved.
- **Reproducibility**: Low – missing implementation details for Shapley and hyperparameters make faithful replication uncertain.
