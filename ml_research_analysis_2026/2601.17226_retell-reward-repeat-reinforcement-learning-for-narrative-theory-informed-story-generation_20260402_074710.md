# Retell, Reward, Repeat: Reinforcement Learning for Narrative Theory-Informed Story Generation

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2601.17226
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17226
- **Reference count**: 21
- **Primary result**: d-RLAIF models achieve higher minLRC and narrativity scores than SFT, approaching human performance on the TimeTravel dataset.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of subjectivity and lack of ground truths in automatic story generation (ASG) by introducing direct Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (d-RLAIF) guided by Todorov's narrative theory. The core method employs LLM-as-judge models, prompted with Todorovian principles, to generate reward signals that drive Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) during post-training. Experiments on the TimeTravel dataset show that d-RLAIF models—particularly those trained with a narrativity-based reward—significantly outperform supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on holistic narrative quality metrics (minLRC, narrativity) while SFT excels in surface-level similarity (BLEU-4). The d-RLAIF outputs are more diverse and aligned with narrative principles, with quality approaching human performance, demonstrating a viable alternative for theory-informed ASG.

## Method Summary
The approach operationalizes Todorov’s five-stage narrative structure into automated evaluation criteria (Logical, Rational, CompleteN, Narrativity). An LLM-as-judge (Selene-1-mini or M-Prometheus) scores 16 candidate story endings per prompt, producing rewards of three types: rational-only (RO), RO with a 5-point scale (RO5), and narrativity-focused (RN). GRPO with LoRA is then used to fine-tune policy LLMs (Llama, Qwen, Olmo) on the TimeTravel unsupervised split. The training loop generates multiple completions, obtains judge scores, computes group-relative advantages, and updates LoRA weights. A separate high-capability validator (Gemini-3-Flash) evaluates all final models using the same Todorovian criteria, ensuring consistent cross-condition assessment. SFT baselines are trained on the supervised split with standard cross-entropy loss. The study compares d-RLAIF against SFT across multiple model sizes and reward configurations, revealing trade-offs between surface similarity and narrative coherence.

## Key Results
- d-RLAIF models, especially with the narrativity reward (RN), achieve higher minLRC (e.g., Llama d-RLAIF-RN-Selene: 2.554 vs. SFT: 2.260) and narrativity scores (4.733 vs. 4.692).
- SFT models attain superior surface similarity (BLEU-4 up to 0.810) but lower holistic narrative quality.
- d-RLAIF produces more diverse and narratively aligned stories, with performance approaching human benchmarks (minLRC 2.811, narrativity 4.718).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Narrative theory principles, operationalized via LLM prompting, can generate reward signals that guide story generation toward higher narrativity and coherence.
- **Mechanism:** Todorov's five-stage structure (Equilibrium, Disruption, Recognition, Attempt, New Equilibrium) is translated into evaluation criteria (Logical, Rational, CompleteN). An LLM-as-judge, prompted with these criteria, scores candidate story endings. These scores (or minLRC aggregation) serve as rewards for GRPO, which updates the policy model to favor outputs judged high on these criteria.
- **Core assumption:** An LLM can reliably apply abstract narrative theory to score story quality in a way that correlates with human judgment and fosters narrative-aligned generation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "prompted with Todorovian principles... to provide reward signals during d-RLAIF"
  - [section 3] Develops annotation scheme from Todorov's theory; uses LLM-as-judge prompted with these criteria.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Rewarding Creativity" and "Can LLMs Generate Good Stories?" explore reward modeling for storytelling, providing indirect support for the viability of theory-informed reward signals, but do not specifically validate Todorovian prompting.
- **Break condition:** If the LLM-as-judge's application of Todorov's theory diverges substantially from human interpretation (as indicated by low inter-annotator agreement in Table 2), the reward signal becomes misaligned, and optimization may not improve perceived narrative quality.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with LoRA enables efficient fine-tuning of LLMs using sparse, ordinal reward signals from an LLM-as-judge without requiring a separate reward model.
- **Mechanism:** For each prompt, the policy model generates multiple completions. The LLM-as-judge scores each completion. GRPO computes relative advantages within the group based on these scores and updates the policy via a clipped surrogate objective, using LoRA to adapt a small subset of parameters. This comparative learning signal mitigates issues with absolute score scaling.
- **Core assumption:** The relative ranking of outputs from the same prompt is a more stable and informative learning signal than absolute scores, especially when using an imperfect LLM-as-judge.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4] "We use GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) to optimise the d-RLAIF training process."
  - [section 4] Describes early stopping based on reward plateau, indicating learning progress tracked via the LLM-as-judge's scores.
  - [corpus] "Semantically Aligned Task Decomposition in MARL" discusses subgoal generation but is not directly about GRPO for ASG; weak relevance.
- **Break condition:** If the reward distribution is too narrow or saturated (e.g., most outputs receive similar scores), GRPO suffers from gradient starvation, and the policy fails to improve meaningfully, as hinted by the authors' experiment with the 5-point scale.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Using a separate, high-quality LLM-as-judge (Gemini-3-Flash) for final evaluation provides a consistent, theory-anchored benchmark that reduces evaluator bias and isolates the effect of the training signal.
- **Mechanism:** After d-RLAIF training, all model outputs (including SFT and base models) are evaluated by a fixed, capable LLM-as-judge (Gemini-3-Flash) prompted with the Todorovian criteria. This "validator" is kept separate from the training judge (Selene/M-Prometheus) to avoid biasing results toward any particular judge's quirks and to approximate a consistent gold standard based on the theory.
- **Core assumption:** A single, high-capability LLM-as-judge can serve as a sufficiently consistent and theory-aligned proxy for human evaluation across all experimental conditions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We use Gemini-3-Flash to evaluate the output of our post-trained models"
  - [section 5] "We evaluate the performance... by prompting Gemini-3-Flash for evaluation based on our criteria."
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "NarraBench" propose comprehensive narrative evaluation frameworks, suggesting a need for consistent validators, but do not directly address this specific validator design.
- **Break condition:** If Gemini-3-Flash itself exhibits systematic biases (e.g., leniency/harshness) that differentially affect certain model outputs, the comparative results across training conditions may be misleading.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Todorov's Narrative Equilibrium**
  - **Why needed here:** It provides the theoretical framework to define and operationalize "narrative quality" for both evaluation and reward. Without such a theory, criteria remain ad-hoc and model learning is directionless.
  - **Quick check question:** Given a 5-sentence story, can you correctly tag each sentence with one of Todorov's five stages? (This tests understanding of the core schema used to build the reward.)
- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF/d-RLAIF)**
  - **Why needed here:** It replaces scarce/expensive human preference data with automated, scalable feedback from an LLM-as-judge. This is crucial for subjective tasks like ASG where human judgments are costly and diverse.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the key difference between RLHF and RLAIF, and why does d-RLAIF (direct) avoid the need for a separate reward model training phase?
- **Concept: GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization)**
  - **Why needed here:** It is the specific RL algorithm used. It handles ordinal, comparative rewards from multiple generations per prompt efficiently, making it suitable for the d-RLAIF setup with an LLM-as-judge.
  - **Quick check question:** How does GRPO compute the advantage function using scores from multiple outputs for the same prompt? (Hint: it uses the group mean and standard deviation.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Policy Model:** The target LLM (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B) being fine-tuned via LoRA to generate story endings.
  2. **LLM-as-Judge (Train-time):** A separate evaluator (Selene-1-mini or M-Prometheus) prompted with Todorovian criteria. It scores 16 generated endings per prompt.
  3. **GRPO Optimizer:** Takes the batch of prompts, policy outputs, and judge scores. Computes group-relative advantages and computes LoRA gradients.
  4. **Validator (Eval-time):** Gemini-3-Flash, a fixed high-quality judge, scores final model outputs on the test set.
  5. **Dataset:** TimeTravel, providing premise/initial/original/counterfactual for prompting.
- **Critical path:** For each training step: (a) Policy generates N endings per prompt. (b) Judge scores each ending. (c) GRPO calculates advantages based on judge scores within each prompt group. (d) LoRA weights updated. (e) Repeat until reward plateau.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Judge Speed vs. Accuracy:* Selene-1-mini is fast but less aligned; M-Prometheus is more aligned but slower. Tradeoff: training throughput vs. reward signal quality.
  - *Reward Granularity:* 3-point vs. 5-point Likert scale. Finer scale may offer more signal but can saturate if judge is not discriminative. (See RO5 experiment's mixed results.)
  - *Teacher Model Size:* Using an 8B teacher (Selene) for an 8B student (Llama) works, challenging the assumption that teacher > student is necessary.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Early convergence without reward plateau:** Policy stops improving before reaching high reward (e.g., Qwen/Olmo with Selene-RO). Signaled by flat reward curve.
  - **High reward, low minLRC:** Model learns to hack the specific judge's scoring rubric but fails on the more holistic metric (minLRC) or human-aligned validator. Indicates reward misspecification.
  - **Degraded CompleteN with RO5:** Reward granularity increase harms narrative structure retention. Suggests the judge struggles to consistently distinguish structural completeness.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Judge Alignment Audit:** On the curated 200-sample set, compute Gwet's AC2 and Cohen's κ for each judge (Selene, M-Prometheus, Gemini) vs. human avg. Identify which judge best balances accuracy and speed for training. *Expected: M-Prometheus for RO; Selene for RN.*
  2. **Baseline d-RLAIF Run:** Pick one policy model (e.g., Llama) and one judge (M-Prometheus/RO). Run full d-RLAIF, track reward curve and minLRC on validation set. Verify convergence to plateau. *Expected: Reward increases; minLRC improves modestly over base.*
  3. **SFT vs. d-RLAIF Comparison:** Train the same policy model on the same dataset with SFT (using human reference endings). Evaluate both SFT and d-RLAIF models on test set with Gemini-3-Flash on minLRC and Narrativity. *Expected: d-RLAIF (especially RN) achieves higher minLRC/Narrativity but lower BLEU-4 than SFT.*

## Limitations
- Unreported judge prompt details and Gemini-3-Flash version/API hinder reproduction.
- LLM-as-judge alignment with human narrativity judgment is moderate (low inter-annotator agreement), risking reward misspecification.
- Generalizability beyond the TimeTravel dataset remains untested.

## Confidence
- **Narrative theory-guided reward works**: Medium.
- **GRPO+LoRA efficiency**: Medium.
- **Validator reliability**: Low.
