# Plug-and-Play Diffusion Meets ADMM: Dual-Variable Coupling for Robust Medical Image Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv**: 2602.23214  
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23214  
- **References**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Dual-Coupled PnP Diffusion eliminates bias-hallucination trade-off, achieving up to +5.95 dB PSNR and ~3.3× faster convergence vs. state-of-the-arts on sparse-view CT and accelerated MRI.

## Executive Summary
Existing plug-and-play diffusion solvers for medical image reconstruction operate in a memoryless fashion, leading to steady-state bias. Restoring the ADMM dual variable provides integral feedback to eliminate bias, but introduces colored artifacts that violate the AWGN assumption of diffusion priors, causing hallucinations. This paper proposes Dual-Coupled PnP Diffusion, combining an explicit dual variable with Spectral Homogenization (SH) – a frequency-domain module that converts structured residuals into pseudo-AWGN inputs. On sparse-view CT and accelerated MRI, the method attains state-of-the-art fidelity with PSNR improvements up to +5.95 dB and ~3.3× faster convergence, effectively resolving the bias-hallucination trade-off.

## Method Summary
Dual-Coupled PnP Diffusion integrates ADMM with diffusion priors. The algorithm iterates: (1) solve data-consistency subproblem; (2) compute dual-shifted variable v = x+u; (3) apply Spectral Homogenization to transform v into a pseudo-AWGN input ˜v; (4) denoise ˜v using a pretrained diffusion model; (5) update dual variable u ← u + (x−z). The dual variable supplies integral feedback to enforce consensus between data-consistent and prior-updated estimates, while SH ensures the denoiser’s input remains statistically aligned with its training distribution. This synergy eliminates bias without hallucinations.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art reconstruction on sparse-view CT and accelerated MRI, with PSNR gains up to +5.95 dB over memoryless PnP diffusion baselines.
- Approximately 3.3× faster convergence (fewer NFEs) compared to existing methods.
- Effective removal of steady-state bias without introducing hallucinated textures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dual Variable as Integral Feedback
The ADMM dual variable u accumulates the primal residual (x−z) across iterations, behaving as an integrator that cancels the data fidelity gradient at convergence and enforces x=z. This guarantees asymptotic convergence to the exact data manifold, unlike memoryless HQS. Evidence: Section 2.2 derives ADMM and identifies u as an integrator; Section 3.1 presents the updates (Alg. 1). Ablation studies show bias elimination. Break condition: If the denoiser is not proximal (e.g., OOD inputs) or ρ is poorly chosen, convergence may degrade.

### Mechanism 2: Spectral Homogenization Prevents Hallucinations
Dual updates generate spectrally colored artifacts; SH estimates the residual PSD (using previous denoised estimate z), computes the spectral deficit relative to a white spectrum, synthesizes complementary noise with random phases, and adds it to v, yielding an approximately flat spectrum. This renders the input pseudo-AWGN, satisfying the diffusion prior’s training assumption. Evidence: Proposition 3.1 proves second-order spectral consistency; Figure 3 visualizes spectrum flattening. Break condition: An inaccurate z in early iterations can bias the PSD estimate, leading to under/over-correction; highly non-sparse artifacts may remain.

### Mechanism 3: Synergy Resolves the Trade-off
Dual integration ensures geometric correctness (bias elimination), while SH maintains statistical compliance (no hallucinations). Their tight coupling allows the solver to follow the optimal trajectory without triggering hallucinations, yielding higher fidelity and accelerated convergence. Evidence: Ablation (Table 2) shows dual-only improves +4.55 dB but full method adds another +1.1 dB; main results confirm SOTA gains and speedup. Limitations: Parameter sensitivity (ρ, SH kernel) and dependence on denoiser proximation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Data Fidelity Update → Dual-Shifted Interface → Spectral Homogenization → Diffusion Denoiser → Dual Variable Update → (loop back)

**Critical path** (per iteration):
1. Update x via data-consistency: x = argmin_x ||Ax−y||² + ρ||x−(z−u)||² (e.g., Conjugate Gradient).
2. Compute v = x + u.
3. Estimate residual r = v − z (using previous denoised image).
4. Apply SH: estimate PSD of r, compute spectral deficit ΔS, synthesize complementary noise ξ, set ˜v = v + ξ.
5. Denoise: z = D_θ(˜v, t) (diffusion denoiser at noise level t).
6. Update dual: u = u + (x−z).
7. Repeat.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Overhead*: SH adds O(N log N) FFTs, negligible compared to diffusion UNet.
- *Hyperparameters*: ρ controls step size; SH kernel bandwidth controls PSD smoothing; both require tuning per task.
- *Stability*: Large ρ may cause oscillations; SH must handle Hermitian symmetry for real images and complex MRI separately.

**Failure signatures**:
- *Persistent bias*: Large data fidelity ||Ax−y||²; check dual growth; may need larger ρ.
- *Hallucinations*: Unnatural high-frequency textures; disable SH to confirm; adjust SH kernel or clipping ϵ.
- *Slow convergence*: PSNR plateaus early; inspect spectral flattening – insufficient whitening.
- *Numerical errors*: Residual imaginary parts in real-valued outputs indicate FFT phase mishandling.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Ablation replication** on a small LACT-90 subset (∼10 slices): compare full method vs. (a) no dual/no SH, (b) dual only, (c) SH only. Expect dual-only to reduce bias but increase LPIPS (hallucinations); full method should dominate PSNR/SSIM.
2. **Spectral validation**: At iterations 5, 10, 20, compute PSD of v (pre‑SH) and ˜v (post‑SH). Plot against ideal white level (σ_t²·HW). Confirm SH flattens spectrum. Visualize denoised outputs to verify reduced high‑frequency hallucinations.
3. **Convergence comparison**: Plot PSNR vs. NFEs for DC‑PnPDP and DiffPIR on SVCT‑20 and LACT‑90. Verify that DC‑PnPDP reaches DiffPIR’s 100‑step PSNR in ∼30 steps (~3.3× speedup).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Convergence analysis**: Under which assumptions does Dual-Coupled PnP Diffusion converge, and at what rate? Current fixed‑point analysis assumes an exact proximal denoiser; diffusion priors are approximate, and stochastic settings with dual feedback remain unaddressed. A stochastic convergence proof with denoiser error bounds, complemented by consistent experimental convergence, would resolve this.
2. **Robustness of SH**: How sensitive is Spectral Homogenization to the accuracy of the residual estimate, and can a more robust alternative be developed? SH uses a single‑iteration bootstrap z^(k) as a clean signal, which may be noisy early on, biasing the PSD. Sensitivity tests (deliberately degrading z) and alternative estimators (e.g., averaging past iterates) are needed.
3. **Extension to non‑linear inverse problems**: Can the framework be extended to non‑linear forward operators while still eliminating bias? The current derivation assumes linear operators and quadratic data terms. Non‑linear data terms alter the x‑subproblem and dual residuals; it is unclear if SH can maintain AWGN compliance. Proposing an adaptation (e.g., linearized ADMM) and testing on phase retrieval would demonstrate viability.
4. **Integration with acceleration techniques**: Can momentum or other accelerations be combined with DC‑PnPDP to reduce NFEs without causing OOD inputs? Acceleration may increase spectral complexity of dual corrections, potentially overwhelming SH and destabilizing convergence. A momentum‑augmented variant (e.g., Nesterov‑accelerated ADMM) with tailored SH, showing faster convergence while preserving fidelity, would address this.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely on the diffusion denoiser acting as an exact proximal operator; deviation may compromise convergence or optimality.
- SH’s PSD estimation uses the previous denoised image, which can be inaccurate in early iterations, leading to sub‑optimal whitening.
- Evaluation is limited to CT and MRI; performance on other imaging modalities or non‑medical tasks remains untested.
