# Knowledge-informed Bidding with Dual-process Control for Online Advertising

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04920  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04920  
- **Reference count:** 11  
- **Primary result:** KBD achieves state-of-the-art normalized return 0.730 and 82.78% constraint satisfaction on iPinYou, with online lifts in GMV (+6.14% to +13.01%) and cost‑exhaust ratio (+8.44% to +14.55%).

## Executive Summary
Traditional black‑box bid optimization suffers from data sparsity, short‑sightedness, and vulnerability to distribution shifts. KBD tackles these issues by embedding human expertise into a two‑stage, knowledge‑informed framework. The macro stage employs IEFormer, an Informed Machine Learning model, to compute robust daily target CPA (tCPA) baselines. The micro stage then applies a dual‑process controller—fusing a rule‑based PID regulator (System 1) with a Decision Transformer (System 2)—to adjust bids hourly. Extensive evaluations on the public iPinYou benchmark and a real‑world e‑commerce platform demonstrate substantial improvements: SOTA metrics on iPinYou and significant online lifts in GMV, cost‑exhaust ratio, and campaign duration. Ablations confirm that every IML component and the dual‑process fusion are critical to performance and robustness.

## Method Summary
KBD’s macro stage uses IEFormer, an Informed Machine Learning architecture that incorporates domain knowledge (e.g., budget constraints, historical performance) to predict a daily tCPA baseline. This baseline anchors the bidding strategy towards long‑term goals. In the micro stage, for each hour, a dual‑process controller operates: a PID controller quickly reacts to immediate deviations in spend or performance (System 1), while a Decision Transformer optimizes the bid sequence over a multi‑step horizon (System 2). The two outputs are combined using a fixed fusion weight β, with the system dynamically favoring the DT when prediction uncertainty exceeds a threshold. This hierarchical design separates strategic planning from tactical adaptation, enabling resilience under data scarcity and distribution shifts.

## Key Results
- On iPinYou, KBD attains a normalized return of 0.730 and constraint satisfaction of 82.78%, establishing a new state‑of‑the‑art.
- Online A/B testing on a commercial e‑commerce platform shows relative improvements: cost‑exhaust ratio +8.44% to +14.55%, GMV +6.14% to +13.01%, and campaign duration +0.58% to +1.36%.
- Ablation studies reveal that removing any IML component or the dual‑process fusion leads to marked performance degradation, confirming their necessity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
KBD’s effectiveness stems from the complementary strengths of its hybrid components. The IEFormer macro model injects human knowledge (e.g., business rules) into learning, improving sample efficiency and producing stable long‑term targets. The dual‑process micro controller balances the PID’s fast, interpretable corrections with the Decision Transformer’s strategic, multi‑step optimization. This fusion allows the system to react swiftly to immediate anomalies while staying aligned with long‑term objectives. Moreover, the separation of macro and micro stages mitigates short‑sightedness and reduces sensitivity to distribution shifts: the macro baseline provides a steady reference, and the micro adaptation flexibly responds to changing conditions without drifting off course.

## Foundational Learning
**Informed Machine Learning (IML)**  
*Why needed:* Integrates domain knowledge (e.g., constraints, expert rules) into model training, crucial for data‑sparse scenarios.  
*Quick check:* Verify IEFormer’s ability to respect symbolic constraints by measuring constraint violations during validation.

**Decision Transformer (DT)**  
*Why needed:* Treats bidding as a conditional trajectory generation problem, capturing long‑horizon dependencies that standard RL or regression methods miss.  
*Quick check:* Evaluate DT’s offline performance by simulating its generated bid sequences against historical logs and measuring achieved cumulative return.

**PID Controller**  
*Why needed:* Provides a simple, stable, and fast response mechanism for immediate deviations, ensuring the system does not overshoot targets.  
*Quick check:* Run PID in isolation and monitor its ability to keep spend within thresholds under volatile traffic.

**tCPA (target Cost Per Acquisition)**  
*Why needed:* Serves as the primary performance metric guiding bid decisions; optimizing tCPA aligns with business goals of acquisition efficiency.  
*Quick check:* Confirm IEFormer’s daily tCPA predictions correlate with actual post‑auction outcomes.

**Dual‑process Fusion**  
*Why needed:* Marries the responsiveness of System 1 (PID) with the foresight of System 2 (DT), achieving both stability and adaptability.  
*Quick check:* Compare combined performance against each component alone to validate synergy.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**  
Data → IEFormer → Daily tCPA → DualProcessController (PID + Decision Transformer) → Hourly Bids → Auction Submission

**Critical Path**  
1. **Feature collection:** Gather real‑time auction features (user, context, historical performance).  
2. **Macro prediction:** IEFormer ingests aggregated data (budget, time, historical metrics) to produce the daily tCPA baseline.  
3. **Micro adjustment:** For each hour, DualProcessController receives current state (spend, remaining budget, time) and emits an hourly bid multiplier.  
   - PID computes an immediate correction based on error (e.g., overspend).  
   - DT generates a sequence‑aware adjustment conditioned on the tCPA target.  
   - The two are fused (β = 0.1 by default) with DT gaining weight when uncertainty (MAPE over last 3 hours) is high.  
4. **Bidding:** The resulting bid value is submitted to the ad exchange.  
5. **Feedback loop:** Post‑auction outcomes (clicks, conversions, cost) update internal state and uncertainty estimates.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *Interpretability vs. optimality:* PID offers clear rule‑based reasoning but may be suboptimal; DT can discover sophisticated strategies but is a black box.  
- *Static vs. adaptive fusion:* Fixed β simplifies implementation but may limit adaptability; a learned fusion policy could improve performance at the cost of added complexity.  
- *Human rule dependence:* The PID’s effectiveness hinges on expert‑crafted rules, which may not scale across diverse campaigns and could introduce bias.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Sudden drop in constraint satisfaction (e.g., < 70 %).  
- Excessive cost‑exhaust ratio far beyond the target.  
- Large oscillations in hourly spend indicative of unstable PID integral windup.  
- DT actions that repeatedly deviate wildly from the daily tCPA baseline.  
- Rising uncertainty threshold with no corresponding shift to DT dominance.

**First Experiments**  
1. **Ablation study:** Ablate PID, DT, and fusion individually to quantify each component’s contribution.  
2. **Parameter sensitivity:** Systematically vary β and the uncertainty threshold to assess robustness.  
3. **Online A/B test:** Deploy KBD against a production black‑box bidding system to measure GMV, cost‑exhaust, and campaign duration lifts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can LLMs be integrated into KBD to produce transparent, causal, and goal‑directed natural‑language explanations for bidding decisions?  
- **Basis:** explicit from Conclusion: authors propose future work with LLMs for explanations.  
- **Why unresolved:** Current KBD lacks any explanation generation capability; this remains unimplemented.  
- **What evidence:** Build an LLM module that consumes KBD’s internal states (e.g., price‑volume estimates, DT actions, PID adjustments) to produce human‑readable justifications, and evaluate via user studies or factual consistency metrics.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is an effective adaptive method to set dual‑process fusion parameters (β and uncertainty threshold) in KBD?  
- **Basis:** inferred from Section "Micro stage: Dual‑process Control with PID & DT": β fixed to 0.1 and uncertainty via MAPE over last 3 hours; no adaptation.  
- **Why unresolved:** Fixed heuristics may not generalize across campaigns; parameters likely need data‑driven tuning.  
- **What evidence:** Design a meta‑learning algorithm that learns these parameters from historical campaign data, and test robustness under distribution shift scenarios.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does joint training of IEFormer and DT yield better performance than the current separate training regime?  
- **Basis:** inferred from Section "Methodology" and Figure 1: macro and micro stages trained independently.  
- **Why unresolved:** Decoupled training may miss cross‑stage optimization opportunities; end‑to‑end learning could align objectives.  
- **What evidence:** Create an end‑to‑end differentiable KBD where gradients flow through DT into IEFormer, train on the same data, and compare key metrics (normalized return, constraint satisfaction) on iPinYou and ECA.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability:** Evaluations are limited to iPinYou and a single e‑commerce platform; performance on other RTB ecosystems (e.g., Tencent, Alibaba) remains unverified.  
- **Complexity:** The two‑stage dual‑process design likely incurs non‑trivial engineering and computational overhead, which has not been quantified.  
- **Expert knowledge dependence:** The PID controller relies on manually crafted rules, introducing subjectivity and potential scalability issues across diverse campaigns.
