# GONE: Structural Knowledge Unlearning via Neighborhood-Expanded Distribution Shaping

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12275
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12275
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** NEDS achieves perfect unlearning efficacy (1.000) on direct and paraphrase probes, strong locality (0.839 on Mistral-7B), and superior resistance to reasoning-based leakage as measured by Knowledge Connectivity Score (KCS) drop.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses a critical gap in machine unlearning for Large Language Models (LLMs): forgetting structured facts in Knowledge Graphs (KGs) without causing multi-hop leakage or catastrophic forgetting. Existing unlearning methods focus on flat, sentence-level data and fail to handle relational knowledge. The authors introduce two key contributions: (1) GONE, the first KG-derived unlearning benchmark built from Wikidata and ConceptNet, which includes multi-hop, inverse, and paraphrase probes to disentangle direct forgetting, reasoning leakage, and catastrophic forgetting; (2) NEDS (Neighborhood-Expanded Distribution Shaping), a novel framework that anchors correlated KG neighbors to enforce a precise decision boundary, suppressing the target fact while stabilizing its semantic neighborhood. Evaluations on LLaMA-3-8B and Mistral-7B show NEDS achieves state-of-the-art performance, with perfect unlearning efficacy, strong locality, and the largest drop in KCS, outperforming gradient-based, preference-based, and editing baselines.

## Method Summary
GONE is a benchmark for structural unlearning in LLMs. It samples factual triples from Wikidata (encyclopedic) and ConceptNet (commonsense) and constructs rigorous retain sets by enforcing topological orthogonality (geodesic distance > 3 and relation disjointness) to isolate unlearning from simple entity degradation. Probes are generated in QA and Fill-in-the-Blank formats covering direct, paraphrase, inverse, and multi-hop queries. NEDS is the proposed unlearning method: it fine-tunes the LLM via LoRA with a dual objective. The forget loss (NPO) suppresses the likelihood of the target fact, while the anchor loss preserves the likelihood on a set of semantically correlated neighbor facts mined from the KG. The combined loss shapes the output distribution boundary so that the forgotten fact is separated from its neighborhood, preventing both insufficient forgetting and collateral damage. Evaluation metrics include Unlearning Efficacy (UE), Locality, Knowledge Connectivity Score (ΔKCS), and Refusal Rate.

## Key Results
- Perfect unlearning efficacy (UE = 1.000) on direct and paraphrase probes for both LLaMA-3-8B and Mistral-7B.
- Strong locality scores (0.839 on Mistral-7B, 0.793 on LLaMA-3-8B) indicating minimal forgetting of unrelated knowledge.
- Largest negative ΔKCS drop among all baselines, demonstrating superior resistance to reasoning-based leakage through multi-hop and inverse paths.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** NEDS achieves precise unlearning by anchoring correlated KG neighbors to shape the output distribution boundary.
- **Mechanism:** For a target fact (x_t, y_t), NEDS minimizes the NPO loss to suppress y_t's probability while simultaneously minimizing cross-entropy loss on a set of semantically correlated neighbor facts N(t). This dual objective forces the parameter update to occur within the null space of neighbor gradients, creating a distributional separation where P(y_t|x_t) decreases relative to P(yn|x_n) for all n ∈ N(t).
- **Core assumption:** Knowledge about a fact is locally distributed; altering the target fact's activation will affect nearby representations unless explicitly constrained by anchoring.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] Describes the anchor loss L_anchor that explicitly preserves neighborhood probabilities.
  - [section 3.3 Proposition 2] Formally states that anchoring acts as approximate null-space projection.
  - [section 5.1] Shows NEDS achieves high direct UE (1.000) with strong locality (0.839 on Mistral), outperforming NPO which has weaker inverse/multi-hop UE.
- **Break condition:** If the mined neighbor set N(t) is semantically disconnected from the target fact (e.g., due to graph sparsity or noisy mining), the anchoring constraints fail to define a meaningful boundary, leading to either insufficient forgetting or collateral damage.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The GONE benchmark's topological orthogonality enforcement prevents confounding unlearning efficacy with general entity degradation.
- **Mechanism:** The retain set is constructed by ensuring geodesic distance d(t, t') > 3 and schema/relation disjointness between forget and retain facts. This creates a guaranteed "topological barrier" where no latent multi-hop inference path exists from retained facts to the forgotten target, isolating evaluation of true unlearning from simple entity forgetting.
- **Core assumption:** Factual knowledge in KGs has graph-theoretic structure; if a retained fact is >3 hops away and relationally disjoint, the model cannot infer the forgotten target from it.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.3 & Figure 2] Explicitly defines the three-stage filtration (schema separation, node disjointness, BFS depth check) to enforce d(t, t') > 3.
  - [section 4.3] States that KCS Drop measures multi-hop leakage, relying on this orthogonality to attribute residual connectivity solely to the unlearning method's failure.
- **Break condition:** If the KG sampling or BFS verification is incomplete, hidden short paths (<3 hops) may exist between forget and retain sets, causing false negatives in locality measurement.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The Knowledge Connectivity Score (KCS) quantifies deep unlearning by measuring structural erosion in the model's latent knowledge graph.
- **Mechanism:** KCS computes mean ROUGE-L recall between model outputs and ground truth across all neighbor probes (paraphrase, inverse, multi-hop). A large negative ΔKCS indicates the model's internal representation of the target fact's neighborhood has been disrupted, not just surface-level suppression.
- **Core assumption:** If a model truly unlearns a fact, it cannot consistently reconstruct it via any relational path (direct, inverse, multi-hop) from the neighborhood; its predictions on neighbor probes will diverge from ground truth.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3] Defines KCS as mean ROUGE-L over N_f and ΔKCS = KCS_post - KCS_pre.
  - [section 5.1 & Figure 3] Reports ΔKCS results, showing NEDS achieves larger negative drops (better) than baselines.
  - [section 2.1] States GONE disentangles "direct forgetting" from "reasoning-based leakage" via multi-hop probes.
- **Break condition:** If probe generation (especially multi-hop chains) is inconsistent or ambiguous, KCS may penalize valid alternative answers rather than measuring true knowledge loss.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Knowledge Graph (KG) Structure
  - **Why needed:** The entire method (GONE benchmark and NEDS) relies on representing facts as triples (h, r, t) and reasoning over graph topology (hops, neighbors, paths). Without understanding KGs, one cannot grasp why multi-hop probes or topological orthogonality matter.
  - **Quick check:** Given triples (Einstein, citizenship, Germany) and (Germany, capital, Berlin), what is the 2-hop answer to "What is the capital of Einstein's citizenship country?" How would you construct a retain set topologically orthogonal to forgetting "Einstein's citizenship"?

- **Concept:** Distributional Divergence as Unlearning Signal
  - **Why needed:** NEDS and NPO optimize in output space (token probability distributions), not parameter space directly. The core mechanism (creating a decision boundary) is defined via log-probability ratios and KL divergence between pre- and post-unlearning distributions.
  - **Quick check:** If before unlearning, log P(y_forget|x_forget) > log P(refuse|x_forget), and after unlearning you want the reverse while keeping log P(y_neighbor|x_neighbor) ≈ constant, what two loss terms would you combine?

- **Concept:** LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) for Efficient Fine-Tuning
  - **Why needed:** All experiments use LoRA (rank r=16, α=32) to update models. This is a critical implementation detail; NEDS's gradients are applied to low-rank matrices, not full weights, affecting how neighborhood constraints propagate.
  - **Quick check:** How does LoRA's low-rank constraint interact with the anchor loss? Could anchoring be less effective if the neighbor gradients cannot be fully expressed in the limited LoRA subspace?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Data Pipeline: KG Sampler → Probe Generator → Verification Filter → Known Probe Identification
Training Core: Frozen reference model π₀ → Trainable LoRA model π_θ → Loss Calculator (Forget NPO loss + Anchor loss weighted by λ) → AdamW optimizer
Evaluation Engine: Probe Tester (QA/FB templates) → Metric Aggregator (UE, Locality, KCS, Refusal Rate) → Result Comparator

**Critical path:**
1. Construct GONE benchmark: sample target triples → enforce d(t,t')>3 for retain → generate multi-hop/paraphrase/inverse probes → filter to known probes.
2. For each training triple: mine top-k=10 correlated neighbors via KG connectivity or embedding similarity.
3. During training: for batch, compute L_NPO on (x_i, y_i) and L_anchor on all n ∈ N(i); sum with λ.
4. After training: evaluate on full probe suite (direct, paraphrase, inverse, multi-hop, retain) → compute UE, Locality, ΔKCS.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Neighbor size (k): Larger k provides more stable anchoring but risks including loosely related facts, weakening the boundary. Paper uses k=10 after empirical balancing.
- λ weight: Higher λ enforces stricter neighborhood preservation but may impede forgetting; tuned via Hmean of UE and Locality.
- Probe template diversity: QA vs FB formats test different aspects (generative vs cloze); using both increases benchmark robustness but complicates analysis.
- KG source: Wikidata provides clean functional relations for strict multi-hop; ConceptNet tests associative knowledge but introduces ambiguity.

**Failure signatures:**
- High direct UE + low inverse/multi-hop UE: Indicates shallow suppression (surface refusal) without deep unlearning; model can still reason to the fact.
- Low locality + high UE: Catastrophic forgetting; anchor loss failed or neighbors were not truly orthogonal.
- High refusal rate on retain set: Model became overly conservative; likely due to excessive λ or poor retain set construction.
- ΔKCS near zero: Model's neighborhood predictions remain unchanged; unlearning did not disrupt the latent knowledge structure.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline replication: Train NPO on a small subset (n=100) of GONE triples. Verify that direct UE improves but inverse/multi-hop UE remains low, confirming Proposition 1's "local drift" propagation.
2. Ablation on neighbor corruption: Replace X% of N(i) with random distant entities (Appendix C). Expect graceful UE degradation and potential locality improvement; catastrophic failure would indicate over-reliance on anchor integrity.
3. Decision boundary validation: Compute pre/post unlearning P(y|x) for forget and retain probes (Appendix B). Expect NEDS to show increased ROC-AUC and log-prob gap versus NPO, confirming distributional separation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can GONE be extended to support evaluation of unlearning for complex compositional and cross-domain reasoning beyond triplet facts? The current benchmark focuses on relations derived from triplets and does not include more complex compositional or cross-domain reasoning. Extending to broader domains and richer reasoning settings is an important direction.
- **Open Question 2:** How does NEDS perform on closed-source large language models and against unlearning methods not included in the current study (e.g., SOUL)? Evaluation is limited to GONE, RWKU, and open-source models; the scope does not encompass all unlearning or model editing methods.
- **Open Question 3:** How robust is NEDS to imperfections in the external knowledge graph, such as missing edges, incorrect relations, or incomplete neighbor sets? NEDS relies on the KG to mine correlated neighbors, assuming the KG accurately reflects the model's semantic structure. Real-world KG noise may be more structured and informative, and its impact is not fully explored.
- **Open Question 4:** Why are commonsense facts from ConceptNet significantly harder to unlearn than encyclopedic facts from Wikidata, and how can unlearning algorithms be improved for such associative knowledge? The paper observes that gradient-based methods show limited efficacy on ConceptNet, and all methods struggle to achieve high unlearning efficacy without collapsing locality. The underlying cause is not analyzed.

## Limitations
- Neighbor mining details (similarity metric, candidate pool, top-k selection) are unspecified, undermining anchor precision.
- Anchor loss parameters (win, λ) are not fully disclosed; tuning may be crucial.
- Probe generation quality (especially multi-hop chains) is not validated; ambiguous probes may lead to metric errors.
- Topological orthogonality (d(t,t')>3) relies on BFS depth; incomplete KG coverage may allow hidden inference paths.
- Experiments are limited to two 7-8B models; scalability to larger or different architectures is unknown.

## Confidence
- Anchoring yields precise unlearning: Medium (theoretical guarantee contingent on neighbor set quality; empirical results strong but neighbor mining is a black box).
- GONE benchmark isolates unlearning from entity forgetting: Medium (BFS check is sound in principle but potential KG sparsity issues).
- KCS captures deep (reasoning) unlearning: Medium (metric correlates with leakage but depends on probe generation fidelity).

## Next Checks
1. Neighbor mining ablation: Systematically vary neighbor selection strategy (graph hops ≤2, embedding cosine similarity, shared relations) and k size; report UE, locality, and ΔKCS to establish robustness.
2. Orthogonality verification: Perform exhaustive BFS (depth ≤5) on the full Wikidata/ConceptNet subgraphs used; quantify any forget-retain paths <4 hops and correlate violations with locality drops.
3. Decision boundary validation: On a held-out set of forget and retain probes, compute model output probabilities; measure ROC-AUC and log-probability gap pre/post unlearning for NEDS vs baselines to confirm boundary separation.
