# FoMo X: Modular Explainability Signals for Outlier Detection Foundation Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17570  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17570  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Modular auxiliary heads achieve efficient outlier explanation with <2% overhead, strong correlation to MC-dropout uncertainty (~99% Spearman synthetic), and sharp error stratification on real data.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the explainability gap in zero-shot tabular outlier detection foundation models (PFNs), which are effective but computationally black-box. It introduces FoMo-X, a modular framework that attaches lightweight auxiliary heads for severity scoring and uncertainty estimation to frozen pretrained PFN embeddings. These heads are trained offline using the same simulator-based prior, distilling expensive signals (e.g., MC-dropout) into cheap, single-pass inference. The approach yields negligible runtime overhead while providing interpretable outlier diagnostics that transfer from synthetic training to real-world benchmarks.

## Method Summary
FoMo-X builds on a pretrained PFN (FoMo-0D) by freezing its backbone and appending two shallow neural heads: a Severity head (predicts outlier magnitude) and an Uncertainty head (predicts epistemic uncertainty). Both heads are trained offline on synthetic data generated from a Gaussian mixture model prior, where ground-truth severity and uncertainty labels are cheaply computed. This distills computationally expensive signals (e.g., MC-dropout ensembles) into immediate predictions at test time. The modular design ensures the explanation mechanism is decoupled from the core detector, enabling zero-shot application without retraining the foundation model.

## Key Results
- Severity head achieves 59% balanced accuracy on synthetic tasks and stratifies real-world errors sharply (0.58% mistakes in "Surely Normal" vs. 15.15% in "Likely Normal").
- Uncertainty head attains ~99% Spearman correlation with MC-dropout on synthetic data and >90% correlation on ADBench.
- Inference overhead is negligible (<2%, ~36 ns per sample).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works because it decouples explanation from detection by leveraging frozen, pretrained embeddings that already encode rich prior knowledge about outliers. The auxiliary heads are trained to mimic expensive, high-fidelity signals (MC-dropout, density-based severity) within the simulator’s distribution, allowing them to produce faithful approximations in a single forward pass. This avoids the cost of post-hoc explanation techniques and ensures the explanations are grounded in the same prior that shaped the foundation model’s outlier representations.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Simulator (GMM prior) → Pretrained PFN (FoMo-0D) → Frozen Embeddings → [Severity Head / Uncertainty Head] → Explanations  

**Critical path:** Input table → PFN embeds each row → Heads produce per-row severity/uncertainty scores → Output outlier scores with explanation.  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Frozen embeddings preserve pretrained knowledge and minimize overhead but limit adaptation to new data distributions.  
- Lightweight heads are cheap and fast but may lack capacity to fully replicate complex signals like MC-dropout.  
- Simulator-based training is cost-effective but introduces a domain gap to real data.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Severity head fails to stratify error rates on real data (e.g., no gap between "Surely Normal" and "Likely Normal").  
- Uncertainty head correlation with MC-dropout drops significantly below 0.9 on real benchmarks.  
- Global heads (e.g., AUROC predictor) yield high error on ADBench, indicating prior misalignment.

**First three experiments:**  
1. Evaluate Severity/Uncertainty heads on the full ADBench suite and analyze performance vs. dataset meta-features (e.g., dimensionality, contamination rate).  
2. Probe frozen embeddings with linear classifiers for global properties (dataset AUROC, optimal threshold) to diagnose why global heads fail.  
3. Ablate head architecture (depth, width) and training data diversity to measure impact on real-world transfer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why do global diagnostic heads (e.g., predicting AUROC or optimal thresholds) fail to transfer from synthetic to real outlier detection datasets, while local heads (severity, uncertainty) succeed?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors explicitly state: "Not all explainability targets transfer equally well from synthetic priors to real data—heads predicting global, dataset-level properties exhibit strong prior dependence and limited generalization." They demonstrate that AUROC and threshold heads achieve high correlation on synthetic tasks but collapse on ADBench.
- **Why unresolved:** Identifying the underlying reasons (e.g., information bottleneck, representational mismatch) is crucial for designing future heads that provide reliable diagnostics under domain shift.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative probing of frozen embeddings to assess what information is retained for global vs local targets; correlation analysis of head performance with dataset meta-features; architectural modifications to head aggregation mechanisms.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can we close the simulator-real gap in the training prior for outlier detection foundation models to improve both detection performance and explainability head transfer?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors visualize (Figure 9) that many ADBench datasets lie outside the support of the synthetic training distribution and state: "Closing the simulator–real gap [...] are therefore key steps toward broader and more faithful explainability." They suggest richer priors and continued pretraining with real data.
- **Why unresolved:** The current GMM-based prior is too simplistic and does not capture key characteristics of real tabular data (e.g., complex dependencies, heavy tails), limiting the model's practical utility.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of more expressive simulators (e.g., using non-Gaussian mixture models, incorporating causal structures) or adaptation of the foundation model on a diverse collection of real OD datasets; evaluation of head transfer and detection AUROC on ADBench.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can we redesign the architecture of PFN-based outlier detectors to preserve feature identity and scale beyond the 100-feature limit, thereby enabling faithful feature-level explanations (e.g., feature attributions, counterfactuals)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors explicitly criticize: "FoMo-0D embeds all raw features through a single dense projection into a fixed token space, which enforces a hard feature limit (100 features) and mixes feature identity early, making it difficult to attach auxiliary heads that produce faithful, column-aligned feature-level explanations."
- **Why unresolved:** Feature-level explanations are essential for providing actionable insights (e.g., root cause analysis) in safety-critical applications, but the current architecture precludes them.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Proposal and empirical evaluation of alternative architectures (e.g., column-wise tokenization, hierarchical feature embeddings) that maintain outlier detection performance while allowing post-hoc feature attribution methods to be applied reliably.

## Limitations
- Severity head modest (59% on synthetic); real validation limited.
- Global heads fail on real data, hindering model-level explanations.
- Slight fidelity drop in Uncertainty head from synthetic to real.
- Reliance on frozen PFN embeddings may propagate biases.
