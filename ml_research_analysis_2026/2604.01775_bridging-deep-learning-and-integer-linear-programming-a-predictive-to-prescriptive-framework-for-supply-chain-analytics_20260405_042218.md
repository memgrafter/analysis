# Bridging Deep Learning and Integer Linear Programming: A Predictive-to-Prescriptive Framework for Supply Chain Analytics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.01775  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01775  
- **Reference count**: 24  
- **Primary result**: N-BEATS forecasting (SMAPE 27.85%) integrated into ILP reduces delivery time by ~21% vs random baseline.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the mismatch between inaccurate demand forecasts and logistics decisions by proposing a predictive-to-prescriptive framework for supply chain analytics. Using a retail transaction dataset, the authors first conduct exploratory data analysis to estimate shipping parameters; then they compare a statistical method (MSTL) with deep learning models (N-BEATS, N-HiTS) for weekly demand forecasting; finally, they feed the most accurate forecast into an integer linear program (ILP) that optimizes shipping mode allocation under budget, capacity, and service constraints. The results show that N-BEATS dramatically outperforms MSTL (SMAPE 27.85% vs 69.08%) and that the ILP reduces total weighted delivery time by approximately 21% compared to a random baseline, while satisfying all constraints. The study highlights the practical benefits of combining modern forecasting with prescriptive optimization, though it also exposes limitations such as the use of a single point forecast and the absence of uncertainty considerations.

## Method Summary
The research uses the DataCo Global Supply Chain dataset (180,519 transactions aggregated to 162 weekly observations). The pipeline consists of three stages: (1) Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) estimates shipping mode parameters—average delivery times \(t_i\), proxy costs \(c_i\), maximum weekly capacities \(K_i\), and identifies a fast-service subset \(F\) for service-level constraints. (2) Forecasting models—MSTL (multiple seasonality decomposition), N-BEATS, and N-HiTS—are trained on weeks 1–158 and evaluated on weeks 159–162 using SMAPE and MAE. N-BEATS achieves the lowest SMAPE (27.85%) and its 4‑week total forecast (1,918 units) becomes the demand parameter \(D_{\text{total}}\). (3) An ILP minimizes total weighted delivery time \(\sum t_i x_i\) subject to: \(\sum x_i = D_{\text{total}}\); \(\sum c_i x_i \leq B_{\text{max}}\); \(x_i \leq K_i\); \(\sum_{i \in F} x_i \geq \alpha D_{\text{total}}\); and \(x_i\) integer ≥ 0. The ILP solution is benchmarked against a random allocation, revealing a ~21% improvement in the objective while meeting all constraints.

## Key Results
- N-BEATS yields a SMAPE of 27.85%, far surpassing MSTL’s 69.08% and N-HiTS’s 31.40%.
- The forecasted total demand over 4 weeks is 1,918 units.
- The ILP reduces total weighted delivery time by ~21% relative to a random baseline while respecting budget, capacity, and service-level constraints.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Deep learning models (N-BEATS/N-HiTS) outperform statistical decomposition (MSTL) on multi-seasonal, noisy retail demand by learning nonlinear patterns without additive structural assumptions.
- **Mechanism**: MLP-based architectures with residual stacks directly approximate trend and seasonality as basis functions, capturing complex interactions that MSTL's sequential Loess-based additive decomposition (trend + multiple seasonals + remainder) cannot when seasonality amplitudes grow or patterns are irregular.
- **Core assumption**: Retail demand volatility and overlapping seasonal cycles violate MSTL's assumptions of smooth, stable seasonality and constant variance, leading to underfitting.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - MSTL SMAPE 69.08% vs. N-BEATS 27.85%.
  - Deep learning models closely follow real demand movements; MSTL captures long-term patterns but underestimates short-term fluctuations.
- **Break condition**: If demand exhibits stable, well-separated seasonality with minimal noise and no nonlinear promotional effects, MSTL's interpretability and robustness may yield comparable accuracy with less computational cost.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Integrating a point forecast into a deterministic ILP transforms predictive output into an operationally feasible, cost-optimal shipping plan that balances speed, cost, and service constraints.
- **Mechanism**: The forecast (1,918 units) becomes a fixed right-hand-side parameter \(D_{\text{total}}\) in the ILP. The solver then minimizes total weighted delivery time \(\sum t_i x_i\) subject to budget (\(\sum c_i x_i \le B\)), capacity (\(x_i \le K_i\)), and service-level (\(\sum_{i \in F} x_i \ge \alpha D_{\text{total}}\)) constraints, yielding a concrete allocation \(x_i\).
- **Core assumption**: A single accurate point estimate is sufficient for optimization; constraint parameters (\(t_i, c_i, K_i, B\)) are stable and known, and the solution remains feasible under small demand realization errors.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - ILP solution reduced total weighted delivery time by ~21% versus a random baseline while meeting budget and service constraints.
  - ILP formulation with explicit constraints and decision variables \(x_i\); allocation results satisfy all constraints.
- **Break condition**: If forecast error is large (±20%+), the ILP's optimal plan may become infeasible or highly suboptimal when actual demand differs from \(D_{\text{total}}\).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The framework's simplicity (single forecast → single ILP) and use of interpretable models (N-BEATS, ILP) enable practical adoption by medium-sized firms without specialized AI/optimization teams.
- **Mechanism**: The three-stage pipeline—EDA to extract \(t_i, c_i, K_i\); off-the-shelf deep learning (N-BEATS) for forecasting; standard ILP solver for allocation—avoids end-to-end complexity, stochastic programming, or custom neural‑optimization hybrids. Each component uses established tools and clear inputs/outputs.
- **Core assumption**: Operational parameters (delivery times, costs, capacities) are static and estimable from historical data; business users can understand and trust each stage's output.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - The methodology is uncomplicated and does not necessitate significant processing power; medium-sized companies might utilize a similar structure.
  - Table 1 lists concrete, sourced parameters (delivery times from EDA, costs/capacities assumed).
- **Break condition**: If constraints are dynamic, non-linear, or involve uncertainty (e.g., stochastic capacity, price volatility), the deterministic ILP with static parameters fails to capture operational realities.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Time Series Decomposition (MSTL)  
  **Why needed here**: To establish a statistical benchmark that isolates trend and multiple seasonalities (weekly/yearly) for comparison against deep learning. MSTL's failure highlights the need for nonlinear models.  
  **Quick check**: Given a series with multiplicative seasonality and increasing amplitude, why might MSTL's additive model produce biased forecasts?

- **Concept**: MLP-based Forecasting (N-BEATS)  
  **Why needed here**: Core predictive engine. Its basis function expansion and residual stacking allow it to model complex, non‑stationary demand patterns without manual feature engineering.  
  **Quick check**: How does N-BEATS' interpretable configuration explicitly model trend and seasonality within its architecture?

- **Concept**: Integer Linear Programming (ILP)  
  **Why needed here**: Translates the forecast into a discrete, constrained optimization problem (shipping allocation) that respects real‑world logistics limitations (budget, capacity, service levels).  
  **Quick check**: In the given ILP, what role does the parameter \(\alpha\) (minimum fast‑shipping share) play in shaping the feasible solution set?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
EDA Module -> Forecasting Module -> Prescriptive Module

### Critical path
Data aggregation → EDA → model training/selection → ILP formulation → solution extraction.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Accuracy vs. Simplicity**: Chose N-BEATS over N-HiTS due to marginally lower SMAPE, despite N-HiTS' theoretical advantages for hierarchical forecasts.
- **Deterministic vs. Stochastic**: Used point forecast in deterministic ILP for simplicity, sacrificing robustness to demand uncertainty.
- **Data usage**: Preserved raw quantities (no log/Box‑Cox) for interpretability, potentially harming model performance on heteroscedastic data.

### Failure signatures
- **High forecasting SMAPE** (>40%): Model mismatch; revisit EDA, try alternative architectures (e.g., Transformers), or include exogenous variables.
- **Infeasible ILP**: Check parameter consistency (e.g., \(B_{\text{max}}\) too low, \(\sum K_i < D_{\text{total}}\)) or relax constraints (increase \(B_{\text{max}}\), decrease \(\alpha\)).
- **Poor baseline comparison**: If random baseline beats "optimal" ILP, likely error in ILP objective/constraint coding or parameter sourcing.

### First 3 experiments
1. **EDA Validation**: Replicate EDA figures; confirm multi-seasonality (weekly/yearly) and parameter distributions (delivery times, volumes per mode). If seasonality is univariate, MSTL may be sufficient.
2. **Forecast Benchmarks**: Train all three models on training set (weeks 1‑158); compute MAE/SMAPE on test (159‑162). If N-BEATS/N-HiTS don't outperform MSTL by >10% SMAPE, investigate data leakage or incorrect model configuration.
3. **ILP Sanity Check**: With known \(D_{\text{total}}\) and parameters, solve ILP manually for a trivial case (e.g., two modes). Verify that the solution minimizes \(\sum t_i x_i\) and satisfies constraints. Then run with actual parameters; check solution for constraint violations and logical allocation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Do deep learning models like N-BEATS and N-HiTS forecast better than MSTL?  
  *Basis*: Explicitly stated as the first research question.  
  *Why unresolved*: Comparison is limited to a single retail dataset and a fixed 4‑week horizon; generalizability to other environments, longer horizons, or different data characteristics is unclear. No explanation of the performance gap.  
  *Resolution evidence*: Replication across diverse retail datasets, multiple horizons, and ablation studies to isolate factors driving superiority.

- **Open Question 2**: If forecasts are more accurate, does that actually help improve operational decisions?  
  *Basis*: Explicitly stated as the second research question.  
  *Why unresolved*: Evidence is limited to one ILP context (minimizing weighted delivery time) and a specific constraint set. Causal link may not hold for other objectives, constraint structures, or supply chain functions.  
  *Resolution evidence*: Systematic experiments manipulating forecast accuracy (e.g., adding noise) and measuring optimization outcomes across various decision problems and industries.

- **Open Question 3**: Can AI forecasting be incorporated into an integer linear optimization model to come up with a specific delivery plan?  
  *Basis*: Explicitly stated as the third research question.  
  *Why unresolved*: The paper demonstrates deterministic integration using a point forecast, but the ILP ignores forecast uncertainty, potentially yielding suboptimal or infeasible plans under demand deviation. The challenge of robustly incorporating probabilistic forecasts remains unaddressed.  
  *Resolution evidence*: Development and testing of stochastic or robust ILP frameworks that consume probabilistic forecasts (e.g., prediction intervals from N-BEATS), with comparative evaluations under various demand scenarios.

## Limitations
- **Reproduction blockers**: Exact hyperparameters for N-BEATS/N-HiTS and precise ILP parameter derivations are undisclosed; values are described as "assumed" or shown only in figures.
- **Conceptual limits**: Deterministic ILP uses a single point forecast, ignoring demand uncertainty; external validity to other supply chain contexts is unclear.
- **Evaluation gaps**: Baseline comparison is against a random allocation, not a heuristic or industry practice; no cost‑benefit analysis of framework adoption.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter replication**: Implement N-BEATS/N-HiTS using default configurations from original papers (e.g., 3 blocks, 256 hidden units) and retrain on the provided train/test split; verify SMAPE within ±2% of reported 27.85%.
2. **Parameter sourcing audit**: From the DataCo dataset, compute \(t_i\) as mean transit time per shipping mode, \(c_i\) as median cost, \(K_i\) as historical max weekly volume per mode; compare to the paper’s sample values to ensure consistency.
3. **Robustness stress test**: Perturb \(D_{\text{total}}\) by ±15–20% (simulating forecast error), resolve the ILP, and record constraint violations and percentage change in objective; if infeasible, explore chance‑constrained ILP as a remedial extension.
