# SALMUBench: A Benchmark for Sensitive Association-Level Multimodal Unlearning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26316
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26316
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces the first benchmark for association-level unlearning in contrastive vision-language models, revealing three distinct failure modes (ineffective forgetting, over-generalization, catastrophic damage) across nine evaluated methods, with no current approach simultaneously achieving high forgetting efficacy and preserved utility.

## Executive Summary
SALMUBench addresses the gap in evaluating unlearning for *association-level* forgetting—removing specific persona-attribute links—in contrastive vision-language models like CLIP. The authors construct SALMU, a large-scale synthetic dataset of 60K image-text pairs with fictitious personally identifiable information, and train two CLIP ViT-B/16 models from a 400M-pair retain base: a **Clean** model (retain only) and a **Compromised** model (retain plus sensitive data). A novel evaluation protocol uses structured holdout sets—*forget* (target deletion), *holdout identity* (inter-identity collateral), and *holdout association* (intra-identity collateral)—with metrics combining forgetting efficacy (e.g., RetFail, AssocStr) and utility impact (e.g., GenKnow, InterIdSim). Evaluating nine unlearning methods uncovers three consistent failure patterns, demonstrating a fundamental trade-off: methods that effectively forget either underperform, over-forget, or catastrophically damage the model’s general knowledge and representational structure.

## Method Summary
The core methodology involves generating SALMU via a multi-stage pipeline: synthesizing identity-anchored images with IP-Adapter-FaceID Plus + RealVisXL, outpainting, filtering, assigning PII via `faker`, and paraphrasing captions with an LLM. Hierarchical splits create forget, holdout identity, and holdout association sets. Two foundational CLIP models are trained using OpenCLIP hyperparameters (32 epochs, batch 8192, LR 5e-4). Unlearning baselines (e.g., Generic Captions, DELETE, Direct Similarity Minimization) are applied to the **Compromised** model with varying compute budgets (1×–20×). The evaluation harness computes efficacy metrics on the forget set and utility/collateral metrics on holdout and external (ImageNet) sets, comparing results statistically to the **Clean** and **Compromised** references.

## Key Results
- **Benchmark novelty:** SALMUBench is the first to target association-level unlearning in CLIP, introducing holdout sets for collateral damage measurement.
- **Three failure modes:** Ineffective forgetting (Generic Captions: RetFail 0.055), over-generalization (DELETE/CLIPErase: RetFail ~0.001 but InterIdSim >0.02), and catastrophic damage (Direct Sim. Min.: GenKnow 0.615, negative structural metrics).
- **Pareto gap:** No evaluated method simultaneously matches the **Clean** model’s forgetting efficacy and the **Compromised** model’s utility preservation, highlighting substantial room for improvement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Ineffective Forgetting via Insufficient Embedding Disruption.** Methods like Generic Captions fail because fine-tuning on forget images with non-sensitive captions provides only a weak, indirect signal that does not sufficiently alter the embedding space’s sensitive connections. The contrastive loss aligns images with generic captions but does not explicitly penalize the original association, leaving the representation largely intact. Evidence: Generic Captions yields high RetFail (0.055), indicating the sensitive retrieval remains strong, and the weak corpus signal (avg FMR 0.446) shows minimal disruption.

**Mechanism 2: Over-Generalization via Aggressive Similarity Minimization.** Methods like DELETE and CLIPErase apply a strong, non-specific penalty on image-text similarity for *any* pair from the forget set. Since sensitive captions share structural patterns (e.g., `{name} {value}`), the method learns to dissociate the identity’s visual features from *any* mention of that identity or similar syntax, affecting unseen associations for the same persona (IntraIdSim) or other personas (InterIdSim). Evidence: These methods achieve near-perfect RetFail but show elevated InterIdSim/IntraIdSim and AssocStr below the Clean baseline (0.142), indicating over-correction.

**Mechanism 3: Catastrophic Damage via Indiscriminate Embedding Space Disruption.** Direct methods like Direct Similarity Minimization and Negative Gradient push embeddings apart without constraints, distorting the global topology learned from the massive retain set. This collapses the contrastive structure needed for zero-shot reasoning and can invert semantic relationships (negative similarities). Evidence: GenKnow drops to 0.615, structural metrics become negative, and a sanity check—applying the method to the **Clean** model—still degrades metrics, proving inherent destructiveness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Contrastive Learning in Dual-Encoder Models (CLIP):** SALMUBench targets CLIP’s InfoNCE loss; unlearning must operate within this alignment framework. *Quick check:* If you minimize cosine similarity for a specific image-text pair without a compensating retention loss, what other pairs’ similarities are likely to be affected, and why?  
  *Answer:* Affected pairs share visual or textual features; the embedding space is shared, so pushing one pair apart induces global shifts that may degrade alignment for semantically related pairs.

- **Synthetic Data for Privacy-Preserving Evaluation:** Synthetic images and fictitious PII provide a controlled, reproducible environment where the sensitive associations are precisely known, avoiding ethical/legal issues of real PII. *Quick check:* Why is paraphrasing captions with an LLM critical for testing *association-level* unlearning rather than surface pattern memorization?  
  *Answer:* Paraphrasing forces the model to rely on semantic associations (persona ↔ attribute) rather than memorizing exact caption tokens; unlearning must break the semantic link, not just the lexical one.

- **Collateral Damage Metrics (Holdout Sets):** Standard forget-retain evaluation cannot detect over-generalization. The holdout identity and holdout association sets are engineered to measure unintended forgetting of *unseen* identities and *unseen* attributes for the same identity, respectively. *Quick check:* What does a significant drop in `IntraIdSim` (mean cosine similarity on holdout association set) tell you about a method’s failure mode?  
  *Answer:* It indicates over-generalization: the method has erased not only the target attribute but also other valid attributes for the same persona, damaging broader association knowledge.

## Architecture Onboarding
Compromised Model -> Unlearning Module -> Unlearned Model -> Evaluation Harness (with holdout sets: forget, holdout identity, holdout association, retain synth/real, ImageNet)

**Critical path:**  
1. Load **Compromised** CLIP model.  
2. Apply unlearning intervention using only the `forget` set (and optionally allowed `retain` subsets) with the chosen method and budget.  
3. Run full evaluation script on all five holdout sets.  
4. Compare computed metrics to **Clean** (efficacy target) and **Compromised** (utility target) baselines.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Unlearning budget:** Increasing forward passes on forget data (1×–20×) generally improves forgetting efficacy but risks higher collateral damage or utility loss; no method scales perfectly.  
- **Retention signal strength:** Methods like CLIPErase distill behavior on retain using a frozen **Compromised** copy. Stronger distillation better preserves utility but may limit forgetting efficacy.  
- **Loss function specificity:** Direct losses on similarity (Direct Sim. Min.) are simple but destructive; more complex objectives (e.g., VLUnlearn’s tri-loss) aim for precision but are harder to adapt to CLIP’s dual-encoder architecture.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Ineffective:** `RetFail` >> `Target (Clean)` (e.g., >0.05) while `GenKnow` ≈ `Compromised`. Example: Generic Captions.  
- **Over-generalized:** `RetFail` ≈ `Target` but `InterIdSim` or `IntraIdSim` << `Compromised` baseline (e.g., <0.1). Example: DELETE, CLIPErase.  
- **Catastrophic:** `GenKnow` << `Compromised` (>2% drop) **and** any `*Sim` metric is negative. Example: Direct Similarity Minimization at high budget.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline Replication:** Run Generic Captions and Finetuning at 5× budget. Expect: Generic Captions high `RetFail` (ineffective), Finetuning moderate `RetFail` with minimal collateral (close to target on all but efficacy). Replicate Table 3 trends.  
2. **Sanity Check - Clean Model:** Apply chosen method (e.g., Negative Gradient) to the **Clean** model. Metrics (`GenKnow`, `*Sim`) should not degrade significantly. Significant change indicates inherent destructiveness.  
3. **Holdout Set Isolation Test:** On a model with `RetFail` ~0.001, manually compute mean cosine similarity only on `holdout identity` and `holdout association`. If values are statistically significantly below the **Compromised** model’s (paired t-test, α=0.05), over-generalization is present.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to diffusion models:** How can SALMUBench evaluate unlearning in diffusion models that use CLIP as a backbone, where sensitive associations might propagate into generated content? Current benchmark targets dual encoders only; generative leakage requires new metrics and holdout sets.
2. **Avoiding all failure modes:** Can a method be designed that simultaneously avoids ineffective forgetting, over-generalization, and catastrophic damage while scaling with compute? All evaluated methods exhibit inherent trade-offs; a Pareto-optimal solution across all metrics remains elusive.
3. **Synthetic data validation:** Is the synthetic dataset’s domain gap adequately validated? The paper admits a KS test on only 100 real vs. synthetic images has limited power and does not rule out subtle distributional differences that could affect real-world performance.
4. **Recoverability diagnostic:** How can a standardized recoverability test be integrated to assess whether forgotten associations are truly unlearned or merely suppressed? Current metrics measure immediate forgetting, not persistence under subsequent fine-tuning.
5. **Caption diversity impact:** To what extent do linguistic variations from LLM paraphrasing affect unlearning difficulty and evaluation? The paper uses paraphrasing to avoid surface memorization but does not analyze its impact on method robustness or failure modes.

## Limitations
- **Undisclosed implementation details:** The paper does not specify exact DataComp preprocessing, CLIPScore thresholds, random seeds for persona splits, or complete hyperparameters for unlearning methods, hindering exact replication.
- **Synthetic data external validity:** The SALMU dataset, while controllable, may not fully capture the complexity and diversity of real-world sensitive associations, raising concerns about generalizability to real deployments.

## Confidence
- **Benchmark novelty and design:** High – First to target association-level unlearning in CLIP with a well-structured evaluation framework and novel holdout sets.
- **Empirical findings on failure modes:** High – Consistent results across nine methods and multiple metrics clearly reveal three distinct failure patterns.
- **Synthetic data realism for real-world forgetting:** Medium – Internal validity is strong due to controlled generation, but the gap between synthetic and real sensitive associations remains unverified at scale.
