# CN-Buzz2Portfolio: A Chinese-Market Dataset and Benchmark for LLM-Based Macro and Sector Asset Allocation from Daily Trending Financial News

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22305
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22305
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Primary result:** Benchmark reveals reasoning LLMs excel in trending sector rotation (Sharpe 1.52) but underperform in volatile macro tasks, with model-driven signals collapsing in oscillating markets.

## Executive Summary
CN-Buzz2Portfolio introduces a reproducible Chinese-market benchmark that maps daily trending financial news to macro and sector ETF allocations. The study employs a Tri-Stage Compression-Perception-Allocation workflow to evaluate nine LLMs over 2024–2025. Results show reasoning-oriented models (e.g., Qwen3-Max-Think) outperform in sector rotation during trending markets but adopt overly defensive positions in volatile macro periods, underperforming the CSI 300 benchmark. Efficacy Ratio analysis confirms model-driven performance significantly exceeds stochastic noise in trending regimes (>2.3) but falls to noise levels in oscillating markets (~1.0). Ablation studies identify a non-linear sweet spot at Top-5/10 news inputs and a regime-dependent scaling paradox where smaller models surpass larger ones in low-yield environments.

## Method Summary
The benchmark centers on a Tri-Stage pipeline: **Compression** filters raw daily trending news to a Top-K subset (e.g., Top-5/10); **Perception** extracts market signals and contextual understanding from the compressed news via LLM prompting; **Allocation** translates these signals into daily ETF weight adjustments for macro and sector portfolios. The workflow is evaluated against Chinese ETF benchmarks (CSI 300 for macro, sector-specific ETFs) using financial metrics like Sharpe ratio. Performance is decomposed by market regime (trending vs. oscillating) to isolate model efficacy from random walk effects.

## Key Results
- Reasoning LLMs achieve Sharpe ratios up to 1.52 in sector rotation during trending markets but consistently underperform CSI 300 in volatile macro allocation tasks due to defensive positioning.
- Efficacy Ratio exceeds 2.3 in trending regimes (model signal dominates noise) but drops to ~1.0 in oscillating markets, indicating performance collapse to stochastic levels.
- Ablation reveals a non-linear sweet spot at Top-5/10 news inputs and a scaling paradox where smaller LLMs outperform larger counterparts in low-yield periods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Tri-Stage workflow succeeds by distilling high-volume daily news into a manageable, information-dense set (Compression), allowing LLMs to focus on salient signals without dilution from noise. Reasoning-oriented models leverage chain-of-thought capabilities during Perception to interpret sector rotation cues in trending markets, where signals are clearer and more persistent. However, in volatile macro regimes, ambiguous news and rapid reversals trigger conservative, defensive allocations, causing underperformance. The Efficacy Ratio quantifies this: only in coherent trending regimes does the model’s signal-to-noise ratio exceed 1, validating its predictive power.

## Foundational Learning
- **Market Regime Classification**: Needed to segment market conditions (trending vs. oscillating) for regime-adaptive strategies. *Quick check*: Can you define a trending regime as consecutive daily returns in the same direction beyond a volatility threshold?
- **ETF Sector Rotation**: Central to allocation targets; requires understanding sector ETF definitions (e.g., consumer, tech) and their macroeconomic drivers. *Quick check*: Do you know how Chinese sector ETFs map to economic cycles?
- **News Sentiment Extraction**: Converts unstructured news into structured signals; relies on LLM’s ability to infer sentiment, impact direction, and magnitude. *Quick check*: How would you prompt an LLM to extract "bullish for tech sector" from a news snippet?
- **Efficacy Ratio**: Metric comparing model-driven return volatility to actual returns; >1 indicates signal exceeds random walk noise. *Quick check*: Is a ratio of 1.5 meaningfully different from 1.0 in a 95% confidence test?
- **Compression Sweet Spot**: Optimal number of news items (Top-K) balancing signal retention and noise reduction. *Quick check*: What happens to Sharpe if Top-K is too low (e.g., 1) or too high (e.g., 50)?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Raw News Corpus -> Compression Module -> Perception Module -> Allocation Module -> Portfolio Returns
Critical path: Daily trending news input → filtered to Top-K → LLM-based signal extraction → ETF weight generation → backtested returns.
Design tradeoffs: Compression level (sweet spot at Top-5/10 avoids under/over-fitting), model size (smaller models less prone to over-defensiveness in low-yield), regime handling (no explicit regime detection, leading to failure in oscillating markets).
Failure signatures: Sharpe below benchmark in macro tasks, Efficacy Ratio ~1.0 in oscillating periods, persistent defensive weights (low sector exposure) during volatility spikes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions beyond those raised in the limitations section; its focus remains on presenting the benchmark and observed results.

## Limitations
- Market regime definitions ("trending" vs. "oscillating") are proprietary, hindering generalizability to other regime taxonomies.
- Daily "trending" news sourcing and filtering criteria are unspecified, raising potential source/platform biases.
- Findings are confined to Chinese ETFs (2024–2025); extrapolation to global markets, other asset classes, or longer horizons is uncertain.

## Confidence
- Dataset & workflow validity: **High** (reproducible pipeline, clear Tri-Stage design).
- Regime-dependent performance (Efficacy Ratio): **Medium** (robust within test period but depends on subjective regime labeling).
- Scaling paradox & input sweet spot: **Medium** (observed in ablations but may be artifact of narrow model/task set).

## Next Checks
1. **Temporal robustness test**: Re-run pipeline on out-of-sample period (e.g., 2023 or post-2025) to verify regime-dependent claims and sweet spot stability.
2. **Cross-model generalization**: Include broader LLM suite (e.g., open-source models <7B, newer reasoning architectures) to confirm scaling paradox and size-performance relationship.
3. **Source diversity audit**: Manually sample and label news corpus to quantify topic/source distribution; correlate with allocation decisions to detect data-source confounders.
