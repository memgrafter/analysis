# Causal Effects with Unobserved Unit Types in Interacting Human-AI Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01339
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01339
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** A novel estimator consistently recovers human-specific total treatment effects (H-TTE) from aggregate data alone in human-AI systems with unobserved types and network, overcoming cancellation in standard baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of estimating causal effects specific to human units in interacting human-AI systems where both individual types (human vs. AI) and the full interaction network are unobserved. The core contribution is a method that leverages noisy per-unit priors (e.g., from a classifier) about human identity to construct strategically varied subpopulations. By fitting a low-dimensional "experimental state evolution" to the aggregate outcome dynamics of these subpopulations, the method identifies and recovers the human total treatment effect (H-TTE). The approach is validated on a high-fidelity LLM-driven dating platform simulator where humans and AI bots exhibit opposite responses to the treatment, demonstrating accurate recovery where conventional methods fail.

## Method Summary
The method operates on panel data with unit-level outcomes \(Y_{i,t}\) and treatments \(W_{i,t}\), alongside per-unit prior probabilities \(Q_i\) that unit \(i\) is human. It constructs multiple subpopulations \(S_k\) that systematically vary in their expected human composition \(q^{(k)} = \mathbb{E}[U_i | i \in S_k]\) (where \(U_i\) indicates human) and in their time-varying treatment exposure paths \(\pi_t^{(k)}\). For each subpopulation, it computes sample-mean outcomes \(\hat{Y}_t^{(k)}\). The key theoretical insight is that, under a type-dependent Gaussian interference model, the high-dimensional dynamics of the full network concentrate to a one-dimensional state evolution (ESE) for these sample means. This ESE is a linear function of the subpopulation's composition, its instantaneous treatment rate, the population-average treatment rate, and the lagged population-mean outcome. The parameters of this ESE—which encode type-specific baselines, direct effects, and averaged interaction effects—are estimated via ordinary least squares (OLS) across all subpopulations and time periods. Finally, the fitted ESE is projected forward under a counterfactual where composition is forced to \(q_S = 1\) (all-human) and treatment is set to all-treated versus all-controlled. The difference in these projected paths is the estimated \(\widehat{\text{H-TTE}}_t\). Consistency is established under asymptotic limits and a rank condition on the subpopulation design.

## Key Results
- On an LLM-driven dating simulator with opposing human (positive) and AI (negative) treatment responses, the proposed estimator achieves low post-warmup MAE (as low as 0.037 under medium prior quality \(a=0.8, \sigma=0.15\)).
- Standard baselines (Causal Message Passing and difference-in-means) yield estimates near zero due to cancellation between the positive human and negative AI effects.
- The method successfully isolates human effects from aggregate outcomes despite unobserved types and an unobserved interaction network.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Aggregate outcome dynamics within a subpopulation depend only on its average human-AI prior, not on the full network or unit-level types.
- **Mechanism:** Under the type-dependent Gaussian interference model (Assumption 1), the high-dimensional outcome recursion (Eq. 2) reduces via concentration to a one-dimensional state evolution (ESE) for the sample mean. The ESE for subpopulation \(S\) (Eq. 4) is a function of its expected human composition \(q_S\), its treatment path \(\pi_{S,t}\), the population-average composition \(q\), and the population outcome trajectory \(\nu_{t-1}\).
- **Core assumption:** Interference weights are Gaussian with means that depend only on the receiver’s latent type (\(U_i\)), and the population size \(N \to \infty\) with fixed \(T\).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "analyze sample-mean outcomes across subpopulations… the evolution of aggregate outcomes within suitably constructed subpopulations depends only on the average of their human–AI priors."
  - [section 4, Theorem 1]: Formal statement that \(\lim_{N\to\infty} \bar{Y}_t^{(S,N)} = \nu_t^S\) where \(\nu_t^S\) is given by Eq. (4).
  - [corpus]: Weak/absent—neighbor papers address causal heterogeneity or network experiments but not this specific type-dependent ESE reduction.
- **Break condition:** If interference weights are not mean-field (e.g., strong network homophily that depends on observed covariates not captured by priors) or if population size is small so concentration fails.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Systematic variation in subpopulation summaries (\(q^{(k)}, \pi_t^{(k)}\)) across constructed batches identifies type-specific parameters (\(\delta_H, \delta_A, \tau_H, \tau_A\)) and interaction means (\(m_H, m_A\)).
- **Mechanism:** The ESE recursion (Eq. 4) is linear in reparameterized coefficients (e.g., \(\Delta\delta = \delta_H - \delta_A\)). By creating subpopulations that vary in both expected human share \(q^{(k)}\) and treatment exposure \(\pi_t^{(k)}\), the aggregate trajectories \(\hat{Y}_t^{(k)}\) provide independent equations to solve for these parameters via OLS in Step II of Algorithm 1.
- **Core assumption:** Design identifiability (Assumption 3) holds: there exist at least two composition strata and within each, at least two distinct treatment intensities, and the population design matrix has full column rank.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.1]: Construction heuristics—stratify by \(Q_i\) (composition) and within stratum, sort by treatment duration \(d_i\) to create batches with varying \(\pi_t^{(k)}\).
  - [section 5.3, Theorem 2]: Consistency proof relies on \(\text{rank}(X_\infty)=7\) from cross-variation in (\(q^{(k)}, \pi_t^{(k)}\)) and temporal variation.
  - [corpus]: Weak/absent—neighbors discuss causal heterogeneity or network experiments but not this specific subpopulation design for latent types.
- **Break condition:** If all subpopulations share nearly identical \(q^{(k)}\) or nearly identical \(\pi_t^{(k)}\) paths, the design matrix becomes rank-deficient and coefficients are not identified.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** After fitting the ESE, projecting counterfactual trajectories with composition set to \(q_S = 1\) (human-only) under full treatment vs. full control yields the human total treatment effect (H-TTE).
- **Mechanism:** The fitted ESE parameters (\(\hat{\theta}\)) describe how aggregate outcomes evolve as a function of composition, treatment, and past outcomes. To estimate the effect on humans alone, we simulate the ESE forward twice: once with \(\pi_t = 1\) (all treated) and once with \(\pi_t = 0\) (all control), while forcing \(q_S = 1\) in the state equation (Step III, Lines 18–19). The difference between these simulated human-only paths is \(\widehat{\text{H-TTE}}_t\).
- **Core assumption:** The fitted ESE correctly captures the type-specific baseline, direct effects, and aggregated interaction effects; and the projection to \(q_S = 1\) is valid because the ESE dependence on \(q\) is linear and correctly modeled.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.2]: "Step III… while setting the human-AI composition to \(q_S = 1\) to isolate human outcomes."
  - [section 6.4]: In simulation, Algorithm 1 recovers ground-truth H-TTE (\(\approx 0.5\)) while baselines near zero, confirming the projection isolates human effects despite opposing AI responses.
  - [corpus]: Weak/absent—neighbors do not address this specific counterfactual projection for latent-type systems.
- **Break condition:** If the true interaction dynamics are strongly nonlinear in \(q\) (violating linearity in Eq. 4) or if the priors \(Q_i\) are poorly calibrated so that \(q=1\) projection is not meaningful, the counterfactual will be biased.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Causal Message Passing (CMP)**
  - *Why needed here:* Provides the low-dimensional state evolution framework that replaces explicit network modeling, enabling analysis of aggregate outcomes under unknown interference.
  - *Quick check question:* How does CMP turn a high-dimensional network of interactions into a scalar state recursion?
- **Concept: Experimental State Evolution (ESE)**
  - *Why needed here:* The deterministic limit (\(\nu_t\)) that governs sample-mean trajectories; estimation targets its parameters.
  - *Quick check question:* What variables appear in the ESE equation (Eq. 4), and which are observable versus latent?
- **Concept: Subpopulation Design for Identification**
  - *Why needed here:* Creates the variation in \(q^{(k)}\) and \(\pi_t^{(k)}\) needed to solve for type-specific parameters when unit types are unobserved.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is it necessary to vary both composition *and* treatment exposure across subpopulations?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Inputs: unit-level panel (\(Y_{i,t}, W_{i,t}\)), priors \(Q_i\), subpopulation definitions \(S_k\). Step I: Compute per-subpopulation averages: \(q^{(k)}, \hat{Y}_t^{(k)}, \hat{\pi}_t^{(k)}\); also population averages \(q, \hat{Y}_t, \hat{\pi}_t\). Step II: Fit linear regression of \(\hat{Y}_t^{(k)}\) on \([1, q^{(k)}, \hat{\pi}_t^{(k)}, q^{(k)}\hat{\pi}_t^{(k)}, \hat{\pi}_t, \hat{Y}_{t-1}, \hat{\pi}_t \hat{Y}_{t-1}]\) across (\(k,t\)) to get \(\hat{\theta}\). Step III: Iterate the fitted state equation forward with boundary conditions (\(\pi_t=0/1, q_S=1\)) to get \(\widehat{\text{H-TTE}}_t\).
- **Critical path:**
  1. Construct subpopulations with sufficient cross-variation in (\(q^{(k)}, \pi_t^{(k)}\)).
  2. Ensure time \(T \geq 3\) and non-constant \(\nu_{t-1}\) for rank condition.
  3. Fit ESE; verify residuals are small (goodness-of-fit).
  4. Run counterfactual projection with \(q_S=1\).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Prior quality vs. variation: Noisier \(Q_i\) (lower \(a\)) increases compositional diversity (good for identification) but increases misclassification (bias). Too precise \(Q_i\) (high \(a\)) reduces diversity (bad for identification).
  - Subpopulation overlap: Overlap is allowed (doesn’t break consistency), but overlapping batches reduce effective sample size; non-overlapping batches may be more efficient.
  - Subpopulation size: Must be large enough (\(|S_k| \to \infty\)) for ESE to hold; too many tiny subpopulations increase noise.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - All \(\widehat{\text{H-TTE}}\) estimates near zero across all priors → likely no cross-variation in design or baseline estimator targeting population-average effect (as in baselines).
  - Estimates wildly unstable across seeds → subpopulations too small or priors too noisy; check \(q^{(k)}\) and \(\pi_t^{(k)}\) separation.
  - Final-round error large relative to mid-rounds → possible model misspecification (e.g., nonlinear \(q\) dependence) or warm-up effects not captured.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. *Sweep composition and exposure:* Create subpopulations from 2–3 \(Q_i\) strata; within each stratum, pick batches with low vs. high treatment duration. Plot (\(q^{(k)}\), mean \(\hat{\pi}_t^{(k)}\)) to confirm four distinct clusters.
  2. *Validate ESE fit:* After Step II, plot observed vs. fitted \(\hat{Y}_t^{(k)}\) across all (\(k,t\)). Compute R²; residuals should be small and patternless.
  3. *Simulator sanity check:* Run the provided LLM simulator with \(a=0.8, \sigma=0.15\). Confirm that ground-truth H-TTE is positive (~0.5) and that CMP/DinM estimate near zero, while Algorithm 1 tracks the human effect.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What is the theoretically optimal way to construct subpopulations to minimize variance or maximize information for H-TTE estimation? The paper provides heuristics but no optimality theory.
- **Open Question 2:** Does the method reliably recover H-TTE on real-world human-AI platforms where ground truth is unknown? Validation is limited to a synthetic LLM simulator.
- **Open Question 3:** How sensitive is the method to violations of the Gaussian interference assumption? Real networks may exhibit non-Gaussian, structured interference.
- **Open Question 4:** How well do LLM-driven simulations approximate human behavioral heterogeneity in real human-AI systems? The simulation's opposing effect assumption may be an artifact of specific prompts.
- **Open Question 5:** What is the practical trade-off between prior quality (classifier accuracy \(a\) and noise \(\sigma\)) and estimator performance? No adaptive procedure is given to select prior parameters from data.

## Limitations
- Exact LLM prompts, response parsing, and error handling in the simulator are not fully documented, hindering precise replication.
- Subpopulation construction details (e.g., number of strata, batch size) are heuristic and underspecified, leading to implementation ambiguity.
- Theoretical assumptions (Gaussian interference, mean-field limit) may not hold in real-world interacting systems or even in the LLM simulation.

## Confidence
- **One-dimensional experimental state evolution (Mechanism 1):** Medium. Theory is robust under stated assumptions, but empirical support relies on one simulator that may approximately satisfy mean-field conditions.
- **Identifiability via subpopulation design (Mechanism 2):** Medium. The linear OLS approach is clear, yet rank conditions depend on underspecified construction choices and could fail.
- **Accurate H-TTE recovery in the dating simulator (Mechanism 3):** High. Across seeds and medium prior quality the estimator achieves low MAE and clearly outperforms baselines.
- **Generalizability to real human-AI systems:** Low. No external validation; real networks often exhibit non-Gaussian, structured interference unknown to the method.

## Next Checks
1. **Design matrix rank verification:** After subpopulation construction, compute the condition number of the OLS design matrix \(X_\infty\). Confirm full column rank (≥7). If rank-deficient, increase the number of composition strata or ensure distinct treatment exposure paths \(\pi_t^{(k)}\).
2. **Opposite-sign human/AI ATE sanity check:** Run separate all-treated and all-controlled simulations. Estimate ATEs for units with \(Q_i>0.6\) and \(Q_i<0.4\). Expect a positive effect on high-\(Q\) units (humans) and a negative effect on low-\(Q\) units (AI bots). If signs are not opposite, revise LLM prompts, temperatures, or persona generation.
3. **Prior quality robustness sweep:** Systematically vary prior slope \(a \in \{0.6,0.7,0.8,0.9\}\) and noise \(\sigma \in \{0.1,0.2,0.3\}\). For each combination, compute post-warmup MAE of the H-TTE estimate to quantify the trade-off between composition variation (identification) and misclassification bias.
