# FalconBC: Flow matching for Amortized inference of Latent-CONditioned physiologic Boundary Conditions
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19331
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19331
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: FalconBC enables amortized inference of cardiovascular boundary conditions, reducing inference time from hours to milliseconds while maintaining accuracy (e.g., 5000 samples in 0.13 s vs 16.9 h MCMC).

## Executive Summary
FalconBC tackles the problem of tuning boundary conditions in patient-specific cardiovascular simulations when inflow waveforms are uncertain and vascular lesions prevent isolated adjustment. It employs conditional flow matching to amortize posterior inference, conditioning on clinical targets (pressures, flow splits), inflow Fourier coefficients, and anatomical embeddings derived from point clouds via an encoder-decoder. The method is demonstrated on an aorto-iliac bifurcation with 48 stenotic geometries and a coronary artery tree, achieving mean absolute reconstruction errors of 4.30 mmHg diastolic, 6.15 mmHg systolic, and 3.79% flow split for six-dimensional RCR outlets. Joint estimation of Fourier coefficients further reduced errors. In the coronary case, generating 5000 posterior samples took 0.13 seconds versus 16.9 hours for MCMC, with posterior scaling factors centered at 1.0 and posterior predictive distributions matching measured covariance. This framework supports rapid retuning without retraining and incorporates anatomical updates via learned point cloud embeddings, making it suitable for digital twin applications.

## Method Summary
FalconBC uses a conditional flow matching model trained on simulation data to directly generate samples from the posterior distribution of boundary condition parameters given observed clinical targets, inflow representations (Fourier coefficients), and patient-specific anatomical embeddings. The anatomical embeddings are produced by an encoder-decoder network processing point cloud geometries. During training, the model learns to map a simple latent distribution (e.g., Gaussian) to the complex conditional posterior. At test time, for a new case, the geometry is encoded, combined with targets and inflow coefficients, and the flow matching decoder produces boundary condition samples in a single forward pass. This amortized approach eliminates the need for per-case optimization like MCMC, enabling real-time inference and straightforward incorporation of updated anatomies or targets without retraining.

## Key Results
- Accurate boundary condition inference on aorto-iliac stenoses: MAE 4.30 mmHg diastolic, 6.15 mmHg systolic, 3.79% flow split (N=1000).
- Massive speedup: 5000 posterior samples in 0.13 s vs 16.9 h for MCMC on coronary tree.
- Joint estimation of inflow Fourier coefficients and boundary parameters reduces errors compared to fixing inflow.
- Posterior scaling factors centered at 1.0 and posterior predictive distributions match measured covariance, indicating reasonable uncertainty representation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Conditional flow matching learns a deterministic transformation from a simple latent distribution to the target posterior distribution of boundary conditions, conditioned on clinical targets, inflow Fourier coefficients, and anatomical embeddings. By training on a diverse dataset of simulations, the model captures the complex relationships between these inputs and the physiological boundary parameters. Amortization means that once trained, the model can generate posterior samples for new cases via a single feedforward pass, bypassing iterative, computationally expensive inference methods. The Fourier coefficients provide a compact, smooth representation of inflow waveforms, while point cloud embeddings allow the model to generalize to unseen geometries by encoding shape information into a fixed-length vector. This combination enables fast, accurate, and flexible inference suitable for real-time digital twin updates.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Point Cloud -> Encoder -> Anatomical Embedding  
Clinical Targets + Fourier Coefficients + Embedding -> Conditional Flow Matching Network -> Latent Variable -> Boundary Condition Parameters (e.g., RCR values)

**Critical Path**  
1. Preprocess patient geometry into point cloud.  
2. Encode point cloud to obtain anatomical embedding.  
3. Combine embedding with clinical targets and Fourier coefficients (or jointly estimate them).  
4. Sample latent variable and pass through conditional flow matching decoder to generate boundary parameters.  
5. Use parameters in hemodynamic simulation.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *Fourier coefficients vs. full waveform*: Fourier provides compactness and smoothness but may lose high-frequency details; full waveform is higher dimensional and possibly noisy.  
- *Point cloud embeddings vs. mesh-based*: Point clouds are easier to acquire from imaging but may lack surface continuity; embeddings must be robust to sampling density.  
- *Amortization vs. per-case optimization*: Amortization gives speed but may sacrifice accuracy on out-of-distribution cases; per-case methods are flexible but slow.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Poor reconstruction when anatomy deviates significantly from training geometries (e.g., extreme stenoses or atypical branching).  
- Inconsistent posterior samples if clinical targets are contradictory or out of the range seen during training.  
- Degenerate flow matching outputs (e.g., boundary parameters far from physiological ranges) indicating training instability or conditioning failure.

**First Three Experiments**  
1. **Hold-out Generalization**: Train on 70% of the 48 aorto-iliac geometries, test on the remaining 30%. Compare MAE on boundary condition reconstruction and flow split.  
2. **Ablation of Anatomical Embedding**: Replace learned embeddings with random or one-hot encoded geometry IDs to quantify the contribution of shape information.  
3. **Robustness to Point Cloud Noise**: Add Gaussian noise to test point clouds (e.g., jitter vertices) and measure change in reconstruction error without retraining, assessing embedding stability.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to two vascular beds (aorto-iliac stenoses and one coronary tree) and RCR boundary conditions; generalizability to other anatomies and BC types is unclear.
- Training data provenance, size, and diversity not reported, raising concerns about overfitting to simulation-only data and reproducibility.
- Uncertainty quantification lacks calibration metrics (e.g., coverage, CRPS); posterior predictive covariance alone is insufficient to assess reliability.

## Next Checks
1. **Hold-out anatomical generalization**: Train on a random subset of the 48 geometries (e.g., 70%), test on the remaining held-out set, and report full error distributions (median, IQR) to quantify generalization gap.
2. **Uncertainty calibration**: Compute 95% predictive interval coverage and CRPS across multiple test cases to formally assess reliability of posterior uncertainty.
3. **Robustness and cost**: Document total training time, memory, parameters. Apply noise/subsampling to test point clouds and measure error change without retraining, verifying embedding stability and retuning efficacy.
