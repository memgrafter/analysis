# Inferring Causal Graph Temporal Logic Formulas to Expedite Reinforcement Learning in Temporally Extended Tasks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.02666
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02666
- **Reference count**: 7
- **Primary result**: GTL-CIRL achieves faster convergence and higher success rates compared to standard RL and counterfactual RL baselines on gene regulation and power grid networks, demonstrating improved sample efficiency and verifiable causal behaviors.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning on graph-structured tasks often ignores network propagation dynamics, leading to low sample efficiency and opaque policies. This paper introduces GTL-CIRL, a framework that jointly learns control policies and mines causal specifications expressed as Causal Graph Temporal Logic (GTL) formulas. The method shapes rewards using the robustness degree of GTL formulas, providing dense feedback aligned with temporally extended goals. It also collects counterexample trajectories where effects fail and generates counterfactuals to refine the causal rule via Gaussian Process Bayesian optimization. Evaluated on gene regulation and power grid networks, GTL-CIRL converges faster, attains higher success rates, and yields interpretable causal behaviors compared to standard RL and counterfactual RL baselines.

## Method Summary
GTL-CIRL operates in a closed loop between policy learning and causal mining. The agent interacts with a graph environment, and its history is aggregated into a τ-augmented MDP (τ-MDP) state to evaluate temporal logic formulas with bounded horizons. A Q-learning policy is trained using rewards derived from the robustness degree ρ of a GTL goal: for eventual goals (♢[0,T]ϕ) the reward is e^βρ; for persistent goals (□[0,T]ϕ) it is -e^{-βρ}. Simultaneously, the framework maintains a candidate causal rule ϕ_c ⇒ ϕ_e. Trajectories that violate the effect (ρ(τ, ϕ_e) ≤ 0) are stored as counterexamples. To assess a candidate cause, the system generates counterfactuals by intervening on the cause (e.g., enforcing ¬ϕ_c) and computes sufficiency and necessity scores from these. An objective combining existence, sufficiency, and necessity is optimized over the continuous parameters θ of ϕ_c using Gaussian Process Bayesian optimization with an RBF kernel and GP-UCB. The optimized cause formula then yields a new robustness-shaped reward, completing the loop.

## Key Results
- Faster convergence: GTL-CIRL reaches high performance with significantly fewer episodes than baselines.
- Higher success rates: Final success rates exceed those of standard RL and counterfactual RL on both tested domains.
- Improved interpretability: The inferred Causal GTL specifications provide verifiable, human-readable descriptions of network dynamics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Reward Shaping via GTL Robustness
The method replaces sparse rewards with a continuous signal based on the robustness degree ρ(τ, ϕ, t), which quantifies the margin of satisfaction of a GTL formula. For eventual goals, R = e^βρ encourages maximizing the minimum future robustness; for persistent goals, R = -e^{-βρ} penalizes violations. This dense feedback alleviates long-horizon credit assignment. The core assumption is that ρ is a differentiable proxy for goal achievement. If ϕ is misspecified or system dynamics break monotonicity, the shaping can mislead.

### Causal Refinement via Counterexamples
Trajectories that violate the effect formula become counterexamples. The system synthesizes counterfactual trajectories by intervening on the cause (e.g., do(¬ϕ_c)) and computes sufficiency (how often cause leads to effect when enforced) and necessity (how often effect fails when cause is negated). These scores, derived from counterexamples and counterfactuals, form an objective to refine the cause parameters. The key assumption is that counterexamples lie near the causal boundary and are maximally informative. If the buffer becomes unrepresentative or interventions fail to isolate causality, refinement degrades.

### Parameter Optimization with Gaussian Processes
The continuous parameters θ of the cause formula are optimized to maximize J(ϕ_c) = -E + λ_S S + λ_N N. A Gaussian Process with an RBF kernel models J from evaluated points, and GP-UCB selects the next θ. This provides sample-efficient exploration under the assumption that J is smooth. The kernel length scale encodes smoothness; if J is highly non-smooth or discrete, convergence may be inefficient or suboptimal.

## Foundational Learning

**Graph Temporal Logic (GTL) Robustness**  
*Why needed*: The core numerical signal bridging logic and learning is the recursive robustness degree ρ, which quantifies how close a trajectory is to satisfying a GTL formula.  
*Quick check*: Given a formula `♢[2,5] (Voltage(v) > 0.95)` and a trajectory where at t=3 Voltage=0.93 and t=4 Voltage=0.96, what is the robustness ρ at t=0? (If there exists a time in [2,5] where the condition holds, ρ is positive, reflecting the minimum margin over that window.)

**Counterfactual Causal Metrics (Sufficiency & Necessity)**  
*Why needed*: The sufficiency (S) and necessity (N) scores are the optimized metrics for causal formula refinement. S measures the fraction of `do(ϕ_c)` counterfactuals where the effect holds; N measures the fraction of `do(¬ϕ_c)` counterfactuals where the effect fails.  
*Quick check*: If a counterfactual under `do(ϕ_c)` yields a high ρ(ϕ_e), does S increase or decrease? (Increase, as S captures how reliably the cause produces the effect.)

**τ-MDP (History-Augmented MDP)**  
*Why needed*: Standard MDP states lack the history needed to evaluate GTL operators with bounded horizons. The τ-MDP aggregates the last τ steps, restoring Markovianity.  
*Quick check*: For a formula with an inner horizon of 5 time steps (e.g., `♢[0,3] (♢[1,2] ϕ)`), what is the minimum τ required? (τ must be at least the maximum lookahead across nested operators; here 3+2=5.)

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Policy Learner → GTL Miner → Counterexample Buffer → Causal Evaluator → GP Optimizer → GTL Miner (cause update). The Causal Evaluator uses the Counterexample Generator to produce counterfactual trajectories from the buffer.

### Critical Path
Exploration → (τ-state, action) → Policy update → Check effect violation → If fail, add to CE → Periodically: For current ϕ_c, evaluate (S,N,E) on CE via counterfactuals → Update GP → Propose new ϕ_c parameters → Loop.

### Design Tradeoffs
- τ-MDP state size τ trades off Markovian completeness against computational and memory overhead.
- The GP’s RBF kernel assumes smoothness in the causal objective; a mismatch reduces sample efficiency.
- The perturbation scale ϵ for counterexample generation must be large enough to reach the causal boundary but small enough to produce valid, relevant states.

### Failure Signatures
- Policy fails to converge → possibly inappropriate reward scale β or unreachable formula.
- GP suggests oscillating parameters → kernel length scale too small or non-smooth objective.
- Counterexample buffer empty or homogeneous → exploration is not discovering effect violations; more directed exploration needed.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Unit Test Robustness Reward**: In a simple gridworld with `♢[0,5]Goal`, verify that the shaped reward e^βρ yields a smoother learning curve than sparse terminal reward.
2. **Validate Counterexample Generation**: With a known ground-truth causal formula, run until an effect violation occurs and check if perturbations produce counterexamples satisfying cause but violating effect.
3. **GP Parameter Optimization**: Fix cause structure; use GP-BO to tune a single threshold θ. Plot J(θ) evaluations and GP predictions to confirm the surrogate models the objective.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scalability** – How does GTL-CIRL scale to large, dynamic, or partially observable networks? The paper tests only small static graphs, with no complexity analysis.
2. **GP Kernel Sensitivity** – Does the RBF kernel’s smoothness assumption prevent discovering non-smooth causal boundaries? No alternative kernels or non-parametric models are considered.
3. **Non-Markovian and Confounded Settings** – How does the method perform when dependencies exceed τ or hidden confounders exist? The τ-MDP truncation may be insufficient, and no robustness to latent variables is shown.
4. **Counterexample Generation Strategy** – What is the optimal perturbation range ϵ and variable selection for generating informative counterexamples? The fixed small ϵ may be suboptimal and no adaptive policy is provided.
5. **Hyperparameter λ Scheduling** – How do the weights λ_S and λ_N affect causal accuracy and learning efficiency? No sensitivity analysis or principled setting is offered.

## Limitations
- Key hyperparameters (β, λ_S, λ_N, GP settings) are omitted, hindering reproducibility.
- Counterexample generation details (intervention policy π'_c, discrete state handling) are vague, increasing implementation risk.
- Evaluation is limited to two small networks (gene regulation, power grid), so scalability and generalizability remain unverified.

## Confidence
- Reward shaping improves sample efficiency: **Medium**
- Counterexample refinement enhances causal discovery: **Low–Medium**
- GP-BO efficiently optimizes parameters: **Medium**
- Overall performance gains: **Medium**
