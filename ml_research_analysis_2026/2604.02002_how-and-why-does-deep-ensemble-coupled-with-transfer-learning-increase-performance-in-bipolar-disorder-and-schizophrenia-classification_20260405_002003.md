# How and why does deep ensemble coupled with transfer learning increase performance in bipolar disorder and schizophrenia classification?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.02002  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02002  
- **Reference count**: 0  
- **Primary result**: Deep ensemble (10 models) with transfer learning achieves 77.06% (±0.74) ROC-AUC for bipolar disorder and 73.94% (±0.63) for schizophrenia, outperforming models without ensemble or transfer learning while significantly reducing prediction variance.

## Executive Summary
This study explores how to improve the reliability of deep learning classifiers for bipolar disorder (BD) and schizophrenia (SCZ) from brain MRI. Two techniques are investigated: deep ensemble (DE), which averages predictions from multiple independently fine-tuned models, and transfer learning (TL) with weights pre-trained via contrastive learning on age prediction from healthy brains. Results show that DE reduces epistemic uncertainty, with performance plateauing at around 10 models, and TL constrains fine-tuning to the same loss basin—evidenced by smooth linear interpolation between TL model weights—leading to more stable and faster convergence. The combination TL+DE attains state-of-the-art ROC-AUC (77.06% for BD, 73.94% for SCZ) and lower variability, offering a robust framework for psychiatric neuroimaging.

## Method Summary
The authors used voxel-based morphometry (VBM) gray matter maps from multi-site MRI datasets: SCZ cohorts (SCHIZCONNECT-VIP, CNP, PRAGUE, BSNIP, CANDI) and BD cohorts (BIOBD, BSNIP, CNP, CANDI). The backbone architecture was DenseNet-121. Two initialization strategies were compared: (1) randomly initialized (RI-DL) and (2) transfer learning (TL) with weights from a model pre-trained on large healthy datasets (OpenBHB, HCP, OASIS 3, ICBM) using contrastive learning to predict age. Models were fine-tuned on the target binary classification tasks (BD vs HC or SCZ vs HC) for up to 200 epochs with a tuned learning rate decay factor (γ). For deep ensemble, predictions from T independently fine-tuned models were averaged. Performance was evaluated primarily via ROC-AUC on held-out test sites, and loss basin connectivity was assessed by linearly interpolating between model weights.

## Key Results
- TL+DE (10 models) yields 77.06% (±0.74) ROC-AUC for bipolar disorder, compared to 74.68% (±1.96) for single TL models and lower scores for RI-DL.
- For schizophrenia, TL+DE achieves 73.94% (±0.63) ROC-AUC versus 72.76% (±1.65) without DE.
- DE performance plateaus at T=10 models; beyond this, gains diminish.
- Linear interpolation between TL model weights maintains high ROC-AUC across the path, indicating models reside in the same loss basin; in contrast, RI-DL pairs show a sharp performance drop, revealing barriers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Deep Ensemble (DE)** reduces epistemic uncertainty by averaging predictions from multiple independently fine-tuned models. Each model converges to a potentially distinct local minimum; averaging smooths out variance from any single model’s parameterization, stabilizing the output distribution. The empirical plateau at T=10 suggests that ensembles become sufficiently correlated beyond this point, yielding diminishing variance reduction.

- **Transfer Learning (TL) Basin Stability**: Pre-trained weights from contrastive learning on age place the model in a favorable region of the loss landscape. Fine-tuning then stays within the same broad, low-loss basin, as demonstrated by flat ROC-AUC curves when interpolating between TL models. This prevents divergence into disparate minima that plague randomly initialized models and ensures consistent performance after the best epoch.

- **Combined Effect**: TL provides a robust starting point that speeds convergence and reduces variability; DE further cancels residual independent errors. Together they produce higher, more stable ROC-AUC for both BD and SCZ classification.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: VBM gray matter maps → DenseNet-121 backbone → Two initialization paths (TL: pre-trained age-contrastive weights; RI-DL: random) → Fine-tuning (≤200 epochs, LR decay) → (For DE) T-model averaging → Classification probability.

- **Critical path**:  
  1. Pre-training (TL only): Train base model on large healthy set via age-aware contrastive learning.  
  2. Fine-tuning: For each of T models, initialize with TL or RI weights; train on target BD/SCZ dataset; save final (and optionally best) epoch weights.  
  3. Inference: For a test sample, run all T fine-tuned models and average their output probabilities.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  *DE size (T) vs compute/inference latency*: Gains plateau at T=10; additional models increase cost linearly.  
  *TL vs RI-DL*: TL offers better performance and stability but depends on pre‑training relevance; RI-DL is simpler but suffers high variability.  
  *Pre-training task choice*: Age captures non‑pathological variation; mismatched tasks (e.g., site classification) may introduce harmful biases.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - DE plateau occurs at T < 10 → insufficient model diversity or training instability.  
  - Linear interpolation shows high barrier for TL models → learning rate too high, severe domain shift, or flawed pre‑training.  
  - RI-DL models exhibit low variance among themselves → all trapped in same poor minimum; DE will not help.  
  - TL performance degrades after best epoch → learning rate decay insufficient or overfitting.

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Baseline Variability Study**: Train ≥30 RI-DL and TL models (different seeds). Compute mean and standard deviation of test ROC‑AUC. Expect: TL higher mean, lower SD.  
  2. **Loss Basin Connectivity Test**: Select two TL and two RI‑DL model pairs. Interpolate weights (λ∈[0,1], 30 steps), evaluate ROC‑AUC on validation. Plot; expect TL curves flat/high, RI‑DL U‑shaped with dip.  
  3. **Ensemble Size Saturation Curve**: From ≥30 trained models per init, bootstrap ensembles (T=2,5,10,15,20,30,40,50,60) 1e5 times. Plot mean ensemble ROC‑AUC and SD versus T; expect plateau near T=10 and TL advantage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Why does transfer learning with age‑aware contrastive pre‑training yield larger performance gains for bipolar disorder than for schizophrenia?
- What property of pre‑trained weights leads fine‑tuned models to remain in the same loss basin, while randomly initialized models fall into different basins?
- Why does deep ensemble performance plateau after about 10 models for BD and SCZ classification, and what determines this optimal number?

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter details (e.g., optimal learning rate decay factor γ) are not reported, hampering exact replication.
- Pre‑trained weights from the contrastive learning stage are not provided; dependence on large external datasets limits reproducibility.
- Findings are restricted to binary classification of BD or SCZ versus healthy controls; generalizability to other psychiatric disorders or multi‑class settings remains unclear.

## Confidence
- Deep Ensemble variance reduction: **High**  
- Transfer Learning basin stability (for this pre‑training task): **High**  
- Transfer Learning convergence/stability: **Medium**
