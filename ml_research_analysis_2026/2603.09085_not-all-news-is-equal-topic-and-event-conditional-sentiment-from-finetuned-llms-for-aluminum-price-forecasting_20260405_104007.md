# Not All News Is Equal: Topic- and Event-Conditional Sentiment from Finetuned LLMs for Aluminum Price Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09085  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09085  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** Finetuned LLM sentiment from Reuters headlines yields a Sharpe ratio of 1.04 in high-volatility periods, versus 0.23 for tabular-only models, with further gains from topic and event filtering.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates whether finetuned large language models (LLMs) can extract predictive signals from news headlines for aluminum price forecasting. Using a finetuned Qwen3 8B model, the authors generate monthly sentiment scores from English (Reuters, Dow Jones) and Chinese (China News Service) news, integrate these with traditional tabular features, and assess forecasting performance via trading simulations from 2007–2024. The results show that news-based sentiment provides significant value primarily during medium-to-high volatility regimes, with Reuters sentiment achieving the highest risk-adjusted returns. Further analysis reveals that filtering news by specific topics and focusing on headlines reporting occurred (past) events—as opposed to forward-looking statements—substantially enhances signal quality.

## Method Summary
The authors combine monthly aluminum price data (Shanghai Metal Exchange) with macro indicators and news headlines from Reuters, Dow Jones, and China News Service. Headlines are filtered for relevance using LLaMA3 few‑shot clustering, then sentiment is labeled (positive/negative/neutral) via a finetuned Qwen3 8B model. Monthly sentiment scores are computed as weighted averages and merged with tabular features. Multiple time‑series models (LSTM, BiLSTM, GRU, ConvLSTM, TFT) are trained using walk‑forward validation with grid‑search hyperparameter tuning. Predicted prices are translated into long/short trading signals; portfolio returns, Sharpe ratios, and hit rates are evaluated across volatility regimes defined by 6‑month rolling standard deviations. Topic and event-type analyses are performed by re‑computing sentiment on filtered Reuters subsets.

## Key Results
- During high volatility, LSTM with Qwen sentiment (Reuters) achieves a Sharpe ratio of **1.04**, versus **0.23** for tabular‑only models.
- In medium volatility, a sentiment‑only strategy reaches **Sharpe 1.19**, outperforming the combined tabular‑plus‑sentiment model (**0.51**).
- Reuters‑based sentiment yields **Sharpe 0.80**, far ahead of Dow Jones (**0.18**) and China News (**0.15**).
- Filtering to eight optimal topics improves the all‑topics benchmark by **23.6%** (Sharpe **1.00** vs. **0.81**).
- Headlines reporting occurred events achieve **Sharpe 0.62**, while forward‑looking statements perform poorly (**Sharpe -0.01**).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Sentiment adds value in high volatility by capturing real‑time event information
- **Claim:** News sentiment generates predictive signals that are most valuable during high‑volatility periods, complementing traditional tabular data.
- **Mechanism:** During market turbulence, historical price correlations break down. News sentiment provides immediate information about unexpected events (supply disruptions, policy shifts) not yet reflected in numerical indicators, offering directional cues.
- **Core assumption:** News contains forward‑looking information not fully incorporated into prices, especially during regime shifts.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - High‑volatility LSTM with sentiment: Sharpe **1.04** vs. tabular‑only **0.23**.  
  - In turbulent markets, sentiment captures panic/recovery signals missed by time‑series models.  
  - Low‑volatility convergence (Sharpe **0.29**–**0.54**) indicates sentiment adds little when markets are efficient.
- **Break condition:** In stable regimes, all strategies converge; sentiment may introduce noise if misaligned with slow‑moving fundamentals.

### Mechanism 2: Topic and event filtering improve signal quality by reducing noise
- **Claim:** Not all news topics or event types carry equal predictive weight; filtering to high‑signal occurred events enhances performance.
- **Mechanism:** Markets efficiently price expectations; predictive content resides in unanticipated realized events rather than consensus forecasts. Focusing on high‑signal topics (e.g., Price Movement, Supply Disruption) and excluding low‑signal ones (e.g., Production Output) reduces noise.
- **Core assumption:** Sentiment from past‑occurred events conveys new information; forward‑looking statements are already priced in.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Eight‑topic filter improves Sharpe by **23.6%** (1.00 vs. 0.81).  
  - Occurred events: Sharpe **0.62** vs. forward‑looking **-0.01**.  
  - Within Price Movement, occurred: **0.89** vs. forward‑looking: **0.20**.
- **Break condition:** Over‑filtering may exclude topics that gain predictive power in specific contexts (e.g., Environmental news during regulatory shocks); optimal topic set is regime‑dependent.

### Mechanism 3: Source quality influences performance via topic allocation and framing
- **Claim:** Differences in news source quality (Reuters vs. Dow Jones vs. China News) drive performance via both topic coverage and reporting clarity/timeliness.
- **Mechanism:** Reuters’ superior Sharpe (**0.80**) stems partly from higher coverage of predictive topics (35.6% Price Movement) and partly from higher signal‑to‑noise ratio and more market‑relevant framing within topics.
- **Core assumption:** Source credibility and reporting style affect how sentiment translates into trading signals.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Reuters Sharpe **0.80**, Dow Jones **0.18**, China News **0.15**.  
  - On Price Movement, Reuters **0.71** vs. Dow Jones **0.53**; on Environmental, Reuters **0.60** vs. Dow Jones **0.347**.
- **Break condition:** If a source’s editorial strategy shifts (e.g., reduces Price Movement coverage), its relative advantage may erode.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Finetuning LLMs for Domain‑Specific Sentiment**  
  - *Why needed:* Base LLMs lack financial nuance; finetuning on financial datasets (FinancialPhraseBank, Chinese Finance Sentiment) adapts Qwen3 to correctly classify sentiment in aluminum‑related headlines.  
  - *Quick check:* What datasets were used to finetune Qwen3, and why is domain adaptation critical for commodity news?

- **Concept: Volatility‑Regime Dependent Modeling**  
  - *Why needed:* Strategy effectiveness is not uniform; sentiment shines in medium/high volatility but adds noise in low volatility. Understanding regime definitions (rolling 6‑month std dev) is key to applying the right model.  
  - *Quick check:* How were the volatility thresholds (8.40%, 19.30%) set, and what do the differing results across regimes imply for deployment?

- **Concept: Economic vs. Statistical Metrics in Strategy Evaluation**  
  - *Why needed:* High R² does not guarantee trading profits; risk‑adjusted returns (Sharpe) and directional accuracy (hit rate) are economically meaningful. The Reuters sentiment model had slightly worse R² but much higher total returns (292% vs. 131%) due to better timing.  
  - *Quick check:* Why might a model with lower prediction error (MAE/RMSE) underperform in a trading simulation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Data Ingestion (tabular + textual) -> Preprocessing (filtering, sentiment scoring, monthly aggregation) -> Feature Engineering (merge sentiment with tabular, rolling windows) -> Modeling (LSTM/BiLSTM/GRU/ConvLSTM/TFT with walk‑forward validation) -> Simulation (signal generation, portfolio returns) -> Evaluation (Sharpe, hit rate, cumulative returns, topic/event sub‑analyses)

- **Critical path:** News filtering -> sentiment generation (Qwen3) -> feature integration -> model training -> backtesting -> performance attribution. The sentiment generation step is a bottleneck; errors propagate downstream.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Temporal granularity: Monthly aggregation required due to sparse news; loses intra‑month dynamics.  
  - Model complexity: More layers/hidden size risk overfitting; optimal ~3 layers, 128 hidden size.  
  - Filtering strictness: Tight topic filtering (8 topics) improves Sharpe but may reduce signal frequency; balance via regime‑dependent rules.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Sharpe collapse in high volatility if sentiment model misclassifies crisis‑related headlines.  
  - Topic‑specific strategies (e.g., Supply Disruption alone) yield negative returns, indicating need for diversification.  
  - Source performance drift (e.g., China News underperforms) may require periodic recalibration of source weights.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Volatility threshold sensitivity test: Vary high/medium/low boundaries (e.g., 30%/70% percentiles) to confirm robustness of strategy switching.  
  2. Topic ablation study: Systematically exclude one topic at a time from the optimal 8‑topic combo to identify linchpin topics and redundancy.  
  3. LLM comparative benchmark: Replace Qwen3 with other finetuned LLMs (e.g., LLaMA3, Mistral) on the same news to isolate model‑specific vs. data‑driven effects.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does the predictive superiority of occurred‑event news over forward‑looking statements generalize to other commodity markets beyond aluminum, and what is the underlying causal mechanism (e.g., information novelty, market efficiency)?
2. What specific characteristics of Reuters news (e.g., phrasing, timeliness, source diversity) enable its sentiment to generate stronger trading signals than Dow Jones or China News Service, even when covering identical topics?
3. Why does combining tabular data with sentiment degrade performance in medium‑volatility periods (Sharpe 0.51 for combined vs. 1.19 for sentiment‑only), and what architectural modifications to the LSTM could mitigate this noise?
4. Is the 23.6% Sharpe improvement from the optimal 8‑topic combination robust across different LLMs (e.g., LLaMA, Mistral) and sentiment lexicons, or is it an artifact of the Qwen3 model’s specific fine‑tuning and the Reuters topic distribution?
5. To what extent can higher‑frequency (daily or intraday) sentiment streams from news or social media improve aluminum price forecasting, and does the volatility‑dependent value of sentiment persist at finer temporal resolutions?

## Limitations
- Proprietary model and data: The finetuned Qwen3‑8B checkpoint and raw headlines (Factiva) / tabular data (WIND) are not publicly released, requiring substitutes that may alter results.
- Volatility thresholds (≤8.40%, 8.40–19.30%, >19.30%) are sample‑specific; robustness to alternative percentile splits is unknown.
- Causal attribution: Source performance differences may conflate topic distribution with framing quality; the paper does not fully disentangle these effects.
- Generalizability: Findings are specific to aluminum and the 2007–2024 period; transfer to other commodities or eras is unvalidated.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Sentiment value in high volatility):** High – consistent in‑sample results show Sharpe improvement (1.04 vs. 0.23) and low‑volatility convergence.
- **Mechanism 2 (Topic/event filtering):** High – clear within‑topic Sharpe gains (e.g., Price Movement occurred: 0.89 vs. forward‑looking: 0.20) and 23.6% improvement from eight‑topic filter.
- **Mechanism 3 (Source quality differences):** Medium – strong comparative Sharpe (Reuters: 0.80, others <0.20) but potential confounding by topic allocation remains.
