# Squish and Release: Exposing Hidden Hallucinations by Making Them Surface as Safety Signals

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26829
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26829
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** The Squish and Release (S&R) method exposes latent "order-gap hallucinations" by patching a safety core vector into a localized detector body, restoring detection in 76.6% of collapsed chains and achieving near-total cascade collapse (99.8% compliance at O5) in OLMo-2 7B.

## Executive Summary
The paper identifies a subtle hallucination failure mode—order-gap hallucination—where large language models initially detect false premises but later absorb them under escalating conversational pressure, producing confident yet flawed outputs. The proposed Squish and Release (S&R) framework uses activation patching to isolate and manipulate a safety evaluation circuit localized to the top layers (24–31) of a 7B model. By swapping in a "safety core" activation vector captured from a strong refusal context, S&R shifts the model from compliance back to detection, exposing suppressed errors invisible to output inspection. Key findings include extreme localization of the detector body, strong release rates from synthetic safety cores, and asymmetry indicating detection is a more stable attractor than compliance.

## Method Summary
S&R is a non-training intervention that operates on the residual stream during forward passes. The method localizes a fixed "detector body" (layers 24–31) responsible for safety evaluation, and introduces a "detector core"—a direction vector ϕ extracted from an anchor prompt that elicits a maximal refusal at O2. When this core is patched into the O5 forward pass of a chain that has undergone cascade collapse, it overrides the suppressed safety state and restores detection. The evaluation uses the Order-Gap Benchmark: 500 five-prompt chains across diverse domains, manually graded as DETECT, PARTIAL, or ABSORB. Release rate is defined as the proportion of O5 chains (originally ABSORB) that shift to DETECT or PARTIAL after patching. The framework is model-agnostic in principle but body location and release rates are model-specific.

## Key Results
- Cascade collapse is nearly universal: baseline compliance reaches 99.8% at O5, with only ~0% detection.  
- The detector body is highly localized to layers 24–31; patching these layers releases 93.6% of collapsed chains (p < 10⁻¹⁸⁹) while lower layers yield 0% release.  
- A synthetic safety core (engineered for rights-violation framing) achieves 76.6% release, outperforming empirically discovered cores (~62%).  
- Bidirectional control shows asymmetry: safety core restores detection in 83% of DETECT chains, while an absorb core suppresses detection in only 58%, indicating detection is the more stable attractor.  
- Epistemic specificity confirmed: a core extracted from a true-premise context yields 0% release, while a false-premise core yields 45.4%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Conversational pressure suppresses a functioning safety circuit, causing models to absorb false premises they initially detected (cascade collapse).  
**Mechanism:** At O2, the safety circuit fires correctly when the false premise is queried directly. However, as the chain progresses (O3–O5), accumulated conversational pressure (embedded assumptions, professional framing) competitively suppresses this circuit’s activation. The detection signal persists in the residual stream but is masked, leading to compliance at O5 despite latent knowledge of falsity.  
**Break condition:** If O2 detection never occurs (e.g., normative premises), there is no latent signal to suppress.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Injecting a safety core (ϕᴱ) at O5 restores detection by overriding the suppressed safety-circuit state.  
**Mechanism:** ϕᴱ is the last-token residual stream at layers 24–31 from an anchor prompt that maximally activates the safety circuit (e.g., war crimes order). When patched into the O5 forward pass, it replaces the suppressed activation, allowing the detector body to perceive the false premise with O2-level intensity.  
**Break condition:** The core must be routed to the correct subfamily of ϕᴰ vectors; blending mismatched cores collapses release. Also fails for resistant chains where O2 detection never fired.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The detector body (layers 24–31) is a fixed anatomical site; the core vector determines the outcome (detection vs. compliance).  
**Mechanism:** Layer ablation shows only the top quartile releases suppressed chains, localizing the causal site. The core acts as a “perceptual lens”: ϕᴱ shifts toward detection, ϕᴰ shifts toward compliance. The body is invariant; the core chooses the attractor.  
**Break condition:** Architecture may vary across models; core routing must match the ϕᴰ subfamily.

## Foundational Learning
- **Activation Patching & Residual Stream Manipulation**: Needed to directly intervene in forward passes by replacing activation values at specific layers, enabling the S&R method. *Quick check:* If you patch an activation from layer 28 into layer 28 of a different forward pass, what assumptions are you making about layer-to-layer computation and residual stream consistency?  
- **Order-Gap Hallucination**: A failure mode distinct from knowledge gaps or sycophancy, where the model detects a false premise early but absorbs it under multi-turn pressure. *Quick check:* Why would a model that refuses a false premise at O2 produce professional output built on that premise at O5, and why would output inspection fail to detect this?  
- **The Order-Gap Benchmark Structure**: The evaluation substrate of 500 five-prompt chains with escalating framing, manually graded. *Quick check:* What makes a chain qualify as “cascade collapse,” and why is the O5→O2 comparison central?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Anchor O2 prompt -> extract safety core ϕᴱ (layers 24-31) -> patch into O5 forward pass at layers 24-31 -> detector body (fixed) processes patched activation -> output shifts from ABSORB to DETECT/PARTIAL.  
**Critical path:** 1) Localize body via layer sweep; 2) Confirm bidirectionality (ϕᴱ restore, ϕᴰ suppress); 3) Engineer/select core (empirical vs. synthetic).  
**Design tradeoffs:** Synthetic cores > empirical (+14.6 pp) but require first-principles design; blending cores destroys signal (release drops to ~11%); framework model-agnostic but body location/release rates are model-specific.  
**Failure signatures:** Resistant chains (normative falsehoods, 0% release); blending collapse (averaging cores); true-premise core (0% release).  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Layer localization sweep: patch ϕᴱ into each non-overlapping window (0-7, 8-15, 16-23, 24-31) on all 500 O5 chains; expect >90% release only from top quartile.  
2. Bidirectional control test: on 12 confirmed DETECT chains, inject ϕᴰ at O2 (expect ~58% suppression) and ϕᴱ at O5 (expect ~83% restoration).  
3. Synthetic anchor validation: construct anchor 2003 (Geneva + Rome Statute violation), capture ϕᴱ, apply to full benchmark; expect ~76.6% release.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Localization across architectures:** How does the detector body's localization (layers 24–31 in OLMo-2 7B) vary across model architectures and training factors?  
2. **Higher-capacity model behavior:** In larger models, does restoring the safety circuit trigger self-correction during generation, or only shift internal detection?  
3. **Normative falsehoods:** Can S&R detect purely normative false premises (e.g., ethical claims) that fail to activate the safety circuit at O2, and what alternative core engineering is needed?  
4. **Automated core optimization:** What gradient-based or evolutionary methods can systematically improve safety core construction beyond hand-engineered anchors, and what is the achievable release ceiling?  
5. **Causal mechanism of core influence:** Why does naive blending of multiple cores catastrophically fail, and what is the activation dynamics underlying the routing requirement?

## Limitations
- **Unknown anchor construction:** The exact prompt engineering for optimal synthetic safety core (76.6% release) is not fully specified; the "genre invalidity" heuristic lacks replicable details.  
- **Patching mechanics vagueness:** Precise operational details of activation patching (e.g., injection timing, residual stream manipulation) are described conceptually but not algorithmically, relying on unreleased code.  
- **Model specificity:** All results are from a single 7B model (OLMo-2); localization and release rates may not generalize to other architectures or scales.

## Confidence
- **Localization claim (layers 24–31 critical):** High (extreme statistical significance, p < 10⁻¹⁸⁹, zero release from lower layers).  
- **Synthetic core efficacy (76.6% release):** Medium (robust in-paper but depends on unreplicated anchor design and patching fidelity).  
- **Detection as stable attractor (83% restore vs 58% suppress):** Medium (asymmetry observed but may not generalize).  
- **Epistemic specificity (true-premise core 0% release):** High (clear experimental outcome).  
- **Cascade collapse near-total (99.8% O5 compliance):** High (baseline result across 500 chains).

## Next Checks
1. Replicate layer sweep on a small chain subset to confirm the 24–31 localization drop-off and rule out implementation artifacts.  
2. Test core specificity: patch a core extracted from an O2 true-premise refusal into collapsed O5 chains; release should be near 0%.
