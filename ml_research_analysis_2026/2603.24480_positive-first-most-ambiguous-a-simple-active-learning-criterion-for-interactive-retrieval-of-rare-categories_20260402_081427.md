# Positive-First Most Ambiguous: A Simple Active Learning Criterion for Interactive Retrieval of Rare Categories

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24480
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24480
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PF-MA achieves higher class coverage (diverse positive retrieval) than strong baselines in early iterations of interactive rare-category retrieval, while maintaining a high proportion of selected positives.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles interactive fine-grained visual retrieval for rare categories under tight annotation budgets and low-latency constraints. Conventional active learning fails here because its symmetric uncertainty sampling is overwhelmed by the vast number of negative (non-target) examples, leading to batches with few relevant positives. The authors propose Positive-First Most Ambiguous (PF-MA), an active learning criterion that explicitly prioritizes ambiguous samples predicted to be positive. This asymmetric scoring ensures early discovery of diverse target examples while still selecting informative negatives. Evaluated on long-tailed visual datasets using frozen CLIP and DINOv2 features, PF-MA consistently outperforms prior methods on a class coverage metric, particularly in the first few critical iterations, and maintains a high positive selection rate without harming downstream classifier performance.

## Method Summary
PF-MA modifies standard uncertainty-based active learning (Most Ambiguous, MA) with an asymmetric scoring function. Given a binary classifier's score \( f(x) \), it defines a per-sample score that:
1. For samples predicted positive (\( f(x) \geq 0.5 \)), scores them by their uncertainty \( 1 - |0.5 - f(x)| \), favoring those near the decision boundary.
2. For samples predicted negative (\( f(x) < 0.5 \)), scores them by raw confidence \( f(x) \), treating them as less urgent.
The batch is filled by taking the top-scoring samples, which inherently privileges boundary-adjacent positives first. A linear SVM is retrained each iteration on frozen features to meet strict latency requirements.

## Key Results
- PF-MA achieves significantly higher class coverage (diversity of retrieved positives) than MA, MP, ALAMP, and diversity-enhanced variants on CIFAR100-LT, ImageNet-LT, and PlantNet300K, with the largest gains in early iterations (e.g., coverage 0.954 vs. ~0.85 for MA at iteration 25 on CIFAR100-LT DINOv2).
- PF-MA maintains a high proportion of selected positives (>80% across iterations), directly improving user satisfaction in the interactive loop, whereas MA selects only ~60% positives.
- The method's effectiveness is robust to unknown class size and choice of visual feature representation (CLIP vs. DINOv2).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** PF-MA corrects the symmetric sampling bias of conventional uncertainty-based active learning by explicitly favoring predicted positives near the decision boundary.
- **Mechanism:** The scoring function applies the uncertainty term only to samples in the predicted positive half-space, while using raw confidence for predicted negatives. This ensures early retrieval of diverse positives while still allowing informative negatives if the budget remains.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states it "explicitly addresses the class imbalance asymmetry"; [section 3.3] formally defines PF-MA and contrasts with MA/MP; empirical results show higher early coverage.
- **Break condition:** If the predicted positive half-space contains very few near-boundary samples (classifier initially overconfident on positives), PF-MA degrades toward MP, reducing boundary refinement informativeness.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** PF-MA maintains a high proportion of selected positives across iterations, improving user satisfaction without sacrificing classifier generalization.
- **Mechanism:** By construction, PF-MA fills the batch first with boundary-adjacent positives before considering negatives. Empirically, this yields >80% positive selection rates, contrasting with MA's negative-heavy batches.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 5.6] Fig. 6 shows PF-MA's positive ratio consistently >80% vs. MA's ~60%; [section 3.1] defines the low-latency, tight-budget problem constraints.
- **Break condition:** If the classifier becomes highly biased early (e.g., poor initialization), the positive half-space may contract, forcing more negative selections and lowering the positive ratio.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** PF-MA's preference for boundary positives inherently promotes diversity across visual modes, as measured by class coverage.
- **Mechanism:** Positives near the decision boundary are more likely to lie in sparser, under-explored regions of the positive manifold. Selecting these first populates more K-means clusters than selecting high-confidence positives (which are tightly clustered) or uncertain negatives (which are irrelevant).
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.4] links boundary samples to mode exploration; [section 5.2] Tab. 3 shows PF-MA's highest coverage; [section 5.5.2] Fig. 5 shows stable performance across class sizes.
- **Break condition:** If the target class is intrinsically unimodal or compact, all positive selection strategies may achieve similar coverage; the boundary-pursuit advantage diminishes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Active Learning under Extreme Class Imbalance**
  - *Why needed:* Standard AL assumes symmetric class utilities and that the most informative samples lie near a balanced decision boundary. In rare-category retrieval, the boundary is overwhelmed by negatives, causing MA to sample mostly irrelevant items.
  - *Quick check:* In a 1:1000 imbalanced binary problem, what does the uncertainty score distribution look like, and why does MA select mostly negatives?

- **Concept: Interactive Retrieval Objectives vs. Classification Objectives**
  - *Why needed:* The goal is to maximize user-perceived discovery (diverse positives) under tight budgets, not test F1. A positive is valuable immediately upon labeling; a negative is only valuable for future boundary refinement.
  - *Quick check:* Why would a strategy that maximizes test F1 at iteration 25 be poor for an interactive user stopping at iteration 5?

- **Concept: Linear Classifiers in Low-Data, Low-Latency Regimes**
  - *Why needed:* The paper uses a linear SVM because it trains in seconds on few labeled examples, meeting the latency constraint. Finetuning deep networks (e.g., CLIP) would violate this.
  - *Quick check:* What are the tradeoffs of using a linear probe on frozen features versus finetuning the backbone for this interactive setup?

## Architecture Onboarding
Feature Extractor (CLIP/DINOv2) -> Linear SVM Classifier -> PF-MA Scorer -> Batch Selector -> User Annotation -> Update Labeled Set -> repeat
- **Critical path:** Pre-compute features (once) -> Train linear SVM -> Compute PF-MA scores for all unlabeled -> Select top-b -> User annotates -> Update set -> repeat. Latency bottleneck is SVM training + scoring; linear model keeps iterations within seconds.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Batch size `b` (larger faster coverage but more fatigue); coverage granularity `K` (finer diversity but noisier for small classes); descriptor choice (DINOv2 better for visual-only, CLIP enables future text queries).
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Positive ratio collapse:** PF-MA's positive ratio drops below ~50% → classifier too confident, boundary positives exhausted. Remedy: adjust threshold or inject random positives.
  2. **Coverage stagnation:** `cov_t` plateaus early → batch diversity insufficient. Remedy: consider post-PF-MA diversification rerank, though paper found unstable.
  3. **Latency overflow:** Iteration > few seconds → feature extraction not cached? or classifier too complex. Revert to linear SVM with pre-extracted features.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce Fig. 4: Run PF-MA, MA, MP on CIFAR100-LT (DINOv2) for T=25. Plot average `cov_t`; expect PF-MA to lead early.
  2. Validate positive ratio: Track proportion of positives per batch. Confirm PF-MA >80% vs. MA ~60%.
  3. Descriptor robustness: Run PF-MA on ImageNet-LT with CLIP and DINOv2. Expect DINOv2 higher coverage, but PF-MA's relative advantage holds.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does PF-MA perform when users query multiple rare classes simultaneously?
- Would human user studies validate the assumption that a high positive ratio improves satisfaction and retention?
- Can PF-MA be extended to multi-class active learning settings?
- How does the method behave if the initial classifier is highly biased or the positive half-space is very small?

## Limitations
- Satisfaction is proxied by positive ratio; no human validation of user experience.
- Evaluation limited to CLIP and DINOv2 features; other representations may behave differently.
- Designed for single-class retrieval; multi-class scenarios are unexplored.
- Theoretical break condition (exhausted near-boundary positives) not observed in experiments but possible with poor initialization.

## Confidence
- **Asymmetric scoring corrects bias:** High – mechanism clear, strong empirical support.
- **High positive ratio improves satisfaction:** Medium – ratio is high, but satisfaction not measured directly.
- **Boundary positives increase diversity:** Medium – coverage gains consistent, but user-level impact indirect.
