# BRIDGE: Benchmark for multi-hop Reasoning In long multimodal Documents with Grounded Evidence

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.07931
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07931
*   **Reference Count:** 29
*   **Primary Result:** State-of-the-art LLMs achieve high judge-based scores (~4.4/5 audit), but a ColPali-based RAG pipeline degrades end-to-end quality (~1.7 audit drop), revealing failures in multi-hop evidence aggregation.

## Executive Summary
BRIDGE is a benchmark designed to evaluate multi-hop question answering over long scientific documents that integrate text, tables, and figures. It addresses a gap in existing benchmarks by providing explicit, step-level annotations for reasoning chains, allowing for finer-grained diagnosis of model capabilities. The dataset comprises 11,857 QA pairs constructed from 262 arXiv papers via a two-stage LLM prompting and dual filtering pipeline. Key findings show that while large language models perform well on answer generation under direct prompting, they struggle with modality-specific reasoning (especially tables) and evidence aggregation across distant pages. Crucially, a standard page-level retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipeline significantly underperforms compared to no-retrieval baselines, highlighting a core challenge in retrieving and synthesizing disparate multi-modal evidence for multi-hop reasoning.

## Method Summary
The benchmark is built by first parsing arXiv PDFs with Adobe's Extract API to obtain a layout-aware representation of text, tables, and figures. A two-stage LLM pipeline then generates QA pairs: Stage 1 mines entity structures (e.g., entities, relations) from each document; Stage 2 uses these structures with formal definitions and multi-hop constraints to produce questions and answers paired with explicit evidence hops. A dual filter—a rule-based step followed by an LLM-as-a-judge filter (Qwen-Plus)—removes low-quality instances by assessing grounding, faithfulness, and reasoning depth using the PDF anchors. Evaluation uses the same LLM judge to score model outputs on accuracy, fidelity to the evidence, and an aggregate "audit" score, alongside lexical metrics like ROUGE-L.

## Key Results
*   State-of-the-art LLMs (e.g., ChatGPT) achieve high audit scores (~4.4/5) with direct prompting, but performance varies significantly with prompting strategy (Direct, CoT, Reflection).
*   A ColPali-based RAG pipeline (top-3 page retrieval + Gemini) causes a substantial audit score drop (~1.7) compared to the Gemini direct baseline, indicating failure in aggregating multi-hop evidence.
*   Table-centric evidence and comparative questions are the most challenging modalities; performance declines monotonically as the location of the required evidence moves to later pages in the document.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** The two-stage LLM prompting and dual filtering pipeline can scalably generate high-confidence multi-hop QA pairs with explicit, verifiable evidence chains from scientific PDFs.
*   **Mechanism:** Stage 1 extracts question-type-specific entity structures; Stage 2 uses these, formal definitions, and multi-hop constraints to generate QA pairs with annotated evidence hops. A rule-based filter removes malformed instances, and an LLM-as-a-judge filter evaluates grounding, faithfulness, and reasoning depth, using PDF anchors to flag hallucinations and single-hop shortcuts.
*   **Core Assumption:** Structured PDF extraction metadata (page index, bounding boxes) provides a reliable ground for identifying and verifying evidence locations across text, tables, and figures.
*   **Break Condition:** If PDF extraction fails to consistently preserve layout-aware coordinates and semantic boundaries for tables/figures, the evidence anchoring becomes unreliable, causing the judge filter to misflag valid hops or accept invalid ones.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Providing explicit step-level reasoning annotations and evaluation exposes systematic failures in evidence aggregation and grounding hidden by conventional answer-only metrics.
*   **Mechanism:** Annotating each required evidence hop (e.g., "Fact from Table 2 → Fact from Fig 3 → Answer") allows evaluation to check both final answer correctness and the fidelity of the retrieved/used evidence chain, revealing discrepancies where an answer is lexically plausible but unsupported.
*   **Core Assumption:** The correctness of a multi-hop answer is contingent on the correct identification and integration of all specified evidence pieces; a failure in any hop invalidates the answer.
*   **Break Condition:** If the annotated evidence chains contain errors/omissions or the LLM judge is inconsistent, the diagnostic value of the annotations is compromised, and observed failures may reflect annotation noise.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** The structural differences between text, tables, and figures create modality-specific reasoning difficulties not equally mitigated by current LLM/RAG architectures.
*   **Mechanism:** Reasoning requires different parsing and integration strategies for linear prose (text), structured data (tables), and visual relationships (figures). RAG systems like ColPali, optimized for page-level visual retrieval, may fail to precisely locate specific table cells or figure axes needed for a hop.
*   **Core Assumption:** The difficulty for table-centric questions is an intrinsic property of the modality's structure (dense, relational, requiring precise alignment).
*   **Break Condition:** If "table-centric" questions are confounded by other factors (e.g., they also appear on later pages or require more hops), the observed performance drop may not be purely modality-driven.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Multi-hop Reasoning**
    *   **Why needed:** The dataset's core purpose is to evaluate reasoning that requires combining multiple pieces of evidence. Understanding what constitutes a "hop" is fundamental to interpreting the dataset structure and model performance breakdowns.
    *   **Quick Check:** If an answer requires comparing a value in Table 2 and a trend in Figure 3, how many hops are involved, and what are the intermediate conclusions for each hop?
*   **Concept: Multimodal Document Parsing & Representation**
    *   **Why needed:** The benchmark relies on a coherent representation of a long PDF as a collection of semantic entities (text spans, table entries, figures) with precise location metadata. This parsing is the prerequisite for any system to access and ground its responses.
    *   **Quick Check:** Why is preserving bounding-box coordinates for a table cell more critical for BRIDGE than for a standard text-only QA dataset?
*   **Concept: Evidence Grounding & Fidelity**
    *   **Why needed:** A key innovation is evaluating not just answer text, but whether the answer is *grounded* in the specific, annotated evidence pieces. This separates a lucky guess from a genuinely reasoned response.
    *   **Quick Check:** How could a model generate a high-ROUGE-score answer that still receives a low fidelity score from the LLM judge?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** PDF Ingestion & Parsing (Adobe PDF Extract API) -> Document Representation -> QA Dataset Generator (Two-stage LLM prompting + Dual Filter) -> Evaluation Engine (LLM-as-a-judge). For RAG baselines: Question -> ColPali Retriever (top-k pages) -> Context Construction -> LLM Generator -> Evaluation.
*   **Critical path:** The end-to-end evaluation of a new model follows: Model Input (Question + Full Doc) -> Model Output (Answer) -> LLM-Judge Scoring -> Audit/Acc/Fid Scores. For RAG: Question -> ColPali Retrieval (Top 3 Pages) -> Constructed Context -> Model Output -> Scoring. Reproducing benchmark results hinges on implementing the judge's evaluation protocol with the provided ground truth evidence chains.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Granularity of retrieval:** Evidence is often sub-page (a table row), but ColPali retrieves entire pages. This coarse-to-fine mismatch is a primary cause of RAG degradation.
    *   **Judge reliability:** Using a single LLM (Qwen-Plus) as judge is scalable but introduces bias/error. Tradeoff is between cost/scalability and the need for multiple human-validated judges.
    *   **Static vs. dynamic evidence:** The benchmark assumes evidence is present in the PDF. A more robust system might need to handle retrieval failure with "evidence not found," but current evaluation does not score abstentions.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **ColPali-RAG degradation:** A >1.0 drop in audit score compared to the "Direct" baseline for the same LLM indicates failure in multi-evidence aggregation due to retrieval missing key pages/hops.
    *   **Modality-specific drop:** A significant performance delta (e.g., >0.5 audit) between "Table" evidence and "Text/Figure" evidence signals inability to process structured tabular data.
    *   **Page-depth decay:** Monotonic audit score decrease as the furthest evidence page index increases indicates long-context search/attention failure.
    *   **Strategy sensitivity:** Large variance in a model's performance across Direct/CoT/Reflection strategies suggests instability in reasoning processes.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Parsing Sanity Check:** Process a sample of 10 BRIDGE PDFs with the Adobe Extract API. Manually verify that all tables/figures cited in the ground truth QA pairs are extracted with correct page numbers and that table structures are parseable. Log failure rate.
    2.  **Judge Validation:** Take 100 QA instances with their gold answers and evidence chains. Have two human annotators score the model's answer (from a strong model like ChatGPT Direct) for accuracy and fidelity independently. Correlate human scores with the Qwen-Plus judge scores.
    3.  **RAG Degradation Replication:** Implement the ColPali (k=3) + Gemini pipeline. Run on the full test set. Confirm the audit score drop of ~1.7 relative to Gemini Direct is reproducible. If not, audit the retrieval step: what percentage of gold evidence pages are *not* in the top-3 retrieved pages?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** Why does the ColPali-based RAG pipeline substantially degrade end-to-end multi-hop QA performance compared to direct prompting? The paper identifies retrieval mismatch but does not diagnose whether the retriever fails to capture cross-page dependencies, multi-modal cues, or hop-level relevance.
*   **Open Question 2:** What inherent properties of tabular data make it the most challenging evidence modality? The paper observes the trend but does not investigate whether difficulty stems from structural complexity, sparse numerical grounding, or model pre-training biases.
*   **Open Question 3:** How does the positional location of required evidence (page depth) systematically impact performance, and what factors—context window limits, retrieval decay, or positional bias—are primary drivers? The correlation is established but not disentangled.
*   **Open Question 4:** Why do different LLMs exhibit contradictory responses to prompting strategies (e.g., Gemini degrades under CoT while Qwen improves)? The paper reports the effect but does not hypothesize underlying causes.
*   **Open Question 5:** To what extent do high judge-based answer scores correlate with correct intermediate reasoning steps, and where do mismatches between final answer correctness and step-level fidelity occur? The benchmark provides step annotations but evaluation focuses on final answers without quantifying step-wise accuracy or error propagation.

## Limitations
*   Proprietary PDF parser (Adobe) and single LLM judge (Qwen-Plus) used; no open replication.
*   Generation/evaluation prompts and hyperparameters undisclosed; results may not be stable.
*   Corpus restricted to arXiv STEM; findings may not transfer to other domains.
*   RAG analysis only with page-level ColPali; finer retrieval not tested.

## Confidence

*   **Pipeline quality** (two-stage generation + dual filter): **Medium**
*   **Performance results** (LLM scores, RAG drop, modality/page effects): **Medium**
