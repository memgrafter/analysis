# Context-Dependent Affordance Computation in Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04419
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04419
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** VLMs exhibit massive context-dependent affordance drift (mean word-level Jaccard similarity = 0.095) across agent personas, with latent orthogonal factors (e.g., "Culinary", "Access") explaining >98% of context variance.

## Executive Summary
This study tests whether vision-language models (VLMs) compute spatial affordances in a context-dependent manner, challenging the geometry-first computer vision paradigm. Using a large-scale experiment with 3,213 scene-context pairs from COCO-2017, the authors primed seven agentic personas (e.g., chef, security) and measured divergence in generated affordance descriptions from Qwen-VL 30B and LLaVA-1.5-13B. Results show over 90% lexical change and substantial semantic drift, far exceeding stochastic generation noise. Tucker decomposition reveals stable orthogonal latent manifolds (e.g., "Culinary" isolated for chef contexts, "Access" spanning child-mobility contrasts). The findings imply that VLM affordance computation is highly dynamic and goal-sensitive, suggesting robotics could benefit from query-dependent ontological projection rather than static world modeling.

## Method Summary
Images from COCO-2017 were paired with seven persona-based context primes (e.g., "You are a chef"). For each image, both VLMs generated structured JSON outputs listing objects with affordances and reasoning. Text was extracted by concatenating `name`, `affordance`, and `reasoning` fields. Primary metrics were word-level and object-level Jaccard similarity (whitespace-tokenized, lowercase) across primes, and sentence-level cosine similarity using `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` embeddings. A tensor of shape `(n_images, n_primes, embedding_dim)` was constructed for images with complete prime coverage and decomposed via Tucker factorization with ranks `[10,3,10]`. Stochastic baselines (multiple seeds/temperatures) ruled out sampling noise, and bootstrap resampling assessed factor stability.

## Key Results
- **Massive lexical drift:** Mean word-level Jaccard similarity between different context conditions = 0.095 (95% CI: [0.093, 0.096], p<0.0001), with over 90% of tokens changing.
- **Substantial semantic drift:** Mean sentence-level cosine similarity = 0.415; 58.5% of variation is context-dependent.
- **Orthogonal latent factors:** Tucker decomposition isolates a "Culinary Manifold" (Chef loading 0.95 on Dim2) and an "Access Axis" (Child +0.72 vs. Mobility –0.60 on Dim3), explaining >98% of captured context variance with tight bootstrap confidence intervals.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Contextual Biasing of Affordance Competition
**Claim:** Context primes bias the VLM's attention toward functionally relevant objects, filtering out non-affording geometry for the current goal state.  
**Mechanism:** Agentic context (Θ) modulates competition among simultaneously active affordance representations (Cisek, 2007). Shifting from "Chef" to "Security" reweights the latent affordance space, causing different objects to dominate. The ~10% invariant signal likely corresponds to geometrically salient but functionally neutral features (e.g., large surfaces, human shapes).  
**Evidence anchors:** Jaccard 0.095; stochastic baseline variance ratio >3 (ruling out noise); weak direct corpus evidence (neighboring papers study affordance grounding but not *quantified context-drift*).  
**Break condition:** If stochastic baseline showed within-prime variance matching cross-prime variance (η² ≈ 0), drift would be an artifact of decoding, not context modulation.

### Mechanism 2: Orthogonal Functional Manifolds
**Claim:** The VLM projects scenes onto a low-dimensional functional manifold where distinct agent contexts occupy approximately orthogonal subspaces (e.g., "Culinary" vs. "Access").  
**Mechanism:** Context primes activate different basis vectors in a latent affordance space. Tucker decomposition reveals Chef, Child, and Mobility map to largely non-overlapping dimensions (Dim2, Dim3), explaining >98% of context variance. This disentanglement likely emerges from training on diverse task-oriented image-text pairs.  
**Evidence anchors:** Factor loadings: Chef (0.95) isolated on Dim2; Child (+0.72) vs. Mobility (–0.60) opposition on Dim3; Dim1 (general salience) explains only 0.9% of variance; bootstrap CIs tight (e.g., Chef on Dim2: [0.948, 0.959]). Moderate corpus FMR (0.58) supports affordance manifold concept but not orthogonality.  
**Break condition:** If Tucker factors failed bootstrap stability (φ < 0.95) or showed high cross-loading (e.g., Chef significantly loading on "Access" dimension), the orthogonal manifold claim would weaken.

### Mechanism 3: Training-Driven Semantic-First Computation
**Claim:** The VLM's training on diverse image-text pairs implicitly teaches it to compute affordances as primary, context-sensitive outputs, bypassing explicit geometric reconstruction.  
**Mechanism:** Pre-training objective (next-token prediction on web-scale alt-text) rewards predicting *functional descriptions*. Since natural language descriptions are inherently goal-laden (e.g., kitchen described by food blogger vs. realtor), the model learns function-first associations; geometry surfaces only if contextually relevant (e.g., "flat surface" for chef).  
**Evidence anchors:** Prediction processing account (Sec 3.4); human baseline annotations are functional (e.g., "a chair to sit on"); strong topical match (FMR 0.68) with studies showing VLMs align with human neural representations.  
**Break condition:** If a control experiment using purely geometric prompts (e.g., "count the edges") showed minimal context effect, it would isolate *affordance-specific* context-dependence vs. general text-generation bias.

## Foundational Learning
- **Affordance Competition (Cisek, 2007):** Neurocomputational framework where multiple action possibilities compete, with contextual biasing selecting a winner.  
  *Why needed:* Explains 90% drift as biased competition among affordances, not creation/destruction.  
  *Quick check:* If Chef and Security contexts activate different affordance populations, what neural mechanism might implement the biasing signal (e.g., prefrontal cortex)?

- **Tucker Decomposition:** Tensor factorization method extracting orthogonal latent factors from multi-way data.  
  *Why needed:* Reveals "Culinary" and "Access" dimensions explaining >98% of context variance.  
  *Quick check:* What does a high loading of "Chef" on Dim2 and near-zero on Dim3 tell us about the relationship between task families?

- **Action-Distance vs. Euclidean Distance:** Hypothesis that spatial reasoning in semantic-first systems operates on affordance-derived metrics, not raw geometry.  
  *Why needed:* Prediction (P3) suggests "Access Axis" may reflect action-distance gradients.  
  *Quick check:* How would you design an experiment to test whether a VLM's spatial predictions align with action-distance (e.g., "path to wheelchair-accessible route") vs. Euclidean shortest path?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
VLM Inference Engine -> Affordance Extractor -> Embedding Module -> Tensor Builder -> Decomposer -> Statistical Suite

**Critical path:** Image/Prime → VLM → JSON → Text → Embeddings → Tensor → Tucker → Factors → Stability analysis.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Tokenization:** Whitespace tokenization reproducible but inflates lexical drift; stopword filtering increases estimated context-dependence (95.2% vs 90.5%).  
- **Temperature:** 0.7 balances diversity/coherence; stochastic baseline shows effect holds even at T=0.0 (η²≈0.27). High T may amplify lexical drift but not change qualitative conclusion.  
- **Tensor rank:** Low rank [10,3,10] yields interpretable factors but only 46.6% explained variance; higher ranks add variance but risk uninterpretable factors.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **High JSON parse failure rate (>10%):** Indicates VLM prompt/format instability; may bias toward simpler contexts.  
- **Tucker factor instability (low φ < 0.9 in bootstrap):** Suggests latent structure not robust; maybe rank chosen poorly or primes not orthogonal in VLM's space.  
- **Context-insensitive outputs (high mean J > 0.3):** Would refute main finding; check if prime wording is salient enough or if model is instruction-tuned to ignore persona prompts.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Cross-model replication:** Run full pipeline on a third VLM (e.g., GPT-4V) to confirm generalizability of orthogonal "Culinary/Access" factor structure.
2. **Prompt ablation test:** Replace persona primes with direct functional queries (e.g., "What can you cook here?") to isolate *semantic content* from *persona framing*; does drift persist?
3. **Human-VLM alignment:** Use TAR dataset to compare VLM context rankings against human task-conditioned affordance judgments; does "Culinary Manifold" match human cooking-task prioritization?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Component ablation (P1):** Does ablation of context encoding cause greater degradation in spatial/affordance tasks than ablation of geometric or object recognition components? Requires systematic lesioning of VLM modules.
2. **Action-distance computation (P3):** Is spatial reasoning computed over affordance-based action-distance rather than Euclidean distance? Needs spatial tasks comparing the two metrics.
3. **Attentional allocation (P4):** Does attention track affordance-relevance to current goals rather than geometric salience? Requires eye-tracking or attention mechanism analysis.
4. **Prompt artifact control:** Are drifts due to specific prompt templates vs. genuine semantic re-interpretation? Needs same-prompt, different-image control.
5. **Processing stage:** At what stage does context modulation occur—early visual representations or output generation? Requires representational probes (e.g., layer-wise probing, activation patching).

## Limitations
- **Unreleased data subset:** Exact COCO images (479 total, 360 with full prime coverage) not provided; risks sampling bias if replicated on full COCO.
- **Unavailable analysis code:** GitHub repo (`studiofarzulla/semantic-vision`) predates paper; re-implementation may differ in tokenization, Tucker rank, or stability metrics.
- **Embedding assumption:** Use of `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` assumes it preserves VLM's functional space; no direct validation.
- **Emergent claim:** Zero citations for core concepts (orthogonal affordance manifolds); phenomenon is novel but not yet established in literature.

## Confidence
- **Massive context-dependent drift (90% lexical change):** High — directly measured (Jaccard 0.095, p<0.0001) with robust stochastic baseline ruling out noise.
- **Orthogonal latent factors (Culinary/Access manifolds):** Medium — relies on Tucker decomposition stability (tight bootstrap CIs) but depends on unreleased image subset and embedding choice.
- **Functional-first, geometry-bypassing computation:** Low — inferred from dataset bias and model behavior; requires geometric control prompt to isolate affordance-specific effects.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-model replication:** Run full pipeline on a third VLM (e.g., GPT-4V) to test generalizability of the orthogonal factor structure.
2. **Prompt ablation test:** Replace persona primes with direct functional queries to determine if drift is driven by persona framing or distinct functional goals.
3. **Human-VLM alignment benchmark:** Compare VLM context-driven affordance rankings against human task-conditioned judgments from a dataset like TAR to test ecological validity.
