# FENCE: A Financial and Multimodal Jailbreak Detection Dataset

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.18154
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18154
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Introduces FENCE, a 10k-sample bilingual finance-specific multimodal jailbreak dataset; a baseline detector achieves 99% in-distribution accuracy; fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL 3B improves average Defense Success Rate by 32.65 percentage points across five external benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
FENCE addresses the lack of finance-focused multimodal jailbreak datasets by introducing a curated collection of 10,000 Korean–English samples featuring image-grounded attacks derived from real financial queries. The dataset emphasizes semantic alignment between benign and harmful pairs, enabling discriminative training of safety models. Empirical results show that a detector trained on FENCE achieves near-perfect in-distribution accuracy and significantly boosts the defense capabilities of small vision-language models on diverse benchmarks, demonstrating its utility for building robust financial AI guardrails.

## Method Summary
Dataset construction starts with Korean financial FAQs, which are transformed into harmful variants using GPT-4o via a two-step prompting process (role-play then evaluation) to preserve semantic proximity. Finance-themed images are sourced from Pixabay using keyword crawls and fused with text via layout templates (e.g., FigStep) to create image-attack (IA) samples. The resulting bilingual dataset is balanced (50:50 benign/harmful) across 15+ categories. For model training, a small VLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL 3B) is fine-tuned on the FENCE training split using cross-entropy loss for binary classification, then evaluated on in-distribution and out-of-distribution benchmarks.

## Key Results
- Baseline detector trained on FENCE achieves 99% in-distribution accuracy on the test split.
- Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL 3B on FENCE increases average Defense Success Rate by 32.65 percentage points across five external benchmarks.
- FENCE's exclusive focus on image-based attacks yields higher Attack Success Rates on VLMs compared to text-only benchmarks, exposing a critical vulnerability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A balanced dataset of semantically aligned benign-harmful query pairs improves a detector's ability to learn discriminative features for finance-specific jailbreak detection.
- **Mechanism:** Real-world financial FAQs are transformed into harmful variants using a controlled GPT-4o prompting (role-play + evaluation). This preserves contextual and lexical similarity, forcing models to detect subtle intent cues rather than keyword mismatches.
- **Core assumption:** Financial jailbreaks require distinguishing harmful intent embedded in plausible, domain-relevant contexts.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract emphasizes finance-relevant queries with image-grounded threats; Section 3.2.2 describes the transformation process and shows t-SNE clustering; Figure 4 illustrates semantic alignment.
- **Break condition:** If harmful queries deviate semantically from benign sources, the discriminative challenge is lost, leading to overfitting on superficial toxicity.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Focusing on image-based attacks (IA) exposes a broader attack surface in VLMs than text-only attacks.
- **Mechanism:** All jailbreak samples are IA (BaseImg, TextImg, FigStep), forcing detectors to learn cross-modal safety signals. This targets the weakness that vision encoders are less aligned than LLMs.
- **Core assumption:** Multimodal jailbreaks in finance often manipulate image content (e.g., forged documents) because text filters are more mature.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract highlights image-grounded threats; Section 2.2 defines IA and notes vision encoder vulnerabilities; Section 3.2.1 describes IA construction; Table 4 shows higher ASR on VLMs for FENCE.
- **Break condition:** If finance-relevant deceptive visuals are implausible or irrelevant, visual threat detection won't generalize to real attacks.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Bilingual (Korean-English), domain-specific construction captures cultural and linguistic nuances of financial harm missing in generic English datasets.
- **Mechanism:** Starting with Korean financial FAQs ensures local product/regulation phrasing. English translation provides accessibility while preserving authenticity via a Korean-first pipeline.
- **Core assumption:** Financial harm expressions are culturally specific; generic English jailbreak datasets miss region-specific patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes bilingual, Korean-first design; Section 3.2.1 explains Korean-first derivation from FAQs; Section 4.2 shows 94–98% accuracy on native Korean test split.
- **Break condition:** If translation loses nuance or Korean sources aren't representative, the bilingual advantage diminishes for non-English deployments.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Pipeline (FAQ collection, GPT-4o transformation, image crawling/fusion) -> Model Hub (pretrained VLMs like Qwen2.5-VL) -> Evaluation Stack (in-distribution and OOD benchmarks, metrics: accuracy, DSR/ASR).
- **Critical path:** Raw Financial FAQs → Benign-Harmful Pair Generation → Image Sourcing & Fusion → Dataset Split → Model Fine-tuning → Cross-Benchmark Evaluation. Most novel/error-prone step: GPT-4o transformation for semantic alignment.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Scale vs. Realism: 10k samples is smaller than general benchmarks but prioritizes domain realism and balanced pairs.
  - Synthetic vs. Real Attacks: GPT-4o generation is controllable but may lack diversity of human-crafted jailbreaks.
  - Bilingual Focus: Korean-first adds overhead but captures local context; limits global applicability.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low ITR (Image-Text Recognition) scores correlating with poor classification accuracy.
  - Severe OOD performance drop (e.g., on JailBreakV-28K), indicating overfitting to finance-specific patterns or IA format.
  - High ASR on FENCE for a "safe" model (e.g., GPT-4o's 3.2%), showing dataset samples are effective jailbreaks.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate baseline detector: Train a classifier (e.g., PaliGemma2) on FENCE train split, verify 99% accuracy on test split.
  2. Fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL 3B on FENCE, measure DSR improvement on external benchmarks (JailBreakV-28K mini, FigStep mini) to reproduce +32.65 pp average gain.
  3. Ablate image modality: Train a text-only classifier on FENCE, compare accuracy to multimodal version; expect significant drop if IA is crucial.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can FENCE be scaled and diversified to support broader linguistic coverage and more comprehensive financial scenarios?
- **Basis in paper:** Limitations note narrower scale/domain and bilingual focus limiting generalization.
- **Why unresolved:** Current design is tailored to specific languages/contexts; expansion requires new data collection/annotation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Release of extended FENCE with additional languages (e.g., Mandarin, Spanish) and financial categories, validated via human annotation.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent do GPT-4o-generated synthetic jailbreak prompts fail to capture real-world, human-crafted multimodal adversarial diversity in finance?
- **Basis in paper:** Limitations acknowledge synthetic prompts may not capture full user behavior variety.
- **Why unresolved:** Single-model generation may introduce biases and miss novel attacks, especially cultural/regulatory nuances.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative study on FENCE vs. human-authored finance jailbreaks from red-teaming, analyzing error patterns and ASR.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can the subjective definition of "harm" in financial contexts be operationalized for objective, legally aligned annotation guidelines?
- **Basis in paper:** Limitations note complexity of defining harm due to legal/regulatory factors, causing subjectivity.
- **Why unresolved:** Disagreements on harmful content (e.g., ambiguous advice vs. fraud) lead to inconsistent labeling; current reliance on GPT-4o and limited human checks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Multi-stakeholder framework (legal experts, compliance, advocates) with measurable inter-annotator agreement, tested against regulatory standards.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Do defensive gains from fine-tuning on FENCE generalize to VLMs with different architectures and training objectives?
- **Basis in paper:** Limitations state evaluation covered limited VLMs, suggesting need for broader validation.
- **Why unresolved:** Primary experiments used Qwen2.5-VL and few others; transferability to diverse model types (e.g., decoder-only, smaller models) is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Fine-tuning and evaluation across diverse VLMs (different architectures, sizes, alignment methods), measuring DSR/ASR shifts and failure modes.

## Limitations
- Dataset access is restricted; full release not publicly available.
- Train/test split ratios and test set size are unreported, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Synthetic jailbreak generation via GPT-4o may not capture full diversity of real-world attacks.
- Bilingual (Korean-first) design may limit applicability to other linguistic contexts.

- IA focus exposes critical multimodal vulnerability: High
