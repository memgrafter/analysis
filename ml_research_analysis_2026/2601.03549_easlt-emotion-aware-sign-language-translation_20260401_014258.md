# EASLT: Emotion-Aware Sign Language Translation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.03549
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03549
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: State-of-the-art BLEU‑4 scores of 26.15 on PHOENIX14T and 22.80 on CSL‑Daily; BLEURT scores of 61.0 and 57.8.

## Executive Summary
Sign language translation without glosses often overlooks facial expressions, causing ambiguity when manual signs are identical. EASLT addresses this with a decoupled multi‑path framework that explicitly processes spatial (hand/body), motion (temporal dynamics), and emotion (facial expressions) features in separate streams. The streams are fused via an Emotion‑Aware Fusion (EAF) module, which enhances emotion representations and uses them as a semantic anchor to modulate the other features. The fused features are temporally modeled by a 1D TCN and fed to an LLM (Flan‑T5/mT5 with LoRA) to generate the target text, trained with cross‑entropy and contrastive alignment losses.

EASLT achieves new state-of-the-art BLEU‑4 scores of 26.15 on PHOENIX14T and 22.80 on CSL‑Daily, along with BLEURT scores of 61.0 and 57.8. Ablation studies confirm the contribution of each component, and qualitative examples show successful disambiguation of signs that share identical manual gestures but opposite facial expressions (e.g., interrogatives, REVENGE vs RECIPROCATE). The work demonstrates the critical role of non‑manual signals and offers a robust fusion mechanism for multimodal sign language translation.

## Method Summary
EASLT’s architecture comprises three dedicated encoders: a CLIP‑ViT‑L/14 operating on multi‑scale frames to capture static hand shapes and body postures; a VideoMAE‑L/16 processing sliding video clips to model motion; and a ViT‑B/16 fine‑tuned on FER2013 that takes facial regions of interest to extract continuous affective dynamics. The outputs are projected to a common dimension. The Emotion‑Aware Fusion module first enhances the emotion stream via channel‑wise gating and quality scoring to produce a global emotion anchor, then adaptively modulates each stream’s features using this anchor. The modulated features are concatenated and passed through a 1D temporal convolutional network followed by a GELU‑MLP, yielding a sequence in the LLM’s latent space. Finally, an instruction‑tuned LLM (Flan‑T5 or mT5) with LoRA generates the translation, supervised by cross‑entropy loss and a contrastive alignment loss that aligns visual and textual embeddings.

## Key Results
- BLEU‑4: 26.15 on PHOENIX14T, 22.80 on CSL‑Daily; BLEURT: 61.0 and 57.8 (new SOTA).
- Ablation study: Removing the emotion stream or the EAF module degrades performance by >1 BLEU‑4 points, confirming each component’s contribution.
- Qualitative analysis shows successful disambiguation of identical manual gestures with opposite facial expressions (e.g., REVENGE vs RECIPROCATE, interrogatives).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Decoupled Multi‑Path Feature Extraction
- **Claim:** Separate encoders preserve modality‑specific information and prevent manual signals from drowning out facial cues.
- **Mechanism:** Spatial encoder (CLIP‑ViT‑L/14 on multi‑scale frames) extracts static hand/body shapes; motion encoder (VideoMAE‑L/16 on sliding clips) captures temporal dynamics; emotion encoder (ViT‑B/16 fine‑tuned on FER2013) processes face ROIs to yield continuous affective embeddings; temporal downsampling and interpolation produce smooth Ze.
- **Core assumption:** Manual and non‑manual signals have distinct statistical properties; joint encoding often leads to modality imbalance.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “separate encoders for spatial, motion, and emotion features”; [section 3.2] detailed encoder descriptions.
- **Break condition:** Face detection failure (occlusion, poor lighting) empties the emotion stream, potentially reverting to ambiguous manual-only processing.

### Mechanism 2: Emotion‑Aware Fusion (EAF) with Modulation
- **Claim:** The EAF module integrates the three streams by using the emotion representation as a dynamic regulator, leveraging affect as a semantic anchor to resolve ambiguities.
- **Mechanism:** Enhancer applies channel‑wise gating to Ze → Z′e, predicts frame‑level reliability scores qk, and computes global anchor Zg_e as a score‑weighted sum of Z′e. Modulator replicates Zg_e toZa, uses an MLP to predict scaling offset Δs and bias Δb, and a gating network σ produces g; modulation: Zmod_q = Zq ⊙ (1 + tanh(Δs)⊙g) + Δb⊙g for each query feature (Zs, Zm, Z′e). Temporal Layer (1D TCN + GELU‑MLP) projects into LLM space.
- **Core assumption:** Facial expressions provide a relatively stable semantic signal that can adaptively reshape the interpretation of manual gestures.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “Emotion‑Aware Fusion (EAF) module that enhances emotion representations and modulates other features using affect as a semantic anchor”; [section 3.3] full mathematical formulation.
- **Break condition:** Noisy or semantically misaligned emotion features (e.g., emotional rather than grammatical facial affect) may distort useful manual information instead of clarifying.

### Mechanism 3: Transfer Learning from General FER to Sign Language NMS
- **Claim:** Leveraging a facial emotion recognition model pre‑trained on FER2013 yields rich, continuous feature extraction for sign language facial expressions, avoiding reliance on discrete labels or MLLM‑generated descriptions.
- **Mechanism:** ViT‑B/16 fine‑tuned on FER2013 (7 basic emotions) produces per‑frame embeddings; temporal downsampling (st=8) reduces redundancy while linear interpolation fills gaps from detection failures; resulting continuous representations capture subtle muscle dynamics that structurally overlap with sign language grammatical markers.
- **Core assumption:** Low‑level facial muscle dynamics learned in general FER transfer to the non‑manual signals domain of sign language despite label differences.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.2] “a ViT fine‑tuned on the FER2013 dataset to extract frame‑level emotion embeddings”; [section 4.5] Table 5 shows fine‑tuned ViT‑B/16 outperforms vanilla ViT, ResNet‑50, and DINOv2.
- **Break condition:** If the sign language employs culturally specific facial expressions under‑represented in FER categories (e.g., nuanced affective blends), transferred features may be insufficient and require sign‑language‑specific fine‑tuning.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
Input video → Frame extraction → Face ROI detection  
├─ Spatial Encoder (CLIP‑ViT‑L/14, multi‑scale) → Zs  
├─ Motion Encoder (VideoMAE‑L/16, sliding clips) → Zm  
└─ Emotion Encoder (ViT‑B/16 fine‑tuned on FER2013) → Ze  
Zs, Zm, Ze → Projection (d=1024)  
Projected Ze → Enhancer (gating, quality scoring) → Zg_e  
Zg_e + (Zs, Zm, Z′e) → Modulator (MLP + gate) → Zmod_s, Zmod_m, Zmod_e  
Concatenated Zmod → TCN (1D conv) + GELU‑MLP → Z  
Z + prompt → LLM (Flan‑T5/mT5 with LoRA) → Translation

**Critical path**:
1. Preprocess video into frames and detect faces.
2. Extract Zs (per‑frame CLIP), Zm (per‑clip VideoMAE), Ze (per‑frame FER‑ViT).
3. Apply temporal downsampling/interpolation to Ze; project all to unified dimension.
4. Compute Zg_e via Enhancer on Ze.
5. Modulate Zs, Zm, Ze using Zg_e → Zmod.
6. Concatenate and run through TCN‑MLP → Z.
7. Feed Z + multilingual prompt to LLM; generate Y with teacher‑forcing.
8. Compute L_ce and L_align; back‑propagate total loss.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Decoupled vs. unified encoder: Decoupling preserves distinct modalities but increases memory; a unified backbone might mix signals but risks modality dominance.
- Emotion encoder choice: FER‑fine‑tuned ViT offers continuous dynamics; MLLM descriptions lose temporal granularity; generic ViT lacks affective specialization.
- Fusion strategy: Adaptive modulation (EAM) avoids simple concatenation and provides dynamic, context‑aware weighting, but adds computational overhead.
- Temporal sampling: Smaller st preserves cues but introduces redundancy; larger st reduces cost but may miss rapid affective shifts; st=8 found optimal.
- LLM selection: Instruction‑tuned models (Flan‑T5) outperform larger non‑instruction models (mT5) due to better prompt following.

**Failure signatures**:
- Face detection failure: ROI extraction returns empty for many frames → Ze becomes all‑zeros; monitor detection rate; consider fallback to a more robust detector (e.g., MTCNN).
- Poor emotion quality: Enhancer quality scores qk consistently low (< 0.1) → may indicate overly subtle expressions; consider lowering gating threshold or increasing face resolution.
- No improvement from Emo: Ablation shows BLEU‑4 drop < 0.5 when Emo removed → emotion features not contributing; check if dataset has sufficient NMS variation or if Ze is misaligned.
- Over‑smoothing: Using mean/max pooling on emotion (instead of single‑frame sampling) degrades R‑L → avoid pooling that attenuates intensity transients.
- Training instability: L_align and L_ce scales differ; if one dominates, adjust λ (currently 1.0) or use gradient clipping.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Ablation validation**: Train with (a) Emo removed, (b) EAF removed, (c) MA removed; expect ≥ +1 BLEU‑4 gain when adding each component.
2. **Emotion encoder comparison**: Replace FER‑fine‑tuned ViT with (a) vanilla ViT‑B/16, (b) ResNet‑50, (c) DINOv2‑ViT‑B/16; evaluate on PHOENIX14T; verify that fine‑tuned FER‑ViT achieves the highest R‑L (≥ 48.0).
3. **Temporal sampling analysis**: Vary st ∈ {4, 8, 16} and aggregation (single frame, mean pooling, max pooling); reproduce optimal st=8 with single frame outperforming pooling by ≥ 0.8 BLEU‑4.

## Limitations
- Face detection failures can remove the emotion stream, potentially reverting to ambiguous manual‑only processing.
- Transfer from general FER to sign language NMS may not generalize to languages with culturally distinct facial grammar.
- Adaptive modulation’s benefit over simpler fusion is confounded by parameter increase; training stability and scaling remain uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Decoupled Multi‑Path Extraction**: Medium
- **Emotion‑Aware Fusion**: Medium
- **FER Transfer for NMS**: High

## Next Checks
1. Measure performance on subsets with failed face detection and evaluate a fallback (e.g., spatial+motion only) to quantify robustness gap.
2. Replace the FER emotion encoder with one trained on sign‑language‑specific facial expressions to assess cultural mismatch and potential gains.
3. Ablate modulation by substituting Ze with shuffled/constant vectors while keeping EAF active; this isolates whether modulation adds value beyond mere emotion presence.
