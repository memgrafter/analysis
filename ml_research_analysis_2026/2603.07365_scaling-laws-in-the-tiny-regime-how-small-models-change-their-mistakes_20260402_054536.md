# Scaling Laws in the Tiny Regime: How Small Models Change Their Mistakes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07365  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07365  
- **Reference count:** 26  
- **Primary result:** Error rate follows power-law scaling with steep exponents; small models make radically different errors (Jaccard 0.35) and show extreme class triage; surprisingly, smallest models are best calibrated.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates neural scaling laws in the sub-20 million parameter regime, training 90 models on CIFAR-100 while varying width. The error rate follows a power law with exponents steeper than those reported for large language models. More strikingly, the set of errors made by the smallest and largest models overlaps by only 35%, indicating a fundamental reshuffling of mistakes. Small models exhibit extreme class triage: the Gini coefficient of per-class accuracy drops from 0.26 to 0.09 as size increases, while accuracy on the bottom-5 classes rises from 10% to 53%. Counter to typical trends, the smallest models are best calibrated (ECE=0.013).

## Method Summary
The authors train two model families (ScaleCNN and MobileNetV2) across widths yielding 22K–19.8M parameters on CIFAR-100. All configurations share fixed hyperparameters: SGD with momentum 0.9, weight decay 5×10⁻⁴, cosine annealing from 0.1 to 1e-5 over 200 epochs, batch size 128, and standard augmentations (random crop, flip, Cutout). Five random seeds per configuration. Primary metric is top-1 error rate, fitted to a power law `error ∼ N^(-α)`. Additional metrics: Jaccard overlap between error masks of different-sized models, Gini coefficient of per-class accuracy, and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) with 15 bins.

## Key Results
- Power-law scaling exponents: α = 0.156 (ScaleCNN), α = 0.106 (MobileNetV2), steeper than LLM exponents.
- Error set reshuffling: Jaccard overlap between smallest and largest ScaleCNN models is 0.35.
- Class triage: Gini coefficient falls from 0.26 to 0.09; bottom-5 class accuracy climbs from 10% to 53% as model size grows.
- Calibration anomaly: Smallest models achieve lowest ECE (0.013), peaking at 0.110 at mid-size.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The authors invoke spectral capacity theory: scaling exponent `α` depends on the data’s spectral decay exponent `β` and the model’s rank efficiency exponent `γ` via `α = γ(β-1)`. Smaller models have lower effective rank, leading to steeper error decay. The radical error reshuffling occurs because tiny models cannot capture rare modes in the data covariance, forcing them to fail on a completely different subset of samples than large models.

## Foundational Learning
- **Neural scaling laws**: Power-law relationships between model size and error. *Why needed*: To predict performance at untested sizes and understand efficiency limits. *Quick check*: Plot log(error) vs. log(parameters); linearity suggests power law.
- **Jaccard overlap**: Intersection over union of misclassified sample indices between two models. *Why needed*: Quantifies how much error sets overlap. *Quick check*: Compute for two models’ error masks; value near 1 means similar mistakes, near 0 means reshuffling.
- **Gini coefficient**: Measure of inequality in a distribution (here, per-class accuracy). *Why needed*: Captures severity of class-level performance disparities. *Quick check*: Sort class accuracies, compute area under Lorenz curve; 0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality.
- **Expected Calibration Error (ECE)**: Weighted average absolute difference between confidence and accuracy across bins. *Why needed*: Assesses reliability of predicted probabilities. *Quick check*: Bin predictions by confidence, compute mean accuracy per bin, sum |confidence - accuracy| weighted by bin size.
- **Spectral capacity theory**: Framework linking scaling laws to data covariance spectrum and model effective rank. *Why needed*: Provides mechanistic explanation for observed exponents. *Quick check*: Estimate effective rank from activation covariances; fit rank vs. parameters to obtain γ; check if α ≈ γ(β-1).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input -> Conv Blocks (ScaleCNN) / Inverted Residual Blocks (MobileNetV2) -> Global Pool -> Linear Classifier.
- **Critical path**: Data flows through convolutional feature extractor, undergoes spatial downsampling, then pooled and classified.
- **Design tradeoffs**: ScaleCNN uses uniform width scaling; MobileNetV2 uses inverted bottlenecks and width multipliers for efficiency, introducing discrete capacity jumps at certain multipliers due to rounding of internal dimensions.
- **Failure signatures**: Under-trained small models (loss change <1% flagged as not converged); hyperparameter mismatch across sizes; MobileNetV2 oscillations at small widths suggest architectural bottlenecks.
- **First three experiments**:
  1. Train smallest (ScaleCNN c=4, MobileNetV2 m=0.10) and largest models; verify test accuracies match reported ranges (~42% vs ~75% for ScaleCNN).
  2. Compute within-config Jaccard overlap across seeds; expect ~0.704 for ScaleCNN c=4, confirming reproducibility of error patterns.
  3. Fit power law to error vs. log(parameters); inspect residuals for curvature indicating non-power-law behavior.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generality**: Do scaling exponents, error redistribution, and class triage generalize beyond CIFAR-100 to other datasets and modalities?
- **Compression equivalence**: Does error reshuffling also occur with structured pruning and post-training quantization applied to large models?
- **Functional form**: Is the scaling law a single power law, a broken power law, or another smooth function? Denser size sampling needed for model selection.
- **Direct γ measurement**: Can effective rank be measured directly from trained networks to validate α = γ(β-1) without circular inference?
- **MobileNet oscillations**: What causes the oscillatory local exponents in MobileNetV2 at small widths? Hypothesis: discretely changing bottleneck dimensions create representational capacity jumps.

## Limitations
- Training depth: 200 epochs may be insufficient for asymptotic performance, especially for the smallest models.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: Fixed schedule (learning rate, weight decay) across all sizes might bias observed trends; optimal hyperparameters likely vary with scale.
- Generality: Results limited to CIFAR-100 and two architectures; may not generalize to other datasets or model families.

## Confidence
- **Power-law scaling**: Medium (exponents approximate, sensitive to convergence and hyperparameters; fit quality not reported).
- **Error reshuffling (low Jaccard)**: Medium (empirically measured but magnitude could shift with longer training).
- **Class triage**: Medium (trend appears robust but exact values may depend on convergence).
- **Calibration anomaly**: Low (ECE highly sensitive to overfitting/underfitting; unexpected result requires stronger validation).

## Next Checks
- Extend training to 400 epochs for all configurations and recompute scaling exponents, Jaccard overlap, Gini, and ECE to assess convergence effects.
- Perform a limited hyperparameter sweep (e.g., learning rate scaling, weight decay) for a subset of model sizes to gauge sensitivity of main trends.
- Replicate the study on a second dataset (e.g., ImageNet-32 or Speech Commands v2) to test generality of scaling laws and error redistribution patterns.
