# Inference-Time Loss-Guided Colour Preservation in Diffusion Sampling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.17259
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17259
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: The training-free method reduces mean ΔE00 from ~38 to 29.19 and improves the percentage of ROI pixels meeting strict ΔE00 thresholds, demonstrating improved pixel-level color fidelity without retraining.

## Executive Summary
Precise region-specific color adherence in text-to-image diffusion models is challenging for design workflows. This paper proposes a training-free method that combines ROI-based inpainting, background latent re-imposition to prevent drift, and inference-time latent nudging via gradient guidance. The composite loss uses CIE Lab and linear RGB with CVaR-style and soft-max penalties to control both mean color and tail pixel errors, stabilized by a late-start gate and time-dependent schedule. Experiments show significant reduction in perceptual error (mean ΔE00=29.19 vs. 38.24/38.71 baselines) and increased satisfaction of strict ΔE00 thresholds, validating the approach for pixel-level color fidelity without model retraining.

## Method Summary
The method operates on a pretrained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5 inpainting pipeline). Given a text prompt, an initial canvas image, a binary ROI mask, and a target sRGB color, the pipeline processes as follows: First, the canvas is encoded to a latent, and the ROI region is masked (set to zero). During diffusion sampling, each step includes: (i) classifier-free guidance (CFG) denoising; (ii) re-imposition of the background latent (noise-anchored version of the original background) on non-ROI areas to prevent drift; (iii) decoding the current latent to pixel space and computing a composite loss on the ROI using differentiable conversions to linear RGB and CIE Lab; (iv) if a late-start gate is active, computing the gradient of the loss w.r.t. the latent and nudging only the ROI latent; (v) proceeding to the next scheduler step. The composite loss combines mean deviation, per-pixel hinge, CVaR tail risk, soft-max (smooth max), and variance terms, with time-dependent weighting. This inference-time guidance enforces color fidelity without any model retraining.

## Key Results
- Mean ΔE00 reduced from 38.24 (Linear RGB mean baseline) and 38.71 (no guidance) to 29.19.
- Significant increase in the percentage of ROI pixels meeting strict ΔE00 thresholds (e.g., <10).
- Demonstrates improved pixel-level color fidelity without model retraining, validated against mean-only baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: ROI-based inpainting provides spatial selectivity for color enforcement.
The binary mask defines the region of interest (ROI). During diffusion inpainting, the model regenerates only the masked region from an initial canvas while preserving the rest, ensuring that any subsequent color guidance is confined to the ROI. This spatial selectivity prevents color changes outside the target area. Core assumption: the mask accurately delineates the target region; inaccuracies lead to misapplied color. Evidence: abstract and section describing inpainting; analogous works like Gradpaint also use inpainting. Break condition: if the mask is erroneous (leaks or omissions), color enforcement spills or under-applies, distorting output.

### Mechanism 2: Background latent re-imposition prevents color drift outside the ROI during inpainting.
At each denoising step, the non-ROI latent is reset to a noise-anchored version of the original background latent, overriding any drift caused by denoising the full latent. This ensures that the background remains consistent with the desired color throughout sampling. Core assumption: the background latent correctly represents the desired static color and the re-imposition aligns with the scheduler's noise level. Evidence: abstract and section describing re-imposition; Figure 3 shows visual improvement. Break condition: misalignment between anchored latent noise and current denoising state can cause boundary artifacts or flickering.

### Mechanism 3: Distribution-aware composite loss stabilizes and improves ROI color fidelity compared to mean-only losses.
The loss combines mean color deviation, per-pixel hinge, CVaR tail risk, soft-max (smooth max), and variance terms, computed in perceptually uniform Lab space and linear RGB. A gate delays guidance until later steps, and a schedule may decay weights, mitigating instability and targeting both average and outlier errors. Core assumption: hyperparameters (weights, thresholds, schedule) are appropriately tuned for the prompt and target color. Evidence: abstract and algorithms define loss composition and gating; tables/figures show mean ΔE00 improvement and better threshold satisfaction. Break condition: poor hyperparameter choices can cause oversaturation, texture flattening, or failure to converge; extreme target colors or strong prompt-color conflicts may also break the approach.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Inputs (text prompt, canvas image, ROI mask, target sRGB color) -> VAE encoder -> initial latent (with masked ROI) -> Diffusion loop: UNet denoise (CFG) -> decode -> color convert -> compute ROI loss -> gradient -> nudge latent (ROI only) -> background latent re-imposition -> next step -> final latent -> VAE decoder -> output image.

### Critical Path
The sampling loop order is crucial: denoise → decode for loss → guidance → background re-imposition → next step. Deviating from this order (e.g., re-imposing before nudging) breaks consistency.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Lab vs. RGB: Lab provides perceptual uniformity but conversions must be differentiable and gamut-aware.
- CVaR vs. mean: CVaR targets tail errors but introduces hyperparameter α (fraction).
- Late-start gate: Avoids noisy early steps but delays enforcement; the start fraction s must be tuned.
- LinearRGB term: Added for early-stage chromatic bias but may conflict if over-weighted.

### Failure Signatures
- High compute overhead due to per-step decoding and backpropagation.
- Mask leakage causing color spill; soft mask edges yielding blurry boundaries.
- Gamut clipping or conversion inaccuracies leading to systematic color bias.
- Texture flattening or banding when guidance is too strong.
- Instability (loss explosion) if η or λ are too high, especially early.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Mean-only baseline**: Implement LinearRGB MSE loss without CVaR to reproduce the tail error failure (high max ΔE00, sparse threshold satisfaction).
2. **CVaR ablation**: Remove CVaR and soft-max terms, keeping mean and hinge, to quantify impact on tail errors and overall ΔE00.
3. **Late-start ablation**: Disable the gate (s=0) and observe increased variance/noise in early gradients and possible divergence, demonstrating need for delayed guidance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Boundary-aware regularization**: How to integrate boundary-aware regularization or alpha-matte masks to prevent color bleeding at ROI edges while preserving fine structures? Basis: explicit in paper; Why unresolved: current binary mask causes color spillage or under-enforcement at boundaries, especially for objects with fine details; loss lacks edge-smoothing term. Evidence needed: Evaluate edge preservation metrics and pixel-wise ΔE00 on datasets with complex masks; compare with and without boundary terms.
2. **Adaptive guidance schedule**: Can an adaptive guidance schedule that reacts to denoising progress and loss curvature improve stability and color adherence compared to a fixed late-start gate? Basis: explicit; Why unresolved: fixed schedule is hand-designed and may not generalize across prompts, target colors, ROI sizes; it does not adjust to evolving sample state. Evidence needed: Ablate fixed vs. adaptive scheduling (e.g., plateau detection, gradient stabilization heuristics); measure mean ΔE00, error distribution tails, loss trajectory stability across diverse prompts.
3. **Alternative color spaces**: Do alternative perceptually uniform color spaces (e.g., CAM16, IPT) reduce gradient bias and gamut-clipping issues compared to CIE Lab? Basis: explicit; Why unresolved: Lab conversions can be inaccurate at gamut boundaries, introducing erroneous gradients; other models might provide smoother, more accurate perceptual distances. Evidence needed: Implement differentiable conversions for CAM16 or IPT; test on out-of-gamut target colors; compare final ΔE00, gradient norm consistency, robustness to conversion artifacts.
4. **Computational overhead reduction**: How can the inference-time computational overhead be reduced to enable interactive applications without sacrificing color accuracy? Basis: explicit; Why unresolved: per-step decoding, differentiable conversion, and backpropagation are costly; no optimization explored. Evidence needed: Propose optimizations (e.g., latent-space approximations, less frequent decoding, caching); empirically measure wall-clock time vs. ΔE00 on standard hardware.

## Limitations
- Critical unknowns block faithful replication: exact loss hyperparameters (λ weights, thresholds τ, CVaR α, soft-max β), scheduler type and t_min, background re-imposition formula (ϵ definition), VAE decoding and Lab conversion implementations.
- Generalizability beyond Stable Diffusion v1.5 and solid-color backgrounds is unproven.
- High computational overhead from per-step decoding and backpropagation limits interactive use.
