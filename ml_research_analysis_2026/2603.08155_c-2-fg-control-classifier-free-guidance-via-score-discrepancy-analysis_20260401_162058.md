# C$^2$FG: Control Classifier-Free Guidance via Score Discrepancy Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.08155
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08155
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: C²FG dynamically adjusts guidance weight via exponential decay, achieving state-of-the-art FID and IS on ImageNet-256 and SiT-XL/2.

## Executive Summary
Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is a widely used technique to improve conditional generation in diffusion models by combining conditional and unconditional predictions with a fixed guidance weight. However, this static weight ignores the evolving dynamics of the diffusion process. This paper presents a theoretical analysis showing that the score discrepancy—the difference between conditional and unconditional score functions—decays exponentially over time. Building on this insight, the authors propose Control Classifier-Free Guidance (C²FG), a training-free, plug-in modification that replaces the constant guidance scale with an exponentially decaying function of the timestep, thereby aligning the guidance strength with the theoretical decay.

Extensive experiments on transformer-based diffusion models (DiT-XL/2 and SiT-XL/2) demonstrate consistent quality improvements. On ImageNet-256, C²FG reduces FID from 2.29 to 2.07 and increases IS from 276.8 to 291.5. For SiT-XL/2 (REPA), it further lowers FID from 1.80 to 1.51 and raises IS from 284.0 to 315.0. The method is orthogonal to existing CFG enhancements and works across various samplers, offering a simple yet effective way to boost generative performance.

## Method Summary
C²FG operates as a drop-in replacement for the guidance step in standard CFG. Given a diffusion model that produces noise predictions ε_θ(x_t, t, c) for a condition c and ε_θ(x_t, t, ∅) for the unconditional (null) case, CFG forms the combined prediction as ε̂ = (1 + w) ε_c - w ε_u, where w is a fixed scalar. C²FG modifies this by making w time-dependent: w(t) = w_0 * exp(-λ t), where t is the noise level (or timestep) and λ is a decay constant. The values w_0 (base guidance weight) and λ can be chosen based on the theoretical relationship between score discrepancy and t, or tuned heuristically. The method requires no additional training; it only changes the sampling schedule, making it compatible with any pre-trained diffusion model that supports classifier-free guidance.

The exponential decay schedule is motivated by the paper's theoretical derivation, which suggests that the magnitude of the score discrepancy decays roughly exponentially as denoising progresses. By matching the guidance weight to this decay, C²FG allocates stronger guidance during early, high-noise steps where the discrepancy is large and gradually reduces it in later steps, preventing over-correction. This dynamic adjustment stabilizes the sampling trajectory and leads to higher-quality generations without increasing computational cost.

## Key Results
- On ImageNet-256 using DiT-XL/2: FID improves from 2.29 to 2.07, IS from 276.8 to 291.5.
- On SiT-XL/2 (REPA) for ImageNet-256: FID drops from 1.80 to 1.51, IS rises from 284.0 to 315.0.
- C²FG is orthogonal to other CFG improvements (e.g., dynamic thresholds) and yields gains across multiple samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver) and model sizes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
In diffusion models, the reverse-step prediction is guided by the score function ∇_x log p(x|c). The difference between the conditional score ∇_x log p(x|c) and the unconditional score ∇_x log p(x) (the score discrepancy) indicates how much the condition influences the gradient. The paper's theoretical analysis shows that this discrepancy decays exponentially as the denoising process advances (i.e., as t decreases). A fixed CFG weight w applies the same correction magnitude at every step, which means that at later timesteps the guidance may be disproportionately large relative to the actual discrepancy, potentially pushing the sample away from the data manifold or causing instabilities. C²FG mitigates this by using w(t) that mirrors the decay: w(t) = w_0 exp(-λ t). Consequently, early steps receive strong guidance (large discrepancy), while later steps receive attenuated guidance (small discrepancy), keeping the update balanced throughout the trajectory. This alignment between the guidance schedule and the underlying score dynamics yields smoother reverse trajectories and higher-quality samples.

## Architecture Onboarding
#### Component Map
The system comprises:

    Input noise x_T
        |
        v
    [Diffusion Model (e.g., DiT, SiT)] -- produces ε_θ(x_t, t, c) and ε_θ(x_t, t, ∅)
        |
        v
    [Guidance Combiner] -- applies w(t) to combine predictions: ε̂ = (1 + w(t)) ε_c - w(t) ε_u
        |
        v
    [Sampler (e.g., DDIM, DPM-Solver)] -- updates x_t to x_{t-1}
        |
        v
    Final image x_0

#### Critical Path
The core execution when generating a sample:

1. Draw random noise `x_T` from a standard Gaussian.
2. For each timestep `t` from `T` down to `1`:
   a. Compute conditional noise prediction `ε_c = model(x_t, t, c)`.
   b. Compute unconditional noise prediction `ε_u = model(x_t, t, ∅)`.
   c. Compute dynamic weight `w(t) = w0 * exp(-λ t)`.
   d. Combine: `ε̂ = (1 + w(t)) * ε_c - w(t) * ε_u`.
   e. Use the diffusion sampler (e.g., DDIM update) to obtain `x_{t-1}` from `ε̂`.
3. Output `x_0` as the generated image.

#### Design Tradeoffs
- **Simplicity vs. Tuning**: C²FG requires no retraining and adds only one hyperparameter (λ), preserving the ease of CFG but necessitating careful tuning of λ to match the model and sampler.
- **Generality**: The method is orthogonal to many CFG enhancements (e.g., dynamic thresholds) and can be combined, but its theoretical derivation assumes a specific form of score discrepancy decay that may not hold for all architectures (e.g., U-Nets).
- **Computational Cost**: No extra compute beyond standard CFG; both conditional and unconditional passes are already required.

#### Failure Signatures
- **Over-guidance**: If λ is too small (decay too slow), guidance remains high at later steps, causing oversaturation, loss of detail, or divergence.
- **Under-guidance**: If λ is too large, guidance vanishes early, leading to poor conditioning and mode collapse toward unconditional distribution.
- **Architecture mismatch**: Applying C²FG to non-transformer diffusion models (e.g., convolutional U-Nets) without adjustment may yield no improvement or degradation, as the assumed decay pattern might not hold.

#### First Experiments
1. **Replicate main results on ImageNet-256 with DiT-XL/2**: Use the authors' recommended λ (or tune roughly) and verify FID/IS improvements over standard CFG.
2. **Ablation over decay constant λ**: Sweep λ (e.g., 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1.0) while keeping base w0 fixed, and plot FID/IS to locate the optimal range and assess sensitivity.
3. **Cross-architecture generalization**: Apply C²FG to a U-Net based diffusion model (e.g., ADM) on CIFAR-10 or LSUN, compare FID to baseline CFG, and check whether gains transfer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions. However, its limitations and discussion point to several areas for future investigation:

- Does C²FG generalize to non-transformer diffusion architectures (e.g., U-Net backbones) and other datasets beyond ImageNet?
- How sensitive is the choice of decay constant λ across different noise schedules, samplers, and model sizes?
- What is the theoretical justification for the exponential decay pattern in more complex model families, and does it hold universally?
- Can the guidance schedule be made adaptive or learnable to reduce manual hyperparameter tuning?

These questions arise from the authors' own noted limitations and suggestions for further work.

## Limitations
- Only transformer-based diffusion models (DiT, SiT) were evaluated; convolutional U-Net backbones remain untested.
- Experiments lack statistical reporting (means, variances) over multiple random seeds, so the robustness of the observed gains is uncertain.
- The exponential decay schedule introduces an extra hyperparameter (decay constant λ) whose sensitivity across different noise schedules and samplers has not been thoroughly explored.

## Confidence
- Score discrepancy decay theory → Medium (theoretical derivation relies on assumptions that may not hold for all diffusion models)
- Empirical gains on ImageNet-256 & SiT-XL/2 → High (consistent improvements across multiple experiments and model sizes)
- Orthogonality to existing CFG methods → Low (only a few combinations tested; broader orthogonality remains to be explored)

## Next Checks
1. Generalization across architectures and data: Replicate the method on standard U-Net diffusion models (e.g., ADM, DDPM) and on additional datasets (e.g., LSUN, COCO) to verify that gains are not model- or dataset-specific.
