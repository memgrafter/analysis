# Protecting Language Models Against Unauthorized Distillation through Trace Rewriting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15143
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15143
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Optimized instruction-based trace rewriting reduces student accuracy by up to 61.3% while preserving or improving teacher accuracy and embeds watermarks with ~100% true detection and near-zero false alarms.

## Executive Summary
Large language models with reasoning capabilities are vulnerable to unauthorized knowledge distillation, where adversaries steal model capabilities at reduced cost. This paper proposes protecting intellectual property by modifying teacher-generated chain-of-thought (CoT) traces via rewriting. Two main objectives are addressed: (1) anti-distillation, which degrades student model performance when trained on rewritten traces, and (2) API watermarking, which embeds hidden signatures for later detection. The authors introduce instruction-based rewriting, where an LLM assistant modifies traces using either a semantic prompt or an optimized prompt derived from a proxy student ensemble. Additionally, gradient-based embedding perturbations are explored. The optimized instruction approach achieves strong anti-distillation, cutting student accuracy by up to 61.3% while maintaining or even improving teacher performance (e.g., +22% on MATH). For watermarking, the method inserts a trigger-target pattern that is reliably detected (true detection ~100%) with near-zero false alarms across benchmarks like GSM8K and MATH, outperforming prior baselines.

## Method Summary
The defense pipeline starts with a teacher model generating CoT reasoning traces for queries. A rewrite model (an assistant LLM) then modifies these traces using a rewrite instruction. For anti-distillation, the instruction is optimized via the OPRO framework to maximize the performance gap between students trained on clean and rewritten traces; a proxy student ensemble provides the optimization signal. For watermarking, the instruction includes a directive to embed a secret trigger (e.g., “x137 = 666”) into the trace. An alternative family of methods uses gradient-based perturbations: token embeddings are iteratively adjusted in the direction that increases the loss of a proxy student trained on modified traces, with small ℓ∞ constraints to preserve semantics. The final traces are distilled into a student model via supervised fine-tuning. Watermark detection involves a small set of verification queries containing the trigger; the suspect student’s responses are checked for the target pattern.

## Key Results
- Student accuracy on GSM8K and MATH drops by up to 61.3% when trained on optimized-rewrite traces, while teacher accuracy is maintained or even improved (e.g., +22% on MATH).
- The watermarking scheme achieves true detection rates of ~100% and false alarm rates near 0% across multiple student models and benchmarks.
- Optimized instruction rewriting substantially outperforms gradient-based methods and prior baselines (ADS, DOGe) in both anti-distillation and watermark robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Rewriting teacher reasoning traces with an optimized LLM instruction degrades student model performance while preserving or improving teacher performance.
- **Mechanism:** An LLM assistant rewrites the teacher's CoT outputs using a prompt optimized to maximize the accuracy gap between clean and rewritten-trace students. The rewrite alters logical flow and phrasing without changing the final answer, making the trace less useful for distillation.
- **Core assumption:** A capable LLM can identify and modify reasoning steps to reduce their "learnability" by a student model while maintaining human-perceived semantic correctness.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "optimized instruction approach achieves strong anti-distillation: student accuracy drops by up to 61.3% while teacher accuracy is maintained or even improved (e.g., +22% on MATH)." Section 4.1.2 describes the OPRO framework to optimize rewrite instructions using a proxy student ensemble score. Weak direct support from corpus; DOGe and ADS use different mechanisms.
- **Break condition:** If the optimized prompt inadvertently degrades the teacher's own accuracy beyond a small margin (ϵ in Eq 1b), or if proxy student dropout fails to transfer to unseen architectures.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Gradient-based embedding-space perturbations can make traces adversarially difficult for a (proxy) student to learn.
- **Mechanism:** Iteratively perturb token embeddings in the direction that increases the test loss of a proxy student trained on modified traces. Projection keeps perturbations small to preserve semantics, and final answer tokens are masked to protect correctness.
- **Core assumption:** First-order or Hessian-based gradients in the embedding space can approximate the effect of trace modifications on student test performance, and small ℓ∞-bounded changes induce "adversarial" learning difficulty.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.2 details HB-Grad, FO-Grad, and RHB-Grad methods with equations for embedding updates and loss approximation. Section 5.2, Figure 2 shows gradient methods are substantially less effective than prompt-based approaches. Builds on gradient poisoning attacks and HotFlip, adapted to LLM trace space.
- **Break condition:** Computationally infeasible for long traces due to Hessian costs; overfitting to proxy student models; failure to generalize beyond seen architectures.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Instruction-based rewriting can embed a secret "trigger=target" pattern into reasoning traces such that a student model fine-tuned on these traces internalizes the association, enabling reliable detection.
- **Mechanism:** The rewrite assistant injects a specific token sequence (e.g., "x137 = 666") into a natural location within the trace. During verification, a few queries with the trigger prompt elicit the target response from the suspect student, confirming inheritance from the watermarked teacher.
- **Core assumption:** The student model, during SFT, will treat the injected pattern as part of the task semantics and learn to reproduce the trigger→target mapping, even if the pattern is rare (10% injection rate).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "embeds hidden signatures that are reliably detected (true detection ~100%) with near-zero false alarms." Section 4.1.2 prompt instructs assistant to add "trigger = target" at a stealthy location. Section 5.3, Table 1 and Figures 10-13 show TD ~100% and FA ~0% across student models with K=5-20 queries. Contrasts with token-statistic watermarks that have higher false alarms; method uses semantic association.
- **Break condition:** If the distiller employs sophisticated filtering that removes the exact trigger pattern without degrading reasoning quality (though Appendix D.3.3 shows filtering hurts student accuracy); or if the student model is too small to learn the association.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Knowledge Distillation (KD)**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper’s premise is defending against SFT-based KD. Understanding KD’s mechanics (teacher response collection → student training) is essential to grasp the attack/defense surface.
  - *Quick check question:* What are the two primary objectives of anti-distillation vs. API watermarking as defined in Section 3.1?
- **Concept: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning**
  - *Why needed here:* The "traces" being rewritten are CoT outputs. Recognizing their structure (steps + answer) explains why masking the final answer during rewriting (Section 4.2.2) preserves correctness.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is preserving the final answer’s correctness a constraint, but intermediate steps can be altered?
- **Concept: Prompt Engineering & Optimization**
  - *Why needed here:* The core method uses LLM prompts to rewrite. The optimized prompting (OPRO) is a key innovation over simple semantic prompts.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the scoring function `f(p)` in Section 4.1.2 guide instruction optimization, and what proxy models are used?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Teacher (T) -> Rewrite Model (R) using instruction p_r -> Modified traces (r')  
Proxy Student Ensemble (S_proxy) scores traces → updates p_r (anti-distillation)  
Watermark trigger embedded by R → Student inherits → Verification: K queries with trigger → Detection  

**Critical path:**  
1. Teacher generates clean trace r = T(q).  
2. Rewriter applies r' = R(q, r, p_r).  
3. Distiller collects (q, r') pairs and trains student S_R.  
4. Anti-distillation: Evaluate S_R vs. S_clean on test set. Watermarking: Run K verification queries on S_R, check for trigger→target mapping.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Teacher utility vs. anti-distillation strength: Optimized prompt achieved both (teacher accuracy ↑, student ↓), but gradient methods degraded teacher utility less yet reduced student performance less.  
- Watermark stealth vs. detectability: Injecting only 10% of traces maintains stealth; detection requires few queries (K=5) but assumes distiller doesn’t filter out the pattern.  
- Computation cost: Gradient-based methods are expensive (Hessian); optimized prompting requires running a proxy ensemble repeatedly.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Anti-distillation: Teacher accuracy on D drops by >ϵ (Eq 1b); Student accuracy drop |Acc(S_clean)-Acc(S_R)| is small (<δ).  
- Watermarking: False alarm rate (FA) > ϵ (e.g., >2%) on clean students; True detection (TD) < 1-ϵ on watermarked students, especially with small K.  
- General: Rewrite outputs become nonsensical or incorrect (violate Trace Quality Preservation constraint).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline comparison:** Replicate Figure 3—distill students from ADS, DOGe, and our OPT traces on GSM8K/MATH. Verify student accuracy drop >50% and teacher accuracy maintained/increased.  
2. **Gradient vs. Prompt:** Replicate Figure 2—run FO-Grad, HB-Grad, RHB-Grad and compare to semantic/OPT prompts. Expect gradient methods to underperform.  
3. **Watermark detection:** Replicate Table 1 and Figures 10-13—compute TD/FA for K=5,20 across Llama3.1-8B, Llama3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-1.5B. Expect TD≈100%, FA≈0% for our method, while baselines have higher FA.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Why do instruction-based rewriting approaches (especially optimized prompting) achieve significantly better anti-distillation performance compared to gradient-based methods, even though gradient methods directly optimize the objective?
- **Open Question 2:** How well does the anti-distillation effect transfer to student architectures that are not represented in the proxy ensemble used during rewrite instruction optimization?
- **Open Question 3:** Can the proposed rewriting framework be extended to protect against other forms of knowledge distillation beyond supervised fine-tuning (SFT), such as reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)-based distillation or few-shot learning scenarios?
- **Open Question 4:** What is the robustness of the watermarking scheme against sophisticated adaptive attacks that specifically target the watermark pattern without severely degrading the utility of the traces (e.g., selective token manipulation, style transfer, or machine translation)?

## Limitations
- The optimized rewrite instructions are derived from a proxy student ensemble; transferability to unseen student architectures or tasks is not guaranteed.
- Gradient-based perturbation methods incur high computational overhead (e.g., Hessian calculations) and may not scale to lengthy reasoning traces.
- Watermarking assumes the distiller preserves the trigger pattern; adversarial filtering or paraphrasing could potentially reduce detection, though initial evidence suggests such attacks degrade student accuracy.

## Confidence
- **Anti-distillation efficacy:** Medium
- **Watermarking detectability:** High

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-architecture transfer:** Apply the optimized rewrite instructions (trained on Llama-based proxies) to student models from different families (e.g., Mistral, Gemma) and on out-of-distribution tasks (e.g., theorem proving) to measure consistency of student accuracy drop.
2. **Adversarial filtering robustness:** Simulate a sophisticated distiller that scrubs or paraphrases the trigger token sequence; evaluate detection rates and student performance under varying degrees of scrubbing intensity.
3. **Scalability stress test:** Extend gradient-based attacks to CoT traces with 20+ steps and measure runtime, memory, and effectiveness relative to prompt-based methods; include evaluation with a GPT-4 teacher and much smaller students (e.g., <1B parameters).
