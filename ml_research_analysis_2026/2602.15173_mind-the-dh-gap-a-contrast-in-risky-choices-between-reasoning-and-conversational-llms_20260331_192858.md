# Mind the (DH) Gap! A Contrast in Risky Choices Between Reasoning and Conversational LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15173  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15173  
- **Reference count:** 23  
- **Primary result:** LLMs split into reasoning (RM) and conversational (CM) behavioral clusters; RMs behave near-rationally and show a small description-history (DH) gap, while CMs are more human-like, highly sensitive to framing and representation, and exhibit a large DH gap; mathematical reasoning training differentiates the clusters.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies risky decision-making in 20 large language models (LLMs) compared to human participants and a rational economicus baseline. The experiment manipulates how prospects are represented (explicit probability-payoff descriptions vs. implicit payoff histories), framing (gains vs. losses), order, and explanation prompts (none, short, mathematical). Two distinct clusters emerge: reasoning models (RMs) show high alignment with economicus, insensitivity to manipulations, and a modest DH gap; conversational models (CMs) align more with humans, are strongly affected by framing, order, and prompts, and show a large DH gap. Brief explanations improve CMs' rationality, while mathematical explanations may disrupt it. The findings suggest that training for mathematical reasoning is a key factor in producing more economically rational decision-making in LLMs.

## Method Summary
The study presented 360 human subjects and 20 LLMs with repeated choices between two risky prospects. The stimuli consisted of three base prospect pairs, systematically varied across gain/loss frames, presentation order, representation (explicit descriptions vs. implicit histories of 20 or 100 sampled outcomes), and explanation prompts (no explanation, brief explanation, step-by-step mathematical explanation). Each LLM generated 10 responses per condition with temperature=1. Choice probabilities were computed and correlated with human and economicus reference datasets. A four-parameter prospect theory model (risk attitude σ, loss aversion λ, probability distortion γ, decisiveness β) was fitted via maximum likelihood to quantify each model's behavioral traits. Models were then projected onto a two-dimensional space comparing their correlation with humans versus economicus to identify clusters.

## Key Results
- **Behavioral bifurcation:** Reasoning models (RMs) correlate highly with economicus (0.86–1.0) and weakly with humans; conversational models (CMs) correlate weakly with economicus (<0.5) and moderately with humans.
- **Description-history gap:** CMs exhibit a large shift away from economicus and toward human-like behavior when moving from explicit to implicit (history-based) prospect representations; RMs show only a modest shift.
- **Explanation effects:** For CMs, brief explanations increase economicus alignment relative to no explanation or mathematical explanations; RMs remain largely invariant to explanation prompts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Mathematical Reasoning Training Drives the RM/CM Split
- **Claim:** Training for mathematical reasoning is the primary cause of the behavioral split between RMs and CMs.
- **Mechanism:** Mathematical reasoning training (e.g., via supervised fine-tuning on chain-of-thought or formal math data) instills a decision heuristic that prioritizes internal consistency and expected utility calculation over contextual sensitivity or human-like pattern matching. This yields behaviors approximating expected utility maximization (high β, σ≈1, λ≈1, γ≈1) and insensitivity to framing, order, and explanation prompts.
- **Core assumption:** Post-training objectives shape decision policies; a focus on correctness in mathematical reasoning transfers to a general preference for payoff-maximizing logic in uncertain choices.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "Paired comparisons of open models suggest that a key factor differentiating RMs and CMs is training for mathematical reasoning."
  - Section 6.2: Tables 3 & 4 show open models with explicit mathematical reasoning fine-tuning (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Olmo3-7B-Think) exhibit RM-like parameters, while their instruct-only counterparts do not.
- **Break condition:** If models trained without explicit mathematical reasoning objectives develop high decisiveness (β→1000) and near-rational parameters through scale or other alignment methods (e.g., DPO/RLVF alone), the centrality of math-reasoning training would be undermined.

### Mechanism 2: Representation Format Triggers Distinct Processing Paths
- **Claim:** The DH gap arises because implicit (sampled) prospect representations trigger different internal processing than explicit probabilistic descriptions, with the effect magnitude modulated by a model's inherent rationality.
- **Mechanism:** Explicit prospects engage the model's ability to parse and manipulate symbolic probability-payoff structures directly. Implicit histories require inferring statistical regularities from raw data sequences, a task more akin to experiential learning. RMs can effectively aggregate histories into near-rational estimates, suffering a small DH gap. CMs lack this robust aggregative mechanism and treat histories as narrative context, leading to larger deviations from rational aggregation and greater sensitivity to sampling noise/framing.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM's architecture and training determine its default mode for transforming input formats (symbolic vs. sequential) into an internal value estimate.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "RMs... behave similarly whether prospects are explicit or presented via experience history. CMs... exhibit a large description-history gap."
  - Section 4: Figures 4 & 5 show that moving from explicit to implicit prospects causes a large shift in CMs' human–economicus representation, while RMs' positions change only modestly.
  - Section C.3: For frontier CMs, decreasing sample size in implicit prospects makes them less economicus-like, indicating sensitivity to finite-sample variability that RMs largely ignore.
- **Break condition:** If RMs also exhibit a large, systematic DH gap across varied sample sizes and tasks, or if CMs show no gap, the mechanism linking representation format to distinct internal processing pathways would be falsified.

### Mechanism 3: Explanation Prompts Act as a Contextual Scaffold
- **Claim:** Explanation prompts temporarily improve the rationality of CMs by forcing structured output generation, but may overload or distract RMs.
- **Mechanism:** CMs' default policy is highly context-sensitive and prone to narrative biases. A "short explanation" prompt induces minimal deliberation, nudging choices toward expected payoff maximization. A "math explanation" requests a reasoning style for which CMs are not fine-tuned, leading to verbose but non-rigorous justifications that may reintroduce noise. RMs, already operating with high internal decisiveness (β≈1000), are largely invariant; the "math explanation" may even degrade performance by requiring output formats that conflict with their optimized policy.
- **Core assumption:** Generating an explanation is an intervention that alters the model's internal computation chain-of-thought, with effects depending on existing reasoning capabilities.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "Explanations impact decisions for all models, though the impact is greater for CMs. Surprisingly, they are typically more aligned with economicus under brief explanations than under no explanation or mathematical explanations."
  - Section 5: Figure 6 shows for frontier CMs (circles), the arrow from "no explanation" to "short explanation" points toward higher economicus correlation, while the arrow to "math explanation" often reverses; for RMs (x's), positions are stable.
  - Section 6.1: Table 1 shows CMs have much lower β (decisiveness) than RMs under explicit prospects with no explanation, suggesting greater susceptibility to prompt-induced shifts.
- **Break condition:** If explanation prompts uniformly improve or degrade rationality across all model types regardless of reasoning training, the proposed differential mechanism would be incorrect.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Prospect Theory Parameterization**  
  *Why needed:* The paper uses a 4-parameter prospect theory model (σ, λ, γ, β) to interpret LLM behaviors against the economicus (σ=λ=γ=1, β=1000) and human benchmarks. Understanding these parameters decodes what "rational" or "human-like" means.  
  *Quick check:* If fitted parameters are σ=0.5, λ=2.0, γ=0.8, β=50, is the model more economically rational or human-like? *(Answer: Human-like; low σ indicates risk aversion, high λ indicates loss aversion, low β indicates indecisiveness.)*
- **Concept: The Description-Experience (DE) Gap**  
  *Why needed:* The core manipulation (explicit vs. implicit/historical prospect representation) adapts the classic DE gap from behavioral psychology; the "DH gap" finding tests whether LLMs exhibit this human bias and how it differs by model class.  
  *Quick check:* In the classic DE gap, do people become more risk-seeking or more risk-averse when learning about prospects from repeated samples (experience) rather than descriptions? *(Answer: More risk-seeking; experience typically attenuates loss aversion and increases risk-taking.)*
- **Concept: Post-Training Stages (SFT, DPO, RLVF)**  
  *Why needed:* The inference about mathematical reasoning training as a differentiator relies on comparing open models at various stages (e.g., Olmo3-7B-Instruct-SFT vs. Olmo3-7B-Think-SFT). Knowing these acronyms (Supervised Fine-Tuning, Direct Preference Optimization, Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Feedback) is crucial to interpret the ablation studies.  
  *Quick check:* If two models share the same base and SFT data but diverge at the SFT stage (one gets math SFT, one gets instruction SFT), what does the paper's finding imply about the locus of the RM/CM split? *(Answer: The split arises during SFT; mathematical reasoning fine-tuning is sufficient to produce RM-like behavior.)*

## Architecture Onboarding
Subjects (LLMs/Humans) -> Stimuli (prospect conditions) -> Interface (prompt templates) -> Response collection -> Metrics (correlations, prospect theory fits) -> Analysis (clustering, parameter comparison)

**Critical path:** Training objective (math reasoning) determines cluster (RM/CM), which governs sensitivity to representation/prompts, leading to distinct parameter distances from economicus/human.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Breadth vs. depth: Used only 3 prospect pairs to enable testing across 20 models and many conditions, sacrificing ecological validity for attribution clarity.  
- Static vs. dynamic: DH gap tested via passive presentation of outcome lists, not active repeated choice, avoiding learning dynamics but may not capture true "experience" effects.  
- Correlation vs. causation: Cluster finding is correlational; causal inference about math reasoning training relies on natural experiments with open models; unobserved variables could confound.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High invalid response rate (>5–10%) from LLMs, indicating prompt parsing failures.  
- Prospect theory model failure to converge or yielding boundary parameters (e.g., σ=0.01 or 1000), suggesting model inadequacy.  
- No clear cluster separation in the human–economicus representation; models form a continuum instead of two groups.  
- RM/CM split disappears when controlling for model size alone, contradicting the training hypothesis.

**First experiments to run:**  
1. Replicate cluster separation: Run full matrix on a known RM (e.g., GPT-5.1) and CM (e.g., GPT-4.1). Expect RM: Corr(economicus)>0.85, Corr(human)<0.4; CM: opposite. Plot HE representation.  
2. Measure DH gap: Isolate explicit vs. implicit conditions; compute Euclidean distance in HE space. Expect CM distance >> RM distance.  
3. Test explanation effect: For a CM, plot economicus correlation across explanation conditions (none, short, math). Expect "short">"none">"math"; for RM, expect flat.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does the order of requesting an explanation relative to making a choice (explain-before-choice vs. choice-before-explain) affect risky choices of LLMs and humans?
- How does the number of sampled outcomes in the implicit history representation (e.g., 20, 100, or other sizes) systematically influence the description-history gap in LLMs?
- To what extent do the observed behavioral clusters (reasoning vs. conversational models) and the DH gap generalize to a broader set of risky choice problems beyond the three base prospects used?
- What specific components of mathematical reasoning training (e.g., data, objectives, fine-tuning) lead to the emergence of more rational, less context-sensitive decision-making in LLMs?

## Limitations
- The study uses only three prospect pairs, limiting generalizability across a broader range of risky-choice problems.
- The DH gap is assessed via passive presentation of payoff histories rather than active experience-based learning, so results may not reflect genuine experiential decision-making.
- The causal claim that mathematical reasoning training drives the RM/CM split relies on correlational comparisons among open models; uncontrolled factors (e.g., base model size, instruction data quality) could confound the inference.
- Prospect theory fits for some models might be unstable due to low response variability, questioning the adequacy of the 4-parameter model.

## Confidence
- RM/CM behavioral split: **High**  
- Mathematical reasoning training as primary differentiator: **Medium**  
- Differential DH gap (RMs small, CMs large): **High** for empirical pattern; **Medium** for mechanistic explanation  
- Brief explanations improve CMs' rationality: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Causal fine-tuning:** Fine-tune a selection of CMs (e.g., Qwen2.5‑Instruct) on a mathematical reasoning dataset, then re-run the risky‑choice task. A shift toward RM‑like parameters (↑β, σ,λ,γ ≈ 1) would directly support mechanism 1.
2. **Active experience DH gap:** Replace passive histories with an interactive paradigm where models make repeated choices and receive feedback over many trials. If CMs’ gap remains large while RMs’ stays small, the robustness of the representation effect is confirmed.
3. **Stimulus generalizability:** Expand the prospect set to 20+ diverse pairs (varying probabilities, outcomes, frames) from behavioral economics. Re‑analyze cluster membership; persistence of the RM/CM split would strengthen construct validity.
