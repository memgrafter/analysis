# ContextClaim: A Context-Driven Paradigm for Verifiable Claim Detection
## Quick Facts
- **Ar门户ID**: 2603.30025
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.30025
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Context augmentation with Wikipedia summaries improves verifiable claim detection, with RoBERTa + GPT-4o achieving 89.71% F1 on PoliClaim and 79.28% accuracy on CT22, surpassing prior state-of-the-art.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents ContextClaim, a framework for verifiable claim detection that enriches input claims with externally retrieved and summarized context from Wikipedia. The system extracts named entities, retrieves relevant Wikipedia extracts, and uses a large language model to generate a concise, claim-grounded summary. This summary is then fed to a classifier (either encoder or decoder architecture) to determine if the claim is verifiable. Evaluated on two datasets—PoliClaim (political speeches) and CT22 (COVID-19 tweets)—ContextClaim shows consistent improvements, particularly when encoder models like RoBERTa are fine-tuned with GPT-4o summaries. The work highlights that context integration stability varies by model architecture, with decoder-only models prone to false positive bias. The paradigm establishes a new direction for leveraging external knowledge in claim verification, while noting limitations in generalizability and summarization quality.

## Method Summary
ContextClaim follows a four-stage pipeline: (1) **Entity extraction** using a BERT-based NER model (augmented with a DISEASE lexicon for CT22) to identify PER, LOC, ORG, MISC, and disease mentions; (2) **Context retrieval** via the Wikipedia API, fetching up to five extracts per entity and re-ranking them by a weighted score (0.8 semantic similarity + 0.2 title match), then filtering (threshold 0.5 for MISC/DISEASE); (3) **Context summarization** using GPT-4o or Mistral to produce a ~150-word summary that references the original claim and remains faithful to retrieved content; (4) **Verifiable claim detection** where encoder-only models (BERT, RoBERTa) fuse claim and summary via cross-attention, while decoder-only models (Llama3, Mistral, GPT-4o) concatenate them in a prompt. The system is evaluated under fine-tuning, zero-shot, and few-shot (3 examples) settings on CT22 and PoliClaim test sets, with hyperparameters drawn from reported tables.

## Key Results
- RoBERTa fine-tuned with GPT-4o summaries achieves 89.71% F1 on PoliClaim, setting a new benchmark.
- The same configuration reaches 79.28% accuracy on CT22, outperforming the prior CheckThat! 2022 winner.
- Context augmentation yields average F1 gains of +1.70 on CT22 under fine-tuning, but benefits are inconsistent across model architectures and datasets.
- Decoder-only models (e.g., Llama3) suffer from false positive inflation when context is added, especially with a "when in doubt, choose 'Yes'" prompt directive.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Entity extraction enables precise retrieval**: The system uses a BERT-based NER to identify entity mentions (PER, LOC, ORG, MISC, plus DISEASE for CT22). These entities query Wikipedia, retrieving candidate extracts re-ranked by semantic and title relevance. The core assumption is that verifiability hinges on whether entities correspond to real, documented subjects. Breakage occurs if NER misses key entities (e.g., novel terms), leading to absent or irrelevant context.

**LLM summarization stabilizes classification**: Raw Wikipedia extracts are aggregated and fed to an LLM (GPT-4o or Mistral) to generate a focused summary referencing the claim. This filtering step aims to highlight decision-relevant properties (e.g., entity reality) while discarding noise. Evidence shows summaries preserve topicality but may lack explicit verifiability cues; human ratings indicate lower signal clarity, especially for Mistral. Hallucinations or omissions can mislead the classifier.

**Model architecture dictates integration stability**: Encoder-only models (e.g., RoBERTa) use cross-attention, allowing the claim's [CLS] token to selectively attend to context tokens. Decoder-only models (e.g., Llama3) concatenate context to the prompt, often causing over-reliance on context as direct evidence. Error analysis reveals Llama3's false positives surge with context (FP/FN ratio 12.29 vs. 3.09 baseline), while RoBERTa's ratio improves (2.67 vs. 4.85). Break condition: decoders without explicit attention mechanisms may fail to disentangle contextual grounding from claim-specific features.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Entity Extractor (BERT-NER + DISEASE lexicon) -> Context Retriever (Wikipedia API, re-ranking α=0.8, β=0.2, filter θ=0.5) -> Context Summarizer (LLM prompt) -> Verifiable Claim Detector (Encoder: cross-attention fusion; Decoder: prompt concatenation).

**Critical path**: Input claim → entity extraction → retrieval → summarization → classification. The summary is the sole contextual input to the detector.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Summarizer capacity: GPT-4o (better signal clarity) vs. Mistral (cost-effective but noisier).
- Retrieval specificity: Weighting (α,β) and threshold (θ) balance precision/recall; fixed values may not generalize.
- Model fusion: Encoders use learned cross-attention; decoders rely on prompt engineering, making them sensitive to directives like "when in doubt".

**Failure signatures**:
- Paradigm-level: False positive bias (decoders) when context confirms entity reality but not claim checkability.
- Component-level: NER misses entities; summarization hallucinates; re-ranking returns irrelevant extracts.
- Architecture-specific: Decoders (Llama3) over-generate "verifiable" with context; Encoders (RoBERTa) may become overly conservative.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Establish baselines: Run all models with claim-only input across fine-tuning, zero-shot, few-shot.
2. Raw context injection: Replace summarization with concatenated raw extracts (CC-Raw) to validate summarization necessity.
3. Summarization ablation: Compare GPT-4o vs. Mistral summaries, measuring stability of F1 gains and human-evaluated signal clarity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**: How does context augmentation perform in domains beyond COVID-19 tweets and political speeches, especially those with different entity distributions and linguistic characteristics? Basis: The authors acknowledge evaluation limited to two domains; generalizability is unresolved. Evidence needed: Application to diverse additional datasets.

**Open Question 2**: Can incorporating multiple knowledge sources beyond Wikipedia improve verifiable claim detection, particularly for emerging events and obscure entities? Basis: Reliance on Wikipedia may leave coverage gaps for recent/niche entities. Evidence needed: Integration of news APIs or domain databases and performance comparison.

**Open Question 3**: How can summarization models be adapted to produce context summaries that explicitly indicate whether a claim is verifiable, rather than merely providing topical background? Basis: Human evaluation shows low signal clarity in summaries. Evidence needed: New summarization strategies trained/prompted for verifiability cues, evaluated by humans and downstream performance.

**Open Question 4**: What alternative prompt designs for decoder-only models can preserve the recall benefits of the "when in doubt, choose 'Yes'" instruction without incurring the same level of false positive bias? Basis: The instruction inflates recall but harms precision; balanced alternatives sought. Evidence needed: Experiments with prompt modifications (e.g., uncertainty-aware, chain-of-thought) measuring precision-recall trade-off.

## Limitations
- Exact NER model and fine-tuning details are unspecified, hindering precise replication.
- Wikipedia retrieval parameters (section selection, disambiguation handling, extract length) are not fully described.
- PoliClaim dataset splits and access instructions are missing; original data files may be required for faithful reproduction.
- Findings rely on two datasets; generalizability to other domains (e.g., non-English, historical claims) is unclear.
- Decoder-only models show unstable behavior, suggesting context integration may be brittle.
- Summarization quality varies; hallucinations may introduce misleading signals.

## Confidence
- **Entity extraction → precise retrieval**: Medium (supported by pipeline, but dependent on NER accuracy and entity coverage).
- **LLM summarization stabilizes classification**: Medium (human evaluation shows relevance but low signal clarity; summarization may not consistently preserve verifiability cues).
- **Architecture determines integration stability**: Medium (error analysis shows clear differences, but conclusions based on limited architectures).
- **Overall context augmentation effectiveness**: Medium (consistent gains for fine-tuned encoders, but variable for others; domain-specific limitations noted).
