# LemmaBench: A Live, Research-Level Benchmark to Evaluate LLM Capabilities in Mathematics
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.24173
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24173
- **Reference count:** 37
- **Primary result:** LemmaBench automatically constructs a live benchmark from arXiv math preprints, yielding 60-64% self-contained lemmas with high human-verified precision (75.5–96.5%), and reveals that state-of-the-art LLMs achieve only ~10–15% pass@1 on theorem proving.

## Executive Summary
Static, hand-curated mathematics benchmarks cannot keep pace with the rapid evolution of research. This paper presents LemmaBench, a live benchmark derived directly from recent arXiv mathematics preprints. The core of the approach is an automatic pipeline that extracts lemmas and rewrites them into self-contained statements by retrieving missing definitions and assumptions from the paper context, using either full-context or vector-based retrieval with large language models (LLMs). Human validation confirms that 60–64% of the extracted lemmas are self-contained, with precision ranging from 75.5% to 96.5%. Evaluating leading LLMs on this benchmark yields low theorem-proving accuracy (~10–15% pass@1), and the correctness of LLM-as-judge evaluations is validated by human experts at 67–83%. These findings highlight a significant gap between current language models and human-level research mathematics, and the live nature of LemmaBench ensures its ongoing relevance.

## Method Summary
The pipeline begins with a continuous feed of new mathematics preprints from arXiv. Each paper is parsed (from PDF or LaTeX source) to identify lemma environments. For each detected lemma, the system retrieves any missing definitions or assumptions from the same document using one of two strategies: (1) full-context, where the entire paper text is provided as context, or (2) vector retrieval, where queries are embedding-matched against paper chunks. The retrieved context is then fed to an LLM along with the original lemma statement; the LLM rewrites the statement to incorporate the necessary background, aiming to produce a self-contained version. A separate LLM judge evaluates whether the rewritten statement is indeed self-contained. Items that pass this automated filter are added to the benchmark, and periodic human validation confirms the overall quality. The pipeline runs automatically as new preprints appear, making LemmaBench a live resource.

## Key Results
- 60–64% of extracted lemmas are judged self-contained; human-verified precision ranges from 75.5% to 96.5%.
- State-of-the-art LLMs achieve only ~10–15% pass@1 on the theorem-proving task using the benchmark.
- LLM-as-judge correctness, compared to human experts, ranges from 67% to 83%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The pipeline's success lies in its targeted combination of extraction, context-aware rewriting, and automated quality control. By retrieving missing mathematical context directly from the source paper, the system ensures that rewritten lemmas are coherent and self-sufficient without requiring external knowledge. LLMs provide the flexibility to synthesize natural language statements that integrate retrieved definitions seamlessly. The self-contained judge acts as a scalable filter, maintaining benchmark quality before human review. The live update mechanism, which continuously ingests new arXiv submissions, prevents benchmark staleness and data leakage, ensuring that evaluations reflect truly current research challenges.

## Foundational Learning
**Lemma extraction from LaTeX/PDF**  
*Why needed:* To automatically locate candidate mathematical statements in raw research papers.  
*Quick check:* Does the system correctly identify all `\begin{lemma}...\end{lemma}` environments in a LaTeX source?

**Context retrieval**  
*Why needed:* To supply the definitions and assumptions that a lemma implicitly depends on, enabling the construction of a self-contained statement.  
*Quick check:* For a lemma that uses the term “compact space”, does the retriever return the definition of compactness from the same paper?

**LLM-based rewriting**  
*Why needed:* To synthesize a natural language statement that incorporates the retrieved context while preserving the original lemma's mathematical content.  
*Quick check:* Is the rewritten statement fully understandable to a mathematician without consulting the original paper?

**Self-contained judgment**  
*Why needed:* To automatically filter out statements that remain dependent on external context, ensuring benchmark items are genuinely self-contained.  
*Quick check:* Does the LLM judge’s binary decision agree with human judgment on a random sample of rewritten lemmas?

**Live benchmark construction**  
*Why needed:* To keep the dataset aligned with the latest mathematical research, avoiding the obsolescence of static benchmarks.  
*Quick check:* Are papers posted to arXiv in the past week automatically processed and incorporated?

**Human validation**  
*Why needed:* To provide a gold standard for quality and to correct any systematic errors from the automated pipeline.  
*Quick check:* Do multiple human annotators agree on the self-contained status of a set of benchmark lemmas (e.g., measured by Cohen’s κ)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
ArXiv Feed -> Document Parser -> Lemma Detector -> Context Retriever -> LLM Rewriter -> Self-contained Judge -> Benchmark

**Critical path**  
For each lemma, the pipeline must: (1) parse the document and extract the lemma, (2) retrieve relevant definitions/assumptions from the same paper, (3) generate a rewritten self-contained statement via the LLM, (4) obtain a positive self-contained judgment. Failure at any step prevents the lemma from entering the benchmark.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Full-context vs. vector retrieval:* Full-context guarantees no missed definitions but increases token usage and may overwhelm the LLM; vector retrieval is more efficient but risks retrieving irrelevant snippets.  
- *LLM vs. rule-based rewriting:* LLMs offer adaptability to varied mathematical language but introduce nondeterminism and potential factual errors; rule-based methods are deterministic but brittle.  
- *Automation vs. human oversight:* Full automation scales to thousands of papers but may lower precision; human validation improves quality but is a bottleneck.  
- *Live updates:* Frequent updates keep the benchmark current but complicate versioning and reproducibility.

**Failure signatures**  
- Lemma detector: unusually low lemma count, or many non-lemma text snippets being flagged.  
- Retriever: high rate of “definition not found” or retrieved snippets that do not contain the needed term.  
- Rewriter: output statements that contradict the original lemma or contain nonsensical phrasing.  
- Judge: extreme imbalance (nearly all pass or all fail) that diverges from human evaluation on a sample.

**First experiments**  
1. *End-to-end sanity check:* Run the pipeline on a single well-known mathematics paper (e.g., a classic monograph) with a manually curated list of lemmas. Verify that all original lemmas are extracted and that the rewritten versions are self-contained to expert judgment.  
2. *Component interaction test:* Replace the LLM rewriter with a simple templating approach (e.g., prepend “Assume that … and define …”) and measure the drop in self-contained pass rate compared to the full LLM rewriter.  
3. *Retrieval recall probe:* For a set of lemmas requiring specific definitions, check whether the retriever (under both modes) returns the correct definition snippet. Compute recall and average retrieval latency.

The paper does not explicitly list open research questions. ## Limitations
- Self-contained precision range (75.5–96.5%) reflects annotator subjectivity; sample size and expertise of annotators are unspecified.
- The theorem-proving evaluation protocol is poorly described, making it hard to interpret the low pass@1 numbers.
- The pipeline relies on LLMs for critical steps (retrieval, rewriting, judging), so any systematic LLM biases or errors propagate into the benchmark.

## Confidence
- Benchmark quality: Medium
- LLM performance: Medium
- LLM-as-judge reliability: Medium-High

## Next Checks
1. *Pipeline replication and annotator agreement:* Run the extraction pipeline on a new random sample of recent arXiv mathematics preprints. Engage multiple domain experts to judge self-containment, compute the self-contained rate and inter-annotator agreement (e.g., Cohen’s κ).
2. *Retrieval component ablation:* Isolate the effect of retrieval method (full-context vs. vector) by constructing two benchmark variants. Compare their self-contained yields and evaluate a fixed LLM on both to assess impact on theorem-proving accuracy.
3. *Broad model evaluation:* Test a diverse suite of LLMs—including math‑specialized systems (AlphaProof, GPT‑4 with code interpreter) and varying sizes—on the public LemmaBench dataset. Report detailed performance breakdowns (by lemma difficulty, field) to identify bottlenecks.
