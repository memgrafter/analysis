# Beyond a Single Extractor: Re-thinking HTML-to-Text Extraction for LLM Pretraining

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.19548
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19548
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Combining outputs of multiple HTML-to-text extractors increases token yield by up to 71% over a single extractor baseline while preserving general language understanding, but extractor choice causes up to 10 percentage point differences on table- and code-specific tasks.

## Executive Summary
Current LLM pretraining pipelines rely on a single fixed HTML-to-text extractor for all webpages, leading to suboptimal data coverage. This paper shows that combining the outputs of diverse rule-based extractors via a simple union operation substantially increases the amount of usable text from Common Crawl. The union of resiliparse, trafilatura, and jusText outputs, after standard filtering, yields 57B tokens from a 1B-page pool compared to 39B with any single extractor—a 71% increase—without degrading performance on the CORE_V2 benchmark. However, the choice of extractor has a dramatic impact on downstream tasks involving structured content: resiliparse outperforms others by up to 10 percentage points on WikiTableQuestions and by 3 points on HumanEval, due to differences in how tables and code blocks are preserved and formatted.

## Method Summary
The authors apply three established rule-based extractors (resiliparse, trafilatura, jusText) in parallel to the same Common Crawl WARC pool used in the DCLM-BASELINE dataset. For each extractor, the standard DCLM filtering pipeline (English language identification, quality threshold, deduplication) is run independently. The final dataset is constructed by taking the union of page IDs that survive filtering across extractors, with a manual priority ordering to resolve overlaps when needed. This union dataset is then used to train language models (1B and 7B parameters) under the same OpenLM training regime as DCLM-BASELINE. To study structured content, the authors create CC-Tables and CC-Code subsets by filtering for HTML tables and code blocks, then mix these with the baseline corpus to train models evaluated on WikiTableQuestions and HumanEval.

## Key Results
- Token yield increases by up to 71% (e.g., 57B vs. 39B tokens for the 1B-1x pool) when using the union of three extractors compared to any single extractor.
- Performance on general language understanding (CORE_V2) remains statistically unchanged, indicating the added pages are not noisier.
- Extractor choice significantly affects structured content tasks: resiliparse yields up to 10 percentage points higher on WikiTableQuestions than jusText, and up to 3 points higher on HumanEval; trafilatura also outperforms jusText on code tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Different rule-based extractors capture largely non-overlapping, high-quality subsets of webpages.
- **Mechanism**: Each extractor uses distinct heuristics (block segmentation, link density, whitespace handling) leading to divergent "survival sets" after filtering.
- **Core assumption**: Heuristics are orthogonal enough that their outputs are complementary.
- **Evidence anchors**: 61% of pages are uniquely kept by one extractor; only 39% survive across multiple extractors.
- **Break condition**: All extractors converge on identical heuristics or web boilerplate becomes universally detectable.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Extractor choice has an outsized, task-specific impact on structured content performance.
- **Mechanism**: Extractors handle HTML structural elements differently (e.g., discarding vs. formatting tables/code), affecting model exposure to patterns.
- **Core assumption**: Format and integrity of structured elements in pretraining data causally affect downstream task learning.
- **Evidence anchors**: Up to 10 p.p. difference on WikiTableQuestions, 3 p.p. on HumanEval; resiliparse best for tables, both resiliparse and trafilatura beat jusText for code.
- **Break condition**: A perfect canonical form for structured content emerges, or evaluations become format-invariant.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Union of extractor outputs increases token yield while maintaining general benchmark performance.
- **Mechanism**: Union recovers pages erroneously filtered by a single extractor, adding diverse content without adding noise that harms general tasks.
- **Core assumption**: Uniquely retained pages are comparable in quality to the intersection; benchmarks aren't saturated at training scale.
- **Evidence anchors**: 71% token increase with CORE_V2 within variance; improved performance under data constraints.
- **Break condition**: Union introduces大量low-quality/duplicate content, or marginal pages are topic-specific and harm balanced benchmarks.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: WARC files -> Parallel extractors (resiliparse, trafilatura, jusText) -> DCLM-BASELINE filters per extractor -> Union of surviving page IDs -> Optional second-round deduplication -> Final dataset -> LM training (OpenLM) -> Evaluation (CORE_V2, WikiTQ, HumanEval)
- **Critical path**: Extraction choice -> Filtering survivor sets -> Union composition -> Token yield & content profile -> Training dynamics -> Structured task performance.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Token yield vs. compute cost (linear increase); Diversity vs. noise (union may add lower-quality pages); Structured content vs. general quality (best extractor for tables may not be best for text).
- **Failure signatures**: Unexpected CORE_V2 drop (union added noise); Token yield not increasing (extractor outputs too overlapping); Structured task performance not improving (task requires different formatting than extractors produce).
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Baseline comparison: Run each extractor alone through DCLM pipeline; measure token yield, page overlap, CORE_V2.  
  2. Union construction: Create Union (Random) and Union (Manual); compare token counts before/after second deduplication; measure CORE_V2.  
  3. Structured content isolation: Build CC-Tables/CC-Code, mix 20% with 80% baseline, train 1B/7B, evaluate on WikiTQ/HumanEval.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What is the optimal number and combination of text extractors to maximize token yield without sacrificing model performance? (The study only tested three rule-based extractors.)
- How can extractor selection be made adaptive to individual page characteristics? (Current union applies all extractors equally; a meta-classifier might choose the best per page.)
- Why do markdown-formatted tables from trafilatura yield lower tabular reasoning performance compared to space-delimited tables from resiliparse? (Formatting vs. other extraction differences not disentangled.)
- What alternative filtering approaches for CC-Tables and CC-Code could yield higher-quality structured content? (FastText classifier used; more advanced methods may help.)

## Limitations
- Exact WARC snapshots for the 7B-2x pool are not specified, hindering exact replication.
- FastText hyperparameters for CC-Tables/CC-Code and DCLM filter thresholds are omitted, requiring reverse-engineering.
- Generalizability limited to three rule-based extractors; neural extractors not evaluated.
- Evaluation restricted to CORE_V2, WikiTQ, HumanEval; other benchmarks and model sizes untested.
- No cost-benefit analysis; union increases compute linearly.

## Confidence
- Complementarity (non-overlapping survival sets): **High** (direct overlap analysis).
- Union token yield & stable general performance: **High** (empirical results show 71% increase with no CORE_V2 degradation).
- Extractor impact on structured tasks: **High** for observed tasks, **Medium** for generalizing beyond tested benchmarks.

## Next Checks
1. Integrate a neural extractor (e.g., Dragnet) into the ensemble and recompute union benefits on token yield and CORE_V2.
2. Perform a systematic sweep of fastText quality thresholds (0.05–0.25) for each extractor and their union to assess robustness.
3. Scale to larger models (13B, 34B) and evaluate on additional structured tasks (TaPas, CodeBLEU) to verify if extractor-specific effects persist.
