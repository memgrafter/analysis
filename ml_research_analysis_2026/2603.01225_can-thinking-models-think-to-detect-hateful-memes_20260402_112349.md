# Can Thinking Models Think to Detect Hateful Memes?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.01225
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01225
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art on Hateful Memes test set with accuracy 81.2% and weighted F1 0.81, improving ~1% over prior best; explanation METEOR 0.52, improving ~3%.

## Executive Summary
Hateful meme detection demands compositional multimodal reasoning beyond unimodal cues. This paper presents a reinforcement learning-based post-training framework using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to enhance thinking-based multimodal large language models. The method enriches the Hateful Memes dataset with weakly supervised chain-of-thought (CoT) rationales distilled from GPT-4.1 and applies a two-stage pipeline: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) warm-up followed by GRPO, jointly optimizing classification accuracy, format compliance, and explanation quality. On the Hateful Memes benchmark, the approach achieves state-of-the-art results, lifting accuracy and weighted F1 by ~1% (to 81.2% and 0.81) and explanation METEOR by ~3% (to 0.52).

## Method Summary
The pipeline builds on a thinking-enabled MLLM (Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking). First, the Hateful Memes dataset is extended with GPT-4.1-generated CoT rationales, human-verified explanations, and fine-grained annotations (protected category, attack type). Each example is formatted as an instruction containing the meme image, its OCR text, and guidelines, expecting a structured output with `<think>`, `Label:`, and `Explanation:` blocks.

Stage 1: Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for 3 epochs minimizes token-level cross-entropy on the full sequence (including CoT when present), warming up the policy to produce valid formats and sound initial decisions.

Stage 2: GRPO post-training starts from the best SFT checkpoint. For each input, K=16 candidate outputs are sampled. A composite reward R(y) = α_fmt·R_fmt + α_lbl·R_lbl + α_len·R_len + α_met·R_met (with α_fmt=0.5, α_lbl=0.4, α_len=0.05, α_met=0.05) is computed:
- R_fmt: binary check for required tags
- R_lbl: exact label match
- R_len: Gaussian reward around 100 words
- R_met: METEOR against the gold explanation
Using group-relative advantages, the policy is updated with a clipped PPO-style surrogate loss and a KL penalty (β=0.04) to the SFT reference. Training uses AdamW (β1=0.9, β2=0.95, ε=1e-8), weight decay 0.1, gradient clipping 1.0, cosine LR with warmup 0.05, and DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 across 4×NVIDIA H200 GPUs. Generation during training uses temperature=1.0, top-p=0.85, and max length=4096 tokens.

## Key Results
- **Classification**: Accuracy 81.2%, Weighted F1 0.81, Macro F1 0.78 on the Hateful Memes test set, beating prior SOTA by ≈1%.
- **Explanation**: METEOR 0.52 (+3%), BERTScore 0.88 (+1.4%).
- **Ablation**: SFT with CoTD rationales yields higher SFT accuracy (79.2%) and METEOR (0.50) than without; GRPO on top further improves all metrics. Cold-start GRPO (no SFT) performs poorly, underscoring the necessity of warm‑up.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **GRPO joint optimization** ties label accuracy to explanation quality and format via a composite reward, preventing the model from exploiting shortcuts like guessing labels without reasoning. Group‑relative advantage estimates and KL regularization stabilize updates. *Break condition*: Mis‑specified reward weights cause over‑optimization of one component (e.g., lengthy but irrelevant rationales) or format degeneration.

- **SFT warm‑up** supplies a strong prior that already generates valid output structure and reasonable decisions, enabling GRPO to focus on refining reasoning rather than learning basics. *Break condition*: Noisy SFT data (incorrect fine‑grained labels or unfaithful CoT) may anchor the policy to flawed patterns that KL constraints later hinder to correct.

- **CoT distillation** injects intermediate reasoning from GPT-4.1, guiding the model to integrate visual and textual cues step‑by‑step and improving both classification and explanation quality over label+explanation supervision alone. *Break condition*: Hallucinated or biased teacher rationales risk being internalized, harming generalization.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Compositional Multimodal Reasoning**  
  Why needed: Hateful memes often rely on the synergy between image and text, where neither alone is hateful.  
  Quick check: Find a meme where the text is harmless, the image is harmless, but together they convey hate. What specific interaction creates the hateful meaning?

- **Concept: Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT) Distillation**  
  Why needed: Training a “thinking” MLLM requires supervision on intermediate steps, not just final answers. Distilling a strong teacher yields scalable, structured traces.  
  Quick check: What risks arise when using a commercial model’s outputs as reasoning labels? How could you detect or mitigate “reasoning shortcuts” in the distilled data?

- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Environment Feedback (GRPO)**  
  Why needed: Standard SFT optimizes token likelihood, which may not improve high‑level reasoning or explanation quality. GRPO directly optimizes task‑level rewards while maintaining format.  
  Quick check: Why does GRPO use a group‑relative baseline (comparing K samples per input) instead of a global average? What problem does this solve?

- **Concept: Hateful Meme Taxonomies**  
  Why needed: Fine‑grained labels (protected category, attack type) provide richer supervision than a binary signal, guiding models toward human‑aligned reasoning pathways.  
  Quick check: Would a model trained only on binary labels likely generate explanations that mention specific protected categories or attack types? Why or why not?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input encoder (image + OCR text) → multimodal fusion → Qwen3‑VL‑8B‑Thinking backbone → structured output generation (`<think>` → `Label:` → `Explanation:`). Training modules: SFT loss (cross‑entropy) → GRPO sampler + reward calculator + policy update.

- **Critical path**: Raw meme → feature extraction → SFT warm‑up (learn format + basic task) → GRPO refinement (optimize reasoning & explanations) → deployable model.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  • Reward weighting: higher α_lbl boosts accuracy but may reduce explanation diversity; higher α_met improves explanations at risk of label errors.
  • CoT supervision: improves performance but may cause “CoT collapse” during GRPO (observed length decrease); R_len partially mitigates.
  • SFT content: adding fine‑grained labels gives small ceiling gains but requires extra annotation.
  • Sampling budget: K=16 stabilizes advantage estimates but raises memory/computation costs.

- **Failure signatures**:
  • Format degeneration: missing `<think>` or malformed output → R_fmt drops. Check regex validation logs.
  • CoT collapse: steadily decreasing `<think>` length during GRPO while rewards rise.
  • Reward over‑optimization: MET ↑ but label accuracy ↓ → explanations become tuned to gold phrasing without correct decisions.
  • Cold‑start instability: GRPO from pretrained model (no SFT) yields noisy rewards and poor final metrics.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Reproduce zero‑shot CoT** on Hateful Memes dev set for various baselines; confirm CoT improves F1 but degrades BERT/METEOR, motivating RL refinement.
  2. **Ablate SFT components** (Cls+Exp, Cls+FG+Exp, Cls+FG+Exp+CoTD); verify CoTD and fine‑grained labels each increase accuracy and METEOR.
  3. **Compare GRPO initializations** (cold‑start, SFT, SFT+FG, SFT+FG+CoTD) by tracking reward and `<think>` length; expect warm‑start, especially with CoTD, to yield smoother reward growth and shorter collapse.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How well does the proposed GRPO-based training generalize to other hateful meme benchmarks, multimodal toxicity tasks, and multilingual contexts?
2. To what extent can the GRPO optimization and two‑stage pipeline be effectively transferred to other thinking‑enabled MLLMs and under varying prompting regimes?
3. Can the framework leverage large‑scale unlabeled or weakly labeled memes via weakly supervised training, and does this improve performance without sacrificing explanation quality?
4. How can self‑supervised objectives be integrated into GRPO for unlabeled data, and can such approaches match supervised performance?

## Limitations
- Evaluation confined to the English Hateful Memes dataset; generalizability to other domains/languages remains unknown.
- GRPO hyperparameters (reward weights, length Gaussian) were chosen empirically; retuning may be required for new tasks.
- High computational cost (4×H200, K=16) limits accessibility.
- Distilled CoT rationales depend on GPT-4.1 quality; their faithfulness was only checked via an LLM‑as‑judge, which may be unreliable.
- Automatic explanation metrics (METEOR, BERTScore) may not reflect true human‑perceived explanation quality; no human evaluation presented.

## Confidence
- SFT warm‑up essential for successful GRPO: **High** (ablation shows cold‑start fails).
- GRPO jointly optimizing components yields state‑of‑the‑art results: **High** (consistent test metric improvements).
- Weakly‑supervised CoT distillation improves SFT and final performance: **Medium** (gains observed but limited by teacher model quality).
- GRPO improves fine‑grained reasoning and prevents reward hacking: **Medium** (supported by experiments but no direct causal analysis).
- Framework generalizes to other datasets/models: **Low** (no evidence beyond Hateful Memes and Qwen3‑VL).

## Next Checks
1. Conduct human evaluation of model explanations (rating for completeness, faithfulness, usefulness) to corroborate automatic metric gains.
