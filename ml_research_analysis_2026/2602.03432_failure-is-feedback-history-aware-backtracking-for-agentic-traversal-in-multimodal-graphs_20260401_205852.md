# Failure is Feedback: History-Aware Backtracking for Agentic Traversal in Multimodal Graphs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03432
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03432
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** FiF averages 80.26% Recall@10 and 83.16% MRR@10 on multimodal retrieval benchmarks, outperforming prior graph-based methods by >12% R@10 and >9% MRR@10.

## Executive Summary
FiF addresses open-domain multimodal document retrieval by framing traversal as a sequential decision process over a layered component graph. It introduces two key innovations: (i) history-aware backtracking that re-uses failed traversal contexts to revise subqueries and re-anchor searches, and (ii) an economically-rational workflow that escalates from cheap vector matching to expensive LLM reasoning only when ambiguity or failure justifies it. On MultimodalQA, MMCoQA, and WebQA, FiF achieves state-of-the-art retrieval (80.26% R@10, 83.16% MRR@10), with ablations confirming that backtracking reduces wasted hops (57–61% fewer LLM calls) and that selective LLM reasoning is critical for accuracy.

## Method Summary
FiF builds a three-layer graph: V₀ (document summaries), V₁ (multimodal components), V₂ (subcomponents). An Orchestrator LLM agent controls a memory state (original query, subqueries, action history) and selects actions: TRAVERSE (with a strategy tuple controlling hop scope, vector granularity, and LLM reasoning level), PLAN (to insert/revise subqueries), or STOP. The Multi-Strategy Traverser executes in two stages: document-level selects candidate documents via vector search (local neighbors or global); component-level selects components within those documents, optionally using LLM reranking. The Traversal Evaluator judges success/failure per subquery. On failure or ambiguity, the Orchestrator backtracks to a prior successful anchor and rewrites the subquery. The process iterates until STOP, when a final reranker orders all accumulated components. Escalation is monotonic within an anchor, starting with coarse, low-cost retrieval and adding LLM reasoning only when needed.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art retrieval: 80.26% Recall@10, 83.16% MRR@10 across three benchmarks.
- Large gains over LILAC (+22.03% R@10, +18.60% MRR@10) and IRCOT (+12.88% R@10, +9.31% MRR@10).
- Backtracking cuts LLM calls by 57–61% while improving accuracy; removing LLM reasoning in traversal degrades R@10 by ~8 and increases runtime by 43%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** History-aware backtracking converts retrieval failures into constructive feedback by re-anchoring searches to promising prior contexts and revising subqueries, avoiding wasted hops.
- **Mechanism:** The Orchestrator logs traversal outcomes (success/failure) in a structured memory. When a subtask fails repeatedly or exhausts local neighbors, it selects a prior successful step as a new anchor, resets the search scope from that context, and rewrites the subquery to incorporate lessons from the failure (e.g., adding missing constraints). This breaks rigid plan execution.
- **Core assumption:** Failed traversal paths contain signals about unproductive routing patterns and under-specified queries that can inform better future hops.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...history-aware backtracking that re-uses failed traversal contexts to re-anchor searches and revise subqueries..."
  - [section 4.3] "Instead of always hopping from the most recent set of documents, it chooses an anchor document set D_anc that best matches the newly formed q_t, potentially restarting from an earlier successful context."
  - [corpus] Weak direct corpus evidence; mechanism is inferred from system design and ablation showing backtracking reduces LLM calls by 57–61% while improving accuracy.
- **Break condition (Assumption):** If failure traces are noisy or do not correlate with actual retrieval utility (e.g., false negatives in the Traversal Evaluator), re-anchoring may move the search to an equally unproductive region.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Economically-rational strategy escalation dynamically trades off accuracy and cost by starting with low-cost vector matching and only invoking expensive LLM reasoning when ambiguity or failure justifies it.
- **Mechanism:** The Orchestrator assigns a strategy tuple (τ_t) per hop, controlling hop scope (local neighbors vs. global vector search), vector scoring granularity (document/component/subcomponent), and LLM-reasoning level (none/component-only/both). It escalates monotonically when the current hop is ambiguous, marked as failure, or shows near-misses.
- **Core assumption:** Not all retrieval hops require the same reasoning depth; many can be resolved with cheap similarity matching, reserving LLM inference for cases where vector search is insufficient.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...economically-rational agentic workflow...escalating to intensive LLM-based reasoning only when the prior failure justifies the additional computational investment."
  - [section 4.2] "The Traverser is a configurable strategy engine... Strategy tuple τ_t controls traversal along three dimensions... Escalation policy (monotonic within the same anchor)..."
  - [corpus] Ablation shows removing LLM reasoning in traversal degrades R@10 by ~8 and increases runtime by 43%, implying that selective escalation is critical.
- **Break condition (Assumption):** If the escalation policy is too conservative (failing to escalate when needed), accuracy drops; if too aggressive, cost increases without meaningful gains.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Casting retrieval as a sequential decision process over an explicit information state enables adaptive reasoning, where the agent refines subqueries and strategies based on accumulated evidence and failure outcomes.
- **Mechanism:** The state is a structured memory (M_t = <Q, Q_t, H_t>) holding the original query, subquery list, and action history with outcomes. The Orchestrator selects actions (TRAVERSE, PLAN, STOP) conditioned on this state. The Planner can insert new subqueries; the Traverser executes with a chosen strategy; the Evaluator updates subquery statuses.
- **Core assumption:** The information need evolves multihop; a static, pre-specified plan cannot adapt to intermediate discoveries or dead ends.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1] "We formalize the process as ⟨S, A, T⟩, where the state is the agent’s information state... each step chooses (i) what to ask next (subquery), (ii) how to retrieve (tool/strategy), and (iii) where to move... conditioned on the current evidence."
  - [section 4.2] "The Orchestrator actively manages plan errors... it can use newly retrieved components to resolve missing information from earlier subqueries, or fuse multiple planned subqueries..."
  - [corpus] Ablation removing the Subquery Planner causes a R@10 drop of ~9–10, showing that plan revision is necessary.
- **Break condition (Assumption):** If the state representation (M_t) fails to capture critical context (e.g., by overwriting old subqueries), the agent loses the ability to diagnose plan errors.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
- Orchestrator (LLM agent): Central controller; decides next action (TRAVERSE/PLAN/STOP) using memory state M_t and neighbor docs.
- Multi-Strategy Traverser: Contains document-level and component-level traversers; executes TRAVERSE using strategy τ_t (scope, granularity, LLM reasoning).
- Subquery Planner (LLM): Implements PLAN; generates 1–2 new subqueries from M_t when plan is stale.
- Traversal Evaluator (LLM): Part of TRAVERSE observation; judges success/failure for current subtask and updates subtask statuses.
- Reranker (LLM): Invoked by STOP; ranks all accumulated candidate components by relevance to original query.

### Critical path
1. Orchestrator receives query Q, initial plan Q_t, and empty memory M_0.
2. For each iteration:  
   a. Orchestrator selects action (typically TRAVERSE first).  
   b. If TRAVERSE: Traverser runs (document-level → component-level) with τ_t, returns candidates.  
   c. Evaluator assesses candidates → updates memory (H_t, Q_t statuses).  
   d. Orchestrator may re-anchor based on failures or PLAN to revise Q_t.  
   e. Loop until STOP → Reranker final output.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Accuracy vs. Cost:** Higher accuracy requires more LLM calls (reasoning at both document and component levels). The escalation policy tries to minimize cost but may under-escalate if ambiguity is undetected.
- **Memory depth vs. Efficiency:** Keeping all prior subqueries (even failed ones) enables backtracking but increases memory size and LLM context length.
- **Global vs. Local scope:** Global vector search escapes local neighborhoods but may introduce noise; local neighbor hops are precise but can get stuck.

### Failure signatures
- Repeated failure on same subtask with same anchor and strategy (failed routing pattern).
- Neighbor docs list exhausted or irrelevant for current subtask.
- Subtask status remains "not answerable" after multiple hops.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can FiF's computational overhead from orchestration and LLM reranking be reduced while preserving retrieval accuracy? The paper notes that the agentic workflow inherently requires multiple LLM calls and sequential decisions, which are costly and slow, hindering scalability.
- **Question 2:** How can FiF handle noisy, unstructured webpages with weak or missing navigational signals? The current design depends on explicit graph edges; without reliable links, traversal may fail or become inefficient.
- **Question 3:** Can FiF's components (parsing, graph construction, policy) be jointly optimized for better generalization across arbitrary corpora? Separate design may not achieve global optimum and requires per-corpus tuning.

## Limitations
- Subcomponent granularity (sentence splitting, table row extraction, image object detection) is underspecified.
- Exact LLM prompts, decoding parameters, and the meaning of "reasoning_effort=low" are not provided.
- Escalation thresholds (e.g., how many failures trigger backtracking) are implicit in the orchestrator.
- Traversal Evaluator, as an LLM judge, might label success/failure inconsistently.
- Baseline implementations (LILAC, IRCOT) may differ in graph construction or prompting, affecting comparison validity.

## Confidence
- History-aware backtracking: **Medium** (ablation shows benefit but relies on undisclosed LLM heuristics).
- Economically-rational escalation: **Medium** (cost savings shown, but thresholds are vague).
- Sequential decision framework: **High** (removing planner causes large performance drops).
- Overall SOTA results (80.26% R@10, 83.16% MRR@10): **Medium** (reproducibility concerns and baseline mismatch risk).
