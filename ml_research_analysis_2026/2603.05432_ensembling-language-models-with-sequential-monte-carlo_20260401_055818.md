# Ensembling Language Models with Sequential Monte Carlo

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.05432
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05432
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Byte-level SMC product ensemble achieves 96.0% expected accuracy on JSON, 15.2% on BBH, and 50.9% on SPIDER, outperforming local averaging and single-model baselines.

## Executive Summary
Ensembling language models during decoding is challenging because locally normalizing token-level aggregations produce biased global approximations, and mismatched tokenizations hinder direct combination. This paper introduces f-ensembles, a unified framework to combine K models via any aggregation function f, and uses a byte-level Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) sampler operating in shared character space to enable consistent global sampling. Experiments on structured tasks (JSON generation, Big-Bench Hard, Text-to-SQL) show that consensus-seeking f-ensembles (product, min) substantially outperform probability averaging. For instance, a token-level SMC product ensemble reaches 96.0% expected accuracy on JSON (best single 95.6%), 15.2% on BBH (13.9%), and 50.9% on SPIDER (best single 53.6%). A cross-model byte SMC min hits 55.5% on SPIDER, surpassing token-level approaches. For consensus operators, better posterior approximations (more particles) correlate positively with expected accuracy, while coverage-seeking show weak or negative correlations.

## Method Summary
The f-ensemble framework defines a target distribution Φ(x) ∝ f(p₁(x), …, pₖ(x)) for any aggregation f, where pₖ(x) are string probabilities from each model in a shared character space. To sample from Φ, the authors employ Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) with a shaping function ψ(x′|x) = f(p₁(x′|x), …, pₖ(x′|x)) that guides particle proposals. Importance weights correct for sampling from the proposal rather than the target, and resampling maintains diversity. Tokenization mismatches are resolved by converting each tokenized language model to a character-level distribution pΣ(x) via marginalization over all tokenizations that decode to a byte string. The method is evaluated on JSON, BBH, and SPIDER using instruction-tuned models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, phi-4), comparing various f (min, product, sum, max) against local averaging and single-prompt baselines.

## Key Results
- Token SMC product ensemble achieves 96.0% expected accuracy on JSON (best single 95.6%), 15.2% on BBH (13.9%), and 50.9% on SPIDER (best single 53.6%).
- Cross-model byte SMC min achieves 55.5% on SPIDER, improving over token-level methods.
- For consensus-seeking operators, posterior approximation quality (E[log Ẑ]) correlates with expected accuracy (Spearman ρ up to 0.31); coverage-seeking shows weak/negative correlations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The f-ensemble framework unifies a wide range of aggregation strategies (e.g., product, min, max) under a single variational principle derived from α-divergences.
- **Mechanism**: For given expert potentials p₁…pₖ over a shared character alphabet Σ, the f-ensemble defines Φ(x) ∝ f(p₁(x),…,pₖ(x)) with a normalizer Z. By minimizing a weighted sum of α-divergences Dₐ(Φ||pₖ), the optimal Φ* becomes the generalized mean: Φ*(x) ∝ (∑ₖ wₖ pₖ(x)^τ)^(1/τ) where τ = 1–α. This recovers product (τ→0), mixture (τ=1), min (τ→–∞), and max (τ→∞).
- **Core assumption**: The aggregation function f is applied to string-level probabilities in a common space (e.g., character-level), ensuring Φ is well-defined.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states the unified framework; Section 4 derives generalized means via Theorem 4.1; corpus provides indirect support from related ensemble work.
- **Break condition**: If f is not annihilative (e.g., f(p₁,p₂)=(p₁–p₂)²), the standard shaping may violate absolute continuity, requiring modified shaping to avoid degenerate proposals.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Operating at the byte/character level sidesteps tokenization mismatches across models, enabling consistent combination.
- **Mechanism**: Each tokenized LM pΔ over tokens induces a character-level distribution pΣ via marginalization over all tokenizations that decode to a given byte string (pΣ(x)=∑_{Y: κ(Y)=x} pΔ(Y)). Ensembling then occurs directly over pΣ(x) values in a shared alphabet Σ.
- **Core assumption**: Mapping token models to character-level distributions is accurate and retains sufficient probability mass for all relevant strings.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions byte-level SMC; Section C formalizes the mapping; neighboring work on pixel-based models supports non-token representations.
- **Break condition**: If character-level marginalization is computationally intractable or loses significant mass due to poor tokenizer coverage, the approach may fail to approximate the true token-level ensemble.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) with an appropriate shaping function provides consistent approximations of the global f-ensemble distribution Φ, unlike local token-level averaging.
- **Mechanism**: SMC builds particles sequentially using a proposal kernel r(·|x) ideally proportional to a shaping function ψ(x′|x) that approximates the intractable next-symbol target. Importance weights correct distortion, and resampling reallocates computation to promising partial sequences. The shaping f(⃗p₁(·),…,⃗pₖ(·)) guides the sampler toward high-probability regions under the global ensemble, not just local token agreements.
- **Core assumption**: The shaping function is a tractable surrogate that preserves the relative ordering of strings under Φ, enabling effective exploration.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 5.2 defines shaping and factorization; Section 5.3 gives SMC algorithm; Section 2 shows local vs. global mismatch; neighboring work demonstrates SMC for LM steering.
- **Break condition**: If shaping is a poor approximation (e.g., local token product when global product prefers longer-range agreement), SMC may converge to a biased distribution, and approximation quality (E[log Ẑ]) will not correlate with task accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Autoregressive language modeling**: The f-ensemble is defined over string probabilities p(x), but LMs factorize these as products of next-token probabilities. This factorization explains why local token-level ensembling approximates a different distribution than global string-level ensembling. Quick check: How does the prefix probability p(γ) relate to the conditional next-token distribution p(γₜ | γ_{<t})?
- **Importance sampling and sequential importance sampling**: SMC relies on importance weights to correct for sampling from a proposal distribution r instead of the target Φ. The weight update formula hinges on factorization of φ(x) via the shaping function. Quick check: Why is the optimal proposal r*(x′|x) proportional to ψ(x′|x), and what does this imply about variance reduction?
- **Generalized means and α-divergences**: The choice of aggregation function f determines ensemble behavior (consensus vs. coverage). The paper derives f from minimizing a weighted sum of divergences, linking τ to agreement strength. Quick check: What behavioral difference arises between τ ≤ 0 (consensus-seeking) and τ ≥ 1 (coverage-seeking), and why does this matter for expected accuracy bounds?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Tokenized LMs (e.g., Llama, Qwen, Phi) -> Character-level mapper (marginalize tokenizations to bytes) -> f-ensemble aggregator (compute φ(x)=f(p₁(x),…,pₖ(x))) -> SMC engine (maintain particles, proposal set to shaping, resample) -> Evaluation module (compute expected accuracy from weights).

Critical path: Mapping token LMs → character-level distributions → defining f and shaping → running SMC loop → aggregating weighted particles → computing expected accuracy.

Design tradeoffs:
- Token vs. Byte SMC: Token-level is faster but requires vocabulary alignment; byte-level is slower but universally applicable and consistent.
- Number of particles (M): More particles improve approximation quality but increase runtime linearly.
- Choice of f: Consensus-seeking (min, product) often outperforms averaging in structured tasks but may fail if base models have low agreement; coverage-seeking (max, sum) is bounded by base model averages.
- Proposal distribution: Using ψ as proposal is locally optimal but may still have high variance if ψ is multi-modal.

Failure signatures:
- Poor approximation quality: Low E[log Ẑ] or high variance across particles; expected accuracy does not improve with more particles.
- Tokenization collapse: Character-level probabilities become zero for many strings due to tokenizers not covering certain byte sequences.
- Resampling failure: Effective sample size consistently below threshold, leading to particle degeneracy.
- f-ensemble mismatch: Consensus-seeking f yields lower accuracy than a single model when base models have disjoint success regions.

First 3 experiments:
1. Prompt intersection replication: Generate from locally normalized product ensemble of two prompts (e.g., "My favorite physicist is" and "My favorite author is"). Compare local vs. global scores to demonstrate bias in local ensembling.
2. f-ensemble comparison: Run token-level SMC with min, product, sum, max on JSON, BBH, SPIDER using within-model prompt pairs and cross-model ensembles. Measure expected accuracy vs. best single-prompt baseline.
3. Particle ablation: For a fixed f (e.g., product), vary M ∈ {1,2,5,10,25,50,75} and plot E[log Ẑ] and expected accuracy. Verify correlation (Spearman ρ) between approximation quality and accuracy for consensus vs. coverage operators.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Do consensus-seeking f-ensembles with SMC improve open-ended generation tasks like creative writing or dialogue?
2. How does ensemble performance and optimal choice of aggregation function scale when combining more than two experts (K > 2)?
3. Can the computational overhead of byte-level SMC be reduced sufficiently for practical deployment while retaining accuracy gains?
4. Do consensus-seeking ensembles (e.g., product, min) also yield accuracy improvements when combining reasoning-specialized models (e.g., chain-of-thought fine-tuned) rather than instruction-tuned models?

## Limitations
- Undisclosed dataset splits and seeds prevent exact replication of numerical results.
- Byte-level marginalization implementation details are vague; approximations may affect accuracy.
- SMC variance and degeneracy risks, especially for coverage-seeking f, require careful tuning and may limit reliability.

## Confidence
- f-ensemble variational derivation: High (solid theorem).
- Byte-level SMC overcoming token mismatches: Medium (conceptually sound, empirical validation limited).
- SMC consistency for global sampling: Medium for consensus (positive correlation), Low for coverage (weak/negative).
- Empirical gains on structured tasks: Medium (reproducible on three datasets, but generalizability uncertain).
