# SongSong: A Time Phonograph for Chinese SongCi Music from Thousand of Years Away

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.24071
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24071
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: SongSong generates Chinese SongCi music with superior style conformity (78.44) and pronunciation accuracy (75.37) compared to general music AI baselines.

## Executive Summary
SongSong introduces the first dedicated model for generating Chinese SongCi music, a classical poetic-musical form with strict rhythmic and stylistic constraints. The system addresses the scarcity of ancient music datasets by creating OpenSongSong (29.9 hours) and employs a two-stage pipeline: first predicting melody from lyrics via rhythm, then separately synthesizing singing voice (using diffusion) and accompaniment (using auto-regressive modeling). Evaluated on 85 unseen SongCi sentences, SongSong significantly outperforms general music generators Suno and SkyMusic in zero-shot SongCi style conformity and pronunciation accuracy, while achieving competitive objective audio quality. This work opens the door to AI-generated ancient Chinese music with cultural authenticity.

## Method Summary
The method comprises two main stages. Stage 1 maps input lyrics to a melody by first extracting rhythmic patterns (aligned with SongCi's fixed ci patterns) and then generating a corresponding pitch sequence. Stage 2 takes the predicted melody and splits generation: singing voice is produced via a diffusion model that conditions on both melody and lyrics to ensure pronunciation, while instrumental accompaniment is generated auto-reggressively to maintain harmonic and structural coherence. The entire system is trained on the newly compiled OpenSongSong dataset, which consists of annotated ancient SongCi performances. The two-stage design enables modular optimization and leverages the strengths of diffusion for high-fidelity voice and auto-regressive for temporal consistency in accompaniment.

## Key Results
- SongSong achieves zero-shot SongCi style conformity of 78.44, far surpassing Suno's 35.30.
- Pronunciation accuracy reaches 75.37 vs. Suno's 62.59, while voice naturality remains comparable.
- Objective Frechet Audio Distance (VGGish) is 5.41, indicating competitive audio distribution similarity to real music.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The two-stage architecture isolates the challenging problem of aligning lyrics with melody from the voice/accompaniment synthesis, allowing each component to specialize. Using rhythm as an explicit bridge respects SongCi's metrical rigor: the rhythm predictor ensures that syllable counts and beat placements conform to traditional ci patterns, which then guides melody generation to produce historically plausible tunes. Separating voice and accompaniment generation lets the system apply diffusion—well-suited for modeling complex audio distributions—to the vocal timbre and expression, while auto-regressive modeling captures the sequential dependencies in instrumental parts. Training on OpenSongSong imbues the models with authentic stylistic nuances that general music datasets lack, explaining the strong zero-shot performance on unseen SongCi sentences.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Lyrics -> Rhythm Predictor -> Melody Generator -> (Singing Voice Diffusion, Accompaniment Auto-regressive) -> Mixed Audio
Critical path: Input lyrics first pass through the rhythm predictor to produce a rhythmic pattern, which together with the lyrics informs the melody generator. The resulting melody then feeds two parallel modules: the voice diffusion synthesizes vocal audio conditioned on melody and lyrics, while the accompaniment auto-regressive generates instrumental tracks. Finally, the two streams are mixed to produce the output music.
Design tradeoffs: The two-stage design improves modularity and style control but introduces potential error propagation from rhythm/melody to voice/accompaniment. Diffusion yields high-quality voice but at slower inference speed; auto-regressive accompaniment is efficient but may suffer from repetition. Using a separate rhythm stage ensures metrical correctness but may limit melodic flexibility.
Failure signatures: Incorrect rhythm alignment (e.g., wrong syllable-beat mapping) leads to unconvincing SongCi structure; voice diffusion failures manifest as garbled pronunciation or unnatural timbre; accompaniment AR may generate Harmonically jarring or repetitive patterns; mixing issues could cause voice-to-accompaniment imbalance.
First experiments:
1. Remove the rhythm predictor and train an end-to-end lyrics-to-melody model; compare style conformity and pronunciation accuracy.
2. Replace the voice diffusion with an auto-regressive model (e.g., WaveNet) to measure impact on voice naturality and inference time.
3. Vary the amount of OpenSongSong training data (e.g., 10h, 20h, full) to assess data efficiency and saturation point.

## Limitations
- The OpenSongSong dataset is relatively small (29.9 hours) and may not fully capture the diversity of SongCi styles.
- Evaluation used only 85 unseen sentences and compared against general music AI baselines, potentially overstating relative gains.
- Reproducibility is hindered by the lack of released code, dataset details, and opaque training hyperparameters.
