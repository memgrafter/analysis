# Semantic Substrate Theory: An Operator-Theoretic Framework for Geometric Semantic Drift

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18699
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18699
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Formalizes semantic drift via a unified operator-theoretic substrate combining embedding geometry and diffusion, defines four coupled drift modes, and introduces "bridge mass" as a curvature-based predictor of neighborhood rewiring.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents Semantic Substrate Theory to address fragmentation in semantic drift research. It introduces a formal framework where drift is modeled through a time-indexed substrate St=(X,dt,Pt) that unifies embedding geometry with local diffusion processes. The theory defines four drift modes—translational, rewiring, dynamical, and process—as coupled projections of this single substrate, enabling mechanistic distinctions between different types of semantic change. A key innovation is "bridge mass," a node-level measure derived from negative curvature, proposed as a predictor of future neighborhood rewiring. While the theory provides a rigorous formal model and falsifiable predictions (P1-P5), it remains untested empirically and relies on assumptions about manifold structure and diffusion processes that require validation.

## Method Summary
The methodology centers on constructing a formal operator-theoretic framework. The core construct is the time-indexed substrate St=(X,dt,Pt), where X represents the embedding space, dt is a diffusion operator capturing local geometric structure, and Pt encodes temporal evolution. From this substrate, four drift modes are mathematically derived as observables, representing different aspects of semantic change. Bridge mass is defined as an aggregate of incident negative curvature at each node. The paper provides a "test contract" with falsifiable predictions (P1-P5) and a strict empirical protocol for validation, but no actual empirical implementation or metrics are presented.

## Key Results
- Introduces a unified operator-theoretic framework (Semantic Substrate Theory) that models semantic drift as evolution of a time-indexed substrate combining embedding geometry and diffusion.
- Defines four drift modes (translational, rewiring, dynamical, process) as mathematically coupled projections of the single substrate, enabling decomposition of observed drift signals.
- Proposes "bridge mass"—a node-level aggregate of incident negative curvature—as a novel predictor for future neighborhood rewiring events.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works by recognizing that multiple observed semantic drift signals (embedding displacement, neighbor changes, etc.) are not independent phenomena but rather different projections of a single underlying geometric-diffusion process. The time-indexed substrate St formalizes this by embedding the point cloud into a diffusion operator framework, where local geometry (via dt) and temporal evolution (via Pt) are jointly considered. The four drift modes emerge as natural observables from this formulation, each capturing a distinct aspect of how the substrate evolves. Bridge mass specifically identifies nodes that act as "bridges" between high-curvature regions, which theory predicts will be sites of future rewiring due to their unstable geometric position.

## Architecture Onboarding
Substrate Construction (X, dt estimation) → Operator Formalism (define Pt, compute modes) → Bridge Mass Calculation (curvature aggregation) → Prediction/Validation (test P1-P5)

Critical path: (1) Estimate diffusion operator dt from embedding geometry at each time point, (2) Construct time-indexed substrate St by linking dt across time via Pt, (3) Derive drift modes from substrate, (4) Compute bridge mass and test its predictive power for rewiring.

Design tradeoffs: Prioritizes theoretical unification over empirical tractability; estimating dt and curvature from finite noisy embeddings may be unstable. Bridge mass is elegant but computationally expensive and sensitive to bandwidth parameters. Requires strong assumptions (manifold, diffusion) that may not hold universally.

Failure signatures: Inability to estimate stable diffusion operator dt from data. Bridge mass showing no correlation with future rewiring. Drift modes failing to capture known semantic changes in benchmarks.

First experiments:  
1. Synthetic substrate simulation: Generate point clouds from known geometric transformations and verify estimated drift modes match ground-truth modifications.  
2. Bridge mass prediction test: On dynamic word embeddings, compute bridge mass at time t and measure correlation with nearest neighbor changes at t+1.  
3. Mode decomposition validation: Apply framework to dataset with annotated semantic changes and check if drift modes align with distinct change types.

## Limitations
- Entirely formal; lacks empirical validation.
- Core assumptions (manifold structure, diffusion process) not proven for real embedding trajectories.
- Bridge mass predictor introduced without empirical support; estimation from finite noisy data is non-trivial.
