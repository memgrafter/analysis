# Anatomy of the Modality Gap: Dissecting the Internal States of End-to-End Speech LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01502
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01502
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** The modality gap in speech LLMs stems from a structural misalignment requiring non-linear early-layer transformation, semantic smearing from speech token redundancy in mid-layers, and a failure of late-layer decision sharpening—not mere distribution shift or information loss.

## Executive Summary
This paper dissects the persistent performance gap between speech and text inputs in end-to-end speech LLMs. Using layer-wise cross-modal Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) with DTW-based token alignment, the authors identify three distinct processing phases. Speech representations first undergo a non-linear structural transformation in early layers to align with the text embedding manifold. In mid-layers, they exhibit a broad cross-modal alignment band due to semantic smearing from high token redundancy. Crucially, even when semantic information is present, late-layer representations fail to achieve decision sharpening, evidenced by negative logit margins and diffuse attention, leading to incorrect final answers. Simple mean/variance calibration catastrophically degrades performance, proving the gap is not a simple distribution shift.

## Method Summary
The core method involves comparing internal representations of speech and text inputs processed by the same LLM backbone. Token correspondence between modalities is established via Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) to handle length mismatches. Representational similarity is measured using layer-wise cross-modal CKA, which is invariant to linear transformations, revealing geometric alignment patterns. To probe decision formation, the authors project hidden states to the output vocabulary ("Logit Lens") and track the margin (correct logit minus best competitor) and attention dispersion from the final token to input positions. Interventions include input-layer statistical calibration, artificial text token redundancy, and token merging to test causal hypotheses about the identified phases.

## Key Results
- Early Transformer layers perform a non-linear structural transformation to align speech features with the text manifold; simple affine calibration at the input causes a catastrophic ~15.5% accuracy drop on BBH.
- Speech token redundancy induces a broad mid-layer cross-modal CKA alignment band ("semantic smearing") and diffuses decision-relevant attention; artificially increasing text redundancy degrades reasoning performance and increases decision entropy.
- Late-layer speech representations fail to sharpen into stable, high-margin predictions despite containing sufficient semantic information (positive probe accuracy), showing negative/weak margins and highly diffuse attention (>50% mass on >100 tokens).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Speech inputs require a non-linear structural transformation in early layers to achieve geometric compatibility with text embeddings; simple statistical calibration is insufficient and harmful.
- **Mechanism:** The speech adapter output initially projects acoustic features into a representation space that is geometrically incompatible with the text embedding manifold. Early Transformer layers (Phase I) perform a non-linear re-parameterization to align these manifolds, which cannot be achieved by affine transformations like mean/variance matching.
- **Core assumption:** The modality gap is not merely a distributional shift but a structural mismatch requiring learned non-linear projection.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...speech representations initially undergo a structural transformation in early layers to achieve geometric compatibility with text." and "Simple mean/variance calibration at the input layer catastrophically degrades performance (-15.5% on BBH), proving the gap is not a mere distribution shift."
  - [section 4.1] "...speech features initially reside in a manifold structurally distinct from text...early Transformer layers must first perform a non-linear projection before alignment becomes possible." and Table 1 showing input-layer calibration causes -15.5% drop on BBH.
  - [corpus] The nearest neighbor paper (arxiv:2510.12116) also identifies geometric misalignment but focuses on static metrics; this paper's evidence for *structural transformation* is primarily from the target text's internal CKA analysis and calibration experiments, not directly replicated in corpus neighbors.
- **Break condition:** If mean/variance calibration at the adapter output (layer 0) recovered downstream performance instead of degrading it, the hypothesis of required non-linear transformation would be falsified. The catastrophic failure under calibration confirms the structural nature of the mismatch.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The inherent redundancy of speech tokens—where a single semantic unit is distributed across multiple frames—causes a broad, smeared cross-modal alignment band in mid-layers (Phase II), contrasting with text's sharp token-to-token correspondence.
- **Mechanism:** Because speech is continuous and redundant, the semantic signal is distributed across many token positions. This forces the model to integrate information gradually over a wider span of layers, resulting in a broad diagonal band in cross-layer CKA rather than a sharp one. This smearing reflects functional processing, not geometric drift.
- **Core assumption:** Text tokens are information-dense discrete symbols, while speech tokens are low-information-density frames containing partial, overlapping semantic content.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...mid-layers, they exhibit a broad cross-modal alignment band due to semantic smearing from token redundancy." and "speech semantics are smeared due to redundancy...broad alignment band rather than sharp 1:1 token correspondence."
  - [section 4.2] "speech information spreads across many tokens...broad alignment band rather than tight diagonal correspondence." and Table 3 showing that artificially increasing text word redundancy degrades accuracy and increases decision entropy, causally linking redundancy to performance loss.
  - [corpus] Dorszewski et al. (ICASSP 2025, cited) note transformer stack redundancy in speech models, but the causal link to reasoning performance via attention dispersion (Table 2) and the broad CKA band is a novel contribution of this paper.
- **Break condition:** If artificially increasing text redundancy did not degrade reasoning accuracy or increase decision entropy, the hypothesis that redundancy causes functional impairment would be weakened. The observed degradation supports the claim.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Even when speech representations achieve mid-layer geometric alignment (Phase II), they fail to complete the late-layer "decision sharpening" phase (Phase III), resulting in diffuse attention, negative logit margins, and incorrect final answers despite sufficient semantic information being present.
- **Mechanism:** Late Transformer layers in LLMs perform a sharpening process that narrows down candidate outputs. Because speech tokens individually carry weak signals due to redundancy, the model cannot form a strong, low-entropy decision state. Information remains distributed (positive probe accuracy) but fails to concentrate onto the correct lexical choice (negative/weak margins, diffuse decision-token attention).
- **Core assumption:** The final reasoning stage requires a collapse of distributed evidence into a stable, high-margin prediction; speech's distributed representation prevents this collapse.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "late-layer representations fail to achieve decision sharpening, evidenced by negative logit margins and diffuse attention, even when semantic information is present."
  - [section 4.3] Figure 6 (logit margin) shows speech margins stay negative/weak in "only t2t" cases while text becomes strongly positive. Figure 7 (probe accuracy) shows information is present, ruling out loss. Table 2 shows decision-token attention is vastly more diffuse for speech (e.g., 101 tokens needed for 90% mass vs. 8 for text).
  - [corpus] Lad et al. (2024) introduced the "residual sharpening" concept for text LLMs; this paper extends it to show speech fails at this stage. Corpus neighbors do not analyze late-layer decision dynamics.
- **Break condition:** If probe accuracy collapsed in late layers for speech (indicating true information loss) *or* if speech margins became as positive as text in late layers for matched correct/incorrect groups, the "decision instability" claim would be undermined. The persistence of probe accuracy coupled with margin failure confirms a readout/sharpening bottleneck.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Cross-modal token alignment via Dynamic Time Warping (DTW)**
  - *Why needed here:* Speech and text sequences have mismatched lengths (many frames per token). DTW finds a monotonic alignment path to pair speech frames with text tokens, enabling token-wise comparisons (CKA, norm metrics) across modalities.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is a simple 1:1 downsampling of speech frames to text tokens insufficient for alignment?
- **Concept: Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) for representational similarity**
  - *Why needed here:* CKA measures structural similarity between activation matrices, invariant to invertible linear transformations. It reveals whether speech/text representations share geometry *beyond* simple vector alignment (e.g., cosine similarity).
  - *Quick check question:* What would a high CKA score but poor downstream performance imply about the modality gap?
- **Concept: Logit Lens / decision margin analysis**
  - *Why needed here:* Projecting hidden states to the output vocabulary ("Logit Lens") traces decision formation. The *margin* (correct logit minus best competitor) quantifies sharpening. Comparing margins layer-wise isolates where speech fails to isolate the correct answer.
  - *Quick check question:* How does a high-probability but low-margin distribution (e.g., 0.4, 0.39, 0.2) differ from a low-probability high-margin one (e.g., 0.7, 0.1, 0.2) in terms of model confidence?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Raw Speech/Text Input -> Modality-Specific Encoder (Speech Adapter for speech, Text Embedding for text) -> Shared Transformer Backbone -> LM Head.
- **Critical path:** Input → [Adapter → Early Layers (Phase I: structural transformation)] → [Mid Layers (Phase II: semantic smearing / broad alignment)] → [Late Layers (Phase III: decision sharpening failure for speech)] → Output.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Token granularity (finer speech tokens increase redundancy but preserve detail), early projection strength (stronger adapter may reduce Phase I load but overfit), late-layer sharpening mechanisms (e.g., contrastive decoding to condense distributed signals).
- **Failure signatures:** Broad mid-layer cross-modal CKA band (vs. sharp diagonal), negative/weak late-layer logit margins for speech despite high probe accuracy, highly diffuse decision-token attention (>50% mass on >100 tokens).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Validate Phase boundaries:** Compute cross-layer CKA heatmaps with DTW alignment (base layer ℓ₀=10 for SpeechMMLU, 20 for BBH) on 100 seed-fixed samples. Identify early dark zone (Phase I), broad diagonal band (Phase II), and late plateau (Phase III).
  2. **Reject distribution-shift hypothesis:** Apply mean/variance calibration (matching speech to text statistics) at adapter output (layer 0). Measure accuracy drop on BBH/SpeechMMLU. Expect catastrophic degradation (~-15%).
  3. **Test redundancy hypothesis:** Perform KV token merging (cosine similarity >0.90) on speech tokens from layer 7 onward. Sweep merge fraction (0.1–0.25) and end layer (15/18/21). Expect modest gains (+0.1–0.5%) if redundancy is a bottleneck.

## Limitations
- Generalizability is limited to a single model family (SLAMs); applicability to other architectures (e.g., cascaded speech-to-text systems) is unknown.
- DTW-based token alignment introduces potential noise, and causal links rely on text perturbations rather than direct speech interventions.
- Evidence for decision sharpening failure is correlational; unmeasured confounders (e.g., training objective mismatches) may contribute.

## Confidence
| Claim | Confidence |
|-------|------------|
| Modality gap is non-linear/structural (calibration catastrophe) | High |
| Token redundancy causes broad mid-layer alignment (text redundancy ablation) | Medium |
| Late-layer decision sharpening failure (margin/attention metrics) | Medium |

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-model calibration test:** Apply mean/variance calibration at the adapter output for at least two other end-to-end speech LLMs (e.g., Whisper-based, SLAM variants). Replicate BBH degradation pattern to confirm the structural mismatch is universal, not model-specific.
2. **Early-phase intervention:** Insert a learnable, non-linear alignment module (e.g., small adapter) immediately after the speech encoder. Measure if this reduces the early CKA dark zone and improves final accuracy, directly testing whether Phase I transformation is the bottleneck.
3. **Sharpening intervention:** Apply a decision-sharpening technique (e.g., contrastive decoding, tuned lens) in late layers specifically for speech inputs. If performance improves without altering mid-layer representations, it confirms Phase III failure as the primary bottleneck.
