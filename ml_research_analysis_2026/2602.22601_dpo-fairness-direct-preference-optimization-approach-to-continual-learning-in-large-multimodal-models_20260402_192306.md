# $φ$-DPO: Fairness Direct Preference Optimization Approach to Continual Learning in Large Multimodal Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22601  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22601  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** φ-DPO achieves state-of-the-art performance on multimodal continual learning benchmarks, with minimal forgetting (e.g., BWT = -0.37% on MLLM-CL Domain).

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles fairness in continual learning for large multimodal models (LMMs), where imbalanced data distributions cause biased gradient updates and catastrophic forgetting. The authors propose φ-DPO, a Fairness Direct Preference Optimization framework that reinterprets continual learning through a DPO-based loss. The loss includes a focusing parameter that balances gradient contributions across imbalanced groups, mitigating both forgetting and distributional bias. Evaluated on three benchmarks (CoIN, MLLM-CL Domain, MLLM-CL Ability), φ-DPO sets new performance records, notably reducing forgetting (e.g., BWT = -0.37%) compared to prior methods like MR-LoRA. The work also offers theoretical analysis linking DPO loss to KL divergence, providing guarantees on policy stability.

## Method Summary
φ-DPO adapts LMMs sequentially using LoRA for parameter efficiency. At each learning step on a new task, the model is trained with a combined loss: a supervised fine‑tuning term (−log p(y|x)) and the proposed Fair DPO loss. The Fair DPO loss extends standard DPO by weighting each preference pair with a focusing factor α_γ(p) that depends on the model’s predicted preference probability p. This weighting balances gradient contributions across imbalanced groups. The previous model checkpoint serves as the reference policy, and a KL‑penalty controlled by β (typically 0.1) regularizes deviation. The focusing parameter γ (typically 2.0) modulates fairness. Training runs for one epoch per task using AdamW and a cosine learning rate schedule. The method constructs preference pairs by using the reference answer as the preferred output (y⁺) and an LLM‑generated “hallucinated” alternative as the dispreferred output (y⁻).

## Key Results
- On MLLM-CL Domain, φ-DPO achieves MFT 74.29%, MFN 74.00%, MAA 75.68%, and BWT -0.37%, significantly reducing forgetting compared to prior methods.  
- Across CoIN, MLLM-CL Domain, and MLLM-CL Ability, φ-DPO consistently outperforms baselines in mean accuracy metrics.  
- The fairness‑focused gradient modulation proves effective even under highly imbalanced data distributions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with pairwise preferences can mitigate catastrophic forgetting in continual learning for LMMs.
- **Mechanism:** The method reformulates the RLHF objective (maximize reward under KL constraint) into a DPO loss that directly optimizes the relative log-odds of preferred (well-retained) outputs over dispreferred (forgotten) ones, using the previous model as reference. This implicitly regularizes the policy update.
- **Core assumption:** That we can construct preference pairs where the positive example is a good response (from reference answer) and the negative is a forgotten response (simulated via LLM hallucination). Also, the previous policy serves as a good proxy for prior knowledge.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "we first propose a new continual learning paradigm based on Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting by aligning learning with pairwise preference signals."
  - [section 3.1] "Continual Learning Paradigm with DPO" and Eq. (7).
  - [corpus] The corpus includes several DPO papers (e.g., "Direct Preference Optimization of Video Large Multimodal Models from Language Model Reward"), indicating DPO is an active alignment method. However, its application to continual learning is novel in this paper.
- **Break condition:** If preference pairs are noisy or the reference model degrades significantly, the DPO loss may not properly regularize updates, leading to forgetting.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Fair DPO loss with a focusing parameter γ balances gradient contributions across imbalanced groups, reducing bias in continual learning.
- **Mechanism:** The loss weighting factor α_γ(p) = (1-p)^(γ-1)[(1-p) + γ p log p] modulates the gradient of each example based on the model's predicted preference p. For large γ, examples with lower confidence (often from minority groups) receive higher weight, equalizing group gradients.
- **Core assumption:** That the imbalanced data distribution leads to biased gradient updates; and that the model's confidence correlates with group membership (i.e., minority group examples tend to have lower confidence). Also, that modulating based on p is effective.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "present a new φ-DPO loss that explicitly addresses distributional biases."
  - [section 3.2] "Fairness DPO in Continual Learning" and Eq. (14).
  - [corpus] The neighbor "Improving Safety Alignment via Balanced Direct Preference Optimization" explores balancing in DPO but for safety, not fairness. The focusing mechanism is inspired by focal loss, which is known in vision. The corpus does not provide direct evidence for this specific mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If γ is too large, the weighting may vanish for confident examples, causing underfitting and poor adaptation; if too small, the loss reduces to standard DPO and fairness gains are lost.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The DPO loss upper and lower bounds the KL divergence between consecutive policies, providing a theoretical guarantee that minimizing DPO loss controls policy drift.
- **Mechanism:** Using transport inequalities (Wasserstein distance) and assumptions about Lipschitz rewards and bounded density ratios, they derive Lemmas 1-2: D_KL(π_{t-1} || π_t) ≥ constant*(log2 - L_DPO)^2 and ≤ constant*L_DPO. This shows that small DPO loss ensures small KL divergence.
- **Core assumption:** The reward function is locally Lipschitz, the policies are close (mixture sampling), and the density ratio is uniformly bounded.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We provide a comprehensive theoretical analysis demonstrating that our approach addresses both forgetting and data imbalance."
  - [section 3.1.2] "Theoretical Analysis of Direct Preference Optimization in Forgetting Mitigation" and Lemmas 1-2.
  - [corpus] The theoretical tools (Wasserstein, Talagrand inequality) are known in probability, but the specific adaptation to DPO is novel. Corpus papers on DPO typically lack such analysis.
- **Break condition:** If the assumptions (Lipschitz, bounded ratio) fail, the bounds may not hold, and DPO loss may not reliably control forgetting.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)
- **Why needed here:** φ-DPO is built upon DPO, reinterpreting the RLHF objective as a pairwise preference loss. Understanding the derivation from the Bradley‑Terry model and the KL‑regularized reward maximization is essential to grasp how DPO replaces reward modeling and PPO.
- **Quick check:** Explain how the DPO loss (Eq. 7) is derived from the RLHF objective (Eq. 3) using the Bradley‑Terry model.

### Concept: Catastrophic Forgetting and Knowledge Distillation
- **Why needed:** The paper positions DPO as an alternative to knowledge distillation (KD) for mitigating forgetting. Knowing the limitations of KD (e.g., bias propagation, representation drift) helps motivate the DPO approach.
- **Quick check:** Why might knowledge distillation amplify biases in imbalanced multimodal data?

### Concept: Focal Loss for Class Imbalance
- **Why needed:** The Fair DPO loss is inspired by focal loss, which down‑weights well‑classified examples. Understanding focal loss helps explain the focusing parameter γ’s role in balancing group gradients.
- **Quick check:** How does increasing γ in focal loss affect the loss landscape for hard vs. easy examples?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - LMM backbone (e.g., LLaVA) with frozen parameters.
  - LoRA adapters (low‑rank matrices) inserted into attention layers; these are trainable.
  - Preference data loader: provides (x, y⁺, y⁻) triples.
  - Loss calculator: computes SFT loss (cross‑entropy on y) and φ‑DPO loss (Eq. 17) with β and γ.
  - Reference model: a copy of the model from previous task (or previous checkpoint) used as π_{t-1}.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialize model with pre‑trained weights.
  2. For each task t:
     a. Construct preference pairs for task data (y⁺ from ground truth, y⁻ from LLM hallucination).
     b. Train using combined loss: L = −log p(y|x) + λ · L_γ^DPO(π_t || π_{t-1}).
     c. After training, set π_{t-1} = π_t for next task.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - β (divergence parameter): Higher β enforces stricter adherence to previous policy (stability), lower β allows more adaptation (plasticity).
  - γ (focusing parameter): Higher γ increases fairness by down‑weighting confident predictions, but may reduce overall learning signal. Needs tuning.
  - LoRA rank: Higher rank increases capacity but also memory/computation; typically rank 32 is used.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - High forgetting (negative BWT): if β is too low or DPO loss weight is too small.
  - Poor adaptation on new tasks: if β is too high or γ is too large (over‑modulation).
  - Unbalanced performance across groups: if γ is too small or preference data quality is low.
  - Training instability: if γ is very large, gradients may vanish; if β is extreme, KL divergence may explode.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Evaluation on MLLM‑CL Domain benchmark (Table 1): Compare φ‑DPO against baselines on metrics MFT, MFN, MAA, BWT. Shows φ‑DPO achieves highest MFT/MFN/MAA and lowest forgetting.
  2. Ablation of Fairness DPO (Table 4): Shows that adding DPO (γ=2, β=0.1) improves over KD and vanilla DPO, demonstrating the benefit of fairness modulation.
  3. Hyperparameter sensitivity (Tables 5 and 6): Vary β and γ to show their impact on stability‑plasticity trade‑off. Best performance at β=0.1, γ=2.

## Limitations
- Precise DPO preference construction details (LLM used, prompt template, human verification criteria) remain undisclosed, risking data quality variability.
- Group partitioning for fairness is not specified, making it unclear how gradients are balanced in practice.
- Theoretical assumptions (locally Lipschitz reward, bounded density ratio) may not hold in large multimodal settings, potentially weakening the KL‑DPO loss guarantees.

## Confidence
- Forgetting mitigation via DPO: **Medium**  
- Fairness modulation with γ: **Medium**  
- Theoretical bounds linking DPO loss to KL divergence: **Low**
