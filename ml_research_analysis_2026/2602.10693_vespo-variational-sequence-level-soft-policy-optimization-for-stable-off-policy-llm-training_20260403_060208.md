# VESPO: Variational Sequence-Level Soft Policy Optimization for Stable Off-Policy LLM Training

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.10693
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10693
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** VESPO achieves 66.9% average accuracy on math reasoning benchmarks vs. 59.0% best baseline, with stable training under staleness ratios up to 64×.

## Executive Summary
VESPO addresses instability in off-policy reinforcement learning for large language models by reformulating importance weight reshaping as a variational optimization over proposal distributions. This yields a closed-form kernel ϕ(W)=W^α exp(-λW) that operates on sequence-level weights without length normalization. Experiments on mathematical reasoning benchmarks demonstrate stable training even under extreme staleness (64×) and fully asynchronous settings. On Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, VESPO achieves 66.9% average accuracy, significantly outperforming the best baseline (59.0%), with consistent gains across both dense and mixture-of-experts models. Ablations confirm that avoiding length normalization prevents collapse and that asymmetric hyperparameters are key to robustness.

## Method Summary
VESPO takes as input a pretrained LLM, an unfiltered math dataset (DAPO-Math), and a rule-based reward (Math-Verify). The core mechanism computes a sequence-level log-importance ratio by summing masked token differences between current policy π and reference policy μ. This raw importance weight W is then reshaped using an asymmetric kernel ϕ(W)=W^c1·exp(c2(1−W)) with (c1,c2)=(2,3) for positive advantages and (3,2) for negative advantages. The kernel output is detached from the computation graph to prevent gradient flow into the reshaping function and multiplied by the advantage and the token-level log probability to form the policy loss. Training uses large global batch sizes (e.g., gbs/mbs=8) and evaluation samples multiple responses per prompt (avg@k) across AIME, AMC, and MATH-500 benchmarks.

## Key Results
- VESPO maintains stable training under staleness ratios up to 64× and in fully asynchronous rollout/training decoupling.
- On Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, VESPO reaches 66.9% average accuracy, surpassing the best baseline (GRPO/GSPO/SAPO) at 59.0%.
- Ablations show that length normalization causes collapse and asymmetric hyperparameters (different c1,c2 for A>0 vs A<0) are essential for robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
VESPO’s stability stems from its variational derivation: by optimizing a KL-regularized objective over a proposal distribution q(W), the optimal q takes the closed-form kernel ϕ(W) ∝ W^α exp(-λW). This shape smoothly down-weights high-importance samples without hard clipping, reducing variance while retaining bias correction. Operating at the sequence level (instead of token-level normalization) avoids the length bias that inflates sequence length in methods like GSPO. The asymmetric hyperparameters allow independent control over suppression of positive-advantage (exploitation) and negative-advantage (exploration) samples, preventing entropy collapse or excessive variance respectively.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: LLM (Qwen3) -> DAPO-Math dataset -> Math-Verify -> Sequence log-importance calculator -> Asymmetric VESPO kernel (detached) -> Policy loss -> Optimizer (veRL) -> Evaluation (avg@k on math benchmarks). Critical path: load model, prepare data, generate responses, compute rewards, calculate sequence-level importance, apply kernel, compute loss, backpropagate, update weights, evaluate. Design tradeoffs: asynchronous training increases throughput but introduces staleness; length normalization reduces bias but causes length explosion; symmetric hyperparameters simplify tuning but sacrifice robustness. Failure signatures: response length >10k tokens (length explosion), token entropy <0.1 within 200 steps (entropy collapse), gradient norm spikes >2.0 under async (instability), reward crash after length growth (normalization collapse). First three experiments: (1) Verify kernel equivalence by implementing the variational objective and checking the derived form on sample W distributions; (2) Train with staleness ratio 64× and monitor length, entropy, gradient norms; (3) Systematically vary (c1,c2) for both advantage signs and measure final accuracy and stability metrics.

## Limitations
- Baseline implementations (GRPO clipping, GSPO normalization, SAPO τpos/τneg) are not fully specified; reproduction may differ subtly.
- Dataset processing (“unfiltered DAPO-Math”) and Math-Verify rule configuration are undocumented.
- Evaluation protocol (number of test problems, sampling procedure, checkpoint averaging) is unclear.
- Infrastructure details (veRL with Megatron, vLLM settings, async weight sync) are missing.
- Results are restricted to mathematical reasoning; generalization to other domains or larger models is unproven.
