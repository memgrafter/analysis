# To Generate or Discriminate? Methodological Considerations for Measuring Cultural Alignment in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.02858  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02858  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Inverse Socio-Demographic Prompting (ISDP) mitigates key confounders of Socio-Demographic Prompting (SDP); GPT-4o predicts country more accurately from real user behavior than from LLM-simulated behavior, while other models show the opposite pattern, and all models converge near random performance at the individual user level.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles confounding factors in Socio-Demographic Prompting (SDP)—such as prompt sensitivity and generation difficulty—that complicate the measurement of LLMs' cultural competency. It proposes Inverse Socio-Demographic Prompting (ISDP), where models discriminate and predict a user's demographic proxy from given behavior (actual or simulated user-identified culture-specific items). Using the Goodreads-CSI dataset with four LLMs, results show GPT-4o predicts country more accurately from real user behavior than from LLM-simulated behavior, while other models (Aya-23, Gemma-2, LLaMA-3.1) perform better with simulated behavior. Across all models, Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) scores diminish and converge near the random baseline (0.61) at the individual user level, indicating limited personalization capacity. The findings suggest ISDP mitigates SDP's confounders and reveals varying model behaviors, with GPT-4o better capturing real user patterns and others leaning toward stereotypical associations.

## Method Summary
The study compares Socio-Demographic Prompting (SDP) and its inverse (ISDP). In SDP, an LLM generates culture-specific item (CSI) spans conditioned on a demographic proxy (country + genre). In ISDP, a model is given CSI spans (either real user or SDP-simulated) and must rank a small set of candidate countries. Behavior is aggregated at three levels: Review (single review), User+Review (multiple reviews), and User (all reviews aggregated per user). The primary metric is Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR), with a random baseline of 0.61. Four LLMs (Aya-23, Gemma-2, GPT-4o, Llama-3.1) serve as both generators (SDP simulation) and discriminators (ISDP). Statistical significance is assessed via paired t-tests.

## Key Results
- ISDP reduces confounders like prompt sensitivity and open-ended generation difficulty by standardizing inputs and using a constrained selection task.
- GPT-4o predicts country more accurately from real user CSI spans than from its own simulated spans; other models (Aya-23, Gemma-2, Llama-3.1) perform better on their simulated behavior.
- MRR scores for all models converge toward the random baseline (~0.61) at the User aggregation level, indicating limited capacity to capture stable, user-specific cultural patterns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Mechanism 1: Task Reframing Reduces Confounds.** ISDP replaces open-ended generation (SDP) with a discrimination task where models select from a small candidate set (countries) given standardized CSI spans. This constrains the output space, reduces stylistic variance, and shifts evaluation from subjective generation to objective selection, making performance more reliably reflective of cultural associations.
- **Mechanism 2: Real vs. Simulated Behavior Comparison Exposes Stereotype Reliance.** The hypothesis is that LLM-simulated behavior (via SDP) is more stereotypical and less variable than real user behavior. If models rely on stereotypes, they should predict simulated behavior more accurately. The finding that only GPT-4o performs better on real behavior suggests it captures nuanced, non-stereotypical patterns present in actual human data, while smaller models lean on stereotypes.
- **Mechanism 3: Aggregation Levels Test Personalization Capacity.** At finer granularities (Review, User+Review), models may detect demographic signals from localized CSI patterns. Aggregating to the User level dilutes these with individual idiosyncrasies, causing MRR to drop toward baseline. This convergence indicates models lack the ability to track persistent, user-specific cultural understanding beyond superficial cues.

## Foundational Learning
- **Socio-Demographic Prompting (SDP) and its confounders.** SDP conditions LLMs on demographic proxies to generate culturally motivated outputs. Key confounders are prompt sensitivity (wording/format changes affect outputs) and generation difficulty (large, open output space). *Why needed:* Understanding SDP's flaws is essential to grasp why ISDP is proposed. *Quick check:* Name two SDP confounders that make poor performance hard to interpret as bias versus task design.
- **Discrimination vs. generation tasks.** Discrimination involves selecting from a fixed set; generation produces diverse, open-ended outputs. *Why needed:* ISDP's core mechanism relies on discrimination's computational and reliability advantages. *Quick check:* Why is a discrimination task generally less confounded than a generation task for probing knowledge?
- **Prototypical behavior vs. stereotypes.** Prototypical behavior captures typical, variable patterns of a cultural group; stereotypes are oversimplified, fixed beliefs. *Why needed:* ISDP aims to measure broader cultural awareness (prototypical) rather than simplistic stereotypes. *Quick check:* How would you differentiate "prototypical behavior" from a "stereotype" in cultural LLM evaluation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Preprocessing -> SDP Simulation Phase -> ISDP Discrimination Phase -> Evaluation
- **Critical path:** Standardize spans -> Generate simulated behavior for all LLMs -> Run ISDP for all (generator, discriminator) pairs at all aggregation levels -> Compute MRR and run paired t-tests.
- **Design tradeoffs:** ISDP sacrifices generative insights (e.g., output quality, explanations) for reliability in discrimination. The three-level aggregation trades off granularity (Review) versus personalization signal (User).
- **Failure signatures:** High MRR variance across repeated prompts indicates prompt sensitivity. All MRRs clustering near 0.61 at User level suggests no personalization capacity. Consistent outperformance on simulated behavior across models indicates strong stereotype reliance.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Span Standardization Validation:** Manually standardize a subset, compute IAA (target >0.95), and verify reduction in span length/uniqueness matches Appendix B.
  2. **Baseline ISDP with Real Behavior Only:** Run ISDP using real user spans at Review level for all models. Expect MRR > random baseline (0.61) if models capture prototypical cues.
  3. **Simulated vs. Real Behavior Comparison:** For each model, compare ISDP MRR on its own simulated spans versus real user spans at User+Review level. Test hypothesis that simulated yields higher MRR for non-GPT-4o models.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **When should researchers use SDP versus ISDP to measure LLMs' cultural competency?** The paper shows ISDP mitigates confounders but offers no guidelines for method selection based on research context or objectives.
- **Why do models like GPT-4o perform better with real user behavior, while others perform better with simulated behavior?** The paper lacks empirical analysis linking performance differences to training data, architecture, or internal representations.
- **To what extent does training on LLM-generated data impair models' ability to understand authentic human cultural behavior?** Inferred from the pattern that smaller models benefit from simulated data, but no direct investigation into training data provenance.
- **How generalizable are these findings to other languages, cultures, and behavioral domains?** Acknowledged limitation: the study uses only English book reviews from three countries and Western-developed models.

## Limitations
- The analysis depends on an unpublished, manually standardized span dataset (Appendix B); its subjective rules and final statistics are a black box, and performance trends may not hold with alternative standardization.
- The SDP simulation's fidelity is unclear due to underspecified user-genre proxy definitions, potentially misaligning "simulated behavior" with intended user prototypes.
- Findings are narrow in scope (single dataset, English, three Anglophone countries, book-review domain) limiting confidence in broader cultural generalizability.

## Confidence
- **ISDP reduces SDP confounders:** Medium (design sound but confound mitigation magnitude inferred, not directly measured in same setup).
- **GPT-4o captures real patterns vs. stereotypes:** Medium (real-vs-simulated comparison is strong evidence, but simulated benchmark validity is uncertain).
- **Models lack user-level personalization:** High (convergence to baseline at user level is a robust statistical result within this task/dataset).
- **Generalizability beyond studied context:** Low (scope is narrow and explicitly acknowledged).

## Next Checks
1. **Standardization Audit:** Replicate Appendix B standardization on a held-out subset; compute IAA with authors' version and quantify divergence in span counts/lengths. A mismatch >5% invalidates downstream comparisons.
2. **Proxy Definition Reconstruction:** Reconstruct a plausible user+genre proxy from Goodreads-CSI metadata (e.g., most-reviewed genre per user). Re-run SDP simulation; if the "simulated behavior advantage" for non-GPT-4o models disappears, the original finding depends on unreported proxy logic.
3. **Cross-Cultural Replication:** Apply ISDP to a new dataset with 4+ culturally distinct regions (e.g., non-Anglophone countries) and a different domain (e.g., movie reviews). Check if the real-vs-simulated performance split and user-level convergence persist.
