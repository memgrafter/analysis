# Not All Tokens See Equally: Perception-Grounded Policy Optimization for Large Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2604.01840  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01840  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** PGPO yields an 18.7% average accuracy gain over strong RLVR baselines (GRPO, DAPO, PAPO, VPPO) on seven challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Standard reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) for large vision‑language models assigns uniform token‑level advantages, which dilutes learning signals for steps that critically depend on visual input. This paper introduces **Token Visual Dependency (TVD)**, a metric that quantifies each token’s reliance on the image via KL divergence between visual‑conditioned and text‑only predictive distributions. The proposed **Perception‑Grounded Policy Optimization (PGPO)** reshapes token advantages using TVD scores through a threshold‑gated, mass‑conserving mechanism, thereby amplifying visually‑dependent tokens while suppressing noise from linguistic priors. Evaluated on Qwen2.5‑VL (3B/7B) across seven benchmarks, PGPO achieves substantial gains and demonstrates improved training stability with lower gradient variance.

## Method Summary
PGPO extends conventional RLVR (e.g., GRPO, DAPO) by weighting token‑level advantages according to visual dependency. During rollout, for each token position *t*, it computes \(S(s_t, I) = D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t, I) \| \pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t))\). The text‑only distribution \(\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t)\) is obtained via attention masking: all visual tokens have their attention mask set to False while preserving positions. The raw scores are compressed logarithmically, min‑max normalized per sequence to obtain \(I_t \in [0,1]\), and passed through a piecewise function: if \(I_t < \tau\) then \(\omega = I_t/(\tau+\epsilon)\); else \(\omega = 1 + \beta\cdot(I_t-\tau)/(1-\tau+\epsilon)\). A sum‑preserving normalization \(\tilde{\omega}_t = \omega(I_t) \cdot T / \sum_{j=1}^{T} \omega(I_j)\) ensures the total advantage mass equals the original sequence length \(T\). The reshaped token advantage becomes \(\tilde{A}_{i,t} = A_i \cdot \tilde{\omega}_t\). The vision encoder is unfrozen; hyperparameters \(\tau=0.4\), \(\beta=2.0\) are used.

## Key Results
- **18.7% average accuracy improvement** over GRPO, DAPO, PAPO, and VPPO on seven multimodal reasoning benchmarks (DynaMath, Geo3k, MathVerse, MathVision, MMK12, LogicVista, MMMU‑Pro).
- **Reduced gradient variance and no performance collapse** during training, while the average visual dependency score \(\tilde{S}\) increases, indicating a more vision‑grounded policy.
- **Ablations confirm necessity** of both threshold‑gating and sum‑preserving normalization; removing either degrades performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Token Visual Dependency (TVD) quantifies visual reliance.
TVD is defined as \(S(s_t, I) = D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t, I) \| \pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t))\), where \(\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t)\) is a text‑only distribution obtained by masking all visual tokens’ attention (setting their mask to False) while keeping token positions unchanged. The KL divergence measures the information gain due to the image; a higher score indicates the token’s prediction is more influenced by visual input. This anchoring assumes the masking yields a valid conditional distribution without altering positional structure. Evidence includes the formal definition in Section 3.1 and the theoretical link to causal influence. Failure could arise if attention masking is incorrectly implemented (e.g., modifying positional indices) or if KL estimation becomes unstable for extreme distributions.

### Mechanism 2: Threshold‑gated, mass‑conserving advantage reshaping amplifies signal, suppresses noise.
Raw TVD scores \(S\) are transformed via \(\tilde{S}=\log(1+S)\) and per‑sequence min‑max normalization to produce \(I_t \in [0,1]\). A piecewise weight \(\omega(I_t)\) is applied: if \(I_t < \tau\) then \(\omega = I_t/(\tau+\epsilon)\); else \(\omega = 1 + \beta\cdot(I_t-\tau)/(1-\tau+\epsilon)\). A sum‑preserving normalization \(\tilde{\omega}_t = \omega(I_t) \cdot T / \sum_{j=1}^{T} \omega(I_j)\) ensures the total reshaped advantage equals the original sequence length \(T\). This retains the zero‑mean property of advantages crucial for stable policy gradients. The assumption is that TVD correlates with visual importance and that a fixed threshold separates signal from noise. Ablations (Section 6.1) show removing either gating or normalization harms performance. Without mass conservation, the mean shift inflates variance quadratically (Appendix F), causing instability.

### Mechanism 3: Reduced gradient variance and prevention of training collapse.
By down‑weighting tokens with low TVD (often high‑variance linguistic priors) and up‑weighting visually‑dependent ones, PGPO curtails gradient noise that would otherwise accumulate from uniform advantage assignment. This mitigates the “variance explosion” typical of RLVR on LVLMs, where many tokens contribute little to the final answer but still receive full advantage. The mass‑conserving step ensures the overall scale of updates remains consistent with the original RLVR objective, avoiding sudden policy shifts that lead to collapse. Monitoring during training confirms lower per‑token gradient variance and a steady increase in average TVD, indicating the policy becomes more vision‑grounded.

## Foundational Learning
- **Token Visual Dependency (TVD)**: Measures how much a token’s prediction relies on the visual input via KL divergence between visual‑conditioned and text‑only distributions. *Why needed*: Enables fine‑grained credit assignment for multimodal reasoning. *Quick check*: Compute average TVD on a batch; non‑visual tokens (e.g., punctuation) should score near zero, while tokens referencing image content should score higher.
- **Attention Masking**: Technique to obtain a text‑only policy \(\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t)\) by setting the attention mask for all visual tokens to False while preserving positions. *Why needed*: Provides a clean counterfactual to isolate visual influence. *Quick check*: Run a forward pass with full masking and verify that early text tokens (before any visual context) produce the same logits as a standard forward pass on a text‑only prompt.
- **KL Divergence as Information Gain**: KL divergence quantifies the asymmetry between two distributions; here it measures how much the image reduces uncertainty about the next token. *Why needed*: Theoretical foundation for TVD’s interpretation as causal information gain. *Quick check*: For a known visual token (e.g., a color mentioned), TVD should be positive and higher than for identical text tokens unrelated to the image.
- **Policy Gradient with Token‑Level Advantages**: Extends standard RLVR by assigning distinct advantages to each generated token rather than a uniform score per sequence. *Why needed*: Aligns updates with actual contribution to the final verifiable reward. *Quick check*: Sum of token advantages in a sequence should equal the original sequence‑level advantage multiplied by sequence length after reshaping; this preserves the zero‑mean baseline.
- **Sum‑Preserving Normalization**: Rescales the reshaped weights \(\omega(I_t)\) so that their total equals \(T\), the sequence length. *Why needed*: Maintains the expected sum of advantages zero, preventing mean shift and variance explosion. *Quick check*: Compute \(\sum_{t} \tilde{\omega}_t\) for a batch; it should be exactly \(T\) within numerical tolerance.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:
Prompt (image I + text q) -> Rollout Generation (π_θ) -> Full sequence of tokens with rewards -> TVD Computation (second forward pass with visual masking) -> Advantage Reshaping (log, min‑max, threshold, sum‑preserve) -> Token‑level advantages -> PGPO loss update -> Updated policy π_θ

**Critical Path**:
1. Generate responses for a batch of prompts using current policy.
2. Compute per‑token rewards based on final answer correctness (verifiable).
3. Compute TVD scores for each token position via attention‑masked forward pass.
4. Transform TVD scores to normalized dependencies \(I_t\).
5. Apply threshold‑gated weighting and sum‑preserving normalization to obtain \(\tilde{\omega}_t\).
6. Reshape token advantages: \(\tilde{A}_{i,t} = A_{i,t} \cdot \tilde{\omega}_t\).
7. Compute PGPO loss (e.g., DAPO‑style) using \(\tilde{A}_{i,t}\) and take an optimization step.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- **Computational overhead vs. precision**: TVD requires an extra forward pass (or parallel masking) over the entire sequence, increasing GPU memory and time. The paper reports ~10% overhead; alternatives (e.g., learned predictors) could reduce cost but may introduce bias.
- **Threshold sensitivity vs. noise suppression**: A low τ boosts many tokens, risking amplification of noise; a high τ makes boosting too sparse, losing useful signal. The chosen τ=0.4 was selected via grid search on ViRL39K; other datasets may need retuning.
- **Mass conservation vs. signal intensity**: While sum‑preserving normalization stabilizes gradients, it also limits the maximum boost any token can receive (proportional to \(T\)). This tradeoff ensures no single token dominates the update but may cap the learning speed for highly visual steps.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Training collapse: sudden drop in accuracy, entropy explosion, or divergence after a few steps. This often indicates incorrect advantage normalization (sum not preserved) or extreme TVD values from unstable KL estimates.
- Poor TVD discrimination: average TVD for non‑visual tokens remains high, or the distribution of \(I_t\) is nearly uniform. Suggests flawed attention masking or that the model already relies heavily on vision at all positions (e.g., image captions).
- No performance gain over baselines: despite correct implementation, if token‑level weights are not meaningfully different from uniform, the threshold may be mis‑set or the TVD scores might be saturated.

**First Three Experiments**:
1. **TVD sanity check**: On a small set (e.g., 100 prompts), compute TVD scores and manually inspect: tokens directly describing image content should have higher scores than generic reasoning tokens. Also compare TVD for the same token across different images to confirm variability.
2. **Ablation of τ and β**: Train for a few updates with varying τ (0.2, 0.4, 0.6) and β (1.0, 2.0, 3.0). Track validation accuracy and distribution of \(\tilde{\omega}_t\) to identify a robust region. Expect performance drop when τ is too high (too few boosted tokens) or too low (noise dominates).
3. **Gradient variance measurement**: During training, log per‑token gradient norms (or variance across the batch) before and after reshaping. Expect a significant reduction in variance without a shift in the mean advantage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Adaptive threshold and boosting**: Can τ and β be automatically tuned during training based on batch statistics instead of fixed values? The paper notes current hyperparameters may need re‑tuning for new datasets or architectures.
- **Scalability to larger models**: Does PGPO’s effectiveness hold for LVLMs beyond 7B parameters (e.g., 13B, 32B)? The experiments were limited to Qwen2.5‑VL‑3B/7B; larger models could exhibit different TVD distributions or instability.
- **Efficient TVD estimation**: Can the ~10% computational overhead from the second forward pass be reduced, perhaps via a learned surrogate predictor? The current method uses Monte‑Carlo KL which is still expensive.
- **Applicability to dense‑dependency tasks**: How does PGPO perform on tasks where visual dependency is spread across many tokens (e.g., detailed scene description)? The threshold‑gated mechanism assumes sparsity, which may not hold for all multimodal tasks.

## Limitations
- **Implementation fidelity**: The paper does not provide exhaustive details on attention masking and KL estimation; subtle bugs (e.g., altering positional structure) could invalidate TVD scores.
- **Generalization gap**: Results are shown only on Qwen2.5‑VL models and a specific reasoning dataset (ViRL39K); unknown if gains transfer to other LVLM architectures (LLaVA, InternVL) or domains (e.g., captioning, detection).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Fixed τ=0.4 and β=2.0 might be dataset‑specific; the paper lacks extensive sensitivity analysis beyond the reported grid search.

## Confidence
- **TVD metric** – *Medium*: The KL‑based formulation is theoretically sound, but practical stability (e.g., numerical issues, masking correctness) is not thoroughly validated in the paper.
- **Threshold‑gated reshaping** – *Medium*: Mathematical derivation appears correct, yet the piecewise function’s sensitivity to τ/β is underexplored; performance may drop with small changes.
- **18.7% accuracy gain** – *Low*: The evaluation is limited to a single model family and a narrow set of benchmarks; cross‑architecture or out‑of‑distribution robustness remains unknown.

## Next Checks
1. **TVD sanity validation**: Compute average TVD for known non‑visual tokens (e.g., “,”, “.”, “\boxed{}”) on a held‑out set; expect values close to zero. Significant deviation would indicate masking or estimator issues.
2. **Hyperparameter stress test**: Ablate τ in [0.2, 0.6] and β in [1.0, 3.0] on a smaller model (e.g., Qwen2‑VL‑2B) using two benchmarks. Plot validation accuracy vs. τ/β to identify robustness windows and potential instability regions.
3. **Overhead and gradient diagnostics**: Measure per‑step wall‑clock time increase from the extra TVD forward pass; aim for ≤15% overhead. Also log per‑token gradient variance before/after reshaping; expect a substantial reduction without a shift in the mean advantage.
