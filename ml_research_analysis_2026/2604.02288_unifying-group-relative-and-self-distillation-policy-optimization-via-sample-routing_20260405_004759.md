# Unifying Group-Relative and Self-Distillation Policy Optimization via Sample Routing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.02288
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02288
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: SRPO on Qwen3-8B achieves 77.4% average accuracy across five benchmarks, improving 3.4% over GRPO and 6.3% over SDPO.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning for large language models with verifiable rewards faces a trade‑off: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) provides stable but coarse token‑level credit assignment, while Self‑Distillation Policy Optimization (SDPO) offers dense logit‑level supervision that accelerates early learning but suffers late‑stage collapse. Sample‑Routed Policy Optimization (SRPO) unifies these approaches by dynamically routing each rollout: correct rollouts are optimized with GRPO’s reward‑aligned reinforcement, while incorrect rollouts with an available correct sibling are sent to an SDPO branch with entropy‑aware token weighting. On Qwen3‑4B and Qwen3‑8B across five benchmarks, SRPO delivers faster early improvement, sustained long‑horizon stability, and higher peak accuracy—raising the five‑benchmark average on Qwen3‑8B by 3.4% over GRPO and 6.3% over SDPO to 77.4%—while also producing moderate response lengths and lowering per‑step compute cost by up to 17.2%.

## Method Summary
SRPO operates in an on‑policy RL loop. For each prompt, the current policy generates multiple rollouts. A verifiable reward function produces a scalar score; a rollout is marked correct if its reward meets a threshold. Teacher information is constructed by selecting a correct sibling rollout (if any) and concatenating it with the original prompt to form a privileged context. The correctness router then dispatches each rollout: correct ones go to the GRPO branch, which computes a group‑relative advantage (normalized across rollouts for the same prompt) and applies a PPO‑style clipped update uniformly to all tokens; incorrect ones with available teacher go to the SDPO branch, which performs on‑policy distillation—minimizing Jensen‑Shannon divergence between the student’s token distribution and the teacher’s (stop‑graded) distribution, with each token’s loss weighted by the teacher’s entropy (β = 1) to down‑weight unreliable targets. The total loss is the sum of routed token losses normalized by the number of tokens in each branch, and the policy is updated with AdamW (lr = 5e‑6). Training uses the verl framework with SGLang rollouts on 8×H20 GPUs, validating with 16 rollouts per prompt at temperature 0.6.

## Key Results
- SRPO on Qwen3‑8B reaches 77.4% average accuracy across the five benchmarks, exceeding GRPO by 3.4% and SDPO by 6.3%.
- Training curves show SRPO matches SDPO’s rapid early improvement while maintaining GRPO‑like long‑horizon stability, avoiding the late‑stage collapse seen in SDPO.
- SRPO produces moderate response lengths and reduces per‑step compute time by up to 17.2% relative to extended training baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Sample Routing Combines Complementary Strengths
**Claim:** Routing samples based on correctness and teacher availability combines the strengths of GRPO and SDPO while avoiding their individual failure modes.  
**Mechanism:** A binary mask decides the branch: correct rollouts (reward ≥ threshold) go to GRPO, where a group‑relative sequence‑level advantage reinforces the whole trajectory; incorrect rollouts are sent to SDPO only if a correct sibling exists to provide teacher context (feedback‑conditioned self‑teacher). The SDPO branch then minimizes token‑level divergence between student and teacher distributions along that trajectory.  
**Core assumption:** Correct rollouts are already aligned with the reward objective and need only reward‑based reinforcement; incorrect rollouts benefit from dense correction when a reliable teacher is available.  
**Evidence:** Abstract and Section 3.1 define the routing mask; ablation studies show that advantage‑level mixing degrades later performance.  
**Break condition:** If teacher information is unavailable for many incorrect rollouts, they fall back to GRPO, which may not correct local errors effectively.

### Mechanism 2: Entropy‑Weighted Distillation Stabilizes SDPO
**Claim:** Entropy‑aware dynamic weighting in the SDPO branch reduces the impact of noisy teacher targets, especially later in training when the self‑teacher’s predictions become uncertain.  
**Mechanism:** For each token in an SDPO‑routed rollout, compute teacher distribution entropy H_i,t. The token‑level loss is multiplied by a normalized weight w_i,t = exp(−β H_i,t) / average over all SDPO tokens (β = 1). High‑entropy tokens receive lower weight; low‑entropy tokens higher weight.  
**Core assumption:** Teacher entropy inversely correlates with prediction reliability; suppressing high‑entropy contributions mitigates degradation of the self‑teacher signal.  
**Evidence:** Abstract; Section 3.2 derivation; ablations show dynamic weighting improves late‑stage performance.  
**Break condition:** β too low makes weighting ineffective; β too high may over‑fit to potentially biased confident tokens.

### Mechanism 3: Automatic Branch Balance During Training
**Claim:** The sample composition routed to each branch adapts automatically: early on many incorrect rollouts activate SDPO for rapid improvement; later, as correctness increases, GRPO dominates, anchoring optimization to the reward signal and preventing collapse.  
**Mechanism:** Routing masks depend on correctness c_i and teacher availability m_i. As the policy improves, c_i rises, so the GRPO fraction (1 − (1−c_i)m_i) increases and the SDPO fraction decreases.  
**Core assumption:** Early training needs dense token‑level guidance; later training needs stable reward‑aligned updates.  
**Evidence:** Abstract; Section 4.2 and Figure 3 (SDPO early lead but collapse; SRPO continues improving); Section 4.3 ablation (Advantage Mix peaking then declining).  
**Break condition:** If policy improvement stalls early, the SDPO fraction remains high, risking late‑stage instability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Estimates advantages by normalizing rewards across multiple rollouts for the same prompt (no critic). The same advantage is broadcast to all tokens, giving stable but coarse credit assignment. *Why needed:* Forms the reward‑only branch for correct samples. *Quick check:* How does GRPO estimate advantages without a critic, and why is the same advantage broadcast to all tokens in a rollout?
- **Self‑Distillation Policy Optimization (SDPO):** Uses a self‑teacher (conditioned on a correct sibling rollout) to produce token‑level soft targets; minimizes KL divergence between student and teacher distributions. It accelerates early learning but suffers late‑stage collapse due to ambiguous updates on already correct samples and degrading teacher quality. *Why needed:* Provides dense correction for incorrect samples. *Quick check:* What is the role of the teacher information (e.g., a correct sibling rollout) in SDPO, and why might applying SDPO to already‑correct samples be harmful?
- **On‑policy sampling and teacher construction:** Rollouts are sampled from the current policy; teacher is built by finding a correct sibling rollout and concatenating it with the original prompt. This ensures the teacher context is on‑policy and relevant. *Why needed:* Determines teacher availability and the self‑teacher’s conditioning. *Quick check:* In SRPO, when is teacher information considered “available” for an incorrect rollout, and why might a correct rollout not use SDPO even if a sibling exists?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Rollout generator → Reward evaluator → Teacher info builder → Correctness router → (GRPO branch and SDPO branch) → Loss aggregator → Parameter update.

**Critical path:**  
Prompt → Rollouts → Reward → Teacher info → Routing → Branch loss computation → Aggregation → Parameter update.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Sample routing (sample‑level) vs. advantage‑level mixing: Routing avoids propagating noisy SDPO advantages into correct samples, preserving long‑term stability; mixing may cause early gains but later degradation.  
- Dynamic weighting adds minimal compute but requires tuning β; overly aggressive weighting may discard useful signals.  
- Teacher construction relies on existence of correct siblings; if none, incorrect rollouts fall back to GRPO, which may be less effective at correcting local errors.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Sudden performance drop after initial gains (SDPO collapse).  
- Excessive verbosity (common in GRPO).  
- Abnormal routing fractions (e.g., nearly all samples to one branch) indicating routing logic errors.  
- Unstable training or lack of late improvement (possible mis‑implementation of entropy weighting).  
- Higher than expected per‑step compute cost (teacher forward passes not skipped for GRPO‑routed samples).

**First 3 experiments (to validate the design):**  
1. **Training curve comparison:** Plot avg@16 over wall‑clock time for GRPO, SDPO, and SRPO on a representative benchmark (e.g., Chemistry). Expect SRPO to match SDPO’s early rise and continue improving while SDPO collapses and GRPO lags.  
2. **Ablation – dynamic weighting:** Train SRPO without entropy weighting (β = 0) under identical conditions. Expect the gap to widen later in training, confirming the weighting’s stabilizing role.  
3. **Ablation – routing strategy:** Replace sample routing with an “Advantage Mix” that linearly combines GRPO and SDPO advantages per token. Expect Advantage Mix to be competitive early but fall behind after several hours, demonstrating the robustness of sample‑level routing.

## Next Checks
1. **Routing fraction monitoring:** Log per‑batch proportions of samples sent to SDPO vs. GRPO. SRPO should show an early SDPO fraction (~30–40%) that declines smoothly; a flat or rising fraction indicates routing errors or stalled policy improvement.
2. **Entropy weighting ablation:** Train SRPO without entropy weighting (β = 0) under identical conditions. Late‑stage performance should drop relative to full SRPO, confirming the weighting’s stabilizing role.
3. **Teacher call audit:** Count teacher forward passes per training step. SRPO must perform teacher inference only on SDPO‑routed tokens, yielding fewer calls than SDPO (teacher on all incorrect) and comparable to GRPO (no teacher). Verify that the per‑step time reduction matches the claimed ~17.2% on the same hardware.
