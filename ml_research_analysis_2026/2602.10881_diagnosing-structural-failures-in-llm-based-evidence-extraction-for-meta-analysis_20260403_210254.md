# Diagnosing Structural Failures in LLM-Based Evidence Extraction for Meta-Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10881
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10881
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Current LLMs exhibit near-zero reliability in extracting full meta-analytic association tuples, with severe degradation in multi-property binding and long-context scenarios.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a diagnostic framework to evaluate whether large language models (LLMs) can meet the precise structural demands of evidence extraction for meta-analysis. Testing two state-of-the-art models (GPT-5.2, Qwen3-VL) across five scientific domains using schema-constrained queries, the study finds that while LLMs achieve moderate performance on single-property extraction, their performance collapses when required to bind multiple elements—such as variables, roles, methods, and effect sizes—into coherent tuples. Failures are exacerbated in long-context, multi-document settings and lead to catastrophic error propagation in downstream aggregation tasks. The findings indicate that current LLM architectures lack the relational binding fidelity and numerical grounding necessary for automated, reliable meta-analysis.

## Method Summary
The study builds a diagnostic framework around a generalized meta-analytic evidence schema (variables, roles, statistical methods, effect sizes, conditions). Five domain-specific corpora (Civil Engineering, Medical, Agricultural, Earth & Environmental, Social Sciences) were sourced, with PDFs converted to structured markdown using MinerU 2.5. Two leading LLMs were evaluated under zero-shot/few-shot prompting with decoding temperature 0.1. Input regimes compared **Per-Paper** (individual documents) versus **Global** (concatenated domain corpora). A tiered query suite mapped to extraction tasks of increasing complexity: isolated property (L1/O1/M1), relational binding (L2/O2/M2), and derived statistical aggregation (C/OC). Outputs were parsed into tuples and evaluated via exact match with document attribution; borderline cases were adjudicated by an LLM judge. Metrics are tuple-level precision, recall, and F1.

## Key Results
- Single-property extraction (L1/O1/M1) yields moderate F1 (e.g., ~0.62 for variable identification).
- Multi-property binding tasks (L2/O2/M2) show a sharp performance drop; highest-arity task (𝑖, 𝐼𝑉, 𝐷𝑉, 𝐴, 𝐶, 𝐸) approaches F1 ~0.00.
- Long-context Global input significantly degrades performance relative to Per-Paper (e.g., Qwen3-VL Overall: 0.35 → 0.18).
- Downstream aggregation queries (C/OC) fail with high frequency (>60% failure for mean sample size) due to error amplification from upstream omissions or misattributions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
LLMs maintain moderate reliability for extracting isolated schema properties (atoms) but performance degrades sharply when tasks require stable binding between multiple properties (e.g., linking an independent variable, dependent variable, statistical method, and effect size). The core limitation is the models' reliance on distributed, context-bound representations that fail to preserve consistent co-references across entities and attributes when compositional demands exceed a threshold, particularly over longer reasoning spans. Evidence includes steep F1 drops from L1 to L2 tasks and near-zero F1 on the most complex binding tuples.

### Mechanism 2
Long-context, multi-document input regimes induce structural failures beyond those seen in per-document extraction, even within token limits. Models exhibit middle-context forgetting and interference across documents, leading to errors like cross-analysis binding drift and instance compression. The Global input forces integration across heterogeneous, full-corpora contexts without discrete boundary signals, overwhelming the attention mechanism's ability to maintain separate analytical units. Evidence includes systematically lower F1 in the Global regime and qualitative identification of binding drift in dense result sections.

### Mechanism 3
Meta-analytic aggregation queries have zero tolerance for incomplete or misattributed upstream tuples. A single upstream error—such as a missing effect size or a variable role swap—propagates into the aggregation, causing incorrect corpus-level statistics even if most extractions are correct. The mechanism is the exhaustive, perfectly-attributed requirement of derived statistics. Evidence shows high failure rates on aggregation tasks (e.g., O_C_Q2) despite non-zero upstream recall, directly tracing errors to upstream omissions or misbindings.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Schema-grounded evaluation
  - **Why needed here:** Using a fixed, generalized schema exposes structural weaknesses (e.g., missing bindings) that open-ended QA metrics would not capture.
  - **Quick check question:** How does evaluating against a fixed schema (vs. ad-hoc QA) help isolate structural weaknesses in LLM outputs?

- **Concept:** Relational binding in multi-entity extraction
  - **Why needed here:** Meta-analysis requires that variables, roles, methods, and effect sizes be correctly linked within the same analytical statement; the paper demonstrates LLMs struggle as tuple arity increases.
  - **Quick check question:** Why does adding a statistical method to a variable pair cause a performance drop beyond simple entity recognition?

- **Concept:** Numerical grounding
  - **Why needed here:** Numbers (sample size, effect size) must be correctly associated with specific variables and conditions; the paper identifies "numeric misattribution" as a key failure in dense sections.
  - **Quick check question:** What distinguishes a numeric reasoning error from a binding error in the context of effect-size extraction?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** PDF Conversion (MinerU 2.5) -> Query Suite (L1/L2/C patterns) -> LLM Inference (GPT-5.2, Qwen3-VL) -> Tuple Parsing & Attribution -> Evaluation (Exact match + LLM judge)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Convert all domain PDFs to structured markdown.
  2. For each model/input regime, run the full query suite across all documents.
  3. Parse model outputs into (document-attributed) tuples.
  4. Compute tuple-level P/R/F1 against gold annotations; use LLM judge for borderline semantic matches.
  5. Aggregate metrics by task group (O1/O2/OC/M1/M2/MC), domain, and input regime.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Per-paper vs. Global:* Per-paper yields higher F1 but requires model-driven aggregation; Global simulates end-to-end long-context use but reveals fragility.
  - *Schema generality vs. specificity:* Minimal cross-domain schema enables systematic comparison but may lose domain nuance.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Role reversal (IV/DV swapped)
  - Cross-analysis binding drift (correct entities paired with wrong method/effect from another analysis in same paper)
  - Instance compression (recall drops in papers with many gold tuples)
  - Numeric misattribution (effect/sample size assigned to wrong variable/condition)
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Execute all L1 atom extraction queries to baseline entity recognition across domains.
  2. Progress to L2 binding queries (e.g., M_L2_Q5 for `(i, IV, DV, A)`), measure F1 drop from M1, and inspect error types.
  3. Compare Per-Paper vs. Global for highest-arity task (M_L2_Q6), quantifying regime-induced degradation and identifying cross-document binding failures.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can LLM architectures be modified to treat multi-entity relational binding as a first-class objective during extraction?
  - **Basis in paper:** The conclusion calls for "schema-aware extraction that treats binding as a first-class objective" to address systematic structural breakdowns in high-arity tasks.
  - **Why unresolved:** Current models achieve moderate single-property extraction but near-zero full-tuple reliability, indicating binding is not natively supported and degrades sharply with relational complexity.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement binding-focused mechanisms (e.g., structured decoding constraints) and evaluate using the paper's framework, measuring improvements on tasks like M_L2_Q6.

- **Question 2:** Why do long-context, multi-document inputs exacerbate binding failures, and can explicit document-scope representations mitigate this degradation?
  - **Basis in paper:** Results show Global input underperforms Per-Paper, with identified failures like "cross-analysis binding drift" in dense documents.
  - **Why unresolved:** The phenomenon is identified but not isolated; causes could be attention span limits, scope boundary ambiguity, or interference between analytical units.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments varying document density, adding explicit scope delimiters, and analyzing attention distributions to determine if scope confusion drives drift.

- **Question 3:** Can verification layers that enforce consistency between extracted variables, roles, methods, and numeric values prevent error amplification in downstream aggregation?
  - **Basis in paper:** The conclusion proposes "verification layers" citing how "downstream aggregation amplifies even minor upstream errors."
  - **Why unresolved:** Derived queries fail even with seemingly sufficient upstream extraction, showing partial correctness is inadequate; no current system integrates such checks.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Build a post-extraction validator that cross-checks tuples against schema constraints and re-run C-queries, expecting reduced corpus-level error rates.

## Limitations
- Model coverage limited to GPT-5.2 and Qwen3-VL; other architectures may differ.
- Fixed cross-domain schema may miss domain-specific evidence nuances.
- Dependence on MinerU 2.5 for PDF conversion and an LLM judge introduces tool-specific biases.
- Only zero-shot/few-shot prompting evaluated; fine-tuned or tool-augmented variants not tested.

## Confidence
- **Sharp binding degradation:** High – steep, consistent F1 drops from L1→L2 across models/domains with identifiable failure modes.
- **Long-context fragility:** Medium – Global regime mixes context length and document heterogeneity; causal attribution to length alone is uncertain.
- **Aggregation vulnerability:** High – direct traceability from upstream errors to derived query failures demonstrates zero-tolerance requirement.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate with newer LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5) to assess if architectural advances improve binding fidelity.
2. Isolate context-length effects by constructing global inputs with controlled document similarity and measuring binding drift vs. token count.
3. Implement retrieval-augmented prompting (per-document retrieval followed by aggregation) to test mitigation of cross-document binding errors.
