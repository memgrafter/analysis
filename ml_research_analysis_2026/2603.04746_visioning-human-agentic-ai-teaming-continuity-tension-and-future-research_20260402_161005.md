# Visioning Human-Agentic AI Teaming: Continuity, Tension, and Future Research

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.04746
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04746
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Extends Team Situation Awareness theory to human-agentic AI, identifying projection congruence (aligned futures and value weightings) as critical for sustained alignment and warning that standard SA dynamics can backfire under open-ended agency.

## Executive Summary
The paper examines how the rise of agentic AI—systems capable of open-ended action trajectories, generative outputs, and evolving objectives—challenges established human-AI teaming (HAT) frameworks. It extends Team Situation Awareness (SA) theory through a continuity-tension lens: continuity retains the perception-comprehension-projection structure while reconceptualizing human and AI awareness for open-ended agency; tension interrogates whether traditional dynamic processes (relational, learning, control) still stabilize teaming. Three novel forms of uncertainty are identified—trajectory, epistemic, and regime—and the analysis shows that iterative updating and shared awareness can actually amplify divergence or mask oversight loss. The authors argue that sustained alignment depends on projection congruence, i.e., shared anticipation of future states and consistent value weighting. The work contributes a theoretical reframing and a research agenda of over 15 questions to guide future studies on governance, authority, and incentive design for increasingly autonomous AI.

## Method Summary
The study employs a theoretical, conceptual methodology. It reviews and synthesizes literature on Team SA, human-AI collaboration, and agency, then reconstructs SA theory to accommodate open-ended agentic behaviors. The authors define continuity (retaining SA's three-level structure) and tension (examining the fragility of traditional stabilizing processes). No empirical data or algorithmic implementation is presented; instead, the paper builds a logical argument identifying mechanisms, backfire risks, and open research challenges. Reproduction would require operationalizing SA levels for both human and AI in a specific domain, designing experiments that induce open-ended uncertainties, and measuring longitudinal alignment beyond snapshot outputs.

## Key Results
- Extends Team SA with a continuity-tension framework, revealing three uncertainty types (trajectory, epistemic, regime) that complicate shared awareness.
- Demonstrates that standard SA dynamic processes (e.g., iterative updating, trust-based reliance) can backfire, leading to amplified divergence or oversight decoupling even when moment-to-moment agreement appears high.
- Positions projection congruence—the alignment of anticipated futures and value weightings—as the pivotal mechanism for sustained alignment and formulates a research agenda of 15+ questions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Diagnostic Human SA Shift
**Claim:** Human Situation Awareness must shift from evaluating discrete outputs to diagnosing structural trajectory shifts and implicit commitments in open-ended action.  
**Mechanism:** Agentic AI generates multi-step, revisable plans. Early intermediate outputs embed path-dependent constraints that shape later feasible actions. Human perception (Level 1 SA) must detect these inflection points and interpret implicit commitments rather than just notice final outputs. Comprehension (Level 2) involves constructing a stable mental model of the evolving task structure, not just validating individual steps. This diagnostic burden intensifies cognitive load.  
**Core assumption:** Human actors can and will interpret trajectory-level cues, not just task-level outputs, to maintain shared team awareness.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract states alignment cannot be secured through agreement on bounded outputs; it must be continuously sustained as plans unfold. Section 3.1.1 describes perception becoming diagnostic rather than event-based. Neighboring papers (e.g., ATSA) discuss SA frameworks but not the diagnostic interpretation of generative trajectory commitments, marking this as a novel extension.  
**Break condition:** If humans consistently fail to detect or misinterpret trajectory inflection points, shared SA degrades silently until a major divergence occurs, undermining coordination despite apparent momentary agreement.

### Mechanism 2: Projection Congruence
**Claim:** Sustained alignment depends on Projection Congruence—shared anticipation of future states and aligned value-weighting under shifting objectives—not just momentary agreement on current task states.  
**Mechanism:** Agentic AI projects multiple futures, revises plans, and rebalances objectives dynamically. Alignment at Levels 1 and 2 is insufficient if humans and AI anticipate different future trajectories or prioritize competing values differently. Projection (Level 3 SA) becomes regime-level: it must account for the stability of governing logics themselves, not just next steps.  
**Core assumption:** Humans and AI can develop sufficiently congruent projections about long-horizon futures and value trade-offs to sustain coordination.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract identifies projection congruence as critical. Section 3.1.2 defines it as the degree to which human and AI anticipate comparable future trajectories and assign similar weightings to competing objectives over time. Direct evidence is weak; neighboring literature focuses on SA frameworks and trust but not projection congruence as a pivot for open-ended alignment.  
**Break condition:** Divergent projections lead to coordinated action that is locally efficient but globally misaligned with overarching team goals or stakeholder values, especially when objectives evolve.

### Mechanism 3: Backfire of Traditional Dynamics
**Claim:** Under open-ended agency, traditional dynamic processes (relational interaction, cognitive learning, coordination/control) can backfire, amplifying divergence, creating oversight decoupling, and fracturing legitimacy despite shared SA.  
**Mechanism:**  
- Relational: Fluent generative outputs boost initial trust/legitimacy but create fragility when epistemic ambiguity surfaces. Planning style differences cause initiative‑asymmetry.  
- Learning: Iterative updating may amplify initial misinterpretations (path‑dependent lock‑in) or stabilize locally coherent but globally misaligned models (feedback endogeneity). Rapid convergence may mask representational drift.  
- Coordination/Control: Coherent outputs can mask policy drift (oversight decoupling). Authority may silently expand across task stages. Accountability blurs as objectives evolve.  
**Core assumption:** The stabilizing dynamics of Team SA (iterative updating builds shared models, trust sustains reliance, clear roles enable control) are compromised when trajectories, representations, and regimes are open‑ended.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract notes Team SA’s dynamic processes can backfire—e.g., iterative updating may amplify rather than correct divergence, and shared awareness may coexist with oversight loss. Section 3.2 discusses relational fragility when planning styles diverge. Section 3.4 points out that shared SA at the output level may coexist with substantive loss of control at the policy level, defining oversight decoupling.  
**Break condition:** Backfire effects become systemic, leading to persistent misalignment, eroded human oversight capacity, and ambiguous accountability despite high scores on traditional SA or trust metrics.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Diagnostic Human SA (Levels 1-3)**  
  Why needed: Pre-existing research treats human engagement with AI as responding to discrete outputs. Agentic AI requires interpreting evolving, structural cues and generative representations. Engineers must design interfaces and metrics that support this shift.  
  Quick check: *Given an agentic AI's multi‑step plan that revises its subgoals after step 2, what specific interface cues or behavioral signals should a human monitor at Level 1 to detect a meaningful trajectory shift versus a routine refinement?*

- **Concept: AI SA Operationalization**  
  Why needed: Alignment assessment is impossible without visibility into the AI's encoded cues, internal task model, and projections. Engineers must instrument agentic systems to emit interpretable artifacts of their awareness states.  
  Quick check: *What observable artifact (e.g., attention heatmap, subgoal list, confidence distribution) best reveals an agentic AI's inferred human intent at Level 2 comprehensions, and how would you validate that artifact corresponds to the true internal representation?*

- **Concept: Projection Congruence**  
  Why needed: This is the proposed pivot for sustained alignment. It moves beyond evaluating current understanding to evaluating the compatibility of anticipated futures and value weightings. Measurement and design must target this.  
  Quick check: *How would you experimentally disentangle whether a human and agentic AI disagree on the next action because of a factual misunderstanding (Level 2) versus a difference in acceptable risk/value trade‑offs for projected futures (Level 3)?*

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:
AI-Sensing Module -> Alignment Assessment Engine -> Governance & Control Layer
Human-Awareness Module <-> Alignment Assessment Engine
Human-Awareness Module -> Governance & Control Layer

Critical path:
1. Instrument the agentic system to emit structured SA artifacts (L1‑L3). Without this, all downstream alignment assessment is inferential and weak.
2. Design human interfaces that transform raw trajectory/planning data into diagnostic cues (e.g., highlighting implicit commitment points, showing branching futures with value trade‑offs).
3. Build longitudinal experiments that track projection congruence and oversight decoupling across multiple interaction cycles, not just snapshot performance.
4. Prototype staged control checkpoints that trigger based on trajectory or regime changes, not just time/output.

Design tradeoffs:
- Transparency vs. Performance: Deep policy visibility (e.g., full reasoning trace) may overwhelm humans or slow the agent; selective, salient transparency is needed.
- Intervention Frequency vs. Autonomy Value: Too many staged checkpoints cripple the benefits of open‑ended agency; thresholds must be dynamic and task‑sensitive.
- Metric Stability vs. Regime Sensitivity: SA metrics must be stable enough to measure but sensitive to shifts in objectives/representations that matter.

Failure signatures:
1. Trajectory Failure: Humans miss implicit commitment points; agentic system makes irreversible path choices humans would have opposed.
2. Epistemic Failure: High output fluency correlates with increasing gap between surface coherence and underlying factual/valuational grounding.
3. Regime Failure: Projection congruence scores drop abruptly after an objective reweighting or capability update, even if task comprehension scores remain stable.

First 3 experiments:
1. Trajectory Diagnosis Study: Compare human ability to detect and correctly interpret simulated "implicit commitment points" in agentic plans (e.g., a subgoal that narrows future options) vs. traditional output errors. Measure impact on subsequent trust and intervention timing.
2. Projection Congruence Calibration: Develop a metric (e.g., distributional overlap on future states + value‑weighting similarity). Have humans and state‑of‑the‑art agentic systems (e.g., Claude with planning) forecast on a multi‑objective, non‑stationary task. Correlate congruence scores with long‑horizon team performance and coordination costs.
3. Oversight Decoupling Demo: Create a high‑dimensional planning task where the agentic system's *output* quality remains high but its *internal policy* (e.g., value prioritization) drifts due to reinforcement learning or prompt changes. Test if human supervisors detect policy drift and maintain control, given access to either (a) only outputs, (b) outputs + simple policy summary, or (c) full policy trace.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** Quantifying projection congruence across branching futures and shifting priorities. Current SA metrics ignore future alignment; generative AI makes measurement harder. Validated metrics that correlate congruence with sustained performance and oversight are needed.
- **Open Question 2:** Operationalizing AI situation awareness (L1‑L3) to be interpretable and measurable. Without observability into AI's internal representations, shared awareness may be superficial. Methodologies that produce structured, interpretable artifacts of AI's perception, comprehension, and projection are required.
- **Open Question 3:** Conditions under which iterative human‑AI interaction leads to convergence versus amplification of misalignments. Team SA assumes iterative updating strengthens alignment, but open‑ended agency can cause asynchronous adaptation and path dependence that amplify early errors. Longitudinal experiments varying trajectory openness and revision frequency are needed to identify boundary conditions.
- **Open Question 4:** Designing decision rights, intervention checkpoints, and incentive structures to maintain human oversight as agentic autonomy expands. Shared SA alone may not prevent authority drift or accountability diffusion when AI policies evolve opaquely. Governance frameworks tested in simulated or field settings are needed to validate staged control and transparency mechanisms.

## Limitations
- AI SA operationalization remains unknown; there is no validated method to extract or approximate an agentic AI's internal Level 1/Level 2 representations for congruence comparison.
- Regime uncertainty (evolving objectives) cannot yet be reliably induced and isolated in laboratory experiments, limiting study of longitudinal projection congruence.
- The continuity‑tension framework and projection congruence are highly novel (zero citations) with limited empirical grounding beyond theoretical inference.

## Confidence
- Continuity (SA structure retention): Medium
- Tension (dynamic processes backfiring): Medium‑Low
- Projection congruence (pivot for alignment): Low
