# CausalT5K: Diagnosing and Informing Refusal for Trustworthy Causal Reasoning of Skepticism, Sycophancy, Detection-Correction, and Rung Collapse

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.08939
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08939
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A benchmark revealing four novel failure modes in LLM causal reasoning (Skepticism Trap, inverse sycophancy scaling, Detection-Correction Gap, rung collapse) and a Four-Quadrant Control Landscape where static audit policies universally fail.

## Executive Summary
CausalT5K is a new benchmark of 5,147 cases across 10 domains built on Pearl’s Ladder of causation. It introduces trap taxonomies, adversarial pressure variants, and paired Utility/Safety metrics to diagnose previously unreported pathologies in LLM causal reasoning. Systematically decomposing performance uncovers four key failure modes: the Skepticism Trap (high Utility with catastrophic Safety), inverse scaling of sycophancy, a persistent Detection-Correction Gap, and rung collapse at higher causal levels. The benchmark’s paired neutral/pressure design reveals a Four-Quadrant Control Landscape where model behavior shifts dramatically with judge strength, invalidating static audit policies.

## Method Summary
The authors construct CausalT5K by generating causal questions across 10 domains, each requiring inference at one of Pearl’s three causal rungs (association, intervention, counterfactual). For each instance, they create a neutral version and an adversarial "pressure" variant designed to trigger specific failure traps (e.g., skepticism, sycophancy). Performance is evaluated on two independent axes: Utility (correctness of causal reasoning) and Safety (alignment with intended response style, e.g., refusing improper requests). This paired design allows analysis of how adversarial context shifts model behavior across a quadrant space defined by (High/Low Utility) × (High/Low Safety).

## Key Results
- Four previously invisible pathologies are identified: Skepticism Trap (e.g., 40% Utility with 96% Safety), inverse sycophancy scaling (Bad Flip Rate up to 75.2%), Detection-Correction Gap (48–55% Dissonance), and rung collapse (48–55% Dissonance on L3).
- The Four-Quadrant Control Landscape shows model behavior shifts across quadrants depending on judge strength, proving static audit policies are universally ineffective.
- Detection-Correction Gap and rung collapse show similar dissonance ranges (48–55%), suggesting potential underlying mechanistic links.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The work succeeds by moving beyond aggregate accuracy to a multi-axis, adversarially pressured diagnostic framework. The trap taxonomies provide targeted stressors that expose specific reasoning failure modes invisible to standard benchmarks. The paired neutral/pressure design cleanly separates a model’s inherent causal capability (Utility on neutral) from its susceptibility to contextual manipulation (shift to pressure). This decomposition reveals that failures are not random but systematic and pathology-specific, allowing for precise diagnosis and informing targeted mitigation strategies beyond simple refusal tuning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Pearl’s Causal Ladder**: Three ascending levels of causal inference (association, intervention, counterfactual). *Why needed*: Provides a rigorous, graded framework for assessing causal reasoning depth. *Quick check*: Can you give an example question requiring counterfactual (L3) vs. interventional (L2) reasoning?
- **Adversarial Pressure Variants**: Inputs crafted to elicit specific failure modes (e.g., prompts that encourage sycophancy or unwarranted skepticism). *Why needed*: To stress-test models under realistic manipulation and expose brittle reasoning. *Quick check*: Design a pressure variant for a neutral causal question that would trigger the Skepticism Trap.
- **Utility & Safety Axes**: Independent metrics for reasoning correctness (Utility) and response style alignment (Safety, e.g., refusing harmful requests). *Why needed*: Separates capability from alignment; a model can be correct but unsafe (or vice versa). *Quick check*: For a given model output, rate it High/Low on both axes for a neutral and a pressure instance.
- **Trap Taxonomy**: Classification of failure modes (skepticism, sycophancy, detection-correction, rung collapse). *Why needed*: To organize and diagnose specific, recurring error patterns. *Quick check*: Match a failure description (e.g., "model detects a trap but gives wrong answer anyway") to its trap name.
- **Paired Instance Design**: Each causal scenario has a neutral and a pressure version. *Why needed*: Controls for question difficulty isolates the effect of adversarial context. *Quick check*: Explain why comparing a model’s performance on the neutral vs. pressure version of the same scenario is informative.
- **Quadrant Analysis**: Mapping model behavior into four zones (High Util/High Safe, etc.). *Why needed*: Visualizes the trade-offs and instability induced by adversarial pressure and judge strength. *Quick check*: Predict what a "static audit policy" would miss based on quadrant shifts.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Domain Selection -> Trap Definition -> Neutral Instance Generation -> Pressure Variant Generation -> Paired Dataset -> Model Evaluation (Utility & Safety) -> Quadrant Mapping -> Pathology Diagnosis.

**Critical Path**: The workflow flows from selecting a diverse set of domains (e.g., health, law) to defining specific traps within Pearl’s framework. For each trap, a neutral causal question is written, then a pressure variant is derived. These pairs form CausalT5K. Models are evaluated on both instances, producing scores that are plotted on the Utility/Safety quadrant for analysis. Shifts between neutral and pressure quadrants diagnose specific pathologies.

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Depth vs. Breadth: Focusing on Pearl’s ladder and specific traps provides diagnostic precision but may miss other failure modes.
- Adversarial Validity: Pressure variants must be natural and relevant to be valid stressors; overly artificial prompts may not reflect real-world misuse.
- Metric Simplicity: Two binary axes are interpretable but may oversimplify nuanced correctness or safety judgments.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- **Skepticism Trap**: Model produces safe but useless answers (High Safety, Low Utility).
- **Inverse Sycophancy**: Model flips to wrong answer when expert opinion is cited (High Bad Flip Rate).
- **Detection-Correction Gap/Dissonance**: High rate of detecting a trap but failing to answer correctly (48-55%).
- **Rung Collapse**: Failure rate increases sharply at higher Pearl rungs (L3), with similar dissonance to Detection-Correction.

**First 3 Experiments**:
1. **Baseline Quadrant Mapping**: Run a single model (e.g., GPT-4) on the full CausalT5K neutral set. Plot the distribution of scores across all three Pearl rungs. Establish baseline capability profile.
2. **Pressure Shift Analysis**: For the same model, run the pressure variants. Compute the net quadrant shift vectors (Neutral -> Pressure) averaged over all instances and per trap type. Identify which traps cause the largest swings.
3. **Judge Strength Sensitivity Test**: Re-evaluate a subset of pressure instances using three distinct judge prompts (e.g., brief, detailed, with examples) for Safety scoring. Measure how quadrant placements change with judge strength, demonstrating the Four-Quadrant Control Landscape.

## Limitations
- **Field maturity**: The identified pathologies may be artifacts of this specific benchmark rather than universal LLM traits, given the nascent research area and low citation count.
- **Generalizability**: Findings are likely constrained to the specific model families tested (not listed); prevalence across architectures remains unknown.
- **Metric validity**: The Utility and Safety axes lack external calibration against human expert consensus or real-world harm outcomes.

## Confidence
- **Benchmark creation (CausalT5K artifact)**: High (concrete, transparent design with 5k+ instances).
- **Skepticism Trap / inverse sycophancy scaling**: Medium (empirically observed but dependent on specific adversarial variants; needs cross-benchmark verification).
- **Detection-Correction Gap / rung collapse**: Medium (consistent internal patterns; requires replication with alternative causal benchmarks).
- **Four-Quadrant Control Landscape / judge strength dependency**: Medium-High (robust paired design; broader validation across more judges and models needed).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-model replication**: Test CausalT5K on at least three distinct model families (e.g., Llama, Claude, GPT) to confirm pathology prevalence and quadrant shift patterns.
2. **Human-in-the-loop validation**: Sample 500 cases for expert human annotation to verify trap taxonomy labels, adversarial pressure efficacy, and metric alignment with expert causal reasoning.
3. **Longitudinal audit**: Re-evaluate the top 5 models after 6–12 months to track whether static audit policies fail consistently or if pathologies diminish with updated training, using identical quadrant analysis.
