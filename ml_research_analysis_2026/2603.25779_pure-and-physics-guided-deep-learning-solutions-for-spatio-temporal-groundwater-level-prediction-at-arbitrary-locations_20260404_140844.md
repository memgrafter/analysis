# Pure and Physics-Guided Deep Learning Solutions for Spatio-Temporal Groundwater Level Prediction at Arbitrary Locations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.25779
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25779
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: STAINet-ILB achieved median MAPE 0.16% and KGE 0.58 in rollout testing, significantly outperforming baselines and producing physically plausible estimates of equation components.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of predicting weekly groundwater levels (GWL) at any arbitrary location within a region, using sparse piezometer measurements and dense weather data. The authors develop STAINet, a pure deep learning model based on spatio-temporal attention, and enhance it with physics-guided strategies that embed the groundwater flow equation via inductive bias (explicit component estimation) and learning bias (physics-based loss terms). The best model, STAINet-ILB, attains high accuracy (median MAPE 0.16%, KGE 0.58) in rollout forecasts and yields physically interpretable diffusion and source/sink term estimates, enhancing trustworthiness and generalization.

## Method Summary
The task is to predict weekly GWL h(x,y,t) at arbitrary coordinates within a region in Piedmont, Italy. Inputs include time series from 28 piezometers (2001–2023) and weekly-aggregated ERA5-Land weather maps (0.1° grid). A lag of T=4 weeks is used. STAINet processes two branches: (1) an autoregressive branch that embeds lagged GWL values and coordinates via a linear layer to spatio-temporal embeddings (ST-VEmb), then applies self-attention (STSA) and cross-attention (STAI) to project to query points; (2) a weather branch that embeds the weather video via a 3D convolution and average pooling, then uses STAI to attend to query points. Both branches use FiLM layers for query coordinate conditioning. The outputs are fused and passed through stacked STSA blocks to predict GWL. Physics-guided variants (PSTAINet) restructure the output to explicitly estimate the autoregressive term (h_prev), diffusion displacement (ΔGW), and source/sink (R), and add loss terms that supervise these components (e.g., L_diff enforces the discretized flow equation on predicted lag fields). Training uses AdamW with specific hyperparameters and early stopping; rollout testing iteratively feeds model predictions back as inputs.

## Key Results
- STAINet-ILB achieved median MAPE 0.16% and KGE 0.58 in rollout testing, significantly outperforming STAINet and other physics-guided variants.
- The model produced physically plausible estimates of diffusion and source/sink terms, improving interpretability and trustworthiness.
- STAINet-ILB also showed superior generalization and stability in long-term forecasts compared to pure data-driven and purely inductive bias models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Attention-Based Spatiotemporal Interpolation
- Claim: The Multi-Head Attention (MHA) mechanism enables predictions at arbitrary spatial points by allowing the model to query information from a sparse set of input piezometer locations (keys/values) for any desired prediction location (queries), thus eliminating the need for post-hoc interpolation.
- Mechanism: The architecture uses parallel branches: an Autoregressive Branch processes the sparse time series at known piezometer locations via self-attention (STSA) and then projects this information to arbitrary query points via cross-attention (STAI). The Weather Branch processes dense spatial weather maps directly via a convolutional embedding and STAI. FiLM layers condition the features on the query coordinates. The attention weights dynamically determine how much each input piezometer contributes to each prediction point based on spatio-temporal proximity and feature similarity.
- Core assumption: The underlying groundwater dynamics are a continuous spatial field where nearby locations and similar temporal contexts have correlated behavior. The attention mechanism can learn these correlation patterns from data.
- Evidence anchors: The abstract states "leveraging both spatially sparse groundwater measurements and spatially dense weather information" and "predict at any desired (i.e., arbitrary) location." Section 4.1.2 describes ST-CEmb creation and how STAI uses input points' ST-CEmb as keys and prediction points' ST-CEmb as queries. Prior work like Andrychowicz et al. also uses attention for sparse-to-dense spatial prediction.
- Break condition: If the spatiotemporal correlation lengths are longer than the spatial extent of the sensor network or if the weather-video relationship to groundwater is highly nonlinear and non-local in a way that attention cannot capture from the given data.

### Mechanism 2: Inductive Bias via Explicit Component Estimation
- Claim: By restructuring the model (PSTAINet-IB) to explicitly output and sum the three physical components—autoregressive term (h_prev), diffusion displacement (ΔGW), and sink/source (R)—the network is architecturally constrained to represent the solution as a sum of these physically motivated parts.
- Mechanism: Instead of a single output head, the model has three output pathways. The h_prev and R are estimated by simple linear layers from projected features. The more complex ΔGW is estimated by a dedicated DBlock (MLP) predicting time-invariant diffusivity D and a ΔGW Module (stacked STSA blocks) predicting the spatial divergence term. The final prediction is h_prev + ΔGW + R. This structure embeds the known equation form h(t) = h(t-1) + ΔGW(t) + R(t) as a hard architectural prior.
- Core assumption: The groundwater dynamics in the region can be adequately represented by the simplified horizontal flow equation with a time-invariant D and a residual R capturing all non-diffusive processes. The model capacity is sufficient to learn the mappings to each component.
- Evidence anchors: The abstract mentions "by introducing an inductive bias, we also estimated the governing equation components." Section 4.2.1 details PSTAINet-IB architecture: "the R_t* and h_P,t*-1 terms are estimated by two linear layers... the diffusion component ΔGW_t* is... multiplication of the estimated D̂ and divergence term."
- Break condition: If the governing equation is a poor approximation (e.g., significant vertical flow, unmodeled anisotropy, or D is highly time-variant), forcing this sum form will hurt accuracy. The DBlock assuming time-invariance is a specific vulnerability.

### Mechanism 3: Learning Bias via Physics-Based Regularization
- Claim: Adding loss terms that penalize deviation from the discretized groundwater flow equation and regularize the residual R̂ (softly) conditions the model to respect physics during training, improving generalization and trustworthiness without hard architectural constraints.
- Mechanism: The total loss L combines data prediction error (L_data) with: L_coh (supervises predicted h_prev against true lag), L_diff (penalizes residual between predicted ΔGW and D̂ * Laplacian(̂h_prev) computed via finite differences on the predicted lag field), and regularization terms L_||R||1/2 on R̂. For PSTAINet-ILRB, L_RCH additionally penalizes positive R̂ outside expert-defined recharge zones. This "soft" enforcement allows flexibility while guiding solutions toward physically plausible ones.
- Core assumption: The residuals of the governing equation computed on the model's own outputs provide a meaningful gradient signal for learning. The balance between data loss and physics loss weights can be tuned for a good trade-off.
- Evidence anchors: The abstract notes "by adopting a learning bias strategy, we proposed the STAINet-ILB, trained with additional loss terms adding supervision on the estimated equation components." Section 4.2.2 defines each loss term. PINN-style learning bias is common in literature (e.g., Secci et al., AirPhyNet).
- Break condition: If physics loss is weighted too heavily, the model overfits to an incorrect or incomplete physical equation, sacrificing data fit. L_diff requires computing spatial derivatives on noisy predicted ̂h_prev early in training, which could destabilize learning. The recharge zone prior in ILRB was found "too restrictive."

## Foundational Learning
### Multi-Head Attention for Set-to-Set Mapping
- Why needed here: To map from a variable-sized set of input piezometer locations (sparse keys/values) to a variable-sized set of arbitrary prediction points (queries). Standard convolutions or MLPs require fixed topology.
- Quick check: If you double the number of input piezometers, how does the computational complexity of the attention mechanism in the STAI module change? (Answer: Quadratic in the number of input keys for the attention weight computation.)

### Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) & Loss Formulation
- Why needed here: To incorporate the groundwater flow PDE as a soft constraint via L_diff. This requires converting a PDE into a residual loss using automatic differentiation on the network's output with respect to its spatiotemporal inputs.
- Quick check: Why is L_diff computed using the predicted ̂h_prev rather than the true lag h_prev? (Answer: To enforce the PDE on the model's own internal dynamics during rollout/forecasting, not just on training data. This is key for long-term stability.)

### Spatiotemporal Feature Engineering (ST-CEmb)
- Why needed here: To provide the model with explicit, cyclical temporal information (day-of-year via sine/cosine) and static spatial context (elevation) alongside coordinates, which are critical for modeling seasonal groundwater cycles and terrain influences.
- Quick check: Why use sine/cosine of day-of-year instead of a raw integer day index or a one-hot encoding? (Answer: Provides a continuous, bounded, and cyclical representation that lets the model learn smooth seasonal patterns without artificial boundaries between Dec 31 and Jan 1.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
1. Input Encoders: Two separate pathways. Autoregressive branch: linear layer on lagged GWL + coordinates → ST-VEmb. Weather branch: Conv3D(3x3) + avg pool on weather video → ST-VEmb.
2. Spatio-Temporal Processing: Autoregressive: STSA (self-attention on input points) → STAI (cross-attention to queries) → STSA (dropout in first STSA) → FiLM (query conditioning). Weather: STAI → FiLM.
3. Fusion & Prediction: Concatenate branch outputs → Stack of 4× STSA blocks → Linear head → Output (h_pred for STAINet; h_prev, ΔGW, R for PSTAINet-IB/ILB/ILRB).
4. Physics Heads (PSTAINet variants): DBlock (3-layer MLP) estimates time-invariant D̂; ΔGW Module (two STSA blocks) estimates divergence term; ΔGW = D̂ * divergence(̂h_prev). h_prev and R̂ estimated by linear layers.
5. Loss Calculation: L = L_data (MAPE) + α_coh L_coh (MAPE on h_prev) + α_diff L_diff (MSE between predicted ΔGW and D̂*∇²(̂h_prev) on control grid) + α_||R||1 L_R1 + α_||R||2 L_R2 (+ α_RCH L_RCH for ILRB) on arbitrary control points.

### Critical path
Raw data → ST-VEmb creation → STAI (weather branch) and STSA/STAI (auto branch) → Fusion → Output/Components → Loss (data + physics). The physics loss terms (L_diff) depend on gradients of ̂h_prev w.r.t. spatial coordinates, requiring a differentiable pipeline from query points back through the network.

### Design tradeoffs
- Inductive vs. Learning Bias: Inductive bias (hard-coded component sum) ensures physical structure but limits flexibility if the equation is wrong. Learning bias (loss terms) is flexible but provides only soft constraints and is computationally heavier (autograd for ∇²̂h).
- STSA vs. Convolution in Weather Branch: Convolutional embedding efficiently captures local spatial patterns; adding STSA modules hurt performance, suggesting local structure is sufficient and self-attention over weather pixels added noise.
- Control Point Density: More control points for L_diff strengthen physics enforcement but linearly increase computation for gradient-based PDE residuals. Paper did not explore this tradeoff systematically.

### Failure signatures
- Spatial: Systematic overestimation (positive NBIAS) in certain regions (e.g., northern sensors) as seen in STAINet/PSTAINet-IB maps (Fig 16). May indicate local model bias or missing physics (e.g., unmodeled abstraction).
- Temporal: Large errors during extreme events (summer 2022 drought drop), especially if model underestimates recharge or overestimates loss (e.g., Racconigi premature recharge in PSTAINet-ILRB, Fig 7d). Suggests R̂ component failure.
- Loss Dynamics: L_diff initially decreasing then rebounding (Fig 9d) signals potential conflict between data and physics losses or optimization instability.
- Component Plausibility: PSTAINet-IB produced "very high absolute value" ΔGW and R̂ (Section 7.3), indicating the pure inductive bias without learning supervision fails to scale components realistically.

### First 3 experiments
1. Pure Data Fit (Ablation): Train STAINet vs. PSTAINet-IB/ILB/ILRB with only L_data (set all α_physics=0). Expect ILB/ILRB to degrade to STAINet-like performance, confirming physics terms add value only when active.
2. Rollout Sensitivity: Freeze D̂ after initial training vs. allow it to update during rollout. A significant drop in KGE/NSE during rollout if D̂ is frozen suggests the model's long-term stability depends on learning a robust, time-invariant D that generalizes, not just fitting short-term sequences.
3. Recharge Zone Ablation: Compare PSTAINet-ILB (no L_RCH) vs. PSTAINet-ILRB. If ILRB underperforms in sensors far from the defined recharge zones, it confirms the prior was "too restrictive" as hypothesized. Also, inspect R̂ maps: ILRB should show strictly positive R̂ only within green buffer, ILB shows mixed signs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can missing data be handled without imputation using attention masking in spatio-temporal groundwater prediction? (Section 9 suggests attention-based masking strategies.)
2. Does Mixture-of-Experts architecture improve capture of complex, context-dependent relationships compared to the current MHA-based STAINet?
3. Does integrating Neural Differential Equation frameworks enhance physics-guided groundwater modeling compared to the simple Euler integration used in PSTAINet-ILB?
4. How do additional exogenous data sources (e.g., land-cover maps, soil moisture products) impact model performance, trustworthiness, and the plausibility of learned equation components?
5. What is the optimal trade-off between computational cost and adherence to physics constraints when selecting the number and placement of control points for learning-bias loss terms? (The study used a fixed arbitrary set; systematic analysis is needed.)

## Limitations
- Sensor selection ambiguity: Results omit some sensors without explanation, undermining the claim of uniform use of all 28 piezometers.
- Physics loss implementation details: The L_diff calculation lacks specification of control point resolution, boundary condition handling, and whether D̂ positivity is enforced, crucial for stability and physical consistency.
- Training schedule opacity: No warm-up phase for physics losses is described; simultaneous optimization may cause gradient conflicts, as hinted by L_diff rebound.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Attention-based interpolation): High
- Mechanism 2 (Inductive bias via component estimation): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (Learning bias via physics regularization): Medium

## Next Checks
1. Control point grid sensitivity: Train PSTAINet-ILB while varying the spatial resolution and alignment of L_diff control points (e.g., 0.5°, 1.0°, 1.5°). Monitor rollout KGE to confirm robustness.
2. Positivity constraint on diffusivity: Modify D̂ output with a softplus activation to ensure D>0. Compare PSTAINet-IB's component magnitudes and rollout KGE against original.
3. Two-stage training schedule: Implement a warm-up: train with L_data only for first 200 epochs, then activate physics losses. Check for reduced L_diff rebound and higher final KGE in long rollouts.
