# CourtGuard: A Model-Agnostic Framework for Zero-Shot Policy Adaptation in LLM Safety

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** arXiv:2602.22557
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22557
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** A retrieval-augmented multi-agent "Evidentiary Debate" framework achieves state-of-the-art safety evaluation across 7 benchmarks without any model fine-tuning and enables instant zero-shot policy adaptation.

## Executive Summary
CourtGuard addresses the critical rigidity of current LLM safety systems, which require costly retraining to enforce new governance rules. It introduces a model-agnostic framework that reimagines safety evaluation as an adversarial debate between specialized agents, with all arguments explicitly grounded in a retrievable policy corpus. This decoupling of safety logic from model weights allows for instant policy adaptation—simply swapping the reference document updates the system's behavior. Evaluated on seven safety benchmarks with two foundational model backbones, CourtGuard achieves state-of-the-art accuracy and F1 scores without fine-tuning. Its utility extends beyond evaluation to high-fidelity dataset curation and policy compliance auditing.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is the "Evidentiary Debate," a retrieval-augmented pipeline. Given a target LLM response, the system first retrieves the most relevant snippets from an external policy corpus using semantic search. These snippets are injected into prompts for three sequential agents: an **Attacker** argues for why the response violates policy, a **Defender** rebuts with compliance arguments, and a **Judge** synthesizes the debate, scores the response on two orthogonal dimensions (regulatory and practical threat), and issues a final verdict (SAFE/BORDERLINE/UNSAFE). This structure forces explicit citation of rules, makes decisions interpretable, and allows the entire safety logic to be changed by updating the policy corpus rather than model weights.

## Key Results
*   Achieves state-of-the-art accuracy and F1 scores across 7 safety benchmarks without any fine-tuning of the backbone LLM.
*   Demonstrates robust zero-shot policy adaptation: swapping to a Wikipedia vandalism detection policy yields 90% accuracy, matching a dedicated, fine-tuned policy-following model.
*   Validated as a tool for automated dataset curation and audit, successfully generating and auditing nine novel datasets of sophisticated adversarial attacks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Grounding in Retrievable Policies for Zero-Shot Adaptability
*   **Claim:** The framework achieves zero-shot policy adaptability by decoupling safety logic from model weights and grounding decisions in retrieved policy documents.
*   **Mechanism:** A RAG pipeline chunks the policy corpus, embeds chunks and the target response, and retrieves top-k relevant snippets. These snippets are injected into all agent prompts, ensuring arguments reference explicit, external rules.
*   **Core Assumption:** The policy corpus has sufficient coverage, and semantic retrieval can fetch the most relevant provisions without weight updates.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract states the system achieves SOTA "without fine-tuning." Section 3.1.1 defines the retrieval formula: `D_k = TopK_{d_i∈D_chunks} sim(E(r), v_i)`. Related work using RAG for security (arXiv:2510.19844) supports the pattern's applicability.
*   **Break Condition:** Retrieval failure (irrelevant or missing clauses) causes fallback to parametric model knowledge, losing zero-shot adaptability.

### Mechanism 2: Structured Adversarial Debate for Thoroughness
*   **Claim:** Iterative debate between specialized Attacker and Defender agents yields more thorough safety analysis than single-pass judgment.
*   **Mechanism:** The system conducts N rounds (default 2). The Attacker proposes risks based on retrieved policies; the Defender rebuts. Agent states accumulate history, refining reasoning across turns.
*   **Core Assumption:** Opposing perspectives surface edge cases, encourage evidence citation, and reduce hallucination/over-reliance on priors.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract describes "Evidentiary Debate." Section 3.1.2 models state evolution. Table 6 ablation shows a second round improves F1 for Llama-70B.
*   **Break Condition:** If agents ignore policies or produce shallow arguments (e.g., Defender concedes), the debate adds no value. Small LLMs may fail to sustain coherent multi-turn dialogue.

### Mechanism 3: Orthogonal Scoring for Interpretable, Auditable Verdicts
*   **Claim:** The Judge's structured two-dimensional scoring provides interpretable, auditable verdicts aligned with regulatory expectations.
*   **Mechanism:** After debate, the Judge produces analysis and assigns numeric scores (1-3) for "regulatory threat" and "practical threat." Their sum is deterministically mapped to SAFE/BORDERLINE/UNSAFE.
*   **Core Assumption:** Breaking safety into two orthogonal dimensions with explicit rubrics yields more consistent and explainable decisions than monolithic classification.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Section 3.1.3 defines the deterministic mapping. Table 2 shows CourtGuard identifies violations missed by humans, indicating strict policy adherence. Table 17 reports high precision (0.919) and specificity (0.946), indicating controlled false positives.
*   **Break Condition:** Judge deviates from rubric or omits citations, making verdict opaque. Smaller models sometimes fail to generate required output structure.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (response + policy corpus) -> RAG Module (chunk, embed, retrieve) -> Agent Orchestration (sequential Attacker -> Defender -> Judge calls) -> Output Parser (extract scores/verdict).

**Critical path:**
1. Embed target response `r` and retrieve top-k relevant policy chunks `C`.
2. If `r` matches a refusal pattern, short-circuit to SAFE.
3. Initialize empty debate history `H`.
4. For `t=1..N` (default 2): Generate Attacker argument `a_t^A`, then Defender argument `a_t^D`; append both to `H`.
5. Judge synthesizes `r, C, H` into scores and verdict.
6. Return verdict and reasoning trace.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Rounds vs. latency:** More rounds increase thoroughness but triple LLM calls; 2 rounds is default.
*   **Model selection per role:** Using a small model for Defender cuts cost but may weaken rebuttal quality; Judge should be strongest.
*   **Retrieval depth (k):** Larger `k` brings more context but risks noise; paper uses `k=3`.
*   **Policy corpus scope:** Larger corpora improve coverage but increase retrieval time/storage.

**Failure signatures:**
*   Low recall on complex attacks (e.g., Puzzler) suggests Attacker lacks creativity.
*   High disagreement with baselines may indicate retrieval failures or labeling inconsistencies.
*   Parser failures (missing regex) when Judge deviates from output format; mitigate with constrained decoding.
*   Cost overruns from excessive LLM calls; monitor tokens and consider per-role model sizing.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Baseline replication:** Run CourtGuard on one benchmark (e.g., JailJudge) with both backbones; verify scores match Table 1.
2.  **Zero-shot policy swap:** Replace policy with Wikipedia Vandalism policy; evaluate on PAN-WVC-10 subset; expect ~90% accuracy (Table 5).
3.  **Ablation - No-RAG:** Disable retrieval (empty context) and evaluate; expect significant performance drop, confirming grounding necessity.

## Limitations
*   Policy corpus coverage and retrieval quality are critical; gaps or semantic misses degrade zero-shot adaptability.
*   Debate effectiveness depends on LLM capability; smaller models may fail to sustain coherent multi-turn arguments.
*   Evaluation limited to two model backbones; performance may not generalize to other architectures.
*   No cost analysis; multi-turn debate increases latency and token use.
*   Utility for dataset curation demonstrated on only nine cases; scalability unproven.

## Confidence
*   **Multi-benchmark SOTA performance:** High (consistent across 7 benchmarks, multiple ablations).
*   **Utility for annotation/auditing:** Medium (promising but limited to nine datasets).
*   **Zero-shot policy adaptability:** Medium (validated on one out-of-domain task; needs broader testing).
