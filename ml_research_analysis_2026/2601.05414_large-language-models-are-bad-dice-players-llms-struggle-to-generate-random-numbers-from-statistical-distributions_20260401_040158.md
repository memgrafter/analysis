# Large Language Models Are Bad Dice Players: LLMs Struggle to Generate Random Numbers from Statistical Distributions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05414
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05414
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** LLMs fail catastrophically at independent sampling (10/11 models 0% pass rate) and show only modest batch success (13% median pass) due to context-dependent self-correction, not true probabilistic generation.

## Executive Summary
This study audits the native probabilistic sampling capabilities of 11 frontier LLMs across 15 statistical distributions using two protocols: Batch Generation (1000 samples per response) and Independent Requests (1000 single-sample calls). Results reveal a stark asymmetry: batch generation achieves a median 13% statistical pass rate, while independent requests collapse completely for nearly all models. Sampling fidelity degrades with distributional complexity and inversely with sample size—larger N increases Wasserstein-1 distance and reduces Kolmogorov-Smirnov test p-values. These failures propagate downstream, causing severe positional bias in multiple-choice question generation and systematic violations of demographic constraints. The findings indicate that current LLMs lack a functional internal sampler, necessitating external tools for any application requiring statistically valid randomness.

## Method Summary
The audit employs a dual-protocol design. In Batch Generation, a single prompt requests N=1000 samples, allowing the model to condition on previously generated outputs within the same context. In Independent Requests, N separate prompts each ask for one sample, isolating stateless generation. Models are evaluated on 15 distributions (Uniform, Gaussian, Bernoulli, Beta, Binomial, Poisson, Exponential, Cauchy, Student’s t, Chi-Square, F-Distribution, Gamma, Weibull, Laplace, Logistic) spanning three complexity tiers. Statistical validity is assessed via Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests (continuous) or χ² goodness-of-fit (discrete) at α=0.01, with Wasserstein-1 distance and KL divergence as fidelity metrics. Downstream tasks test propagation: multiple-choice question generation (uniform answer positions) and attribute-constrained prompt synthesis (demographic distributions).

## Key Results
- **Protocol asymmetry:** Batch generation median pass rate 13%; independent requests: 10 of 11 models pass 0% of distributions.
- **Complexity degradation:** Pass rates decline monotonically from Tier I (simple) to Tier III (complex) distributions; Tier III yields 0% pass even in batch mode.
- **Inverse scaling with N:** In batch mode, Wasserstein-1 distance increases and KS p-values decrease as sample size grows beyond 1000, contradicting convergence expectations.
- **Downstream propagation:** All models exhibit extreme positional bias in MCQ generation (χ² p < 0.001) and systematic demographic skew in constrained synthesis (e.g., >75% female for Llama-4).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Batch generation’s modest success (13% median pass) stems from within-context self-correction, not true independent sampling.
- **Mechanism:** Autoregressive conditioning on previously generated samples lets the model adjust subsequent outputs toward surface statistics (e.g., mean, variance) over short horizons, creating an illusion of sampling competence without internalizing the generative process.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports 13% median pass rate; Section 3.1 describes history-dependent correction; Section 5.1 notes drift accumulation with longer horizons.
- **Break condition:** As N increases, accumulated deviation overwhelms correction, raising W1 and collapsing KS p-values (Figure 3).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Independent requests fail catastrophically (0% pass for 10/11 models) because LLMs lack a functional internal sampler for stateless draws.
- **Mechanism:** Without shared context, the model defaults to parametric priors and token-level biases, sampling from a fixed, distorted “induced distribution” Qθ that does not match the target P.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes 10/11 models pass none; Section 5.1 emphasizes dependence on long-context dependencies; related work on coin-flip bias supports absence of true sampler.
- **Break condition:** Statistical tests fail (p < 0.01) for all but simplest distributions, with W1 substantially higher than in batch mode (Tables 2 vs. 3).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Sampling fidelity degrades with distributional complexity and larger N, revealing a fundamental limitation in approximating complex forms.
- **Mechanism:** LLMs encode only crude, low-order statistics of frequent empirical distributions; complex forms (heavy tails, bounded supports, multi-parameter dependencies) exceed their pattern-matching capacity, and larger N magnifies subtle biases.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states degradation with complexity and inverse N scaling; Section 5.1/Figure 2 shows Tier I→Tier III pass decline; Figure 3 shows W1 rising with N.
- **Break condition:** For Tier III distributions (e.g., Cauchy), no model passes even in batch mode; W1 systematically higher than for Tier I.

## Foundational Learning
- **Probabilistic sampling (i.i.d. draws)**  
  *Why needed:* Core question is whether LLMs can internally perform this operation; understanding true randomness vs. biased generation interprets dual-protocol results.  
  *Quick check:* What distinguishes generating 1000 numbers in one response versus 1000 separate calls, and why does the latter expose absence of an internal sampler?

- **Wasserstein-1 (W1) distance**  
  *Why needed:* Quantifies geometric discrepancy between generated and target distributions; sensitive to shape and location shifts, detecting subtle biases across continuous/discrete cases.  
  *Quick check:* How does W1 capture tail differences, and why might it be more informative than KL divergence here?

- **Dual-protocol experimental design**  
  *Why needed:* Disentangles whether apparent competence arises from contextual self-correction (batch) or genuine stateless stochasticity (independent); asymmetry is the central diagnostic.  
  *Quick check:* If an LLM passes Uniform in batch but fails independently, what does that imply about its sampling mechanism?

## Architecture Onboarding
Distribution suite -> Sampling protocols -> Evaluation metrics -> Downstream tasks -> Models

**Critical path:**  
Prompt design -> Sampling execution (batch/independent) -> Sample collection -> Metric computation (W1, KS/χ²) -> Statistical pass/fail -> Downstream task replication -> Propagation analysis.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Batch vs. Independent:* Batch probes context-aware correction but conflates sequential dependencies; Independent isolates intrinsic bias but loses shared-context power.  
- *Sample size N:* Larger N increases statistical power but may induce drift in batch mode.  
- *Distribution complexity:* Simple distributions may show near-passable results; complex forms amplify failures, but testing across tiers is necessary.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Protocol asymmetry: Batch pass rate > Independent for same model/distribution.  
- Complexity gradient: Pass rate ↓, W1 ↑ from Tier I → Tier III.  
- Inverse scaling: W1 ↑, KS p ↓ as N increases (Figure 3).  
- Downstream propagation: Positional bias in MCQ (χ² p<0.001); demographic skew in attribute prompts.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Baseline asymmetry replication: For Uniform, run both protocols on GPT-4o. Expect batch W1≈0.01 (pass) vs. independent W1≈0.15 (fail).  
2. Complexity stress test: Run both protocols for Cauchy across models. Expect 0% pass in both, but higher W1 in independent.  
3. Downstream propagation check: Generate 1000 MCQs via independent calls with uniformity instruction; extract answer positions and run χ². Expect p<0.001 for all models, with extreme skews.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** What mechanism enables batch generation’s relative success over independent sampling?  
*Basis:* Asymmetry is demonstrated but causal process unidentified; paper suggests “within-context self-correction” but does not isolate factors.  
*Why unresolved:* Without interventions (e.g., masking previous outputs), it is unclear if “correction” is meaningful adaptation or artifact.  
*Resolving evidence:* Controlled disruptions of context, micro-analysis of attention patterns and hidden states.

**Open Question 2:** Which mathematical properties of distributions (support boundedness, tail heaviness, multimodality) most strongly predict LLM sampling failure?  
*Basis:* Tiered analysis shows monotonic degradation but tiers conflate multiple properties.  
*Why unresolved:* No ablation over distributional features; cannot isolate critical driver (e.g., tails vs. parameter count).  
*Resolving evidence:* Systematic suite varying one property at a time (e.g., truncated Gaussians, Beta with varying α/β) with multi-metric analysis.

**Open Question 3:** Why does sampling fidelity inversely scale with sample size N in batch generation, contrary to concentration-of-measure?  
*Basis:* Section 5.1/Figure 3: W1 rises steadily as N exceeds 1000, KS p collapses.  
*Why unresolved:* Ruled out simple finite-sample variance; cause (exposure bias, attention saturation, cumulative errors) not diagnosed.  
*Resolving evidence:* Fine-grained diagnostics per token (evolving empirical CDF/moments every 200 samples); layer-wise attribution to detect contextual interference.

**Open Question 4:** Can architectural modifications (stochastic layers, external sampler heads, training objectives) endow LLMs with competent native sampling?  
*Basis:* Conclusion states LLMs lack a functional sampler; paper is diagnostic only, no remedial tests.  
*Why unresolved:* Unknown if failures are inherent to decoder-only transformers or fixable via redesign.  
*Resolving evidence:* Implement and benchmark modified models: fine-tuning with distribution-matching losses, external RNG tool delegation, attention tweaks for invariant parameter representations.

**Open Question 5:** Do LLMs fail when target distributions are implicit in natural language rather than explicit parameters?  
*Basis:* Study tests only explicitly parameterized distributions; limitations note real-world processes may be implicit.  
*Why unresolved:* No evaluation of verbally described (“roughly uniform,” “long tail”) or ambiguous distributions.  
*Resolving evidence:* Prompts with qualitative distribution descriptions; measure agreement with human-interpreted targets across phrasing variations.

## Limitations
- **Model heterogeneity:** 11-model sample may not represent all frontier architectures; results could shift with newer releases or open-weight variants.
- **Prompt sensitivity:** Fixed prompts may not optimize instruction-following; minor wording changes could alter bias patterns.
- **Causal inference:** Link between internal sampling failure and downstream bias remains correlational; confounding factors (training data skew) not fully isolated.

## Confidence
- **Protocol asymmetry (batch vs. independent):** High – robust across 11 models and 15 distributions with clear metrics.
- **Degradation with complexity/N:** Medium – trend consistent but may conflate recognizability with intrinsic complexity.
- **Downstream propagation:** Low – demonstrated in controlled tasks but not proven in diverse real applications.
