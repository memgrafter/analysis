# Latent Structural Similarity Networks for Unsupervised Discovery in Multivariate Time Series

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.18803  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18803  
- Reference count: 8  
- Primary result: An unsupervised discovery method applied to 20 cryptocurrencies (hourly returns, Apr 2024–Apr 2025) produced a sparse similarity network with 64 edges at threshold 0.90, of which 16 pairs were cointegrated at 95% confidence.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a task‑agnostic, unsupervised discovery layer for multivariate time series. The pipeline extracts overlapping windows, learns latent representations with a sequence‑to‑sequence LSTM autoencoder, aggregates these into a single vector per entity, and induces a sparse similarity network via cosine similarity thresholding. When evaluated on hourly cryptocurrency returns, the resulting network at a threshold of 0.90 contained 64 edges; a post‑hoc Engle‑Granger cointegration test confirmed that 16 of these pairs share a long‑run equilibrium relationship. The approach demonstrates that unsupervised temporal encodings can surface both known and potentially novel cross‑asset relationships.

## Method Summary
The method operates as follows: (1) Raw OHLC data are converted to log returns and normalized; (2) For each asset, overlapping windows of length L=30 hours are generated; (3) A shared LSTM‑based sequence‑to‑sequence autoencoder is trained on all windows to minimize reconstruction error, yielding a 64‑dimensional latent vector per window; (4) The window embeddings for a given asset are averaged to produce a stable entity‑level vector; (5) Pairwise cosine similarities between asset vectors are computed; (6) A fixed threshold τ=0.90 is applied to retain only strong similarities, forming an undirected weighted graph that represents the discovery hypothesis. The graph is the final output, intended for further analysis.

## Key Results
- The thresholded network contained 64 edges among the 20 cryptocurrencies.  
- 16 of these edges passed the Engle‑Granger cointegration test (95% confidence), indicating alignment with classical linear relationships.  
- The discovered network exhibits non‑uniform topology, with hubs and connected components, suggesting the latent space captures structural similarities beyond simple correlation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** An LSTM‑based sequence‑to‑sequence autoencoder compresses multivariate windowed sequences into latent vectors that preserve salient temporal dynamics, enabling meaningful cross‑entity comparison.  
**Mechanism:** The encoder maps an input window \(X_i^{(\tau)} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}\) to a fixed‑length latent vector \(h_i^{(\tau)}\). The decoder reconstructs the sequence from this vector. Training minimizes reconstruction error, forcing the latent space to encode temporal patterns necessary for accurate reconstruction. This creates a shared embedding space where similarity reflects structural alignment in dynamics.  
**Core assumption:** Reconstruction loss is an appropriate unsupervised objective for learning representations that capture temporal characteristics relevant to relational discovery.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “learns window‑level sequence representations using an unsupervised sequence‑to‑sequence autoencoder”; [section III‑C] “LSTM‑based architecture…explicitly models temporal dependencies and non‑linear dynamics…trained…by minimising a reconstruction loss”; corpus shows related encoder‑decoder architectures.  
**Break condition:** If underlying temporal patterns are not reconstructible from a low‑dimensional bottleneck (e.g., pure noise), the latent space may degenerate.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Aggregating latent embeddings across all rolling windows for a given entity yields a stable, entity‑level vector that reduces sensitivity to transient local variations and supports robust pairwise comparison.  
**Mechanism:** For entity \(i\), the mean \(z_i = \frac{1}{K_i}\sum_{\tau} h_i^{(\tau)}\) over its \(K_i\) windows produces a single representative. This averaging acts as a stabilizer, smoothing out window‑specific noise or regime‑specific quirks to expose persistent structural traits.  
**Core assumption:** The mean of window embeddings is a sufficient statistic for the entity’s core temporal structure relevant to similarity.  
**Evidence anchors:** [section III‑D] “Aggregation reduces sensitivity to transient local variation and yields stable per‑entity representations”; [section IV‑C] “Latent embeddings are then aggregated across windows to obtain a single representative latent vector per asset.”  
**Break condition:** If an entity’s behaviour is highly non‑stationary such that no stable pattern exists across windows, aggregation could produce an unrepresentative average or mask critical regime‑specific relationships.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Thresholding cosine similarity in the aggregated latent space induces a sparse graph that compresses the full pairwise search space into a tractable set of candidate relationships, explicitly encoding the discovery hypothesis.  
**Mechanism:** Cosine similarity \(s_{ij} = \frac{z_i \cdot z_j}{\|z_i\|\|z_j\|}\) measures directional alignment of entity vectors. A high threshold (e.g., 0.90) retains only edges with strong latent alignment, yielding a sparse, interpretable network. The graph structure (components, hubs) is the primary output, not the similarity scores themselves.  
**Core assumption:** High cosine similarity in this learned latent space is a meaningful indicator of latent structural similarity for discovery, distinct from classical co‑movement measures.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “induces a sparse similarity network by thresholding a latent‑space similarity measure”; [section III‑E] “To obtain an interpretable and non‑degenerate network, a similarity threshold τ is selected…This network is intended as an analyzable abstraction that compresses the pairwise search space”; [section V‑B] “Applying a fixed cosine similarity threshold of τ=0.90 yields a sparse weighted undirected graph…64 candidate relationships (edges) are retained.”  
**Break condition:** If the latent space geometry does not produce a meaningful separation in cosine similarity (e.g., distribution is tight around a single value), thresholding becomes arbitrary.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sequence‑to‑sequence representation learning**  
  Why needed: The entire discovery pipeline hinges on transforming variable‑length multivariate windows into fixed‑length vectors that encode temporal dynamics for cross‑entity comparison.  
  Quick check: If you increase the latent dimension k drastically, what is the likely risk to the utility of the induced similarity network?  

- **Windowing for non‑stationarity**  
  Why needed: Rolling windows create locally stationary segments, allowing the model to learn dynamics within limited regimes rather than assuming global stationarity. This is core to handling real‑world time series like crypto returns.  
  Quick check: How would you expect the network topology to change if you halved the window length L from 30 to 15?  

- **Graph sparsification via thresholding**  
  Why needed: The discovery output is a sparse hypothesis graph. Understanding that the threshold is a *selection mechanism* (not an optimization target) is crucial; it trades off sensitivity vs. interpretability.  
  Quick check: What qualitative change in the graph’s connected components would you anticipate if you lowered the similarity threshold from 0.90 to 0.80?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
1. **Data Ingest & Preprocess**: Convert raw OHLC to log returns, store as `(entity, time, feature)` tensor.  
2. **Windower**: Generate overlapping windows of length L (stride=1) per entity.  
3. **Representation Learner**: Shared sequence‑to‑sequence LSTM autoencoder; maps window to latent vector h (dim k).  
4. **Aggregator**: Compute mean of all window vectors per entity → entity vector z.  
5. **Similarity Computer**: Compute all pairwise cosine similarities between entity vectors.  
6. **Thresholder & Graph Builder**: Apply threshold τ (e.g., 0.90) to form sparse undirected weighted graph.

### Critical Path
Raw Data → Windower → **[TRAIN AUTOENCODER]** → Aggregator → Similarity Matrix → Thresholding → Graph.  
Autoencoder training is the most computationally heavy step and must be completed before aggregation and similarity computation.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **LSTM vs. other sequence models**: LSTMs balance temporal modeling with parameter efficiency for moderate L; Transformers/TCNs may capture more complex interactions but require more data and compute.  
- **Window length L**: Smaller L increases training samples and captures local regimes but may omit longer patterns; larger L encodes longer dynamics but assumes more stationarity and reduces sample size.  
- **Aggregation statistic**: Mean is simple; alternatives (median, attention‑weighted sum) could improve robustness but add complexity.  
- **Threshold selection**: Fixed τ (e.g., 0.90) is straightforward; alternatives include targeting edge density, percentile cutoffs, or stability‑driven selection.

### Failure Signatures
- **Degenerate latent space**: All z vectors nearly identical → cosine matrix near all‑ones. *Diagnostic*: Compute variance of z norms and pairwise diversity; should be non‑trivial.  
- **Temporal instability**: Graph topology changes drastically across adjacent time blocks. *Diagnostic*: Implement temporal stability protocol (e.g., Jaccard index across blocks).  
- **Cointegration saturation**: Very high proportion of edges pass cointegration test, indicating a bias toward linear stochastic trends. *Diagnostic*: Compare against random graph baseline of same size.  
- **Non‑converging autoencoder**: Reconstruction loss plateaus at high value. *Implication*: Latent representations are not meaningful; consider increasing model capacity or training duration.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Reconstruction sanity check**: After training, reconstruct held‑out windows. Plot original vs. reconstructed sequences and compute MSE; ensure it is significantly below a naïve (window mean) baseline.  
2. **Aggregation stability test**: Vary rolling window stride (e.g., 1 vs. 5) and regenerate z vectors. Compute correlation between z variants per asset; low correlation indicates instability.  
3. **Threshold sensitivity sweep**: For τ in [0.85, 0.95], plot network metrics (edge count, largest component size, clustering coefficient). Identify a “knee” or plateau; τ=0.90 should lie in a low‑sensitivity regime.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Threshold sensitivity**: The paper fixes τ=0.90 and mentions a robustness check but does not explore a range. A systematic sweep over τ (e.g., 0.85–0.95) measuring edge stability, network metrics, and cointegration enrichment is needed to understand the trade‑off between sparsity and coverage.  
- **Similarity measure choice**: Cosine similarity is used by design, yet no comparison with alternatives (Euclidean, correlation, learned metrics) is provided. Comparative experiments on the same latent space could determine the most coherent measure.  
- **Temporal stability of discovered relationships**: The proposed temporal stability protocol is not executed. Evaluating edge persistence across contiguous time blocks would reveal whether core relationships are stable or fleeting.

## Limitations
- Exact cryptocurrency list and date range are unspecified; ranking‑based selection introduces reproducibility ambiguity.  
- Key architecture details (latent vector extraction method, learning rate, early stopping, normalization) are not fully documented, hindering faithful recreation.  
- Ambiguity exists whether “multivariate” refers to multiple features per timestep (OHLC‑derived) or merely cross‑sectional assets; this affects input dimensionality and learned representations.
