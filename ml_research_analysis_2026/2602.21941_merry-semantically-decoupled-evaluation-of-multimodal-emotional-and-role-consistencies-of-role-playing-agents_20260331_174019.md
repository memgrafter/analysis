# MERRY: Semantically Decoupled Evaluation of Multimodal Emotional and Role Consistencies of Role-Playing Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21941
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21941
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MERRY introduces a semantically decoupled evaluation framework that transforms multimodal role-playing agent assessment into a bidirectional-evidence-finding task, significantly improving LLM-as-Judge human agreement and revealing that training on real-world data improves emotional consistency while synthetic data degrades it.

## Executive Summary
Current evaluations of multimodal role-playing agents (MRPAs) conflate semantic consistency (emotional and role traits) with modality-synthesis quality, making it hard to pinpoint failure sources. The MERRY framework decouples these by using eight refined metrics—five for Emotional Consistency (EC) and three for Role Consistency (RC)—and converts scoring into a structured bidirectional evidence-finding task for LLM judges. Built on MERRY-Data, a real-world multimodal dataset derived from Chinese TV series, the framework shows that fine-tuning on real data improves EC metrics, while synthetic datasets worsen them. Experiments reveal persistent issues like emotional templatization, positive-bias, and poor handling of fine-grained negative emotions in existing models.

## Method Summary
MERRY’s evaluation pipeline decouples semantic assessment from modality fidelity. Inputs are a role profile, previous dialogue, and the agent’s multimodal response (facial, body, speech, content descriptions). First, a formatter validates and repairs JSON outputs. Then, five diverse LLM experts perform Emotion Recognition in Conversation (ERC) on each modality’s utterances, producing emotion distributions. EC metrics are computed from these: `MEC` (exact emotion match F1), `CEC` (Krippendorff’s alpha cross-modal), `ED` (normalized entropy), `EED`/`RCD` (Hellinger distance on intra/inter-turn transition matrices). For RC, two LLMs find explicit evidence *for* and *against* Experience, Characteristic, and Relationship consistency; a deterministic mapping converts this to 1–5 scores. MERRY-Data contains 25,507 samples from CPED, with human-verified multimodal semantic descriptions. Training uses LoRA on MiniCPM-o-2.6.

## Key Results
- The bidirectional evidence-finding method for RC achieves Krippendorff’s α ≈ 0.62–0.75, substantially exceeding direct subjective scoring (α ≈ 0.39–0.56).
- Fine-tuning on real-world MERRY-Data improves emotional consistency (e.g., `MEC_lower`=0.291), while fine-tuning on synthetic datasets (OmniCharacter, MMRole) degrades it.
- Existing models show emotional templatization, positive-bias, and bottlenecks in fine-grained negative emotions; simple prompting helps weak models but constrains strong ones.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Semantic-Decoupled Evaluation
- **Claim:** Separating semantic consistency (what is expressed) from modality-synthesis fidelity (how it is expressed) eliminates ambiguous error attribution in MRPA evaluation.
- **Mechanism:** EC/RC metrics operate on ground-truth annotated multimodal descriptions (face, body, speech, content), independent of signal-level quality. This orthogonalization isolates whether failures originate in the agent’s semantic planning (MLLM) or the talker module.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract identifies conflation as the core problem; Section II.A contrasts text-only benchmarks with synthesis metrics; neighboring work (e.g., MMRole) still couples assessment.
- **Break condition:** If cross-modal semantic alignment is always corrupted by modality artifacts, decoupling becomes infeasible.

### Mechanism 2: Bidirectional Evidence Finding
- **Claim:** Transforming RC scoring into a structured evidence-finding task (for/against Experience, Characteristic, Relationship) markedly improves LLM-as-Judge human agreement vs. direct scoring.
- **Mechanism:** LLMs perform comparative textual analysis against a clear rubric, reducing scorer subjectivity. A deterministic table maps evidence patterns to 1–5 scores.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section III.C details the approach; Section IV.D/Table X shows Krippendorff’s α for evidence finding (0.745, 0.619, 0.721) far exceeds subjective scoring (0.558, 0.386, 0.482).
- **Break condition:** Floor effects if no positive evidence exists for a given sample, collapsing discrimination.

### Mechanism 3: Real vs. Synthetic Data Distribution
- **Claim:** Training MRPAs on real-world video-derived data (MERRY-Data) improves emotional consistency; training on synthetic multimodal datasets degrades it.
- **Mechanism:** Real data contains authentic, co-occurring multimodal expressions constrained by role and context. Synthetic data often oversimplifies emotions, exhibits positive-bias, and misaligns modalities, leading models to learn unrealistic patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the finding; Table VIII shows MiniCPM-o-2.6 fine-tuned on MERRY-Data achieves `MEC_lower=0.291` while synthetic fine-tuning drops EC metrics; Section IV.C.1 attributes synthetic data to "emotional templatization."
- **Break condition:** If synthetic data generation converges to real-world distributions or real data is too noisy/sparse.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multimodal Emotional Consistency (EC) Metrics**
  - *Why needed here:* Quantifies whether an agent’s multimodal outputs (face, body, speech, content) convey the same emotion and align with the role’s emotional dynamics. Without decoupled EC, one cannot tell if, e.g., an angry face contradicts happy speech due to planning vs. synthesis failure.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the difference between `MEC_lower` (exact emotion label match across all modalities) and `CEC_lower` (Krippendorff’s alpha across modality raters), and why measure both?

- **Concept: Role Consistency (RC) as Bidirectional Evidence**
  - *Why needed here:* RC evaluation (Experience, Characteristic, Relationship) must be reliable for LLM-as-Judge. Direct scoring is subjective; evidence finding forces justification and standardization. The mapping (Table IV) is key to interpreting final scores.
  - *Quick check question:* If an LLM finds strong evidence *for* Characteristic consistency but ambiguous evidence *against* Experience consistency, what final score (1–5) is likely assigned?

- **Concept: Intra-turn vs. Inter-turn Emotional Transitions**
  - *Why needed here:* EC metrics decompose emotional dynamics: `M_intra` captures shifts within a single response (multiple utterances); `M_inter` captures shifts between dialogue turns. A model may handle one but not the other, revealing specific weaknesses.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might a role’s intra-turn emotion transition be more indicative of core personality than inter-turn transitions?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Formatter -> ERC Pipeline -> EC Metrics Calculator & RC Evaluator -> Output Scores. MERRY-Data Loader provides inputs (role profile, image, prev dialogue, agent response).
- **Critical path:** Raw model output → [Pydantic validation] → JSON → [5 LLM ERC experts] → emotion labels/distributions → [EC Metrics] + [2 LLM RC evidence finders] → final scores.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - ERC Expert Count vs. Cost: 5 experts (Doubao, GPT, Gemini, Claude, Deepseek) improve robustness but increase latency/API cost.
  - Ambiguity Threshold τ=0.7: Higher τ reduces noise but discards more utterances; lower τ includes uncertain labels, harming reliability.
  - Real vs. Synthetic Data: MERRY-Data uses real videos (CPED) requiring heavy human verification; synthetic data scales easily but harms EC.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Repeated reformatting failures → malformed JSON or missing fields.
  - Low CEC + High MEC → cross-modal misalignment (e.g., facial="angry", speech="happy").
  - High ED (Emotional Discrepancy) → ERC experts uncertain; output semantically ambiguous or descriptions low quality.
  - RC score collapse to 1 → bidirectional evidence finder finds no supporting role-consistency evidence.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce baseline scores for Gemini-2.5-pro and MiniCPM-o-2.6 on MERRY test set (`type=All`); verify Table VII numbers match within expected variance.
  2. Fine-tune MiniCPM-o-2.6 on MERRY-Data (real) vs. OmniCharacter/MMRole (synthetic); compare MEC, CEC, EDD changes as in Table VIII.
  3. Replicate Krippendorff’s α experiment for RC (Table X): have 3 human raters score 100 samples using bidirectional evidence instructions; compare against LLM scores.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How well does MERRY's semantically decoupled evaluation framework generalize to languages and cultural contexts beyond Chinese, given its reliance on Chinese-specific data and human evaluators?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Due to the limitations of qualified human resource for quality control and human assessment, this paper primarily focuses on Chinese." (Conclusion)
- **Why unresolved:** Emotional expressions and role-playing norms vary across languages and cultures; the framework's metrics and ERC experts may not capture these nuances, limiting cross-lingual applicability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate MERRY's pipeline (dataset construction, evaluation) in diverse languages/cultures with local human raters; compare metric reliability and human agreement against language-specific baselines.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What architectural or training factors in current MRPAs cause the observed "emotional templatization" and positive-bias, particularly the poor handling of fine-grained negative emotions?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] "Existing models suffer from emotional templatization and simplification, exhibiting positive-bias and performance bottleneck in fine-grained negative emotions." (Abstract, Section IV-C1)
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies symptoms but not root causes, which may stem from RLHF objectives, dataset imbalances, or model biases toward high-frequency emotions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies on training data emotion distributions; probing model attention/representations for bias toward positive classes; controlled fine-tuning with reweighted negative emotion samples.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why does simple prompting simultaneously improve weak models but degrade strong models' performance in role-playing tasks, and how can prompting be optimized across model capabilities?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] "Simple prompting method strengthens the weak models but constrains the strong ones" (Abstract, Section IV-B, Figure 6 analysis).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper notes the phenomenon speculatively (e.g., "long-context input may act as a constraint") without testing hypotheses; the trade-off between guidance and flexibility remains unexplained.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments varying prompt detail, context length, and model scale; analyze prediction entropy or role adherence under different prompts to measure constraint effects.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Why does training on synthetic multimodal role-playing datasets (e.g., OmniCharacter, MMRole) reduce emotional consistency compared to training on real-world data (MERRY-Data)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] "Training on synthetic datasets tends to reduce emotional consistency, whereas training on real-world datasets improves it" (Abstract, Table VIII results).
- **Why unresolved:** Synthetic data may lack emotional nuance, have unnatural transitions, or misalign multimodal expressions; the paper does not quantify these discrepancies or test hybrid training.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare emotional distributions (e.g., transition matrices, emotion co-occurrence) between synthetic and MERRY-Data; train models on mixed datasets to isolate synthetic data's detrimental factors.

## Limitations
- **Prompt/Implementation Gaps:** Exact LLM prompts for ERC and bidirectional evidence-finding are not released, risking protocol drift in reproduction.
- **Dataset Construction Fidelity:** Details of human verification for MERRY-Data (disagreement resolution) and speech prompt generation from audio features are underspecified, limiting replicability.
- **Generalization Scope:** Evaluation uses 257 roles from TV series; performance on original characters or cross-cultural roles is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Semantic Decoupling):** High – Clear experimental separation; orthogonal failure modes validated.
- **Mechanism 2 (Bidirectional Evidence):** Medium-High – Strong inter-rater agreement gains, but depends on specific LLM judges and rubrics not fully detailed.
- **Mechanism 3 (Real vs. Synthetic Data):** Medium – Results consistent but may be confounded by dataset size/domain mismatch; role-specific effects need deeper analysis.
