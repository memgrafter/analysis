# Patch-Based Spatial Authorship Attribution in Human-Robot Collaborative Paintings

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.17030  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17030  
- **Reference count:** 26  
- **Primary result:** A patch-based CNN achieves 88.8% patch-level and 86.7% painting-level authorship attribution; hybrid collaborative patches exhibit 64% higher predictive entropy than pure human/robot patches (p=0.003).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method for spatially resolved authorship attribution in human-robot collaborative paintings, where traditional single-author techniques fail. By analyzing small grayscale patches from high-resolution scans, a custom convolutional neural network learns fine-grained brushstroke textures that discriminate between human and robotic painting styles. The approach uses leave-one-painting-out cross-validation to ensure generalization to new artworks, achieving high patch- and painting-level accuracy even with only 15 training paintings. For hybrid works, the model’s predictive entropy—computed after renormalizing human and robot probabilities—reliably flags regions of stylistic overlap, offering a tool for quantifying mixed authorship without explicit hybrid training.

## Method Summary
The method processes 1200 DPI scans by extracting 300×300 pixel grayscale patches with 50% overlap. Patches are labeled as blank (≥95% white), human, or robot based on their source painting. A compact VGG-style CNN is trained from scratch using weighted cross-entropy to handle class imbalance. To prevent data leakage, evaluation employs leave-one-painting-out cross-validation: in each fold, the model trains on all patches from 14 paintings and tests on every patch from the held-out painting. For hybrid Collaborative paintings, the model—trained only on pure works—computes conditional Shannon entropy on the human/robot probability distribution to quantify stylistic ambiguity in each patch.

## Key Results
- Patch-level accuracy: **88.8%** under leave-one-painting-out cross-validation, outperforming texture (LBP+RF: 65.9%) and pretrained feature (DINOv2+SVM: 84.7%) baselines.
- Painting-level (majority vote) accuracy: **86.7%**.
- In 5 hybrid paintings, patches manually annotated as mixed show **64% higher predictive entropy** than patches from pure human or robot paintings (Mann-Whitney U, p=0.003), indicating the model detects stylistic overlap rather than misclassifying.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Local Brushstroke Texture Learning**  
A patch-based CNN learns fine-grained, locally discriminative features (e.g., pressure consistency, edge smoothness) that distinguish human from robotic styles. Dense patch extraction expands the effective training set from 15 paintings to ~137k patches, enabling the network to capture micro-structural cues invariant to overall composition. Learning from scratch avoids domain mismatch from ImageNet pretraining. *Evidence:* Outperforms texture and pretrained baselines; confusion matrix shows errors primarily between human/robot classes, confirming a stylistic boundary is learned. *Break condition:* Would fail if stylistic differences were primarily global or semantic rather than local.

**Mechanism 2: Conditional Entropy as Ambiguity Proxy**  
Predictive Shannon entropy, conditioned on non-blank content and renormalized over only human/robot probabilities, serves as a proxy for mixed authorship. When a hybrid patch contains intermediate visual characteristics, confidence in pure classes drops, increasing entropy. *Evidence:* Hybrid patches show 64% higher entropy than pure (p=0.003); pure human and robot medians are nearly identical (0.11 vs 0.13, p=1.0), validating the signal isn’t class-dependent. *Break condition:* Would fail if hybrid patches fell far outside pure class distributions (true OOD), causing uniformly low confidence instead of a human/robot trade-off.

**Mechanism 3: Painting-Level Cross-Validation**  
Leave-one-painting-out CV prevents leakage of spatial correlations or painting-specific artifacts, testing true generalization to unseen artworks by the same agent pair. *Evidence:* Protocol explicitly treats the painting as the statistical unit; per-fold accuracy varies (69.0%–99.4%), indicating some paintings are harder but overall mean remains high (88.79%). *Break condition:* Would fail if intra-agent stylistic variance exceeded the inter-agent gap, making style inconsistent across paintings.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Patch-Based Learning for Data Scarcity  
  *Why needed:* With only 15 paintings, whole-image training is infeasible; patch extraction creates a large, diverse set to learn local texture statistics.  
  *Quick check:* If trained on whole images, overfitting to painting-specific composition would increase, likely lowering accuracy from 88.8% to near random chance.

- **Concept:** Conditional Entropy for Ambiguity Detection  
  *Why needed:* Hybrid ground truth is ill-defined; standard classification is meaningless. Conditional entropy provides an unsupervised signal for stylistic conflict.  
  *Quick check:* Why renormalize over only human/robot classes? To ignore the blank class and bound entropy to [0,1], making it comparable across patches regardless of blank content prevalence.

- **Concept:** Leave-One-Group-Out Cross-Validation  
  *Why needed:* Random patch splits leak information because adjacent patches are spatially correlated; holding out entire paintings tests generalization to new creative works.  
  *Quick check:* If random 80/20 patch split were used, validation accuracy would overestimate true performance due to leakage from spatially adjacent training patches.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input Pipeline (1200 DPI scan → 300×300 px grayscale patches, stride 150, blank label ≥95% white) → Model (5-block VGG-style CNN: Conv2d-BN-ReLU ×2 + MaxPool per block, then Dropout(0.4), GAP, FC 512→512→128→3) → Loss (Weighted Cross-Entropy: w_blank=0.01·N_total/N_blank, w_human=1.0, w_robot=0.75·N_total/N_robot) → Uncertainty Module (if p_human+p_robot > τ=0.2, renormalize and compute H = -Σ P·log₂ P).

**Critical path:**  
Data Collection (15 pure paintings) → Scanning → Patch Extraction (~137k patches) → Define 15 folds (one painting per fold) → For each fold: Train CNN on 14 paintings’ patches → Select best epoch on held-out painting’s patches → Aggregate predictions across folds → Compute metrics & hybrid entropy.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Compact CNN vs. Pretrained: Smaller model avoids overfitting on 14 training paintings but captures less generic texture than DINOv2; tradeoff yields higher sample efficiency (88.8% vs 84.7%).  
- Stride 150 (50% overlap): Increases samples ~4× vs. non-overlap, capturing transitions, but introduces spatial correlation mitigated by painting-level CV.  
- Entropy threshold τ=0.2: Excludes truly blank patches; lower τ includes ambiguous low-content areas, higher τ might miss subtle hybrid zones; not sensitivity-tested.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High confusion between Human and Robot classes indicates inherent stylistic overlap the model cannot resolve.  
- Low entropy on annotated hybrid patches would invalidate the hybrid detection claim.  
- Large per-fold accuracy variance (69.0%–99.4%) suggests some paintings are outliers, possibly due to scan quality or anomalous style.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Reproduce LBP+RF (65.9%), DINOv2+SVM (84.7%), and custom CNN (88.79% ±10.41%) results using identical 15-fold CV splits.  
2. **Entropy distribution validation:** On held-out pure paintings, compute conditional entropy for non-blank patches; confirm pure Human and pure Robot medians are near-identical (p≈1.0) and hybrid medians are 64% higher (p<0.05).  
3. **Single-patch sampling stress test:** Train on one random non-blank patch per painting; expect accuracy ~62%, demonstrating the benefit of high patch density.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. Does the patch-based CNN generalize across multiple distinct human-robot pairs, or is it specific to the single pair studied?  
2. Can incorporating temporal stroke-ordering information from the painting process improve spatial attribution, especially in hybrid regions?  
3. Would training on a small set of explicitly labeled hybrid patches reduce uncertainty and improve hybrid attribution accuracy?  
4. How do different collaboration protocols (concurrent vs. sequential, varying human modification of robot strokes) affect predictive entropy patterns in hybrid regions?

## Limitations

- Tiny dataset: only 15 pure paintings from one human-robot pair limits statistical power and generalizability.  
- Class weighting α factors (blank=0.01, human=1.0, robot=0.75) are arbitrary and not ablated.  
- Hybrid patch annotations are manual and subjective; no inter-rater agreement reported.  
- Entropy threshold τ=0.2 chosen without sensitivity analysis.

## Confidence

- Patch-level accuracy (88.8%): **Medium** (solid CV but tiny dataset, no external validation).  
- Entropy as hybrid proxy: **Medium** (significant effect but threshold dependence and calibration unaddressed).  
- Leave-one-painting-out CV validity: **High** (proper group-wise split prevents leakage).
