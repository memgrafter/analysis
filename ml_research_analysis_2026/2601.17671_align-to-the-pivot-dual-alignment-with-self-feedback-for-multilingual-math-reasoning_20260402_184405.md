# Align to the Pivot: Dual Alignment with Self-Feedback for Multilingual Math Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.17671
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17671
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: PASMR significantly improves multilingual math reasoning; e.g., Mistral-7B-Instruct accuracy on MGSM increases by +25.5% (27.0→52.5), and low-resource language average rises from 11.6 to 44.4.

## Executive Summary
Large language models exhibit uneven performance across languages in mathematical reasoning, with low-resource languages lagging due to misaligned understanding and reasoning. This paper introduces PASMR, a framework that employs English as a pivot language to enforce dual alignment: Pivot-Aligned Mapping (PAM) translates questions into English to standardize the reasoning foundation, and Self-feedback Reinforcement Learning (SRL) optimizes target-language answers by rewarding consistency with the pivot-derived answer, using self-generated signals instead of external supervision. Experiments on MGSM and MSVAMP show substantial gains across multiple model families, particularly for low-resource languages, and confirm the critical role of SRL through ablations. The approach maintains robust generalization to out-of-distribution data.

## Method Summary
PASMR consists of two sequential stages. First, Pivot-Aligned Mapping (PAM) fine-tunes the model on synthetic data where each target-language question is accompanied by an English translation of the question and the target-language answer. This forces the model to process the problem through a consistent English representation before generating the answer in the original language, aligning reasoning patterns. Second, Self-feedback Reinforcement Learning (SRL) initializes the policy with the PAM-fine-tuned model. For each target-language question during RL, the model first generates an English translation of the question (extracted from its output) and a target-language answer. The English question is then re-prompted to obtain a pivot-language answer. A token-wise reward is computed based on string or numeric agreement between the target and pivot answers, and REINFORCE++ updates the policy to increase the likelihood of trajectories that produce consistent answers, using a KL penalty to preserve the PAM reference policy. This self-supervised loop refines reasoning without needing gold answers or external reward models.

## Key Results
- Mistral-7B-Instruct: MGSM accuracy +25.5% (27.0→52.5); low-resource average 11.6→44.4.
- Similar gains observed for Llama-3 and Deepseek-math on both MGSM and MSVAMP.
- Ablations confirm SRL is critical; removing it significantly lowers performance.
- PASMR maintains robust generalization to out-of-distribution test data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Explicit translation to a pivot language (English) during training aligns the model's internal reasoning patterns across languages by reducing variance in the initial question interpretation step.
- **Mechanism**: The Pivot-Aligned Mapping (PAM) stage fine-tunes the model to first map a target-language question `Q_T` into a structured English prompt `Q_En`, then generate the reasoning answer `A_T` in the target language. This forces the model to process the problem through a consistent, high-resource linguistic representation before outputting in the target language.
- **Core assumption**: The model's dominant language (English) possesses a more robust and well-aligned internal representation for mathematical reasoning, and explicitly guiding inputs through this channel standardizes the reasoning foundation.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "...first translates questions into the pivot language to facilitate better alignment of reasoning patterns."
  - [section 2.1] Formal loss function `L_SFT(θ) = -Σ log P_θ(Q_En, A_T | Q_T)` where special tokens delimit `Q_En`.
  - [corpus] Neighbor paper "MAPO" also leverages a pivot language for alignment, suggesting a common hypothesis.
- **Break condition**: If the pivot translation (`Q_En`) itself introduces semantic distortion (e.g., losing numeric precision or problem structure), PAM may propagate errors rather than align understanding.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Self-feedback reinforcement learning (SRL) optimizes the target-language reasoning process by rewarding consistency between the target answer and the answer derived from the pivot translation, without needing external gold answers.
- **Mechanism**: For a target-language rollout `[Q_En, A_T]`, the system extracts `Q_En` and generates a pivot-language answer `A_En`. A token-wise reward is computed based on whether `A_T` matches `A_En` (1 for match, 0.1 for mismatch, 0 for format error). REINFORCE++ then updates the policy, reinforcing trajectories where the target reasoning converges to the pivot reasoning.
- **Core assumption**: Consistency between target and pivot answers is a valid, self-supervised proxy for reasoning correctness, as the pivot reasoning is assumed to be more reliable.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "...supervised by the pivot language’s reasoning answers, thereby establishing a cross-lingual self-feedback mechanism without relying on external correct answers or reward models."
  - [section 2.2] Detailed reward function `R_i` and KL-penalized PPO optimization.
  - [section 3.4 ablation] "Removing SRL (wo. SRL) consistently reduces performance."
- **Break condition**: If the pivot answer `A_En` is incorrect (due to poor initial reasoning or translation), the reward signal becomes misleading and may reinforce incorrect target-language reasoning patterns aligned to a wrong pivot.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The dual-stage design (PAM + SRL) improves both multilingual *understanding* (via PAM) and multilingual *reasoning consistency* (via SRL), leading to compounded gains, especially for low-resource languages.
- **Mechanism**: PAM first addresses input mapping biases that disproportionately affect low-resource languages. SRL then iteratively refines the generation policy to produce answers that are consistent with the more reliable pivot reasoning, closing the gap between understanding and final output.
- **Core assumption**: Performance gaps in low-resource languages stem from a compound effect of poor initial comprehension *and* weak cross-lingual answer mapping; tackling both sequentially yields multiplicative benefits.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "Our method enhances both the model’s understanding of questions and its reasoning capabilities."
  - [section 3.3 main results] "Languages with much lower baseline performance... show the largest gains." For Mistral on MGSM, low-resource Avg: 11.6→44.4 (+32.8%).
  - [section 3.4] "Increasing reward values lead to higher accuracy in pivot-aligned answers." Figure 3 shows reward and alignment rates moving together.
  - [corpus] Neighbor "TAPO" also combines translation augmentation with RL, indicating this dual-stage insight is active in the field.
- **Break condition**: If either stage is severely compromised (e.g., catastrophic forgetting in PAM, or reward hacking in SRL), the dual-stage process could degrade performance more than a single-stage approach.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Multilingual LLM Processing Asymmetry  
  - *Why needed here*: PASMR is predicated on the observed fact that LLMs do not process all languages equally well; they rely on an internal "dominant language" (usually English). Understanding *why* this asymmetry exists (training data distribution, representational capacity) is key to justifying the pivot strategy.  
  - *Quick check question*: If a model was trained on perfectly balanced multilingual data, would PASMR's pivot mechanism still be necessary or beneficial?

- **Concept**: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning  
  - *Why needed here*: The task is math reasoning, which requires intermediate steps. PASMR's reward compares final answers, but the core goal is to align the *reasoning process*. Knowing that CoT exposes the model's latent reasoning chain is essential to interpreting what "consistency" between pivot and target answers actually signifies.  
  - *Quick check question*: Does a matching final answer between pivot and target languages guarantee identical reasoning steps? What are the implications if they differ?

- **Concept**: Reinforcement Learning from Human/Model Feedback (RLHF/RLAIF)  
  - *Why needed here*: SRL is an online RL algorithm (REINFORCE++) using self-generated rewards. A baseline understanding of policy gradient methods, reward shaping, and the KL penalty's role in preventing reward over-optimization is necessary to grasp SRL's design and potential failure modes.  
  - *Quick check question*: What is the purpose of the `β*KL` term in the reward, and what might happen if `β` is set too high or too low?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Data Pipeline → PAM Module → SRL Rollout Engine → Reward Verifier → RL Optimizer  
  (Details: Data Pipeline generates synthetic (Q_T, Q_En, A_T) triples; PAM Module fine-tunes model on these; SRL Rollout Engine generates (Q_En, A_T) from Q_T and re-prompts with Q_En to get A_En; Reward Verifier computes token-wise reward based on A_T vs A_En; RL Optimizer updates policy via REINFORCE++ with KL penalty relative to PAM model.)

- **Critical path**:
  1. Prepare pivot-aligned supervised dataset (PAM data).
  2. Run PAM SFT to get a model that reliably produces `Q_En` and `A_T`.
  3. For SRL, initialize policy with PAM model. For each `Q_T` in RL data: a) generate rollout, b) extract `Q_En` and get `A_En`, c) compute reward, d) compute advantages, e) update policy.
  4. Iterate step 3. A failure at step 2 (PAM) will cripple SRL, as `Q_En` extraction or `A_T` generation will be unreliable.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Self-feedback vs. External Rewards**: Avoids cost/errors of gold answer or external reward models, but risks reinforcing the model's own biases if `A_En` is systematically wrong.
  - **Explicit vs. Implicit Pivot**: Explicitly inserting `Q_En` tokens ensures clean extraction but adds sequence length. Implicit methods (e.g., hidden state alignment) might be more efficient but less controllable.
  - **Token-wise vs. Sequence Reward**: Token-wise reward in REINFORCE++ provides dense feedback but may incentivize copying `A_En` verbatim rather than semantically equivalent reasoning.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Pivot Extraction Failure**: Model fails to insert proper delimiters for `Q_En`, causing the SRL stage to feed garbage prompts.
  - **High Pivot Failure Rate**: During SRL, `A_En` is often wrong. Then, a "matching" `A_T == A_En` gets a reward of 1, reinforcing a *wrong* answer. Monitor Figure 4's "Pivot Fail" and "Answer Align / Pivot Fail" curves.
  - **Catastrophic Forgetting in SRL**: Model's English reasoning ability (the pivot) degrades during RL, causing `A_En` to become unreliable. Measure accuracy on a held-out English set during SRL.
  - **Over-alignment to Format**: Model learns to make `A_T` string-match `A_En` while reasoning differently (e.g., correct logic but wrong final number formatting).

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **PAM Sanity Check**: Fine-tune on PAM data only. Evaluate: (a) Accuracy on target languages, (b) `Q_En` extraction success rate (delimiters present?), (c) `A_T` vs `A_En` consistency rate. Expect improved target accuracy and high extraction rate.
  2. **SRL Reward Correlation**: Run SRL for a small number of steps without updating weights. Log: proportion of `(A_T correct)` given `(A_En correct)` vs `(A_En wrong)`. Expect high correlation (Figure 4). If low, pivot quality is the bottleneck.
  3. **Ablation - SRL Off**: Compare full PASMR (PAM+SRL) vs. PAM-only model on test sets. A significant drop when SRL is removed confirms its contribution. Also compare to PAM-only model *with* gold-pivot inference (using perfect `Q_En` translations) to isolate the value of self-feedback RL vs. better pivot inputs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Does the choice of pivot language (other than English) affect multilingual math reasoning performance for low-resource languages?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The framework uses English as the pivot; no alternative pivots are tested.
- **Why unresolved**: Different pivot languages may align reasoning patterns differently, especially for non-Latin scripts.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Run PASMR with various pivot languages (e.g., Chinese, Arabic) and compare accuracy and consistency on MGSM/MSVAMP.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How does the quality of the pivot translation used during training affect PASMR’s performance?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors show that a gold pivot at inference improves results, stating “pivot language quality matters.”
- **Why unresolved**: Training uses automatic translations; it is unknown if higher quality training pivots would further boost gains.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Train with improved pivots (e.g., human translations, stronger MT) and evaluate on test sets.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How does the self-feedback RL handle cases where the pivot answer is incorrect, and can the reward be made more robust?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Analysis (Figure 4) shows target correctness strongly correlates with pivot correctness; mismatches occur when pivot is wrong.
- **Why unresolved**: The reward penalizes mismatches, which may incorrectly penalize correct target answers and hinder learning.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Analyze training rewards distribution; design alternative rewards (e.g., numeric equivalence) and compare final accuracy.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Can PASMR be applied to other multilingual reasoning tasks beyond math, such as commonsense or logical reasoning?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] All experiments are on math datasets (MGSM, MSVAMP).
- **Why unresolved**: Math problems have unique answer formats; generalization to other task types is not established.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Adapt PASMR to benchmarks like X-COPA and measure improvements across languages.

## Limitations
- Pivot translation quality may introduce semantic distortions (e.g., numeric errors, idiomatic losses) that propagate through alignment, especially in low-resource languages.
- Self-feedback fragility: SRL assumes the pivot answer is reliable; if the model’s English reasoning degrades or the translation is poor, the reward signal misaligns and may reinforce incorrect reasoning.
- Task specificity: Evaluation is confined to math reasoning; it is unclear whether PASMR generalizes to other domains (e.g., commonsense, code) where reasoning patterns differ.

## Confidence
- **Dual-stage design (PAM+SRL) efficacy**: High (consistent gains across 3 models, strong ablations).
- **Necessity of English as pivot**: Medium (no alternative pivots tested; assumes English-dominance without proof).
- **Self-feedback RL as key driver**: Medium (ablation shows drop without SRL, but reward reliability not fully validated).
- **Low-resource gains robustness**: Medium (impressive numbers but possible data leakage from translation pipeline).

## Next Checks
- **Alternative pivot ablation**: Replace English with another high-resource language (e.g., Chinese, Spanish) as pivot. Measure accuracy changes to test if gains are pivot-language-specific or transferable.
- **Corrupted pivot robustness**: During SRL, systematically degrade `Q_En` translations (e.g., random number swaps, entity replacement). Quantify performance drop vs. clean pivot; exposes sensitivity to translation fidelity.
- **Cross-task zero-shot transfer**: Apply PASMR-trained models to non-math multilingual reasoning benchmarks (e.g., logical deduction, multilingual QA). Evaluate if alignment benefits generalize beyond math.
