# Learning in the Null Space: Small Singular Values for Continual Learning

## Quick Facts
- ArXiv ID: 2602.21919
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21919
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: Competitive average accuracy with near-zero forgetting (e.g., 72.56% ACC, -0.17% BWT on CIFAR-100, 10 tasks).

## Executive Summary
NESS (Null Space Embedding for Stable Gradient updates) addresses catastrophic forgetting in continual learning by constructing an approximate null space of past task inputs and constraining weight updates to lie within it. For each layer, the method identifies singular vectors associated with the smallest singular values of the input covariance, freezes them as a basis \(U\), and parameterizes updates as \(\Delta W = UV\) with a trainable matrix \(V\). Regularizing \(\|V\|_2\) provides an explicit stability guarantee. Unlike prior orthogonal projection methods, NESS enforces orthogonality by construction, avoiding per-step projection costs.

Empirical results on CIFAR-100, 5-datasets, and MiniImageNet show that NESS attains competitive accuracy while maintaining backward transfer (BWT) near zero or even positive, demonstrating the effectiveness of the small singular-value subspace approach.

## Method Summary
NESS processes each layer independently for each new task. First, it gathers activation samples from the current task and computes the singular value decomposition of the centered activation matrix. The left singular vectors with singular values below a threshold \(\varepsilon_1\) define an approximate null space matrix \(U\). The backbone weights are frozen, and a low-rank adaptation \(\Delta W = UV\) is introduced, where \(U\) is fixed and \(V\) is initialized to zero and trained alongside the task. The training loss includes cross-entropy and weight decay on \(V\) to keep its spectral norm below a calculated bound, ensuring that output changes for any past input remain bounded. After training, \(\Delta W\) is merged into the backbone.

## Key Results
- **CIFAR-100 (10 tasks)**: 72.56% ACC, -0.17% BWT (SAM); 72.46% ACC, +0.03% BWT (SGDm)
- **5-datasets (5 tasks)**: 90.98% ACC, -0.86% BWT (SAM); 90.20% ACC, -0.58% BWT (SGDm)
- **MiniImageNet (20 tasks)**: 63.48% ACC, -0.26% BWT (SAM); 63.72% ACC, +0.41% BWT (SGDm)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Updates constrained to singular vectors associated with small singular values are approximately orthogonal to the input subspaces of previous tasks, reducing interference.
- **Mechanism**: For a layer's concatenated previous inputs \(I_t\), the SVD identifies directions (left singular vectors) with low energy (small \(\sigma\)). Directions orthogonal to the dominant subspace (large \(\sigma\)) are those with minimal projection onto past inputs. Restricting \(\Delta W\) to the span of these small-\(\sigma\) vectors ensures \(x^\top \Delta W \approx 0\) for most \(x \in I_t\).
- **Core assumption**: Small singular value directions have low covariance with past task inputs, so changes along them minimally affect outputs on previous tasks.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "...small singular values correspond to directions that are nearly orthogonal to the input space of previous tasks... updates remain approximately in the null space of previous inputs."
  - [section 3.2]: "Directions associated with small singular values correspond to directions with low energy in previously observed inputs. Restricting updates to these directions reduces interference."
  - [corpus]: Neighbors explore null-space and low-rank adaptation but do not directly evidence this specific mechanism; the claim is paper-specific.
- **Break condition**: If the selected small-\(\sigma\) subspace still contains directions with non-negligible projection onto some past input \(x\), interference may occur unless the magnitude of \(\Delta W\) is tightly controlled (addressed by Mechanism 3).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Parameterizing \(\Delta W_t^{(l)} = U_t^{(l)} V_t^{(l)}\) with frozen \(U_t^{(l)}\) (from small-\(\sigma\) vectors) and trainable \(V_t^{(l)}\) embeds the orthogonality constraint by construction, avoiding per-step gradient projection.
- **Mechanism**: The update structure is fixed at task start. \(U_t^{(l)}\) is an orthogonal basis for the approximate null space. Gradients update only \(V_t^{(l)}\), so all effective weight changes live in \(\text{span}(U_t^{(l)})\) regardless of optimizer dynamics.
- **Core assumption**: A fixed low-rank basis \(U_t^{(l)}\) adequately captures the null space of all previous task inputs for that layer, and learning within this subspace is sufficient for adapting to the new task.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "NESS constructs an approximate null space... parameterizes task-specific updates via a compact low-rank adaptation... The subspace basis is fixed... ensures that the resulting updates remain approximately in the null space."
  - [section 3.2, "Practical Enforcement"]: "By construction, updates lie in an approximate null subspace of previous inputs."
  - [section 3.3]: "Only \(V_t^{(l)}\) is trainable, while \(U_t^{(l)}\) remains fixed."
- **Break condition**: If the fixed basis \(U_t^{(l)}\) poorly approximates the true null space (e.g., due to insufficient data for SVD or poor \(\varepsilon_1\) choice), updates may leak into interfering directions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Regularizing \(\|V_t^{(l)}\|_2\) enforces an explicit bound on output perturbation for any previous input, guaranteeing stability.
- **Mechanism**: From the derivation: \(\|x^\top \Delta W_t\|_2 \leq \|\Sigma_{\text{small}}\|_2 \|V_t\|_2 \leq \varepsilon_1 \|I_t\|_F \|V_t\|_2\). Enforcing \(\|V_t\|_2 \leq \sqrt{\varepsilon / (\varepsilon_1 \|I_t\|_F)}\) via weight decay ensures \(\|x^\top \Delta W_t\|_2^2 \leq \varepsilon\) for all \(x \in I_t\).
- **Core assumption**: The spectral norm bound on \(V_t\) suffices to control the worst-case output change across *all* previous inputs, and the SVD-based construction accurately isolates the small-\(\sigma\) subspace.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.2, "Explicit Stability Bound"]: Full derivation and inequality (7) showing \(\|x^\top \Delta W_t\|_2^2 \leq \varepsilon\).
  - [section 3.2, "Practical Enforcement"]: "Thus, by construction, updates lie in an approximate null subspace... while their magnitudes remain controlled."
  - [section 3.3]: Weight decay is applied to \(V_t^{(l)}\) to enforce the bound.
- **Break condition**: If the bound on \(\|V_t\|_2\) is not tightly enforced during training, or if the inequality's assumptions (e.g., orthogonality of \(\tilde{V}_t\)) are violated in practice, stability may degrade.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) and Subspace Geometry  
  **Why needed**: To identify the null space (directions of minimal variance) in the input covariance of previous tasks. Small singular values define this approximate null space.  
  **Quick check**: Why do the left singular vectors corresponding to the smallest singular values of \(I_t I_t^\top\) approximately span the orthogonal complement to the subspace spanned by most past inputs?

- **Concept**: Catastrophic Forgetting & Stability-Plasticity Trade-off  
  **Why needed**: The fundamental CL problem NESS addresses. Stability = preserving past performance; Plasticity = learning new tasks. NESS trades plasticity (restricted update space) for stability.  
  **Quick check**: How does constraining weight updates to a subspace differ from regularizing weight magnitudes (e.g., L2) in preventing forgetting?

- **Concept**: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA-style Parameterization)  
  **Why needed**: To make the constrained update parameterization efficient and trainable. \(\Delta W = UV\) with frozen \(U\) and small \(V\) reduces trainable parameters and embeds the geometric constraint.  
  **Quick check**: In \(\Delta W = UV\), if \(U\) is fixed and orthogonal, what property of \(V\)'s updates directly influences the magnitude of the output change on a given input \(x\)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input Collector -> Subspace Constructor -> Adapter Initializer -> Constrained Trainer -> Weight Merger
- **Critical path**: For each layer and each task: forward pass to collect activations → compute SVD → select small-σ basis U → initialize trainable V → train V with regularization → merge UV into backbone weights.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **ε1 threshold**: Lower ε1 yields fewer trainable parameters and stronger stability but may limit plasticity; higher ε1 increases plasticity but risks interference.
  - **Memory vs. Statistics**: Requires a forward pass per task for SVD; uses online covariance accumulation to avoid storing full activation matrices.
  - **Layer-wise scope**: Applied only to linear/conv layers; biases and BatchNorm remain unconstrained, simplifying implementation but possibly limiting capacity.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High forgetting (BWT << 0): ε1 too large, rank too high, or weight decay on V insufficient.
  - Poor new task accuracy: ε1 too small, rank too restrictive.
  - Full-rank V growth: if input subspace near full-rank or ε1 very high, nullifying efficiency and stability benefits.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train without NESS on Task 1 to establish baseline accuracy.
  2. Sweep ε1 (e.g., {0.01, 0.001, 0.0005, 0.0001}) on CIFAR-100 (10 tasks) with SGDm to observe BWT vs. ACC trade-off.
  3. Compare NESS to GPM (gradient projection) with matched rank budgets on first 5 tasks of MiniImageNet; measure BWT, accuracy, and per-task training time.

## Limitations
- ε1 threshold selection is heuristic and dataset-dependent; no automatic tuning.
- Assumes past task input subspaces are well-separated and low-rank; unconstrained biases/BatchNorm may limit effectiveness.
- Memory overhead from per-task SVD not fully quantified; evaluation limited to image classification.

## Confidence
- Orthogonality via small-σ subspace: Medium
- Parameterization embedding constraints: High
- Stability bound via V regularization: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **ε1 Robustness Sweep**: Systematically vary ε1 across multiple datasets (e.g., Permuted MNIST, CORe50) to establish general tuning guidelines and identify failure modes.
2. **Rank-Matched Comparison to GPM**: Implement GPM with identical effective rank budgets as NESS; compare BWT, accuracy, and per-task training time to isolate efficiency gains from "by construction" stability.
3. **Projection Metric Validation**: During training, directly measure ‖xᵢᵗᵀ ΔW‖ for past task samples xᵢ to verify orthogonality guarantees hold empirically, not just via final BWT.
