# Tighnari v2: Mitigating Label Noise and Distribution Shift in Multimodal Plant Distribution Prediction via Mixture of Experts and Weakly Supervised Learning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08282
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08282
- **Reference count:** 8
- **Primary result:** Ranked 3rd in GeoLifeCLEF 2025 (private F1 = 0.21689) and outperformed the 2nd‑place 2024 method (F1 = 0.36908).

## Executive Summary
Plant species distribution prediction from presence‑only (PO) data suffers from label noise and geographic distribution shift. This paper introduces Tighnari v2, a multimodal framework that mitigates these issues through three main innovations: (1) a pseudo‑label aggregation strategy that merges PO surveys within the geographic extent of a satellite image patch to produce less noisy, PA‑like labels; (2) a stackable serial tri‑modal cross‑attention fusion mechanism that sequentially propagates information from dominant satellite imagery through tabular and temporal modalities; and (3) a Mixture of Experts (MoE) inference pipeline that partitions test samples by proximity to professional presence‑absence (PA) training data, training separate models for in‑distribution and out‑of‑distribution regions. Evaluated on the GeoLifeCLEF 2025 challenge (which includes OOD test samples), Tighnari v2 achieved 3rd place with a private test F1 of 0.21689. On the 2024 in‑distribution leaderboard, it outperformed the 2nd‑place method with 0.36908. The MoE strategy substantially improved performance under distribution shift compared to a single model trained on mixed data.

## Method Summary
The method processes three data modalities: high‑resolution Sentinel‑2 satellite patches (64×64, 10 m), medium‑resolution Landsat time‑series cubes, and tabular environmental/soil features. A Swin Transformer Base encodes the Sentinel images; a Temporal Swin Transformer encodes the Landsat time cubes; and TabM encodes the tabular features. These embeddings are fused via a stackable serial tri‑modal cross‑attention module: modalities are updated in a fixed order (Sentinel→Landsat→Tabular) where each attends to the latest features of the others using shared Key/Value projections. The fused representation is passed through a linear head producing 11 255 binary logits (one per plant species). Training uses the Asymmetric Loss (ASL) to handle class imbalance. To address label noise in PO data, the authors aggregate PO labels within the 640 m×640 m geographic footprint of each primary PO survey, merging and deduplicating species lists; a reserved‑sample filtering strategy preserves rare species. For inference, a Mixture of Experts approach partitions test samples based on spatial proximity to any PA training sample (using a BallTree with 10 km radius). Two expert models are trained: an in‑distribution (ID) model on PA data only, and an out‑of‑distribution (OOD) model trained in two stages—first on mixed PA+pseudo‑PO, then fine‑tuned on PA exclusively. Partition‑specific post‑processing (Threshold Top‑K and neighbor aggregation) is applied.

## Key Results
- GeoLifeCLEF 2025 (OOD test): 3rd place, private F1 = 0.21689.
- GeoLifeCLEF 2024 (in‑distribution): exceeded 2nd place, F1 = 0.36908.
- MoE partitioning improved OOD performance from 0.19107 (mixed PA+PO single model) to 0.21689.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Geographic aggregation of PO labels reduces negative noise
Claim: Merging PO surveys within a satellite patch creates pseudo‑PA‑like samples with denser, less noisy negatives.  
Mechanism: For each primary PO survey, all PO surveys whose locations fall within its 640 m×640 m satellite patch have their species labels merged and deduplicated. This spatial smoothing assumes high ecological similarity within the patch, turning sparse citizen‑science records into a collective quadrat‑survey analogue.  
Core assumption: Species co‑occurrence is high within 640 m, so negatives from different surveys are more likely to be true absences.  
Evidence: Abstract and Section 3.1 describe the geographic alignment; prior work “Tighnari” (ArXiv:2501.02649) used neighborhood aggregation.  
Break condition: If beta diversity is extremely high within the patch (e.g., sharp micro‑habitat gradients), aggregation may introduce false presences by mixing truly absent species with present ones.

### Mechanism 2: Serial tri‑modal cross‑attention preserves dominant modality
Claim: Stackable serial attention lets the dominant satellite modality propagate information first, capturing higher‑order dependencies without dilution.  
Mechanism: Modalities are updated sequentially (e.g., Sentinel→Landsat→Tabular). Each modality attends to the latest features of the others via shared Key/Value projections, then undergoes LayerNorm, FFN, and residual addition. This design is stackable and gives priority to the satellite stream.  
Core assumption: Satellite imagery is the most informative modality; a fixed serial order enhances its signal before other modalities influence it.  
Evidence: Section 3.3 notes satellite alone performed best; Table 3 shows serial fusion (0.21705) outperforms simple concatenation (0.20306).  
Break condition: In scenarios where satellite data is corrupted (e.g., clouds) and another modality becomes most informative, the fixed order may suppress critical information from that modality.

### Mechanism 3: MoE inference mitigates geographic distribution shift
Claim: Partitioning test samples by proximity to PA training data and using dedicated models prevents OOD performance collapse.  
Mechanism: A BallTree on PA training coordinates splits test samples: those within 10 km of any PA sample go to the ID expert; others to the OOD expert. The OOD expert is trained in two stages: initial mixed PA+pseudo‑PO (high LR) followed by PA‑only fine‑tuning (low LR). Different post‑processing rules are applied per partition.  
Core assumption: Geographic proximity to PA samples reliably indicates similarity of the underlying feature distribution.  
Evidence: Section 2.1 identifies OOD test regions (e.g., Ukraine); Section 3.6 details the MoE split; Table 4 shows OOD private score improvement from 0.19107 (single mixed model) to 0.21689.  
Break condition: If 10 km is a poor proxy for ecological similarity (e.g., due to dispersal corridors or elevation changes), samples may be misrouted, harming performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Weakly Supervised Learning (Pseudo‑labeling from presence‑only data)**  
  *Why needed:* The core challenge is leveraging vast PO data with noisy negatives. The aggregation strategy provides distant supervision by heuristically deriving absence labels from spatial co‑occurrence.  
  *Quick check:* How does aggregating labels within a satellite patch change the statistical properties of negative labels compared to raw PO negatives?

- **Distribution Shift (Geographic Covariate Shift)**  
  *Why needed:* The test set contains regions (e.g., Eastern Europe) with no PA training data, representing a covariate shift in geographic/ecological space. Models trained on the PA/PO mixture fail on these OOD samples.  
  *Quick check:* Why would a model trained on French/Dutch PA data perform poorly on Ukrainian test points, even if the environmental *features* (from satellite/tabular) are similar?

- **Multimodal Fusion with Cross‑Attention**  
  *Why needed:* Species presence depends on heterogeneous signals: high‑res spatial context (Sentinel), medium‑res temporal dynamics (Landsat), climate (bioclim), and location/trait (tabular). Cross‑attention allows modalities to query each other’s features.  
  *Quick check:* In serial cross‑attention, why might updating the satellite modality first be beneficial if it’s the most informative?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
SwinTransformerBase (Sentinel) -> TemporalSwin (Landsat) -> TabM (Tabular) -> TriSerialCrossAttention -> LinearHead (11,255 logits). MoE controller: BallTree on PA training coordinates partitions test samples by 10 km PA‑proximity.

### Critical Path
1. Preprocess: Apply Algorithm 1 (BallTree) to aggregate PO labels within 640 m patches; implement reserved‑sample filtering (strict/loose/balanced).  
2. Train ID model: Use PA data only (with ASL) → Threshold Top‑K post‑processing (threshold tuned on PA validation) + neighbor merging (5 nearest PA >80% frequency).  
3. Train OOD model: Stage 1 (3 epochs, LR=0.0001) on mixed PA+pseudo‑PO; Stage 2 (5 epochs, lower LR) fine‑tune on PA only. Post‑process: Threshold Top‑K (~0.45–0.5) + merge with 6 nearest strict‑filtered PO (>50% presence).  
4. Inference: For each test sample, query BallTree → route to ID or OOD expert → apply partition‑specific post‑processing.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Serial vs. parallel fusion:** Serial is stackable and prioritizes the dominant modality but introduces sequential dependency; parallel is faster but may dilute strong signals.  
- **Two‑stage OOD training:** Protects PA performance but risks catastrophic forgetting of PO‑only species; balance managed by ASL and fine‑tuning duration.  
- **Fixed 10 km radius:** Simple geometric rule; ignores ecological barriers (mountains, rivers) that could make a <10 km sample truly OOD.

### Failure Signatures
- **Pseudo‑label failure:** Validation W_F1 drops sharply after aggregation if local ecological turnover is high; check per‑region species accumulation curves.  
- **MoE mis‑routing:** OOD‑partitioned samples that are actually similar to PA regions receive poor OOD‑model predictions; inspect if OOD performance is region‑specific (e.g., Ukraine).  
- **Overfitting on PO:** Training loss continues to drop on PO batches while PA validation loss rises; indicates need for earlier stage switch or stronger ASL clipping.

### First 3 Experiments
1. Ablate reserved‑sample filtering strategies (loose/balanced/strict) on a PA‑validation set using raw PO as held‑out “test‑like” data; metric: per‑region species accumulation curves and W_F1 drop.  
2. Test fusion stackability by training models with 1, 2, and 3 serial cross‑attention layers; monitor gradient norms and validation W_F1 to ensure stable gains.  
3. Audit MoE partitioning accuracy: manually label “obviously OOD” regions (e.g., Ukraine). Does the 10 km BallTree correctly classify >90%? If not, adjust radius or use an environmental‑distance classifier.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Class‑dependent label noise in PO data** – The current Asymmetric Loss treats all negatives uniformly; species‑specific noise rates are not modeled. Resolving would require estimating a noise transition matrix from PA data and incorporating it into the loss.  
2. **Dynamic K prediction in post‑processing** – Fixed Threshold Top‑K may under‑ or over‑select species. Could predict K per sample from tabular features; needs a regression head and evaluation against fixed‑K baselines.  
3. **Species co‑occurrence graph via GNNs** – Independent binary classification ignores ecological correlations. A graph built from PA co‑occurrence could regularize predictions, especially for rare species.  
4. **Optimal MoE partitioning radius** – The 10 km radius is heuristic; ecological similarity decay may vary. An ablation over radii on validation could identify a better threshold.  
5. **Spatial scale of pseudo‑label aggregation** – The 640 m patch size is assumed optimal; different scales may better balance noise reduction vs. fine‑grained information. Test alternative aggregation radii (e.g., 320 m, 1280 m) and compare performance across species types.

## Limitations
- The 640 m pseudo‑label aggregation radius may fail in high beta‑diversity areas, risking positive label noise.  
- Serial fusion assumes satellite imagery is always dominant; corrupted satellite data (e.g., clouds) could break this assumption.  
- The 10 km geometric proxy for PA‑proximity ignores ecological barriers, potentially misrouting test samples.  
- Critical hyperparameters for the OOD model’s Stage 2 fine‑tuning are unreported, hindering exact reproduction.  
- The “Threshold Top‑K” post‑processing implementation details are ambiguous.

## Confidence
- **Pseudo‑label aggregation mitigates negative noise:** High  
- **Serial cross‑attention improves fusion:** Medium  
- **MoE partitioning handles distribution shift:** Medium
