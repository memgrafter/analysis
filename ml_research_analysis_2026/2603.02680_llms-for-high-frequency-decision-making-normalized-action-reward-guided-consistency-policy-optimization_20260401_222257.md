# LLMs for High-Frequency Decision-Making: Normalized Action Reward-Guided Consistency Policy Optimization
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02680
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02680
- **Reference count:** 6
- **Primary result:** NAR-CP achieves up to 96.79% general success in UAV pursuit with ~114 ms inference latency and near-zero policy bias, addressing LLM failures in high-frequency decision-making via reward normalization and policy alignment.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) struggle with high-frequency decision-making due to sparse numerical rewards and misalignment between sub-task and composite task policies. This paper introduces NAR-CP, a two-part solution: (1) Normalized Action Reward (NAR) applies Z-score normalization to dense environmental rewards, amplifying gradient signals while preserving optimal policy, and (2) Decoupled Consistency Policy (CP) aligns the joint distribution of independent sub-task policies with the composite policy via KL divergence on a Top-K action subset. In UAV pursuit experiments, NAR-CP significantly outperforms baselines, achieving high success rates and dramatically lower inference latency, though performance declines in complex novel environments.

## Method Summary
NAR-CP fine-tunes an LLM (Qwen2.5-3B) using PPO for high-frequency discrete control. First, for a given state, a dense reward *R_D* is computed for each candidate action. NAR normalizes these rewards per timestep (Z-score) to combat vanishing gradients from low-variance rewards, theoretically preserving optimality via a potential function. Second, CP addresses policy misalignment: the LLM infers separate sub-task policies from decoupled observations, whose product forms a joint policy. The composite policy is generated from a unified prompt. A consistency loss minimizes the KL divergence between the Top-K actions of the joint policy and the composite policy. This loss is added to the PPO objective, aligning global and sub-semantic decisions. Training uses LoRA for parameter efficiency.

## Key Results
- NAR-CP achieves 96.79% general and 91.33% precise success rates for direction tracking, with policy bias reduced to 0.006.
- Inference latency drops from ~1968 ms (base LLM) to ~114 ms, enabling real-time operation.
- Generalization to unseen scenarios (e.g., randomized distances) is effective, but performance severely degrades in complex novel composite tasks (e.g., 19.62% → 1.08% general success).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Normalized Action Reward (NAR) amplifies weak gradient signals from numerically dense but low-variance rewards, enabling effective policy convergence in high-frequency decision-making, without altering the optimal policy.
- **Mechanism:** Given a set of candidate actions at timestep *t*, compute the mean (µ) and standard deviation (σ) of the raw dense rewards *R_D*. Apply Z-score normalization: *R_N = (R_D - µ) / (σ + ε₀)*. This rescales rewards to unit variance, increasing the gradient norm ∥∇J(θ)∥ above the effective update threshold (≥10⁻²). A potential function *ϕ(s) = µ_D / ((σ_D + ε₀)(1-γ))* is defined to show the normalized reward can be expressed as *R_N = (1/(σ+ε₀))R_D + γϕ(s') - ϕ(s)*, which is a reward transformation preserving optimality.
- **Core assumption:** The minimal reward differences (diameter κ ~ 10⁻³–10⁻²) cause gradient vanishing; normalizing per-timestep candidate action sets effectively amplifies signal. The state transition between adjacent steps is negligible, allowing the potential function derivation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "applies Z-score normalization to dense environmental rewards, amplifying gradient signals while theoretically preserving optimal policy."
  - [section 3.2] Formal derivation of Eq. (8)–(13), proving *π*ₘₙ = π*ₘᴅ*.
  - [corpus] Neighbors discuss reward normalization (e.g., "Normalized Weight Functions" in 2406.10795) and vanishing gradient issues ("Hybrid Differential Reward" in 2511.16916), but do not directly validate this specific theoretical preservation claim.
- **Break condition:** If the candidate action set size |A_t| is too small (< 3), σ becomes unreliable, destabilizing normalization. If state transitions are large (non-negligible), the potential function derivation fails, potentially biasing the optimal policy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Decoupled Consistency Policy (CP) reduces policy misalignment between joint sub-task policies and the composite task policy by minimizing their KL divergence over a Top-K action subset, enabling knowledge reuse without semantic disruption.
- **Mechanism:** Train independent sub-task policies πᵢ(aᵢ|sᵢ). Form the joint policy π_Joint({aᵢ}|{sᵢ}) = ∏πᵢ. For a composite observation *s*, extract the Top-K highest-probability actions from π_Joint to form A_top-k. Compute the composite policy π_Full(a|s) via the LLM on the unified composite prompt. Define the consistency loss L_consistency = D_KL(π_J-top-k ∥ π_F-top-k) over A_top-k. This loss term (weighted by α_c) is added to the PPO policy loss, aligning the distributions.
- **Core assumption:** The optimal composite policy should equal the joint policy of sub-tasks (π_Full(a|s) ≈ π_Joint({aᵢ}|{sᵢ})). Top-K selection provides a computationally tractable alignment target that preserves the core decision consensus.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "consistency loss ensuring precise alignment between global semantic policies and sub-semantic policies."
  - [section 3.3] Equations (15)–(19) and Fig. 2 detail the Top-K selection and KL divergence construction.
  - [corpus] Neighbors explore policy decomposition (e.g., "Rewoo" in 2305.18323) and reward-guided search (e.g., "From Outcomes to Processes" in 2506.12446), but the specific KL consistency mechanism for composite LLM policies is novel in this corpus.
- **Break condition:** If sub-tasks are not truly independent, the joint product assumption fails, causing irreconcilable misalignment. If Top-K is too small (k << |Aᵢ|), it may exclude the true optimal composite action, harming performance. LLM-generated π_Full-top-k must be well-calibrated; severe miscalibration makes KL minimization misdirected.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The combination of NAR and CP creates a synergistic effect where stable sub-task learning (from NAR) feeds a consistent joint policy, which CP then aligns to the composite task, yielding superior convergence and reduced bias.
- **Mechanism:** NAR ensures each sub-task policy πᵢ converges reliably by providing strong, non-biasing gradient signals. These converged πᵢ form a stable π_Joint. CP then aligns π_Full to π_Joint, transferring the sub-task competency to the composite scenario without relearning from scratch. The ablation study (Table 4) shows both components are necessary; each alone leaves significant performance gaps or policy bias.
- **Core assumption:** Sub-tasks are foundational building blocks for the composite task. Improving sub-task policy quality (via NAR) directly improves the quality of the joint policy that CP aims to match.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.5] Ablation: "NAR CP" (✓✓) achieves 82.54% general rate and 0.008 bias, while single-component ablations (✓✗, ✗✓) perform poorly (≤78.96% general, bias ≥0.025 or 7.174).
  - [section 4.2] Training curves (Fig. 3) show NAR-CP converges faster and higher than baselines (Twosome, Twosome-DR) across all tasks.
  - [corpus] No direct neighbor evidence; this synergistic design is a novel architecture contribution of this paper.
- **Break condition:** If sub-tasks are not well-defined or are conflicting, improving them individually may not help the composite task. CP's KL loss could force a poor composite policy to mimic a flawed joint policy if NAR fails to deliver converged sub-task policies.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Markov Decision Process (MDP) & Reward Shaping**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper frames LLM decision-making as an MDP (M_num-LLM). Understanding MDPs is essential to grasp how dense rewards *R_D* and normalized rewards *R_N* affect policy optimization (Bellman equations, Eq. 1–2, 11–13). Reward shaping (dense vs. sparse) is the core problem NAR addresses.
  - *Quick check question:* Why does potential-based reward shaping (Ng et al., 1999) preserve optimal policy, and how does the paper's normalization (Eq. 8) relate to this principle?
- **Concept: Policy Gradient & Gradient Vanishing**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper's central challenge is that small reward variance (Eq. 4–5) causes gradient norm ∥∇J(θ)∥ < 10⁻² (Wei et al., 2025), stalling learning. NAR explicitly manipulates reward variance (Eq. 9) to combat this.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the variance of the reward estimate (Var(∇J(θ))) depend on Var(R_D) and why does Z-score normalization fix it?
- **Concept: KL Divergence & Policy Distillation**
  - *Why needed here:* CP uses KL divergence (Eq. 19) to align distributions. This is akin to knowledge distillation where the joint policy (teacher) guides the composite policy (student). Understanding KL as a measure of distributional discrepancy is key.
  - *Quick check question:* In Eq. (19), why is KL computed only over the Top-K set A_top-k instead of the full action space? What are the trade-offs?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  LLM Agent (Qwen2.5-3B) -> Reward Model -> Normalization Module -> Sub-Observation Decoupler -> Sub-Policies (π₁, π₂,...) -> Joint Policy Constructor -> Top-K Selector -> Consistency Loss Calculator -> PPO Trainer (with LoRA) -> Action Execution -> Environment
- **Critical path:** Composite observation s -> Decouple into {sᵢ} -> Infer πᵢ for each -> Form π_Joint = ∏πᵢ -> Select A_top-k from π_Joint -> Generate π_Full from composite prompt -> Compute L_consistency = KL(π_J-top-k ∥ π_F-top-k) -> Sample action a_t from π_Full (or π_J) -> Execute -> Get state S_num' -> Compute dense R_D(s,a) -> Normalize to R_N -> Update LLM and critic with PPO loss + α_c·L_consistency.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Discrete action space:* Enables clear candidate set A_t for normalization and Top-K selection, but limits applicability to continuous control.
  - *Top-K alignment:* Balances computational cost (k small) with alignment fidelity (k large). Too small loses optimal action; too large increases noise.
  - *Separate vs. unified training:* Decoupling sub-tasks improves sample efficiency but risks semantic inconsistency; CP mitigates this at inference cost.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - *Training instability:* Reward normalization fails if |A_t| is small or σ_D,t ≈ 0 → check candidate action sampling diversity.
  - *High policy bias:* L_consistency weight α_c too low → joint and composite policies diverge; too high → over-constrains composite policy, hurting task-specific optimization.
  - *Poor generalization:* Model relies on exact numerical patterns from training; test scenarios with novel distributions or dynamics cause failure.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Comparison Experiment:** Train NAR-CP vs. baselines (Qwen2.5-3B, Twosome, Twosome-DR) on Direction/Distance/Integrated tracking. NAR-CP shows higher reward curves and success rates, demonstrating improved convergence and reduced bias.
  2. **Generalization Experiment:** Test trained NAR-CP on unseen scenarios (randomized distances, circular trajectories, composite unknowns). General/Precise rates drop in complex novel environments, revealing limitations of discrete action space and distributional shift.
  3. **Decision Latency Verification:** Measure inference time from numerical text input to action output. NAR-CP (~114 ms) is significantly faster than base Qwen2.5-3B (~1968 ms) due to shorter prompt length and decoupled reasoning, validating real-time feasibility.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Continuous action space extension:** How can NAR-CP be effectively extended from discrete to continuous action spaces for truly agentic high-frequency decision-making? The current design assumes discrete candidate sets.
2. **Generalization failure factors:** What specific factors in "complex novel environments" cause the significant performance drop, and can the method's generalization be improved? The paper attributes this to discrete action space but does not isolate root causes.
3. **Synergistic vs. additive effects:** Are the improvements from NAR-CP primarily due to additive effects of its two modules, or is there a critical synergistic interaction? Ablation shows both help but does not test conditional dependencies.
4. **Theoretical guarantee robustness:** How sensitive is the theoretical guarantee of optimal policy preservation under NAR to violations of its assumptions, particularly the potential function ϕ(s) being action-independent and state differences being negligible? Real-world stochasticity may break these assumptions.

## Limitations
- Generalization to complex novel environments is weak (e.g., success rate drops to 1.08% in complex composite tasks).
- Method is limited to discrete action spaces; continuous control is not validated and remains an open challenge.
- Key implementation details (prompt templates, reward coefficients, PPO hyperparameters) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- **Normalized Action Reward (NAR): Medium** – Proof holds under assumptions; empirical validation only in discrete UAV setting.
- **Decoupled Consistency Policy (CP): Medium** – Ablation confirms benefit but independence assumption not tested.
- **Synergy of NAR+CP: Low** – Observed improvement but no theoretical or causal linkage established.

## Next Checks
1. **Continuous-action test:** Implement NAR-CP for continuous UAV controls (e.g., thrust vectoring) and compare against discrete baseline to check scalability.
2. **Sensitivity analysis:** Vary Top-K (1,5,20,full) and candidate pool size (3,10,100) during training; monitor stability, bias, and convergence to define safe operating boundaries.
3. **Theoretical stress test:** Exaggerate UAV dynamics (e.g., 5× velocity) to break the negligible-transition assumption; measure performance drop relative to an optimal policy oracle to quantify degradation of NAR's guarantee.
