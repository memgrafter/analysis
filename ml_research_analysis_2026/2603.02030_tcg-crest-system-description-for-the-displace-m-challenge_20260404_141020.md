# TCG CREST System Description for the DISPLACE-M Challenge

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02030
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02030
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** The Diarizen system reduced relative DER by 39% over a SpeechBrain baseline, achieving 10.37% (dev) and 9.21% (eval) with AHC clustering and a median filter window of 29, ranking 5th among 11 Phase I participants.

## Executive Summary
This work tackles speaker diarization in noisy, spontaneous rural healthcare conversations from the DISPLACE-M challenge. It compares a modular SpeechBrain pipeline (ECAPA-TDNN embeddings) with the hybrid end-to-end Diarizen system, which uses a pre-trained WavLM backbone. Extensive evaluation of voice activity detection and clustering algorithms found that Diarizen with Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering (AHC) and a median filtering window of 29 frames performed best. This configuration achieved a 39% relative diarization error rate (DER) reduction versus the baseline, reaching 10.37% on the development set and 9.21% on the evaluation set.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates two diarization systems on DISPLACE-M Phase I data (78 dev, 71 eval files). The **SpeechBrain pipeline** used separate VAD (Silero or Pyannote) and ECAPA-TDNN speaker embeddings (pre-trained on VoxCeleb2), followed by clustering. The **Diarizen system** is a hybrid end-to-end architecture: a pruned WavLM model with a Conformer block processes short, overlapping audio chunks to produce frame-level speech posteriors and 192-dimensional speaker embeddings. These embeddings are clustered globally using algorithms like AHC, VBx, k-means, or spectral clustering variants. A median filter smooths the final per-frame labels. The best configuration used Diarizen with AHC and a median filter window of 29. All evaluations used the challenge’s DER metric and official scoring script.

## Key Results
- Diarizen with AHC and median filter window 29 achieved DERs of **10.37% (dev)** and **9.21% (eval)**.
- This represented a **~39% relative DER reduction** compared to the SpeechBrain baseline.
- The team ranked **5th among 11 participants** in DISPLACE-M Phase I.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: End-to-End Neural Diarization with Pre-trained WavLM
**Claim:** Diarizen's hybrid end-to-end design with a frozen, pre-trained WavLM backbone yields more robust frame-level speaker embeddings and voice activity detection in noisy, conversational audio than a modular pipeline with separately trained VAD and ECAPA-TDNN embeddings.

**Mechanism:** Diarizen processes raw audio in short, overlapping chunks via a single large self-supervised model (WavLM), fine-tuned for diarization. This joint architecture allows the model to learn representations specifically attuned to the diarization task and to the dataset's noise and channel characteristics. The embeddings are then clustered globally.

**Core assumption:** A general-purpose pre-trained model like WavLM, when adapted to diarization, transfers better to the challenging acoustic conditions (rural healthcare noise, spontaneous speech) than a model (ECAPA-TDNN) trained primarily on clean, read speech (VoxCeleb2).

**Evidence anchors:**  
- The paper reports a ~39% relative DER reduction for Diarizen vs. SpeechBrain.  
- Section 2.2 describes Diarizen's architecture and its use of WavLM for joint speech detection and embedding extraction.

**Break condition:** If the target domain's acoustic conditions diverge significantly from WavLM's pre-training corpus, the transfer advantage may diminish. The mechanism would break if a modular system with an in-domain fine-tuned VAD and embedding extractor outperformed the frozen WavLM frontend.

### Mechanism 2: Simplicity of AHC for Two-Speaker Diarization
**Claim:** Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering (AHC) outperforms more complex spectral clustering variants for this dataset when used with Diarizen embeddings.

**Mechanism:** With high-quality, discriminative embeddings from Diarizen, the simple cosine similarity and a well-tuned distance threshold in AHC are sufficient to separate the two speakers. Spectral clustering's graph construction and eigenvector steps introduce sensitivity to parameters (neighborhood size, affinity kernel) that is unnecessary for the relatively clean embedding space and low speaker count (2).

**Core assumption:** The Diarizen embedding space is already well-separated for the two-speaker case, making a distance-based algorithm like AHC more robust and parameter-stable than graph-based methods.

**Evidence anchors:**  
- Table 1 shows AHC (10.54% Dev) performs at least as well as spectral variants (e.g., SC-adapt 10.48%).  
- Section 4 states other clustering techniques did not perform better than AHC in the Diarizen pipeline.

**Break condition:** If the number of speakers increases substantially beyond 2 or the embedding space becomes noisier/more overlapping (e.g., higher overlap percentage), spectral clustering's ability to model non-convex clusters might provide an advantage over AHC's spherical cluster assumption.

### Mechanism 3: Longer Median Filter for Temporal Consistency
**Claim:** Applying a median filter with a larger window (29 vs. default 11) reduces DER by more aggressively suppressing short, spurious speaker turn predictions and enforcing temporal consistency.

**Mechanism:** A median filter operates on the final per-frame speaker label sequence. A larger window (e.g., 29 frames ≈0.6s) determines a frame's label by majority over a longer temporal context, smoothing out brief misassignments from momentary acoustic confusion or VAD errors common in spontaneous, overlapping speech.

**Core assumption:** The conversational dynamics (average ~16 turns/minute) and primary error sources (brief misassignments) are such that a longer context improves segmentation without incorrectly merging distinct, rapid speaker turns.

**Evidence anchors:**  
- Section 4 reports that increasing the window from 11 to 29 improved DER by a relative 1.61%, yielding 10.37%.  
- Section 1 notes active turn-taking at approximately 16 turns per minute, supporting that turns are not extremely rapid.

**Break condition:** In conversations with extremely rapid turn-taking (e.g., >30-40 turns/minute) or many very short interjections, a large window (29) could incorrectly merge two different speakers' activity into one segment, increasing confusion error.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (raw waveform) -> Diarizen Neural Frontend (WavLM + Conformer) -> Frame-level speech posteriors & speaker embeddings -> Clustering (AHC/VBx/k-means/SC variants) -> Per-frame speaker labels -> Median filter -> Output (RTTM)
- **Critical path:** Diarizen embeddings quality -> AHC clustering threshold -> median filter window (size 29). Degradation in any step directly impacts DER.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *End-to-end vs. modular:* Diarizen trades modularity for joint optimization, yielding better VAD/embedding co-adaptation but higher model complexity (~32M params) and less interpretability.  
  - *Clustering simplicity vs. sophistication:* AHC offers stability and fewer hyperparameters for 2-speaker cases; spectral clustering may help with more speakers or noisier embeddings.  
  - *Temporal smoothing aggressiveness:* Larger median filter (29) reduces fragmentation but risks over-smoothing rapid turns; chosen window matches corpus turn-taking rate (~16/min).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *High speech/non-speech error:* Diarizen VAD failure starves pipeline of embeddings. Diagnose by visualizing speech probabilities vs. reference.  
  - *Cluster merging/splitting:* AHC threshold too low causes speaker split; too high causes merge. Inspect dendrogram cut or affinity matrix for high-DER files.  
  - *Anomalous file:* `3836246.wav` showed ~50% DER for both systems, likely due to annotation error or atypical conditions (extreme noise, 3+ speakers).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Run Diarizen with **Oracle VAD** (from ground truth) to isolate embedding+clustering upper bound, then with default VAD to quantify VAD's contribution.  
  2. On Dev set, fix Diarizen embeddings and median filter (11), sweep **AHC threshold** and compare to all spectral clustering variants (tune their hyperparameters).  
  3. Using best AHC setting, sweep **median filter window** (11,15,21,25,29,33) on Dev; plot DER vs. window to confirm sweet spot at 29 and validate on Eval.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** To what extent can more robust VAD methods improve modular pipelines (like SpeechBrain) in noisy rural healthcare conversations?  
  **Basis:** Authors note SpeechBrain performed well with high-quality VAD but degraded with standard estimated VADs; they did not explore other robust VADs.
- **Question 2:** Can fusing the complementary strengths of Diarizen and SpeechBrain lead to a lower DER than either alone?  
  **Basis:** File-wise analysis showed each system excelled on different recordings; fusion strategies were proposed but not implemented.
- **Question 3:** Would supervised fine-tuning of Diarizen on the development set improve DER, and by how much?  
  **Basis:** The study used an unsupervised setting; fine-tuning could potentially adapt the model to domain-specific conditions.
- **Question 4:** Can a structured statistical model using recording-level features (e.g., overlap, SNR, turn-taking) predict and explain DER variability across sessions?  
  **Basis:** Exploratory feature analysis was done, but no multivariate model was built to formally relate session properties to error.

## Limitations
- **Critical hyperparameters omitted:** Exact AHC distance threshold and Diarizen's internal VAD decision thresholds are not specified, forcing reliance on default settings or Dev-set tuning.
- **Generalizability uncertain:** The WavLM transfer advantage is validated only on this specific corpus; performance on other domains (more speakers, different noise/dialects) is unknown.
- **Anomalous file cause unknown:** File `3836246.wav` had ~50% DER for both systems, but the root cause (e.g., annotation error, extreme conditions) was not diagnosed, limiting error analysis.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Diarizen vs. Modular): High** – Strong evidence from ~39% DER reduction and clear architectural justification.
- **Mechanism 2 (AHC Superiority): Medium** – Supported by Table 1, but advantage is narrow (10.54% vs. 10.48%) and context-specific (2-speaker, clean embeddings).
- **Mechanism 3 (Median Filter Window 29): Medium** – Evidence shows improvement over 11, but justification relies on assumed turn-taking rate (~16/min); break condition (rapid turns) plausible but untested.
