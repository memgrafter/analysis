# VISion On Request: Enhanced VLLM efficiency with sparse, dynamically selected, vision-language interactions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.23495
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23495
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: VISOR achieves up to 8.6× FLOPs reduction while matching or exceeding state-of-the-art accuracy across benchmarks; combined with token reduction, up to 18× FLOPs savings.

## Executive Summary
Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) suffer from efficiency bottlenecks due to the quadratic cost of processing visual tokens. VISOR addresses this by sparsifying layer interactions rather than discarding visual tokens, thereby avoiding information loss that harms fine-grained reasoning. It inserts efficient cross-attention layers to provide visual context to text tokens and a small, dynamically selected set of self-attention layers to refine visual representations. A universal model trained across computational budgets, paired with a lightweight policy router, selects the optimal per-sample configuration. Experiments demonstrate up to 8.6× FLOPs reduction with competitive accuracy and up to 18× when combined with token reduction, with significant gains on complex, high-resolution tasks.

## Method Summary
VISOR modifies a standard LVLM (e.g., LLaVA-OV) by adding two types of layers to the LLM: cross-attention (CA) layers where text tokens attend to static visual tokens (cheap, O(N_t * N_v * d)) and self-attention (SA) layers that process the full concatenated sequence (expensive, O((N_t+N_v)^2 * d)). The number of CA and SA layers is limited (up to L/3 each) and uniformly distributed. This design retains all visual tokens but selectively updates them.

Training proceeds in two stages: first, pre-train a model with maximum CA and SA layers on a 4M knowledge dataset, freezing the vision encoder and connector; then fine-tune the entire model on a 3.2M high-quality dataset. Crucially, during training a random viable configuration (a subset of SA layers, with all CA layers always active) is sampled per step, yielding a universal model robust to different computational budgets. At inference, a lightweight MLP router attached to a special routing token predicts the optimal configuration for each sample. The router is trained offline: for each training sample, the most efficient configuration achieving ≥99% of full-model accuracy and lowest loss is selected as the label.

## Key Results
- Up to 8.6× reduction in transformer FLOPs (excluding vision encoder) while matching or exceeding SOTA accuracy on 12 vision-language benchmarks.
- Significant improvements on complex, high-resolution tasks requiring detailed visual understanding compared to prior token reduction methods.
- When combined with token reduction (e.g., VisionZip), further FLOPs savings up to 18× are achieved.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
VISOR decouples visual context injection from visual token refinement. Cross-attention layers provide cheap, static visual context to all text tokens without modifying visual tokens. Self-attention layers, which are computationally heavy, are applied sparingly and only to a dynamically chosen subset, thereby refining visual representations for fine-grained reasoning only when needed. The universal training ensures that any subset of SA layers can produce reasonable outputs, while the router learns to predict per-sample difficulty (hence required refinement) from a routing token. This dynamic allocation avoids the information loss inherent in token reduction, especially on hard tasks, yet retains compatibility with token reduction for additional savings.

## Foundational Learning
- **Cross-Attention (CA)**: Allows text tokens to attend to static visual tokens (no visual token update). *Why needed*: Provides visual context at low computational cost. *Quick check*: Are visual tokens updated in CA? No.
- **Self-Attention (SA)**: Processes the full sequence of text and visual tokens, updating both. *Why needed*: Enables fine-grained interactions and refinement of visual tokens. *Quick check*: What is the computational complexity of SA? Quadratic in sequence length.
- **Universal Training**: Trains a single model on multiple sub-network configurations (different SA layer subsets). *Why needed*: Shares parameters across computational budgets, eliminating separate models. *Quick check*: Does the universal model match the accuracy of individually trained models? - **Policy Router**: A lightweight MLP that predicts the optimal configuration from a special routing token. *Why needed*: Dynamically allocates computation per sample based on difficulty. *Quick check*: What is the router's input? A special token appended to the prompt.
- **Viable Configurations**: The set of SA layer subsets that maintain reasonable accuracy when used with all CA layers. *Why needed*: Defines the search space for the router and universal training. *Quick check*: How are viable configurations identified? By evaluating accuracy of models with different SA subsets on a validation set.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Vision Encoder (SigLIP) -> MLP Connector -> LLM (Qwen2) with inserted CA/SA layers -> Router (MLP on routing token). Optional: Token reduction module before connector.

**Critical path**: For a given image and text: image -> vision encoder -> visual tokens (N_v). Text tokens plus a special routing token are embedded. The LLM processes the concatenated sequence. At predetermined CA layers, only text tokens attend to static visual tokens (visual tokens not updated). At SA layers (active ones according to configuration), the full sequence (text+visual) undergoes self-attention, updating all tokens. The router, attached to the routing token's representation before the first SA layer, predicts which subset of SA layers to activate for the remainder of the forward pass.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Number of CA layers: More CA layers inject visual context at many depths but do not refine visual tokens; too few may limit performance.
- Number of SA layers: More SA layers improve fine-grained reasoning but increase FLOPs quadratically; the viable set balances accuracy and efficiency.
- Universal training vs. specialized models: Universal training shares parameters across budgets, reducing storage and training cost, but may not reach peak accuracy of a single-budget model.
- Router complexity: A more powerful router could make better predictions but adds overhead; the paper uses a simple MLP.
- Token reduction: Higher reduction yields more savings but harms hard tasks; combination with VISOR can offset some loss but the optimal tradeoff per task is unknown.

**Failure signatures**:
- Configuration collapse: router always predicts the same configuration (often minimal). Detect by low entropy of router outputs.
- Universal training instability: after training, some viable configurations still underperform relative to the full model. Check per-configuration accuracy spread.
- Degradation on hard tasks with token reduction: when combining with aggressive token reduction, performance drops significantly on fine-grained benchmarks (e.g., ChartQA, DocVQA). Monitor these datasets specifically.
- Router overfitting: pseudo-labels may not generalize; if router accuracy on validation is much higher than on test, it may be overfitting to the pseudo-label set.

**Three first experiments**:
1. Build the base model: take a pretrained LLaVA-OV (0.5B) checkpoint, insert CA and SA layers at equally spaced intervals (e.g., for a 24-layer LLM, insert up to 8 CA layers at layers 3,6,9,... and up to 8 SA layers at layers 4,7,10,...). Initialize new layers with standard deviations.
2. Train the universal VISOR: first stage, freeze vision encoder, connector, and LLM except the inserted CA/SA layers; train on the 4M knowledge dataset with AdamW (lr=1e-4, batch=128) for one epoch. Second stage, unfreeze all and fine-tune on the 3.2M high-quality dataset (lr=1e-5, batch=128) for two epochs. At each training step, randomly sample one viable configuration (subset of SA layers, all CA layers active) and run forward/backward with that sub-network.
3. Train the router: after universal training, generate pseudo-labels by evaluating the universal model on a random subset (e.g., 10%) of the 3.2M training data. For each sample, run all viable configurations, compute loss and accuracy; select the most efficient configuration that achieves at least 99% of the full model's accuracy and has the lowest loss. Then train the router MLP (attached to the routing token) with cross-entropy loss to predict this configuration. Use a held-out validation set to monitor router accuracy and configuration distribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does the design of the routing policy network impact its ability to generalize to unseen tasks or samples?**  
   The paper uses a simple MLP trained via offline pseudo-labeling but does not analyze sensitivity to architecture or training objective. Ablations on router capacity and evaluation on out-of-distribution datasets are needed.

2. **What are the precise, sample-level visual or linguistic features that correlate with the need for additional self-attention layers?**  
   The router makes per-sample decisions but the criteria are not extracted. A correlation analysis between input features (e.g., query complexity, image entropy) and chosen configuration would provide interpretability.

3. **For the universal model trained across multiple computational budgets, is random sampling of configurations optimal?**  
   Training uses uniform random sampling from a viable set. Alternative strategies (curriculum, importance-weighted) might improve coverage or reduce required configurations.

4. **What is the theoretical or empirical boundary where combining VISOR with token reduction becomes counterproductive?**  
   The paper shows synergistic savings but does not analyze when information loss from token reduction conflicts with sparse attention. A systematic study varying both factors and a joint meta-router could define the optimal frontier.

5. **Does the VISOR principle scale effectively to larger LVLM architectures and to multimodal inputs beyond single-image VQA?**  
   Experiments are on small models (0.5B, 1.5B) and single-image tasks. Extension to video, multi-image, and larger backbones is needed to test generality.

## Limitations
- **Unspecified viable configuration set**: The exact set of self-attention layer subsets used during universal training and evaluation is not provided. This omission hinders exact replication and prevents a precise understanding of the computational budget coverage or comparison with other dynamic methods.
- **Incomplete pseudo-labeling details**: The offline routing training procedure (aggregate loss metric, calibration of the 99% threshold, and data subset) is vague. Without these details, it is challenging to reproduce the router's training or assess its generalizability and robustness.
- **Dependence on external token reduction methods**: The claimed 18× FLOPs savings rely on combining VISOR with token reduction techniques (e.g., VisionZip). The integration process is not fully described, making it difficult to independently verify the combined savings or to apply VISOR without those methods.

## Confidence
- **Efficiency-accuracy trade-off**: Medium confidence. The architectural idea is sound, but missing configuration details prevent exact replication of the reported results and precise assessment of the trade-off curve.
- **Superiority on complex tasks**: Medium confidence. Benchmarks are standard and results are promising, but per-dataset performance depends on the unknown configuration set, which may introduce variability and limit generalizability claims.
- **Combination with token reduction**: Medium-Low confidence. This relies on external token reduction methods and precise integration details that are not fully described, making the 18× savings claim difficult to verify independently and the optimal combination points unclear.
