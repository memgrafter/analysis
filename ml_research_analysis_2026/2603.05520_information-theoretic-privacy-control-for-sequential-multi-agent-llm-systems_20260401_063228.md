# Information-Theoretic Privacy Control for Sequential Multi-Agent LLM Systems
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05520
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05520
- **Reference count:** 24
- **Primary result:** A privacy-regularized training framework (MINE-Reg) using neural mutual information estimation reduces cumulative privacy leakage by 75–90% in sequential multi-agent LLM pipelines while preserving task utility.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles **compositional privacy leakage** in sequential multi-agent LLM systems, where sensitive information propagates and amplifies through intermediate outputs even if each agent locally satisfies privacy constraints. The authors propose **MINE-Reg**, a training framework that explicitly penalizes the mutual information between each agent’s output and its local sensitive variables using a neural estimator (MINE). Theoretically, they show global leakage can grow exponentially with pipeline depth under per-agent constraints. Empirically, across medical, financial, and privacy-specific benchmarks with 2–5 agent pipelines, MINE-Reg substantially reduces measured mutual information and improves privacy metrics (e.g., Sensitive Blocked, PARI) with minimal utility loss. The work demonstrates that system-level privacy cannot rely on local constraints alone and offers a practical, train-time regularization approach.

## Method Summary
The core method is **MINE-Reg**, a privacy-regularized training framework for sequential agent pipelines. For each agent \(i\) in a chain of \(N\) agents, a separate MINE (Mutual Information Neural Estimation) critic network is trained to estimate \(\hat{I}_{\text{MINE}}(O_i; S_i)\), the mutual information between agent \(i\)’s output \(O_i\) and its local sensitive variable \(S_i\). Training alternates between three phases per minibatch: (1) forward pass through all agents to compute task utility loss (cross-entropy or accuracy), (2) maximizing the Donsker–Varadhan (DV) bound w.r.t. each critic’s parameters to refine MI estimates, and (3) minimizing the total loss \(L_{\text{total}} = L_{\text{utility}} + \sum_i \beta_i \hat{I}_{\text{MINE}}(O_i; S_i)\) w.r.t. the agents’ LoRA adapter parameters. Critics are 2-layer MLPs (hidden dim 256, ReLU). Minibatch shuffling provides marginal samples for MINE. Privacy weights \(\beta_i\) are hyperparameters balancing privacy and utility.

## Key Results
- **MI Reduction:** Average mutual information between final outputs and all sensitive variables reduced by 75–90% compared to unregularized baselines.
- **Utility Preservation:** Task utility (accuracy/CE) largely maintained across MedQA, FinQA, and PrivacyLens benchmarks.
- **Metric Improvement:** Sensitive Blocked rates improved by 0.35–0.50 points; composite PARI metric increased substantially (e.g., 0.477→0.903 on MedQA).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism directly targets **compositional leakage** by penalizing local MI during training. MINE provides a differentiable, neural estimate of \(I(O_i; S_i)\) that can be minimized via gradient descent. By including \(\hat{I}_{\text{MINE}}(O_i; S_i)\) in the loss for *each* agent, the framework forces every intermediate output to reveal less about its *own* sensitive data, which theoretically and empirically bounds the global leakage \(I(O_N; S_1,\dots,S_N)\). The alternating optimization separates critic convergence from agent updates, stabilizing MI estimation. The exponential growth bound arises because, without such regularization, each agent’s output can act as a new source of leakage for downstream agents, leading to multiplicative effects.

## Foundational Learning
- **Mutual Information (MI):** Quantifies information shared between two variables. *Why needed:* The privacy goal is to minimize information flow from sensitive variables \(S_i\) to outputs \(O_i\) and ultimately to \(O_N\). *Quick check:* Verify that \(I(X;Y)=0\) iff \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent.
- **MINE (Mutual Information Neural Estimation):** Uses a neural network (critic) to estimate MI via the Donsker–Varadhan bound. *Why needed:* Provides a differentiable, trainable estimator for MI in high-dimensional spaces (LLM embeddings). *Quick check:* Implement a simple MINE for Gaussian variables and compare to analytical MI.
- **Compositional Privacy Leakage:** Privacy loss that accumulates when private information passes through multiple, locally private modules. *Why needed:* Highlights that local differential privacy or local MI constraints are insufficient for system-level privacy. *Quick check:* Construct a two-step pipeline where each step leaks a little; measure total leakage vs. sum of local leakages.
- **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation):** Parameter-efficient fine-tuning by injecting trainable low-rank matrices. *Why needed:* Enables applying MINE-Reg to large LLMs without full fine-tuning. *Quick check:* Apply LoRA to a small transformer and verify it learns a downstream task.
- **DV Bound (Donsker–Varadhan):** A variational representation of KL divergence used to upper-bound MI. *Why needed:* MINE optimizes this bound to estimate MI. *Quick check:* Derive DV bound from KL definition.
- **Privacy-Aware Reasoning Index (PARI):** Composite metric balancing utility and privacy. *Why needed:* Provides a single number to evaluate trade-offs. *Quick check:* Recompute PARI from reported SB and BS scores.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Sensitive Variables \(S_i\) → Agent \(i\) (LLM + LoRA) → Output \(O_i\) → [MINE Critic \(i\) estimates \(\hat{I}(O_i;S_i)\)] → Loss \(L_{\text{total}}\) → Agent \(i\) update  
(Repeat for \(i=1..N\) in sequential order; final output \(O_N\) used for utility loss)

**Critical Path:**  
1. Data batch with \(S_i\) and task labels \(y\).  
2. Forward pass: \(O_1 \to O_2 \to \dots \to O_N\).  
3. Task utility loss \(L_{\text{utility}}(O_N, y)\).  
4. For each agent \(i\):  
   a. Sample joint \((O_i, S_i)\) and marginal \((O_i, \tilde{S}_i)\) from minibatch.  
   b. Update MINE critic \(i\) to maximize DV bound → \(\hat{I}(O_i;S_i)\).  
   c. Compute \(L_{\text{total}} = L_{\text{utility}} + \sum_i \beta_i \hat{I}(O_i;S_i)\).  
5. Backpropagate \(L_{\text{total}}\) through all agents (update LoRA params).  
6. Repeat.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Privacy vs. Utility:* Higher \(\beta_i\) increases privacy but risks utility collapse.  
- *Estimation Bias:* MINE provides a lower bound on MI; too small critics or insufficient training leads to underestimation.  
- *Scalability:* Separate critic per agent adds parameters; shared critic could reduce overhead but may not capture agent-specific distributions.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- MINE estimates diverge or become NaN (unstable critic training).  
- Task accuracy drops sharply early in training (β too high).  
- MI reduction minimal despite training (critics not learning meaningful statistics).

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Sanity Check:** Train a 2-agent pipeline on a toy dataset with known \(S_i\) (e.g., synthetic text with embedded secret tokens). Verify MINE estimates correlate with ground-truth MI and that MINE-Reg reduces final \(I(O_2; S_1,S_2)\).  
2. **Hyperparameter Sweep:** On MedQA with N=2, vary \(\beta \in \{0.01, 0.1, 1.0\}\) and critic learning rate \(\{1e-4, 1e-3\}\). Plot MI_avg vs. accuracy to locate Pareto frontier.  
3. **Depth Scaling:** Fix \(\beta\) and model size; train pipelines with N=2,3,4,5 on PrivacyLens. Measure \(I(O_N; S_1..S_N)\) and PARI to check for exponential leakage growth in baseline and its suppression under MINE-Reg.

## Limitations
- **Undefined \(S_i\):** Exact construction of agent-local sensitive variables per benchmark is not specified, hindering replication.
- **Hyperparameter Gaps:** Critical settings (β schedules, critic LR, update frequency) are omitted, leaving training dynamics ambiguous.
- **Theoretical Assumptions:** The exponential leakage bound assumes independence between agents’ outputs and downstream sensitive variables, which may not hold in practice.

## Confidence
- **Exponential leakage growth bound:** Medium (derivation sound, assumptions may oversimplify real LLM interactions).
- **MI reduction (75–90%) and utility preservation:** Medium (empirical results lack full reproducibility details).
- **Sensitive Blocked / PARI improvements:** Low to Medium (metrics depend on undefined \(S_i\) and may be benchmark-specific).

## Next Checks
1. **Release reference \(S_i\) construction:** Provide code that exactly builds agent-local sensitive variables for MedQA, FinQA, and PrivacyLens, and validate they capture intended private information.
2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis:** Systematically vary β schedule, critic LR, and critic update frequency to map the privacy-utility trade-off and recover reported optimal points.
3. **Empirical test of exponential leakage:** Train fixed-depth pipelines (N=2–5) with identical seeds and measure \(I(O_N; S_1..S_N)\) to confirm predicted exponential growth pattern, controlling for model capacity and data.
