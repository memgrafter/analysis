# Retrieval or Representation? Reassessing Benchmark Gaps in Multilingual and Visually Rich RAG

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04238
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04238
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Improved document representation (OCR quality and language-specific preprocessing) allows classical retrievers like BM25 to recover nearly all performance gaps on multilingual and visually rich benchmarks, demonstrating that representation quality is the dominant factor.

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the common interpretation that multimodal retrievers inherently outperform classical methods like BM25 on benchmarks such as VisR-Bench. Through a controlled study holding the retrieval algorithm fixed, the authors systematically vary the upstream document representation—specifically OCR model and language-specific preprocessing. They find that gains from modern OCR and tailored preprocessing alone enable BM25 to recover most, if not all, of the performance gap previously attributed to advanced retrieval models. This effect is especially pronounced on figure-heavy pages when semantic descriptions are added. The work thus reframes benchmark results, arguing that many reported improvements are confounded by representation quality rather than retrieval mechanism efficacy, and calls for decomposed evaluation in future benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The core method isolates the impact of document representation on retrieval performance. Using the VisR-Bench dataset, the authors fix the retriever to BM25 while systematically varying two upstream stages: (1) the OCR/transcription model (Adobe Document Extract, EasyOCR, Mistral OCR 3, Ministral 3B) and (2) language-specific preprocessing (stemming, lemmatization, morphology, segmentation) across 15 languages. For visually rich pages, they additionally test the effect of appending a semantic description generated by a VLM (Ministral 3B). Performance is measured via page-level Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy. The same representation variations are also applied to dense retrievers (SBERT, BGE-M3) to confirm the generality of the representation bottleneck. This design allows a clean attribution of benchmark performance to transcription and preprocessing quality, decoupling it from the choice of retrieval algorithm.

## Key Results
- On multilingual benchmarks, upgrading from a baseline to the best OCR/preprocessing configuration yields BM25 gains of up to **+8.9 Top-5 accuracy points** averaged over 15 languages.
- On figure-heavy pages, adding a VLM-generated semantic description to the indexed representation produces gains of **up to +31.1 Top-5 points**.
- The performance gap between BM25 and state-of-the-art multimodal retrievers **virtually disappears** when using the best representation pipeline (Ministral 3B with appropriate preprocessing), indicating that representation quality, not retrieval mechanism, is the primary bottleneck in these benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Transcription Quality as a Lexical Bottleneck
- **Claim:** OCR accuracy is the dominant bottleneck for lexical and dense text retrievers; improving it allows BM25 to recover most performance gaps.
- **Mechanism:** BM25 relies on lexical overlap; better OCR recovers more tokens (including from figures, tables, low-resource scripts), increasing term frequency and matching efficacy.
- **Core Assumption:** Query-document matching hinges on surface lexical overlap of key terms often lost in poor transcription.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract reports +8.9 average multilingual gains and +31.1 on figures with descriptions. Section 3.2 notes modern OCR substantially improves baseline accuracy. Table 4 shows Ministral (Top-5 77.24%) vs. Adobe (46.12%) on figures.
- **Break Condition:** Gains saturate when OCR accuracy is near-perfect and all textual content (including figure captions/labels) is extracted.

### Mechanism 2: Language-Specific Preprocessing as a Morphological Normalizer
- **Claim:** Preprocessing (stemming, lemmatization, segmentation) is critical for morphologically rich or non-Latin script languages by reducing vocabulary sparsity.
- **Mechanism:** Normalizes surface forms to common roots, increasing query-document term match probability under BM25's bag-of-words model.
- **Core Assumption:** Queries and documents contain words in varied morphological forms; canonical normalization improves recall.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Section 3.1 notes languages with complex morphology (Japanese, Arabic) benefit disproportionately. Section B.1 identifies "preprocessing-dominated" languages where lemmatization/morphology accounts for most gains. Section B.2 states enabling/disabling a single preprocessing step can change Top-5 by over 10 points.
- **Break Condition:** Benefit diminishes for languages with minimal morphology or when the retrieval model inherently handles variation (e.g., via subwords).

### Mechanism 3: Textual Anchoring from Figure Descriptions
- **Claim:** For figure-heavy pages, adding a textual anchor (caption, labels, VLM description) to the index is a bifurcation point for retrieval success.
- **Mechanism:** Evidence resides in non-runtext elements; VLM-generated descriptions convert visual information into lexical tokens, creating anchor points for term matching.
- **Core Assumption:** Queries about visual content can be satisfied by matching lexical terms derived from that content.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract reports +31.1 gains on figures with descriptions. Section 3.2 observes sharp degradation when figures lack any description. Section C.2 notes VLM transcriptions recover lexical anchors (numbers, variable names). Appendix D shows Ministral output including terms like "Octagonlock Scaffold: 75%" missing from Adobe/EasyOCR.
- **Break Condition:** Returns diminish for queries requiring non-lexical reasoning (spatial relationships, visual style, graphical trends).

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: BM25 & Lexical Retrieval Fundamentals**
  - *Why needed:* The study uses BM25 as the fixed retriever to isolate representation effects. Its reliance on term frequency, inverse document frequency, and bag-of-words explains why transcription completeness matters.
  - *Quick check:* "How does BM25's score change if a key query term is missing from the document's indexed representation?"

- **Concept: OCR Pipeline Variability**
  - *Why needed:* The independent variable is OCR/transcription method. Understanding trade-offs (text-only vs. layout-aware vs. VLM-based) explains performance differences.
  - *Quick check:* "What content (e.g., table structures, figure annotations) is likely missed by a text-only OCR but captured by a VLM transcription?"

- **Concept: Evaluation Decomposition & Confounders**
  - *Why needed:* The paper's causal claim rests on decoupling transcription from retrieval. Recognizing pipeline components that must be held constant is key to fair algorithm comparison.
  - *Quick check:* "In a fair retrieval algorithm comparison, why must the OCR/preprocessing stage be held constant, and how is it often not?"

## Architecture Onboarding

Document Ingestion & Transcription -> Text Preprocessing & Indexing -> Retrieval & Ranking

**Critical Path:** The quality of the final indexed representation (stages 1+2) is the strongest determinant of performance for text-based methods. Any downstream retriever is bounded by this representation. Prioritize optimizing transcription and preprocessing before changing the retrieval model.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- **OCR choice:** Speed/cost (EasyOCR) vs. accuracy/feature richness (Mistral OCR 3, Ministral 3B). VLM transcriptions add latency but produce semantic descriptions.
- **Preprocessing complexity:** Simple lowercasing/tokenization (fast, language-agnostic) vs. language-specific pipelines (morphology, segmentation) requiring per-language models.
- **Indexing footprint:** Verbose transcriptions (e.g., VLM descriptions) increase index size and query latency.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Sudden drop on a specific language subset → likely a language-specific preprocessing gap (check segmentation/stemming).
- Poor performance on figure/table-heavy documents → likely missing textual anchors (audit OCR output for figure labels/captions).
- BM25 gap with multimodal retrievers that persists after OCR optimization → indicates queries requiring true visual reasoning.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. **Baseline Replication:** Run BM25 with the benchmark's default OCR (Adobe) on multilingual and figure-focused splits. Record Top-1/Top-5. This establishes the "gap".
2. **Controlled OCR Swap:** Keep BM25 fixed. Re-index corpus with a single alternative OCR (e.g., Ministral 3B). Re-run retrieval. Measure delta. Repeat for other OCRs.
3. **Add Semantic Figure Descriptions:** Using the best OCR from Exp 2, augment indexing by appending a VLM-generated description (Ministral 3B) for each page's primary figure. Re-run on figure-heavy subset.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. To what extent do current multimodal RAG benchmarks confound evaluation of retrieval mechanisms with upstream document representation quality (OCR and preprocessing)?
2. How should future RAG benchmarks be designed to separately measure transcription and retrieval capabilities, enabling correct attribution of progress?
3. What specific types of visual content in figure-heavy pages remain fundamentally unrecoverable through improved text transcription, and why do multimodal retrievers still outperform text-based methods on these cases?
4. Can VLM-based transcription (e.g., Ministral 3B) be optimized via prompting to produce lexically richer, query-agnostic descriptions that further boost lexical retrieval on visually rich documents?
5. What linguistic and document characteristics determine whether performance gains are "OCR-dominated" versus "preprocessing-dominated" for a given language?

## Limitations
- Exact model versions and inference parameters for OCR systems (Mistral OCR 3, Ministral 3B prompt settings) are undisclosed, blocking precise replication.
- BM25 implementation details (library, k1/b parameters, tokenizer) are absent, risking parameter sensitivity as a confounder.
- Data splits and validation protocols for selecting "best" per-language configurations are not described, potentially enabling overfitting.

## Confidence

- **Core claim (representation quality > retrieval mechanism):** High. Fixed-retriever design with systematic OCR/preprocessing variation strongly isolates representation effects.
- **Multilingual preprocessing impact:** Medium. Gains are demonstrated but rely on per-language tuning; portability to unexamined languages is uncertain.
- **Figure-heavy gains via semantic descriptions:** Medium. Evidence supports lexical anchoring, but the boundary for "fundamentally visual reasoning" queries remains anecdotal.

## Next Checks

1. **Exact replication run:** Re-execute experiments using disclosed OCR model versions (e.g., Mistral OCR 3 API vs. local model) and default BM25 parameters from a standard library (e.g., Pyserini) to confirm reported Top-5 deltas, especially for low-resource languages.
2. **Validation set audit:** Re-train per-language best configurations on a held-out validation split (if available) and compare test performance to the paper's selected configs to detect possible overfitting.
3. **Cross-benchmark test:** Apply the optimal Ministral+lemmatization pipeline to a distinct multilingual/visual benchmark (e.g., VDocRAG) to verify if representation gains generalize and to probe the break condition for non-lexical visual queries.
