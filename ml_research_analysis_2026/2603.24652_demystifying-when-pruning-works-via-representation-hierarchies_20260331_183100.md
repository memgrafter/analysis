# Demystifying When Pruning Works via Representation Hierarchies
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24652
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24652
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Pruning preserves accuracy on non-generative tasks but catastrophically fails for auto-regressive generation due to softmax amplification and error propagation.

## Executive Summary
This paper explains why network pruning is often safe for classification or retrieval tasks but leads to complete collapse in open-ended text generation. The core insight is that language model computation can be decomposed into three sequential representation spaces: embeddings (hidden states), logits (pre-softmax outputs), and probabilities (post-softmax distributions). Pruning introduces small perturbations that remain stable in the embedding and logit spaces due to linear transformations and model robustness. However, the nonlinear softmax function amplifies these minor logit shifts into large probability-space deviations. In autoregressive generation, this initial mismatch then propagates across decoding steps via self-attention, causing rapid output degradation. Non-generative tasks avoid this failure because they either directly use robust embedding/logit representations or consider only a small, stable subset of tokens (e.g., multiple-choice options). The work provides a clear theoretical and empirical foundation for predicting pruning outcomes based on the task's reliance on the fragile probability space.

## Method Summary
The study combines empirical layer-wise interventions on several open-weight instruction-tuned models (Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama3-8B) with theoretical analysis. Pruning is applied in two forms: inter-layer removal of entire transformer blocks (simulating structured pruning) and intra-layer weight sparsity (via Wanda/SparseGPT). For each intervention, the authors measure representation similarity (cosine similarity) and distributional shift (KL divergence) across the three spaces—embedding, logit, and probability—using fixed prompts. They track these metrics across decoding steps in generative settings and contrast them with performance on non-generative benchmarks. A second-order Taylor expansion models local perturbation behavior, while a derived theorem quantifies softmax sensitivity. The empirical results validate the theoretical predictions: embedding and logit spaces are robust, probability space is fragile, and autoregressive context amplifies initial errors.

## Key Results
- Pruning causes minimal deviation in embedding and logit spaces (high cosine similarity) but large KL divergence in probability space due to softmax amplification.
- Non-generative tasks (e.g., MMLU multiple-choice) show small accuracy drops (~3%) even with aggressive layer/MLP removal, while generative tasks (e.g., GSM8K) collapse (48.4% → 0%).
- In autoregressive generation, KL divergence between pruned and original models spikes after the first decoding step and grows cumulatively, leading to incoherent outputs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Pruning-induced perturbations remain small and stable in the embedding and logit representation spaces due to the linearity of transformations and inherent model robustness.
- **Mechanism:** Local pruning deviations (∆h, ∆z) are attenuated in orthogonal direction relative to representation norm (∥∆⊥∥²/∥h∥²) because layer-wise and head projections are approximately linear in small neighborhoods, yielding high cosine similarity.
- **Break condition:** Aggressive pruning that breaks the small-perturbation assumption, especially at the first/last layers.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The nonlinear softmax transformation amplifies small logit perturbations into large probability-space deviations, particularly for high-probability tokens.
- **Mechanism:** Softmax sensitivity is weighted by p_i²/∥p∥². For a sharply peaked distribution, even modest logit variance yields significant probability shifts, scaling inversely with T².
- **Break condition:** Nearly uniform output distributions (high entropy) or very high temperature reduce amplification but harm generation quality.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** In autoregressive generation, amplified probability shifts cause token sampling mismatches that propagate and compound across decoding steps via the self-attention context.
- **Mechanism:** After the first step, the pruned model conditions on a diverging history (x_gen_{P+1:t}), introducing an error term F(∆x_gen) in subsequent representations, creating a feedback loop.
- **Break condition:** If the initial probability shift never changes the argmax/sample, propagation is minimal; but typical pruning levels cause non-negligible mismatches.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Cosine Similarity as a Local Stability Metric**
  *Why needed:* 1–CosineSim (∥∆⊥∥²/∥x∥²) quantifies representational deviation; interpreting Figures 4,5,16–18 relies on this second-order form.
  *Quick check:* If ∥∆h∥=0.1, ∥h∥=10.0, and ∆h orthogonal to h, cosine deviation ≈ 0.0005.

- **Concept: Softmax as a Variance Amplifier**
  *Why needed:* Theorem 2 shows softmax maps logit perturbations into probability shifts weighted by p²; this explains global distribution fragility despite logit stability.
  *Quick check:* If ∆z_i = constant c, KL divergence = 0 (shift invariance); sensitivity depends on variance relative to mean.

- **Concept: Autoregressive Context as an Error Accumulator**
  *Why needed:* Self-attention makes current output a function of all past tokens; pruning errors in earlier generated tokens become input for later steps.
  *Quick check:* In a non-generative multiple-choice task, does output at step t depend on tokens generated at steps < t? No—only on the prompt; hence no propagation loop.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Tokenizer -> Embedding Layer -> LLM Backbone (L layers: self-attention + MLP + residual) -> LM Head (Linear) -> Softmax (temperature T).
- **Critical path:** Token IDs → Embeddings (h) → Layer outputs (h^(l)) → Logits (z) → Probabilities (p). Compare each space between baseline and pruned model.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Pruning granularity: Inter-layer (drop blocks) vs. intra-layer (sparse weights); both show similar space-specific effects.
  - Task alignment: Safe only for tasks using robust spaces (embedding/logit or small categorical subsets); risky for open-ended generation.
  - Temperature: Higher T reduces amplification (∝ 1/T²) but flattens distributions and harms generation quality; not a viable fix.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Probability space: High KL (>0.1) after first generation step; top-5 probabilities shift dramatically.
  - Generation: Repetitive/incoherent output within 5–10 steps (e.g., Table 2 "Drop-8M").
  - Non-generative: Stability if only categorical token logits (A/B/C/D) are used; failure if full distribution analyzed.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Layer-wise ablation: Zero out each layer ℓ, compute 1–CosineSim in embedding, logit, probability spaces (Figure 4). Expect high similarity in embedding/logit, low in probability.
  2. Subspace comparison: For a multiple-choice prompt, compare (a) top-10 vocabulary probabilities and (b) log-probabilities of option tokens (A/B/C/D). Expect (a) large shifts, (b) stable argmax.
  3. Generation step tracking: Generate 50 steps; at each t compute KL(p_t^baseline || p_t^pruned). Expect near-zero at t=1, then sharp increase if pruning heavy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** To what extent can post-pruning fine-tuning recover generative capabilities, and how does it reshape the representation hierarchy? The paper focuses on training-free pruning and explicitly leaves fine-tuning for future work.
- **Question 2:** How do perturbations from pruning accumulate across multiple layers, and can the single-layer-local analysis be extended to predict global behavior under heavy pruning? The theoretical analysis assumes localized perturbations; a compositional theory across layers is lacking.
- **Question 3:** Are the first and last layers unusually sensitive to pruning, and can layer-aware pruning (preserving these layers) mitigate generative collapse? The paper notes higher deviation at first/last layers but does not test targeted preservation strategies.
- **Question 4:** Do task-specific token distributions (e.g., correct answers in probability tails) explain robustness in multiple-choice tasks, and can this predict task robustness? The tail stability hypothesis is noted but not systematically validated.

## Limitations
- Exact pruning protocols (layer indices for uniform removal, precise prompts for analysis) are unspecified, requiring assumptions for replication.
- Findings are demonstrated on 7B–8B models; transfer to much smaller or larger scales is uncertain.
- The theoretical boundary between "non-generative" and "generative" tasks is phenomenologically clear but not formally quantified.
- No exploration of fine-tuning as a mitigation, limiting practical deployment guidance.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Embedding/Logit Robustness):** Medium. Supported by empirical cosine similarities and Taylor theory, but linearity may break under extreme sparsity or early/late layers.
- **Mechanism 2 (Softmax Amplification):** Medium. Theorem 2 provides a clear bound, but tightness depends on the "sharply peaked" assumption; high-entropy outputs may differ.
- **Mechanism 3 (Error Propagation):** High. Figure 7 shows KL divergence spiking after first step; Appendix D’s decomposition includes ∆x_gen term; feedback loop is empirically validated.

## Next Checks
1. **Temperature scaling test:** Re-run generative evaluation (e.g., GSM8K) with pruned models across T ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0}. Higher T should reduce initial KL (∝ 1/T²) but degrade quality; the trade-off curve tests the prediction.
2. **Layer-order ablation:** For fixed sparsity (remove 8 layers), compare orders: (a) uniformly spaced, (b) early-heavy, (c) late-heavy. If embedding/logit robustness holds universally, all orders yield similar non-generative accuracy; variance would indicate hidden nonlinearities.
3. **Non-autoregressive control:** Evaluate pruned models on pure classification (e.g., SST-2) using full vocabulary probability distribution (not just class tokens). Failure here would contradict the claim that only open-ended generation suffers; success would reinforce the propagation hypothesis.
