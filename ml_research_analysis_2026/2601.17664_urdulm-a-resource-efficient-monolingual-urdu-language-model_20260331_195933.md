# UrduLM: A Resource-Efficient Monolingual Urdu Language Model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.17664
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17664
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: A 100M-parameter monolingual Urdu LM trained on 33GB corpus achieves 66.6% sentiment accuracy and 30.59 BLEU, competing with models 10–30× larger, at under $100 training cost.

## Executive Summary
Urdu, spoken by over 230 million people, has lacked dedicated language models, leading to poor performance in multilingual systems and high computational costs. This paper introduces UrduLM, a monolingual decoder-only model with 100 million parameters, trained on a curated 33GB Urdu corpus. Key innovations include a custom BPE tokenizer that reduces tokenization overhead by 20–30% compared to multilingual alternatives, and a low-resource training pipeline that limits environmental impact to approximately 32kg CO2 emissions. In few-shot evaluations, UrduLM achieves competitive results on sentiment classification (66.6% accuracy) and grammar correction (30.59 BLEU) despite being 10–30 times smaller than state-of-the-art multilingual models. The authors release all resources openly.

## Method Summary
The authors built a 33GB Urdu monolingual corpus by aggregating news articles, literature, web blogs, Wikipedia entries, OCR-scanned books, and translated content from FineWeb. The data was cleaned using regex-based noise removal, digit conversion to Urdu numerals, character normalization (UrduHack), deduplication via MinHash LSH, and stored as CSV samples separated by end-of-text tokens. A custom BPE tokenizer was trained on this corpus with a 32k vocabulary, using script-aware regex patterns for the Perso-Arabic Unicode range (U+0600–U+06FF), achieving low token fertility (1.11). The model is a decoder-only transformer with 12 layers, 768-dimensional embeddings, 16 attention heads, and 1024-token context. Training used PyTorch Distributed Data Parallelism (DDP) on four NVIDIA V100 GPUs, an effective batch size of 0.5M tokens via gradient accumulation, Adam optimizer (β1=0.9, β2=0.95), gradient clipping (1.0), cosine learning rate decay with linear warmup, and fp32 precision. The total training cost was under $100 with ~32kg CO2 emissions.

## Key Results
- UrduLM (100M) matches or exceeds performance of multilingual models 10–30× larger on few-shot tasks: 66.6% accuracy on sentiment classification, 30.59 BLEU on grammar correction, and 7.2/10 on fluency/relevance (LLM-as-a-Judge).
- The custom Urdu-specific tokenizer reduces sequence length by 20–30% relative to OpenAI's o200k_base, improving training efficiency.
- Training cost and carbon footprint are dramatically low: <$100 and ~32kg CO2 for three epochs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Custom Tokenizer Efficiency
A custom BPE tokenizer trained exclusively on the curated Urdu corpus captures the language's Perso-Arabic script and morphology better than multilingual tokenizers. Using a 32k vocabulary and Urdu-adapted regex, it achieves low token fertility (1.11), reducing sequence length by 20–30%. Shorter sequences decrease attention computation overhead and increase semantic density per token, improving training efficiency and model performance within a fixed compute budget.

### Mechanism 2: Monolingual Specialization
Monolingual pretraining avoids the "curse of multilinguality" by dedicating all model parameters to Urdu. A 100M-parameter UrduLM uses its full capacity for Urdu patterns, whereas a multilingual model of 3B parameters may allocate only a fraction (e.g., ~30M) to Urdu due to data proportions and cross-linguistic interference. This specialization yields better performance on Urdu tasks despite far fewer total parameters.

### Mechanism 3: Resource-Efficient Pipeline
The entire pipeline—modest corpus size (33GB), small model (100M), and efficient training (DDP, gradient accumulation, fp32)—lowers barriers for low-resource languages. The custom tokenizer further reduces computational load. This approach achieves "good enough" performance at an affordable cost (<$100), making it accessible to researchers with limited resources.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Custom BPE Tokenizer -> Data Loader -> Decoder-only Transformer -> DDP Training Loop -> Evaluation Harness (few-shot + LLM-as-a-Judge)

**Critical path:** 1. Curate and clean 33GB Urdu corpus → 2. Train 32k-vocab tokenizer on final corpus → 3. Tokenize corpus into 1024-token chunks → 4. Initialize model with embedding matrix matching tokenizer vocab → 5. Train with DDP, Adam, fp32, gradient accumulation → 6. Evaluate via in-context learning on SC/GEC/QA.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Model size: 100M chosen for feasibility; larger models (1B) would likely perform better but increase cost.
- Vocabulary size: 32k selected for low fertility (1.11) vs. smaller 10k vocab (higher fertility).
- Precision: fp32 used for stability on V100s, sacrificing memory/speed vs. bf16.
- Task coverage: Evaluated on 4 tasks; omitted summarization/translation due to benchmark scarcity.

**Failure signatures:**
- Tokenizer: High OOV rate or fertility >1.3 on validation text.
- Training: Validation loss diverges from training loss early, or plateaus at high perplexity.
- Evaluation: LLM-as-a-Judge scores <5/10, or few-shot performance near random (<50% for binary SC).
- Resources: Out-of-memory errors during batch simulation.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Tokenizer fertility benchmark**: Process a fixed 500-word Urdu paragraph with o200k_base and custom tokenizer; verify ≥20% reduction in token count for 32k variant.
2. **Single-GPU smoke test**: Train a tiny model (e.g., 10M params) for 100M tokens on 1GB subset; ensure loss decreases smoothly.
3. **Full pilot training**: Train full 100M-32k model for 1 epoch on full corpus; monitor loss curves for convergence before committing to 3 epochs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Scaling frontier**: What performance gains result from training 1B–3B parameter versions of UrduLM, and how does efficiency (FLOPs/CO2) scale? (Preliminary 1B experiments were infeasible due to resource limits.)
- **Instruction tuning**: Does fine-tuning on native Urdu instruction data improve generative task performance? (Preliminary work exists but systematic results are pending.)
- **Cross-lingual transfer**: Does the monolingual pipeline generalize to other low-resource languages (e.g., Bengali, Pashto)? (Not yet validated.)
- **Bias audit**: What biases (gender, religious, regional) does UrduLM exhibit? (No systematic analysis conducted.)

## Limitations
- Corpus reconstruction is uncertain due to lack of specific source details (URLs, book titles, translation subsets).
- Evaluation datasets are opaque; it is unclear whether benchmarks are original or translated, and judging used an English-dominant LLM (Gemini) that may not fully grasp Urdu.
- No ablation study isolates the contribution of each component (tokenizer, monolingual pretraining, pipeline optimizations).

## Confidence
- Custom tokenizer reduces sequence length by 20–30%: **High** (supported by Table 1 and Figure 1).
- Monolingual pretraining allows 100M model to compete with 10–30× larger multilingual models: **Medium** (logical argument based on "curse of multilinguality" but lacks controlled comparison).
- Training cost <$100 and ~32kg CO2: **High** (directly reported with hardware/training details).
- Reported benchmark scores (66.6% SC, 30.59 BLEU, 7.2/10 judge) reflect true capability: **Low** (depends on unreproducible benchmarks and non-native judge).
