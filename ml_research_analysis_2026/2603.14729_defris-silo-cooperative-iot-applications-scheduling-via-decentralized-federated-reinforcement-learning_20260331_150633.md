# DeFRiS: Silo-Cooperative IoT Applications Scheduling via Decentralized Federated Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14729
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14729
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Reduces average response time by 6.4%, energy consumption by 7.2%, tail latency risk (CVaR₀.₉₅) by 10.4%, and achieves near-zero deadline violations compared to the best baseline.

## Executive Summary
DeFRiS addresses the challenge of scheduling IoT applications across multiple autonomous organizations (silos) with heterogeneous resources, non-IID workloads, and potential adversarial threats. It proposes a decentralized federated reinforcement learning framework where silos collaboratively train a policy without sharing raw data. The system introduces three key innovations: an action-space-agnostic actor network for cross-silo knowledge transfer, a silo-optimized local learner combining GAE and clipped updates to handle sparse rewards, and a dual-track robust aggregation protocol using gradient fingerprints for the actor and gradient tracking for the critic. Evaluated on a distributed testbed with 20 heterogeneous silos and real IoT workloads, DeFRiS significantly outperforms baselines in efficiency, robustness, and scalability.

## Method Summary
DeFRiS formulates the scheduling problem as a distributed Markov Decision Process. Each silo runs a local Actor-Critic agent. The Actor uses a multi-encoder architecture to score candidate resources, making it agnostic to specific action-space sizes. The Critic employs Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) and clipped policy updates to stabilize learning under sparse, delayed rewards. For cross-silo coordination, a decentralized protocol aggregates model updates. The Actor's parameters are aggregated using similarity weights derived from gradient fingerprints, with anomaly detection via median absolute deviation. The Critic uses gradient tracking to share optimization momentum while preserving local value accuracy. A neighbor manager selects peers based on round-trip time.

## Key Results
- Reduces average response time by 6.4% and energy consumption by 7.2% versus the best baseline.
- Lowers tail latency risk (CVaR₀.₉₅) by 10.4% and achieves near-zero deadline violations.
- Demonstrates over 3× better performance retention when scaling from 5 to 30 silos.
- Exhibits over 8× better stability under 30% malicious nodes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Action-Space-Agnostic Policy for Heterogeneous Resources
- **Claim:** Enables cross-silo knowledge transfer despite heterogeneous resource configurations by decoupling policy parameters from specific physical dimensions.
- **Mechanism:** Each silo computes a score for every feasible resource using a multi-encoder (task, resource, global state). A softmax over scores yields a probability distribution over actions, and a feasibility mask ensures only valid actions are considered. The same network parameters can thus handle varying numbers and types of resources.
- **Core assumption:** Resource features can be embedded into a shared semantic space across silos, and learned matching patterns between task requirements and resource attributes are transferable.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes the "action-space-agnostic policy using candidate resource scoring." Section IV-A details the multi-encoder architecture and scoring equations (32–37). The corpus indicates existing works assume fixed action spaces, suggesting novelty.
- **Break condition:** If silos have incomparable resource feature sets (e.g., disjoint sensor types), the scoring function may fail to generalize, harming transfer.

### Mechanism 2: Silo-Optimized Local Learning for Sparse Rewards
- **Claim:** Provides stable and sample-efficient learning under sparse delayed rewards typical of long-horizon scheduling.
- **Mechanism:** Combines an Actor-Critic architecture with Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) to densify reward signals via multi-step TD errors, and uses clipped policy updates (à la PPO) to constrain step size of policy changes, maintaining a trust region.
- **Core assumption:** Delayed rewards can be accurately estimated via multi-step temporal differences, and limiting the policy update ratio (within [1–ε, 1+ε]) ensures stable improvement.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract cites "silo-optimized local learning mechanism combining Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) with clipped policy updates." Section IV-B presents Algorithm 1 and equations (40–44). Corpus suggests most DRL schedulers lack explicit sparse-reward handling.
- **Break condition:** Extremely long delays (hundreds of steps) may cause high GAE variance; aggressive clipping (small ε) may restrict learning, while loose clipping may cause instability.

### Mechanism 3: Dual-Track Robust Aggregation under Non-IID and Adversarial Conditions
- **Claim:** Achieves robust cross-silo aggregation in Non-IID and adversarial settings by distinguishing trustworthy knowledge and preserving personalized value functions.
- **Mechanism:** For Actor networks: computes gradient fingerprints (average of resource feature gradients over recent steps) to measure cosine similarity between silos; aggregates parameters with weights proportional to similarity, after excluding anomalous neighbors detected via median absolute deviation (MAD) of similarities. For Critic networks: uses gradient tracking to mix local gradients with neighbor gradient differences, sharing optimization momentum while maintaining local value accuracy.
- **Core assumption:** Gradient directions reflect strategic preferences and are comparable across silos; Critic value functions are environment-specific but their optimization directions can be shared without corruption.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "dual-track non-IID robust decentralized aggregation protocol leveraging gradient fingerprints... and gradient tracking." Section IV-C details Algorithm 2 and equations (45–54), including fingerprint computation, similarity, MAD thresholding, and gradient tracking. Corpus shows existing federated approaches use simple averaging and lack such robust anomaly detection.
- **Break condition:** If silos have disjoint resource feature spaces, gradient fingerprints become incomparable; if >50% of neighbors are malicious, MAD-based detection may fail to isolate all bad actors.

## Foundational Learning
- **Distributed Markov Decision Process (MDP)**  
  *Why needed:* Formalizes sequential decision-making under uncertainty with local observations and global objectives. Each silo has its state and action spaces; the joint policy optimizes a weighted sum of cumulative rewards.

- **Federated Learning with Heterogeneous Agents**  
  *Why needed:* DeFRiS operates in a multi-silo setting where data and models remain local, but knowledge must be shared without a central server, requiring solutions for Non-IID data and action-space heterogeneity.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Action-Space-Agnostic Actor -> Silo-Optimized Learner (Critic + GAE + Clipping) -> Dual-Track Aggregator (Actor: fingerprint+similarity; Critic: gradient tracking) <- Topology Manager (RTT-based neighbor selection) & Anomaly Detector (MAD-based)
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Each silo performs local training (Algorithm 1) for Ω steps.  
  2. It computes gradient fingerprints from recent gradients.  
  3. Exchanges fingerprints, parameters, and tracking variables with neighbors.  
  4. Performs anomaly detection (MAD) and updates: Actor via similarity-weighted aggregation, Critic via gradient tracking.  
  5. May update neighbor set based on RTT improvements.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Decentralization avoids single points of failure but increases communication complexity. Robust anomaly detection may reject dissimilar but honest silos, reducing knowledge diversity. Critic gradient tracking balances cooperation/personalization but introduces hyperparameter α_cag.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Diverging performance across silos or high variance in weighted cost.  
  - Many neighbors flagged as anomalous (low similarity), indicating attacks or severe distribution shift.  
  - Slow convergence or plateau at high cost, possibly due to inadequate local learning or poor aggregation.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Convergence validation:** Deploy on 5 heterogeneous silos with subset of workloads; monitor response time and energy over 100 iterations; expect smooth descent and stabilization.  
  2. **Ablation study:** Run variants removing ASAP, GAE+CLIP, gradient fingerprint, or gradient tracking individually; compare final weighted cost to quantify each component’s contribution.  
  3. **Robustness test:** Introduce 30% malicious nodes (e.g., gradient reversal) in a 20-silo testbed; compare DeFRiS with full defense versus without anomaly detection and baselines; expect near-zero degradation with defense.

## Limitations
- Generalization across highly heterogeneous/disjoint resource feature spaces (e.g., different sensor types) is untested; performance may degrade if silos share no common embedding.
- Robust aggregation relies on gradient fingerprint similarity; severe Non-IID workloads or >50% malicious nodes may evade MAD-based detection, limiting trust scalability.
- Evaluation used only 20 silos; scaling to hundreds introduces untested communication overhead, dynamic topology, and convergence delays.

## Confidence
- **Action-space-agnostic policy:** Medium (novel architecture plausible, but lacking validation on truly incomparable resource sets).
- **Silo-optimized local learning (GAE+clipping):** High (well-established RL technique; results demonstrate improved stability and sample efficiency).
- **Dual-track robust aggregation:** Medium (MAD-based anomaly detection is simple; limited evidence beyond 30% malicious nodes; may miss sophisticated attacks).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain heterogeneity test:** Deploy with silos possessing fundamentally different resource taxonomies (e.g., temperature sensors vs. cameras) to measure transferability limits.
2. **Adversarial scalability stress test:** Gradually increase malicious node ratio up to 60% and track degradation in CVaR₀.₉₅ and deadline violations, comparing against other robust FL baselines.
3. **Large-scale communication overhead:** Simulate 100+ silos with dynamic RTT-based neighbor selection; quantify convergence iterations, bandwidth consumption, and resilience to network churn.
