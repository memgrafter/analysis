# Full-Stack Domain Enhancement for Combustion LLMs: Construction and Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19268
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19268
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: RLVR-Opt achieves 43.8% accuracy on FlameBench, a +17.0 point gain over the base Qwen-8B (26.8%) and an 11.7-point improvement over the best RAG-augmented model (32.1%).

## Executive Summary
General-purpose large language models (LLMs) often produce physically implausible outputs in combustion science due to insufficient domain knowledge and inability to enforce fundamental conservation laws. This paper introduces a comprehensive full-stack enhancement workflow to adapt LLMs for combustion reasoning. The process begins with automated construction of a large (30B-token) combustion-specific corpus, followed by incremental pre-training (CPT), supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with chain-of-thought data, and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) that explicitly enforces conservation constraints. Model performance is measured on a newly proposed FlameBench benchmark. The final RLVR-Opt model achieves 43.8% accuracy—a significant improvement over the base Qwen-8B (26.8%) and the best retrieval-augmented model (32.1%)—and even surpasses closed-source models like GPT-5 (15.6%) and GLM-4 (32.6%). The results demonstrate that internalizing domain knowledge and integrating physics constraints via RL are crucial for reliable scientific reasoning in specialized domains.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a multi-stage enhancement pipeline. First, they automatically compile a 30-billion-token combustion corpus from diverse scientific sources. This corpus is used for incremental pre-training (CPT) of a base LLM (Qwen-8B), exposing the model to domain-specific terminology and concepts. Next, they perform supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on curated chain-of-thought (CoT) data to teach explicit reasoning steps. Finally, they apply reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), where the reward function checks compliance with conservation laws (mass, energy, momentum) and other physical constraints. The model's capabilities are evaluated on FlameBench, a new benchmark comprising combustion science questions that often require multi-step reasoning and physical consistency. The RLVR-Opt variant consistently outperforms baseline models across multiple metrics.

## Key Results
- RLVR-Opt reaches 43.8% accuracy on FlameBench, representing a +17.0 point gain over the base Qwen-8B (26.8%).
- It exceeds the best retrieval-augmented model (32.1%) by 11.7 points.
- It substantially outperforms closed-source models: GPT-5 (15.6%) and GLM-4 (32.6%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
General-purpose LLMs lack the specialized knowledge and physical reasoning constraints needed for combustion science. The proposed workflow addresses these gaps in three ways: (1) The large combustion corpus provides dense domain-specific information during CPT, allowing the model to internalize facts and relationships; (2) SFT with chain-of-thought data trains the model to generate step-by-step reasoning, a key skill for solving multi-step problems; (3) RLVR introduces explicit reward signals tied to conservation laws, guiding the model to produce outputs that are not only contextually appropriate but also physically plausible. This combination bridges the knowledge gap and enforces hard constraints that are otherwise difficult to capture through language modeling alone.

## Foundational Learning
### Combustion Domain Knowledge
*Why needed*: The model must understand specialized concepts (e.g., flame speed, reaction kinetics, turbulent mixing) to interpret and answer questions correctly.  
*Quick check*: Ask the model to define key terms or explain fundamental principles without external aids.

### Conservation Laws Enforcement
*Why needed*: Physical implausibility often stems from violating mass, energy, or momentum conservation. Enforcing these constraints ensures scientifically valid outputs.  
*Quick check*: Present a problem where conservation can be checked numerically (e.g., balance atoms in a reaction) and verify the model respects it.

### Chain-of-Thought Reasoning
*Why needed*: Combustion problems typically require multiple inference steps; CoT encourages the model to articulate intermediate reasoning, reducing errors.  
*Quick check*: Provide a multi-step question and inspect the model's intermediate reasoning for logical consistency.

### Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)
*Why needed*: Traditional supervised learning may not guarantee adherence to explicit physical constraints; RLVR optimizes for verifiable outcomes directly.  
*Quick check*: Train a small-scale RLVR on a toy constraint (e.g., keeping a reaction exothermic) and test if the model respects it after training.

### Incremental Pre-Training on Domain Corpus
*Why needed*: Starting from a general LLM, domain adaptation via CPT efficiently injects new knowledge without catastrophic forgetting.  
*Quick check*: Measure the model's knowledge acquisition on a downstream QA task after CPT versus before.

### Benchmark Evaluation (FlameBench)
*Why needed*: A dedicated benchmark is essential to track progress and compare approaches on domain-specific tasks.  
*Quick check*: Verify that benchmark items are solvable by domain experts and cover a range of subfields.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Combustion Corpus -> Incremental Pre-Training (CPT) -> SFT with CoT -> RLVR with Verifiable Rewards -> RLVR-Opt Model -> FlameBench Evaluation

### Critical Path
The pipeline proceeds linearly: data collection must precede CPT; CPT yields a domain-adapted model that is further refined by SFT; RLVR is the final stage, requiring a reward function that encodes conservation laws. Evaluation occurs after RLVR-Opt is produced. The critical path is: Corpus -> CPT -> SFT -> RLVR -> Evaluation.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Corpus scale vs. quality**: The 30B-token corpus is massive, but automated construction risks including low-quality or irrelevant data, which could dilute learning.
- **CPT computational cost**: Incremental pre-training is expensive; trade-offs exist between number of tokens and GPU time.
- **RLVR reward design**: Defining verifiable rewards that are both accurate and computationally efficient is challenging; oversimplified constraints may not capture nuanced physics.
- **SFT data selection**: Chain-of-thought data must be high-quality and representative; inadequate coverage could limit generalization.

### Failure Signatures
- **CPT failure**: Model performance on basic combustion fact QA does not improve relative to base model.
- **SFT failure**: Model produces reasoning traces that are incoherent or do not lead to correct answers despite correct intermediate steps.
- **RLVR failure**: The model meets the reward signal but still produces outputs that violate physical laws in ways not captured by the reward (reward hacking).
- **Evaluation leakage**: Benchmark performance is inflated due to overlap between training data and FlameBench items.

### First Experiments
1. **Baseline assessment**: Evaluate the base Qwen-8B on FlameBench to establish the initial performance gap.
2. **CPT impact**: Train a CPT-only variant (without SFT/RLVR) and measure accuracy to isolate the effect of domain corpus exposure.
3. **RLVR constraint ablation**: Remove conservation law rewards from RLVR to quantify their contribution to final performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
While not explicitly enumerated, the paper's approach and limitations suggest several open questions:
1.  How does the automated construction of the 30B-token combustion corpus ensure diversity and avoid contamination from non-authoritative or synthetic sources?
2.  To what extent do the enforced conservation laws in RLVR generalize to more complex, multi-phase, or non-ideal combustion scenarios not explicitly covered in the reward design?
3.  Can the full-stack pipeline (CPT → SFT → RLVR) be effectively compressed or reordered for domains with smaller available corpori or different reasoning patterns?
4.  How does the performance of RLVR-Opt on FlameBench translate to real-world combustion engineering tasks, such as design optimization or diagnostic interpretation?
5.  What are the diminishing returns and computational trade-offs of scaling the base model size (beyond Qwen-8B) within this specific enhancement workflow?

## Limitations
- **FlameBench validity**: Novel benchmark with unknown design rigor; potential leakage from training corpus or narrow coverage of combustion subfields.
- **Corpus curation**: "Automated construction" details unspecified; risks of low-quality, duplicated, or synthetic data contamination.
- **RLVR constraint specifics**: "Verifiable rewards" and enforced conservation laws not detailed; generalizability to unseen physics scenarios uncertain.
- **Closed-source comparison**: Claims of superiority over GPT-5/GLM-4 lack replication; performance may be influenced by evaluation setup differences.
- **Evaluation scope**: Success measured solely on FlameBench; unclear if gains transfer to practical combustion problems or other scientific domains.

## Confidence
- **High**: Reported incremental gains (Qwen-8B → CPT → SFT → RLVR) within the paper’s controlled evaluation setup.
- **Medium**: FlameBench as a meaningful measure of combustion reasoning; assumes benchmark items are authentic and unbiased.
- **Low**: Claims of superiority over closed-source models (GPT-5, GLM-4), given their opaque architectures, undisclosed training, and lack of multi-benchmark replication.

## Next Checks
1.  **Benchmark audit**: Cross-reference FlameBench items against standard combustion textbooks/papers to detect training-data overlap; assess question diversity across kinetic mechanisms, turbulent flames, and diagnostics.
2.  **Corpus forensics**: Sample the 30B-token corpus for source authenticity (e.g., peer-reviewed articles vs. low-quality websites); check for contamination from LLM-generated text.
3.  **Constraint stress-test**: Evaluate RLVR-Opt on out-of-distribution physics problems (e.g., non-ideal gas laws, multi-phase reactions) to probe robustness of enforced conservation laws.
4.  **Ablation replication**: Re-run the CPT-only and RLVR-ablated experiments to validate the reported contribution of each pipeline stage.
5.  **Transfer assessment**: Test RLVR-Opt on a separate, established combustion QA dataset (if available) to measure generalization beyond FlameBench.
