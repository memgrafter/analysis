# GeoLAN: Geometric Learning of Latent Explanatory Directions in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19460  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19460  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Geometric regularization via KT-CW and KT-Attn improves latent space isotropy and disentanglement in mid-sized LLMs (4–8B), enhancing interpretability and fairness with limited accuracy impact; smaller models degrade, larger ones incur alignment costs.

## Executive Summary
GeoLAN addresses the opacity of large language models by treating token representations as geometric trajectories and applying differentiable regularizers inspired by Kakeya set theory. The method introduces two losses: KT-CW, which enforces isotropy by penalizing variance of random projections, and KT-Attn, which promotes diverse attention patterns by maximizing spectral entropy. Together, they aim to satisfy "K-stickiness," a property that decomposes the latent space into low-interference grains. Experiments on Gemma-3 (1B, 4B, 12B) and Llama-3-8B reveal scale-dependent outcomes: mid-sized models (4–8B) maintain or improve task accuracy (MMLU) while significantly improving geometric metrics (reduced cone concentration, increased IsoScore) and robustness (TruthfulQA stability, reduced stereotype bias). The smallest model (1B) degrades both geometrically and in performance, while the largest (12B) experiences a minor accuracy drop despite fairness gains. These results highlight a trade-off between interpretability and performance, with an optimal "Goldilocks zone" for geometric regularization.

## Method Summary
GeoLAN fine-tunes pre-trained LLMs on a subset of the C4 dataset (10B tokens) with an auxiliary loss combining cross-entropy and two geometric regularizers: KT-CW and KT-Attn. KT-CW operates on token embeddings at each layer: it samples random unit vectors, projects embeddings onto them, and penalizes deviation of the projected variance from the isotropic baseline \(1/d\). KT-Attn operates on attention matrices: it computes the singular value distribution, calculates spectral entropy, and penalizes deviation from the maximum entropy \(\log d_k\). Both losses are scaled by coefficients \(\lambda_1(t)\) and \(\lambda_2(t)\) that anneal linearly from 0 to target values (\(\lambda_1=10^{-3}\), \(\lambda_2=10^{-2}\)) over the first 500 training steps. Training uses AdamW (learning rate \(2 \times 10^{-5}\), weight decay 0.0), sequence length 2048, and bf16 precision. The models are evaluated on MMLU (accuracy), geometric metrics (IsoScore, cone concentration), interpretability proxies (PCA probe efficiency, grain stability), and fairness/stability benchmarks (TruthfulQA, CrowS-Pairs), with comparisons to a control baseline (weight decay 0.10).

## Key Results
- Mid-sized models (Gemma-3-4B, Llama-3-8B) maintain or improve MMLU accuracy, reduce cone concentration (dCohen = -1.33 for Llama-3-8B), increase IsoScore (dCohen = 0.85), and improve PCA probe efficiency (dCohen = 0.97).
- Smallest model (Gemma-3-1B) shows increased cone concentration (dCohen = +1.35), decreased IsoScore, and degraded MMLU performance.
- Largest model (Gemma-3-12B) exhibits improved fairness (reduced stereotype bias on CrowS-Pairs, dCohen = -0.38) but a slight accuracy drop (dCohen = -1.08), indicating an alignment cost.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** KT-CW loss reduces anisotropic cone collapse by forcing token representations toward spherical distribution.
- **Mechanism:** It estimates the variance of embeddings projected onto random unit directions and penalizes deviation from the isotropic baseline (variance = 1/d). This directly operationalizes Axiom 1 (Semantic Collapse Constant) by punishing high variance along any principal direction, thus discouraging many tokens from clustering in a narrow cone.
- **Core assumption:** Model capacity is sufficient to support isotropic representations without harming expressivity (the "Goldilocks zone" of 4–8B parameters).
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "KT-CW for isotropy... reduced cone concentration (dCohen = -1.33)".  
  - [section 3.1] Formal definition of \(L^{(l)}_{CW}\) as \(\mathbb{E}[ \text{Var}(\langle x, u \rangle) - 1/d ]^2\).  
  - [section 4.2.1] "Llama-3-8B... statistically significant reduction in cone concentration".  
  - [corpus] Weak direct support; neighbor papers focus on other geometric aspects (e.g., "Frayed RoPE" on positional geometry, "How Language Directions Align" on multilingual geometry). No direct citation of Kakeya-inspired regularization.
- **Break condition:** In models below ~4B parameters (e.g., Gemma-3-1B), enforcing isotropy conflicts with necessary superposition, causing increased cone concentration (dCohen = +1.35) and performance degradation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** KT-Attn loss prevents rank collapse in attention heads by maximizing spectral entropy, enforcing Axiom 2 (Attention Interaction Constant).
- **Mechanism:** It computes the normalized singular value distribution of each attention matrix and penalizes deviation from maximal entropy (log \(d_k\)). High entropy implies full-rank, diverse attention patterns; low entropy indicates collapse onto a few tokens/directions.
- **Core assumption:** Diverse attention patterns are necessary for disentangled representations and that spectral entropy is a valid differentiable proxy for the combinatorial attention dispersion constraint.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "KT-Attn for diverse attention".  
  - [section 3.2] \(L^{(l)}_{Attn} = (\log d_k - H(A))^2\) sum over heads.  
  - [section 4.2.4] Reduction in stereotype bias for Gemma-3-12B (dCohen = -0.38) is attributed to high-rank attention dismantling entangled low-rank bias attractors.  
  - [corpus] No direct anchor; neighbor papers (e.g., "Concept-based explainability") use attention but not spectral entropy regularization. Assumption that attention rank relates to bias is inferred from paper's causal narrative.
- **Break condition:** In very large models (e.g., Gemma-3-12B), convex spectral entropy regularization may be incompatible with complex non-linear manifolds learned during pretraining, leading to alignment cost (accuracy drop dCohen = -1.08).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Enforcing K-stickiness (via KT-CW + KT-Attn) yields a latent space decomposable into orthogonal "grains" (submanifolds) with bounded cross-grain interference, enhancing interpretability and robustness.
- **Mechanism:** The Semantic Stickiness Theorem (Theorem 1) proves that if representation fields satisfy the Wolff axioms (low CA, CB), then token tubes are contained within disjoint grains. This allows linear probes (\(w_j = a_j\)) to recover semantic factors with \(O(K^{-1})\) interference from other grains, and output perturbations that preserve grain membership are Lipschitz-bounded.
- **Core assumption:** The continuous trajectory model (\(\gamma_i(t)\)) and measurable grain decomposition (Lemma 3) accurately reflect Transformer computation; the orthogonality condition (\(\|P_p P_q\| \leq 1/K\)) holds in practice for semantically meaningful grains.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "decomposable into low-interference components... linear probe efficiency (dCohen = 0.97)".  
  - [section 2.3] Theorem 1 statements: grain containment, orthogonality (\(\text{Vol}(G_p \cap G_q) = 0\)), Lipschitz output change, probe efficiency.  
  - [section 4.2.1] Significant PCA probe efficiency gain for Llama-3-8B.  
  - [section E.1] Grain stability metrics and rational symmetry phenomenon.  
  - [corpus] No direct support; neighbor papers use different decomposition methods (SAEs, dictionary learning). The grain concept is novel to GeoLAN.
- **Break condition:** Grain stability across seeds is low (e.g., Llama-3-8B dCohen = -0.97), indicating rotational symmetry—the geometry is isotropic but not aligned, hampering mechanistic interpretability. Also, in small models, grains may not form properly due to superposition.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Geometric measure theory (Kakeya sets, Wolff axioms)**  
  - **Why needed here:** Provides the mathematical foundation for quantifying "messiness" (anisotropy, collapse) as tube clustering in high-dimensional space. The KT losses are differentiable analogs of combinatorial Wolff axioms.  
  - **Quick check question:** How does the Kakeya needle problem's "no excessive clustering" principle map to the Semantic Collapse Constant (Axiom 1) in latent spaces?
- **Concept: Isotropy vs. Anisotropy in embeddings**  
  - **Why needed here:** Anisotropy (cone collapse) is the core problem GeoLAN addresses. IsoScore and cone concentration are key metrics. Understanding why collapsed representations limit capacity and increase entanglement is crucial.  
  - **Quick check question:** Why does a narrow cone in embedding space reduce effective model capacity and worsen polysemanticity?
- **Concept: Mechanistic interpretability (SAEs, superposition)**  
  - **Why needed here:** GeoLAN is an *in-training* alternative to post-hoc SAEs. Understanding superposition (features > neurons, non-orthogonal encoding) explains why isotropy fails in small models (scale floor).  
  - **Quick check question:** Why might forcing monosemantic directions (isotropy) harm a small model that relies on superposition for compression?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Theoretical core: Token trajectories (\(\gamma_i(t)\)) → Spacetime tubes (\(T_i^\delta\)) → Representation field (\(\Phi_l(S)\)) → Semantic Wolff axioms (CA, CB) → K-stickiness.  
Loss functions: KT-CW (isotropy via random projection variance) + KT-Attn (attention rank via spectral entropy) → Combined with CE loss (annealing schedule).  
Evaluation suite: Geometry metrics (IsoScore, cone concentration) → Interpretability metrics (PCA probe efficiency, grain stability) → Robustness/fairness (TruthfulQA stability, CrowS-Pairs).

**Critical path:** Axioms → Differentiable surrogates → Insert into training loop (after warm-up) → Measure geometric metrics → Correlate with downstream tasks (MMLU, bias). The annealing schedule is critical to avoid early optimization instability.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Computational cost: KT-CW requires random projections and variance estimation; KT-Attn needs SVD per head. ~15–20% training slowdown.  
- Scale floor: Below ~4B parameters (e.g., 1B), isotropy conflicts with superposition → degradation.  
- Alignment cost: Above ~12B, convex regularization may disrupt complex manifolds → accuracy drop.  
- Rotational symmetry: Isotropic geometry achieved but not aligned across seeds → grains unstable for cross-seed analysis.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Small model (1B): ↑ cone concentration, ↓ IsoScore, ↓ MMLU.  
- Large model (12B): ↓ MMLU accuracy, potential mismatch with complex manifolds.  
- General: Grain stability low across seeds (rational symmetry), paraphrase stability drop in constrained models (over-crystallization).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Validate KT-CW: Measure cone concentration (top-10 eigenvalue fraction) and IsoScore across layers for Llama-3-8B. Expect ↓ cone, ↑ IsoScore.  
2. Validate KT-Attn: Compute spectral entropy distribution of attention heads. Expect ↑ mean entropy, ↓ variance across heads; correlates with ↓ stereotype rate.  
3. Test grain decomposition: Perform PCA probe efficiency (k components needed for fixed variance recovery). Expect ↓ k for mid-sized models; also measure grain purity/stability across seeds.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Formalization of messiness:** Can geometric measure theory rigorously formalize latent space messiness? The Semantic Wolff axioms work for mid-models but fail for 1B models where superposition is needed, suggesting the theory may not capture all capacity regimes. Extension to superposition-aware constraints or testing with SAE-discovered features could resolve this.
2. **Differentiable regularizers without performance loss:** Can differentiable geometric regularizers enforce stickiness without degrading performance? GeoLAN shows scale-dependent trade-offs: mid-models benefit, small degrade, large lose accuracy. Systematic sweeps over regularization strength, annealing schedules, and adaptive losses are needed.
3. **Interpretability gains:** Does geometric intervention yield more interpretable, stable, and disentangled representations? Mixed evidence: improved PCA efficiency but grain instability across seeds; fairness gains only in 12B. Causal mediation on specific grains and longitudinal grain persistence studies would help.
4. **Scale-dependent mechanisms:** Why do mid-sized models benefit most? Hypotheses: small models rely on superposition for compression; large models exploit complex non-linear manifolds that convex regularization disrupts. Quantifying superposition (e.g., via SAEs) and intrinsic dimension across scales, or ablating depth/width, could verify.

## Limitations
- Scale-dependent effects: unclear mechanistic boundary between 1B degradation and 12B alignment cost.
- Grain instability across seeds (rotational symmetry) limits utility for mechanistic interpretability.
- Low external citation count; theoretical foundations lack independent validation from neighboring work.

## Confidence
- Geometric improvements in mid-models (IsoScore, cone reduction, MMLU stability): **High**  
- Causality of KT-CW/A losses: **Medium**
