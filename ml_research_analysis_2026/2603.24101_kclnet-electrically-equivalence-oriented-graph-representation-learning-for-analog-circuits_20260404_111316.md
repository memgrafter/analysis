# KCLNet: Electrically Equivalence-Oriented Graph Representation Learning for Analog Circuits

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24101
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24101
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** KCLNet achieves 5.56% higher classification accuracy, 43.36% better subcircuit detection mAP, and up to 3.8% lower graph edit distance prediction MAE compared to state-of-the-art methods.

## Executive Summary
KCLNet tackles analog circuit representation learning by embedding fundamental electrical principles into graph neural networks. The framework converts analog circuits into directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) and employs asynchronous message passing that mirrors current flow from voltage to ground. A contrastive learning objective enforces Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) by maintaining equal sums of current-related embeddings across depths. Experiments on classification, subcircuit detection, and graph edit distance prediction show substantial improvements over baselines, demonstrating the value of physics-aware representation learning.

## Method Summary
KCLNet first transforms an analog circuit netlist into a bipartite graph (devices and nets), then adds voltage and ground nodes and performs a topological sort to create a DAG with depth labels. An asynchronous GNN propagates node embeddings strictly from lower to higher depths, simulating current flow. For pretraining, it computes a depth-wise sum of node embeddings (interpreted as total current at that depth) and applies a contrastive loss: positives are pairs from different depths (encouraging KCL compliance), and negatives are generated by zeroing out the top-k nodes (by L2 norm) to create electrically inconsistent sums. The pretrained encoder is then fine-tuned on downstream tasks with task-specific heads.

## Key Results
- 5.56% higher **Acc@1** for analog circuit classification.
- 43.36% better **mAP** for subcircuit detection.
- Up to 3.8% lower **MAE** for graph edit distance prediction.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of KCL-based positive sample generation.
- Asynchronous message passing outperforms synchronous GNNs by over 19% in classification accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Directed Acyclic Graph Conversion and Asynchronous Message Passing
Converting analog circuits to DAGs with voltage/ground as terminals and using asynchronous message passing aligns the GNN’s information flow with physical current direction. The bipartite graph is topologically sorted to assign depths; message passing only aggregates from the previous depth, simulating current flow from sources to ground. This captures electrical characteristics more effectively than synchronous GNNs that mix all neighbors regardless of depth. Evidence appears in the abstract and Section 2.1. A break condition occurs if voltage/ground are misidentified or the circuit violates bipartiteness, leading to cycles and incorrect depth ordering.

### Mechanism 2: KCL-Based Contrastive Learning
The contrastive objective enforces that the sum of node embeddings (treated as current proxies) at different depths remain equal, preserving Kirchhoff's Current Law in embedding space. Positives are pairs of depth-wise sums from different depths; negatives are created by dropping the top-k nodes (by L2 norm) to deliberately break current conservation. This encourages the model to learn representations where current is consistently distributed. Evidence is in Section 3.2 and Equation (4). The mechanism assumes node embedding norms correlate with current magnitudes; if this fails, negatives may not represent valid electrical contradictions.

### Mechanism 3: Theoretical Guarantee of KCL Preservation
The contrastive learning objective theoretically ensures there exists a linear map that makes embeddings from different depths equivalent, thus preserving KCL. Theorem 4 shows that if the number of depth samples is less than the embedding dimension, a non-trivial linear transformation exists; the contrastive loss pushes embeddings close enough (small ϵ) to make this feasible (Corollary 2). Evidence is in Appendix A.7. The guarantee weakens if the number of depths is large relative to embedding dimension or if embeddings are not sufficiently aligned by training.

## Foundational Learning
- **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs):** Needed to encode circuit graphs into embeddings. Quick check: What is the key difference between synchronous and asynchronous message passing? Async respects depth ordering, sync aggregates all neighbors indiscriminately.
- **Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL):** Essential for enforcing current conservation in the embedding space. Quick check: How can KCL apply to a supernode (all nodes at a depth)? The sum of currents entering the supernode equals the sum leaving; in the DAG, this translates to equal depth-wise sums.
- **Contrastive Learning:** Used to define positives/negatives based on electrical equivalence. Quick check: In SimCLR, positives are augmented views of the same graph; in KCLNet, positives are depth-wise sums from the same circuit, reflecting KCL.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Netlist → Bipartite graph → DAG conversion (add voltage/ground, topological sort, assign depths) → Asynchronous GNN (depth-by-depth propagation) → Compute depth-wise sums (I_Δ) → KCL contrastive loss → Pretrained encoder → Downstream fine-tuning (task-specific head)

**Critical path:**  
1. Convert circuit to DAG with correct voltage/ground identification.  
2. Assign depth labels via topological sort.  
3. Propagate embeddings asynchronously: for depth ℓ, aggregate only from depth ℓ-1.  
4. For each depth Δ, sum node embeddings to get I_Δ.  
5. Compute KCL loss using positives (I_Δ vs. I_Δ′) and negatives (top-k dropped ˆI_Δ).  
6. Backpropagate to train the encoder.  
7. Freeze or fine-tune on downstream tasks.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Async vs. sync GNN:* Async aligns with DC physics but requires careful depth management; sync GNNs (e.g., GIN) may incorrectly mix currents across depths.  
- *Positive sample generation:* Using different depths as positives is simple and physics-based but may not capture all equivalences (e.g., same depth, different branches).  
- *Negative sample generation:* Top-k dropout assumes embedding norm ∝ current magnitude; if violated, negatives may not be electrically valid.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *DAG conversion errors:* Cycles indicate misassigned voltage/ground or non-bipartite connections; verify all edges go from lower to higher depth.  
- *KCL loss collapse:* All embeddings become zero; monitor variance of I_Δ across depths.  
- *Negative sample degeneracy:* Top-k dropout always masks the same nodes (e.g., voltage source); check that masked nodes vary across batches.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Circuit classification:** Pretrain on ANALOG-CLS-428k, fine-tune a linear classifier on 12 classes, report Acc@1.  
2. **Subcircuit detection:** Pretrain on ANALOG-DET-242k, fine-tune a node-level detector for 12 subcircuit types, report mAP and IoU.  
3. **Graph edit distance prediction:** Pretrain on ANALOG-GED-194k, train a regression head (MLP on concatenated graph embeddings) to predict GED between two circuits, report MAE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **KVL integration:** How can Kirchhoff's Voltage Law be incorporated to complement KCL, since KVL encodes voltage conservation in loops?
- **Better encoders:** Would a graph transformer, with global self-attention, yield more powerful circuit representations than the GCN-based encoder?
- **Multimodal inputs:** How can additional data like SPICE netlists or simulation results be fused into the pretraining pipeline?
- **AC/transient extension:** Can the asynchronous message passing and KCL objective be adapted for AC or transient circuits, where currents and voltages vary with frequency or time?

## Limitations
- **Dataset accessibility:** The ANALOG dataset is not publicly released; reproduction requires implementing the proprietary synthesis framework from Zhao & Zhang 2022.
- **Assumption in negative generation:** The top-k dropout relies on node embedding L2-norm correlating with current magnitude, which is unvalidated.
- **Implementation ambiguity:** Details of batched asynchronous message passing and the contrastive loss temperature τ are unreported, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- **High:** DAG conversion and asynchronous propagation are theoretically grounded (Theorem 1) and align with DC current flow.
- **Medium:** KCL-based contrastive loss shows empirical gains (ablation studies), but its effectiveness depends on the norm-current correlation assumption.
- **Low:** Theoretical guarantee for KCL preservation (Theorem 4) holds only under strict dimensional conditions (n < d) and may not scale to practical settings.
