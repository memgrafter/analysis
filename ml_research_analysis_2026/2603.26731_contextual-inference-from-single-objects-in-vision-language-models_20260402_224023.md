# Contextual inference from single objects in Vision-Language models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26731
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26731
- **Reference count:** 25
- **Primary result:** Single objects in VLMs enable above-chance contextual inference (e.g., LLaVA scene 58.6% normal/85.5% relaxed, superordinate 93.8%), with accuracy modulated by object properties and representational stability predicting success.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how much scene context can be inferred from a single isolated object in vision-language models (VLMs), addressing a gap analogous to human scene perception. The authors test two VLMs—LLaVA and InternVL—by presenting either full scenes or isolated foreground objects and probing for fine-grained scene category, superordinate indoor/outdoor label, and object identity. Results show both models achieve above-chance contextual inference from objects alone, with accuracy systematically influenced by object properties like size, frequency, and specificity, mirroring patterns observed in humans. Mechanistically, the stability of object-patch token representations across background removal predicts contextual accuracy, and scene versus superordinate schemas are grounded differently in the networks. The study reveals that VLMs encode object-scene co-occurrence statistics and highlights distinct representational pathways for different levels of contextual information.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate two vision-language models, LLaVA and InternVL, using a dataset of images where foreground objects are either presented in full scenes or isolated with backgrounds removed. For each input, the models are prompted to predict the scene’s fine-grained category (e.g., “forest”), the superordinate indoor/outdoor label, and the object identity. To probe internal representations, they extract per-layer activations of image patch tokens and compute cosine similarity between full-scene and object-only conditions (representational stability). They also project image token logits onto the output vocabulary to assess decoding of scene and superordinate information across layers. Additionally, they conduct a multivariate logistic regression to measure the influence of object-level statistical properties (size, frequency, specificity) on contextual inference accuracy.

## Key Results
- Single objects support above-chance contextual inference: scene category (LLaVA: 58.6% normal, 85.5% relaxed) and superordinate (93.8%), with similar trends in InternVL.
- Inference accuracy is modulated by object size, frequency, and specificity in a manner that mirrors human scene categorization patterns.
- Representational stability of object-patch tokens across background removal positively predicts contextual accuracy; InternVL exhibits higher stability and tighter coupling than LLaVA.
- Scene identity is encoded in image tokens throughout the network, whereas superordinate information emerges only in late layers (LLaVA) or mid‑late layers (InternVL).
- Correct object identification is not necessary for contextual inference, indicating a partial dissociation between object and context processing.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Representational stability predicts contextual inference
**Claim:** The stability of an object’s internal representation across background contexts (i.e., cosine similarity of object-patch tokens between full-scene and object-only inputs) causally predicts the model’s ability to infer contextual information.
**Mechanism:** When an object’s visual token representation remains robust after background removal, it indicates that the object’s features encode self‑sufficient, context‑independent cues. These stable features allow downstream layers to access scene or superordinate information without relying on background integration.
**Evidence:** Layer‑wise analysis shows a consistent, statistically significant positive difference in cosine similarity between correct and incorrect trials across tasks and models.
**Break condition:** If controlled experiments that artificially manipulate stability (e.g., via adversarial perturbations) fail to alter contextual accuracy, or if stability differences disappear when object properties are matched, the causal link would be weakened.

### Mechanism 2: Differential grounding of scene vs. superordinate schemas
**Claim:** Scene‑level and superordinate (indoor/outdoor) schemas are grounded via fundamentally different pathways in the visual processing stream, explaining their dissociable behavioral patterns.
**Mechanism:** Scene identity information is encoded directly in the image token space from early layers onward, making it accessible throughout the network. Superordinate information, however, either emerges very late or is not represented in image tokens at all (in LLaVA), suggesting it relies on higher‑level, possibly LLM‑mediated abstractions rather than direct visual grounding.
**Evidence:** Vocabulary projection (ROC‑AUC) reveals high and significant scene decoding across nearly all layers in both models, while superordinate decoding remains near chance until very late layers in LLaVA and only rises in mid‑late layers in InternVL.
**Break condition:** If alternative probing methods (e.g., linear decoders) reveal early superordinate information, or if scene information is only decodable late, the dissociation claim would need revision.

### Mechanism 3: Object properties modulate contextual inference via learned co‑occurrence statistics
**Claim:** Contextual inference from single objects is modulated by specific statistical object properties (size, frequency, specificity) in a manner that mirrors human scene categorization, indicating models internalize object‑scene co‑occurrence statistics.
**Mechanism:** Larger, more frequent, and more scene‑specific objects provide stronger contextual cues because training data contain more consistent co‑occurrence patterns with particular environments. The models’ predictions therefore reflect these diagnostic values.
**Evidence:** Multivariate logistic regression on z‑scored properties shows specificity and size as strong, significant predictors for scene classification, while size dominates superordinate classification; effects replicate human findings (Wiesmann & Vö, 2023).
**Break condition:** If property effects vanish when controlling for low‑level visual features or upon retraining on synthetic data with broken correlations, the claim that models learn natural scene statistics would be challenged.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Vision‑Language Pretraining on Paired Image‑Text Data**  
  *Why needed:* Both LLaVA and InternVL are built by aligning a frozen visual encoder (ViT) to a frozen LLM via a small projection, trained on multimodal instruction data. This joint training is foundational for learning any correspondence between visual tokens and linguistic concepts like scene categories.  
  *Quick check:* What objective function aligns the visual and textual modalities during the instruction‑tuning phase (e.g., next‑token prediction on captions, or contrastive alignment)?

- **Concept: Transformer‑Based Token Processing**  
  *Why needed:* The analysis relies on accessing and interpreting per‑layer hidden states of image patch tokens. Understanding transformer architecture (self‑attention, residual streams, layer norms) is necessary to interpret representational stability and vocabulary projections.  
  *Quick check:* How does the model’s token processing differ between the visual backbone (ViT) and the LLM, and where does the MLP projection intervene?

- **Concept: Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA) and Cosine Similarity Metrics**  
  *Why needed:* The paper uses cosine similarity between full‑scene and object‑only activations as a key dependent variable. This requires grasping how to compute and interpret similarity in high‑dimensional neural representation spaces, and why cosine is chosen over Euclidean distance.  
  *Quick check:* Why might cosine similarity be a more appropriate measure than Euclidean distance for comparing activations across input conditions in a transformer?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Input processor -> Visual encoder -> Token adapter -> LLM backbone -> Output head
- **Critical path:** The image is resized and divided into square patches. Patch embeddings are processed by a ViT visual encoder. For InternVL, a pixel‑shuffling step compresses the token grid; LLaVA uses all tokens directly. The resulting visual tokens are concatenated with text prompt embeddings and fed into the LLM (Vicuna‑13B for LLaVA, Qwen3‑14B for InternVL). Final hidden states are unembedded to vocabulary logits, and greedy decoding produces the answer.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  • *Resolution vs. token count:* Higher input resolution captures finer details but increases token count, taxing the LLM’s context window.  
  • *Integration tightness:* LLaVA uses a simple MLP adapter; InternVL’s pixel‑shuffling and richer pretraining yield tighter coupling (higher consistency, stability).  
  • *Tokenizer alignment:* The visual token vocabulary must align with the LLM’s textual token space; misalignment could force indirect mapping.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  • Large gap between full‑scene and object‑only accuracy, especially for superordinate tasks.  
  • Low representational stability (cosine similarity near chance).  
  • Scene and superordinate AUC in image tokens diverge (scene high, superordinate low).  
  • Inconsistency between predicted object and scene/superordinate labels.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline contextual inference:** For each model, run the three tasks (scene, superordinate, object) on full‑scene vs. object‑only images to confirm the main behavioral pattern (drop in scene accuracy, robustness of superordinate).  
  2. **Object property modulation:** Run the multivariate logistic regression on object‑only trials to replicate the log‑odds coefficients for size, frequency, specificity. Check that specificity is the top predictor for scene and size for superordinate.  
  3. **Representational stability profiling:** Compute cosine similarity of object‑patch tokens across conditions per layer. Validate that stability is higher in InternVL and that the correct/incorrect trial difference appears in middle layers for InternVL and later layers for LLaVA.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly outline open questions beyond the limitations and future work implied in the discussion.

## Limitations
- Causal link between representational stability and contextual accuracy remains unproven.
- Analysis of object properties may confound low‑level visual features.
- Findings are limited to two models, one with a proprietary encoder, restricting generalizability.
- Use of a relaxed evaluation metric inflates contextual performance measures.

## Confidence
- Contextual inference from single objects: **High**
- Object property modulation: **Medium**
- Representational stability prediction: **Medium**
- Differential grounding (scene vs. superordinate): **Medium**
- Model differences (InternVL vs. LLaVA): **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Stability manipulation:** Apply adversarial perturbations targeting object‑patch token consistency across conditions; measure resulting change in contextual accuracy to test causality.
2. **Alternative superordinate probing:** Train linear decoders on early‑layer activations for indoor/outdoor classification; if performance exceeds chance early, the late‑emergence claim weakens.
3. **Low‑level feature control:** Replicate property analysis on object images matched for color, texture, and complexity; if property effects vanish, original findings may reflect confounds.
