# Thinking with Deltas: Incentivizing Reinforcement Learning via Differential Visual Reasoning Policy

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06801
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06801
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** DVRP yields a 22.2% relative improvement on average, achieving 76.4% accuracy on medical benchmarks and surpassing larger models like GPT-4o on PathVQA.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the critical issue of perception-reasoning decoupling in multimodal reinforcement learning, where models become "blind reasoners" by exploiting linguistic priors instead of genuinely processing visual evidence. The authors propose the **Thinking with Deltas** framework, introducing a **Differential Visual Reasoning Policy (DVRP)** that operates on visual triplets—original, masked, and noise-perturbed inputs. DVRP enforces two constraints: maximizing reasoning divergence from masked inputs (visual sensitivity) and minimizing divergence from perturbed inputs (visual robustness). This forces the model’s reasoning to align strictly with the delta (change) in visual information. Evaluated on the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, DVRP demonstrates substantial gains across general and medical benchmarks, with significant improvements even on smaller 3B models, indicating the effectiveness of differential constraints in enhancing visual perception without external annotations.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is the DVRP, which processes three versions of an input image: the original, a masked version (with key regions hidden), and a noise-perturbed version. During training, the policy optimizes two complementary objectives. First, it maximizes the divergence between the reasoning produced from the original image and that from the masked image, ensuring the model’s logic is sensitive to removed visual content. Second, it minimizes the divergence between reasoning from the original and the perturbed image, promoting robustness to irrelevant noise. By jointly training with these constraints, the model is incentivized to base its reasoning exclusively on the differential visual information present in the original image versus the masked/perturbed variants, thereby mitigating reliance on superficial linguistic patterns.

## Key Results
- DVRP on Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves a **22.2% relative improvement** in average accuracy over the base model across general and medical benchmarks.
- Reaches **76.4% accuracy** on medical tasks, surpassing larger models like GPT-4o on the PathVQA benchmark.
- Even on the smaller Qwen2.5-VL-3B model, DVRP yields a **67.6% relative gain**, demonstrating strong scalability.
- Ablation studies confirm that both sensitivity and robustness constraints are necessary and synergistic.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The differential triplet-based training directly counters "blind reasoning" by making the model's internal reasoning state explicitly contingent on the *change* in visual input. Maximizing divergence from masked inputs forces the model to acknowledge what visual evidence is missing; if it ignored the mask, its reasoning would remain similar. Minimizing divergence from perturbed inputs prevents the model from overfitting to noise or irrelevant details, ensuring it only uses meaningful visual deltas. This creates a tight coupling between perception and reasoning: the model cannot generate a reasoning trace that is either invariant to critical visual removals or variable to irrelevant noise. The reinforcement learning setup rewards policies that satisfy both, aligning the reasoning process with the true visual delta.

## Foundational Learning
- **Perception-Reasoning Decoupling**: The phenomenon where multimodal models produce correct answers without genuinely using visual input, relying on dataset biases or language priors. *Why needed*: To diagnose and address a fundamental flaw in current vision-language models. *Quick check*: Compare model performance on original vs. fully masked images; a "blind reasoner" shows minimal drop.
- **Visual Sensitivity**: The degree to which a model’s reasoning output changes when critical visual information is removed. *Why needed*: Ensures the model’s logic depends on the presence of visual evidence. *Quick check*: Compute reasoning divergence (e.g., embedding distance) between outputs from original and masked images.
- **Visual Robustness**: The consistency of reasoning under irrelevant perturbations (e.g., noise). *Why needed*: Prevents the model from reacting to non-semantic changes, focusing instead on true content. *Quick check*: Measure similarity between outputs from original and perturbed images; high similarity indicates robustness.
- **Differential Policy Optimization**: Training a policy (here, the reasoning process) based on differences between related inputs rather than absolute performance. *Why needed*: To explicitly shape the reasoning trajectory to follow visual deltas. *Quick check*: Ablate one constraint and observe degradation in benchmark performance and failure modes.
- **Triplet-Based Contrastive Learning**: Using three related instances (original, negative/masked, positive/perturbed) to define a learning objective. *Why needed*: Provides a natural definition of what "aligned with visual evidence" means. *Quick check*: Verify that gradients from the two constraints push reasoning in opposing but complementary directions.
- **Reinforcement Learning from Visual Feedback**: Framing reasoning improvement as a reinforcement problem where rewards are derived from visual triplet consistency. *Why needed*: Allows fine-grained optimization of the reasoning trace, not just final answer accuracy. *Quick check*: Monitor per-step reasoning token rewards during training; they should correlate with triplet constraints.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**:
  Input Image -> Triplet Encoder (produces embeddings for original, masked, noise) -> DVRP Module (computes reasoning for each, applies constraint losses) -> Reasoning Output (final answer + trace) -> Reward Signal (based on triplet divergence metrics) -> Policy Update (via RL algorithm).
- **Critical Path**: The processing flow that must execute correctly for the differential incentive to function: image -> triplet encoding -> individual reasoning generation -> divergence calculation -> loss application -> backpropagation. A break anywhere (e.g., flawed masking) nullifies the constraint.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: 
  - **Sensitivity vs. Robustness**: Over-emphasizing sensitivity may make reasoning overly reactive to minor masking; over-emphasizing robustness may ignore subtle but critical visual cues. The balance is a key hyperparameter.
  - **Compute vs. Gain**: Processing three images per sample triples encoder cost; the paper does not report FLOPs/epoch, so efficiency versus baseline is unclear.
  - **Backbone Lock-in**: The constraints are applied on top of a frozen or fine-tuned VL model; effectiveness may depend on the base model’s initial visual grounding capability.
- **Failure Signatures**:
  - High sensitivity *and* low robustness: Reasoning changes drastically with both masks and noise, indicating it is not focusing on meaningful visual delta but is brittle.
  - Low sensitivity *and* high robustness: Reasoning is static across all triplets, suggesting the policy ignores visual constraints entirely (a persistent blind reasoner).
  - Degraded performance on perturbed-only benchmarks: If the model becomes too sensitive, it might fail on images with natural variations not seen in training perturbations.
- **First Three Experiments**:
  1. **Replication on Qwen2.5-VL-7B**: Run the exact training protocol on the same model checkpoint with reported hyperparameters, verifying the 22.2% gain on the specified benchmark suite.
  2. **Transfer to a Different Architecture**: Apply DVRP to a distinct VL model (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision or InternVL2) using the same triplet procedure to test architectural independence.
  3. **Constraint Ablation on PathVQA**: Systematically remove (a) only sensitivity loss, (b) only robustness loss, and (c) both, measuring accuracy drop and reasoning trace divergence to confirm synergy.
