# Sharp analysis of linear ensemble sampling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08026
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08026
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Ensemble size \(m = \Theta(d \log n)\) enables \(\tilde{O}(d^{3/2}\sqrt{n})\) high‑probability regret; \(m \le d/2\) yields linear regret.

## Executive Summary
This paper delivers a sharp analysis of linear ensemble sampling (ES) in stochastic linear bandits with Gaussian perturbations. The key innovation is a continuous‑time embedding—via the Dambis‑Dubins‑Schwarz theorem—that recasts the self‑normalized martingale transforms as independent Brownian motions observed at their quadratic variation times. This reduction transforms the adaptive exceedance problem into a time‑uniform control problem for Brownian motions. Leveraging this view, the authors prove that with an ensemble size \(m = \Theta(d \log n)\), ES attains \(\tilde{O}(d^{3/2}\sqrt{n})\) regret with high probability, matching Thompson sampling. Conversely, they show that \(m \le d/2\) leads to linear regret with constant probability, establishing a sharp dimension‑dependent threshold for ensemble size.

## Method Summary
The analysis proceeds in two conceptual stages. First, a **master regret theorem** (Lemma 5.1) shows that if the self‑normalized exceedance frequency of the ensemble’s perturbations is uniformly bounded below by a constant \(p\) across all directions and times, then the regret is at most \(O(\frac{1}{p} \cdot \gamma_\delta \beta_\delta \cdot \sqrt{d t \log(1 + t/(d\lambda)) + \sqrt{t \log(1/\delta)}})\). Here \(\gamma_\delta\) and \(\beta_\delta\) are standard confidence‑scale parameters. Second, for fixed directions, the authors use the Dambis‑Dubins‑Schwarz embedding to represent the exceedance process as independent Brownian motions evaluated at a common time parameter. A dedicated time‑uniform Brownian exceedance result (Theorem 5.4) then guarantees that with \(m = \Theta(d \log n)\), the fraction of Brownian motions exceeding a fixed threshold exceeds \(p = \Theta(1)\) uniformly over a time interval of length \(O(n)\) with probability \(1-O(\delta)\). A covering argument then lifts the guarantee from fixed directions to all directions, at the cost of an additional logarithmic factor. Putting the pieces together yields the \(\tilde{O}(d^{3/2}\sqrt{n})\) high‑probability regret when \(m = \Theta(d \log n)\). A lower‑bound construction using adversarial priors demonstrates that if the ensemble is too small (\(m \le d/2\)), the span of the initial perturbations may miss \(\theta^*\), causing linear regret with constant probability.

## Key Results
- For \(m = \Theta(d \log n)\), the pseudo‑regret after \(n\) rounds satisfies \(R_n \le C \, d^{3/2} \sqrt{n} \, \text{polylog}(n,d)\) with probability at least \(1-4\delta\).
- If \(m \le d/2\), there exists a constant such that \(\mathbb{P}(R_n \ge \Omega(n)) \ge 1/2\) for all \(n\).
- The required ensemble size scales linearly with the dimension \(d\) (up to a logarithmic factor), matching the lower bound up to log factors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Continuous‑time embedding of diagonal Gaussian martingales
- **Claim:** The self‑normalized martingale transforms in ES can be exactly represented as independent Brownian motions evaluated at their quadratic variation times.
- **Mechanism:** For diagonal transforms \(M_t = \sum_{s=0}^t D_s \xi_s\) with \(D_s\) predictable diagonal and \(\xi_s\) i.i.d. standard Gaussian, the Dambis‑Dubins‑Schwarz theorem embeds each coordinate \(M^j_t\) as \(W^j(\langle M^j \rangle_t)\). Because ES yields scalar multiples of the identity, the clocks \(A_t^2 = \sum_{s=0}^t D_s^2\) are common across coordinates, producing independent Brownian motions with the same time parameterization.
- **Core assumption:** Gaussian perturbations and diagonal martingale structure (due to ridge regression with a Gaussian prior).
- **Evidence anchors:** Theorem 5.3 (Appendix D) constructs the embedding; the abstract explicitly mentions the reduction to a time‑uniform exceedance problem for \(m\) independent Brownian motions.
- **Break condition:** The embedding relies critically on Gaussianity and scalar diagonal matrices; it fails for non‑Gaussian noise or non‑diagonal designs.

### Mechanism 2: Reduction of regret to exceedance frequency
- **Claim:** The \(n\)-step regret of ES is bounded (high probability) by an expression inversely proportional to a uniform lower bound on exceedance frequencies \(E_{t,m}(u,1/\gamma)\).
- **Mechanism:** Lemma 5.1 (master bound) states: if \(\min_{t\in[n]} \inf_{u\in S^{d-1}} E_{t,m}(u,1/\gamma) \ge p\) holds with prob. \(\ge 1-\delta\), then \(R_n \le O(\frac{1}{p} \gamma_\delta \beta_\delta \sqrt{d t \log(1+t/(d\lambda)) + \sqrt{t \log(1/\delta)}})\). Choosing \(p = \Theta(1)\) and \(\gamma_\delta = O(\sqrt{d \log n})\) yields \(\tilde{O}(d^{3/2}\sqrt{n})\).
- **Core assumption:** (i) all ensemble parameters stay within a ball of radius \(\gamma_\delta \beta_\delta\) w.h.p.; (ii) the confidence ellipsoid contains \(\theta^*\) w.h.p.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions the reduction; Lemma 5.1 and Lemma 5.2 (fixed→uniform direction) implement it; prior work (Janz et al., 2024) had weaker exceedance control giving a worse \(\tilde{O}(d^{5/2}\sqrt{n})\) bound.
- **Break condition:** If the uniform exceedance lower bound is not constant, the \(1/p\) factor destroys the sublinear rate, which occurs for too small \(m\) or too large \(\gamma\).

### Mechanism 3: Time‑uniform Brownian exceedance
- **Claim:** For \(m\) independent Brownian motions, \(\inf_{t\in[\tau,\tau']} \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=1}^m \mathbf{1}\{ W^j(t)/\sqrt{t} \ge c \} \ge p\) holds with probability at least \(1-\delta\) provided \(m \ge \frac{4}{p} \log\left( \frac{\lceil \log(\tau'/\tau)/h \rceil}{\delta} \right)\), for small \(c,p\) and a fixed \(h\) (e.g., \(h=1/250\)).
- **Mechanism:** Discretize \([\tau,\tau']\) geometrically with step \(h\); Chernoff bounds ensure many exceedances at grid points. Ornstein‑Uhlenbeck time‑change and Brownian supremum estimates propagate the high count across intervals. A union bound yields uniformity.
- **Core assumption:** \(c \le 1/20\), \(p \le 1/10\), and \(h=1/250\) are admissible; in ES, \(\tau = \lambda\), \(\tau' = \lambda+n\) so \(\log(\tau'/\tau) = O(\log n)\).
- **Evidence anchors:** Theorem 5.4 and its proof (Appendix E); Darling‑Erdős (1956) for static exceedance; time‑uniform continuous version is novel.
- **Break condition:** Larger \(c\) or \(p\) reduces Gaussian tails and increases required \(m\); larger time span increases the \(\log\log n\) factor.

## Foundational Learning

### Stochastic linear bandits
- **Why needed:** The entire setting (actions, rewards, ridge regression updates, confidence ellipsoids) assumes the linear model. The algorithm and analysis rely on this structure.
- **Quick check:** Write the ridge estimate \(\hat{\theta}_{t-1}\) and the confidence ellipsoid \(\Theta^{\delta}_{t-1}\).

### Martingale concentration and self‑normalized processes
- **Why needed:** The exceedance frequencies \(E_{t,m}(u,1/\gamma)\) involve self‑normalized martingale transforms; concentration inequalities (e.g., Abbasi‑Yadkori et al., 2011) are used to bound quantities like \(\| \sum_{s=1}^t \xi_s X_s \|_{V_t^{-1}}\).
- **Quick check:** State a high‑probability bound for \(\| \sum_{s=1}^t \xi_s X_s \|_{V_t^{-1}}\) when \(\xi_s\) are 1‑subgaussian.

### Dambis‑Dubins‑Schwarz theorem
- **Why needed:** It provides the exact representation of each coordinate's martingale as a Brownian motion with a time change, the core embedding step that reduces the discrete‑time adaptive analysis to a continuous‑time time‑uniform problem.
- **Quick check:** What does the DDS theorem say about a continuous local martingale \(M\) with \(\langle M \rangle_\infty < \infty\)?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Algorithm 1 (Ensemble Sampling) → Analysis modules: Lemma 5.1 (exceedance→regret), Lemma 5.2 (fixed→uniform direction), Theorem 5.3 (Brownian embedding), Theorem 5.4 (time‑uniform exceedance), Lemma G.1 (\(\gamma_\delta\) bound), Theorem 4.2 (lower bound).

### Critical path
1. Assume uniform exceedance bound on \(E_{t,m}(u,1/\gamma)\).
2. Apply Lemma 5.2: reduces to fixed \(u\) if \(m = \Omega(d \log(1/p) \log\log n)\) (\(p = \Theta(1)\)).
3. For fixed \(u\), Theorem 5.3 embeds the self‑normalized process as \(W^j(A^2_t)/\sqrt{A^2_t}\) with \(A^2_t = \|u\|_{V_{t-1}}^2 \in [\lambda, \lambda+n]\).
4. Apply Theorem 5.4 on \([\lambda, \lambda+n]\) with \(c=1/20\), \(p=1/10\), \(h=1/250\) → requires \(m = \Omega((1/p) \log\log n)\).
5. Combine: \(m = \Omega(d \log n)\) yields uniform exceedance.
6. Plug into Lemma 5.1 and use Lemma G.1 (\(\gamma_{\delta,t}=O(\sqrt{d \log n})\)) to get \(R_n = \tilde{O}(d^{3/2}\sqrt{n})\).

### Design tradeoffs
- **Ensemble size m:** Larger \(m\) improves exploration (higher exceedance probability) but increases per‑round cost \(O(m d^2)\). Theory shows \(m = \Theta(d \log n)\) is necessary and sufficient for \(\tilde{O}(d^{3/2}\sqrt{n})\) regret; smaller \(m\) (e.g., \(m \le d/2\)) causes linear regret.
- **Noise inflation \(\gamma\):** Controls optimism threshold \(1/\gamma\). Larger \(\gamma\) makes exceedance easier but increases the \(\gamma_\delta\) factor in the regret; analysis sets \(\gamma=40\).
- **Regularization \(\lambda\):** Affects \(\beta_{\delta,t}\) and \(\gamma_\delta\); \(\lambda \ge 80\) required for concentration.
- **Confidence \(\delta\):** Smaller \(\delta\) increases \(\beta_{\delta,t}\) and the logarithmic factors in the required \(m\).

### Failure signatures
- **\(m \le d/2\):** The span of initial perturbations may miss \(\theta^*\) → linear regret (Theorem 4.2).
- **Non‑Gaussian perturbations:** The DDS embedding (Theorem 5.3) fails; no sharp bound known.
- **Large action set diameter:** The Lipschitz constant in Lemma 5.2 scales with \(\sqrt{1 + n/\lambda}\) times \(\|V_{t-1}\|\), which may increase if actions are not in the unit ball, raising required \(m\).
- **Overly large \(c\) or \(p\) in Theorem 5.4:** The admissible step \(h\) shrinks, increasing the \(\log\log n\) factor.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Embedding validation:** Simulate ES for a fixed direction \(u \in \mathbb{R}^d\) (start with \(d=1\)). At each time \(t\), compute the self‑normalized martingale transform \(M^j_t = u^\top V_{t-1}^{-1} S_{t-1}^j\). Compare the distribution of \(\{M^j_t\}_{j=1}^m\) to a Gaussian with variance \(\|u\|_{V_{t-1}}^2\), and verify that the time‑changed process \(M^j_t\) behaves like Brownian motion at time \(A_t^2 = \|u\|_{V_{t-1}}^2\). Check independence across coordinates \(j\).
2. **Exceedance test:** For \(m\) independent Brownian motions on an interval \([\lambda, \lambda+n]\) (e.g., \(\lambda=80\), \(n=10^4\)), threshold \(c=0.05\), and target fraction \(p=0.1\), compute the empirical lowest proportion \(\min_t \frac{1}{m}\sum_j \mathbf{1}\{W^j(t)/\sqrt{t} \ge c\}\). Estimate the probability that this minimum is at least \(p\) as a function of \(m\); verify that the bound from Theorem 5.4 is roughly tight.
3. **Regret scaling:** Run ES on the unit ball in dimension \(d=2\) with a random \(\theta^*\). Vary the ensemble size \(m\) (e.g., \(2d, 4d, 8d, 16d\)). For each \(m\), compute average and 90‑percentile regret over many trials as \(n\) grows. Plot \(R_n\) vs \(\sqrt{n}\) and \(R_n / d^{3/2}\) vs \(\sqrt{n}\). Verify that for \(m = \Theta(d \log n)\) the regret scales like \(\sqrt{n}\), while for \(m \le d/2\) the regret appears linear. Also compare to Thompson sampling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### 1. Non‑Gaussian perturbations?
- **Question:** Can time‑uniform self‑normalized exceedance frequency guarantees be established for ensemble sampling with non‑Gaussian perturbations (e.g., Rademacher) using tools like Skorokhod embeddings or strong approximation couplings?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors conjecture this holds but note the proof relies on Gaussianity; alternative tools are needed.
- **Why unresolved:** The Dambis‑Dubins‑Schwarz embedding fails for non‑Gaussian noise; new probabilistic machinery would be required.
- **Resolution evidence:** A sharp exceedance bound for non‑Gaussian perturbations, perhaps via constructing suitable time‑changed processes or embeddings.

### 2. Nonlinear or generalized linear models?
- **Question:** Do the continuous‑time embedding techniques extend to ensemble sampling for nonlinear or generalized linear models, where the effective noise retains an approximately conditionally independent component structure?
- **Basis:** The paper mentions this as an interesting open direction.
- **Why unresolved:** Nonlinear models break the diagonal structure and independence of martingale components; the analysis is tightly coupled to linearity.
- **Resolution evidence:** A rigorous analysis showing a similar time‑uniform exceedance control for a class of nonlinear bandit models leading to optimal or near‑optimal regret.

### 3. Geometry‑aware refinements?
- **Question:** Can the continuous‑time embedding be combined with geometry‑aware techniques (e.g., from Abeille et al., 2025) to obtain tighter, dimension‑free or geometry‑dependent regret bounds for ensemble sampling with structured action sets?
- **Basis:** The paper suggests integrating ideas from Abeille et al. (2025).
- **Why unresolved:** Current analysis is tailored to the worst‑case unit ball and does not exploit additional geometric structure; combining the embedding with geometry‑dependent tools is nontrivial.
- **Resolution evidence:** A refined bound that scales with problem‑dependent geometric parameters (e.g., minimal eigenvalue of design matrix, covering number) and improves known rates for specific action sets.

### 4. Broader applications of the embedding?
- **Question:** Are there broader applications of the exact continuous‑time representation for adaptive martingale transforms in analyzing other randomized exploration algorithms beyond ensemble sampling?
- **Basis:** The authors hope their work inspires further use of continuous‑time embeddings.
- **Why unresolved:** The embedding technique is novel and has not yet been explored in other settings.
- **Resolution evidence:** New analyses of randomized algorithms (e.g., Thompson sampling, Bayesian optimization) that employ similar continuous‑time embeddings to obtain sharp regret bounds.

## Limitations
- The constant factor 2000 in the theoretical bound \(m \ge 2000\, d \log(n/\delta)\) is likely very loose; the true minimal ensemble size for \(\Theta(d \log n)\) scaling remains unknown.
- The analysis crucially assumes Gaussian perturbations; extensions to non‑Gaussian noise are not addressed.
- Inflation parameter \(\gamma=40\) and regularization \(\lambda=80\) are not optimized; their impact on hidden constants is unclear.
- The results provide high‑probability regret bounds; expected (mean) regret is not studied.
- The action set is restricted to the unit Euclidean ball; for general bounded sets the required \(m\) may be larger.

## Confidence
- Regret \(\tilde{O}(d^{3/2}\sqrt{n})\) for \(m = \Theta(d \log n)\): **High** (proved via exact Brownian embedding and time‑uniform exceedance).
- Linear regret for \(m \le d/2\): **High** (adversarial lower‑bound construction).
- Exact Brownian embedding reduction: **High** (direct application of Dambis‑Dubins‑Schwarz).
- Time‑uniform Brownian exceedance bound: **Medium** (depends on fixed discretization constants; may be loose but sufficient).
