# The Mask of Civility: Benchmarking Chinese Mock Politeness Comprehension in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03107
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03107
- **Reference Count:** 3
- **Primary Result:** Incorporating theoretical definitions significantly improves classification accuracy, with Doubao achieving 0.91 under knowledge-enhanced prompting.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates how well large language models (LLMs) comprehend Chinese mock politeness (虚假礼貌), a pragmatic phenomenon where polite forms convey impolite intent through implicature. Researchers constructed a 100-sample dataset across three categories—true politeness, impoliteness, and mock politeness—and tested six models under four prompting strategies: zero-shot, few-shot, knowledge-enhanced (with definitions), and hybrid (definitions + examples). Results show that providing explicit theoretical definitions dramatically reduces the persistent misclassification of mock politeness as true politeness, with Chinese-developed models (e.g., DeepSeek, Doubao) generally outperforming others. The work highlights that LLM pragmatic competence can be activated by theory-aligned prompts and is influenced by training data provenance.

## Method Summary
The research assembled a Chinese pragmatic classification dataset containing 100 authentic and simulated dialogues, evenly distributed across three labels. Four prompting strategies were applied: zero-shot (no examples), few-shot (with examples), knowledge-enhanced (providing theoretical definitions of each category), and hybrid (combining definitions with examples). Six LLMs—including GPT-5.1, DeepSeek, Doubao, Qwen variants, and Kimi—were queried via API to produce JSON outputs with a predicted label and rationale. Accuracy was computed against ground truth, with confusion matrices analyzed to assess misclassification patterns, particularly for the nuanced mock politeness category.

## Key Results
- Knowledge-enhanced and hybrid prompting strategies significantly boosted overall accuracy compared to zero-shot and few-shot.
- Doubao achieved peak accuracy (0.91) under the knowledge-enhanced condition.
- The persistent misclassification of mock politeness as true politeness (8–10% error in zero/few-shot) was nearly eliminated when theoretical definitions highlighting the “mismatch” criterion were provided.
- Chinese-developed models (DeepSeek, Doubao, Qwen) consistently outperformed non-Chinese models across most conditions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Definitions Act as Discriminative Constraints**  
Providing explicit theoretical definitions (e.g., “mismatch between politeness and impoliteness”) overrides the model’s default reliance on surface lexical cues. The mismatch criterion forces computation of inferential gaps rather than simple pattern matching, activating latent pragmatic knowledge. Evidence: Misclassification of mock politeness dropped sharply with definition-based prompts [abstract, section 4.2]. This fails if definitions are overly abstract or the model lacks foundational knowledge.

**Mechanism 2: Pretraining Data Provenance Drives Cultural-Pragmatic Competence**  
Chinese models’ superior performance stems from greater exposure to Chinese socio-pragmatic patterns (e.g., irony, critical use of honorifics) in their pretraining corpora. This embeds cultural-pragmatic distributions that align with the dataset’s authentic and simulated examples. Evidence: DeepSeek and Doubao led in zero-shot accuracy [section 4.1], consistent with related language-specific benchmarks. The advantage may diminish if test data diverges from the model’s training distribution.

**Mechanism 3: Few-Shot Learning Lacks Generative Rules for Category Boundaries**  
Few-shot examples alone underperform because they provide positive exemplars but not the explicit theoretical “negative space” (the mismatch rule). Without this, models interpolate from examples but default to the most frequent pattern: polite form equals polite intent. Evidence: Few-shot improved only marginally for some models, while misjudgement rates remained high until definitions were added [section 4.3].

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Pragmatic Classification vs. Semantic Sentiment**  
  *Why needed:* Mock politeness is not negative sentiment; it is a positive form with negative implicature. Confusing lexical polarity with speech-act intent causes the core misclassification error.  
  *Quick check:* Why is “Sure, sure (laughs)” in response to a degrading request not true politeness, despite “sure” being a positive word?

- **Concept: Prompt Engineering as Theory Injection**  
  *Why needed:* Performance hinges on how pragmatic theory is encoded in prompts (definitions vs. examples). Engineers must structure prompts that operationalize theoretical constructs (e.g., “mismatch”) for the model.  
  *Quick check:* Design a zero-shot prompt for “mock politeness” using only the core idea of “mismatch” without providing examples.

- **Concept: Model Origin and Cultural Pragmatics**  
  *Why needed:* The performance gap between Chinese and non-Chinese models suggests pragmatics is not language-agnostic. Model training data provenance is critical for culture-specific speech acts.  
  *Quick check:* For an Arabic politeness benchmark, should you prioritize parameter count or the developer’s regional/linguistic focus?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Dataset -> Prompt Template (strategy selection) -> Model API Call -> Parsed JSON Output -> Accuracy Calculation -> Confusion Matrix Generation.  
- **Critical path:** A curated 100-sample dataset feeds into a prompt templating engine that instantiates one of four strategies; the prompt is sent to a model API; the JSON response is parsed; predictions are compared to ground truth to compute accuracy and generate confusion matrices.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Definition clarity vs. flexibility: Overly precise definitions may not cover edge cases; vague ones fail to guide.  
  - Example diversity vs. noise: Few-shot examples must be prototypical but not overly narrow.  
  - Model capability vs. latency: Chinese models showed better accuracy, but latency metrics were unreported; hybrid strategies add token cost.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Consistently high “Mock Politeness → True Politeness” confusion in zero/few-shot.  
  - Performance drop when switching from few-shot to knowledge-enhanced for some models (e.g., GPT-5.1, Doubao), indicating poor definition integration.  
  - Impoliteness accuracy plateau (~28–31%) across strategies, suggesting the bottleneck is exclusively the nuanced mock politeness category.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Baseline replication: Run all four prompting strategies on the dataset with Doubao to reproduce Table 4 and verify hybrid strategy stability.  
  2. Definition ablation: For the Def-based strategy, systematically remove key phrases from the mock politeness definition (e.g., “mismatch,” “implicature”) and measure accuracy delta.  
  3. Cross-category stress test: Generate boundary samples between Impoliteness and Mock Politeness (e.g., highly sarcastic but contextually appropriate) and test if Doubao Def-based maintains its advantage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Dataset Representativeness**  
   How can future research address the small dataset size and reliance on simulated data to better capture the nuanced, organic use of Chinese mock politeness in real-world discourse?  
   *Basis:* Limitations explicitly state the 100-sample size is small and simulated data may not fully capture organic speech.  
   *Why unresolved:* Small, synthetic datasets limit generalizability and may not represent full complexity.  
   *Evidence needed:* A larger-scale, primarily authentic dataset with fine-grained pragmatist annotations, followed by replication.

2. **Depth of Pragmatic Reasoning**  
   Beyond classification accuracy, what specific aspects of pragmatic reasoning (e.g., implicature strength, contextual sensitivity, stance inference) remain unassessed, and how can they be measured in LLMs?  
   *Basis:* Limitations note that classification accuracy may not reflect depth of reasoning.  
   *Why unresolved:* Accuracy treats classification as categorical, not probing inferential processes or graded interpretations.  
   *Evidence needed:* Targeted probe tasks (e.g., ranking implicature likelihood, explaining reasoning) for mock politeness.

3. **Root of Chinese-Model Advantage**  
   Is the superior performance of Chinese-developed models primarily due to (a) training data composition, (b) explicit cultural/linguistic alignment, or (c) architectural choices optimized for Chinese?  
   *Basis:* Results show Chinese models outperforming others; paper suggests exposure to Chinese conversational data but does not isolate the factor.  
   *Why unresolved:* Study compares models without controlling for training data characteristics, tuning objectives, or architecture.  
   *Evidence needed:* Controlled ablation experiments varying training data sources, fine-tuning stages, or architecture variants.

4. **Critical Definition Components**  
   Which specific components of the provided theoretical definitions (e.g., “mismatch” criterion, “impoliteness implicature” clause) are most responsible for reducing misclassification?  
   *Basis:* Def-based and hybrid strategies reduced 8–10% misclassification, attributed to “pragmatic theory,” but no decomposition of definitional elements.  
   *Why unresolved:* Prompt templates combine multiple elements; the pivotal component is unknown.  
   *Evidence needed:* Systematic ablation of definition clauses (e.g., removing “mismatch”) with error analysis on mock politeness samples.

## Limitations
- The full 100-sample dataset is unreleased; reconstruction from six seed examples may yield different difficulty and distribution.
- Model versions (e.g., GPT-5.1, Doubao-Seed-1.6) lack clear public documentation/APIs, risking comparison against updated or inaccessible variants.
- Exact prompt structuring (system/user message split, hybrid strategy order) and handling of invalid outputs are unspecified.

## Confidence
- **Def-based prompting eliminates mock politeness confusion:** Medium (strong within-paper evidence but dependent on unreleased dataset quality and definition wording).
- **Chinese-developed models inherently outperform:** High (consistent zero-shot results align with related language-specific benchmarks).
- **Few-shot underperforms due to lack of mismatch rule:** High (directly supported by reported misclassification rates and their elimination via definitions).

## Next Checks
1. **Dataset reconstruction & agreement study:** Generate a new 100-sample dataset using the paper’s criteria from the six provided examples. Have two annotators label all samples and compute Cohen’s κ; if κ < 0.8, original labeling stability is questionable.
2. **Definition component ablation:** For the top model (Doubao) under knowledge-enhanced prompting, systematically remove key phrases from the mock politeness definition (e.g., remove “mismatch between…”) and measure accuracy drop per removal.
3. **Cross-benchmark replication of model-origin effect:** Apply the same four prompting strategies to the ADAB Arabic politeness benchmark. Test if Chinese models still outperform others; failure to replicate would indicate dataset-specific rather than general cultural-pragmatic strength.
