# Causal-INSIGHT: Probing Temporal Models to Extract Causal Structure

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25473  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25473  
- **Reference count:** 38  
- **Primary result:** Introduces a model-agnostic framework that extracts directed temporal causal structures from frozen time series predictors via systematic input clamping and an unsupervised Qbic graph selection criterion.

## Executive Summary
Causal-INSIGHT addresses the challenge of interpreting black-box temporal predictors by probing them to reveal underlying causal dependencies without altering training. The method clamps individual variables at specific times, measures resulting prediction deviations across all variables and future steps to form an influence tensor, reduces this to a soft adjacency matrix via peak detection, and selects a sparse graph using the novel Qbic score. Evaluations on synthetic, Lorenz-96, and fMRI benchmarks show competitive structural accuracy (F1), significant improvements in temporal delay precision, and reduced error metrics compared to baselines, while scaling linearly with the number of variables.

## Method Summary
The framework first trains a temporal predictor under causal masking to prevent leakage. Once frozen, a probing engine systematically clamps each variable at each time point to an extremal value, runs forward passes, and computes the absolute deviation between clamped and unclamped predictions for all variables and future times, yielding a 3D influence tensor *S*. A temporal reducer then compresses *S* into a peak adjacency matrix by taking the maximum magnitude per variable pair and recording the dominant lag. Finally, a graph selector evaluates candidate graphs (from top *m* peaks) using Qbic: it masks all but the inferred parents for each variable, measures prediction error, and combines this with a sparsity penalty to pick the optimal edge count *m̂* without ground-truth labels.

## Key Results
- Competitive structural accuracy (F1) on synthetic and Lorenz-96 benchmarks; statistically significant improvements in Precision of Delay when probing CausalFormer.
- On fMRI data, attains lower Structural Hamming Distance and False Discovery Rate while improving True Positive Rate versus deep learning and classical baselines.
- Linear computational scaling with the number of variables, adding manageable overhead to the backbone predictor.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Systematic input clamping triggers measurable deviations in a frozen predictor's outputs, which encode directed temporal influences.
- **Mechanism:** For each variable *i*, a single time point is clamped to an extremal value while other inputs remain fixed. The absolute difference between clamped and unclamped predictions for all variables over time forms a 3D influence tensor *S*. A large |*S*<sub>i,j,t</sub>| indicates that clamping *i* at *t*<sub>0</sub> affects the prediction for *j* at time *t*, implying the predictor relies on *i*'s past to predict *j* at lag *t-t*<sub>0</sub>.
- **Core assumption:** The trained temporal predictor encodes Granger-causal dependencies; clamping isolates the marginal contribution of a single variable's history.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "probes a frozen predictor via systematic input clamping, measures the resulting prediction deviations across time"; [section IV.B] "We define the influence signal tensor *S* as the absolute deviation between clamped and baseline predictions."
- **Break condition:** If the predictor's predictions are insensitive to the clamped variable's history (e.g., due to poor training or architectural incapacity), *S* will not contain meaningful peaks, leading to random or empty graphs.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Summarizing each variable pair's influence tensor with a peak response yields a soft adjacency matrix with associated dominant lags.
- **Mechanism:** For each (i→j) pair, the maximum magnitude across the time dimension of *S* is taken (*S*<sub>peak</sub><sub>i,j</sub> = max<sub>t</sub> |*S*<sub>i,j,t</sub>|). The lag *ℓ*<sub>i,j</sub> at which this peak occurs is recorded. This compresses distributed temporal effects into a single, most influential delay, forming a directed edge candidate.
- **Core assumption:** The strongest causal influence manifests as a salient peak in the response profile; multiple or diffuse lags are secondary or represent indirect effects.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section IV.D] "To summarize the temporal influence profile for each variable pair, we compute the peak deviation across time: *S*<sub>peak</sub><sub>i,j</sub> = max<sub>t</sub> *S*<sub>i,j,t</sub>."
- **Break condition:** For systems with genuinely distributed or multi-frequency influences, peak selection discards meaningful information, reducing recall or mislocalizing delay.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The Qbic score selects a graph sparsity level by balancing predictive fidelity (under parent-based masking) and structural complexity, without ground-truth labels.
- **Mechanism:** For a candidate graph *G* with *m* edges, Qbic = Σ<sub>j</sub> [ *n* log(MSE<sub>j</sub>) + *λ k*<sub>j</sub> log(*n*) ]. MSE<sub>j</sub> is computed by masking all inputs except the inferred parents *Pa(j)*. Lower Qbic indicates a graph where parent sets are sufficient for the predictor's predictions (low MSE) but not overly dense (penalized by *k*<sub>j</sub>). The optimal *m̂* minimizes Qbic over a range.
- **Core assumption:** If inferred parent sets are correct, masking non-parents should not severely degrade the predictor's performance; omitting true parents increases error. The penalty term prevents overfitting to noise.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section IV.E] "Qbic scores a candidate graph by measuring how well its inferred parent sets preserve the predictor’s behavior... *Qbic*(*G*<sub>(m)</sub>) = Σ<sub>j=1</sub><sup>N</sup> [*n*log(MSE<sub>j</sub>) + *λ k*<sub>j</sub> log(*n*)]."; [section VII.A] Ablation shows Qbic minimum correlates with high F1 (Pearson: -0.77±0.36) and achieves 91%±7% of max F1.
- **Break condition:** If the predictor's performance is not sensitive to removing specific inputs (e.g., due to redundancy), MSE differences between candidate graphs vanish, making Qbic uninformative. The fixed *λ*=0.4 may also be suboptimal for all datasets.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Granger causality (temporal).**  
  *Why needed here:* The entire framework interprets predictor dependencies through a Granger lens: variable *i* influences *j* at lag *ℓ* if past *i* improves prediction of *j* beyond other histories. Causal-INSIGHT's clamping probes this directly.  
  *Quick check question:* Given a predictor, how would you test if the past values of variable A at lag 2 are used to predict variable B?

- **Concept: Model-agnostic post-hoc interpretation.**  
  *Why needed here:* The method must work with any frozen backbone (MLP, CNN, LSTM, Transformer) without altering training. Understanding this separation (train once, probe later) is key to its claimed generality.  
  *Quick check question:* What does "model-agnostic" mean in the context of extracting causal structure, and what are its trade-offs compared to training with causal constraints?

- **Concept: Sparsity-regularized model selection (BIC-style).**  
  *Why needed here:* Graph structure is unknown; Qbic adapts the BIC principle to an unsupervised setting by using prediction error under masking as a proxy for "fit" and edge count for complexity.  
  *Quick check question:* Why not simply pick the graph with the highest *S*<sub>peak</sub> values? What problem does the Qbic penalty solve?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Causal masking front-end -> Backbone predictor head -> Probing engine -> Temporal reducer -> Graph selector (Qbic)
- **Critical path:**  
  Train backbone under temporal masking → Freeze model → For each variable *i*: clamp *X*<sub>i,t₀</sub> → get ˆ*X*<sup>(*i*)</sup> → compute *S*<sub>:,:,t</sub> = |ˆ*X*<sup>(*i*)</sup> - ˆ*X*| → aggregate peaks → generate candidate graphs for range of *m* → for each graph, compute Qbic via parent masking → select *m̂*.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Clamping value *x*<sup>*</sup>: Extremal (max or 0) gives strong signals but may induce out-of-distribution responses; moderate values are safer but noisier.  
  - Peak vs. distributed lags: Peak selection simplifies but may miss multi-lag effects; retaining full *S*<sub>i,j,:</sub> is possible but complicates graph selection.  
  - Qbic lambda (*λ*=0.4 fixed): Avoids tuning but may not suit all datasets; a per-dataset sweep could improve F1 but breaks model-agnostic simplicity.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Low structural F1 (<0.5 on synthetic): predictor failed to learn causal dependencies, or clamping magnitude too weak.  
  - High SHD/FDR on fMRI: Qbic selected too many/few edges, or causal signals *S* were corrupted.  
  - PoD <<1.0: peak lag selection missed true lags, possibly because influences are distributed or peak-finding failed.  
  - Runtime not scaling linearly: unexpected architecture cost (e.g., full Transformer attention) dominates.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Synthetic sanity check:** Train a small MLP on a known fork/v-structure dataset. Run Causal-INSIGHT; expect F1 >0.8 and correct peak lags.  
  2. **fMRI benchmark replication:** Use provided CausalFormer backbone on one fMRI dataset. Compare SHD/TPR/FDR against paper's Table III.  
  3. **Qbic trajectory sanity:** For a dataset, plot Qbic vs. *m* and F1 vs. *m*. Confirm minimum Qbic near max F1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can Causal-INSIGHT be extended to multi-horizon predictors?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion explicitly lists “extending the probing framework to multi‑horizon predictors” as future work.
- **Why unresolved:** The current formulation targets single‑step prediction, where clamping effects are measured one step ahead. Multi‑horizon predictions involve multiple future steps, requiring new aggregation strategies for influence signals across horizons.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Propose and implement a multi‑horizon probing scheme, evaluate on synthetic and real multivariate time series with known multi‑step causal structures, and demonstrate improved structural and temporal accuracy over baselines.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can adaptive clamping schemes better capture distributed or multi‑lag causal influences without excessive computational cost?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion mentions “developing adaptive clamping schemes to better capture distributed or multi‑lag influence patterns” as future work.
- **Why unresolved:** The current approach uses fixed, single‑time‑point clamping per variable. Distributed influences may span multiple lags or require varying clamping magnitudes; a fixed scheme could miss such patterns or require many trials.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Introduce an adaptive algorithm that selects clamping times/magnitudes based on model response gradients or signal quality, validate on benchmarks with known multi‑lag effects, and show gains in delay precision and F1 with comparable runtime.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can Causal-INSIGHT efficiently incorporate multi‑variable interventions to recover higher‑order causal interactions?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper notes that “because Causal‑INSIGHT clamps one variable at a time, it primarily captures marginal influences and may miss higher‑order interactions that manifest jointly across variables. Extending to multi‑variable interventions introduces combinatorial complexity.”
- **Why unresolved:** Joint interventions are needed to identify synergistic or confounding effects, but the number of possible sets grows exponentially. A scalable method for selecting relevant intervention sets is lacking.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Design a greedy or approximation scheme for selecting informative multi‑variable clamping sets, test on synthetic graphs with known higher‑order dependencies, and demonstrate improved edge recall and lower false discovery rate without exceeding feasible runtime.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What are the theoretical guarantees (e.g., consistency, asymptotic behavior) of the Qbic graph selection criterion?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Qbic is introduced as “a heuristic graph‑label‑free score” with no formal justification; the paper only shows empirical correlation with structural F1.
- **Why unresolved:** Without theoretical analysis, Qbic’s reliability under different data regimes (e.g., sample size, noise, sparsity) remains uncertain, limiting principled use and comparison to established criteria like BIC.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Derive asymptotic properties of Qbic under standard assumptions (e.g., correctly specified predictor, i.i.d. errors), prove consistency, and validate on extensive simulations varying sample size and sparsity levels.

## Limitations
- Core assumption that the frozen predictor has learned Granger-causal dependencies; if not, signals become noisy.
- Peak summarization discards distributed temporal influences, inappropriate for multi-lag systems.
- Qbic uses fixed λ=0.4 and assumes predictor sensitivity to parent removal; may fail under redundancy.
- fMRI "ground truth" is community consensus, not a gold standard, limiting claim strength.
- Clamping with extreme values risks out-of-distribution responses, potentially distorting *S*.
