# When Scaling Fails: Mitigating Audio Perception Decay of LALMs via Multi-Step Perception-Aware Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02266
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02266
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MPAR² mitigates audio perception decay in LALMs, improving CAFE perception accuracy from 31.74% to 63.51% and achieving 74.59% on MMAU.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates why scaling inference compute via structured reasoning (e.g., chain-of-thought) in Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs) often fails to improve performance, unlike in text-only models. Using the CAFE evaluation framework, the authors uncover "audio perception decay": as reasoning length increases, the model's ability to correctly perceive and utilize audio events deteriorates, harming reasoning. To address this, they propose MPAR², a multi-step perception-aware reasoning and review paradigm. MPAR² employs a two-stage training (supervised cold-start followed by reinforcement learning) with rewards that enforce explicit audio perception, stepwise perception-reasoning, and a final review to validate audio evidence. This approach improves perception accuracy on CAFE from 31.74% to 63.51% and boosts reasoning accuracy on the MMAU benchmark to 74.59%. MPAR² also mitigates perception decay, strengthens audio attention during reasoning, and dynamically adapts reasoning length to task complexity.

## Method Summary
MPAR² is a two-stage training framework for Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs). Stage 1 (cold-start SFT) fine-tunes the base model (Qwen2.5-Omni) on 4.6k curated samples, training it to generate a structured output format with four stages: `<perception>` (explicit audio event summary), `<reasoning>` (stepwise logic), `<review>` (self-consistency check), and `<answer>`. Stage 2 uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a reward model (Qwen3-32B) to refine the policy. The reward combines four components: perception accuracy (R_perception), stepwise perception-reasoning validity (R_spr) using a geometric mean of step scores and holistic chain consistency, reasoning accuracy (R_rea) gated by review quality, and format compliance (R_format). Training uses 41.5k samples derived from the AVQA dataset: audio captions generated by Gemini-2.5-Pro, QA synthesized by Qwen3-32B, and filtered for quality and base-model difficulty. The approach is evaluated on MMAU, MMAR, and CAFE benchmarks.

## Key Results
- CAFE perception accuracy improves from 31.74% (base) to 63.51% (MPAR²-7B).
- MMAU reasoning accuracy reaches 74.59% with MPAR²-7B, surpassing prior audio-LALMs.
- MPAR² successfully mitigates audio perception decay, sustains audio attention over long reasoning chains, and dynamically adapts reasoning length to task complexity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Audio Perception Decay:** Extended reasoning chains in LALMs suffer from a decline in audio perception accuracy as token length increases, a phenomenon the paper terms "audio perception decay." This is evidenced by a strong negative correlation (Pearson coefficients 0.65–0.91, p<0.05) between reasoning length and perception/reasoning accuracy across multiple models (Section 3, Figure 3). The mechanism is attributed to attention drift and insufficient grounding of audio representations: as the model generates more textual tokens, attention to audio features diminishes, causing cumulative errors in event identification and utilization.

**Explicit Perception Step:** MPAR² introduces a dedicated perception stage at the start of reasoning, forcing the model to first output a chronological summary of audio events grounded in the audio tokens. This "think-while-listening" paradigm creates a persistent reference that subsequent reasoning steps can query, reducing reliance on transient audio attention during decoding. Ablation studies show that removing the perception reward (w/o R_perception) degrades performance (Section 5.2), and attention visualizations confirm MPAR² maintains higher audio attention ratios across token lengths compared to the base model (Section 6, Figure 6).

**Review Step:** A final review branch re-validates audio evidence and logical consistency after reasoning, correcting errors propagated from earlier perception failures. This step is rewarded via RL, encouraging the model to self-audit its reasoning against the fixed perceptual snapshot. The review reward is gated by final answer correctness to ensure quality. Ablations removing the review component (w/o R_review) lead to consistent performance drops (Section 5.2), demonstrating the review's role in closing the perception-reasoning loop.

## Foundational Learning

### Reinforcement Learning from Human/Model Feedback (RLHF/RLAIF)
**Why needed:** MPAR² uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with generative rewards from a teacher model (Qwen3-32B) to shape multi-stage reasoning. Understanding RLHF is key to grasp how reward signals guide structured output.
**Quick check:** How does GRPO differ from standard PPO in handling relative reward scaling within a group of sampled responses?

### Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Test-Time Scaling
**Why needed:** The paper investigates why scaling inference compute via longer CoT fails in LALMs. Foundational knowledge of CoT's role in LLM reasoning and its adaptation to multimodal settings is essential.
**Quick check:** What is the hypothesized difference between text-only CoT scaling and audio CoT scaling that leads to perception decay?

### Audio Tokenization in Multimodal LLMs
**Why needed:** LALMs process audio via discrete tokens or continuous features merged with text embeddings. Perception decay may relate to how audio representations are compressed and attended to over long contexts.
**Quick check:** How do models like Qwen2.5-Omni represent audio in the transformer architecture, and what limits their ability to maintain fine-grained audio attention over hundreds of tokens?

### Evaluation via LLM-as-a-Judge for Process Metrics
**Why needed:** CAFE uses a judge model (Gemini-3-Pro, Qwen3-32B) to extract and categorize audio events from reasoning traces. This requires understanding automated process evaluation beyond final-answer accuracy.
**Quick check:** What are the five event categories (Matched, Hallucinated, etc.) defined in CAFE, and how do they disentangle perception vs. reasoning errors?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Cold-Start SFT Policy (Qwen2.5-Omni fine-tuned on MPAR² format) → GRPO Optimizer (using RL Reward Models based on Qwen3-32B) → Trained MPAR² model; CAFE Evaluator (Gemini-3-Pro) for analysis.

**Critical path:** Data construction (AVQA → Gemini-2.5-Pro captions → Qwen3-32B QA synthesis → filtering) → Stage 1 SFT (4.6k samples, 3 epochs, lr 5e-5) → Stage 2 GRPO (41.5k samples, reward weights α=1.5, β=1.0, γ=1.5, δ=0.1) → Evaluation on MMAU/MMAR/CAFE.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Perception length vs. reasoning budget: Longer perception summaries improve fidelity but consume token budget, potentially shortening reasoning steps.
- Reward complexity: Multiple generative rewards increase training instability and computational cost (multiple LM evaluations per step) but provide fine-grained control.
- Generalization vs. specificity: Training data derived from AVQA (video QA) may not fully transfer to pure audio tasks; reliance on Gemini for captions introduces evaluator bias in CAFE.

**Failure signatures:**
1. Reward hacking: Model learns to format outputs correctly but generates empty or trivial perception/review sections to score on R_format.
2. Perception-review mismatch: Review step fails to detect hallucinations in perception because reward model is not sufficiently discriminative.
3. Over-adaptation to CAFE: Model optimizes for event extraction metrics without improving actual reasoning on benchmarks.
4. Decay persists despite MPAR²: If base model's audio encoder has limited capacity, explicit perception may still decay over very long chains (>600 tokens).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline CAFE profiling: Run base model (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B) on CAFE with varying CoT lengths to confirm perception decay trend and quantify Pearson correlation.
2. Ablation study: Train MPAR² variants removing one reward at a time (as in Table 2) to measure contribution of each component to final accuracy and CAFE metrics.
3. Attention visualization: Compute audio-token attention ratios across layers during reasoning (like Fig 6) to verify that MPAR² increases and sustains audio attention compared to base model.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What is the precise mechanistic cause of "audio perception decay" in LALMs as reasoning length increases? Is it due to attention dilution, error accumulation, or a specific architectural limitation in modality fusion layers?
2. How do the specific reward components in MPAR² (perception, SPR, review) interact, and what are their individual contributions to mitigating perception decay versus improving reasoning? Is the geometric mean in SPR Reward optimal?
3. Does MPAR²'s mitigation of perception decay and adaptive reasoning budget generalize to larger model scales (e.g., >7B parameters) and other modalities (e.g., video or multimodal speech-gesture)?
4. To what extent does MPAR²'s performance depend on the choice of the "reward model" (Qwen3-32B) for scoring perception and reasoning steps? Would a less capable or differently aligned evaluator degrade the RL training?
5. Is the dynamic adaptation of reasoning budget in MPAR² (Figure 7) primarily driven by the SPR Reward's chain consistency term (S_all-reason) or by implicit biases from the training data distribution? Could simpler length penalties achieve similar adaptivity?

## Limitations
- Heavy reliance on proprietary Gemini for captioning and a 32B reward model; alternatives may degrade performance and increase cost.
- Incomplete SFT training details (optimizer settings, LR schedule) hinder exact replication.
- MPAR² trained on AVQA-derived data; unclear if gains transfer to diverse audio-only tasks (e.g., Clotho, Environmental Sound Classification).

## Confidence
- **Audio perception decay exists**: High
- **MPAR² mitigates decay via explicit perception + review**: Medium
- **MPAR² generalizes to MMAU/MMAR**: Medium
