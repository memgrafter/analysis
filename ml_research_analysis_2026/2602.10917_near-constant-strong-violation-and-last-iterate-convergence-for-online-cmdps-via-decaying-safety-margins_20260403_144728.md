# Near-Constant Strong Violation and Last-Iterate Convergence for Online CMDPs via Decaying Safety Margins

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10917
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10917
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: FlexDOME achieves near-constant strong constraint violation (Õ(1)) and last-iterate convergence in online CMDPs via a decaying safety margin.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses safe reinforcement learning in Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs) under strong metrics that avoid error cancellation. Existing online CMDP methods either suffer from growing constraint violations or lack last-iterate convergence. The proposed algorithm, FlexDOME, integrates a time-decaying safety margin, time-varying entropy, and ℓ2 regularization into a primal-dual framework. A term-wise asymptotic dominance strategy ensures the safety margin eventually outweighs per‑episode optimization and statistical errors, clamping cumulative violations to Õ(1). A policy‑dual Lyapunov argument establishes last-iterate convergence. Theoretical results include strong regret Õ(T^{5/6}), bounded violation, and a non‑asymptotic convergence rate of Õ(ε^{-4} log(1/ε)) iterations to an ε‑optimal, zero‑violation policy. Tabular experiments confirm near‑zero instantaneous and cumulative violations.

## Method Summary
FlexDOME operates online: per episode, the agent interacts with an unknown CMDP, updates a primal policy (e.g., via penalized softmax gradient) and a dual multiplier (for constraints). Central is a safety margin added to the constraint threshold that decays polynomially. This margin asymptotically dominates per‑episode errors, ensuring constant cumulative violation under strong metrics. Time‑varying entropy and ℓ2 regularization stabilize updates as the margin shrinks. The analysis uses term‑wise asymptotic dominance to bound violation and a Lyapunov function combining primal and dual variables to prove last‑iterate convergence.

## Key Results
- Strong regret: Õ(T^{5/6})
- Strong constraint violation bounded by Õ(1) (near‑constant)
- Last‑iterate convergence: Õ(ε^{-4} log(1/ε)) iterations to ε‑optimal policy with zero violation
- Tabular experiments show near‑zero instantaneous and cumulative violation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
FlexDOME’s two‑fold mechanism: (1) Decaying safety margin acts as a shrinking buffer on the constraint threshold. Because it decays slower than the accumulation of per‑episode errors, it eventually dominates those errors, forcing the total violation to stop growing. (2) A policy‑dual Lyapunov function guarantees that the primal‑dual iterates converge to a region satisfying both optimality and feasibility. Time‑varying entropy/ℓ2 regularization further smooths updates, preventing early large violations and maintaining stability as the margin tightens.

## Foundational Learning
- **Constrained MDPs (CMDPs)**: A CMDP extends a standard MDP by incorporating multiple constraint cost functions. The agent must maximize cumulative reward while ensuring that the expected sum of each cost does not exceed a given budget. Formally, a CMDP is defined by a tuple (S, A, P, r, c, d, γ) or an episodic variant, where c are cost functions and d are thresholds. The constrained optimization is often addressed via Lagrange multipliers, leading to a Lagrangian formulation.
- **Primal‑Dual Methods**: These methods handle constraints by alternating between updating the policy (primal) to maximize the penalized Lagrangian and updating the dual multipliers to enforce constraint satisfaction. Typical updates involve policy gradient steps on the primal and ascent steps on the dual based on observed constraint violations.
- **Strong Metrics**: Unlike standard analyses that may use signed cumulative errors, strong metrics measure the sum of positive deficits: ∑_{i} max(0, cumulative cost_i - budget_i). This prevents cancellation between over‑satisfaction and violation, providing a more meaningful measure of constraint adherence.
- **Safety Margin**: A time‑decaying additive term m_t (e.g., m_t = t^{-α}) added to the constraint budget. This margin acts as a shrinking buffer that allows higher early violations but asymptotically tightens, ensuring that the cumulative violation under strong metrics remains bounded.
- **Lyapunov Functions**: A scalar function V(π_t, λ_t) combining distances (e.g., KL divergence or ℓ2) between current primal and dual variables and their optimal values. A decreasing Lyapunov function over iterations proves stability and last‑iterate convergence to optimal and feasible solutions.
- **Time‑Varying Regularization**: Entropy regularization (e.g., −η_t H(π)) and ℓ2 penalties on policy parameters, with coefficients η_t, β_t that decay over time. This design encourages exploration early on while reducing bias as the safety margin vanishes, supporting asymptotic optimality.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component Map:
Safety Margin Scheduler -> Constraint Threshold Adjuster -> Primal Update Module <-> Dual Update Module <- Reward/Cost Estimator

Critical Path: (1) Agent executes current policy, (2) estimates rewards/costs, (3) updates primal policy using penalized objective (Lagrangian + safety margin – λ·cost), (4) updates dual multiplier based on violation, (5) decays safety margin, (6) adjusts entropy/ℓ2 coefficients, repeat.

Design Tradeoffs: Safety margin decay α: too fast → early violations; too slow → slow convergence. Entropy regularization: high initially aids exploration but must decay to avoid suboptimality. ℓ2 penalty stabilizes but introduces bias.

Failure Signatures: (1) Unbounded cumulative violation growth, (2) primal‑dual oscillations or divergence, (3) Lyapunov function not decreasing.

## Limitations
- Regret Õ(T^{5/6}) is slower than optimal Õ(√T); analysis may be loose.
- Requires known problem constants (e.g., Slater’s gap) for margin schedule; robustness unknown.
- Last‑iterate rate Õ(ε^{-4}) could be pessimistic in practice.
- Experiments only on small tabular CMDPs; scalability untested.

## Confidence
