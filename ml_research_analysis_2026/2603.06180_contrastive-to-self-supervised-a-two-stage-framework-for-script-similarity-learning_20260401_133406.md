# Contrastive-to-Self-Supervised: A Two-Stage Framework for Script Similarity Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06180  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06180  
- **Reference Count:** 27  
- **Primary Result:** Proposes a two-stage framework that achieves superior script-level ranking (NDCG@10 up to 0.3178 on ResNet-50) by separating supervision on invented alphabets from exploratory learning on historical scripts.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of learning similarity metrics for writing systems where historical relationships are uncertain, making traditional cross-script negative pairs unreliable. The solution is a two-stage framework: first, a teacher encoder is trained via supervised contrastive learning on labeled invented alphabets to establish a robust, discriminative feature space; second, this teacher guides a student network through BYOL-style distillation on unlabeled historical scripts, allowing the student to reorganize embeddings to capture latent script proximities without explicit cross-script negatives. Evaluated on Omniglot and Unicode datasets, the approach yields state-of-the-art script-level ranking metrics (NDCG@10) and competitive glyph-level retrieval, demonstrating that clean supervision on invented data can bootstrap learning on historically ambiguous scripts.

## Method Summary
The method separates supervision and exploration across two stages with strictly partitioned data. Stage 1 uses 15 invented alphabets from Omniglot (350 character classes) to train a teacher encoder with a supervised contrastive (SupCon) loss, encouraging tight intra-class clustering and clear inter-class separation. Stage 2 initializes student and target networks from the teacher’s weights and trains them on 25 historical scripts (unlabeled) using a BYOL loss. Here, the student predicts the target’s representations for different augmentations of the same glyph, with no cross-script negatives. The target is updated via exponential moving average (EMA). After training, script-level similarity is computed by symmetrically averaging nearest-neighbor glyph distances between scripts, which is then evaluated against curated similarity levels using NDCG@10 and Spearman’s ρ.

## Key Results
- Achieves highest script-level NDCG@10 (0.3178) on ResNet-50, surpassing BYOL (0.2708) and Barlow Twins (0.2997).
- Maintains competitive glyph-level 20-way 1-shot retrieval (93.0% Top-1 on ResNet-50).
- Improves separability ratio by 35% from teacher to student, indicating sharper historical script proximities in the embedding space.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Separating reliable supervision (invented alphabets) from uncertain historical relations prevents the embedding space from being biased by unverifiable cross-script negative pairs.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 trains a teacher via supervised contrastive learning on glyphs from invented alphabets, where different character classes are guaranteed independent. This forces the teacher to learn deformation-invariant features within a clean, well-clustered space. Stage 2 then uses this teacher to guide a student via BYOL-style distillation on historical scripts. The student matches the teacher’s representations without exposure to explicit cross-script negatives, allowing latent similarities to emerge organically.
- **Core assumption:** Historical script relationships are uncertain enough that asserting "unrelated" negatives would introduce harmful bias; a clean discriminative prior from invented data is sufficiently general to bootstrap learning on real scripts.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "The obstacle is asymmetric supervision... We propose a two-stage framework that separates what can be supervised from what must remain exploratory."  
  [section 3.1.1] "In Stage 1, a teacher encoder is trained with contrastive loss on fictional scripts... producing an embedding space with clean intra-class clustering and inter-class separation."  
  [section 4.2] Hybrid approach achieves highest NDCG@10, indicating better script-level ranking than methods using cross-script negatives.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Asymmetric teacher-student distillation (BYOL adaptation) enables the student to inherit the teacher's geometric structure while freely reorganizing embeddings to capture historical script proximities suppressed by the teacher's hard inter-class boundaries.
- **Mechanism:** Both student and target networks are initialized from the teacher. The student, with a predictor, matches the EMA-updated target’s representations on different augmented views of the same glyph (positive pairs only). The absence of explicit negatives, combined with the predictor’s asymmetry, prevents collapse while allowing the student’s embedding geometry to relax from the teacher’s rigid clustering. This lets glyphs from historically related scripts move closer, as measured by the improved separability ratio.
- **Core assumption:** The teacher’s embedding space contains a useful "skeleton" of visual feature hierarchies (e.g., stroke, closure, symmetry) that transfer to historical scripts, but its hard boundaries between script-level clusters are artifacts of the invented alphabet supervision and need softening.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 3.1.4] "Our approach departs from standard BYOL... both networks are initialized from the teacher... This provides a semantically structured initialization... allowing representations to reorganize where needed while preserving the teacher’s geometric regularities."  
  [section 4.2.3] Separability ratio improves by 35% from teacher to student; t-SNE shows tighter clustering for related scripts (Greek/Latin vs. CJK) in the student embedding.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Script-level similarity derived from nearest-neighbor glyph matching aggregates glyph-level deformation-invariant embeddings into a script distance metric that reflects historical relationships, validated by NDCG@10 and Spearman’s ρ against curated similarity levels.
- **Mechanism:** Individual glyph embeddings are learned to be invariant to handwriting and augmentation. Script-level distance is computed symmetrically via average nearest-neighbor dissimilarity across all glyphs in two scripts. This allows many-to-many associations (e.g., a glyph in Script A linking to several in Script B), which the paper links to historical evolution. The ranking quality then measures if historically similar scripts are ranked nearer to a query script than dissimilar ones.
- **Core assumption:** Historical script similarity manifests as aggregate *glyph-level* visual similarity in a deformation-invariant embedding space, and a nearest-neighbor aggregation scheme correctly captures script-to-script relationships.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 3.2.2] "To compare two scripts s1 and s2, we aggregate glyph-level dissimilarities through a nearest-neighbor matching scheme... This formulation allows multiple glyphs from one script to be associated with the same glyph in another to reflect the historical evolution of writing systems."  
  [section 4.2.1] The method achieves the best NDCG@10 on most backbones, validating this aggregation against curated linguistic levels.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Supervised Contrastive Loss (SupCon)**  
  **Why needed here:** Creates a clean, discriminative embedding space in Stage 1 where all instances of the same invented character class are attracted and all instances of different classes are repelled. This establishes a robust geometric prior.  
  **Quick check:** In SupCon, what is the role of the temperature parameter τ, and how would a very low τ vs. a very high τ affect the teacher's embedding space structure?
- **Concept: BYOL-style Self-Distillation**  
  **Why needed here:** Enables learning from unlabeled historical scripts *without* cross-script negatives, preventing the introduction of unverifiable historical assumptions while still producing a non-collapsed embedding space via the predictor/target asymmetry.  
  **Quick check:** Why does BYOL not collapse even with only positive pairs? What specific architectural asymmetry (student predictor vs. EMA target) is essential?
- **Concept: Nearest-Neighbor Script Aggregation**  
  **Why needed here:** Transfers glyph-level embedding quality to the script-level ranking task. The symmetric average nearest-neighbor distance is a practical metric for comparing sets of glyphs where many-to-many historical correspondences are expected.  
  **Quick check:** Why is the script distance defined as a symmetric average of directed nearest-neighbor distances? What would be lost if only one directed distance were used?
- **Concept: Exponential Moving Average (EMA) Update**  
  **Why needed here:** Stabilizes the target network in Stage 2, providing a slowly evolving representation that the student follows, which is critical to prevent collapse in the absence of negatives.  
  **Quick check:** How does the EMA decay rate κ affect the stability and speed of learning in BYOL? What happens if κ is too low or too high?
- **Concept: Embedding Separability Ratio (R)**  
  **Why needed here:** Quantifies how much more closely related scripts cluster compared to unrelated ones in the embedding space, serving as an intermediate diagnostic for script-level geometry.  
  **Quick check:** If R decreases from teacher to student, what does that imply about the distillation process? Should R increase or decrease for better script proximities?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input Pipeline (labeled invented glyphs / unlabeled historical glyphs) -> Backbone Encoder (CNN/ResNet) -> Stage 1 Teacher (SupCon loss) -> Stage 2 Student (with predictor) & EMA Target (BYOL loss) -> Inference Embeddings -> Script Distance (symmetric nearest-neighbor aggregation) -> Ranking Metrics (NDCG@10, Spearman’s ρ).
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Train teacher to convergence on SupCon using *only* supervised invented script data.  
  2. Initialize student and target networks from the teacher checkpoints.  
  3. Train student/target on BYOL loss using *only* unsupervised historical script data.  
  4. Evaluate: (a) 20-way 1-shot retrieval on held-out historical scripts; (b) Compute script dissimilarity matrix via nearest-neighbor aggregation; (c) Compute NDCG@10 and Spearman’s ρ against curated script similarity levels.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Data Split Purity:** Strict separation between supervised (invented) and unsupervised (historical) datasets is mandatory. Mixing them would reintroduce unverifiable negative pairs.  
  - **Backbone Capacity:** Mid-size ResNets (18/34) sometimes suffer glyph-level Top-1 degradation vs. Barlow Twins, suggesting capacity mismatch with the teacher's structured space. ResNet-50 best balances both metrics.  
  - **Predictor Absence:** Paper omits BYOL's projection MLP, arguing backbone embeddings are already compact. This reduces parameters but may limit flexibility for larger backbones.  
  - **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** EMA decay (0.95–0.999), temperature (0.05–0.22), and long schedules (300–500 epochs) are critical. Optuna tuning is recommended.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **NDCG@10 < 0.25** (near random for 3-level relevance): Student failed to reorganize teacher space toward historical proximities. Check: (a) Teacher Stage 1 converged properly? (b) EMA decay too low/high? (c) Student predictor capacity insufficient?  
  - **Separability Ratio R > 0.3** (no improvement from teacher): Distillation did not sharpen script geometry. Likely student collapsed or drifted. Check: BYOL loss not decreasing? Predictor output distribution degenerate?  
  - **Glyph Top-1 << 90% on ResNet-50:** Degraded deformation invariance. Check: Data augmentation strength in Stage 2 sufficient? Student learning rate too high causing overwrite of teacher features?
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Ablation: Frozen Teacher.** Evaluate Stage 1 teacher *without* Stage 2 on historical scripts. Expect: decent glyph-level accuracy but worse NDCG@10 than full hybrid (as in Table 1: e.g., ResNet-50 SupCon NDCG=0.3078 vs. Hybrid 0.3178). Confirms need for Stage 2 reorganization.  
  2. **Ablation: Randomly Initialized BYOL.** Train standard BYOL on the *combined* unsupervised+supervised pool (as baselines do). Expect: lower NDCG@10 than hybrid, as cross-script negatives from invented data bias the space. Validates the "asymmetric supervision" premise.  
  3. **Component Swap: Use Target vs. Student at Inference.** Compare NDCG@10 and Spearman for `f_ξ` (EMA target) vs. `f_θ` (student). Paper shows they are nearly identical for most backbones, suggesting target’s smoother representations are equally effective for script ranking.

## Limitations
- **Reproduction Blockers:** Exact script splits for Omniglot (15 invented, 25 unsupervised, 10 evaluation) are not provided. Optimal hyperparameters per backbone (τ, EMA decay, predictor size, LR schedules) are ranges, not exact values. Predictor architecture specifics (depth, normalization) are vague. Unicode script mappings and similarity labels are absent.
- **Core Assumptions:** The method assumes invented alphabets are "neutral" with respect to historical bias. If visual biases exist, the teacher’s prior misleads. It also assumes script-level similarity is aggregatable from glyph-level visual similarity; non-visual historical relationships would break this.
- **External Validity:** Evaluation is limited to two datasets (Omniglot, pre-19th c. Unicode). Broader validation across diverse writing systems and modern fonts remains open.

## Confidence
- **Primary result (NDCG@10 improvement):** High — Metrics are clearly defined, consistently reported, and surpass multiple self-supervised baselines.
- **Causal mechanism (Stage 2 reorganization improves script proximities):** Medium — Evidence (t-SNE, separability ratio improvement) is supportive but not conclusive without full ablations (e.g., varying teacher quality).
- **Generalizability beyond evaluated scripts/datasets:** Medium — Strong performance on Omniglot/Unicode does not guarantee transfer to other writing systems or modern fonts.
