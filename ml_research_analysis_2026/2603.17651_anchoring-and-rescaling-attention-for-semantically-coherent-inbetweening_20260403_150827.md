# Anchoring and Rescaling Attention for Semantically Coherent Inbetweening

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.17651
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17651
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Combined KAB and ReTRo plug‑ins achieve state‑of‑the‑art generative inbetweening on TGI‑Bench (81 frames: PSNR 18.17, LPIPS 0.3818, FVD 0.2458, VBench 10.022).

## Executive Summary
Generative inbetweening (GI) synthesizes intermediate video frames between sparse keyframes under text guidance, but often produces outputs with semantic misalignment, inconsistent frames, and uneven pacing. This paper introduces two training‑free plug‑ins for video Diffusion Transformers (DiTs): Keyframe‑anchored Attention Bias (KAB) and Rescaled Temporal RoPE (ReTRo). KAB derives cross‑attention anchors from the first and last keyframes, interpolates them across time, and adds a small logit bias to guide semantics and pacing. ReTRo modulates self‑attention positional encodings by scaling temporal RoPE frequencies higher near keyframes (preserving details) and lower in the middle (broadening temporal context). The authors also release TGI‑Bench, the first text‑conditioned GI benchmark. Evaluations on 81‑frame sequences show that the combination of KAB and ReTRo outperforms state‑of‑the‑art baselines (e.g., Wan) across objective metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, FVD, VBench) and human evaluations, with particularly strong gains on challenging cases such as dynamic motion and occlusion.

## Method Summary
The method extends a video DiT backbone (e.g., Wan) with two training‑free interventions. KAB operates in cross‑attention layers: it extracts average cross‑attention maps for the first and last keyframes over all heads, compresses them into two “anchors”, linearly interpolates these anchors for each intermediate frame, and computes a logit bias $B^{(t)} = \log(M^{(t)}+\varepsilon) - \log(\bar{A}^{(t)}+\varepsilon)$. The bias is added to the original cross‑attention logits, scaled by a tapered coefficient $\beta^{(t)}$, and applied only to layers 5–12 and early diffusion steps. Additionally, the asymmetric fusion of keyframe and text conditions is replaced by symmetric triple cross‑attention (first key, last key, text), each biased by its own interpolated anchor, then averaged. ReTRo modifies self‑attention by redefining the temporal component of RoPE: a scaling factor $s(t)$ is applied to the temporal frequency row, with $s_{\text{edge}} > 1$ for the first and last $w_{\text{edge}} \approx 0.1f$ frames (preserving keyframes) and $s_{\text{mid}} < 1$ for the remaining middle frames (broadening temporal receptive field). The scaled RoPE is used to rotate queries and keys before computing self‑attention. Together, these plug‑ins steer the diffusion process without any additional training.

## Key Results
- On 81‑frame TGI‑Bench, the proposed combination achieves PSNR **18.17** (↑0.54), LPIPS **0.3818** (↓0.0127), FVD **0.2458** (↓0.0311), and VBench **10.022** (↑0.118) compared to the Wan baseline.
- Semantic fidelity (X‑CLIP, VQA, CLIPSIM‑frames), frame consistency (LPIPS‑frames), and pace stability all improve significantly; human ratings confirm gains across all three dimensions.
- The method shows the largest improvement on challenging subsets, notably dynamic motion and occlusion, indicating robustness to difficult inbetweening scenarios.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Keyframe‑anchored Attention Bias (KAB)
KAB creates cross‑attention anchors by averaging the attention maps of the first and last keyframes over all heads and spatial queries. These anchors encode the semantic allocation patterns conditioned on each keyframe. By linearly interpolating them across time, we obtain a per‑frame target distribution $M^{(t)}$. Adding a small logit bias $B^{(t)}$ (scaled by $\beta^{(t)}$) to the original cross‑attention logits steers intermediate frames toward the keyframe semantics without overwriting the model’s prior. The triple isolated cross‑attention (first key, last key, text) ensures symmetric treatment of all conditions, improving both global semantic fidelity and temporal pacing.

### Rescaled Temporal RoPE (ReTRo)
ReTRo adapts the temporal positional encoding in self‑attention. By scaling the temporal frequency row of the RoPE tensor, it directly controls the locality of temporal attention. Higher scaling ($s_{\text{edge}} > 1$) near the first and last $\approx10\%$ of frames sharpens the attention pattern, preserving keyframe details. Lower scaling ($s_{\text{mid}} < 1$) in the middle frames broadens the temporal receptive field, allowing each token to attend more globally and thus stabilize intermediate content. This adaptive locality balances preservation of anchor frames with overall consistency.

### Complementary Synergy
KAB and ReTRo target distinct aspects: KAB shapes the semantic and pacing guidance at the level of cross‑attention, while ReTRo enforces temporal structure in self‑attention. Ablations confirm that each plug‑in alone improves a subset of metrics, but only their combination yields state‑of‑the‑art performance across all measured qualities (semantic fidelity, frame consistency, pace stability). The synergy demonstrates that generative inbetweening requires both conditional anchoring and adaptive temporal context.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Keyframes → VAE encoder → keyframe latents; Text → UMT5 encoder → text context → Concatenate with keyframe latents → Context vector set → DiT blocks (layers 5‑12 with KAB bias; all layers with ReTRo RoPE scaling) → Denoised latent video → VAE decoder → Output frames

**Critical path**  
1. Encode keyframes and text.  
2. Construct unified context (keyframes + text).  
3. Iteratively denoise latent video via DiT blocks, applying KAB to cross‑attention in later layers and early steps, and ReTRo to self‑attention throughout.  
4. Decode final latents to frames.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Training‑free vs. performance*: No fine‑tuning required, but improvements are limited by base model capacity and need hyperparameter tuning.  
- *Guidance strength*: The schedule $\beta^{(t)}$ must balance keyframe anchoring and model creativity; overly strong biases cause freezing.  
- *ReTRo edge width*: Choice of $w_{\text{edge}}$ trades off keyframe preservation (larger $w_{\text{edge}}$) against middle‑frame consistency (smaller $w_{\text{edge}}$).

**Failure signatures**  
- *KAB over‑bias*: Intermediate frames become static or unnaturally rigid.  
- *KAB under‑bias*: No improvement; semantic drift persists.  
- *ReTRo extreme scaling*: $s_{\text{edge}}$ too high creates edge artifacts; $s_{\text{mid}}$ too low blurs middle frames.  
- *Combination mismatch*: Poorly tuned hyperparameters cause conflicting effects (e.g., broadened attention vs. over‑anchoring).

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Ablate KAB: run with ReTRo only; expect consistency gains but limited semantic/pacing improvements.  
2. Ablate ReTRo: run with KAB only; expect semantic and pacing gains but weaker frame‑level consistency.  
3. Full combination: apply both plug‑ins with default hyperparameters; should achieve SOTA on TGI‑Bench and human evaluations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Does KAB generalize beyond the Wan backbone to other video generation architectures (e.g., U‑Net‑based models or other DiTs)?  
- Can per‑video adaptation of the hyperparameters ($\beta^{(t)}$, $s_{\text{edge}}$, $s_{\text{mid}}$, $w_{\text{edge}}$) improve results, given varying motion dynamics?  
- Would combining KAB with explicit motion guidance (e.g., optical flow) further enhance performance on highly dynamic or occluded sequences?

## Limitations

- Hyperparameter sensitivity: Default schedules may not be optimal across all video lengths or domains.  
- Implementation ambiguities: Details of anchor extraction, bias recomputation frequency, and triple cross‑attention weighting are not fully specified.  
- Break conditions: Performance degrades if keyframe conditioning is poor or scaling factors are set extremely.  
- Generalization: Method validated only on a single DiT (Wan), leaving transferability to other architectures uncertain.

## Confidence

- **KAB semantic/pacing improvements**: Medium  
- **ReTRo frame consistency gains**: Medium  
- **Combined synergy (SOTA results)**: High  
- **Generalization to other DiTs**: Low
