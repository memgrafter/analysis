# Discrete Stochastic Localization for Non-autoregressive Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16169
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16169
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** DSL fine-tuning of an MDLM checkpoint yields dramatic efficiency gains on OpenWebText (e.g., MAUVE 0.057→0.639 at T=128, matching autoregressive quality at T=1024 with ~4× fewer steps) and achieves ≤1.45 BPC on Text8, while improving calibration and reducing over-refinement.

## Executive Summary
Non-autoregressive language generation via masked diffusion and remasking suffers from a training-inference mismatch: denoisers are trained on binary mask corruptions but must operate on self-generated partial drafts during refinement, causing error accumulation. This paper introduces Discrete Stochastic Localization (DSL), a training framework that learns a single SNR-invariant denoiser across a continuous mixture of corruption levels. DSL exposes the model to both smooth intermediate noise (lognormal SNR) and smoothed endpoint masks (ROAR), aligning training with refinement-time drafts while preserving compatibility with existing samplers. A softmax converter maps noisy embeddings to token-simplex mixtures, stabilizing attention. On OpenWebText and Text8, DSL fine-tuning dramatically improves generation quality and efficiency, matching autoregressive performance with far fewer decoding steps.

## Method Summary
DSL fine-tunes a pretrained Masked Diffusion Language Model (MDLM) using a novel corruption procedure. Instead of binary masks, each token is corrupted with a sampled signal-to-noise ratio (γ). With probability 1/k (k=10), γ is drawn from smoothed ROAR-style ranges near the endpoints (very low or near γ_max); otherwise, γ is sampled from a lognormal distribution over intermediate SNR values. The noisy embedding z = γx + √γε (ε∼N(0,I)) is processed by a CONVERT module: a linear layer plus softmax over spherical token embeddings, producing a token-simplex mixture. This mixture, alongside the norm ‖z‖, is fed to a DiT-style Transformer backbone without any time/SNR conditioning. The training objective remains token-wise cross-entropy. During inference, the resulting denoiser is used with existing masked diffusion samplers (e.g., ReMDM) using a step-budget-aware remasking intensity schedule (η_cap) to control over-refinement.

## Key Results
- On OpenWebText (L=1024), DSL fine-tuning of an MDLM checkpoint improves MAUVE from 0.057 to 0.639 at T=128 and from 0.216 to 0.661 at T=256, matching autoregressive quality at T=1024 (0.722 vs 0.760) with ~4× fewer steps.
- On Text8, DSL achieves ≤1.45 BPC (trained 0.5M steps), beating continuous diffusion baselines.
- DSL improves confidence calibration and reduces over-refinement compared to standard MDLM/ReMDM.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core improvement stems from addressing a training-inference mismatch in non-autoregressive iterative refinement. Standard MDLM denoisers are trained on contexts with binary mask corruptions derived from ground-truth sequences. At inference, however, they must denoise self-generated partial drafts that occupy an intermediate corruption distribution. This distribution shift causes parallel prediction errors to compound over refinement steps, leading to poor sample quality and over-refinement. DSL mitigates this by explicitly training the denoiser on a token-wise mixture that includes both continuous intermediate SNR corruptions (mimicking self-generated drafts) and smoothed endpoint corruptions (ensuring compatibility with masked diffusion decoders). The smoothed ROAR endpoints avoid training singularities at extreme noise levels, which improves calibration near the clean endpoint where critical refinement decisions occur.

A secondary theoretical insight enables a time-invariant backbone. By constraining token embeddings to the unit hypersphere and using Gaussian noise with isotropic covariance scaled by γ, the MMSE denoiser becomes SNR-invariant: the posterior mean ˆx(z) simplifies to E[x exp(z·x)]/E[exp(z·x)], with no dependence on γ. This allows a single network to denoise across all corruption levels without explicit time conditioning, simplifying architecture and improving data efficiency.

The CONVERT module further stabilizes training. Directly feeding noisy embeddings z into the Transformer causes the input distribution to drift with SNR (both norm and direction), forcing the backbone to re-learn normalization. CONVERT(z) = W_trans softmax(βzW_token^T + b) maps z to a token-simplex mixture whose entropy shrinks smoothly with increasing γ, yielding a more stationary input distribution and preventing training instability.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  W_token (spherical token embeddings) -> Convert(z) (linear + softmax) -> f_θ (DiT backbone, no time conditioning) -> cross-entropy loss.  
  Global ‖z‖ is also fed to f_θ.  
  Corruption sampler produces per-token γ from LogNormal or smoothed ROAR.

- **Critical path (training)**:  
  1. Sample clean tokens s ∈ V^L, get embeddings x = W_token[s].  
  2. For each token, decide ROAR endpoint (prob 1/k) or continuous SNR.  
  3. Sample γ accordingly: for ROAR, uniform in [0, γ_min] or [c·γ_max, γ_max]; for continuous, LogNormal(µ=1.65, σ=0.9) clipped to [γ_min, γ_max].  
  4. Compute z = γx + √γε, ε∼N(0,I).  
  5. h = Convert(z); logits = f_θ(h, ‖z‖); p = softmax(logits).  
  6. Loss = average token-wise cross-entropy between p and s.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Convert decouples noisy embedding space from Transformer space, improving stability but adding parameters.  
  - Time-invariant DiT simplifies architecture and matches SNR-invariant theory; may lose explicit time-awareness.  
  - ROAR smoothing parameters (γ_min, γ_max, c) trade off endpoint sharpness vs. gradient variance.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Over-refinement: High remask rate r_t with negligible token changes Δ_t at late steps → MAUVE drop, GenPPL improves.  
  - Poor calibration: High ECE near large SNR (near-clean) → confidence misalignment → mis-targeted remasking.  
  - Training instability: Large γ variance causing gradient explosions (mitigated by clipping or careful initialization).  
  - Incorrect time conditioning: Adding learned timestep embeddings mismatches the SNR-invariant theory.

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Toy cyclic recovery**: Train DSL on K=7 cyclic shifts (Appendix B). Verify denoiser recovers clean sequence from partially masked + wrong visible tokens under ReMDM-loop.  
  2. **Endpoint smoothing ablation**: Compare atomic ROAR (γ∈{0,γ_max}) vs smoothed ROAR (ranges) on held-out OWT; measure ECE at high SNR and MAUVE vs steps.  
  3. **Uncertainty calibration sweep**: During forced decoding on corrupted data, plot reliability diagrams across several γ levels (e.g., γ=10,50,100) to confirm smoothing yields better calibration near clean endpoint.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scaling and conditional generation** – How does DSL scale to larger model sizes and maintain benefits in conditional generation tasks? Future work includes scaling DSL to larger backbones and conditional generation. Experiments only evaluate unconditional OpenWebText generation with a fixed-size backbone; scaling and conditional settings remain unexplored. Evidence needed: Train DSL on larger models (e.g., GPT-2 Large) and conditional benchmarks (e.g., translation, summarization), compare step-efficiency against baselines.

2. **Learned remasking policies** – Can we replace the hand‑tuned η_cap(T) remasking schedule with a learned policy that adaptively optimizes coverage‑quality trade‑offs? Future work mentions learning remasking policies that directly optimize coverage–quality trade‑offs under compute budgets. The current scalar function of step budget does not adapt to per‑sample uncertainty or draft quality. Evidence needed: Implement a reinforcement learning or gradient‑based scheduler that adjusts remask probability based on real‑time diagnostics (e.g., token uncertainty, rewrite rate), then show improved MAUVE–GenPPL frontiers.

3. **Theoretical guarantees for distribution shift** – What theoretical guarantees exist that mixed corruption training reduces the train–inference distribution shift in NAR refinement? The paper lacks a formal framework linking corruption distribution to stability or convergence of the refinement Markov chain. Evidence needed: Derive bounds on mixing time or error propagation under DSL’s mixed corruption using stochastic localization theory; verify empirically via variance of diagnostics across corruption mixtures.

4. **Optimal endpoint smoothing distribution** – How does the choice of endpoint smoothing distribution (beyond uniform ranges) affect DSL training calibration and final generation quality? Smoothing uses heuristic uniform ranges near endpoints; impact of distribution shape (e.g., Gaussian, beta) and parameterization is unknown. Evidence needed: Train DSL with alternative smoothing distributions and varying concentrations; measure ECE, MAUVE, and GenPPL to identify optimal strategy.

5. **Isolating prior mismatch elimination** – To what extent does DSL’s elimination of prior mismatch contribute to empirical gains over continuous diffusion models? Appendix A.5 claims DSL avoids prior mismatch, but improvements may stem from multiple factors (mixed corruption, endpoint smoothing, architecture). Evidence needed: Compare DSL against diffusion models with matched priors or ablate prior mismatch in DSL by artificially introducing a mismatch; quantify contribution.

## Limitations
- Exact specifications of the CONVERT module (dimensionalities, β and bias initialization) and the precise way global ‖z‖ is incorporated into the backbone are ambiguous without the official code.
- Hyperparameters for the continuous LogNormal corruption (clipping bounds, γ_min/γ_max for continuous branch) and the ROAR smoothing parameter c are not fully described or systematically ablated.
- DSL’s benefits on larger model sizes and conditional generation tasks remain unexplored, limiting claims about general applicability.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (training–inference mismatch)**: High – well‑known NAR issue; diagnostics strongly support.
- **Mechanism 2 (SNR‑invariant denoiser)**: Medium – derivation sound under unit‑norm assumptions; practical necessity of feeding ‖z‖ and effect of non‑ideal embeddings need confirmation.
- **Mechanism 3 (mixed corruption training)**: Medium – ablations show benefit, but mixture distribution appears tuned and may not generalize.
- **Overall DSL improvement**: High – gains on OWT and Text8 are substantial; dependence on η_cap schedule introduces potential fragility.

2. **Endpoint smoothing ablation**: Train DSL models with atomic ROAR (γ∈{0,γ_max}) versus smoothed ROAR (ranges) while keeping all other settings identical. Measure ECE at high SNR and MAUVE at T=128 to isolate smoothing effect.
