# ViHERMES: A Graph-Grounded Multihop Question Answering Benchmark and System for Vietnamese Healthcare Regulations

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.07361
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07361
*   **Reference Count:** 26
*   **Primary Result:** Introduces ViHERMES, a benchmark for multihop reasoning over Vietnamese healthcare regulations, and a graph-aware QA system achieving SOTA performance (F1 0.8334) by modeling legal relations in a structured knowledge graph.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical gap in multihop question answering for Vietnamese healthcare regulations, a challenging low-resource domain with complex hierarchical and amended legal structures. The authors present ViHERMES, a novel benchmark dataset constructed through semantic clustering and LLM-based generation, featuring questions that require explicit cross-document legal reasoning. To solve this task, they propose a graph-aware, multi-agent QA system built upon a Structure-driven Regulatory Knowledge Graph (SRKG) that encodes formal legal relations (e.g., Amends, Refers_To). The system uses seeded retrieval and relation-aware propagation to collect legally valid evidence, which is then verified by an Auditor before answer generation. Evaluated on ViHERMES, the system significantly outperforms strong baselines like HippoRAG2, demonstrating the effectiveness of structure-grounded reasoning in a specialized regulatory context.

## Method Summary
The proposed system constructs an SRKG from the regulatory corpus by extracting legal units (articles/clauses) and encoding both structural relations (hierarchy) and legal semantic relations (Amends, Replaces, Supplements, Refers_To) via rule-based patterns. For a query, an Interpreter agent determines if it is regulatory. If so, a Pathfinder agent performs **seeded retrieval**: it ranks all legal units using a hybrid score combining dense and sparse retrieval, selects top seeds, and then executes **relation-aware propagation** along SRKG edges. Propagation applies relation-specific rules—for amendment chains, it recursively follows edges to terminal (currently effective) nodes; for references, it restricts expansion to direct neighbors to prevent drift. The retrieved context set is validated by an Auditor agent for consistency and grounding. Finally, a Conductor orchestrates an LLM (GPT-4o-mini) to generate the final answer from the verified context, with the ability to abstain if grounding is insufficient.

## Key Results
*   The proposed system achieves state-of-the-art results on ViHERMES with an F1 score of **0.8334**, an LLM-Judge score of **0.7554**, and Recall@5 of **0.8461**, outperforming graph-based baselines like HippoRAG2.
*   Ablation studies confirm the critical contribution of each multi-agent component: removing the Interpreter causes the largest drop in F1 (to 0.6540), removing the Auditor significantly reduces the LLM Judge score (to 0.6823), and removing the Pathfinder's seeded retrieval lowers F1 (to 0.7734).
*   Despite the overhead of graph construction, the system maintains competitive inference efficiency with an average latency of **14.7 seconds** and uses fewer graph tokens during retrieval than HippoRAG2.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim**: Modeling formal legal relations in the SRKG enables legally valid multihop retrieval by tracing amendment chains and limiting reference expansion.
*   **Mechanism**: The SRKG encodes relations like Amends, Replaces, Supplements, and Refers_To. During propagation, for amendment relations the system recursively follows outgoing edges until a terminal node (with no further amendments) is reached, preferring terminal nodes as they represent currently effective provisions. For reference relations, expansion is restricted to direct neighbors to prevent contextual drift.
*   **Core assumption**: Legal dependencies can be captured by a fixed set of typed relations, and traversing these relations yields the necessary evidence for multihop reasoning over regulations.
*   **Evidence anchors**:
    *   [abstract] states the system "models formal legal relations at the level of legal units and supports principled context expansion for legally valid and coherent answers."
    *   [section 4.1] defines SRKG and the relation types.
    *   [section 4.2] details validity tracing and contextual supplementation.
    *   [corpus] The related work corpus includes graph RAG methods (e.g., HippoRAG2) but they do not explicitly model legal relations, highlighting the gap this mechanism fills.
*   **Break condition**: If legal relations are incompletely extracted (e.g., missing amendment links), propagation may fail to retrieve the latest valid provision or may retrieve outdated ones, leading to legally incorrect answers.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim**: Hybrid seeded retrieval concentrates the search on high-relevance anchor nodes before graph expansion, reducing noise and ensuring propagation builds on relevant contexts.
*   **Mechanism**: The Pathfinder ranks all regulatory nodes using a hybrid score s(v,q)=λ s_dense(v,q)+(1-λ)s_sparse(v,q), selects top K as seeds, and then expands along SRKG edges. This two-stage approach narrows the initial candidate set to legally meaningful units.
*   **Core assumption**: A small set of highly relevant legal units can serve as entry points to the relevant regulatory subgraph; expansion along structured edges will then retrieve additional necessary evidence.
*   **Evidence anchors**:
    *   [abstract] mentions "seeded retrieval and propagation."
    *   [section 4.2] describes the seeded retrieval process with hybrid scoring and propagation.
    *   [section 5.5] ablation shows replacing Pathfinder with flat retrieval degrades performance (F1 from 0.8334 to 0.7734).
    *   [corpus] Corpus contains graph-based QA systems (LightRAG, HippoRAG2) that use different retrieval strategies; the seeded approach is distinctive.
*   **Break condition**: If no seed node covers the query's legal intent (e.g., due to embedding mismatch or lexical gap), propagation starts from irrelevant anchors and fails to collect necessary evidence.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim**: Multi-agent orchestration with explicit verification (Interpreter, Auditor, Conductor) reduces hallucination and improves answer reliability by enforcing grounding and conservative behavior.
*   **Mechanism**: Interpreter analyzes query intent and routes regulatory queries to the Pathfinder. Pathfinder returns context set C(q). Auditor validates consistency between C(q) and intermediate claims; if grounding is insufficient, it signals the Conductor, which may abstain or request clarification. This separation prevents unverified information from reaching the final answer.
*   **Core assumption**: Decomposing the QA pipeline into specialized agents with verification checkpoints improves robustness compared to end-to-end generation.
*   **Evidence anchors**:
    *   [abstract] notes the system is a "multi-agent QA system" and ablation confirms "importance of its multi-agent components."
    *   [section 4.3] details each agent's role.
    *   [section 5.5] ablation: removing Interpreter drops F1 to 0.6540; removing Auditor drops LLM Judge to 0.6823; removing Pathfinder drops F1 to 0.7734.
    *   [corpus] Corpus includes single-agent RAG systems (Naive RAG, IRCoT) that lack such verification, which likely contributes to their lower performance.
*   **Break condition**: If the Auditor fails to detect inconsistencies (false negative) or the Interpreter misclassifies non-regulatory queries as regulatory, the system may either generate ungrounded answers or unnecessarily invoke graph retrieval, increasing latency.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map**:
    Interpreter -> Pathfinder -> Auditor -> Conductor -> LLM (GPT-4o-mini)
    Pathfinder <-> SRKG (query, expand)
    SRKG: Legal Unit Nodes (id, title, text) with Edges (Has_Article, Has_Clause, Amends, Replaces, Supplements, Refers_To)

*   **Critical path**:
    1.  User query -> Interpreter determines regulatory intent.
    2.  If regulatory, Pathfinder retrieves seed nodes via hybrid scoring and propagates along SRKG edges to form context set C(q).
    3.  Auditor validates C(q) and intermediate reasoning for consistency.
    4.  Conductor passes C(q) to LLM to generate final answer; if Auditor flags insufficient grounding, Conductor may abstain.

*   **Design tradeoffs**:
    *   Graph construction overhead vs. retrieval quality: Building SRKG with precise legal relations requires manual patterns or high-precision extraction, increasing upfront cost but yielding more accurate propagation.
    *   Constrained propagation (e.g., only direct neighbors for references) vs. completeness: Limiting expansion avoids drift but may miss indirect evidence; the system balances this via relation-specific rules.
    *   Multi-agent latency vs. robustness: Adding agents introduces coordination overhead (~14.7s average) but improves correctness and reduces hallucinations.

*   **Failure signatures**:
    *   Interpreter misclassification: Non-regulatory queries sent to Pathfinder (unnecessary latency) or regulatory queries misrouted (no answer).
    *   Pathfinder seed failure: No relevant seeds -> empty or irrelevant context set.
    *   Propagation gap: Missing amendment links -> retrieval of repealed provisions.
    *   Auditor over-rejection: Valid contexts flagged -> systematic abstention.
    *   LLM hallucination: Despite grounding, LLM generates unsupported claims (Auditor may catch but not always).

*   **First 3 experiments**:
    1.  **Main evaluation**: Compare against Naive RAG, IRCoT, MiniRAG, RAPTOR, LightRAG, HippoRAG2 on ViHERMES test set using F1, LLM Judge, Recall@5. Shows proposed system achieves highest scores (F1 0.8334).
    2.  **Ablation study**: Remove each agent (Interpreter, Auditor, Pathfinder) to measure contribution; removal of Interpreter causes largest drop (F1 to 0.6540), Auditor drop in LLM Judge (0.7554→0.6823), Pathfinder drop in F1 (0.8334→0.7734).
    3.  **Efficiency analysis**: Measure average latency and graph construction statistics (nodes, edges, tokens). Proposed system maintains competitive latency (14.74s) despite larger SRKG, and uses fewer graph tokens than HippoRAG2.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How can temporal validity be formally integrated into the SRKG to handle amendment chains and effective dates, ensuring retrieval returns only currently applicable provisions without prohibitive overhead?
    *   *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The conclusion states future work will focus on "enriching temporal validity for improved robustness," acknowledging current SRKG lacks explicit temporal modeling.
    *   *Why unresolved:* Healthcare regulations frequently amend prior provisions, and answers must reflect the legally effective version at query time. The current SRKG models structural and legal semantic relations but assumes static legal status, risking retrieval of outdated nodes.
    *   *What evidence would resolve it:* Develop a time-aware SRKG extension with validity intervals per node; evaluate on a temporally annotated subset of ViHERMES (e.g., questions requiring "latest version" reasoning). Measure degradation in F1/Recall@5 when temporal constraints are disabled versus full system, and assess latency increase.

*   **Open Question 2:** What are the precise boundaries of relation-aware propagation in SRKG—specifically, which relation types and maximum hop depths yield the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off for different multihop complexity levels?
    *   *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The conclusion mentions "constraining propagation volume" as future work, and Table 4 shows graph token counts and latency, implying current propagation may be suboptimal.
    *   *Why unresolved:* The system uses fixed propagation rules (e.g., validity tracing until terminal nodes, single-step for Refers_To). However, unconstrained traversal could introduce noise or excessive context, while over-constraining may miss necessary evidence. The optimal settings likely vary by hop count and dependency pattern (e.g., amendment chains vs. cross-document synthesis).
    *   *What evidence would resolve it:* Conduct systematic propagation ablation on the ViHERMES test set, varying allowed relation types (e.g., permitting vs. forbidding Supplements during expansion) and maximum path lengths. Plot F1 and Recall@5 against average retrieved tokens and latency, identifying Pareto-optimal configurations per hop level.

*   **Open Question 3:** Does the structure-driven SRKG approach remain effective when applied to other Vietnamese regulatory domains (e.g., education, labor law) that may have different hierarchical conventions or relation expression patterns?
    *   *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The conclusion claims the formulation is "applicable to other regulatory domains," but all experiments are on healthcare regulations. No cross-domain validation is provided.
    *   *Why unresolved:* The SRKG construction relies on rule-based pattern matching for standard legal expressions (e.g., "sửa đổi" for amends). Other domains might use divergent phrasing or organizational structures, potentially reducing relation extraction precision and degrading multihop reasoning.
    *   *What evidence would resolve it:* Replicate the entire pipeline (SRKG construction + QA system) on existing Vietnamese legal QA datasets from other domains (e.g., ALQAC for general statutes, ViBidLQA for bidding). Compare performance against HippoRAG2 and naive baselines on those datasets' multihop subsets (if available) or adapt ViHERMES-style questions. Report relative F1 drops and analyze extraction error rates.

## Limitations
*   Exact hyperparameters for seeded retrieval (number of seeds K, hybrid weight λ) and complete LLM prompt templates for the Interpreter, Auditor, and Conductor are not specified, hindering exact replication.
*   The SRKG construction relies on rule-based pattern matching for a fixed set of legal relations, which may not capture complex, non-standard, or cross-domain legal dependencies.
*   System performance and design choices are validated only on the Vietnamese healthcare regulation domain; generalizability to other legal domains or languages remains an open question.
