# MIBench: Evaluating LMMs on Multimodal Interaction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13427
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13427
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduction of MIBench reveals that multimodal interaction in LMMs remains constrained, with models showing textual bias and poor synergy despite scaling.

## Executive Summary
MIBench is a new benchmark designed to evaluate multimodal interaction in large multimodal models (LMMs). It consists of 10,030 vision‑text context pairs across 32 tasks, organized by three interaction patterns (vision‑centric, text‑centric, synergy) and three cognitive levels (Recognition, Understanding, Reasoning). The authors evaluate 30 LMMs and find that overall performance is limited (50–65% on vision‑centric, ~40% on synergy), with a pronounced textual bias (consistency below 60%) and particular weakness in synergy, especially at the Reasoning level. Native LMMs struggle even with basic perception, and scaling model size yields minimal improvements. Closed‑source models hold a ~10% advantage largely due to better text and synergy handling.

## Method Summary
The study introduces MIBench, a carefully curated benchmark with 10,030 vision‑text pairs spanning 32 tasks. Each task is classified by interaction pattern (vision‑centric, text‑centric, synergy) and cognitive level (Recognition, Understanding, Reasoning). thirty existing LMMs are evaluated in a standardized setting to measure their ability to integrate and reason across modalities. The analysis focuses on accuracy, consistency (stability under irrelevant textual variations), and performance scaling across model architectures and sizes.

## Key Results
- Multimodal interaction remains constrained (50–65% on vision‑centric, ~40% on synergy) with scaling providing only marginal gains.
- Models exhibit strong textual bias; consistency scores below 60% indicate they are easily distracted by text when processing vision.
- Synergy capability is particularly weak, especially at the Reasoning level, and native LMMs perform poorly even on basic perception tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
LMMs built on pre‑trained LLM backbones inherit a linguistic bias that dominates multimodal processing. Visual embeddings are typically projected into the text token space and processed by the same layers, causing attention to focus on text‑related patterns. This is evidenced by low consistency (<60%) and visual information selection being highly susceptible to textual interference.

Scaling model parameters and data yields only limited improvements because current training objectives (e.g., next‑token prediction) emphasize surface‑level correspondence rather than deep cross‑modal reasoning. Additional capacity mainly enhances memorization and text fluency without enabling iterative refinement, as shown by negligible gains from version updates and a plateau beyond 32B parameters.

Synergy is constrained by the shallow fusion mechanisms (e.g., simple concatenation) that support only unimodal reasoning on the dominant modality. The autoregressive nature of LLMs fixes vision tokens after a single pass, preventing iterative cross‑modal exchange. Consequently, higher‑level synergy tasks (Reasoning) consistently score below 50%, and native LMMs suffer from poor basic perception, creating a bottleneck for complex interactions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal Interaction Patterns (Vision‑centric, Text‑centric, Synergy):** These patterns diagnose whether a model relies on the correct modality and separate true synergy from bias. *Quick check:* For a vision‑centric question, does changing irrelevant text alter the answer? If yes, the model fails.
- **Hierarchical Cognitive Levels (Recognition, Understanding, Reasoning):** This hierarchy pinpoints the depth at which cross‑modal reasoning breaks down. *Quick check:* Can a model that recognizes objects also reason about their relationships or simulate changes based on text? If not, it lacks higher‑level understanding.
- **Consistency Metric:** Quantifies stability of modality selection across varying irrelevant contexts. *Quick check:* For the same image but different texts, does the model produce identical answers? Consistency <100% indicates sensitivity and inadequate selective attention.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input → (Vision encoder → Projector) ∪ (Text tokenizer) → Fusion (concatenation/attention) → LLM backbone → Output head (language modeling)

**Critical path:**  
1. Encode image into patch tokens.  
2. Tokenize text + question.  
3. Merge: `<text> <vision_tokens>` (or interleaved).  
4. Autoregressive generation conditioned on the full sequence.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Native vs. non‑native:* Scratch‑trained native LMMs avoid text bias but require massive multimodal data; LLM‑backboned models are data‑efficient but inherit bias.  
- *Fusion depth:* Early token‑level fusion enables richer interaction but may harm pure text performance; late fusion is simpler but limits synergy.  
- *Scaling:* More parameters mainly boost text understanding; visual representation quality may not scale proportionally.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High text‑centric, low vision‑centric accuracy → textual bias.  
- Low consistency on vision‑centric with varied text → inability to ignore text.  
- Performance drop on Synergy Lv2/3 → shallow fusion.  
- Native models low on Vision Lv1 → underfitting or poor visual grounding.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Modality selection test:** For 200 vision‑centric samples, apply five textual contexts (correct, misleading, irrelevant, etc.). Measure accuracy and consistency per context type. Expect open‑source models’ accuracy to plummet with misleading text and consistency below 60%.  
2. **Scaling analysis:** Within a single series (e.g., Qwen2.5‑VL 3B, 7B, 32B, 72B), plot synergy accuracy against model size. Expect a plateau at larger sizes.  
3. **Native vs. non‑native comparison:** Evaluate Emu3/Chameleon vs. InternVL2/LLaVA on Vision‑centric Level 1 tasks. Expect a significant performance gap.

## Limitations
- Benchmark scope may not capture all real‑world multimodal interactions; synergy tasks could oversimplify iterative reasoning.
- Poor native LMM performance might stem from limited multimodal pretraining data rather than architecture alone.
- Findings rely on 30 LMMs; broader validation across newer models (e.g., GPT‑4o, Claude 3.5) is needed.

## Confidence
- **Textual bias:** High – strong evidence from consistency metrics and systematic misalignment across 30 models.  
- **Scaling inefficiency:** Medium – clear plateau in specific families; other architectures may differ.  
- **Synergy gap:** Medium – consistently low scores but benchmark design may bias toward unimodal shortcuts.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate modality selection test on an independent benchmark (e.g., MMStar or HallusionBench) to confirm textual bias generalizes beyond MIBench.
2. Cross‑architecture scaling analysis: Compare scaling curves for decoder‑only (LLaVA), encoder‑decoder (Flamingo), and hybrid (KOSMOS) LMMs on synergy tasks to test if plateau is universal.
3. Native LMM data ablation: Train a small native LMM on MIBench‑scale synthetic vs. real multimodal data to isolate whether performance deficit is architectural or data‑driven.
