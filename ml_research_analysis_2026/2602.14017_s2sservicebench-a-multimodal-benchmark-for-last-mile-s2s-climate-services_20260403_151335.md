# S2SServiceBench: A Multimodal Benchmark for Last-Mile S2S Climate Services

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14017
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14017
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Even the best MLLMs (GPT-5.2) exhibit low accuracy on Level I (~0.36) and face persistent operationalization bottlenecks (TTH, FC, UC) in Levels II–III, with performance collapsing for dynamic hazards.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces S2SServiceBench, a multimodal benchmark designed to evaluate the "last-mile gap" in subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) climate services: translating operational forecast products into trusted, actionable decision support under uncertainty. The benchmark comprises 150+ curated cases across 10 service products and 6 domains, structured around three capability levels: signal comprehension, decision-making handoff, and decision analysis & planning. Evaluation of state-of-the-art MLLMs and a standardized agentic workflow reveals that even top models struggle with basic signal comprehension from complex service plots and suffer from operationalization failures—particularly in trigger/time clarity, feasibility constraints, and uncertainty handling. Results indicate that current generic MLLMs and agents cannot reliably generate operationally compliant deliverables, pointing to the need for dedicated climate-service agents with service-specific guardrails and tooling.

## Method Summary
S2SServiceBench is built from an operational climate-service system, featuring real multimodal artifacts (maps/outlooks) and metadata. It defines two task types: Schema-Constrained Structured Completion (SSC) for Level I and Structured Report Generation (SRG) for Levels II–III, each requiring specific output schemas. Evaluation uses automatic metrics: SSC scores combine schema validity and field accuracy (with numeric tolerance), while SRG scores use an LLM-as-Judge (GPT-5.2) across six rubric dimensions. The benchmark includes 150+ cases spanning 10 service products (e.g., Drought Outlook, Tropical Cyclone Outlook) across six domains, with each case mapped to the three capability levels. The study tests six models (GPT-5.2, Grok-4, Claude 4.5 Opus, Gemini 3 Pro, Qwen3-VL-32B, Llama 4 Maverick) under direct prompting and a standardized DeepAgent workflow (LangChain + Tavily search). No training is involved; inference is zero/few-shot with structured output constraints. Reproduction requires accessing the dataset (not yet public), implementing the schemas, and running the models with the specified settings, followed by rubric-based scoring.

## Key Results
- Top model GPT-5.2 scores: Level I (signal comprehension) overall 0.3613, Level II (decision handoff) 0.6379, Level III (decision analysis & planning) 0.5088.
- Operationalization dimensions—Trigger & Time/Horizon Clarity (TTH), Feasibility & Constraints (FC), and Uncertainty & Confidence handling (UC)—are dominant bottlenecks, especially for dynamic hazards (Tropical Cyclone, Atmospheric River) where scores can approach zero.
- A standardized agentic scaffold yields minor gains for Level I but degrades performance for Levels II–III, indicating that generic agent frameworks are insufficient for climate service tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Signal Comprehension Bottleneck**  
Current MLLMs fail to reliably extract actionable signals from operational S2S service products due to difficulties in strict multimodal interpretation and time-localization. The models must parse complex, visually dense templates (e.g., maps with overlays) to produce precise, machine-consumable slots (regions, risk tiers, confidence cues). Even top models show low Level I accuracy (~0.36), indicating that misinterpretation of visual elements (color coding, hatching) or confusion between initialization and valid times leads to critical slot errors. Break condition: model achieves >90% slot-level accuracy on simple canonical templates.

**Mechanism 2: Operationalization Failure in Decision Handoff**  
For Level II tasks, MLLMs struggle to transform probabilistic/qualified signals into deterministic, rule-like handoffs with clear triggers, feasible constraints, and scenario-based branches. The models produce generic advice without explicit thresholds, ignore resource/policy constraints, or misapply confidence levels. This creates outputs that are not executable. Break condition: outputs include specific threshold values, cite constraints, and correctly branch by confidence tier across >80% of cases.

**Mechanism 3: Planning Instability for Dynamic Hazards**  
In Level III, model performance collapses for dynamic hazards (Tropical Cyclone, Atmospheric River) due to instability in producing evidence-grounded, longer-horizon reasoning. Models hallucinate drivers, propose infeasible plans, or vacillate between contradictory implications. Even when evidence grounding is strong, scores on operationalization dimensions (ACT, TTH, FC, UC) drop near zero. Break condition: model maintains >3.5 on TTH/FC/UC for dynamic hazards with <5% variance across runs.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Schema-Constrained Structured Outputs (SSC/SRG)**  
  The benchmark evaluates deliverables via strict schemas (JSON via Pydantic) and Minimum Structure Requirements. Misalignment leads to zero scores.  
  *Quick check:* If a model outputs valid JSON but omits a required field, the task score is zero for that field and the average of fields becomes the task score.

- **Concept: Uncertainty-Aware Service Reasoning**  
  Service products encode uncertainty via confidence overlays, risk tiers, ensemble spread. Models must not treat forecasts as deterministic; outputs should reflect conditional actions.  
  *Quick check:* In Level II, a low-confidence hatched overlay should not yield a single definite action without low-regret alternatives or confidence-conditioned branching.

- **Concept: Operationalization Dimensions (TTH, FC, UC)**  
  These are the primary bottlenecks: Trigger & Time/Horizon Clarity, Feasibility & Constraints, Uncertainty & Confidence handling. Any agent design must explicitly address them.  
  *Quick check:* A plan that says "monitor the situation" without stating *when* or *what threshold* triggers escalation violates TTH.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Input Processor -> Capability Router -> (Tool Augmentation?) -> Guardrail Enforcer -> Output.  
  For agentic workflows: add Planner -> Workspace (intermediate notes) -> Verifier before Guardrail.

- **Critical path:** The service product is first parsed into structured evidence by Input Processor. Then the Capability Router directs to the appropriate task-level generator. Tool Augmentation may be used to fetch external constraints. The Guardrail Enforcer validates against MSR and operational constraints. Finally, output is produced.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Strictness vs. Flexibility: Tight guardrails improve compliance but may suppress creative phrasing; start strict, relax after >80% baseline.  
  - Tool Use: Web search can improve FC but risks noise and EG; use only for retrievable facts (e.g., regulations).  
  - Single vs. Multi-Agent: Monolithic simpler but may conflate capabilities; multi-agent isolates failures but increases orchestration complexity.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Level I: low schema validity/high slot errors → Input Processor or vision grounding failure.  
  - Level II: high EG but low TTH/FC/UC → Guardrail not enforcing specificity; model relies on generic knowledge.  
  - Level III: score collapse on dynamic hazards → lack of stable planning; agent over-thinks or hallucinates.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Baseline capability audit: Run direct prompting on a 20-case subset (all levels/products). Record overall and per-product scores to identify easy/hard products.  
  2. Bottleneck isolation: For Level II/III, run with/without Guardrail Enforcer. Measure delta in TTH/FC/UC. If guardrail improves, implementation lacks; if no change, model fundamentally cannot produce required content.  
  3. Tool augmentation test: For Level II tasks on constraint-heavy products (Drought, Solar), enable web search for region-specific constraints. Compare FC scores. If FC does not improve, tool retrieval is ineffective.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Can current MLLMs or agentic workflows reliably generate operationally compliant decision support deliverables from operational S2S service products across the three core service capabilities?**  
   *Basis:* Central research question in Section 1. *Why unresolved:* Benchmark shows low performance, product-dependent variability, and generic agent scaffold only minorly helps Level I but harms Levels II–III. *What evidence would resolve:* Benchmark newer models and domain-specific agents on S2SServiceBench, focusing on TTH/FC/UC gaps.

2. **Why do operationalization failures (TTH, FC, UC) persist as primary bottlenecks even when models maintain strong Evidence Grounding?**  
   *Basis:* Section 5.2 identifies these as dominant bottlenecks while EG is consistently strong. *Why unresolved:* Models align with evidence but fail to translate signals into executable actions, precise timing, calibrated uncertainty. *What evidence would resolve:* Fine-grained error analysis (human audit) or ablations separating product comprehension from operational reasoning; test models trained on operational guidelines or with constraint-checking tools.

3. **What factors drive strong product-dependent performance variability, and why do dynamic hazard products collapse in Level III planning?**  
   *Why unresolved:* Paper notes variance (e.g., strong on Drought/Extreme Precip, near-zero on Tropical Cyclone for some dimensions) but does not investigate causes. *What evidence would resolve:* Controlled studies correlating product characteristics (map complexity, uncertainty representation, temporal dynamics) with performance; qualitative failure analysis per product; build product-agnostic vs. product-specific agent components.

4. **How should dedicated climate-service agents be architected to overcome limitations, and which service-specific components are most critical?**  
   *Basis:* Conclusion argues for service-specific training/tooling; proposed components remain speculative. *Why unresolved:* Generic DeepAgent is insufficient; no concrete agent design evaluated. *What evidence would resolve:* Develop and benchmark candidate agents (fine-tuning, retrieval, modular pipelines) on S2SServiceBench; ablation studies isolating contributions (e.g., trigger generators, constraint checkers).

5. **To what extent do the benchmark tasks and schema-constrained evaluation introduce artifacts that may not capture real-world “last-mile” service readiness, and how might the protocol be refined?**  
   *Basis:* Inferred; structured formats and LLM-as-Judge may oversimplify; no expert validation. *Why unresolved:* Benchmark's formats may not capture trust, communication clarity, iterative refinement; judge bias and MSR constraints may misalign. *What evidence would resolve:* Human expert evaluation of outputs vs. LLM-as-Judge; re-engineer tasks for open-ended deliverables; user studies with practitioners; refine rubric based on expert feedback.

## Limitations
- Dataset not publicly released; exact format, licensing, and accessibility unclear, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Model inference details (hyperparameters, prompt templates, Gemini integration) underspecified, risking replication variance.
- Corpus signals indicate nascent niche (avg citations=0, high FMR=0.42), limiting external corroboration of claims.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Level I signal comprehension bottleneck): Medium – supported by internal benchmarks but lacks independent validation.
- Mechanism 2 (Level II/III operationalization failures in TTH/FC/UC): High – rubric dimensions clearly defined and consistently reported.
- Mechanism 3 (dynamic hazard collapse in Level III): Medium – evidence strong for specific products but may be sample-size sensitive.
