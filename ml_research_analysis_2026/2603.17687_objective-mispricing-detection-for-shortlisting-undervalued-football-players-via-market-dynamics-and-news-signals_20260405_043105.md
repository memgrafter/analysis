# Objective Mispricing Detection for Shortlisting Undervalued Football Players via Market Dynamics and News Signals

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.17687
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17687
- Reference count: 23
- Primary result: Full model (market dynamics + news NLP) achieves ROC-AUC 0.677 for undervaluation shortlisting, significantly outperforming ablations.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an objective, data-driven approach to identify undervalued football players by predicting their "fair" market value from historical structured data (e.g., transfer history, performance trends, contract details) and comparing it to the observed valuation. The mispricing signal replaces subjective expert labels with a reproducible metric. News-derived natural language processing features—sentiment scores and semantic embeddings—are integrated to supplement the structured data, capturing narrative-driven uncertainty. In chronological evaluation, a gradient-boosted regression model explains 93.5% of the variance in log-transformed market values. For the binary shortlisting task (top 15% mispriced), the combined model yields a ROC-AUC of 0.677, demonstrating that news features provide consistent, secondary gains that improve robustness, especially in volatile regimes.

## Method Summary
The method proceeds in three stages. First, an XGBoost regression model is trained to predict the log(1+market_value) using structured features only, with the current valuation explicitly excluded to prevent leakage. Second, mispricing is computed as the difference between predicted and observed log-values; players above the 85th percentile are labeled as undervalued. Third, a separate XGBoost classifier is trained on the full feature set (structured + news-derived features) to predict this binary label. News articles per player are aggregated over a lookback window, cleaned, and transformed into sentiment statistics and PCA-reduced sentence embeddings before concatenation with structured features. The pipeline uses a chronological train-test split to avoid temporal leakage.

## Key Results
- Regression performance: XGBoost explains 93.5% of log-value variance (R²) on the test set.
- Shortlisting performance: Full model (market + NLP) ROC-AUC = 0.677; No-Text model ROC-AUC = 0.604; Text-Only model ROC-AUC = 0.514.
- SHAP analysis reveals market dynamics features (value trend, volatility, age) dominate predictions; news features contribute marginally but consistently across ablations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Objective mispricing detection replaces subjective expert labels with a reproducible, data-driven undervaluation signal by comparing model-predicted and observed market values.
- **Mechanism:** Train a regression model (XGBoost) on structured features (market dynamics, biographical, contract, transfer history) to predict the log-transformed market value, explicitly excluding the current valuation as an input. Compute mispricing as the log-difference between predicted and observed values. Flag players in the top 15% of mispricing as undervalued for shortlisting.
- **Core assumption:** Structured historical market data and player attributes contain sufficient signal to model a reasonable "expected" fundamental value, and deviations from this expectation represent actionable mispricing.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "estimate an expected market value from structured data... and compare it to the observed valuation to define mispricing." [section 3.4] Details the log-transform, mispricing formula (Eq. 4), and 85th-percentile thresholding (Eq. 5).
- **Break condition:** Regression R² on held-out data falls below a domain-relevant threshold (e.g., <0.7), indicating structured features cannot reliably establish a baseline "fair" value.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Market dynamics are the dominant drivers of player valuation, while news-derived NLP features provide consistent but secondary gains in shortlisting robustness.
- **Mechanism:** An ablation study shows removing text features ("No Text") causes a systematic ROC-AUC drop across models, while a "Text Only" model performs near random. SHAP analysis reveals market trend (`mv_trend`), volatility (`mv_std`), and `Age` have the largest global impacts on the regression model's output, with news features having marginal, case-specific contributions.
- **Core assumption:** Efficient or near-efficient market mechanisms mean long-term value is primarily determined by sustained performance, career trajectory, and contract status, with news serving as a contextual modifier.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "market dynamics are the primary signal, while NLP features provide consistent, secondary gains." [section 4.2] Table 3: Full model (0.677) outperforms No Text (0.604) and Text Only (0.514). [section 4.3] SHAP summary plot (Fig. 2) shows `mv_trend`, `mv_std`, `Age` dominate.
- **Break condition:** In multiple temporal or league-specific evaluations, the performance gap between Full and No Text models becomes statistically insignificant, or SHAP values for news features consistently show zero or inverse impact.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** News-derived sentiment and semantic embeddings enhance shortlisting by capturing narrative-driven uncertainty (e.g., injury rumors, form slumps, transfer speculation) that precedes or accompanies market underreaction, particularly in high-volatility regimes.
- **Mechanism:** Text features encode soft information not yet fully reflected in price movements. PCA-reduced embeddings and aggregated sentiment statistics are concatenated with market features. The model learns to weight these features more heavily when market volatility is high.
- **Core assumption:** Football markets exhibit delayed reactions to narrative information; media coverage contains leading indicators of value-changing events that structured historical data misses.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "news-derived volatility cues amplifying signals in high-uncertainty regimes." [section 6] "narratives can contextualize or precede market adjustments... textual information does not replace structured market data, but serves as a robust supplementary signal."
- **Break condition:** Granger-causality or lagged-feature analysis reveals news sentiment/embeddings do not significantly predict future mispricing corrections when controlling for market dynamics, or NLP gains disappear when modeling only low-volatility players.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Log-Transformation of Skewed Target Variables.
  - **Why needed here:** Market values are right-skewed; modeling raw values causes heteroscedasticity and overweighting high-value players, harming residual-based mispricing calculation.
  - **Quick check:** If you applied linear regression to raw market values, what primary issue would you expect in residuals and how would it affect mispricing prediction?
- **Concept:** Chronological (Leakage-Aware) Evaluation.
  - **Why needed here:** Valuations and news are time-series; a random split would use future data to predict the past, creating overly optimistic performance.
  - **Quick check:** Why is a random split of player records by time period inappropriate, even if player IDs are unique?
- **Concept:** SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) for Tabular Model Interpretation.
  - **Why needed here:** XGBoost is a black-box ensemble; SHAP quantifies each feature's contribution to individual predictions, building trust by showing why a player is flagged.
  - **Quick check:** How does a SHAP summary plot differ from a simple feature importance bar chart in what it reveals about the model's reasoning?

## Architecture Onboarding
Data Ingestion -> Preprocessing -> Feature Store -> Modeling (Stage 1: Regression, Stage 2: Mispricing Calculation, Stage 3: Classification) -> Deployment API
Critical path: Data freshness -> Feature calculation (time-series market dynamics) -> Regression prediction -> Mispricing computation -> Shortlist ranking.
Design tradeoffs: XGBoost chosen over TabNet for performance/simplicity; PCA reduces embedding dimensions (768D -> ~50D) at potential semantic loss; fixed 15% threshold is simple but not volatility-adaptive.
Failure signatures: (1) Regression R² drops >0.05 from 0.935 or shortlisting ROC-AUC drops >0.05 from 0.677; (2) No Text model matches or exceeds Full model in future seasons; (3) SHAP top features conflict with domain knowledge or show extreme instability.
First 3 experiments: (1) Reproduce regression baseline: train on chronological train split, verify test R² ±0.02 of 0.935; (2) Validate ablation gains: train Full, No Text, Text Only classifiers, confirm Full ROC-AUC highest and Text Only ~0.51; (3) Inspect SHAP: verify `mv_trend`, `mv_std`, `Age` are top 3 by mean |SHAP|.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Does news sentiment precede or follow changes in football player market valuations? *Basis:* Future Work notes need for lag-structure modeling. *Unresolved:* Observational study cannot determine causality. *Evidence needed:* Granger-causality or VAR predicting next-period valuation changes from current news, controlling for past trends.
- **Open Question 2:** How does inclusion of multilingual and region-specific news sources affect generalizability across diverse leagues? *Basis:* Limitations cite English-centric corpus; Future Work proposes extending across leagues and incorporating multilingual sources. *Unresolved:* English bias may overlook undervaluation in less-covered regions. *Evidence needed:* Multi-language news corpus with multilingual NLP, compare ROC-AUC across league tiers/regions.
- **Open Question 3:* Can sophisticated temporal and source-weighted aggregation of news articles (e.g., decay functions, credibility weighting) improve extraction of influential narratives? *Basis:* Future Work suggests decay, source weighting, attention-based pooling. *Unresolved:* Current mean-pooling ignores recency and source reliability. *Evidence needed:* Implement alternative aggregations, measure incremental ROC-AUC gains in ablation.
- **Open Question 4:* Which specific narrative themes (e.g., injury reports, transfer rumors) systematically precede corrections in mispricing, and can causal inference identify them? *Basis:* Future Work proposes causal inference for theme-specific effects. *Unresolved:* Aggregate NLP masks theme-specific impacts. *Evidence needed:* Extract themes via topic modeling, apply causal methods (e.g., difference-in-differences) to test if theme prevalence predicts mispricing resolution.

## Limitations
- Static 15% undervaluation threshold does not adapt to market cycles.
- PCA on embeddings risks losing rare but predictive semantic signals.
- English-language news corpus may bias toward high-visibility leagues.
