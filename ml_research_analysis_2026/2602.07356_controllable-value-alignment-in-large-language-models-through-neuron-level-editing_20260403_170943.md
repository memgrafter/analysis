# Controllable Value Alignment in Large Language Models through Neuron-Level Editing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.07356
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07356
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: NeVA achieves higher control success rate across all 10 Schwartz values while reducing value leakage by ~40% relative to baselines, maintaining high fluency and minimal MMLU degradation.

## Executive Summary
Large language models often exhibit value leakage: steering toward a target human value unintentionally activates non-target values due to entangled representations. This paper introduces metrics (Normalized Leakage Ratio and Normalized Group Leakage Ratio) to quantify this phenomenon and proposes NeVA, a neuron-level editing framework that isolates value-relevant neurons for precise, context-aware modulation. Evaluated on LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct across ten Schwartz values, NeVA outperforms existing steering methods in control success rate, significantly reduces cross-value leakage, and preserves model fluency and general capability.

## Method Summary
NeVA operates in three stages. First, it trains high-accuracy linear probes on a context-controlled dataset to estimate value-sensitive directions in the residual stream of specific middle layers (probe accuracy >95%). Second, for each retained layer, it decomposes the global direction into neuron-level contributions via cosine similarity, selecting a small subset of neurons with the strongest positive (aligned) and negative (opposed) signatures (Top-K based on a sparsity ratio α). Third, during inference, NeVA modulates the pre-activation of these selected neurons using a sign-consistent scaling rule: \(m_{\text{edit}} = m \cdot (1 + \text{sign}(m) \cdot \text{sign}(s) \cdot \beta)\), where \(s\) is the neuron's alignment score. This intervention is applied only at the chosen layers, leaving the base model frozen. The result is fine-grained, value-specific steering with minimal interference on other values or general fluency.

## Key Results
- NeVA achieves higher Control Success Rate (CSR) across all 10 Schwartz values compared to baselines like ConVA and CAA.
- NeVA reduces average Normalized Leakage Ratio (NLR) by ~40% relative to ConVA (0.402 vs. 0.673), with residual leakage largely confined to within-group semantically related values.
- NeVA maintains high Fluency Rate (>97%) and minimal degradation on MMLU (64.1% vs. base 65.7%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Sparse neuron identification isolates alignment effects
**Claim:** Identifying a sparse subset of value-relevant neurons isolates alignment effects to the target value.
**Mechanism:** NeVA trains linear probes to estimate a value-sensitive direction \(W\). It then computes the cosine similarity between each neuron's value vector \(v_k\) and \(W\), selecting neurons with the strongest positive (aligned) and negative (opposed) similarities; all others are excluded. This limits the scope of alignment operations.
**Core assumption:** Human value expressions are sparsely represented by a small number of neurons, allowing localized intervention without globally perturbing the representation space.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "identifies sparse, value-relevant neurons"; Section 3.3 states that decomposition limits scope; concept is grounded in neuron-level editing literature (e.g., Geva et al., 2021).
**Break condition:** If value representations are distributed broadly across the network, neuron selection would arbitrarily cut necessary components, leading to weak alignment or degradation.

### Mechanism 2: Context-aware activation modulation preserves semantic roles
**Claim:** Context-aware activation modulation preserves semantic roles and prevents unintended amplification of non-target values.
**Mechanism:** For each selected neuron, NeVA edits its activation via \(m_{k,\text{edit}} = m_k \cdot (1 + \text{sign}(m_k) \cdot \text{sign}(s_k) \cdot \beta)\). This amplifies contributions when the neuron's contextual activation polarity matches its directional alignment, and suppresses when they oppose. It respects the neuron's inherent role and current context.
**Core assumption:** A neuron's effect on value expression depends jointly on its intrinsic bias (value vector direction) and its context-specific activation.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "fine-grained control"; Section 3.4 explains that intervention adjusts magnitude without introducing new directions or altering underlying semantics, and conditioning restricts modulation to neurons that are both relevant and active.
**Break condition:** If the polarity interaction is noisy or unstable across contexts, modulation could suppress aligned neurons or amplify opposed ones, worsening leakage.

### Mechanism 3: Localized intervention reduces cross-value leakage
**Claim:** Localized, neuron-level intervention fundamentally reduces cross-value and cross-group leakage compared to dense steering.
**Mechanism:** By restricting edits to a small, value-specific neuron set, NeVA avoids dense additive perturbations that entangle multiple value dimensions. This spatial limitation constrains propagation of the alignment signal.
**Core assumption:** Value leakage arises from representational entanglement; a dense intervention moves activation along multiple correlated value directions. Sparsity at the neuron level breaks this coupling.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "residual effects largely confined to semantically related value classes"; Section 4.4 shows diagonal-dominant NGLR heatmaps, indicating cross-group leakage is reduced.
**Break condition:** If value-relevant neurons for different values overlap heavily, editing one value will inevitably affect others, limiting leakage reduction.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Offline: Value-Specific Prober → Neuron Decomposer → (store neuron signatures)
Online: Input → Frozen LLM Core (with Context-Aware Editor) → Output

**Critical path:**
1. Offline: Train linear probes per layer; retain layers with validation accuracy ≥95%; compute cosine similarities and select signed Top-K neurons (aligned/opposed) for each retained layer.
2. Online: Process input through the model; at each retained layer, before the FFN computation, modulate the pre-activation of selected neurons using the sign-consistent rule with chosen β; continue forward pass to generate output.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Sparsity ratio (α):** Larger α increases CSR but risks greater neuron overlap across values (more leakage) and may harm fluency.
- **Editing strength (β):** Higher β boosts CSR but can cause unnatural generations if over-modulating; optimal β varies per value/model.
- **Layer selection:** Intervening too early may have weak value signal; too late may disrupt linguistic fluency. Middle layers are typically chosen to balance control and fluency.

**Failure signatures:**
- High Average NLR (>0.6) indicates extensive cross-value leakage.
- NGLR heatmap with strong off-diagonal mass shows cross-group spillover.
- Significant MMLU drop (>2% absolute) suggests general capability degradation.
- Low CSR despite high β suggests poor neuron selection (e.g., inappropriate layer choice or low probe accuracy).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Probe Accuracy Profiling:** Plot probe accuracy across layers (e.g., Figure 12). Identify layers with >95% accuracy that are not final layers to balance signal and fluency.
2. **CSR & FR Sweep:** For a target value (e.g., Achievement), sweep α (0.01–0.05) and β (0.5–1.0) to find a Pareto front of high CSR with FR >97%. Validate results against reported tables.
3. **Neuron Selection Ablation:** Replace NeVA's selected neurons with an equal number of random neurons. Measure CSR drop; if performance falls to near-base, neuron selection is meaningful.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Multi-value alignment**  
   NeVA is designed for single-value interventions. Extending it to multiple, potentially conflicting, values requires managing trade-offs; naive superposition of neuron edits may cause unstable interactions. A joint optimization approach (e.g., Pareto optimization) is needed to produce controllable multi-value outcomes.

2. **Long-horizon stability**  
   Evaluation focused on single-turn prompting. It is unknown whether NeVA's alignment effects remain stable over multi-turn interactions; leakage might accumulate or drift over time. Multi-turn dialogue experiments are required to assess durability.

3. **Sparsity vs. disentanglement**  
   While random neuron editing yields near-base CSR, suggesting that meaningful selection matters, the paper does not measure overlap between neuron sets for different values. It remains unclear whether low leakage is due to truly independent value neurons or simply to editing fewer units. Computing Jaccard similarity between neuron sets and comparing against sparsity-matched random baselines would clarify the role of disentanglement.

## Limitations
- The core sparsity assumption—that value-relevant neurons form a small, distinct subset—may not hold for larger models or diverse value taxonomies.
- Probe accuracy threshold (>95%) and layer selection are empirical heuristics lacking theoretical grounding and may not generalize to new architectures.
- Hyperparameter optimization (α, β) details are undisclosed, hindering full reproducibility.
- Evaluation relies on LLM-as-judge for CSR and FR, which may introduce biases compared to human judgment.

## Confidence
- NeVA yields higher CSR and lower NLR than baselines: **High**
- Neuron-level editing isolates target values and reduces cross-group leakage: **Medium**
- Context-aware modulation preserves fluency: **High**

## Next Checks
1. **Neuron selection stability:** Re-train probes with different random seeds and compute Jaccard overlap of selected neuron sets; high overlap (>80%) would confirm robustness of the identification process.
2. **Human validation of alignment:** Conduct human evaluation on a stratified sample of NeVA outputs to verify CSR and FR against LLM-as-judge scores.
3. **Sparsity across scales:** Test NeVA on larger models (e.g., 70B) and measure neuron set overlap across values; increasing overlap should correlate with higher NLR, testing the sparsity premise.
