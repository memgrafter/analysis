# Chitrakshara: A Large Multilingual Multimodal Dataset for Indian languages

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.23521
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23521
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Created Chitrakshara, a large-scale multilingual multimodal dataset for 11 Indian languages, with 193M images, 30B tokens, and 50M interleaved documents, demonstrating superior scale and quality over existing resources like mOSCAR.

## Executive Summary
Chitrakshara addresses the severe underrepresentation of Indian languages in multimodal resources by constructing a large dataset from Common Crawl. It comprises Chitrakshara-IL (50M interleaved documents, 193M images, 30B tokens) and Chitrakshara-Cap (44M image–caption pairs, 733M tokens). A tailored filtering pipeline yields significantly higher volume and language-specific quality than prior efforts such as mOSCAR. Domain distribution is dominated by news (76%) but includes educational and other topics, and temporal sourcing across a decade enriches cultural diversity. The work demonstrates the feasibility of large-scale inclusive data collection for low-resource languages and paves the way for culturally aligned vision‑language models.  
The release of Chitrakshara provides a foundation for training multilingual VLMs and sets a template for constructing multimodal datasets from web crawls.

## Method Summary
The pipeline processes 95 Common Crawl WARC dumps spanning 2013–2023. FastText language identification filters URLs for 11 Indian languages. Distributed S3‑to‑S3 retrieval fetches the WARC files, avoiding HTTP throttling. HTML is parsed with selectolax; DOM pruning removes navigation, ads, and boilerplate via class/id patterns. The cleaned DOM is linearized into interleaved text–image sequences while preserving structural markers (e.g., `<SECTION>`, `<FIGURE>`), line breaks, and paragraph boundaries. Images are downloaded in parallel using `img2dataset` (55% success rate). Hierarchical filtering is applied: image level (format, size ≥150 px, aspect ratio 1:5–5:1, placeholder filename removal), paragraph level (≥8 words, stopword density >5%), document level (1–30 images, coherence checks, NSFW keyword removal). For Chitraskhara‑Cap, alt‑text is required to have ≥5 words and image resolution is classified (Low <200 px, Mid 200–600 px, High >600 px).

## Key Results
- **Scale**: Chitrakshara-IL contains 50M interleaved documents, 193M images, and 30B tokens across 11 Indian languages, far surpassing mOSCAR (e.g., Hindi: 25.4M vs. 579K documents).
- **Quality**: Tailored heuristics yield higher language-specific retention and multimodal balance (1–30 images per document).
- **Diversity**: Domain distribution spans news (76%), educational, and other topics; topic modeling confirms broad coverage; temporal sourcing across a decade enriches cultural content.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Language‑Specific Filtering Heuristics
- **Claim**: Language‑specific filtering (stopword density ≥5%, paragraph length ≥8 words) significantly improves retrieval of Indian language content compared to generic English‑optimized pipelines.
- **Mechanism**: Hierarchical filtering at node, paragraph, and document levels, with rules adapted for Indic linguistic patterns, avoids aggressive removal by English models and preserves valid Indic text.
- **Core assumption**: Generic perplexity filters misclassify morphologically rich Indo‑Aryan and Dravidian text as low‑quality.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions tailored filtering; Section 3.4 & 6.5.1 details paragraph‑level heuristics; comparative corpus signals highlight differences with mOSCAR.
- **Break condition**: If LID or heuristics cause high false‑negative rates for valid Indic content (e.g., dialogs, poetry), linguistic diversity degrades.

### Mechanism 2: Preserving Interleaved Structure
- **Claim**: Maintaining original interleaved text‑image sequences enables better real‑world document comprehension and multi‑image in‑context learning versus isolated image‑caption pairs.
- **Mechanism**: Linearize cleaned DOM while retaining layout markers (e.g., `<SECTION>`, `<FIGURE>`), line breaks, and paragraph boundaries to preserve semantic relationships between images and surrounding text.
- **Core assumption**: Sequential context and spatial arrangement are crucial for Vision‑Language Models (e.g., Flamingo, OBELICS).
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract cites human‑like processing; Section 3.3 describes following OBELICS; corpus neighbors include interleaved modeling papers.
- **Break condition**: If linearization loses image context (e.g., from `<figcaption>`) or breaks logical flow, utility for interleaved VLMs diminishes.

### Mechanism 3: Broad Temporal Sourcing
- **Claim**: Using 95 Common Crawl dumps (2013–2023) instead of a few recent dumps (like mOSCAR’s three) greatly increases volume and diversity of Indian language documents, addressing the 1% representation gap.
- **Mechanism**: Distributed pipeline processes a much larger WARC set, mitigating sparse Indic content per crawl; S3‑to‑S3 transfer handles 24 TB metadata efficiently.
- **Core assumption**: Indian web content has grown over the last decade and older dumps contain unique culturally significant material absent in recent crawls.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract attributes scale difference to decade‑spanning dumps; Section 6.1 describes processing volume; Table 1 shows initial distribution.
- **Break condition**: If older dumps are mostly low‑quality or irrelevant, scale gains may not translate to higher dataset utility.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:  
Crawl Selection (FastText LID on CC metadata) -> WARC Retrieval (distributed S3 download) -> DOM Pruning (selectolax parser with rule‑based removal) -> Document Assembly (linearization with structural tokens) -> Hierarchical Filtering (node/paragraph/document) -> Post‑processing (NSFW, metadata removal) -> Image Download (img2dataset with proxy distribution)

**Critical path**: The sequence from WARC retrieval → DOM pruning → assembly → filtering is linear and dependent. Failures in DOM pruning propagate downstream and cannot be fixed later.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Scale vs. Quality*: Aggressive filtering increases precision but may discard atypical documents (e.g., image galleries, poetry).
- *Language‑specificity vs. Generality*: Heuristics tuned for Indian scripts may inadvertently filter legitimate code snippets or technical terms.
- *Computational Cost vs. Coverage*: 95‑dump strategy maximizes recall but requires significant distributed resources (5,120 cores, 900 batches).

**Failure signatures**:
- High duplicate rate post‑deduplication: Indicates ineffective paragraph‑level deduplication, leading to storage waste and model overfitting.
- Low retrieval success rate (~55%): Points to network issues, dead links, or robots.txt restrictions; requires proxy management and retries.
- Skewed language distribution post‑LID: Suggests FastText misclassification (e.g., Hindi vs. Urdu); may need fine‑tuning or correction.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Ablation of Language Filters: Run pipeline with/without paragraph‑level English‑line removal and stopword‑density filter on 1% sample; measure change in valid Indic document count and noise proportion.
2. Interleaving Integrity Check: Randomly sample 100 documents; verify image placements in linearized output match original webpage visual reading order (using screenshots); quantify failure rate.
3. Temporal Diversity Validation: Compare domain/topic distributions (LDA) across documents from 2013‑2016 vs. 2020‑2023 dumps; assess whether topic shifts indicate quality decay in oldest dumps.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How do vision‑language models trained on Chitrakshara perform on Indian language multimodal benchmarks compared to models trained on existing datasets like mOSCAR?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The conclusion states that future work involves “training a multilingual ViT and an interleaved VLM to evaluate its effectiveness,” but no such evaluation is presented.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only describes dataset construction and analysis; downstream model performance and comparative gains remain unmeasured.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Rigorous benchmarking of VLMs trained on Chitrakshara versus mOSCAR or other baselines across diverse Indian language multimodal tasks, reporting accuracy, cultural alignment, and language‑specific metrics.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Does the heuristic‑based filtering pipeline sacrifice quality or diversity relative to a more computationally expensive LLM‑based filtering approach for Indic languages?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Section 6.5.1 notes that LLM‑based filtering was considered but rejected due to cost, implying a potential quality trade‑off that is unquantified.
- **Why unresolved**: No comparison is made between heuristic and LLM filtering outcomes; the adequacy of the chosen method for preserving linguistically and culturally relevant content is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A controlled ablation study comparing dataset subsets from heuristic vs. LLM filtering, followed by VLM training to assess differences in downstream task performance and content diversity.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: To what extent does the news‑dominated domain distribution (76%) in Chitrakshara bias VLMs towards news contexts, and how does this affect generalization to other domains like entertainment, health, or daily life?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Figure 3 reveals a heavy news skew, but the paper does not analyze potential biases or discuss domain balance beyond reporting the distribution.
- **Why unresolved**: The impact of this skew on model applicability across non‑news scenarios is not evaluated; topical diversity may not compensate for domain imbalance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Evaluation of VLMs trained on Chitrakshara on domain‑disaggregated test sets, measuring performance drop‑offs in underrepresented domains, or creating domain‑balanced versions of the dataset.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Does Chitrakshara adequately capture culturally specific multimodal semantics (e.g., regional festivals, traditional attire, local contexts) beyond surface‑level language and topic diversity to enable truly culturally inclusive VLMs?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The abstract claims cultural inclusivity, but the analysis focuses on language counts, domain shares, and topic modeling without deeper cultural content assessment.
- **Why unresolved**: Cultural representation is inferred from broad categories; nuanced cultural expressions and their preservation in the dataset are not examined, leaving inclusivity claims speculative.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Qualitative expert analysis of sample documents for cultural markers, or quantitative metrics for cultural entity density correlated with model performance on culturally grounded tasks (e.g., identifying region‑specific practices).

## Limitations
- Unspecified filter hyperparameters (FastText LID thresholds, stopword density formula, NSFW lexicon, coherence check details).
- Low image download success (~55%) and potential over‑filtering of linguistically diverse content (e.g., poetry, dialogs).
- Uncertain quality and cultural relevance of pre‑2016 Common Crawl dumps, despite scale gains.
