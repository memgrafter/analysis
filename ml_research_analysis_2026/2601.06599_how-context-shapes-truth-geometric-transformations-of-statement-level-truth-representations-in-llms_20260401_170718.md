# How Context Shapes Truth: Geometric Transformations of Statement-level Truth Representations in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06599
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06599
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** Adding context causes a consistent three-phase directional change in truth vectors across layers and generally increases their magnitude, with model size affecting how relevant context is distinguished.

## Executive Summary
This study examines how context geometrically transforms truth representations—quantified as truth vectors—in the residual streams of large language models. By comparing activations for true versus false statement completions with and without context, the authors measure two metrics: directional change (θ) and relative magnitude. Across four models (3B–12B parameters) and eight English datasets, they find a robust three-phase pattern: θ is high in early layers (orthogonal truth vectors), drops in middle layers (convergence), then stabilizes or rises in later layers, with conflicting contexts producing the largest shifts. Context also amplifies the separation between true and false representations (magnitude), peaking in middle layers. Notably, larger models (LLaMA 8B, Mistral 12B) signal relevant context primarily through greater θ, while smaller models (Qwen 4B, SmolLM 3B) rely more on magnitude changes. These geometric transformations are statistically significant and offer insights into how LLMs integrate contextual information with parametric knowledge.

## Method Summary
For each factual statement, four prompt variants are constructed: supporting or refuting the statement, each with and without context. The authors extract residual stream activations (after the MLP) at the final token position of the first generated token using greedy decoding. Truth vectors are computed as the difference between activations for true and false completions (`v = a_true - a_false`) for each condition. Layer-wise directional change θ is the arccosine of the normalized dot product between context-free and context truth vectors, while relative magnitude is the ratio of their L2 norms. Averaging across statements per layer yields smooth trends, and statistical comparisons (Wilcoxon signed-rank with Bonferroni correction) assess differences between context types (e.g., relevant vs. random). The methodology assumes truth is linearly separable in the residual stream, which is confirmed via probing.

## Key Results
- Truth vectors exhibit a three-phase θ pattern across layers: orthogonal in early layers, converging in middle layers, and stabilizing or diverging in later layers; conflicting context yields the largest θ increases.
- Adding context generally increases truth vector magnitude, with a peak in middle layers; final-layer magnitudes are mostly >1 and statistically significant.
- Larger models distinguish relevant from irrelevant context primarily through greater directional change (θ), while smaller models show this distinction through greater relative magnitude shifts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Three-Phase Directional Change
Adding context induces a consistent, layer-dependent directional shift (θ) in truth vectors, characterized by an initial orthogonal phase, a convergence phase in middle layers, and a stabilization or divergence phase later. This pattern mirrors the hierarchical processing of LLMs: early layers focus on syntactic and local patterns, yielding dissimilar truth representations with and without context (high θ). Middle layers integrate semantic and factual information, causing truth vectors to converge (θ drops). Later layers, dominated by next-token prediction and potential conflict processing, either stabilize or further diverge the representations. The core assumption is that each residual stream layer represents a distinct computational stage, and the geometric relationship between conditions changes predictably. Evidence includes the abstract’s description, layer-wise plots (Figure 4), and discussion linking phases to known layer functions. The pattern fails if models show no systematic trend or if early layers already have low θ, contradicting the syntax-semantics hierarchy.

### Mechanism 2: Magnitude Amplification
Context generally increases the relative magnitude (separation) between true and false representations, peaking in middle layers. Context provides an additional discriminative signal that amplifies the distinction; this amplification is strongest where semantic integration is most effective. The core assumption is that the L2 norm difference between truth vectors reflects model certainty or separation. Evidence: the abstract notes the increase; Section 5.2 and Figure 5 show a middle-layer spike; Table 2 reports final-layer magnitudes >1. The effect fails if context consistently decreases magnitude or shows no peak, suggesting it does not enhance truth discrimination.

### Mechanism 3: Model-Scale Distinction
Larger models (LLaMA 8B, Mistral 12B) distinguish relevant from irrelevant context mainly through greater directional change (θ), while smaller models (Qwen 4B, SmolLM 3B) do so through greater magnitude changes. The core assumption is that parameter count influences representational sophistication: larger models develop specialized, directionally sensitive mechanisms, whereas smaller models scale overall separation rather than reorienting. Evidence: abstract claim; Section 5.3 and Tables 3–5 show that for larger models θ differences (relevant vs. random) are more often significant, while for smaller models magnitude differences dominate. The distinction fails if all models show the same primary signal or no significant differences between context types.

## Foundational Learning

### Truth Vectors
- **Why needed:** They are the core subject of analysis; the study measures how these vectors change with context. Understanding that truth can be represented as a linear direction in activation space is prerequisite.
- **Quick check:** How are truth vectors extracted from model activations, and why is the first token position used?

### Residual Stream Activations
- **Why needed:** The geometric transformations are measured in this specific activation space (post-MLP). It's the standard location for probing high-level concepts in LLMs.
- **Quick check:** Why are residual stream activations (as opposed to other layers/outputs) chosen for probing truth representations?

### Directional Change (θ) and Relative Magnitude
- **Why needed:** These are the two primary metrics quantifying geometric transformation. θ captures *how much the direction* of truth changes; relative magnitude captures *how much the separation* changes.
- **Quick check:** What does a high θ value indicate about the truth representation with versus without context? What does a relative magnitude >1 indicate?

### In-Context Learning & Knowledge Conflict
- **Why needed:** The experiment manipulates context to study its effect. Understanding that models process in-context information alongside parametric knowledge (and may conflict) is key to interpreting results like those on ConflictQA.
- **Quick check:** Why might context that conflicts with parametric knowledge produce larger geometric changes (as seen with ConflictQA-Counter)?

### Probe Accuracy & Linear Separability
- **Why needed:** The method assumes truth is linearly separable in the activation space, which is validated by probe performance (Appendix A.4). This justifies using linear differences (vectors) as truth representations.
- **Quick check:** Why is it important that probe accuracy peaks in middle layers, and how does that support the three-phase θ pattern?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
LLM (instruction-tuned) → Activation Extractor → Truth Vector Calculator → Metric Engine → Statistical Comparator

**Critical path:**  
Data preparation → Prompt generation → Activation extraction → Truth vector computation → Metric calculation → Layer-wise averaging → Statistical testing → Visualization

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- First-token extraction is simpler and aggregative but may miss distributed truth signals across multiple tokens.  
- Greedy decoding ensures reproducibility but may not capture stochasticity; sampling could alter activation distributions.  
- Dataset selection (mix of real/synthetic, fact-checking/legal/movie domains) improves generality but introduces domain-specific confounds (e.g., LegalBench's low readability).  
- Random context types (char, word, salad, wiki, shuffle) test sensitivity, but length-matching only controls for one variable.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low instruction-following rate (<~70% for a condition) invalidates activation comparisons.  
- Non-significant θ or magnitude differences between relevant and random context (for expected model sizes) suggests experiment failure or dataset insensitivity.  
- Probe accuracy near random chance (~50%) in middle layers undermines the linear truth representation assumption.  
- Extreme layer-wise variance (high SEM in supplementary figures) indicates unreliable vector estimation per statement.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Reproduce three-phase θ pattern: For a single model-dataset pair (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B + ConflictQA), plot layer-wise θ with/without context. Expect high early θ, a drop in middle layers (~10–20 for 7B–12B models), then plateau or rise.  
2. Confirm magnitude increase with context: Compute final-layer relative magnitude for all datasets. Expect mean >1 and statistical significance (Wilcoxon p<0.05, Bonferroni-corrected) for most datasets.  
3. Test model-scale hypothesis: For LLaMA/Mistral vs. Qwen/SmolLM, compare significance of θ vs. magnitude differences when contrasting relevant vs. random context. Expect larger models to show more θ-significance; smaller models to show more magnitude-significance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Question:** Does the geometric transformation of truth vectors depend on which token position in the generation is analyzed, or is the first token sufficient to capture context-induced changes?  
   *Basis:* The authors explicitly state in the limitations (Section 7) that they extract truth representations from only the first token position, noting that "relevant information may be distributed across multiple tokens."  
   *Why unresolved:* The core methodology relies on first-token activations, assuming aggregation via causal attention. If later tokens encode distinct truth-related information, the reported layer-wise patterns might be incomplete or biased.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Extract truth vectors from multiple token positions and compare layer-wise θ and magnitude patterns with those from the first token. Systematic differences would indicate positional dependence.

2. **Question:** How do directional change (θ) and relative magnitude evolve across all layers when comparing relevant versus random or irrelevant context, rather than only in the final layer?  
   *Basis:* The authors note in the limitations (Section 7) that comparisons between relevant and random context rely on the final layer only, despite having layer-wise data for the main analyses.  
   *Why unresolved:* Middle layers are where semantic and task representations form; it is unknown whether the sensitivity to context relevance is consistent throughout or localized.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Compute θ and relative magnitude differences between relevant and each random context type for every layer across all model-dataset pairs to reveal layer-specific or network-spanning relevance signals.

3. **Question:** To what extent are the reported effects on ConflictQA (particularly strong changes for Counter context) an artifact of dataset contamination during pretraining, versus a genuine signal of knowledge conflict?  
   *Basis:* The authors acknowledge that ConflictQA was constructed for larger parametric knowledge models and that their studied models may have encountered the dataset during pretraining.  
   *Why unresolved:* ConflictQA-Counter and -Parametric are synthetic datasets created using GPT-4. If the models were trained on such pairs, the observed changes might reflect memorization rather than a clean manipulation of conflicting information.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Test on a version of ConflictQA with paraphrased or novel facts not present in common pretraining corpora, or use models with documented training data to control for exposure.

4. **Question:** Are the observed geometric transformations (θ and magnitude) causally linked to changes in model output behavior (e.g., truthfulness, accuracy), or are they merely correlated epiphenomena?  
   *Basis:* The authors state their analysis is correlational, not causal, and note weak correlations between geometric metrics and output probabilities.  
   *Why unresolved:* Without intervention, it remains unknown whether the geometric changes are functionally significant or byproducts of other processing.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Perform activation steering: intervene by adding/subtracting the difference vector (v_c - v_nc) to the residual stream at different layers and measure impact on truth judgments. Systematic output changes aligned with the intervention would indicate causality.

## Limitations
- Exact prompt templates (instruction text and few-shot exemplars) are not fully specified, risking prompt engineering confounds.
- Implementation details for activation extraction (e.g., precise layer indexing, batched processing) are underspecified.
- The generation and length-matching procedure for random contexts (character, word, salad, etc.) lacks code, potentially introducing uncontrolled variance.
