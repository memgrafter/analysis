# SPM-Bench: Benchmarkmarking Large Language Models for Scanning Probe Microscopy

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22971
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22971
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** qwen3.5-plus achieves exact match 0.832 and SIP-F1 0.881 on SPM-Bench.

## Executive Summary
SPM-Bench introduces a PhD-level multimodal benchmark specifically designed for scanning probe microscopy (SPM) to address critical gaps in current large language model (LLM) evaluation, notably data contamination and task complexity. The authors develop an automated pipeline that extracts high-fidelity image-text pairs from recent arXiv and journal papers (2023-2025), carefully curated to avoid overlap with common LLM training corpora. The pipeline employs an Anchor-Gated Sieve (AGS) for selective page filtering, a hybrid cloud-local VLM approach for precise "llbox" cropping, and an adversarial audit process to ensure scientific validity. The resulting dataset comprises 2,703 multi-select question-answer pairs requiring integrated visual and physical reasoning.

Evaluation of state-of-the-art LLMs using Exact Match (EM), Standard Partial Credit (SPC), and the novel Strict Imperfection Penalty F1 (SIP-F1) reveals a wide performance spread, with the best model (qwen3.5-plus) achieving EM 0.832 and SIP-F1 0.881. SIP-F1 further uncovers a bimodal distribution of model "personalities": "Wise" models maintain tight alignment between SPC and SIP-F1 (gap <0.02), while "Aggressive" models exhibit large gaps (>0.04) due to over-selection of options. The benchmark establishes a reproducible paradigm for measuring scientific reasoning integrity in AI systems.

## Method Summary
SPM-Bench is constructed through a fully automated pipeline that transforms scientific literature into a high-quality multimodal QA dataset. The process begins with the Anchor-Gated Sieve (AGS), which heuristically discards text-heavy PDF pages and extracts only pages containing both image blocks and valid figure caption anchors. Each candidate page is rendered to a low-resolution thumbnail and processed by a cloud-based vision-language model (VLM) that returns normalized bounding boxes ("llboxes") around the primary visual panels. A local component then maps these coordinates to the original high-resolution PDF to extract crisp sub-figures. Each crop is paired with its caption and relevant discussion snippets, and fed to a VLM instructed to generate a chain-of-thought, a multiple-choice question, correct options, and a scoring rubric. An "Advisory Model" audits each generated pair across multiple criteria, refining or rejecting low-quality items. The final dataset includes 2,703 pairs spanning various SPM modalities (TEM, STM, SEM, AFM). Model performance is measured by Exact Match (EM), Standard Partial Credit (SPC), and the proposed Strict Imperfection Penalty F1 (SIP-F1), the latter deliberately penalizing hallucinations (false positives) to reflect the high cost of false claims in scientific discovery.

## Key Results
- SOTA LLMs show EM scores ranging from 0.43 to 0.68; qwen3.5-plus leads with EM 0.832 and SIP-F1 0.881.
- SIP-F1 analysis reveals a bimodal distribution: "Wise" models (SPC-SIP gap < 0.02) and "Aggressive" models (gap > 0.04), indicating divergent strategies in option selection.
- The strict penalty successfully differentiates models that hallucinate from those that provide precise, confident answers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Anchor-Gated Sieve (AGS) for Academic Purity
**Claim:** AGS efficiently filters non-figure pages and extracts high-value image-caption pairs from scientific literature, reducing noise and computational cost.

**Mechanism:** Heuristic spatial layout analysis triggers extraction only when a page contains both image blocks and figure caption anchors (e.g., "Figure 1"). A strict regex identifies caption starts, avoiding body-text citations.

**Core assumption:** Figure captions in premier journals are expert-verified and serve as reliable anchors for associated visuals.

**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "employing Anchor-Gated Sieve (AGS) technology, we efficiently extract high-value image-text pairs from arXiv and journal papers"
- [section 2.1] "By heuristically filtering out over 95% of text-heavy noise pages, AGS drastically improves processing efficiency"
- [corpus] Neighboring papers on AI for microscopy (e.g., "Machine Learning-Based Reward-Driven Tuning") validate the need for targeted figure extraction, but do not directly evidence AGS.

**Break condition:** Unconventional figure labeling (e.g., integrated captions, multi-panel mislabeling) causes false negatives or mispairing.

### Mechanism 2: Hybrid Cloud-Local VLM llbox Cropping
**Claim:** The pipeline uses VLMs to return only spatial coordinates ("llbox") for precision cropping, achieving extreme token savings while maintaining high-fidelity visual inputs.

**Mechanism:** Remote VLM processes downsampled thumbnails to output normalized bounding boxes; local engine maps coordinates to original high-res images for cropping. This decouples visual evidence from surrounding text.

**Core assumption:** VLMs can semantically distinguish visual panels from textual annotations/graphics in complex scientific layouts.

**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "hybrid cloud-local architecture where VLMs return only spatial coordinates 'llbox' for local high-fidelity cropping... achieves extreme token savings"
- [section 2.2] "By instructing the model to generate a unified bounding box strictly for visual panels, we achieve a 'clean' decoupling of data from description"
- [corpus] Weak indirect support; neighboring works (e.g., "Unsupervised Reward-Driven Image Segmentation") address segmentation in microscopy but not the specific llbox approach.

**Break condition:** VLM fails on highly abstract schematics or when visual/textual regions overlap semantically (e.g., labels inside plots).

### Mechanism 3: Strict Imperfection Penalty F1 (SIP-F1) for Reasoning Integrity
**Claim:** SIP-F1 penalizes hallucinations (over-selection) more severely than under-selection, revealing model "personalities" (Wise vs. Aggressive) and aligning evaluation with scientific rigor.

**Mechanism:** Introduces a cutoff coefficient λ=0.6 and penalty factor Γ=6. Perfect answers score 1.0; imperfect answers are downscaled by λ and then by an asymmetric F1 (F1-Gamma) that heavily weights precision via Γ.

**Core assumption:** In scientific discovery, false positives (hallucinated options) are more detrimental than incomplete recall; model confidence should correlate with precision.

**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "introduce the Strict Imperfection Penalty F1 (SIP-F1) score... heavily penalizes hallucinations and rewards precision"
- [section 3.3] "the Γ coefficient multiplies its impact on the denominator, causing precision to drop sharply... forcing the model to pursue precise reasoning"
- [corpus] Not directly evidenced; neighboring benchmarks (e.g., "PhysReason") use standard F1, making SIP-F1's design novel but unvalidated externally here.

**Break condition:** If penalty parameters are mis-set (e.g., Γ too low), aggressive strategies may still score competitively, failing to induce the desired bimodal separation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) in Scientific Reasoning**
  - *Why needed here:* SPM-Bench questions require correlating visual features (e.g., lattice distortions) with physical laws (e.g., LDOS). CoT forces the generator to synthesize evidence before forming questions, ensuring internal consistency.
  - *Quick check question:* Why must a CoT step be embedded during Q&A generation for a figure showing a moiré superlattice?

- **Concept: Adversarial Data Curation**
  - *Why needed here:* To avoid "shallow-pattern-matching" questions, an Advisory Model audits each Q&A pair for scientific consistency and distractor plausibility, raising cognitive load.
  - *Quick check question:* How does an adversarial loop between generator and auditor mitigate the risk of synthetically easy questions?

- **Concept: Sensitivity Analysis of Evaluation Metrics**
  - *Why needed here:* SIP-F1's parameters (λ, Γ) must be robust across question complexities to reliably diagnose "personalities." Understanding the tradeoff between penalizing hallucinations and preserving partial credit is key.
  - *Quick check question:* If a model gets TP=2, FP=1 on a 3-option question, how does changing Γ from 4 to 8 affect its SIP-F1 score relative to a conservative TP=1, FP=0?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Pre-filter (AGS) → Cloud VLM Encoder → Local Cropper → Question Generator → Advisory Model → Persistence & Deduplication
- **Critical path:** Paper ingestion → AGS filtering → VLM llbox inference → high-res cropping → generator with CoT/rubrics → advisory audit → final Q&A pair. Blocking step: VLM llbox must precede cropping.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Token efficiency vs. fidelity:* Downsampling for VLM saves tokens but risks missing sub-pixel details; local recovery mitigates this but requires precise coordinate mapping.
  - *Automation vs. purity:* Fully automated pipeline scales but depends on AGS/VLM heuristics; failures introduce noise.
  - *Metric strictness vs. granularity:* SIP-F1's harsh penalty (Γ=6) prioritizes precision but may undervalue partial recall in extremely complex questions.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High delta between SPC and SIP-F1 (>0.04) indicates aggressive over-selection.
  - Low coverage in "Defect/Topology" domains suggests latent knowledge gaps in non-ideal structures.
  - AGS pass rate <5% may indicate non-standard figure layouts or caption formats.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Validate AGS on 100 randomly sampled non-filtered pages: manual check for false negatives (missed figures) and false positives (text pages passed).
  2. Measure VLM llbox accuracy: compare automated llbox coordinates to manually drawn bounding boxes on 50 complex multi-panel figures; compute IoU >0.8 as success threshold.
  3. Tune SIP-F1 parameters: run inference on 100 model outputs with known TP/FP; adjust Γ until aggressive strategies (high FP) consistently score below conservative partials.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Pipeline generalizability to other domains?
- **Basis in paper:** Abstract claims "generalizable paradigm" but only SPM validated.
- **Why unresolved:** Domain-specific figure layouts may need different AGS/VLM tuning; untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply pipeline to another domain (e.g., astronomy) and evaluate benchmark quality via experts and model scores.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Long-term data purity as models ingest newer corpora?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 1 uses 2023-2025 papers for temporal separation but lacks plan for future contamination.
- **Why unresolved:** Future training will include these papers, making benchmark stale and potentially inflating scores.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Monitor model scores over time; check for gains correlated with training data cutoffs; develop synthetic data alternatives.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why is Defect Engineering a universal bottleneck and what skills are missing?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 4.3 reports 10-15% lower defect scores and suggests "higher-order dual-task reasoning" without analysis.
- **Why unresolved:** Without pinpointing deficits (e.g., symmetry-breaking detection), we cannot improve models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Error analysis on defect responses; design probing tasks for subskills; compare with human expert traces.

## Limitations
- Data contamination risk persists despite using 2023–2025 sources; model training cutoffs are unspecified.
- Pipeline robustness (AGS heuristics and VLM cropping errors) is noted but not quantitatively stress-tested.
- SIP-F1 penalty parameters (λ=0.6, Γ=6) are chosen without external calibration; bimodal separation may be sensitive to these choices.

## Confidence
- AGS extraction efficiency: Medium
- llbox cropping fidelity: Medium-Low
- SIP-F1 as a "personality" metric: Low
- Overall benchmark validity for SPM reasoning: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **AGS robustness audit:** Manually inspect 200 randomly sampled filtered-out pages to measure false negative (missed figures) and false positive (non-figure pages passed) rates.
2. **llbox accuracy test:** Obtain expert-drawn bounding boxes for 50 diverse multi-panel SPM figures; compute IoU against automated llbox coordinates (target >0.8).
3. **SIP-F1 parameter sensitivity:** Systematically vary λ (0.5–0.7) and Γ (4–8) on a held-out set; evaluate stability of the SPC-SIP gap distribution and its alignment with human-rated answer precision.
