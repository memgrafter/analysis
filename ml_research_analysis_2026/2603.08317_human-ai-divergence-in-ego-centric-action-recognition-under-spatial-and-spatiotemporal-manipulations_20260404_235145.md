# Human-AI Divergence in Ego-centric Action Recognition under Spatial and Spatiotemporal Manipulations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08317
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08317
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** Humans rely on sparse, critical hand-object interactions, showing sharp performance declines under minimal spatial removal, while AI relies on broader context, sometimes improving under spatial reduction and showing insensitivity to temporal scrambling.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the fundamental differences between human and machine perception in egocentric action recognition. By introducing Minimal Identifiable Recognition Crops (MIRCs) and systematically reducing spatial and spatiotemporal information in videos from EPIC-KITCHENS, the authors reveal divergent recognition strategies. Humans fail abruptly when minimally critical hand-object interactions are removed, demonstrating a reliance on sparse, semantically anchored features. In contrast, a state-of-the-art AI model (Side4Video) degrades gradually, often improves with context pruning, and shows distinct, class-dependent sensitivity to temporal scrambling. The findings highlight a systematic gap in how artificial and biological vision interpret and prioritize visual cues for action understanding.

## Method Summary
The study introduces the Epic-ReduAct dataset derived from EPIC-KITCHENS-100, comprising 36 curated egocentric videos (18 Easy, 18 Hard). For each video, a hierarchical spatial cropping procedure identifies the smallest crop (MIRC) with ≥50% human recognition accuracy; smaller children are sub-MIRCs. Temporal scrambling via 5-block permutation is applied to spatial MIRCs. Over 3,000 human participants provided free-text labels for all clips via an online platform. The AI model, Side4Video (using a frozen OpenCLIP backbone and a trainable side network), was evaluated on all clips. Performance divergence is quantified using two metrics: Recognition Gap (RG), measuring the performance drop from MIRC to sub-MIRC, and Average Reduction Rate (ARR), the mean positive drop across degrading parent-child pairs. Feature analysis uses SAM for object segmentation (Active Hand, Active Object, Contextual Objects) and GBVS for mid-level saliency.

## Key Results
*   Humans exhibit sharp performance declines from MIRC to sub-MIRC (mean RG +37.3% spatial, +24.7% spatiotemporal), indicating reliance on sparse, semantically critical hand-object interactions. AI shows gradual degradation and often improves under spatial reduction (mean RG -3.7% spatial, -3.8% spatiotemporal), relying on contextual and mid-level features.
*   Temporally, humans remain robust to scrambling when spatial cues are preserved; AI displays insensitivity to temporal disruption, with High Temporal Actions (HTA) showing much less improvement from scrambling (~26%) than Low Temporal Actions (LTA) (~60%).
*   Qualitative analysis reveals AI failures stem from systemic collapse of scene structure (loss of context and mid-level features), while human failures are strictly object-focused; AI can recover from failure via context pruning even when the active object is reduced.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Humans rely on sparse, semantically critical hand-object interactions, leading to sharp performance declines when these cues are minimally removed.
- **Mechanism:** Human vision prioritizes high-level semantic features (active hand and manipulated object). Recognition fails abruptly once these core elements are occluded or reduced below a threshold, reflecting a "recognition boundary" defined by MIRCs.
- **Core assumption:** Human action recognition is feature-sparse and semantically anchored; a minimal set of diagnostic features suffices for reliable identification.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Humans exhibit sharp performance declines from MIRC to sub-MIRC (mean RG +37.3% spatial, +24.7% spatiotemporal), indicating reliance on sparse, semantically critical hand-object interactions."
  - [section 4.3.2] "For humans, failure is strictly object-focused. The loss of the Active Object in human failure cases is substantial (∆ = −0.24), nearly double the corresponding loss observed for the AI."
- **Break condition:** If the active hand-object interaction is preserved but context is removed, humans often succeed; if the active hand-object is removed, recognition fails regardless of context.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** AI models depend on distributed contextual and mid-level visual features, causing gradual degradation under spatial reduction but occasional improvement via noise reduction (context pruning).
- **Mechanism:** The model uses a broad set of features (contextual objects, mid-level statistics like flicker, colour, motion). When spatial cropping removes irrelevant background ("noise"), performance can improve by reducing interference. Failure occurs when the global scene structure collapses.
- **Core assumption:** AI models are sensitive to global image statistics and may overfit to contextual patterns; they can benefit from the removal of extraneous information.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "AI shows gradual degradation and often improves under spatial reduction (mean RG -3.7% spatial, -3.8% spatiotemporal), relying on contextual and mid-level features."
  - [section 4.3.2] "Failure emerges when spatial reduction induces a synchronised collapse of the entire scene structure, wherein environmental context, lighting, motion, and the primary object degrade simultaneously." and "recovery represents an act of surgical pruning...provided that the Contextual Objects remain at 34%."
- **Break condition:** If both contextual objects and mid-level features are degraded, AI fails. Recovery is possible if context is preserved even when active hand-object is largely removed.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Humans are robust to temporal scrambling when spatial cues are preserved, while AI models are insensitive to temporal disruption and exhibit class-dependent behavior (High vs. Low Temporal Actions).
- **Mechanism:** Humans can infer actions from fragmented motion if the static spatial configuration (hand-object) is informative. AI models rely on short-range motion and static context, hence show limited sensitivity to frame order and benefit from scrambling on Low Temporal Actions (LTA) where static context suffices.
- **Core assumption:** Human action recognition is semantically driven and can compensate for missing temporal structure with spatial information; AI models are statistical and rely on temporal patterns or static context without true temporal reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Temporally, humans remain robust to scrambling when spatial cues are preserved, while AI displays insensitivity to temporal disruption, with distinct behaviors for High Temporal Actions (HTA) vs Low Temporal Actions (LTA)."
  - [section 4.4.2] "HTA (≈26%) yields substantially less improvement over scrambling for the AI model compared to LTA (≈60%)." and "the AI treats LTA and HTA classes as fundamentally distinct recognition problems, largely invariant to temporal coherence."
- **Break condition:** For HTA, scrambling causes a performance drop for both humans and AI, but humans more so. For LTA, AI may improve because static context is sufficient; humans show moderate robustness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Egocentric Action Recognition
  - *Why needed:* The paper studies first-person videos, which differ from third-person views in having limited global context and a focus on hand-object interactions. This affects what features are informative.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the egocentric perspective change the spatial and temporal cues available for action recognition compared to a third-person view?

- **Concept:** Minimal Identifiable Recognition Crops (MIRCs)
  - *Why needed:* MIRCs are the core experimental manipulation: the smallest spatial or spatiotemporal region that humans can reliably recognize. They define the boundary where recognition fails.
  - *Quick check question:* How are MIRCs and sub-MIRCs identified, and what does the sharp human drop from MIRC to sub-MIRC imply about feature use?

- **Concept:** Ventral and Dorsal Visual Streams
  - *Why needed:* The paper frames human vision in terms of two parallel pathways (ventral "what" for object identity, dorsal "where/how" for spatial and motion processing). This biological inspiration contrasts with AI architectures.
  - *Quick check question:* What are the functional differences between the ventral and dorsal streams, and how might they relate to the paper's finding that humans rely on hand-object interactions (ventral) while AI uses context (dorsal-like)?

- **Concept:** Hierarchical Visual Processing
  - *Why needed:* Both human vision and AI models (CNNs, ViTs) build representations hierarchically. The paper examines how these hierarchies behave under degradation.
  - *Quick check question:* How does hierarchical processing in deep networks compare to that in the visual cortex, and why might this lead to different robustness patterns?

- **Concept:** Spatial vs. Temporal Features in Action Recognition
  - *Why needed:* The paper systematically manipulates spatial and temporal information separately to understand their relative importance and how humans and AI trade them off.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might spatial information be more critical than temporal structure for humans in egocentric actions, and how do current video models weight these cues?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Epic-ReduAct Dataset -> Human Evaluation (Gorilla) & AI Evaluation (Side4Video) -> Metrics (RG, ARR) & Feature Analysis (SAM, GBVS) -> Mechanism Analysis
- **Critical path:**
  1. **Data preparation:** Select Easy/Hard videos from EPIC-KITCHENS-100 using an AI classifier (MOFO) and manual curation.
  2. **MIRC identification:** Recursively crop each video into quadrants; test human recognition to find the smallest quadrant with ≥50% accuracy (MIRC). Children with <50% are sub-MIRCs.
  3. **Temporal scrambling:** For each spatial MIRC, create temporally scrambled (5-block shuffle) sub-MIRCs and test human recognition.
  4. **Human evaluation:** Run online experiments for all quadrants (spatial and spatiotemporal) with ~3,000 participants; clean responses and compute accuracy per clip.
  5. **AI evaluation:** Run Side4Video on all quadrants; record verb confidence.
  6. **Metric computation:** Calculate RG and ARR for each class and overall.
  7. **Qualitative analysis:** For spatial reductions, track feature preservation across transitions where AI predictions flip (correct↔incorrect). Compute correlations to identify failure/recovery patterns.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Human vs. AI scale:** Human experiments are costly, limiting base videos to 36. This may reduce generalizability but allows dense quadrant testing.
  - **MIRC pruning:** Heuristics (overlap-based) reduce experimental space but risk missing informative quadrants.
  - **Model choice:** Single S4V model used; other architectures (e.g., MOFO, video LLMs) may show different patterns.
  - **Feature proxies:** SAM and GBVS are off-the-shelf; they may not perfectly capture human or AI internal representations.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Human:** Abrupt drop from MIRC to sub-MIRC; correlated with loss of Active Hand/Object. Failures are object-focused.
  - **AI:** Gradual degradation but also recovery in some sub-MIRCs. Failures correlate with systemic collapse (loss of context + mid-level features). Recoveries correlated with context preservation even when active object is reduced.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Spatial reduction:** Compare human and AI accuracy across hierarchical crops. Expected: humans show sharp decline at MIRC→sub-MIRC; AI degrades gradually or improves.
  2. **Temporal scrambling:** Compare on spatiotemporal sub-MIRCs. Expected: humans more robust when spatial cues preserved; AI insensitive to scrambling, with HTA vs. LTA differences.
  3. **Feature transition analysis:** For spatial reductions, compute feature preservation (objects, GBVS) on AI prediction flips. Expected: AI failures tied to context collapse; recoveries tied to context pruning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can AI models be explicitly guided to rely on human-critical spatial cues like active hand-object interactions rather than contextual and mid-level features?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The authors suggest incorporating active objects or contextual objects as auxiliary supervisory signals to shift model reliance toward more semantic, human-aligned features, but this remains unimplemented (Section 5.1).
  - *Why unresolved:* No experiments test whether such fine-tuning changes model behaviour on MIRC/sub-MIRC transitions or alters failure modes like systemic collapse.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Train Side4Video with an auxiliary loss for active object segmentation or attention supervision; evaluate changes in recognition gaps, feature correlations, and recovery patterns on Epic-ReduAct.

- **Open Question 2:** Can MIRC-based spatial and spatiotemporal manipulations be integrated into the training process to produce models that robustly recognize actions from minimal cues?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The future work proposes using the MIRC framework as a training signal rather than only an evaluation tool (Section 5.3).
  - *Why unresolved:* The current study only assesses pre-trained models; it is unknown whether models can learn to maintain accuracy across progressive reductions.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Introduce a curriculum or loss that penalizes large performance drops between MIRC and sub-MIRC during training; test if resulting models show sharper recognition boundaries and more human-like feature usage on unseen reductions.

- **Open Question 3:** Do the human-AI divergences observed in egocentric kitchen actions generalize to other egocentric domains and modern video architectures (e.g., video transformers, multimodal models)?
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The limitations note the study is confined to a single model (Side4Video) and one domain (EPIC-KITCHENS) (Section 4.5 and 5.3), raising questions about broader applicability.
  - *Why unresolved:* Different architectures and visual contexts may change how spatial and temporal information is prioritized, potentially altering the identified patterns.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Replicate the MIRC/spatiotemporal pipeline on diverse egocentric datasets (e.g., Ego4D) with multiple SOTA video models, comparing recognition gaps, average reduction rates, and qualitative feature analyses across settings.

## Limitations
- **Epic-ReduAct exact composition unknown:** The 36 base video IDs are not listed, preventing exact dataset reconstruction and limiting reproducibility.
- **MIRC selection heuristics underspecified:** The quadrant pruning algorithm (overlap thresholds, child selection criteria) lacks implementation details, risking variability in the MIRC/sub-MIRC boundary.
- **Human-AI calibration ambiguity:** The class-specific confidence threshold for AI (matching human MIRC accuracy) and the exact similarity threshold for human responses are not fully specified.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Human sparsity):** High. Anchored in sharp, consistent performance drops and strong object-focused failure analysis.
- **Mechanism 2 (AI context reliance & pruning):** Medium. Evidence is model-specific (Side4Video) and lacks corpus support; generalizability is uncertain.
- **Mechanism 3 (Temporal HTA/LTA dichotomy):** Medium. The dichotomy is derived from the paper's data; the corpus shows no direct prior work on scrambling effects, raising dataset-specificity concerns.
