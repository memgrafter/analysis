# Characterizing the Predictive Impact of Modalities with Supervised Latent-Variable Modeling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16979  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16979  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** PRIMO matches unimodal accuracy when a modality is missing and multimodal accuracy when all modalities are present, while quantifying instance-level modality impact via a variance-based metric.

## Executive Summary
PRIMO addresses a key challenge in multimodal learning: incomplete data where modalities may be missing during training or inference. Unlike prior work focused on imputation, PRIMO treats missing modalities as latent variables and learns to marginalize over plausible completions. This enables accurate predictions regardless of modality availability and provides a new instance-level metric (V) that quantifies how much a missing modality influences the prediction. Evaluated on synthetic, audio‑vision, and healthcare tasks, PRIMO achieves competitive performance and yields interpretable insights—for example, revealing that time‑series data is critical for predicting respiratory disease but irrelevant for neoplasms in MIMIC‑III.

## Method Summary
PRIMO is a supervised latent-variable model based on conditional variational autoencoders. It introduces a continuous latent variable *z* to capture label‑relevant information from a missing modality *x_m*. The predictive distribution *p(y|x_o)* for observed modalities *x_o* is obtained by integrating *p(y|x_o,z)* over *z ~ p(z|x_o)*. The model is trained jointly on complete examples (using *p(z|x_o,x_m)*) and partial examples (using *p(z|x_o)*), with a regularizer that ties the two priors to prevent drift. At inference, multiple *z* samples are drawn from the appropriate prior; predictions are averaged, and the variance across samples (total variation distance from the mean) defines the impact metric *V*.

## Key Results
- PRIMO attains accuracy comparable to unimodal baselines when a modality is missing and to multimodal baselines when all are present, across synthetic XOR, Audio‑Vision MNIST, and MIMIC‑III tasks.
- The *V* metric correctly identifies known modality dependencies (e.g., missing audio harms predictions more for certain digits in AV‑MNIST).
- On MIMIC‑III, *V* reveals task‑specific modality importance: high for time‑series in respiratory disease prediction, low for neoplasms.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Representing the missing modality as a latent variable *z* enables marginalization over plausible completions, preserving predictive uncertainty without requiring a single imputed value.  
**Mechanism:** PRIMO defines *p(y|x_o) = ∫ p(y|x_o,z) p(z|x_o) dz*. During training, it learns *p(z|x_o)* and *p(z|x_o,x_m)* via conditional VAEs.  
**Core assumption:** All label‑relevant information from *x_m* is captured by *z*, i.e., *y ⊥ x_m | (x_o, z)*.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "PRIMO models the missing modality through a latent variable that captures its relationship with the observed modality in the context of prediction."  
- [section 2.1] Equations (1) formalize the marginalization.  
**Break condition:** If the latent space is too small or the prior mis‑specified, marginalization may not cover the true distribution, biasing predictions.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Joint training on complete and partial examples with tied priors aligns the latent space, enabling a single network to handle varying modality availability.  
**Mechanism:** Total loss combines two ELBOs (for complete and missing cases) and a regularizer *R* that anchors *p(z|x_o)* to *N(0,I)* and ties *p(z|x_o,x_m)* to *p(z|x_o)* for the same *x_o*.  
**Core assumption:** The same *x_o* should yield similar prior distributions whether *x_m* is observed or not.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [section 2.1] "We jointly maximize both ELBOs... We break this symmetry by anchoring *p_ω(z | x_o)* to *N(0, I)* and tying *p_ω(z | x_o, x_m)* to *p_ω(z | x_o)* for the same *x_o* using a regularizer *R*."  
- [abstract] "PRIMO enables the use of all available training examples, whether modalities are complete or partial."  
**Break condition:** Omitting *R* allows priors to drift arbitrarily, causing inference inconsistencies.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The variance‑based metric *V* (expected total variation distance between per‑sample predictions and their mean) quantifies instance‑level predictive impact of the missing modality.  
**Mechanism:** Given *x_o*, sample *K* latent vectors *z_k ~ p(z|x_o)*, compute predictions *p_k = p(y|x_o,z_k)*, then *V = (1/K) Σ TVD(p_k, p̄)* where *p̄* is the average. Larger *V* means predictions vary more across completions, implying the missing modality matters.  
**Core assumption:** TVD is a suitable measure of distributional change, and Monte Carlo estimation with sufficient *K* approximates the expectation.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "PRIMO quantifies the predictive impact of a modality at the instance level using a variance‑based metric..."  
- [section 2.2] Equation (9) defines *V* explicitly.  
**Break condition:** Small *K* yields high‑variance, unreliable *V* estimates.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Variational Inference**  
*Why needed:* PRIMO approximates intractable posteriors over latent *z* via the ELBO, providing a tractable training objective.  
*Quick check:* "What are the two components of the ELBO, and how do they balance reconstruction and regularization?"

**Concept: Conditional Independence in Graphical Models**  
*Why needed:* The assumption *y ⊥ x_m | (x_o, z)* justifies the factorization *p(y|x_o,z)* and simplifies the directed acyclic graph.

**Concept: Monte Carlo Marginalization**  
*Why needed:* At inference, PRIMO approximates *∫ p(y|x_o,z) p(z|x_o) dz* by averaging over *z* samples; this is key for both prediction and computing *V*.  
*Quick check:* "How does increasing the number of Monte Carlo samples affect the bias and variance of the estimated predictive distribution *p(y|x_o)*?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Modality encoders (separate networks per modality) → Fusion layer (concatenates available representations; zeros/mask for missing) → Prior networks *p_ω(z|·)* (two variants: *p_ω(z|x_o)* and *p_ω(z|x_o,x_m)*) and Posterior networks *q_ϕ(z|·)* (two variants: *q_ϕ(z|x_o,y)* and *q_ϕ(z|x_o,x_m,y)*) → Reparameterization to sample *z* → Classifier *p_θ(y|x_o,z)*. Regularizer *R* ties the priors.

**Critical path (training):**  
1. Forward pass: encode modalities; zero out missing.  
2. Sample *z* from posterior *q_ϕ* via reparameterization.  
3. Compute classifier prediction *p_θ(y|x_o,z)*.  
4. Compute ELBO loss: negative log‑likelihood + KL(*q_ϕ||p_ω*).  
5. Add regularizer *R* to anchor priors.  
6. Backpropagate and update all parameters.

**Critical path (inference):**  
1. Encode available modalities; zero missing.  
2. Draw *K* samples *z_k* from appropriate prior (*p_ω(z|x_o)* if missing, *p_ω(z|x_o,x_m)* if complete).  
3. For each *z_k*, compute *p_k = p_θ(y|x_o,z_k)*.  
4. Final prediction: *p̄ = (1/K) Σ p_k*.  
5. Impact metric: *V = (1/K) Σ TVD(p_k, p̄)*.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Diagonal Gaussian prior/posterior is efficient but may be inflexible; normalizing flows could help but risk instability.  
- Shared amortized networks enable fast inference but may not perfectly adapt to every conditioning; separate networks increase capacity but also parameters.  
- Regularizer *R* is essential to prevent prior drift and posterior collapse, but its implicit weight requires tuning.  
- Monte Carlo sampling yields uncertainty but slows inference; deterministic approximations could trade off *V* fidelity for speed.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Posterior collapse (KL term → 0) indicating *z* ignores data; mitigated by batch norm on posterior mean but not foolproof.  
- High variance in *V* across runs suggests unstable training or insufficient *K*.  
- Poor missing‑modality accuracy below unimodal baseline implies latent *z* fails to capture predictive signal.  
- Drifting priors (without *R*) cause inconsistency when modality availability changes.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Synthetic XOR:** Validates that *V* correctly reflects when the missing modality is needed (x_o<0) vs. not needed (x_o>0), and checks prediction accuracy in both settings.  
2. **Audio‑Vision MNIST:** Tests on multimodal image/audio; compares accuracy against unimodal/multimodal baselines and shows *V* distributions differ when missing audio vs. vision.  
3. **MIMIC‑III:** Real‑world healthcare data for mortality and ICD‑9 prediction; demonstrates task‑dependent modality importance (e.g., time‑series critical for respiratory disease, low for neoplasm).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**  
*Question:* How can we obtain ground truth to validate instance‑level modality impact estimates (*V*) in real‑world settings where labels for missing modalities are unavailable?  
*Basis in paper:* [explicit] "In practical multimodal settings, we often do not have access to certain modalities at inference time, and in many applications labels may also be missing. This makes it challenging to evaluate whether instance‑level modality impact estimates are correct."  
*Why unresolved:* No existing methodology provides a reliable way to verify if *V* correctly identifies truly informative instances without paired complete data or expert annotation.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Validation protocols on synthetic datasets with known modality importance, or incorporation of clinician feedback in healthcare to assess alignment with human judgment.

**Open Question 2**  
*Question:* How can we design and curate benchmarks with three or more modalities and heterogeneous missingness patterns to better evaluate models like PRIMO?  
*Basis in paper:* [explicit] "Current standard benchmarks focus primarily on audio, vision, and text. This limits evaluation in settings with sensory data, tabular data, and multiple imaging modalities."  
*Why unresolved:* Most public multimodal datasets have only two modalities and simplistic random missingness, not reflecting complex real‑world scenarios.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Release of multi‑modal datasets (e.g., combining imaging, text, time‑series, genomics) with realistic structured missingness and standardized tasks.

**Open Question 3**  
*Question:* Can the latent‑variable sampling inference in PRIMO be made more efficient while preserving the quality of predictive distribution estimates and variance computation?  
*Basis in paper:* [inferred] PRIMO uses 500–2000 Monte Carlo samples during inference, which may be prohibitive for large‑scale or real‑time applications.  
*Why unresolved:* The paper does not explore alternatives (e.g., closed‑form approximations, importance sampling, amortized inference) that could reduce overhead.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Empirical study showing a more efficient method (e.g., uncertainty quantification heads) yields similar *V* and accuracy compared to extensive sampling.

**Open Question 4**  
*Question:* To what extent does the conditional independence assumption (*y ⊥ x_m | x_o, z*) limit PRIMO's ability to capture interactions between observed and missing modalities?  
*Basis in paper:* [inferred] The graphical model assumes *z* captures all label‑relevant information from *x_m*; direct dependencies may exist.  
*Why unresolved:* No ablation compares against models allowing direct *x_m → y* edges; in domains like healthcare, modality interactions may be critical.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Extending PRIMO to model residual dependencies and evaluating on synthetic datasets with engineered interactions.

**Open Question 5**  
*Question:* Is total variation distance (TVD) the optimal metric for quantifying predictive impact, or could alternative divergences (e.g., KL, Wasserstein) provide more nuanced scores?  
*Basis in paper:* [inferred] PRIMO uses TVD intuitively but does not compare against other divergences or assess correlation with human judgment or task utility.  
*Why unresolved:* TVD’s properties (symmetry, boundedness) may not suit all scenarios; other measures might capture tail sensitivity or directionality.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic comparison of *V* computed with different divergences on synthetic tasks with known true impact, and correlation analysis with decision‑quality metrics.

## Limitations
- The latent representation *z* may fail to capture complex cross‑modal interactions if too compact or if the conditional independence assumption is violated.  
- *V* metric relies on Monte Carlo sampling; stability across runs and sensitivity to sample count *K* are not thoroughly analyzed.  
- Inference cost (500–2000 samples) may hinder real‑time deployment; no efficient alternatives are explored.  
- Evaluation is limited to three datasets; broader benchmarks (especially high‑dimensional modalities) are needed to support generalizability claims.  
- Posterior collapse remains a risk despite heuristic mitigations (batch norm), especially with more powerful decoders.

## Confidence
- **Latent marginalization (Mechanism 1):** Medium. Supported by synthetic and real‑task results, but diagonal Gaussian prior may be restrictive and theoretical guarantees are lacking.  
- **Joint training with tied priors (Mechanism 2):** Medium‑High. Ablations show the regularizer prevents drift and joint training yields high accuracy in both full and missing settings; sensitivity to regularizer weight is not fully mapped.  
- **V metric for predictive impact (Mechanism 3):** Medium. *V* correlates with known importance on XOR and yields plausible patterns on MIMIC‑III, but real‑world validation is indirect and relies on a single distance measure.  
- **Overall performance:** High. PRIMO matches unimodal and multimodal baselines across all three benchmarks with appropriate comparisons.

## Next Checks
1. **Evaluate on diverse modalities and missingness patterns:** Test PRIMO on additional multimodal datasets (e.g., video+audio, text+image) with random and structured missingness to probe robustness and scalability.  
2. **Quantify stability of *V*:** Run multiple independent inference trials (different seeds) to measure variance of *V* estimates; explore how sample count *K* affects reliability and recommend robust thresholds.  
3. **Systematic hyperparameter sensitivity:** Vary latent dimensionality and regularizer weight (e.g., β) to map their impact on accuracy, *V* consistency, and posterior collapse; produce practical tuning guidelines for practitioners.
