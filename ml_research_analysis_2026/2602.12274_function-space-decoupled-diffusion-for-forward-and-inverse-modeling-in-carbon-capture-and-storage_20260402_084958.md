# Function-Space Decoupled Diffusion for Forward and Inverse Modeling in Carbon Capture and Storage
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12274
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12274
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves 7.7% forward error with only 25% permeability observations (11× improvement over standard surrogates) and generates physically consistent inverse solutions with 4× higher sample efficiency than rejection sampling.

## Executive Summary
Carbon capture and storage (CCS) depends on accurate subsurface flow modeling to monitor CO₂ plume migration, but inverse problems—recovering geological properties from sparse dynamic data—are ill-posed and computationally demanding. This paper presents Fun-DDPS, a decoupled framework that combines a function-space diffusion prior over permeability fields with a differentiable neural operator surrogate for forward physics. The diffusion model learns a robust prior solely from static permeability data, while the surrogate (a Local Neural Operator) is independently trained to map permeability to saturation. At inference, the diffusion generates candidate permeability realizations, and gradient guidance from the surrogate steers them to match sparse dynamic observations. This separation avoids the guidance attenuation failure of joint-state diffusion models and enables high-fidelity reconstruction even with extremely sparse data.

Experiments on a synthetic 2D radial CCS scenario show state-of-the-art performance: forward prediction error of 7.7% with only 25% permeability coverage (an 11× improvement over a deterministic surrogate’s 86.9%), and inverse posterior samples that match rejection sampling ground truth (Jensen-Shannon divergence <0.06) while using 75% fewer samples. Fun-DDPS consistently produces geologically plausible, artifact-free realizations, demonstrating the power of decoupled diffusive sampling for subsurface modeling.

## Method Summary
Fun-DDPS employs a two-phase training pipeline followed by decoupled sampling. First, a single-channel function-space diffusion model (U-NO backbone) is trained unconditionally on a dataset of permeability fields to learn a prior distribution p(m). The forward process adds spatially correlated Gaussian noise (via a Matérn covariance kernel) to preserve geological continuity. Second, a Local Neural Operator (LNO) surrogate is trained on paired (permeability, saturation) data to approximate the PDE-based forward model F(m) ≈ s. At inference, for a given set of dynamic observations y_dyn (sparse saturation measurements), the framework performs diffusion posterior sampling (DPS). Starting from a noise sample, at each reverse step the diffusion model estimates a denoised permeability m̂0. The surrogate then predicts the corresponding saturation ŝ = Lϕ(m̂0). The gradient of the data misfit ||M_dyn ⊗ (ŝ - y_dyn)||² with respect to m̂0 is computed and used to guide the diffusion state, effectively conditioning the prior on the sparse observations. For forward modeling (predicting saturation from partial permeability), the guidance is applied directly on the observed permeability values. This decoupled design allows each component to specialize: the diffusion prior captures complex permeability statistics without being constrained by forward physics, while the surrogate provides accurate, differentiable gradients that propagate sparse constraints throughout the field.

## Key Results
- Forward robustness: With only 25% permeability coverage, Fun-DDPS attains 7.7% relative L2 error on saturation prediction, an 11× improvement over the deterministic surrogate (86.9%).
- Inverse accuracy: Posterior samples from Fun-DDPS achieve Jensen-Shannon divergence <0.06 against rejection sampling ground truth, matching the joint-state baseline but without high-frequency artifacts.
- Sample efficiency: Fun-DDPS requires 4× fewer samples than rejection sampling to approximate the true posterior, while maintaining physical consistency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Decoupled Training Architecture
Joint-state diffusion models suffer from guidance attenuation when observations are sparse because the likelihood gradient becomes ineffective in low-density regions of the joint (m,s) space. Fun-DDPS circumvents this by training the diffusion prior exclusively on permeability fields, thereby learning an expressive and well-defined prior p(m). The forward physics is handled separately by a pre-trained neural operator Lϕ. During inference, the gradient ∇m ||Lϕ(m) - y_dyn||² is computed explicitly and fed into the diffusion process. This explicit gradient, derived from a differentiable surrogate, remains informative even when observations are sparse, because Lϕ’s global receptive field propagates information from observed points to the entire permeability field. The decoupling thus isolates the prior learning from the physics, preventing the diffusion model from being biased toward regions of the joint distribution that are rarely visited under sparse data.

### Mechanism 2: Surrogate-Gradient Propagation for Sparse Data
The Local Neural Operator surrogate serves as a high-fidelity, differentiable proxy for the forward PDE solver. Its architecture combines global Fourier layers with local DISCO paths, enabling it to capture long-range dependencies while avoiding ringing artifacts. When used to compute the guidance gradient, the surrogate effectively translates sparse constraints in the saturation space into dense updates in the permeability space. The Jacobian ∂Lϕ/∂m acts as a conditioning operator that spreads the influence of observed saturation values across all permeability locations. This is particularly crucial when observations cover less than 1% of the domain, where direct inversion would be underdetermined. The surrogate’s accuracy (low relative error on test pairs) ensures that the gradient points in a direction that improves data consistency without violating physical plausibility.

### Mechanism 3: Function-Space Diffusion Prior for Reconstruction
The diffusion prior operates in function space, adding noise according to a Gaussian Random Field with a Matérn covariance kernel. This forward process respects the spatial continuity inherent to geological media, so the denoising network learns to reverse perturbations while maintaining coherent structures. When conditioned via gradient guidance on a few observed points, the diffusion process fills in missing permeability details by sampling from this learned, spatially continuous prior. As a result, even with only 25% of the permeability field observed, the model can reconstruct the full field with low error (7.7% relative L2). The prior’s quality is validated by comparing unconditional samples’ variograms and marginal distributions to the training data, which show strong alignment.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  Geomodel Diffusion Prior (Dθ) -> Forward Surrogate (Lϕ) -> Guidance Module -> Noise Scheduler
  (During inference, Dθ generates candidate m, Lϕ predicts s, Guidance computes ∇m loss, Scheduler updates diffusion state.)
- **Critical path**:
  1. Train Lϕ on 12k (m,s) pairs to high accuracy.
  2. Train Dθ unconditionally on permeability fields only.
  3. For given sparse y_dyn, run DPS: initialize m_σ, iterate: compute m̂0 = Dθ(m_σ, σ), compute ŝ = Lϕ(m̂0), compute gradient g = ∇m ||M_dyn ⊗ (ŝ - y_dyn)||², update m_σ ← m_σ - ζ_dyn * g, apply scheduler.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Decoupled (Fun-DDPS): Requires two models but avoids joint-state artifacts, robust to data scarcity, and allows specialized architectures. Higher storage/training cost.
  - Joint-state (Fun-DPS): Single model, simpler training, but prone to guidance attenuation and high-frequency noise under sparse observations.
  - Surrogate error tolerance: Fun-DDPS is somewhat robust to minor Lϕ errors due to prior regularization, but large errors bias posteriors.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Inverse samples show high-frequency, non-physical noise → joint-state model guidance failure.
  - Forward relative L2 error >30% at 25% coverage → weak prior or insufficient guidance weight.
  - Low acceptance rate in rejection sampling benchmark or JS divergence >0.1 → posterior distribution mismatch, possibly due to poor Lϕ or ζ_dyn.
  - Lϕ validation error >15% on held-out pairs → surrogate too inaccurate for guidance.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Forward robustness test: Evaluate Fun-DDPS with geomodel masks at 100%, 50%, 25% coverage; expect <10% error at 25% vs >80% for deterministic Lϕ.
  2. Inverse validation: Generate 1k+ posterior samples for extreme sparsity (<1% coverage); compute Jensen-Shannon divergence of hyperparameters against 26k-sample rejection sampling reference; expect JS <0.06.
  3. Prior quality assessment: Generate 1k unconditional m samples; compute variograms and CDFs; compare against test set statistics; expect strong overlap (P5-P95 bands).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to spatiotemporal trajectories**: The current method handles only single-time snapshots, while real CCS monitoring involves continuous time-series data. Extending Fun-DDPS to process full trajectories would enhance operational applicability.
2. **Impact of surrogate approximation errors**: The framework relies entirely on the pre-trained surrogate’s gradients; however, the paper does not quantify how surrogate inaccuracies propagate to posterior bias or artifacts. Systematic error sensitivity analysis is needed.
3. **Generalization to complex real-world geology**: Training data use simplified SGSIM permeability fields and single-phase flow. Performance on reservoirs with faults, fractures, multi-phase effects, and field-scale heterogeneity remains an open question.
4. **Theoretical understanding of artifact suppression**: Why the decoupled architecture consistently avoids high-frequency noise in posterior samples is not formally explained. A spectral or regularization analysis could illuminate the mechanism and guide future designs.

## Limitations
- Key hyperparameters (noise schedule, sampler details) are not disclosed, making exact numerical replication difficult.
- Reimplementation of the Local Neural Operator and U-NO architectures is nontrivial due to missing implementation specifics (e.g., spectral factorization, DISCO kernels), potentially introducing variability.
- The method’s generalization to real CCS reservoirs with complex multi-scale heterogeneity and multi-phase flow is unvalidated; current experiments rely on synthetic 2D radial models.
