# A Data-Driven Predictive Framework for Inventory Optimization Using Context-Augmented Machine Learning Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05033
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05033
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** Incorporating external context (weekday, holiday, sales deviation flag) reduced XGBoost's MAE by 51% to 22.7, with the sales deviation flag as the most influential feature.

## Executive Summary
This study tackles inaccurate demand forecasting in supply chains by integrating external contextual factors—weekday patterns, public holidays, and machine outage indicators—into machine learning models. Using a large-scale vending machine sales dataset, the authors compare four models (XGBoost, ARIMAX, Prophet, SVR) with and without these features. The results show that context-augmentation dramatically improves accuracy, especially for XGBoost, which achieves a 51% MAE reduction. The sales deviation flag—a binary indicator triggered when sales drop below 30% of a rolling average—proves exceptionally valuable, explaining 89% of outlier events and yielding a 37% MAE reduction on its own. The work highlights the superiority of tree-based models for capturing complex, heterogeneous interactions between historical demand and external variables, while also noting that holiday effects vary significantly by location type.

## Method Summary
The study analyzes daily transactional data from 1,500 vending machines (Sayyad et al., 2024), spanning 2013–2017. The preprocessing pipeline handles missing values via linear interpolation and forward-filling, one-hot encodes store and item IDs, and engineers three key contextual features: cyclical encoding of weekday (sine/cosine transforms), a binary public holiday flag (derived from India's holidays library), and a sales deviation flag (set to 1 when daily sales fall below 30% of a rolling average). Models are trained on a chronological split (January 2013–July 2017 for training, August–December 2017 for testing) and evaluated using MAE, RMSE, and R². Four models are implemented with fixed hyperparameters: XGBoost (n_estimators=100, learning_rate=0.1, reg_lambda=0.1), ARIMAX (p=1, d=0, q=0), Prophet (multiplicative seasonality, 25 changepoints), and SVR (RBF kernel, C=1.0, ε=0.1). The experiment compares two scenarios: (1) using only historical lag features and (2) adding the three external context features.

## Key Results
- XGBoost’s MAE dropped from 46.13 (baseline) to 22.7 with external features, a 51% reduction.
- The sales deviation flag alone reduced XGBoost’s MAE by 37% (42.15 → 26.6) and was ranked the most important feature, explaining 89% of outlier events.
- Other models (ARIMAX, Prophet, SVR) improved less dramatically, underscoring XGBoost’s strength in modeling complex context interactions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Sales Deviation Flag
- **Claim:** Including a binary sales deviation flag improves model accuracy by helping distinguish between true demand shifts and machine outage anomalies.
- **Mechanism:** The flag is triggered when daily sales fall below 30% of a rolling average. This explicitly labels data points likely caused by operational failures (e.g., vending machine breakdown) rather than genuine low demand. Models can then learn to ignore or correct for these non-demand-related drops, preventing them from distorting learned patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Unrecorded operational disruptions create demand "black swans" that degrade forecasts if treated as true demand signal.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] Explicitly lists "sales deviation indicators" as a key context-augmenting feature.
  - [section 3.2, Page 11] Reports a 37% MAE reduction for XGBoost (42.15 → 26.6) specifically from the deviation flag, and states it "helped models distinguish between true demand shifts and machine outages - explaining 89% of outlier events."
  - [corpus] Neighbor corpus shows limited direct evidence; related papers focus on general context integration (e.g., weather, holidays) but not specific anomaly flags. *Assumption: The mechanism is inferred from the paper's controlled experiment and feature importance analysis.*
- **Break condition:** If operational disruption data (e.g., maintenance logs) is available and integrated directly, the inferred "deviation flag" may become redundant or less precise.

### Mechanism 2: Weekday Cyclical Encoding
- **Claim:** Encoding weekday cyclical patterns (via sine/cosine transforms) captures recurring weekly demand cycles (e.g., weekday vs. weekend), reducing error for periodicity-sensitive models.
- **Mechanism:** Transforming integer weekdays (0-6) into two continuous features (sin, cos) preserves the circular nature of time (where Monday follows Sunday). This allows linear models (like ARIMAX) and tree-based models to better learn the periodic relationship without arbitrary ordinal jumps.
- **Core assumption:** Demand exhibits a stable, weekly repeating pattern that is not fully captured by lag features alone.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.3, Page 4] Describes the feature engineering: "Cyclical variables (e.g., month, weekday) were encoded using sine and cosine transformations to preserve periodicity."
  - [section 3.2, Page 11] Quantifies the lift: XGBoost saw a 15% MAE reduction (46.13 → 39.2) from weekday features, and notes its value for "office-located machines" with weekday/weekend volume ratios.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Data-driven jet fuel demand forecasting" and "Optimizing Sales Forecasts through Automated Integration of Market Indicators" support the general principle of cyclical encoding for periodicity, but do not validate this specific implementation.
- **Break condition:** If demand cycles are irregular (e.g., disrupted by irregular shift patterns) or the forecast horizon extends beyond weekly cycles without monthly/quarterly features.

### Mechanism 3: Public Holiday Indicators
- **Claim:** Public holiday indicators improve forecasts by capturing systematic, location-specific demand shocks that are not aligned with standard weekly cycles.
- **Mechanism:** A binary holiday flag injects knowledge of calendar anomalies. Models can then learn separate demand distributions for "holiday" vs. "non-holiday" states. The paper notes this effect is highly location-dependent (airport machines up, corporate park machines down), so the mechanism's efficacy depends on the homogeneity of the training set's location types.
- **Core assumption:** Public holidays cause statistically significant, consistent shifts in demand that are not autocorrelated from prior days.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Lists "public holiday" as one of the three key external variables.
  - [section 3.2, Page 11-12] Shows mixed results: Prophet was resilient (1.8% MAE change), while XGBoost's MAE increased by 7.5%. It explains the variance: "Holiday impacts varied dramatically by location type."
  - [corpus] Strong support from neighbors like Vollmer et al. (2021) [ref 11] and Dai & Huang (2021) [ref 19] advocating exogenous holiday variables for retail/logistics demand.
- **Break condition:** If the training data merges locations with opposing holiday response profiles (e.g., airports and residential areas) without a location-type interaction term, the single holiday flag may introduce noise rather than signal.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Time Series Lag & Rolling Features**
  - *Why needed here:* The models (especially ARIMAX and the baseline) rely on autoregressive components. Creating lagged sales features (e.g., sales from 1, 7, 30 days prior) is fundamental to providing any model with a historical view of demand.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the risk of using a lag feature from a day that was an unrecorded machine outage? (Answer: It injects a false demand signal, degrading model learnings.)
- **Concept: Model Class Foundations (Tree vs. Linear vs. Bayesian)**
  - *Why needed here:* The performance divergence (XGBoost vs. Prophet vs. ARIMAX) stems from architectural biases. XGBoost (ensemble of trees) excels at heterogeneous feature interaction; ARIMAX (linear) assumes additive relationships; Prophet (decomposable) assumes smooth trend/seasonality changes. Understanding these biases is key to interpreting *why* context helps some models more.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might Prophet show less improvement from the sales deviation flag compared to XGBoost? (Answer: Prophet's core decomposition may smooth out or attribute single-point anomalies to noise, rather than using a binary flag to explicitly correct them.)
- **Concept: Feature Engineering for Context Injection**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire study hinges on transforming raw data (a date) into actionable context (weekday sin/cos, holiday boolean, deviation flag). This is not automatic; it requires domain hypothesis about *what* external factors matter and *how* to represent them.
  - *Quick check question:* If you had weather data, what are two potential feature engineering approaches besides a simple "rainy day" flag? (Answer examples: Continuous temperature, or interaction term like "hot * weekend".)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Ingestion → Preprocessing → Feature Engineering → Temporal Split → Model Registry → (Model Training → Evaluation) → Analysis & Visualization
- **Critical path:** Data Ingestion → Preprocessing → Feature Engineering → Temporal Split → Model Training → Evaluation. The feature engineering step is the critical differentiator; omitting it collapses the experiment to Scenario 1.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **XGBoost:** High accuracy & feature interaction capture vs. lower interpretability & potential overfitting on noise.
  - **ARIMAX:** Strong temporal linear foundation vs. poor handling of complex non-linear context interactions.
  - **Prophet:** Robust to missing data & intuitive decomposition vs. limited ability to leverage exogenous features beyond its built-in holiday mechanism.
  - **SVR:** Effective in high-dimensional spaces vs. extreme sensitivity to feature scaling and hyperparameters, leading to inconsistent performance here.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Data Leakage:** Future information in engineered features (e.g., a holiday in test set known during training). **Check:** Ensure all feature calculations (like rolling averages for deviation flag) use only past data.
  - **Location Heterogeneity:** A single "holiday" flag used on a dataset mixing airports and offices will show degraded or inconsistent performance. **Check:** Stratify analysis by location type if metadata exists.
  - **Hyperparameter Mismatch:** Using default SVR parameters. **Check:** Start with the exact hyperparameters provided; if performance diverges, perform a coarse grid search around given values.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate Baseline (Scenario 1):** Train all four models only on lagged sales and store/item IDs. Confirm MAEs align with Table 4's first column.
  2. **Add Single Context - Weekday:** Retrain models with the engineered weekday feature. Expect ~15% MAE drop for XGBoost, smaller for others.
  3. **Add Single Context - Deviation Flag:** Retrain with only the sales deviation flag added to the baseline. Expect largest single-feature lift for XGBoost (~37%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How do additional external variables (e.g., weather, promotions, economic indicators) quantitatively impact the accuracy of context-augmented forecasting models in vending machine supply chains?  
- *Basis in paper:* Section 4.4 suggests incorporating more external features as a future direction.  
- *Why unresolved:* The study only tested a limited set (weekday, holiday, sales deviation flag); the incremental value of richer exogenous data remains unmeasured.  
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled experiments extending the feature set with variables like weather or promotion data, comparing MAE/RMSE improvements against the baseline model on the same dataset.

**Open Question 2:** Can federated learning approaches improve the generalizability and robustness of demand forecasting models across diverse regional or channel-specific datasets compared to centralized training on a single dataset?  
- *Basis in paper:* Section 4.4 explicitly recommends exploring federated learning to address limitations of training on a single dataset with a fixed temporal split.  
- *Why unresolved:* The current model was trained on one dataset; its performance across different geographic regions, vending machine types, or retail channels is unknown and may suffer from overfitting to local patterns.  
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Implementing a federated learning framework across multiple decentralized datasets from various regions/channels, evaluating cross-validation performance and comparing it to locally trained and centrally aggregated models.

**Open Question 3:** How can explainable AI (XAI) techniques like SHAP or LIME be integrated into XGBoost-based forecasting to provide interpretable, actionable insights for supply chain managers regarding feature contributions and prediction logic?  
- *Basis in paper:* Section 4.4 notes that explainability methods were not applied and should be integrated to support decision-making.  
- *Why unresolved:* While XGBoost showed high accuracy, its "black-box" nature limits trust and operational insight; managers cannot understand why certain predictions are made.  
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Applying SHAP/LIME to the trained XGBoost model to generate global and local explanations, then conducting user studies with supply chain professionals to assess usefulness for inventory decisions.

**Open Question 4:** Is the high feature importance of the sales deviation flag consistent across all product categories and vending machine locations, or does its predictive value vary significantly with product type or contextual factors (e.g., machine in airport vs. office)?  
- *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The sales deviation flag was the top feature in XGBoost globally, but the paper does not analyze whether this holds universally across heterogeneous items or locations, despite noting location-specific holiday effects.  
- *Why unresolved:* The aggregated feature importance masks potential heterogeneity; the flag's effectiveness might depend on product demand volatility or location stability.  
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Performing a segmented analysis: train separate models or compute feature importance subsets for different product categories (e.g., snacks vs. beverages) and location types (e.g., corporate vs. public), then compare the relative importance of the sales deviation flag.

**Open Question 5:** What are the performance and stability implications of deploying context-augmented models in real-time streaming environments with continuous learning, compared to the offline batch training and static evaluation used in this study?  
- *Basis in paper:* Section 4.4 identifies real-time forecasting and continuous learning as a key future direction, noting the study's static temporal split and lack of streaming data.  
- *Why unresolved:* The evaluation used a fixed historical split; real-world deployment involves streaming data, concept drift, and the need for model updates, which could degrade accuracy or introduce latency not captured.  
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Simulating a real-time deployment using a data stream from vending machines, implementing online learning or periodic retraining, and tracking MAE over time to assess drift adaptation, update frequency, and operational feasibility.

## Limitations
- The Sayyad et al. (2024) dataset is not publicly available, blocking direct replication and external validation.
- Critical feature engineering details are vague: the rolling window size and aggregation level for the sales deviation flag, and the exact lag periods used, are unspecified, risking implementation drift.
- Model configurations may be suboptimal (e.g., SVR defaults, fixed ARIMAX orders); without hyperparameter tuning, performance comparisons could be biased.
- Location heterogeneity (e.g., airport vs. office) is noted but not stratified in analysis, potentially masking important effect moderation.
- Neighbor corpus shows limited corroboration (average FMR 0.48, zero citations), reducing confidence in broader generalizability.

## Confidence
- **Context-augmentation (external features) significantly improves forecast accuracy:** Medium – internal evidence strong (e.g., 51% MAE drop for XGBoost) but relies on a single non-public dataset.
- **Sales deviation flag is the most influential feature:** Medium – paper reports high importance and outlier explanation, but this could be dataset-specific and proxy-based (actual outage logs unavailable).
- **XGBoost outperforms other models for context-aware forecasting:** Medium – consistent with known strengths, but other models might reach parity with proper tuning; SVR performance could be config-limited.
- **Holiday impacts are highly location-dependent:** High – directly observed mixed results across locations, a robust empirical pattern within the study.
