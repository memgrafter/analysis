# Never Saddle for Reparameterized Steepest Descent as Mirror Flow

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02064
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02064
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Adaptive optimizers like Adam outperform SGD in fine-tuning because steeper descent (e.g., sign descent) accelerates saddle-point escape and promotes depth-dependent sparsity via geometric mirror flows, while decoupled weight decay (AdamW) stabilizes this feature learning.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes a geometric framework—reparameterized steepest descent as mirror flow—to explain why adaptive optimizers (e.g., Adam) often surpass SGD in fine-tuning. Using deep diagonal linear networks as a proxy for attention mechanisms, the authors prove that steeper descent (smaller q in Lp norms) enables faster escape from saddle points at small learning rates, a regime where standard gradient descent (q=2) fails unless learning rates are unrealistically large. The theory also reveals that depth amplifies the sparsity-inducing bias of steepest flows, and decoupled weight decay (AdamW) prevents the extreme sparsity that degrades performance with standard weight decay. Empirical validation on vision (ResNet, ViT) and language (BERT) models confirms Adam’s superior saddle escape and depth-dependent sparsity, aligning with the derived geometric principles.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze training dynamics via mirror flows, where reparameterized gradients follow steepest descent under an Lp norm (parameterized by q). The model is a deep diagonal linear network (product of L scalar weights), serving as a simplified analog for attention (KQ products). Key variables include the initialization balance λ (difference in Lp norms between first and last layer weights) and a metric exponent m = q(L-1)/L that governs stability and sparsity. The study compares coupled versus decoupled weight decay and links optimizer geometry (e.g., Adam ≈ sign descent, q=1) to implicit biases and saddle escape rates, supported by theory (balance equations, coercivity constants) and experiments on synthetic and real models.

## Key Results
- Steeper descent (q < 2) achieves exponentially faster saddle escape than gradient descent (q=2) at small learning rates, with the escape rate governed by a coercivity constant µ ∝ λ^{q(L-1)}.
- Increasing depth L in steepest flows (q<2) raises the metric exponent m = q(L-1)/L, amplifying sparsity in the learned features—a depth-dependent effect not captured by prior max-margin theory.
- Decoupled weight decay (AdamW) yields a milder sparsity bias (L^{L/(L+1)} for q=1) versus the extreme L¹ sparsity of coupled decay, preventing feature collapse in deep reparameterizations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Steeper Descent Accelerates Saddle Escape
- **Claim:** Smaller q (e.g., q=1 for sign descent, approximating Adam) enables significantly faster escape from saddle points compared to gradient flow (q=2), especially at small learning rates common in fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism:** For a λ-balanced initialization near a saddle set S, the coercivity constant µ controlling escape rate scales as λ^{q(L-1)} (Corollary 4.10). Smaller q yields larger µ for small λ. The stability condition q(L-1)/L ≤ 1 is also easier to satisfy for smaller q (Corollary 4.14), avoiding the high-exponent regime that causes exponential slowdown near saddles.
- **Core assumption:** Initialization is λ-balanced (λ ≈ 0) and learning rate is small.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 1 (Figure 1) show SignGF converges faster than GF from a saddle-point initialization. Section 4 (Theorem 4.8, Corollary 4.10) derives µ and its dependence on q and L.
- **Break condition:** For q=2 and L>2, the Bregman function becomes invalid (Corollary 4.14), leading to instability and failure to escape saddles with small learning rates.

### Mechanism 2: Decoupled Weight Decay Stabilizes Feature Learning
- **Claim:** Decoupled weight decay (AdamW) enforces different balance equations than standard weight decay, preventing excessive sparsity that degrades performance in deep reparameterizations.
- **Mechanism:** The balance equation (Lemma 4.5) includes a factor exp(-q∫αₛds) from weight decay. For SignGF (q=1), decoupled decay induces an implicit regularizer corresponding to L^{L/(L+1)} sparsity, whereas coupled decay yields extreme L¹ sparsity at any depth (Table 1). This milder bias avoids the "extreme sparsity and performance degradation" seen with coupled decay (Kolb et al., 2025).
- **Core assumption:** Balanced initialization (λ=0) or weight decay drives λ → 0 sufficiently fast (Lemma 4.5).
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states decoupled decay stabilizes feature learning. Section 4 (Table 1) contrasts manifold regularizers. Section 5 (Figure 5b) shows AdamW induces sparsity only for deep nets (L=10) with strong decay, while coupled decay does so even for L=2.
- **Break condition:** Coupled weight decay at high L drives parameters to extreme L¹ sparsity, collapsing accuracy (Table 7).

### Mechanism 3: Depth-Dependent Geometry Induces Sparsity in Adam-like Flows
- **Claim:** In steepest mirror flows (especially q=1/SignGF), increasing depth L increases the metric exponent m = q(L-1)/L, which amplifies the sparsity-inducing implicit bias.
- **Mechanism:** For λ=0 balanced initialization, the Legendre function’s inverse metric ∇²R⁻¹ ∝ |x|^{q(L-1)/L} (Lemma 4.13). Larger m (approaching 1 as L→∞ for q=1) corresponds to a stronger sparsity bias. This geometric effect is missed by prior max-margin analyses (Theorem F.1), which show depth-independent L∞ margin.
- **Core assumption:** Deep diagonal reparameterization with λ ≈ 0 and steepest descent (q<2).
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 1 (Figure 2) illustrates higher L increases m for SignGF. Section 4 (Lemma 4.13) gives explicit RLp,L. Section 5 (Figure 4) shows Adam (q≈1) recovers sparser ground truth margin at L=10 vs. L=2.
- **Break condition:** If q is too large (e.g., q=2 for GF), m may exceed 1 for L>2, violating Bregman conditions (Corollary 4.14) and preventing stable feature learning.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
λ-balanced initialization -> Deep diagonal linear network (x = ∏_{i=1}^L w_i) -> Steepest mirror flow optimizer (q, weight decay type) -> Task loss (MSE/exponential) -> Learned features

**Critical path:**  
1. Initialize weights near λ-balanced with small λ (λ = ||w₁||^q - ||w_L||^q) to start near saddle set S.  
2. Train with chosen steepness q (controls geometry) and weight decay coupling (coupled/decoupled).  
3. Dynamics follow balance equation (Lemma 4.5); metric exponent m = q(L-1)/L governs stability and sparsity.  
4. For decoupled decay, sparsity requires larger L (Table 1); for coupled decay, sparsity is aggressive even at shallow L.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Depth L ↑ increases sparsity bias (higher m) but risks instability for gradient flow (q=2).  
- Coupled weight decay induces strong sparsity early but may collapse features; decoupled requires deeper nets for sparsity but is more stable.  
- q ↓ (steeper) aids saddle escape but may slow convergence near optimal point.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Gradient flow (q=2) with L>2 fails to converge from small λ-balanced initialization (Lemma 4.14, invalid Bregman function).  
- Coupled weight decay at high L drives parameters to extreme L¹ sparsity, collapsing accuracy (Table 7).  
- Adam-like (q=1) with small learning rate and shallow L may not induce sufficient sparsity for feature learning.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Linear regression with deep diagonal nets:** Verify faster saddle escape for q=1 vs. q=2 at small η (Figures 1, 7).  
2. **Classification with separable data:** Test depth-sparsity link; Adam (q≈1) should recover sparser margin at higher L (Figures 4, 6).  
3. **Fine-tuning vision/NLP models:** Compare Adam vs. SGD on validation accuracy and Hessian spectra; Adam should exhibit fewer negative eigenvalues (Figures 5, 21–24).

## Limitations
- The theory relies on deep diagonal linear networks as a proxy for attention, omitting nonlinearities, layer norms, and multi-head dynamics present in real transformers.
- Analysis assumes λ-balanced initialization and small learning rates (fine-tuning regime); applicability to standard training from random init is unclear.
- Adam is approximated by sign descent (q=1), but Adam’s ε, β₁, β₂, and bias-correction deviate from pure steepest descent, potentially weakening the geometric equivalence.
- Empirical validation for sparsity uses indirect metrics (weight distributions); direct verification of predicted balance equations in real models is lacking.
- Real networks with SGD often train stably despite theory predicting failure for q=2, L>2, suggesting unmodeled factors (e.g., batch norm, residuals).

## Confidence
- **Steeper descent accelerates saddle escape (theory):** High for the simplified model; Medium for scalability to real architectures.
- **Decoupled weight decay stabilizes feature learning:** Medium (supported by experiments, but relies on idealized assumptions).
- **Depth-dependent sparsity induction in Adam-like flows:** Medium-Low (empirical trend observed, but direct causal link to metric exponent m is inferred, not measured).
