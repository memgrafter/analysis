# World Properties without World Models: Recovering Spatial and Temporal Structure from Co-occurrence Statistics in Static Word Embeddings

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.04317
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04317
*   **Reference count:** 3
*   **Primary result:** Linear probes achieve high R² (0.66-0.87) for city latitude/longitude and moderate R² (~0.50) for birth years from static embeddings, indicating co-occurrence statistics encode worldly structure.

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the common inference that a language model's ability to linearly predict real-world properties (e.g., location, time) indicates it has learned an internal "world model" that goes beyond textual statistics. It tests this by applying ridge regression probes to static word embeddings (GloVe, Word2Vec) to predict city coordinates, climate, and historical birth years. The results show substantial, linearly recoverable geographic signal and a weaker temporal signal. Interpretable lexical gradients (e.g., country names, climate terms) are identified as key carriers of this signal, and ablating these subspaces severely damages performance. The authors conclude that simple co-occurrence statistics alone can preserve rich worldly structure, cautioning against equating probe success with representational advances that integrate non-textual reasoning.

## Method Summary
The authors train linear ridge regression probes on top of frozen, pre-trained static word embeddings (GloVe, Word2Vec). The probes are trained to predict continuous properties (latitude, longitude, birth year) or classify categorical ones (climate) for target entities (cities, historical figures). Semantic analyses are performed to identify embedding dimensions most correlated with correct predictions, looking for interpretable lexical gradients (e.g., a direction where vector values change from "southern" to "northern" city names). To test necessity, the authors ablate (zero out) these identified subspaces and re-evaluate probe performance. Negative control properties (elevation, GDP) are also probed to assess specificity.

## Key Results
*   Geographic coordinates are recoverable with high R²: latitude (0.71–0.87), longitude (0.66–0.87).
*   Birth years show a weaker but present linear signal (R² ≈ 0.48–0.52).
*   Ablating subspaces associated with lexical gradients like country names or climate terms leads to a significant drop in geographic prediction accuracy.
*   No recoverable signal is found for negative control properties (elevation, GDP).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism is that large-scale textual co-occurrence statistics implicitly preserve spatial and temporal regularities. For geography, cities co-occur with region-specific descriptors ("Mediterranean," "Alpine," "tropical") whose distribution across the corpus correlates with physical location. These cues form smooth gradients in the embedding space along which geographic coordinates vary linearly. For time, historical figures co-occur with era-specific language ("Renaissance," "Victorian," "Cold War"), embedding a cruder temporal axis. Linear probes succeed because the target properties vary monotonically along these pre-existing, co-occurrence-derived embedding directions.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Static word embeddings (Word2Vec, GloVe):** Fixed vector representations of words trained on word-word co-occurrence statistics. *Why needed:* They are the subject of probing; their training objective is hypothesized to preserve worldly structure. *Quick check:* What is the core training objective difference between Word2Vec (skip-gram) and GloVe (global co-occurrence matrix factorization)?
*   **Linear probing:** Using a simple, often linear, classifier/regressor to predict an external property from a model's internal representations. *Why needed:* A standard diagnostic to test if information is encoded in a linearly accessible way. *Quick check:* Why might a non-linear probe find signal that a linear probe misses?
*   **Ridge regression (ℓ2-regularized linear regression):** A linear model that adds a penalty on the squared magnitude of coefficients to prevent overfitting. *Why needed:* Essential for stable training on high-dimensional embedding spaces with collinear features. *Quick check:* What effect does the regularization parameter λ have on the bias-variance trade-off?
*   **Co-occurrence statistics:** The frequency with which words appear in the same contextual window across a corpus. *Why needed:* The foundational training signal for static embeddings; the paper's central hypothesis is that worldly structure is latent here. *Quick check:* How might a biased corpus (e.g., English Wikipedia) distort geographic co-occurrence patterns?
*   **Subspace ablation:** Zeroing out or removing specific components (e.g., a set of embedding dimensions) from representations. *Why needed:* To test the causal necessity of identified subspaces for the probe's performance. *Quick check:* What is the difference between ablating a *direction* vs. ablating a *set of features*?
*   **R² (coefficient of determination):** A metric from regression analysis indicating the proportion of variance in the dependent variable predictable from the independent variables. *Why needed:* Primary quantitative measure of probe success. *Quick check:* What are the limitations of R² for comparing performance across different prediction tasks (e.g., lat/long vs. birth year)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Text corpus -> Co-occurrence matrix -> Static embedding model (GloVe/Word2Vec) -> Pre-trained word vectors -> Linear ridge probe (trained on city/person data) -> Predicted property (lat/long/year)

**Critical Path:** The workflow depends on first training high-quality static embeddings on a large, diverse corpus. The probe training is secondary and must use a separate, labeled dataset of entities with known properties. The entire analysis is post-hoc and does not alter the base embeddings.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   **Linear vs. Non-linear Probes:** Linear probes offer interpretability and efficiency but may understate the complexity of encoding. Non-linear probes could reveal more sophisticated, less transparent structures.
*   **Static vs. Contextual:** Static embeddings provide a single vector per word, simplifying analysis but losing sense disambiguation. Contextual models (BERT, GPT) offer richer, token-level representations but introduce variability that complicates the probing task.
*   **Aggregation Strategy:** For entities represented by multiple tokens (e.g., "New York"), the choice of aggregation (mean, first token) can significantly affect results.

**Failure Signatures:**
1.  Poor probe performance (R² ~0) on all target properties, suggesting no linear signal.
2.  Strong performance on negative control properties (e.g., elevation), indicating the probe is exploiting dataset artifacts or spurious correlations.
3.  No significant degradation in performance after ablating the proposed interpretable subspaces, challenging the claimed mechanism.
4.  Inconsistent results across different random seeds for embedding training or probe initialization.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Replication on alternative static embeddings:** Run the exact same probing pipeline on FastText embeddings to test robustness across different static embedding algorithms.
2.  **Compare linear and non-linear probes:** For the same city coordinate task, train a small feed-forward neural network probe (with one hidden layer) and compare R² to the linear ridge probe.
3.  **Control for entity frequency:** When probing for city coordinates, include the log frequency of the city name in the training corpus as a control variable in the ridge regression to isolate the geographic signal from sheer textual prominence.

## Limitations
*   Findings are limited to static embeddings (GloVe, Word2Vec); it is unknown if the same degree of linear recoverability holds for contextualized models (BERT, GPT).
*   The geographic signal may be disproportionately driven by a few high-frequency, well-documented cities or culturally Western-centric corpora, raising bias concerns.
*   The use of a linear probe may overstate the "simplicity" of the representation; non-linear structures could also be present.
*   The interpretation of negative control failure relies on the assumption that properties like elevation are not systematically encoded via proxy terms in the corpus.

## Confidence
*   **High:** Substantial linear recoverability of geographic coordinates (lat/long) from static word embeddings is robustly supported by R² values (0.66-0.87) and ablation evidence.
*   **Medium:** Recoverable temporal signal (birth year) is present but weaker (R² ~0.50); its consistency and generalizability are less certain.
*   **Medium:** The overarching conclusion that co-occurrence statistics alone can preserve rich worldly structure is well-supported for geography; extrapolating this to fully undermine "world model" claims for modern LLMs is a logical extension needing direct testing.

## Next Checks
1.  **Replicate with Contextual Embeddings:** Apply identical linear ridge probes to contextual embeddings (e.g., BERT-base [CLS] token) for the same datasets and compare R² to static models.
2.  **Control for Corpus Salience:** Re-analyze geographic recoverability while statistically controlling for city frequency in the corpus and external proxies for textual prominence (e.g., Wikipedia page views).
3.  **Test Alternative Temporal Markers:** Replace "birth year" with "decade of major historical event participation" or "career peak year" to check if the weaker temporal signal is specific to biographical data or a general limit.
