# Towards Principled Dataset Distillation: A Spectral Distribution Perspective

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01698
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01698
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CSDM achieves a 14.0% absolute accuracy gain over state-of-the-art on CIFAR-10-LT (IPC=10) and shows exceptional stability when tail class samples drop from 500 to 25 (only 5.7% accuracy decline).

## Executive Summary
Dataset distillation synthesizes small representative datasets that train accurate models, but existing methods fail on long-tailed real-world data due to heuristic metrics and uniform class treatment. This paper introduces Class-Aware Spectral Distribution Matching (CSDM), a principled framework that reformulates distribution alignment via the spectrum of a universal kernel. CSDM decomposes the resulting spectral distance into amplitude (diversity) and phase (realism) components and applies class-specific weighting: head classes emphasize diversity while tail classes emphasize realism. On long-tailed benchmarks, CSDM substantially outperforms prior work, maintains low memory (~3.45 GB) and fast iteration (~0.33 s), and demonstrates remarkable stability under severe imbalance.

## Method Summary
CSDM distills a synthetic dataset by minimizing a class-aware spectral distribution distance (SDD) between real and synthetic feature distributions. Using a universal shift-invariant kernel (RBF), SDD is defined as an integral over frequency space of the squared difference of characteristic functions. This integral is approximated by sampling frequencies from the kernel’s spectral measure. The per-class loss decomposes into an amplitude term (matching spectral magnitudes, encouraging diversity) and a phase term (aligning phase angles, encouraging realism). For each class c, a weight α(c) ∈ [0,1] scales the amplitude term; the phase term is implicitly weighted by 1−α(c). Head classes use higher α (≈0.7–0.9) to preserve intra-class variation; tail classes use lower α (≈0.1–0.3) to maintain semantic fidelity. Features are extracted by a fixed network (randomly initialized or pre-trained) and normalized to enable a fixed kernel scale (γ=2). The loss is minimized via gradient descent on synthetic pixel parameters. After distillation, a fresh model is trained on the synthetic set and evaluated on the real test set.

## Key Results
- **Absolute gain:** 14.0% over prior state-of-the-art on CIFAR-10-LT with 10 images per class.
- **Stability:** When tail class samples drop from 500 to 25, accuracy declines by only 5.7%, far less than other methods.
- **Efficiency:** Low memory footprint (~3.45 GB) and fast iteration speed (~0.33 s per iteration) while scaling to high IPC.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A universal kernel-based spectral distance (SDD) aligns the full distribution (all moments) of real and synthetic features, overcoming the first-order limitation of linear-kernel MMD.
- **Mechanism:** SDD uses a shift-invariant universal kernel (e.g., RBF) whose spectral measure is derived via Bochner’s theorem. The squared MMD is reformulated as an integral over frequency space of the squared difference of characteristic functions, weighted by the kernel’s spectral density. Because a universal kernel’s RKHS is dense in continuous functions, matching the kernel MMD implies matching all moments.
- **Core assumption:** The kernel is universal and shift-invariant, ensuring its spectral measure exists and that the mean embedding is injective (MMD=0 iff distributions are identical).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “reformulates distribution alignment via the spectrum of a well-behaved kernel function”; [section 4.2.1] “MMD with a universal kernel can match full distributions”; [section 4.2.2] “squared MMD can be expressed in the frequency domain as MMD² = ∫ ||φ_P(t)−φ_Q(t)||² dµ(t)”.
- **Break condition:** If the kernel is not universal or the spectral measure is misestimated (e.g., too few frequency samples), SDD may fail to distinguish distributions with identical means but different higher-order statistics.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Decomposing SDD into amplitude (diversity) and phase (realism) components disentangles two distinct aspects of distribution quality, enabling targeted optimization for class-imbalanced data.
- **Mechanism:** The squared difference of characteristic functions ||φ_T−φ_S||² is split into a term comparing magnitudes (amplitude) and a term comparing angles (phase). The amplitude term |||φ_T|−|φ_S|||² reflects spectral energy distribution; matching it encourages synthetic data to cover the same range of feature variations (diversity). The phase term 2|φ_T||φ_S|(1−cos(θ_T−θ_S)) captures alignment of phase angles at frequencies where both distributions have energy; minimizing this enforces structural fidelity (realism) by preserving phase coherence needed for recognizable patterns.
- **Core assumption:** The amplitude of a characteristic function correlates with the spread/variance of the feature distribution (diversity), while its phase correlates with multi-modal structure or semantic alignment (realism), as established in signal processing and generative modeling.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “exploit the unified form of SDD to perform amplitude-phase decomposition, which adaptively prioritizes the realism in tail classes”; [section 4.2.4] “The amplitude difference quantifies the discrepancy in the spectral magnitude, which reflects the diversity of the feature distribution. Phase Difference... is indicative of the realism of the data.”; [section C] “in Fourier analysis, the phase spectrum encodes most of the structural and edge information.”
- **Break condition:** If the feature space does not align with semantics (e.g., deep features collapse class distinctions), the amplitude-phase interpretation may not correlate with actual diversity/realism. The decomposition also relies on both |φ_T| and |φ_S| being non-zero at frequency t; if one is near zero, the phase term vanishes regardless of misalignment.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Class-aware weighting of amplitude vs. phase terms adapts to long-tailed imbalance: head classes (data-rich) emphasize diversity (amplitude) to capture intra-class variation, while tail classes (data-scarce) emphasize realism (phase) to preserve faithful semantics from few samples.
- **Mechanism:** For each class c, a coefficient α(c)∈[0,1] scales the amplitude term (weight 1 for head classes) and implicitly scales the phase term (weight 1−α(c), larger for tail classes). The overall SDD is a weighted sum over classes. During distillation, tail classes’ loss is dominated by phase alignment, preventing unrealistic synthetic samples; head classes’ loss is dominated by amplitude matching, preventing mode collapse and preserving diversity.
- **Core assumption:** Head classes contain rich diversity that must be preserved in the tiny synthetic set, while tail classes suffer primarily from overfitting to noise or unrealistic synthesis when real samples are extremely few, making phase (structural) fidelity more critical than full diversity.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “apply class-specific weights to prioritize diversity for head classes and realism for tail classes”; [section 4.2.4] “In long-tailed settings, tail classes demand more realism to prevent mode collapse while head classes benefit from greater diversity.”; [section 5.3.2] Ablation shows optimal performance when α(head)≈0.7–0.9; reversing the weighting degrades accuracy.
- **Break condition:** If class priors between real and synthetic data differ significantly, the class-weighted sum may not represent the true joint distribution. If α(c) is set uniformly (e.g., 0.5 for all), the method reduces to non-class-aware SDD and loses its long-tail advantage. Extreme α(c) values (0 or 1) risk losing either amplitude or phase terms entirely per class, possibly harming convergence.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) and Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS)  
  **Why needed:** CSDM recasts dataset distillation as minimizing MMD between real and synthetic feature distributions. Understanding MMD’s dependence on kernel choice explains why a linear kernel fails (only aligns means) and why a universal kernel succeeds (aligns all moments).  
  **Quick check:** Given two distributions with identical means but different variances, will linear-kernel MMD be zero? Why? How does changing to an RBF kernel affect this?

- **Concept:** Characteristic functions and spectral domain analysis  
  **Why needed:** SDD is defined as an integral of squared characteristic function differences weighted by a spectral measure. This spectral view enables linear-complexity computation and the amplitude-phase decomposition.  
  **Quick check:** What property of a characteristic function guarantees that equal characteristic functions imply equal distributions? How does Bochner’s theorem link a kernel to a spectral measure?

- **Concept:** Class imbalance in dataset distillation  
  **Why needed:** Uniform treatment of head and tail classes causes existing methods to fail on long-tailed data. CSDM’s class-aware weighting directly addresses this by allocating optimization focus differently per class.  
  **Quick check:** If a head class has 5,000 samples and a tail class has 25 in the real dataset, what is the likely failure mode if we synthesize 10 images per class uniformly? Why might tail-class synthetic images become unrealistic?

## Architecture Onboarding
1. **Feature Extractor (f_θ):** Pre-trained or randomly initialized network (e.g., 3-layer ConvNet) that maps raw images to feature vectors. Not updated during distillation.
2. **Spectral Distance Calculator:** Given real/synthetic batches per class, computes empirical characteristic functions at sampled frequencies t_i ~ p(t) (from kernel’s spectral measure), then approximates SDD = mean over i of ||φ_T(t_i)−φ_S(t_i)||².
3. **Class-aware Weighting Module:** For each class c, computes α(c) (fixed schedule or adaptive). Decomposes per-class SDD into amplitude and phase terms. Combines as d_c = α(c)*Amp_c + 2(1−α(c))*M_c*P_c. Aggregates across classes.
4. **Synthetic Data Optimizer:** Gradient descent on synthetic pixel parameters (or frequency coefficients if using FreD/NSD) to minimize total class-weighted SDD.

Critical path: Real dataset → (offline) fit feature extractor → distillation loop: sample mini-batches from real and synthetic per class → compute features → sample frequency vectors → compute per-class characteristic functions → decompose into amplitude/phase → weight with α(c) → sum to loss → backpropagate to synthetic data parameters.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Kernel choice (RBF/Laplace):** Universal kernels ensure full distribution matching but introduce scale parameter γ. Poor γ leads to under/over-fitting. CSDM normalizes features and fixes γ=2, trading some adaptivity for robustness.
- **α(c) schedule:** Must be set manually or via heuristic (e.g., α(head)=0.8, α(tail)=0.2). Adaptive α(c) based on class size might improve but adds complexity; paper uses fixed based on imbalance factor.
- **Memory vs. speed:** SDD computation is O(LND) with L sampled frequencies, linear in N. This avoids quadratic kernel matrices, enabling large IPC. Trajectory methods (MTT, DATM) are OOM at high IPC.

Failure signatures:
1. **No gain on long-tail:** If α(c) not class-aware (i.e., α(c)=0.5 all), performance degrades on tail classes.
2. **Sensitivity to γ:** Without feature normalization, RBF γ=1 nearly matches linear kernel, losing universality benefits.
3. **Poor cross-architecture transfer:** If synthetic data overfits to feature extractor used during distillation, performance drops on unseen architectures. CSDM’s kernel-based metric helps but still sees some drop (78.1% → 65.9% on ResNet vs ConvNet).
4. **Mode collapse on heads:** If α(head) too low (<0.5), head classes lose diversity; if too high (>0.95), may ignore phase entirely but amplitude matching may still be okay.
5. **Unrealistic tail images:** If α(tail) too high (>0.5), phase term under-weighted, tail samples may look noise-like.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Kernel ablation:** Compare linear MMD vs. RBF-SDD vs. Laplace-SDD on CIFAR-10-LT (IPC=10, imf=200). Expect RBF/Laplace to significantly outperform linear.
2. **α(c) sensitivity sweep:** For fixed imbalance (imf=100), vary α(head) from 0.1 to 0.9 (α(tail)=1−α(head)). Plot test accuracy; expect peak around 0.7–0.9 for head amplitude weight.
3. **Long-tail scaling:** Keep IPC fixed (e.g., 10), vary imbalance factor β from 10 to 200. Measure accuracy drop. Expect CSDM’s drop to be much smaller than baselines, demonstrating stability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can CSDM be effectively combined with other structural regularizers such as class centralization (IID) or semantic covariance modeling (DSDM) to further improve performance on long-tailed dataset distillation, and are there synergistic or antagonistic interactions between these components?
- **Open Question 2:** What is the theoretical relationship between characteristic function discrepancy (CFD) and downstream classification accuracy, and can we derive a training objective for frequency samplers (as in NCFM) that directly optimizes for task performance rather than an intermediate proxy?
- **Open Question 3:** Can we design adaptive neural frequency samplers that learn the spectral measure while preserving the metric properties (universality, non-negativity, identity of indiscernibles) of the Spectral Distribution Distance (SDD), and does such adaptivity improve distillation across datasets and modalities?

## Limitations
- Exact feature extractor configuration (random vs. pretrained, augmentation, parameter sampling) is unspecified, affecting feature distribution.
- Class-aware weighting α(c) is given only as a range (0.7–0.9 for heads, 0.1–0.3 for tails); the precise values used for reported numbers are unknown.
- Feature normalization procedure is unclear; improper normalization would break the RBF universality assumption.
- The amplitude-phase decomposition’s link to diversity/realism is assumed but not directly validated on synthetic images.
- Performance claims depend on unreported hyperparameters, so absolute numbers may vary.

## Confidence
