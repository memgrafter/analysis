# CogFormer: Learn All Your Models Once
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20520
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20520
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A single transformer network can perform accurate amortized Bayesian inference across a combinatorial set of cognitive model variants (e.g., DDMs, RDMs, CDMs), achieving NRMSE within ~15% of single-model baselines.

## Executive Summary
Cognitive modeling often requires amortized simulation-based inference (SBI) to quickly estimate model parameters from behavioral data. However, traditional amortization fails when model configurations change (e.g., different parameterizations or design matrices), necessitating expensive retraining. CogFormer solves this by training one transformer-based network to handle a whole family or class of structurally related cognitive models. It uses a meta-simulator that expresses diverse models under a unified generalized linear model (GLM) framework with parameter masks. The architecture employs a Set Transformer encoder to process variable-sized trial data and a conditional flow matching decoder to learn flexible posteriors. Evaluations on diffusion decision models show that this family-level amortization yields competitive accuracy (NRMSE 0.078–0.112) and calibration, with only a slight reduction in posterior contraction and a modest amortization gap that shrinks with more simulations.

## Method Summary
CogFormer introduces a meta-simulator that generates synthetic datasets across a combinatorial space of model configurations by expressing them via a GLM parameterization: Θ = g(X(M ⊙ B)), where design matrix X, coefficient matrix B, and binary mask M define specific model instances. A single encoder–decoder transformer processes inputs: a Set Transformer encoder condenses variable-length observations (X, Y) into fixed summary tokens S; the decoder conditions on S, positional embeddings (regressor, parameter, model indices), the mask M, time t, and noise Zₜ to output a velocity field û via conditional flow matching. Training minimizes a masked flow matching loss ∥M⊙(û − (Z₁−B))∥² over linear interpolation paths Zₜ. After 32 million simulations, inference samples from the posterior by integrating the learned ODE from t=1 to t=0. This setup enables “train once, infer many” for any configuration within the trained model family or class.

## Key Results
- Family-level amortization across five DDM benchmarks and 12 random configurations yields NRMSE between 0.078 and 0.112—within 15% of single-model baselines.
- Expected calibration errors (ECE) range from 0.020 to 0.061, indicating good posterior calibration.
- Posterior contraction is slightly reduced by 2–4 percentage points compared to single-model training.
- Classifier-two-sample tests (C2ST) show a modest amortization gap in joint posterior structure that diminishes with increased simulation budget.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The meta-simulator enables generation of data across a combinatorial set of model configurations by expressing them under a unified GLM framework with parameter masks.
- **Mechanism:** A parameter matrix B is constructed from a prior and a design matrix X, then transformed by element-wise link functions g to produce intrinsic parameters Θ. A binary mask M selects which parameters are active, allowing nested configurations. The model family is encoded via positional embeddings.
- **Core assumption:** All target cognitive models can be represented as special cases of this GLM parameterization, with structural variations captured by masking and design changes.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “using a meta-simulator that generates data under a flexible GLM parameterization.” [section 2.2] “Y ∼ Model(Θ; RNG), Θ = g(X(M ⊙ B))”. The corpus shows active SBI research but no direct evidence for this specific meta-simulator; the mechanism is proposed in this paper.
- **Break condition:** Fails for models that cannot be expressed in the GLM form (e.g., models with non-GLM likelihoods or discrete latent states) or when mask complexity exceeds representation capacity.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Set Transformer encoder processes variable-sized observations and design matrices in a permutation-invariant manner, enabling generalization across trial counts and experimental designs.
- **Mechanism:** The encoder receives (X, Y) as a set of trial-level inputs and uses self-attention to produce fixed-size summary tokens S, which are invariant to order and size. This avoids padding and handles varying numbers of trials and regressors.
- **Core assumption:** The summary statistics extracted by the Set Transformer are sufficient for posterior inference across all configurations in the class.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “encoder-decoder structure with a Set Transformer to process variable-sized observations.” [section 2.3] “The encoder is a Set Transformer that processes the design matrix and the responses (model observables) (X,Y) into a sequence of L learned summary representations.” Neighbors include SimFormer (Gloeckler et al., 2024) using transformers for SBI, supporting the approach.
- **Break condition:** If the Set Transformer fails to capture critical dependencies in the data for certain model families (e.g., circular diffusion with angular responses) or when the summary dimension L is too small.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Conditional flow matching learns accurate, multi-modal posteriors without restrictive assumptions and permits efficient sampling via ODE integration.
- **Mechanism:** Flow matching trains a neural network to approximate a velocity field ϑ that transports a Gaussian noise distribution Z₁ to the target posterior B along linear paths Zₜ = (1−t)B + tZ₁. The loss matches the learned velocity to the conditional velocity (Z₁ − B) for active parameters.
- **Core assumption:** The flow matching objective yields a consistent estimator of the posterior when the network has sufficient capacity and simulation budget.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “flow matching objective to learn target posteriors.” [section 2.3] Detailed derivation of the flow matching loss and its use with the decoder. [corpus] Arruda et al. (2025) review shows flow matching is competitive for SBI, providing external support.
- **Break condition:** If the posterior is too complex (e.g., strongly multi-modal with many modes) or the simulation budget is insufficient for the amortization scope, the learned velocity field may be inaccurate.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Amortized Inference  
  **Why needed:** CogFormer extends amortized SBI to multiple models; understanding amortization explains the core benefit of “train once, infer many.”  
  **Quick check:** What is the key advantage of amortized inference over iterative methods like MCMC? After training, inference on new data is fast, avoiding repeated optimization.
- **Concept:** Set Transformer  
  **Why needed:** The encoder uses a Set Transformer to handle variable-sized inputs (different trial counts and designs) without padding.  
  **Quick check:** How does a Set Transformer maintain permutation invariance? Through self-attention operations that treat set elements symmetrically.
- **Concept:** Flow Matching  
  **Why needed:** CogFormer uses flow matching to learn flexible posterior distributions that can be sampled efficiently via ODE solves.  
  **Quick check:** What does the time variable t represent in flow matching? It parameterizes the interpolation between noise and data along a probability path.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Meta-simulator -> Encoder (Set Transformer) -> Decoder (mixed-attention) -> Velocity output û  
  Training: Simulate (B, A) -> Encode -> Sample t, Zₜ -> Decode -> Loss  
  Inference: Encode (X,Y) -> Integrate ODE from Z₁ to B̂
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Sample model family F, design X, mask M, coefficient matrix B from meta-simulator.  
  2. Simulate observations Y from the selected cognitive model.  
  3. Encode (X, Y) → S via Set Transformer.  
  4. **Training:** sample t ~ Uniform(0,1), compute Zₜ, decode Zₜ, S, embeddings → û; minimize masked loss ∥M⊙(û − (Z₁ − B))∥².  
  5. **Inference:** initialize Z₁ ~ N(0,I), integrate ODE dZₜ/dt = û to obtain samples B̂.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **GLM meta-simulator:** Broad coverage but may not capture all model nuances (e.g., non-linear dependencies). Adding new models may require re-expression in GLM form.  
  - **Flow matching:** Flexible posteriors but requires more simulations than mean-field methods; ODE integration can be slow if high precision is needed.  
  - **Transformer size:** Larger models improve performance but increase memory/compute; paper uses 8 layers and 256dim as compromise.  
  - **Mask representation:** Binary masks handle nested configurations well but cannot express arbitrary structural changes (e.g., adding a new accumulator in RDM).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Poor recovery on held-out configurations: high NRMSE (>0.15) or low posterior contraction (<0.7), especially for rarely activated parameters.  
  - Calibration errors: ECDF deviating from ideal uniform distribution, indicating over/under-confidence.  
  - High C2ST values (>0.7) between meta-amortized and single-model posteriors, suggesting joint distribution mismatch.  
  - Training instability: flow matching loss not decreasing or ODE solver diverging during inference.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline replication:** Train a single-model flow matching network for the DDM with intercept-only configuration and compare NRMSE/calibration to Table 1 baseline. Ensures implementation correctness.  
  2. **Family-level amortization:** Train CogFormer(F) on the DDM family with all five benchmark configurations. Evaluate on a held-out configuration (e.g., “with interaction”) and measure recovery correlations. Verifies generalization within family.  
  3. **Cross-family transfer:** Use the class-level CogFormer(C) trained on DDM, RDM, CDM. Sample a random configuration (e.g., RDM with fixed variability) and check if posterior means are within prior ranges and calibration is acceptable. Tests scope of amortization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** Can the CogFormer framework be extended to handle hierarchical Bayesian models and dynamic cognitive models, which involve non-exchangeable structures and latent temporal dependencies?  
  **Basis:** [explicit] Authors state it only deals with exchangeable models and cannot yet amortize hierarchical or dynamic models.  
  **Why unresolved:** Current meta-simulator and transformer assume exchangeability across trials and parameters; hierarchical and dynamic models introduce additional structure not accommodated.  
  **What evidence would resolve:** Develop modifications to support hierarchical priors and dynamic processes; validate on benchmarks like hierarchical DDM, showing comparable performance to single-model SBI.
- **Question 2:** Can new model families be integrated into a pre-trained CogFormer without full retraining, using fine-tuning or continual learning techniques, and what is the impact on posterior accuracy?  
  **Basis:** [explicit] Authors note adding entirely new models would currently necessitate retraining or fine-tuning; they intend to explore this.  
  **Why unresolved:** Current training is a single joint optimization over a fixed set; introducing a new family may cause catastrophic interference or require adaptation.  
  **What evidence would resolve:** Implement fine-tuning/continual learning strategies on a CogFormer trained on a subset of families; evaluate on a held-out family, measuring degradation on old tasks and accuracy on new.
- **Question 3:** What factors determine the amortization gap in joint posterior structure observed between CogFormer and single-model baselines, and how can it be minimized given a fixed simulation budget?  
  **Basis:** [inferred] C2ST shows a modest amortization gap that shrinks with increased simulation budget; underlying cause not analyzed.  
  **Why unresolved:** The gap suggests the meta-amortized posterior does not exactly match single-model posteriors; whether due to architectural constraints, insufficient simulation, or inherent trade-offs is unclear.  
  **What evidence would resolve:** Systematic ablation studies varying model family similarity, network size, and simulation budget to identify key factors; theoretical analysis of representation learning under multi-task distribution.
- **Question 4:** How does CogFormer perform on real cognitive experimental data, where model misspecification and measurement noise may differ from simulations?  
  **Basis:** [inferred] All experiments used simulated data from known generative models; no test on actual participant data.  
  **Why unresolved:** Real data may violate assumptions (e.g., parameter distributions, noise properties), degrading performance; model selection uncertainty not addressed.  
  **What evidence would resolve:** Apply to benchmark datasets (e.g., from SSM literature), compare parameter estimates and model fits to traditional MCMC or single-model SBI, and assess robustness to misspecification.

## Limitations
- The GLM meta-simulator may not cover non-GLM models (e.g., hierarchical), and training priors may not span all experimental designs.
- The reported 32M simulations could be insufficient for complex posteriors, causing the amortization gap.
- Missing hyperparameter details (optimizer, LR schedule) hinder reproducibility.
- Evaluation only on simulated data leaves real-world validity open.

## Confidence
- **Meta-simulator expressivity:** Medium (self-proposed mechanism, limited external validation).
- **Set Transformer generalization:** Medium (architecture known but new use case).
- **Flow matching accuracy:** Medium (supported by SBI literature but not directly compared here).
- **Overall ~15% NRMSE claim:** Medium (validated on limited configurations).
