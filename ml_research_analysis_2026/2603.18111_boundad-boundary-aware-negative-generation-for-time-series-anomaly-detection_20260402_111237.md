# BoundAD: Boundary-Aware Negative Generation for Time Series Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18111
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18111
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** Achieves AUC scores of 0.9606 on Collective Seasonal anomalies and 0.9438 on Point anomalies, with notably lower false positives on pattern anomalies.

## Executive Summary
BoundAD addresses a critical limitation in contrastive time series anomaly detection: the poor quality of negative samples. Existing methods struggle to generate negatives that preserve temporal semantics while effectively supervising the decision boundary. This paper proposes a three-stage reconstruction-driven framework. First, a reconstruction model is pretrained on normal data. Second, a reinforcement learning agent adaptively perturbs this model’s parameters to generate hard negative samples from normal inputs, steering them toward the normal manifold boundary without predefined anomaly injection. Third, triplet contrastive learning and a trainable prototype module refine the representation, enlarging the separation between normal and anomalous patterns. Experimental results demonstrate competitive detection performance and improved generalizability, particularly for pattern anomalies.

## Method Summary
BoundAD’s pipeline begins with pretraining a reconstruction network on normal time series windows. An RL agent then interacts with this network: based on the recent reconstruction loss history and prior update magnitude, it outputs an action that determines a signed gradient step applied to the reconstruction model. This controlled perturbation transforms a normal input into a pseudo-anomalous sample located near the normal manifold boundary. These generated negatives, alongside augmented normal samples, train an encoder via triplet contrastive loss to maximize the geometric margin in latent space. Finally, a set of learnable prototype vectors is optimized with multiple losses (normal compactness, anomaly separation, prototype dispersion, and balancing) to model the normal manifold structure and produce anomaly scores via minimum prototype distance.

## Key Results
- AUC of **0.9606** on Collective Seasonal anomalies.
- AUC of **0.9438** on Point anomalies.
- **Lower false positives** on pattern anomalies compared to state-of-the-art methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Reinforcement learning adaptively perturbs a pretrained reconstruction model to generate boundary-aware hard negatives from normal samples without predefined anomaly injection.
- **Mechanism:** An RL agent (Actor network) outputs an action `a_t` based on a state vector comprising recent reconstruction losses and the previous signed update. This action is mapped to a signed step `u_t` that controls a single manual gradient update on the reconstruction model's parameters. The updated model then reconstructs the normal input, producing a pseudo-anomalous window `X_neg`. The reward incentivizes updates that move the reconstruction loss toward a target band, encouraging samples that are semantically shifted but still near the normal manifold boundary.
- **Core assumption:** The reconstruction error of a normal sample serves as a proxy for its proximity to the normal manifold boundary. Perturbing model parameters in a controlled direction can induce outputs that lie near this boundary, providing informative negatives for contrastive learning.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "proposes a reconstruction-driven framework using reinforcement learning to adaptively adjust the optimization update magnitude according to the current reconstruction state." [section 2.3] Details the state construction, action mapping, reward function (`r_t = |l_before - l*| - |l_after - l*| + I(...)`), and the signed step update rule (`θ' = θ - u_t ∇θL_rec`).
- **Break condition:** If the RL policy fails to converge or generates samples that are either indistinguishable from normal samples (loss within band but no semantic shift) or completely semantically corrupted (loss far from band after update), the boundary-aware property degrades. This would manifest as poor triplet loss convergence in Stage 2 or lack of separation in the prototype space.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Triplet contrastive learning with RL-generated pseudo-anomalies enlarges the geometric margin between normal and anomalous patterns in the latent space.
- **Mechanism:** Using samples from Stage 2 (normal `X_pos`, lightly augmented normal `~X_pos`, and pseudo-anomalous `X_neg`), an encoder `f(·)` is trained with a triplet loss (`L_tri`) that pushes `f(X_pos)` and `f(~X_pos)` closer together while pulling `f(X_neg)` away by a margin `m`. A compactness loss (`L_com`) further tightens the normal cluster.
- **Core assumption:** Pseudo-anomalies generated via the RL-guided reconstruction perturbation are sufficiently challenging (hard negatives) and semantically aligned with the normal boundary to force the encoder to learn discriminative features for anomaly detection.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "induce samples near the normal manifold boundary for contrastive learning." [section 2.4] Explicitly defines the triplet composition and losses (`L_tri`, `L_com`, and combined `L_stage2`).
- **Break condition:** If the RL-generated negatives are not truly "hard" (e.g., too easy or too far), the triplet loss may converge to a trivial margin or fail to create separation. Evidence would be a decreasing `L_tri` without corresponding improvement in downstream AUC or prototype separation metrics, or embeddings where normal and pseudo-anomaly clusters overlap.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A trainable prototype model with multi-objective losses further refines the normal manifold and amplifies anomaly deviations, providing a robust anomaly score via minimum prototype distance.
- **Mechanism:** After Stage 2, a set of `K` learnable prototype vectors `{c_k}` is initialized from normal embeddings. The encoder and prototypes are jointly optimized with four losses: normal compactness (`L_n`) attracts normal samples to prototypes; anomaly separation (`L_a`) pushes pseudo-anomalies away; prototype dispersion (`L_sep`) prevents prototype collapse; and prototype balancing (`L_bal`) ensures uniform usage. During inference, the anomaly score is the squared distance to the nearest prototype.
- **Core assumption:** Multiple prototypes can better model the multimodal structure of normal data, and jointly optimizing these constraints creates a more discriminative and calibrated scoring function than using a single centroid or static clustering.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "prototype-driven discriminative refinement." [section 2.5] Details all four loss terms and the joint training procedure. [section 3.3.2] Observes that normal compactness remains low and prototypes stay separated, suggesting the losses are functioning as intended.
- **Break condition:** If prototype collapse occurs (many prototypes unused, as indicated by imbalanced `α_k(z)` assignments), or if pseudo-anomalies are not sufficiently separated (high `L_a`), the scoring function degenerates. Failure signatures include a single prototype dominating, high variance in anomaly scores for normal windows, or degraded AUC/F1 compared to an ablation without prototypes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reconstruction-based Representation Learning**
  - **Why needed:** Stage 1 pretrains a reconstruction model to capture normal temporal patterns, providing the feature extractor and a manifold proximity signal (reconstruction loss) for the RL agent.
  - **Quick check:** Why is a reconstruction model suitable as a "manifold estimator" for normal time series, and what are its limitations when anomalies are present in the training data?

- **Contrastive Learning with Hard Negatives**
  - **Why needed:** The core of Stage 2 relies on a discriminative objective that requires informative negative samples. The paper's premise is that hard negatives near the decision boundary are more effective than random or predefined injected anomalies.
  - **Quick check:** What makes a negative sample "hard" in the context of TSAD, and why might boundary-aware negatives be superior to simple time-shifted negatives or augmented normals?

- **Reinforcement Learning for Adaptive Control**
  - **Why needed:** RL provides a framework to adaptively decide the magnitude and sign of perturbations to the reconstruction model based on its current state (loss history), replacing fixed heuristic rules for generating pseudo-anomalies.
  - **Quick check:** How does the reward function (`r_t`) encourage the generation of boundary-adjacent samples rather than arbitrarily corrupted ones?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Normal window → Reconstruction Network (`M_θ`) → RL Agent (Actor `π_ϕ`) → Signed Step Mapper → Pseudo-Anomaly Generator (parameter update `θ'` and reconstruction `X_neg`) → Buffer → Triplet Encoder (`f(·)`) → Prototype Module (`C={c_k}`) → Scoring function `min_k ||f(X_i) - c_k||^2`.
- **Critical path:** Pretrain `M_θ` on normal data. For RL episodes: compute state from `l_t`, get action `a_t` → `u_t` → update `θ` to `θ'` → generate `X_neg`. Collect `(X_pos, ~X_pos, X_neg)` triplets. Train `f(·)` with triplet loss. Initialize `C` from normal embeddings, then jointly train `f(·)` and `C` with multi-loss objectives.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Band width in RL reward: narrow band risks no reward and poor policy; wide band yields non-boundary samples.
  - Prototype count `K`: more prototypes better model multimodal normals but increase overfitting risk and data needs.
  - Stage 2 vs. Stage 3 encoder: joint fine-tuning can improve coherence but risks catastrophic forgetting if loss weights are mis-specified.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - RL phase: Actor loss not decreasing, or `u_t` distribution highly skewed (e.g., always zero).
  - Triplet training: `L_tri` decreases but `L_a` remains high for pseudo-anomalies, or normal `L_n` does not decrease.
  - Prototype collapse: One or two prototypes dominate assignment statistics (`α_k ≈ 1` for few `k`).
  - Inference: High variance in anomaly scores for normal validation windows.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Run RL on held-out normal data; plot histogram of `u_t` values and `l_t` trajectories. Expect non-zero `u_t` frequently and `l_t` oscillating around target band.
  2. After Stage 2, project `f(X_pos)`, `f(~X_pos)`, `f(X_neg)` via t-SNE/UMAP. Expect tight cluster for normals with negatives forming a surrounding or distinct cloud.
  3. After Stage 3, check prototype assignment distribution for uniformity and compare anomaly score distributions between normal and anomalous windows. Expect low variance for normals and high separation.

## Limitations
- RL convergence and reward sparsity are not thoroughly analyzed.
- Using reconstruction loss as a manifold proxy may fail for complex or multivariate series.
- The three-stage pipeline risks catastrophic forgetting; no mitigation is described.
- Evaluation is restricted to two datasets, limiting generalizability claims.
- Sensitivity of the prototype module (e.g., number `K`, loss weights) is not ablated.
