# Scaling Data Difficulty: Improving Coding Models via Reinforcement Learning on Fresh and Challenging Problems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07779
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07779
- **Reference Count:** 9
- **Primary Result:** Training on the curated MicroCoder dataset yields 3× larger performance gains on LiveCodeBench within 300 RL steps versus DeepCoder, with up to 17.2% relative improvement overall and +40.4% on LeetCode Medium.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of low-quality, imbalanced data in code generation training. It introduces a four-stage processing pipeline that automatically filters for multi-dimensional difficulty and recency to create MicroCoder, a 13,300-problem dataset of recent competitive programming challenges. The authors show that training standard RL algorithms (GRPO, DAPO) on MicroCoder leads to substantial and consistent improvements on unseen, difficult benchmarks like LiveCodeBench, especially for medium and hard problems. The work validates that deliberate curation for problem difficulty and freshness is a highly effective lever for boosting code model capabilities.

## Method Summary
The authors construct MicroCoder via: (1) **Collection** from diverse public (TACO, OlympicCoder, DeepCoder) and private contest sources. (2) **Processing** to standardize formats, remove noise, and optimize test cases (cap at 15 longest per problem). (3) **Filtering** using hard requirements (text-only, unique, unseen) followed by an adaptive **predict-calibrate-select** stage: an LLM scores problems on five weighted difficulty dimensions, scores are calibrated against a base model's pass rates to set thresholds (e.g., remove score <2.5), and simplistic problems are dropped. (4) **Verification** via manual spot-checks. The resulting dataset is then used for RL training (GRPO/DAPO) with binary pass/fail rewards, and models are evaluated on strictly unseen benchmarks (LiveCodeBench, AtCoder, LeetCode).

## Key Results
- Training on MicroCoder for 300 steps achieves 3× larger gains on LiveCodeBench compared to training on DeepCoder.
- Relative improvement peaks at 17.2% overall, with a dramatic +40.4% on LeetCode Medium (under DAPO).
- Gains are consistent across model scales (1.7B to 14B) and RL algorithms (GRPO, DAPO), confirming the robustness of difficulty-aware curation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Difficulty-Aware Filtering
- **Claim:** Removing simplistic problems improves performance on medium/hard benchmarks by focusing training on tasks that stretch model capabilities.
- **Mechanism:** The predict-calibrate-select pipeline uses GPT-4O to score problems across five dimensions (Algorithmic Thinking 45%, Implementation Difficulty 35%, etc.). Scores are calibrated against actual model pass rates (Qwen-3-4B-thinking) to set adaptive thresholds (easy/medium at 2.5). Filtering removes 30% of data but discards >65% of easy problems.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract reports strong gains on medium/hard problems; Section 2.2 details filtering efficacy; neighboring work supports LLM-based difficulty perception.
- **Break Condition:** Poor calibration correlation between LLM scores and model performance leads to misclassification; gains may not materialize if the model lacks capacity for harder problems.

### Mechanism 2: Recency Emphasis
- **Claim:** Prioritizing recent contest problems reduces memorization from pretraining and improves generalization to fresh benchmarks.
- **Mechanism:** Collection focuses on problems from recent contests (AtCoder, CodeChef) and verifies low cosine similarity (0.04–0.14) with test benchmarks, minimizing train-test contamination.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract emphasizes recency; Section 3 reports low similarity metrics.
- **Break Condition:** If recent problems are still present in pretraining due to data leakage, or if they are noisier, the advantage diminishes.

### Mechanism 3: Standardization and Test-Case Optimization
- **Claim:** Unified formats and high-quality, optimized test cases reduce reward noise and improve RL training efficiency.
- **Mechanism:** All problems are converted to a single prompt format; missing test cases are LLM-generated; excessive cases are capped at the 15 longest to focus on complexity.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract cites addressing format inconsistency and data quality; Section 2.1.2 details steps; Figure 6 shows higher test accuracy with lower training critic reward for MicroCoder.
- **Break Condition:** Format conversion errors or inadequate test case selection corrupt the reward signal.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:**
  - Raw Data Sources -> Collection -> Processing -> Filtering -> Verification -> MicroCoder Dataset
  - MicroCoder + Base Model (e.g., Qwen3-4B) -> RL Training (GRPO/DAPO) -> Fine-tuned Model
  - Fine-tuned Model -> Evaluation (LiveCodeBench, AtCoder, LeetCode) -> Metrics
- **Critical Path:**
  1. Gather raw problems from public and private sources.
  2. Process: translate, denoise, standardize format, optimize test cases.
  3. Filter: apply hard requirements (uniqueness, unseen), then predict-calibrate-select difficulty.
  4. Manually verify final set.
  5. Train model with RL on MicroCoder.
  6. Evaluate on unseen benchmarks.
- **Design Tradeoffs:**
  - Recency vs. diversity: Emphasizing recent contests may exclude valuable older problems; pipeline includes both to balance.
  - Test case length vs. coverage: Capping at 15 longest assumes length ≈ difficulty, potentially missing critical short edge cases.
  - LLM-based scoring: Relies on costly GPT-4O with potential bias; multiple assessments average noise but add compute.
  - Calibration specificity: Thresholds based on Qwen-3-4B-thinking may not generalize to other models without re-calibration.
- **Failure Signatures:**
  - Poor calibration: Low correlation between LLM scores and model pass rates causes ineffective filtering.
  - Over-filtering: Threshold too low drastically reduces dataset diversity, harming learning.
  - Format conversion errors: Mistakes render problems unsolvable in the expected format.
  - RL instability: Dataset too difficult/easy causes policy plateau or divergence.
  - Flawed test case generation: LLM-generated tests are incorrect or incomplete, corrupting rewards.
- **First 3 Experiments:**

  3. Filtering analysis: apply the difficulty filter to existing datasets (OlympicCoder, KodCode) and show filtered versions improve performance (Table 1).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can the difficulty-aware curation framework be applied to other code-related tasks such as program repair and code translation?
- How can automatic difficulty assessment be made dynamic to adapt to improving model capabilities over time?
- How reliable is GPT-4O-based multi-dimensional difficulty scoring compared to human judgments?
- Are the fixed weights of the five difficulty dimensions optimal for all model scales and problem categories?

## Limitations
- Private raw data sources for MicroCoder are undisclosed, hindering full replication.
- LLM scoring prompts, calibration method details, and exact DAPO implementation are missing.
- Train-test leakage risk persists despite low similarity metrics; test-case quality not independently validated.
- Relative contributions of difficulty vs. recency are not disentangled.

## Confidence
- **Difficulty filtering yields medium/hard gains**: Medium – strong ablation but calibration opacity.
- **Recency ensures generalization**: Medium – low similarity, but leakage not fully ruled out.
- **Standardization/test-cases improve RL efficiency**: Low – indirect evidence, no direct ablation.

## Next Checks
1. **Calibration replication**: Score a public dataset (e.g., TACO) with the LLM dimensions; compute correlation with model pass rates; verify threshold stability across scales.
2. **Recency ablation**: Train on difficulty-matched subsets with and without recent contests; benchmark on LiveCodeBench to isolate the recency effect.
3. **Test-case audit**: Manually evaluate 200 randomly sampled LLM-generated test cases for correctness and edge-case coverage; correlate failures with RL reward variance.
