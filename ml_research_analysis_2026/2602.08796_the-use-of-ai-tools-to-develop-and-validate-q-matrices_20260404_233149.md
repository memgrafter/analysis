# The Use of AI Tools to Develop and Validate Q-Matrices

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08796
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08796
- Reference count: 3
- Primary result: In May 2025, Gemini 2.5 Pro achieved κ = 0.63, exceeding human experts; by Jan 2026, best model (Gemini 3 Pro) dropped to κ = 0.42, all underperforming humans.

## Executive Summary
The study evaluated whether AI tools could automate the construction of Q-matrices for cognitive diagnostic modeling using a reading comprehension test. AI models and four human experts received identical training materials and coding instructions; outputs were compared to a validated Q-matrix via Cohen’s kappa. In May 2025, Google Gemini 2.5 Pro achieved the highest agreement (κ = 0.63), surpassing all human experts.

A follow-up in January 2026 using newer model versions revealed a sharp decline: the top model, Gemini 3 Pro, reached only κ = 0.42, and every AI model scored below the human experts. The findings highlight challenges in AI consistency and rule adherence over time, suggesting that while AI shows promise, reliability remains a concern for Q-matrix development.

## Method Summary
The researchers used a validated 20-item reading comprehension Q-matrix (covering five skills) as the ground truth. AI models from major providers (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, DeepSeek) and four domain experts were given identical materials: the test passages, items, skill definitions, and coding scheme. AI outputs were generated via zero-shot prompting, requesting a binary matrix plus justifications. Agreement with the reference was measured using Cohen’s kappa. Models were evaluated at two timepoints—May 2025 (e.g., ChatGPT 4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro) and January 2026 (e.g., ChatGPT 5.2, Gemini 3 Pro)—to compare performance across iterations.

## Key Results
- Gemini 2.5 Pro (May 2025) achieved κ = 0.63, the highest among all raters, including human experts.
- All AI models in January 2026 performed worse; the best (Gemini 3 Pro) attained κ = 0.42.
- Every AI model underperformed the human experts in the follow-up, indicating a regression in alignment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Differences in AI model outputs stem from how each model processes and interprets the *identical* provided training materials and cognitive framework.
- **Mechanism:** Each model's pre-training data, architecture, and instruction-tuning determine how it parses the reading passages, items, skill definitions, and coding scheme. This leads to divergent internal representations of the "required skill" construct, resulting in varied 0/1 assignments.
- **Core assumption:** The validated Q-matrix (Li & Suen, 2013) represents a stable ground truth, and variation in AI output is primarily due to model-specific interpretation of the task, not noise in the benchmark.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.2] "AI models were asked to follow the same procedures as the human experts and were provided with identical training materials."
  - [section 3.1] "Gemini 2.5 Pro showed the highest agreement... ChatGPT 4o (κ = 0.12)." Demonstrates large performance spread from same input.
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighbor papers focus on evaluation alignment, not mechanistic causes for Q-matrix divergence.
- **Break condition:** If multiple, independent runs of the *same* AI model on the *same* prompt produce highly inconsistent Q-matrices (low intra-model reliability), the mechanism shifts from deterministic interpretation to stochastic instability.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Advances in general reasoning capability in newer model versions do not guarantee better adherence to specific, provided coding rules, potentially harming task alignment.
- **Mechanism:** Newer models employ more aggressive reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) or "constitutional AI" to improve helpfulness and reasoning. This can incentivize models to "improve" or reinterpret the task (e.g., questioning the necessity of linguistic skills) rather than strictly executing the provided schema.
- **Core assumption:** The primary goal for the AI is rule-following fidelity, not open-ended reasoning about the construct.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] "ChatGPT 5.2 did not code linguistic skills... justified this decision by stating that 'vocabulary and syntax were not coded because no item hinges on...'"
  - [section 3.2] "This pattern is consistent with prior literature indicating that instruction-tuned large language models are encouraged to reason about and reinterpret rules rather than adhere to them rigidly (Bai et al., 2022)."
  - [abstract] "newer AI versions... revealed lower agreement... all models underperforming the human experts."
- **Break condition:** If a newer model's justification for deviations aligns perfectly with the theoretical framework of the original study (Li & Suen, 2013), the break is in the benchmark's assumptions, not the model's adherence.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Both human and AI raters share a relative strength in identifying comprehension-based skills (Skills 3-5) over linguistic skills (Skills 1-2) due to the inherent concreteness of the former in the item texts.
- **Mechanism:** Skills like "extracting explicit information" (Skill 3) have more direct, surface-level textual correlates (e.g., a fact stated verbatim). Skills like "vocabulary" (Skill 1) or "syntax" (Skill 2) require inference about lexical difficulty or grammatical complexity, which is more abstract, subjective, and potentially inconsistently defined in the training materials.
- **Core assumption:** The validated Q-matrix's assignments for linguistic skills are the most contentious or difficult to operationalize, creating a "hard sub-problem" where both human and AI agreement are naturally lower.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1] "Both human experts and AI tools showed better accuracy in coding comprehension skills... than linguistic skills."
  - [section 2.1] The skills are defined: linguistic (vocabulary, syntax) vs. comprehension (extracting, connecting, inferring).
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers on "expert-aligned evaluation" (e.g., arxiv:2504.13068) suggest alignment problems are acute for abstract/nuanced categories.
- **Break condition:** If a model shows *higher* agreement on linguistic skills than comprehension skills, it suggests the model has adopted a non-human, possibly lexical-over-paraphrase heuristic that diverges from the intended construct.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Q-Matrix Structure**
  - **Why needed here:** It is the core artifact being generated and validated. Understanding its binary (item x skill) format is essential to interpret kappa scores and diagnose where agreements/disagreements occur (e.g., row-wise vs. column-wise).
  - **Quick check question:** "If an item requires Skill 1 (Vocabulary) and Skill 4 (Connecting), what does its row in the Q-matrix look like?"
- **Concept: Cognitive Diagnostic Modeling (CDM)**
  - **Why needed here:** Provides the *purpose* for the Q-matrix. The validity of downstream CDM inferences (e.g., skill mastery estimates) is entirely dependent on the Q-matrix's correctness. This contextualizes why agreement with a "validated" matrix matters.
  - **Quick check question:** "What would be the consequence for a student's diagnostic profile if their Q-matrix incorrectly omitted a required skill for a key item?"
- **Concept: Cohen's Kappa (κ)**
  - **Why needed here:** It is the primary metric for quantifying agreement between AI/human outputs and the benchmark. Understanding its interpretation (e.g., κ=0.63 = "good" per Landis & Koch) is critical to judge practical significance.
  - **Quick check question:** "Does a κ of 0.42 indicate 'moderate' or 'fair' agreement according to the study's cited guidelines?"

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  **Input:** Raw test (4 passages, 20 MC items) + Skill definitions + Coding instructions + Example annotations (from Li & Suen, 2013).
  2.  **Processing Engine:** LLM (Gemini, ChatGPT, etc.) via API. Zero-shot/few-shot prompt parsing.
  3.  **Output:** Raw text response containing justifications and a proposed 20x5 binary matrix.
  4.  **Post-Processor:** Script to extract matrix from text, handle parsing errors (e.g., if model outputs prose instead of table).
  5.  **Validator:** Code to compute pairwise Cohen's κ against the validated benchmark matrix.
- **Critical path:** Input Preparation -> Prompt Engineering -> LLM Inference -> Output Parsing -> Kappa Calculation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Prompt Specificity vs. Flexibility:** A very detailed prompt with examples may improve adherence but reduce a model's ability to use its internal knowledge. A vague prompt increases variance.
  - **Zero-shot vs. Fine-tuning:** Zero-shot is generalizable but less reliable. Fine-tuning on the validated Q-matrix would artificially inflate agreement and lack ecological validity for assisting *new* test development.
  - **Single Output vs. Ensemble:** Using one model output is simplest. Averaging or majority-vote ensembles from multiple models/run seeds could improve reliability but obscures individual model characteristics.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Parsing Failure:** Model response lacks a clear matrix; requires manual correction or fails entirely.
  - **Schema Drift:** Model changes the skill set (e.g., adds/removes skills), making κ computation impossible without forced mapping.
  - **Categorical Collapse:** Model assigns 0 to all items for an entire skill column (as with ChatGPT 5.2 and linguistic skills), leading to very low κ.
  - **High Intra-Model Variance:** The same prompt & model yields different matrices across API calls (due to temperature), indicating non-deterministic output is a primary failure mode.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Prompt Robustness Replication:** Re-run the May 2025 prompt on the Jan 2026 models (and vice-versa) to isolate the effect of *model update* from *prompt sensitivity*. Does the 5.2 model improve if given the older, more permissive prompt?
  2.  **Stability Stress Test:** For the best May 2025 model (Gemini 2.5 Pro), run the same prompt 30 times with temperature=0.7. Measure the variance in the produced Q-matrix (e.g., using Fleiss' kappa across runs). Quantify the "noise floor" from stochastic generation.
  3.  **Skill Category Diagnostic:** For the Jan 2026 models, compute separate κ scores for the "Linguistic" (Skills 1-2) and "Comprehension" (Skills 3-5) skill subsets. Test if the observed overall drop is driven primarily by catastrophic failure on linguistic skills (as suggested for ChatGPT 5.2) or a uniform decline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do AI tools' performances in generating Q-matrices generalize across diverse reading comprehension assessments and varying cognitive skill frameworks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states future research should "examine different reading assessments and cognitive frameworks."
- **Why unresolved:** The study used only one reading test with a specific five-skill framework, limiting understanding of AI robustness to different constructs and test designs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the study with multiple reading assessments (e.g., narrative vs. expository) and alternative skill taxonomies, comparing AI-generated Q-matrices to expert-validated ones across contexts.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What specific prompting strategies (e.g., few-shot examples, step-by-step reasoning instructions, attribute definitions) most improve the accuracy and consistency of AI-generated Q-matrices for complex reading skills?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion calls for exploring "alternative prompting strategies and training approaches."
- **Why unresolved:** The study used a single zero-shot prompt, providing no insight into how prompt engineering might mitigate observed errors, particularly for linguistic skills.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically vary prompt designs (e.g., providing exemplars, requiring justifications per skill) in a factorial experiment, measuring changes in Cohen's kappa with the validated Q-matrix and reduction in skill-specific errors.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What mechanism explains the substantial degradation in AI model alignment with the validated Q-matrix from May 2025 (Gemini 2.5 Pro, κ=0.63) to January 2026 (Gemini 3 Pro, κ=0.42), despite general model improvements?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper documents a sharp performance drop for newer versions and speculates models may "reinterpret task requirements" instead of adhering to provided rules.
- **Why unresolved:** The authors hypothesize about rule reinterpretation but did not analyze model outputs or updates to identify whether changes stemmed from altered training data, safety fine-tuning, or architectural shifts affecting rule-following.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a fine-grained error analysis comparing item-level coding from both model versions, correlated with release notes and internal model documentation (if available) to pinpoint changes in handling ambiguity or instruction adherence.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** To what extent does the inherent complexity and abstractness of reading comprehension skills (e.g., inference vs. vocabulary) differentially impact AI versus human expert accuracy in Q-matrix construction?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Figure 2 shows both humans and AIs performed better on comprehension skills than linguistic skills, but the paper does not analyze whether the *gap* in performance between AI and human varies by skill type.
- **Why unresolved:** The study aggregates skill-level kappas but does not test if AI's disadvantage (or advantage) is concentrated in specific skill categories, which is crucial for understanding AI's cognitive modeling limits.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform a mixed-effects analysis with skill category as a predictor, interacting with rater type (AI vs. human), using item-level coding data to identify which skills show the greatest divergence from the validated matrix.

## Limitations
- Material access: The exact reading test materials from Li & Suen (2013) are unreported, preventing direct replication of AI prompts.
- Human benchmark: The four expert Q-matrices are unavailable, limiting full comparison (AI vs. human vs. validated).
- Causal speculation: Explanations for performance drops are not experimentally validated, remaining inferential.

## Confidence
- Newer models underperformed older ones: High
- Performance divergence stems from model-specific interpretation: Medium
- Linguistic skills are a "hard sub-problem": Medium
