# FreqLens: Interpretable Frequency Attribution for Time Series Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08768  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08768  
- **Reference count:** 21  
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art or competitive forecasting performance while automatically discovering physically meaningful frequency cycles (e.g., 24-hour and 12-hour patterns on Traffic, weekly cycles on Weather) with formal attribution guarantees.

## Executive Summary
Time series forecasting models often lack interpretability, particularly at the level of frequency contributions. FreqLens addresses this gap with a framework that combines learnable frequency discovery and axiomatic attribution. It introduces sigmoid-parameterized, diversity-regularized bases to automatically identify dominant periodic patterns without prior domain knowledge. An additive prediction head, where each selected frequency has an independent contribution, ensures that attributions satisfy Completeness, Faithfulness, Null-Frequency, and Symmetry—equivalent to Shapley values. On benchmark datasets, FreqLens reduces mean squared error by up to 2× compared to strong baselines and consistently recovers known physical cycles across independent runs, providing both high accuracy and genuine frequency-level explanations.

## Method Summary
FreqLens operates in three stages. First, it learns a set of frequency bases by parameterizing frequencies via a sigmoid function to keep them within plausible bounds (`[f_min, f_max]`) and applies a diversity regularization on sorted log-frequencies to ensure spread across the spectrum. Second, it projects the input onto these bases to obtain coefficients, scores each base, and selects a sparse subset via a Gumbel-Softmax mechanism. Third, each selected frequency feeds into an independent MLP head (with no bias and ReLU activation) to produce a contribution. A residual path captures non-periodic components. The final forecast is a weighted sum of frequency contributions and the residual. The additive structure of the frequency path ensures that each frequency's contribution is its Shapley value, satisfying formal attribution axioms.

## Key Results
- Discovered the 24-hour (2.5% error) and 12-hour (1.6% error) cycles on the Traffic dataset and the weekly cycle (4.8% error) on Weather, despite input windows 10× shorter than the periods.
- Reduced MSE by 2× on Weather and 77.9% on Traffic versus baselines like DLinear and iTransformer.
- Achieved consistent frequency discovery across 5 independent runs, demonstrating stable knowledge extraction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Learnable frequency bases, parameterized via a sigmoid mapping and regularized for diversity, enable automatic discovery of dominant periodic patterns without domain knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** Frequencies are bounded as `f_i = f_min + (f_max - f_min) * sigmoid(θ_i)` to stay within physically plausible limits. A diversity loss penalizes gaps in sorted log-frequencies, encouraging well-spaced bases. Bases reconstruct the input via projection coefficients `c_i` and contribute to the forecast.
- **Core assumption:** The time series contains dominant, approximately stationary periodicities representable by a sparse set of cosine bases.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "learnable frequency discovery via sigmoid-parameterized bases and diversity regularization"; [Section 3.2] details the parameterization (Eq. 2), basis construction (Eq. 3), and diversity loss (Eq. 6).
- **Break condition:** Performance degrades on non-periodic or strongly non-stationary data; low-frequency convergence is inconsistent (Limitations, Section 5.2).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** An additive prediction head, where each selected frequency has an independent MLP contribution, provably satisfies Completeness, Faithfulness, Null-Frequency, and Symmetry axioms, with attributions equal to Shapley values.
- **Mechanism:** The forecast is a strict sum `y_hat_freq = sum_{f in S} MLP_f(c_f)`. This additive form directly maps each frequency's marginal contribution to its attribution `A(f)`. Theorem 3.2 proves the axioms hold because (A1) Completeness follows from summation, (A2) Faithfulness from marginal contribution equaling `MLP_f(c_f)`, (A3) Null-Frequency from `MLP_f` designed with no bias and ReLU (so `MLP_f(0)=0`), and (A4) Symmetry from functional identity. Additivity implies Shapley value equivalence.
- **Core assumption:** The prediction can be decomposed into independent frequency contributions without significant interactions captured by the residual path.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "axiomatic frequency attribution... with per-frequency contributions equivalent to Shapley values"; [Section 3.4] formal axioms, Theorem 3.2 proof, and additive design (Eqs. 9–10).
- **Break condition:** If the additive decomposition is violated (e.g., shared parameters across frequency MLPs or non-linear interactions between frequency features), the axioms no longer hold.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Combining learnable frequency discovery with axiomatic attribution creates a genuine knowledge discovery loop: frequencies that minimize forecast error also provide formal, faithful explanations of predictions.
- **Mechanism:** Training optimizes a joint loss (forecast error, reconstruction, diversity, sparsity). Frequencies that improve prediction while remaining diverse and reconstructing the input are selected. Their learned values are mapped to physical periods post-hoc. The additive attribution head ensures that what the model *uses* for prediction (selected frequencies) is exactly what it *explains* (attributions).
- **Core assumption:** Dominant physical periods are stable across training runs and align with minimal reconstruction error and high prediction contribution.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "genuine frequency-level knowledge discovery"; [Section 4.3.1] shows cross-seed consistency in discovered periods; [Section 4.3.2] case studies align frequencies with domain knowledge.
- **Break condition:** If diversity regularization is too weak, frequencies collapse and discovery fails. If the residual path is insufficient, non-periodic components interfere with clean frequency attribution.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Sigmoid-Parameterized Bounded Frequencies**  
  *Why needed here:* To constrain learned frequencies within physically plausible ranges (e.g., `[1/(10L), 0.5]`) without hard clamping, which causes dead gradients. The sigmoid ensures smooth gradients everywhere.  
  *Quick check:* Why use sigmoid instead of exponential (`f_i = exp(θ_i)`) or direct parameterization? What happens at the boundaries?

- **Concept: Log-Frequency Diversity Regularization**  
  *Why needed here:* To prevent multiple bases from converging to the same period. Operating in log-space ensures proportional spacing is penalized (e.g., 0.01 vs 0.02 treated similarly to 0.1 vs 0.2).  
  *Quick check:* What happens if diversity regularization is removed? How does `L_div` (Eq. 6) enforce spacing?

- **Concept: Additive Decomposition for Shapley Values**  
  *Why needed here:* Shapley values require a cooperative game with value function `v(S)`. The additive form `v(S) = sum_{f∈S} contribution(f)` makes marginal contribution `v(S) - v(S\{f})` equal `contribution(f)`, satisfying the axioms.  
  *Quick check:* Why can't standard SHAP be applied to the entire model? What structural constraint makes frequency attribution equivalent to Shapley values?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input -> Linear Projection -> Frequency Basis Generator (sigmoid `θ_i`, phase `φ_i`) -> Basis Projection (`c_i = <H, ψ_i>`), Scorer MLP (`s_i`), Gumbel-Softmax Selection (`S = TopK`) -> Independent per-frequency MLP Heads (`contribution(f)`) -> Residual MLP (`y_res`) -> Fusion (`ŷ = α * sum + (1-α) * y_res`)

- **Critical path:** Frequency discovery (θ_i, φ_i) must converge to meaningful periods → Selection stabilizes via Gumbel-Softmax → Additive attribution yields explanations. Diversity loss (`L_div`) is crucial early to avoid collapse.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Periodicity assumption vs. residual capacity: Solely relying on frequencies fails on non-periodic data; a strong residual path is essential but can dilute attribution purity (lower `α`).
  - Number of bases (N) vs. diversity: More bases risk collapse without strong `L_div`; fewer may miss important frequencies.
  - Top-K (K) selection: Small K forces sparsity but may omit relevant frequencies; large K risks including noise and violating sparse-set assumption.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Frequency collapse: Learned `f_i` cluster tightly (check sorted `f_i` plot). Indicates `λ_div` too low or initialization issue.
  - Poor reconstruction: `L_recon` remains high (>1e-3). Bases don't span input space; increase `λ_recon` or N.
  - Low `α` values (<0.3) across datasets: Model not finding periodic structure; may need longer training, adjust `λ_div`, or data may be non-periodic.
  - High variance across seeds in discovered periods: Indicates unstable training; reduce learning rate or increase `λ_div`.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablation: Remove residual path. Expect MSE to increase dramatically (cf. Table 8, Traffic: 3.14 vs 0.268). Validates residual necessity.
  2. Ablation: Remove diversity regularization (`L_div`). Check for frequency collapse (multiple `f_i` near identical). Measure reconstruction error—may stay low but discovery quality degrades.
  3. Frequency convergence tracking: Plot learned `f_i` (converted to periods) vs. training epoch. Verify they stabilize to plausible values (e.g., near 24h for hourly traffic) within 50 epochs. If not, adjust differential LR or `λ_div`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can FreqLens improve low-frequency (weekly/monthly) discovery consistency across random seeds? Current diversity regularization focuses on log-frequency spacing but doesn't address convergence speed differences between high/low frequencies.
- What causes the discrepancy between high frequency attribution (`α≈1`) and poor forecasting performance on the Electricity dataset? The paper observes this pattern but doesn't diagnose whether learned frequencies are spurious or residual capacity insufficient.
- Can FreqLens be extended to non-stationary time series where periodicity changes over time? The method assumes stationary periodicities; no solution proposed.
- Should the number of learnable frequency bases (N) be determined automatically rather than fixed? Paper uses fixed `N=32` with limited ablation; optimal N likely varies by dataset.

## Limitations
- Unspecified architectural details: "lightweight scorer" and per-frequency MLP dimensions, activation choices, and exact `L_variance` definition.
- Ambiguous Gumbel-Softmax annealing schedule (epoch vs. batch).
- Frequency collapse if `λ_div` is too low; low-frequency (weekly) convergence is inconsistent.
- Method assumes stationary periodicities; performance degrades on non-stationary or irregular series.
- Axiom guarantees rely on strict additivity; any deviation (e.g., shared parameters) invalidates the axioms.

## Confidence
- **C1 (Frequency discovery):** Medium. Sigmoid-bounded bases and diversity loss theoretically enable automatic pattern finding, but empirical instability on low-frequency cycles and failure on non-periodic data limit confidence.
- **C2 (Axiomatic attribution):** High. The additive decomposition directly satisfies the four Shapley axioms; the proof is sound under the stated structural constraints.
- **C3 (Knowledge discovery & performance):** Medium. Competitive results are claimed, but missing implementation details prevent full verification, and the residual path may dilute attribution purity.
