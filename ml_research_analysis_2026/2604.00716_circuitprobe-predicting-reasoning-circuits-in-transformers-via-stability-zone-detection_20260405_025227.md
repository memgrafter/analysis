# CircuitProbe: Predicting Reasoning Circuits in Transformers via Stability Zone Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00716
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00716
- **Reference count:** 2
- **Primary result:** CircuitProbe predicts reasoning circuit locations in under 5 CPU minutes, achieving a 3–4 order magnitude speedup over brute-force sweeps, and matches optimal circuits within 2 layers across 9 models.

## Executive Summary
Finding localized reasoning circuits in transformers via brute-force sweeps is prohibitively expensive, requiring 7–25 GPU hours per model. CircuitProbe offers a dramatic speedup by predicting circuit locations from activation statistics in under 5 minutes on CPU. The method detects two distinct circuit types: stability circuits in early layers (10–25% depth) via the derivative of representation change, and magnitude circuits in late layers (85–100% depth) via anomaly scoring. Validated across 9 models spanning 6 architectures, CircuitProbe’s top predictions match or are within 2 layers of the optimal circuit. A scaling experiment reveals that layer duplication based on its predictions benefits models under 3B parameters (+0.4% to +10.0% on benchmarks) but degrades 7B+ models.

## Method Summary
CircuitProbe operates in three stages. First, a calibration set of 10–50 text examples is processed through the model with hooks to capture residual stream activations after each layer. From these, five per-layer statistics are computed: representation change magnitude (∥hᵢ − hᵢ₋₁∥₂), self-similarity (cosine between consecutive layers), norm growth ratio (∥hᵢ∥/∥hᵢ₋₁∥), cross-example variance, and representation rank. Second, every candidate block of 3–5 consecutive layers is scored using two functions: a stability score that aggregates z-scores of the derivative of representation change, norm growth, a transition signal, and rank; and an anomaly score that aggregates z-scores of representation change magnitude, negated self-similarity, cross-example variance, and rank. Third, the two scores are min-max normalized across all blocks, and the final ranking is obtained by taking the maximum of the normalized scores per block. The entire pipeline runs in under 5 minutes on CPU.

## Key Results
- **Speedup:** Reduces circuit prediction time from 7–25 GPU hours to under 5 CPU minutes (3–4 orders of magnitude faster).
- **Detection accuracy:** Identifies stability circuits in early layers and magnitude circuits in late layers; top-1 or top-3 predictions match optimal circuits within 2 layers across all 9 tested models.
- **Scaling boundary:** Layer duplication based on CircuitProbe predictions improves performance for models under 3B parameters (+0.4% to +10.0% on benchmarks) but degrades 7B+ models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Stability Circuits via Derivative of Representation Change
- **Claim:** Stability circuits in early layers (10–25% depth) are detected by the derivative of representation change magnitude, identifying zones where representation change flattens.
- **Mechanism:** For a candidate block, the mean absolute difference between consecutive representation change values indicates volatility. Lower values signal a transition from chaotic to stable representations, where reasoning circuits are hypothesized to reside.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "stability circuits in early layers (10–25% depth), detected through the derivative of representation change"
  - [section 4.2] "In the very first layers... representation change magnitude varies by 10–50x... The stability circuit begins where this derivative flattens out"
- **Break condition:** Fails if reasoning circuits occur in volatile early layers or if the derivative profile is flat across early layers (e.g., TinyLlama’s best circuit ranked 4th).

### Mechanism 2: Magnitude Circuits via Anomaly Scoring
- **Claim:** Magnitude circuits in late layers (85–100% depth) are detected by anomaly scoring of activation statistics, identifying blocks with extreme values.
- **Mechanism:** An anomaly score aggregates z-scores of representation change magnitude, negated self-similarity, cross-example variance, and rank against model-wide distributions. Late reasoning circuits show exceptional values (e.g., 238× average change in Phi-4 layer 38), making them outliers.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "magnitude circuits in late layers (85–100% depth), detected through anomaly scoring"
  - [section 4.2] "Magnitude circuits... characterized by large absolute representation changes and high cross-example variance"
- **Break condition:** Fails if late layers are not anomalous (e.g., highly normalized) or if circuits are not magnitude-based.

### Mechanism 3: Max-Combination Ranking
- **Claim:** The final ranking uses `C(s,e) = max(Ŝ_stability, Ŝ_anomaly)` to prioritize blocks excelling on either signal.
- **Mechanism:** Stability and anomaly scores are min-max normalized to [0,1] across all candidate blocks. Taking the maximum captures both early stability and late magnitude circuits without dilution, as they are mutually exclusive in depth.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 4.3] "The final CircuitProbe score combines both signals by taking the maximum of the two normalized scores"
  - [section 5.3] On Phi-4, combined score identified both known circuits in top 10 predictions.
- **Break condition:** Could miss circuits with moderate scores on both signals, or fail if circuits overlap in depth.

## Foundational Learning
- **Representation Change Magnitude (∥hᵢ − hᵢ₋₁∥₂):** Measures per-layer transformation activity. Why needed: Base statistic for both scoring functions; low values indicate stable layers. Quick check: A layer with very low change likely preserves information; early stability circuits are where change decreases after initial volatility.
- **Derivative as Stability Indicator:** Using first difference of representation change to detect transition zones. Why needed: Identifies where representation volatility decreases, marking stability circuits. Quick check: The rate of change highlights the flattening point better than absolute change because it captures transition dynamics.
- **Z-score Normalization and Anomaly Detection:** Comparing block statistics to model-wide distribution. Why needed: Allows architecture-agnostic outlier detection without fixed thresholds. Quick check: A z-score of +2.5 for representation change means the block’s average change is 2.5 standard deviations above the mean, indicating high activity for anomaly scoring.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Calibration Data Loader -> Activation Hook -> Scoring Engine -> Ranking Aggregator
- **Critical path:** Load calibration examples -> single forward pass with hooks -> compute per-layer statistics -> sliding-window block scoring (width 3–5) -> min-max normalization of scores -> max combination per block -> output ranked list.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - The max-combination is fixed; practitioners should examine both top-stability and top-anomaly predictions separately, as they have different benefit profiles (broad vs. narrow).
  - Calibration data: only 10 examples suffice but should be representative; method is robust across languages.
  - Block size: optimal circuit size typically 3–5 layers; top-1 might be off by ±1 layer, so consider top 3–5 predictions.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - No clear stability valley in early layers (derivative flat/noisy) → stability score uninformative.
  - Late layers not anomalous (homogeneous statistics) → anomaly score fails.
  - Predictions shift with different calibration sets → indicates non-robust model or bug.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate Table 1 on Phi-4: predict stability [5–10) and magnitude [37–40) circuits; verify they appear in top predictions.
  2. Calibration robustness on Qwen2.5-0.5B: vary examples (10,20,50) and language (English, French); confirm stability circuit start position invariant.
  3. Scaling boundary probe: run on Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen2.5-7B, duplicate top stability circuit; expect improvement on 3B and degradation on 7B.

## Limitations
- Weak theoretical justification: The derivative of representation change as a stability marker has no corpus evidence.
- Heuristic ranking: Max-combination lacks analytical grounding; may fail for circuits with moderate scores on both signals.
- Implementation underspecification: Exact formulas for `t_transition` and representation rank are unclear, risking reproduction drift.
- Scaling degradation: Benefit for <3B models and harm for ≥7B models is observed but not explained.
