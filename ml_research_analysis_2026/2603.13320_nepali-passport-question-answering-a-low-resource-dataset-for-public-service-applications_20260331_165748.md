# Nepali Passport Question Answering: A Low-Resource Dataset for Public Service Applications
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13320
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13320
- **Reference Count:** 35
- **Primary Result:** Fine-tuned multilingual transformer models, particularly `intfloat/e5-base`, achieve near-perfect ranking metrics (MRR@10=1.0) on the Nepali passport FAQ retrieval task, substantially outperforming the lexical BM25 baseline.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the scarcity of annotated data for Nepali information retrieval by creating a dedicated FAQ dataset focused on passport services. The authors scraped and preprocessed official Nepali government resources, then used GPT-4 for data augmentation to expand the dataset tenfold. They fine-tuned several multilingual sentence-transformer models using Multiple Negative Ranking Loss (MNRL) and implemented a hybrid retrieval system combining dense semantic search with lexical BM25. The fine-tuned `intfloat/e5-base` model achieved the best individual performance, while a hybrid of BM25 and `e5-base` attained a perfect Mean Reciprocal Rank at 10 (MRR@10). The work demonstrates the effectiveness of transfer learning and hybrid methods for low-resource language public service applications.

## Method Summary
The methodology involves constructing a Nepali QA dataset from passport-related FAQs, preprocessing to clean and deduplicate text, and applying GPT-4-based data augmentation to generate paraphrased questions for the training and validation splits. The test set comprises 82 queries, with a corpus of 37,013 documents (820 relevant, 36,193 irrelevant). Pretrained multilingual embedding models (SBERT variants, E5) are fine-tuned on the augmented training pairs using MNRL. Evaluations compare fine-tuned dense retrievers against a BM25 lexical baseline and a hybrid approach that fuses BM25 scores with dense cosine similarities. Performance is measured via Recall@10, NDCG@10, and MRR@5/10.

## Key Results
- The `intfloat/e5-base` model achieved a Recall@10 of 0.8573, NDCG@10 of 0.8973, and perfect MRR@5 and MRR@10 (1.0000).
- The hybrid retrieval model (BM25 + `intfloat/e5-base`) also achieved a perfect MRR@10 of 1.0000, with a Recall@10 of 0.8317.
- All fine-tuned models significantly outperformed the BM25 baseline (Recall@10=0.4793).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Fine-tuned transformer embedding models capture semantic similarity in Nepali FAQ retrieval more effectively than lexical matching (BM25).
- **Mechanism:** Models are fine-tuned using Multiple Negative Ranking Loss (MNRL) on a pair-structured dataset. This optimizes the embedding space so that a query and its correct answer have a higher cosine similarity than other random answers within the batch.
- **Core assumption:** The test queries and corpus exhibit semantic patterns transferable from multilingual pre-training or learnable from domain-specific fine-tuning data.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract states fine-tuned models outperformed BM25, with multilingual E5 achieving highest performance.
  - Section VI.A reports `intfloat/e5-base` metrics (Recall@10=0.8573, NDCG@10=0.8973, perfect MRR).
- **Break condition:** If data augmentation introduces semantic drift where generated pairs misrepresent original intent, the learned embedding space degrades on held-out test queries.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A hybrid retrieval approach (BM25 + dense embedding) combines complementary lexical precision and semantic recall, leading to robust top-rank performance.
- **Mechanism:** BM25 and fine-tuned dense model produce separate relevance scores. These are normalized and combined (likely via linear sum), allowing a document strong in either signal to be highly ranked.
- **Core assumption:** Relevant documents often contain either exact keywords or semantically equivalent paraphrases, and these signals are not perfectly correlated.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract mentions hybrid retrieval integration and perfect MRR@10.
  - Section VI.A confirms hybrid improves performance and achieves perfect MRR@10.
- **Break condition:** If BM25 and dense retrievals retrieve highly overlapping wrong documents, the hybrid provides no new signal, e.g., in a homogeneous corpus or with systematic component errors.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** GPT-4-based data augmentation increases question phrasing diversity while preserving answer semantics, regularizing the model and improving generalization.
- **Mechanism:** For each original QA pair, GPT-4 generates multiple novel but semantically equivalent questions. Training on these augmented pairs forces the model to map paraphrased questions to the same answer embedding, improving robustness to lexical variation.
- **Core assumption:** GPT-4 can generate high-quality, semantically equivalent Nepali paraphrases in the passport domain. Augmentation is applied only to train/val splits, avoiding test-set leakage.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Section III.B describes augmentation using GPT-4 to generate variations while preserving meaning.
  - Table I shows dataset expanded from 548 to ~5k pairs.
- **Break condition:** If GPT-4 generates questions that alter core meaning or introduce non-representative Nepali dialects, the model learns incorrect associations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Sentence Embedding & Dense Retrieval**
  - **Why needed:** The task is to rank 37k+ documents by semantic similarity to a query. Requires understanding how a bi-encoder maps text to fixed-dimension vectors and how cosine similarity approximates relevance.
  - **Quick check:** Given two Nepali sentences, explain how MNRL ensures their vectors are closer than vectors of an unrelated sentence.
- **Concept: BM25 and Hybrid Scoring**
  - **Why needed:** BM25 is the lexical baseline and hybrid component. Understand its term-frequency/inverse-document-frequency mechanics to diagnose its failure on semantic matches and how to fuse scores with dense similarity (e.g., normalization).
  - **Quick check:** Why would BM25 fail on query "How can I get a new passport if my old one is lost?" if the document uses the synonym "मेरो पारोसो हराएको" (my passport is lost)?
- **Concept: Multiple Negative Ranking Loss (MNRL)**
  - **Why needed:** This is the fine-tuning objective. Must implement correctly: within a batch, an anchor query's similarity to its positive answer should be higher than to all other answers (negatives) in that batch.
  - **Quick check:** In a batch of size 4 with one query and three answers (one positive, two negatives), what does the loss compute? What is a failure mode in batch construction?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Raw corpus (scraped FAQs + raygx/Nepali-Extended-Text-Corpus) -> Preprocessing (HTML removal, Unicode normalization, deduplication) -> Data Augmentation (GPT-4 generates paraphrased questions) -> Embedding Model (pretrained multilingual encoder, fine-tuned via SentenceTransformer with MNRL) -> Lexical Retriever (BM25 index) -> Hybrid Scorer (normalizes and sums scores from dense and lexical retrievers) -> Evaluator (InformationRetrievalEvaluator computes metrics).
- **Critical path:** Corpus Collection -> Preprocessing & Augmentation -> Model Fine-Tuning (hyperparam search on val) -> Final Evaluation on Test -> Statistical Testing vs. BM25.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Model Size vs. Latency:** `e5-large` gives higher Recall@10 than `e5-base` (0.8902 vs 0.8573) but higher compute cost and slightly lower MRR@10 (0.9817 vs 1.0000). Base offers best balance.
  - **Augmentation Scope:** The paper augmented train/val queries and *test answers*. This expands the relevant document pool but risks inflating test performance if augmented answers are lexically closer to augmented train queries. Isolated benefit not shown.
  - **Hybrid Simplicity:** Hybrid uses simple score sum. More sophisticated methods (RRF, learned weighting) might yield gains but add complexity.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Validation loss does not decrease: Likely MNRL implementation error (e.g., not treating other batch items as negatives), poor batch construction, or incorrect LR.
  - MRR@10 << 1.0 but moderate Recall@10: Model retrieves relevant docs but cannot rank the correct one first. Check if positive embedding is pulled correctly from in-batch negatives.
  - Hybrid worse than dense alone: If BM25 and dense retrievals have high overlap and BM25 scores dominate (improper normalization), it drowns semantic signal.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate BM25 baseline:** Build BM25 index (e.g., `rank_bm25`) on preprocessed corpus. Run 82 test queries. Record metrics to confirm environment and correctness.
  2. **Fine-tune a single model (e.g., `intfloat/e5-small`):** Use SentenceTransformer, batch size=8, lr=4e-5, MNRL. Train with early stopping (patience=5). Evaluate best checkpoint. Expect lift over BM25 but not perfect MRR.
  3. **Implement and test the hybrid:** For each query, get top-100 from BM25 and dense model. Normalize scores (test per-query vs. global min-max). Sum scores, re-rank. Verify if MRR@10 reaches 1.0. If not, debug normalization and fusion logic.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** How can retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) frameworks be effectively integrated with the fine-tuned Nepali embedding models to generate contextually rich and natural answers for passport FAQs?
  - **Basis in paper:** Conclusion proposes exploring RAG combining lexical/semantic retrieval with generative models (GPT, mT5) for natural responses.
  - **Why unresolved:** Study focuses solely on retrieval; quality and fluency of generated answers remain unaddressed, critical for real-world public service.
- **Question:** To what extent do the fine-tuned Nepali embedding models generalize to other domain-specific QA tasks (e.g., healthcare, education) beyond passport services?
  - **Basis in paper:** Conclusion suggests assessing adaptability to other Nepali domains to evaluate transferability.
  - **Why unresolved:** Models trained/tested only on passport FAQ dataset; cross-domain performance unknown, limiting broader applicability.
- **Question:** What linguistic biases, semantic distortions, or unnatural constructions does GPT-4-based data augmentation introduce in the Nepali QA dataset, and how do they affect model robustness on authentic queries?
  - **Basis in paper:** Inferred from use of GPT-4 for augmentation to address low-resource constraints, with no analysis of augmentation quality or impact.
  - **Why unresolved:** Unchecked augmentation risks creating syntactically/semantically flawed Nepali variants, potentially harming model generalizability and reliability.
- **Question:** Why does the hybrid retrieval model (BM25 + e5-base) achieve perfect MRR@10 but slightly lower Recall@10 than e5-base alone, and what trade-offs does this reveal between lexical precision and semantic recall?
  - **Basis in paper:** Inferred from Table V: hybrid has Recall@10=0.8317 vs. e5-base=0.8573, despite equal/superior MRR. Unexplained interaction effects.
  - **Why unresolved:** Paper does not analyze why combining lexical and semantic methods reduces recall while maintaining top-rank accuracy; key for optimizing hybrid systems.

## Limitations
- **Major uncertainties:** (1) Data augmentation details (GPT-4 prompt, temperature, deduplication threshold) are unreported, risking semantic drift or test-set leakage. (2) Hybrid retrieval fusion formula (weighting, normalization) is unspecified, hindering exact replication. (3) Test corpus construction ambiguity—augmenting *test answers* may inflate performance by simplifying lexical matching.

## Confidence
- **Fine-tuned embeddings > BM25:** High. Directly supported by reported metrics and standard evaluation.
- **Hybrid perfect MRR@10:** Medium. Result is clear but implementation details missing; may depend on unspecified score fusion.
- **Augmentation benefit:** Low. Contribution not isolated; improvement could stem from fine-tuning alone.

## Next Checks
1. **Replicate BM25 baseline:** Build BM25 index on described corpus (37,013 docs, 82 queries). Confirm reported Recall@10=0.4793 to rule out evaluation errors.
2. **Test hybrid fusion variants:** Compare min-max per-query vs. global normalization, and linear sum vs. Reciprocal Rank Fusion, to match the paper’s perfect MRR@10.
3. **Ablate data augmentation:** Train `e5-base` *without* augmentation on original 548 pairs. If performance drop is small, augmentation’s role is marginal.
