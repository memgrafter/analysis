# TEGRA: Text Encoding With Graph and Retrieval Augmentation for Misinformation Detection
## Quick Facts
- *arXiv ID*: 2602.11106
- *Source URL*: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11106
- *Reference count*: 18
- *Primary result*: TEGRA improves average macro-F1 by **4.42%** over a RoBERTa text-only baseline across four misinformation datasets, with peak scores of 96.39% (PolitiFact) and 98.30% (CoAID).

## Executive Summary
TEGRA is a framework for misinformation detection that combines structured text representations with class-specific knowledge retrieval. It first parses a document into an OpenIE graph of factual triples, then enriches this graph by retrieving relevant triples from two knowledge graphs built from the training data (one for true, one for misinformation). A Triple Selection module filters noisy additions, and the resulting hybrid representation is encoded via a Graph Attention Network and concatenated with a RoBERTa text embedding. End‑to‑end training yields consistent improvements over pure language model baselines on multiple benchmarks, demonstrating the value of explicit relational reasoning and retrieval augmentation for verifying claims.

## Method Summary
The method proceeds as follows: (1) Extract an OpenIE graph of (subject, predicate, object) triples from the raw document using either OpenIE6 or an LLM‑based KGI extractor. (2) Build two class‑specific knowledge graphs (KG_true, KG_misinfo) by aggregating all training triples of each label. (3) Link entities in the document graph to DBpedia URIs and retrieve all matching triples from the two KGs, producing two enriched graphs G_true and G_misinfo. (4) Optionally apply a learnable Triple Selection module that assigns a relevance score to each retrieved triple and scales its contribution. (5) Encode the original text with a fine‑tuned RoBERTa model; encode each graph (original + enrichments) with a GAT over frozen fastText embeddings, followed by max‑ and mean‑pooling. (6) Concatenate the three embeddings, pass through a 2‑layer MLP, and train end‑to‑end with Adam (lr = 1e‑5) under early stopping.

## Key Results
- TEG (graph‑only) boosted average macro‑F1 by **2.97%** over the RoBERTa baseline.
- Adding retrieval (TEGRA) brought a total average macro‑F1 gain of **4.42%** versus text‑only, reaching 96.39% on PolitiFact and 98.30% on CoAID.
- The Triple Selection module contributed an additional average **1.51%** macro‑F1 improvement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Structured Graph Representation via OpenIE
- **Claim**: Converting raw text into an OpenIE graph improves misinformation detection by creating an entity‑centric, structured representation that integrates more readily with external knowledge and captures factual relationships.
- **Mechanism**: The OpenIE extractor generates subject‑predicate‑object triples, producing a graph that explicitly models actions and properties, mitigates long‑range dependency issues, and aligns with knowledge‑graph formats.
- **Core assumption**: The extracted factual assertions and relational structure are more predictive of misinformation than raw token sequences or stylistic cues alone, especially for fake news.
- **Evidence anchors**: The abstract states that the hybrid representation enhances performance compared to language models alone; Section 3.1 motivates the exhaustively structured, entity‑centric nature of OpenIE graphs.
- **Break condition**: Performance degrades on rumor detection (GossipCop), where stylistic and propagation features likely dominate.

### Mechanism 2: Retrieval‑Augmented Graph Enrichment
- **Claim**: Enriching the document’s OpenIE graph with triples from class‑specific knowledge graphs (KG_true, KG_misinfo) provides contextual evidence and highlights narrative alignment or contradiction, boosting accuracy.
- **Mechanism**: For each document graph, all triples from the two KGs that share an entity are retrieved and added, creating dual enriched graphs (G_true, G_misinfo). The classifier learns to recognize patterns where the document’s facts align more with one narrative.
- **Core assumption**: Training data contains sufficient high‑quality examples to build KGs that capture prototypical fact patterns per class; narrative alignment with misinformation articles is a discriminative signal.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 3.3 describes the KG construction and notes that KG_misinfo contains possible lies and inconsistencies when compared to legitimate triples. Section 5.2 reports a 4.42% average F1 gain from knowledge enrichment on top of prior enhancements.
- **Break condition**: Small, noisy, or temporally misaligned training data can introduce harmful bias; the Triple Selection module mitigates this.

### Mechanism 3: Triple Selection as a Noise Filter
- **Claim**: The learned Triple Selection (TS) module down‑weights irrelevant or low‑quality retrieved triples during graph encoding, yielding a consistent average F1 gain of ~1.5%.
- **Mechanism**: TS computes a relevance score µ for each added triple by comparing the semantic similarity between the frozen fastText embedding of the source document and the triple’s embedding (average of its parts). This score scales the embeddings of the added entities/edges before message passing in the GAT.
- **Core assumption**: Semantic similarity between the document embedding and the triple embedding is a viable, learnable proxy for the triple’s relevance to the classification task.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 3.4 details the TS computation; Section 5.2 reports a 1.51% average F1 gain. Error analysis (Appendix C) shows TEGRA performs best when retrieved information is limited, underscoring the need for selection.
- **Break condition**: The TS module may filter out relevant triples if the document embedding is a poor summary or if the triple’s surface form differs despite factual pertinence. Anomalies appear in Table 1 (e.g., KGI‑TS vs. KGI on PolitiFact).

## Foundational Learning
- **Open Information Extraction (OpenIE)**
  - *Why needed*: It is the foundational step that transforms unstructured text into the structured graph of triples that the TEG/TEGRA architecture relies upon; understanding its output and noise characteristics is essential.
  - *Quick check*: What are the three components of an OpenIE triple, and why is its “surface‑level” and “noisy” nature both a limitation and a feature for this approach?
- **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), specifically Graph Attention Networks (GATs)**
  - *Why needed*: The hybrid graph representation must be encoded into a fixed‑size vector for classification; GAT performs message passing over node embeddings, enabling the model to leverage relational data.
  - *Quick check*: In TEGRA, what is the purpose of the “graph pooling” step after the GAT layers?
- **Retrieval‑Augmented Classification (RAG Paradigm)**
  - *Why needed*: TEGRA implements a form of RAG where retrieval is from task‑specific, class‑discriminative knowledge graphs rather than a general corpus, and the “generation” is replaced by graph augmentation and classification.
  - *Quick check*: How does TEGRA’s retrieval mechanism differ from standard RAG used in LLMs, and what is a key risk of its “naive” entity‑based retrieval?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**
Input → OpenIE Extractor → Entity Linking (optional) → Knowledge Graph Builder (offline) → Graph Enricher → Triple Selection (optional) → Graph Encoder (fastText + GAT + pooling) ──┬─→ Classifier Head (concatenate with RoBERTa text embedding) → Binary output
Text Encoder (RoBERTa) ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

**Critical path**: Raw Text → OpenIE Graph → Graph Enrichment (→ Triple Selection) → GAT Encoding → Concatenation with RoBERTa embedding → Classification. Knowledge graph construction is an offline prerequisite.

**Design tradeoffs**
- *Graph extraction*: OpenIE6 (lighter, faster) vs. KGI (LLM‑based, potentially richer but costly).
- *Retrieval scope*: Entity‑matching retrieves all triples sharing an entity, which is simple but noisy; a vector‑similarity retriever could improve precision at the cost of complexity.
- *Triple Selection*: Adds ~270.9k parameters and training complexity; generally beneficial but can over‑ or under‑filter.
- *Model size*: Graph parts add ~724k parameters; total dominated by RoBERTa‑base (125M) but inference slows with graph processing.

**Failure signatures**
1. **Sparse OpenIE output** (fewer than 5 triples) leads to degraded TEGRA performance, as seen on CoAID with OpenIE6.
2. **TS module misfire**: When TS under‑filters (or over‑filters), classification scores drop; observed on some dataset/configurations (e.g., KGI‑TS vs. KGI on PolitiFact).
3. **KG contamination**: Including test‑set triples during KG construction inflates results; must enforce identical train/val/test splits.
4. **Entity linking failures**: Low URI linking rate reduces retrieved triples; fallback linkers may be needed.

**First 3 experiments**
1. **Baseline replication**: Fine‑tune RoBERTa‑base on each dataset (80/10/10 splits, Adam lr = 1e‑5, early stopping) to establish text‑only benchmarks.
2. **TEG sanity check**: Run TEG with OpenIE6 and KGI extractors (no retrieval). Verify that at least one configuration beats the RoBERTa baseline on fake‑news datasets (PolitiFact, CoAID, Horne2017). Expected average F1 gain ~2.97%.
3. **TEGRA core comparison**: For the best TEG configuration, enable retrieval augmentation and train with/without TS. Expect (a) TEGRA > TEG on average (4.42% total gain over text‑only) and (b) TS provides ~1.5% additional gain, though per‑dataset variations must be validated against Table 1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalizability**: How well does TEGRA perform on misinformation topics beyond politics and COVID‑19? The authors admit the approach has not been tested in a general setting.
- **Graph properties**: Which specific graph characteristics (size, connectivity, triple type distribution) extracted by OpenIE6 vs. KGI most strongly correlate with performance differences across datasets?
- **Triple Selection utility**: Under what conditions (text length, triple density, topic) is the TS module most and least beneficial? Can its decisions be interpreted to guide when to use TEG vs. TEGRA?
- **Knowledge graph quality**: Can the class‑specific KG construction be improved to avoid spurious or non‑causal correlations from training data, given that KG_misinfo contains “possible lies”?
- **Factual vs. stylistic grounding**: To what extent do TEGRA’s gains stem from better factual grounding versus capturing stylistic/linguistic patterns present in the class‑specific KGs?

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps**: Critical hyperparameters (OpenIE6 thresholds, GAT layers/heads, TS projection sizes) and the KGI prompt template are not disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- **KG quality dependency**: Performance hinges on class‑specific KGs built from limited, potentially noisy training data; small or temporally misaligned KGs risk biased retrieval.
- **OpenIE noise sensitivity**: Sparse or low‑quality OpenIE extraction (e.g., few triples) can cripple the graph‑based pipeline, as evidenced by CoAID results.
- **TS module heuristic**: The relevance scoring based on fastText embeddings is a heuristic that may misfire, filtering relevant triples or retaining irrelevant ones.

## Confidence
- **High**: TEGRA outperforms the RoBERTa baseline on macro‑F1 across the four evaluated datasets (supported by Table 1).
- **Medium**: Mechanistic explanations (graph structure helps, retrieval provides narrative alignment, TS filters noise) are plausible but rely on untested assumptions about KG quality and triple relevance.
- **Low**: Assertions about generalizability and robustness without full hyperparameter disclosure or detailed KG contamination checks.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sweep**: Systematically vary OpenIE6 confidence thresholds and GAT hidden dimensions to quantify performance sensitivity and identify configurations matching the reported results.
2. **KG contamination test**: Deliberately include test‑set triples during KG construction to measure the maximum possible inflation of TEGRA’s gains, establishing a contamination upper bound.
3. **TS ablation with random retrieval**: Replace entity‑matched retrieval with random triples from the same class KGs. If TEGRA’s performance drops to/below TEG’s, it confirms TS’s critical filtering role; if it remains high, it suggests the model may be exploiting dataset artifacts rather than semantic relevance.
