# Not Like Transformers: Drop the Beat Representation for Dance Generation with Mamba-Based Diffusion Model

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08023
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08023
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** MambaDance significantly outperforms prior state-of-the-art in dance fidelity, physical plausibility, and beat alignment on AIST++ and FineDance datasets.

## Executive Summary
MambaDance addresses the challenges of music-driven dance generation, where long, rhythmic sequences must be aligned to musical beats. It replaces Transformer attention with Mamba state-space blocks to overcome quadratic complexity and weak autoregressive bias, introduces a Gaussian decay beat representation for smooth, tempo‑adaptive conditioning, and employs a two‑stage diffusion process to maintain coherence across variable lengths. Evaluated on AIST++ and FineDance, the method achieves state‑of‑the‑art results on fidelity (FID), physical plausibility (Foot sliding), and beat alignment (BAS), with human evaluations confirming its superiority. The work demonstrates that structured state‑space models can effectively capture the sequential nature of dance while remaining efficient.

## Method Summary
The system consumes raw music waveforms, extracting Librosa features (m_total) and binary beats (b_raw). A Gaussian function transforms b_raw into a smooth beat cue (b_total). An MLP fuses m_total and b_total into a condition embedding c_m, processed by a Mamba encoder. Generation follows a two‑stage diffusion: a global diffusion pass predicts sparse key motions (hard and soft cues) over the full sequence; a local diffusion then fills details in overlapping windows, anchored by the key cues. Both stages share a decoder stack of eight blocks, each containing Temporal Selective SSM (SMM), Cross‑Modal Mamba (CMM), Adaptive Linear Modulation (AdaLM), and a feed‑forward network. This architecture yields linear complexity, strong temporal coherence, and adaptive cross‑modal conditioning.

## Key Results
- **Fidelity:** On FineDance, FIDk drops to 51.36 (vs. 84.99) and FIDg to 43.11 (vs. 64.57); AIST++ FIDg reaches 26.58, outperforming baselines.
- **Physical Plausibility:** Foot sliding (PFC) reduces to 0.0119 on FineDance and 1.0622 on AIST++, indicating smoother motion.
- **Beat Alignment:** BAS improves to 0.2441 (FineDance) and 0.2701 (AIST++), surpassing previous methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Mamba state-space blocks replace Transformer attention to enable linear complexity and stronger autoregressive inductive bias for long dance sequences.
- **Mechanism:** Selective SSM scanning processes sequences with time‑varying parameters, capturing long‑range dependencies without quadratic memory growth and embedding sequential progression bias.
- **Core assumption:** Dance motion is fundamentally sequential and temporally causal; prior Transformer‑based methods suffer from discontinuity and inefficiency in long horizons.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...replacing attention with Mamba state‑space blocks... quadratic complexity, weak autoregressive modeling."
  - [section 3.1] "SMM transforms input noisy motion latent with two Temporal SSM Blocks... Bidirectional Spatial SSM Block."
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers (e.g., MotionMamba, AlignYourRhythm) explore SSMs for motion but do not provide comparative ablations against pure Transformer baselines on dance metrics.
- **Break condition:** If dance dynamics are not strongly sequential (e.g., abstract pose interpolation), Mamba's sequential bias may not yield gains over non‑autoregressive architectures.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Gaussian decay beat representation explicitly encodes rhythmic structure, providing smooth, tempo‑adaptive guidance that improves beat‑motion alignment without sacrificing naturalness.
- **Mechanism:** For each frame, computes exp(–NBD²/(2·α·inter‑beat‑interval)²), creating bell‑shaped cues centered on beats that decay smoothly with temporal distance normalized by local tempo.
- **Core assumption:** Beats anchor choreographic phrasing; effective conditioning should give stronger signals near beats and decay rapidly but smoothly away, while adapting to varying tempos.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] "Gaussian provides a bell‑shaped smooth, localized emphasis around beat frames, and the tempo‑adaptive bandwidth α·l(i) yields consistent guidance."
  - [table 2] Ablation: "Mamba+NBD" (using NBD instead of Gaussian) yields highest BAS but degrades PFC and FID; Gaussian balances alignment and plausibility.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like BeatDance and Align Your Rhythm propose beat‑aware representations but do not evaluate Gaussian decay against NBD on full‑length dance generation metrics.
- **Break condition:** If musical beats are absent or irrelevant (e.g., non‑rhythmic movement), this representation would inject misleading biases.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Two‑stage diffusion with Mamba decoder (SMM, CMM, AdaLM) enables length‑robust generation by separating global choreographic structure from local motion detail while maintaining temporal coherence.
- **Mechanism:** Global diffusion predicts sparse key motions over long windows; local diffusion fills details per short window, anchored by key cues via inpainting and early‑step guidance, all processed with Mamba blocks.
- **Core assumption:** Coarse‑to‑fine hierarchical generation prevents error accumulation in long sequences; Mamba blocks preserve continuity within each stage.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] "Given total music features... partition into k non‑overlapping segments... global diffusion predicts characteristic key motions... local diffusion... anchored by hard cues..."
  - [section 4.3] "Our approach maintains stable performance across both short and long sequences, whereas EDGE and POPDG tend to degrade."
  - [corpus] Lodge [15] introduced two‑stage diffusion for long dances; this work adapts that pipeline to Mamba but does not compare against other hierarchical approaches (e.g., recurrent chunking).
- **Break condition:** If the dance requires globally consistent motifs that cross key‑motion boundaries, the hard/soft cue anchoring may cause repetitive or disjoint transitions.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Input preprocessor:** Extracts `m_total` (Librosa music features, D=35) and `b_raw` (binary beat); applies Gaussian function to produce `b_total`.
  2. **Music‑Beat Mixer:** MLP fuses `m_total` and `b_total` into `c_m`.
  3. **Mamba Encoder:** Processes `c_m` into condition embedding (not detailed in diagram).
  4. **Two‑stage diffusion decoder:**
     - Global diffusion: Takes full‑length `c_m,g`, outputs key motions `m_key` (5 hard `d_h`, 8 soft `d_s`).
     - Local diffusion: Takes windowed `c_m,l`, uses `d_h` (inpainting at window boundaries) and `d_s` (early‑step guidance) to generate full sequence.
  5. **Decoder block (shared):** Input MLP → [SMM → CMM → AdaLM → FFN]×8 → Output MLP.
     - SMM: Temporal SSM (forward along time) + Bidirectional Spatial SSM (across feature channels).
     - CMM: Concatenates motion latent `z_d`, condition `c_m`, timestep token `e_t`; Temporal SSM; extract motion part.
     - AdaLM: GroupNorm + affine transform from pooled `c_mod` (from `c_m` and `e_t`).

- **Critical path:** `music waveform → Librosa features + beat → Gaussian beat → Music‑Beat Mixer → Mamba encoder → condition `c_m` → (Global diffusion: `c_m,g` → key motions) → (Local diffusion: windowed `c_m,l` + key motions → final dance)`. The decoder blocks are the main computational engine within both diffusion stages.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Mamba vs. Transformer:** Linear vs. quadratic complexity; Mamba has sequential bias but may be less parallelizable during training; tradeoff is efficiency for long sequences vs. potential speed.
  - **Gaussian beat vs. binary/NBD:** Gaussian provides smooth, normalized cues but introduces hyperparameter α; tradeoff is alignment robustness across tempos vs. added complexity.
  - **Two‑stage vs. single‑stage:** Hierarchical generation improves long‑sequence coherence but requires careful key‑motion selection and anchoring; tradeoff is stability vs. potential loss of fine‑grained global diversity.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High PFC:** Foot sliding or unnatural COM acceleration; indicates poor physics modeling, possibly from insufficient velocity/acceleration losses or Mamba instability.
  - **Low BAS:** Beat misalignment; could be from inadequate beat representation (α too large/small) or weak cross‑modal fusion in CMM.
  - **High FID + High Diversity:** Artifacts like jitter or frozen poses inflating diversity; check if Div_g is far from GT (table 1: EDGE/POPDG have high Div_g but also high FID).
  - **Inconsistent long sequences:** Discontinuities at key‑motion boundaries; inspect mirrored soft cues placement and hard cue anchoring during local diffusion.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation on Mamba components (table 3):** Remove CMM or AdaLM to isolate their contributions to FID/PFC/BAS. Expected: Removing CMM hurts BAS/Div; removing AdaLM hurts PFC/FID.
  2. **Beat representation comparison (table 2):** Compare "Mamba‑only" (no beat), "Mamba+NBD", and "Ours" (Gaussian) on FIDk, PFC, BAS. Expected: Gaussian gives best balance; NBD boosts BAS but harms PFC/FID.
  3. **Sequence length robustness:** Train/infer with N∈{128,256,1024} on both datasets; measure metrics per length. Expected: Our method's performance degrades minimally vs. baselines (EDGE/POPDG) which drop on longer sequences.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why does Mamba's selective scanning specifically confer advantages for dance generation over other structured state space models (SSMs) or hybrid architectures, and what inductive biases are uniquely captured?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors contrast Mamba with Transformers and hybrid Mamba‑Transformer designs, claiming Mamba's autoregressive inductive bias and linear complexity better suit long, rhythmic sequences. However, they do not isolate which aspects of Mamba (e.g., time‑varying parameters, selective scanning) are critical for dance dynamics vs. other SSMs.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper compares to Transformer‑based methods and a "Mamba‑only" ablation but does not benchmark against other SSM variants (e.g., S4, Mamba2) or control for architectural differences. The improvement could stem from general SSM properties rather than Mamba‑specific mechanisms.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study comparing Mamba to other SSMs (e.g., S4, Mamba2) with identical training setups, analyzing performance on long‑sequence coherence and beat alignment metrics. Additionally, probing experiments to measure how well different models capture tempo‑adaptive rhythms.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the optimal design and parameterization for beat representations in music‑driven dance, and how does the Gaussian decay's smoothing factor α interact with dance styles and musical genres?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper proposes Gaussian beat representation with a smoothing factor α but does not explore its sensitivity or optimal value across datasets. It is evaluated with a fixed α without justification or analysis of how α affects different dance genres or tempos.
- **Why unresolved:** The Gaussian representation is presented as superior to binary beats and NBD, but its hyperparameter α is set heuristically. The interplay between α, inter‑beat intervals, and dance phrasing (e.g., staccato vs. legato movements) is not studied.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic evaluation varying α across a range of values and measuring beat alignment (BAS), physical plausibility (PFC), and fidelity (FID) per genre/tempo subset. Qualitative analysis of generated dances could reveal if α misalignment causes under‑ or over‑emphasis on beats.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the two‑stage diffusion pipeline (global key motion + local refinement) scale to extremely long dances (e.g., hours) or real‑time generation, and what are the failure modes at extreme lengths?
- **Basis in paper:** Inference in the paper supports N∈{128,256,1024} and n∈{64,256}, but longer sequences require more segments and potential stitching artifacts. The paper does not evaluate beyond 1024 frames or discuss computational/memory bottlenecks for hours‑long generation.
- **Why unresolved:** The pipeline is designed for length‑agnostic generation but is only tested up to 1024 frames (~34 seconds at 30 FPS). Real‑world applications (e.g., full‑song dance) may involve thousands of frames, increasing segment count and reliance on key motion augmentation (mirroring, copying). Stitching discontinuities or drift could accumulate.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Generating and evaluating dances of 1‑hour equivalent length, measuring temporal coherence metrics (e.g., long‑term PFC, BAS consistency) and computational costs (memory, time). Analyzing failure cases where key motion augmentation fails to preserve global structure.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can the proposed MambaDance architecture be extended to an end‑to‑end system that directly outputs rendered 3D avatars with realistic materials, lighting, and camera motion, and what are the challenges in bridging motion synthesis to rendering?
- **Basis in paper:** The conclusion states: "A natural extension can be an end‑to‑end system that maps music to a dancing 3D avatar, including a rendering stack for camera, materials, and lighting."
- **Why unresolved:** The current work focuses on motion generation (SMPL poses) and does not address rendering. Integrating motion with a differentiable renderer would require handling scene geometry, illumination, and viewpoint, which are not modeled. The impact of motion artifacts (e.g., foot sliding) on rendered realism is also unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementing a prototype that couples MambaDance with a neural renderer (e.g., NeRF, differentiable rasterization) and conducting user studies comparing rendered outputs against ground‑truth videos. Analyzing degradation when motion errors propagate to rendering.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** What causes the observed trade‑off between motion diversity and physical plausibility/beat alignment, and can it be mitigated without sacrificing fidelity?
- **Basis in paper:** Ablations show that adding the Gaussian beat representation improves BAS but reduces diversity (Divk, Divg) compared to "Mamba‑only". The paper notes diversity is "balanced rather than maximized," but the underlying mechanism (e.g., regularization effect of beat prior, overfitting to rhythmic patterns) is not investigated.
- **Why unresolved:** The trade‑off is empirically observed but not explained. It is unclear whether the beat representation over‑constrains the model, reducing exploration, or if physical losses (Lfoot, Lpos) inherently limit diversity. The relationship between diversity metrics and human perception of dynamism is also ambiguous.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyzing latent space traversals or sampling entropy with/without beat conditioning. Correlating diversity metrics with user study ratings on "dynamic movements." Testing adjustable guidance scales for the beat prior to find a Pareto frontier of diversity vs. alignment.

## Limitations
- The evaluation is restricted to sequences up to 1024 frames; longer dances (e.g., hour‑long) are not tested, leaving scalability uncertain.
- The Gaussian beat representation includes a smoothing factor α whose sensitivity across genres and tempos is not thoroughly analyzed.
- The paper does not compare Mamba to alternative SSM architectures (e.g., S4, Mamba2), making it unclear whether gains are unique to Mamba or generalize to SSMs.

- **Gaussian beat representation enhances rhythmic alignment without harming naturalness** – Evidence: Table 2 (beat ablations) – **High**.
- **Two‑stage diffusion provides robustness to sequence length** – Evidence: Section 4.3 (length robustness), but limited to 1024 frames – **Medium**.
