# Amortised and provably-robust simulation-based inference

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11325
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11325
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces NSM-Bayes and NSM-Bayes-conj, which provide amortised simulation-based inference with provable outlier robustness, achieving 100% empirical coverage and state‑of‑the‑art MMD on contaminated benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Simulation‑based inference (SBI) often relies on amortised neural density estimators, but these can be highly sensitive to outliers. This paper proposes neural score‑matching Bayes (NSM‑Bayes) and its conjugate variant NSM‑Bayes‑conj. Both methods train a flexible conditional density surrogate \(q_\phi(x|\theta)\) on simulated data, then perform generalized Bayesian inference using a weighted score‑matching loss that down‑weights outliers via an inverse‑multi‑quadratic kernel. NSM‑Bayes‑conj further imposes an exponential‑family surrogate and Gaussian prior, yielding closed‑form Gaussian posterior updates without MCMC. Theoretical results guarantee global‑bias robustness under mild conditions, and experiments on g‑and‑k, SIR, and radio‑propagation models show excellent coverage and fidelity even under 10% contamination, with the conjugate version offering orders‑of‑magnitude speedup.

## Method Summary
NSM‑Bayes consists of two phases. **Training**: a conditional density estimator \(q_\phi(x|\theta)\) (e.g., a normalising flow or an energy‑based model) is fitted by minimising the (unweighted) score‑matching loss on simulated pairs \((\theta,x)\). **Inference**: for a contaminated dataset \(\{x_i^o\}\), robust location and scatter \(\hat{\nu},\hat{\Xi}}\) (e.g., via Minimum Covariance Determinant) are computed and weights \(w_\zeta(x) = (1 + \|x-\hat{\nu}\|^2_{\hat{\Xi}^{-1}})^{-1/\zeta}\) are formed. The posterior is defined by the generalised Bayes rule \(\pi(\theta|x^o,\hat\phi) \propto \exp(-\beta\,L_{\text{NSM}}(\theta;x^o,\hat\phi))\,\pi(\theta)\) where \(L_{\text{NSM}}\) is the weighted score‑matching loss. For arbitrary priors or surrogates, posterior draws are obtained with MCMC (e.g., slice sampler) after calibrating \(\beta\) to achieve nominal coverage. NSM‑Bayes‑conj restricts \(q_\phi\) to the exponential family \(q_\phi(x|\theta) \propto \exp(T_\phi(x)^\top\theta + b_\phi(x))\) and assumes a Gaussian prior, which leads to a Gaussian posterior with closed‑form mean and covariance updates (Proposition 1). This eliminates MCMC and reduces inference to solving linear systems and adding ridge‑regularised matrices.

## Key Results
- NSM‑Bayes‑conj attains **100% empirical coverage** and MMD²_ref ≤ 0.20 on the g‑and‑k model with 10% one‑sided outliers; NSM‑Bayes also achieves 100% coverage and MMD²_ref = 0.13.
- Both methods **outperform** classical NLE, robustified NLE (RSNLE, NPE‑RS), NPL‑MMD, ACE, and GBI‑SR in outlier settings while preserving amortisation.
- The **conjugate variant** reduces inference time by orders of magnitude compared to MCMC‑based approaches, making it suitable for real‑time applications.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Neural surrogate amortisation
**Claim:** A neural surrogate density estimation enables amortisation in SBI by approximating the intractable likelihood once and reusing it for multiple observations.  
**Mechanism:** A conditional model \(q_\phi(x|\theta)\) (e.g., a normalising flow) is trained via maximum likelihood on simulated pairs \((\theta_i, x_i)\). After training, the fitted surrogate \(\hat{q}_{\hat{\phi}_m}(x|\theta)\) replaces the true likelihood \(p(x|\theta)\) in the posterior, avoiding repeated simulator calls for new data.  
**Core assumption:** The surrogate \(q_\phi\) is flexible enough to approximate \(p(x|\theta)\) well and its score \(\nabla_x \log q_\phi(x|\theta)\) is accessible.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract]: “The core method uses a neural conditional density estimator trained via standard negative log-likelihood.”  
- [section 3]: Describes training the neural surrogate via Equation (2) and using it in the GBI framework.  
- [corpus]: Neighbors like “Misspecification-robust amortised simulation-based inference using variational methods” also use neural surrogates for amortization, but NSM‑Bayes uniquely integrates them with weighted score‑matching.  
**Break condition:** If \(q_\phi\) cannot adequately approximate the true simulator density due to architectural limits or insufficient simulations, the posterior will be misspecified regardless of robustness properties.

### Mechanism 2: Weighted score‑matching robustness
**Claim:** Weighted score‑matching loss with an IMQ weight function confers provable outlier robustness by down‑weighting high‑density‑contour regions where outliers lie.  
**Mechanism:** The loss \(L_{\text{NSM}}\) weights each observation’s contribution via \(w(x)^2\) from an inverse‑multi‑quadratic (IMQ) kernel. For residuals \(x\) far from the robust location estimate \(\hat{\nu}_n\), \(w(x) \rightarrow 0\), diminishing their influence on the posterior update.  
**Core assumption:** The weight function \(w(x)\) is bounded and has bounded gradient (Assumption A1), and the neural surrogate’s score/Hessian are pointwise controlled by integrable functions of \(\theta\) offset by \(w(x)\) (Assumptions A4/A4′).  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract]: “NSM‑Bayes and NSM‑Bayes‑conj... combine amortisation with formal outlier robustness guarantees... down‑weighting outliers via an IMQ weight function.”  
- [section 3.3 & 4]: Formalizes weight choice as \(w_\zeta(x) = (1 + \|x - \hat{\nu}_n\|^2_{\hat{\Xi}_n^{-1}})^{-1/\zeta}\) and proves global‑bias robustness under conditions.  
- [corpus]: Robustness via weighting is common (e.g., “Robust Bayesian Optimisation with Unbounded Corruptions”), but NSM‑Bayes provides first provable guarantees for amortized SBI.  
**Break condition:** If contamination is not “one‑sided” (i.e., symmetrically distributed), the IMQ weight may down‑weight also non‑outlying points, or if the robust estimators \(\hat{\nu}_n, \hat{\Xi}_n\) fail (e.g., too few samples), the weight mis‑specifies.

### Mechanism 3: Conjugacy for efficient inference
**Claim:** Specifying the neural surrogate as an exponential‑family energy‑based model with a Gaussian prior yields a closed‑form conjugate posterior, eliminating MCMC and drastically reducing inference time.  
**Mechanism:** With \(q_\phi(x|\theta) \propto \exp(T_\phi(x)^\top \theta + b_\phi(x))\) and \(\pi(\theta) = N(\mu, \Sigma)\), the NSM‑Bayes posterior is Gaussian. Its mean and covariance are updated via simple expressions involving the surrogate’s gradient and Hessian summed over the data (Proposition 1).  
**Core assumption:** The surrogate is linear in \(\theta\) (Assumption A3) and the prior is Gaussian; the weight function is diagonal.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract]: “NSM‑Bayes‑conj... using a specific energy‑based model and Gaussian prior yields closed‑form posterior updates, eliminating MCMC.”  
- [section 3.2 & Proposition 1]: Provides the closed‑form expressions for the posterior parameters.  
- [corpus]: Limited direct evidence; neighbors focus on amortization or robustness separately. The “Robust and conjugate spatio‑temporal Gaussian processes” (Laplante et al., 2025a) uses similar conjugacy ideas but for GP regression, not SBI.  
**Break condition:** If the prior is non‑Gaussian or the surrogate is not an exponential family in \(\theta\), conjugacy fails and MCMC is required (NSM‑Bayes).

## Foundational Learning
- **Score Matching (Hyvärinen, 2006)**  
  *Why needed:* Score matching estimates unnormalised densities without requiring a normalising constant, which is crucial because simulator likelihoods are intractable. NSM‑Bayes uses its weighted variant as the GBI loss.  
  *Quick check:* Why is score matching suitable for training energy‑based model surrogates in SBI?

- **Generalized Bayesian Inference (Bissiri et al., 2016)**  
  *Why needed:* GBI updates beliefs via an arbitrary loss function rather than the log‑likelihood, enabling robustness by replacing \(\frac{1}{n}\sum \log p(x_i|\theta)\) with a weighted score‑matching loss that down‑weights outliers.  
  *Quick check:* How does the GBI posterior \(\pi(\theta|x) \propto \exp(-\beta L(\theta;x))\pi(\theta)\) relate to standard Bayes when \(L = -\log p(x|\theta)\)?

- **Amortized Inference**  
  *Why needed:* Training a neural surrogate once allows applying it to any new observation without re‑running simulations or retraining, which is essential for computational efficiency in real‑time applications.  
  *Quick check:* What is the key difference between partially amortized (NLE) and fully amortized (NPE) methods?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Training: Simulator & prior -> Simulated data -> Training module -> q_φ (neural density estimator)  
Inference: Observed data -> Weight function module -> Posterior module -> (Calibration module) -> Posterior samples

**Critical path:**  
- Training: Simulator → Simulate data → Train q_φ.  
- Inference: Observed data → Compute w(x) → Evaluate loss → Sample/update posterior.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Flexibility vs. conjugacy:* General NSM‑Bayes allows any \(q_\phi\) and prior but needs MCMC. Conjugate version is faster but requires Gaussian prior and exponential‑family surrogate linear in \(\theta\).  
- *Speed vs. robustness tuning:* Smaller \(\zeta\) in IMQ weight improves robustness for flows but may increase sensitivity to moderate outliers; requires cross‑validation for \(\beta\) and \(\zeta\) (paper uses fixed \(\zeta=1\)).  
- *Training time:* Score‑matching training is slower than NLE due to Hessian computation; conjugate version trains similarly to NLE but uses different loss.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Ill‑conditioned \(A_n\) in the conjugate update leading to large numerical errors; check condition number and increase ridge \(\lambda\) if needed.  
- β calibration not reaching target coverage due to unstable importance weights in Algorithm 2; monitor effective sample size (ESS) and re‑run MCMC when ESS < 30%; also clip importance weights.  
- Score‑matching MLP underfitting; track validation loss; if high, increase hidden units or epochs.  
- MMD lengthscale from median heuristic may be too small in high dimensions; verify kernel values and consider cross‑validated lengthscale.  
- For non‑differentiable simulators (SIR, radio), GBI‑SR with pseudo‑marginal MCMC may require more than 500 posterior samples for mixing; diagnose with trace plots and autocorrelation.  
- Contamination level not as specified; verify by plotting robust distance (e.g., Mahalanobis) of observations.

**First three experiments:**  
1. **g‑and‑k with 10% one‑sided outliers:** Verify that NSM‑Bayes‑conj concentrates near the true \(\theta^\star\) while NLE does not; check MMD²_ref < 0.20 and coverage ≈ 100%.  
2. **SIR undercounting (ϵ=5%, retention 50%):** Confirm posterior coverage and low MSE; compare inference times (NSM‑Bayes‑conj should be < 20 s).  
3. **Radio propagation with noise‑only outliers (ϵ=10%):** Ensure conjugate version handles non‑differentiable simulator; check bias in \(\theta_3\) is reduced versus NLE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multimodal posteriors:** How to make score‑based robust SBI handle multimodal posteriors? The conclusion notes that score‑based methods are ill‑suited for highly multimodal, well‑separated likelihoods and may miss posterior structure. A mixture extension of NSM‑Bayes with provable robustness is needed.
- **Discrete/structured data:** Can robust amortised SBI be extended to discrete or structured data? The current continuous‑data formulation and integrating discrete/structured score‑matching extensions is future work. This requires reformulating assumptions and handling combinatorial score estimation.
- **Other misspecifications:** How to extend NSM‑Bayes beyond outliers to other misspecifications such as covariate shift? The outlier weight function \(w(x)\) does not address distributional shifts; new weighting based on density ratios may be needed but could break robustness assumptions.

## Limitations
- Several implementation details are unspecified, including MAF architecture (layers, hidden features) for baselines, RSNLE summary statistics and hyperparameters, MinCovDet parameters (`support_fraction`), slice‑sampler settings, GBI‑SR kernel choices, and reference NLE training seeds. These gaps may affect reproducibility and comparison validity.
- The conjugate variant requires a Gaussian prior and a surrogate linear in \(\theta\), restricting its applicability to a subset of models; for non‑conjugate cases MCMC is still needed, which can be slow.

## Confidence
- **Weighted score‑matching robustness:** High (theorems with clear assumptions; empirical coverage ~100% reported).
- **Conjugate posterior efficiency:** Medium (closed‑form derivation provided, but depends on unspecified architectural choices and numerical stability).
- **Amortization via neural surrogates:** Medium (standard in SBI, but surrogate flexibility and training details partially unknown).
