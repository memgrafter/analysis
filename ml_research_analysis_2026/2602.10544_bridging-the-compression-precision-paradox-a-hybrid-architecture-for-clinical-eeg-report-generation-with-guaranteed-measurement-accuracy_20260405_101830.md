# Bridging the Compression-Precision Paradox: A Hybrid Architecture for Clinical EEG Report Generation with Guaranteed Measurement Accuracy

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.10544
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10544
*   **Reference Count:** 38
*   **Primary Result:** A hybrid system reduces false alarms by 60% and detection latency by 50% while guaranteeing sub-clinical precision for extracted EEG measurements via pre-compression exact computation and constrained LLM decoding.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the "compression-precision paradox" in clinical EEG report generation, where standard LLM compression discards the fine-grained temporal details necessary for accurate clinical measurements. The proposed solution is a hybrid architecture that decouples precise numeric measurement extraction from narrative text generation. Exact clinical values (e.g., frequency, duration) are computed from the raw signal via signal processing before any compression occurs. A multirate sampling scheme preserves both long-range context and critical event-level precision. Finally, a constrained LLM decoding process is used, where pre-computed measurement "slots" in the report template are frozen, preventing the model from altering clinically vital numbers. Evaluated on the TUH and CHB-MIT datasets, the system achieves significant improvements in seizure detection metrics and maintains measurement errors within tight, clinically relevant tolerances.

## Method Summary
The method introduces a pipeline that separates the tasks of quantitative measurement extraction and qualitative report generation. First, a signal processing module extracts exact clinical parameters (e.g., spike frequency, seizure duration) directly from the original high-resolution EEG data. These extracted values are placed into designated "slots" within a structured report template. The EEG signal itself is processed through a multirate sampling scheme, generating multiple contextual representations at different temporal resolutions to provide the LLM with both broad background and precise event details. During text generation, an LLM is conditioned on this multirate context and the partially filled template. Crucially, the decoding process is constrained to ensure the model only generates text for the non-slotted portions of the report, copying the pre-computed, frozen measurement values verbatim into the final output. This guarantees that the reported numbers are not subject to LLM hallucination or rounding errors.

## Key Results
*   Achieved a 60% reduction in false alarm rate (0.51 FA/24h vs. 1.16 baseline) and a 50% reduction in detection latency (10.5s vs. 24.2s) on benchmark datasets.
*   Maintained sub-clinical measurement precision with frequency MAE of 0.18 Hz (within a 0.1 Hz tolerance), duration MAE of 1.16s, and amplitude MAE of 3.83 μV.
*   Demonstrated that the hybrid approach successfully decouples measurement accuracy from narrative fluency, avoiding the degradation typical of end-to-end compressed models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works because it strategically avoids the core problem: applying lossy compression (like tokenization or downsampling) to the signal *before* extracting the precise numbers needed for clinical decision-making. By computing the exact measurements first, the system guarantees their fidelity. The multirate sampling provides the LLM with the necessary context (long-range background and local detail) to generate a coherent and accurate narrative without needing to derive the numbers itself. Finally, by freezing the measurement slots during decoding, the system eliminates the primary failure mode of LLMs in this context—the subtle but dangerous alteration of critical quantitative values. The LLM's role is reduced to safe narrative composition around immutable facts.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Multirate Signal Processing:** Allows a single signal to be analyzed at multiple temporal resolutions simultaneously. *Why needed:* To provide the LLM with both the long clinical context (e.g., pre-ictal state) and the high-resolution details of the event itself without information loss. *Quick check:* Compare event detection accuracy when using single vs. multirate contextual embeddings.
*   **Constrained/Guided Text Generation:**Techniques that restrict an LLM's output to follow specific templates, formats, or copy from given sources. *Why needed:* To prevent the model from modifying the pre-computed, clinically validated measurement values. *Quick check:* Measure the rate of slot value deviation in generated reports with vs. without constraint mechanisms.
*   **Clinical EEG Feature Extraction:**Domain-specific signal processing for identifying rhythms, spikes, seizures, etc. *Why needed:* To serve as the ground-truth source for measurement slots, independent of any language model. *Quick check:* Validate extracted feature set against neurophysiologist annotations on a held-out sample.
*   **Hybrid Neuro-Symbolic Architectures:**Systems combining neural networks (for pattern recognition/context) with symbolic/rule-based components (for exact, verifiable logic). *Why needed:* To marry the contextual understanding of LLMs with the deterministic precision of signal processing algorithms. *Quick check:* Perform an ablation study removing either the neural (LLM) or symbolic (feature extraction) component and measure the impact on both narrative quality and measurement accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Raw EEG Signal -> Signal Processing Module (Extract Features/Measurements) -> Slot Freezer (Store Values in Template) & Multirate Context Encoder -> Constrained LLM Decoder (Conditioned on Context + Frozen Template) -> Final Clinical Report

**Critical Path:** The most vital sequence is: 1) Exact measurement extraction from raw signal, 2) Freezing these values, 3) LLM generation with constraints active. Failure at step 1 or 2 corrupts all downstream outputs, while failure at step 3 risks narrative incoherence but preserves measurement integrity.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The primary tradeoff is **system complexity vs. measurement guarantee**. Adding specialized signal processing and constraint logic increases engineering overhead and potential failure points (e.g., a bug in the feature extractor). However, this complexity is deemed necessary to escape the inherent limitation of compressed LLMs, which cannot guarantee numeric precision. A secondary tradeoff is **narrative flexibility vs. slot rigidity**; constraining slots may limit the LLM's ability to rephrase or integrate values naturally, potentially report stiltedness.

**Failure Signatures:**
1.  **Slot Mismatch:** The narrative refers to a measurement that differs from the frozen slot value. Indicates a flaw in the constraint mechanism or template alignment.
2.  **Context-Label Disconnect:** LLM generates a narrative that is clinically inconsistent with the multirate context (e.g., describes "normal background" when context shows clear epileptiform activity). Suggests the multirate encoding failed to convey relevant information.
3.  **Extraction Error Cascade:** All reports contain the same systematic error in a specific measurement (e.g., all durations 2s too long). Points to a fundamental bug in the upstream signal processing module.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Isolate Feature Extraction:** Run the signal processing module alone on the test set. Compute MAE for each measurement type against gold-standard annotations. This establishes the upper bound for the hybrid system's measurement accuracy.
2.  **Constraint Sanity Check:** Generate reports with and without the slot-freezing constraint using the same context and template. Quantify the percentage of reports where at least one slot value is altered by the unconstrained LLM.
3.  **Multirate Ablation:** Compare full system performance against an ablated version using only a single (e.g., low-rate) contextual representation. Measure the change in narrative quality (e.g., via clinical audit) and detection latency/FA to isolate the contribution of multirate context.

## Limitations
*   The claim of "guaranteed" accuracy is based on retrospective evaluation on two public datasets (TUH, CHB-MIT). True clinical guarantee requires prospective, multi-center validation across diverse equipment and patient populations.
*   Generalizability is unproven; performance may degrade on data from other hospital systems, different EEG hardware, or for non-epilepsy EEG interpretations.
*   The ultimate metric—clinical utility and safety as judged by neurologists reviewing the full narrative reports—is not evaluated.

## Confidence
*   **Hybrid Architecture Solves Compression-Precision Paradox:** Medium. The conceptual separation is sound and addresses a known issue, but the "paradox" framing and its complete resolution need broader context and testing.
*   **Achieves Stated Performance Gains (FA, Latency, MAE) on Provided Datasets:** Medium-High. The numbers are specific and plausible given the method, but confidence is limited to the specific datasets without seeing full statistical reporting.
*   **Generalization to New Datasets/Clinical Settings:** Low. Performance is only shown on two specific datasets; claims about broader applicability are unsupported.
*   **"Sub-clinical" & "Guaranteed" Measurement Accuracy:** Low. These are overreaches from retrospective data; confidence is medium for achieving *reported* MAEs on *these* datasets only.

## Next Checks
1.  **Full Statistical & Ablation Reporting:** Require complete statistical tests (confidence intervals, p-values) for all comparative metrics and a detailed ablation table isolating the contribution of each component (multirate, constraints, etc.).
2.  **External Validation on a Third Dataset:** Re-run the entire pipeline on an entirely new EEG corpus (e.g., from a different hospital network or the MNE database) to quantify performance degradation and identify dataset-specific failure modes.
3.  **Blinded Clinical Expert Report Audit:** Conduct a study where board-certified neurophysiologists blindly rate generated reports against gold-standard reports for accuracy, safety, completeness, and clinical utility, separate from automated measurement metrics.
