# Brain Tumor Classifiers Under Attack: Robustness of ResNet Variants Against Transferable FGSM and PGD Attacks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11646
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11646
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: BrainNeXt achieved >87% black-box robustness but generated weak transferable attacks; BrainNet and DilationNet were mutually vulnerable (accuracy drops up to ~35-37% under PGD) on shrunk non-augmented data; shrinking and removing augmentation severely reduced robustness despite high clean accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates adversarial robustness of three ResNet variant families (BrainNet, BrainNeXt, DilationNet) for brain tumor MRI classification under transferable FGSM and PGD attacks. Across varying input resolutions (full-sized vs. shrunk) and data augmentation (augmented vs. non-augmented), BrainNeXt (ResNeXt-based) consistently demonstrated the highest black-box robustness, maintaining over 87% accuracy under transferred attacks, but generated the least transferable attacks itself. Conversely, BrainNet and DilationNet exhibited strong mutual vulnerability, with accuracy reductions of up to 35–37% under PGD when trained on shrunk, non-augmented data. The study reveals a critical trade-off: reducing image resolution and omitting data augmentation dramatically compromises adversarial resilience, even when clean accuracy remains high.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate adversarial robustness on a public brain tumor MRI dataset (4,023 images, 3 classes) using three data pipelines: full-sized (512×512) with augmentation (random flips/rotations), shrunk (160×160) with augmentation, and shrunk without augmentation. Seven models are trained: BrainNet (ResNet-101 with two extra residual blocks and staged unfreezing), three BrainNeXt variants (ResNeXt-50/101/152 with cardinality 32), and three DilationNet variants (ResNet-101 with dilation rates 2,3,4 in the last two residual blocks). Training uses Adam (LR=1e-4, batch size 10) for up to 150 epochs with early stopping. Adversarial samples are generated using FGSM (ϵ∈0.02–0.05) and PGD (ϵ=0.03, α=ϵ/4 or 0.0075, iterations=10–20) on an attack set, with each model serving as a surrogate. Transferability is measured by accuracy drop when these adversarial examples are fed to all other models.

## Key Results
- BrainNeXt models achieved the highest black-box robustness (>87% accuracy under transferred attacks) but generated the weakest transferable attacks.
- BrainNet and DilationNet were highly vulnerable to each other’s attacks, with accuracy drops up to ~35–37% under PGD (higher α/iterations) on shrunk non-augmented data.
- Shrinking input resolution and removing data augmentation severely reduced robustness across all models, despite high clean accuracy, highlighting a trade-off between efficiency and adversarial resilience.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** BrainNeXt's higher cardinality (multiple parallel grouped convolution paths) contributes to its superior black-box robustness.
- **Mechanism:** The grouped, smaller-filter convolutions within each block encourage learning more diverse and distributed feature representations. This creates a decision boundary that is less sensitive to perturbations optimized for a specific model's gradient, making transferred adversarial examples less effective.
- **Core assumption:** Architectural diversity in feature extraction (via cardinality) reduces gradient alignment between models, hindering transferability.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "...BrainNeXt models exhibit the highest robustness to black-box attacks, **likely due to their increased cardinality**..."; Section 4.4: "...ResNeXt-based architectures...use cardinality, i.e., multiple parallel transformation paths via grouped convolutions, which encourages richer and more diverse feature representations..." (corpus lacks direct evidence for medical models).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Architectural similarity (shared residual block topology) increases the transferability of adversarial examples between BrainNet (ResNet) and DilationNet.
- **Mechanism:** BrainNet and DilationNet share the fundamental ResNet building block structure. Adversarial perturbations generated on one exploit weaknesses in this shared hierarchical feature extraction process. The gradients during attack generation align closely, so the perturbed input remains effective when transferred to the other.
- **Core assumption:** Models with structurally similar latent space representations are more vulnerable to mutual adversarial transfer.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "...BrainNet and Dilation models are **more vulnerable to attacks from each other**..."; Section 4.4: "...ResNet and DilationResNet models produced stronger and more transferable attacks against each other, **likely due to their architectural similarities and shared feature extraction pathways**..." (corpus supports general principle but not specific to these variants).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Combined use of shrunk input resolution and absence of data augmentation severely degrades robustness, even for models with high clean accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** Shrinking removes fine-grained spatial details crucial for learning robust, high-frequency invariant features. Non-augmented data exposes the model only to a narrow distribution, preventing it from learning invariances to spatial transformations that often align with adversarial directions. The model learns brittle, low-resolution, canonical-pattern detectors that are easily fooled.
- **Core assumption:** Robustness requires models to be exposed to and learn from diverse spatial contexts and resolutions during training.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "...**shrunk and non-augmented data significantly reduce model resilience**, even when the untampered test accuracy remains high..."; Sections 4.2/4.3: Consistent severe accuracy drops on "shrunk non-augmented" datasets; Section 4.4: "...reduced image resolution and lack of augmentation **stripped the model of exposure to spatial and contextual variability**..." (corpus includes literature on augmentation for defense but not joint effect).

## Foundational Learning
- **Adversarial Transferability & Black-Box Attacks:** Understanding that gradients from a white-box source can be useful in a black-box target setting is fundamental to interpreting all results. Quick check: "What property of a neural network's decision boundary allows an input perturbed to fool Model A to also fool a different Model B?"
- **ResNet, ResNeXt, and Dilated Convolution Fundamentals:** Grasping architectural differences (cardinality in ResNeXt, receptive field modification via dilation) is necessary to reason about robustness differences. Quick check: "How does increasing 'cardinality' in ResNeXt differ architecturally from increasing 'depth' in ResNet, and how might this affect gradient propagation during an attack?"
- **Role of Data Augmentation in Model Generalization & Robustness:** Augmentation teaches invariance to transformations, which can regularize against adversarial noise. Quick check: "Why might randomly rotating and flipping an MRI image during training make the resulting model's gradients less 'consistent' and thus less transferable to an unseen model?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** ResNet-101 -> BrainNet (+2 residual blocks, staged unfreezing); ResNeXt -> BrainNeXt (cardinality 32); ResNet-101 -> DilationNet (dilation rates 2/3/4 in last 2 blocks); Data configs (Full Aug, Shrunk Aug, Shrunk NoAug) -> Training; Trained models (7 variants + DenseNet) -> Attack generation (FGSM/PGD) -> Transfer evaluation -> Robustness metrics.

**Critical path:**
1. Implement and preprocess the three data pipelines.
2. Train all seven models on each data configuration, tracking clean validation accuracy.
3. For each trained model as surrogate, generate FGSM/PGD adversarial samples on the attack subset.
4. Evaluate all target models on these adversarial sets, recording accuracy drop to build transferability matrices.
5. Analyze patterns: (a) BrainNeXt's cross-target robustness, (b) mutual vulnerability of BrainNet/DilationNet, (c) degradation on shrunk/non-augmented data.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Cardinality (BrainNeXt): Increases parameter count and compute but may improve robustness via feature diversity.
- Dilation: Captures multi-scale context but risks overfitting to specific scales if dilation rates mismatch tumor sizes.
- Resolution vs. Robustness: Higher resolution may improve robustness at the cost of compute; shrinking improves efficiency but catastrophically harms robustness, especially without augmentation.

**Failure signatures:**
- Architectural mismatch: BrainNeXt should be robust against all transferred attacks but generate weak attacks.
- Similar architecture attack: BrainNet and DilationNet should show >30% accuracy drops from each other's attacks, especially on shrunk non-augmented data under high-α PGD.
- Data pipeline failure: Any model on shrunk non-augmented data should exhibit severely degraded robustness (~60% accuracy) despite high clean accuracy (>85%).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline: Train all seven models on full-sized augmented data; record clean test accuracies.
2. Transferability stress (shrunk augmented): Use one model (e.g., BrainNet) as surrogate to generate PGD attacks (ϵ=0.03, α=0.0075, iters=20); evaluate all other models; repeat rotating surrogates.
3. Isolate data effects: Train Dilation3 on (a) full-sized augmented, (b) shrunk augmented, (c) shrunk non-augmented; for each, generate attacks from a counterpart (e.g., Dilation2) and evaluate robustness across conditions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can adversarial training or attention-guided regularization mitigate the vulnerability of BrainNet and DilationNet?**  
   Basis: Explicitly stated in Future Work. Unresolved because no defenses were tested. Resolution: Train with adversarial training/attention regularization and compare black-box robustness to BrainNeXt.

2. **Why do shrunk, non-augmented MRI data disproportionately degrade robustness despite high clean accuracy?**  
   Basis: Inferred from consistent results. Unresolved due to lack of mechanistic analysis. Resolution: Measure gradient stability, feature invariance, or decision boundary smoothness across data configurations.

3. **Which specific architectural properties (cardinality, dilation rates) most directly determine the robustness-transferability trade-off?**  
   Basis: Inferred; authors speculate cardinality but confounded variables exist. Unresolved due to no ablation. Resolution: Systematically vary cardinality, dilation rates, depth independently and evaluate.

4. **Why are BrainNet and DilationNet mutually vulnerable while both are resilient to BrainNeXt attacks?**  
   Basis: Inferred; authors propose architectural similarity but no test. Unresolved due to lack of feature analysis. Resolution: Analyze internal representations (activations, gradient alignments) or test hybrid models.

## Limitations
- Dataset class distribution and exact split ratios are unreported, risking improper stratification and skewed attack set composition.
- Architectural specifics (e.g., which layers are dilated, exact ResNeXt group counts) are inferred from standard configurations, not explicitly defined.
- Claims rely on internal reasoning; the corpus provides no direct evidence linking cardinality to transfer robustness in medical CNNs, nor on the joint impact of shrinking and augmentation removal.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (cardinality → BrainNeXt robustness): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (architectural similarity → BrainNet/DilationNet mutual vulnerability): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (shrunk/non-augmented data → severe robustness drop): High

## Next Checks
1. **Cardinality ablation:** Train BrainNeXt models with varied cardinality (4, 8, 16, 32) on full-sized augmented data; generate transferred PGD attacks between them. Expect robustness to increase monotonically with cardinality.
2. **Architectural similarity control:** Replace DilationNet’s residual blocks with plain convolutional blocks; test mutual transferability with BrainNet. Expect significantly reduced vulnerability if residual topology drives similarity.
3. **Augmentation dissection:** For a fixed model (e.g., Dilation3) on shrunk data, train with augmentation policies: none, flip-only, rotation-only, flip+rotation, flip+rotation+scaling. Evaluate transferred PGD robustness. Expect geometric transforms to recover most robustness.
