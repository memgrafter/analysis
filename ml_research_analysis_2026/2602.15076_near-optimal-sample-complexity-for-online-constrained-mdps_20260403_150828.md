# Near-Optimal Sample Complexity for Online Constrained MDPs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.15076
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15076
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Model-based primal-dual algorithm achieving Õ(SAH³/ε²) sample complexity for relaxed feasibility and Õ(SAH⁵/(ε²ζ²)) for strict feasibility, matching known lower bounds.

## Executive Summary
The paper studies online reinforcement learning in Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs) with the goal of maximizing cumulative reward while ensuring safety via cost constraints. It introduces a model-based primal-dual algorithm that employs optimistic bonuses for reward estimation and pessimistic bonuses for cost estimation, with a transition model updated lazily when visitation counts double. The algorithm operates in two settings: relaxed feasibility, where small constraint violations are allowed, and strict feasibility, requiring zero violations. For relaxed feasibility, it achieves an ε-optimal policy with ε-bounded violation after Õ(SAH³/ε²) episodes, matching the lower bound for unconstrained MDPs. For strict feasibility, under a Slater condition (positive ζ), it returns an ε-optimal zero-violation policy after Õ(SAH⁵/(ε²ζ²)) episodes, which matches the generative model lower bound for CMDPs. The approach uses a mixture of primal-dual iterates per episode and discretized dual updates to ensure tractability.

## Method Summary
The algorithm maintains an empirical transition model updated only when the visitation count of any state-action pair doubles, thereby reducing model correlation and enabling tighter concentration. For each episode, it solves the empirical CMDP via T primal-dual iterations: the primal step finds a policy that maximizes the augmented reward (reward minus Lagrange multiplier times cost) under the current model; the dual step updates the multiplier using gradient descent on constraint violation, with discretization for computational tractability. The episode policy is the uniform mixture of the T iterates. Optimistic bonuses (added to reward) and pessimistic bonuses (subtracted from cost) drive exploration, while a shifted constraint constant (b+τ for relaxed, b−Δ for strict) balances safety and optimality. After K episodes, the output is the average of all episode policies. The sample complexity analysis relies on the doubling trick, Bernstein-style bonuses, and careful error decomposition, yielding near-optimal bounds for both settings.

## Key Results
- For relaxed feasibility, the algorithm returns an ε-optimal policy with expected cost at most b+ε after Õ(SAH³/ε²) episodes.
- For strict feasibility (assuming ζ>0), it returns an ε-optimal policy with cost ≤ b after Õ(SAH⁵/(ε²ζ²)) episodes.
- The rates match the unconstrained MDP lower bound (relaxed) and the generative model lower bound for CMDPs (strict), establishing near-optimality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**1. Doubling Batch Updates**
The empirical transition model is updated only when the total visitation count for any (s,a,h) doubles. This lazy update groups data into batches of geometrically increasing size, reducing correlation between model estimates and policy values and enabling the use of concentration inequalities with high probability. It assumes steady visitation growth and a stationary MDP. Evidence: Section 4. If visitation counts grow too slowly, model staleness may increase regret.

**2. Optimistic Rewards and Pessimistic Costs with Constraint Shift**
Bernstein-style bonuses are added to reward estimates (optimism) and subtracted from cost estimates (pessimism). Additionally, the constraint constant is shifted upward (b+τ) for relaxed feasibility and downward (b−Δ) for strict feasibility. This combination promotes exploration while cautiously avoiding cost violations, yielding bounded violations or zero violations. Evidence: Abstract and Section 4. Incorrect bonus calibration or shifts can lead to over-/under-exploration or constraint failures.

**3. Primal-Dual with Discretization and Policy Mixing**
The empirical CMDP is solved by alternating primal (policy improvement) and dual (multiplier update) steps. The dual variables lie on a discretized grid Λ to keep union bounds polynomial. The episode policy is a mixture of T iterates, which averages out optimization errors from finite iterations and discretization. Strong duality and appropriate step sizes ensure convergence. Evidence: Abstract and Section 5. If T is too small, optimization error dominates; if discretization is too coarse, approximation error accumulates.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:
Data buffer -> visitation counts (¯N) -> empirical transition model (bP); bP, bonuses, and λ -> primal-dual iterations (producing policy iterates bπ_k^t) -> mixture policy π_k; π_k executed -> new transitions -> update counts and conditionally recompute bP. The constraint shift (τ or Δ) and discretized dual set Λ are inputs.

**Critical path**:
For episode k = 1,...,K:
- Run T primal-dual iterations: initialize λ_k^1; for t=1..T compute bπ_k^t via policy improvement (value iteration on augmented reward using bP and bonuses), then update λ_k^{t+1} via discretized gradient step.
- Set π_k as uniform mixture over bπ_k^t.
- Execute π_k for H steps, collect (s,a,r,c,s').
- Update counts ¯N_h(s,a); if any count doubles, recompute bP from all accumulated data and recompute bonuses accordingly.
After K episodes, output final policy ¯π = (1/K) Σ π_k.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- T vs computation: Larger T reduces optimization error but increases per-episode cost; theory prescribes T = O(H⁴/ε⁴).
- Bonus constants (c₁=460/9, c₂=544/9): Tuned for Bernstein concentration; deviations may break high-probability bounds.
- Constraint shift: τ = ε/2 (relaxed) or Δ = ζε/(2H) (strict); too large hurts reward, too small causes violations or loose bounds.
- Discretization: ε₁ = O(ε³/H³) (relaxed) or O(ε²ζ²/H⁴) (strict); smaller ε₁ improves approximation but increases the size of the dual grid (U = O(H/ε) or O(H/ζ)), affecting union bounds.
- Doubling update frequency: Infrequent updates reduce computational overhead but risk stale models; the doubling threshold assumes visitation counts grow steadily.

**Failure signatures**:
- Persistent constraint violation in strict feasibility: Δ may be ≥ ζ or cost bonuses insufficiently pessimistic; check ζ estimate and Δ value.
- Regret substantially exceeding Õ(SAH³K): inadequate exploration (bonus miscalculation) or model staleness (missing updates).
- Model update never triggers: poor exploration leading to slow visitation growth; verify counts increment correctly.
- High variance in performance across seeds: policy mixing length T may be insufficient; test with larger T.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Sanity check on tiny MDP**: Implement with S=A=2, H=2, K=100. Known optimal policy; verify regret ~0 and violation ≤ ε (ε=0.1). Ensure bonuses and model updates behave as expected.
2. **Sensitivity to constraint shift**: For relaxed feasibility, vary τ and measure average violation; expect violation ≈ τ. For strict, vary Δ and confirm zero violation only if Δ < ζ.
3. **Sample complexity scaling**: Vary S and A (e.g., 2,3,4) with fixed H and ε. Plot log-log of episodes needed vs. SA; slope should match Õ(SAH³/ε²) (relaxed) or Õ(SAH⁵/(ε²ζ²)) (strict). Also test ζ dependence by scaling b.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Extending the algorithm to CMDPs with function approximation (e.g., linear or neural representations) while preserving near-optimal sample complexity.
2. Handling multiple cost constraints; the sample complexity for M > 1 constraints is unknown.
3. Whether the Slater constant ζ must be known; an adaptive method that avoids ζ is open.
4. The case of unknown reward and cost functions; the paper claims easy extension but no rigorous proof exists.
5. Extending results to infinite-horizon discounted or average-reward CMDPs.

## Limitations
- Exact finite-sample constants in the Õ bounds are not provided; choosing them conservatively may increase practical sample complexity.
- The algorithm requires prior knowledge of the Slater constant ζ for strict feasibility, which is often unknown; small ζ leads to prohibitively large sample complexity (1/ζ²).
- Solving the empirical CMDP exactly by enumerating all deterministic policies is computationally intractable for all but tiny state spaces; the paper does not discuss approximate planning.

## Confidence
- Relaxed feasibility bound Õ(SAH³/ε²): High – proof extends unconstrained techniques clearly.
- Strict feasibility bound Õ(SAH⁵/(ε²ζ²)): Medium – ζ dependence may be loose and relies on strict Slater condition.
- Zero-violation guarantee for strict feasibility: High – follows from pessimistic cost estimates and constraint shift.
- Optimality of the doubling-batch update: Medium – plausible but not empirically validated; alternative batching could yield similar rates.
