# Best-of-Both-Worlds Multi-Dueling Bandits: Unified Algorithms for Stochastic and Adversarial Preferences under Condorcet and Borda Objectives

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18972
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18972
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The paper resolves whether a single algorithm can optimally handle both stochastic and adversarial environments in multi-dueling bandits, proposing MetaDueling for Condorcet objectives and SA-MiDEX for Borda objectives, achieving optimal regret bounds without prior regime knowledge.

## Executive Summary

This paper studies multi-dueling bandits, where an agent selects a subset of \(m\) arms and observes only the winner. The goal is to minimize regret under two objectives: Condorcet (maximizing the probability of selecting the optimal arm against challengers) and Borda (maximizing average performance across opponents). The central question is whether a single algorithm can achieve optimal regret in both stochastic and adversarial settings without knowing the environment type in advance. For Condorcet objectives, the authors introduce MetaDueling, a black‑box reduction that converts any dueling bandit algorithm into a multi‑dueling one by constructing multisets containing only two distinct arms. This yields an unbiased pairwise signal and, when combined with the Versatile‑DB base algorithm, achieves \(O(\sqrt{KT})\) adversarial pseudo‑regret and the instance‑optimal \(O\bigl(\sum_{i\neq a^*} \log T / \Delta_i\bigr)\) stochastic pseudo‑regret. For Borda objectives, they present SA‑MiDEX, which begins with a thorough baseline exploration, then runs a stochastic successive‑elimination phase while monitoring deviations via a martingale‑based detector. If significant deviation is detected, it irreversibly switches to an adversarial EXP3 fallback. SA‑MiDEX attains \(O(K^2\log(KT) + K\log^2 T + \sum_{i:\Delta_i^B>0} K\log(KT)/(\Delta_i^B)^2)\) stochastic pseudo‑regret and \(O(K\sqrt{T\log(KT)} + K^{1/3}T^{2/3}(\log K)^{1/3})\) adversarial pseudo‑regret, both of which are near‑optimal relative to the lower bounds. Importantly, both algorithms operate without prior knowledge of the regime.

## Method Summary

For Condorcet objectives, MetaDueling is a wrapper that takes any dueling bandit algorithm as a black box. Each round, it obtains a pair \((x_t, y_t)\) from the base, forms a multiset \(A_t\) of size \(m\) containing only these two arms (with counts balanced to preserve symmetry), and plays \(A_t\). The observed winner is mapped to a binary outcome \(o_t \in \{0,1\}\) indicating whether \(x_t\) won; this outcome is fed back to the base algorithm. The key insight is that \(o_t\) is an unbiased estimate of an affine transformation \(bP_t(x_t,y_t) = \alpha_m + \beta_m P_t(x_t,y_t)\), and the transformation preserves the Condorcet winner while scaling gaps by \(\beta_m \ge 1/2\). By selecting a best‑of‑both‑worlds dueling base (e.g., Versatile‑DB), the wrapper inherits both stochastic instance‑optimal and adversarial \(\sqrt{KT}\) guarantees, with a constant‑factor inflation.

For Borda objectives, SA‑MiDEX integrates stochastic successive elimination with adversarial fallback. It first executes a baseline exploration of \(\Theta(K^2\log T)\) rounds to estimate all pairwise preferences \(\hat{P}^{T_0}\). Afterwards, it maintains an active set \(C\) and empirical Borda scores \(\hat{b}_t(i)\). In stochastic mode, it alternately selects \(x_t \in C\) (round‑robin) and random \(y_t\), updates statistics, and eliminates arms whose Borda score is dominated with high confidence. Simultaneously, a background random sampler (probability \(p_t = \min(1, K\log t / t)\)) collects extra pairs to monitor cumulative deviations \(D_t(i,j)\) from the baseline using a martingale. If any deviation exceeds a threshold \(\tau(\sqrt{N_t(i,j)-N_0(i,j)}+1)\), the algorithm switches irrevocably to adversarial mode. In adversarial mode, it uses EXP3 with explicit uniform exploration and importance‑weighting updates fed by transformed feedback \(g_t = (o_t - \alpha_m)/\beta_m\), which is unbiased and bounded. The stochastic regret benefits from efficient elimination after \(\tilde{O}(K\log T / (\Delta_i^B)^2)\) pulls, plus \(\tilde{O}(K\log^2 T)\) overhead; adversarial regret is dominated either by the pre‑switch \(\tilde{O}(K\sqrt{T})\) bound or by the switch‑triggered EXP3 \(\tilde{O}(K^{1/3}T^{2/3})\) bound.

## Key Results

- **Condorcet objectives:** MetaDueling (with Versatile‑DB) attains \(O(\sqrt{KT})\) adversarial pseudo‑regret and the instance‑optimal \(O\bigl(\sum_{i\neq a^*} \log T / \Delta_i\bigr)\) stochastic pseudo‑regret, matching lower bounds.
- **Borda objectives:** SA‑MiDEX achieves \(O(K^2\log(KT) + K\log^2 T + \sum_{i:\Delta_i^B>0} K\log(KT)/(\Delta_i^B)^2)\) stochastic and \(O(K\sqrt{T\log(KT)} + K^{1/3}T^{2/3}(\log K)^{1/3})\) adversarial pseudo‑regret, which are near‑optimal.
- Both algorithms are unified, operating without any prior knowledge of whether the environment is stochastic or adversarial.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: MetaDueling Black‑Box Reduction

- **Claim:**  
  MetaDueling transforms any dueling bandit algorithm into a multi‑dueling one by constructing multisets containing only two distinct arms, turning winner‑only feedback into an unbiased pairwise signal.

- **Mechanism:**  
  At each round the base dueling algorithm proposes a pair `(x_t, y_t)`.  MetaDueling forms a multiset `A_t` of size `m` with `n_x` copies of `x_t` and `n_y` copies of `y_t`, symmetric for even `m` and randomized for odd `m`.  The observed winner `o_t` is an unbiased estimate of the *rescaled* preference `bP_t(x_t, y_t) = α_m + β_m P_t(x_t, y_t)`.  The rescaled matrix `bP_t` is a valid preference matrix that preserves the Condorcet winner and gaps (Lemma C.1).  Moreover, Lemma 3.7 shows `E[R_C^T] = (1/β_m) E[ bR_{DB}^T ]`, reducing multi‑dueling regret to dueling regret under `bP_t`.

- **Core assumption:**  
  The environment follows the pairwise‑subset choice model and has a Condorcet winner.  The base dueling algorithm accepts binary outcomes `o_t ∈ {0,1}` and achieves bounded pseudo‑regret on any valid preference matrix.

- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “by transforming multi‑way winner feedback into an unbiased pairwise signal”.  
  - Section 3.2 (Lemma 3.3) gives the unbiased feedback calculation.  
  - Corpus: *Adversarial Multi‑dueling Bandits* (Gajane, 2024) studies adversarial multi‑dueling but lacks stochastic guarantees; MetaDueling fills this gap by enabling existing best‑of‑both‑worlds dueling algorithms to handle multi‑dueling directly.

- **Break condition:**  
  If the base algorithm relies on gap properties not preserved under affine rescaling (e.g., assumes raw gaps equal to `P(a*,i)‑1/2` exactly), or if the multiset construction deviates from two distinct arms, the reduction invalidates the regret equivalence.

### Mechanism 2: Rescaling and Gap Preservation

- **Claim:**  
  The rescaling factor `β_m ≥ 1/2` bounds the multiplicative regret inflation and scales gaps proportionally, allowing the instance‑optimal stochastic guarantee `O(∑_{i≠a*} logT / Δ_i)` to be transferred from the dueling algorithm.

- **Mechanism:**  
  From the symmetric multiset, `β_m = m/(2(m‑1))` for even `m` and `β_m = (m+1)/(2m)` for odd `m`.  By Lemma 3.5, the rescaled gap satisfies `bΔ(i) = β_m Δ(i)`.  Since `β_m ≥ 1/2`, `Δ(i) ≤ 2 bΔ(i)`.  If the dueling algorithm (e.g., Versatile‑DB) achieves `E[ bR_{DB}^T ] ≤ ∑_{i≠a*} (c·logT) / bΔ(i)` in the stochastic regime, then by Lemma 3.7 the multi‑dueling regret becomes  
  `E[R_C^T] ≤ (1/β_m) ∑ (c·logT) / bΔ(i) = (c/β_m) ∑ logT / Δ(i) ≤ 2c ∑ logT / Δ(i)`.  
  Hence the instance‑optimal form is preserved up to a constant factor.

- **Core assumption:**  
  A unique Condorcet winner exists and gaps `Δ_i` are positive; the dueling base algorithm provides gap‑dependent stochastic regret bound expressed in terms of its own gaps `bΔ_i`.

- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “the instance‑optimal `O(∑_{i≠a*} logT / Δ_i)` pseudo‑regret”.  
  - Section 3.3: Lemma 3.5 (Gap Rescaling) and the derivation of `β_m`.  
  - Corpus: *A Blackbox Approach to Best of Both Worlds in Bandits and Beyond* (Seldin & Slivkins, 2014) introduced black‑box reductions for bandit problems; our reduction extends that idea to multi‑dueling via explicit rescaling.

- **Break condition:**  
  The argument would fail if `β_m` could be arbitrarily small (making `1/β_m` large).  For `m≥2` we have `β_m ≥ 1/2`, so it is safe.  If the Condorcet winner assumption is violated, the gap definitions may not translate cleanly.

### Mechanism 3: SA‑MiDEX’s Stochastic‑And‑Adversarial Switching

- **Claim:**  
  SA‑MiDEX attains best‑of‑both‑worlds for Borda objectives by running a stochastic successive‑elimination phase, monitoring for adversarial deviations via martingale concentration, and irreversibly switching to an adversarial EXP3 fallback when needed.

- **Mechanism:**  
  The algorithm first performs a baseline exploration (Stage 0) to collect `Θ(logT)` pairwise samples for every ordered pair.  Afterwards it enters stochastic mode: it maintains an active set `C`, selects `x_t ∈ C` in round‑robin, samples `y_t` uniformly, and updates empirical Borda scores.  Background random sampling with probability `p_t = min(1, K log t / t)` feeds a deviation detector that tracks per‑pair cumulative deviations `D_t(i,j)` from the baseline.  If `D_t(i,j) > τ(√(N_t(i,j)‑N_0(i,j))+1)`, the algorithm switches irrevocably to adversarial mode, where it uses EXP3 with explicit exploration and importance‑weighted updates based on transformed feedback `g_t = (o_t‑α_m)/β_m`.  The transformed feedback is unbiased (Lemma E.1) and bounded.  The analysis shows: in the stochastic case, suboptimal arms are eliminated after `O(K log T / (Δ_i^B)^2)` pulls (Lemma 5.11), background monitoring adds `O(K log² T)` overhead, and no false alarms occur (Lemma 5.8); in the adversarial case, either a switch occurs early (pre‑switch regret bounded by `O(K √(T log KT)` from the detection threshold) or no switch occurs (deviation itself bounded), and post‑switch EXP3 gives `O(K^{1/3} T^{2/3} (log K)^{1/3})` (Lemma 5.12).

- **Core assumption:**  
  The outcomes follow the pairwise‑subset model; transformed feedback `g_t` has bounded variance; martingale concentration (Freedman’s inequality) holds; the adversary’s cumulative preference shift either exceeds the threshold (triggering switch) or remains below it, limiting undetected manipulation.

- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “SA‑MiDEX combines stochastic successive elimination with martingale‑based deviation detection and an adversarial EXP3 fallback.”  
  - Section 5.1: Full algorithm description, including the detection statistic and EXP3 update.  
  - Corpus: *Adversarial Multi‑dueling Bandits* (Gajane, 2024) provides lower bounds and an adversarial‑only algorithm for Borda; SA‑MiDEX improves upon it by also handling the stochastic regime optimally.

- **Break condition:**  
  The guarantees rely on the fixed exploration budget `T_0` to establish a reliable baseline.  If `T_0` is too small or the adversary manipulates the baseline itself, the deviation detector may either miss a shift or raise false alarms, breaking the “no false alarm” guarantee and potentially causing linear regret.  Additionally, if the feedback model deviates from pairwise‑subset (e.g., non‑linear choice), the unbiasedness of `g_t` fails.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Multi‑armed bandits and regret minimization  
  - *Why needed here:* The work builds on standard bandit frameworks; understanding expected regret, exploration‑exploitation trade‑offs, and pseudo‑regret vs. high‑probability bounds is essential.  
  - *Quick check question:* What is the difference between pseudo‑regret and expected regret? (Pseudo‑regret uses the best fixed arm in hindsight; both are expectations but may be defined differently.)

- **Concept:** Dueling bandits and Condorcet/Borda objectives  
  - *Why needed:* Multi‑dueling generalizes pairwise comparisons to `m`‑way; one must grasp preference matrices, pairwise‑subset choice, and the two notions of optimality.  
  - *Quick check question:* How does a Borda winner differ from a Condorcet winner? (Borda aggregates performance against all opponents; Condorcet requires pairwise majority against every other arm.)

- **Concept:** Best‑of‑both‑worlds algorithms  
  - *Why needed:* The goal is to achieve optimal rates in both stochastic and adversarial regimes without prior knowledge.  Key techniques include Tsallis entropy regularization (Versatile‑DB) and stochastic‑and‑adversarial switching (SA‑MiDEX).  
  - *Quick check question:* What is the typical minimax adversarial regret for multi‑armed bandits? (Θ(√(KT)).)

- **Concept:** Martingale concentration and deviation detection  
  - *Why needed:* SA‑MiDEX uses Freedman’s inequality to bound the martingale formed by deviations from the baseline; this enables detection of adversarial shifts with provable error probabilities.  
  - *Quick check question:* In the detection statistic `D_t(i,j)`, what role does the threshold `τ√n` play? (It scales with the square root of the number of comparisons, reflecting typical martingale deviation.)

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  - MetaDueling wrapper (coordinates multiset construction, feeds binary outcomes to base dueling algorithm).  
  - Base dueling algorithm (e.g., Versatile-DB) – maintains arm selection distributions and updates from pairwise outcomes.  
  - SA-MiDEX core – includes modules: baseline explorer (round‑robin pairs), active‑set manager, Borda estimator, deviation detector, mode switch controller, adversarial EXP3 module.  
  - Feedback transformer (g_t = (o_t‑α_m)/β_m).  
  - Confidence radius calculator (for elimination).

- **Critical path:**  
  - *Condorcet:* Versatile-DB proposes `(x_t, y_t)` → MetaDueling builds `A_t` → play `A_t`, get `o_t` → MetaDueling sends `o_t` to Versatile-DB → update → repeat.  
  - *Borda:* Stage 0 baseline collection → lock `ˆP_T0`. Then loop: if stochastic mode, select x_t ∈ C (active set) and y_t ~ uniform; if |C|>1 pick round‑robin x_t and uniform y_t; else (x_t,y_t)=(i*,i*). Else (adversarial): sample (x_t,y_t) from q_t. Play A_t via MetaDueling, get winner I_t and o_t. Compute g_t = (o_t‑α_m)/β_m. Update counts, empirical Borda scores, elimination check, deviation detection; if triggered switch. In adversarial mode, update S̃ with importance‑weighted g_t.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *MetaDueling:* Simplicity (black‑box) vs. information loss – the construction only uses two arms, discarding potential information from `m>2` comparisons.  The factor `1/β_m ≤ 2` is a small constant overhead.  
  - *SA‑MiDEX:* Exploration overhead `T_0 = Θ(K² log T)` may dominate for small `T`.  The detection threshold `τ = √(8 log(K²T²/δ))` trades off false alarms (smaller `τ` yields more false switches) vs. detection delay (larger `τ` lets adversary manipulate longer).  The exploration probability `p_t = min(1, K log t / t)` adds `O(K log² T)` overhead but ensures uniform sampling for robust martingale bounds.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *High stochastic regret:* Suboptimal arms not eliminated on time → check that confidence radius uses correct `δ` and that elimination condition `ˆb_t(i)‑conf_t(i) > ˆb_t(j)+conf_t(j)` is implemented correctly.  
  - *No switch in clearly adversarial environment:* Possibly because baseline was already adversarial (so no deviation observed); this is actually covered by the adversarial bound but might lead to linear regret if the environment is non‑stationary.  Verify that `T_0` is sufficient for accurate baseline estimates.  
  - *Explosion in EXP3 weights:* Ensure `γ` (exploration) and `η` (learning rate) set as `Θ(T^{-1/3})` and `γ = Θ(T^{-1/3})` respectively; otherwise the adversarial regret may exceed `O(K^{1/3} T^{2/3})`.  
  - *Feedback mismatch:* The `g_t` transformation must use the correct `α_m, β_m` for the current `m`.  Using wrong constants violates unbiasedness and breaks all bounds.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **MetaDueling unbiasedness test:** With a known preference matrix `P` and a fixed pair `(x,y)`, run MetaDueling’s multiset construction many times, compute average `o_t`.  Verify it equals `α_m + β_m P(x,y)` within sampling error.  
  2. **SA‑MiDEX stochastic elimination test:** On a synthetic Borda instance with known gaps `Δ_i^B`, record how many times each suboptimal arm `i` is pulled before elimination.  Check that `N_T(i) = O(K log T / (Δ_i^B)²)` holds across multiple runs.  
  3. **SA‑MiDEX adversarial switch detection test:** Design an adversary that plays a fixed preference matrix for the first `T_0` rounds (so baseline is set), then abruptly changes the preference for a particular pair by a large `ϵ`.  Measure the round at which SA‑MiDEX switches and confirm it occurs within `O(√T)` rounds, and that post‑switch regret follows the `O(K^{1/3} T^{2/3})` trend.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Can we achieve best‑of‑both‑worlds guarantees without assuming a Condorcet winner?**  
   - *Basis:* Section 7, first bullet.  
   - *Why unresolved:* All presented algorithms require a fixed Condorcet winner; cyclic preferences break that foundation and demand a new, robust regret notion.  
   - *What evidence would resolve:* A single algorithm attaining sublinear expected regret in both stochastic and adversarial settings for arbitrary preference matrices, perhaps using a Borda‑based benchmark.

2. **Can multi‑way comparisons (m>2) be exploited more efficiently than via multisets containing only two distinct arms?**  
   - *Basis:* Section 7, second bullet.  
   - *Why unresolved:* MetaDueling reduces multi‑dueling to pairwise using only two distinct arms per round, discarding information from the full m‑way interaction that could accelerate learning.  
   - *What evidence would resolve:* An algorithm that extracts unbiased signals from all pairwise comparisons within the m chosen arms, yielding regret improvement that explicitly depends on m.

3. **Can we compete with time‑varying benchmarks (e.g., changing Condorcet winners) and achieve dynamic regret scaling with the number of changes or path length?**  
   - *Basis:* Section 7, third bullet.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Current analysis assumes stationary preferences; non‑stationarity invalidates gap‑dependent bounds and requires new techniques for dynamic environments.  
   - *What evidence would resolve:* Dynamic regret bounds for multi‑dueling bandits in both regimes (e.g., O(√(K T)) plus a variation term for adversarial; gap‑dependent bounds with a switching cost for stochastic).

4. **Can structural assumptions on the preference matrix (e.g., low rank, Plackett‑Luce) be leveraged to reduce the O(K²) exploration overhead in the Borda setting?**  
   - *Basis:* Section 7, fourth bullet.  
   - *Why unresolved:* SA‑MiDEX learns all pairwise preferences to estimate Borda scores, incurring an O(K² log T) exploration phase; structured models might avoid this full pairwise learning.  
   - *What evidence would resolve:* Algorithms exploiting such structure (e.g., low‑rank matrix factorization) with regret scaling in intrinsic dimension (e.g., O(R(T log T)^{3/4})) rather than K², applicable to both regimes.

5. **Can these techniques extend beyond winner‑only feedback to richer models like full rankings or pairwise margins?**  
   - *Basis:* Section 7, fifth bullet.  
   - *Why unresolved:* Winner‑only feedback severely limits information per round; richer feedback would require different estimators and concentration arguments while maintaining best‑of‑both‑worlds guarantees.  
   - *What evidence would resolve:* Unified algorithms for multi‑dueling with ranking or margin observations achieving sublinear regret in both stochastic and adversarial settings without prior knowledge of the regime.

## Limitations

- **Dependence on unspecified base algorithm**: MetaDueling's guarantees rely on using a dueling bandit algorithm that itself achieves best‑of‑both‑worlds performance; the exact implementation of Versatile‑DB is not detailed, introducing uncertainty.
- **Parameter sensitivity in SA‑MiDEX**: The deviation detector's threshold, confidence radii, and baseline size must be set exactly as in the analysis; mis‑tuning can lead to false switches or missed adversarial changes, harming regret.
- **Strong structural assumptions**: MetaDueling requires a unique Condorcet winner; SA‑MiDEX assumes stationary pairwise‑subset model and an accurate baseline. Violations (e.g., cyclic preferences, non‑stationarity) can invalidate the theoretical guarantees.
