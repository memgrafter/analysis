# Early Evidence of Vibe-Proving with Consumer LLMs: A Case Study on Spectral Region Characterization with ChatGPT-5.2 (Thinking)

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.18918
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18918
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Resolved Conjecture 20, delivering a complete characterization of nonreal eigenvalues for 4-cycle row-stochastic matrices via an auditable generate-referee-repair pipeline.

## Executive Summary
This case study demonstrates that consumer-grade LLMs like ChatGPT-5.2 (Thinking) can contribute to nontrivial mathematical research when combined with rigorous human oversight. Using a generate-referee-repair workflow across seven threads and four proof drafts, the authors resolved a long-standing conjecture about spectral regions of 4-cycle row-stochastic matrices. The LLM excelled at high-level proof architecture and algebraic manipulations, while human experts were essential for correctness-critical steps such as branch conventions, inequality domains, and endpoint handling. The work highlights both the potential and the current bottlenecks (particularly detailed algebra verification) of AI-assisted mathematics, and it provides an auditable record of the entire process.

## Method Summary
The researchers employed an iterative generate-referee-repair pipeline: (1) generate candidate proof steps or skeletons using ChatGPT-5.2 (Thinking); (2) conduct referee passes (by human experts or fresh LLM sessions) to identify hidden obligations (e.g., missing branch assumptions, sign checks, endpoint admissibility); (3) repair targeted subclaims via parallel independent sessions to produce verifiable patches. The problem had known scaffolding: the target spectral region was bounded by the Karpelevich right boundary and a conjectured left boundary G(a,b)=0, and a classical trigonometric method (Dmitriev–Dynkin) provided a template. The LLM adapted this template, proposing trigonometric parametrization and convexity-based arguments. The final proof was built through four draft versions (A–D), with human verification dominating time on correctness-critical closure and algebraic detail suggesting mechanization as a key future step.

## Key Results
- **Theorem proven**: Complete characterization of nonreal eigenvalues λ = a+ib for 4-cycle row-stochastic matrices: necessary and sufficient conditions are 0 ≤ a ≤ 1, a + |b| ≤ 1, and G(a,|b|) ≥ 0; strict interior points are attainable, and boundary points are explicitly realized by specific matrix families.
- **Workflow validated**: A generate-referee-repair pipeline with seven auditable threads and four proof drafts successfully combined LLM-generated insights with human verification.
- **Contribution analysis**: ChatGPT-5.2 (Thinking) excels at high-level proof architecture and algebraic manipulations but requires human oversight for subtle correctness issues (branching, domains, endpoints).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Scaffolded proof search
Prior mathematical scaffolding constrains the proof search space, enabling the LLM to propose viable high-level strategies. The problem's known boundaries (Karpelevich right boundary, conjectured left boundary G(a,b)=0) and the existence of a classical trigonometric method (Dmitriev–Dynkin) provide a template. The LLM is prompted with the target theorem and asked to adapt that template, producing a trigonometric parametrization and convexity-based argument. Core assumption: the problem is amenable to reduction via a known conceptual framework; without such scaffolding, the LLM may wander. Break condition: if the problem lacks recognizable structure or known boundaries, the LLM may fail to generate a coherent proof architecture.

### Mechanism 2: Iterative refinement via generate-referee-repair
The workflow cycles: (1) generate candidate steps/proof skeletons with LLM; (2) referee pass (human or fresh LLM) to surface hidden obligations (branch conventions, sign checks, endpoint admissibility); (3) repair targeted subclaims using parallel independent sessions to produce verifiable patches. This isolates errors and prevents regression. Core assumption: human experts can reliably detect subtle mathematical errors and convert them into explicit obligations; parallel sessions produce independent patches that can be cross-checked. Break condition: if error density is too high or human experts miss critical gaps, flawed steps may propagate; also if parallel sessions converge on invalid patches due to shared bias.

### Mechanism 3: Delegation with human correctness-critical closure
Delegating routine algebraic manipulations to the LLM while reserving human effort for correctness-critical closure and mechanization of bottleneck steps yields efficient progress. The LLM quickly produces symbolic expansions, factorizations, and trigonometric substitutions. Humans verify these manipulations and handle delicate sign/branch issues. The most time-consuming verification load is algebraic expansion and inequality-domain checking, which is ripe for offloading to CAS or certified checkers. Core assumption: LLM can generate syntactically correct algebra but may omit implicit assumptions; humans can spot missing conditions and guide mechanization. Break condition: if algebraic complexity exceeds human tolerance or CAS integration is unavailable, the verification bottleneck may stall progress.

## Foundational Learning
- **Trigonometric parametrization**: Converts multiplicative constraints (e.g., (z+t₁)...(z+t₄)=t₁t₂t₃t₄) into an angle-based optimization via u = arg(z+t), leading to function F(u) and feasible set P. This reparameterization is essential for applying convexity arguments. Quick check: derive t(u) = y cot u - x and |z+t(u)| = y csc u from u = arg(z+t).
- **Convexity and majorization (Jensen/Karamata)**: Controls sums of F(uₖ) over the feasible set, yielding the max principle and right boundary via Jensen. Strict convexity ensures uniqueness of the maximizer. ## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: LLM engine (ChatGPT-5.2 Thinking) -> Human expert -> Parallel session manager -> Version control & regression tracker -> CAS/mechanization tooling -> Audit repository
- **Critical path**: 1. Select problem with known scaffolding; 2. formulate target theorem and provide context; 3. prompt LLM for high-level strategy; 4. generate initial draft; 5. run referee pass to produce gap list; 6. launch parallel repair sessions for each gap; 7. verify patches and integrate into next draft; 8. iterate until all obligations discharged; 9. finalize theorem and boundary constructions.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Consumer LLM vs specialized (accessibility vs error rate); human verification depth (deep catches subtle errors but time-consuming); parallelism vs coordination (diversity vs overhead); mechanization integration (improves reliability but adds complexity).
- **Failure signatures**: Recurrent inverse-trigonometric branch errors; missing sign conditions before squaring/cross-multiplying; endpoint admissibility failures; compressed algebra omitting steps; regression via notation drift or dropped constraints.
- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Scaffolding sufficiency test: take a simpler problem (e.g., spectral region for 3×3 stochastic matrices) with known trigonometric proof; vary scaffolding (none, hints, full method); measure time to first viable skeleton. 2. Parallel patch search diversity: introduce known gap (e.g., Lemma 2 feasibility); launch ≥5 independent repair threads; measure correct-fix convergence rate and false positive rate. 3. Mechanization offload: integrate CAS (e.g., Sympy) to verify algebraic expansions and factorizations for the tight regime step; compare human verification time and error-detection rate vs human-only baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
Can individual researchers using consumer LLMs achieve substantive research progress with explicit human verification?
- **Basis in paper**: Introduction directly asks: "can individual researchers produce mathematically substantive progress with consumer-access models and explicit human verification?"
- **Why unresolved**: The study provides only one successful case study; it remains unclear how generalizable the result is to other domains, LLM versions, or researcher expertise, and whether required human effort scales favorably.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Multiple independent case studies across diverse mathematical areas, with standardized reporting of human effort, LLM model, and outcome metrics; controlled experiments varying prompting strategies to establish best practices.

### Open Question 2
How can the verification bottleneck—specifically manual checking of algebraic expansions and inequality domains—be automated in LLM-assisted theorem proving workflows?
- **Basis in paper**: The Discussion identifies the dominant verification load as "algebraic expansion/simplification and inequality-domain checking" and suggests mechanization would target the slowest work.
- **Why unresolved**: Human verification consumes most iteration time and is prone to subtle errors; automating it could drastically improve efficiency and reliability of the generate-referee-repair cycle.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Integration of CAS or proof assistant modules that automatically validate algebraic steps and inequality conditions; experimental evaluation showing reduced cycle times and error detection rates on algebra-heavy proof benchmarks; qualitative assessment of which sub-tasks are most amenable to automation.

### Open Question 3
Can the vibe-proving approach be effective on open mathematical problems that lack a known reduction template (e.g., no pre-existing trigonometric parametrization)?
- **Basis in paper**: The limitations section admits: "We do not test problems lacking such structure," referring to reliance on a classical template.
- **Why unresolved**: The LLM's success hinged on following a well-known strategy; it is unknown whether LLMs can discover or adapt new structural approaches for problems without such scaffolding.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematically applying the pipeline to a curated set of unsolved problems across various fields that have no known solution template; analyzing whether the LLM contributes novel proof ideas or remains limited to routine algebraic adjustments; measuring success rates and human effort compared to template-based problems.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability**: Evidence comes from one spectral theory problem; other domains may not allow similar LLM‑human synergy.
- **Expert Dependency**: The workflow required mathematicians to set scaffolding and referee; unknown whether non‑experts could use it effectively.
- **Undetected Errors**: Proof lacks formal verification; residual mistakes might remain despite iterative repair.

## Confidence
- **Theorem proven**: High – audited through seven threads and four drafts with expert verification.

- **LLM role (architecture & algebra)**: Medium – single‑problem observation.
- **Human criticality**: High – directly observed; human effort dominated correctness‑critical steps.
- **Bottleneck analysis**: High – time logs show disproportionate effort on algebraic detail.
