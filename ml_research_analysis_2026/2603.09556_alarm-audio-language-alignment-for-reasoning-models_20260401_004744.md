# ALARM: Audio-Language Alignment for Reasoning Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09556  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09556  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art open-source results on MMAU-speech and MMSU benchmarks, ranking third overall and surpassing most larger models while maintaining textual capabilities with lower training cost.  

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses a critical failure mode: standard self-generation for audio-language models breaks down when using reasoning LLMs because their chain-of-thought traces expose textual input cues, producing unnatural outputs. The core solution is **self-rephrasing**—a frozen reasoning LLM first generates an initial response from metadata, then rewrites it into an audio-grounded style, preserving distributional alignment. Additionally, multiple domain-specific audio encoders are fused via cross-attention and Perceiver methods to create compressed, robust representations without ASR dependencies. Trained on a newly constructed 6M-instance corpus, the 4B-parameter ALARM-E model achieves SOTA on MMAU-speech and MMSU, outperforms many larger models, and retains the backbone's textual skills while requiring far less training cost and data than competitors that fully fine-tune their LLMs.

## Method Summary
The method combines two main components. **Self-rephrasing** uses a frozen reasoning LLM to generate an initial response from textual metadata and a prompt, then rewrites it with rephrasing instructions to replace text-based phrasing with auditory-perception language, yielding audio-grounded targets without distribution shift. **Multi-encoder fusion** employs four frozen domain-specific encoders (Whisper for speech, W2V-BERT-2.0, MuQ for music, SSLAM for sound). Their frame-level features are aggregated, temporally compressed via adapters, and fused through one of three strategies: cross-attention stacks (ALARM-CA), Perceiver prefixes (ALARM-P), or an ensemble (ALARM-E). Only the adapters and fusion modules are trained; the LLM backbone is frozen to avoid catastrophic forgetting.

## Key Results
- SOTA open-source performance on MMAU-speech and MMSU benchmarks, ranking 3rd overall.  
- Surpasses most larger models despite a 4B parameter backbone.  
- Maintains textual capabilities (e.g., MMLU) while using substantially lower training cost and data volume than competing systems.  

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Self-Rephrasing for Distributional Alignment
- **Claim:** Converts self-generated responses into audio-understanding variants compatible with reasoning LLMs while preserving distributional alignment.  
- **Mechanism:** A frozen reasoning LLM first generates an initial response `R0` from textual metadata `A_text` and a prompt `P`. The same LLM then rewrites `R0` into `R_text` using rephrasing rules `I_reph`, replacing text-based cues with auditory-perception phrasing.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.1 describe the two-stage process; the 6M-instance corpus applies rephrasing to nearly all samples.  
- **Break condition:** Incomplete rephrasing leaves residual textual references (e.g., metadata quantities), causing the model to rely on text cues and produce unnatural outputs.  

### Mechanism 2: Multi-Encoder Fusion for Robust Representations
- **Claim:** Fusing multiple domain-specific audio encoders via cross-attention and Perceiver yields compressed, robust representations without ASR, avoiding VAD/ASR noise.  
- **Mechanism:** Four frozen encoders produce frame-level features. These are aggregated over layers, temporally compressed to 25/50 Hz via adapters, and fused using cross-attention stacks (ALARM-CA), Perceiver prefixes (ALARM-P), or ensembling (ALARM-E). The fused sequence is fed to the frozen LLM.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.2 detail fusion; Section 4.3 shows single-encoder ablations and multi-encoder superiority on diverse tasks.  
- **Break condition:** Misaligned feature spaces or over-compression cause loss of fine-grained details (e.g., temporal cues), degrading performance on certain tasks.  

### Mechanism 3: Large-Scale Diverse Corpus for Reduced Hallucinations
- **Claim:** A large-scale, diverse multi-task corpus with aligned prompts and audio reduces hallucinations and enables effective training.  
- **Mechanism:** From a preliminary dataset with audio and metadata, a fixed instruction LLM generates 20 candidate prompts per sample, filters for answerability and non-textual exposure, and uniformly samples one. The final response is generated by the reasoning LLM via self-rephrasing.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions 6M instances; Section 2.1 describes the pipeline; Section 4 shows SOTA results.  
- **Break condition:** If filtering permits prompts requiring absent metadata, hallucinated targets enter training; rephrasing may not fully naturalize responses, risking textual leakage.  

## Foundational Learning
- **Self-Generation for Modality Alignment**  
  Why needed: Standard self-generation uses textual surrogates (e.g., transcripts), but for reasoning models the chain-of-thought exposes textual input. Self-rephrasing hides textual input during generation.  
  Quick check: Why does standard self-generation fail for reasoning LLMs, and how does self-rephrasing mitigate that?  

- **Multi-Encoder Fusion via Cross-Attention and Perceiver**  
  Why needed: Different audio domains (speech, music, sound) require specialized encoders; naive concatenation is costly. Cross-attention and Perceiver provide efficient fusion and compression.  
  Quick check: What are the trade-offs between ALARM-CA, ALARM-P, and ALARM-E in terms of token rate, compute, and performance across domains?  

- **Catastrophic Forgetting in Multimodal Fine-Tuning**  
  Why needed: Fine-tuning the LLM on audio data can degrade text capabilities. Freezing the LLM preserves text performance.  
  Quick check: How does freezing the LLM backbone help maintain textual capabilities, and what is the computational cost of training only adapters?  

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Audio → Four frozen encoders (Whisper, W2V-BERT2.0, MuQ, SSLAM) → Adapters (temporal compression & projection) → Fusion module (ALARM-CA/ALARM-P/ALARM-E) → Frozen RLM backbone (Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507) → Response  

Critical path:  
1. Audio processed by each encoder to frame-level features.  
2. Features aggregated over layers and temporally compressed via adapters to 25/50 Hz.  
3. Fusion combines adapted features into a single sequence.  
4. Sequence prepended to instruction prompt; LLM autoregressively generates response.  
5. Loss computed on target tokens; gradients update only adapters and fusion modules.  

Design tradeoffs:  
- Token rate: Lower (25 Hz) reduces compute but may lose details; higher (50 Hz) improves performance but increases memory.  
- Fusion: CA enables dynamic interaction but may dilute Whisper's ASR strength; P preserves Whisper separately but compresses others fixed-length; E combines both at inference cost of two passes.  
- Encoder selection: Domain-specific encoders boost non-speech tasks but add parameters and potential misalignment.  

Failure signatures:  
- Speech degradation in ALARM-CA: If Whisper features are overwritten, speech tasks suffer.  
- Model copies metadata: Outputs contain "from the metadata" if rephrasing ineffective.  
- Catastrophic forgetting: If LLM accidentally fine-tuned, text benchmarks drop.  
- Memory explosion: Fusion outputs long sequences (>500 tokens/sec) cause OOM.  

First 3 experiments:  
1. Train single-encoder variants (AL-Whisper-R, AL-W2VBERT2-R, etc.) to isolate domain contributions and validate multi-encoder need.  
2. Compare fusion methods (CA, P, E) on MMSU and MMAU to confirm design choices; ALARM-E should be best overall.  
3. Evaluate text capabilities (MMLU-Pro, GSM8K) to verify no catastrophic forgetting when LLM frozen.  

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Optimal fusion strategies:** What fusion architectures best combine multiple audio encoders without degrading Whisper's content understanding? Basis: ALARM-CA drops speech reasoning; no systematic ablation of fusion design space.  
2. **Generalizability of self-rephrasing:** Does it work for reasoning LLMs beyond Qwen3-4B-Thinking? Basis: Only Qwen used; other RLMs may have different behaviors.  
3. **Data scaling:** Is the 6M-instance corpus sufficient, or could smaller high-quality datasets suffice? Basis: No systematic scaling study performed.  
4. **Speech vs. non-speech trade-off:** Why does ALARM-E excel on speech but lag on sound/music compared to Qwen2.5-Omni, and how can this be alleviated? Basis: Fusion asymmetry not investigated.  

## Limitations
- The 6M-instance ALARM training corpus is not publicly released, blocking direct replication.  
- Exact fusion module architectures (e.g., cross-attention heads, dropout) and rephrasing instruction templates are not detailed in the paper.  
- The impact of the thinking budget (B=1536 tokens) on rephrasing quality and final performance is unquantified.  

## Confidence
- **Self-rephrasing mechanism:** Medium (evidence from paper; break condition not fully ruled out).  
- **Multi-encoder fusion:** Medium (ablations support benefit; exact hyperparameters missing).  
- **Large-scale corpus:** Low (dataset not public; pipeline specifics sparse).  
- **SOTA results & efficiency:** Medium (checkpoints available but depend on unreleased data).
