# Token-Weighted Multi-Target Learning for Generative Recommenders with Curriculum Learning

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2601.17787
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17787
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Token-weighted multi-target learning with curriculum improves Hit@5 by 6.11% and NDCG@5 by 7.14% on average over TIGER.

## Executive Summary
Generative recommenders that generate item semantic IDs autoregressively typically treat all tokens equally, overlooking that tokens have different importance. This paper introduces two token-weighting strategies: Front-Greater Weighting, which prioritizes early tokens that most reduce item uncertainty given their prefixes, and Frequency Weighting, which upweights rare tokens to counteract popularity bias. These are integrated into a multi-target learning framework alongside standard likelihood, using adaptive logit-scaling parameters and curriculum learning to shift emphasis from stability (Front-Greater and standard loss) to fine-grained discrimination (Frequency loss) over training. Experiments on four datasets demonstrate consistent gains over strong baselines, averaging +6.11% Hit@5 and +7.14% NDCG@5 relative to TIGER, with robust performance across head and tail items and different semantic-ID constructions (RQ-VAE and PQ). Ablation studies confirm that each component contributes to the overall improvement.

## Method Summary
The method comprises four key elements:  
1. **Front-Greater weighting**: For each token position \(k\), items sharing the same top-\(k\) tokens are grouped. The reduction in expected Fréchet variance when adding the \(k\)-th token (\(\delta_k\)) measures its discriminative contribution; weights are proportional to \(\max(\delta_k,0)\) and normalized by position.  
2. **Frequency weighting**: Token weight is inversely proportional to its effective sample size \(E_n = (1-\beta^n)/(1-\beta)\) (\(\beta=0.99\)), upweighting rare tokens that carry higher marginal information.  
3. **Multi-target loss**: Three objectives (\(L_{fg}, L_{fr}, L_{or}\)) are scaled by learnable parameters \(\lambda_j\) and normalized to \(\alpha_j\). Scaling logits by \(\alpha_j\) before softmax yields an equivalent weighted sum of losses.  
4. **Curriculum learning**: Over training steps \(t\), scaling factors are decayed: \(\lambda'_{fg} = e^{-ct}\lambda_{fg}\), \(\lambda'_{or} = e^{-ct}\lambda_{or}\), \(\lambda'_{fr} = (1-e^{-ct})\lambda_{fr}\), then re-normalized to \(\alpha'_j\). This gradually shifts focus from Front-Greater and standard loss to Frequency loss.  

Token weights are precomputed; the generative model (T5 backbone) is trained with this composite objective. Inference uses constrained beam search to produce valid semantic IDs.

## Key Results
- **Consistent improvement**: Over four datasets, the proposed method yields average gains of +6.11% Hit@5 and +7.14% NDCG@5 compared to the strong TIGER baseline.
- **Ablation efficacy**: Removing any component (Front-Greater, Frequency, multi-target scaling, or curriculum) degrades performance, demonstrating that each contributes uniquely.
- **Head–tail robustness**: Larger relative improvements are observed on tail items, confirming the bias mitigation effect of Frequency weighting; gains hold for both RQ-VAE and PQ semantic IDs, indicating robustness to ID construction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Front-Greater Token Weighting**  
*Claim*: Early tokens in hierarchical semantic IDs maximize reduction in item uncertainty, enabling coarse-to-fine identification.  
*Mechanism*: For prefix length \(k\), items with shared top-\(k\) tokens form groups. The expected Fréchet variance \(\mu_k\) (mean squared distance from group centroid) quantifies within-group dispersion. Disambiguation \(\delta_k = \mu_{k-1} - \mu_k\) (non-negative by Lemma 4.1) measures information gain; weight \(w_{fg}^k \propto \max(\delta_k,0)\) and is normalized across positions.  
*Break condition*: If semantic IDs lack hierarchical structure (e.g., random token assignments), prefix groups do not meaningfully refine items, making \(\delta_k\) arbitrary and potentially harmful.

**Frequency Token Weighting**  
*Claim*: Rare tokens carry more discriminative power under long-tailed distributions, and upweighting them mitigates popularity bias.  
*Mechanism*: For a token appearing \(n\) times, its effective number of samples is \(E_n = (1-\beta^n)/(1-\beta)\) (\(\beta=0.99\)). The marginal information gain \(\Delta_n = \beta^{n-1}\) decreases as \(n\) increases; thus weight \(w_{fr}^k \propto 1/E_{n_k}\) emphasizes rare tokens, with length-wise normalization.  
*Break condition*: On uniform or very small datasets, rare tokens may be noisy, causing overfitting and degraded generalization.

**Multi-Target Learning with Curriculum**  
*Claim*: A curriculum that decays emphasis on coarse semantics and gradually increases focus on rare tokens stabilizes training and improves fine-grained discrimination.  
*Mechanism*: Three losses (\(L_{fg}, L_{fr}, L_{or}\)) have learnable \(\lambda_j\). At step \(t\), curriculum applies: \(\lambda'_{fg} = e^{-ct}\lambda_{fg}\), \(\lambda'_{or} = e^{-ct}\lambda_{or}\), \(\lambda'_{fr} = (1-e^{-ct})\lambda_{fr}\). Normalized \(\alpha'_j = \lambda'_j / \sum \lambda'\) scale corresponding logits. Early training heavily weights Front-Greater and original loss; later training shifts to Frequency.  
*Break condition*: Inappropriate curriculum rate \(c\) (too high or too low) disrupts the intended progression; Figure 4 shows performance peaks at moderate \(c\).

## Foundational Learning
**Autoregressive generation**  
*Why needed*: Models sequential user behavior by generating next-item IDs token-by-token, borrowing from language modeling.  
*Quick check*: How does constrained beam search ensure generated token sequences are valid item IDs (respecting codebook index ranges per position)?

**Semantic IDs (RQ-VAE/PQ)**  
*Why needed*: Discretizes continuous item representations into fixed-length token sequences, enabling discrete token prediction. RQ-VAE uses residual quantization to capture hierarchy; PQ splits embeddings into independent sub-vectors.  
*Quick check*: What distinguishes RQ from PQ? (RQ sequentially captures residuals; PQ quantizes independent segments.)

**Token-weighted cross-entropy**  
*Why needed*: Standard cross-entropy treats all tokens equally, missing their varying importance. Weighting modifies gradients to reflect token-specific relevance.  
*Quick check*: In multi-target scaling, how does scaling logits by \(\alpha_j\) before softmax affect the contribution of each objective to the overall gradient?

**Information gain via variance reduction**  
*Why needed*: Provides a principled measure of how much a token reduces uncertainty about the target item within prefix-defined groups, guiding Front-Greater weights.  
*Quick check*: Why measure dispersion with Fréchet variance? It naturally extends Euclidean distances to group-level uncertainty and is computationally efficient.

**Curriculum learning dynamics**  
*Why needed*: Aligns loss weighting with the learning trajectory—early stages require stable coarse signals, while later stages need fine-grained discrimination.  
*Quick check*: Why decay Front-Greater and original weights while rising Frequency? Early focus on stability avoids poor local optima; later focus on rare tokens aids tail item separation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Data processor → Sequence splits; ID generator (RQ-VAE/PQ) → Semantic IDs; Token-weighting modules → Precomputed weights (Front-Greater, Frequency); Generative model (T5 backbone) ↔ Curriculum scheduler ↔ Multi-target head (scaled logits) → Training loop; Inference engine (constrained beam search) → Recommendations.

**Critical path**  
1. Preprocess interactions and train ID generator.  
2. Compute token weights from item embeddings (Front-Greater) and token counts (Frequency).  
3. Training per step: load batch → fetch weights → compute three token-weighted cross-entropy losses → apply current \(\alpha'_j\) (from curriculum) to scale logits → sum losses → backpropagate → update model and learnable \(\lambda_j\) → advance curriculum (update \(\alpha'_j\)).  
4. Inference: load checkpoint, run constrained beam search to output valid ID sequences.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Balancing Front-Greater vs. Frequency: Too much Frequency early destabilizes; too little harms tail performance.  
- Curriculum rate \(c\) tuning: Highly sensitive (Figure 4), may require dataset-specific adjustment.  
- Front-Greater dependence on hierarchical IDs: May not generalize to non-hierarchical representations.  
- Precomputation overhead: Token weights require item embeddings and dispersion calculations ahead of training.

**Failure signatures**  
- Tail performance collapse: If \(\alpha_{fr}\) rises too quickly, head items suffer; if too slow, tail items unchanged. Monitor \(\alpha\) curves.  
- Gradient imbalance: One loss dominates, causing unstable training; check relative magnitudes of individual losses.  
- Incorrect Front-Greater weights: Weights not decreasing with position or sum ≠ ID length → implementation error (e.g., missing clamp or normalization).  
- Poor PQ vs. RQ results: Could indicate misaligned codebook training or that Front-Greater weights were computed on different embeddings.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Main results** (RQ1): Evaluate on four datasets; proposed method outperforms TIGER and other generative baselines with average +6% Hit@5 and +7% NDCG@5.  
2. **Ablation study** (RQ2): Remove components one by one; each removal hurts performance, confirming additive value.  
3. **Head–tail analysis** (RQ5): Partition test items by popularity; gains are substantially larger on tail items (e.g., >10% relative improvement), demonstrating bias mitigation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Generalization to alternative item representation methods beyond RQ-VAE and PQ (e.g., contrastive or multimodal features).  
2. Integration of additional objectives (fairness, diversity, user-intent modeling) within the multi-target framework without harming core performance.  
3. Optimality of the exponential decay curriculum schedule across datasets; whether dynamic, dataset-adaptive schedules could improve results.  
4. Fixed vs. adaptive selection of the frequency weighting parameter \(\beta\) based on dataset tail characteristics.  
5. Transfer of token-weighting strategies to other generative recommendation settings (e.g., conversational, multi-behavior).

## Limitations
- Curriculum decay schedule and its start step are not fully specified; per-dataset tuning of \(c\) is required.  
- Inference details (constrained beam search constraints and beam width) are omitted, leaving implementation ambiguity.  
- The method relies on precomputed item embeddings (LLaMA2-7B), which may not be universally accessible or consistent.  
- Front-Greater weighting assumes hierarchical prefix structure; effectiveness may degrade for non-hierarchical IDs.  
- Frequency weighting could overfit rare tokens on small datasets, risking instability.

## Confidence
- **Overall performance gains** (average +6% over TIGER): High confidence (consistent across four datasets, thorough ablations).  
- **Front-Greater weighting mechanism** (early tokens reduce uncertainty): Medium confidence (theoretically grounded, but limited external evidence; depends on hierarchical IDs).  
- **Frequency weighting mechanism** (rare token upweighting mitigates bias): Medium confidence (sound derivation and observed tail gains, but risks on small data).

## Next Checks
1. Reproduce the ablation study to verify each component’s isolated contribution.  
2. Conduct a sensitivity analysis of curriculum decay constant \(c\) over a wider range (e.g., \(1e^{-5}\) to \(1e^{-4}\)) and across datasets of varying sparsity.  
3. Evaluate the method with shuffled (non-hierarchical) semantic IDs; a breakdown of Front-Greater would confirm its dependence on hierarchical prefix structure.
