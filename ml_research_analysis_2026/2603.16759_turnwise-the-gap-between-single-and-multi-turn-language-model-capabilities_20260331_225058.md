# TurnWise: The Gap between Single- and Multi-turn Language Model Capabilities

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.16759
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16759
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: Training with as little as 10k multi-turn conversations during DPO reduces the multi-/single-turn performance gap by 12% on TurnWiseEval-Self, while maintaining single-turn evaluation scores.

## Executive Summary
The paper identifies a significant gap between single- and multi-turn language model capabilities, attributing it to the dominance of single-turn training and evaluation paradigms. To address this, the authors introduce **TurnWiseEval**, a benchmark that isolates multi-turn ability by comparing model performance on a multi-turn conversation against an equivalent single-turn prompt, and **TurnWiseData**, a pipeline for scalable synthetic multi-turn conversation generation. Experiments on Olmo 3 7B Instruct show that incorporating a modest 10k synthetic multi-turn dialogues into preference tuning with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) narrows the performance gap by 12% without harming single-turn results.

## Method Summary
TurnWiseEval evaluates multi-turn proficiency by constructing a pairwise comparison: for a given multi-turn conversation, a flattened single-turn prompt is derived that contains all information from the conversation history. The model is scored on both the original multi-turn context and its single-turn equivalent; the difference isolates the model’s multi-turn handling ability. TurnWiseData generates synthetic multi-turn conversations by starting from single-turn prompts and iteratively generating user turns and assistant responses, ensuring diversity and avoiding drift. The generated conversations are then used to create preference pairs for DPO training. The authors experiment with Olmo 3 7B Instruct, first applying supervised fine-tuning (SFT), then augmenting with DPO on synthetic multi-turn data, and finally evaluating on TurnWiseEval and standard single-turn benchmarks.

## Key Results
- TurnWiseEval successfully isolates multi-turn ability via pairwise comparison with equivalent single-turn prompts.
- DPO with 10k synthetic multi-turn conversations reduces the multi-turn/single-turn performance gap by **12%** on TurnWiseEval-Self.
- Single-turn evaluation scores remain unaffected after multi-turn DPO training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The benchmark’s isolation relies on the premise that flattening a multi-turn conversation into a single prompt presents an equivalent task in terms of required knowledge and reasoning, thereby attributing any performance difference to the model’s ability to manage dialogue state and context across turns. TurnWiseData’s independent turn generation avoids compounding errors while maintaining coherent and diverse interactions. DPO, being a preference-based method, leverages relative quality signals and is less sensitive to the absolute quality of synthetic data compared to supervised fine-tuning; this robustness allows the model to improve multi-turn skills without degrading single-turn performance that depends on high-quality direct demonstrations.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Multi-turn Dialogue Modeling**  
   *Why needed:* Real-world interactions span multiple turns, requiring models to maintain context and coherence over time.  
   *Quick check:* Model maintains consistent persona and references across dialog turns in human evaluation.

2. **Synthetic Data Generation**  
   *Why needed:* Scale multi-turn training data without costly human annotation.  
   *Quick check:* Generated conversations exhibit diversity, coherence, and realistic turn patterns.

3. **Preference Tuning (DPO)**  
   *Why needed:* Align models with human preferences using a simple, stable objective.  
   *Quick check:* DPO fine-tuned model outperforms SFT on held‑out preference pairs.

4. **Evaluation Isolation (TurnWiseEval)**  
   *Why needed:* Measure multi-turn ability disentangled from single-turn competence.  
   *Quick check:* Verify that single-turn equivalents are perceived as equally difficult via human annotation.

5. **TurnWiseData Pipeline**  
   *Why needed:* Generate controllable, scalable multi-turn dialogues for training.  
   *Quick check:* Ablate pipeline steps and measure impact on downstream model performance.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Base Model (Olmo 3 7B Instruct) → DPO Training (with TurnWiseData) → Evaluated Model (gap‑reduced)

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate synthetic multi‑turn conversations via TurnWiseData.  
2. Construct preference pairs (chosen/rejected) from these conversations.  
3. Apply DPO on top of the base SFT model.  
4. Evaluate on TurnWiseEval and standard single‑turn benchmarks.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Synthetic vs. real data:* Synthetic data offers scale and control but may lack nuanced human traits.  
- *DPO vs. SFT:* DPO tolerates noisier data and preserves single‑turn skills better; SFT may overfit to data imperfections.  
- *Data volume:* 10k conversations already yield measurable gains; more data may help but with diminishing returns.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Multi‑turn gap unchanged → synthetic data too noisy or unrealistic.  
- Single‑turn scores drop → DPO overfitting to synthetic style or distribution shift.  
- DPO loss diverges → preference pairs too ambiguous or noisy.

**First experiments:**  
1. Compare DPO and SFT using identical synthetic multi‑turn data.  
2. Ablate the amount of multi‑turn data (1k, 10k, 100k) and measure gap reduction.  
3. Test robustness of TurnWiseEval by feeding intermediate turns from various generators (e.g., GPT‑4, human, smaller models).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How robust is TurnWiseEval to the quality of intermediate assistant turns?
- **Basis in paper:** The Limitations state: "TURNWISEEVAL scores may vary with the quality of the intermediate turns... While this affects absolute score, it does not typically affect overall model rankings."
- **Why unresolved:** Varying intermediate quality could affect absolute and possibly relative scores, threatening evaluation validity. Only two generators (GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 Turbo) were tested; broader impacts are unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate a fixed set of target models using intermediate turns from a diverse array of generators (different sizes, architectures, human-written) and compute score/rank correlations. Consistent rankings across generators would support robustness.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why does Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) retain single-turn performance better than supervised fine-tuning (SFT) when training on synthetic multi-turn data?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors conjecture: "We conjecture that this is due to higher sensitivity to the data generator’s abilities with SFT than with preference tuning since preference tuning is less dependent on the absolute quality of the data."
- **Why unresolved:** This explanation remains speculative; the fundamental reason (e.g., implicit reward modeling, relative losses) is unclear. Without understanding, we cannot generalize to other data types or training scenarios.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments comparing SFT and DPO learning dynamics (gradients, loss curvature) on synthetic data with controlled errors. Theoretical analysis comparing the two algorithms' use of absolute vs. relative quality could also clarify.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Do the observed benefits of multi-turn training extend to larger models, different architectures, and varied pre‑training objectives?
- **Basis in paper:** Experiments were restricted to Olmo 3 7B Instruct: "We perform all training experiments on Olmo3-7B-Instruct-SFT... our experimental settings are limited in scope to focus on dimensions specific to multi-turn training."
- **Why unresolved:** The 7B model may not represent broader trends. Scaling could amplify or diminish gains, and architectures like encoder‑decoders might respond differently. This limits the paper’s conclusions for the wider community.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the multi‑turn data augmentation pipeline across a spectrum of model scales (e.g., 1B‑100B), architectures (decoder‑only, encoder‑decoder), and pre‑training objectives. Track changes in the multi‑/single‑turn gap using TurnWiseEval and single‑turn benchmarks.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can more sophisticated, stateful user simulations (e.g., with dialogue state tracking and goal alignment) improve multi‑turn training beyond the independent turn generation of TurnWiseData?
- **Basis in paper:** TurnWiseData independently generates each user turn to avoid drift and expensive online simulation. Related work notes user simulation challenges (state tracking, goal alignment) but does not evaluate advanced simulations for training.
- **Why unresolved:** Independent turn generation may lack long‑term coherence and natural goal evolution, potentially limiting the model’s ability to handle realistic multi‑turn interactions. Richer simulations could yield larger gains but remain unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Build user simulators with explicit state tracking and goal‑driven behavior; generate multi‑turn conversations of varying complexity; train models with identical data budgets. Compare performance on TurnWiseEval and, critically, on human evaluations or real user interaction logs.

## Limitations
- **Benchmark isolation validity (Medium):** The claim that TurnWiseEval successfully "isolates" multi‑turn ability by flattening conversation context may not fully control for task complexity, as context management and history introduce unique challenges not captured by a single prompt.
- **Synthetic data fidelity (Low):** Confidence is low that TurnWiseData’s synthetic distribution matches real human‑AI dialogues in diversity and error patterns, which could limit generalizability of observed gains.
- **Result generality (Medium):** Experiments are limited to Olmo 3 7B Instruct; it is uncertain if the 12% gap reduction and preservation of single‑turn scores hold for other model scales, architectures, or training regimes.

## Confidence
- **Benchmark Design & Utility:** Medium
- **Synthetic Data Pipeline Efficacy:** Low
- **DPO Improvement & Gap Reduction:** Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Human Annotation Study:** Conduct a human evaluation where annotators judge whether the "single‑turn equivalent" prompt in TurnWiseEval truly presents an equivalent task difficulty and information requirement as the original multi‑turn conversation context.
2. **Data Quality Ablation:** Perform an ablation study comparing DPO training using TurnWiseData synthetic conversations versus a smaller set of high‑quality, human‑written multi‑turn dialogues from existing sources (e.g., ShareGPT) to quantify the performance delta attributable to synthetic data quality.
3. **Cross‑Model Replication:** Replicate the key DPO experiment (10k multi‑turn conversations) on at least two additional, diverse decoder‑only LLMs (e.g., Llama 3, Mistral) to test the robustness of the observed performance gap reduction.
