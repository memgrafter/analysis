# A New Family of Poisson Non-negative Matrix Factorization Methods Using the Shifted Log Link

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05845
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05845
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** Introduces Poisson NMF with a shifted-log (log1p) link parameterized by *c*, enabling a continuous spectrum between additive (large *c*) and multiplicative (small *c*) factor combinations, with an efficient approximate algorithm for sparse data that reduces computational complexity.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel extension of Poisson non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) by replacing the standard log link with a shifted-log (log1p) link function controlled by a single tuning parameter *c*. This modification relaxes the implicit additive assumption of standard Poisson NMF, allowing the model to capture multiplicative-like interactions between latent factors when *c* is small. An efficient approximate maximum likelihood algorithm is developed, exploiting sparsity to reduce computational cost from *O(nmK)* to *O((ω+n+m)K + (n+m)K²)*. The method is applied to three diverse count datasets—bulk RNA-seq, single-cell RNA-seq, and text documents—demonstrating that *c* substantially influences the resulting factors: larger *c* tends to produce sparser, more correlated factors akin to clustered solutions, while smaller *c* yields denser, less correlated, and more modular representations. The approach provides a flexible tool for exploring count data decomposition, with interpretability benefits tailored to the scientific context.

## Method Summary
The core method models a non-negative count matrix **Y** ∈ ℕ<sup>n×p</sup> as **Y** ~ Poisson(Λ), where Λ = *L F*<sup>⊤</sup> with non-negative factor matrices *L* and *F*. Instead of the canonical log link *g*(λ) = log(λ), the shifted-log link is defined as *g*(λ; *c*) = α<sub>*c*</sub> log(1 + λ/*c*), with α<sub>*c*</sub> = 1 for small *c* and α<sub>*c*</sub> ≈ *c* for large *c*. This link interpolates between a multiplicative scale (for small *c*, where *g*(λ) ≈ log(λ)) and an additive scale (for large *c*, where *g*(λ) ≈ λ). Parameters are estimated via alternating optimization: fixing *F*, each row of *L* is updated by solving a non-negative Poisson regression (using cyclic coordinate ascent); fixing *L*, each column of *F* is updated similarly. For large sparse matrices, an approximate log-likelihood is used where the expensive exponential term is replaced by a quadratic Chebyshev expansion *only for zero-count entries*, dramatically reducing computational burden. Row-specific size factors account for library size, and a rank-1 warm-start improves convergence. Final *L* columns are rescaled to unit maximum.

## Key Results
- The choice of *c* meaningfully alters factor interpretations across all three applications: e.g., in MCF-7 RNA-seq, *c*=1 yields a clear baseline factor, while *c*=∞ produces correlated factors obscuring key genes; in pancreatic single-cell data, *c*=1 gives modular cell-type representations, *c*=∞ gives clustered solutions; in BBC news, *c*=0.001 reveals "double-duty" topics absent at *c*=∞.
- Systematic variation shows larger *c* produces sparser factor loadings and higher inter-factor correlation; smaller *c* produces denser loadings and less correlated, more modular factors.
- The Chebyshev approximation maintains accuracy (likelihood ratio ≈1) even for small *c*, whereas a Taylor expansion fails, enabling scalable inference for sparse count data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The shifted-log link mediates between additive and multiplicative factor combination.
- **Mechanism:** The link *g*(λ; *c*) = α<sub>*c*</sub> log(1 + λ/*c*) has two regimes: as *c*→∞, α<sub>*c*</sub>→*c* and *g*(λ)≈λ (additive); as *c*→0, α<sub>*c*</sub>=1 and *g*(λ)≈log(λ)−log(*c*) (multiplicative). Theorem 2 shows small *c* acts as a log-fold-change.
- **Evidence:** Theorem 1/2 and Figure 1 show limiting behavior. Applications (Sec 4) demonstrate interpretational shifts: e.g., *c*=1 separates baseline factor in MCF-7, while *c*=∞ clusters factors.
- **Break condition:** Mismatched *c* relative to data-generating process yields factors that fail to capture true structure (e.g., correlated gene scores obscuring markers for *c*=∞).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Quadratic Chebyshev approximation on zero entries reduces computational complexity for sparse data.
- **Mechanism:** The exact log-likelihood sums over all *nm* entries, with exponentials exp(∑<sub>k</sub> l<sub>ik</sub> f<sub>jk</sub>) that are costly. For sparse matrices (ω ≪ *nm* zero counts), the approximation replaces the exponential for zeros with a quadratic expansion *x* ↦ η<sub>0</sub> + η<sub>1</sub>*x* + η<sub>2</sub>*x*<sup>2</sup> over [0, log(1+1/*c*)], and pre-computes matrix traces (e.g., tr(*F*<sup>⊤</sup>*F L*<sup>⊤</sup>*L F*<sup>⊤</sup>*F*)) to compute sums efficiently. Only non-zero entries require exact exponentials.
- **Evidence:** Sec 3.2 derives equation (16), complexity Table 1, and empirical scaling Figure 2. Simulation Figure 3 shows Chebyshev yields likelihood ratio ≈1; Taylor fails for small *c*.
- **Break condition:** Approximation fails if zero entries correspond to large inferred rates (e.g., extreme *c* or factors), making the quadratic expansion inaccurate over the relevant range.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** *c* modulates solution sparsity and factor correlation: large *c* → sparse, correlated factors; small *c* → dense, uncorrelated, modular factors.
- **Mechanism:** Additive combination (*c*=∞) encourages factors to explain shared variance via overlapping *F* columns, while multiplicative combination (*c*→0) forces distinct multiplicative contributions, favoring orthogonal factors to remain expressive. The likelihood gradient shape also changes with *c*.
- **Evidence:** Sec 4 and Figure 9 quantify trends across *c*. Appendix F gives a preliminary geometric argument for *c*→0: achievable λ vectors lie on convex hull of *F* rows, favoring sparsity and orthogonality.
- **Break condition:** The relationship may not hold universally; theory is preliminary and dataset-dependent.

## Foundational Learning
- **Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF):** Decomposes a matrix into parts-based representations via non-negativity constraints. Needed here as the underlying framework; distinguishes from PCA by promoting additive combinations of non-negative components.
  - **Quick check:** Why does non-negativity promote "parts-based" decompositions rather than "holistic" ones?
- **Generalized Linear Models (GLMs) and Link Functions:** The Poisson rate λ is linked to the linear predictor via a monotonic function *g*. Standard Poisson NMF uses *g*(λ)=log(λ); log1p NMF uses a custom link.
  - **Quick check:** How does changing the link function alter the interpretation of factor loadings as effect sizes?
- **Sparse Matrix Computation:** Many count matrices are predominantly zero. Exploiting sparsity is key to reducing computational complexity from O(*nmK*) to O((ω+*n*+*m*)*K* + (*n*+*m*)*K*<sup>2</sup>).
  - **Quick check:** What computational bottleneck shifts when moving from a sum of linear terms (standard Poisson) to a sum of exponentials (log1p Poisson) in the likelihood?
- **Cyclic Coordinate Ascent:** Solves the non-negative Poisson regressions by updating one coefficient at a time with projected Newton steps.
  - **Quick check:** Why is cyclic coordinate ascent suitable for non-convex, box-constrained problems like these regressions?
- **Chebyshev Polynomial Approximation:** Used to approximate exp(*x*) quadratically over a defined interval, balancing accuracy and computational gain.
  - **Quick check:** How does choosing the approximation interval [0, log(1+1/*c*)] adapt to the link parameter *c*?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input count matrix **Y**, rank *K*, tuning constant *c* → compute size factors *s* → initialize via rank-1 fit → Alternate: fix *F* → solve *n* Poisson regressions for rows of *L* (using Chebyshev approx for zeros) → fix *L* → solve *p* Poisson regressions for columns of *F* → repeat → post-hoc rescale *L* to max=1 → output factors *L*, *F*.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Preprocess: filter low-count features, compute size factors *s*<sub>*i*</sub> (mean 1).
  2. Initialize: fit rank-1 log1p model to obtain seed factor; fill remaining columns with small random values.
  3. Alternate until convergence:
     - With *F* fixed: for each row *i*, solve non-negative Poisson regression *y*<sub>*i*·</sub> ~ *F* (or diag(*s*) *F* if scaling) via cyclic coordinate ascent.
     - With *L* fixed: for each column *j*, solve non-negative Poisson regression *y*<sub>·*j*</sub> ~ *L*.
     - If approximating: pre-compute *L*<sup>⊤</sup>*L*, *F*<sup>⊤</sup>η<sub>1</sub>, etc., before each update block.
  4. Post-process: Rescale columns of *L* so max per column = 1; scale *F* accordingly.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Exact vs. approximate likelihood: Exact for small dense problems; approximate (Chebyshev) for large sparse data (ω ≪ *nm*).
  - Chebyshev vs. Taylor approximation: Chebyshev maintains accuracy across *c*; Taylor degrades for small *c* (Fig 3).
  - *c* selection: Small *c* (multiplicative) yields modular, dense factors; large *c* (additive) yields sparse, correlated, clustered factors. Tradeoff between interpretability style and sparsity.
  - Rank *K*: Higher *K* increases expressivity but risks overfitting; selection not addressed in paper.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Runtime blowup: If data not sparse or *K* large, O((*n*+*m*)*K*<sup>2</sup>) dominates → slow. Ensure approximation path active for sparse matrices.
  - Convergence to poor local optimum: Non-convex problem; use rank-1 initialization and multiple random restarts.
  - Uninterpretable factors: No clear separation/biological markers. Could be due to wrong *c* or *K*. Check factor correlation/sparsity trends.
  - Numerical instability: Very small *c* can cause overflow in exponentials; Chebyshev approximation mitigates but may fail if η<sub>max</sub> large.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Synthetic recovery:** Simulate log1p-NMF data with known *c* ∈ {0.1, 1, ∞}, *K*=3, moderate sparsity. Fit varying *c*, assess factor alignment (cosine similarity) and whether CV likelihood recovers true *c*.
  2. **Scaling test:** Generate large sparse matrix (*n*=*m*=10,000, *K*=50, sparsity 98%). Time exact vs. approximate objective; verify approximate yields similar likelihood.
  3. **Application sanity check:** On MCF-7, fit *c*=1 and *c*=∞ with *K*=3. Confirm *c*=1 gives a baseline factor with high loadings across samples; treatment factors show known marker genes (e.g., *CYP26B1*). *c*=∞ should yield more correlated factors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Q1: Sparse extensions** How can sparsity penalties (L1, non-convex) be integrated into log1p NMF to yield sparse solutions tractably?
  - **Basis:** Explicitly mentioned as future work; current small *c* yields dense factors.
  - **Why unresolved:** Adding constraints complicates optimization and may interact with the link in unpredictable ways.
  - **Evidence needed:** Algorithmic development + empirical evaluation showing improved interpretability without sacrificing fit.
- **Q2: Identifiability theory** What are necessary/sufficient conditions for unique recovery of *L*, *F* across *c*?
  - **Basis:** Explicitly called out; only preliminary geometric argument for *c*→0.
  - **Why unresolved:** Full identifiability theory for the whole *c* range is lacking.
  - **Evidence needed:** Theoretical derivation (e.g., convex hull conditions) + simulation validation.
- **Q3: Principled *c* selection** How to choose *c* in a data-driven way, balancing interpretability and goodness-of-fit?
  - **Basis:** Inferred; paper compares few values but provides no general method.
  - **Why unresolved:** Optimal *c* likely depends on data and scientific goals; no criterion proposed.
  - **Evidence needed:** Methods like CV-likelihood, stability selection, or downstream task metrics tested across datasets.
- **Q4: Feature ranking** How to reliably identify relevant features per factor, especially for dense "double-duty" factors?
  - **Basis:** Explicitly mentioned for MCF-7 and BBC; suggests borrowing from sparsity literature.
  - **Why unresolved:** Simple ranking by loadings conflates general high-expression features with factor-specific signal.
  - **Evidence needed:** Hypothesis testing, Bayesian variable selection, or sparsity extensions validated by semantic/biological coherence.
- **Q5: Multinomial extension** Can we fit a multinomial model with shifted-log link directly and efficiently?
  - **Basis:** Explicitly left for future work; Poisson model does not enforce row-wise probability sum-to-1.
  - **Why unresolved:** Efficient exact fitting algorithm for multinomial log1p NMF is unknown.
  - **Evidence needed:** Derivation of likelihood optimization + demonstration of comparable results with proper probability semantics.

## Limitations
- Empirical trends linking *c* to sparsity/correlation are supported by three datasets and preliminary theory but lack general theoretical guarantees; may be dataset-dependent.
- The method yields dense solutions for small *c*, potentially hurting interpretability; sparsity-promoting extensions are an open question.
- Exact hyperparameters for the Poisson regression solver (tolerances, line search) and precise preprocessing pipelines (gene filters, stop-word lists) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Mathematical derivation of link function limiting behaviors (Theorem 1/2) and computational complexity reduction via Chebyshev approximation for sparse data—both formally proven and validated in simulations (Fig 3).
- **Medium confidence:** Empirical claim that tuning *c* systematically modulates sparsity and factor correlation across diverse datasets; supported by three applications and Appendix F but not generalizable without further evidence.
- **Low confidence:** Broader applicability of observed interpretability patterns (e.g., *c*=1 always yielding modular representations) without extensive testing across more domains; the paper provides case studies but no prescriptive framework.
