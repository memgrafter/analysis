# Woosh: A Sound Effects Foundation Model

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2604.01929  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01929  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Woosh achieves state-of-the-art audio reconstruction (MelDist 0.032 on AudioCaps) and text-to-audio generation (Frechet Distance 109.1, CLAP score 0.3719) on public benchmarks, while models trained on commercial sound libraries drastically outperform on professional sound effects.

## Executive Summary
The Woosh paper presents an open-source foundation model for professional sound effects generation, addressing the scarcity of high-quality generative models in this domain. It introduces a modular system comprising a VOCOS-based autoencoder (Woosh-AE) for high-fidelity audio compression, a CLAP-style text-audio alignment model (Woosh-CLAP), and latent diffusion models for text-to-audio (Woosh-Flow) and video-to-audio (Woosh-VFlow), with distilled variants for efficient inference. Woosh-AE significantly outperforms prior codecs on reconstruction metrics, and Woosh-Flow-Public sets a new state-of-the-art on the AudioCaps benchmark. Crucially, models trained on commercial sound libraries (Woosh-Flow-Private) achieve dramatic improvements on a proprietary professional sound effects set, demonstrating the importance of domain-specific data. The authors release code and model weights to foster open research.

## Method Summary
Woosh employs a three-stage modular pipeline. First, Woosh-AE is a VOCOS-based autoencoder trained with a GAN objective, predicting STFT magnitude, real, and imaginary parts to avoid phase wrapping, and using multi-scale spectral and adversarial losses to ensure high-fidelity reconstruction. It compresses 48 kHz audio to 100 Hz latents of dimension 128. Second, Woosh-CLAP fine-tunes a RoBERTa text encoder and a PaSST audio encoder with a contrastive loss to align text and audio embeddings. Third, Woosh-Flow and Woosh-VFlow are latent diffusion transformers trained with a flow matching objective, using the autoencoder latents and conditioned on text (and video for VFlow). For efficient inference, MeanFlow distillation combined with adversarial loss produces Woosh-DFlow and Woosh-DVFlow, achieving 4 neural function evaluations (NFEs) with minimal quality loss. Training uses large-scale public datasets (AudioCaps, WavCaps, etc.) and proprietary commercial sound libraries for the private models.

## Key Results
- Woosh-AE achieves MelDist of 0.032 on AudioCaps reconstruction, far surpassing StableAudio-Open (0.217) and Encodec.
- Woosh-Flow-Public attains lower Frechet Distance (109.1) and higher CLAP score (0.3719) than TangoFlux on AudioCaps.
- Models trained on commercial data (Woosh-Flow-Private) drastically outperform public-data models on the professional InternalSFX set (CLAP 0.655 vs 0.188), while public models fail on that domain.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: High-Fidelity Autoencoder via Modified VOCOS and Adversarial Training
- **Claim:** The modified VOCOS autoencoder produces high-fidelity latent representations by predicting STFT coefficients (magnitude, real, imaginary) and using a GAN with multiple discriminators, enabling accurate audio reconstruction.
- **Mechanism:** Avoids phase wrapping by not directly predicting phase; uses multi-scale spectral losses, multi-period and multi-band STFT discriminators, and feature matching to preserve spectral detail and perceptual quality.
- **Core assumption:** Phase continuity can be managed implicitly via real/imaginary prediction, and adversarial training captures perceptual fidelity across scales.
- **Evidence:** Outperforms StableAudio-Open and Encodec on reconstruction metrics (MelDist 0.032 vs 0.217 on AudioCaps).
- **Break condition:** Degradation on complex phase patterns (dense polyphonic music) or discriminator collapse, leading to spectral smearing or metallic artifacts.

### Mechanism 2: Domain-Specific Training on Commercial Sound Libraries
- **Claim:** Training on commercial sound effect libraries aligns the model with professional acoustic characteristics and caption semantics, drastically improving performance on professional sound design tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Commercial libraries provide high-quality, curated sounds with consistent technical annotations, teaching the model spectral-temporal properties and labeling conventions distinct from public datasets.
- **Core assumption:** A substantial domain shift exists between public audio datasets and professional sound libraries.
- **Evidence:** Woosh-Flow-Private attains best FD (246.9) on InternalSFX; commercial-data models achieve CLAP 0.655 vs 0.188 for public-data models on InternalSFX.
- **Break condition:** Model may misalign or degrade on natural language prompts or general environmental audio.

### Mechanism 3: Efficient Generation via Flow Matching and Distillation
- **Claim:** Flow matching combined with MeanFlow distillation and adversarial loss enables high-quality generation with only 4 NFEs, making real-time deployment feasible.
- **Mechanism:** Flow matching provides stable training; MeanFlow distillation teaches the student to approximate the average velocity over a time interval, enabling larger denoising steps; adversarial loss on partially denoised latents refines outputs.
- **Core assumption:** The teacher velocity field is smooth enough for interval averaging, and adversarial training closes the gap from aggressive step reduction.
- **Evidence:** Distilled models maintain quality with only 4 NFEs; Woosh-DFlow (4 NFEs) is close to Woosh-Flow (~70 NFEs) on AudioCaps.
- **Break condition:** Under-trained teacher or complex audio may cause quality drop; adversarial training can be unstable.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Text → Woosh-CLAP (RoBERTa) → conditioning tokens → Woosh-Flow/DFlow (transformer diffusion) → audio latents → Woosh-AE decoder → audio.  
  Video → SynchFormer → video tokens → Woosh-VFlow/DVFlow (with optional text) → audio latents → Woosh-AE decoder → audio.  
  Woosh-CLAP also includes PaSST during training for audio-text contrastive learning.
- **Critical path:** (1) Tokenize text and encode with RoBERTa (second-last layer). (2) Sample noise latent and iterate denoising using flow matching (or distilled Euler steps) with classifier-free guidance. (3) Decode final latent with Woosh-AE.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Compression ratio: Woosh-AE uses 128-dim latents at 100 Hz (0.26 bits/sample) vs. SAO’s 64-dim at 21.5 Hz. Higher bitrate improves quality but increases diffusion cost.  
  - Model size vs. efficiency: Woosh-Flow (337M) is smaller than TangoFlux (515M) but achieves better FD on AudioCaps. Distillation trades negligible quality for 17.5× fewer NFEs.  
  - Data specialisation: Private models excel on professional data but fail on public datasets; public models are more general but weaker on professional content.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Woosh-AE: High MelDist/STFTDist on validation; audible artifacts (buzzing, muffledness).  
  - Woosh-CLAP: Low recall@K on audio-text retrieval; poor alignment in generated samples.  
  - Woosh-Flow: High FD and low CLAP score; noisy or semantically incoherent outputs.  
  - Distilled models: Significant quality gap (>10% FD increase) vs. teacher; adversarial distillation instability (loss spikes).  
  - Woosh-VFlow: Low ImageBind score; perceptible audio-visual desynchronization.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Autoencoder sanity check: Reconstruct 100 validation AudioCaps clips. Compute MelDist, STFTDist. Listen for artifacts.  
  2. CLAP retrieval test: Encode 500 audio-caption pairs. Compute text→audio and audio→text recall@10. Target >0.5 on AudioCaps for public model.  
  3. Generation quality check: Generate 50 samples from Woosh-Flow-Public with diverse prompts. Compute FD and CLAP score against validation set. Conduct human evaluation for semantic alignment and fidelity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can we develop evaluation metrics that reliably assess audio generation quality for professional sound effects, given the bias in publicly-trained metrics like CLAP?  
  *Basis:* Section 4.5 hypothesizes that KL and CLAP are biased for commercial data because they use models trained on public audio, leading to misaligned comparisons.  
  *Why unresolved:* Current metrics may not capture acoustic and semantic nuances of studio-quality sound effects.
- **Question 2:** What metrics should be adopted for audio-visual synchronization that correlate with human perception, given that existing automated metrics like DeSync can be unreliable?  
  *Basis:* Section 5.5 reports DeSync frequently assigns lower scores to ground truth videos than generated samples, indicating lack of perceptual validity.  
  *Why unresolved:* Unreliable metrics prevent accurate assessment of video-to-audio models' temporal alignment.
- **Question 3:** Why do CLAP models trained on public audio data fail to generalize to professional sound effects, and how can this domain gap be bridged?  
  *Basis:* Section 3.4 shows Woosh-CLAP-Public performs poorly on InternalSFX (T2A-R@10 0.198) while Woosh-CLAP-Private excels (0.655).  
  *Why unresolved:* The domain shift limits applicability; specific factors (audio fidelity, caption specificity) are not isolated.
- **Question 4:** Can we improve the computational efficiency of flow matching for audio diffusion without relying on distillation, which may degrade quality?  
  *Basis:* Section 4.4 states training from scratch with MeanFlow was slow due to expensive JVP operations, necessitating distillation that introduces potential quality loss.  
  *Why unresolved:* Distillation adds complexity and may sacrifice fidelity; more efficient training formulations could maintain quality.

## Limitations
- Private data specifics (size, curation, caption style) are undisclosed, limiting trust in professional-domain claims.
- Critical hyperparameters missing (exact CFG schedule, distillation noise reweighting, video tokenization details) block faithful reproduction.
- Evaluation relies heavily on automatic metrics (FD, CLAP) that may not correlate with human perception of sound effect quality.

## Confidence
- **Woosh-AE:** High – outperforms baselines on AudioCaps reconstruction metrics, but only evaluated on that set.
- **Woosh-Flow (public):** Medium – SOTA FD/CLAP on AudioCaps vs. TangoFlux, but no human validation.
- **Woosh-Flow (private):** Low – Huge gains on InternalSFX, but dataset is opaque; likely severe domain overfit.
- **Distillation (DFlow/DVFlow):** Medium – Maintains quality with 4 NFEs in tables, but adversarial distillation stability not explored.
- **Woosh-VFlow:** Low – Video-to-audio evaluation uses unimodal ImageBind and unreliable DeSync; no perceptual metrics.
