# The Compression Paradox in LLM Inference: Provider-Dependent Energy Effects of Prompt Compression

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23528
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23528
- **Reference count:** 5
- **Primary result:** Input-token reduction via compression alone is not a reliable energy optimization strategy due to severe quality loss and unpredictable provider-dependent energy effects, including "output token explosion."

## Executive Summary
This study examines whether compressing input prompts can reduce the energy consumption of large language model inference, a key concern for sustainable AI deployment. Through a large-scale experiment with over 28,000 API calls across three major providers (OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepSeek) and five coding/math benchmarks, the authors find that compression drastically harms task performance—with pass rates collapsing from 26% to 1.5% at moderate compression—and produces inconsistent, often detrimental, energy outcomes. Critically, DeepSeek-Chat exhibits a severe "output token explosion" where compressed prompts trigger disproportionately long outputs, increasing energy by over 2000%. Meanwhile, GPT-4o-mini shows mixed energy effects. The conclusion is that relying on input compression for energy savings is risky; instead, model selection and controlling output length offer more predictable trade-offs.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a controlled experiment using commercial APIs from three providers: OpenAI GPT-4o-mini, Anthropic Claude-3.5-Sonnet, and DeepSeek-Chat. For each provider, they invoked the model on five benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP, GSM8K, MATH, MMLU) with four compression ratios applied via deterministic truncation (retaining the start of the prompt at r=1.0, 0.7, 0.5, 0.3). A total of 28,421 successful calls were recorded. Energy consumption was not measured directly on provider servers; instead, a token-based proxy was used, calibrated against local direct measurements on a smaller set of models. Quality was tracked via standard benchmark pass rates. This design isolates the effect of input length on output behavior and estimated energy across provider/model boundaries.

## Key Results
- **Severe quality degradation:** Compression ratios at or above 0.7 caused near-total collapse in benchmark pass rates (from 26.0% at baseline to 1.5% at r=0.7), indicating extreme sensitivity of complex reasoning to context loss.
- **Provider-dependent energy effects:** DeepSeek-Chat demonstrated "output token explosion," where output length surged from 21 to 798 tokens at r=0.3, leading to an estimated energy increase of up to 2140%. GPT-4o-mini showed mixed effects, including a 26% reduction at r=0.5 but increases at other ratios.
- **Unreliable energy savings:** Input-token reduction did not yield consistent energy benefits; effects varied dramatically by provider and compression ratio, invalidating compression as a straightforward optimization strategy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The observed effects stem from provider-specific decoding heuristics and safety tuning that interact unpredictably with truncated prompts. For DeepSeek-Chat, compressed inputs appear to trigger a decoding behavior that generates excessively verbose outputs—possibly due to misinterpreted context or safety fallbacks—overwhelming any input-token savings. For GPT-4o-mini, the energy response is non-monotonic, suggesting internal conditioning mechanisms that sometimes benefit from shorter contexts but often do not. The quality collapse is a direct consequence of removing essential problem context from reasoning-intensive benchmarks.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Token-based energy proxy**  
   *Why needed:* Direct energy telemetry from commercial APIs is inaccessible; a scalable estimator is required.  
   *Quick check:* Validate proxy by correlating token counts with local GPU power draw on open-weight models.
   
2. **Prompt compression techniques**  
   *Why needed:* To reduce computational cost and latency by shortening inputs.  
   *Quick check:* Compare extractive (truncation) vs. abstractive (summary) methods on quality retention at fixed ratios.

3. **API provider variability**  
   *Why needed:* Different model architectures, decoding strategies, and safety post-processing lead to divergent behaviors.  
   *Quick check:* Run identical prompts across providers and measure output length distribution and failure modes.

4. **Output length as energy driver**  
   *Why needed:* Inference energy scales primarily with output token generation.  
   *Quick check:* Measure per-token energy cost on local hardware for target models across tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
User Query → Prompt Compressor → API Provider (Model) → Output Tokens → Token-based Energy Proxy → Metrics

Critical path: The compressed prompt is sent to the provider's model, which generates an output whose length is determined by internal decoding heuristics; this output length then feeds into the energy proxy to estimate consumption.

Design tradeoffs:  
- Compression ratio: Higher ratio saves more input tokens but risks severe quality loss and unpredictable output behaviors.  
- Provider selection: Different models exhibit fundamentally different output-length responses to compression.  
- Output-length control: Post-hoc truncation could mitigate energy spikes but may not be feasible via API.

Failure signatures:  
- Sudden output token explosion (e.g., DeepSeek-Chat at r=0.3).  
- Abrupt quality collapse at moderate compression ratios across all providers.  
- Non-monotonic energy response (e.g., GPT-4o-mini improving at r=0.5 but worsening at r=0.7).

First experiments:
1. Measure output length distribution for a simple QA task at each compression ratio to establish baseline provider behavior.
2. Compare quality (pass rate) on a held-out reasoning benchmark with and without a fixed output token limit to isolate the effect of output explosion.
3. Calibrate the token-based energy proxy by running local inference on a comparable open-weight model and measuring actual power draw.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
None explicitly listed; the paper focuses on empirical findings and limitations.

## Limitations
- **Energy proxy uncertainty:** Estimates rely on a token-based proxy not validated against provider-side actual energy telemetry.
- **Scope constraints:** Results are limited to three specific models and five technical benchmarks; generalizability to other domains or model families is unknown.
- **Compression method:** Only deterministic truncation was tested; effects with semantic or learned compression techniques may differ.

## Confidence
- **Severe quality degradation with compression:** High (effect size large, consistent across benchmarks).
- **Provider-dependent energy effects (including explosion):** Medium (patterns clear but mediated by unverified proxy and single-model-per-provider sampling).
- **Input-token reduction unreliable for energy savings:** Medium (supported for tested setup but hinges on proxy accuracy and specific models/ratios).

## Next Checks
1. **Direct provider-side measurement:** Partner with an API provider to obtain actual energy consumption data (e.g., GPU utilization, power draw) for identical inference calls to validate the token-based proxy.
2. **Expand model and task diversity:** Replicate with multiple additional models per provider family (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-3-Opus) and across diverse benchmark categories (e.g., HellaSwag, summarization) to test robustness of "output token explosion."
3. **Compare compression algorithms:** Replace deterministic truncation with state-of-the-art methods (e.g., GIST, LLMLingua) to assess whether different compression types mitigate quality drop and avoid output length explosions.
