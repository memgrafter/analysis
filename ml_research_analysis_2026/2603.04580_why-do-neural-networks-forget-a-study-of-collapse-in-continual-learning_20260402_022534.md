# Why Do Neural Networks Forget: A Study of Collapse in Continual Learning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04580  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04580  
- **Reference count:** 26  
- **Primary result:** Catastrophic forgetting is linked to representational and structural collapse measured by effective rank (eRank); Experience Replay most effectively preserves eRank and accuracy while Learning without Forgetting fails to prevent weight-space collapse.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies representational and structural collapse—quantified via the effective rank (eRank) of activations and weights—as the core mechanism behind catastrophic forgetting in neural networks. Evaluating multiple architectures (MLP, ConvGRU, ResNet-18, Bi-ConvGRU) on sequential image classification tasks under various continual learning strategies reveals that lower eRank strongly correlates with higher forgetting. Experience Replay (ER) succeeds by maintaining feature diversity through replay, preserving both activation and weight eRank. In contrast, Learning without Forgetting (LwF) stabilizes output behavior but does not prevent ongoing weight-space collapse. Recurrent architectures exhibit early representational compression, delaying initial forgetting but limiting long-term capacity. The findings position loss of plasticity, tracked by eRank degradation across layers, as the fundamental driver of forgetting.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates four architectures (MLP, ConvGRU, ResNet-18, Bi-ConvGRU) on Split MNIST and Split CIFAR-100 under three training regimes: standard SGD (baseline), Learning without Forgetting (LwF), and Experience Replay (ER) with a 2000-sample buffer. Task-Incremental (Task-IL) and Class-Incremental (Class-IL) settings are used to isolate forgetting in shared layers. The key diagnostic metric is effective rank (eRank), computed from activation covariance matrices (across batch and spatial dimensions for Conv layers) and weight matrices, grouped by network depth (early, middle, classification head). Accuracy and forgetting are tracked per task, and correlations between eRank trajectories and performance are analyzed across experiments.

## Key Results
- Effective rank (eRank) of activations and weights declines monotonically as forgetting increases, establishing a strong correlation between collapse and performance loss.
- Experience Replay (ER) preserves both activation and weight eRank most effectively, minimizing forgetting across architectures and datasets.
- Learning without Forgetting (LwF) stabilizes output accuracy but fails to prevent continuous weight eRank degradation, leading to eventual forgetting.
- Recurrent architectures (ConvGRU, Bi-ConvGRU) compress representations early, delaying immediate forgetting but capping long-term representational capacity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Representational Collapse Drives Forgetting
Catastrophic forgetting is caused by the compression of internal activation subspaces into a low-dimensional manifold during sequential training. Gradient updates for new tasks overwrite directions in activation space that encode old task features, reducing the effective rank (eRank) of activations. As eRank declines, the number of linearly independent feature directions shrinks, causing old class features to overlap and become inseparable. The network’s capacity to learn new tasks thus depends on preserving a high-dimensional, diverse feature space.

**Break condition:** Forgetting halts or reverses if activation eRank stabilizes or increases across tasks, indicating preserved feature diversity.

### Mechanism 2: Architectural Biases Shape Collapse Trajectory
Architectural design determines the rate and pattern of collapse. Feedforward networks (MLP, ResNet-18) maintain high initial capacity but suffer rapid, permanent collapse. Skip connections in ResNet-18 delay early gradient interference but do not prevent long-term degradation. Recurrent architectures (ConvGRU) use gated state updates to compress representations early; this reduces gradient interference and delays forgetting but also constrains the feature space to low rank early, limiting long-term learning potential.

**Break condition:** An architecture that maintains high weight and activation eRank over many tasks without early compression would decouple stability from capacity loss.

### Mechanism 3: Replay vs. Distillation in Mitigating Collapse
A successful continual learning strategy must address both representational (activation) and structural (weight) collapse. Experience Replay (ER) replays past data, forcing mixed-gradient updates that preserve old feature directions, thus maintaining both eRank types. Learning without Forgetting (LwF) uses knowledge distillation on outputs to stabilize decision boundaries but imposes no direct constraint on weight matrices or activation distributions; it stabilizes output behavior while weight eRank continues to decay, leading to late-term forgetting.

**Break condition:** The mechanism is falsified if a non-replay strategy (e.g., advanced regularization) preserves both eRank types equivalently to ER, or if ER fails under large task sequences or very small buffers.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Effective Rank (eRank)**  
  *Why needed:* It is the core metric linking geometric collapse (loss of dimensionality in weight/activation matrices) to behavioral forgetting, making internal degradation observable.  
  *Quick check:* If a layer’s activation eRank drops from 50 to 5 over 5 tasks, what does this imply about the network’s ability to learn a 6th, dissimilar task?

- **Concept: Task-Incremental (Task-IL) vs. Class-Incremental (Class-IL) Settings**  
  *Why needed:* They define where forgetting manifests. Task-IL isolates forgetting to shared layers (separate output heads), while Class-IL forces all classes into one output space, making representational collapse in shared layers more catastrophic.  
  *Quick check:* In Class-IL, why is representational collapse in shared convolutional layers more catastrophic than classifier weight collapse alone?

- **Concept: Architectural Biases (Skip Connections, Recurrent Gates)**  
  *Why needed:* These control gradient propagation and feature reuse, directly influencing eRank decay rates and explaining behavioral differences between architectures under the same CL strategy.  
  *Quick check:* How does a residual skip connection in ResNet-18 theoretically delay early representational collapse compared to a plain MLP?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input -> Early Feature Extractors (initial conv/FC layers) -> Middle/Late Processing Layers (residual blocks, recurrent stages) -> Classification Head (final FC layer).  
- **Critical path:** Monitor peak-normalized activation eRank in the penultimate layer and grouped weight eRank across all tasks. The diagnostic is the *divergence* between eRank curves and accuracy/forgetting curves; a sharp eRank drop precedes or accompanies a forgetting spike.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Replay Buffer Size (ER): Larger buffers better preserve eRank but increase memory/compute; diminishing returns beyond 2000 samples.  
  - Distillation Temperature (LwF): Higher T softens probabilities but still does not address weight collapse.  
  - Architectural Capacity: Higher initial eRank (e.g., ResNet vs. MLP) delays collapse but does not prevent it; recurrent gating trades capacity for stability.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. Activation eRank in shared layers collapses toward 1–5 (from initial ~40–60) within first few tasks under SGD.  
  2. Weight eRank in early/middle groups declines monotonically under all strategies except ER; LwF patterns resemble SGD.  
  3. Classifier layer eRank drops sharply upon new task introduction; only ER shows recovery.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline SGD:** Train each architecture on Split MNIST/CIFAR-100. Expect rapid accuracy drop and steep eRank decline in all groups, confirming inherent collapse.  
  2. **LwF Application:** Apply LwF. Measure: (a) final-task accuracy improvement vs. SGD? (b) activation eRank stabilization? (c) weight eRank still decline? Expect: Yes to (a) and (b), Yes to (c)—validating output stabilization without structural preservation.  
  3. **ER Application:** Apply ER (buffer=2000). Measure same as LwF. Expect: Yes to all—both eRank types remain high or increase, forgetting minimized—establishing ER’s dual-preservation mechanism.

## Limitations
- Causal link between eRank collapse and forgetting is inferred from correlation; no direct manipulation experiments (e.g., eRank regularization) are conducted.
- Evaluation is limited to small-scale image datasets (Split MNIST, CIFAR-100) and a narrow set of architectures; generalizability to large-scale or non-image tasks is unknown.
- External validation is weak: cited papers show low impact, and the work does not engage directly with spectral collapse literature despite conceptual overlap.

## Confidence
- **eRank-forgetting correlation:** High (strong within-paper evidence across architectures/strategies).  
- **Architectural trajectory differences:** High (consistent empirical patterns).  
- **ER vs. LwF mechanism (dual vs. single preservation):** High (clear eRank divergence in results).  
- **Causal assertion ("driven by"):** Medium (correlational; no ablation of eRank).  
- **Generalizability beyond tested settings:** Low (limited task/architectural scope).

## Next Checks
1. **Causal intervention:** Introduce an explicit eRank maximization regularization during training and measure if forgetting reduces without replay, isolating causality.
2. **Scalability test:** Replicate on a large-scale, diverse benchmark (e.g., ImageNet-1000 or temporal sequence tasks) to verify eRank-forgetting patterns under higher dimensionality and semantic complexity.
3. **Architectural diversity:** Test modern transformer-based continual learning models (e.g., ViT, MoE) to determine if eRank collapse mechanism is universal or architecture-specific.
