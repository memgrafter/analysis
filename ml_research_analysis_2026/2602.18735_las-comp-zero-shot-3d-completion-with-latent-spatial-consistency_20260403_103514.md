# LaS-Comp: Zero-shot 3D Completion with Latent-Spatial Consistency

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.18735
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18735
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: Outperforms prior zero-shot 3D completion (ComPC) by 27.2% in CD and 29.0% in EMD; on Omni-Comp, improvements reach 49.6% (CD) and 39.4% (EMD) with ~20s runtime per shape.

## Executive Summary
LaS-Comp introduces a training-free, category-agnostic framework for 3D shape completion from arbitrary partial inputs. It operates within the latent space of pre-trained latent-generative 3D foundation models (e.g., TRELLIS, Direct3D-S2). The method employs a two-stage iterative pipeline: an Explicit Replacement Stage (ERS) that preserves fidelity of observed geometry by spatially injecting partial inputs into the latent, and an Implicit Alignment Stage (IAS) that refines the boundary coherence through a single gradient step per timestep. This design achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot completion across diverse partial patterns (single scans, random crops, semantic parts) while maintaining efficiency.

## Method Summary
LaS-Comp performs iterative denoising in the latent space of a frozen VAE and denoising network from a foundation model. Starting from random noise `x_T`, each timestep `t` executes:
1. **Explicit Replacement Stage (ERS)**: The current latent `x_t` is denoised to a predicted clean latent `x̂₀|ₜ`, which is decoded to a shape `Ŝ₀|ₜ`. Observed regions from the partial input `S_p` (via occupancy mask `M`) replace corresponding regions in `Ŝ₀|ₜ`, yielding `Ŝ'₀|ₜ`. This hybrid shape is re-encoded to a clean latent `x*₀|ₜ`. Concurrently, a noisy branch uses a Partial-aware Noise Schedule (PNS) to construct `x*₁|ₜ` that preserves partial information. The updated latent `x*ₜ` is interpolated from these two branches.
2. **Implicit Alignment Stage (IAS)**: From `x*ₜ`, a noise-free latent `x̂₀|ₜ` is obtained and decoded. A binary cross-entropy alignment loss `L_align` between `Ŝ₀|ₜ ⊙ M` and `S_p ⊙ M` is computed. A single gradient descent step on `x̂₀|ₜ` (with fixed small learning rate) aligns the boundaries. The next latent `x_{t-dt}` is derived from this aligned latent and the denoising prediction.
After final denoising, the latent `x_0` is decoded to the completed shape `S_c`.

## Key Results
- Redwood & synthetic datasets: **27.2%** lower Chamfer Distance (CD) and **29.0%** lower Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) versus ComPC.
- Omni-Comp benchmark (single-scan, random crop, semantic part): Average **49.6%** CD and **39.4%** EMD improvement over ComPC.
- Runtime: **~20 seconds** per shape, over 3× faster than existing zero-shot approaches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by decoupling fidelity from coherence. ERS enforces that observed geometry is never altered by the generative prior, guaranteeing high fidelity to the input. IAS then operates on a clean, input-consistent latent to fine-tune only the boundary regions via a lightweight, one-step gradient optimization, ensuring a seamless transition without full backpropagation. The Partial-aware Noise Schedule strategically adds noise only to unobserved regions during denoising, preventing the generative process from overwriting known parts. This two-stage, training-free manipulation leverages the strong priors of foundation models while remaining input-consistent.

## Foundational Learning
- **Latent Generative 3D Models**: Pre-trained VAEs and diffusion models that represent shapes in a compressed, structured latent space. *Why needed*: Enables efficient manipulation and high-quality generation without task-specific training. *Quick check*: Encode/decode a known shape; reconstruction should be accurate.
- **Diffusion Denoising**: The iterative process of predicting and removing noise from a random latent. *Why needed*: Provides a stochastic path for generation from noise. *Quick check*: Denoise a pure noise latent with the model's `G` network; output should be a valid shape.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)**: Technique to steer unconditional generation toward a condition (e.g., text) by blending conditional and unconditional denoising predictions. *Why needed*: Allows conditioning on partial input (via the mask/geometry). *Quick check*: With CFG scale >1, output should more closely match the conditioning signal.
- **Occupancy Grid Voxelization**: Converting point clouds to a binary 3D grid. *Why needed*: Provides a structured, model-compatible representation for spatial masking and loss computation. *Quick check*: Voxelize a point cloud; visual inspection should show shape occupancy.
- **Binary Cross-Entropy Alignment Loss**: Supervision signal comparing voxel logits to ground-truth occupancy in observed regions. *Why needed*: Directly optimizes for geometric agreement at the boundary. *Quick check*: Compute loss between aligned and target masked voxels; should decrease after gradient step.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:
`Partial Input (S_p)` -> `Voxelization (M)` -> `Latent Denoising Loop (ERS -> IAS)` -> `Decoded Output (S_c)`

**Critical Path**: The iterative loop (t=T to t=0) where ERS and IAS are applied sequentially per timestep. The latent `x_t` is the central state updated each step.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- **Fidelity vs. Coherence**: ERS locks observed regions (high fidelity) but may create hard boundaries; IAS softens boundaries (high coherence) but risks slight drift. The one-step gradient is a computational compromise.
- **Training-Free vs. Adaptability**: No training required, but fixed hyperparameters (η, CFG) may not suit all input qualities.
- **Efficiency vs. Quality**: 100 steps balance runtime and quality; more steps may improve细节 but linearly increase cost.

**Failure Signatures**:
1. **Visible seams/holes** at observed-synthesized interface → IAS gradient step insufficient or η too low.
2. **Warped or missing observed geometry** → ERS replacement faulty; check voxel alignment or re-encoding.
3. **Nonsensical global shape** → PNS misapplied; noise added to observed regions or missing regions not noised enough.

**First 3 Experiments**:
1. Validate ERS fidelity: After ERS replacement, measure UCD/UHD between `Ŝ'₀|ₜ ⊙ M` and `S_p`; should be near-zero.
2. Test IAS impact: Compare CD/EMD with IAS disabled; expect significant drop in boundary quality.
3. Check denoising schedule: Run with 50, 100, 200 steps; performance should plateau near 100 steps, confirming efficiency claim.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Outlier-Removal for Noisy Inputs**: How to design modules that detect and remove scattered noise points or inconsistent clusters before completion? Current method oversmooths fine details under extreme noise.
- **Confidence-Aware Refinement**: How to adapt denoising strength regionally based on estimated reliability? Uniform IAS update may cause artifacts in high-uncertainty areas.
- **GT-Based Normalization Elimination**: How to remove dependence on ground-truth bounding boxes for normalization, which is impractical for in-the-wild partial scans? Robust methods using only partial extents are needed.

## Limitations
- **Implementation Ambiguities**: Exact voxel downsampling method (max vs. average), CFG integration details, and continuous-time denoising schedule (`dt`, path) are unspecified, affecting replicability.
- **Normalization Dependency**: Uses GT-based normalization to [-0.5, 0.5]³, which is not zero-shot in practice and may inflate results.
- **Generalization Uncertainty**: Claims on Omni-Comp rely on precise mask generation for patterns like semantic parts; details are lacking, and robustness to real-world noise is limited.
