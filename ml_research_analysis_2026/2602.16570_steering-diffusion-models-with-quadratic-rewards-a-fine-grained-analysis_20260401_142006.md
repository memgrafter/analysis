# Steering diffusion models with quadratic rewards: a fine-grained analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16570
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16570
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The paper characterizes computational tractability for sampling from diffusion models tilted by quadratic rewards: linear rewards always admit efficient samplers; positive‑definite low‑rank quadratics are efficiently samplable via a lifting algorithm; negative‑definite quadratics are intractable even for rank 1 under the assumption NP ⊈ BPP.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the problem of steering diffusion models toward samples that maximize a quadratic reward function \( r(x) = x^\top A x + b^\top x \). The authors provide a fine‑grained complexity analysis based on the rank and definiteness of \( A \). For linear rewards (\( \text{rank}(A)=0 \)), they show a simple closed‑form transformation of the score function yields an efficient sampler. For positive‑definite low‑rank quadratics (\( A \succ 0 \), constant rank), they design an algorithm using the Hubbard‑Stratonovich transform to lift the distribution to a linear tilt in a higher‑dimensional space, then repeatedly invoke the linear sampler. In contrast, for negative‑definite quadratics (\( A \preceq 0 \)), they prove that even rank‑1 instances are computationally intractable (assuming NP ⊈ BPP) by reducing from the PARTITION problem. The results reveal a sharp phase transition in tractability.

## Method Summary
The algorithmic approach builds on a base score oracle for a diffusion model. For linear rewards (\( v = b \)), the tilted score admits an explicit shift and scaling: \( \nabla\log p^\sigma(x; v) = v/\sqrt{1-\sigma^2} + \nabla\log p^\sigma(x + \sigma^2 v/\sqrt{1-\sigma^2}) \). This oracle is fed into an existing approximate sampler (UnadjustedSampler) to produce samples. For low‑rank positive‑definite quadratics, write \( A = \frac{1}{2} L^\top L \) with \( L \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d} \). The Hubbard‑Stratonovich transform expresses the tilted distribution as a mixture over an auxiliary variable \( z \in \mathbb{R}^r \): \( p^*(x) \propto \int Z(z) e^{-\|z\|^2/2} p(x; L^\top z) \, dz \), where \( Z(z) = \int p(x) e^{\langle Lx, z\rangle} dx \). The algorithm discretizes the space of \( z \) on a grid, estimates each \( Z(z) \) via a telescoping product of ratios (each requiring calls to the linear sampler), samples \( z \) from the resulting discrete distribution, and finally samples \( x \) from the linear tilt \( p(\cdot; L^\top z) \). The total Wasserstein‑2 error is bounded by \( \epsilon_{\text{final}} \) with runtime polynomial in \( d \), \( C_{\text{norm}} \), \( \|L\|_2 \), \( D^r \), \( r^r \), and \( \epsilon_{\text{final}}^{-r} \), where \( D = \sup_{x\in\text{supp}(p)} \|Lx\|_2 \).

## Key Results
- Linear‑reward tilts are always efficiently sampleable via a closed‑form score transformation.
- Positive‑definite low‑rank quadratic tilts admit an efficient algorithm with runtime polynomial in \( d \), \( C_{\text{norm}} \), \( \|L\|_2 \), \( D^r \), \( r^r \), and \( \epsilon_{\text{final}}^{-r} \).
- Negative‑definite quadratic tilts are computationally intractable even for rank 1 (assuming NP ⊈ BPP).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Linear rewards always efficient
The score of the tilted distribution \( p(x; v) \propto p(x) e^{\langle x, v\rangle} \) satisfies the exact transformation \( \nabla\log p^\sigma(x; v) = v/\sqrt{1-\sigma^2} + \nabla\log p^\sigma(x + \sigma^2 v/\sqrt{1-\sigma^2}v) \). This converts an exact base score oracle into a score oracle for the tilt. Combined with the polynomial‑time UnadjustedSampler (under bounded support), it yields an efficient sampler. The approach breaks if the score is approximate or the support is unbounded.

### Mechanism 2: Positive‑definite low‑rank quadratics
Write \( A = \frac{1}{2} L^\top L \) with \( L \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d} \). The Hubbard‑Stratonovich transform yields \( p^*(x) \propto \int Z(z) e^{-\|z\|^2/2} p(x; L^\top z) \, dz \). The algorithm discretizes the \( r \)-dimensional \( z \)‑space on a grid \( S \), estimates each \( Z(z) \) using a telescoping Monte Carlo procedure that repeatedly calls the linear‑reward sampler (LinTiltSampler), and then samples \( z \) from the discrete distribution before drawing \( x \) from \( p(\cdot; L^\top z) \). The low rank \( r \) keeps the grid size polynomial in the problem parameters. Runtime blows up if \( r \) is not constant or if \( D = \sup_{x} \|Lx\|_2 \) is large.

### Mechanism 3: Negative‑definite intractability
For a rank‑1 negative matrix \( A = -\beta w w^\top \) with \( \beta = d+5 \), take \( p \) uniform on \( \{\pm1\}^d \). The tilted distribution \( q_w(x) \propto p(x) e^{-\beta (w^\top x)^2} \) concentrates on the hyperplane \( \{x: w^\top x = 0\} \) as \( \beta \) grows. That hyperplane encodes solutions to the PARTITION problem. An efficient sampler for \( q_w \) would give a one‑sided error algorithm for PARTITION, implying NP ⊆ BPP. Hence the sampling task is intractable under the standard assumption. The reduction uses exponentially large \( \beta \) and a discrete base; it is unknown whether intractability extends to continuous bases or bounded‑norm \( A \).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: The system consists of BaseScoreOracle (provides \( \nabla\log p_\sigma \)), UnadjustedSampler (base approximate sampler), LinTiltSampler (applies linear score transformation and calls UnadjustedSampler), EstimateNormalization (estimates \( Z(v) \) using LinTiltSampler), and PSDTiltSampler (top‑level for PSD quadratics, which builds a grid over \( z \), calls EstimateNormalization to get densities, samples \( z \), then calls LinTiltSampler with tilt \( L^\top z \)).

**Critical path**:
1. Implement & test LinTiltSampler on a Gaussian base (analytic tilted distribution). Validate mean/variance.
2. Implement EstimateNormalization; compare estimated \( Z(v) \) to ground truth Monte Carlo for small \( d \).
3. Implement PSDTiltSampler; tune parameters (\( R \), \( \gamma \)) to meet \( \epsilon_{\text{final}} \). Verify that runtime scales polynomially with \( r \) but not exponentially.
4. Explore negative‑definite regime to observe limitations (e.g., Langevin struggles for large \( \beta \)).

**Design tradeoffs**:
- In PSDTiltSampler, grid step \( \gamma \) controls discretization error (\( \gamma \le \epsilon/D \)) but \( |S| \sim (R/\gamma)^r \); smaller \( \gamma \) → higher runtime.
- EstimateNormalization uses \( N = C_{\text{norm}} \|v\|_2 \) steps; larger \( N \) inflates total calls (\( M \approx N^2/\epsilon^2 \)). Trade‑off between step size and per‑step sample count.
- Total error \( \epsilon_{\text{final}} \) is split among discretization (\( \epsilon_3 \)), normalization estimation (\( \epsilon_1 \)), and LinTiltSampler (\( \epsilon_2 \)); overspending one component may force underspending others.
- Exact score access is assumed; real models have approximation error, which may degrade guarantees.

**Failure signatures**:
- LinTiltSampler: For Gaussian base, empirical mean ≠ \( v \) or covariance ≠ \( I \) indicates bug in transformation or UnadjustedSampler step size.
- EstimateNormalization: High variance in \( \hat{\kappa}^{(n)} \) across grid points; product drift from true normalization.
- PSDTiltSampler: Exponential blow‑up when \( r > 3 \); missing modes if \( D \) underestimated.
- Negative‑definite tilts: Attempting to apply PSDTiltSampler fails (weights become negative or integral diverges).

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Linear tilt sanity check**: \( p = \mathcal{N}(0, I_{10}) \), \( v \) random. Run LinTiltSampler (\( \epsilon=0.01 \)). Compute empirical mean; \( W_2 \) to \( \mathcal{N}(v, I) \) should ≤ 0.1.
2. **Low‑rank PSD tilt**: \( d=20 \), \( r=2 \), \( L \) orthonormal rows → \( A = \frac{1}{2} L^\top L \) has eigenvalue 0.5. \( p = \mathcal{N}(0, I) \). PSDTiltSampler with \( \epsilon_{\text{final}}=0.1 \). Compare empirical covariance to \( (I-2A)^{-1} \); \( W_2 \le 0.1 \). Check runtime scales ~ poly(r).
3. **Negative tilt and Langevin failure**: \( p \) uniform on \( \{\pm1\}^{50} \), \( A = -\beta w w^\top \) with \( \beta = d+5 \). Use Langevin (add reward gradient to score). Monitor \( w^\top x \) over iterations; observe extremely slow convergence due to concentration on a thin manifold.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Negative‑definite quadratics with bounded norm**: Does a polynomial‑time sampler exist for rank‑1 negative‑definite rewards when \( \|A\|_2 \) is polynomial in \( d \)? The current hardness uses exponentially large entries; the bounded‑norm case remains open.
2. **Robustness to score errors**: How to handle approximate score oracles from trained diffusion models? The analysis assumes exact scores, but practical oracles have errors that may accumulate.
3. **Beyond quadratic rewards**: What structural properties of reward functions and base distributions ensure efficient steering for more complex (e.g., non‑quadratic) rewards?
4. **Rank dependence**: Can the runtime dependence on rank \( r \) for PSD quadratics be improved from exponential (\( r^r \)) to polynomial while retaining polynomial dependence on other parameters?

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely on exact score oracles and bounded support; real diffusion models provide approximate scores and may have heavy tails.
- The PSD‑quadratic algorithm’s runtime includes factors \( D^r \) and \( r^r \), making it impractical for moderate \( r \) (only constant rank is tractable).
- The negative‑definite intractability result uses a discrete base distribution and exponentially large \( \beta \); it may not cover continuous bases or small \( \beta \), leaving a conceptual gap.

## Confidence
- Linear‑reward tilts always efficiently sampleable: **High** (explicit transformation reduces to well‑studied sampler).
- Positive‑definite low‑rank quadratic tilts admit efficient sampler: **Medium** (algorithm is polynomial in rank but with potentially large constants and loose bounds; runtime may be prohibitive for non‑constant rank).
- Negative‑definite quadratic tilts are intractable (rank 1) assuming NP ⊈ BPP: **Medium** (reduction is plausible but restricted to specific base and large \( \beta \)).
