# Debiasing Sequential Recommendation with Time-aware Inverse Propensity Scoring

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04986
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04986
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** TIPS reduces exposure and selection biases in sequential recommendation, yielding average improvements of ~6% in HR@10 and ~5% in NDCG@10 across four datasets, with up to 8.87% HR@10 gain on Music4All.

## Executive Summary
Sequential recommendation suffers from exposure and selection biases because models are trained only on observed interactions, ignoring which items were never shown to users. This paper introduces Time-aware Inverse Propensity Scoring (TIPS), a plug-in framework that estimates time-varying exposure propensities using counterfactual item-time pairs (similar items, popular items, same item at perturbed times) and integrates them via a cross-attention mechanism. TIPS reweights interactions in the training objective to correct for both biases. Applied to various sequential backbones (e.g., Attention, DiffuRec), TIPS consistently improves hit rate and NDCG, with larger gains on larger-scale datasets where user engagement is higher. Ablations confirm the necessity of both temporal modeling and IPS adjustment.

## Method Summary
TIPS employs a dual embedding design: one table \( H^{(C)} \) for interaction semantics used by the backbone recommender, and another \( H^{(E)} \) for exposure distribution used to estimate propensities. For each factual interaction \((u, v, t)\), it generates three counterfactual exposure samples: \((v_{\text{sim}}, t)\) with a similar item from \(H^{(E)}\), \((v_{\text{pop}}, t)\) with a popular item from a recent time window, and \((v, t^*)\) with a perturbed time. These, plus the factual, form positive examples for training an exposure propensity estimator \(f_\phi\). This estimator uses cross-attention: the candidate exposure embedding (item from \(H^{(E)}\) plus time embedding) queries the user’s historical interaction sequence (items from \(H^{(C)}\) plus time embeddings) to output a scalar propensity \(s_{v,t}\). The recommendation model \(g_\theta\) (any sequential backbone) scores item compatibility. The final loss combines a weighted BPR loss—where each positive interaction is weighted by \(w_{v,t} = \exp(-\mu \cdot \Delta t) / \max(s_{v,t}, \epsilon)\)—with a binary cross-entropy loss \(L_{EP}\) for exposure estimation. The time decay \(\exp(-\mu \cdot \Delta t)\) emphasizes recent interactions, while the inverse propensity corrects for exposure bias.

## Key Results
- TIPS improves HR@10 by ~6% and NDCG@10 by ~5% on average across four datasets when applied to multiple sequential backbones.
- Gains are larger on larger-scale datasets (e.g., up to 8.87% HR@10 improvement on Music4All).
- Ablation studies show that removing temporal modeling (¬time) or IPS adjustment (¬IPS) significantly degrades performance, confirming both components are necessary.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Counterfactual item-time pairs enable accurate estimation of time-varying exposure propensities without exposure logs.
- **Mechanism:** For each factual interaction \((u,v,t)\), three counterfactual exposures are generated: \((v_{\text{sim}}, t)\) where \(v_{\text{sim}}\) is a similar item (from \(H^{(E)}\)); \((v_{\text{pop}}, t)\) where \(v_{\text{pop}}\) is a popular item (from top-K in a time window); and \((v, t^*)\) where \(t^*\) is a perturbed time. These serve as positive exposure samples alongside the factual \((v,t)\), while random item-time pairs are negatives. A cross-attention model is trained via binary classification to predict exposure probability.
- **Core assumption:** These counterfactuals reflect plausible exposure scenarios given user preferences and temporal dynamics (e.g., similar items co-exposed, popular items widely shown, same item may be re-exposed shortly after).
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract]: "estimates time-varying exposure propensities using counterfactual item-time pairs (similar items, popular items, same item at perturbed times)"
  - [section 3.2]: Describes construction of similar, popular, and perturbed-time counterfactuals.
- **Break condition:** If counterfactual constructions do not match actual exposure distributions (e.g., due to dataset-specific recommendation policies), the learned propensity scores become inaccurate, undermining debiasing.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Cross-attention between exposure embeddings and historical interaction sequences effectively captures how exposure influences user preference modeling.
- **Mechanism:** For a candidate item-time pair, its exposure embedding \(\hat{e}\) (item from \(H^{(E)}\) plus time embedding) is used as query; the user's historical interaction sequence \(S\) (items from \(H^{(C)}\) plus time embeddings) provides keys and values. Cross-attention outputs a pooled scalar propensity score, indicating the likelihood of exposure given history.
- **Core assumption:** The user's past interactions inform the exposure probability of a candidate item at a given time, and attention can dynamically weight relevant history.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 3.3]: "We incorporate exposure information into the interaction sequence through the cross attention mechanism. Specifically, the exposure embedding ˆe𝑖 serves as the query q, while the embeddings of each interaction in the sequence S are employed as both key k and value v."
- **Break condition:** If exposure is driven mainly by system-level factors (e.g., inventory constraints) rather than user history, then conditioning on historical interactions fails to yield accurate propensities.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Time-aware inverse propensity scoring in the training objective corrects both exposure and selection biases by reweighting interactions according to estimated exposure likelihood and recency.
- **Mechanism:** The recommendation loss is a weighted BPR loss: for positive \((v_i,t_i)\) and negative \((v_j,t_j)\) pairs, weight \(w_{v_i,t_i} = \exp(-\mu\cdot(t_i - t_{i-1})) / \max(s_{v_i,t_i}, \epsilon)\). Here \(s_{v_i,t_i}\) is the time-aware propensity from the cross-attention estimator. The inverse propensity and time decay emphasize recent, under-exposed interactions.
- **Core assumption:** The time-aware propensity \(s_{v,t}\) accurately approximates \(P(E=1|u,v,t)\), and the reweighting yields an unbiased estimate under counterfactual uniform exposure.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract]: "TIPS consistently improves performance ... with average gains of ~6% in HR@10 and ~5% in NDCG@10"
  - [section 3.5.1]: Equation (21) and (22) define the weighted loss.
- **Break condition:** Misestimated propensities (due to poor cross-attention or counterfactuals) lead to high-variance IPS weights or new biases, harming performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Exposure and Selection Bias  
  - **Why needed:** These are the core problems TIPS addresses. Exposure bias treats unexposed items as irrelevant; selection bias treats exposed-but-unclicked items as negative. Understanding them clarifies why standard SR fails.  
  - **Quick check:** Describe a scenario where an item is exposed but not clicked, and how selection bias would misinterpret it.

- **Concept:** Inverse Propensity Scoring (IPS)  
  - **Why needed:** IPS reweights observed interactions by inverse exposure probability to correct missing-not-at-random data. TIPS extends IPS to handle temporal dynamics.  
  - **Quick check:** Why is static IPS insufficient for sequential recommendation? What temporal aspects does it miss?

- **Concept:** Counterfactual Estimation  
  - **Why needed:** Without true exposure logs, we must infer what could have been exposed. TIPS creates counterfactual item-time pairs to approximate the exposure distribution.  
  - **Quick check:** What are the three counterfactual sample types in TIPS and what real-world patterns do they mimic?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input user sequence -> Dual embedding lookup (\(H^{(C)}\) for interactions, \(H^{(E)}\) for exposures) -> Time embedding MLP -> Counterfactual sampler (similar, popular, perturbed time) -> Cross-attention propensity estimator -> Backbone recommender (e.g., Attention, DiffuRec) -> Loss modules (weighted BPR + exposure BCE) -> Optimized joint training.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Input: user's historical interaction sequence with timestamps.  
  2. For each positive interaction \((v_i,t_i)\), retrieve interaction embedding \(c_i\) from \(H^{(C)}\) and construct exposure embedding \(\hat{e}_i = e_v + \text{time}(t_i)\) from \(H^{(E)}\).  
  3. Compute propensity \(s_{v_i,t_i} = \text{cross-attention}(\hat{e}_i, S)\), where \(S = \{c_m + \text{time}(t_m)\}\) for history.  
  4. Compute recommendation score \(y\) via backbone (using \(c\) for candidate items).  
  5. Compute loss: \(L_{\text{BPR-TIPS}}\) uses weight \(w = \exp(-\mu\cdot\Delta t)/\max(s,\epsilon)\) to weight positive-negative pairs; \(L_{EP}\) uses factual and counterfactual samples.  
  6. Joint optimization of \(\theta\) and \(\phi\).

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Dual embeddings decouple interaction/exposure signals but double embedding parameters.  
  - Counterfactual types: similar (requires precomputed similarity), popular (time-windowed count), perturbed time (cheap). More types improve coverage but increase compute.  
  - Time decay \(\mu\): smaller retains long-term, larger focuses on recency; needs tuning per dataset.  
  - IPS weighting increases variance; \(\epsilon\) stabilization is a Band-Aid; consider clipping or self-normalization.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Propensity scores show low discrimination (positives and negatives overlap). Indicates poor cross-attention or unrealistic counterfactuals.  
  - Training instability (NaNs) due to extreme IPS weights (very small \(s\)). Check propensity distribution and \(\epsilon\).  
  - Performance drops on short sequences (<5 interactions) because cross-attention lacks context.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Main results:** Apply TIPS to Attention (traditional) and DiffuRec/CVAE (generative) on four datasets. Compare HR@10 and NDCG@10 against baselines.  
  2. **Ablation study:** Remove temporal component (¬time), remove IPS adjustment (¬IPS), remove both (¬EP&time) to quantify each contribution.  
  3. **Hyperparameter analysis:** Vary \(\mu\) (time decay) and \(\gamma\) (exposure loss weight) to determine sensitivity and optimal values (e.g., \(\mu=0.5\), \(\gamma=0.3\) on ML-1M).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Are the three counterfactual construction strategies (similar items, popular items, same item at perturbed time) sufficient and necessary for accurate time-aware exposure estimation, or do other counterfactual scenarios (e.g., user-specific temporal patterns) provide additional benefits?
- **Basis:** Section 3.2 describes the three counterfactual types based on assumptions without empirical validation of their relative contributions or exploration of alternatives.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not ablate each counterfactual type individually or compare against other plausible counterfactual generation strategies; thus it is unclear whether the chosen three are optimal.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study isolating the impact of each counterfactual type and a systematic exploration of other counterfactual designs (e.g., using user-temporal clustering) on multiple datasets.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why are generative sequential recommenders (e.g., DiffuRec, CVAE) less sensitive to the removal of temporal modeling in TIPS compared to attention-based models?
- **Basis:** Section 5.2 reports that HyperG¬time causes smaller performance drops for generative models (~1%) than for attention models (~2.68%), suggesting generative models may inherently capture temporal signals.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper speculates that generative models "capture signals akin to temporal information" but provides no analysis of the internal representations (e.g., does the diffusion process implicitly model time?).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A deeper analysis of the learned representations in generative vs. sequential models (e.g., probing temporal sensitivity, visualizing attention weights over time) to understand the source of temporal robustness.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Is the proposed cross-attention mechanism for estimating time-aware exposure propensities (Section 3.3) more effective than alternative architectures (e.g., MLPs, RNNs) that also incorporate temporal dynamics?
- **Basis:** The methodology introduces cross-attention as a design choice but does not compare it to other plausible models for propensity estimation; the ablation only removes the entire exposure module.
- **Why unresolved:** Without architectural comparisons, it is unclear whether cross-attention is essential or if simpler, more efficient models could achieve similar performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study varying the propensity estimation network (e.g., using MLP, LSTM, or different attention variants) while keeping counterfactual construction fixed, measuring impact on recommendation performance and computational cost.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What drives the greater performance gains of TIPS on larger-scale datasets (e.g., Music4All) compared to smaller ones (e.g., ML-1M)? Is it solely due to higher user engagement, or also due to richer item catalogs and more diverse exposure patterns?
- **Basis:** Section 5.1 attributes larger improvements on Music4All to higher user engagement (5.76 monthly interactions per user vs. 1.14 average) but does not disentangle factors like dataset size, item count, or interaction sparsity.
- **Why unresolved:** The correlation between dataset scale and improvement is observed but causally unexplained; multiple confounding variables (number of items, interaction density, temporal span) differ across datasets.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments on synthetic or real datasets with varied scales while holding other factors constant, or a multi-variate analysis correlating improvement with dataset characteristics (size, sparsity, engagement).

## Limitations
- Counterfactual construction relies on heuristics that may not match true exposure distributions under system-level constraints.
- Hyperparameters (\(\mu\), \(\gamma\), \(\epsilon\)) require per-dataset tuning, raising reproducibility concerns.
- Dual embeddings double model size and risk overfitting on sparse datasets.
- IPS weighting can cause high variance; \(\epsilon\) clipping is a band-aid fix.

## Confidence
- **Effectiveness:** Medium. Strong cross-dataset gains reported but lack external validation.
- **Propensity estimation via counterfactuals:** Low. Core assumption untested without true exposure data.
- **Cross-attention modeling:** Medium. Ablations support contribution, but architecture details are sparse.
- **Temporal IPS reweighting:** Medium. Theoretically sound but sensitive to hyperparameters and propensity accuracy.
