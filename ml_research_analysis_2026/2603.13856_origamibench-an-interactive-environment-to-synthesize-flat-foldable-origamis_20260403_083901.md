# OrigamiBench: An Interactive Environment to Synthesize Flat-Foldable Origamis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.13856
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13856
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Modern VLMs excel at associative visual matching (e.g., Qwen3-VL-32B 96.18% accuracy) but fail on causal reasoning about fold transitions (43.37% for same model, 25–31% for others) and coherent multi-step synthesis (IoU ≤0.18, SS ≤0.26), revealing a gap in visual-language integration and structured planning.

## Executive Summary
OrigamiBench introduces an interactive benchmark to evaluate vision-language models on flat-foldable origami synthesis, a task requiring sequential folding decisions under precise geometric constraints. The benchmark includes a one-step multiple-choice task (associative vs. causal) and a full-step interactive synthesis task, using a dataset of 366 origami designs across semantic categories and complexity levels. Results show that while current VLMs achieve near-human performance on associative shape matching, they struggle dramatically with causal reasoning about fold transitions and coherent multi-step planning. This indicates that scaling model size alone does not yield the integrated visual-symbolic representations needed for physical transformation tasks.

## Method Summary
The environment maintains an internal `CreasePattern` graph (vertices, edges, mountain/valley assignments) representing the current folded state. This state is rendered to PNG images (current folded state, current crease pattern) for model input. In the one-step tasks, the model receives a target image, current images, and selects the correct next fold from distractors (associative: any valid fold; causal: fold from same sequence). In full-step interactive synthesis, the model iteratively proposes crease additions (single or multi-crease actions) in JSON format. Each action is validated by a flat-folder solver enforcing Kawasaki’s and Maekawa’s theorems and layer legality. Valid actions update the `CreasePattern`; invalid ones leave the state unchanged. After a fixed budget or convergence, final geometric similarity (IoU of silhouettes) and semantic similarity (CLIP-based) against the target are computed.

## Key Results
- VLMs exhibit a >50% accuracy drop from Associative to Causal tasks; Qwen3-VL-32B drops from 96.18% to 43.37%, while others fall near random (25–31%).
- In full-step synthesis, models produce syntactically valid actions (query efficiency >0.68) but achieve very low geometric similarity (IoU ≤0.18) and semantic similarity (≤0.26), indicating failure to generate coherent multi-step strategies.
- Scaling model size does not close the causal reasoning gap, underscoring persistent weaknesses in integrating visual perception with symbolic causal reasoning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Current VLMs exhibit a representational gap between visual perception and symbolic causal reasoning, leading to failure on tasks requiring fine-grained geometric transformation understanding.** Models leverage powerful associative pattern matching on rendered images but fail to ground these perceptions in the symbolic, rule-based causal structure of fold operations (e.g., Kawasaki's theorem, layer ordering). The environment's closed-loop feedback provides outcome supervision but not an internal model of action→state transitions. Evidence includes the >50% accuracy drop from Associative to Causal tasks and the statement that "visual and language representations remain weakly integrated."

**VLMs lack a structured internal "world model" for simulating multi-step physical transformations, resulting in myopic, non-coherent planning.** The interactive synthesis task requires predicting a sequence where later steps depend on earlier ones. Models emit syntactically valid but geometrically meaningless actions (high query efficiency, low geometric/semantic similarity), indicating they do not maintain or reason over a consistent internal state during planning. They treat each step as an isolated perceptual guess rather than a link in a chain. Evidence includes low final similarity despite valid intermediate actions and the design where the model receives only rendered images and binary feedback, not the structured `CreasePattern` state.

**The task's difficulty stems from the need for causal abstraction—inferring latent geometric constraints (foldability laws) from observable states.** The Causal task's distractors are drawn from the same folding sequence, forcing discrimination based on the effect of a single fold rather than overall shape similarity. Models fail here because they lack learned heuristics for the irreversibility and layer legality constraints that define valid transitions (e.g., a front-view change must imply a specific back-view change). Success requires learning a constrained transition function sensitive to topological/geometric legality, not superficial appearance.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Origami Geometric Axioms (Kawasaki's & Maekawa's Theorems)**  
Why needed: These are the fundamental physical constraints the Flat-Folder solver checks. A model must implicitly or explicitly respect these to generate valid folds. Understanding them explains why certain crease patterns are invalid.  
Quick check: Given a vertex with four incident creases alternating mountain/valley, must the sum of alternating angles equal 180° (Kawasaki)? How does adding a new crease affect this balance at adjacent vertices?

**Concept: State Representation as Crease Pattern Graph (vertices, edges, assignments)**  
Why needed: The environment's ground-truth state is this graph. The model's observations are renderings of this state. Bridging the visual (image) and structural (graph) domains is core to the integration challenge.  
Quick check: If two folded shapes look identical (same silhouette), how might their underlying crease patterns differ in edge assignments (M/V) and thus in foldability for the next step?

**Concept: Action Semantics & Dependencies (Single vs. Multi-crease, Layer Ordering)**  
Why needed: The action schema is precise (add_crease/add_creases with coordinates and M/V). Success requires selecting actions that are locally valid (solver accepts) and globally contributory to the target. Dependencies exist (e.g., a squash fold may require pre-existing creases).  
Quick check: Can a single valley fold ever reverse the layer order established by a previous mountain fold? What visual cues in the current folded state might indicate an incorrect layer ordering that needs a "re-layering" crease?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** The system comprises a VLM backbone that processes multimodal prompts (target PNG, current PNG, current CP PNG, feedback) to output JSON actions; a prompt engine formatting inputs into a strict template; an action validator that parses JSON and calls the Flat-Folder solver to check geometric feasibility; a state executor that updates the internal `CreasePattern` graph upon valid actions; a rendering pipeline converting the graph to SVG then PNG; and a metrics module computing final IoU and CLIP similarity.

**Critical path:** 1. Initialize episode: load target `.fold`, render target PNG; set CP = blank square. 2. Loop: build prompt from few-shot examples, target/current/CP images, last feedback → VLM → action → validator. 3. If valid: executor updates CP → renderer updates current PNGs → feedback = "foldability: true". 4. If invalid: state unchanged → feedback = "foldability: false". 5. Terminate at max steps or convergence; compute final metrics.

**Design tradeoffs:** Visual fidelity (high-res 512x512 PNGs) vs. computational cost; action granularity (single-crease for reliability vs. multi-crease for efficiency) vs. solver failure risk; feedback sparsity (binary `foldability` vs. detailed error messages) affects learning but breaks black-box VLM assumption.

**Failure signatures:** High query efficiency (QE ~1.0) paired with low geometric similarity (GS ~0) indicates valid but irrelevant actions; near-zero invalid action rate suggests no exploration; rapid early improvement then plateau shows inability beyond simple primitives; Causal task accuracy near random (25%) confirms reliance on associative matching.

**First 3 experiments:** 1. Baseline replication: run one-step Associative/Causal and full-step (10/25 steps) for Qwen3-VL-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B to validate reported metrics. 2. Input ablation: occlude back-view images in the Causal task to measure accuracy drop and quantify reliance on dual-view consistency. 3. State-hint probe: augment full-step prompt with textual summary of current CP (vertex/edge list, M/V assignments) to test if structured state description improves planning (GS/SS) without increasing QE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions, but its findings imply several critical research directions:
1. How can VLMs be architected to better integrate visual perception with symbolic causal reasoning for physical transformation tasks?
2. What training paradigms or inductive biases encourage the development of an internal world model that supports coherent multi-step planning?
3. Does scaling dataset diversity (beyond 366 designs) and incorporating more complex origami categories resolve the observed generalizability and causal reasoning gaps?

## Limitations
- Evidence for the representational gap is indirect (performance drop); no probing confirms how visual and symbolic features bind—or fail to.
- The claim of a missing world model is correlational; without internal state tracing (e.g., attention analysis), it remains inferred from poor planning metrics.
- Dataset scale (366 designs) may limit category diversity and risk overfitting to rendering artifacts; sparse binary feedback provides no error diagnosis, hindering learning.

## Confidence
- Representational Gap: Medium (supported by consistent Associative→Causal drop across models, but no causal intervention)
- World Model Absence: Medium-Low (inferred from planning failure, but could be due to other factors like search capacity)
- Causal Abstraction: Medium (Causal task design isolates this ability, but alternative explanations exist)

## Next Checks
1. Replicate core metrics on at least three additional VLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, LLaVA-NeXT) to test generalizability of the performance gap.
2. Visual cue ablation: remove back-view images in the Causal task; a significant accuracy drop would confirm reliance on dual-view consistency for causal inference.
3. State representation intervention: replace PNGs with structured text descriptions of the current crease pattern; measure change in geometric similarity (IoU) and query efficiency to assess sensitivity to input modality.
