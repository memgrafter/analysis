# Full waveform inversion method based on diffusion model

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22307
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22307
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Primary result:** Conditional diffusion FWI incorporating velocity-density coupling significantly improves accuracy and robustness over traditional and unconditional diffusion FWI on synthetic benchmarks under noise, limited-shot, and low-frequency-missing conditions.

## Executive Summary
Full-waveform inversion (FWI) is a highly nonlinear ill-posed problem sensitive to initial models. This paper introduces a conditional diffusion model regularization that explicitly accounts for the physical coupling between subsurface velocity and density. The method modifies a U-Net denoising network to accept 2D density maps as conditional input, enabling the diffusion prior to generate velocity models consistent with the given density structure. During inference, conventional FWI gradient updates are alternated with diffusion-based corrections, gradually refining a smoothed initial model. Experiments on OpenFWI and Marmousi datasets show consistent improvements in quantitative metrics (MAE, MSE, SSIM) and qualitative structural fidelity, particularly under noisy and data-limited scenarios.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework uses a conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) pre-trained on paired velocity–density samples. The U‑Net backbone is adapted to take a noisy velocity, diffusion timestep, and a 2D density map, predicting the noise component. For inversion, the algorithm starts from a highly smoothed model equivalent to a noisy sample at timestep `t_start`. Within each diffusion timestep, several iterations of standard FWI (minimizing data misfit) are performed, followed by one reverse diffusion step that projects the updated model onto the learned prior manifold, conditioned on density. This alternating scheme repeats from `t_start` down to 0, yielding the final inverted velocity. The hybrid approach leverages data fitting while constraining solutions to physically plausible velocity–density relationships.

## Key Results
- Quantitative gains: Across OpenFWI and Marmousi models, conditional diffusion FWI reduces MAE and MSE while increasing SSIM compared to both traditional FWI and unconditional diffusion FWI.
- Robustness: The method maintains high accuracy under additive Gaussian noise, limited‑shot acquisition (3 sources), and missing low frequencies (<3 Hz), where conventional FWI fails.
- Qualitative improvements: Inverted models show sharper interfaces, better preservation of multi‑scale structures, and fewer artifacts, demonstrating effective enforcement of inter‑property physical constraints.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Conditional Input Enforces Physical Coupling
- **Claim:** Incorporating 2D density maps as conditional input to the diffusion model’s U‑Net improves inversion fidelity by enforcing velocity‑density physical coupling.
- **Mechanism:** During the reverse diffusion process, the network’s noise prediction is guided not only by the current noisy velocity and timestep but also by the co‑located density structure. This biases the generated velocity toward regions that are physically plausible given the density distribution, leveraging the joint distribution learned from training data.
- **Evidence:** \[abstract\], \[section 1.3 & Fig 4\] describe the modified network; neighbor paper *Geological and Well prior assisted full waveform inversion using conditional diffusion models* (arxiv:2412.06959) supports the conditioning paradigm.
- **Break condition:** If the provided density map is highly inaccurate, noisy, or geologically mismatched, the conditional bias may force the inversion toward an implausible coupled state, degrading performance below unconditional or traditional FWI.

### Mechanism 2: Diffusion as an Expressive Data‑Driven Prior
- **Claim:** The diffusion model provides a more expressive implicit prior than handcrafted regularizers (e.g., TV), better capturing complex, non‑smooth geological structures.
- **Mechanism:** The DDPM is pre‑trained on a large corpus of velocity models, implicitly learning the high‑dimensional distribution `p(v)`. During FWI, instead of applying a fixed penalty (e.g., `λ||∇v||`), the inversion is regularized by projecting intermediate models onto this learned manifold, preserving sharp interfaces and high‑wavenumber details.
- **Evidence:** \[abstract\], \[section 1.2 & 1.3\] state that diffusion models “can more naturally preserve non‑smooth interfaces”; neighbor diffusion‑based FWI papers (e.g., arxiv:2603.16393) indicate a shift toward learned priors.
- **Break condition:** If the target subsurface model lies far outside the support of the training distribution (e.g., a novel geological setting), the learned prior may filter out valid high‑wavenumber details or impose incorrect structures.

### Mechanism 3: Hybrid Optimization Stabilization
- **Claim:** Alternating between FWI gradient updates and diffusion‑based posterior sampling stabilizes inversion by periodically re‑projecting the intermediate model onto the high‑probability manifold of the learned prior.
- **Mechanism:** Within each diffusion timestep `t`, several FWI iterations fit the observed data, moving `v_t` toward a data‑consistent solution. Then a single reverse diffusion step corrects `v_t` to `v_{t-1}` using the conditional prior, preventing divergence from the prior manifold. Repeating from a noisy initial model (`t=T`) gradually refines the solution.
- **Evidence:** \[section 1.3 & Fig 3\] explicitly describe the alternating scheme; \[section 2.2\] shows improved stability under noise and low‑frequency missing conditions.
- **Break condition:** If the number of FWI iterations per diffusion step is too high, the model may drift far from the prior before correction, causing instability or convergence to a poor local minimum; if too low, progress may be prohibitively slow.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Data & Condition Input Pipeline → Integration Controller  
Integration Controller → FWI Forward Solver & Gradient Engine  
Integration Controller → Pre-trained Conditional Diffusion Model  
FWI Forward Solver & Gradient Engine → Integration Controller  
Pre-trained Conditional Diffusion Model → Integration Controller  

### Critical Path
1. **Pre‑training:** Train the conditional U‑Net on paired (velocity, density) samples from OpenFWI using DDPM noise‑prediction loss.
2. **Inference:**  
   a. Start with initial model `v_T` (equivalent to a noisy sample at diffusion timestep `T`).  
   b. For `t = T` down to `1`:  
      &nbsp;&nbsp;i. Perform `N_fwi` iterations of gradient‑based FWI update on `v_t`.  
      &nbsp;&nbsp;ii. Apply one reverse diffusion step: `v_{t-1} = denoise(v_t, t, density_map)` using the pre‑trained network.  
   c. Output final `v_0` as the inverted velocity model.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Conditional input fidelity vs. availability:** The method assumes an accurate, co‑registered 2D density map is available. In practice, density might come from well logs, rock physics, or joint inversion. A trade‑off exists between the added physical constraint and potential error propagation from an inaccurate density input.
- **Diffusion step count `T` vs. computation:** Higher `T` allows more gradual, stable correction but increases total steps. Lower `T` is faster but may reduce the stabilizing effect.
- **FWI iterations per step `N_fwi`:** High `N_fwi` risks drifting from the prior manifold between corrections; low `N_fwi` may slow data‑fitting progress. This is a key hyperparameter requiring tuning.

### Failure Signatures
1. **Density misalignment artifacts:** Structural edges in the final velocity model distort or kink exactly where the conditioning density map has errors or abrupt changes.
2. **Over‑smoothing / loss of detail:** Final model appears overly smooth and lacks expected high‑wavenumber features despite good data fit, indicating excessive prior influence.
3. **Stagnation at poor data fit:** Algorithm converges to a model with high data misfit that the diffusion prior considers “likely,” suggesting prior bias or training‑target mismatch.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Sanity check: Unconditional baseline replication.** Implement the unconditional diffusion‑regularized FWI from reference [19] on a simple OpenFWI model (e.g., FlatVel‑A). Verify matching of baseline results in Fig 8b/9b to ensure correct FWI‑diffusion integration before adding conditioning.
2. **Conditional prior verification.** Train the conditional U‑Net on a small, fixed subset of OpenFWI (e.g., 500 samples). Generate velocity samples conditioned on held‑out density maps. Visually/quantitatively check if generated velocities respect the density structure (e.g., correlation between velocity and density patches) before using in FWI.
3. **Single‑property inversion test.** Run the full pipeline on a scenario where density is constant (or perfectly correlated with velocity). The conditional model should perform identically to unconditional diffusion; any significant performance drop indicates a flawed conditioning mechanism.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does the conditional diffusion FWI method maintain its accuracy advantage on real field seismic data, which often contain unmodeled complexities like irregular noise and anisotropy?
- **Basis in paper:** Experiments are confined to synthetic OpenFWI and Marmousi datasets; no validation on actual field data is presented despite claims of practical value.
- **Why unresolved:** Synthetic data may not fully replicate the noise characteristics, acquisition gaps, and geological heterogeneity of real‑world scenarios, potentially overstating robustness.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Testing on publicly available real seismic datasets (e.g., from industry or research consortia) with independent ground truth (e.g., well logs) and comparing metrics like SSIM and MAE against traditional FWI.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the conditional diffusion framework be extended to incorporate additional physical properties beyond density (e.g., attenuation Q or anisotropy parameters) to further improve multi‑parameter inversion?
- **Basis in paper:** The introduction states that existing methods ignore “the inherent physical coupling relationship between velocity and density and other physical properties,” yet the proposed method only conditions on 2D density.
- **Why unresolved:** FWI often inverts for multiple parameters; ignoring other couplings may limit the model’s ability to handle full wave equation physics in complex media.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Modifying the U‑Net to accept multi‑channel conditional inputs (e.g., density and Q‑maps), training on multi‑parameter synthetic datasets, and evaluating inversion accuracy for all parameters.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the computational overhead of the conditional diffusion regularization compared to unconditional diffusion and traditional FWI, and how does it scale with model resolution or 3D extension?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper reports only accuracy metrics (MAE, MSE, SSIM); no discussion of training time, inference steps, or memory usage is provided.
- **Why unresolved:** Diffusion models are inherently iterative and computationally intensive; adding conditional inputs may increase network complexity, affecting feasibility for large‑scale or real‑time applications.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting benchmark experiments on standardized hardware, measuring wall‑clock time, memory consumption, and convergence rates for all methods across varying grid sizes (e.g., 2D vs. 3D).

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How sensitive is the conditional diffusion FWI to errors or uncertainties in the provided density maps, and can the method self‑calibrate under such conditions?
- **Basis in paper:** Density is treated as an accurate conditional input, but its acquisition (e.g., from well logs or empirical relationships) often involves errors; the paper does not address input sensitivity.
- **Why unresolved:** Inaccurate density conditions could bias the diffusion prior, leading to erroneous velocity updates, yet no robustness analysis is performed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Introducing controlled perturbations (e.g., Gaussian noise, smoothing, or bias) into density inputs and quantifying the degradation in inversion metrics; potentially exploring joint inversion or uncertainty quantification.

## Limitations
- **Density map source and quality:** Unclear whether OpenFWI provides paired density or if Gardner’s equation is used; inaccurate density could degrade performance.
- **Incomplete methodological details:** Exact U‑Net conditioning (e.g., concatenation), hyperparameters (diffusion steps, FWI iterations per step, learning rates), and wave solver configuration hinder reproducibility.
- **Generalization risk:** Learned prior may not extend to geological settings outside the OpenFWI training distribution, potentially filtering valid high‑wavenumber details.
