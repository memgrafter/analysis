# Efficient Policy Learning with Hybrid Evaluation-Based Genetic Programming for Uncertain Agile Earth Observation Satellite Scheduling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08447
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08447
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** HE-GP reduces average training time by 17.77% compared to exact-evaluation GP while achieving the highest average rank across 16 uncertain scheduling scenarios, significantly outperforming handcrafted heuristics and single-evaluation GP variants.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the Uncertain Agile Earth Observation Satellite Scheduling Problem (UAEOSSP), where uncertainties in profit, resource consumption, and target visibility complicate scheduling. It introduces Hybrid Evaluation-based Genetic Programming (HE-GP), which evolves interpretable scheduling policies. A hybrid evaluation mechanism within the online scheduling algorithm dynamically switches between exact (feasibility-guaranteed) and approximate (computationally cheap) filtering modes during policy fitness assessment. This adaptation balances evaluation accuracy and computational cost throughout evolution. Evaluated on 16 simulated instance sets, HE-GP achieves superior solution quality with substantially lower training times than exact or approximate evaluation alone.

## Method Summary
HE-GP employs a Genetic Programming Hyper-Heuristic framework to evolve tree-based scheduling policies. Each policy is evaluated by an Online Scheduling Algorithm (OSA) that constructs schedules sequentially under realized uncertainty scenarios. The core innovation is the Hybrid Evaluation (HE) mechanism applied within OSA's candidate filtering step. The exact mode uses four constraint checks, including a two-stage binary search for the earliest feasible observation window, guaranteeing correctness. The approximate mode replaces the costly observation window calculation with a lookup based on a precomputed maximum transition time matrix, reducing time complexity per check from O(log w) to O(1). An adaptive switching controller determines the per-generation probability of using exact filtering (`P_exact`) based on evolutionary stage (`f_aces`) and population diversity (`f_acpd`). Training uses mini-batch rotation over scenario instances. The method is benchmarked against exact-evaluation GP (EE-GP), approximate-evaluation GP (AE-GP), and several handcrafted heuristics.

## Key Results
- HE-GP reduced average training time by 17.77% compared to EE-GP while maintaining statistically indistinguishable solution quality.
- HE-GP achieved the highest average rank (1.4375) across all 16 test scenario sets, significantly outperforming EE-GP, AE-GP, and all handcrafted baselines.
- The hybrid approach effectively balanced exploration (using approximate mode for speed in early generations) and exploitation (increasing exact mode usage later for finer fitness discrimination).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hybrid Evaluation Cost-Quality Trade-off
- **Claim:** The HE mechanism reduces computational cost during policy fitness evaluation while maintaining solution quality by adaptively combining exact and approximate filtering modes within the OSA.
- **Mechanism:** The Filter subroutine processes candidate requests using two modes. Exact mode employs four check modules (including a two-stage binary search for the earliest observation window) to guarantee feasibility. Approximate mode replaces the expensive observation window calculation with a preprocessed maximum transition time matrix, reducing time complexity from O(log w) to O(1) per request.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] explicitly describes HE combining modes for cost/accuracy balance. Section IV details the exact/approximate filtering flows, highlighting observation window calculation as the key difference. Corpus context notes fitness evaluation as a primary GP bottleneck.
- **Break condition:** If the approximate mode's observation window heuristic systematically biases fitness estimates (e.g., consistently over/under-estimating feasibility), the adaptive switching may mis-rank policies, degrading final solution quality.

### Mechanism 2: Adaptive Switching Controller
- **Claim:** An adaptive switching controller, driven by evolutionary state metrics (stage and diversity), dynamically allocates evaluation modes to balance global exploration and local exploitation, mitigating evaluation-dependent local optima.
- **Mechanism:** Two factors are computed each generation: `f_aces` (progress ratio = current generation / total generations) and `f_acpd` (unique fitness ratio). Their weighted sum determines `P_exact` (Eq. 25-26). Early generations/low diversity favor approximate mode for speed; later stages/high diversity increase exact mode usage.
- **Evidence:** Section IV-C defines `f_aces`, `f_acpd`, and the switching equation. Fig. 7 shows HE-GP's optimal policy improves more consistently than single-mode baselines, suggesting switching aids escape from stagnation.
- **Break condition:** If the chosen factors (`f_aces`, `f_acpd`) become decoupled from actual search dynamics (e.g., low diversity during a globally explorative phase), the switching may misapply modes, harming convergence.

### Mechanism 3: Policy-Driven Constructive MDP
- **Claim:** The policy-driven, timeline-based MDP OSA generates feasible schedules for UAEOSSP under uncertainty by repeatedly filtering candidate requests via constraint checks and executing the policy's priority function, with HE accelerating the state-update loop.
- **Mechanism:** At each idle decision point, OSA filters the candidate pool to retain only requests with feasible observation windows. The policy computes heuristic values for the filtered set; the highest-priority request is scheduled, state updates (time, memory), and the loop repeats. The HE mechanism specifically accelerates the Filter subroutine.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] describes the policy-driven online scheduling algorithm. Section III-C introduces OSA as the constructive MDP method. Algorithm 2 details the iterative scheduling loop. Corpus confirms MDPs are standard for autonomous satellite scheduling.
- **Break condition:** If uncertainty distributions shift such that the precomputed maximum transition times become poor approximations for most candidate pairs, the approximate filter may discard feasible requests or retain infeasible ones, leading to suboptimal/infeasible schedules.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  GP Population -> Evaluation (OSA(sp, env) -> Filter (HE-switched) -> Schedule/Profit) -> Fitness (mean over mini-batch) -> Selection/Crossover/Mutation -> New Population
  Subcomponents: OSA includes Policy, HE-OSA Filter (Exact/Approximate), Switching Controller. Genetic Operators apply to GP Population.

- **Critical path:** The evaluation loop is the critical path. HE targets the Filter subroutine within OSA, which is invoked repeatedly during the constructive scheduling process for each individual in each generation.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Exact vs. Approximate Filter:** Exact guarantees feasibility but is slower (O(log w)); approximate is O(1) but risks feasibility errors.
  - **Switching metric simplicity:** Using only generation count and fitness diversity is cheap but may not perfectly capture search stage needs.
  - **Policy complexity:** HE does not control policy tree size; simpler policies evaluate faster regardless, but HE aims to speed up evaluation itself.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **No training time reduction vs. EE-GP:** HE's approximate mode rarely triggered; switching logic flawed.
  - **Quality degradation vs. EE-GP:** Approximate mode too aggressive; `P_exact` too low later, corrupting fitness.
  - **Early population stagnation:** Switching over-uses exact mode early, slowing exploration; or policies evolved under noisy approximate fitness lack refinement.
  - **High variance across runs:** Sensitive hyperparameters (`φ_es`, `φ_pd`) not tuned; random seed greatly affects switching schedule.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Baseline comparison:** Run HE-GP, EE-GP, AE-GP on all 16 scenario sets. Record test-set profit and training time. Expected: HE-GP near EE-GP quality with ~17% less time; AE-GP faster but lower quality.
  2.  **Parameter sensitivity:** Sweep `(f_aces, f_pd)` pairs from (0,1) to (1,0) in 0.1 steps, 10 runs each. Measure average rank. Expected: Balanced weights (e.g., 0.6,0.4) perform best; extremes worse.
  3.  **Evolutionary dynamics tracking:** For a representative scenario (e.g., 100 requests), log per-generation: best fitness, avg policy size, `f_aces`, `f_pd`, % evaluations using exact mode. Expected: Approximate mode dominates early (~80%); exact mode increases after ~30% generations or when diversity drops. Observe correlation between exact-mode spikes and fitness improvements.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multi-satellite extension:** The current implementation is limited to single-AEOS scheduling; future research could extend to constellations of multiple AEOSs, introducing inter-satellite coordination and shared resource complexities.
- **Hyperparameter optimization:** The hyperparameters of the HE mechanism (`φ_es`, `φ_pd`) require further systematic tuning across a broader range of problem instances and uncertainty levels. The paper's sensitivity analysis found no single best setting.
- **Policy redundancy and interpretability:** Existing GP-based methods produce policies with redundant structures (e.g., `max(EM_UR, EM_UR)`), reducing decision-making efficiency and hindering user understanding. Effective simplification techniques integrated into GP evolution are needed.

## Limitations
- **Switching logic validity:** The adaptive controller uses simple metrics (`f_aces`, `f_acpd`) that may not reliably detect evolutionary state; no ablation of alternative state detectors is provided.
- **Approximate mode feasibility bias:** The precomputed maximum transition time matrix is a worst-case heuristic; it may systematically bias feasibility estimates, potentially corrupting fitness rankings in later generations.
- **Instance generation fidelity:** The paper references STK-based ground-track methods but provides no open-source data; reproduction may suffer from subtle mismatches in visibility window distributions.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (HE cost/quality trade-off):** **High** — Clear algorithmic description; time complexity reduction is analytically sound; ablation (EE vs. AE) shows expected patterns.
- **Mechanism 2 (Adaptive switching efficacy):** **Medium** — Switching logic is specified but lacks theoretical justification; Fig. 7 suggests benefit but no direct ablation of the controller itself.
- **Overall superiority (HE-GP ranks best):** **Medium** — Results are consistent across 16 scenarios with statistical tests, but reproducibility is uncertain due to unreleased code/data and potential implementation variance.
