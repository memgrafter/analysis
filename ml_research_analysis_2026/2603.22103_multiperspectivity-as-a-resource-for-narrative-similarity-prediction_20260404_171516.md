# Multiperspectivity as a Resource for Narrative Similarity Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.22103
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22103
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieved test accuracy of 0.705 on SemEval-2026 Task 4, ranking 13th out of 47 teams.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the inherent subjectivity in narrative similarity prediction by leveraging multiperspectivity through an ensemble of 31 diverse LLM personas. Each persona interprets stories from a distinct analytical or lay viewpoint, producing similarity judgments. Aggregating these judgments via majority voting across three model backbones yields robust performance, attaining 0.705 test accuracy on a competitive benchmark. Key findings reveal that ensemble accuracy grows with size, practitioner personas—despite lower individual accuracy—contribute less correlated errors and thus improve collective outcomes, and that the use of gender-focused interpretive vocabulary consistently correlates with lower benchmark accuracy, highlighting a gap between benchmark criteria and broader interpretive diversity.

## Method Summary
The system addresses binary narrative similarity classification: given an anchor story and two candidates, decide which candidate is more similar. The authors assembled 31 persona definitions (e.g., "Feminist Literary Critic", "Tour Guide") spanning analytical practitioners and lay readers. For each test item, these personas were applied to three LLMs (Gemma 27B-it, Qwen3-14B, gpt-oss-20b), generating JSON responses with similarity scores (1–10) for both candidates. A binary prediction favored the higher-scoring candidate. The final decision aggregated the 93 votes (31 personas × 3 models) by simple majority. The approach was evaluated on the SemEval-2026 Task 4 dataset (400 test story triplets), achieving 0.705 accuracy.

## Key Results
- Ensemble accuracy increases monotonically with the number of personas, aligning with Condorcet Jury Theorem dynamics despite moderate error correlations (r≈0.4–0.6).
- Practitioner personas individually perform worse (mean 71.0%) than lay personas (71.7%) yet exhibit lower pairwise error correlation (r=0.388 vs. 0.461), leading to higher ensemble accuracy (76.0% vs. 75.3%).
- Across all persona groups, the occurrence of gender-focused interpretive vocabulary (e.g., "female protagonist", "patriarchal") in responses negatively correlates with correctness, suggesting such perspectives may not align with the benchmark’s implicit criteria.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Condorcet-style aggregation under weakened independence.** Each persona acts as a voter with >50% competence. Majority voting aggregates many such voters, causing random errors to cancel out. The paper observes monotonic accuracy gains as ensemble size grows (Table 2), supporting that even with correlated errors, the ensemble benefits follow a Condorcet Jury Theorem-like pattern. The break condition arises if errors become perfectly correlated.

**Mechanism 2: Practitioner error diversity compensates lower individual accuracy.** Practitioner personas ([P]) have lower mean individual accuracy than lay personas ([L]) but produce less correlated errors (r=0.388 vs. 0.461). Under majority voting, diverse error patterns are more likely to be outvoted, yielding a higher majority vote accuracy for [P]-heavy ensembles (76.0% vs. 75.3%). This demonstrates that diversity can outweigh individual competence in collective decisions.

**Mechanism 3: Broad majority voting generalizes better than optimized subset selection.** Exhaustively searching for a small optimal subset (k=8) yields high dev accuracy (82.0%) but poor test accuracy (0.705), creating an 11.5 percentage point dev-test gap. In contrast, the full 31‑persona majority vote shows a smaller gap (5.6 pp), indicating that broad aggregation averages out idiosyncratic overfits to the small dev set’s specific interpretive quirks.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Condorcet Jury Theorem (CJT)**
- **Why needed**: Provides the theoretical foundation for expecting ensemble gains from independent, competent voters. The paper tests its applicability under weakened independence (common in LLM ensembles).
- **Quick check**: What two key conditions must hold for CJT to guarantee improved collective decisions, and how does the paper address potential violations?

**Concept: Error Diversity in Ensembles**
- **Why needed**: Explains why [P] personas, though individually worse, improve majority vote outcomes. Diversity (low error correlation) allows error cancellation, boosting collective accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Persona Generators -> Model Inference Layer -> Voting Aggregator -> Evaluation Harness

**Critical Path:** Design personas (definitions and prompts) -> Run inference on development set to evaluate ensemble properties -> Analyze results (ensemble size scaling, practitioner vs. lay differences, error vocabulary) -> Select/vote on test set using full majority voting.

**Design Tradeoffs:** 
- *Simplicity vs. Optimization*: Simple majority voting with all personas vs. exhaustive subset search. The former generalizes better; the latter overfits the small dev set.
- *Independence vs. Diversity*: Using personas from the same model family reduces voter independence. The use of multiple model families partially restores independence but increases computational cost.

**Failure Signatures:** 
- Overfitting: Large dev-test accuracy gap when using optimized small ensembles (e.g., 11.5 pp for k=8).
- Vocabulary penalty: Personas that employ gender‑focused terms (e.g., "female protagonist") show lower correctness, indicating misalignment with benchmark criteria.
- Model collapse: The gpt-oss‑20B model’s majority vote accuracy is low (60.8%) despite high oracle coverage, suggesting high inter‑persona disagreement.

**First 3 Experiments:** 
1. **Ensemble size scaling**: Vary the number of personas included in the majority vote (E = 1,3,5,10,20,30,31) and measure accuracy on the dev set to confirm monotonic gains.
2. **Practitioner vs. Lay comparison**: Compare mean individual accuracy and pairwise error correlation between the two persona groups to test the diversity hypothesis.
3. **Gender vocabulary correlation**: For each persona group, compute the point‑biserial correlation between the presence of gender‑distinctive terms (lift ≥ 5) and binary correctness across dev items; expect negative correlations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1: How does multi-agent debating compare to majority voting?** The paper only evaluated independent majority voting; structured deliberation among personas may yield additional gains. Resolution requires implementing a debate framework and comparing accuracy on the SemEval-2026 Task 4 dataset.

**Open Question 2: How can benchmarks for narrative similarity incorporate multiple valid interpretations?** Current single‑ground‑truth evaluations penalize legitimate alternative readings. Resolution demands benchmarks with multiple gold labels per item and metrics that reward capturing the range of plausible interpretations.

**Open Question 3: Do observed patterns generalize to other narrative understanding tasks?** Findings are based on one dataset; reproducibility on other tasks (e.g., story cloze, plot similarity) is unknown. Resolution requires replicating the persona ensemble methodology on at least two additional narrative benchmarks.

**Open Question 4: Does gender‑focused vocabulary causally reduce accuracy or merely correlate?** The negative correlation may be confounded by other narrative features. Establishing causality needs controlled interventions (e.g., prompting models to suppress such terms) or causal mediation analysis.

## Limitations
- The SemEval-2026 Task 4 dataset and the gpt-oss‑20b model are not publicly available, hindering exact replication.
- The development set contains only 200 items, increasing the risk of overfitting when optimizing ensemble subsets.
- Pairwise error correlations among personas (r≈0.4–0.6) violate the independence assumption of the Condorcet Jury Theorem, potentially capping theoretical gains.

## Confidence
- Ensemble size improves accuracy via Condorcet‑like aggregation: **High**
- Practitioner personas yield larger ensemble gains due to less correlated errors: **Medium**
- Broad majority voting generalizes better than optimized subset selection: **High**
- Gender‑focused interpretive vocabulary correlates negatively with accuracy: **High**
