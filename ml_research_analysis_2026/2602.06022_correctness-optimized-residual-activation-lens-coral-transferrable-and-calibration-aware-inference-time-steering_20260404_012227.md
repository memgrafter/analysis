# Correctness-Optimized Residual Activation Lens (CORAL): Transferrable and Calibration-Aware Inference-Time Steering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06022
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06022
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A regularized MLP probe trained on frozen residual activations directly predicts residual correctness and steers outputs, improving accuracy by ~10% and ECE by ~50% in-distribution, with zero-shot transfer gains of ~14% accuracy and ~49% ECE reduction across four held-out benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) remain miscalibrated after alignment, and existing inference-time steering methods optimize proxies for correctness rather than correctness itself. CORAL introduces a lightweight, compute-efficient approach: a regularized MLP probe is trained on frozen residual stream activations to directly predict *residual correctness*—the gap between a model's assigned probability and the ideal target probability. During inference, these predictions steer the model's output probabilities toward better calibration. Evaluated on three 7B instruct models across multiple MCQA benchmarks, CORAL consistently improves both accuracy and expected calibration error (ECE). Crucially, the same probe transfers without retraining to four diverse held-out test sets, demonstrating generalizable gains. Sparse autoencoder analysis confirms that correctness signals are distributed across many neurons; individual neurons have negligible causal impact, but weight-decay regularization successfully aggregates this weak, distributed signal.

## Method Summary
CORAL addresses LLM miscalibration by training a probe to predict a residual correctness target defined for each multiple-choice option: \(r_j = 1-p_j\) if the option is correct, and \(r_j = -p_j\) otherwise, where \(p_j\) is the model's normalized probability for that option. This target aligns directly with Brier score minimization. For each option, the method mean-pools hidden states from a single middle layer of the model's residual stream. These activations are z-score normalized using training set statistics. A 4-hidden-layer MLP (1024-512-256-128 units, ReLU, dropout 0.2, tanh output) is trained via mean squared error on the residuals, plus an output penalty term \(\lambda_{\text{out}} \cdot \text{mean}(\hat{r}_j^2)\) using AdamW with weight decay. This regularization forces the probe to learn a low-norm direction that aggregates distributed signals rather than overfitting. At inference, for a new question, the probe predicts residuals \(\hat{r}_j\) from the same layer's activations. These are centered to sum to zero (\(\tilde{r}_j = \hat{r}_j - \frac{1}{n}\sum \hat{r}_j\)), scaled by a tuned strength \(\gamma\) (typically 1.0), and added to the base logits before a final softmax: \(p'_j = \text{softmax}(p_j + \gamma \tilde{r}_j)\). The argmax of \(p'\) is selected as the answer.

## Key Results
- In-distribution (on training benchmark test sets), CORAL improves average accuracy by **10%** and ECE by **50%** across three 7B models.
- Zero-shot transfer to four held-out benchmarks (HellaSwag, OpenBookQA, ARC-Challenge, Math-MC) yields average accuracy gains of **14%** and ECE reductions of **49%** using a single probe.
- CORAL significantly outperforms prior steering methods (ITI, SteerConf, CCPS) on both in-distribution and transfer scenarios, with concurrent improvements in Brier score and accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Regularized Probe Aggregation of Distributed Signals
**Claim:** Weight-decay MLP probes successfully aggregate correctness signals distributed across many neurons, which individual neurons or SAE features cannot capture alone.  
**Mechanism:** L2 regularization (via AdamW weight decay) forces the probe to learn a low-norm direction that captures the collective signal from many weak features rather than overfitting to any single strong feature. The MLP architecture allows nonlinear combination across dimensions.  
**Core assumption:** Correctness information is distributed across many neurons/features, each contributing marginally; sparse feature selection fails because no single feature has sufficient causal impact.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "correctness signals are distributed; individual neurons have negligible causal impact, but weight-decay regularization successfully aggregates them." [section 5.2] SAE ablations show "individual features produce negligible causal effects" and "aggregating beneficial features for steering also fails to recover meaningful gains." [section 6] "Regularization Captures What Sparse Methods Miss."

### Mechanism 2: Residual Correctness as Direct Calibration Target
**Claim:** Residual correctness \(r_j = (1-p_j \text{ if correct}, -p_j \text{ if incorrect})\) provides a direct, gradient-friendly target for improving the Brier score, which jointly optimizes accuracy and calibration.  
**Mechanism:** Brier score decomposes exactly as \(\sum (p_j - y_j)^2 = \sum r_j^2\). Training a probe to predict \(r_j\) and applying \(p'_j = p_j + \gamma \cdot (\hat{r}_j - \text{mean}(\hat{r}_j))\) minimizes expected squared error between predicted and ideal probabilities, thereby improving both accuracy (by upweighting correct options) and calibration (by correcting over/under-confidence).  
**Core assumption:** The residual target is a sufficient statistic for Brier score improvement; small corrections based on predicted residuals will move the distribution toward the ideal target distribution.  
**Evidence anchors:** [section 3.1] Mathematical derivation showing Brier = \(\sum r_j^2\) and that \(p'_j = p_j + \hat{r}_j\) minimizes expected squared error. [abstract] "directly predict residual correctness—the gap between a model's probability and the ideal target." [section 4.1] Consistent improvements in Brier score across models and datasets.

### Mechanism 3: Transferability via Regularization-Induced Generality
**Claim:** Weight-decay regularization prevents overfitting to dataset-specific patterns, forcing the probe to extract a general-purpose correctness subspace that transfers across benchmarks without retraining.  
**Mechanism:** The L2 penalty limits the probe's capacity to fit spurious correlations present only in the training distribution. Instead, it must find a direction that captures consistent, cross-dataset patterns in residual correctness. This learned direction generalizes because correctness signals share structure across reasoning tasks.  
**Core assumption:** A shared, abstract notion of "correctness" exists in activation space across different MCQA tasks, and regularization helps isolate this general component from task-specific noise.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "gains transfer without retraining to four held-out benchmark test sets, yielding average accuracy gains of 14% and ECE reductions of 49%." [section 4.3] Transfer results table shows CORAL beating baselines on HellaSwag, OpenBookQA, ARC-Challenge, Math-MC. [section 6] Contrast with CCPS: "CCPS shows limited transfer despite strong in-distribution results, possibly because its contrastive training captures dataset-specific distinctions."

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Residual Correctness (\(r_j = 1-p_j\) if correct, \(-p_j\) if incorrect)**  
  **Why needed here:** This is the core supervisory signal that makes CORAL's objective mathematically aligned with Brier score minimization. It reframes calibration from a post-hoc probability adjustment into a direct prediction problem on residuals.  
  **Quick check question:** Given a model assigns \(p_{\text{correct}}=0.9\) to the correct option and \(p_{\text{wrong}}=0.05\) to each of two wrong options, compute residual correctness for all options.

- **Concept: Distributed vs. Localized Representations in Neural Networks**  
  **Why needed here:** The paper's central hypothesis is that calibration information is distributed, which justifies using regularized full-layer probes rather than sparse SAE feature selection. Understanding this distinction explains why CORAL works while sparse steering fails.  
  **Quick check question:** If individual SAE features have negligible causal impact on calibration (mean ECE change ~1e-4), how can the model still be miscalibrated? Where does the signal reside?

- **Concept: Inference-Time Steering vs. Model Retraining**  
  **Why needed here:** CORAL is positioned as a lightweight alternative to retraining. Understanding this paradigm clarifies its efficiency benefits and constraints (e.g., training probes on frozen activations rather than updating model weights).  
  **Quick check question:** What are the key practical advantages (and limitations) of modifying activations during forward passes instead of fine-tuning the model?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Activation Extractor -> Normalizer -> Regularized MLP Probe -> Steering Engine

**Critical path:**  
*Training:* (1) Collect activations & compute residual labels on training questions. (2) Train probe on frozen activations with validation-based hyperparameter selection (layer, γ, λ_out). (3) Store probe weights and normalization statistics.  
*Inference:* (1) For new question, extract activations at the same layer used during training. (2) Normalize using stored stats. (3) Run probe to get \(\hat{r}_j\). (4) Center and apply steering to base probabilities. (5) Select argmax answer.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Probe depth:** MLPs with 1–3 hidden layers underperform linear probes by 0.5–0.75%. Adding more layers (beyond 4) yields no gains, suggesting modest nonlinearity suffices.  
- **Layer choice:** Middle layers (17–21 for Deepseek-7B) yield best results. Early layers (0–6) degrade accuracy; late layers (24+) show diminishing returns.  
- **Steering strength γ:** Grid search reveals γ=1 consistently optimal. Higher γ risks over-correction and probability invalidation; lower γ underutilizes the signal.  
- **Regularization λ_out:** Prevents probe from predicting large residuals; tuned via validation R². Too strong λ loses signal; too weak leads to overfitting and poor transfer.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Early-layer steering:** Accuracy drops below baseline. Indicates correctness signals not yet formed in early representations.  
- **Sparse feature steering:** Using top SAE features by activation or correlation yields negligible or negative improvements. Signals are distributed, not sparse.  
- **Multi-layer concatenation:** No gain over single-layer probes. Correctness information appears concentrated enough at a single layer; concatenation adds noise.  
- **Transfer to dissimilar tasks:** Smaller gains on HellaSwag (longer answer options) suggest distribution shift. Probe may underperform when answer length/structure differs significantly from training.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **In-distribution validation sweep:** For each model, train probe on 80% of training data. Evaluate steering across γ={0.25,0.5,…,3.0} and all layers. Identify optimal layer and γ that maximize validation R² while improving accuracy/ECE on held-out validation set.  
2. **Baseline comparison:** Run CORAL (with optimal hyperparameters) against ITI, CCPS, SteerConf on in-distribution test sets (RACE, CommonsenseQA, MMLU subset). Verify consistent accuracy/ECE improvements.  
3. **Transfer test:** Apply Probe 1 (trained on CommonsenseQA+RACE) to full test sets of ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, Math-MC, OpenBookQA without retraining. Confirm average 14% accuracy gain and 49% ECE reduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does the correctness signal identified by CORAL generalize to free-form text generation tasks where answer correctness is nuanced and non-binary?
- Can CORAL's probe training be adapted to operate without large-scale labeled correctness data, perhaps via self-supervised or synthetic supervision?
- What specific properties of tasks determine the transferability of CORAL's correctness direction, and can we predict or improve cross-task transfer?
- Is the distributed encoding of correctness signals a universal characteristic of LLMs, or does it depend on model scale, architecture, or training regime?

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility Ambiguities:** Key implementation details are unspecified, including exact prompt templates and answer tokenization for each benchmark, the precise method for computing base probabilities \(p_j\), and the full hyperparameter search grid.
- **Generality Scope:** Demonstrated transferability is confined to multiple-choice QA benchmarks; performance on tasks with different output formats (e.g., free-form generation) or substantially different reasoning patterns shows diminished returns.
- **Causal Interpretation:** The probe's learned direction remains a correlational predictor; the exact causal mechanisms by which steering residual activations improves calibration are not definitively isolated from other entangled task representations.

## Confidence
- **In-Distribution Performance (Accuracy & ECE):** High. Supported by consistent empirical results across three models and two training sets, and strong ablations against sparse methods.
- **Zero-Shot Transferability:** Medium. Cross-benchmark transfer results are compelling but rely on a single source training set; observed degradation on dissimilar tasks indicates conditional generality.
- **Mechanism: Distributed Signals & Regularization:** Medium. Evidence from SAE ablations is strong for the specific models and MCQA tasks studied, but this is an empirical observation, not a universal proof.
- **Mathematical Alignment (Residual Target & Brier Score):** High. The derivation is sound, and concurrent improvements in Brier, ECE, and accuracy empirically validate the residual formulation in the experimental setting.
