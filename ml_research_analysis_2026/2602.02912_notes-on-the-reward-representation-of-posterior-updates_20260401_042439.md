# Notes on the Reward Representation of Posterior Updates

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02912
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02912
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** Under posterior identification (optimizer equals Bayes posterior) and order-independence (single event-based continuation value), KL-regularized updates correspond exactly to Bayesian posteriors, with reward-value interactions equal to conditional pointwise mutual information (PMI), absolute rewards ambiguous up to context-specific baselines, and a commutativity constraint linking reward parametrizations across conditioning orders.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates when a KL-regularized update mechanism produces the same posterior as a Bayesian update within a fixed probabilistic model. Two structural assumptions are central: posterior identification, which equates the optimizer of the KL-regularized objective to a Bayes posterior from a single ambient joint distribution; and order-independence, which requires the continuation value function to depend only on the information event, not the update direction. Under these, the authors show that the interaction between reward and value must equal the conditional pointwise mutual information scaled by the inverse temperature parameter, that only relative rewards within a context are identifiable, and that a commutativity constraint couples the reward functions for different conditioning orders. This clarifies the precise conditions under which KL-regularization mimics Bayesian reasoning and exposes inherent gauge freedoms in reward representation.

## Method Summary
The analysis is theoretical and algebraic. Starting from a fixed joint distribution \(P(x,y,z)\) and a KL-regularized objective \(J_z\) parameterized by a reward \(r_z(x|y)\) and a value function \(V\), the authors first impose posterior identification: the optimizer \(\arg\max J_z\) equals the Bayes posterior \(P(x|y,z)\). This forces the exponent in the soft update to match the log-ratio \( \log \frac{P(x|y,z)}{P(x|y)} \), which is the conditional pointwise mutual information \(i(x;z|y)\). The decomposition of this PMI-shaped term into separate \(r_z\) and \(V\) components is shown to have a gauge freedom: adding any context-specific baseline \(c(y,z)\) to both \(r_z(x|y)\) and \(V(y,z)\) leaves the optimizer unchanged. Then, by considering a second update direction (conditioning \(z\) on \(y,x\)) and requiring the same \(V\) to be reused (order-independence), the symmetry of PMI yields a commutativity constraint: \(r_z(x|y) - V(y,z) = r_x(z|y) - V(x,y)\). The results are illustrated with discrete joint distributions where all conditionals are positive and the log-partition is finite.

## Key Results
- The reward-value interaction in the KL-regularized objective must equal the conditional pointwise mutual information \(i(x;z|y)\) scaled by \(1/\alpha\): \(\alpha[r_z(x|y) + V(x,y,z) - V(y,z)] = i(x;z|y)\).
- Absolute reward magnitudes are ambiguous; only the relative incentive \(r_z(x|y) + V(x,y,z)\) within each context \((y,z)\) is determined by the posterior. Shifting \(r_z\) and \(V(y,z)\) by a context-specific baseline \(c(y,z)\) leaves the optimizer unchanged.
- Order-independence (permutation-invariant \(V\)) imposes a commutativity constraint: \(r_z(x|y) - V(y,z) = r_x(z|y) - V(x,y)\), linking the reward parametrizations for conditioning on \(x\) versus \(z\) given the same prior \(y\).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: PMI-Shape Identity from Posterior Identification
Posterior identification forces the optimizer of the KL-regularized objective to match the Bayes posterior \(P(x|y,z)\). Taking the log-ratio of this posterior against the baseline \(P(x|y)\) yields equation (6), which by definition equals the conditional pointwise mutual information \(i(x;z|y)\). Substituting the exponential-tilt form of the optimizer (equation 3) then gives \(\alpha[r_z(x|y) + V(x,y,z) - V(y,z)] = i(x;z|y)\) (equation 7). This derivation appears in [section 5] and is summarized in the [abstract]. The identity holds pointwise for all \((x,y,z)\) under the assumption that the ambient joint \(P\) is fixed and the optimizer is exactly the Bayesian posterior. If posterior identification fails (e.g., the KL optimizer differs from any Bayes posterior), the interaction term need not equal PMI.

### Mechanism 2: Gauge Freedom in Absolute Rewards
The posterior \(P(x|y,z)\) depends only on differences in the exponent \(r_z(x|y) + V(x,y,z)\) across \(x\) within a fixed \((y,z)\). Adding any function \(c(y,z)\) that does not depend on \(x\) to both \(r_z(x|y)\) and \(V(y,z)\) cancels out in the softmax normalization (equation 8), leaving the optimizer unchanged. This is stated in [section 5] and the [abstract]. Thus, posterior identification determines only the relative incentive signal; absolute reward levels are ambiguous without an external baseline convention (e.g., setting \(r_z(x_0|y)=0\) for a reference \(x_0\)). The gauge freedom is a direct consequence of the softmax's invariance to uniform shifts.

### Mechanism 3: Commutativity Constraint from Order-Independence
Order-independence requires that the continuation value function \(V\) depends only on the information event (the set of known variables) and not on the sequence of updates. Applying posterior identification to both update directions—computing \(P(x|y,z)\) and \(P(z|y,x)\)—and using the fact that conditional PMI is symmetric, \(i(x;z|y)=i(z;x|y)\), one finds that the PMI-shaped interaction terms must match. With permutation-invariant \(V\) (i.e., \(V(x,y,z)=V(z,y,x)\)), rearranging yields \(r_z(x|y) - V(y,z) = r_x(z|y) - V(x,y)\) (equation 13). This derivation is in [section 7]. The constraint couples the reward parametrizations for the two conditioning orders. If \(V\) is allowed to depend on order (e.g., \(V(y,z) \neq V(y,x)\)), the constraint is not required and the reward families become independent.

## Foundational Learning
- **KL-regularized optimization (free-energy principle)**  
  *Why needed:* Defines the soft update mechanism (equation 2) whose optimizer is analyzed. The exponential-tilt form (equation 3) and log-partition value (equation 4) are the starting algebraic structure.  
  *Quick check:* Given \(J_z(\tilde{P}) = \sum_x \tilde{P}(x|y)[r_z(x|y) - \frac{1}{\alpha}\log\frac{\tilde{P}(x|y)}{P(x|y)} + V(x,y,z)]\), what is the form of the optimizer \(\tilde{P}^*\) and how does it depend on \(\alpha\)?

- **Conditional pointwise mutual information (PMI)**  
  *Why needed:* Under posterior identification, the reward-value interaction is forced to equal \(i(x;z|y)\) (equation 7). PMI quantifies the pointwise evidence that \(z\) provides about \(x\) given \(y\).  
  *Quick check:* If \(P(x|y,z) = P(x|y) \exp(i(x;z|y)) / \text{normalizer}\), what must be true about \(\sum_x P(x|y)\exp(i(x;z|y))\)?

- **Posterior identification as a modeling assumption**  
  *Why needed:* It is the key non-generic constraint linking the KL-regularized optimizer to a specific ambient joint distribution \(P\). Without it, the optimizer need not resemble a Bayes posterior.  
  *Quick check:* Is posterior identification a generic property of KL-regularization? If not, what does it require beyond the objective form (2)?

## Architecture Onboarding
Ambient joint P -> KL objective J_z -> optimizer \tilde{P}^*(x|y). Rewards r_z(x|y) and values V(x,y,z), V(y,z) parameterize the objective. Assumptions: Posterior identification (A1) equates optimizer to P(x|y,z); Order-independence (A2) requires V permutation-invariant.

Critical path:
1. Define ambient joint P and KL objective (2) with unknown r, V.
2. Impose A1: equate optimizer to P(x|y,z) → derive PMI-shape identity (7).
3. Recognize gauge freedom (8) in splitting interaction into r and V.
4. Impose A2 for second update direction P(z|y,x) → derive commutativity (13) linking r_z and r_x.

Design tradeoffs:
- Choice of conditioning order: You may specify r_z and V for one direction (e.g., updating x given y,z), but then A1 and A2 fix compatible r_x and V(z,y,x) via (13) and symmetry.
- Baseline convention: To assign absolute reward meaning, you must choose a gauge (e.g., set r_z(x|y)=0 for a reference x in each (y,z)). Different conventions yield different r but same posteriors.

Failure signatures:
- Non-PMI interaction: If log[P(x|y,z)/P(x|y)] ≠ α[r_z(x|y) + V(x,y,z) - V(y,z)] pointwise, posterior identification fails.
- Violated commutativity: After fitting r_z, r_x from two directional updates, if r_z(x|y) - V(y,z) ≠ r_x(z|y) - V(x,y), then either A2 is violated or the ambient P is inconsistent.
- Gauge confusion: Interpreting r_z(x|y) as absolute reward without fixing a baseline leads to non-identifiable parameters; ensure baseline shifts are context-only (independent of x).

First 3 experiments:
1. **PMI-shape validation:** For a known discrete P, compute i(x;z|y). Choose any V and define r_z(x|y) = (1/α)i(x;z|y) - V(x,y,z) + V(y,z) + c(y,z). Verify that maximizing J_z recovers P(x|y,z) exactly.
2. **Gauge invariance check:** Apply transformation (8) with a random c(y,z). Confirm that the optimizer P(x|y,z) remains unchanged.
3. **Commutativity test:** Using same P, compute both i(x;z|y) and i(z;x|y) (equal). Choose symmetric V. Set r_z(x|y) = (1/α)i(x;z|y) - V(x,y,z) + V(y,z) and r_x(z|y) = (1/α)i(z;x|y) - V(z,y,x) + V(y,x). Verify equation (13) holds identically.

## Limitations
- The analysis hinges on posterior identification (A1), assuming the KL-regularized optimizer exactly equals a Bayes posterior under a single fixed joint distribution; this is a strong assumption that may not hold under misspecification.
- Order-independence (A2) requires the continuation value function V to be permutation-invariant; in many sequential or causal settings, the value of information can depend on acquisition order, limiting applicability.
- Results assume finite log-partition values and positive probabilities for all conditioning events—conditions that may be non-trivial in continuous or high-dimensional spaces.
- The paper provides no empirical validation; the theory remains untested on real data.

## Confidence
- PMI-shape identity (Eq. 7) under A1: High (direct algebraic derivation).
- Gauge ambiguity of absolute rewards (Eq. 8): High (follows from softmax normalization).
- Commutativity constraint (Eq. 13) under A2: Medium (relies on PMI symmetry and V permutation-invariance; adds a substantive behavioral assumption).
- Generality to continuous spaces / nonparametric models: Low (not addressed; measurability issues may arise).

## Next Checks
1. Exact analytic verification: For a small discrete joint distribution (e.g., uniform over four outcomes), compute PMI, choose V and r_z satisfying the gauge, and confirm that the KL optimizer matches P(X|Y,Z) and Eq. 13 holds for both directions.
2. Commutativity stress test: Construct an order-dependent V (e.g., V(y,z) ≠ V(y,x) when {x,z} swap) and verify that posterior identification can still be satisfied but Eq. 13 fails.
3. Normalization sanity check: For a family of distributions approaching zero-probability events, confirm that the log-partition Z(y,z) remains finite under chosen r and V; if not, identify conditions (e.g., bounded rewards) to guarantee well-definedness.
