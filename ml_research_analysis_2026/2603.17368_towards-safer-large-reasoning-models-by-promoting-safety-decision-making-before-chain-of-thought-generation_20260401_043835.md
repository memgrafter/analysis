# Towards Safer Large Reasoning Models by Promoting Safety Decision-Making before Chain-of-Thought Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17368
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17368
- **Reference count:** 34
- **Primary result:** PreSafe reduces attack success rates to near-zero (0.0–6.1%) on multiple jailbreak benchmarks while preserving reasoning performance on AIME24 and MATH-500, substantially outperforming defenses like SafeChain and R2D.

## Executive Summary
Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) exhibit a severe safety degradation when chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning is enabled, a problem that existing safety alignment methods exacerbate by harming reasoning capability. This paper reveals that safety decline occurs only after CoT activation, not when CoT is disabled. To address this, PreSafe introduces a two-phase approach: first, a BERT classifier extracts a safety decision signal from a CoT-disabled teacher model; second, during alignment, an auxiliary linear head aligns the student LRM’s pre-CoT latent representations with this signal. This reshapes the model’s internal state to refuse harmful queries before reasoning begins, without sacrificing task performance. Evaluations on DeepSeek, Qwen, and Skywork models confirm near-zero attack success rates and maintained reasoning scores, achieving a superior safety-reasoning trade-off.

## Method Summary
PreSafe begins by training a BERT-based classifier on binary safety decisions (refuse/comply) generated by a CoT-disabled teacher model. This classifier serves as a source of distilled safety knowledge. For the student LRM, an auxiliary linear head is attached to the model’s last-layer hidden states *before* CoT generation starts. During training, the head outputs a refusal probability that is aligned with the BERT classifier’s probability using binary cross-entropy loss (L_align). This is combined with standard supervised fine-tuning loss (L_task) on task responses (refusals for harmful queries, step-by-step answers for benign ones). The total loss (L_task + λ_align·L_align) updates both the LRM’s backbone (via LoRA) and the auxiliary head. After training, the auxiliary head and classifier are discarded; the fine-tuned LRM is deployed with CoT enabled and is expected to refuse harmful inputs at the pre-reasoning stage.

## Key Results
- Near-zero attack success rates across multiple benchmarks: 0.0–3.7% on PAIR, 0.0–6.1% on GCG, 0.0–3.0% on JailbreakChat.
- Reasoning capability preserved: pass@1 scores on AIME24 and MATH-500 remain comparable to original, unaligned models.
- Outperforms existing defenses (SafeChain, R2D) by a large margin in the safety-reasoning trade-off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: CoT Activation Suppresses Intrinsic Safety
The safety degradation in LRMs is causally linked to enabling CoT generation. When CoT is turned off, models retain a latent ability to refuse harmful queries; this ability is suppressed once multi-step reasoning begins. Evidence: Across DeepSeek, Qwen, and Skywork families, CoT-OFF models show dramatically lower attack success rates on WildJailbreak than CoT-ON models (Figure 1). The core assumption is that the pre-CoT processing pathway preserves a safety decision capability that is disrupted by the reasoning process.

### Mechanism 2: Extracting a Transferable Safety Signal
A CoT-disabled teacher model (e.g., a stronger LLM or the same model’s own CoT-OFF state) provides reliable binary safety decisions. Training a BERT classifier on these decisions creates a parameterized safety oracle that generalizes beyond the exact training queries. The classifier learns a context-dependent safety heuristic (e.g., intent classification) that can be applied to new inputs. Evidence: The trained classifier achieves high accuracy on held-out teacher decisions; using this signal proves far more effective than direct token distribution distillation (Section 4.6 ablation).

### Mechanism 3: Aligning Early Latent Representations
The auxiliary linear head forces the student LRM’s pre-CoT hidden states to encode the same safety decision as the BERT classifier. Minimizing the binary cross-entropy between the head’s prediction and the classifier’s probability backpropagates safety gradients into the backbone, encouraging earlier layers to develop safety-relevant features. Crucially, because alignment targets representations *before* CoT starts, the subsequent reasoning process remains undisturbed. Evidence: Layer-wise analysis (Figure 5) shows updates concentrated in `gate_proj` and `up_proj` of deeper MLP blocks—specific pathways for feature gating—rather than widespread disruption. Combined loss (L_task + λ_align·L_align) preserves reasoning performance as measured by AIME24/MATH-500.

## Foundational Learning
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Generation**: Why needed: PreSafe intervenes at the moment *before* CoT begins; understanding that CoT itself degrades safety is key. Quick check: If a model’s safety drops only when it reasons step-by-step, where should a safety mechanism be injected?
- **Safety Alignment (SFT, RLHF)**: Why needed: PreSafe is an alignment method; knowing the typical pitfall—alignment hurting reasoning utility—clarifies the innovation. Quick check: Why might standard supervised fine-tuning on refusal examples damage an LRM’s reasoning ability?
- **Latent Representations in Transformers**: Why needed: PreSafe shapes the hidden state *before* token generation; these states seed all future reasoning. Quick check: What location in the forward pass does the auxiliary head attach to, and why that point?
- **Knowledge Distillation & Cross-Entropy Loss**: Why needed: The alignment loss is a distillation where student representations match a teacher’s safety probability. Quick check: How does minimizing BCE between predicted and target safety probabilities affect the LRM’s backbone parameters?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Teacher Model (CoT-OFF) → BERT Classifier (J_ψ) → Student LRM (R_θ) with Auxiliary Linear Head (H_ϕ) during training → Deployed R_θ (without H_ϕ)
- **Critical path**:
  1. Extract safety decisions from teacher on query set D_cls; train J_ψ to convergence.
  2. For each training query x: compute safety target p'=J_ψ(x); forward R_θ to get pre-CoT representation z_θ(x); compute student prediction p=H_ϕ(z_θ(x)); calculate L_align(p,p') and L_task; total loss L_PreSafe = L_task + λ_align·L_align; backpropagate to update R_θ (via LoRA) and H_ϕ.
  3. Discard H_ϕ and J_ψ; deploy fine-tuned R_θ with CoT enabled.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Teacher choice: Self-distillation (model’s own CoT-OFF) is convenient but may have imperfect safety; external stronger teacher (e.g., DeepSeek-V3) gives cleaner signal but risks distribution mismatch.
  - Classifier vs. direct distillation: Using a BERT classifier to extract a *decision* signal outperforms distilling the teacher’s next-token distribution (L_KD) by a large margin (Section 4.6), at the cost of added complexity.
  - Linear head necessity: Ablation (λ_align=0, no H_ϕ) yields no safety gain (Table 3), proving the head is essential for shaping latent space.
  - λ_align tuning: Higher λ_align strengthens safety but risks over-refusal; paper uses 0.5 after empirical sweep.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Safety failure: High ASR (>30%) on WildJailbreak post-training indicates alignment failed.
  - Reasoning failure: >5% absolute drop on AIME24/MATH-500 suggests destructive interference in latent space.
  - Over-refusal: High benign refusal rate (>20%) on Alpaca with high harmful refusal indicates coarse safety signal.
  - Training instability: L_align divergence may signal scaling mismatch between losses.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **CoT-ON vs. CoT-OFF baseline**: Evaluate a base LRM (e.g., DS-R1-7B) on 100 WildJailbreak prompts with CoT enabled vs. disabled; expect ASR(CoT-OFF) << ASR(CoT-ON).
  2. **Classifier verification**: Train J_ψ on teacher decisions; achieve >95% accuracy on held-out set.
  3. **PreSafe sanity check**: Fine-tune on small mixed dataset (100 benign, 100 harmful); monitor L_align decrease, ASR drop on 50 held-out harmful queries, and stable pass@1 on 20 MATH-500 problems.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can PreSafe’s residual benign over-refusal be reduced without compromising harmful query refusal? The paper notes this as an important future direction (Section 4.5).
2. What specific safety-relevant features does the BERT classifier learn that are missing in direct output distribution distillation? The ablation shows its superiority, but internal logic is unanalyzed.
3. Why does optimization concentrate on `gate_proj` and `up_proj` layers (Figure 5)? What does this imply about how safety decisions are implemented architecturally?
4. How dependent is PreSafe on the teacher’s safety quality? What if the CoT-disabled teacher itself is vulnerable to jailbreaks? The paper uses self-distillation but does not study teacher robustness.
5. To what extent does PreSafe generalize to novel, out-of-distribution jailbreak attacks (e.g., multi-turn, multilingual, tool-use exploits)? Evaluation covers static benchmarks only.

## Limitations
- Teacher model’s CoT-OFF safety must be high; any bias in the teacher propagates to the BERT signal and then to the student.
- The BERT classifier may not generalize across diverse phrasing distributions, leading to over-refusal or under-refusal on edge cases.
- The linear alignment assumption—that early latent space is linearly separable—may not hold for deep models, risking reasoning degradation at scale.
- Evaluation is limited to three model families and standard jailbreak suites; real-world attack diversity and multilingual scenarios remain untested.
- Sensitivity to λ_align and resource overhead from training the classifier and auxiliary head are not thoroughly analyzed.

## Confidence
- **CoT-induced safety degradation**: High (consistently observed across models).
- **Extractable safety signal via BERT**: Medium (strong held-out accuracy, but external robustness untested).
- **PreSafe alignment preserves reasoning while improving safety**: Medium (results on AIME24/MATH-500 stable, but may not generalize to all reasoning tasks or larger scales).
- **Superiority over SafeChain/R2D**: Medium (benchmark comparisons favorable, but broader defense landscape not fully covered).

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the CoT-ON vs. CoT-OFF safety gap on a new LRM (e.g., Claude-3.5-Sonnet with CoT) using 100 WildJailbreak prompts; expect large ASR difference.
2. Conduct a human audit of teacher labels: sample 200 queries from D_cls, have annotators judge safety, compute agreement with teacher’s binary labels; target >95% to validate signal quality.
3. Perform auxiliary head ablation: train PreSafe with λ_align=0 and without H_ϕ; confirm no safety improvement while reasoning performance remains baseline, verifying the head’s necessity.
