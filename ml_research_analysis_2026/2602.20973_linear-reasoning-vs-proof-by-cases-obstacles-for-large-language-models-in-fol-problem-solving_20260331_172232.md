# Linear Reasoning vs. Proof by Cases: Obstacles for Large Language Models in FOL Problem Solving

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20973  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20973  
- **Reference count:** 29  
- **Primary result:** State-of-the-art LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) achieve high accuracy on linear FOL reasoning (85.13%) but struggle substantially with proof-by-cases (51.08%), a gap attributed to case identification errors and longer reasoning steps.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces PC-FOL, a manually curated dataset of 2,044 first-order logic problems split evenly between linear reasoning and proof-by-cases. Evaluation of leading LLMs reveals a large and consistent performance gap: models excel on linear chains but fail on case-based proofs. Theoretical analysis using a graphical model attributes this disparity to two factors: a lower probability of correctly identifying all necessary cases and an exponential decay from increased reasoning steps. Manual error analysis confirms that misapplication of disjunctive statements is the predominant failure mode for case-based proofs.

## Method Summary
The authors construct PC-FOL by collecting natural language FOL stories, manually writing expert proofs, and annotating each problem as linear or proof-by-cases. To mitigate memorization, they create PC-FOL-Replace with lexical substitution (nouns replaced by random tokens). They evaluate multiple LLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, o4-mini, Llama-3-70B, Deepseek-V3, Qwen3 variants) in zero-shot and few-shot settings, measuring accuracy (truth value), ROUGE (proof similarity), and pass@k (proof correctness via GPT-4o judge). A probabilistic graphical model formalizes reasoning as a sequence of steps, deriving formulas for success probabilities in linear vs. case-based proofs. The model's predictions are compared against empirical results, and a subset of model outputs are manually analyzed to categorize errors.

## Key Results
- Large performance gap across models: GPT-4o achieves 85.13% accuracy on linear problems but only 51.08% on proof-by-cases (0-shot, PC-FOL).
- Error analysis: Misapplication of disjunctive statements accounts for the majority of failures in case-based proofs (>45% of incorrect proofs).
- Theoretical model: Case-based success probability factors into probability of correct case enumeration (pF,cases) and per-step accuracy raised to the total number of steps (p^kF), explaining the steep drop.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LLMs exhibit a large performance gap between linear-reasoning and proof-by-cases FOL problems.
- **Mechanism:** The PC-FOL dataset provides a clean, balanced split of problem types, revealing that state-of-the-art LLMs (e.g., GPT‑4o) achieve high accuracy on linear chains (85.13%) but struggle with case‑based proofs (51.08%). The gap persists across models and prompting regimes.
- **Core assumption:** The dataset’s manual annotation and lexical substitution successfully isolate the reasoning difficulty rather than surface memorization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: “evaluation of leading LLMs reveals a substantial and consistent performance gap: for instance, GPT‑4o achieves 85.13% accuracy on linear problems but only 51.08% on proof‑by‑cases.”
  - [Section 5]: Table 5 and surrounding text show the gap for all tested models.
  - [corpus]: Neighbor papers (e.g., “Towards Advanced Mathematical Reasoning for LLMs via First‑Order Logic Theorem Proving”) also note difficulties in complex FOL, supporting the observed gap.
- **Break condition:** If models are extensively fine‑tuned on proof‑by‑cases data, the gap narrows (fine‑tuning results in Section G show mixed gains).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The performance drop is explained by two factors: (i) lower probability of identifying all required cases (pF,cases) and (ii) exponential decay from longer reasoning steps (p^kF).
- **Mechanism:** A probabilistic graphical model abstracts each proposition as a node; the joint probability factorizes into step‑wise conditional probabilities. For linear proofs the success probability is p^kF, where pF is the per‑step accuracy. For case‑based proofs, the product includes pF,cases (the chance to enumerate all cases correctly) and an increased total step count, causing a steeper drop.
- **Core assumption:** The per‑step accuracy pF is constant and independent of context; the model’s ability to select the next correct proposition does not vary with the premises or previous steps.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section 6]: Theorem 6.2 formalizes the case‑based probability as pF,cases·p^(∑|steps|) and states that “two primary factors leading to LLMs’ errors: incorrect selection of cases and an excessive number of steps.”
  - [Section 5.4]: Manual analysis identifies “misapplication of disjunctive statements” as the predominant error, directly linking to pF,cases failure.
  - [corpus]: Related work on FOL datasets (e.g., FOLIO, P‑FOLIO) lacks case‑based annotation, which the paper argues is essential to capture this factor.
- **Break condition:** If pF is not constant across contexts (e.g., varies with problem complexity), the model’s step‑wise degradation might be less severe than predicted; likewise disjunction handling improvements could raise pF,cases.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The most frequent concrete error in case‑based proofs is mishandling of disjunctive premises (misinterpreting “or”, failing to treat cases separately).
- **Mechanism:** Proof‑by‑cases requires splitting on disjunctive statements, deriving independent chains, and combining results. LLMs often treat disjunctions as conjunctions or skip case separation, leading to invalid proofs.
- **Core assumption:** The manual categorization of errors (four main reasons) is accurate and the sample of GPT‑4o outputs is representative.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section 5.4]: Table 8 shows 45.79% “Wrong Label with Wrong Proof” for case‑based problems; the text states “the predominant cause of incorrect proof is the misapplication of disjunctive statements.”
  - [Section 3.3]: Example right side of Table 2 illustrates the correct case‑based reasoning; contrast with typical model failures.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors (e.g., “Diagnosis via Proofs of Unsatisfiability for First‑Order Logic with Relational Objects”) discuss disjunction challenges in logic, aligning with the paper’s finding.
- **Break condition:** With targeted training on disjunction patterns (e.g., augmenting data with explicit case splits), this error source could diminish, narrowing the gap.

## Foundational Learning
- **First‑Order Logic (FOL):** Formal language with quantifiers and predicates. *Why needed:* All problems are expressed in natural language but require FOL reasoning. *Quick check:* Translate “All birds can fly; Tweety is a bird; therefore Tweety can fly” into FOL notation.
- **Proof by Cases:** A proof technique that splits on a disjunction and shows the conclusion holds in each case. *Why needed:* The core contrast in the paper is linear vs. case‑based reasoning. *Quick check:* For “A or B; if A then C; if B then C; therefore C,” outline the case analysis.
- **Probabilistic Graphical Model:** A graph encoding conditional dependencies among random variables. *Why needed:* The theoretical framework uses such models to formalize step‑wise generation. *Quick check:* Write the factorization for variables X→Y→Z.
- **Lexical Substitution:** Replacing content words with nonsensical tokens to prevent memorization. *Why needed:* Used in PC‑FOL‑Replace to ensure models reason rather than recall. *Quick check:* If “cat” → “xzy”, does the logical structure remain identical?
- **Evaluation Metrics (Accuracy, ROUGE, pass@k):** Measures for truth‑value correctness and proof similarity. *Why needed:* Quantify model performance on both outcomes and reasoning processes. *Quick check:* Explain why pass@k may be more lenient than exact match.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data pipeline -> LLM interface -> Automated evaluation -> Manual verification -> Theoretical analyzer
- **Critical path:** Data collection (B.1) -> annotation & quality control (B.2) -> experiment runs (E.2) -> automated metrics -> manual error analysis (5.4) -> theoretical modeling (6)
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Dataset size vs. annotation cost: PC‑FOL is modest (2k instances) but manually verified; larger auto‑generated datasets risk logical errors.
  - Lexical substitution vs. naturalness: Substitution prevents memorization but may harm linguistic coherence and model understanding.
  - Graphical model simplicity: Assumes constant per‑step accuracy; more realistic context‑dependent p would complicate analysis but might better fit data.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Consistent >30% accuracy gap between linear and case‑based sets (as in Table 5).
  - Low pass@k (≈46% at k=1 for cases) indicating few correct proofs.
  - Manual analysis shows high rates of disjunction‑related errors (>45% of case failures).
  - Fine‑tuning on mixed data sometimes reduces case performance (Table 12).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Why do LLMs exhibit a consistent and substantial accuracy gap between linear-reasoning and proof-by-cases FOL problems, and to what extent is this gap attributable to case identification errors versus increased reasoning steps?
- Basis: [explicit] The paper demonstrates the gap (e.g., GPT-4o: 85.13% vs. 51.08%) and theoretical analysis (Theorems 6.2/6.3) attributes it to two factors: lower pF,cases (probability of correctly identifying all necessary cases) and exponential decay from more steps (pkF). Manual analysis identifies misapplication of disjunctive statements as the predominant error.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies correlates but does not establish a causal breakdown or quantify each factor's contribution across diverse problems. It remains unclear whether improving case identification or step efficiency would yield greater gains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments where LLMs are given explicitly enumerated cases (isolating case identification) versus problems with streamlined case chains (isolating step efficiency). Error analysis correlating specific error types (case omission, subcase contradiction handling) with failure rates.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the theoretical assumption of uniform per-step accuracy (pF) across all reasoning contexts hold for proof-by-cases problems, and how does pF actually vary with case complexity, premise structure, or disjunctive statement handling?
- Basis: [inferred] The graphical model (Section 6) assumes a constant pF for any augmented context, simplifying analysis but likely unrealistic for case-based reasoning where cases introduce divergent logical pathways and potential contradictions.
- Why unresolved: The paper calculates a single pF (e.g., 0.9262 for GPT-4o on linear problems) but does not test its stability across case-based instances. The model's inability to generalize pF to non-linear structures is a key limitation of the theory.
- What evidence would resolve it: Fine-grained evaluation measuring LLM accuracy on individual reasoning steps within each case of proof-by-cases problems, then modeling pF as a function of case count, premise interdependence, or disjunction type (inclusive/exclusive).

### Open Question 3
- Question: To what extent does the limited scale and lexical diversity of PC-FOL (compared to pretraining corpora) constrain the observed performance gap, and would larger, more diverse case-based datasets reduce the gap?
- Basis: [inferred] The paper notes PC-FOL's small size (2044 instances) and uses lexical substitution to test memorization (Section 5.2), finding only marginal performance drops. However, it does not address whether scaling dataset size could close the gap by improving case-based pattern recognition.
- Why unresolved: The lexical substitution experiment suggests memorization is not the primary issue, but dataset scale may still affect the model's exposure to varied case structures and disjunction patterns. The gap's persistence on a small, curated dataset may not reflect potential with massive case-based data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Curate or synthetically generate a much larger proof-by-cases FOL dataset (e.g., 10×–100× PC-FOL) with diverse logical forms, then retest LLMs to see if the accuracy gap narrows while controlling for premise count and complexity.

## Limitations
- Missing step-length distribution PD(|X|=k) blocks theoretical validation of the graphical model.
- pF,cases estimation method unspecified; how case identification is measured from outputs is unclear.
- GPT-4o judge for proof correctness may introduce bias; no independent human gold standard.

## Confidence
- **Performance gap exists:** High. Consistently observed across models and prompts (Table 5); supported by neighboring literature.
- **Two-factor theoretical explanation:** Medium. Graphical model is plausible but relies on missing data (step distribution) and constant pF; partial empirical fit.
- **Disjunction mishandling as primary error:** Medium. Manual analysis points to this, yet sample size and classification subjectivity limit certainty.
