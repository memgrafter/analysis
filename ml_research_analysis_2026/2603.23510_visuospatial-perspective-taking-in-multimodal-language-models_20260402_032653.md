# Visuospatial Perspective Taking in Multimodal Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23510
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23510
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Multimodal language models exhibit consistent deficits in Level 2 visuospatial perspective taking, performing well only at fully shared (0°) or opposite (180°) orientations but failing at intermediate rotations, and showing severe impairments when visual and spatial perspective demands combine.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates the ability of multimodal language models (MLMs) to perform visuospatial perspective taking (VPT)—a core socio-cognitive skill for understanding others’ views—using two adapted human tasks: the Rotating Figure Task (varying angular disparity) and the Director Task (referential disambiguation under occlusion). Testing four state-of-the-art models (GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, o4-mini, o3) with systematically generated stimuli reveals a robust failure pattern: models struggle with Level 2 VPT, which requires inhibiting their own egocentric perspective and mentally transforming spatial relations. Performance follows U- or M-shaped curves across angular disparity, with pronounced drops at intermediate angles, and declines further when visual and spatial transformation demands are combined. These findings expose fundamental limitations in MLMs’ capacity for collaborative reasoning in real-world settings.

## Method Summary
The authors adapted two classic cognitive tasks to evaluate MLMs. The Rotating Figure Task presents an arrowed figure with a symbol on one side; the model must report the symbol’s location from a different viewing angle (varied from 0° to 359°). The Director Task uses a grid of objects where one is occluded from a “director’s” view; the model must interpret instructions like “the star on the left” based on the director’s perspective, requiring both visual (tracking occlusion) and spatial (left–right reversal) transformations. Models were tested on multiple variants of each task, separating Level 1 (visibility only) from Level 2 (transformation) demands, and included control trials to validate basic perception. Stimuli were generated algorithmically to ensure systematic variation and avoid contamination from training data.

## Key Results
- **Rotating Figure Task:** All tested models show U- or M-shaped accuracy profiles across angular disparity, exciting only at fully aligned (0°) or opposite (180°) orientations; integrated visual-spatial Level 2 VPT drops to near-chance (~25%) at maximum disparity.
- **Director Task:** Both visual (occlusion tracking) and spatial (left–right reversal) perspective-taking reduce accuracy, with catastrophic failure when both are required simultaneously; models often answer from their own egocentric view.
- **Model variability:** Reasoning-optimized models (o3, o4-mini) exhibit M-shaped curves, while GPT-4o variants show mixed patterns, suggesting differing heuristic reliance but no model achieves human-like monotonic performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Shallow heuristic reliance without continuous mental rotation**
Multimodal language models fail at intermediate angular disparities (45°–135°) because they do not construct a continuous, incremental transformation of the visuospatial scene. Instead, they rely on brittle pattern-matching heuristics acquired from training data—such as inverting left/right when a figure faces the viewer—that only succeed at discretized endpoints (0° and 180°). This yields the characteristic U‑ or M‑shaped accuracy profile. The core assumption is that training on static scenes and text prevents learning of analog spatial transformations; models compress angular disparity into coarse “shared vs. opposite” buckets. Evidence comes directly from the disparity sweeps showing non‑monotonic performance, and the near‑chance results at intermediate angles. A break condition would be a monotonic accuracy increase across all angles, indicating a dose–response mental rotation.  

**Mechanism 2: Egocentric inhibition failure in Level 2 VPT**
Level 2 perspective taking requires suppressing one’s own visual input to construct an allocentric representation. MLMs exhibit a default egocentric bias that becomes insurmountable when spatial judgments demand reversal (e.g., mapping “right” from another’s frame). The cognitive operation of decoupling self from other is not supported by current architectures, likely because they lack a dedicated module analogous to the human right TPJ. Evidence: consistent deficits in Level 2 VPT across tasks, and strong convergent findings from neighboring papers explicitly documenting egocentric bias in vision‑language models. A break condition is equal performance on Level 2 and Level 1 trials when difficulty is controlled, suggesting successful suppression of egocentric interference.  

**Mechanism 3: Partial dissociation of visual and spatial processing streams**
The visual (symbol appearance) and spatial (left/right relations) components of VPT appear to engage separable pathways in MLMs, leading to modality‑specific failure patterns that differ across model families. For instance, GPT‑4o shows a monotonic spatial decline but improved visual accuracy at 180°, while o3 remains robust spatially but falters visually. The underlying assumption is that the multimodal fusion does not integrate these streams into a unified perspective space; instead, they are treated as independent queries, preventing coherent transformation. Evidence comes from the Task 3 analysis separating visual and spatial questions and finding divergent profiles. A break condition is equivalent performance on visual and spatial VPT probes at matched angular disparities, implying a unified transformation mechanism.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Level 1 vs. Level 2 VPT**  
*Why needed here:* The paper’s contrast between simple visibility (Level 1) and appearance/spatial transformation (Level 2) explains why models fail at higher‑level perspective taking.  
*Quick check question:* Does knowing “what is visible to another person” require mentally rotating the scene? (No; that is Level 1. Level 2 requires rotation.)

**Concept: Egocentric vs. Allocentric Frames**  
*Why needed here:* All VPT tasks require shifting from self‑centered (egocentric) to other‑centered (allocentric) coordinates; models’ failures are framed as inability to inhibit egocentric representations.  
*Quick check question:* If you stand opposite someone, their “right” is your “left.” Which frame is your own default? (Egocentric.)

**Concept: Referential Disambiguation in Communication**  
*Why needed here:* The Director Task situates VPT within a communicative act where the partner’s occluded view must be taken into account to correctly interpret spatial terms like “left” or “the visible star.”  
*Quick check question:* Under what condition does a speaker say “the blue one on the left” but mean an item that is on the listener’s right? (When speaker and listener face opposite directions, and speaker refers to their own left—thus requiring listener to adopt speaker’s spatial perspective.)

**Concept: Angular Disparity as Continuous Manipulation**  
*Why needed here:* The Rotating Figure Task varies orientation continuously from 0° to 359°, allowing precise mapping of performance; U‑/M‑shapes reveal discrete heuristics versus continuous mental rotation.  
*Quick check question:* How does human reaction time typically change with increasing angular disparity in Level 2 VPT? (It increases monotonically, indicating continuous transformation.)

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Image → Visual encoder → Cross-modal tokens → LLM processing (prompt + tokens) → Reasoning steps (think() calls) → Decision → submit(answer)

**Critical path:**  
The model receives an image (grid or figure scene) and a textual query. The visual encoder extracts patch or region embeddings. These are merged with text tokens into a multimodal sequence processed by the LLM core. During generation, the ReAct‑style `think()` step may be used for chain‑of‑thought reasoning. The final hidden state triggers an output head that produces a short answer (symbol, LEFT/RIGHT, coordinate). VPT failure occurs within LLM processing, where the model must simultaneously represent its own view and the target perspective.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **ReAct vs. direct generation:** Allowing intermediate `think()` steps does not guarantee robust transformation; it may facilitate heuristic articulation (e.g., “swap left and right when figure faces me”).  
- **Vision–language fusion:** The model’s limited ability to extract precise viewing direction (Control 2) suggests the visual encoder may not encode 3D orientation adequately; deeper visual reasoning layers might be needed.  
- **Parameter scale vs. reasoning effort:** Reasoning‑optimized models (o3, o4‑mini) show distinct profiles (M‑shape) compared to GPT‑4o variants, but increased inference‑time search can expose heuristics without fixing representational gaps.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Rotating Figure: U‑ or M‑shaped accuracy across angular disparity, with deep dips at 45°–135°.  
- Director Task: Errors clustered on the egocentric side (e.g., answering “right” when director’s left is required).  
- High invalid response rate in Control 2 for smaller models, indicating parsing failure of facing direction.  
- Performance sometimes improves at 180° relative to 90°, indicating reliance on simple mirroring heuristics.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Control validation:** Run Control 1 (symbol identification, left/right judgments) and Control 2 (figure facing direction) to confirm baseline perception; exclude ambiguous corner‑facing trials.  
2. **Disparity sweep:** Administer Rotating Figure Tests 1–3 across all 360° rotations, binned into 45° intervals, to profile U/M‑shapes and isolate Level 1 vs. Level 2 deficits.  
3. **Combined demand test:** Run Director Task with both visual occlusion and spatial reversal, including “from my point of view” phrasing; compare image vs. ASCII versions to separate visual processing from pure VPT limitations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What architectural components support Level 1 vs Level 2 VPT in MLMs, and how do they map onto dissociable neural substrates?**  
  *Basis:* Section 3 calls for mechanistic analyses to identify architectural components. *Why unresolved:* No such analyses exist yet; resolving requires comparing model variants and correlating with human neuroimaging.

- **Why do models exhibit an M‑shaped accuracy curve in Level 2 VPT (high at 0°/180°, low elsewhere) and does this reflect reliance on mirroring heuristics?**  
  *Basis:* Results show M‑profile; Discussion suggests “simple mirroring strategies… lack representational capacity.” *Why unresolved:* Internal reasoning is opaque; evidence needed would be chain‑of‑thought outputs showing rotation steps, stimuli where mirroring fails, and attention pattern analyses.

- **Will VPT deficits in controlled tasks predict failures in real‑world collaborative AI, and what benchmarks can reliably expose these limitations?**  
  *Basis:* Introduction and Discussion stress implications for collaboration and note current benchmarks may hide failures. *Why unresolved:* Real‑world interaction combines multiple demands; isolating VPT’s causal role requires developing interactive embodied benchmarks (e.g., VR joint tasks, robotic instruction following) and correlating scores with deployment success.

## Limitations
- Stimuli are synthetic (grids/rotated arrows), limiting ecological validity to real‑world 3D scenes.
- Models tested are proprietary and narrow (four variants), restricting generalizability across architectures and training regimes.
- Tasks isolate VPT but lack integrated social reasoning (e.g., belief tracking, pragmatic inference); no process measures like reaction time or confidence to distinguish heuristics from transformation.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (U/M‑shaped accuracy):** High – directly supported by angular disparity results across multiple models.
- **Mechanism 2 (Egocentric inhibition failure):** High – consistent Level 2 VPT deficits and strong converging evidence from neighboring papers on egocentric bias.
- **Mechanism 3 (Separable visual/spatial streams):** Medium – patterns differ across models but causal dissociation remains inferential; architectural basis unclear.

## Next Checks
1. **Diverse model probe:** Test open‑source VLMs (e.g., LLaVA, Fuyu) and newer multimodal LLMs (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 2.0) on identical stimuli to assess reproducibility.
2. **Naturalistic VPT:** Replace synthetic grids with real‑world images containing continuous orientation cues (e.g., people facing different directions in a room) and measure accuracy as a function of angular disparity.
3. **Process tracing:** Administer the Rotating Figure Task with chain‑of‑thought prompting and log intermediate reasoning steps to check for monotonic mental rotation vs. bucket‑based heuristics.
