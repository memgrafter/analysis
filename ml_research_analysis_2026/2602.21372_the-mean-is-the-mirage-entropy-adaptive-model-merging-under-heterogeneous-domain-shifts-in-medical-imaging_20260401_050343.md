# The Mean is the Mirage: Entropy-Adaptive Model Merging under Heterogeneous Domain Shifts in Medical Imaging

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21372
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21372
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** An entropy-adaptive, fully online model-merging method that computes batch-specific coefficients from unlabeled target data, consistently outperforming seven state-of-the-art baselines on nine medical and natural image classification datasets under domain shift.

## Executive Summary
Naive model merging via mean averaging fails when combining models trained on heterogeneous private medical data from different clinics, as test-time domain shifts cause misalignments in both encoder representations and classification heads. This paper introduces an entropy-adaptive merging technique that operates fully online at test time. For each incoming unlabeled target batch, it computes model-specific coefficients by measuring the predictive entropy of each model on that batch, weighting models proportionally to their confidence. Crucially, it decouples the merging coefficients for the encoder and the classification head to address their distinct misalignment patterns. Evaluated on nine classification datasets with ViT backbones, the method achieves higher accuracy than seven baselines while retaining the inference efficiency of a single merged model.

## Method Summary
The proposed method is a test-time, data-adaptive model merging algorithm. Given a set of pre-trained domain-specific models and an unlabeled target batch, it first performs a forward pass through each model to obtain predictions. For each model, it calculates the predictive entropy of its output distribution over the batch, using entropy as an unsupervised proxy for model confidence on the current distribution. It then derives separate coefficient vectors for the encoder (feature extractor) and the classification head: coefficients are computed as a normalized inverse of the entropy, scaled by a temperature parameter, and optionally refined via a sign-matching step to resolve parameter sign conflicts. The final merged model is formed by linearly combining the parameters of all models using these batch-specific coefficients. The entire coefficient computation requires only forward passes on the target batch, with no gradient updates or access to source training data.

## Key Results
- Outperforms seven state-of-the-art merging baselines (Ensemble, DiWA, TIES-Merging, AdaMerging, Task Arithmetic, RegMean++, Fisher-Merging) across nine medical and natural image classification datasets under heterogeneous domain shifts.
- Achieves higher accuracy than naive mean averaging and task-arithmetic-based methods, demonstrating robustness to unseen test-time distribution shifts.
- Maintains single-model inference efficiency after merging, avoiding the computational cost of maintaining an ensemble at test time.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Entropy serves as an effective, unsupervised signal of model confidence on a given batch. Under domain shift, models trained on different source domains will exhibit varying levels of uncertainty on a target batch. By weighting models inversely to their entropy (i.e., higher weight to lower entropy/more confident predictions), the method dynamically selects models that are more aligned with the current target distribution. Decoupling encoder and head coefficients is critical because the misalignment patterns differ: the encoder's feature space may require a consensus-style merge, while the classification head often suffers from label space conflicts (e.g., incompatible logit scales or directions). Treating them separately allows the method to apply appropriate merging strategies to each component, avoiding the failure of a single uniform coefficient set.

## Foundational Learning
- **Model merging:** Combining parameters of multiple specialized models into a single model. *Why needed:* To leverage heterogeneous private data without sharing raw data or retaining multiple models. *Quick check:* Review Task Arithmetic (ILM'22) and RegMean (ICLR'23) for linear merging principles.
- **Domain shift / distribution shift:** Test data distribution differs from training data. *Why needed:* The core problem; naive merging fails under such shifts. *Quick check:* Review covariate shift in medical imaging (e.g., scanner differences).
- **Predictive entropy:** Measure of uncertainty in a model's predicted probability distribution. *Why needed:* Unsupervised confidence estimate for weighting models on unlabeled target batches. *Quick check:* Compute entropy from softmax outputs; lower entropy indicates higher confidence.
- **Forward-pass-only adaptation:** Making decisions using only inference passes, no gradients. *Why needed:* Enables test-time adaptation without source data or retraining. *Quick check:* Contrast with gradient-based TTA methods like TENT.
- **ViT (Vision Transformer):** Architecture used in all evaluations. *Why needed:* Understand potential differences from CNN-based feature alignment. *Quick check:* Review ViT's patch embedding and self-attention mechanism.
- **Decoupled parameter merging:** Applying different merging rules to different parts of a model. *Why needed:* Encoder and head have different misalignment characteristics. *Quick check:* Ablation study in the paper comparing coupled vs. decoupled merging.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map** (plain text flow):  
Target Batch -> Forward Pass (each model) -> Compute Predictive Entropy (per model) -> Compute Encoder Coefficients (inverse entropy + sign match) -> Compute Head Coefficients (inverse entropy) -> Merge Encoder Parameters (using encoder coeffs) -> Merge Head Parameters (using head coeffs) -> Single Merged Model -> Inference

**Critical path:** The sequence during test time for a new batch: (1) run all models forward, (2) compute entropies, (3) derive two sets of coefficients, (4) apply linear combination of stored model parameters to form a new merged model instance, (5) run inference on the batch with the merged model. Steps 1-3 are O(N) in number of models; step 4 is parameter aggregation; step 5 is standard inference.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Batch size vs. entropy stability:* Small batches yield noisy entropy estimates, leading to unstable coefficients. Larger batches improve reliability but increase memory/compute for multiple forwards.  
- *Computational overhead vs. adaptation quality:* Multiple forward passes per batch add latency and memory proportional to number of models. The method trades this overhead for better accuracy than a static merge.  
- *Simplicity vs. generality:* Entropy weighting is simple and unsupervised but may fail if all models are equally uncertain (e.g., out-of-distribution batch) or if confidence does not correlate with correctness.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Performance drops significantly when batch size is very small (e.g., 1), indicated by high variance in accuracy across batches.  
- No improvement over mean averaging if all source models have similarly high entropy on the target domain (no confident model to weight).  
- Degradation on tasks where entropy is a poor confidence measure (e.g., segmentation with aleatoric uncertainty) or on architectures where encoder/head misalignment patterns differ from classification ViTs.

**First three experiments to onboard:**  
1. **Batch size sensitivity:** Run the method with batch sizes {1, 8, 32, 128} on a medical dataset (e.g., ISIC) and a natural one (e.g., ImageNet-R). Plot accuracy vs. batch size to quantify stability of entropy estimates.  
2. **Architecture generality:** Replace ViT-B/16 with ResNet-50 on CIFAR-10-C. Compare performance against baselines to see if decoupling and entropy weighting hold for CNNs.  
3. **Overhead measurement:** Measure wall-clock time and peak memory for coefficient computation (forward passes + entropy calc) relative to a single forward pass of the merged model. Determine break-even batch size where adaptive merging is slower but more accurate.

## Limitations
- Entropy weighting may be unstable with small batches; sensitivity not studied.
- Evaluation limited to ViT and classification; unknown for CNNs or segmentation/detection.
- Test-time overhead from multiple forward passes not quantified; memory cost unclear.

## Confidence
- Naive mean fails under domain shift: **High** (well-known, empirically shown).
- Entropy-adaptive merging outperforms baselines: **Medium-High** (strong results on nine datasets but no significance tests).
- Decoupling encoder and head coefficients helps: **Medium** (likely from ablations, not public).
- Fully online, forward-only: **High** (uses forward passes only).
- Maintains single-model inference efficiency: **Medium** (merging overhead unquantified).
