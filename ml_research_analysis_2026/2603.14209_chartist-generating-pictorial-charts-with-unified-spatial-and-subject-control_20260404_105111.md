# ChArtist: Generating Pictorial Charts with Unified Spatial and Subject Control

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14209
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14209
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** ChArtist achieves state-of-the-art data accuracy and visual similarity in generating pictorial charts by decoupling spatial and subject control via a skeleton representation and Spatially Gated Attention.

## Executive Summary
ChArtist is a framework for generating pictorial charts—infographics that combine data visualizations with illustrative imagery—by balancing two competing goals: strict adherence to the underlying data structure (spatial control) and faithful incorporation of a user-provided visual subject (subject control). It introduces a minimal, chart-specific skeleton representation to encode only the essential geometry of bar, line, and pie charts. This sparse spatial condition is combined with a reference image via two separately trained LoRA adapters on a frozen Diffusion Transformer (FLUX). To prevent interference between these dual control signals during generation, the method employs Spatially Gated Attention, which dynamically modulates the influence of the subject reference based on the spatial layout defined by the skeleton. The authors contribute a 30,000-sample dataset and a structure-aware F1 metric for evaluation, demonstrating superior performance over existing controllable generation methods.

## Method Summary
The core innovation lies in the decomposition of control into two orthogonal signals. Spatial control is achieved through a compact skeleton: vertical lines for bars, polylines for trends, and radial lines for pie slices. Subject control uses a standard image encoder for the reference visual. Both signals are injected into a pretrained Diffusion Transformer (FLUX) via two low-rank adaptation (LoRA) modules trained on disjoint data pairs—`LoRA_S` on (skeleton, chart) pairs and `LoRA_R` on (reference, chart) pairs. During inference, Spatially Gated Attention first uses the attention map between skeleton tokens and latent image tokens to derive a spatial mask. This mask then gates the attention weights from the reference subject tokens, suppressing their influence in regions outside the data-encoding chart structure. This sequential conditioning ensures the skeleton defines the chart's framework, while the reference populates it with visual content.

## Key Results
- **Spatial-only control:** Achieves the highest average data accuracy across chart types (e.g., line data accuracy 0.920 vs. 0.858 for ControlNet-Depth).
- **Dual spatial+subject control:** Leads in data accuracy (bar 0.931, line 0.905, pie 0.753) and visual similarity (DINO/CLIP-I) compared to baselines.
- **User study:** Places second overall among compared methods in a 300-participant study assessing data faithfulness and visual appeal.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A minimal, chart-specific skeleton representation enables precise spatial control while preserving flexibility for visual subject integration.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of dense pixel-level conditions (e.g., Canny edges), ChArtist represents charts with sparse geometric primitives (vertical lines for bars, polylines for trends, radial lines for pie slices). This encodes only the essential data-encoding dimensions, leaving the remaining image area unconstrained for subject-driven visual content.
- **Core assumption:** Visual expressiveness requires structural minimalism; overly dense conditions restrict stylistic variation, while overly sparse ones lack data precision. The skeleton occupies a "sweet spot."
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "introduces a minimal skeleton-based representation for spatial control, capturing only chart-critical geometry"
  - Section 4.1: "We propose a skeleton-based representation... encodes only the data-encoding information of the chart, allowing for the easy incorporation of reference visuals without a rigid outline constraint."
- **Break condition:** If a chart type requires non-linear or highly complex data encodings not reducible to simple primitives (e.g., a scatter plot with cluster density), the skeleton may fail to capture necessary structural nuances.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Task-specific, separate LoRA adapters can independently learn spatial and subject control signals within the same DiT backbone without mutual interference—*during training*.
- **Mechanism:** Two lightweight LoRA modules are trained on disjoint data pairs: `LoRA_S` on (skeleton, pictorial) pairs for spatial alignment, and `LoRA_R` on (reference image, pictorial) pairs for subject identity. The unified sequence `[T, X, C]` with RoPE-based positional offsets allows each LoRA to condition on its respective signal without architectural conflict.
- **Core assumption:** Spatial and subject control can be decomposed into orthogonal feature spaces amenable to separate low-rank adaptation. The backbone's pretrained knowledge provides a shared generative prior.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "couples it with subject control via two LoRA adapters on a Diffusion Transformer"
  - Section 4.2: "We train two task-specific LoRA adapters, `LoRA_S` for spatial control and `LoRA_R` for subject control... Their training differs... spatial control requires spatial alignment with the latent X, whereas subject control does not."
- **Break condition:** If spatial and subject features are not linearly separable in the DiT's feature space (i.e., subject style inherently alters spatial layout), separate LoRA training will not prevent interference at inference.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Spatially Gated Attention mitigates cross-condition interference at inference by making subject control *dependent* on spatial control, preventing style leakage and structural misalignment.
- **Mechanism:** During sampling, a spatial mask `M` is derived from the attention map between skeleton tokens and latent tokens. This mask gates the subject's attention weights `W_X→R`, suppressing subject influence outside the data-encoding regions (controlled by β). This establishes a sequential dependency: spatial control defines the "where," subject control fills the "what."
- **Core assumption:** Interference arises from parallel, unmodulated LoRA composition. Dynamically weighting subject influence based on spatial alignment resolves this without retraining.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "Spatially Gated Attention sequentially modulates the interaction between spatial control and subject control... avoid interference"
  - Section 4.3: "To resolve this interference, we move from a parallel-competition paradigm to a sequential-conditioning paradigm... use a spatial mask... to dynamically gate the influence of the subject signal."
  - Section 5.3, Fig. 8b: Qualitative ablation shows β=0.3 improves data accuracy (reduced leakage), while β=0.9 increases leakage.
- **Break condition:** If the skeleton-derived spatial mask `M` is inaccurate (e.g., due to poor skeletal abstraction), gating will incorrectly suppress subject content *within* valid chart regions or allow leakage in invalid regions.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Diffusion Transformers (DiT)**
  - **Why needed here:** ChArtist fine-tunes FLUX (a DiT) with LoRA. Understanding DiT's tokenized latent space and attention mechanisms is key to grasping how spatial/subject tokens are integrated and how positional encoding (RoPE) distinguishes their roles.
  - **Quick check question:** How does a DiT differ from a U-Net diffusion model in terms of conditioning flexibility?

- **Concept: LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**
  - **Why needed here:** The core method uses two independently trained LoRA modules (`LoRA_S`, `LoRA_R`) to inject control signals into the frozen DiT backbone. One must understand that LoRA updates only a small subset of weights (in the attention layers) and how multiple LoRAs are typically composed.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might naively adding two LoRA weight updates in parallel cause "cross-condition interference"?

- **Concept: Structure-Aware Evaluation for Charts**
  - **Why needed here:** Standard pixel-level metrics (IoU) fail for sparse, abstract skeletons. The paper's weighted F1 score based on distance fields is central to claiming "data faithfulness." One must understand why chart evaluation requires a custom metric tied to data-encoding dimensions (e.g., bar height, line trend).
  - **Quick check question:** Why would a high CLIP-I or DINO score be misleading for a pictorial chart if data accuracy is low?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  Pretrained FLUX Backbone -> Input Encoders (Skeleton S, Reference R, Text T) -> Unified Sequence [T, X, C] with RoPE offsets -> LoRA_S & LoRA_R (applied to attention layers) -> Spatially Gated Attention (mask M from S) -> Updated Latent -> Diffusion Sampling Loop -> Final Pictorial Chart P

- **Critical path:**
  Input: (Skeleton S, Reference R, Text T) -> Encode all conditions -> Form unified sequence [T, X, C] with positional offsets -> During each denoising step: Pass through frozen DiT + active LoRA(S) and LoRA(R) -> At attention layers, apply Spatially Gated Attention to subject-conditioned attention using mask M derived from S -> Output updated latent -> Repeat until final image P.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Data Faithfulness vs. Visual Expressiveness:** The skeleton's minimalism trades off precise object shape fidelity for semantic flexibility. A more detailed skeleton would better constrain shape but reduce the "pictorial" effect.
  - **Parallel vs. Sequential Conditioning:** Parallel LoRA composition is simpler but causes interference. Spatially Gated Attention adds computational overhead (extra attention computation for mask) but enforces dependency. The β parameter is a tunable knob for this tradeoff.
  - **Dataset Cost vs. Generalization:** CHARTIST-30K enables training but is synthetic. Real-world diversity (e.g., non-photorealistic styles, occlusions) may not be fully covered.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Structure Misalignment:** Generated chart shape deviates from skeleton (e.g., bar height wrong, line trend distorted). Indicates `LoRA_S` was overpowered by `LoRA_R` or mask `M` was ineffective.
  - **Style Leakage:** Visual elements from the reference image appear *outside* the intended chart region (e.g., in background). Indicates insufficient gating (β too high) or inaccurate mask `M`.
  - **Incomplete Population:** Chart region is partially blank or lacks coherent visual elements. Common with inpainting baselines; for ChArtist, may indicate `LoRA_R` was over-suppressed (β too low) or training data lacked sufficient variety.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation on Spatially Gated Attention:** Fix `R` and `S`, vary β (0.0 to 1.0). Measure: (a) Data Accuracy (F1), (b) Visual Consistency (DINO/CLIP-I). Expected: Data Acc peaks at low β (e.g., 0.3), Visual Consistency peaks at high β (e.g., 0.9). The paper's Tab. 4 shows this tradeoff.
  2. **Single-Control Sanity Check:** Train/evaluate `LoRA_S` alone (spatial-only) and `LoRA_R` alone (subject-only). Measure: `LoRA_S` should match/exceed ControlNet-Depth in data accuracy (as in Tab. 1). `LoRA_R` should show high visual consistency but poor data accuracy (random spatial alignment). This validates each LoRA learns its intended signal.
  3. **Skeleton Representation Ablation:** Replace the proposed skeleton with alternative sparse controls (e.g., bounding boxes for bars, keypoints for line charts). Compare data accuracy and CLIP-T against the proposed skeleton. Hypothesis: Proposed skeleton will achieve the best balance as it encodes chart-specific semantics (heights, angles) directly.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does the proposed skeleton-based spatial control generalize to chart types beyond bar, line, and pie (e.g., scatter plots, radar charts, or treemaps)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The skeleton representation is defined specifically for bar (vertical lines), line (polylines), and pie (radial lines) charts. The paper discusses two pipelines for dataset generation but does not evaluate or define skeletons for other chart topologies, implicitly assuming the representation's applicability is limited to these.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper's contributions and experiments are confined to three common chart types. The skeleton's design principle—encoding only data-encoding geometry—is described generally, but its feasibility for more complex or multi-dimensional encodings remains untested and could face representational limitations.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend the CHARTIST-30K dataset to include additional chart types with appropriately designed skeletons. Conduct quantitative and qualitative evaluations on these new types, measuring data accuracy and visual expressiveness compared to baselines, to validate or refine the skeleton's generalizability.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the synthetic nature of the CHARTIST-30K dataset limit the real-world performance and robustness of models trained on it, particularly regarding the diversity of visual styles, object deformations, and user-created pictorial charts?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The dataset is described as "synthetic" and generated via two "specialized pipelines" with "manual verification and filtering." The paper acknowledges this construction but does not analyze potential biases or gaps compared to authentic user-generated pictorial charts.
- **Why unresolved:** Synthetic data may not capture the full variability, imperfections, or creative choices found in real design workflows. The pipelines prioritize data faithfulness and visual consistency but might introduce systematic artifacts or miss stylistic nuances that affect generalization.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Curate a benchmark of real-world pictorial charts (e.g., collected from design platforms or user studies) and evaluate ChArtist's performance on this set compared to the synthetic test set. Perform failure mode analysis to identify discrepancies and potentially augment training with real or mixed data.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the optimal trade-off between data accuracy and visual consistency in dual-control generation, and how universally does the suppression factor β in Spatially-Gated Attention achieve this balance across different chart types, visual concepts, and user preferences?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper introduces β to control subject expressiveness in background regions and shows an ablation (Tab. 4, Fig. 8b) where varying β affects the trade-off: lower β improves data accuracy but may reduce visual consistency. The user study ranks ChArtist second overall, indicating room for improvement in balancing these objectives.
- **Why unresolved:** The optimal β is set to 0.6 based on empirical observation, but the trade-off is inherently subjective and context-dependent. The paper does not explore whether a single β value is optimal for all scenarios (e.g., bar vs. line charts, photorealistic vs. illustrative references) or how user intent might dictate the balance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a large-scale user study varying β across a wide range of chart types and reference images, asking participants to rate the importance of data fidelity versus visual appeal. Analyze the results to derive a context-aware β selection strategy or learn a dynamic gating mechanism from human preferences.

## Limitations
- **Representational scope:** The skeleton is defined only for bar, line, and pie charts; its adequacy for complex encodings (e.g., scatter plots with density, non-linear mappings) is an untested break condition.
- **Feature orthogonality assumption:** Training separate LoRAs on disjoint data does not guarantee linearly separable spatial/subject features in the DiT's latent space; interference may persist at novel compositions.
- **Dataset syntheticity:** CHARTIST-30K is procedurally generated, likely lacking the stylistic diversity, occlusions, and imperfections of real-world user-created pictorial charts, limiting generalization robustness.

## Confidence
- **Spatial control via minimal skeleton:** Medium (supported by ablations but limited to three chart types).
- **Dual LoRA training without interference:** Medium (training on disjoint data shown; feature orthogonality unproven in latent space).
- **Spatially Gated Attention efficacy:** High (ablation in Fig. 8b clearly demonstrates β trade-off).
- **Overall quantitative superiority:** High (consistent benchmark wins across metrics), but **user-study robustness:** Medium (second place; sample diversity and preference variability unclear).
