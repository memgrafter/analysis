# Did You Forget What I Asked? Prospective Memory Failures in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.23530
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23530
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: LLMs exhibit prospective memory forgetting under concurrent task load, with compliance dropping 2–21% and up to 50% for terminal constraints, but this can often be recovered to 90–100% using a trailing reminder prompt.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates why large language models fail to follow formatting instructions when also performing demanding tasks. The authors frame this as prospective memory forgetting. Through controlled experiments combining verifiable IFEval constraints with benchmark tasks of increasing cognitive load (TriviaQA, MMLU, GSM8K, CNN/DailyMail), they measure compliance and task accuracy across three models and over 8,000 prompts. Results show compliance drops under concurrent task load, with terminal constraints (requiring action at response end) degrading most severely. A salience-enhanced prompt format—explicit instruction framing plus a trailing reminder—recovers compliance in many settings. Critically, interference is bidirectional: adding formatting constraints reduces task accuracy, and stacking multiple constraints causes joint compliance to decline. The study demonstrates that instruction forgetting in LLMs mirrors human prospective memory failures, is highly type-dependent, and can be mitigated by increasing constraint salience at the prompt boundary.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates compliance with IFEval's 15 deterministic formatting constraints when each is combined with a single benchmark task (TriviaQA, MMLU, GSM8K, or CNN/DailyMail). Two prompt conditions are tested: Natural Embedding (constraint integrated into original IFEval prompt with a neutral connector) and Salience-Enhanced (explicit instruction framing with a trailing reminder). Three models are used: o4-mini, DeepSeek-V3.1, and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct. Greedy decoding is applied across 90 fixed IFEval items and 90 sampled benchmark items per condition, with three random seeds. Primary outcomes are strict IFEval compliance and task accuracy (extracted via heuristics like last-number match). The forgetting delta (Δ) is computed as baseline compliance minus combined-task compliance. The experimental design isolates the effect of concurrent cognitive load on constraint retention and the efficacy of the reminder intervention.

## Key Results
- Compliance degrades under task load: drops of 2–21% across models, with terminal constraints (e.g., `end_checker`, `json_format`) forgetting up to 50%.
- A trailing reminder prompt recovers compliance to 90–100% for many single-constraint scenarios.
- Bidirectional interference: adding formatting constraints reduces task accuracy (e.g., o4-mini GSM8K accuracy falls from 93% to 27% with a constraint present).
- Stacking multiple formatting constraints causes joint compliance to decline sharply, and the reminder mitigation becomes inconsistent.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Representational Competition
- Claim: Formatting instruction forgetting under concurrent task load results from **representational competition** during generation, where task-relevant hidden states crowd out the constraint's influence.
- Mechanism: When the model generates tokens for a demanding distractor task, internal representations become dominated by task-specific computations. This reduces the effective attention weight or activation strength of the formatting constraint, leading to non-compliance despite the instruction remaining visible.
- Core assumption: The model's decoding process involves a form of resource-limited monitoring for active constraints, which can be displaced by concurrent cognitive demands.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Systematic degradation across models and tasks, and recovery via a trailing reminder that re-elevates constraint salience right before generation.
  - Weak direct support; neighbor paper "Decoupling Task-Solving and Output Formatting in LLM Generation" addresses related separation challenges but does not test prospective memory framing.
- Break condition: If compliance does not recover with a trailing reminder, the mechanism may not be primarily salience-based competition.

### Mechanism 2: Temporal Distance for Terminal Constraints
- Claim: **Terminal constraints** are disproportionately forgotten due to **temporal distance** between instruction presentation and required action point.
- Mechanism: Constraints that must be satisfied at the very end of a long generation sequence suffer from decay in effective salience over time. The model's attention or monitoring window naturally emphasizes recent tokens; an instruction presented early loses influence by the time hundreds of task-related tokens have been generated.
- Core assumption: The model lacks a persistent, position-invariant "intention buffer" and relies on temporal proximity for constraint activation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - `end_checker`, `json_format`, and `number_bullet_lists` (often terminal) show the largest deltas; avoidance constraints (`no_comma`, `forbidden_words`) are robust.
  - Neighbor "Modelling prospective memory and resilient situated communications via Wizard of Oz" explicitly models temporal aspects of memory in human-robot interaction, providing a conceptual analog.
- Break condition: If terminal constraints show no greater vulnerability than pervasive ones when controlling for task load, temporal distance is not the primary factor.

### Mechanism 3: Shared Resource Bottleneck
- Claim: The bidirectional interference—where adding formatting constraints also degrades **task accuracy**—indicates a **shared resource bottleneck** in the model's generation or reasoning pipeline.
- Mechanism: Satisfying formatting constraints and solving the primary task compete for the same computational budget (e.g., attention heads, MLP capacity, or steps in a reasoning chain). Diverting resources to maintain format monitoring reduces the fidelity of task-solving.
- Core assumption: There is a finite capacity for concurrent objective fulfillment during autoregressive generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Large, reproducible task accuracy drops (e.g., GSM8K 93% → 27%) when formatting constraints are added.
  - The non-monotonic scaling in accuracy across constraint stacks suggests complex resource allocation dynamics.
  - "Decoupling Task-Solving and Output Formatting in LLM Generation" directly aims to separate these processes, implying they are currently entangled.
- Break condition: If task accuracy remains invariant when adding simple constraints, or if constraints only ever help accuracy, this resource competition model is incorrect.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Prospective Memory (PM) Theory**
  - Why needed: The paper's framing—forgetting under load, cue salience effects, temporal distance—maps onto classic human PM phenomena. Understanding PM provides the analogical model for why LLMs forget instructions.
  - Quick check: What are the two main pathways in human PM (monitoring vs. spontaneous retrieval), and which one does the "reminder" intervention likely target?

- **Concept: IFEval Evaluation Protocol**
  - Why needed: The study's dependent variable (compliance) relies on IFEval's verifiable, programmatic checkers. Knowing its 15 constraint types and deterministic evaluation is core to interpreting all results.
  - Quick check: What is the difference between IFEval's "strict" and "loose" compliance modes, and why might loose compliance show smaller forgetting deltas?

- **Concept: Representational Competition in Transformer Decoding**
  - Why needed: The proposed causal mechanism (that hidden states get dominated by task content) requires a basic mental model of how constraints influence token probabilities via attention/MLP pathways during generation.
  - Quick check: If a constraint's textual description remains in the context window, why might its *effective influence* on token selection still decay over a long generation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map:
  - Prompt Composer -> combines IFEval constraint (from fixed pool of 90 items) with benchmark task (TriviaQA/MMLU/GSM8K/CNN-DM) using Natural or Reminder template
  - Model Backend -> three distinct API endpoints (o4-mini, DeepSeek-V3.1, Llama-3.3-70B) with greedy decoding
  - Checker Suite -> 15 deterministic code-based validators (one per IFEval type) plus task-specific accuracy extractors (last-number, regex, ROUGE)
  - Metrics Engine -> computes Δ (forgetting delta), joint compliance for stacking, and dual-task interference (accuracy drop)

- Critical path:
  1. Reproduce Table 1's "Natural Embedding" column: For each model, run the 6 conditions (baseline + 5 load tasks) with 3 seeds. Validate that base compliance and forgetting deltas match within bootstrap CIs.
  2. Reproduce the Reminder recovery (Table 1 right). This is the key salience test.
  3. Probe constraint-type vulnerability: Extract per-type Δ from the GSM8K condition and reproduce the heatmap pattern (Figure 5), especially terminal vs. avoidance.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Deterministic checkers vs. LLM-as-judge: Paper avoids subjective evaluation but may miss semantic compliance (e.g., "capital letters" vs. proper noun exceptions).
  - Operational load (TriviaQA→CNN/DM) vs. empirical cognitive cost: The load ordering is behavioral, not measured (e.g., via FLOPs or attention entropy).
  - Single-turn vs. multi-turn: Findings may not generalize to conversational settings where forgetting could compound.

- Failure signatures:
  - Domain shift: Constraint satisfied in IFEval subtask, violated during distractor task (e.g., capital letters dropped for proper nouns during trivia).
  - Terminal omission: Required ending produced prematurely, then generation continues (common for `end_checker`).
  - Spatial compartmentalization: Constraint applied to one output section but not globally (e.g., quotation marks around each part separately).
  - Structural abandon: Formatting pattern (e.g., bullet points) maintained for first subtask but replaced for second.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. **Baseline Compliance Check**: Run the 90 IFEval items (no added task) for all three models. Confirm base strict compliance rates are reasonable (e.g., 86–89% as in Table 1). If baseline is low, the model's inherent constraint-following capability is weak, confounding load effects.
  2. **Load Effect Replication**: For a single high-impact constraint type (e.g., `end_checker`), append a GSM8K problem using the Natural template. Verify the forgetting delta (Δ) is positive and statistically significant (McNemar p<.05). If no drop occurs, the cognitive load manipulation failed.
  3. **Reminder Efficacy Test**: Take the same `end_checker` + GSM8K condition and switch to the Reminder template. The compliance should increase by ~15–20pp and approach/baseline levels. If the reminder hurts compliance, prompt formatting may be broken (e.g., constraint extraction error).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: What model-specific factors (architecture, scale, training objectives) explain the varying degrees of prospective memory failure and dual-task interference observed across the three evaluated models?
  - Basis in paper: The discussion notes: “These patterns suggest that the factors driving forgetting interact with model-specific training choices in ways that require further investigation.”
  - Why unresolved: The study only included three models from two providers, preventing isolation of the contributing factors.
  - What evidence would resolve it: A comparative study spanning diverse model families (e.g., different architectures, sizes, and training recipes) while holding the experimental paradigm constant, to correlate specific characteristics with forgetting metrics.

- **Open Question 2**: Which elements of the salience-enhanced prompt (explicit instruction framing, trailing reminder, or both) drive the observed recovery in compliance, and can the effect be further improved?
  - Basis in paper: The limitations state: “The reminder condition modifies multiple prompt components simultaneously; we do not ablate their individual contributions.”
  - Why unresolved: Without disentangling the components, we cannot determine the minimal effective intervention or optimize it.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Ablation experiments that systematically remove or modify parts of the reminder template, measuring compliance under various cognitive loads and constraint stacks, possibly with larger samples.

- **Open Question 3**: Does prospective memory forgetting persist, worsen, or ameliorate in multi-turn conversational settings where formatting instructions appear in early turns?
  - Basis in paper: The limitations acknowledge: “We test only single-turn scenarios; multi-turn settings may exhibit stronger forgetting.”
  - Why unresolved: Real-world deployments often involve multi-turn interactions; forgetting could accumulate or be mitigated by turn structure.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Extend the composition framework to multi-turn dialogues, tracking compliance over turns and analyzing how instruction retention decays with dialogue depth.

- **Open Question 4**: Can we define a more precise, model-agnostic metric of cognitive load that predicts forgetting better than coarse task-category labels (TriviaQA, MMLU, etc.)?
  - Basis in paper: The limitations note: “The cognitive load ordering is based on task complexity rather than empirically measured computational cost, and the non-monotonic patterns we observe suggest this ordering does not fully capture what drives forgetting.”
  - Why unresolved: The coarse load levels fail to explain non-monotonic effects, implying a need for nuanced load measures.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Collect fine-grained metrics (e.g., number of reasoning steps, hidden-state divergence, attention entropy) for each task and model, and correlate them with forgetting rates to build a predictive model of load.

## Limitations
- Major unknowns impede full replication: exact model endpoints (e.g., "o4-mini" identifier), complete IFEval checker implementations (especially for terminal constraints like `end_checker`), and precise sampling/grouping for multi-step GSM8K chains.
- System prompt wording beyond the quoted line is unspecified.
- The study only includes three models, limiting generalizability of model-specific factors.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Representational competition)**: Medium. The trailing reminder recovery supports a salience-based interference, but direct evidence of hidden-state crowding is absent; corpus analogs are conceptual.
- **Mechanism 2 (Temporal distance for terminal constraints)**: High. The paper's per-type analysis shows disproportionate forgetting for terminal constraints, consistent with decay in monitoring over long sequences.
- **Mechanism 3 (Bidirectional resource bottleneck)**: High. Large, reproducible task accuracy drops (e.g., GSM8K 93% → 27%) when formatting constraints are added strongly indicate shared computational constraints during generation.
