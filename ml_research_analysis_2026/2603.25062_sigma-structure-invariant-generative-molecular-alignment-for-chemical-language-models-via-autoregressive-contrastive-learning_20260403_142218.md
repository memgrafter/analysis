# SIGMA: Structure-Invariant Generative Molecular Alignment for Chemical Language Models via Autoregressive Contrastive Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25062  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25062  
- **Reference count:** 23  
- **Primary result:** SIGMA achieves a Fréchet ChemNet Distance of 0.752 with 99.8% validity on ZINC-250k, improving unique scaffold discovery by 20–40% over baselines; IsoBeam doubles scaffold count at large beam sizes.

## Executive Summary
SIGMA tackles a fundamental issue in autoregressive molecular generation: trajectory divergence caused by SMILES permutations, which fragments the latent manifold and harms optimization and diversity. It introduces a token-level contrastive learning objective that aligns latent representations of structurally equivalent partial graphs, combined with Isomorphic Beam Search (IsoBeam) to prune redundant SMILES variants during decoding. On ZINC-250k, SIGMA achieves state-of-the-art generative quality (FCD 0.752, 99.8% validity) and substantially boosts scaffold diversity in multi-parameter optimization. IsoBeam’s dynamic pruning breaks redundancy saturation in beam search, demonstrating superior sample efficiency and structural diversity at large beam widths.

## Method Summary
SIGMA augments a GPT-2–based autoregressive language model with two complementary mechanisms. First, a **dense trajectory alignment** objective constructs positive pairs of SMILES prefixes that share an identical suffix but differ in tokenization (verified via RDKit/InChIKey). A projection head maps token hidden states into a metric space where a contrastive loss (InfoNCE) maximizes similarity between suffix representations of the two views while contrasting against structural negatives; gradients backpropagate to align the prefix encoders. Second, **Isomorphic Beam Search** dynamically prunes beam candidates that correspond to molecular graphs already present (via InChIKey hashing), reallocating search budget to structurally distinct candidates. The total loss combines standard MLE and the contrastive term. Evaluated on ZINC-250k, SIGMA improves manifold invariance (TIS), generative quality (FCD), and diversity metrics, with IsoBeam scaling effectively to very large beam sizes.

## Key Results
- Fréchet ChemNet Distance (FCD) of **0.752** with **99.8% validity** on ZINC-250k, outperforming sequence baselines.
- **20–40% improvement** in unique scaffold discovery for multi-parameter optimization tasks.
- IsoBeam **doubles scaffold count** at large beam sizes (e.g., K=50,000), breaking the redundancy saturation observed in standard beam search.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SIGMA’s gains arise from three interconnected mechanisms. (1) **Trajectory divergence**: Autoregressive models treat each SMILES permutation as a distinct sequence, causing latent states of equivalent partial graphs to drift apart and fragment the manifold (abstract, Sec. 1). (2) **Token-level contrastive alignment**: By aligning suffix token representations across different views of the same molecule, gradients enforce invariance on the prefix contexts due to autoregressive dependency (abstract, Sec. 3.4). (3) **Isomorphic Beam Search**: During decoding, candidates mapping to already-seen graphs (via InChIKey) are pruned, preventing syntactic redundancy from crowding the beam and boosting effective diversity (abstract, Sec. 3.5, Fig. 6).

## Foundational Learning
- **Autoregressive Molecular Generation via SMILES**: Molecules are modeled as token sequences; permutations create distinct training trajectories. *Why needed*: Understanding this paradigm clarifies how trajectory divergence emerges. *Quick check*: Why would two SMILES strings for the same molecule yield different loss landscapes during training?
- **Manifold Fragmentation & Latent Space Isotropy**: A well-formed generative model should have a smooth latent space grouped by chemical structure, not string syntax. *Why needed*: Fragmentation is the core problem SIGMA solves. *Quick check*: What would a t-SNE plot of terminal hidden states for 50 SMILES of one molecule look like in a fragmented vs. invariant model?
- **Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE) & Projection Heads**: Contrastive losses align representations in a metric space; projection heads decouple generative and invariant features. *Why needed*: SIGMA’s training objective relies on this separation. *Quick check*: Why apply contrastive loss to a projection (z_t) instead of directly to Transformer hidden states (h_t)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Backbone (GPT-2 Small) → Projection Head (2-layer MLP) → View Generator (splits graph, creates two prefix variants with dummy atom) → Oracle Verifier (RDKit/InChIKey). IsoBeam integrates RDKit checks into the beam search loop.

**Critical path:**  
*Training*: For each molecule, View Generator produces two views (different prefixes, same suffix). Backbone processes both; Projection Head yields per-token embeddings. Contrastive loss aligns suffix tokens of the two views (positives) and contrasts with suffix tokens from other molecules (structural negatives). Combined with MLE loss, backpropagated.  
*Inference*: Standard beam expansion; for each valid partial SMILES, compute InChIKey; if already seen, prune; else retain and continue.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Suffix alignment assumes stable suffix fragments; splitting inside rings may destabilize.  
- Projection head bottleneck (d_proj=128) filters syntactic noise but risks losing generative information.  
- IsoBeAM’s per-step RDKit checks add computational overhead but are necessary for large-K diversity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High Trajectory Invariance Score (TIS): poor manifold alignment (λ too low or view generation flawed).  
- High validity + low scaffold diversity: IsoBeam not used or broken.  
- Low validity after SIGMA: contrastive loss interfering with MLE (λ too high or projection head leakage).

**First three experiments:**  
1. **Replicate Fig. 4 (t-SNE)**: Train baseline and SIGMA; for 10 molecules, generate 50 random SMILES each; extract terminal hidden states; t-SNE should show scattered clouds for baseline and tight clusters for SIGMA.  
2. **Replicate Fig. 5 (Token-wise alignment)**: For acetophenone, get two divergent SMILES; compute cosine similarity matrix of token hidden states; expect large off-diagonal blocks for SIGMA vs. diagonal-only for baseline.  
3. **IsoBeam scaling (Fig. 6)**: Run standard and IsoBeam for K ∈ {100, 5k, 50k}; plot validity and scaffold count; expect scaffold count saturates early for standard beam but grows linearly for IsoBeam.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does SIGMA’s token-level contrastive objective fully capture geometric invariance for molecules with non‑local symmetries where equivalent substructures span multiple tokens?
2. How sensitive is SIGMA’s training to the choice of probe suffix fragments used in the Dual Consistency Condition for verifying structural equivalence?
3. Can IsoBeam be extended to prune isomorphic redundant partial graphs even when they are chemically invalid (e.g., open rings), thereby improving sample efficiency at early decoding steps?
4. How effectively does SIGMA transfer to other molecular string representations like SELFIES, which guarantee semantic validity and have semantically meaningful tokens?

## Limitations
- The contrastive loss weight λ is unreported; its tuning may be critical and not generalize across datasets.
- View generation details (bond sampling, ring splits, probe suffix) are vague, risking inconsistent positive pair construction.
- IsoBeam’s per‑step RDKit computational cost is unquantified; scalability beyond K=50k is unknown.
- No ablation of projection head design; limited evaluation outside ZINC‑250k.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (trajectory divergence):** Medium. Evidence from TIS and t‑SNE supports fragmentation, but alternative causes not ruled out.  
- **Mechanism 2 (contrastive alignment):** Medium. Theoretical argument and TIS improvement plausible; hyperparameter sensitivity unexplored.  
- **Mechanism 3 (IsoBeam):** High. Clear empirical scaling (Fig. 6) confirms redundancy pruning.  
- **Overall results:** Medium‑Low. Missing λ and view specification hinder faithful replication of FCD/validity numbers.
