# Momentum Guidance: Plug-and-Play Guidance for Flow Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20360
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20360
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Momentum Guidance reduces FID by 36.68% without CFG and 25.52% with CFG, achieving FID 1.597 on ImageNet-256 at 64 steps, with no extra model evaluations.

## Executive Summary
Flow-based generative models often produce oversmoothed, blurry samples. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) improves fidelity but doubles computational cost by requiring separate conditional and unconditional model evaluations. Momentum Guidance (MG) introduces a lightweight, plug-and-play modification that reuses past velocity evaluations along the ODE trajectory to extrapolate the current velocity, sharpening details without additional forward passes. MG maintains an exponential moving average (EMA) of prior velocities, using the difference between the current velocity and this smoother reference to amplify high-frequency information. Extensive experiments on ImageNet-256, Stable Diffusion 3, and FLUX.1-dev show MG consistently improves sample quality both as a standalone guidance method and when combined with CFG, yielding significant FID reductions with negligible memory overhead.

## Method Summary
Momentum Guidance modifies the Euler integration step of pretrained flow model sampling. At each timestep, the current velocity \(v_t = v_\theta(Z_t, t|c)\) is computed as usual. A momentum vector \(m_t\) is maintained as an EMA of past velocities: \(m_{t+1} = (1-\beta)v_t + \beta m_t\), initialized to the first velocity. The sample update incorporates an extrapolation term: \(Z_{t+1} = Z_t + \Delta t [v_t + \alpha (v_t - m_t)]\), where \(\alpha\) controls guidance strength. This term pushes the trajectory away from the smoother EMA reference toward the current, sharper velocity estimate. MG can be applied to the raw model velocity or to a CFG-adjusted velocity \(v_{\text{CFG}} = \omega v_{\text{cond}} + (1-\omega) v_{\text{uncond}}\), in which case it further refines the guided path. The method requires storing only the momentum vector \(m_t\), which has the same dimensionality as the latent variable \(Z_t\), and does not increase the number of neural network evaluations per step.

## Key Results
- MG reduces ImageNet-256 FID by 36.68% over baseline flow sampling without CFG.
- When combined with CFG, MG yields an additional 25.52% FID reduction.
- At 64 steps, MG+CFG achieves FID 1.597 on ImageNet-256.
- On Stable Diffusion 3 and FLUX.1-dev, MG consistently improves human preference metrics (HPSv2.1, ImageReward) over CFG alone.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Oversmoothing Correction via Temporal Extrapolation
- **Claim:** Momentum Guidance reduces oversmoothing by extrapolating the current velocity away from an EMA of past velocities, which acts as a naturally smoother reference.
- **Mechanism:** MG computes an exponential moving average \(m_t\) of previously evaluated velocities. The update \(Z_{t+1} = Z_t + \Delta t [v_t + \alpha(v_t - m_t)]\) adds a correction term \((v_t - m_t)\) that points from the smoother EMA estimate toward the current, higher-frequency velocity. This amplifies details without requiring new model evaluations. Early-time velocities are inherently smoother due to the marginal smoothing property of rectified flows, providing a suitable reference.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states MG "extrapolates the current velocity using an exponential moving average of past velocities... matches the effect of standard guidance without extra computation." Section 3 explains that "past velocities already supply the 'smoother' reference needed for guidance." The corpus analysis shows no prior method reuses past velocities this way.
- **Break condition:** If the EMA decay \(\beta\) is too high, \(m_t\) becomes excessively smooth and lags, reducing the corrective direction’s magnitude.

### Mechanism 2: Negligible Memory Overhead
- **Claim:** MG's memory overhead is negligible because it only stores a momentum vector \(m_t\) with the same dimensionality as the latent variable.
- **Mechanism:** The only added state is \(m_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_Z}\), where \(d_Z\) is the latent size (e.g., \(32\times32\times4\) for ImageNet-256). This is orders of magnitude smaller than model parameters (billions for SD3/FLUX) and does not scale with network depth. No extra forward passes are needed; MG reuses the already-computed \(v_t\).
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3 explicitly notes "the only extra state... momentum vector \(m_t\)... orders of magnitude smaller than the underlying network parameters." Section 4.1 confirms latent sizes remain small even for high-resolution models like FLUX (128×128×16). The corpus highlights MG's plug-and-play nature versus methods that add auxiliary networks.
- **Break condition:** In pixel-space flow models with extremely high-dimensional latents, the memory savings could be less pronounced, but this is atypical for modern latent diffusion.

### Mechanism 3: Compatibility and Additivity with CFG
- **Claim:** MG is compatible with and additive to CFG, improving sample quality both alone and in combination.
- **Mechanism:** When CFG is enabled, the velocity input to MG becomes \(v_{\text{CFG}}\). MG’s extrapolation term \(\alpha(v_{\text{CFG}} - m_t)\) then refines the already guided trajectory. The paper argues that CFG’s conditional-unconditional extrapolation and MG’s temporal smoothing correction address partially independent aspects of oversmoothing, leading to additive gains.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract reports FID reductions of 36.68% without CFG and 25.52% with CFG. Tables 1–3 in Section 4.1 show consistent FID improvements for MG over CFG alone across ImageNet, SD3, and FLUX. Algorithm 2 in Section 7.1 explicitly applies MG to the CFG-adjusted velocity.
- **Break condition:** At very high CFG scales (\(\omega > 3\)), both methods may over-concentrate the distribution, causing artifacts and diminishing MG’s relative gains.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Rectified Flow (RF)**
  - *Why needed here:* MG operates within the RF sampling framework where the generative process follows an ODE \(dZ_t = v_\theta(Z_t, t|c) dt\). RF’s linear interpolation path (Eq. 1) and marginal smoothing (Eq. 7) imply that early-time velocity fields are smoother, making them suitable references for MG’s EMA.
  - *Quick check question:* Why does the conditional velocity field \(v^*(x, t|c)\) correspond to a smoother distribution at smaller \(t\)?

- **Concept: Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)**
  - *Why needed here:* CFG is the predominant method for improving fidelity in flow and diffusion models. MG is benchmarked against and combined with CFG. Understanding CFG’s extrapolation \(v_{\text{CFG}} = \omega v_{\text{cond}} + (1-\omega) v_{\text{uncond}}\) and its cost (doubling evaluations) is essential to appreciate MG’s efficiency gain.
  - *Quick check question:* How does CFG’s reliance on an unconditional branch double inference cost, and what trade-off does it impose between fidelity and diversity?

- **Concept: Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**
  - *Why needed here:* MG’s momentum \(m_t\) is an EMA of past velocities. The decay hyperparameter \(\beta\) controls the EMA’s window; larger \(\beta\) yields a smoother but laggier reference, directly affecting the extrapolation direction.
  - *Quick check question:* If \(\beta\) is close to 1, what happens to \(m_t\) relative to recent velocities, and how does this impact the term \((v_t - m_t)\) used in the MG update?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Pretrained flow model (\(v_\theta\)) -> Momentum buffer (\(m_t\)) -> Euler sampler with MG update (optionally wrapped by CFG) -> Final latent \(Z_T\)

- **Critical path:**
  1. Sample initial noise \(Z_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\).
  2. Compute initial velocity \(v_{t_0} = v_\theta(Z_0, t_0|c)\) (or CFG-adjusted). Initialize momentum \(m_0 = v_{t_0}\).
  3. For each timestep \(t_i\) in the time grid:
     - Compute current velocity \(v_{t_i} = v_\theta(Z_{t_i}, t_i|c)\) (or CFG-adjusted).
     - Update sample: \(Z_{t_{i+1}} = Z_{t_i} + \Delta t \left[ v_{t_i} + \alpha (v_{t_i} - m_{t_i}) \right]\).
     - Update momentum: \(m_{t_{i+1}} = (1-\beta) v_{t_i} + \beta m_{t_i}\).
  4. Return final latent \(Z_T\) (and decode if pixel-space).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - \(\alpha\) (extrapolation strength): Low values yield negligible effect; high values risk oversharpening and artifacts (Fig. 5 U-curve).
  - \(\beta\) (EMA decay): Low \(\beta\) makes \(m_t\) noisy; high \(\beta\) makes it laggy, under-correcting.
  - CFG interval: Applying CFG/MG only at later timesteps can improve diversity [33], but paper shows full-interval works well (Tables 1,4).
  - Normalization/unbiased EMA: Optional refinements (Appendix Fig. 9) give minor gains at negligible complexity.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Loss of diversity:** Excessively high \(\alpha\) or CFG scale \(\omega\) over-concentrates samples (Fig. 3 recall drop).
  - **Oversaturation/artifacts:** High \(\alpha\) can introduce unnatural high-frequency noise or color shifts (Fig. 4).
  - **Instability:** Very high \(\beta\) (e.g., >0.9) makes \(m_t\) too static, under-correcting oversmoothing.
  - **No improvement:** If \(\alpha=0\) or \(\beta\) poorly tuned, MG reduces to baseline (Fig. 5 ridge).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Hyperparameter sweep:** Grid search \(\alpha \in [0,1]\), \(\beta \in [0,0.8]\) on a validation set (e.g., FID-10K) for target NFEs and CFG scales; expect U-shaped FID landscape (Fig. 5).
  2. **Ablation on CFG interaction:** Compare MG alone vs. MG+CFG at varying \(\omega\); verify additive gains (Table 1) and note diminishing returns at high \(\omega\).
  3. **Scaling test:** Apply MG to large models (SD3, FLUX) with minimal tuning; expect consistent metric improvements (HPSv2, ImageReward) with same \(\alpha,\beta\) ranges (Tables 2,3).

## Limitations
- Checkpoint sources and exact time discretization for SD3/FLUX are not specified, hindering exact reproducibility.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (\(\alpha,\beta\)) may vary across architectures; reported optimal ranges might not generalize.
- Memory savings assume moderate latent sizes; pixel-space flows could alter the overhead trade-off.

## Confidence
- **Oversmoothing reduction (Mechanism 1):** High (strong ablations, theoretical grounding in RF).
- **Negligible memory overhead (Mechanism 2):** High (scaling argument, empirical evidence).
- **CFG compatibility (Mechanism 3):** Medium-High (consistent gains but plateau at high CFG).
