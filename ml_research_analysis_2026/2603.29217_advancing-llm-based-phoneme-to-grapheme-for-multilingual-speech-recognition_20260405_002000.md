# Advancing LLM-based phoneme-to-grapheme for multilingual speech recognition

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29217  
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29217  
- **References:** 0  
- **Result:** Average WER reduced from 10.56% to 7.66% on CV-Lang10 via S-SKM and oversampling; unified multilingual model achieves 8.22% weighted WER, competitive with language-specific WFST (9.20%).

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles scaling LLM-based phoneme-to-grapheme (P2G) conversion for multilingual speech recognition, addressing two core challenges: language-aware grapheme generation from a universal phoneme space and severe cross-language training data imbalance. The proposed solution combines a simplified robustness training method (S-SKM) that efficiently approximates CTC path marginalization using sampled paths, and a simple oversampling strategy for low-resource languages. On the ten-language CV-Lang10 benchmark, these techniques yield a 2.9% absolute reduction in average WER. The resulting unified multilingual P2G model, conditioned on a language identifier token, matches or exceeds the performance of traditional language-specific WFST graphs while eliminating the need for per-language decoding resources.

## Method Summary
The system uses a two-stage cascade: a Conformer-CTC S2P model first converts speech to phoneme sequences; a decoder-only LLM (Qwen3) then maps phonemes to graphemes. For robustness, the S2P model’s CTC output is used during P2G training to sample multiple phoneme hypotheses per utterance. Simplified Sampling-K Marginalization (S-SKM) samples *K* CTC state paths, collapses each to a phoneme sequence, and trains the P2G model with equal-weight loss averaging over these alternatives—avoiding costly CTC probability weighting. To counter data imbalance, low-resource languages (<240h) are oversampled to match that exposure. At inference, S2P beam search produces *K* phoneme hypotheses; the P2G model generates candidate graphemes for each, and candidates are rescored using the S2P probabilities. The P2G model uses a prepended `<lid>` token to condition generation on the target language.

## Key Results
- Average WER reduced from 10.56% to 7.66% (absolute) on CV-Lang10 using S-SKM + oversampling.
- Unified multilingual LLM-P2G achieves 8.22% hours-weighted average WER, outperforming a language-specific WFST baseline (9.20% weighted).
- Language identification accuracy exceeds 97% for all languages, demonstrating effective implicit LID via the `<lid>` token.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Simplified Sampling-K Marginalization (S-SKM)
**Claim:** S-SKM provides computationally efficient robustness training for the P2G model by approximating expected likelihood under S2P uncertainty without requiring per-hypothesis CTC probability computation.  
**Mechanism:** During fine-tuning, the S2P model’s CTC posterior is sampled *K* times to yield distinct CTC state paths `π_k`. Each path is deterministically collapsed to a phoneme sequence `h_k = B(π_k)`. The P2G loss is averaged equally over these *K* training targets, approximating the marginal log-likelihood `log p(y|x) ≈ (1/K) Σ log p(y|h_k)`. This Monte Carlo estimator is unbiased and eliminates the forward-backward algorithm.  
**Core assumption:** The variance from equal-weight averaging is acceptable and does not harm convergence relative to CTC probability-weighted SKM.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes S-SKM “avoids CTC-based S2P probability weighting”; section 3.3 provides the unbiased estimator formulation and states it “removes the need for running the forward algorithm.” monolingual ablations show near-identical results to SKM.

### Mechanism 2: Low-resource oversampling
**Claim:** Simple oversampling of low-resource language utterances mitigates severe cross-language data imbalance, improving low-resource WER without degrading high-resource performance.  
**Mechanism:** For any language with training hours `H_l < 240`, utterances are repeatedly sampled within epochs until effective exposure reaches `H_tgt = 240` hours. This increases gradient updates for low-resource languages, counteracting optimizer bias toward high-resource data.  
**Core assumption:** The bottleneck is insufficient exposure to language-specific patterns, not fundamental incompatibility of shared phonemes; oversampling overcomes this without catastrophic interference.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes oversampling for “severe cross-language data imbalance”; section 5.2 reports it “does not harm high-resource languages” while reducing unweighted average WER; Table 2 shows large gains for ky (10.07%→2.82%) and other low-resource languages.

### Mechanism 3: Unified multilingual P2G with `<lid>` token
**Claim:** A unified LLM-P2G model with a prepended language identifier token reliably performs language-aware grapheme generation, eliminating per-language decoding graphs.  
**Mechanism:** Input is formatted as `<ipa>{phonemes} | <lid>{language_id}`. The decoder is trained to first generate the correct `<lid>` token, then the language-specific graphemes. The model learns to condition orthography on this token, jointly performing implicit LID and generation.  
**Core assumption:** The phoneme sequence alone contains sufficient signal (via phoneme distribution, frequency, co-occurrence) for the LLM to reliably infer language, making `<lid>` an effective conditioner.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 3.1 describes the format and states it “encourages the model to jointly handle language identification (LID) and orthography generation”; section 5.2 & Table 3 show LID accuracy >97% for all languages, macro average >98.8%.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: CTC Path Marginalization & Sampling**  
  *Why needed:* S-SKM approximates marginalization over exponential CTC alignments. CTC defines a distribution over frame-level labelings (including blanks), not just phoneme sequences; sampling paths captures alignment uncertainty better than sampling phoneme sequences.  
  *Quick check:* What is the difference between sampling a phoneme sequence from an S2P model (e.g., beam search) and sampling a CTC *state path* `π`? Why does the latter better capture alignment uncertainty?
  
- **Concept: Multilingual Tokenization with Explicit Conditioning**  
  *Why needed:* A single LLM must generate multiple orthographies. The `<lid>` token is explicit language conditioning; without it, the model must infer language implicitly from phonemes, which is harder.  
  *Quick check:* In the input format `<ipa>{p} | <lid>{g}`, what roles do the pipe `|` and special tokens `<ipa>`/`<lid>` play? How does this differ from just concatenating phonemes and graphemes?
  
- **Concept: Training Data Oversampling for Class/Language Imbalance**  
  *Why needed:* The multilingual set has >100x hour disparity; standard SGD would spend >95% of updates on high-resource data. Oversampling increases effective sample size of minority languages.  
  *Quick check:* If you oversample low-resource utterances by factor `F`, how does this change (a) parameter updates per epoch, and (b) expected contribution of each language’s gradients? What is a potential downside?

## Architecture Onboarding
S2P Module -> Hypothesis Generator -> P2G LLM Module -> LID & Decoding Module

**Critical path:**  
(a) For each utterance, obtain S2P CTC outputs.  
(b) Generate *K* training phoneme sequences via clean lexicon lookup, DANP, or S-SKM (sampled paths).  
(c) Create *K* training examples: input=`<ipa>`+phonemes, output=`<lid>`+reference text.  
(d) Compute P2G loss averaged over the *K* samples per utterance.  
Key engineering: manage *K*-fold batch expansion efficiently.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **S-SKM vs SKM:** S-SKM trades statistical efficiency (probabilistic weighting) for computational simplicity (no CTC forward-backward). Evidence suggests positive tradeoff in large multilingual settings.  
- **Oversampling target `H_tgt`:** `H_tgt=240h` is heuristic (above smallest high-resource, Italian 271.5h). Too low under-balances; too high risks overfitting low-resource data and reduces high-resource exposure.  
- **Unified vs language-specific:** Unified model sacrifices some peak performance on very low-resource languages for infrastructure simplicity (no WFST graphs) and better weighted average WER.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Systemic LID failure:** `<lid>` accuracy below ~90% macro average likely causes grapheme errors.  
- **S2P-P2G mismatch:** High S2P PER (>10%) coupled with poor gains from robustness methods indicates S2P quality is the bottleneck.  
- **Oversampling instability:** If oversampling causes >0.5% abs WER increase on high-resource languages (en, es, fr, it), interference is occurring.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Replicate monolingual S-SKM vs SKM ablation on one language (e.g., pl) with fixed S2P; expect <0.2% WER difference.  
2. Isolate oversampling effect within multilingual S-SKM (E3 vs E4); expect large gains on low-resource (<5% abs) and neutral on high-resource.  
3. Inspect LID confusion matrix under oversampling (E4); watch for per-language drift that correlates with WER changes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can pronunciation-lexicon-free end-to-end training be effectively implemented for joint S2P and P2G modeling in multilingual ASR?  
  *Basis:* Conclusion notes “incorporating pronunciation-lexicon free end-to-end training … is potentially interesting future work.” Unresolved because current cascade avoids error propagation but requires lexicons; joint training would need modeling variable-length phonemes and orthographies within one framework.

- **Open Question 2:** For languages with high S2P error rates (e.g., Swedish), what complementary improvements to S2P modeling (beyond P2G robustness) are most effective in reducing overall ASR WER?  
  *Basis:* Inferred from observation that robustness/oversampling improve but do not fully close gaps for high-PER languages; S2P quality and P2G robustness should be considered jointly.

- **Open Question 3:** Can more sophisticated data balancing strategies (e.g., loss weighting, dynamic oversampling) outperform the fixed-target oversampling used for low-resource languages in multilingual LLM-P2G?  
  *Basis:* Inferred: fixed oversampling (H_tgt=240h) is a heuristic; optimality and risks of overfitting/suboptimal gradients remain unknown.

## Limitations
- S2P model (Whistle-large) details rely on unreported reference [6]; reproduction requires unavailable specifics.  
- Decoding parameter *S* (candidates per phoneme hypothesis) is undisclosed, affecting final WER.  
- Break conditions for S-SKM and oversampling are untested; performance may degrade under higher S2P uncertainty or greater language divergence.

## Confidence
- **S-SKM:** Medium. Monolingual ablation shows parity with SKM, but variance across S2P confidence regimes uncharacterized.  
- **Oversampling:** Medium. Gains demonstrated on CV-Lang10 with single target; optimality and overfitting risks unknown.  
- **Unified `<lid>` P2G:** Medium. Achieves >97% LID and weighted WER 8.22% vs WFST 9.20%, but cross-dataset generalization unproven.

## Next Checks
1. **S-SKM variance analysis:** On a fixed language, vary S2P quality (beam size, noise) and compare S-SKM vs SKM WER stability and compute cost.  
2. **Oversampling sensitivity sweep:** Test oversampling targets (120h, 240h, 480h) on CV-Lang10; monitor per-language WER, especially high-resource degradation.  
3. **Cross-dataset transfer:** Evaluate full pipeline on external multilingual corpus (e.g., MLS) without adaptation to assess domain robustness.
