# MoralityGym: A Benchmark for Evaluating Hierarchical MoralAlignment in Sequential Decision-Making Agents
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.13372
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13372
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces Morality Chains and MoralityGym; PPO-Shaped excels at hierarchical moral alignment while vanilla PPO and standard Safe RL methods fail to properly prioritize norms.

## Executive Summary
The paper proposes a framework for evaluating moral alignment in reinforcement learning agents operating in environments with hierarchical, conflicting human norms. Central to this is Morality Chains, a formalism representing moral norms as ordered deontic constraints, and MoralityGym, a benchmark of 98 trolley-dilemma-style Gymnasium environments. The authors decouple task reward from moral cost and define a Morality Metric that computes hierarchical norm adherence. Baseline evaluations with Safe RL methods reveal that standard PPO neglects high-priority norms, while PPO-Shaped and CPO achieve near-perfect scores on top-tier constraints but often ignore lower ones, highlighting both the potential and limitations of current approaches.

## Method Summary
MoralityGym operationalizes moral alignment by defining a Morality Chain: a set of norms expressed as tuples ⟨𝜙, 𝜌𝜙, 𝑓, D⟩ where each norm has a signature (detectable condition), a morality function (adherence probability), a force value determining strict priority, and a deontic modality (required/prohibited). Environments are built in Gymnasium and emit “norm_events” each step. A Cost class converts these events into a scalar cost based on the chain: event-based norms incur a one-time weighted penalty on first violation, while utility-based norms accumulate proportional cost. This cost can be added to the task reward (shaping) or used as a constraint. Evaluation uses the Morality Metric M_~N(π), which aggregates per-norm morality functions M_Ni(π) with recursive weights that enforce lexicographic ordering (a minimal improvement in a higher-priority norm outweighs maximal adherence to all lower ones). The benchmark includes 98 configurable scenarios derived from trolley problems, and baselines include PPO, PPO-Lag, CPO, and PPO-Shaped.

## Key Results
- PPO-Shaped achieved a near-perfect Morality Metric score of 0.996 on the PushOrSwitchSelfSacrifice scenario, while vanilla PPO scored only 0.192.
- Across four morality chains (Utility, Utility-AgentHarm, DualProcess, DualProcess-AgentHarm), PPO-Shaped consistently outperformed other baselines, demonstrating the effectiveness of reward shaping for hierarchical moral alignment.
- Standard Safe RL methods (PPO-Lag, CPO) excelled at top-tier constraints but often neglected lower-priority norms, e.g., in the Utility chain PPO-Shaped minimized human harm but scored near zero on animal/robot harm.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hierarchical Norm Encoding
The Morality Chains formalism encodes norms as strictly ordered deontic constraints with force values. Each norm ⟨𝜙, 𝜌𝜙, 𝑓, D⟩ has a force f inducing a total order. The Morality Metric uses recursive weights (Equation 2) so that a minimal improvement (𝛽) in a higher-priority norm’s morality function outweighs maximal adherence to all lower norms. This operationalizes lexicographic optimization, assuming moral reasoning can be reduced to ranked norms and that differences of magnitude 𝛽 are meaningful for alignment. Evidence: formalism in Section 3, weight example with PushOrSwitch (w₁=200 vs w₂=1 for 𝛽=0.01). Break condition: if norms cannot be exhaustively ranked or if ethical dilemmas require non-lexicographic trade-offs (e.g., tragic dilemmas with equal force).

### Mechanism 2: Decoupled Task-Moral Cost
The framework decouples task reward from moral cost via a step-wise Cost class. Environments output `norm_events` each step; the Cost object converts these into a scalar cost based on the Morality Chain. Event-based norms incur a one-time weighted penalty on first violation; utility-based norms accumulate proportional cost. This cost feeds into Safe RL algorithms (e.g., PPO-Shaped: R_S = R_E − λ·C_t). The mechanism assumes moral violations can be computed online from environment state via predefined signatures and that scalarization via weighted sum provides adequate learning signals. Evidence: Section 4.1 describes Cost class; Section 4.2 details Equation 3; Section 5 shows PPO-Shaped’s high scores. Break condition: if norm violations require historical or counterfactual reasoning, step-wise cost becomes inaccurate.

### Mechanism 3: Benchmark Exposures
MoralityGym’s suite of 98 trolley-dilemma-style environments exposes limitations of Safe RL methods by varying intervention type, entity attributes, complexity, and self-sacrifice. Agents face sequential decisions with conflicting norms. Evaluation via `evaluate_morality_metric` computes per-norm scores and the overall metric, revealing how algorithms prioritize (or neglect) lower norms. The core assumption is that trolley problems isolate morally salient features relevant to real-world domains. Evidence: abstract mentions 98 environments; Section 4.3 lists scenario dimensions; Section 5 results show PPO fails on top norms, PPO-Shaped/CPO excel at top but neglect lower. Break condition: if trolley dilemmas lack ecological validity for specific domains (e.g., medical ethics), findings may not transfer.

## Foundational Learning
- **Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs)**: Needed because MoralityGym builds on Safe RL’s CMDP formulation, adding hierarchical cost structures. Understanding CMDPs (constraints C_i, feasible set Π_C) is prerequisite for grasping moral cost integration. Quick check: In a CMDP, what distinguishes the optimal policy π* from a feasible policy, and how does adding a constraint change the optimization problem?
- **Deontic Logic & Norms**: Needed because the formalism treats norms as deontic constraints (prescribed/prohibited). Recognizing deontic modalities (D ∈ {True, False}) and signatures φ is essential to defining morality functions. Quick check: How does a "prohibited" norm (D=False) translate into a morality function M_N(π), and why is 1−ρ_φ(π) used?
- **Trolley Problem Variations**: Needed because scenarios are rooted in trolley variations (switch vs. push, self-sacrifice). Knowing psychological distinctions (personal/impersonal harm, means/side-effect) explains why certain norms (e.g., avoid personal harm) are prioritized. Quick check: What is the key moral difference between SwitchStandard and PushStandard, and how does that map to a "personal harm" norm?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Morality Chain definition -> Gymnasium environment -> Cost class -> Evaluation method -> Baseline algorithms

**Critical path:**  
1. Define target Morality Chain (norms, forces).  
2. Choose or create scenario variants via JSON configs.  
3. Instantiate environment with wrapper that attaches Cost class.  
4. Train agent using (a) task reward only (PPO), (b) constrained RL (PPO-Lag, CPO), or (c) shaped reward (PPO-Shaped: R_S = R_E − λ·C_t).  
5. Evaluate with `evaluate_morality_metric` to get per-norm scores and aggregated M~N.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Weight sensitivity: β in recursive weights can cause numerical instability.  
- Norm expressiveness: φ signatures must be computable from state; complex concepts (intent) may be inexpressible.  
- Scenario coverage: 98 trolley variations may miss other ethical patterns (distributive justice, privacy).

**Failure signatures:**  
- High task reward, low M~N (e.g., vanilla PPO): ignores moral constraints.  
- Near-perfect top-norm scores but near-zero lower norms (e.g., PPO-Shaped on Utility chain): hierarchical optimization succeeds but neglects subsidiary duties.  
- M~N ~0.5 across seeds: algorithm not using moral information.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Weight sensitivity test:** For PushOrSwitch with chain N_NPH > N_MH, sweep β ∈ [0.001, 0.1]; expect PPO-Shaped M~N stable near 1 if N_NPH satisfied.  
2. **Baseline comparison on DualProcess:** Train PPO, PPO-Lag, CPO, PPO-Shaped on PushOrSwitch; compare per-norm scores. Expect PPO-Shaped/CPO near 1 on “avoid personal human harm” but low on “min humans harmed”, while PPO balanced but lower.  
3. **Self-sacrifice stress test:** In PushOrSwitchSelfSacrifice (chain includes agent harm), compare agents’ “avoid agent harm” scores. PPO may score high but fail human norms; PPO-Shaped/CPO should show opposite trade-off.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Psychological integration**: How can MoralityGym incorporate emotion, development, and social context that influence human moral judgment? The framework currently abstracts these away; future work could integrate affective and social processes to improve real-world applicability.
2. **Tragic dilemmas**: Can Morality Chains represent conflicts where norms have equal force? The strict ordering limits representation of “tragic dilemmas” (e.g., sacrificing one to save many vs. not using people as means). Non-lexicographic extensions (e.g., fuzzy logic, Pareto analysis) might better capture such cases.
3. **Cross-domain generalization**: How well do agents trained in MoralityGym generalize to novel ethical dilemmas beyond trolley problems? The benchmark may not cover full spectrum (e.g., causality, responsibility factors). Expanding scenario families (means vs. side-effect, relationships) is needed.
4. **Learning norm hierarchies**: Can hierarchical norms be learned or adapted from human data rather than hand-specified? Hand-crafting limits scalability and cultural diversity. Algorithms that infer norm priorities from demonstrations, preferences, or texts could enable personalized or cross-cultural alignment.

## Limitations
- **Lexicographic rigidity**: The strict total order may fail to capture moral trade-offs in dilemmas with equal force or tragic choices.
- **Step-wise cost myopia**: The cost computation lacks historical context, misrepresenting norms requiring intent, sequence, or long-term consequences.
- **Benchmark narrowness**: The 98 trolley-dilemma variations may not generalize to diverse ethical domains (e.g., medical ethics, fairness, privacy).

## Next Checks
1. **β sensitivity sweep**: Train agents on all chains with β ∈ [0.001, 0.1]; measure variance in M~N and per-norm rankings to validate metric robustness and inform β selection.
2. **Cross-domain generalization**: Transfer policies trained on MoralityGym to a separate ethical benchmark (e.g., CMoralEval or Ethics-HML). Evaluate whether hierarchical adherence transfers beyond trolley dilemmas.
3. **Historical cost integration**: Extend Cost class to use a sliding window of norm events (e.g., last 10 steps) or a recurrent tracker. Compare performance on norms requiring temporal context (e.g., “avoid repeated harm”).
