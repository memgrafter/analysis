# Look Inward to Explore Outward: Learning Temperature Policy from LLM Internal States via Hierarchical RL

## Quick Facts
- ArXiv ID: 2602.13035
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13035
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: IntroLLM, a hierarchical RL framework for dynamic temperature control, achieves 40.91% Avg@8 and 73.50% Pass@8 on average math benchmarks with Qwen3-4B, surpassing strongest baseline by 1.25% and 4.29%.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Introspective LLM (IntroLLM), a novel approach to reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) that addresses the limitation of static or heuristic sampling temperatures. The core idea is to train a lightweight temperature policy that leverages the model's internal hidden states to dynamically adjust the sampling temperature at each decoding step. This policy is jointly optimized with the token policy via a coordinate ascent scheme using GRPO, enabling the model to adapt its exploration based on reasoning uncertainty. Experiments on mathematical reasoning benchmarks demonstrate consistent improvements over fixed-temperature and heuristic baselines, with minimal computational overhead.

## Method Summary
IntroLLM frames temperature selection as a hierarchical decision: a high-level temperature policy observes the current hidden state and outputs a mixed discrete-continuous action (a Bernoulli variable controlling whether to update the temperature and a Beta-distributed value for the new temperature). The low-level token policy then samples tokens using the chosen temperature. Both policies are trained from downstream rewards (binary correctness) via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). To avoid non-stationary dynamics, updates are alternated: first the token policy is updated with temperatures fixed from rollouts, then the temperature policy is updated with token choices fixed. The method is applied to Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-4B models fine-tuned on the MATH dataset, showing gains on AIME24, AMC23, MATH-500, and other benchmarks.

## Key Results
- On Qwen3-4B-Base, IntroLLM achieves 40.91% Avg@8 and 73.50% Pass@8 on average math benchmarks, surpassing the strongest baseline by 1.25% and 4.29% respectively.
- It demonstrates improved out-of-domain generalization to unseen math problems and maintains higher policy entropy during training, mitigating collapse.
- The approach adds negligible computational overhead (≈0.122% parameter increase) and minimal change to FLOPs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hidden-state-driven temperature modulation
- Claim: The temperature policy learns to modulate sampling stochasticity based on hidden states, aligning exploration intensity with reasoning uncertainty at each decoding step.
- Mechanism: A lightweight MLP head maps the model's hidden state \(h_t\) to a mixed discrete-continuous action: a Bernoulli variable \(c_t\) decides whether to update temperature, and if so, a Beta distribution samples a new temperature \(\tau_t\) from \([\tau_{\min}, \tau_{\max}]\).
- Core assumption: The hidden state \(h_t\) encodes sufficient information about the current reasoning phase and uncertainty.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract: "allows the model to observe its internal state during generation and decide how much stochasticity to inject at each step." Section 4.2: "temperature policy \(\pi_\phi\) that observes hidden state \(h_t\) and selects sampling temperature \(\tau_t\)" and "enables the model to introspect its own uncertainty and generation context."
- Break condition: If hidden states fail to reliably signal reasoning uncertainty, the temperature policy may misallocate exploration, harming performance.

### Mechanism 2: Coordinate ascent stabilization
- Claim: Coordinate ascent optimization stabilizes joint training of token and temperature policies by decoupling gradient updates, preventing non-stationary dynamics.
- Mechanism: Within each GRPO iteration, the token policy \(\pi_\theta\) is updated first with temperatures \(\{\tau_t\}\) fixed from the rollout, then the temperature policy \(\pi_\phi\) is updated with token choices \(y_t\) fixed.
- Core assumption: Separating updates allows each policy to effectively optimize under the current behavior of the other.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract: "Temperature and token policies are jointly optimized from downstream rewards using a coordinate ascent scheme." Section 4.3: "We address this through coordinate ascent, alternating between token policy updates (with fixed temperatures \(\tau\)) and temperature policy updates (with fixed tokens \(y\))."
- Break condition: If the optimal policies are highly co-adapted, alternating updates might converge to suboptimal equilibria.

### Mechanism 3: Mixed action space for stability
- Claim: The mixed discrete-continuous action space (Bernoulli + Beta) balances exploration flexibility with training stability by learning both when to adjust temperature and how much.
- Mechanism: The discrete Bernoulli component \(c_t\) gates whether temperature changes, while the continuous Beta component parameterizes the magnitude of change when \(c_t=1\). This reduces unnecessary temperature fluctuations.
- Core assumption: Not all decoding steps benefit from temperature adjustment; selective updates are sufficient and more stable.
- Evidence anchors: Section 4.2: "This design allows the model to learn both when temperature adaptation is beneficial and how much adjustment is required, while avoiding unnecessary variance from frequent changes." Section 5.3 ablation: The "Always-update" variant underperforms both prompt-level and IntroLLM's selective approach.
- Break condition: If reasoning tasks require fine-grained temperature control at nearly every step, the Bernoulli gating might be too sparse.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)
  - Why needed: RLVR provides a training paradigm where rewards are based on final answer correctness (e.g., math solutions), not step-wise process rewards. This sparsity makes exploration critical, and temperature directly controls trajectory diversity.
  - Quick check: How does RLVR differ from standard RL with dense rewards, and why does this make sampling temperature a key lever?

- **Concept**: Sampling Temperature in Autoregressive Decoding
  - Why needed: Temperature \(\tau\) scales logits before softmax, modulating policy entropy: higher \(\tau\) increases exploration, lower \(\tau\) favors exploitation. In RLVR, this affects the diversity of sampled trajectories for policy gradient updates.
  - Quick check: What is the mathematical effect of temperature on the token probability distribution, and how does it influence exploration vs. exploitation?

- **Concept**: Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL)
  - Why needed: IntroLLM frames temperature selection as a high-level policy over token-level generation, forming a two-level hierarchy. This separates exploration control (temperature) from content generation (tokens).
  - Quick check: In HRL, what are the roles of the manager (high-level) and worker (low-level) policies, and how do they interact in IntroLLM?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Base LLM decoder -> (hidden state h_t) -> Temperature policy head (MLP) -> (c_t, z_t) -> Temperature τ_t -> Token policy (Softmax(logits/τ_t)) -> Token y_t. GRPO optimizer computes group-relative advantages and updates both policies. Coordinate ascent controller alternates updates.

- **Critical path** (training):
  1. Sample prompt batch D.
  2. For each prompt, rollout G trajectories: at each step t, temperature policy decides (c_t, z_t) -> τ_t; token policy samples y_t with τ_t.
  3. Compute verifiable reward R(x,y) and group advantages.
  4. Update token policy θ with fixed τ (from rollout).
  5. Update temperature policy ϕ with fixed y.
  6. Repeat.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Mixed action space vs. fully continuous: Discrete gating reduces variance but may miss fine-grained control; continuous-only could be unstable.
  - Coordinate ascent vs. simultaneous updates: Alternating stabilizes but may slow convergence; simultaneous risks non-stationarity.
  - Lightweight temperature head: Minimal parameter overhead (~0.1%) but capacity limited; deeper heads might improve performance but increase cost.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Temperature policy collapses to constant τ.
  - Training instability: high variance in loss curves or rapid entropy collapse in token policy.
  - No difficulty scaling: Average temperature does not increase with problem difficulty.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Ablation: Compare "Always-update" (every token) vs. IntroLLM's selective Bernoulli gating on validation accuracy and training stability.
  2. Analysis: Plot predicted temperatures across MATH-500 difficulty levels (L1–L5) to verify positive correlation.
  3. Rollout inspection: Visualize per-token temperatures on sample MATH-500 problems to check for "reasoning rhythm" (peaks at logical pivots, valleys during computation).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Can IntroLLM jointly optimize multiple decoding parameters (top-p, top-k) with temperature?
- Basis in paper: The conclusion states the work opens avenues for optimizing other generation-time decisions.
- Why unresolved: Only temperature was explored; joint control effects are unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extend the framework to multi-parameter control, train on math benchmarks, compare performance and stability.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does incorporating a memory of past hidden states into the temperature policy improve reasoning rhythm capture?
- Basis in paper: The current policy uses only h_t (Sec 4.2) despite rhythmic patterns (Fig 3).
- Why unresolved: The benefit of temporal context is not investigated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Add a recurrent module, evaluate on reasoning tasks, compare accuracy and entropy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the learned temperature policy generalize to other NLG tasks (code, dialogue) without fine-tuning?
- Basis in paper: Evaluation is restricted to math and reasoning benchmarks.
- Why unresolved: Transferability to diverse tasks remains untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Apply the pretrained policy to code/dialogue datasets, measure task-specific metrics, compare to fixed-temperature baselines.

## Limitations
- The paper assumes hidden states encode reasoning uncertainty but provides no direct evidence (e.g., probing).
- Hyperparameter settings (learning rates, temperature bounds, group size) are not fully justified and may be dataset-specific.
- Evaluation is restricted to mathematics benchmarks; generalization to other domains remains unknown.
