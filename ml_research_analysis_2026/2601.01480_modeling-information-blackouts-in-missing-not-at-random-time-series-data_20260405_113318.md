# Modeling Information Blackouts in Missing Not-At-Random Time Series Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.01480
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01480
- **Reference count**: 15
- **Primary result**: Proposes a latent state-space model with MNAR missingness that reduces imputation RMSE from 7.02 (LOCF) to 4.23 (~64% MSE reduction) on Seattle traffic data, with an additional 0.8% gain from explicit MNAR modeling.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of sensor blackouts in traffic time series, which are typically Missing Not At Random (MNAR) but often treated as Missing At Random (MAR). The authors introduce a latent state-space model that combines linear dynamical traffic dynamics with a Bernoulli observation channel whose dropout probability depends on the underlying latent state. Inference is performed using an Extended Kalman Filter with Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoothing, and parameters are learned via an approximate expectation-maximization algorithm. On real Seattle loop detector data, incorporating latent dynamics yields a dramatic reduction in imputation error (RMSE from 7.02 to 4.23). Explicitly modeling the MNAR mechanism provides a small but consistent further improvement. Controlled synthetic experiments with state-dependent blackouts confirm that the MNAR advantage grows stronger when the missingness is more informative. The work demonstrates that capturing temporal dynamics is essential, while principled MNAR modeling offers a valuable refinement, especially in regimes where missingness is genuinely informative.

## Method Summary
The model is a linear dynamical system (LDS) with a latent state \(z_t \in \mathbb{R}^K\) evolving as \(z_t \sim \mathcal{N}(A z_{t-1}, Q)\). Observations \(x_t\) (traffic speeds) are generated from \(x_t \sim \mathcal{N}(C z_t, R)\). Missingness indicators \(m_{t,d}\) for detector \(d\) follow a Bernoulli distribution with success probability \(\pi_{t,d} = \sigma(b_d + \phi_d^\top z_t + \psi_d^\top f_t + \eta_d^\top g_d)\), where \(\sigma\) is the logistic function and \(f_t, g_t\) are optional features. During inference, an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) integrates both the observed speeds (when available) and a Gaussian-approximated pseudo-observation derived from the linearized Bernoulli likelihood. Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoothing then yields full-trajectory state estimates. Learning proceeds via an approximate EM algorithm: the E-step performs EKF forward filtering and RTS backward smoothing to compute expected sufficient statistics; the M-step updates the LDS parameters \((A, Q, C, R)\) in closed form and updates the missingness parameters (e.g., \(\phi_d\)) via gradient ascent. Training typically begins with a MAR LDS (ignoring state dependence) and then warms up the MNAR model. Key hyperparameters include latent dimension \(K=20\) and diagonal observation noise \(R\).

## Key Results
- On Seattle data, modeling latent dynamics (MAR LDS) reduces imputation RMSE from 7.02 (LOCF) to 4.23, a ~64% reduction in MSE.
- Explicit MNAR modeling further lowers imputation RMSE to 4.20, a 0.8% relative improvement over MAR.
- In synthetic METR-LA experiments with state-dependent blackouts, MNAR improves imputation RMSE (5.48 → 5.35) and 6-step forecast RMSE (5.11 → 4.79).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Latent dynamics capture traffic evolution during blackouts
The model propagates a low-dimensional latent state \(z_t\) through linear dynamics even when observations are missing. This allows the system to infer plausible internal states during outages, which are then mapped to observations. The core assumption is that traffic evolution can be approximated as a time-invariant linear-Gaussian process over short horizons. Evidence: The large RMSE drop from LOCF to MAR LDS on both real and synthetic data. A break condition would be if traffic dynamics are highly nonlinear or non-stationary over blackout durations, causing the linear model to fail.

### Mechanism 2: State-dependent missingness provides a consistent refinement
The Bernoulli dropout probability depends on the latent state \(z_t\). During inference, the missingness pattern acts as a pseudo-observation that refines the latent state estimate via the EKF update. This mechanism leverages the informative nature of blackouts: they are more likely under certain traffic conditions. The core assumption is that sensor blackouts systematically correlate with the unobserved traffic state, not just observed data. Evidence: On Seattle data, MNAR yields a small but consistent improvement over MAR; synthetic experiments show larger gains as the true state-dependence strengthens. A break condition would be if blackouts are truly MAR or the dependence is too weak to estimate reliably, leading to overfitting.

### Mechanism 3: Approximate EKF + EM makes joint learning tractable
The nonlinear Bernoulli likelihood is Gaussianized around the EKF prediction and treated as a pseudo-observation. EM alternates between smoothing (E-step) and maximizing a lower-bound, with missingness parameters updated via gradient. The core assumption is that local linearization of the logistic function is sufficiently accurate. Evidence: The EM objective increases steadily; ablation shows robustness to variance modeling details. A break condition would be if the missingness function is highly nonlinear in \(z_t\) or latent uncertainty is large, causing poor approximation and filter divergence.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: The system consists of: (1) Latent State Module (\(z_t \in \mathbb{R}^K\)); (2) Linear Dynamics (\(A, Q\)); (3) Observation Model (\(C, R\) for speeds); (4) MNAR Channel (Bernoulli dropout with log-odds linear in \(z_t\) and detector features); (5) Inference Engine (EKF forward filter + RTS smoother); (6) Learning Loop (approximate EM); (7) Downstream Tasks (imputation and \(k\)-step forecasting).

**Critical path**: Data → Preprocess (mask blackouts) → Initialize MAR LDS → Train MAR via EM → Warm-start MNAR parameters → Train MNAR via EM (with missingness gradient updates) → Evaluate on held-out masks.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Linearity vs expressivity: Linear dynamics enable tractable EKF/RTS but may underfit complex traffic; nonlinear SSMs require more complex inference (e.g., particle filters).
- Approximation fidelity: Gaussianizing the Bernoulli likelihood is simple but crude; using a tighter variational bound could improve accuracy at cost of complexity.
- Parameter sharing: Detector-specific \(\phi_d\) offers flexibility but increases parameters; a global \(\phi\) would be more robust with less data.
- EM stability: Approximate E-step introduces bias; regularization (e.g., shrinkage of \(A\), cap on \(\text{tr}(Q)\)) is used heuristically.

**Failure signatures**:
- EM objective decreases or oscillates → indicates instability; reduce missingness learning rate or increase regularization.
- Imputation RMSE >5.0 on Seattle → likely due to poor masking protocol, bad initialization, or EKF divergence during long blackouts.
- MNAR does not improve over MAR → check that missingness gradient updates are active and that the MNAR channel is included.
- Forecast errors grow rapidly with horizon → could indicate overfitting to short-term smoothing, poor \(A\) matrix, or inability of LDS to capture long memory.
- Data shape mismatch → ensure preprocessing to regular 5-minute intervals and proper handling of naturally missing data.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Baseline sanity check**: Run LOCF and linear interpolation on a short, clean blackout. Verify MAR LDS achieves substantial RMSE reduction (<5.0 on Seattle) to confirm dynamics are working.
2. **MAR vs MNAR on real data**: On the aligned validation pool (300 windows), run both models. Confirm MNAR RMSE is consistently lower (even if by ~0.03) and that the gap is stable across random seeds.
3. **Synthetic MNAR strength sweep**: On METR-LA with injected state-dependent blackouts, vary the true \(\phi\) magnitude. Verify MNAR advantage grows with dependence strength (ΔRMSE becomes more negative) as in Table III/Fig 7.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Nonlinear or graph-structured dynamics**: Can extending the model to nonlinear or graph-structured state-space models significantly improve imputation and forecasting, especially during prolonged blackouts? The current linear-Gaussian assumption is a noted limitation.
2. **Weak MNAR gains in real data**: Does the modest MNAR improvement (~0.8%) on Seattle reflect genuinely weak state-dependent missingness or model misspecification (e.g., inadequate missingness parameterization or inference approximation)?
3. **Operational conditions for MNAR benefit**: Under what conditions (e.g., blackout duration, time-of-day, detector type) does MNAR modeling provide the most practical benefit over MAR dynamics alone? The paper notes heterogeneous improvements but does not analyze drivers.
4. **Exogenous covariates for missingness**: Does enriching the missingness model with explicit covariates (e.g., time-of-day/week, network-wide outage indicators, detector health statistics) improve identification of MNAR mechanisms and overall accuracy? The conclusion identifies this as a key future direction.

## Limitations
- **Linearity & Gaussian approximations**: The linear-Gaussian dynamics and Gaussianized Bernoulli likelihood may not capture nonlinear traffic evolution or complex missingness, especially under high uncertainty.
- **Identifiability**: MNAR parameters (e.g., \(\phi_d\)) can be weakly identified from missingness patterns alone, increasing overfitting risk with limited detector data.
- **EM stability**: The approximate E-step introduces bias; training stability depends on heuristic regularization (e.g., \(A\) shrinkage, \(Q\) cap) without theoretical guarantees.

## Confidence
- **Dynamics improvement (LOCF→MAR)**: High
- **MNAR refinement over MAR**: Medium
- **Inference/training stability**: High

## Next Checks
1. **Random seed robustness**: Train MAR and MNAR with multiple seeds (e.g., 5) to confirm the MNAR advantage is statistically significant and not a fluke.
2. **Synthetic parameter recovery**: Simulate data with ground-truth MNAR parameters (varying \(\phi\) magnitudes); fit the model and assess correlation/bias between true and estimated \(\phi\) to validate learning.
3. **Nonlinearity stress test**: Replace the linear dynamics/observations with a neural SSM (using particle filter or VI) and evaluate on extended blackouts (>10 steps). If MNAR still outperforms MAR, the benefit is not solely due to the linear assumption.
