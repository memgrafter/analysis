# Believe Your Model: Distribution-Guided Confidence Calibration

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.03872
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03872
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on multiple reasoning benchmarks, e.g., 77.84% on DeepSeek-R1-8B (+3.09% over prior best) and 76.53% on Qwen3-32B (+1.31%).

## Executive Summary
Confidence-based test-time scaling methods for large reasoning models often fail to fully utilize the information in confidence distributions, leading to selections that are confident yet incorrect. This work proposes **DistriVoting**, a post-processing technique that separates the mixed confidence distribution into positive and negative components via a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM), applies a reject filter to eliminate false positives, and uses hierarchical voting to aggregate the remaining trajectories. Additionally, **SelfStepConf** (SSC) dynamically steers inference by monitoring per-step confidence and injecting a reflection token when confidence declines, thereby increasing the separation between the positive and negative confidence distributions. Experiments across 16 model variants and 5 reasoning benchmarks demonstrate consistent and significant gains. For instance, on DeepSeek-R1-8B, the combined approach yields an average accuracy of 77.84%, a 3.09% absolute improvement over the best baseline (WSC-Top50, 74.75%). On Qwen3-32B, accuracy reaches 76.53%, a 1.31% gain over WSC-Top50’s 75.22%. These results establish a new state of the art in confidence-based test-time scaling for reasoning models.

## Method Summary
The objective is to improve confidence-based test-time scaling (TTS) for large reasoning models. Two complementary components are introduced:

- **SelfStepConf (SSC)**: During generation, each reasoning step’s confidence (average negative log-probability over step tokens) is monitored. An exponential moving average threshold τ_conf is maintained. If a step’s confidence C_Gm drops below δ·τ_conf (δ=0.8) *and* is lower than the previous step, a reflection token is injected to force reconsideration, steering the trajectory toward higher-confidence paths.
- **DistriVoting**: After generating a budget of trajectories (e.g., 128), each trajectory’s confidence is taken as the confidence of its final answer-containing step. The method then: (1) fits a two-component GMM to all confidences, assigning the higher-mean component to the positive set V_pos and the lower to V_neg; (2) runs a reject filter: computes a negative consensus answer A_neg via hierarchical voting on V_neg weighted by −C_neg; if A_neg ≠ A_pos (the positive consensus), removes from V_pos any trajectory whose answer equals A_neg (optionally refitting the GMM); (3) performs hierarchical voting on the final V_pos: the confidence range is split into NC intervals, each interval yields a weighted majority answer, and a final weighted majority over interval answers produces the prediction.

The system is evaluated on reasoning benchmarks (HMMT2025, GPQA-D, AIME2024/2025, BRUMO2025) with models such as DeepSeek-R1-8B and Qwen3-32B. With Budget=128 and 64 repeats, the combined SSC+DistriVoting method consistently outperforms prior confidence-based TTS approaches.

## Key Results
- DeepSeek-R1-8B: DistriVoting+SelfStepConf achieves 77.84% average accuracy, a 3.09% absolute gain over WSC-Top50 (74.75%).
- Qwen3-32B: 76.53% accuracy, a 1.31% improvement over WSC-Top50’s 75.22%.
- The method sets a new state-of-the-art across 16 model variants and 5 benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Distribution-Aware Filtering via GMM
- **Claim:** Modeling confidence distributions as a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) separates trajectories into "positive" (likely correct) and "negative" (likely incorrect) components more adaptively than fixed-threshold filtering.
- **Mechanism:** The method fits a two-component GMM to the confidence scores of sampled trajectories. The component with the higher mean is assigned as the positive distribution (`N(µ_pos, σ²_pos)`). Trajectories falling under this component form the initial candidate pool (`V_pos`). This leverages the observed bimodality in confidence scores between correct and incorrect answers.
- **Core assumption:** The confidence distributions of correct and incorrect responses are approximately normal and bimodal, allowing effective separation by mean.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1] "decomposes the mixed confidence distribution into positive and negative components using Gaussian Mixture Models"
  - [section 2.2] models distributions as `X_pos ~ N(µ_pos, σ²_pos)`, `X_neg ~ N(µ_neg, σ²_neg)`
  - [section 4.3, Table 2] shows GMM significantly outperforms Top50, K-Means, and MeanShift in voting accuracy (77.60% vs 74.47% for Top50) and AUROC (0.5831 vs 0.5117).
- **Break condition:** If the confidence distributions are not bimodal or heavily overlapping, GMM separation fails. Evidence: Table 2 shows GMM still works but Table 14 shows substantial overlap remains (`Stage I` to `Stage II` improves accuracy but `Stage II` to `Stage III` shows further gains are possible via reject filtering).

### Mechanism 2: Negative-Answer Rejection
- **Claim:** Using trajectories identified as likely incorrect (`V_neg`) to reject false positives from the candidate positive pool (`V_pos`) reduces overlap and improves final voting reliability.
- **Mechanism:** After GMM filtering, the negative distribution is voted on (using negative confidences as weights) to get a consensus negative answer (`A_neg`). If `A_pos` (voted from `V_pos`) differs from `A_neg`, the `V_pos` trajectories that produced `A_neg` are rejected. The remaining `V_pos` is re-fit via GMM to produce a purer positive distribution for final voting.
- **Core assumption:** The most frequent answer among high-confidence *incorrect* trajectories (`A_neg`) is a reliable indicator of a specific distractor pattern that can be removed from the positive candidate set.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1] "apply a reject filter based on positive/negative samples from them to mitigate overlap"
  - [section 3.3] `A_neg = f_vote(V_neg, -C_neg)` and `if A_pos != A_neg then reject filter...`
  - [section 5.1, Table 4] shows `Reject Filter` (Stage III) consistently improves accuracy and weighted accuracy over `GMM Filter` (Stage II) across benchmarks (e.g., Avg Acc: 80.41% vs 77.60%).
- **Break condition:** If the negative distribution's consensus answer (`A_neg`) is often the same as the true answer, this filter would wrongly discard correct trajectories. Evidence: The method assumes `A_pos != A_neg` is a necessary condition for filtering; if this rarely holds, the filter is inactive.

### Mechanism 3: Step-Level Confidence Steering (SelfStepConf)
- **Claim:** Dynamically monitoring and adjusting generation via step-level confidence triggers reflection when confidence declines, increasing the separation (`δ = µ_pos - µ_neg`) between the positive and negative confidence distributions.
- **Mechanism:** During generation, a moving threshold (`τ_conf`) is maintained. If a reasoning step's confidence (`C_Gm`) drops significantly below `τ_conf` *and* below the previous step, reflection is triggered. Reflection injects a special token (e.g., "wait") to force the model to reconsider, aiming to recover a higher-confidence reasoning path.
- **Core assumption:** Temporary confidence drops indicate a reasoning error or hallucination, and injecting reflection can redirect the trajectory to a higher-quality path, shifting the overall distribution of generated confidences.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] "monitor step-level confidence in real-time... when confidence declines significantly... triggers self-reflection"
  - [section 5.2, Figure 2] shows SSC-generated distributions have less overlap (`δ=5.043` vs `3.182`) and higher correctness density in the positive interval.
  - [section 5.2] Theorem 2.1 proves voting accuracy lower bound increases with `δ`.
  - [section 5.6, Figure 8] shows a concrete trajectory where reflection after a confidence drop led to a correct answer, while BasicInference produced an incorrect one.
- **Break condition:** If reflection tokens are ineffective at steering the model or if confidence drops are not predictive of incorrectness, this mechanism adds overhead without benefit. Evidence: Table 5 shows SSC can reduce token count; Table 7 shows sensitivity to step-splitting method ("\n\n" works best).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Confidence as negative log-probability**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire method relies on a scalar confidence score per trajectory. Understanding how `C_traj = - (1/NG) Σ logP_i(j)` (Eq 1) aggregates token-level uncertainty is fundamental.
  - *Quick check question:* If a trajectory has consistently high token probabilities (low negative log-prob), what does its confidence score indicate, and why is this potentially correlated with correctness?
- **Concept: Test-time scaling (TTS) and Best-of-N**
  - *Why needed here:* DistriVoting operates within a TTS framework where multiple trajectories are sampled. The goal is to select the best answer from `Budget` samples. Must understand the baseline (e.g., Self-Consistency, Weighted SC) this method improves.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the key difference between standard Best-of-N (BoN) and Weighted Self-Consistency (WSC) that DistriVoting builds upon?
- **Concept: Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) for clustering**
  - *Why needed here:* GMM is the core tool for unsupervised separation of confidence distributions. Understanding its components (`π`, `µ`, `σ²`) and expectation-maximization (EM) fitting is necessary to grasp why it's chosen over K-Means.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might a GMM be more suitable than K-Means for modeling confidence distributions that are assumed to be normal?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Generator (with SelfStepConf Controller) → Confidence Scorer → GMM Fitter → Reject Filter → Hierarchical Voter.
- **Critical path:** Generator → (optional: SelfStepConf Controller during generation) → Confidence Scorer → GMM Fitter → Reject Filter (conditional) → Hierarchical Voter.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Adaptivity vs. Stability:* SelfStepConf's `α` (EMA factor) and `δ` (trigger threshold) balance responsiveness vs. spurious triggers. Fig 9-10 show insensitivity in a range.
  - *Filtering strength:* GMM is adaptive per-question but assumes bimodality. Reject Filter adds robustness but relies on `A_neg` being a correct distractor. Hierarchical voting (`NC>1`) adds robustness when filtering is weak (Fig 12).
  - *Computation:* GMM fitting is O(n) but cheap (Table 2: ~0.34ms/it). SelfStepConf adds ~2-3% overhead (Table 5). Overall, DistriVoting+SSC is tractable for `Budget=128`.
  - *Break condition:* If the confidence distributions are not bimodal or heavily overlapping, GMM separation fails; further gains possible via reject filtering.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **No distribution separation:** Plot `C` histograms for correct/incorrect; if unimodal/overlapping, GMM and SSC gains will be minimal.
  2. **Reject filter backfires:** Check `A_pos == A_neg` rate; if very high, filter may be removing true positives.
  3. **Reflection ineffective:** If `Pass@1` from SSC is not higher than BasicInference at low `K` (Fig 6), or if reflection count is near zero (Fig 10b), SSC is not engaged meaningfully.
  4. **Over-splitting steps:** Using too fine a step split (e.g., fixed 256-token windows or high-entropy tokens) can cause excessive reflection (Table 7).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline replication:** Run WSC-Top50 and WSC-GMM (without SSC) on one benchmark (e.g., HMMT2025) with `Budget=128` to confirm GMM improvement (expect ~2% gain over Top50, per Table 1).
  2. **SCC trigger validation:** Generate 1 trajectory per question with SSC (`temp=0`), log step confidences and reflection triggers. Verify triggers occur when `C_Gm` drops and that reflection tokens change the distribution (visualize as in Fig 8).
  3. **Distribution separation quantification:** With `Budget=512`, compute `µ_pos` and `µ_neg` from GMM for BasicInference vs SSC. Calculate `δ` and correlate with voting accuracy improvement (should follow Theorem 2.1 trend).

## Limitations
- Assumptions may break: confidence distributions require bimodality for effective GMM separation; the negative consensus answer (`A_neg`) must be a reliable distractor; step-level confidence drops must be predictive of reasoning errors.
- Implementation gaps: exact definition of the “tail step” for trajectory confidence, details of the second GMM refit after reject filtering, and precise weighting scheme in hierarchical voting are not fully specified.
- Sensitivity: SelfStepConf’s performance depends on step‑splitting method, reflection token choice, and hyperparameter settings (`α`, `δ`); the reject filter could harm accuracy if `A_neg` often equals the true answer.

## Confidence
- **GMM Distribution Filtering:** High – Empirical results (Table 2, Table 4) demonstrate clear gains in voting accuracy and AUROC over fixed-threshold and other clustering methods.
- **Negative-Answer Rejection:** Medium – Stage III improvements (Table 4) are consistent, but the risk of discarding correct trajectories when `A_neg` is correct is not fully quantified.
- **SelfStepConf Steering:** Medium – Increased distribution separation `δ` (Fig 2) and accuracy gains (Table 1) support its effectiveness; however, dependence on reflection token efficacy and threshold tuning introduces uncertainty.
- **Overall SOTA Claim:** High – The method achieves state-of-the-art performance across 5 benchmarks and 16 model variants (Table 1), with statistically significant improvements.

## Next Checks
