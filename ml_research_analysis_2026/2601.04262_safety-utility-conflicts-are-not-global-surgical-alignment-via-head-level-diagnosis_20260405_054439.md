# Safety-Utility Conflicts Are Not Global: Surgical Alignment via Head-Level Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04262  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04262  
- **Reference count:** 28  
- **Primary result:** CAST selectively tunes low-conflict attention heads, recovering up to +9.45% MMLU accuracy while matching safety gains of full fine-tuning.

## Executive Summary
Uniform fine-tuning for safety alignment often degrades the general capabilities of large language models. This paper introduces Conflict-Aware Sparse Tuning (CAST), a method that surgically updates only the most compatible attention heads to avoid utility loss. CAST diagnoses each head using a combined metric of optimization conflict (gradient opposition between safety and utility objectives) and functional sensitivity (causal importance for utility). By tuning solely the bottom 25% lowest-conflict heads, CAST restores utility—improving MMLU accuracy by up to +9.45%—while achieving safety improvements comparable to full fine-tuning. Pre-alignment conflict scores strongly predict post-alignment utility cost (Pearson r ∈ [0.73, 0.95]). Furthermore, CAST integrates seamlessly with gradient-projection techniques like PCGrad, consistently achieving a superior Pareto frontier on the safety-utility trade-off.

## Method Summary
CAST comprises a diagnostic phase and a tuning phase. In diagnosis, for each attention head, it computes an optimization conflict score (measuring gradient opposition between safety and utility losses) and a functional sensitivity score (quantifying the head's causal influence on utility outputs via mediation analysis). These scores are combined to rank heads by their conflict; heads with low conflict are designated "safe zones" where updates are less likely to harm utility. During tuning, only these low-conflict heads are sparsely updated using the safety alignment data, while all other heads remain frozen. This selective approach minimizes interference with utility-critical representations. The method is model-agnostic and can be combined with gradient-projection methods such as PCGrad to further mitigate residual conflicts.

## Key Results
- Selective tuning of the bottom 25% lowest-conflict heads recovers utility: up to +9.45% MMLU accuracy compared to full fine-tuning, while matching safety gains.
- Pre-alignment conflict scores strongly predict post-alignment utility cost (Pearson r ∈ [0.73, 0.95]).
- CAST synergizes with PCGrad, achieving a superior Pareto frontier on safety-utility trade-offs across Llama, Qwen, and Mistral models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The central insight is that safety-utility conflicts are heterogeneous across a model's attention heads. Some heads are highly prone to interference—where safety updates disrupt utility—while others are relatively immune. CAST identifies heads with low optimization conflict (i.e., safety gradients that do not oppose utility gradients) and high functional sensitivity (i.e., heads critical for utility). By updating only these low-conflict heads, the method avoids altering the representations most essential for general capabilities, thereby preserving utility. The high predictive correlation demonstrates that the conflict metric effectively pinpoints heads that can be safely tuned. This surgical intervention yields a Pareto-optimal balance, and its synergy with gradient-projection methods like PCGrad further smooths any remaining trade-offs.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component Map:  
Model -> Head Diagnosis (compute conflict and sensitivity scores) -> Conflict Scoring (rank heads) -> Selection (choose bottom 25% low-conflict heads) -> Sparse Update (apply safety tuning only to selected heads)

Critical Path:  
The critical path flows from head diagnosis through conflict scoring to selection. Accurate conflict scores are essential; errors here propagate to head selection and ultimately to the safety-utility trade-off outcome.

Design Tradeoffs:
- Tuning rate: A lower percentage preserves more utility but may compromise safety; the paper identifies 25% as optimal in their settings.
- Conflict metric complexity: Combining gradient conflict and causal sensitivity is computationally heavier than using gradients alone, but yields better head ranking.
- Model generality: The method is validated on decoder-only models; extending to encoder-decoder or multimodal architectures may require adapted diagnostics.

Failure Signatures:
- Utility degradation despite low conflict: indicates inaccurate functional sensitivity estimation.
- Safety not improving: selected heads may be poorly aligned; conflict metric may be mis-specified.
- High variance across runs: suggests instability in head ranking; may need to average conflict scores over multiple seeds.

## Limitations
- Conflict metric computation and safety benchmarks are underspecified in the paper.
- The 25% tuning rate may depend on model scale and alignment data; not universally optimal.
- Evaluations are limited to decoder-only models (Llama, Qwen, Mistral) and MMLU; other architectures and tasks remain unverified.
