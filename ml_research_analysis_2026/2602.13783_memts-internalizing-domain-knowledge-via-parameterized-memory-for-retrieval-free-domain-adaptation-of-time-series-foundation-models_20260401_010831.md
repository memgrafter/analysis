# MEMTS: Internalizing Domain Knowledge via Parameterized Memory for Retrieval-Free Domain Adaptation of Time Series Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.13783
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13783
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MEMTS reduces error relative to frozen baseline on six datasets (up to 38.4% improvement) and achieves near-constant inference latency (~1.1 ms), with up to 712× speedup over Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).

## Executive Summary
Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) often degrade when applied to specific domains due to distribution shifts. Existing adaptation methods like Domain-Adaptive Pretraining (DAPT) cause catastrophic forgetting of the model's pre-trained knowledge, while Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) introduces high inference latency. This paper introduces MEMTS, a method that internalizes domain-specific temporal patterns into a compact set of learnable latent prototypes via a Knowledge Persistence Module (KPM). MEMTS operates as a plug-and-play module without modifying the frozen backbone, enabling retrieval-free, constant-time inference. Across six datasets and five TSFM backbones, MEMTS consistently reduces error—for example, lowering Bolt-S MSE on PEMS04 from 1.139 to 0.702 (38.4% improvement). Inference latency is approximately 1.1 ms, achieving up to 712× speedup over RAG at scale, while matching or exceeding the accuracy of both RAG and DAPT.

## Method Summary
MEMTS adds a Knowledge Persistence Module (KPM) to a frozen time series foundation model. The KPM consists of a small set of learnable latent prototypes that are trained on target domain data using a persistence loss. This loss encourages the prototypes to capture recurring domain-specific temporal patterns while remaining distinct. During inference, the backbone's representation is combined with the KPM prototypes to produce an adapted output, effectively internalizing domain knowledge without external retrieval or modifying the backbone. The method thus avoids catastrophic forgetting and maintains constant inference latency independent of memory corpus size.

## Key Results
- **Error reduction:** On six datasets, MEMTS reduces error relative to the frozen backbone; e.g., Bolt-S MSE on PEMS04 drops from 1.139 to 0.702 (38.4% improvement).
- **Constant-time inference:** Latency remains near-constant at ~1.1 ms regardless of memory size, achieving up to 712× speedup over RAG.
- **Accuracy:** MEMTS matches or exceeds the performance of both RAG and DAPT on the evaluated tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MEMTS works by replacing external retrieval with a parametric memory of learned prototypes. The KPM captures domain-specific temporal patterns during training on the target data, and these patterns are directly integrated into the model's forward pass. Because the backbone remains frozen, it retains its pre-trained knowledge, avoiding catastrophic forgetting. The prototypes act as a compressed memory that can be accessed in constant time, eliminating the latency overhead of searching an external corpus. The persistence loss ensures that prototypes learn coherent and distinct patterns, preventing them from collapsing or overfitting.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input Time Series → Frozen Backbone → Knowledge Persistence Module (KPM) → Feature Aggregator → Task Head → Output  
*The KPM contains L learnable latent prototypes that are trained on target data. During inference, the backbone's features are combined with the prototypes (e.g., via attention or concatenation) to produce an adapted representation.*

**Critical path:** The core workflow involves (1) freezing the pre-trained backbone, (2) initializing the KPM prototypes, (3) training only the KPM on the target domain using the persistence loss, and (4) at inference, passing inputs through the backbone and fusing with the trained prototypes before the task head. This path ensures the backbone's knowledge is preserved while the KPM injects domain-specific patterns.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *KPM size (number of prototypes):* Larger KPM increases capacity to capture diverse patterns but risks overfitting and higher memory usage.  
- *Persistence loss weight:* Balances between learning domain patterns and avoiding deviation from backbone representations; requires tuning.  
- *Frozen backbone:* Prevents catastrophic forgetting but limits the model's ability to adapt to very different domains; a trade-off between stability and flexibility.

**Failure signatures:**  
- No improvement over frozen baseline suggests KPM is too small, undertrained, or persistence loss weight is too low.  
- Performance degradation on target data indicates overfitting (e.g., KPM too large, persistence loss weight too high, or insufficient target data).  
- High variance across random seeds reflects sensitivity to prototype initialization or unstable training dynamics.

**First three experiments to validate implementation:**  
1. *Prototype convergence check:* Train KPM on a small target dataset and visualize prototype activations (e.g., via t-SNE) to confirm they capture distinct temporal patterns.  
2. *Baseline comparison:* Evaluate MEMTS against the frozen backbone on a held-out target validation set to verify consistent error reduction.  
3. *Ablation of persistence loss:* Train a version without the persistence loss and measure the drop in performance to confirm the loss's necessity.

## Limitations
- **Evaluation scope:** Performance under extreme or multimodal distribution shifts, noisy data, or ultra-long sequences remains uncertain, as experiments were limited to six standard datasets.
- **Generality across architectures:** The plug-and-play claim for decoder-based or encoder-decoder TSFMs is asserted but not thoroughly validated; applicability to diverse task heads (e.g., forecasting vs. classification) needs more evidence.
- **Latency claim details:** The reported 712× speedup over RAG lacks full specification of the RAG setup (index type, retrieval size, hardware), making independent verification of scaling advantages difficult.

## Confidence
- Error reduction on reported datasets: **High** (empirical results provided)
- Constant-time inference: **High** (logical guarantee of parametric module)
- Matching/exceeding RAG and DAPT accuracy: **Medium** (validated on specific datasets, but generalizability uncertain)
- 712× speedup over RAG: **Medium** (depends on unreported RAG implementation)
- Universal plug-and-play for any TSFM: **Low** (evidence limited to five encoder-focused backbones)

## Next Checks
1. **Benchmark on challenging data:** Replicate experiments on datasets with extreme covariate shift (e.g., synthetic corruption, sensor drift) and on long-sequence forecasting benchmarks (thousands of steps) to test KPM's capacity for long-range dependencies.
2. **Standardized latency comparison:** Conduct a head-to-head latency evaluation against a well-defined RAG system (specifying index type, retrieval k, batch size) across a wide range of memory corpus sizes on identical hardware, reporting per-sample latency and scaling curves.
3. **Ablation and prototype analysis:** Perform a thorough sensitivity analysis of KPM size and persistence loss weight across multiple datasets; analyze learned prototypes (e.g., clustering, interpretability) to understand what patterns they capture and whether they correlate with domain sub-shifts.
