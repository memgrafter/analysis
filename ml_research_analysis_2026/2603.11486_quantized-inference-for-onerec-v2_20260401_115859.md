# Quantized Inference for OneRec-V2

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11486
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11486
- **Reference count:** 7
- **Primary result:** FP8 post-training quantization reduces end-to-end inference latency by 49% and increases throughput by 92% with no degradation in core recommendation metrics, confirmed via online A/B testing.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of applying low-precision quantization to recommender systems by targeting OneRec-V2, a generative recommendation model whose weight and activation distributions resemble those of large language models. The authors develop an FP8 post-training quantization framework for its compute-dominant linear and mixture-of-experts (MoE) operators and tightly integrate it with an optimized inference infrastructure. The joint solution achieves a 49% reduction in end-to-end inference latency and a 92% increase in throughput under a production serving configuration, with no loss in recommendation quality validated by online A/B testing.

## Method Summary
The approach applies post-training quantization (PTQ) only to the most compute-intensive operators: linear layers (attention projections, dense FFN) and MoE experts. Weights are quantized per-channel offline, while activations use dynamic per-token scaling at runtime. FP8 TensorCore matrix multiplications accumulate in FP32 before casting back to FP16. Crucially, quantization is integrated into a custom inference stack built directly with TensorRT, incorporating fused quantization-GEMM kernels, a Radix-based TopK implementation, an optimized attention kernel for batch-parallel workloads, and Hopper TMA-accelerated MoE grouped GEMM. This co-design minimizes overhead and maximizes hardware utilization.

## Key Results
- End-to-end inference latency reduced by 49% under production serving (batch size 32).
- Throughput increased by 92% with the same hardware configuration.
- No statistically significant degradation in core online A/B metrics (Stay Time, Watch Time, Video View) after deployment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Distribution-Controlled Quantization**
- **Claim:** Low-precision (FP8) quantization is feasible for OneRec-V2 because its weight and activation distributions have low variance and magnitude, similar to LLMs.
- **Mechanism:** Tight distributions allow coarse-grained scaling (per-channel for weights, per-token for activations) without significant numerical distortion, as the dynamic range fits within FP8's representable range.
- **Core assumption:** Small variance and extreme values (AbsMax/AbsP99) ensure quantization errors remain tolerable for recommendation accuracy.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states statistics are "significantly more controlled and closer to those of large language models." Section 3.2 (Figure 1) shows mean weight variance <0.1 and activation AbsP99 orders of magnitude smaller than traditional RecSys models.

**Mechanism 2: Compute-Bound Inference Pattern**
- **Claim:** OneRec-V2's compute-intensive inference pattern ensures that accelerating its dominant operators (Linear/MoE) with FP8 translates to proportional end-to-end throughput gains.
- **Mechanism:** Dense linear and MoE operations dominate compute time; FP8 TensorCores increase arithmetic intensity and reduce time spent on memory-bound or control-bound operations.
- **Core assumption:** Over 70% of inference FLOPs occur in quantizable operators, making overall throughput scaling roughly linear with per-op speedup.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes "more compute-intensive inference pattern with substantially higher hardware utilization." Section 1 contrasts with traditional RecSys's "limited hardware utilization" and "memory or control bound" workloads.

**Mechanism 3: Joint Optimization of Quantization and Infrastructure**
- **Claim:** Quantization alone is insufficient; integration with an optimized inference infrastructure is necessary to overcome runtime overheads.
- **Mechanism:** Quantization reduces per-operator cost, but without kernel fusion, efficient operators, and optimized memory layout, overhead from scaling, dequantization, and kernel launches erodes gains. Infrastructure optimizations (fused kernels, RadixTopK, attention redesign, MoE TMA kernels) reduce this overhead.
- **Core assumption:** Quantization speedup must exceed overhead of dynamic scaling and data movement; infrastructure optimizations keep overhead acceptable.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports results from integrated solution. Section 4.2 details infrastructure optimizations. Section 5.2 shows additive gains: quantization (+42%) and infrastructure (+27%) are synergistic.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** OneRec-V2 backbone (Attention, Dense FFN, Sparse MoE) -> Quantization layer (per-token dynamic activation scaling, per-channel weight scaling) -> FP8 TensorCore GEMM -> FP32 accumulation -> Cast to FP16 -> Optimized TensorRT execution graph (custom kernels: fused quant/GEMM, RadixTopK, batched attention, Hopper TMA MoE) -> Output

**Critical path:** Input activations -> dynamic per-token quantization -> FP8 matmul with pre-quantized weights (per-channel) -> FP32 accumulation -> dequantize/cast to FP16 -> subsequent layers. Bottlenecks: quantization latency, GEMM throughput, TopK selection.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Granularity: Per-token activation scaling adds overhead but adapts to dynamic ranges; per-channel weight scaling is static but stable.
- Coverage: Quantizing only Linear/MoE (compute-dominant) vs. all ops. Risk: Unquantized ops become new bottlenecks.
- Precision: FP8 (E4M3) chosen for stability vs. lower bits (INT8/FP4) which may need more calibration.

**Failure signatures:**
- Accuracy: Degradation in online A/B metrics (e.g., Watch Time drop >0.1%).
- Performance: Latency reduction <20% or throughput gain <30% indicates poor hardware utilization or high quantization overhead.
- Stability: High tail latency (>2x mean) suggests kernel launch or memory access inefficiencies.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Reproduce distribution analysis (Section 3.2): Collect weight/activation stats of your model variant; verify mean variance <0.5 and AbsP99 <10 before proceeding.
2. Microbenchmark MFU: Run FP16 vs. FP8 GEMM kernels on representative layer shapes; confirm >1.5x speedup and MFU >60% in FP8.
3. Small-scale A/B test: Deploy FP8 on 1% traffic for 24h; monitor core metrics for >0.05% relative change (statistical significance) before full rollout.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can more aggressive quantization precisions (e.g., INT8, FP6, FP4) be applied to generative recommendation models like OneRec-V2 without significant quality degradation? The paper only validates FP8 and does not explore lower precisions.
2. How can the proposed quantization and infrastructure optimizations be made less dependent on custom, high-effort engineering stacks to improve reproducibility and portability? The solution relies on specific optimizations (TensorRT-based graph building, custom kernels) that are not easily transferable.
3. Do the quantization-friendly properties and deployment benefits observed in OneRec-V2 generalize to other generative recommendation architectures? Conclusions are drawn from a single model; different architectures may behave differently.
4. Is the controlled numerical distribution a necessary condition for successful low-precision quantization, or could algorithmic adjustments achieve similar gains with less controlled distributions? The analysis is observational; causality is not established.

## Limitations
- Limited reproducibility: Production model checkpoint and RecoGEM infrastructure details are undisclosed, preventing independent verification.
- Single-model evaluation: Conclusions are based solely on OneRec-V2; generalization to other generative recommendation models is unclear.
- Weak external validation: Corpus analysis shows neighboring papers focus on LLM quantization, not RecSys-specific distribution properties.

## Confidence
- LLM-like distribution enabling FP8 PTQ: Medium (internal evidence but no public model to confirm)
- Compute-intensive inference pattern: Low (contradicts typical RecSys profiles; lacks third-party benchmarking)
- Joint quantization + infrastructure gains: Medium (internally consistent but infrastructure specifics are proprietary)
