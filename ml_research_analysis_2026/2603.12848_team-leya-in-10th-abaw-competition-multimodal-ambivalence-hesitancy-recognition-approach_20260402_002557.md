# Team LEYA in 10th ABAW Competition: Multimodal Ambivalence/Hesitancy Recognition Approach

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.12848
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12848
*   **Reference Count:** 36
*   **Primary Result:** A multimodal fusion model combining scene, face, audio, and text modalities with a Transformer encoder and prototype-based classification achieved 83.25% average Macro F1-score on the development set. An ensemble of five such models reached the final test performance of 71.43%.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenging problem of video-level ambivalence/hesitancy (A/H) recognition in unconstrained videos. The proposed system integrates four modalities—scene, face, audio, and text—using specialized pre-trained encoders. Their embeddings are fused via a Transformer encoder and classified with an auxiliary prototype-based objective. While unimodal models peaked at 70.02% MF1, multimodal fusion substantially improved validation performance to 83.25%. The final submission, an ensemble, obtained 71.43% on the test set, demonstrating the value of complementary multimodal cues and robust fusion strategies for recognizing this subtle behavioral state.

## Method Summary
The method processes synchronized video, audio, and text inputs through four independent unimodal feature extractors: VideoMAE for scene clips, EmotionEfficientNetB0 with statistical pooling for facial expressions, EmotionWav2Vec2.0 followed by a Mamba encoder for audio, and a fine-tuned EmotionDistilRoBERTa for transcripts. Each modality's embedding is projected into a shared 128-dimensional space. These projected tokens, along with learned modality embeddings, are fed into a 6-layer Transformer encoder to model cross-modal interactions. The fused representation is mean-pooled (masking missing modalities) and classified by a linear layer. An auxiliary loss compares the fused vector to learnable class-specific prototypes (16 per class) via cosine similarity, encouraging a discriminative embedding structure. The final system is an ensemble of five models trained with different random seeds.

## Key Results
*   Best unimodal model (text) achieved 70.02% average Macro F1-score.
*   Best single fusion model (prototype-augmented, all four modalities) achieved 83.25% average Macro F1-score on the development/validation set.
*   Final test performance was 71.43% Macro F1-score, obtained by ensembling five prototype-augmented fusion models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Multimodal fusion captures complementary cues that unimodal models miss, improving A/H recognition.
*   **Mechanism:** The system extracts specialized embeddings from four modalities using dedicated pretrained encoders. These are projected into a shared space and processed by a Transformer encoder, which models cross-modal interactions. The fused representation is then classified.
*   **Core Assumption:** Ambivalence/hesitancy manifests through inconsistencies or subtle patterns across modalities, making it inherently multimodal.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] states "Multimodal fusion substantially improved performance... demonstrating the effectiveness of complementary multimodal cues." [section 3.5] describes the projection and Transformer-based fusion. [Table 1] shows fusion gains over unimodal results.
*   **Break Condition:** If one modality is consistently noisy or poorly extracted, fusion may underperform the best unimodal model.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** The prototype-augmented classification objective regularizes the fusion space, encouraging class-discriminative clustering and improving generalization.
*   **Mechanism:** During training, the fused representation is compared to learnable class-specific prototypes via a cosine similarity metric (with temperature τ). This auxiliary loss (`L_proto`) encourages embeddings of the same class to be close to their prototypes.
*   **Core Assumption:** Explicitly modeling a prototypical structure for A/H and non-A/H in the fused embedding space provides a useful inductive bias for this subtle, heterogeneous expression.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [section 3.5] defines the prototype head and its loss term `L_proto`. [section 4.2] notes its use in the final ensemble. [Table 1 ID12] shows the prototype-augmented fusion (83.25%) outperforms the non-prototype variant (82.66%) on the dev/valid split.
*   **Break Condition:** If hyperparameters (K, τ, λ_proto) are mis-specified, the auxiliary loss could conflict with the main classification loss, harming performance. The diversity loss (`L_div`) was disabled (λ_div=0).

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Temporal modeling within each modality (especially audio via Mamba and face via statistical pooling) is critical for capturing the dynamics of A/H.
*   **Mechanism:** For audio, frame-level Wav2Vec2.0 features are processed by a Mamba encoder (a selective state space model) to capture long-range dependencies before pooling. For face, statistical pooling (mean and standard deviation) aggregates frame-level emotional embeddings over time, capturing distributional shifts.
*   **Core Assumption:** A/H is not static; it involves temporal patterns like fluctuations, pauses, or inconsistencies over the video duration.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [section 3.3] describes the Mamba encoder for acoustic representations. [section 3.2] details the statistical pooling for face embeddings. [abstract] highlights the Mamba-based temporal encoder for audio.
*   **Break Condition:** If video/audio clips are very short or A/H cues are instantaneous, temporal pooling/encoding might dilute the signal. Mamba's state size and kernel must be tuned for typical clip length.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Self-supervised video/audio pretraining (VideoMAE, Wav2Vec2.0).
    *   **Why needed:** These models provide robust, generalizable feature extractors trained on large-scale unlabeled data, essential for unconstrained videos with variation in lighting, pose, and background noise.
    *   **Quick Check:** What is the key difference between a masked autoencoder (VideoMAE) and a contrastive pretraining approach for video? (Answer: VideoMAE reconstructs masked patches in a discriminative way, while contrastive methods like CLIP align video-text pairs.)
*   **Concept:** Statistical pooling for temporal aggregation.
    *   **Why needed:** To convert variable-length frame-level embeddings into a fixed-length video-level representation that captures central tendency and dispersion, which may relate to hesitancy (e.g., variable facial expressiveness).
    *   **Quick Check:** Why use both mean and standard deviation in pooling, rather than just mean? (Answer: Standard deviation can capture variability or inconsistency in emotional expressions over time, which may be informative for A/H.)
*   **Concept:** Prototypical networks for classification.
    *   **Why needed:** The prototype head provides an alternative similarity-based decision rule that may be more robust to subtle class boundaries by reasoning in a metric space.
    *   **Quick Check:** How does the prototype loss (`L_proto`) differ from a standard cross-entropy loss? (Answer: `L_proto` encourages embeddings to cluster around class-specific prototypes via a softmax over cosine similarities, while cross-entropy uses a linear layer directly on embeddings.)

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** Input (video, audio, text) -> Unimodal Encoders (VideoMAE, EmotionEfficientNetB0+stats-pool, EmotionWav2Vec2.0+Mamba, EmotionDistilRoBERTa) -> Fixed Embeddings -> Projection Layers (Linear+LN+GELU+Dropout) -> Shared 128-d Tokens + Modality Embeddings -> 6-layer Transformer Encoder -> Masked Mean Pooling -> [Branch 1] Linear Classifier; [Branch 2] Prototype Head (cosine similarity with τ=0.3, K=16) -> Combined Loss (`L_cls` + λ_proto`L_proto`) -> Prediction.
*   **Critical Path:** Extract unimodal embeddings -> Project to shared space -> Fuse with Transformer -> Pool fused tokens -> Classify (with prototype auxiliary loss).
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   **Modality Selection:** Adding scene improves complementarity but increases compute. Omitting the strongest unimodal (text) severely hurts performance.
    *   **Fusion Architecture:** Transformer allows explicit cross-modal attention but requires careful masking for missing modalities. Simpler concatenation+MLP would be less expressive.
    *   **Prototype Head:** Adds regularization and a modest gain but increases hyperparameter search complexity (K=16, τ=0.3).
    *   **Ensembling:** Essential for final test robustness but increases inference cost 5x.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   **Unimodal extractor failure:** Face detector returns no faces → fallback to full frame, but may hurt performance.
    *   **Modality mismatch:** Poor audio-text alignment or transcription errors corrupt the text modality.
    *   **Fusion collapse:** One modality (e.g., text) dominates, causing the Transformer to ignore others, reducing complementarity.
    *   **Overfitting prototype loss:** If λ_proto is too high, the model may overfit to prototype geometry rather than the classification boundary.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  Validate unimodal models independently on the provided validation set. Target MF1s: Text (~70%), Audio (~69%), Face (~62%), Scene (~62%). Ensure no catastrophic failure (<50%).
    2.  Test basic fusion: Combine the best two unimodal embeddings (e.g., text+scene) with a simple linear layer (no Transformer). Compare to unimodal MF1s to confirm fusion gain is not solely from Transformer complexity.
    3.  Ablate prototype head: Train the exact fusion model with `λ_proto=0` vs. `λ_proto=0.2`. Measure validation MF1 difference. The prototype variant should consistently match or exceed the baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** What specific mechanisms within the Transformer-based multimodal fusion module enable it to effectively integrate ambiguous and potentially inconsistent cues across scene, face, audio, and text modalities for ambivalence/hesitancy (A/H) recognition?
*   **Basis in paper:** The proposed fusion model clearly outperforms unimodal models and prior fusion approaches, yet the analysis focuses on overall performance gains rather than interpreting the fused representations or attention patterns that might explain why this architecture handles subtle, context-dependent A/H better.
*   **Why unresolved:** Without analyzing the model's internal behavior (e.g., attention weights between modality tokens), it is unclear whether the fusion genuinely captures cross-modal inconsistencies characteristic of A/H.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies comparing attention distributions for A/H vs. non-A/H samples; probing experiments that systematically perturb one modality's input; visualization of fused representations colored by ground-truth labels or modality disagreement levels.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** To what extent does the scene modality contribute unique, complementary information to A/H recognition compared to the more traditionally dominant text and face modalities, and what specific scene characteristics correlate with ambivalence/hesitancy?
*   **Basis in paper:** The ablation study shows adding scene improves results (e.g., text+scene: 80.39% avg), but the paper does not analyze what scene features are actually informative for A/H, treating VideoMAE as a black-box.
*   **Why unresolved:** The specific role of scene—whether it provides contextual grounding, captures behavioral avoidance, etc.—remains speculative, limiting understanding of its optimal use.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Analysis of VideoMAE attention maps or gradients on A/H-positive vs. negative videos; correlating specific scene dynamics (e.g., camera motion, object presence) with A/H labels; controlled experiments where scene is artificially degraded.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** How does the prototype-augmented classification objective specifically improve the model's ability to handle the inherent uncertainty and inter-subject variability in A/H expressions, given that the final test performance gain from ensembling suggests stability issues?
*   **Basis in paper:** The prototype loss is introduced, but `L_div` is disabled. The authors note prototype-augmented single models achieved the best validation score (83.25%), yet the final test score for a single prototype model (65.21%) is substantially lower, and ensembling was crucial (71.43%).
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper does not analyze why the prototype mechanism leads to such a significant drop from validation to test for single models, nor how the prototypes themselves evolve. The role of the disabled diversity loss is also unclear.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Visualization of learned prototypes in embedding space; analysis of prototype assignment confidence for validation vs. test samples; reintroducing `L_div` with varying weights; error analysis comparing prototype-based vs. main classifier logits on misclassified test samples.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** Why is there a substantial performance discrepancy between the development/validation average (83.25% for best prototype fusion model) and the final private test result (71.43% for ensemble), and what does this reveal about dataset bias, overfitting, or limitations in the proposed fusion strategy's robustness?
*   **Basis in paper:** Table 1 shows a large gap: best single fusion (ID 12) 83.25% on dev vs. 65.21% on test. The ensemble (ID 14) reaches 71.43% on test. The authors attribute the ensemble's success to "robust model aggregation" but do not investigate the cause of the dev-test gap.
*   **Why unresolved:** The gap suggests potential overfitting to the validation split, non-stationarity between splits, or that the fusion model may be learning spurious correlations. Without diagnosing this, it's unclear if the approach is fundamentally limited.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative statistical analysis of feature distributions across train/val/test splits; identifying which modality combinations or sample characteristics drive the drop; cross-validation or multiple random splits; testing whether simpler models or stronger regularization reduce the gap.

## Limitations
*   **Data Access & Splits Unknown:** Exact BAH corpus train/val/test splits are not provided, preventing direct performance replication and raising suspicion of overfitting given large dev-test gaps (e.g., 83.25%→65.21%).
*   **Implementation Gaps:** Critical fusion details are unspecified, including the exact MLP architecture after Transformer pooling, initialization of modality/prototype embeddings, LR scheduler schedule, and precise missing-modality masking logic.
*   **Baseline Reproducibility:** Pre-trained unimodal checkpoints and full training recipes (augmentations, early stopping) are not provided, risking deviation from reported unimodal strengths and subsequent fusion performance.

## Confidence
*   **Multimodal Fusion Gains (High):** Strongly supported by Table 1 comparisons showing consistent improvement from unimodal (~70%) to fusion (~83% on dev). The mechanism is clear and standard.
*   **Prototype Augmentation Benefit (Medium):** A small but consistent gain is shown in Table 1 (ID11 82.66% → ID12 83.25%). However, there is no full ablation study, so the benefit could be marginal or highly hyperparameter-sensitive.
*   **Temporal Modeling Necessity (Medium-Low):** The use of Mamba and statistical pooling is well-motivated and described, but there is no direct ablation isolating the contribution of temporal modeling versus static features. It is possible spatial cues dominate.
*   **Final Test Performance Generalizability (Low):** The large and unexplained performance drop from validation to test (83.25%→65.21% for a single model) severely undermines confidence in the method's robustness under distribution shift. The ensemble mitigates this but does not explain it.
