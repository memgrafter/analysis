# Towards Faithful Reasoning in Comics for Small MLLMs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.02991
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02991
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A modular MoCoT pipeline with GRPO-based fine-tuning yields a 3B model that outperforms all ≤4B MLLMs on comic VQA benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Comic visual question answering (VQA) poses unique challenges for small multimodal language models (MLLMs) due to the need for symbolic abstraction and narrative humor understanding. Naive chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting often leads to unfaithful reasoning, degrading performance. This paper introduces MoCoT, a modular reasoning pipeline that decomposes tasks into typed subgoals (plan–execute–verify) and employs GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning with a structured VERA reward. This design enforces evidence grounding, procedural consistency, and symbolic coherence. Evaluated on five comic VQA benchmarks, the proposed 3B model surpasses all models with ≤4B parameters and matches larger ones. Plug-in experiments show an average 12.1% improvement across diverse backbones, and faithfulness metrics improve, with key failure patterns reduced.

## Method Summary
MoCoT is a modular pipeline that structures reasoning into three typed subgoals: plan, execute, and verify. The model first plans a sequence of actions based on the input, then executes those actions (e.g., extracting visual evidence, performing symbolic operations), and finally verifies the consistency and grounding of the intermediate steps. Reinforcement fine-tuning uses the GRPO algorithm with a reward function named VERA, which incorporates structured criteria to reward evidence grounding, procedural consistency, and symbolic coherence. This reward design mitigates unfaithful reasoning patterns such as state entanglement, spurious transitions, and exploration inefficiency that plague naive CoT approaches.

## Key Results
- MoCoT 3B model outperforms all ≤4B MLLMs on five comic VQA benchmarks and matches larger models.
- Plug-in implementation yields an average 12.1% performance improvement across diverse backbones.
- Faithfulness metrics (CAS, USR) show significant gains, and failure patterns (satirical target confusion, symbolic misalignment, salient cue omission) are systematically reduced.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The modular decomposition in MoCoT isolates reasoning stages, reducing state entanglement and spurious transitions. The plan step ensures a high-level strategy, the execute step focuses on concrete evidence extraction, and the verify step enforces self-consistency and grounding. GRPO-based reinforcement learning with the VERA reward explicitly optimizes for faithfulness by rewarding steps that maintain evidence linkage and symbolic coherence. This combination prevents the model from taking exploratory yet irrelevant reasoning paths, leading to more reliable and interpretable outputs.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Input -> Plan -> Execute -> Verify -> Output  
(Reinforcement loop: GRPO updates model parameters based on VERA rewards from Verify)

**Critical Path**:  
1. Receive a comic image and question.  
2. Plan module generates a high-level reasoning plan.  
3. Execute module performs subgoals (e.g., detect visual elements, extract text, apply symbolic operations).  
4. Verify module checks consistency of intermediate results and final answer against evidence.  
5. VERA reward computed; GRPO updates the model.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- Modularity vs. overhead: Decomposition adds sequential steps but reduces error propagation.  
- Reward complexity: VERA’s multi-faceted reward improves faithfulness but may require careful tuning.  
- Plug-in flexibility: MoCoT can be added to various backbones, but gains may vary with backbone capacity.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- Satirical target confusion  
- Symbolic misalignment  
- Salient cue omission

**First Experiments**:  
1. Ablation study: Remove modular decomposition (run end-to-end) and compare performance/faithfulness.  
2. Reward ablation: Replace VERA with simple accuracy reward to assess impact.  
3. Transfer test: Apply MoCoT to non-comic VQA (e.g., VQA v2) to evaluate cross-domain generalization.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to five comic VQA benchmarks; unknown generalization to other domains.
- Faithfulness metrics (CAS, USR) are non-standard and unvalidated.
- Plug-in backbone diversity unspecified; improvement claims need verification across broader model families.

## Confidence
- Comic VQA challenges for small MLLMs and naive CoT degradation: Medium
- MoCoT yields superior performance on Comic VQA vs. ≤4B models: Medium
- Plug-in improvement (~12.1%) across diverse backbones: Medium
- Faithfulness gains and failure pattern reduction: Low
