# Cross-Domain Demo-to-Code via Neurosymbolic Counterfactual Reasoning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18495
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18495
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** NESYCR improves task success rate by 31.14% over Statler and achieves up to 87.5% higher success than Demo2Code in real-world experiments by adapting demonstrations to new deployment domains via neurosymbolic counterfactual reasoning.

## Executive Summary
Converting a demonstration video into a robotic control policy that functions in a different deployment environment is challenging due to mismatches in perceptual and physical properties. The NESYCR framework addresses this by abstracting the demonstration into a symbolic trajectory, detecting inconsistencies by forward-simulating actions under counterfactual conditions from the deployment scene, and revising the procedure through VLM-proposed actions validated by a symbolic tool. This neurosymbolic loop yields deployment-grounded code policies. Evaluated on 440 simulated scenarios and real-world tasks, NESYCR substantially outperforms state-of-the-art baselines like Statler and GPT4V-Robotics in success rate and goal completion, while maintaining robustness under increasing domain gaps and task complexity.

## Method Summary
NESYCR operates in three phases. First, it constructs a symbolic world model from the demonstration video: a vision-language model (VLM) extracts scene graphs (symbolic states) at each timestep and predicts action operators (preconditions/effects). A symbolic tool verifies consistency by forward simulation, ensuring each action’s effects reproduce the next state. Second, it performs counterfactual adaptation: the VLM translates the deployment observation into an initial counterfactual state. The symbolic tool then simulates the demonstrated procedure on this state, flagging actions with unsatisfied preconditions or incorrect effects. For each inconsistency, the VLM proposes alternative actions that repair violated conditions; the symbolic tool verifies each proposal. This iterative loop continues until the goal condition is met. Finally, the adapted symbolic procedure is compiled into executable code using a set of primitive APIs. The approach tightly couples VLM perception and proposal with symbolic validation to ensure causal coherence across domain shifts.

## Key Results
- In simulation, NESYCR achieves a 31.14% absolute improvement in task success rate over Statler and relative gains of 27.73% (SR) and 15.16% (GC) over GPT4V-Robotics.
- Real-world experiments show 87.5% higher success rate than Demo2Code and 37.5% higher than Statler/GPT4V.
- Ablations confirm the necessity of both counterfactual adaptation (removal causes >20% SR drop) and the symbolic world model (removal causes >35% SR drop).
- NESYCR remains robust as domain gap and task complexity increase, whereas baseline performance degrades significantly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Symbolic World Model Construction
The VLM extracts structured symbolic states (scene graphs) and action operators from the demonstration video. A symbolic tool verifies each action by forward simulation: applying the action’s effects to the preceding state must yield the subsequent state. This creates a verifiable procedural model and assumes VLMs can ground perceptual data into accurate predicates. Break condition: repeated verification failures abort construction.

### Mechanism 2: Counterfactual Inconsistency Detection
Given the deployment observation, the VLM generates a counterfactual initial state. The symbolic tool simulates the demonstrated procedure on this state, flagging an action as inconsistent if its preconditions are unsatisfied or its effects deviate from the expected state progression. This reveals causal mismatches caused by domain shifts. Break condition: if most actions are inconsistent, the domain gap likely exceeds adaptation capacity.

### Mechanism 3: Neurosymbolic Procedure Revision
For each inconsistent action, the VLM proposes an alternative that restores violated preconditions. The symbolic tool verifies the candidate’s preconditions and effects before acceptance. This iterative exploration, guided by symbolic constraints, adapts the procedure until the goal state is reached, yielding a deployment-grounded plan. Break condition: reaching the maximum exploration iterations without achieving the goal causes adaptation failure.

## Foundational Learning
- **STRIPS-style symbolic planning**
  - **Why needed:** Represents tasks as actions with preconditions/effects and uses forward simulation to verify procedural consistency and detect violations during adaptation.
  - **Quick check:** Given `(GripperHolding block)` as an effect, what precondition would allow applying `MoveGripperTo`? What must be false in the state after execution?

- **Vision-language model grounding**
  - **Why needed:** The VLM must translate raw pixels into structured symbolic states (scene graphs) that capture object entities and relations needed for planning.
  - **Quick check:** From an image showing a block on top of a table, list the predicates a VLM should extract. Which relations require depth estimation?

- **Counterfactual inference**
  - **Why needed:** To adapt the demo, NESYCR must simulate "what if" the deployment conditions differ—i.e., run the symbolic procedure under altered initial states.
  - **Quick check:** If a demo shows picking up a red block, but in deployment the red block is under a blue block, which precondition likely fails first? How would you modify the procedure?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  VLM (state translation, action prediction, counterfactual translation, alternative generation) -> Symbolic tool (forward executor/verifier) -> Code synthesizer (compiles symbolic procedure to Python)
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Input: demonstration video + deployment observation  
  2. Construct symbolic world model from demo via VLM + verification  
  3. Generate counterfactual initial state from deployment scene  
  4. Iteratively: forward-simulate demonstrated steps → detect inconsistencies → VLM proposes alternatives → symbolic verification → accumulate adapted procedure  
  5. When goal condition satisfied, translate procedure into code policy  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - VLM accuracy vs symbolic verification: Symbolic checks catch VLM errors but fixed predicate set may lack expressivity for novel situations.  
  - Exploration depth vs runtime: More iterations improve adaptation but increase latency; hyperparameters balance complexity.  
  - General predicates vs domain-specific: Universal predicates ensure transferability but may force approximation of domain-specific nuances.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Repeated "Symbolic verification failed" → corrupted world model; inspect VLM prompts for scene graph consistency.  
  - High inconsistency rate (>80%) → domain gap too large; consider additional demonstrations or richer predicates.  
  - Stagnant procedural deviation (PD) → VLM generates plausible but invalid actions; reduce exploration temperature or increase verification strictness.  
  - Maximum iterations exceeded → adaptation timeout; increase budget or simplify domain gap.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline reconstruction:** Run symbolic world model construction on demo alone. Should reconstruct demonstrated procedure with low procedural deviation.  
  2. **Single-factor gap:** Apply Obstruction level 1 to a simple Pick&Place task. Expect NESYCR to identify blocking object and insert removal action.  
  3. **End-to-end known case:** Replicate drawer-organizing example. Validate code modifications (removal of "release magnetic hook", insertion of hook retrieval/screw aggregation, reordering). Execute on real robot; confirm SR improvement over Statler.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can NESYCR's counterfactual reasoning be extended to handle tasks where the deployment scenario's complexity fundamentally exceeds the information provided in a single demonstration, requiring the agent to infer entirely new causal relations? The current framework adapts an existing symbolic world model but cannot generate novel action operators or predicates for unseen subtasks.

### Open Question 2
To what extent does performance depend on the completeness of the fixed predicate set, and can this be overcome through automated predicate invention? The hand-designed predicates (Fig. 11) may be insufficient for diverse tasks, potentially causing silent failures; the framework cannot self-improve its representation.

### Open Question 3
What are the failure modes and robustness limits of VLM-based components when faced with perceptual noise, ambiguous scenes, or demonstrations containing non-optimal/redundant actions? VLM errors in constructing the symbolic world model may propagate and cause irrecoverable plan corruption; the system lacks a mechanism to question demonstration quality.

### Open Question 4
Can the neurosymbolic counterfactual adaptation scale efficiently to very long-horizon, highly interdependent tasks? The greedy, linear repair strategy may lead to exponential branching or failure to converge when local repairs cause downstream conflicts, especially as task complexity increases.

## Limitations
- The pipeline relies on VLM reliability for extracting accurate symbolic predicates; noisy perceptions could break state/action extraction, yet robustness to visual variance is unquantified.
- The fixed predicate vocabulary may be insufficient for unseen domain shifts or complex manipulations, forcing invalid abstractions and limiting expressivity.
- Real-world validation uses a limited object set and single robot platform (Franka Emika); generalizability to diverse hardware and cluttered environments remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Simulation improvements over baselines:** High (440 scenarios, consistent gains across factors)
- **Real-world success rate gains:** Medium (few tasks, limited environmental diversity)
- **Counterfactual reasoning as key mechanism:** Medium (ablations support necessity but causal attribution relies on simulation; real-world failure modes unanalyzed)
