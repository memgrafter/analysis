# AC-Foley: Reference-Audio-Guided Video-to-Audio Synthesis with Acoustic Transfer
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.15597
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15597
- **Reference count**: 21
- **Primary result**: AC-Foley achieves state-of-the-art performance on Foley generation with audio conditioning, reducing Fréchet Distance by 20%, Kullback-Leibler divergence by 28%, and Mel Cepstral Distortion by 22% compared to prior work.

## Executive Summary
AC-Foley introduces reference-audio conditioning into video-to-audio synthesis to overcome the coarse semantic control of text-guided methods. By injecting acoustic features from a user-provided sound through a pretrained VAE encoder, the system enables fine-grained control over timbre and other acoustic attributes. A two-stage training strategy (overlapping then non-overlapping audio segments) teaches the model to adapt the reference sound to the video timing rather than simply copying it. Evaluated on large-scale datasets, AC-Foley sets a new state-of-the-art on Foley generation with audio conditioning across multiple metrics and supports applications such as timbre transfer and zero-shot generation. The approach remains competitive even when reference audio is omitted.

## Method Summary
The task is to generate an 8-second audio track for a silent video, optionally guided by a text prompt and a 2-second reference audio clip. The architecture comprises pretrained encoders: CLIP for video and text, a VAE for audio, and Synchformer for audio-visual synchronization features. These embeddings are fused in a multimodal transformer with 7 multimodal and 14 single-modal blocks, using adaptive layer normalization (adaLN) to condition the transformation on the reference audio. The model predicts audio latents that are decoded by the VAE decoder into mel-spectrograms, which are then converted to waveforms by a vocoder. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage I uses a random 2-second overlapping segment from the target audio as conditioning to learn correspondence; Stage II switches to a non-overlapping segment (the last 2 seconds) to force temporal adaptation rather than copying. After 260k iterations with AdamW, a finetuning phase on a high ImageBind score subset refines semantic alignment.

## Key Results
- Reduces Fréchet Distance by 20% and Kullback-Leibler divergence by 28% compared to prior state-of-the-art.
- Enables precise fine-grained synthesis (e.g., footsteps on different surfaces) and timbre transfer.
- Remains competitive in pure video-to-audio settings without reference conditioning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
AC-Foley works because it directly injects low-level acoustic features from the reference audio via a pretrained VAE, bypassing the semantic bottleneck of text. The adaLN mechanism seamlessly integrates these features into the multimodal transformer, allowing the model to modulate the generated content while preserving video-synchronized timing. The two-stage training curriculum is crucial: Stage I establishes basic audio-visual alignment using overlapping segments, while Stage II, with non-overlapping segments, prevents trivial copy-paste and encourages the network to transform the reference sound to match the video's temporal structure. This combination yields both high acoustic fidelity and accurate event synchronization.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Video (CLIP) + Reference Audio (VAE encoder) + Text (CLIP) + Sync (Synchformer) -> Multimodal Transformer (adaLN) -> Audio Latent -> VAE Decoder -> Mel Spectrogram -> Vocoder -> Audio Waveform

Critical path:
1. Input preprocessing: Extract video frames and compute CLIP visual features; encode reference audio to VAE latent; extract text embedding if provided; compute Synchformer sync features.
2. Fuse all embeddings in the transformer, predicting the next-frame audio latent sequence.
3. Decode the latent sequence to mel-spectrogram via VAE decoder.
4. Synthesize waveform with a vocoder.

Design tradeoffs:
- Overlap vs non-overlap training: Overlap risks direct copying; non-overlap enforces adaptation but may harm early alignment.
- Reference audio length: Longer clips provide richer timbre but may conflict with target duration; 2 seconds is a compromise.
- Pretrained vs trainable encoders: Pretrained CLIP and Synchformer freeze semantics and sync but limit domain adaptation; VAE is also frozen to maintain a consistent latent space.

Failure signatures:
- High Mel Cepstral Distortion (MCD) between generated audio and reference indicates copy-paste behavior (Stage I failure).
- High DeSync score suggests poor temporal alignment, often due to sync feature miscomputations or resampling errors.
- Low ImageBind (IB) score reveals semantic mismatch with the visual content, possibly from inadequate text conditioning or dataset bias.
