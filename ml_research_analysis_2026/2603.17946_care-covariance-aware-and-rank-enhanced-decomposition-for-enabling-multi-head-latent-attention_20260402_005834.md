# CARE: Covariance-Aware and Rank-Enhanced Decomposition for Enabling Multi-Head Latent Attention

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17946  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17946  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** CARE reduces one-shot perplexity by up to 215× and improves mean accuracy by up to 1.70× versus uniform SVD at matched KV budgets.

## Executive Summary
Converting pretrained grouped-query attention (GQA) to multi-head latent attention (MLA) reduces KV cache memory but naive low-rank factorization (e.g., weight-only SVD) causes significant activation drift. CARE addresses this with three coordinated components: (1) activation-aware factorization using whitened SVD on √**C** **W**, where **C** is the activation covariance; (2) adjusted-rank allocation that distributes a fixed KV budget across layers by spectral priority; and (3) KV-parity reparameterization that preserves cache size while mapping GQA weights to MLA. Evaluations on Llama-3.1 and Qwen3 models show dramatic improvements in one-shot quality, and brief post-conversion finetuning fully recovers original accuracy.

## Method Summary
CARE’s pipeline begins with a calibration run to collect per-layer activations and estimate activation covariance **C** (with optional shrinkage). Next, for each layer’s key/value weight matrices (conceptually replicated from GQA groups), it computes the spectrum of √**C** **W** and runs a greedy water-filling algorithm to assign ranks under a total KV budget, prioritizing layers where the next singular value captures the largest normalized residual energy. Then, for each assigned rank, it performs truncated SVD on √**C** **W** and unwhitens to obtain low-rank factors **W**ₐ, **W**ᵦ that approximate the original weights in activation space. Finally, these factors are inserted into the MLA architecture along with partial RoPE adapters to maintain positional behavior; optional light supervised finetuning can further heal any residual degradation.

## Key Results
- Reduces one-shot perplexity by up to 215× compared to uniform SVD baselines at identical KV budgets.
- Improves mean accuracy by up to 1.70× over uniform SVD across multiple model families.
- Light post-conversion finetuning (1–3B tokens) fully recovers the original model’s accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Activation-aware factorization**  
CARE replaces vanilla SVD on weight matrix **W** with SVD on √**C** **W**, where **C** is the activation covariance from a calibration set. After obtaining top-*r* factors *U*ᵣ, Σᵣ, *V*ᵣ of √**C** **W**, it sets *W*ₐ = √**C**⁻¹ *U*ᵣ Σᵣ^{1/2} and *W*ᵦ = Σᵣ^{1/2} *V*ᵣᵀ, so that *W*ₐ*W*ᵦ ≈ **W** in activation norm ∥*X* **W**−*X* **Ŵ**∥₂² = ∥√**C**(**W**−**Ŵ**)∥₂². This aligns the approximation with actual input geometry rather than weight values. Evidence includes theoretical derivation in Section 3.3 and empirical gains over weight-only SVD. Break condition: covariance **C** becomes unrepresentative under severe distribution shift or near-singular, misguiding the factorization.

**Mechanism 2: Adjusted-rank allocation**  
Given a fixed total KV budget **R**ₜₒₜ, CARE greedily distributes ranks across layers using priority score *s*⁽ˡ⁾(*r*) = σ²_{*r*+1} / ∑_{i>*r*} σ²_i, i.e., the normalized energy of the next singular value from √**C** **W**’s spectrum. Starting from minimal ranks, it repeatedly assigns one rank to the layer with highest priority until the budget is exhausted. This allocates capacity to layers with steep residual spectra, optimizing Frobenius norm reduction under the constraint. Evidence includes depth-dependent rank profiles in Fig. 3 and consistent gains over uniform rank. Break condition: if spectra are flat, allocation converges to uniform; static ranks cannot adapt if spectral needs change during later finetuning.

**Mechanism 3: KV-parity reparameterization**  
CARE maps GQA’s grouped key/value weights *W*ₖ^{(g)}, *W*ᵥ^{(g)} to MLA’s latent representation by conceptually replicating each group to full size *fW*ₖ, *fW*ᵥ ∈ ℝ^{D×nₕdₕ}. The factorization then provides *W*ₐ, *W*ᵦ with rank *r* = *g d*ₕ (GQA’s KV width), ensuring identical KV cache size. Partial RoPE adapters (*W*ᴿₖ, *W*ᴿᵩ) preserve positional alignment without increasing cached state. Evidence includes schematic in Fig. 1(b) and memory parity guarantees. Break condition: incompatible positional encodings (e.g., ALiBi) or mismatches between *r* and *g d*ₕ break parity.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Transformer attention and KV cache scaling**  
Why needed: CARE converts between MHA/GQA and MLA; understanding that MHA caches separate vectors per head (size ∝ nₕ dₕ) while GQA reduces to ∝ g dₕ is prerequisite.  
Quick check: Given nₕ=32, dₕ=128, L=2048, B=1, FP16, compute KV cache size in MB. (Answer: ~33.55 MB.)

**Concept: Low-rank matrix approximation via SVD**  
Why needed: CARE’s factorization relies on truncated SVD of √**C** **W**; Eckart–Young theorem guarantees best rank-*r* approximation in Frobenius norm.  
Quick check: If **W** = **U**Σ**V**ᵀ and truncated to rank *r*, what is the residual ∥**W**−**W**ᵣ∥₂₂²? (Answer: ∑_{i=r+1} σᵢ².)

**Concept: Covariance and activation statistics**  
Why needed: CARE uses **C** = (1/N) Σ *X*ᵀ*X* to capture second-order input geometry; whitening aligns weight error with activation error.  
Quick check: Show why 1/N Σ∥*X*_b **W**∥₂² = ∥√**C** **W**∥₂² given **C** = (1/N) Σ *X*ᵀ*X*. (Hint: trace cyclicity and Frobenius inner product.)

**Concept: Greedy resource allocation (water-filling)**  
Why needed: Adjusted-rank allocation uses a greedy algorithm under a hard budget; marginal gains are non-increasing per layer, so greedy is optimal for the defined priority.  
Quick check: How to optimally allocate R total ranks across L layers with per-layer priority s(l)(r)? (Answer: Initialize ranks to minimum; repeatedly assign one rank to layer with highest s(l)(current rank).)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Calibration collector → Covariance estimator → Spectral allocator → Factorizer → KV-parity integrator → (Optional) Light finetuning

**Critical path**  
- Steps 2–4 must complete per layer before final assembly.  
- Covariance computation is embarrassingly parallel across layers/GPUs.  
- Water-filling requires full spectra for all layers, making it a sequential global step.  
- KV-parity mapping is algebraic but requires careful indexing when replicating grouped weights.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Calibration cost*: N=256, T_b=256 usually suffice; longer sequences increase O(N D²) cost.  
- *Rank scheduling*: Water-filling is optimal for the normalized-energy priority; alternative priorities possible.  
- *Shrinkage*: α=0.01 default; larger α aids invertibility but biases toward isotropic.  
- *Healing vs. one-shot*: CARE improves one-shot quality; 1–3B tokens of finetuning always recovers original performance.

**Failure signatures**  
- High zero-shot perplexity despite good weight fit: suggests activation-space error; switch from uniform SVD to CARE.  
- KV cache size mismatch: verify rank *r* equals *g d*ₕ and no extra KV heads introduced.  
- Depth-invariant rank profile: indicates impoverished covariance (e.g., single-sample batches) or flat spectra.  
- Attention logit mismatch: compare attention weights; systematic deviation points to rank allocation or RoPE issues.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Reproduce vanilla SVD baseline: convert target model (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B) using joint SVD on (*W*ₖ, *W*ᵥ) with uniform rank *r* = *g d*ₕ; measure zero-shot accuracy/perplexity.  
2. Run CARE-U vs. CARE-E: apply CARE with activation-aware SVD but uniform rank (CARE-U) and with adjusted-rank (CARE-E); confirm contribution of each component via >2-point accuracy changes.  
3. Calibration sweep: fix target rank (e.g., 256) and vary N ∈ {8,32,128,512} and T_b ∈ {32,128,512}; plot metrics to identify saturation and OOM thresholds.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Multimodal extension: Does CARE preserve attention fidelity in cross-attention modules of vision-language models under KV-cache constraints?  
- Data-free calibration: Can activation covariance be estimated from internal statistics (e.g., Fisher) or synthetic data to avoid domain-specific calibration?  
- Structured covariance: Would Kronecker-factorized or other structured covariance approximations improve activation preservation over the diagonal proxy?  
- Dynamic ranks: Can per-layer ranks adapt at inference time based on sequence characteristics while maintaining a global KV budget?

## Limitations
- Calibration distribution shift may misguide factorization if **C** poorly represents runtime inputs.  
- Flat spectral profiles cause adjusted-rank allocation to degenerate to uniform rank, negating adaptive benefits.  
- Non-standard positional encodings (e.g., ALiBi) may break the KV-parity mapping.

## Confidence
- Activation-aware factorization: **High** (theoretically grounded, consistent gains).  
- Adjusted-rank allocation: **Medium** (greedy optimality only for defined priority; static ranks may drift post-finetuning).  
- KV-parity mapping: **High** for standard RoPE models; **Low** for alternative encodings.

## Next Checks
1. **OOD calibration test**: Estimate **C** from a mismatched domain (e.g., code data for a web-pretrained model) and measure one-shot perplexity change; expect >2× drop if break condition holds.  
2. **Spectral diversity audit**: Compute per-layer tail-energy ratios across multiple model families; if >80% of layers have ratios within 10% of the mean, CARE-E should offer no advantage over uniform rank—verify empirically.  
3. **Positional encoding stress test**: Apply CARE to models with non-standard RoPE variants or ALiBi; check KV-cache size parity and attention logit distributions for failures.
