# Transformer-Based Reinforcement Learning for Autonomous Orbital Collision Avoidance in Partially Observable Environments

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06088
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06088
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A Transformer-XL-based POMDP solver reduces fuel use by 7.8% and thrust magnitude by 10.7% compared to an MLP baseline under medium sensing degradation, while always satisfying collision probability constraints.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles autonomous orbital collision avoidance under partial observability, where sensor quality degrades with object distance. It introduces a framework combining a configurable encounter simulator, a distance-dependent observation model (noise and masking), an Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) for state estimation, and a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent with a Transformer-XL (TrXL) policy. The reward uses a Mahalanobis-distance surrogate from the UKF covariance to approximate collision risk. Evaluated across five observability regimes, both the TrXL and MLP baselines maintain collision probability below 1e-4, but the TrXL policy achieves significantly better fuel and thrust efficiency, with the largest gains at intermediate degradation levels.

## Method Summary
The task is a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP) for a single debris object. The environment uses a Keplerian two-body dynamic model and an inverse-encounter generator to produce physically consistent, collision-prone initial conditions. Observations are distance-dependent: as separation increases, position noise grows and measurements are randomly masked (hold-last). An UKF fuses these noisy observations to estimate the 6D relative state. The RL agent (TrXL or MLP) outputs a Gaussian action (thrust vector). Training uses PPO with a composite reward: a risk term based on the Mahalanobis distance between the predicted state and a collision boundary, a cumulative fuel penalty, and a trajectory deviation penalty. Performance is evaluated under five predefined observability regimes defined by anchor distances and corresponding noise/masking parameters.

## Key Results
- Both architectures satisfied the collision probability constraint (Pc < 1e-4) in all evaluation episodes.
- At medium observability, Transformer-XL reduced fuel consumption by 7.8% (1.30 vs 1.41 m/s) and mean thrust magnitude by 10.7% (1.34 vs 1.50 m/s) compared to MLP.
- Transformer-XL achieved a 9.6% closer closest approach (12.3 vs 13.6 km), indicating more efficient maneuvers.
- Performance gains were largest at intermediate levels of sensing degradation; out-of-sample evaluation suggested generalization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Transformer-XL's recurrent mechanism allows it to maintain a memory of past (potentially masked) observations, mitigating the effects of partial observability better than a feedforward MLP which sees only a fixed-length window. The UKF provides a statistically consistent state estimate from intermittent noisy data, and its covariance naturally feeds into the Mahalanobis-distance reward surrogate, which smoothly approximates collision risk without requiring expensive Monte Carlo probability calculations. This combination lets the policy learn maneuvers that are proactive (using estimated risk) rather than reactive to immediate noisy measurements.

## Foundational Learning
- **POMDP:** Formalizes the problem of optimal control with imperfect observations. *Why needed:* Real sensors degrade with range, making full state information unavailable. *Quick check:* Can you write the belief update equation?

- **Mahalanobis Distance:** Metric for outlier detection in multivariate space. *Why needed:* Provides a differentiable surrogate for collision probability using the UKF's covariance. *Quick check:* Is it scale-invariant and accounts for correlations?
- **Transformer-XL:** Sequence model with segment-level recurrence and relative positional encoding. *Why needed:* Captures long-range dependencies from a history of variable-length observations, crucial under masking. *Quick check:* How does the recurrence mechanism differ from standard Transformer?
- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** On-policy RL algorithm with clipped objective. *Why needed:* Stable policy updates for continuous control with sparse/delayed rewards. *Quick check:* What is the purpose of the clipping parameter?
- **Keplerian Orbital Dynamics:** Two-body motion in inertial frame. *Why needed:* Physically realistic simulation of spacecraft and debris trajectories. *Quick check:* Are the equations of motion integrated with a suitable scheme (e.g., RK4)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Encounter Simulator -> Distance-Dependent Observation Model -> Unscented Kalman Filter -> Policy Network (TrXL/MLP) -> Action (Thrust) -> Environment Step

**Critical Path:**  
1. Generate initial relative state via inverse-encounter.  
2. At each step, compute true separation `d` → interpolate noise `σ(d)` and masking `M(d)` → apply to clean relative position measurement.  
3. UKF ingests this noisy/masked measurement to update belief (mean `δx`, covariance `P`).  
4. Policy takes recent history of observations (or belief?) and outputs thrust.  
5. Dynamics propagate state; reward computed via UKF covariance at predicted TCA.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *TrXL vs MLP:* TrXL handles variable histories and masking but is computationally heavier; MLP is simpler but loses long-term context.  
- *UKF fidelity:* More accurate process/measurement noise models improve state estimates but may overfit if mis-specified; simpler models are robust but less precise.  
- *Reward shaping:* Mahalanobis surrogate is cheap but approximates true Pc; piecewise-linear terms balance competing objectives.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Persistent Pc violations → UKF misconfiguration (Q/R) or incorrect TCA covariance projection.  
- High variance in fuel consumption → reward component scaling imbalance; risk term may dominate early training.  
- TrXL underperforms MLP → attention not leveraging past (check masking handling or segment length).

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. Validate inverse-encounter generator: forward-propagate initial states and confirm predicted closest approach, relative speed, and approach angle match descriptor tolerances.  
2. Sweep UKF parameters (Q, R) on held-out episodes; correlate estimated Mahalanobis-based Pc with true Monte Carlo Pc to ensure surrogate validity.  
3. Ablate TrXL segment length (e.g., 32, 64, 128) under medium observability; measure impact on fuel and Pc to confirm memory sensitivity.
