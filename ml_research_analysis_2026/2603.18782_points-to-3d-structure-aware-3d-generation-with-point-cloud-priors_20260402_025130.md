# Points-to-3D: Structure-Aware 3D Generation with Point Cloud Priors

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18782
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18782
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Points-to-3D integrates point cloud priors into the TRELLIS diffusion framework, achieving PSNR 22.91 and F-score 0.964 on Toys4K and Chamfer Distance 0.013 on 3D-FRONT, even with VGGT-estimated point clouds.

## Executive Summary
Points-to-3D addresses geometry-controllable 3D generation by incorporating visible-region point cloud priors into the TRELLIS diffusion system. The method replaces TRELLIS’s noise-only sparse structure latent initialization with a voxelized and encoded point cloud, anchoring the generation to observed geometry while completing unseen areas. A mask-aware inpainting network, trained on visibility-aware partial-complete pairs, performs a two-stage sampling: structural inpainting followed by boundary refinement. Evaluations on Toys4K and 3D-FRONT datasets show consistent improvements over baselines, with notable gains in rendering quality and geometric metrics, and maintain performance when using point clouds estimated from single images via VGGT.

## Method Summary
Points-to-3D builds upon TRELLIS’s 3D asset generation pipeline. Given a point cloud (accurate or estimated from images), visible points are voxelized into a 64³ binary occupancy grid. This grid is encoded by TRELLIS’s pre-trained sparse structure (SS) VAE to produce a partial SS latent. The latent is combined with a binary downsampled mask (indicating preserved regions) and Gaussian noise for missing parts, forming the initial latent. This conditioned latent is fed to a mask-aware flow transformer, which iteratively denoises. Sampling follows a two-stage schedule: the first 25 of 50 steps apply the mask (structural inpainting), while the remaining steps use an all-ones mask (boundary refinement). The final latent is decoded into a voxel occupancy grid, which is then processed by TRELLIS’s structured latent generator and decoder to produce the final 3D representation (e.g., Gaussian splats). The inpainting network is trained on pairs of partial SS latents (derived from rendered visible points) and complete SS latents using conditional flow matching loss.

## Key Results
- PSNR increased from 21.94 to 22.91 and F-score from 0.832 to 0.964 on Toys4K with accurate point cloud priors.
- Chamfer Distance reduced from 0.034 to 0.013 on 3D-FRONT scenes, demonstrating superior geometric fidelity.
- Using VGGT-estimated point clouds from single images, the method still outperforms the TRELLIS baseline, showing robustness to noisy priors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Anchoring Diffusion with Point Cloud Prior
- **Claim:** Points-to-3D anchors the diffusion process to observed geometry by replacing TRELLIS’s noise-only SS latent initialization with a latent derived from the visible point cloud.
- **Mechanism:** The point cloud is voxelized into binary occupancy \(M'\) (64³), then encoded via the SS VAE encoder \(E_s\) to obtain \(q_{\text{vis}}\). A downsampled occupancy mask \(m_s\) (16³) marks preserved regions. The initial latent is \(q_{\text{comb}} = m_s \odot q_{\text{vis}} + (1-m_s)\odot\epsilon_s\) (Gaussian noise). This latent is concatenated with \(m_s\) and fed to the mask-aware inpainting transformer \(G_{\text{inp}}\).
- **Core assumption:** \(E_s\) can faithfully map the voxelized point cloud into a latent that preserves observable geometry, and \(m_s\) accurately separates fixed from generatable regions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Equations (1)-(3) in Section 3.2; the abstract explicitly states the replacement of pure-noise latent. Neighboring works focus on point cloud completion but do not inject priors into diffusion latents.
- **Break condition:** Poor VAE reconstruction of the voxelized point cloud or inaccurate \(m_s\) (e.g., due to sparse/noisy input) leads to drift or artifacts in generated regions.

### Mechanism 2: Two-Stage Sampling for Global Coherence
- **Claim:** The two-stage sampling strategy (first \(s\) steps: structural inpainting; remaining \(t-s\) steps: boundary refinement) generates a globally coherent skeleton while avoiding “holes” along visibility boundaries.
- **Mechanism:** During the first \(s=25\) steps (out of \(t=50\)), \(G_{\text{inp}}\) denoises while constantly re‑applying the mask \(m_s\) to the latent, forcing preservation of anchored geometry. In the remaining \(t-s=25\) steps, an all‑ones mask is used, converting the process to standard denoising. This permits subtle boundary adjustments without disturbing the global scaffold.
- **Core assumption:** The inpainting model can produce a plausible global structure within \(s\) steps, and subsequent denoising can refine boundary inconsistencies without overwriting the anchored geometry.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.4 describes the schedule and rationale; Figure 6 (ablation) shows that using only inpainting (\(s=50\)) leaves boundary holes, while the two‑stage approach resolves them.
- **Break condition:** If \(s\) is too low, the global structure remains inconsistent; if \(s\) is too high, boundary artifacts persist. If the inpainting model lacks capacity to generate coherent structures from partial latents, refinement may not recover connectivity.

### Mechanism 3: Visibility-Aware Training Data Construction
- **Claim:** Training the inpainting network on pairs of partial and complete structure latents, where partial latents mimic real visible regions, teaches the model to propagate structural cues from observed to unseen areas.
- **Mechanism:** For each training 3D asset, \(T=24\) views are rendered. The complete point cloud is transformed to camera coordinates; a point is marked visible if its projected depth matches the rendered depth within tolerance \(\tau=0.05\times\)depth range. The resulting visible point cloud \(P_t\) is voxelized and encoded to obtain \(q_{\text{comb}}^t\) and mask \(m_s^t\), paired with the full SS latent \(q_{\text{gt}}\). The network is trained under conditional flow matching (CFM) loss.
- **Core assumption:** Depth‑consistent projection accurately simulates which surface points would be visible from a given viewpoint, and the resulting partial‑complete pairs capture physically plausible structure completion.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 details the visibility extraction and training procedure; parameters are reported in Section 4.1. Neighboring papers use similar rendering-based visibility but not within a diffusion training pipeline.
- **Break condition:** An inappropriate \(\tau\) or low rendering resolution yields simulated visible point clouds with false occlusions or missing visible regions, misaligning training pairs and harming inpainting realism.

## Foundational Learning

#### Concept: Latent 3D Diffusion with TRELLIS
- **Why needed:** Points-to-3D modifies TRELLIS’s core generation pipeline; understanding its two‑stage latent (SS latent → SLAT) and Flow Transformer architecture is essential to see where point cloud integration occurs.
- **Quick check:** How does TRELLIS decode a sparse structure latent into a voxel occupancy grid, and how is the structured latent subsequently generated from it?

#### Concept: Point Cloud Processing (Voxelization & Encoding)
- **Why needed:** The point cloud prior must be transformed into the SS latent space. Voxelization discretizes Euclidean space, and the pre-trained SS VAE encodes the grid; both steps must preserve geometric fidelity.
- **Quick check:** What are the resolution trade‑offs (voxel grid 64³ vs SS latent 16³) and how might downsampling affect thin structures in the point cloud?

#### Concept: Diffusion Inpainting with Spatial Masks
- **Why needed:** The task is fundamentally inpainting: generate missing geometry while respecting masked (visible) regions. The mask‑aware conditioning in the Flow Transformer and the staged sampling are inspired by 2D diffusion inpainting.
- **Quick check:** How does concatenating the binary mask \(m_s\) to the latent \(q_{\text{comb}}\) along the channel dimension inform the transformer about which regions to preserve versus generate?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map (plain text):**  
  Point cloud -> Voxelizer (64³ binary M') -> SS VAE encoder (E_s) -> Partial SS latent q_vis; Downsample M' -> Mask m_s; Latent formation: q_comb = m_s ⊙ q_vis + (1-m_s) ⊙ ϵ_s; Concatenate with m_s -> Mask‑aware Flow Transformer G_inp; Two‑stage sampling (first s steps with m_s, remaining with ones) -> Completed SS latent q_pred -> SS decoder D_s -> Voxel occupancy -> TRELLIS Structured Latent Generator (G_l) and Decoder -> Final 3D asset (e.g., Gaussian splats).

- **Critical path:** The sequence that must function correctly: point cloud voxelization, SS VAE encoding, mask generation, latent initialization, iterative denoising with G_inp (two‑stage schedule), and final decoding. The key modifications are the point cloud injection (steps 2‑4) and the sampling schedule (step 7).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Mask concatenation vs. cross‑attention:* Concatenation is simple and directly injects region constraints but may limit flexibility; cross‑attention could allow more nuanced conditioning but requires architectural changes.
  - *Fixed mask during first stage vs. evolving mask:* Keeping \(m_s\) fixed during structural inpainting strongly anchors visible regions but risks boundary holes; the refinement stage loosens this.
  - *Synthetic visibility training vs. real sensor data:* Synthetic rendering provides abundant data but may not capture real sensor noise; training on real data is not currently done.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Visible region drift:** Generated geometry deviates from input point cloud in observed areas (measure point‑to‑mesh distance in visible regions).
  - **Boundary holes/gaps:** Inpainting edges show missing connectivity or jagged artifacts (visible in normal maps, increased Chamfer distance near boundaries).
  - **Over‑smoothing in refinement:** Excess refinement steps cause subtle loss of fine details in visible regions (drop in visible‑region PSNR/F‑score).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline replication:** Reproduce TRELLIS on Toys4K/3D‑FRONT and verify that the pretrained SS VAE can reconstruct voxelized ground‑truth point clouds with high fidelity (e.g., >95% IoU).
  2. **Accurate point cloud prior test:** Use ground‑truth visible point clouds (from rendering) as input; expect >1dB PSNR gain and >0.1 F‑score improvement over TRELLIS on visible regions (Table 1).
  3. **VGGT noise sensitivity test:** Corrupt accurate point clouds with synthetic noise (Gaussian jitter, dropout, misalignment) and evaluate degradation in CD/F‑score; if drop exceeds 20%, recommend preprocessing (e.g., radius outlier removal) before voxelization.

## Limitations
- TRELLIS integration fidelity: Exact modifications to VAE and flow transformer, training hyperparameters, and architecture tweaks are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- Training data simulation: Unspecified rendering details (camera distribution, resolution, depth map generation) and point sampling strategy could affect inpainting behavior and generalizability.
- VGGT robustness: Real‑world point cloud noise, sparsity, and misalignment (without post‑hoc alignment) may degrade performance more than reported; the paper assumes alignment to ground truth for evaluation.

## Confidence
- Latent injection mechanism (Mechanism 1): Medium confidence (architectural change is clear but VAE fidelity on noisy/sparse clouds less characterized).
- Two‑stage sampling strategy (Mechanism 2): Medium‑High confidence (ablation in Figure 6 supports benefit; choice of s=25 is empirical).
- Visibility‑aware training data construction (Mechanism 3): Medium confidence (standard rendering simulation; sensitivity to τ and view sampling not studied).
- Overall superiority over TRELLIS: High confidence on Toys4K and 3D‑FRONT benchmarks (Tables 1‑2), but limited to those datasets and metrics.
