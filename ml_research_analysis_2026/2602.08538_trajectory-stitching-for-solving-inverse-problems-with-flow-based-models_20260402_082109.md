# Trajectory Stitching for Solving Inverse Problems with Flow-Based Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08538
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08538
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MS-Flow reduces memory to constant while scaling to large flow-based models and achieves higher PSNR/SSIM on inverse problems.

## Executive Summary
Flow-based generative models (e.g., via flow matching) have been applied to inverse problems by optimizing a latent code through the ODE dynamics, but this process is memory-intensive and numerically unstable due to long trajectories. This paper introduces MS-Flow, a multiple-shooting framework that represents the trajectory with intermediate latent states (shooting points) and enforces dynamics locally with soft penalties. Through an alternating minimization scheme, MS-Flow decouples trajectory updates from data consistency, achieving constant memory usage regardless of discretization steps and improved numerical conditioning. Experiments on image restoration tasks (inpainting, super-resolution, CT) show that MS-Flow outperforms prior flow-based solvers in reconstruction quality while being significantly more memory efficient, and it scales to large latent diffusion models where backpropagation through full trajectories is infeasible.

## Method Summary
MS-Flow reformulates inverse problem solving with flow-based priors as an ODE-constrained optimization. Instead of a single initial latent code, it optimizes a sequence of shooting points along the trajectory. The objective combines data consistency at the terminal point, a radial Gaussian prior on the initial point, and quadratic penalties that enforce continuity between consecutive segments via explicit Euler integration. Optimization proceeds by alternating between (1) updating the shooting points using a Jacobian-free coordinate descent (backward sweep) that avoids storing the computational graph for the entire trajectory, and (2) updating the terminal solution via a closed-form Tikhonov step for linear operators or a few gradient steps for nonlinear decoders. This approach reduces peak GPU memory to constant with respect to the number of integration steps and mitigates numerical instability from long-horizon sensitivity.

## Key Results
- Memory consumption becomes independent of the ODE discretization steps, enabling scaling to large latent flow models (e.g., Stable Diffusion 3.5).
- MS-Flow achieves higher PSNR and SSIM than prior flow-based solvers (D-Flow, Flow-Prior, PnP-Flow, FlowDPS) on standard image restoration benchmarks (inpainting, super-resolution, CT).
- The method demonstrates improved convergence stability and handles large-scale models where full backpropagation through the ODE is impractical.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** MS-Flow reduces memory consumption by avoiding backpropagation through the entire ODE trajectory.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of optimizing a single initial latent code `x(0)` and differentiating through all `nt` integration steps (single-shooting), MS-Flow introduces intermediate latent states (`x0, ..., xK`) as independent variables. The dynamics are enforced locally via soft penalties between consecutive shooting points, enabling an alternating minimization where trajectory updates do not require storing the computational graph for the full horizon.
- **Core assumption:** The flow dynamics `vθ` are locally smooth enough that enforcing continuity via quadratic penalties on short segments yields a solution close to the true ODE trajectory when penalties are sufficiently weighted.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "MS-Flow represents the trajectory as a sequence of intermediate latent states rather than a single initial code... This reduces memory consumption."
  - Section 3.1: The objective (Equation 7) replaces the hard ODE constraint with soft stitching penalties, decoupling trajectory integration from end-to-end differentiation.
  - Section 3.3: Complexity analysis shows peak memory `O(M_net)` constant w.r.t. discretization, unlike D-Flow's `O(nt M_net)`.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Multiple shooting improves numerical conditioning and convergence stability compared to single shooting.
- **Mechanism:** The long-horizon sensitivity of a single trajectory (exponential error amplification per Grönwall's inequality, Eq. 14) is mitigated by splitting the interval into `K` segments. Errors in one segment are contained by local penalties, preventing accumulation. This better-conditioned subproblem enables stable coordinate descent updates.
- **Core assumption:** The flow vector field `vθ` is Lipschitz continuous in `x`, so local error growth is bounded by `e^{LΔt}` per segment vs. `e^{L}` globally.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 4.1: Explicitly contrasts single-shooting's exponential sensitivity with multiple-shooting's local amplification control.
  - Section 4.2: Proposition 4.1 proves descent and stationarity for the inner trajectory updates under Lipschitz-gradient assumptions, guaranteeing stable convergence.
  - Corpus: Neighbors like "Multiple-Shooting/Single-Shooting in ML" cite classical optimal control theory (Bock & Plitt, 1984) supporting this design.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Alternating minimization decouples the neural dynamics from the data consistency term, enabling efficient and flexible optimization.
- **Mechanism:** The MS-Flow objective (Eq. 7) is optimized by alternately: (1) updating shooting points `X` to satisfy trajectory consistency and latent regularization (Eq. 10a), and (2) updating terminal estimate `x*` to satisfy data consistency (Eq. 10b). This separation means the expensive `vθ` evaluations are only needed for local segment integration during trajectory updates, not for full backward passes.
- **Core assumption:** The data consistency subproblem (Eq. 10b) is sufficiently simple/strongly convex (e.g., quadratic `Φ`) to yield a closed-form or efficiently solvable proximal step.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 3.2: Explicitly describes the two subproblems and shows for quadratic `Φ` the data consistency update is a closed-form Tikhonov solution (Eq. 13).
  - Algorithm 1: Outlines the alternating loop.
  - Section 3.3: Time complexity `O(LK C_fwd)` vs. D-Flow's `O(nt(C_fwd + C_vjp))` demonstrates efficiency gain from avoiding global backprop.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: ODE-constrained optimization**
  - *Why needed here:* MS-Flow reformulates latent space optimization as minimizing an objective subject to the flow ODE dynamics, a classic problem in optimal control. Understanding constraints, adjoint methods, and shooting methods is key.
  - *Quick check question:* How does relaxing a hard ODE constraint with a quadratic penalty change the optimization landscape compared to enforcing it exactly via single shooting?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Pre-trained flow model vθ (frozen) -> Shooting points X (optimized) -> Terminal state x* (alternating update) <-> Data consistency via A (measurements y)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialization: Compute initial x0* from measurements (e.g., filtered back-projection) and sample x0 from prior; linearly interpolate shooting points between x0 and x0*.
  2. Outer loop (alternating minimization):
     a. Trajectory update: For L inner steps, perform a backward sweep updating xK, x_{K-1}, ..., x0 using coordinate descent with Jacobian-free gradient approximation (I+Δk Jvθ ≈ I).
     b. Data consistency update: For linear operators A, solve closed-form Tikhonov: x* = (AᵀA+αI)⁻¹(Aᵀy+αxK); for nonlinear, use few gradient steps.
  3. Check convergence (e.g., change in x*) and repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Penalty weights (γ for trajectory, α for data consistency, λ for prior): Too low γ → shooting points drift; too high → overfit to flow and ignore measurements. α balances fidelity vs. prior; λ controls prior strength. Paper uses fixed γ=0.01 robustly.
  - Jacobian-free approximation: Neglecting Jvθ in VJP reduces memory but introduces bias; backtracking line search ensures stability.
  - Number of shooting points K: Larger K improves local conditioning but increases variables; compute scales with K. Empirically K=6-12 for 64x64, K~29 for latent SD 3.5.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Exploding trajectory loss J: Reduce step size η or increase γ.
  - x* diverges from plausible images: Increase α or check conditioning of A.
  - Stagnation at low PSNR: Increase inner steps L or adjust λ to better match prior typical set.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Convergence sanity check:** On a small problem (e.g., 32x32 inpainting), run with L=10, K=3. Monitor trajectory loss and PSNR; should decrease (Prop 4.1).
  2. **Memory scaling test:** Vary K (3,6,12) and measure peak GPU memory; should be constant vs. D-Flow which increases linearly with nt.
  3. **Reconstruction quality baseline:** Compare against D-Flow and FlowDPS on a standard task (e.g., Gaussian deblurring on CelebA). Verify MS-Flow achieves competitive/better PSNR/SSIM with fewer timesteps.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to stochastic differential equation (SDE) models:** How to adapt MS-Flow to diffusion models (SDE-based) which involve stochasticity? Requires new consistency constraints.
2. **Adaptive penalty updates:** Can adaptive schedules for penalties (e.g., increasing γ) improve robustness and convergence across diverse inverse problems?
3. **Convergence guarantees under non-convexity:** What are the convergence properties when the flow model and data consistency term are non-convex, and how does the Jacobian-free approximation affect global convergence?
4. **Choosing K and penalties:** How to optimally trade off K, reconstruction accuracy, memory, and time for a given inverse problem? Currently tuned on small validation set.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter choices (γ, α, λ) lack extensive robustness analysis; may need task-specific tuning.
- Exact flow model architecture and training details are not fully disclosed, hindering exact reproduction.
- For nonlinear data consistency, only few gradient steps are used with no convergence theory; may not converge to optimal solution.
