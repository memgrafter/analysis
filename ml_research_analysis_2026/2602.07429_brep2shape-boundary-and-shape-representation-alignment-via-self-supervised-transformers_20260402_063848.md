# Brep2Shape: Boundary and Shape Representation Alignment via Self-Supervised Transformers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.07429  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07429  
- **Reference count**: 32  
- **Primary result**: Self-supervised pre-training on 250k B-rep models with a Dual Transformer and topology attention achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on CAD classification (TMCAD: 0.8264) and segmentation (MFCAD++: 0.9934, IoU 0.9799) with faster convergence (100 vs. 350 epochs).

## Executive Summary
B-rep2Shape bridges the fundamental representation gap between abstract boundary representations (B-rep) used in CAD and intuitive 3D shape geometry. The core innovation is a self-supervised pre-training framework that predicts dense spatial points from parametric Bézier control points, forcing the model to learn a coherent mapping between symbolic and geometric domains. A Dual Transformer backbone processes surface and curve entities in parallel streams, while a topology attention module injects cross-stream geometric biases derived from the B-rep adjacency graph, ensuring consistency across shared boundaries. Pre-trained on a large corpus of 250k unlabeled B-rep models, the learned representations generalize effectively; fine-tuning yields state-of-the-art results on multiple downstream benchmarks. The method also exhibits favorable scaling properties—increased pre-training data and model size consistently reduce loss and improve downstream accuracy, while converging significantly faster than prior works.

## Method Summary
Given a CAD model in B-rep form (a set of NURBS entities and face-edge topological adjacency), Brep2Shape first decomposes each NURBS entity into a fixed set of low-order Bézier primitives (edges into segments, faces into Bézier triangles) to obtain uniform tokenizable units. For each entity, a small tokenizer (MLP followed by a 3‑layer Transformer) aggregates the primitive control points into a single face or edge token. These tokens feed a Dual Transformer backbone with parallel face and edge streams; each layer applies standard self‑attention but with a bias term computed from the embeddings of adjacent entities in the other stream (topology attention). The pre‑training task is to reconstruct analytically sampled dense spatial points from the entity tokens, using separate MLP heads that output *m* points per entity. The loss is the mean squared error between predicted and ground‑truth points. After pre‑training, the backbone is frozen or fine‑tuned, and task‑specific heads are added for downstream classification or segmentation, trained with cross‑entropy.

## Key Results
- TMCAD classification accuracy: **0.8264** vs. BRT’s 0.8090.
- MFCAD++ segmentation: accuracy **0.9934** and mean IoU **0.9799**, surpassing prior state‑of‑the‑art.
- Favorable scaling: larger pre‑training datasets and model size consistently lower pre‑training loss and boost downstream metrics; convergence in **100 epochs** versus 350 for baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Aligning Abstract B-rep with Intuitive Shape Geometry
- **Claim**: Pre‑training on predicting dense spatial points from parametric Bézier control points aligns abstract boundary representations with intuitive shape representations.  
- **Mechanism**: The model minimizes mean squared error between predicted and analytically sampled spatial points, forcing it to learn the mapping from control point coefficients (abstract) to actual 3D occupancy (intuitive). This creates a shared latent space where both representations are coherent.  
- **Core assumption**: The control point set, though parametric, contains sufficient information to reconstruct the full manifold when processed with proper topological context.  
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract] “aligns abstract boundary representations with intuitive shape representations… predicting dense spatial points from parametric Bézier control points.”  
  - [section 3.1] Formalizes pre‑training task as `minθ ∥U − fθ(P,G)∥²` where `U` is sampled points and `P` is control point set.  
  - [corpus] Neighbors focus on generative or alternative representations but do not directly challenge this alignment mechanism.  
- **Break condition**: If pre‑training loss plateaus despite sufficient data/model size, it may indicate the control point representation itself is insufficient for certain geometries (e.g., highly rational NURBS) or the decoder cannot effectively map latent space to spatial points.

### Mechanism 2: Topology Attention Enforces Geometric Continuity
- **Claim**: Topology attention via cross‑stream geometric features as attention biases enforces geometric continuity and topological consistency between adjacent B‑rep entities.  
- **Mechanism**: The face and edge streams incorporate each other’s embeddings as dynamic bias in self‑attention, derived from the B‑rep adjacency graph and its dual. This biases attention scores toward topologically adjacent entities, ensuring that shared boundaries (edges) impose consistency constraints across adjacent faces.  
- **Core assumption**: B‑rep topology (face‑edge adjacency) encodes critical boundary constraints that are not captured by local control points alone.  
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract] “topology attention is integrated to model the interdependencies between surfaces and curves, thereby maintaining topological consistency.”  
  - [section 3.2] Details Topo‑Attn: “the value of attention bias between two face tokens is explicitly derived by linearly projecting the embedding of their shared edge token.”  
- **Break condition**: If fine‑tuned segmentation shows errors specifically at face boundaries while interior regions are correct, topology attention may be failing to propagate edge‑level consistency signals effectively.

### Mechanism 3: Dual Transformer Preserves Modality‑Specific Geometry
- **Claim**: Dual Transformer with parallel face/edge streams preserves distinct geometric properties and enables bidirectional information flow, outperforming entangled representations.  
- **Mechanism**: Separate Transformer encoders process face and edge tokens independently, maintaining modality‑specific inductive biases (surfaces vs. curves). The cross‑stream topology attention couples them without forcing homogeneous processing, allowing each stream to specialize while staying globally consistent.  
- **Core assumption**: Surfaces (2D manifolds) and curves (1D boundaries) have intrinsically different geometric characteristics that benefit from separate processing pipelines.  
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract] “Dual Transformer backbone with parallel streams that independently encode surface and curve tokens to capture their distinct geometric properties.”  
  - [section 3.2] Ablations in Table 4 show Dual Transformer outperforms Face‑only and Face+Edge (entangled) models on segmentation (MFCAD++ IoU: 0.9799 vs. 0.9487 for entangled).  
- **Break condition**: If model scaling (more layers) leads to diminishing returns or degradation, the dual‑stream architecture may not fully utilize added capacity due to insufficient cross‑stream coupling or redundancy between streams.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map (plain text)**
B‑rep STEP file → Bézier decomposition → Tokenizers (face/edge MLP+Transformer) → Dual Transformer (parallel streams + topology attention) → Pre‑training heads / Downstream heads

**Critical path**
1. Load raw B‑rep STEP file → extract NURBS entities and face‑edge adjacency.  
2. Decompose each entity into Bézier primitives (edges: segments; faces: triangles via rectangle split).  
3. Tokenize: For each entity, aggregate primitive control points via small Transformer → entity token.  
4. Dual Transformer: Propagate face/edge tokens with topology attention for *L* layers.  
5. Pre‑training: Attach point‑prediction heads, compute MSE loss between predicted and analytically sampled points, backpropagate.  
6. Fine‑tuning: Freeze or fine‑tune backbone, replace heads with task‑specific layers, train with cross‑entropy.

**Design tradeoffs**
- Fixed‑degree Bézier primitives vs. fidelity: Simpler primitives enable uniform tokenization but introduce approximation error near trimmed boundaries. Tradeoff controlled by chord‑to‑arc ratio *τ*.  
- Dual‑stream vs. single‑stream: Dual preserves modality‑specific features but doubles stream management; single‑stream (like BRT) is simpler but may conflate surface/curve geometries.  
- Topology attention complexity: Injecting cross‑stream biases adds minimal compute but requires correct adjacency graph construction; errors in topology propagate to attention.

**Failure signatures**
- High pre‑training loss on curves (vs. faces) may indicate inadequate modeling of 1D geometry in edge stream.  
- Downstream segmentation failures predominantly at face boundaries suggest topology attention is not enforcing edge consistency.  
- Poor scaling (loss not decreasing with more data/layers) could signal decomposition artifacts overwhelming learning signal or optimization issues.

**First 3 experiments**
1. Ablate topology attention: Replace with standard self‑attention in Dual Transformer; expect drop in segmentation IoU (Table 4 shows 0.9582 vs. 0.9799).  
2. Vary pre‑training data size: Train on 25k, 50k, 100k, 250k subsets; monitor pre‑training loss and downstream accuracy (Figure 4). Expect consistent improvement.  
3. Test edge‑level supervision importance: Remove edge loss during pre‑training; face loss should increase (Table 3), and downstream tasks with boundary‑sensitive labels (e.g., Fusion360Seg) should degrade.

## Limitations
- Topology attention bias aggregation method (e.g., mean vs. max for shared edges/faces) is unspecified.
- Exact hyperparameters for tokenizer and Dual Transformer (FFN dimensions, dropout rates) are unreported.
- Sampling strategy *m* (number of points per primitive) and precise pattern (random vs. grid) remain ambiguous.
- Train/validation/test splits for downstream datasets (FabWave, TMCAD, MFCAD++, Fusion360Seg) are not provided, hindering fair comparison.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (point prediction alignment)**: High confidence. Formal MSE objective and scaling (loss ↓ with data/model size) validate the learned mapping.  
- **Mechanism 2 (topology attention)**: Medium confidence. Ablation shows a –0.0217 IoU drop, indicating importance; boundary errors would signal failure.  
- **Mechanism 3 (Dual Transformer streams)**: Medium confidence. Outperforms entangled baseline (+0.0312 IoU), but scaling may reveal underutilized capacity.
