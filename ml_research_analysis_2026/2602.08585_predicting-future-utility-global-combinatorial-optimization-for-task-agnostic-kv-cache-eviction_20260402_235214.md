# Predicting Future Utility: Global Combinatorial Optimization for Task-Agnostic KV Cache Eviction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08585  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08585  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: LU-KV reduces KV cache size by 80% with minimal performance degradation, improving accuracy from 40.54% (AdaKV) to 46.21% for Mistral-7B and achieving 69.98% vs. 37.48% on RULER-16K.

## Executive Summary
LU-KV addresses the growing memory and latency costs of autoregressive generation by reformulating KV cache eviction as a global combinatorial optimization problem. Instead of relying on instantaneous heuristic scores that ignore long-horizon utility heterogeneity across attention heads, LU-KV introduces the concept of Optimality Gap to correct for metric imperfections, uses convex-hull relaxation (via isotonic regression) to make the problem tractable, and applies a greedy algorithm that attains the global optimum for the relaxed objective. A data-driven offline profiling step computes per-head budget profiles for any target compression, enabling zero-overhead runtime lookups. Evaluations on LongBench and RULER show that LU-KV compresses the KV cache by 80% while recovering most of the performance gap relative to full cache, outperforming state-of-the-art methods like AdaKV and PyramidKV.

## Method Summary
The method consists of three stages: offline profiling, online lookup, and eviction. During offline profiling, a synthetic coherent long context and diverse queries are used to compute the Oracle Importance of each token—defined as its maximum potential output perturbation across future decode steps, weighted by attention. For a chosen heuristic metric π (e.g., SnapKV or KeyDiff), the raw eviction loss L_ℓ,h(b) for head (ℓ,h) is the sum of Oracle Importance of tokens not retained in the top‑b by π. The marginal gains g(b)=L(b−1)−L(b) are then adjusted via isotonic regression (PAVA) to enforce a non‑increasing sequence, which relaxes the original non‑convex loss into a convex surrogate. Greedy allocation then distributes a global budget B across all heads by repeatedly incrementing the head with the largest next marginal gain, yielding per‑head budgets b_ℓ,h. These budgets are averaged over calibration queries to produce a profile table Φ(π)(σ) mapping a global retention ratio σ to a local retention ratio per head. At runtime, given a target compression ratio, the system looks up the corresponding local ratios, translates them to integer budgets, and each head independently retains its top‑b tokens according to π. This approach decouples the metric from the allocation, making LU‑KV metric‑agnostic.

## Key Results
- Compressing the KV cache by 80% on LongBench and RULER, LU‑KV incurs only minor accuracy drops, significantly outperforming AdaKV and PyramidKV.
- For Mistral‑7B with KeyDiff, average accuracy rises from 40.54% (AdaKV) to 46.21% (LU‑KV), recovering 84% of the gap to the full‑cache upper bound.
- On RULER‑16K, LU‑KV achieves 69.98% versus 37.48% for AdaKV, while also reducing inference latency and GPU memory consumption.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
We present three core mechanisms that explain LU‑KV's effectiveness.

**Mechanism 1: Correcting the Optimality Gap**
- **Claim:** Instantaneous heuristic metrics fail because they assume score magnitudes are consistent proxies for importance across all heads, overlooking heterogeneity in predictive fidelity for long-horizon utility.
- **Mechanism:** LU‑KV introduces the *Optimality Gap* (Δ) to quantify the discrepancy between a heuristic metric (π) and the Oracle Importance (π*). The total eviction loss decomposes into an inherent Oracle loss (unavoidable due to compression) plus the Optimality Gap (arising from metric imperfection). Optimal allocation must account for Δ, not just raw scores.
- **Core assumption:** The long-horizon contribution of a token (Oracle Importance) can be bounded by its maximum potential output perturbation across future steps.
- **Evidence anchors:** The paper formally defines Oracle Importance (Eq. 4) and the loss decomposition (Eq. 7). Empirical comparisons with AdaKV and PyramidKV, which rely on magnitude‑based heuristics, support the claim.
- **Break condition:** If a metric perfectly ranks tokens according to Oracle Importance for all heads and budgets (Δ=0), then LU‑KV’s correction provides no gain and uniform allocation becomes optimal under that metric.

**Mechanism 2: Convex Relaxation Enables Near‑Optimal Allocation**
- **Claim:** The global budget allocation problem is combinatorial and non‑convex; relaxing it to a convex surrogate allows a greedy solver to achieve the global optimum.
- **Mechanism:** The discrete loss sequence L_ℓ,h(b) may be non‑convex. LU‑KV applies the Pool Adjacent Violators Algorithm (PAVA) to the marginal gains to enforce monotonic non‑increasing order, producing a convex, non‑increasing surrogate. The resulting marginal gain g(i) satisfies diminishing returns, so a greedy algorithm that always picks the head with the largest next marginal gain is provably optimal for the relaxed objective.
- **Core assumption:** The convex relaxation preserves the ordering of marginal benefits well enough that the greedy solution is near‑optimal for the original problem.
- **Evidence anchors:** The paper derives the relaxation (Section 4.2), proves greedy optimality for the relaxed problem (Eq. 17), and empirically shows greedy matches dynamic programming on calibration data (Figure 2a).
- **Break condition:** If the raw marginal gains are already non‑increasing (the loss is convex), the relaxation is superfluous but still correct; greedy remains optimal.

**Mechanism 3: Stable Offline Profiles Remove Runtime Overhead**
- **Claim:** A static, data‑driven offline profiling protocol can capture the expected utility profile of each head across tasks, enabling zero‑overhead online execution.
- **Mechanism:** Using a synthetic coherent long‑context and diverse queries, the system computes Oracle Importance offline and solves the relaxed optimization for a grid of global compression ratios. This yields per‑head local retention ratios that are averaged into a profile table Φ(π)(σ). During inference, a simple lookup maps the target compression to integer budgets.
- **Core assumption:** Attention heads exhibit consistent optimal local compression ratios across diverse downstream tasks (structural stability).
- **Evidence anchors:** The calibration procedure is detailed in Section 4.3. Figure 2b shows that profiles are stable across different task groups, supporting the assumption.
- **Break condition:** If head utility profiles are highly task‑specific and unstable across distributions, the static lookup table becomes misaligned, and dynamic per‑instance allocation would be necessary.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Offline Profiler -> Lookup Module -> Budget Translator -> Eviction Engine

**Critical path**  
- Offline: generate synthetic narrative + diverse queries → compute Oracle Importance for each token and head → for each heuristic π, compute raw loss L(b) → apply PAVA to marginal gains → for a grid of global retention ratios, run greedy allocation to obtain per‑head local ratios → average across queries to build profile Φ(π).  
- Online: given global compression target σ → retrieve local ratios from Φ(π) → compute integer budgets per head (adjust for rounding) → each head applies π to retain top‑b tokens → continue generation.

**Design tradeoffs**  
The offline profile gives near‑zero runtime overhead at the cost of potential distribution shift; it assumes structural stability of head utilities across tasks. The method is metric‑agnostic, allowing any plug‑in scoring function π. However, the profile is static—if the data distribution changes significantly, performance may degrade relative to dynamic methods.

**Failure signatures**  
- Misaligned profile: calibration corpus not representative → poor Φ(π) → high eviction loss.  
- Severe metric-oracle mismatch: even optimal allocation cannot compensate if Δ is large → performance ceiling.  
- Over‑aggressive compression: violating the minimal retention (e.g., 1% per head) can collapse heads that rely on “sink” tokens.  
- Rounding errors: naive floor operation may leave unused budget; must reallocate remaining units to heads with highest next marginal gain.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Validate convex relaxation: Compare the greedy solution against exact dynamic programming for the relaxed objective on calibration data (Figure 2a, Appendix A.2) to confirm optimality.  
2. Ablate Optimality Gap: Compare LU‑KV “without Δ” (allocating budgets based solely on total oracle importance) against the full LU‑KV on LongBench (Table 3) to demonstrate the benefit of gap correction.  
3. Visualize loss distribution: Compare per‑head eviction loss heatmaps of LU‑KV versus AdaKV/PyramidKV (Figure 4) to show suppression of “loss bursts” and more uniform loss across heads.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How robust are the offline allocation profiles to variations in calibration data distribution, and can they generalize to entirely unseen data domains or model architectures? The paper assumes consistency across tasks but only tests on fixed benchmarks; cross‑domain generalization and transfer to new architectures remain unexplored.  
- **Open Question 2:** Under what conditions does the convex‑hull relaxation via isotonic regression fail to approximate the true combinatorial optimization, and what is the theoretical bound on the suboptimality gap introduced? The relaxation is empirically validated but lacks general theoretical guarantees, especially for metrics with high discordance from oracle importance.  
- **Open Question 3:** Can the calibration overhead be eliminated by discovering a universal allocation pattern across models or metrics, enabling zero‑shot budget distribution without per‑model profiling? The paper does not analyze similarities in profiles across different models or scoring functions; if such commonalities exist, profiling could be avoided.

## Limitations
- The calibration corpus and query set are undisclosed, making it uncertain whether the derived profiles generalize to arbitrary data distributions.  
- Precise algorithmic details of KeyDiff are omitted, risking implementation mismatches that could affect reproducibility.  
- The convex‑hull relaxation via PAVA is ambiguously described (e.g., applied to loss vs. marginal gains), which may lead to variations in allocation and suboptimal performance.  
- Stability of per‑head utility profiles across tasks is supported by only one synthetic experiment; broader validation is needed.
