# GRASP: Gradient Realignment via Active Shared Perception for Multi-Agent Collaborative Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00717  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00717  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** GRASP consistently outperforms state-of-the-art MARL baselines (e.g., MAPPO, HAPPO, MA2E) in sample efficiency and asymptotic performance, achieving 96.7% win rate vs. 71.7% for MAPPO on SMAC MMM2 and reducing failure rate from 5.32% to 3.03% in Super Hard 27m vs 30m.

## Executive Summary
GRASP tackles non-stationarity in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) arising from concurrent policy updates, where agents passively perceive others' changes through environmental interactions, causing equilibrium oscillations and slow convergence. The method introduces *active shared perception*: each agent computes a local policy gradient, and a consensus gradient **u*** is derived as the minimum-norm vector in the convex hull of all agents' gradients. This **u*** aligns individual updates with a team-level generalized Bellman equilibrium, with existence guaranteed by the Kakutani Fixed-Point Theorem. Empirical evaluations across SMAC, SMACv2, Google Research Football, and Multi-Agent Particle Environments show GRASP surpasses leading baselines in both sample efficiency and final performance, demonstrating robustness in large-scale, stochastic tasks.

## Method Summary
GRASP builds on the PPO framework with a centralized critic and decentralized actors. After collecting on-policy trajectories, each agent computes its local policy gradient **g**ᵢ. A quadratic program solves for **u*** = argmin ||**u**||² subject to **u** = Σ cᵢ**g**ᵢ, Σ cᵢ=1, cᵢ≥0, yielding the minimum-norm consensus vector in the convex hull of all **g**ᵢ. Each agent’s actor update then uses the hybrid direction **v** = **g**ᵢ + **u*** within PPO’s clipped surrogate objective, ensuring stable trust-region updates. The critic is trained via MSE on TD(λ) targets with GAE. Key hyperparameters mirror MAPPO (e.g., learning rate 5×10⁻⁴, PPO epochs 5, GRU hidden size 64). The consensus computation is performed once per training iteration after data collection, not per mini-batch.

## Key Results
- Win rate of 96.7% vs. 71.7% for MAPPO on SMAC MMM2.
- Failure rate reduction from 5.32% to 3.03% in Super Hard 27m vs 30m.
- Consistent superiority over MAPPO, HAPPO, and MA2E across SMAC, SMACv2, GRF, and MPE benchmarks in sample efficiency and asymptotic performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** GRASP converts passive perception of other agents' policy changes into active shared perception by computing a consensus gradient **u*** from all agents' local updates.
- **Mechanism:** Each agent computes its local policy gradient; a quadratic program solves for **u*** as the minimum-norm vector in the convex hull of these gradients. This **u*** is added to individual gradients, aligning updates with a team objective.
- **Core assumption:** The convex hull formulation ensures **u*** has non-negative projection onto all local gradients (Pareto improvement), and the QP solution is continuous with respect to input gradients.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "computing a consensus gradient u* as the minimum-norm vector in the convex hull... aligns individual updates with a team-level generalized Bellman equilibrium." [section 3.2.2]: "GRASP solves for the minimum-norm vector u* within the Convex Hull..." Corpus confirms non-stationarity is known but GRASP’s active perception via convex optimization is novel; direct evidence for consensus mechanism is limited.
- **Break condition:** If local gradients are highly conflicting or span a degenerate convex hull (e.g., all opposing), **u*** may approach zero, failing to provide meaningful consensus and leading to stagnation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The generalized Bellman equilibrium (where **u*** = 0) serves as a stable convergence point, theoretically guaranteed by Kakutani Fixed-Point Theorem.
- **Mechanism:** Define consensus equilibrium as no joint improvement direction exists; prove existence by showing the GRASP update operator satisfies Kakutani's conditions (upper hemi-continuity, convexity on compact set).
- **Core assumption:** Policy parameter space is nonempty, compact, convex; update mapping is continuous.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "theoretically guaranteed via the Kakutani Fixed-Point Theorem." [section 3.3.1]: "Theorem 3.5: GRASP Equilibrium Point Existence... leverages the Kakutani Fixed-Point Theorem." Related work like "Learning Closed-Loop Parametric Nash Equilibria" uses fixed-point theory for Nash equilibria, supporting relevance but not direct application to consensus gradients.
- **Break condition:** Non-compact or non-convex parameter spaces, or discontinuous update mappings (e.g., from aggressive hyperparameters), may violate theorem conditions, causing divergence.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Combining consensus gradient with PPO's trust-region clipping ensures stable policy updates and monotonic improvement.
- **Mechanism:** Hybrid update direction **v** = **g**ᵢ + **u*** is applied, but PPO's clipping restricts policy probability ratios, bounding KL divergence and maintaining trust region despite consensus bias.
- **Core assumption:** PPO clipping effectively enforces a trust region even with external gradient **u***; consensus term **u*** is within Pareto cone.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "Empirical results... show GRASP consistently outperforms..." [section 3.3.2]: "The update rule... guarantees a dual-layer stability property termed Safe Pareto-Optimality." Corpus includes works highlighting trust-region importance in MARL, aligning with GRASP’s design.
- **Break condition:** If **u*** is consistently opposed to clipped gradients, effective update magnitude may diminish, reducing consensus benefits; or if clipping thresholds are too loose, policy collapse may occur.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Trajectories → Local Gradient Module (computes **g**ᵢ) → Consensus QP Solver (computes **u***) → Actor Update (applies **g**ᵢ + **u*** with PPO clipping)  
  Critic Update (parallel: trains value network with GAE)  
  Policy Network (GRU 64) underlies both actor and critic

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Sample trajectories with current policies.  
  2. Compute global advantages and local gradients **g**ᵢ for each agent.  
  3. Solve QP for consensus gradient **u***.  
  4. For multiple epochs: shuffle data, update critic via MSE, update each actor with hybrid gradient under PPO clip.  
  5. Rotate old policies.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  On-policy bias vs. efficiency: Natively on-policy ensures fresh gradients; off-policy adaptation (Appendix G) adds bias but improves sample reuse.  
  QP scalability: Exact QP solves in O(N³) time; for large N (>100), approximate methods (e.g., Frank-Wolfe) may be needed but could lose optimality.  
  Consensus strength: **u*** magnitude scales with gradient conflict; in high stochasticity, **u*** may be small, reducing gains (as noted in conclusion).

- **Failure signatures:**  
  Win rate plateaus below baselines, indicating ineffective consensus.  
  High variance in ‖**u***‖₂ across batches, suggesting unstable convex hull.  
  PPO clip ratio consistently at 1±ϵ, implying trust region violations from large **u***.  
  Critic TD error diverges, pointing to value estimation mismatch from aggressive updates.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Validate on SMAC 5m vs 6m: Confirm GRASP surpasses MAPPO in win rate and sample efficiency.  
  2. Test scalability with SMACv2 Terran 10v10: Measure performance retention as agent count and randomness increase.  
  3. Stress robustness in GRF Academy Corner (11 agents): Check if GRASP maintains advantage in sparse-reward, high-dimensional settings.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** How can we augment GRASP's gradient consensus with representation-level cues to maintain efficacy under extreme stochasticity?  
  **Basis:** [explicit] The conclusion states: "Future work will explore hybrid consensus operators combining representations and gradients for robust estimation, maintaining efficacy under extreme stochasticity."  
  **Why unresolved:** Current GRASP relies solely on gradient-space consensus, whose magnitude shrinks when high stochasticity expands the convex hull. Representation-based signals might provide a more stable alignment target.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Implement and compare several hybrid operators that fuse gradients and latent representation similarities on SMACv2 with amplified randomization; evaluate sample efficiency and asymptotic win rates relative to vanilla GRASP.

- **Question:** How robust are GRASP's monotonic improvement guarantees when implemented with approximate gradients and neural policies in partially observable tasks?  
  **Basis:** [inferred] Theoretical analysis assumes exact gradients and convex smoothness; empirical tests use RNNs with partial observability. Appendix A.1 notes off-policy estimation bias.  
  **Why unresolved:** Real-world function approximation introduces errors that violate proof conditions; the remaining guarantee strength and potential failure modes are unquantified.  
  **Resolving evidence:** In a controlled environment (e.g., MPE Simple Spread), systematically vary network capacity and observation history length; measure rate of Pareto-violations (⟨gᵢ, u*⟩ < 0) and empirical gap from theoretical improvement bounds.

- **Question:** What algorithmic approximations make the O(N²) convex-hull QP tractable for thousands of agents while preserving the Pareto-improvement property?  
  **Basis:** [inferred] GRASP solves a QP over N gradients with O(N²) storage; experiments only reach N=30 despite abstract claims of "superior scalability."  
  **Why unresolved:** Quadratic (storage) and cubic (solve) complexities become prohibitive for large N; common approximations (e.g., random subsets, clustering) might break the property u*^⊤ gᵢ ≥ 0.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Develop approximate consensus methods (e.g., stochastic mixing, gradient sketching) and evaluate on synthetic particle environments with 100–1000 agents, comparing exact vs. approximate GRASP in wall-clock time, memory, and final team reward.

## Limitations
- QP solver details and handling of degenerate convex hulls are unspecified; may cause instability.
- Unclear how often **u*** is recomputed during PPO epochs; affects gradient alignment efficacy.
- Theoretical guarantees rely on compact convex policy spaces and continuous updates; neural networks may violate these conditions.
- Exact QP scales O(N³); for large N, approximations may lose optimality or Pareto property.
- Empirical results depend on careful hyperparameter tuning; sensitivity not fully explored.

## Confidence
- Active shared perception mechanism: **Medium**. Novel and empirically supported, but convex hull properties need more validation.
- Kakutani equilibrium existence: **Medium**. Proof conditions may not hold with function approximation.
- Empirical superiority across benchmarks: **High**. Strong cross-benchmark results, but reproducibility concerns due to missing implementation specifics.
