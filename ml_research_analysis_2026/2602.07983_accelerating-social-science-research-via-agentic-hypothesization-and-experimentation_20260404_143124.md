# Accelerating Social Science Research via Agentic Hypothesization and Experimentation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.07983  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07983  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Agentic framework discovers 2–4× more statistically significant hypotheses than prior methods, with 7–17% higher predictive accuracy and a real-world A/B test showing a 344% increase in sign-ups.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents EXPERIGEN, an agentic framework that automates the end-to-end cycle of hypothesis generation and experimental validation for data-driven social science research. Two LLM agents—a Generator and an Experimenter—collaborate in a Bayesian-optimization-inspired two-phase search, combining exploration of novel hypotheses with iterative refinement under statistical control. Across diverse domains, EXPERIGEN significantly outperforms prior methods in the number and quality of discovered hypotheses, handles complex multimodal and relational data, and yields actionable insights validated both statistically and in real-world deployments.

## Method Summary
EXPERIGEN operates via two LLM agents: the Generator proposes testable hypotheses conditioned on a dataset summary and a dynamic hypothesis bank; the Experimenter evaluates each hypothesis by operationalizing features (computable via a persistent code interpreter or semantic via an LLM annotator) and running appropriate statistical tests. A two-phase search balances outer-loop novelty-guided seed generation with inner-loop refinement (up to T steps), where feedback on p-values, effect sizes, and confounds drives hypothesis updates. Validated hypotheses are stored in a bank that conditions future seeds, enforcing diversity and preventing redundancy. This iterative loop mimics Bayesian optimization, enabling efficient navigation of the vast hypothesis space while maintaining rigorous statistical validation (Bonferroni correction).

## Key Results
- Discovers 2–4× more statistically significant hypotheses than prior methods, with a controlled false discovery rate of ∼5% and 7–17% higher predictive accuracy.  
- Expert evaluation (5 senior faculty) rates 88% of generated hypotheses as novel and 70% as research-worthy; experimental rigor comparable to senior graduate students.  
- Real-world A/B test with a Fortune 500 brand confirms a generated hypothesis: horizontally centered forms with soft shadows increased sign-ups by 344% (p < 1e‑6).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Two-Phase Search Balances Exploration and Exploitation**  
- **Claim:** The outer (novelty-guided seed generation) and inner (iterative refinement) loops efficiently navigate the vast hypothesis space.  
- **Mechanism:** The outer loop samples diverse seeds using an acquisition objective combining plausibility (from prior validated hypotheses) and novelty (distance from existing bank). Each seed enters an inner loop where the Generator and Experimenter interact: the Experimenter returns statistical evidence and identifies confounds; the Generator proposes targeted refinements (e.g., adding covariates). Validated hypotheses update the bank and guide future seeds, akin to Bayesian optimization.  
- **Core assumption:** The Generator’s implicit prior can be steered by conditioning on validated hypotheses, and iterative feedback reduces Type I error.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.4 describe the two-phase iterative search; Section 6.1.2 shows “No Novelty” ablations yield 40–45% fewer significant hypotheses; corpus results show 2–4× more hypotheses and ∼5% false discovery rate.  
- **Break condition:** If the Generator fails to propose meaningful refinements (e.g., due to limited reasoning), the inner loop may accept spurious correlations that collapse under deeper scrutiny.

**Mechanism 2: Decoupled Toolset Enables Rigorous Validation Across Modalities**  
- **Claim:** The Experimenter’s use of a code interpreter (for computable/relational features) and an LLM feature extractor (for semantic features) supports reproducible statistical analysis across diverse data.  
- **Mechanism:** The Experimenter, a ReAct agent, plans feature extraction: computable features (e.g., length, regex, temporal ranks) are implemented via a persistent code interpreter; semantic features (e.g., sentiment, persuasion strategies) are annotated by an LLM-as-judge. The code interpreter maintains state across iterations, enabling complex operations (e.g., position within thread). Statistical tests are automatically selected, effect sizes computed, and robustness checks (covariate control) applied.  
- **Core assumption:** LLM-based feature extraction achieves sufficiently high inter-annotator agreement (F1≈0.80) to support hypothesis validation; persistent execution avoids context-reloading errors.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.3 describes the two tools; Section 6.3.5 reports F1=0.80; Section 8.1 shows relational features (e.g., first-comment position) are expressible via code.  
- **Break condition:** Feature extraction errors (e.g., misclassifying semantic constructs) propagate and inflate false discovery rates; system degrades gracefully under 10–20% noise but may reject valid hypotheses if noise is excessive.

**Mechanism 3: Dynamic Hypothesis Bank Enforces Diversity and Scalability**  
- **Claim:** A fixed-capacity bank with a novelty constraint forces discovery of diverse, non-redundant hypotheses, improving coverage as dataset size grows.  
- **Mechanism:** Validated hypotheses are stored in a bank of capacity K. New seeds are conditioned on the bank to avoid duplication and encourage semantically distant exploration. When the bank overflows, a greedy subset selection retains the most diverse hypotheses (using embedding distances). This prevents fixation on a few salient patterns and pushes toward deeper insights.  
- **Core assumption:** Embedding-based novelty correlates with semantic diversity of underlying mechanisms.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.4.1 defines the novelty term; Section 6.1.2 shows “No Novelty” ablations yield 40–45% fewer hypotheses; Section 12.1 indicates favorable scaling with dataset size due to enforced diversity.  
- **Break condition:** If the embedding model fails to capture meaningful distinctions, the novelty constraint may prune valid hypotheses or encourage irrelevant exploration.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept 1: Two-Phase Search (Exploration vs. Exploitation)**  
- **Why needed:** Understanding how EXPERIGEN moves from broad hypothesis generation to focused refinement is key to grasping its efficiency gains over one-pass methods.  
- **Quick check:** In the outer loop, what two terms compose the acquisition objective A(H), and how do they trade off?

**Concept 2: Feature Operationalization and Tools**  
- **Why needed:** Hypotheses must be translated into computable features. The separation between the Generator (proposing relationships) and Experimenter (instantiating tests via code/LLM) is critical; learners must understand how different data types map to available tools.  
- **Quick check:** For the hypothesis “counterarguments that use citations are more persuasive,” which tool (code interpreter or LLM feature extractor) would extract the feature `has_citation` and why?

**Concept 3: Statistical Validation and Multiple Testing**  
- **Why needed:** The system must avoid false discoveries; it uses Bonferroni correction across inner-loop steps and multivariate regression to assess independent explanatory power. Misunderstanding this could lead to overinterpreting raw p-values.  
- **Quick check:** When evaluating a hypothesis over T refinement steps, what significance threshold is applied to accept a hypothesis into the bank?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Dataset summary -> Generator (proposes seed hypotheses) -> Experimenter (evaluates via code interpreter / LLM feature extractor) -> Generator (refines based on feedback) -> (repeat up to T steps) -> accept/reject -> Hypothesis Bank (stores validated hypotheses) -> conditions next Generator seed.

**Critical path**  
1. Initialization: load dataset; compute summary D̂; empty hypothesis bank.  
2. Outer iteration: Generator proposes seed H₁ optimizing acquisition A(H) using D̂ and current bank.  
3. Inner loop (j=1..T): Experimenter evaluates Hⱼ, returns evidence Eⱼ (p-value, effect size, confound analysis); Generator refines to Hⱼ₊₁.  
4. After T steps, apply Bonferroni correction; accept hypotheses with p < α/T; add best (by effect size) to bank.  
5. Loop until N outer iterations complete.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- LLM vs. specialized tools: flexibility vs. inconsistent reasoning; code interpreter ensures reproducibility for computable features.  
- Novelty vs. plausibility: acquisition objective balances distance from bank (exploration) against plausibility from prior patterns (exploitation).  
- Persistent state vs. stateless execution: persistent kernel avoids re-loading data but risks state leakage.  
- LLM-as-judge vs. embedding-based extraction: higher accuracy (F1 0.80) but costlier and less deterministic.

**Failure signatures**  
- High false discovery rate: Experimenter’s tests ignore confounds or multiple testing correction fails.  
- Stagnant hypothesis bank: Generator repeats minor reformulations; check pairwise embedding distances.  
- Code interpreter errors: Undefined variable errors increase; monitor error rate per iteration (target ~1%).  
- Feature extractor drift: LLM annotation consistency degrades; periodically recompute F1 against experts (target >0.75).  
- Outer-loop convergence: Accuracy plateaus after ~20 iterations; if no significant hypotheses after 10 outer iterations, investigate prompts/model.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Smoke test:** Run EXPERIGEN on a small known dataset (e.g., Deceptive Reviews, 100 samples) with 2 outer iterations, T=2. Verify Generator produces testable hypothesis, Experimenter runs a statistical test (e.g., t-test on `length`), and bank updates. Check logs for tool calls and evidence structure.  
2. **Ablation – Novelty constraint:** Disable novelty instruction in Generator prompt. Compare hypothesis diversity (embedding pairwise distances) and count of accepted hypotheses against full model on same random seed. Expect ~40% fewer significant hypotheses and more lexical overlap.  
3. **Feature extractor validation:** Sample 50 instances from CMV; have Experimenter annotate a semantic feature (e.g., “contains concession”). Compare against expert labels; compute F1. If below 0.7, inspect prompt clarity and LLM version. Also inject 10% label noise and observe impact on hypothesis validation rate.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**  
- **Question:** What is the minimum LLM capability threshold for the Generator and Experimenter to produce statistically valid hypotheses?  
- **Basis in paper:** Scaling experiments (Sections 6.3.2, 6.3.3) show smaller models and models without reasoning yield fewer valid hypotheses and lower accuracy.  
- **Why unresolved:** Not systematically evaluated across a broader range of model sizes and reasoning capabilities.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation across model sizes/capabilities to identify precise thresholds for validity, novelty, and significance.

**Open Question 2**  
- **Question:** How does realistic error in LLM-based feature extraction impact the false discovery rate of generated hypotheses?  
- **Basis in paper:** Feature extractor achieves F1=0.80; noise ablations with 10–20% random flips show accuracy degradation, but real annotation errors may not be random.  
- **Why unresolved:** No linkage between extractor error rates and downstream hypothesis validity; noise experiments are simplistic and decoupled from expert false discovery judgments.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments injecting errors based on actual annotator disagreement patterns, measuring changes in statistical significance and expert-rated false discovery rates.

**Open Question 3**  
- **Question:** Can inference over large hypothesis banks achieve both scalability and interpretability, overcoming the trade-off between plateauing LLM selection and black‑box AutoML?  
- **Basis in paper:** LLM inference plateaus after ~20 hypotheses while AutoML continues improving (Figure 5, Section 6.3.4); paper treats them as mutually exclusive.  
- **Why unresolved:** No investigation of hybrid approaches combining LLM reasoning with AutoML.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and evaluation of hybrid systems (e.g., LLM‑guided feature selection for AutoML), measuring predictive performance and interpretability metrics.

**Open Question 4**  
- **Question:** Is Bonferroni correction optimal for controlling false discoveries in EXPERIGEN’s sequential hypothesis testing, or could more powerful FDR methods like Benjamini–Hochberg improve discovery rates?  
- **Basis in paper:** Bonferroni is applied across refinement steps (Section 2.4) but no comparison to alternative corrections.  
- **Why unresolved:** Bonferroni is conservative; benefits of less stringent corrections not tested.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Re-running discovery with different correction schemes on same datasets, comparing accepted hypotheses and estimating false discovery rates via expert review or cross‑validation.

## Limitations
- Exact agent prompts and memory formats are not fully disclosed, hindering precise replication.  
- Implementation details of the persistent code interpreter and LLM feature extractor (e.g., prompts, temperature) are vague.  
- Hypothesis bank management (embedding model, pruning algorithm) and dataset splits for benchmarks are missing, as are full baseline reimplementation details.
