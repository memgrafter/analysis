# Negation is Not Semantic: Diagnosing Dense Retrieval Failure Modes for Trade-offs in Contradiction-Aware Biomedical QA

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17580  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17580  
- **Reference Count:** 26  
- **Primary Result:** A decoupled retrieval architecture using BM25 with negation filtering for contradictions and MedCPT for support achieves Weighted MRR 0.790 on SciFact and top ranks at TREC BioGen 2025.

## Executive Summary
To reduce hallucinations in biomedical QA, this work identifies a "Simplicity Paradox": complex adversarial dense retrieval catastrophically fails on contradiction detection (MRR 0.023) due to semantic collapse that obscures negation signals. The authors propose a decoupled architecture that separates support and contradiction retrieval. It leverages a high-recall lexical BM25 backbone with a negation filter for contradictions and a high-precision MedCPT reranker for support. On the SciFact benchmark, this approach achieves strong performance (Weighted MRR 0.790, support recall 0.810, contradiction detection 0.750). At TREC BioGen 2025, the system ranked 2nd in Task A contradiction F1 (8.57) and 3rd in Task B citation coverage (98.77%) with zero contradict rate, demonstrating scalable epistemic integrity.

## Method Summary
The core method decouples the retrieval task into two specialized pipelines. For contradiction detection, a BM25 retriever first obtains a high-recall set of candidate documents, which are then filtered using a negation detector to surface likely contradictory statements. For supporting evidence, the same BM25 initial retrieval is followed by a semantic reranking using MedCPT, a contrastively pre-trained biomedical dense retriever. This design intentionally avoids using a single dense retriever end-to-end, instead combining the reliability of lexical matching for negation (a surface-form signal) with the precision of dense semantic similarity for support.

## Key Results
- Achieved Weighted MRR 0.790 on SciFact, with 0.810 support recall and 0.750 contradiction detection F1.
- At TREC BioGen 2025: 2nd place in Task A contradiction F1 (8.57); 3rd in Task B citation coverage (98.77%); zero citation contradict rate.
- In failure analysis, an adversarial dense retriever yielded a contradiction MRR of 0.023, confirming severe negation-blindness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system succeeds because it recognizes that negation—a critical signal for contradiction—is poorly captured by dense semantic representations ("semantic collapse"). Dense models tend to map negated and non-negated statements close together in embedding space if their topical content is similar, causing contradiction signals to vanish. By decoupling, the architecture uses BM25’s exact matching to preserve lexical negation cues for the contradiction pathway, while reserving the dense MedCPT model (trained on semantic similarity) for the support pathway where topical alignment is paramount. This trade-off aligns retrieval mechanism with signal type: lexical for negation, semantic for support. This explains why complex adversarial dense retrieval—relying on holistic semantic similarity—fails to preserve negation cues, leading to the observed Simplicity Paradox.

## Foundational Learning
- **Dense Retrieval Semantic Collapse:** Dense embeddings can obscure fine-grained linguistic differences like negation when topical similarity dominates. *Why needed:* Explains why state-of-the-art dense retrievers fail on contradiction. *Quick check:* Embed and visualize nearest neighbors for a negated query; check if non-negated statements dominate.
- **Negation as a Lexical Signal:** In biomedical text, contradiction often hinges on explicit negation markers (e.g., "no evidence," "not associated"). *Why needed:* Justifies using lexical retrieval for contradictions. *Quick check:* Measure precision@k of BM25 vs. dense retriever on queries containing known negated claims.
- **High-Recall vs. High-Precision Trade-off:** Contradiction detection requires high recall to avoid missing rare opposing evidence, while support retrieval benefits from high precision. *Why needed:* Informs the dual-pipeline design. *Quick check:* Vary retrieval depth k and plot recall vs. precision separately for support and contradiction subsets.
- **Biomedical QA Epistemic Integrity:** Biomedical claims demand verifiable citations; contradictions must be explicitly flagged to prevent hallucination. *Why needed:* Defines the problem’s stakes. *Quick check:* Audit system outputs for uncited claims or undetected contradictions in a sample of clinical questions.
- **MedCPT Contrastive Pre-training:** MedCPT is trained on PubMed abstract pairs with citation links, learning to rank relevant documents highly. *Why needed:* Provides a strong semantic reranker for support. *Quick check:* Evaluate MedCPT’s nDCG@10 on a biomedical semantic similarity probe.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Query -> BM25 Retriever -> [Negation Filter -> Contradiction Candidates] & [MedCPT Reranker -> Support Documents] -> Fusion/Decision -> Output (Support + Contradiction Documents)

**Critical Path:**  
1. Query preprocessing (tokenization, negation detection).  
2. BM25 retrieval from a fixed corpus (e.g., PubMed).  
3. Parallel branches: (a) negation filter applied to BM25 results to extract contradictions; (b) MedCPT reranks BM25 results to select top support documents.  
4. Fusion step combines results, ensuring contradictions and supports are presented distinctly and without overlap.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- **Simplicity vs. Performance:** The decoupled design opts for interpretable, modular components over an end-to-end dense model, yielding higher contradiction recall at the cost of additional pipeline complexity.  
- **Recall-Precision Split:** Dedicated high-recall retrieval for contradictions (via BM25+negation) vs. high-precision for support (via MedCPT) tailors each pathway to its goal but requires careful threshold tuning.  
- **Corpus Dependency:** BM25 performance depends on lexical overlap; MedCPT on semantic match. Biases can emerge if negation expressions vary widely or if citations are sparse.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Contradiction pipeline failure: Negation filter misses implicit negations (e.g., "failed to show") or BM25 fails to retrieve the contradicting document due to vocabulary mismatch.  
- Support pipeline failure: MedCPT reranks a relevant document lower due to embedding bias toward certain subfields.  
- Fusion failure: Overlap between support and contradiction sets indicates unclear decision boundaries.

**First Experiments:**  
1. **Replication on SciFact:** Run the full pipeline on the SciFact test set with provided checkpoints to confirm Weighted MRR 0.790 and per-task metrics.  
2. **Negation Filter Ablation:** Disable the negation filter and measure drop in contradiction F1 to quantify its contribution.  
3. **Support Precision-Recall Curve:** Vary MedCPT’s top-k threshold and plot support recall vs. precision to identify optimal operating point for a given application.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do other dense retrieval architectures (e.g., hybrids, cross-encoders) handle negation, and does the "Simplicity Paradox" extend beyond the specific adversarial dense retriever tested?
- Does the decoupled approach generalize to domains with different negation patterns (e.g., legal, social science) where negation may be more implicit or syntactically complex?
- What is the impact of citation sparsity in the corpus on the independence of retrieved supporting evidence, and how does it affect the system's epistemic integrity claims?

## Limitations
- The "Simplicity Paradox" conclusion rests on a single adversarial dense retriever failure; other model families (e.g., cross-encoders, hybrid retrievers) are unexplored.  
- Generalizability beyond biomedical corpora (SciFact, BioGen) is unproven; negation patterns in legal or social science domains may differ.  
- Corpus analysis shows low citation impact among retrieved neighbors, raising questions about the independence of supporting evidence.

## Confidence
- **High:** Reported metrics on SciFact and TREC BioGen 2025 (e.g., Weighted MRR 0.790, Task A F1 8.57) are concrete and competition-verified.  
- **Medium:** The decoupled architecture’s gains are supported by evaluation but may not transfer to noisier, real-world biomedical corpora with less curated contradictions.  
- **Low:** The causal link between "negation is not semantic" and the dense retrieval failure is speculative without controlled experiments isolating negation from other linguistic factors (e.g., syntax, domain jargon).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation over negation operators:** Systematically mask/restore negation terms (e.g., "not", "no", "lack of") in queries and documents to isolate the performance impact specifically on negation, separate from general OOV or vocabulary gaps.  
2. **Cross-domain contradiction benchmark:** Evaluate the decoupled system on non-biomedical negation-heavy datasets (e.g., FEVER, NewsClimateQA) to test if gains generalize outside curated biomedical negation patterns.  
3. **Human error analysis:** Sample 100 contradictory examples where the system failed (e.g., false negatives) and label whether failures stem from (a) missing negation cues, (b) complex syntax, or (c) world knowledge gaps, directly probing the semantic collapse hypothesis.
