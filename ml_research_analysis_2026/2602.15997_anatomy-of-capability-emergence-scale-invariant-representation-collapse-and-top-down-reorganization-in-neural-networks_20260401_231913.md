# Anatomy of Capability Emergence: Scale-Invariant Representation Collapse and Top-Down Reorganization in Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15997
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15997
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Training initiates with a universal, scale-invariant representation collapse to task-specific floors, with collapse propagating top-down through layers and representation geometry (RANKME) reliably preceding behavioral emergence for hard tasks.

## Executive Summary
This study systematically tracks geometric measures during neural network training on controlled algorithmic tasks to understand capability emergence. It finds that training begins with a universal collapse of internal representations to a task-specific minimum dimensionality, a pattern that is scale-invariant for tasks with low intrinsic dimensionality (e.g., modular arithmetic). This collapse starts in output-facing layers and propagates upward. Furthermore, the representation geometry measure RANKME consistently leads behavioral emergence for difficult tasks, while other loss landscape metrics are synchronous or lagging. However, this geometric monitoring has limited predictive power for fine-grained ordering and fails to replicate in naturalistic pre-training due to distributional misalignment.

## Method Summary
The authors trained GPT-2-style transformers (5 scales, 405K–85M parameters) on a mixture of 24 algorithmic tasks (8 base tasks × 3 difficulty levels) with dense checkpointing. For each checkpoint, they computed task-specific geometric metrics (RANKME, Fisher effective rank, local learning coefficient, Hessian top eigenvalue) from hidden states on fixed diagnostic sets. Behavioral emergence was detected using accuracy thresholds. They analyzed temporal precedence via cross-correlation and tested predictive power by comparing early-geometry rankings against final emergence order. The approach was extended to Pythia language models using external diagnostic benchmarks.

## Key Results
- Training begins with a universal, scale-invariant representation collapse to a task-specific floor; e.g., modular arithmetic collapses to RANKME ≈ 2.0 across a 210× parameter range (CV=0.08).
- Collapse propagates top-down through layers in 28/32 task×model combinations, contradicting bottom-up feature-building intuition.
- Representation geometry (RANKME) is a reliable precursor to behavioral emergence for hard tasks (100% precursor rate), while the local learning coefficient is synchronous and global Hessian lags.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Training begins with a universal representation collapse to task-specific floors that are scale-invariant for tasks with low intrinsic dimensionality.
- **Mechanism:** The network reorganizes random representations into a minimally complex feature space sufficient to distinguish task-relevant input classes, with this minimum determined by the task's intrinsic dimensionality rather than model capacity.
- **Core assumption:** Every task has a fundamental representational complexity floor; tasks with compact latent solutions impose a hard lower bound on effective rank regardless of parameter count.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "training initiates with a universal representation collapse to task-specific floors that are scale-invariant... modular arithmetic collapses to RANKME ≈ 2.0 across a 210× parameter range"
  - [section 4.2] Collapse floor analysis showing MOD CV=0.08, ADD CV=0.18, MUL CV=0.42 across five model sizes.
  - [corpus] Neighbors show related collapse phenomena but not the scale-invariant floor property.
- **Break condition:** The floor becomes capacity-dependent if task intrinsic dimensionality exceeds the model's minimal representational capacity or if the task has diffuse semantic structure.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The representation collapse propagates top-down through layers (output-facing layers collapse first).
- **Mechanism:** Gradient flow during backpropagation is strongest at output layers early in training, causing them to adapt and commit to task-relevant structure before intermediate layers adjust.
- **Core assumption:** The loss gradient's magnitude decays with layer depth during early training, making output layers the initial locus of representational commitment.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "collapse propagates top-down through layers in 28/32 cases"
  - [section 4.3] Per-layer RANKME analysis showing monotonic decrease from input to output layers at collapse minimum (e.g., MOD micro: layer0=8.2 vs. layer3=1.7).
  - [section 4.4] Hidden learning probes show deeper layers improve probe accuracy 2–11× more than shallow layers.
- **Break condition:** The top-down pattern weakens or reverses when model capacity is high relative to task difficulty (4/32 exceptions).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A geometric hierarchy exists where representation geometry (RANKME) leads behavioral emergence for hard tasks, while loss landscape measures (LLC, Hessian) are synchronous or lagging.
- **Mechanism:** Representation reorganization is an upstream prerequisite; the model must first compress and then recover its feature space before the loss surface and local singularity structure reflect the new task dynamics.
- **Core assumption:** Loss geometry integrates over the full parameter space and responds only after sufficient feature-space rearrangement has accumulated.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "representation geometry (RANKME) reliably precedes behavioral emergence for hard tasks (100% precursor rate)... local learning coefficient is synchronous (0/24 precursor) and global Hessian lags"
  - [section 4.5] Precursor rate table: RANKME 100% for hard tasks across all scales vs. LLC 0%, Hessian 17%.
  - [corpus] Singular learning theory predicts LLC-Fisher correspondence but this breaks during training (Appendix D).
- **Break condition:** For easy tasks emerging during universal collapse, geometric and behavioral transitions become simultaneous at larger scales.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Task-conditioned representation geometry
  - **Why needed:** Measuring representational dimensionality *conditioned on each task's inputs* isolates per-task developmental dynamics that global metrics dilute.
  - **Quick check:** Why would measuring RANKME on random text fail to predict arithmetic emergence? (It averages across tasks, washing out task-specific signals.)
- **Concept:** Emergence detection with continuous vs. thresholded metrics
  - **Why needed:** Distinguishing accuracy-based “sudden” emergence from continuous log-probability learning exposes the “emergence mirage” and defines a robust emergence step.
  - **Quick check:** If a model's mean token probability for correct answers crosses 50% at step 1,000 but accuracy stays below 50% until step 5,000, which step counts as emergence? (They use accuracy for main results but note the gap.)
- **Concept:** Scale invariance and collapse floors
  - **Why needed:** The scale-invariant RANKME floor is a central signature of task-intrinsic complexity; distinguishing true learned collapse from initialization artifacts is critical.
  - **Quick check:** How would you verify a collapse floor is genuinely scale-invariant? (Check the init-to-floor ratio; a 4–9× drop confirms a learned collapse.)
- **Concept:** Top-down vs. bottom-up reorganization
  - **Why needed:** The finding that collapse propagates from output to input layers challenges standard feature-hierarchy intuition and implies gradient-driven commitment dynamics.
  - **Quick check:** What would you expect to see in per-layer gradient norms during early training if top-down collapse holds? (Output layers should show stronger, earlier changes.)
- **Concept:** Geometric prediction boundaries
  - **Why needed:** The paper shows geometric measures predict coarse difficulty ordering but fail at fine-grained within-class ordering, defining a clear boundary for their utility.
  - **Quick check:** If RANKME at collapse floor predicts easy/hard task dichotomy, why might it fail to order two hard tasks that emerge at different times? (Within-class timing may depend on model size or other factors not captured by a single scalar metric.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - Data generation (on-the-fly algorithmic tasks) -> Model (GPT-2-style decoder) -> Dense checkpointing -> Per-checkpoint evaluation (accuracy, log-prob) -> Geometric computation (RANKME, Fisher, LLC, Hessian) -> Temporal cross-correlation -> Prediction tests (early-geometry vs. emergence order)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Train model with dense checkpoints across mixed task curriculum.
  2. At each checkpoint, evaluate all task×level combos to detect emergence steps.
  3. Compute task-specific RANKME using diagnostic examples to identify collapse floor/recovery.
  4. Compute other geometric measures (Fisher, LLC, Hessian) at same checkpoints.
  5. Cross-correlate geometric time series with behavior to classify precursors.
  6. Stress-test predictive power: early RANKME vs. emergence ordering, within-class concordance.
  7. Transfer to Pythia: replicate collapse patterns, test precursor signals on naturalistic training.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Checkpoint density vs. compute:** Early steps need higher frequency (every 100) to capture collapse; later steps sparser (every 2000). Missing the collapse window ruins precursor analysis.
  - **Task-specific vs. global measures:** Task-conditioned RANKME succeeds; global Hessian fails because multi-task loss averages signals.
  - **Accuracy threshold vs. continuous metrics:** Accuracy defines clear emergence but creates mirage; log-probability tracks continuous learning.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **No universal collapse:** RANKME at step 0 should be high (∼10–15) and drop sharply within first few hundred steps. If not, verify initialization and training start.
  - **No top-down propagation:** Likely due to shallow model (<4 layers). Use ≥4 layers and check per-layer RANKME at collapse minimum.
  - **Unclear precursor:** Focus on hard tasks (ADD, MOD, MUL) and ensure enough pre-emergence checkpoints; use accuracy-based emergence.
  - **Absolute RANKME mismatch:** Compare relative patterns (collapse then recovery) rather than absolute numbers; small model size or tokenization skew scales.
  - **Pythia replication failure:** Diagnostic sets are small and external; exact prompts may be needed. Naturalistic training may not show task-specific precursors.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reproduce collapse floors:** Train nano (405K) on MOD_L2; plot RANKME across checkpoints. Confirm drop to ~2.0 within first 500 steps, stable across all 5 scales.
  2. **Test top-down propagation:** Compute per-layer RANKME at collapse minimum for ADD on micro (4 layers). Expect monotonic decrease from layer0 to layer3 (80%+ reduction).
  3. **Verify geometric hierarchy:** On small model (7.4M), compute cross-correlation between MOD RANKME and accuracy. Expect peak correlation at negative lag (precursor). Repeat for LLC and Hessian; expect synchronous/lagging.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** Does top-down representation collapse causally drive capability emergence, or is it a correlated epiphenomenon?
**Basis in paper:** The freeze experiment at nano scale shows asymmetric timing effects (output-layer freeze delays hard tasks, input-layer freeze accelerates them), supporting an output-commitment-first hypothesis. However, this did not replicate at the small scale, leaving the causal interpretation inconclusive.
**Why unresolved:** Freeze manipulations at larger scales were either too mild or produced inconsistent signals. The nano results could reflect freezing half the parameters rather than layer-specific causation.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Stronger causal tests at frontier scales (≥7B parameters), such as selectively freezing <10% of output-facing layers during the collapse window, or artificially collapsing only intermediate-layer representations to test sufficiency.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** Why do task-specific geometric precursor signals disappear under naturalistic pre-training despite replicating collapse patterns?
**Basis in paper:** On Pythia, the universal collapse pattern and cross-benchmark RANKME ordering replicate, but per-task temporal precedence fails. The authors attribute this to a lack of task–training alignment: Pythia’s heterogeneous training distribution blurs the clean task boundaries.
**Why unresolved:** The diagnostic sets used (16–200 examples) are small and may not fully capture Pythia’s internal task representations. It remains unclear whether the precursor relationship is truly absent or masked by noise.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Running the same geometric monitoring on instruction-tuned models (where prompts define clear task boundaries) to see if per-task precursors reappear, and/or scaling diagnostic set size.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Are scale-invariant collapse floors a universal signature of tasks with low-dimensional latent solutions?
**Basis in paper:** This is presented as a falsifiable prediction: if scale invariance persists for models with billions of parameters, it reflects intrinsic task dimensionality; if not, it may be an artifact of the 405K–85M regime. MOD’s floor showed CV=0.08, but MUL’s floor increased with capacity.
**Why unresolved:** The largest model tested is 85M parameters. Tasks with richer compositional structure may not exhibit true scale invariance. The interaction between task complexity and model capacity at the collapse floor lacks theoretical formalization.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Extending the controlled platform to ≥7B parameters and measuring collapse floors for a wider range of tasks. If low-dimension tasks maintain scale-invariant floors while high-dimension tasks show capacity-dependent floors, the conjecture gains support.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** What theoretical principle explains why representation geometry provides coarse difficulty ranking but fails at fine-grained within-class emergence ordering?
**Basis in paper:** RANKME at collapse floor correlates with emergence ordering (ρ=0.57–0.90), but within-class concordance drops to 52–69% and the swap test fails at 26%. The predictive power is largely redundant with the easy/hard dichotomy.
**Why unresolved:** Easy tasks emerge during universal collapse, making their timing a property of initialization dynamics. For hard tasks, within-class ordering varies with model size non-systematically. No metric achieved high concordance, implying geometric measures may lack granularity.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Identifying mechanistic progress measures that track specific computational subroutines within tasks, or analyzing whether finer-grained geometric descriptors (e.g., layer-wise RANKME trajectories, specific eigenspectrum features) combined into a multidimensional predictor yield better within-class separation.

## Limitations
- Diagnostic example construction for task-specific RANKME is underspecified; variations could shift collapse floor estimates.
- Pythia transfer analysis depends on external checkpoints and benchmarks, limiting independent replication.
- Geometric measures capture only coarse task difficulty; fine-grained ordering within task classes fails (52–69% concordance).
- Precursor signals are absent for easy tasks and in naturalistic pre-training due to misalignment between diagnostic probes and training distribution.
- Findings may be specific to transformers and algorithmic tasks; generalizability to other architectures or data modalities is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Scale-invariant collapse floors:** High (consistent across 5 scales, low CV).
- **Top-down propagation of collapse:** Medium (28/32 cases; exceptions occur at high capacity).
- **Geometric hierarchy (RANKME precedes behavior for hard tasks):** High (100% precursor rate).
- **Limited predictive power of early geometry:** High (quantified concordance and reversal rates).
- **Replication of collapse patterns in Pythia:** Medium (patterns hold but precursor signals missing).

## Next Checks
1. **Intrinsic dimensionality control:** Design tasks with tunable minimal latent dimension (e.g., modular arithmetic over varying bases) and verify the collapse floor matches this dimension across model sizes.
2. **Gradient-based top-down test:** Record per-layer gradient norms and covariance during early training; correlate their timing with RANKME collapse to confirm output layers reorganize first.
3. **Aligned Pythia probing:** Fine-tune small Pythia models on data mixtures that include diagnostic tasks; then test whether task-specific RANKME precedes emergence, addressing distributional misalignment.
