# Visualising Information Flow in Word Embeddings with Diffusion Tensor Imaging

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05713
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05713
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** Cross-model analysis reveals distinct layer utilization patterns (BERT under-utilizes top layers; Longformer distributes flow more evenly; GPT-2 shows low overall diffusion) and context-sensitive flow changes in BERT enhance interpretability for pronoun resolution and metaphor detection.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DONALD-D, a method that adapts diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) to visualize directional information flow between tokens in transformer-based language models. Unlike prior embedding visualizations that treat words in isolation, DONALD-D processes layer-token activation matrices to produce ellipsoid-based representations that encode flow direction (via principal eigenvectors) and anisotropy (via eigenvalue differences). The approach enables cross-model comparisons of layer utilization and demonstrates sensitivity to contextual changes in tasks like pronoun resolution and metaphor detection, offering a new lens into model internals.

## Method Summary
DONALD-D performs a post-hoc analysis on hidden state tensors (layers × tokens × hidden dimension). The pipeline: (1) collapse the hidden dimension via arithmetic mean pooling to obtain a 2D matrix M (layers L × tokens T); (2) apply row-wise min-max normalization so each layer’s activations span [0,1]; (3) compute spatial gradients ∂xM and ∂yM along token and layer axes using centered finite differences; (4) smooth gradient fields with a Gaussian kernel; (5) assemble second-moment components (J_xx, J_xy, etc.) from smoothed gradients to form 2×2 structure tensors; (6) eigen-decompose each tensor to obtain principal eigenvector v1 (flow direction) and eigenvalues λ1 ≥ λ2; (7) compute anisotropy A = (λ1−λ2)/(λ1+λ2+ε); (8) render each (layer, token) cell as a colored tile (color maps to v1 angle) with an overlaid ellipsoid (semi-axes ∝ √λ1, √λ2, transparency ∝ A). The visualization highlights directional coherence and strength of activation changes across the transformer.

## Key Results
- **BERT:** Mean layer utilization 50.63% (SD 27.35%), indicating under-utilized top layers and high variability.
- **Longformer:** Mean 54.94% (SD 11.37%), showing more even distribution of token-to-token diffusion across layers.
- **GPT-2:** Mean 40.90% (SD 25.13%), reflecting low overall diffusion and high layer-specific variance.
- **PEGASUS:** Similar to BERT, mean 56.18% (SD 23.53%).
- **Applications:** In BERT, pronoun resolution and metaphor detection tasks exhibit context-sensitive flow changes, particularly in layers encoding semantic and syntactic features.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DONALD-D treats the (layer, token) activation grid as a 2D continuous field analogous to spatial data in medical DTI. Gradients capture how activations change along the token (horizontal) and layer (vertical) axes. The structure tensor aggregates these gradient orientations locally, acting as a diffusion tensor that describes the predominant direction and magnitude of activation flow. Its principal eigenvector points along the direction of maximum variance (i.e., the dominant flow direction), while the eigenvalue spread (anisotropy) measures how coherent that direction is. By rendering an ellipsoid at each grid cell, the method simultaneously communicates direction (ellipsoid orientation), strength (semi-axis lengths), and coherence (transparency), making global flow patterns interpretable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Structure Tensor:** A 2×2 matrix formed from outer products of gradients; encodes local orientation distribution. *Why needed:* Provides a mathematically grounded way to extract principal flow directions from noisy gradient fields. *Quick check:* Verify it is symmetric positive semi-definite; eigenvalues should be non-negative.
- **Anisotropy:** Normalized eigenvalue difference (λ1−λ2)/(λ1+λ2). *Why needed:* Quantifies directional preference; high values indicate strong unidirectional flow. *Quick check:* Value ∈ [0,1]; near 0 implies isotropic diffusion, near 1 implies highly directional.
- **Row-wise Min-Max Normalization:** Scales each layer’s activations independently to [0,1]. *Why needed:* Preserves relative token-to-token differences within a layer while removing absolute magnitude biases across layers. *Quick check:* After normalization, each row should have min=0 and max=1.
- **Eigen-decomposition:** Factorization of symmetric tensor into eigenvectors and eigenvalues. *Why needed:* Extracts principal direction (v1) and magnitudes (λ1, λ2) for ellipsoid construction. *Quick check:* λ1 ≥ λ2 ≥ 0; v1 ⊥ v2.
- **Gradient Smoothing:** Application of Gaussian kernel to gradient fields. *Why needed:* Reduces noise and small-scale variations that would otherwise produce erratic tensor estimates. *Quick check:* Increasing kernel width (σ) should yield smoother gradient maps.
- **Token-to-Token Diffusion:** Conceptual extension of spatial diffusion to the activation grid; measures how information propagates between tokens across layers. *Why needed:* Enables visualization of contextual information flow beyond static embeddings. *Quick check:* High diffusion values should align with semantically linked tokens in a sentence.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Embedding Extraction → Collapse Hidden Dimension (mean pooling) → Row-wise Min-Max Normalization → Compute Gradients (∂x, ∂y) → Smooth Gradients (Gaussian) → Compute Second-Moments → Smooth Second-Moments → Assemble Structure Tensors → Eigen-decomposition → Compute Anisotropy & Principal Vectors → Render Ellipsoids.
- **Critical path:** Correct tensor shapes (L×T×H) → accurate row-wise normalization → stable gradient computation (handling boundaries) → reliable eigen-decomposition → consistent ellipsoid rendering. Failure at any step propagates to final visualization.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Hidden dimension collapse:* Mean pooling discards feature-specific interactions but simplifies to 2D.  
  - *Smoothing σ:* Larger σ robustifies against noise but may blur fine-grained flow patterns.  
  - *Boundary handling:* Edge cells lack neighbors; choices (zero-padding, mirroring) affect gradient estimates at borders.  
  - *Ellipsoid scaling:* Global scale factor must balance visibility across cells; too large causes overlap, too small loses detail.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *All ellipsoids isotropic (circular):* Likely over-smoothing or incorrect normalization eliminating directional gradients.  
  - *Random ellipsoid orientations:* Eigenvector angle miscalculation or gradient field too noisy; check atan2(v1_y, v1_x) and gradient magnitude.  
  - * Extreme ellipsoids at edges:* Boundary padding artifacts; inspect gradient values in first/last rows/columns.  
  - *Uniform color across layers:* Row-wise normalization applied globally instead of per-row.
- **First experiments:**  
  1) **Sanity run:** Process a fixed sentence (e.g., from Figure 2) across all target models; verify shapes (L×T), that ellipsoids appear within tiles, and color variation exists.  
  2) **Sensitivity to smoothing:** Vary Gaussian σ (e.g., 0.5, 1.0, 2.0); observe changes in global anisotropy and orientation stability.  
  3) **Utilization check:** Compute per-layer utilization for BERT; confirm top layers show lower values compared to middle layers, consistent with reported under-utilization.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter choices (Gaussian kernel σ, ellipsoid scaling factor, boundary treatment) are not fully specified, preventing exact replication of quantitative results.
- Collapsing the hidden dimension via arithmetic mean pooling discards feature-level information that could influence flow estimates.
- The per-layer utilization metric reduces complex token-to-token diffusion patterns to a single scalar per layer, potentially oversimplifying.
- Visualizations become cluttered for long sequences; scalability and readability for extended contexts are unaddressed.

## Confidence
- DONALD-D can visualize directional information flow: **Medium**
- Cross-model layer utilization patterns differ: **Low**
- Context-sensitive flow changes in pronoun resolution/metaphor detection improve interpretability: **Low**
