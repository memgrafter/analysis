# Adaptive Acquisition Selection for Bayesian Optimization with Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.07904  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07904  
- **Reference Count**: 40  
- **Primary Result**: LMABO reduces average simple regret by 9.7% vs. best static acquisition function (EI) and 16.6% vs. best adaptive portfolio (GP-Hedge) across 50 benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Bayesian Optimization (BO) relies on acquisition functions (AFs) to balance exploration and exploitation, but selecting the optimal AF per iteration remains challenging. This paper introduces LMABO, a framework that uses a pre-trained Large Language Model (LLM) as an online strategist. LMABO serializes the optimization state—including process metrics, performance history, and Gaussian Process model characteristics—into a structured prompt. The LLM then selects the most appropriate AF from a fixed portfolio each iteration without any fine-tuning. Empirical results on 50 benchmark problems show LMABO achieves lower simple regret than static AFs, adaptive portfolios, and other LLM-based methods, demonstrating that LLMs can effectively reason about dynamic BO strategies.

## Method Summary
LMABO integrates a pre-trained LLM (e.g., Gemini, GPT-4o) into the standard BO loop. At each iteration, it constructs a textual state summary `S_t` from the current dataset `D_{t-1}` and fitted Gaussian Process surrogate. This summary includes metrics like iteration count, remaining budget, incumbent value, function value range, shortest distance to prior evaluated points, and GP hyperparameters (lengthscale and outputscale statistics). The LLM is prompted with an initial instruction `P0` defining its role, available AFs, required output format, and the state schema. For each iteration, `P_t = P0 + S_t` is sent to the LLM, which outputs an AF abbreviation and a brief justification. The chosen AF is then optimized to propose the next evaluation point. A fallback to UCB handles invalid LLM outputs. No model fine-tuning occurs; the LLM operates entirely in-context.

## Key Results
- LMABO reduces average simple regret by 9.7% compared to the best static acquisition function (EI) and by 16.6% compared to the best adaptive portfolio method (GP-Hedge) over 50 benchmark problems.
- It outperforms LLM-based baselines (including prompt-engineering variants and an offline supervised approach) by over 54% in simple regret.
- Ablation studies confirm that all five state components (process status, performance history, and GP characteristics) are necessary; removing any leads to statistically significant performance degradation (p < 1e-6). The instruction to avoid ineffective AFs is also critical.
- Analysis of LMABO’s decisions reveals dynamic, bi-directional switching between exploration- and exploitation-oriented AFs, unlike the “sticky” policies of portfolio methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The structured state serialization provides the LLM with a comprehensive, multi-faceted view of the optimization progress, enabling context-aware acquisition function (AF) selection.
- **Mechanism**: Numerical quantities (number of evaluated points, remaining budget, dimensionality, incumbent value, function value range, shortest distance to prior points, and GP hyperparameters) are extracted and formatted into a fixed textual template `S_t`. This template is embedded into the LLM prompt, which already contains the role instruction and available AFs. The LLM processes `S_t` as part of its in-context text generation, jointly considering all fields when outputting an AF choice.
- **Core assumption**: The LLM's transformer architecture can attend to and integrate the numerical values presented in a structured textual format to form a coherent representation of optimization state.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [Abstract] "serializes the optimization state—including process status, performance history, and GP model characteristics—into a structured prompt"
  - [Section 4.3] details the five state components and their formatting
  - [Section 5.3, Table 2] removing any of these components ("Remaining budget", "GP model characteristics", "Shortest distance information") significantly degrades performance (p-values < 1e-6)
- **Break condition**: If serialization omits critical information or the format is ambiguous, the LLM may misparse values or ignore key fields. Also, if the LLM attends primarily to the first/last fields and neglects others, synthesis fails.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The pre-trained LLM's embedded knowledge of optimization principles allows it to act as a zero-shot strategist without fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism**: During pre-training on large text corpora containing scientific papers, code, and tutorials, the LLM learned patterns associating optimization scenarios with effective strategies. The initial prompt `P0` frames the task as an "expert in Bayesian Optimization". When given `S_t`, the LLM retrieves relevant patterns (e.g., "early stage with high uncertainty → exploration", "budget exhausted → exploitation") and maps them to AF choices.
- **Core assumption**: LLM pre-training data includes sufficient Bayesian Optimization knowledge to inform effective AF selection in-context.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [Abstract] "casts a pre-trained Large Language Model (LLM) as a zero-shot, online strategist"
  - [Introduction] "LLMs...possess a rich, nuanced, and implicitly encoded understanding of optimization principles"
  - [Section 5.3] LMABO with smaller model (8B) performs worse than 30B/120B and the main Gemini version; 30B recovers much of the gap, indicating performance scales with model knowledge capacity.
- **Break condition**: If pre-training lacks BO-specific knowledge or if the prompt fails to activate the correct reasoning pathway, the LLM may produce suboptimal or random selections.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: LMABO dynamically balances exploration-exploitation by switching AF groups in response to real-time optimization signals, avoiding the sticky policies of adaptive portfolio methods.
- **Mechanism**: The LLM monitors state variables like incumbent improvement (`f_min` change), shortest distance (sample density), and remaining budget. When `f_min` stagnates and shortest distance is small → likely over-exploitation → selects explorative AFs (TS, MES, PES). When a new `f_min` is found or budget is low → selects exploitative AFs (EI, LogEI, PosMean). This switching is bi-directional, not unidirectional.
- **Core assumption**: The LLM correctly interprets the state variables as indicators of stagnation vs. improvement and can map them to the appropriate AF category (explorative/exploitative).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [Abstract] "LLM adapts its strategy dynamically, balancing exploration and exploitation based on real-time progress"
  - [Section 6] "LMABO employs an active, bi-directional strategy, not only learning to exploit on improvement but also decisively switching back to exploratory functions to escape stagnation."
  - [Section 6, Figure 2b] LMABO has high switch frequency between exploration and exploitation groups, while portfolio baselines are "sticky".
  - [Section 6, Figure 2c] for LMABO, exploitative AFs are used more during "Improvement" phases than during "Stagnation".
  - [Section 5.3, ablation "Avoid ineffective AFs" (LMABO-AB4) causes a large performance drop, confirming the instruction's importance.
- **Break condition**: If state signals are noisy or if the LLM misinterprets them (e.g., interprets small distance as good rather than over-exploitation), switching may become counterproductive.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Objective function f(x) -> Dataset D -> Gaussian Process surrogate -> State serializer -> LLM strategist -> Portfolio of acquisition functions -> AF optimizer -> Fallback handler

- **Critical path**:
  1. Initialize: Construct `P0` (role, available AFs, state schema, output format). Send `P0` to LLM.
  2. For `t = 1` to `T`:
     a. Fit GP on `D_{t-1}`.
     b. Compute state metrics from `D_{t-1}` and GP.
     c. Serialize into `S_t`.
     d. Build prompt `P_t = P0 + S_t`.
     e. Query LLM(`P_t`) -> parse `α_t`.
     f. Optimize `α_t` -> `x_t`.
     g. Evaluate `y_t = f(x_t) + noise`.
     h. Append `(x_t, y_t)` to `D_t`.
  3. Return `arg min_{(x,y)∈D_T} y`.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **State schema**: Ablation shows all components matter; however, "Remaining budget" removal caused smallest drop, suggesting it is least critical. Still, include it. Balance token count (keep concise) vs completeness; avoid omitting any without ablation validation.
  - **LLM selection**: Larger models (GPT-4o mini, Gemini) yield best performance; smaller open-source models (8B/30B) degrade but can be acceptable for cost. Always use temperature=0 for determinism. Implement fallback to UCB; if fallback rate >0.5%, revisit prompt formatting.
  - **Portfolio composition**: Must include a mix of explorative and exploitative AFs. LMABO prefers EI/LogEI/TS; ensure these are present. Curated portfolios (3 AFs) speed up adaptive baselines but may limit coverage; for LMABO, larger portfolio gives more flexibility.
  - **Token management**: If context window is limited, consider summarizing history (e.g., last 5 f_min values) rather than full `D_{t-1}`. Current design does not include full history, relying on LLM memory of prior `P_t`; verify that LLM retains context across iterations.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **High fallback rate** (>1%): Prompt likely not enforcing strict output format; strengthen instruction in `P0`.
  - **No improvement over static EI**: LMABO not adapting; check that state serialization updates correctly each iteration and that LLM outputs vary.
  - **Absence of AF switching** (all iterations same AF): State may lack indicators of progress; ensure "Shortest distance" and "f_min" change are correctly computed.
  - **Poor performance when removing GP characteristics**: Ablation shows these matter; verify extraction of lengthscale and outputscale statistics from GP is accurate.
  - **Inconsistent across seeds** (high CV): LLM outputs may be sensitive to prompt phrasing; avoid random phrases, use deterministic formatting.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **GP + AF sanity check**: Without LLM, run BO with static EI on a 2D benchmark (e.g., SixHumpCamel) for 30 iterations. Confirm GP fitting and AF optimization yield monotonically decreasing simple regret.
  2. **Prompt formatting milestone**: Manually construct `P_t` using a canned state (e.g., from a real run). Query the target LLM and verify the response matches the required format (`AF_ABBR: justification`). Test fallback by sending a prompt lacking the last line.
  3. **End-to-end LMABO on a single short problem**: Run LMABO for 20 iterations on a low-dimensional problem (D=2, T=20). Log AF choices each iteration. Plot simple regret curve and compare to static EI. Observe that AF choices vary and that at least some switching occurs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How well does LMABO generalize to Bayesian optimization using surrogate models beyond Gaussian processes, such as random forests, neural networks, or Tree-structured Parzen Estimators?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "This paper focused on standard BO with Gaussian Processes, and future work could adapt LMABO to other surrogate models and optimization settings."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only evaluates LMABO with GP surrogates; the framework's reliance on GP-specific hyperparameters (lengthscales, outputscale) may need adaptation for other models.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Re-implementing LMABO with alternative surrogates and evaluating on the same 50-benchmark suite, measuring whether performance gains persist and what adjustments to the state representation are needed.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can LMABO be effectively extended to constrained Bayesian optimization, and how should the state representation be modified to incorporate constraint information?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "For instance, in constrained BO, the LLM could be leveraged to dynamically balance objective improvement against constraint satisfaction."
- **Why unresolved**: The current state summary only includes objective-related information; handling multiple objectives or constraints requires new signals (e.g., constraint violation magnitudes, feasibility probabilities) and the LLM's ability to reason about trade-offs is unproven.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Designing a constrained version of LMABO that adds constraint metrics to the prompt, then testing on standard constrained BO benchmarks (e.g., Synthesizer,受压梁) to verify if the LLM can learn to balance improvements against constraint satisfaction.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Is the manually designed state representation (process status, performance history, GP characteristics) optimal, or could an adaptive or learned representation yield better performance?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The authors designed the representation based on domain intuition and showed ablations, but did not explore whether alternative or automatically generated encodings could improve performance.
- **Why unresolved**: The ablation study confirms each component contributes, but the specific format (e.g., raw values vs. normalized, textual descriptions vs. structured tables) and inclusion of other possible signals (e.g., diversity of evaluated points, gradient approximations if available) remain untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conducting systematic experiments with varied state encodings (e.g., different aggregation statistics, alternative textual formats, or even letting the LLM generate its own summary from raw history) and comparing their impact on benchmark performance.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Can we extract a human-interpretable decision policy from LMABO's acquisition function selections that replicates its performance, thereby providing mechanistic insight into its adaptive strategy?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper analyzes selection frequencies and justifications qualitatively, but does not provide a formal model of the decision logic; such a model could validate that the LLM is following recognizable expert heuristics.
- **Why unresolved**: While justifications mention "exploration" and "exploitation", they are often high-level; the precise triggers (e.g., exact thresholds for remaining budget, stagnation definition, or lengthscale interpretations) are unclear and may vary across contexts.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Training a supervised model (e.g., decision tree, rule-based system) on LMABO's state-action pairs across many runs, then testing whether this surrogate policy achieves comparable performance and yields interpretable rules (e.g., "if remaining budget < 20% and f_min improved in last iteration → use EI").

### Open Question 5
- **Question**: Does maintaining a static portfolio of 12 acquisition functions limit LMABO's potential? Could a dynamic portfolio that evolves during optimization (e.g., adding promising new AFs or dropping underperforming ones) lead to further improvements?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The framework assumes a fixed portfolio; while ablations show all components matter, the composition of the portfolio itself is treated as constant, and no analysis explores whether certain AFs become obsolete as optimization progresses or whether newer AFs not in the initial set could be beneficial.
- **Why unresolved**: The portfolio is hand-picked from existing BO literature; it's unknown whether the LLM might benefit from having access to acquisition functions tailored to specific problem classes or whether some standard AFs are consistently harmful in certain regimes.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Implementing a mechanism where the LLM can suggest new acquisition functions (e.g., by describing desired properties) or where a meta-controller prunes rarely used AFs based on early performance, then evaluating such dynamic portfolios against the static 12-AF baseline across diverse benchmarks.

## Limitations
- Evaluated only on synthetic benchmarks; real-world generalizability (e.g., NAS, materials design) remains unproven.
- Relies on assumed LLM pre-training knowledge of BO; this knowledge is not directly verified and may exploit superficial patterns.
- Per-iteration LLM query cost and latency may outweigh regret gains in practical settings, especially with large models.
- Fixed textual state serialization may omit critical signals; token limits restrict detail and could hinder performance on complex problems.
- Results may be portfolio-dependent; other acquisition function sets could yield different conclusions.
