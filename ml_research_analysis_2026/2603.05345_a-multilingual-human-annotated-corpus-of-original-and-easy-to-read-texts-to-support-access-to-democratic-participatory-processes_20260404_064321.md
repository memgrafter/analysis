# A Multilingual Human Annotated Corpus of Original and Easy-to-Read Texts to Support Access to Democratic Participatory Processes

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.05345
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05345
*   **Reference count:** 0
*   **Primary result:** First sentence-level Easy-to-Read (E2R) corpus for Catalan and new substantial resources for Spanish and Italian, with fine-grained annotations of simplification operations on political/deliberative texts.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a new parallel corpus to address the scarcity of high-quality, human-annotated Easy-to-Read (E2R) data for less-resourced languages (Spanish, Catalan, Italian). The goal is to support the development of text simplification systems that improve accessibility to democratic participatory processes. Expert translators manually simplified original political texts to a CEFR A2 level using a unified, cross-lingual annotation schema, tagging each simplified sentence with the specific simplification criteria applied. The resulting resource provides approximately 10,400–11,700 words per language and contains detailed, sentence-level annotations of linguistic operations, filling a critical gap for training and evaluation in these languages and domains.

## Method Summary
The core method involves creating a multilingual, sentence-aligned parallel corpus. For each language (Spanish, Catalan, Italian), experts first developed a detailed annotation schema based on established E2R guidelines (e.g., UNE, DINCAT). Then, human translators manually simplified original political, legislative, and news documents—selected for relevance and copyright compliance—into E2R (CEFR A2) suitable text. Crucially, for every simplified sentence segment, the translator identified and tagged which specific simplification criterion from the schema was applied (e.g., "syntactic splitting," "use of common verbs"). The final corpus is structured in JSON format, containing metadata, text chunks, original-to-simplified sentence alignments (supporting 1:1 and 1:N splits), and the operation annotations.

## Key Results
*   Created the first sentence-level E2R corpus for Catalan and significantly expanded resources for Spanish and Italian, with ~10,400–11,700 words per language.
*   Provided fine-grained, expert-annotated labels for simplification operations, enabling analysis of which criteria are most frequently applied (e.g., syntactic splitting at 22.8% in Spanish, lexical simplification at 55.30% in Catalan).
*   Delivered a domain-specific resource focused on political/deliberative texts, directly targeting barriers to democratic participation for people with low literacy, disabilities, or migrants.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: High-Quality Parallel Data Enables Supervised Learning
*   **Claim:** The corpus provides sentence-aligned, expert-simplified text pairs that serve as gold-standard training data for supervised text simplification models in less-resourced languages.
*   **Mechanism:** Models learn a mapping from original to simplified sentences via sequence-to-sequence training (e.g., minimizing cross-entropy loss). The sentence-level alignment supports standard training paradigms, and the operation tags can act as auxiliary supervision.
*   **Core assumption:** Expert-produced simplifications are consistent with E2R principles and of sufficient quality to be a reliable target for model learning.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   Abstract states it creates "the first sentence-level E2R corpus for Catalan" and fills a gap for "high-quality, expert-annotated data."
    *   Section 3.2 describes sentence-level alignments mapping original IDs to simplified IDs.
    *   Corpus statistics (Tables 6, 10) show hundreds of aligned sentences per language.

### Mechanism 2: Fine-Grained Annotations Support Analysis and Controllable Generation
*   **Claim:** The per-sentence annotation of applied simplification criteria allows researchers to analyze effective operations and train models that can selectively apply them.
*   **Mechanism:** Tags (e.g., "Use of periods or commas to separate sentences") can be integrated as control codes or multi-task learning targets. They also enable quantitative analysis of operation frequencies across languages.
*   **Core assumption:** The annotation schema accurately captures expert decisions and is applied consistently.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   Section 3.1 details the central role of the language-specific annotation scheme and the requirement for translators to identify and tag applied rules.
    *   Section 4 (Tables 7-9) presents the distribution of annotation tags, showing frequent operations.
    *   Corpus JSON includes "annotations specifying complex components and applied simplification criteria."

### Mechanism 3: Multilingual Domain-Specific Resource Fosters Cross-Lingual Transfer and Democratic Inclusion
*   **Claim:** The corpus's focus on political/deliberative texts across three languages supports multilingual system development and addresses a key accessibility gap in democratic participation.
*   **Mechanism:** Data from multiple languages in one domain enables cross-lingual transfer learning (e.g., via multilingual models). The political domain is prioritized because linguistic barriers here directly hinder civic engagement.
*   **Core assumption:** Simplification strategies are similar enough across these languages to benefit from shared training, and domain specificity is more valuable than general-domain data for the target application.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   Abstract notes texts are "compiled from domains related to this topic [democratic participation]."
    *   Section 2.1 frames E2R as essential for democratic inclusion of marginalized groups.
    *   Section 3.2 specifies selection criteria for text types (political, legislative, news, policy).

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Text Simplification (TS)
    *   **Why needed:** To understand the core task (lexical, syntactic, discourse operations) and evaluation challenges (meaning preservation, fluency, simplicity).
    *   **Quick check:** What are the primary types of simplification operations and how do they affect text meaning and complexity?
*   **Concept:** Easy-to-Read (E2R) Guidelines
    *   **Why needed:** The corpus is constructed according to E2R standards (e.g., CEFR A2, UNE, DINCAT); knowing these principles is essential to interpret the simplifications.
    *   **Quick check:** What are key E2R recommendations for sentence structure and vocabulary, and how do they differ from general plain language?
*   **Concept:** Annotation Schema Design
    *   **Why needed:** The corpus's value lies in its fine-grained tags; understanding schema design trade-offs (granularity, consistency, cross-lingual adaptation) is crucial for use.
    *   **Quick check:** What are the trade-offs between a detailed annotation schema and a simpler one for training data, and how might tag sparsity affect model learning?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Data Loader → Preprocessor → Model → Training Loop → Evaluator → Analysis Module
*   **Critical path:** Data Loader (reads JSON, extracts sentences/tags) → Preprocessor (tokenizes, formats tags) → Model (sequence-to-seq architecture, e.g., T5) → Evaluator (computes BLEU, SARI, BERTScore, readability).
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Sentence-level vs. document-level: Sentence-level simplifies modeling but may lose cross-sentence coherence.
    *   Tag Integration: Use tags as control codes (explicit control) vs. multi-task targets (improved representation); trade-off between control fidelity and training complexity.
    *   Multilingual vs. per-language: Single multilingual model leverages transfer but risks interference; separate models avoid interference but lose shared learning.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   Low automatic metric scores (BLEU, SARI) indicating poor simplification quality.
    *   Model outputs ignore tags (e.g., long sentences when tag mandates splitting).
    *   Over-simplification (content loss) or under-simplification (complex structures remain), measured via readability metrics not reaching A2.
    *   For multilingual models, one language performs significantly worse, suggesting data imbalance or interference.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  Baseline Reproduction: Train a standard seq2seq model (e.g., T5-small) on the Spanish subset without tags. Report BLEU, SARI, and readability.
    2.  Tag Ablation Study: Compare (a) original sentence only, (b) original + concatenated tag string, (c) original + tag embeddings (multi-task with tag prediction). Measure impact on quality and tag fidelity.
    3.  Cross-Lingual Transfer Experiment: Train a multilingual model (e.g., mT5) on all three languages combined vs. separate monolingual models. Evaluate per-language performance and analyze shared operations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question 1:** How consistent are the E2R annotations across translators and languages? *Why unresolved:* Inter-annotator agreement metrics are not reported. *Resolving evidence:* Compute Cohen’s kappa on a double-annotated subset.
*   **Question 2:** Is the corpus size (~10k words per language) sufficient for training effective text simplification models? *Why unresolved:* No empirical results demonstrate model performance with this dataset size. *Resolving evidence:* Run learning-curve experiments with incremental data portions.
*   **Question 3:** Are the language-specific E2R schemas empirically validated for comprehensibility with target audiences (e.g., people with intellectual disabilities)? *Why unresolved:* Schemas were developed via expert consultation but not user-tested. *Resolving evidence:* Conduct comprehension studies with the target group.
*   **Question 4:** Can this multilingual corpus support cross-lingual transfer learning given its domain specificity and annotation differences? *Why unresolved:* No cross-lingual experiments are reported. *Resolving evidence:* Train and evaluate multilingual vs. monolingual models on the combined corpus.

## Limitations
*   Proprietary annotation guidelines (for Spanish/Italian) prevent exact replication of simplification logic.
*   Original source texts are undisclosed due to copyright/ethical constraints, blocking source verification.
*   Inter-annotator agreement is not reported, leaving consistency of tags across translators unknown.
*   Corpus size (~10k words per language) may be insufficient for training robust deep models without overfitting.

## Confidence
*   **Existence/availability of multilingual E2R corpus:** High
*   **Fine-grained annotation schema design and application:** Medium (schema described, but full guidelines and agreement metrics missing)
*   **Domain-specific (political/deliberative) focus:** High
*   **Generalizability of simplification patterns across languages/domains:** Low (no cross-domain validation)
