# BALD-SAM: Disagreement-based Active Prompting in Interactive Segmentation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10828
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10828
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** BALD-SAM ranks first or second in normalized ΔIoU on 14 of 16 benchmarks, outperforms human and oracle prompting on categories like Dog (0.843 vs 0.248) and Stop sign (1.0 vs 0.276), sweeps all medical and underwater sets, and exceeds one-shot geometric baselines.

## Executive Summary
Interactive segmentation with the Segment Anything Model (SAM) typically requires multiple user prompts to achieve high-quality masks. BALD-SAM introduces an active prompting framework that selects the next point prompt to maximize information gain per interaction, thereby reducing the number of required prompts. The method freezes the entire SAM backbone and adds a lightweight Bayesian prediction head (~35K parameters) on top of SAM's mask decoder features. Bayesian inference is performed via Laplace approximation on the head only, providing a Gaussian posterior that enables efficient Monte Carlo sampling of plausible model configurations. The acquisition function is based on Bayesian Active Learning by Disagreement (BALD), which computes mutual information between the model’s epistemic uncertainty and the candidate query location, conditioned on the accumulated prompt history. Across 16 datasets covering natural, medical, underwater, and seismic domains, BALD-SAM achieves state-of-the-art efficiency, ranking first or second in normalized ΔIoU on 14 benchmarks. It notably surpasses both human and oracle prompting on several categories (e.g., Dog: 0.843 vs 0.248), sweeps all medical and underwater sets, and outperforms one-shot geometric baselines on complex objects, while exhibiting lower variance than human prompts.

## Method Summary
BALD-SAM freezes the entire SAM backbone (ViT-H image encoder, prompt encoder, mask decoder) and attaches a lightweight Bayesian prediction head (~35K parameters) to SAM's mask decoder features. The head—a two-layer CNN with ReLU and dropout—is trained on synthetic prompt data generated via six strategies (random, boundary, center, grid, mixed, uncertainty‑simulated) using pixel‑wise log‑likelihood. Bayesian inference is localized to this head via a Laplace approximation: a subset of the training data (e.g., 1000 samples) is used to compute the Hessian of the negative log‑posterior at the MAP estimate, providing a Gaussian posterior \(\mathcal{N}(\hat{\theta}_{head}, H^{-1})\).

During interactive segmentation of a test image, the current prompt history \(S_t\) is encoded with the frozen SAM to obtain the feature map. The system then draws \(K\) samples from the Laplace posterior, each yielding a mask probability map. The BALD acquisition score (mutual information) is computed per pixel as \(MI(q) = h_2(\bar{p}(q)) - \frac{1}{K}\sum_k h_2(p_k(q))\), where \(h_2\) is binary entropy. The pixel with the highest score becomes the next point prompt; its label is added to \(S_t\), and the process iterates. Stopping criteria include a maximum number of prompts (15) or a low maximum MI (e.g., ≤0.01). This approach dynamically conditions query selection on all previous prompts, maximizing information gain per interaction while maintaining low computational cost.

## Key Results
- Ranks first or second in peak, mean, and AUC normalized ΔIoU on 14 of 16 diverse datasets.
- Surpasses human and oracle prompting on categories like Dog (0.843 vs 0.248) and Stop sign (1.0 vs 0.276); sweeps all medical and underwater sets.
- Outperforms one-shot geometric baselines on complex objects (e.g., Tie: 0.845 vs K-Medoids 0.649) and exhibits lower variance than human prompting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Treating spatial pixel locations as queries in a sequential active learning loop maximizes information gain per interaction.
- **Mechanism:** At each iteration, candidate locations are scored by mutual information (BALD) conditioned on the current prompt history \(S_t\). The location with the highest score is queried, its label added to \(S_t\), and scores are recomputed, adapting the acquisition function to the evolving model state.
- **Core assumption:** The informativeness of a spatial query is not static but depends on the accumulated context of previous prompts; re-scoring after each interaction captures this dynamic.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "formally... next point prompt is selected as an information-driven query and must be recomputed after each user interaction."
  - [section III]: "informativeness scores are recomputed after every user interaction, and each new query is conditioned on the updated prompt history."
  - [corpus]: Weak direct support; neighboring papers (e.g., AutoProSAM) also frame prompting as sequential, but BALD-SAM's conditional formulation is distinctive.
- **Break condition:** If the prompt history \(S_t\) is not correctly updated or the recomputation of scores does not incorporate all previous prompts, the conditioning fails, leading to suboptimal or redundant queries.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Freezing the entire SAM backbone and applying Bayesian uncertainty modeling only to a lightweight (~35K parameter) prediction head makes epistemic uncertainty estimation tractable for a multi-million parameter foundation model.
- **Mechanism:** SAM's pretrained weights \(\theta_{SAM}\) are fixed (\(\delta(\theta_{SAM} - \theta^*_{SAM})\)). A small trainable head \(h_{\theta_{head}}\) is placed on SAM's decoder features. A Laplace approximation over \(\theta_{head}\) provides a posterior \(p(\theta_{head}|D) \approx \mathcal{N}(\hat{\theta}_{head}, H^{-1})\), enabling Monte Carlo sampling for uncertainty estimation without altering SAM's representations.
- **Core assumption:** The epistemic uncertainty relevant for prompt selection resides primarily in the task-specific decision layer (the head) rather than the general-purpose, pretrained backbone. SAM's features provide a sufficiently rich, fixed embedding space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section IV-A2]: "We freeze SAM's image encoder, prompt encoder, and mask decoder... Bayesian inference is performed only on a lightweight trainable head."
  - [section IV-B1]: "We add a lightweight prediction head... We train the head... only the head parameters are treated as uncertain."
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like SAM-PTx and Enhancing SAM also use lightweight adapters, supporting the trend of parameter-efficient tuning for foundation models.
- **Break condition:** If the frozen SAM backbone's features are misaligned with the target domain (e.g., seismic imagery), the head may learn a poor mapping despite tractable Bayesian inference, limiting overall performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Laplace-approximated posterior sampling of the lightweight head yields a practical disagreement-based (BALD) acquisition function for spatial prompts.
- **Mechanism:** For a candidate location \(q\), \(K\) weight samples \(\{\theta^k\} \sim \mathcal{N}(\hat{\theta}_{head}, H^{-1})\) produce \(K\) mask probability maps \(\{P_{\theta^k}\}\). Mutual information \(MI(q) = h_2(\bar{p}(q)) - \frac{1}{K}\sum_k h_2(p_k(q))\) is computed, where \(h_2\) is binary entropy. High \(MI(q)\) indicates high epistemic disagreement among plausible head configurations.
- **Core assumption:** The Laplace approximation accurately captures the curvature (Hessian) of the negative log-posterior around \(\hat{\theta}_{head}\), and sampling from this Gaussian yields a representative ensemble for estimating model disagreement.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section IV-B2]: "We approximate this integral with Monte Carlo sampling... draw \(K\) parameter samples... Disagreement among these \(K\) maps captures epistemic uncertainty."
  - [section V-A4] (Table 2): Ablation over Laplace subset size and sample count shows performance plateaus with adequate Hessian estimation (subset >=500) and samples (K>=30), validating the approximation's practical sufficiency.
  - [corpus]: Limited direct support; neighboring works often use MC dropout or ensembles, making Laplace's use for a small head a specific, less common variant.
- **Break condition:** If the Hessian \(H\) is rank-deficient (e.g., due to too small a Laplace subset) or the posterior is multimodal (poorly approximated by a single Gaussian), the sampled ensemble will not represent true model uncertainty, misguiding prompt selection.

## Foundational Learning
### Active Learning (Pool-Based)
- **Why needed here:** BALD-SAM transposes the classic active learning paradigm from querying *which unlabeled image* to label, to querying *which spatial location* within a single image to prompt, conditioned on an evolving prompt set.
- **Quick check question:** How does the acquisition function in active learning differ when the "unlabeled pool" is a set of images versus a set of pixel locations within one image, and why must it be recomputed after each query in the latter case?

### Bayesian Neural Networks & Epistemic Uncertainty
- **Why needed here:** BALD explicitly targets epistemic uncertainty (model uncertainty due to finite data) by quantifying disagreement among posterior samples. This distinguishes it from entropy-based methods that conflate epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty.
- **Quick check question:** What is the key difference between epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty, and why is isolating epistemic uncertainty crucial for an information-theoretic acquisition function like BALD in interactive segmentation?

### Laplace Approximation
- **Why needed here:** It provides a computationally tractable Gaussian approximation to the posterior over the trainable head's parameters, enabling efficient Monte Carlo sampling for BALD without full Bayesian inference on SAM.
- **Quick check question:** What two main components are required to construct a Laplace approximation for a neural network weight posterior, and what are the key limitations of this Gaussian assumption?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
1. **Frozen SAM Backbone:** Image encoder (ViT-H), prompt encoder, mask decoder. Outputs decoder feature map \(\phi_{mask} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times d}\).
2. **Bayesian Prediction Head:** Lightweight CNN (two conv layers, ReLU, dropout) on \(\phi_{mask}\). Parameters \(\theta_{head}\) are learned via pixel-wise log-likelihood on a synthetic prompt dataset \(D\).
3. **Laplace Fitting Module:** Uses a subset of \(D\) to compute Hessian \(H\) of negative log-posterior at \(\hat{\theta}_{head}\). Stored for sampling.
4. **Active Prompting Engine:** For test image \(I\) and prompt history \(S_t\): (a) Get \(\phi_{mask}\) from frozen SAM. (b) Sample \(K\) \(\theta^k \sim \mathcal{N}(\hat{\theta}_{head}, H^{-1})\). (c) Generate \(K\) probability maps \(P_{\theta^k}\). (d) Compute per-pixel \(MI(q)\) via Eq. 13. (e) Select \(q_{t+1} = \arg\max MI(q)\), query label, update \(S_{t+1}\).
5. **Stopping Criteria Module:** Monitors \(H_{total}\) and \(\max MI(q)\) against thresholds \(\tau_{ent}, \tau_{MI}\), or enforces max prompts (15).

### Critical Path
1. Train Bayesian head on \(D\) (synthetic prompts from 6 strategies).
2. Fit Laplace approximation using a subset (e.g., 1000 samples) of \(D\).
3. For each test image in interactive loop: encode \(I\) & \(S_t\) with frozen SAM → get \(\phi_{mask}\) → sample \(K\) heads → compute \(MI\) map → select next prompt.
4. Update \(S_t\), repeat from step 3 until stopping criterion.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Frozen vs. Trainable:** Freezing SAM preserves zero-shot generalization and drastically reduces computational cost for Bayesian treatment, but limits adaptation to severe domain shifts (e.g., seismic). The head must learn to map generic SAM features to accurate probabilities.
- **Laplace Subset Size vs. Cost:** Larger subsets give more accurate Hessian estimates (better posterior) but increase one-time fitting cost. Table 2 shows subset >=500 yields Pareto-optimal performance; smaller subsets cause rank-deficiency.
- **Posterior Sample Count \(K\) vs. Inference Cost:** More samples improve MI estimate fidelity but linearly increase runtime. Diminishing returns beyond \(K=30-40\) (Table 2). \(K=30\) is chosen as baseline.

### Failure Signatures
- **Poor Head Calibration:** If the trained head's mean prediction \(\bar{p}(q)\) is systematically miscalibrated (high ECE), MI scores will be misleading. Monitor validation ECE during head training (Table 2 shows ECE ~0.01).
- **Hessian Rank-Deficiency:** If Laplace subset is too small (<300), \(H\) may be singular or poorly conditioned, causing unstable or degenerate sampling. Symptoms: wildly variable MI scores across runs, poor final IoU.
- **Domain Shift Overwhelming Frozen Features:** On highly OOD data (e.g., seismic), SAM's \(\phi_{mask}\) may lack semantics for the target structure. Even a perfect Bayesian head will fail. Evidence: BALD's normalized ΔIoU still ranks 2nd on seismic (Table 6), but absolute final IoU is low (Table 7).
- **Stopping Criterion Mis-set:** \(\tau_{MI}=0.01\) may be too high/low for some domains, causing premature stopping or wasted prompts. Validate on a held-out set.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Backbone Ablation (Table 1):** Compare ViT-H, ViT-B, ViT-Tiny with identical head training and Laplace settings. ViT-H consistently dominates, justifying its use for all further experiments. *Why:* Confirms richer pretrained features yield better Bayesian posterior for the head.
2. **Laplace Posterior Ablation (Table 2):** Systematically vary Laplace subset size (100, 300, 500, 700, 1000) and posterior sample count \(K\) (10, 20, ..., 100). Metrics: Val IoU, ECE, relative inference cost. Identifies Pareto-optimal configuration (Laplace=1000, \(K=30\)). *Why:* Establishes practical operating point balancing accuracy, calibration, and cost.
3. **Full Active Prompting Benchmark (Tables 3-7, Figure 3):** Run BALD-SAM (with optimal Laplace config) vs. Oracle, Entropy, Human, Random on all 16 PointPrompt datasets. Metrics: Peak/Mean/AUC normalized ΔIoU, Mean Final IoU. *Why:* Validates cross-domain efficiency and superiority of the BALD objective.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Which domain-specific visual attributes (e.g., contrast, edge coherence) cause the smoother per‑iteration improvement in medical and seismic images compared to natural images?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state that medical/seismic datasets show smoother normalizedΔIoU trajectories and that this “domain‑dependent” dynamic “merits deeper dataset‑specific investigation in future work.”
- **Why unresolved:** No analysis identifies the specific attributes responsible, limiting the design of domain‑adaptive prompting strategies.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compute quantitative image metrics (e.g., edge strength, texture homogeneity) across all 16 datasets and correlate with trajectory smoothness; also manipulate natural images to emulate medical/seismic characteristics in controlled tests.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does modeling uncertainty only in the lightweight prediction head (with frozen SAM) provide epistemic uncertainty estimates sufficient for effective BALD‑based active prompting across all domains?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method factorizes the posterior, placing all Bayesian inference on a ~35K‑parameter head while keeping SAM frozen. This crucial design choice is not substantiated by experiments that vary the uncertainty scope.
- **Why unresolved:** It remains unclear whether head‑only uncertainty captures the full model’s epistemic uncertainty needed for optimal BALD, especially under strong domain shift where SAM’s features may be unreliable.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Run comparative studies where uncertainty is also estimated in deeper SAM layers (e.g., via Laplace on the full model, MC dropout on the backbone, or ensembles covering both backbone and head) and compare active prompting performance; if head‑only remains competitive, the approximation is validated.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can SAM’s zero‑shot segmentation performance be improved for out‑of‑distribution domains such as seismic imaging, where absolute IoU remains low even after active prompting?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors explicitly note that the lower final IoU on seismic datasets reflects “a meaningful limitation tied to the SAM backbone rather than the acquisition function.”
- **Why unresolved:** The paper freezes SAM entirely to preserve zero‑shot capability, but this prevents addressing the domain shift; no exploration of SAM adaptation (e.g., fine‑tuning, domain‑specific pretraining) is presented.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Fine‑tune SAM on a small set of seismic examples or on synthetic seismic‑like data, then apply BALD‑SAM and measure changes in final IoU and prompting efficiency; comparisons against the frozen baseline would quantify potential gains.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Why does BALD‑based prompt selection sometimes exceed the performance of oracle prompting that selects points based on absolute prediction error (observed on Dog and Stop sign categories)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The results table shows BALD‑SAM achieving higher peak normalizedΔIoU than oracle on Dog (0.843 vs 0.604) and a perfect score on Stop sign (1.0 vs 0.276); this striking reversal is presented but not explained.
- **Why unresolved:** The oracle criterion is intuitively optimal for reducing error; BALD’s superiority suggests either the oracle metric is misaligned with interactive progress or BALD possesses advantages (e.g., robustness to noise, better exploration) that are not understood.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze the spatial patterns and quantities of prompts chosen by each method on those categories; test modified oracle strategies (e.g., weighting errors by uncertainty) and examine whether BALD’s advantage persists across varying data characteristics or model calibrations.

## Limitations
- Synthetic prompt generation details (strategy mix, counts, uncertainty simulation) are unspecified, hindering exact replication of the training phase.
- The selection process for the Laplace subset and the precise Hessian computation (e.g., full vs. diagonal) are not described, making it difficult to assess the quality of the posterior approximation.
- The assumption that epistemic uncertainty resides solely in the lightweight head may break under severe domain shifts; frozen SAM features could become misaligned, limiting overall performance.
