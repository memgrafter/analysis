# VietMed-MCQ: A Consistency-Filtered Data Synthesis Framework for Vietnamese Traditional Medicine Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03792
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03792
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** A consistency-filtered RAG pipeline yields VietMed-MCQ (3,190 VTM MCQ); Qwen2.5 with Chinese priors outperforms Vietnamese-optimized models by 7.21% in accuracy.

## Executive Summary
Vietnamese Traditional Medicine (VTM) suffers from a lack of high-quality evaluation benchmarks, leading to degraded LLM performance in this specialized domain. This paper introduces VietMed-MCQ, a dataset of 3,190 multiple-choice questions spanning three difficulty levels, created via a Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework with a teacher-student consistency filter. A 70B teacher model (Llama-3.1-70B) generates questions, answers, reasoning, and evidence from VTM textbooks; a 32B student model (Qwen2.5-32B) independently validates answer consistency and evidence substring grounding. The pipeline automatically discards 7.78% of candidates. Human validation (one expert, four students) achieves 94.2% approval with Fleiss' κ=0.82. Benchmarking seven open-source models reveals that general-purpose models with strong Chinese priors (Qwen2.5) surpass Vietnamese-optimized models by 7.21%, demonstrating effective cross-lingual conceptual transfer. All models struggle with complex diagnostic reasoning, and larger models consistently outperform smaller ones.

## Method Summary
The pipeline comprises four stages: (1) **Chunking** – raw VTM textbooks are split into 2000-token chunks with 200-token overlap using RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter. (2) **Generation** – a Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct teacher model processes each chunk with a structured prompt to produce JSON-formatted multiple-choice questions, including the question, four options, the correct answer, an explanation, and a quoted evidence span from the context. (3) **Consistency Filtering** – a separate Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct student model receives the same question and context (without the teacher’s answer) and predicts an answer; the candidate is retained only if the student’s answer matches the teacher’s and the quoted evidence is a substring of the context chunk. (4) **Human Validation** – a stratified sample of 500 questions is evaluated by one VTM expert and four students using a rubric covering correctness, distractor quality, relevance, and language; approvals require at most minor revisions. The final dataset of 3,190 questions is used for zero-shot and 3-shot evaluation of various models, with options shuffled at inference to avoid positional bias.

## Key Results
- VietMed-MCQ consists of 3,190 VTM multiple-choice questions; human validation yields 94.2% approval and substantial inter-rater agreement (Fleiss' κ=0.82).
- Qwen2.5 models (pre-trained on large Chinese corpora) outperform Vietnamese-optimized models by an average of 7.21% in zero-shot accuracy, highlighting effective cross-lingual conceptual transfer.
- The consistency filter automatically removes 7.78% of generated candidates; larger models (32B, 70B) achieve higher accuracy, while all models exhibit difficulty with complex diagnostic reasoning questions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A dual-model consensus filter reduces hallucinations in synthetic medical QA generation by requiring independent answer agreement.
- **Mechanism:** A high-capacity "Teacher" model (Llama-3.1-70B) generates questions, answers, reasoning, and quoted evidence from retrieved context. A separate "Student" model (Qwen2.5-32B) re-answers the same question blindly using only the context. Only samples where the Student's answer matches the Teacher's (`\hat{a} = a`) and the quoted evidence is a substring of the source (`e ⊆ c_i`) are retained.
- **Core assumption:** Independent model agreement on the correct answer, combined with substring evidence grounding, is a sufficient proxy for question fidelity for this domain.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the dual-model approach; Section 3.3 formalizes Answer Consistency (`\hat{a}=MS(q,O|c_i)` must equal `a`) and Evidence Grounding (`Valid(e) = I(e⊆c_i)`).
- **Break condition:** The filter fails if the Student systematically disagrees due to model biases, or if substring matching passes evidence that is out-of-context or semantically unsupported (acknowledged in Section 6).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Cross-lingual transfer from models with strong Chinese language priors (TCM knowledge) outperforms Vietnamese-optimized models on VTM tasks due to shared Sino-Vietnamese medical terminology.
- **Mechanism:** Qwen2.5 models, pre-trained on extensive Chinese data containing Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) concepts, can map Vietnamese Sino-Vietnamese terms (e.g., *Âm Dương*, *Ngũ Hành*) to their Chinese conceptual counterparts, leveraging overlapping knowledge structures. Vietnamese-centric models, fine-tuned primarily on general Vietnamese text, lack this embedded domain-specific conceptual knowledge.
- **Core assumption:** Conceptual overlap in medical systems (VTM/TCM) is a stronger determinant of performance than native-language fluency for this highly specialized, culturally-bound knowledge domain.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports Chinese-prior model superiority; Section 5.2 hypothesizes overlap due to shared Sino-Vietnamese roots.
- **Break condition:** The advantage diminishes if VTM contains significant unique doctrines or terminology not present in TCM, or if Vietnamese-optimized models are fine-tuned on substantial VTM-specific corpora.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Positional answer bias in LLM-generated MCQ datasets (e.g., 50.3% correct answer is Option B) can be mitigated at evaluation time via randomized option shuffling, ensuring models are tested on knowledge rather than positional shortcuts.
- **Mechanism:** During dataset creation, the Teacher model exhibits a strong bias toward selecting Option B as the correct answer. During model evaluation, the options for each question are randomly permuted, and the ground-truth label is updated accordingly. This forces the evaluated model to determine correctness based on content, not option position.
- **Core assumption:** Models would otherwise learn and exploit the spurious correlation between option position (B) and correctness during training or in-context learning.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions the bias and mitigation; Section 4.3 analyzes distribution and states mitigation through randomized shuffling.
- **Break condition:** Shuffling is ineffective if models develop biases based on other positional features (e.g., option length) or if shuffling introduces label mapping errors.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**  
  *Why needed:* The entire synthesis pipeline depends on retrieving relevant context chunks from VTM textbooks to ground question generation, ensuring factual grounding in authoritative sources and reducing hallucination.  
  *Quick check:* Why use retrieval instead of letting the LLM recall from its parameters? → To anchor generation in specific source texts and avoid parametric memory errors.

- **Concept: Zero-Shot Evaluation**  
  *Why needed:* The benchmark tests innate model knowledge without task-specific fine-tuning, isolating the effect of pre-training data (e.g., Chinese vs. Vietnamese corpora) on VTM performance.  
  *Quick check:* How might few-shot results differ for a Vietnamese-optimized model, and what would that imply? → Improvement suggests the model understands the format but lacks embedded VTM knowledge; degradation suggests conflict between in-context examples and internal representations.

- **Concept: Inter-Rater Reliability (Fleiss' κ)**  
  *Why needed:* Human validation involves multiple annotators; κ=0.82 quantifies substantial agreement, lending credibility to the 94.2% approval rate as a measure of dataset quality, not just individual opinion.  
  *Quick check:* What does Fleiss' κ=0.82 indicate about the annotation process? → Consistent judgments beyond chance, implying clear guidelines and objective criteria.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Corpus & Chunker -> Teacher (MT) -> Consistency Filter (MS) -> Post-Filter Dataset -> Evaluation Harness
- **Critical path:** Chunking → Teacher Generation → (Answer & Evidence) Student Validation → (Pass) → Dataset → Shuffled Evaluation → Metrics.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Substring evidence check vs. NLI:* Substring check (`e ⊆ c_i`) is trivial but may accept out-of-context or semantically unsupported quotes; an NLI check would be more accurate but slower.  
  - *Teacher/Student size choice:* Using a 70B teacher and 32B student balances generation creativity with validation rigor; swapping models alters the consensus threshold’s meaning.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Low validity rate (e.g., Mistral-7B's 9.5% zero-shot) indicates formatting compliance failure, not necessarily knowledge lack.  
  - High positional bias (>40% Option B) in raw dataset signals teacher generation artifacts requiring shuffling.  
  - Discrepancy between high human approval (94.2%) and low model performance (max ~65%) shows the benchmark separates human-expert from model gaps.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Pipeline Retention Test:** Run the full generation-validation pipeline on a small corpus (e.g., 50 chunks). Verify the 7.78% filter rate and inspect rejected samples to confirm the Student catches clear hallucinations.  
  2. **Human Validation Pilot:** Have the 5 annotators label the same 50-question pilot set. Calculate initial Fleiss' κ. If κ < 0.7, revise guidelines before full annotation.  
  3. **Debiasing efficacy check:** Evaluate a strong model (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B) on the unshuffled test set vs. the shuffled set. A significant accuracy drop (>10%) on the unshuffled set confirms the model was exploiting positional bias.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the evidence grounding check be improved beyond substring containment to reliably verify semantic entailment and reasoning sufficiency?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state the substring-based check "cannot verify semantic entailment, reasoning correctness, or evidence sufficiency" and identify it as a fundamental limitation (Section 6).
- **Why unresolved:** The current filter is a necessary but insufficient quality gate; without stronger verification, the pipeline risks accepting questions where the evidence exists but does not logically support the answer or is taken out of context.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comparative study measuring dataset quality (via human validation or downstream model performance) when replacing substring checks with NLI models or retrieval-augmented fact-checking, quantifying reductions in undetected hallucinations or logical flaws.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What specific linguistic and conceptual features enable cross-lingual transfer from TCM-prior models (Qwen) to VTM, and under what conditions does this transfer break down?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper hypothesizes that shared Sino-Vietnamese medical terminology explains why Qwen2.5 outperforms Vietnamese-optimized models, but this is presented as a hypothesis, not a validated mechanism (Section 5.2).
- **Why unresolved:** The finding is correlational; precise features (shared characters, conceptual mappings, pre-training corpus overlap) and their contributions are unknown. It is also unclear if the transfer holds for all VTM sub-domains.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled ablation studies manipulating linguistic features (e.g., translating questions to pure Vietnamese vs. retaining Sino-Vietnamese terms) and probing model internal representations to isolate the contribution of shared terminology versus general medical reasoning.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What are the specific characteristics of the 7.78% of candidate questions filtered out by the teacher-student consistency check, and do they represent systematic failure modes?
- **Basis in paper:** The pipeline filters 269 of 3,459 candidates (7.78%), but the paper only reports the aggregate retention rate; no analysis of filtered samples is provided despite the filter being a core contribution.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding what causes disagreement between teacher and student is critical for improving the consistency filter. Disagreements could stem from teacher hallucination, student overcaution, ambiguous contexts, or valid but subtle reasoning differences.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Qualitative and quantitative analysis of the filtered question-answer pairs. Categories could include: (a) teacher hallucination (no evidence), (b) incorrect teacher reasoning, (c) student over-penalization of valid inferences, (d) ambiguous context, and (e) formatting issues. Error analysis should correlate with question difficulty or topic.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** To what extent does the current textbook-based source corpus limit the realism and difficulty spectrum of the benchmark, and how would incorporating clinical cases and differential diagnosis scenarios change model performance profiles?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors acknowledge that the source corpus "primarily consists of foundational VTM textbooks" and that incorporating "clinical case studies, differential diagnosis scenarios, and contemporary treatment protocols" would increase the proportion of Hard questions and enhance real-world applicability (Section 6).
- **Why unresolved:** The dataset's 26.3% Hard questions are based on "complexity features" from textbook material, not authentic clinical reasoning. The benchmark may not fully capture diagnostic and integrative reasoning needed in practice, potentially underestimating model deficiencies in applied VTM.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A new dataset variant using expanded source materials (case studies, clinical notes) and a comparative evaluation showing shifts in model accuracy gaps, error types, and correlation between question source type and model performance.

## Limitations

- **Unspecified source corpus:** The VTM textbooks used are not identified (no titles/authors/access instructions), preventing exact replication.
- **Undisclosed generation prompts:** Exact prompt templates and JSON-enforcement methods are unreported, introducing implementation variance.
- **Weak evidence verification:** The substring-based evidence grounding (`e ⊆ c_i`) may accept semantically unsupported quotes and lacks NLI-style entailment checks.

## Confidence

- Dual-model consensus filter reduces hallucinations: **High**
- Cross-lingual transfer from Chinese-prior models: **Medium**
- Option shuffling mitigates positional bias: **Medium**

## Next Checks

1. **Evidence Sanity Audit:** Manually inspect 100 retained questions to estimate the rate of false-positive evidence (quotes that are substrings but do not semantically justify the answer). Target: reveal if >5% of samples have this flaw.
2. **Bias Confirmation Test:** Evaluate a strong model (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B) separately on the unshuffled and shuffled test sets. A significant accuracy drop (>10%) on the unshuffled set confirms positional bias exploitation and validates the shuffling protocol.
3. **Prompt Sensitivity Ablation:** Generate questions using varied teacher prompts (with/without few-shot examples, different system messages) on a fixed corpus slice. Measure changes in question difficulty distribution, bias (Option B %), and diversity to gauge robustness to prompt design.
