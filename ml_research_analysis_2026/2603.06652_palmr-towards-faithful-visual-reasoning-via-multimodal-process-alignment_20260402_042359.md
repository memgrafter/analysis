# PaLMR: Towards Faithful Visual Reasoning via Multimodal Process Alignment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.06652  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06652  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: PaLMR achieves state-of-the-art HallusionBench (70.9) while maintaining strong performance on MMMU (59.3), MathVista (73.8), and MathVerse (47.5), demonstrating reduced process hallucinations via multimodal process alignment.

## Executive Summary
PaLMR addresses the problem of process hallucinations in multimodal reasoning, where models produce correct final answers but their intermediate reasoning does not align with the visual evidence. The framework introduces two complementary layers: a perception-aligned data layer that constructs process-aware training data with structured pseudo-ground-truth captions, and a process-aligned optimization layer that uses Vision-Guided GRPO (V-GRPO) to enforce visual faithfulness through a hierarchical reward. Training Qwen2.5-VL-7B on 4.7K samples yields a new SOTA on HallusionBench (70.9) without sacrificing accuracy on MMMU, MathVista, or MathVerse, confirming that aligning the reasoning process itself is essential for reliable visual grounding.

## Method Summary
PaLMR’s pipeline comprises data preparation and reinforcement learning. The Perception-aligned Data Layer (PaDLayer) samples ~1.5K images per domain from FineVision (19 domains), filters out samples with unstable or trivially high base model accuracy via exact-match rules, generates a structured question-agnostic caption using Gemini‑2.5‑Flash, and obtains a reference response via Best‑of‑N sampling from the base model, resulting in 4.7K training instances. The Process-aligned Optimization Layer (PaOLayer) employs Vision‑Guided GRPO (V‑GRPO): for each rollout, a pairwise judge (Qwen3‑30B‑A3B) compares its chain‑of‑thought against the reference response conditioned on the pseudo‑GT caption, producing a binary perception‑aware score \(S_\text{vis}\). The final reward is \(R = S_\text{vis} \cdot (\alpha \cdot S_\text{ans} + (1\!-\!\alpha) \cdot S_\text{fmt})\) with \(\alpha = 0.1\). The policy (Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B) is updated via EasyR1 (GRPO without KL penalty) for 20 epochs with group size 16 and learning rate \(1\text{e}{-6}\), directly penalizing visually inconsistent reasoning.

## Key Results
- HallusionBench accuracy reaches **70.9**, a new SOTA, indicating significant reduction in process hallucinations.
- Performance on standard benchmarks remains strong: **MMMU 59.3**, **MathVista 73.8**, **MathVerse 47.5**.
- Outperforms both vanilla GRPO and prior multimodal RL methods, validating the hierarchical visual gating mechanism.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core innovation is the decoupling of visual grounding from answer correctness. The perception‑aware score \(S_\text{vis}\) explicitly checks whether the reasoning chain references only elements present in a structured caption that is itself grounded in the image. By multiplying \(S_\text{vis}\) with answer and format scores, the model receives a gradient only when its reasoning is visually faithful, preventing exploitation of spurious correlations. The binary gating suppresses hallucinations, while pairwise judging with a strong model (Qwen3‑30B‑A3B) reduces positional bias. This targeted alignment forces the policy to develop verifiable reasoning chains, not just plausible ones.

## Foundational Learning
- **Process Hallucination**  
  *Why needed*: Pinpoints the failure mode where reasoning diverges from visual evidence despite a correct final answer.  
  *Quick check*: Find examples on HallusionBench where the model mentions non‑existent objects or attributes.

- **Perception‑Aligned Data Layer (PaDLayer)**  
  *Why needed*: Supplies a question‑agnostic, structured description of visual facts to serve as a reliable reference for judging reasoning.  
  *Quick check*: Spot‑check Gemini‑generated captions for completeness and factual accuracy relative to the image.

- **Hierarchical Reward Fusion (V‑GRPO)**  
  *Why needed*: Combines visual fidelity, answer correctness, and format compliance in a multiplicative manner, ensuring visual consistency is a gating condition.  
  *Quick check*: Validate that the reward distribution shows a clear gap between rollouts with and without visual alignment.

- **Pairwise Judging with Strong Model**  
  *Why needed*: Provides a robust relative preference signal, less sensitive to absolute scoring biases.  
  *Quick check*: Measure judge‑human agreement on a validation set of reasoning pairs.

- **EasyR1 Framework**  
  *Why needed*: Simplifies implementation of GRPO for multimodal LMs with group rollouts and no KL penalty.  
  *Quick check*: Run a small‑scale training and confirm loss decreases over a few steps.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
PaDLayer → V‑GRPO Reward Computation → EasyR1 Training Loop → Policy Model (Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B).  
External services: Gemini‑2.5‑Flash (caption generation), Qwen3‑30B‑A3B (pairwise judge).

**Critical path**:  
1. Curate FineVision subset → apply learnability filter → generate pseudo‑GT captions → obtain reference responses.  
2. For each batch: generate K rollouts → compute \(S_\text{vis}\) via shuffled pairwise judge → compute \(S_\text{ans}\) and \(S_\text{fmt}\) → combine into final reward.  
3. Compute advantages → perform GRPO update → repeat for 20 epochs.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Strictness of \(S_\text{vis}\)*: Higher strictness reduces hallucinations but may cause reward starvation; \(\alpha = 0.1\) balances visual and answer signals.  
- *Judge capacity*: The method assumes the judge is stronger than the policy; if not, the visual gate becomes noisy.  
- *Synthetic captions*: Scalable but may contain errors, directly affecting reward reliability.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Mean \(S_\text{vis}\) per batch persistently below 0.5 → judge too harsh or caption‑reasoning mismatch.  
- Sudden drop in MMMU/MathVista accuracy → over‑emphasis on visual gating at the expense of general reasoning.  
- High variance in rewards across rollouts for identical sample → unstable judge; consider temperature adjustment.

**First experiments**:  
1. **Reward distribution sanity check** – Plot histograms of \(S_\text{vis}\), \(S_\text{ans}\), and final reward on initial batches; aim for \(S_\text{vis}\) mean between 0.5–0.7.  
2. **Judge‑policy alignment audit** – Manually label 200 rollout/reference pairs for visual faithfulness; compute agreement with Qwen3‑30B‑A3B; target >85% alignment.  
3. **Filtering threshold ablation** – Vary learnability criteria (e.g., base model accuracy between 20–80% vs. 40–60%) on a fixed 2K subset; measure HallusionBench performance to assess data sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can auxiliary visual expert models be integrated to enhance the perceptual baseline beyond the base MLLM's limitations in PaLMR?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Section 4.6 identifies the framework's reasoning upper bound as constrained by the base model's foundational capabilities, explicitly suggesting future exploration of auxiliary visual experts.
- **Why unresolved**: PaLMR currently relies solely on the base MLLM's visual encoder, which may lack fine-grained perception for complex scenes, limiting its ability to correct deep perceptual hallucinations.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Ablation studies adding specialized vision modules (e.g., segmentation or OCR experts) to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, evaluating gains on HallusionBench and visual fidelity metrics against a PaLMR baseline.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can semantic-aware consistency metrics replace exact lexical matching in the perception-aware score to reduce false penalties and improve reward robustness?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Section 4.6 notes that strict exact‑matching penalizes semantically valid but lexically mismatched descriptions (e.g., "blue" vs. "cyan"), calling for more robust metrics.
- **Why unresolved**: The current rule‑based visual score uses binary exact match on extracted visual statements, failing to capture linguistic variation and leading to spurious negative rewards that misguide optimization.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Replace exact matching with embedding‑based similarity (e.g., BERTScore) in V‑GRPO; compare stability, human alignment of rewards, and final model accuracy on datasets with diverse annotations like AOKVQA.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Why does the pairwise visual reward mechanism degrade when the target model surpasses the judge model's capabilities, and how can reward design adapt to maintain discrimination?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Table 2 shows PaLMR underperforming GRPO on Qwen3-VL-8B, with the text attributing this to the judge model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) losing discriminative precision as the target model advances.
- **Why unresolved**: The pairwise scoring relies on a reference response from a weaker annotator model; when the target model's reasoning exceeds that of the reference, the comparison becomes uninformative, reducing the visual gating effect.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Analyze reward distributions and human alignment rates using judges of varying strengths (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-30B, GPT-4o) across model scales; develop self‑supervised or reference‑free visual consistency checks (e.g., cross‑modal entailment).

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: How do the learnability‑based filtering and synthetic caption generation in PaDLayer affect training data diversity and generalizability across visual reasoning domains?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Section 3.2 describes filtering by model solvability and using Gemini for captions; Section 4.5's error analysis reveals persistent failures in datasets like Geometry3k and AOKVQA, suggesting data‑related limitations.
- **Why unresolved**: Filtering removes samples considered too hard or trivial by the base model, potentially discarding learnable but challenging examples; synthetic captions may contain domain biases or inaccuracies that propagate into the reward signal.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conduct controlled experiments varying filtering thresholds (e.g., accuracy bands) and caption sources (human vs. multiple synthetic models); measure performance on held‑out domains and correlate caption quality (human evaluation) with final model hallucination rates.

### Open Question 5
- **Question**: What hierarchical reward structure (e.g., α coefficient) optimally balances training stability and peak performance across different model scales and task types?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Section 4.4's ablation shows PaLMR (α=0.1) offers stable but not highest peaks on MathVerse, while Visual Mix/Bonus achieve higher peaks but volatile training; Section 4.3 notes performance saturation on Qwen3-VL-8B.
- **Why unresolved**: The fixed α=0.1 in PaLMR strictly gates on visual fidelity, which may be too restrictive for lower‑capacity models or too lenient for advanced ones; the trade‑off between exploration (answer correctness) and exploitation (visual grounding) lacks theoretical or empirical scaling laws.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Perform a grid search over α across multiple model scales (3B, 7B, 32B) on a suite of benchmarks; analyze training dynamics (response length, accuracy curves) and final performance to identify scale‑adaptive scheduling or task‑specific tuning.

## Limitations
- **Dataset construction vagueness**: The exact thresholds for “learnability‑based filtering” (e.g., rollout accuracy ranges) are unspecified, making the final 4.7K sample subset irreproducible.
- **Judge‑dependent fidelity scoring**: Reliance on Qwen3‑30B‑A3B for pairwise visual faithfulness introduces potential ceiling effects and positional bias despite shuffling.
- **External API dependence**: Using Gemini‑2.5‑Flash for pseudo‑ground‑truth captions creates cost/access barriers and potential model‑version drift, hindering replication.
