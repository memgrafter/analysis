# Beyond Attention Heatmaps: How to Get Better Explanations for Multiple Instance Learning Models in Histopathology

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08328
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08328
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Attention heatmaps are consistently unfaithful for MIL in histopathology; perturbation ("Single"), LRP, and IG are superior, with the best method depending critically on the MIL architecture and task type.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the unreliability of standard attention-based heatmaps used to interpret Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) models for whole-slide histopathology images. The authors introduce a model-agnostic, label-free evaluation framework based on progressively removing patches from a slide (patch-flipping) to measure the faithfulness of any heatmap. Through a large-scale benchmark of six explanation methods across 60 experimental settings—varying MIL architectures (Attention, Transformer, Mamba), backbones, and tasks (classification, regression, survival)—they demonstrate that explanation quality is determined primarily by the MIL architecture and task type. Attention heatmaps perform near-random levels, while methods like perturbation scoring ("Single"), Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP), and Integrated Gradients (IG) are consistently more faithful. The top methods are further validated via spatial transcriptomics correlation and used to discover distinct biological prediction strategies.

## Method Summary
The core contribution is a patch-flipping evaluation framework to quantify heatmap faithfulness without ground-truth patch labels. For a given MIL model and WSI, a heatmap assigns a score to each patch. The framework then removes patches in ascending and descending order of these scores in 100 steps, recomputing the model's slide-level prediction on the remaining bag each time. The area between the two resulting prediction curves—the Symmetric Relevance Gain (SRG)—serves as the faithfulness metric: a higher SRG means removing high-scoring patches first causes a larger change in prediction, indicating the heatmap correctly identified influential patches. This procedure was applied to six explanation methods (Attention, Gradient×Input, Grad², Integrated Gradients, LRP, and "Single" perturbation) across a suite of trained MIL models on ten public histopathology datasets. Methods were compared via mean rank score (MRS) and paired statistical tests on the SRG distributions.

## Key Results
- Attention heatmaps are almost always substantially less faithful than LRP, IG, and "Single", often performing near random.
- The superior explanation method depends on the MIL architecture and task: LRP is best for Transformer-based MIL; perturbation ("Single") often excels for Attention- and Mamba-based MIL.
- These findings are consistent across 60 experimental settings (3 MIL architectures × 3 task types × 2 backbones × 10 datasets), overriding the choice of backbone feature extractor.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Patch-Flipping Evaluation
The claim that patch-flipping reliably measures faithfulness rests on using patch removal (not occlusion) to preserve the input distribution expected by the MIL aggregator. The SRG metric captures whether patches ranked highly by a heatmap are truly influential: if they are, removing them first should drastically alter the prediction. This avoids needing pixel-level ground truth. The mechanism breaks down if the MIL model is extremely sensitive to bag composition in a non-linear way that losing patches fundamentally shifts the feature distribution expected by the aggregation module.

### Architecture & Task Dependency
Different MIL aggregation architectures (e.g., single-head attention vs. self-attention vs. state-space models) create distinct computational pathways for information flow. Explanation methods interact with these pathways differently—for instance, LRP has specific propagation rules for attention and Mamba layers, while "Single" perturbation directly measures predictive impact. The task type (classification, regression, survival) changes the backpropagation target for gradient-based methods and the functional meaning of the output score. The benchmark’s consistent results across datasets show these factors dominate over backbone choice.

### Attention Heatmap Unfaithfulness
Raw attention weights indicate where the model *looks* for feature aggregation, not the *contribution* to the final prediction. They are always positive, lack class-specific sign information, and are not designed to decompose the output. The patch-flipping test empirically confirms their low faithfulness, as removing patches with high attention scores does not reliably change the model’s decision.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multiple Instance Learning (MIL)**
  - *Why needed:* The paper's domain is MIL for histopathology, where a whole-slide image (bag) contains many patches (instances) with only a slide-level label. Understanding the MIL assumption (e.g., at least one positive patch for binary classification) and the standard pipeline (patch feature extraction → aggregation → task head) is essential.
  - *Quick check:* If a WSI has a positive label, does the standard MIL assumption require exactly one positive patch or at least one? (Answer: At least one; the label could be due to multiple positive patches.)

- **Concept: Faithfulness/Fidelity in XAI**
  - *Why needed:* The paper's core metric (SRG) evaluates whether a heatmap truthfully reflects the model's decision-making process, distinct from human-interpretable plausibility.
  - *Quick check:* If a heatmap highlights regions a pathologist expects, does that guarantee it is faithful to the model? (Answer: No; faithfulness is a technical property measured by perturbation or decomposition, independent of human validation.)

- **Concept: Common MIL Architectures (AttnMIL, TransMIL, MambaMIL)**
  - *Why needed:* The main finding is that the best explanation method depends on the specific MIL aggregation architecture's internal structure.
  - *Quick check:* How does TransMIL's use of self-attention differ from AttnMIL's single attention head in terms of patch interactions? (Answer: TransMIL models arbitrary pairwise relationships between all patches; AttnMIL computes a weighted average without direct patch-patch interactions.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
WSI → Patch Extractor → Bag of Patch Embeddings (via frozen foundation model, e.g., UNI2, Virchow2) → MIL Aggregation Module (AttnMIL / TransMIL / MambaMIL) → Task Head (Classification/Regression/Survival FNN) → Slide-level Prediction. Explanation Engine applies one of six methods (Attention, Single, IG, LRP, G×I, Grad2) to the aggregation module's inputs/outputs to generate heatmap scores. Evaluation Engine runs the patch-flipping procedure on the bag and model to compute SRG.

### Critical Path
1. Train MIL models (different architectures/tasks) on dataset-specific train/val splits to acceptable performance.
2. For each held-out test WSI, generate a heatmap for every explanation method using the trained model.
3. For each heatmap, execute the patch-flipping procedure: rank patches, remove in 100 ascending/descending steps, recompute prediction on each reduced bag, calculate SRG.
4. Concatenate SRG scores across all test slides for a given (model, method) pair.
5. Perform pairwise statistical comparisons (Wilcoxon signed-rank with FDR) and compute Mean Rank Score (MRS) to rank methods.
6. Select the top method for the specific (architecture, task) combination for validation tasks (e.g., transcriptomics correlation).

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Computational Cost:** "Single" (K forward passes with one patch) scales linearly with bag size; IG (many integral path steps) and LRP (full backward pass) are more expensive per slide.
- **Faithfulness vs. Interpretability:** LRP and IG provide signed contributions (positive/negative evidence); "Single" gives unsigned magnitude. In histopathology, signed scores may help distinguish tumor-promoting vs. suppressing regions.
- **Generality vs. Specialization:** The patch-flipping framework is model-agnostic but assumes the MIL aggregator can handle variable bag sizes; fixed-input architectures would require adaptation.

### Failure Signatures
- **Patch extraction/feature mismatch:** Background patches not filtered, or using a different foundation model version than training, leading to misaligned data.
- **Model training failure:** MIL model performance (AUROC, C-index) far below published baselines, indicating hyperparameter mismatch or training instability.
- **Patch-flipping logic error:** SRG scores negative or perturbation curves cross anomalously, suggesting incorrect ordering or removal implementation.
- **Statistical leakage:** SRG scores computed on training/validation slides, or patient IDs not properly独立 across folds, invalidating comparisons.

### Three First Experiments
1. **Replicate benchmark for one setting:** Train AttnMIL for regression on TCGA BRCA using UNI2 features, generate all six heatmaps on test slides, compute SRG, verify "Single" and LRP outperform Attention.
2. **Validate SRG calculation:** For a single slide, manually plot perturbation curves for one method; ensure descending removal causes a steeper prediction change than ascending removal, yielding a positive SRG.
3. **Test code portability:** Run the provided evaluation script on a different dataset (e.g., CAMELYON16) to confirm the pipeline handles varying WSI sizes and tissue content without modification.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can higher-order attribution methods be developed for MIL to capture interactions between patches, given current methods only assign first-order scores?
2. Beyond faithfulness, what other properties (robustness, contrastivity, magnitude calibration) should be used to evaluate MIL heatmaps, and how can they be measured?
3. How do inherently interpretable MIL models (e.g., additive MIL) compare to standard black-box MIL models post-hoc explained with state-of-the-art methods in terms of explanation quality and predictive performance trade-offs?
4. To what extent do the findings on explanation method superiority generalize beyond histopathology to other domains like radiology, spatial transcriptomics, or multimodal models?

## Limitations
- Patch-flipping assumes patch removal preserves the model's expected input distribution; this may fail for MIL aggregators highly sensitive to bag composition.
- The benchmark covers only three MIL architectures and ten datasets; findings may not generalize to newer architectures (e.g., graph-based MIL) or different feature extractors.
- Faithfulness (SRG) does not guarantee clinical utility; a heatmap could be faithful but fail to highlight biologically relevant regions.

## Confidence
- **Patch-flipping reliability:** Medium. Strong empirical evidence across 60 settings, but the distribution-preserving assumption may break for some aggregators.
- **Architecture/Task dependency:** High. Consistently observed effect sizes and statistical significance override backbone choice.
- **Attention heatmap unfaithfulness:** High. Robust underperformance for standard attention MIL; may not apply to variants where attention weights are designed as decompositions.

## Next Checks
1. Test patch-flipping on MIL models with non-differentiable or highly non-linear aggregators (e.g., clustering-based pooling) to probe the breakdown condition of the distribution assumption.
2. Conduct a pathologist user study comparing top-performing methods (LRP, IG, Single) against attention heatmaps for diagnostic tasks (e.g., tumor grading) to assess clinical utility beyond faithfulness.
3. Repeat the benchmark with end-to-end training (jointly fine-tuning the feature extractor and MIL head) to evaluate whether the relative ranking of explanation methods remains stable under feature representation shifts.
