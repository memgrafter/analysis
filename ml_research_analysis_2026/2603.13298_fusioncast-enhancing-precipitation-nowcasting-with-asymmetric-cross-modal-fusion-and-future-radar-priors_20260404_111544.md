# FusionCast: Enhancing Precipitation Nowcasting with Asymmetric Cross-Modal Fusion and Future Radar Priors

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.13298
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13298
- **Reference count**: 13
- **Primary result**: FusionCast significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, achieving a Critical Success Index (CSI) of 0.784 vs. 0.478 at τ=0.1 mm/h for 10-minute lead, with lower continuous error (RMSE: 1.004 mm/h, MAE: 0.103 mm/h) compared to NowcastNet.

## Executive Summary
FusionCast is a precipitation nowcasting model that fuses three heterogeneous data sources: historical GNSS-derived Precipitable Water Vapor (PWV), historical radar Quantitative Precipitation Estimation (QPE), and future radar QPE priors generated by an existing model (NowcastNet). It introduces an asymmetric Radar-PWV Fusion (RPF) module that uses spatial and channel attention with a gating mechanism to adaptively combine features, avoiding noise from simple concatenation. Evaluated on the Mississippi River Basin dataset, the model demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across multiple precipitation thresholds and lead times, with ablation studies confirming the necessity of both the PWV and prior branches and the critical role of the gated fusion module.

## Method Summary
FusionCast employs an encoder-decoder architecture with ConvLSTM units. Three parallel encoder branches process inputs: a simpler two-layer Conv+ConvLSTM branch for sparse PWV and historical radar, and a deeper ConvLSTM branch for the future radar prior. The core innovation is the RPF module, which applies spatial attention to PWV features to generate a gate map highlighting regions with sufficient water vapor, and channel attention to radar features to refine textural information. The gate then modulates the channel-refined radar features via a residual connection. The decoder, initialized with the fused hidden and cell states, generates forecasts autoregressively. The model is trained end-to-end on the Mississippi River Basin data from March to May 2023, with validation in June and testing in February 2023.

## Key Results
- Achieves a CSI of 0.784 at τ=0.1 mm/h for 10-minute lead, a substantial improvement over NowcastNet's 0.478.
- Maintains superior CSI across all thresholds (τ=0.1, 1.0, 4.0 mm/h) and longer lead times (up to 120 minutes).
- Records lower continuous error: RMSE 1.004 mm/h and MAE 0.103 mm/h.
- Ablation studies show that removing the RPF module, the prior branch, or the PWV branch all cause significant performance degradation, especially for heavy rainfall at longer leads.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Gated Asymmetric Fusion (RPF Module)
- **Claim**: The RPF module adaptively combines sparse PWV and dense radar features via spatial and channel attention with a gating mechanism, avoiding noise from concatenation.
- **Mechanism**: PWV features undergo spatial attention to produce a gate map `M_spatial` that highlights regions with sufficient water vapor for precipitation. Radar features undergo channel attention to select informative channels. The gate then modulates the channel-refined radar features (`HF_used = M_spatial ⊗ F'_Radar + F_Radar`), allowing high-vapour regions to pass radar details while suppressing erroneous echoes in low-vapour regions.
- **Core assumption**: The spatial distribution of PWV provides a physically meaningful prior for trusting radar precipitation signals, and channel attention can isolate radar features most relevant to intensity.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract] explicitly describes the RPF module and its purpose.
  - [section 3.3] provides equations for the spatial gate (`M_spatial`) and channel attention (`W_channel`), and the gated fusion formula (Eq. 5).
  - [section 4.4 ablation] shows that removing RPF (using simple concatenation) notably degrades CSI for heavy rain (τ=4.0) at long leads (>80 min).
- **Break condition**: If PWV spatial patterns are poorly correlated with precipitation formation (e.g., in stratiform rain), the gate may misregulate. Channel attention might also weight irrelevant radar channels.

### Mechanism 2: Future Radar Prior Branch
- **Claim**: Using future radar QPE frames predicted by NowcastNet as a prior branch provides explicit trajectory guidance, improving accuracy at longer lead times.
- **Mechanism**: A dedicated, deeper encoder branch processes the prior frames with increased filter capacity (up to 128) to capture richer semantic context about anticipated storm motion and evolution. This prior is fused with other modalities, allowing the model to incorporate learned extrapolation trends.
- **Core assumption**: The prior model's forecasts contain useful information about future storm location and structure that can be refined, not discarded.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract] states that incorporating a prior modality enhances performance.
  - [section 3.2] describes the prior branch's deeper architecture.
  - [section 4.4 ablation] ("w/o Prior") shows a sharp CSI decline, especially beyond 40-minute leads.
- **Break condition**: If the prior model (NowcastNet) has systematic biases (e.g., speed errors) or fails on certain storm types, the prior branch could inject errors. The model might over-rely on the prior at the expense of physical constraints from PWV.

### Mechanism 3: Asymmetric Multimodal Encoding
- **Claim**: Separate encoder branches with tailored processing respect fundamental differences between sparse PWV point-data and dense radar fields, preventing feature interference.
- **Mechanism**: Three parallel encoders: PWV and historical radar use a simpler 2-layer Conv+ConvLSTM stack, while the prior branch uses a deeper ConvLSTM stack. This allows each modality to extract representations at appropriate complexity levels before fusion.
- **Core assumption**: Modalities have inherently different statistical and spatial characteristics requiring distinct feature extraction pathways.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] mentions "differentiated modelling of heterogeneous modalities."
  - [section 3.2] details the asymmetric architectures.
  - [section 1] contrasts dense radar fields with point-based PWV measurements.
- **Break condition**: If the task benefits from early, dense cross-modal interaction (not late fusion), separate branches may miss cues. The design also assumes the prior branch's depth is optimal; incorrect depth could cause over/under-fitting.

- **Why needed here**: Using another model's forecast as an input branch provides a "best guess" of future radar evolution, which the fusion model can accept or correct.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Inputs (PWV grid, Historical radar frames, Future radar priors) -> Encoder Branches (PWV: 2x Conv + ConvLSTM; Historical Radar: same; Prior: deeper ConvLSTM) -> RPF Fusion (PWV spatial attention -> Radar channel attention -> Gated fusion with residual) -> Decoder (ConvLSTM + upsampling blocks) -> Output forecasts.
- **Critical path**: Input sequences are processed by their respective encoders to extract hidden (H) and cell (C) states. The RPF module merges these states via attention and gating. The fused H and C initialize the decoder, which generates future frames autoregressively.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Deeper prior branch increases capacity to interpret prior but adds computational cost.
  - Gating adds complexity vs. simple concatenation but reduces feature interference.
  - Using a fixed external prior (NowcastNet) introduces dependency but leverages SOTA performance.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Without RPF (using concatenation): Degraded CSI for heavy rain (τ=4.0) at long leads (>80 min).
  - Without prior branch: Reduced CSI stability over time, especially beyond 40-minute leads.
  - Without PWV branch: Lower CSI across all thresholds, indicating loss of physical constraint.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train a baseline single-branch ConvLSTM on historical radar only to establish a lower bound.
  2. Add the prior branch with simple concatenation fusion to isolate the prior's contribution.
  3. Introduce the full RPF module with gating and compare against concatenation fusion (as in ablation Fig. 7).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalization**: Does FusionCast generalize to diverse geographic and climatic regions beyond the Mississippi River Basin? (No multi-regional validation performed.)
- **Prior quality sensitivity**: How does the quality of the prior radar QPE (from NowcastNet) affect performance, especially at longer lead times? (No analysis of error propagation or robustness to noisy/biassed priors.)
- **PWV sparsity sensitivity**: How sensitive is FusionCast to the sparsity and interpolation of GNSS PWV data? (Interpolation effects not quantified.)
- **Physical validity of gating**: Is the physical interpretation of the gating mechanism (spatial energy regulator) valid across different atmospheric conditions? (Interpretation postulated but not validated with independent physical metrics or expert knowledge.)

## Limitations
- Missing critical training hyperparameters (optimizer, learning rate, batch size) and the exact loss function composition, hindering faithful replication.
- Unclear details on prior generation: whether NowcastNet priors are frozen or fine-tuned during FusionCast training, and risk of data leakage or biased inputs.
- No direct ablation for asymmetric encoders; a symmetric baseline is absent, so the claimed necessity of differentiated branches is unverified.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (RPF fusion)**: High – Ablation (w/o RPF vs. full) shows clear superiority, especially for heavy rain at long leads.
- **Mechanism 2 (Future radar priors)**: High – Ablating the prior branch causes significant CSI drop, particularly beyond 40 min, confirming its trajectory guidance role.
- **Mechanism 3 (Asymmetric encoding)**: Medium – Plausible rationale based on modality differences but lacks comparative experiment; could be over-fitted.

## Next Checks

2. Visualize and correlate PWV spatial attention maps with independent precipitation labels (e.g., gauge data) to validate physical relevance.
