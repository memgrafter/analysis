# Improving Data and Reward Design for Scientific Reasoning in Large Language Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08321
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08321
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art GPQA-diamond (63.2) and GPQA-general (32.4) scores using Qwen3-4B-Base, outperforming o1-mini and GPT-4o.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) struggle with open-ended scientific reasoning due to unreliable supervision and poorly designed reward signals. This paper introduces Dr. SCI, a dataset of one million STEM questions with verifiable/open-ended splits, difficulty annotations, and fine-grained rubrics. Coupled with a post-training pipeline—Exploration‑Expanding SFT, Dynamic Difficulty Curriculum, and SciRubric‑Guided RL—the approach yields substantial improvements on challenging benchmarks (GPQA‑diamond and GPQA‑general), even surpassing models like o1‑mini and GPT‑4o. Ablations confirm the contribution of each pipeline component.

## Method Summary
The authors first apply Exploration‑Expanding SFT to diversify reasoning patterns: they select a subset of examples from a candidate pool (MegaScience, WebInstruct‑Verified) that maximizes unique 4‑gram coverage, training separate thinking and instruct modes for four epochs. For reinforcement learning, they use GRPO with a Dynamic Difficulty Curriculum: the training set is partitioned into discard (easy), pending (hard), and middle sets; when a sample’s accuracy exceeds 0.9 it is replaced with a harder pending sample. Open‑ended questions are rewarded via a verifier model (non‑thinking Qwen3‑4B) that checks against a fine‑grained rubric (categories weighted 1.0, 0.7, 0.3, 0.9) plus a strong final‑answer correctness term (4.0). Verifiable questions use rule‑based rewards. Training runs up to ten epochs with a KL penalty.

## Key Results
- Dr. SCI dataset: 1M questions, split into 461K verifiable and 545K open‑ended, each annotated with a difficulty score (0/8–8/8) and a rubric for open‑ended instances.
- Performance: On GPQA‑diamond (multiple‑choice) the model attains 63.2 % pass@1; on GPQA‑general (open‑ended conversion) 32.4 %, exceeding o1‑mini (61.2) and GPT‑4o (30.1).
- Ablations show that removing any pipeline component (Exploration‑Expanding SFT, dynamic curriculum, or rubric weighting) degrades performance, confirming their individual contributions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The pipeline succeeds by systematically addressing the weaknesses of standard post‑training for scientific reasoning. Exploration‑Expanding SFT injects lexical diversity, reducing mode collapse and encouraging a broad range of reasoning approaches. The Dynamic Difficulty Curriculum ensures the model is continually challenged with appropriately hard problems, preventing plateauing on easy samples. SciRubric‑Guided RL provides dense, fine‑grained feedback that incentivizes coverage of essential reasoning steps while penalizing common pitfalls. The combination of rule‑based rewards for verifiable questions and rubric‑based rewards for open‑ended ones yields a unified training signal that scales to millions of examples.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning (GRPO):** Policy gradient method with group‑relative advantages that stabilizes training by reducing variance. Needed for optimizing non‑differentiable reward signals. *Quick check:* Can implement GRPO with the `verl` library.
- **Curriculum Learning:** Gradually increasing task difficulty improves sample efficiency and final performance. In this work, difficulty is static but samples are dynamically swapped. *Quick check:* Verify that replacement threshold (0.9 accuracy) triggers appropriately.
- **Reward Shaping with Rubrics:** Decomposes a complex answer into finer components, providing intermediate feedback. Weighted categories (Essential, Important, Optional, Pitfall) allow fine‑tuning credit assignment. *Quick check:* Ensure rubric weights sum to a meaningful scale and do not cause reward saturation.
- **Exploration‑Expanding Data Selection:** Uses 4‑gram coverage as a proxy for reasoning pattern diversity. *Quick check:* On a small candidate pool, confirm that the greedy algorithm indeed increases unique n‑grams vs random selection.
- **Dynamic Difficulty Adjustment:** Continuously monitors model mastery and replaces mastered samples with harder ones, keeping the training frontier aligned with the model’s current ability. *Quick check:* Track per‑sample accuracy and difficulty to see that the training set’s average difficulty rises over time.

## Architecture Onboarding
Candidate Pool (MegaScience + WebInstruct‑Verified) -> Exploration‑Expanding SFT (thinking & instruct models) -> Initial RL policy (Qwen3‑4B‑Base) with Dynamic Difficulty Curriculum (Ddiscard, Dpending, Dtrain) -> SciRubric‑Guided RL (GRPO) -> Final model.

**Critical Path:** Exploration‑Expanding SFT produces a diverse starting policy; RL then refines it using curriculum and rubric rewards. The verifier (non‑thinking Qwen3‑4B) is used at every RL step to compute open‑ended rewards.

**Design Tradeoffs:** Rule‑based rewards for verifiable questions give perfect signal but are limited to closed‑form answers; LLM‑as‑judge for rubrics scales to open‑ended formats but introduces noise and potential reward hacking. Dynamic curriculum relies on a static difficulty estimate; if annotation is noisy, easy samples may remain or hard samples may be inaccessible. 4‑gram novelty is a simple lexical metric that may miss conceptual diversity.

**Failure Signatures:** 
- Collapse to short, formulaic responses that exploit rubric weighting without genuine reasoning.
- Explosive growth in response length (>8k tokens) without corresponding reward increase, indicating “point‑chasing.”
- Plateau in training reward despite curriculum updates, suggesting mismatch between difficulty bins and model ability.

**First Three Experiments:**
1. Replicate Exploration‑Expanding SFT selection: run Algorithm 1 on a 100K sample candidate pool, compare unique 4‑gram coverage against random baseline.
2. Verify rubric reward calculation: sample 500 open‑ended questions, compute rewards with the verifier, and check that final‑answer correctness contributes >70% of the total reward.
3. Monitor RL training dynamics: track average response length, reward standard deviation, and per‑sample accuracy distribution across difficulty bins for the first 3 epochs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** How effective is the 4‑gram novelty metric in capturing true reasoning pattern diversity for Exploration‑Expanding SFT, and could alternative diversity metrics lead to better exploration and RL performance?  
**Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper uses 4‑gram sets as a lexical proxy for reasoning pattern coverage in Algorithm 1 and Section 3.1 to select SFT data.  
**Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates performance gains but does not analyze whether 4‑gram novelty correlates with semantic, structural, or logical diversity of reasoning traces. It may miss higher‑level variations critical for scientific reasoning.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Compare 4‑gram‑based selection against alternative diversity measures (e.g., semantic embedding similarity, reasoning step taxonomies) on downstream RL performance, reasoning pattern analysis, and generalization across scientific domains.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** To what extent does the static difficulty annotation (based on Qwen3‑32B rollouts) limit the adaptability of the Dynamic Difficulty Curriculum as model capabilities evolve during RL training?  
**Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 3.2 describes using a fixed difficulty score derived from Qwen3‑32B to partition data into discard, pending, and train sets, with replacements driven by model mastery thresholds.  
**Why unresolved:** Difficulty estimates are computed once from a specific model and remain unchanged, while the policy being trained evolves significantly. This may cause misalignment between the curriculum’s difficulty progression and the model’s actual learning frontier over long training runs.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Track model mastery rates per difficulty bin over epochs, and experiment with dynamic re‑estimation of difficulty (e.g., periodic re‑evaluation with the current policy). Compare performance and sample efficiency against the static difficulty approach.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** What biases or inconsistencies exist in LLM‑generated rubrics (using o3) for open‑ended scientific questions, and how do they affect the stability and effectiveness of SciRubric‑Guided RL?  
**Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 2.2 and Appendix G.2 detail using OpenAI o3 to generate fine‑grained rubrics with categories (Essential, Important, Optional, Pitfall) for each open‑ended instance.  
**Why unresolved:** The paper assumes rubrics are reliable but provides no human evaluation or inter‑rater reliability. LLM‑generated rubrics may reflect dataset skew, miss domain‑specific subtleties, or produce inconsistent criteria that yield noisy or misaligned reward signals during RL.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct human expert evaluation of rubric quality (completeness, accuracy, category assignment) across STEM subjects. Ablate using expert‑curated rubrics vs. LLM‑generated ones and measure RL training stability, reward correlation with human judgment, and final performance.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** How does the Dr. SCI pipeline handle interdisciplinary scientific questions that span multiple STEM domains, where rubrics may need to integrate criteria from different fields?  
**Basis in paper:** [inferred] The dataset covers eight distinct STEM subjects (Section 2.3) but focuses on single‑domain questions. Interdisciplinary problems are not explicitly addressed in data processing, rubric generation, or training.  
**Why unresolved:** Real‑world scientific reasoning often involves cross‑disciplinary knowledge (e.g., computational biology, environmental economics). Current rubrics are generated per subject and may not capture necessary integrations, potentially limiting model performance on such questions.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Construct a benchmark of validated interdisciplinary scientific questions. Extend the rubric generation pipeline to multi‑domain inputs and evaluate model performance, rubric coherence, and reward effectiveness compared to single‑domain rubrics.

### Open Question 5
**Question:** To what extent do the improvements from the Dr. SCI pipeline generalize to larger model scales or different base architectures beyond Qwen3‑4B?  
**Basis in paper:** [inferred] All experiments use Qwen3‑4B‑Base; baselines include larger models (e.g., 32B) but the pipeline itself is not scaled. The paper does not test whether component contributions (EESFT, curriculum, rubric RL) hold for larger models or other architectures.  
**Why unresolved:** Training dynamics, data efficiency, and reward sensitivity may change with scale. Hyperparameters (e.g., curriculum thresholds, rubric weights) are tuned for 4B and may not transfer. The pipeline’s design assumptions might not scale.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the full pipeline on larger models (e.g., Qwen3‑14B, Llama‑3.1‑70B) with scaled data and adjusted hyperparameters. Analyze performance scaling curves, component ablations at each scale, and compute efficiency relative to model size.

## Limitations
- **Major uncertainties:** Unclear source details for the SFT candidate pool (generation parameters, ratios); verifier prompt/training unspecified; dynamic difficulty thresholds may be brittle without robustness checks.
- **Dataset contamination risk:** Dr. SCI’s construction process may overlap with test benchmarks; no thorough audit reported.
- **Rubric reliability:** LLM‑generated rubrics lack human validation, introducing potential noise and bias in reward signals.

## Confidence
- **Dataset quality:** Medium (scale and splits documented, but annotation reliability and contamination checks rely on authors’ assertions).
- **Pipeline contribution:** Medium (ablations isolate components, but missing specifics hinder full replication).
- **Performance on GPQA:** Medium (numbers surpass strong baselines, but reward‑hacking risks and curriculum stability remain open).
