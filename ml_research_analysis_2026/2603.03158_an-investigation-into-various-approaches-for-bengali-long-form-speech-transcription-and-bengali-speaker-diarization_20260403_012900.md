# An Investigation Into Various Approaches For Bengali Long-Form Speech Transcription and Bengali Speaker Diarization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.03158
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03158
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: DER 0.27 (private leaderboard), 0.19 (public); WER 0.38 (private) on Bengali long-form speech.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenging tasks of Bengali long-form speech transcription and speaker diarization, compounded by the language's low-resource status and the presence of hour-long audio recordings. The authors propose a system that combines a Whisper-medium model for automatic speech recognition with a Pyannote-based diarization pipeline, enhanced by a custom-trained segmentation model tailored to Bengali speech. Key methodological strategies include task-specific segmentation to chunk long audio, a two-pass diarization inference that constrains the speaker count, and algorithmic post-processing to eliminate repeated speech segments. The reported results are promising, achieving a diarization error rate (DER) of 0.27 on a private leaderboard (0.19 on a public split) and a word error rate (WER) of 0.38 on the private leaderboard. However, the study also highlights several unresolved challenges, such as the difficulty of fine-tuning speaker embeddings for Bengali and the observed degradation in ASR performance when fine-tuning Whisper, which warrant further investigation.

## Method Summary
The ASR component employs a Whisper-medium model, which the paper indicates is fine-tuned, though the authors note that fine-tuning attempts sometimes led to degraded performance compared to the base model, possibly due to pipeline inconsistencies. For speaker diarization, the system builds upon the Pyannote framework but replaces its segmentation module with a custom-trained model specifically designed for Bengali speech. Long-form audio is segmented using this task-specific model into manageable chunks. The diarization process incorporates a two-pass inference: an initial pass estimates speaker segments and speaker count, and a second pass uses this count to constrain clustering, aiming to reduce errors. Additional post-processing algorithms remove repeated speech segments to refine the output. The authors also experimented with Demucs-based speech enhancement, finding it beneficial for diarization but detrimental to ASR performance.

## Key Results
- Diarization error rate (DER): 0.27 (private leaderboard), 0.19 (public leaderboard).
- Word error rate (WER): 0.38 (private leaderboard) for transcription.
- Demucs speech enhancement improved diarization but harmed ASR accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper attributes performance gains primarily to the custom segmentation model that better handles Bengali acoustic characteristics, the two-pass diarization that enforces speaker count consistency, and the repetition removal post-processing. However, the authors did not conduct ablation studies to isolate the individual contributions of these components, leaving the precise mechanism uncertain. The failure to improve ASR through Whisper fine-tuning suggests a potential mismatch between the Bengali data distribution and Whisper's pretraining, possibly exacerbated by segmentation inconsistencies. Similarly, the unsuccessful embedding fine-tuning attempts point to challenges in transferring speaker representation models across languages without careful alignment of segment lengths and phonetic content.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Raw audio -> Custom segmentation model -> Segments -> (1) Whisper-medium (ASR) -> Transcripts; (2) Pyannote diarization (with custom segmentation) -> Speaker labels -> Two-pass refinement -> Post-processing (repetition removal) -> Final diarization. Demucs enhancement optionally applied before segmentation for diarization only.

**Critical path:**  
Successful execution requires: (a) segmentation model to produce chunks of appropriate length and speech content; (b) Whisper to transcribe each chunk accurately; (c) Pyannote to extract robust speaker embeddings and cluster correctly; (d) two-pass and post-processing to improve DER without harming WER. Any break (e.g., segmentation failure, embedding mismatch) propagates errors.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Whisper-medium chosen for speed/accuracy tradeoff; larger models might improve WER but increase latency.  
- Custom segmentation replaces Pyannote's default to better fit Bengali, but requires labeled Bengali speech for training.  
- Two-pass inference adds computational overhead but reduces speaker count errors.  
- Post-processing risks removing valid speech if repetitions are under-detected.  
- Demucs enhancement benefits diarization by cleaning signals but harms ASR due to distribution mismatch with Whisper's pretraining.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Degraded ASR performance when fine-tuning Whisper, suggesting data or pipeline issues.  
- Failure to improve diarization by fine-tuning speaker embeddings (WeSpeaker, ECAPA-TDNN), likely due to padding/alignment inconsistencies.  
- Discrepancy between public and private leaderboard scores (0.19 vs. 0.27 DER) indicates dataset shift or evaluation inconsistencies.  
- Lack of ablation means we cannot pinpoint which component contributes most to errors; failures may be hidden by combined effects.

**First experiments:**  
1. Evaluate the custom segmentation model in isolation on a Bengali VAD test set to measure its speech/non-speech detection accuracy and chunking consistency.  
2. Run the full pipeline on a short, manually annotated Bengali conversation to compute DER and WER, comparing against the reported numbers to verify replication.  
3. Disable the two-pass diarization constraint and measure the change in DER to quantify its isolated impact.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the speaker embedding component of the Pyannote diarization pipeline be effectively fine-tuned for Bengali speech, given that attempts with WeSpeaker and ECAPA-TDNN embeddings failed or degraded performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "...only the segmentation component was fine-tuned... Attempts to fine-tune a WeSpeaker embedding model did not yield improvements, and substituting ECAPA-TDNN embeddings resulted in degraded performance, likely due to padding and alignment inconsistencies."
- Why unresolved: The authors identify padding and alignment as probable causes but lack empirical validation; no successful domain-adaptive embedding fine-tuning was achieved for Bengali.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments fine-tuning embeddings on Bengali data with standardized segment lengths and alignment protocols, measuring DER improvements over fixed embeddings.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Which clustering strategies (beyond PLDA) are optimal for Bengali speaker diarization in noisy, conversational settings with unconstrained speaker counts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Alternative clustering strategies were not systematically explored due to time constraints."
- Why unresolved: The pipeline relies on PLDA clustering, but Bengali acoustic variability and speaker overlap may require tailored approaches; no comparative analysis exists.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic benchmarking of clustering algorithms (e.g., spectral, agglomerative) on Bengali diarization datasets, with metrics like DER under varying overlap and noise conditions.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Why does task-specific fine-tuning of the Whisper-medium ASR model degrade performance on Bengali long-form speech, and how can fine-tuning be adapted to avoid this?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "For ASR, although task-specific fine-tuning is generally known to improve performance, our fine-tuning attempts resulted in degraded accuracy compared to the non-fine-tuned model, likely due to pipeline and methodological inconsistencies."
- Why unresolved: The hypothesis cites inconsistencies but lacks detailed diagnosis; fine-tuning protocols successful for other languages fail here, suggesting Bengali-specific data or pipeline issues.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies isolating factors (data noise, segmentation inconsistency, regularization) during Whisper fine-tuning on Bengali long-form corpora, with WER as the metric.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can alignment-aware optimization (e.g., CTC-based or hybrid alignment) improve long-form Bengali ASR by reducing word error rates in spontaneous speech with speaker turns?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Incorporating task-specific fine-tuning with CTC-based alignment or hybrid alignment strategies could potentially enhance phoneme-level consistency and reduce word error rates."
- Why unresolved: The proposed strategies were not implemented; current system relies on Whisper's decoder, which may misalign segments across long, multi-speaker audio.
- What evidence would resolve it: Modify Whisper fine-tuning to incorporate CTC/sequence-to-sequence hybridization, evaluating WER on long-form Bengali test sets with timestamps aligned to speaker diarization.

### Open Question 5
- Question: Why does Demucs-based speech enhancement improve Bengali speaker diarization but harm ASR performance when using Whisper, and what explains this modality-specific discrepancy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Using it in long-form ASR did not yield any noticeable positive results, possibly due to mismatch between enhanced signals and Whisper’s pretraining distribution."
- Why unresolved: The speculation about pretraining mismatch is unverified; the contrasting effects on diarization vs. ASR suggest task-dependent sensitivity to enhanced features.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyze feature distributions (e.g., spectrograms, embeddings) of raw vs. Demucs-enhanced Bengali speech, correlate with Whisper's training data statistics, and test diarization/ASR performance under controlled enhancement levels.

## Limitations
- The training and evaluation corpora are not described in detail (size, speaker diversity, recording conditions), limiting assessment of generalizability and reproducibility.
- No ablation studies isolate the contributions of the two-pass diarization constraint, post-processing, or custom segmentation, making it unclear which components drive performance.
- The significant discrepancy between public (DER 0.19) and private (DER 0.27) leaderboard scores is unexplained, suggesting potential data leakage or evaluation inconsistencies.

## Confidence
- Reported end-to-end performance metrics (DER 0.27 private, WER 0.38 private): Medium confidence (specific but unverified on a single private dataset).
- Efficacy claims for the two-pass diarization constraint and post-processing: Low confidence (no ablation evidence).
- Generalizability to broader Bengali speech variations (accents, noise, spontaneous speech): Low confidence (single, uncharacterized dataset).
