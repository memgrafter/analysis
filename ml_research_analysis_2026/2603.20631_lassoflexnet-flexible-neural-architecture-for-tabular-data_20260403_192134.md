# LassoFlexNet: Flexible Neural Architecture for Tabular Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.20631
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20631
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Across 52 datasets from three benchmarks, LassoFlexNet matches or outperforms leading tree-based and deep tabular models, achieving up to a 10% relative gain and establishing a new state-of-the-art on the TabRed benchmark under distribution shift.

## Executive Summary
LassoFlexNet addresses the long-standing underperformance of deep neural networks on tabular data relative to tree-based models. It introduces a tabular-native architecture that incorporates key inductive biases through three main contributions: Piecewise Linear Encoding (PLE) to align with feature axes and capture localized irregularities; Per-Feature Embeddings with Tied Group Lasso for interpretable feature selection; and a Sequential Hierarchical Proximal Adaptive Gradient optimizer with EMA to ensure stable convergence of the non-convex sparse structure. Empirical evaluation on 52 datasets demonstrates that LassoFlexNet matches or exceeds the performance of gradient-boosted trees and state-of-the-art deep tabular models, with gains of up to 10% and robust behavior under distribution shift.

## Method Summary
The architecture consists of three stages. First, numerical features are transformed via Piecewise Linear Encoding (PLE), which partitions each feature into data-driven intervals and applies a piecewise linear mapping; categorical features are one-hot encoded. Each feature’s encoding is processed by an independent Per-Feature Embedding (PFE) ResNet block, producing an embedding vector. After parameter-free BatchNorm, embeddings are mean-pooled per feature to obtain ¯z. The model then combines a linear skip connection β⊤¯z with an MLP-Mixer module that captures cross-feature interactions, weighted by a curriculum scalar τ. Training occurs in two phases: (1) pre-training with AdamW to learn representations without proximal constraints; (2) λ-training where a novel Sequential Hierarchical Proximal Adaptive Gradient with EMA enforces an ℓ1 penalty on β and a hierarchical constraint ∥W^(1)∥∞ ≤ M|β| linking the Mixer’s first-layer weights to β. The λ path is increased polynomially until sparsity is achieved.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance on 52 datasets, with up to 10% relative improvement over tree-based and deep tabular models.
- Establishes new SOTA on the TabRed benchmark under distribution shift.
- Provides interpretable feature selection via sparse β, validated on synthetic noisy feature benchmarks where it outperforms two-stage pipelines without oracle knowledge of relevant variables.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Piecewise Linear Encoding (PLE)
- **Claim:** PLE breaks undesirable rotational invariance and increases expressivity for localized irregularities without inflating parameter count.
- **Mechanism:** PLE partitions each numerical feature into axis-aligned intervals via data-driven breakpoints. This encoding makes the network's function representation dependent on the original coordinate system; a rotation changes bin boundaries, thus the learned representation. The encoding yields a linear spline class per feature, requiring only T slopes for T bins (T−1 knots). Compared to a standard one-hidden-layer ReLU MLP needing m ≥ T−1 hidden units for similar knot complexity, PLE provides the same capacity with a fixed parameter budget.
- **Core assumption:** Tabular data's meaningful axes are aligned with feature semantics and should be preserved; local decision boundaries are beneficial.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract]: "introduces ... Piecewise Linear Encoding ... to incorporate key inductive biases like robustness to irrelevant features, axis alignment, localized irregularities"
  - [section 4.2.1]: "To inherently adapt to tabular heterogeneity, PLE partitions each numerical feature ... This formulation generates Ki·k localized gradients ... lifting the neural capacity at a linear rate"
  - [section 6]: Proposition 6.1 formally states PLE breaks rotational invariance; Proposition 6.2 states it increases expressivity without extra parameters.
- **Break condition:** If breakpoints are poorly chosen (e.g., uniform instead of data-driven) the bin structure may not capture local irregularities, reducing expressivity gains.

### Mechanism 2: Tied Group Lasso on Per-Feature Embeddings
- **Claim:** Tied Group Lasso on Per-Feature Embeddings provides feature selection based on nonlinear representations while maintaining interpretability and robustness to irrelevant features.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of applying sparsity to raw inputs, LassoFlexNet applies an ℓ1 penalty on the mean-pooled embeddings ¯zi. Because embeddings come from independent per-feature networks (PFE), the single scalar βi controls the entire embedded group (Tied Group Lasso). This decouples feature importance from the nonlinear transformation, allowing the model to select features based on their marginal predictive contribution in a learned representation space.
- **Core assumption:** A linear skip connection can guide a nonlinear module if feature importance is estimated on informative embeddings rather than raw inputs.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "evaluates the linear and nonlinear marginal contribution of each input via Per-Feature Embeddings, and sparsely selects relevant variables using a Tied Group Lasso mechanism"
  - [section 4.2.2]: "We apply the linear skip connection strictly to the mean-pooled embeddings ¯zi. This mathematically implements a Tied Group Lasso, wherein a single scalar βi controls the entire i-th embedded group."
  - [section 6.1]: Ablation shows no PFE leads to much worse performance (RMSE 0.5371 vs 0.4047).
- **Break condition:** If embeddings are poorly trained (e.g., due to optimizer instability), β may not reflect true feature importance; the sequential update (Mechanism 3) is required to prevent the neural module from dominating.

### Mechanism 3: Sequential Hierarchical Proximal Adaptive Gradient with EMA
- **Claim:** Sequential Hierarchical Proximal Adaptive Gradient with EMA (Seq-Hier-Prox-EMA) ensures stable convergence of the non-convex, sparse architecture by decoupling feature selection from the neural module and smoothing gradient noise.
- **Mechanism:** Unlike LassoNet's joint hierarchical proximal update, Seq-Hier-Prox-EMA performs two steps: (1) update β via soft-thresholding (Lasso prox) using EMA-smoothed gradients; (2) update W^(1) under constraint ∥W^(1)∥∞ ≤ M|β|, where β is now fixed from step 1. The EMA is applied to parameters during the prox evaluation, reducing variance and preventing sign flips in β that destabilize training. The soft curriculum (τ) downscales the Mixer gradients initially to let β converge first.
- **Core assumption:** Standard proximal methods fail under high stochastic variance in sparse neural networks; decoupling the optimization trajectories reduces gradient interference.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "develop a Sequential Hierarchical Proximal Adaptive Gradient optimizer with exponential moving averages (EMA) to ensure stable convergence"
  - [section 5]: "We fundamentally redesign the proximal operator to overcome three severe failure modes of HPG... 1) joint→sequential optimization; 2) proximal gradient→proximal adaptive gradient; 3) EMA stabilization"
  - [section 6.1]: Ablation removing EMA increases RMSE from 0.4047 to 0.4195; original Hier-Prox (LassoNet) gives 0.4320.
- **Break condition:** If τ is not properly scheduled or EMA decay is too high/low, either the Mixer overwhelms β or learning becomes too slow; if M is mis-set, constraint enforcement fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Rotational invariance in neural networks**
  - Why needed: The paper argues tree-based models outperform DNNs partly because they are axis-aligned; DNNs suffer from rotational invariance which mixes feature axes and obscures heterogeneity. PLE explicitly breaks this.
  - Quick check: Why is axis alignment beneficial for tabular data, and how does PLE achieve it?

- **Proximal gradient methods and hierarchical constraints**
  - Why needed: LassoFlexNet uses Lasso-style sparsity, requiring proximal operators for ℓ1 regularization under hierarchical constraints (∥W^(1)∥∞ ≤ M|β|). Understanding these is key to why naive joint optimization fails.
  - Quick check: What is the difference between solving (β, W) jointly vs. sequentially in a hierarchical Lasso problem?

- **Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) and lazy training**
  - Why needed: Theoretical analysis (Section 6) uses NTK to show PLE breaks rotational invariance in the infinite-width regime. The slope/value NTK analysis explains PLE's localized expressivity.
  - Quick check: In the NTK view, how does PLE change the kernel's structure across features?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input features -> [PLE for numerical (binning + clipping), one-hot for categorical] -> Per-Feature Embedding (PFE) ResNet block (with fixed BatchNorm) -> pooled embeddings ¯z -> Tied Group Lasso linear skip (β⊤¯z) + τ·[MLP-Mixer module] -> output.
- **Critical path:** The success depends on (a) PLE capturing local irregularities, (b) PFE embeddings being normalized so β magnitude reflects feature importance, (c) τ preventing Mixer from bypassing the skip connection early, (d) Seq-Hier-Prox-EMA stably enforcing ∥W^(1)∥∞ ≤ M|β|.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - PLE bins: more bins increase local expressivity but may overfit; few bins may underfit irregularities.
  - τ < 1: soft curriculum stabilizes selection but may underutilize Mixer capacity; τ = 1 risks overfitting.
  - EMA decay: higher decay smoother but may lag optimal weights; lower decay less stable.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - β magnitudes do not separate relevant from irrelevant features → PFE not trained properly or τ too high.
  - Validation loss diverges during λ-training → Seq-Hier-Prox failing; reduce λ step size or increase EMA decay.
  - Poor performance on noisy feature benchmarks → PLE breakpoints not capturing signal; check binning strategy.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation study (California Housing):** Disable EMA, remove Mixer, replace Seq-Hier-Prox with Hier-Prox, verify each component's contribution (Table 2).
  2. **Feature selection under noise:** Run on 12 datasets with 50%/75% random or correlated extra features; compare to two-stage pipelines with oracle feature count (Table 3,4). Expect LassoFlexNet to outperform despite no prior knowledge of k.
  3. **TabZilla distribution shift:** Full benchmark on 35 datasets; verify consistent accuracy gains over GBDT and deep models (Figure 2). Use 5-fold CV with Optuna hyperparameter tuning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: Statistical Consistency of Tied Group Lasso on Nonlinear Embeddings
- **Question:** Under what data-generating assumptions does LassoFlexNet’s feature selection via Tied Group Lasso on Per-Feature Embeddings achieve model selection consistency (i.e., recovery of the true support) as the sample size grows?
- **Basis:** The paper leverages the Lasso’s sparsity properties but does not provide theoretical guarantees for consistency in the nonlinear embedding regime. While Propositions 6.1 and 6.2 address expressivity and rotation invariance, nothing is said about the statistical properties of the selection mechanism under high dimensionality or noise, which is a natural extension of LassoNet’s unresolved theoretical gaps.
- **Why unresolved:** Consistency is fundamental for trustworthy interpretability and generalization. Without it, the robustness of feature selection to sampling variability, especially when embedding nonlinearities are involved, remains unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Formal theorems showing that, under conditions like restricted eigenvalue or compatibility conditions on the embedding Jacobian, the Seq-Hier-Prox-EMA estimator recovers the correct support with high probability and yields √n-consistent estimates for nonzero coefficients, ideally in a high-dimensional asymptotic regime.

### Open Question 2: Computational Complexity and Scalability Limits
- **Question:** What is the asymptotic time and memory complexity of LassoFlexNet as the number of features d and samples n grow, and at what thresholds does it become computationally inferior to tree-based methods?
- **Basis:** The authors acknowledge in the Introduction that scalable, tree-competitive representation learning is an open problem that LassoFlexNet targets. They benchmark across 52 datasets but only describe per-feature embedding and sequential proximal updates geometrically without theoretical or empirical scaling analysis beyond moderate dimensions.
- **Why unresolved:** Tree-based models often scale subquadratically and handle massive data efficiently. LassoFlexNet’s per-feature ResNets and MLP-Mixer, coupled with sequential proximal steps, could become bottlenecks for d in the thousands or n in the millions; this trade-off is not quantified.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A detailed complexity analysis (big-O notation) for training and inference, coupled with empirical runs on datasets with d > 10,000 and n > 1e6, comparing wall-clock time and memory usage against XGBoost/CatBoost and establishing break-even points.

### Open Question 3: Theoretical Justification and Role of the Curriculum Scalar τ
- **Question:** How does the scalar τ in the objective ˆy = β⊤¯z + τ·NN_W(z) formally influence the implicit bias of the optimizer, and what is the optimal τ in terms of a bias–variance trade-off?
- **Basis:** τ is introduced as a soft curriculum to downscale the Mixer’s gradients so the linear skip learns first. Ablation shows τ < 1 stabilizes training, but the paper offers no theory linking τ to the optimization trajectory, prox-grad dynamics, or generalization bounds.
- **Why unresolved:** τ is selected via hyperparameter search (range 0.01–2.0) without principle. Understanding its effect could yield automatic tuning or adaptive schedules and clarify why it helps beyond empirical stabilization.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analysis showing that τ modulates the effective Lipschitz constant of the non-convex loss or the signal-to-noise ratio for the proximal mapping. Possibly, an implicit bias result in an overparameterized regime or KL-divergence control between sparse and dense solutions.

### Open Question 4: Sample Complexity and Optimal Binning Strategy for PLE
- **Question:** How should the number of bins K_i for each numerical feature in Piecewise Linear Encoding be chosen to minimize generalization error as a function of sample size and feature distribution?
- **Basis:** PLE’s expressivity proof (Proposition 6.2) shows a linear increase in capacity with K_i, but the paper does not address the statistical cost: more bins increase variance and discretization error. The hyperparameter search ranges over K ∈ [4, 32] or [16, 64] heuristically, with no theoretical guidance.
- **Why unresolved:** Binning is a critical modeling choice; too few bins limit flexibility for localized irregularities, too many cause overfitting especially with limited data. No principle (e.g., based on histogram asymptotics or VC-dimension) is provided.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Sample complexity bounds for piecewise linear functions with K bins, showing how K should grow with n to achieve optimal rates. Alternatively, a theoretically justified data-driven binning method (e.g., using influence functions or cross-validation with theoretical justification) that outperforms uniform binning.

### Open Question 5: Convergence of Seq-Hier-Prox-EMA under General Non-convexity without Proximal-PL
- **Question:** Does the Sequential Hierarchical Proximal Adaptive Gradient with EMA converge to a stationary point for the full non-convex objective without assuming a proximal-PL condition, and what is its convergence rate?
- **Basis:** The convergence analysis in Section 6 and Appendix E relies on a proximal-PL assumption (Proposition 6.4) and bounded iterates. For general deep networks, such conditions may not hold. The empirical success suggests robustness, but a more general guarantee (e.g., under the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property or using variance reduction) is missing.
- **Why unresolved:** The proximal-PL condition is strong and non-universal. Without it, the theoretical justification for the algorithm’s stability is incomplete, especially since LassoFlexNet mixes convex (Prox) and non-convex (Mixer) components.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Prove convergence to an (ε,δ)-stationary point under milder assumptions (e.g., smoothness, bounded gradients) using tools like the Moreau envelope or stochastic differential equation approximations. Alternatively, establish that EMA specifically mitigates branch-switch instabilities even without strict PL.

## Limitations
- Missing precise hyperparameter defaults (e.g., PLE bins, Mixer depth, τ schedule) and categorical encoding details, forcing extensive tuning.
- Ambiguity in PLE breakpoint strategy (tree-based vs. uniform) that could affect robustness and reproducibility.
- Incomplete specification of the proximal algorithm’s EMA integration and λ scaling, risking implementation errors and instability.
