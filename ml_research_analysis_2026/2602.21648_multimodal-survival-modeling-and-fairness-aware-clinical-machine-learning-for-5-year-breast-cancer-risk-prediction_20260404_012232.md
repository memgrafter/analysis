# Multimodal Survival Modeling and Fairness-Aware Clinical Machine Learning for 5-Year Breast Cancer Risk Prediction
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.21648
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21648
- **Reference Count**: 29
- **Primary Result**: Developed a reproducible multimodal framework achieving test AUC 96.6 (CoxNet) and 92.5 (XGBoost) for 5-year breast cancer survival prediction, with stable discrimination and calibrated probabilities across key clinical subgroups.

## Executive Summary
This study presents a governance-oriented, reproducible framework for 5-year breast cancer survival prediction using multimodal data (clinical, transcriptomic, CNA) from the METABRIC cohort. The pipeline enforces rigorous evaluation practices: stratified splitting, out-of-sample testing, calibration assessment, and fairness auditing across clinically relevant subgroups. Two model classes were compared: elastic-net regularized Cox regression (CoxNet) and gradient-boosted survival trees (XGBoost). CoxNet achieved superior discrimination (AUC 96.6) and calibration, while XGBoost captured nonlinear interactions but required post-hoc probability calibration. The work emphasizes that high discrimination alone is insufficient for clinical translation, mandating explicit calibration science and fairness-by-design in high-dimensional survival modeling.

## Method Summary
The analysis uses METABRIC data with a complete-case design for the 60-month fixed-horizon outcome, excluding patients censored before 60 months. A deterministic stratified split (70/15/15) creates non-overlapping train, validation, and test sets. Preprocessing (variance/sparsity filtering, truncated SVD for omics) is fit solely on the training set to prevent data leakage. Models are trained on the training set with hyperparameter tuning via the validation set. CoxNet optimizes the elastic-net penalized Cox partial likelihood. XGBoost optimizes a Cox-based boosting objective. The Breslow estimator computes the baseline hazard on the training data to convert model scores to 5-year survival probabilities; XGBoost’s raw scores are further calibrated using isotonic regression fitted on the validation set. Final evaluation on the held-out test set includes discrimination (AUC, AP), calibration (slope, intercept, Brier, ECE), and fairness metrics computed per subgroup (age, ER status, molecular subtype, menopausal state), with 1000 bootstrap resamples for confidence intervals.

## Key Results
- CoxNet achieved a test AUC of 96.6 and AP of 80.4; XGBoost achieved a test AUC of 92.5 and AP of 79.9.
- Both models demonstrated stable discrimination across age, ER status, molecular subtype, and menopausal state subgroups.
- CoxNet exhibited stronger probability calibration (closer to ideal slope/intercept) while XGBoost required isotonic regression to address significant raw probability misalignment (slope 0.305).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim**: Elastic-net regularized Cox regression (CoxNet) achieves stable high-dimensional survival estimation by balancing sparsity and coefficient stability.  
**Mechanism**: The Cox partial likelihood is maximized under an elastic-net penalty `λ(α||β||₁ + (1−α)||β||₂²)`. The L1 component performs embedded feature selection by driving coefficients to zero, while the L2 component mitigates multicollinearity and reduces variance. This dual regularization addresses the `p >> n` problem by preventing overfitting to noise in high-dimensional multimodal features.  
**Core assumption**: The true underlying hazard is approximately linear on the log-scale (proportional hazards) and sparse or grouped relative to the feature space.  
**Evidence anchors**: 
- [abstract] "CoxNet provides embedded feature selection and stable estimation in high-dimensional settings."
- [section 3.5.1] Full derivation of penalized partial likelihood and hyperparameter tuning via cross-validation.
- [corpus] Neighboring papers (e.g., Kernel Cox models) address high-dimension but not the exact regularization scheme.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim**: Gradient-boosted survival trees (XGBoost) capture nonlinear covariate effects and complex feature interactions absent in linear Cox models.  
**Mechanism**: Additive regression trees are sequentially optimized to minimize the regularized negative Cox partial log-likelihood. Each tree splits on feature thresholds, enabling the model to learn nonlinear decision boundaries and interactions (e.g., between a gene expression and a clinical stage) without explicit specification.  
**Core assumption**: Useful prognostic information exists in nonlinear relationships and interactions among the multimodal features that are missed by linear terms.  
**Evidence anchors**:
- [abstract] "XGBoost captures nonlinear effects and higher-order feature interactions."
- [section 3.5.2] Details the boosting objective `ℒ = ∑ℓcoxᵢ + Ω(f)` and additive function `f̂(x) = ∑fₖ(x)`.
- [corpus] Neighbors like ModalSurv explore multimodal deep/ensemble methods, supporting the premise that nonlinearity adds value.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim**: A governance-oriented pipeline enforcing stratified data splitting, out-of-sample evaluation, and subgroup fairness auditing mitigates data leakage, overfitting, and inequitable performance across clinical strata.  
**Mechanism**: Deterministic stratified splitting preserves event balance. Preprocessing is fit only on the training set. Performance is evaluated on a held-out test set with bootstrapped CIs. Fairness metrics (AUROC₉, Brier₉, calibration slope) are computed per subgroup to detect disparities.  
**Core assumption**: Model evaluation must mimic prospective deployment conditions, and subgroup performance stability is a prerequisite for safe clinical translation.  
**Evidence anchors**:
- [abstract] "This work introduces a governance-oriented multimodal survival modeling framework that emphasizes calibration science, algorithmic fairness assessment, and reproducibility-by-design."
- [section 3.9] Explicit fairness evaluation protocol across subgroups.
- [section 3.11] Reproducibility protocol with fixed seeds and persisted splits.
- [corpus] Multiple neighbors highlight the importance of fairness auditing in healthcare AI.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: **Survival analysis with censoring**.  
  *Why needed*: The outcome is time-to-event (overall survival) with right-censoring. Models must account for incomplete follow-up, and fixed-horizon (5-year) risk requires proper handling of patients censored before 60 months.  
  *Quick check*: Why are patients censored before 60 months excluded from the fixed-horizon discrimination analysis, and how does the Breslow estimator estimate the baseline hazard?

- **Concept**: **Regularization for high-dimensional data (elastic-net)**.  
  *Why needed*: The transcriptomic and CNA blocks have thousands of features (`p >> n`). Without regularization, Cox partial likelihood maximization yields infinite or unstable solutions. Elastic-net combines L1 sparsity and L2 stability.  
  *Quick check*: What is the effect on model coefficients when `α = 0` (ridge-only) versus `α = 1` (lasso-only) in this context?

- **Concept**: **Calibration of predicted probabilities**.  
  *Why needed*: Discrimination (AUROC) can be high even if predicted probabilities are misaligned with true risks. Clinical decisions rely on absolute risk, requiring well-calibrated probabilities. Brier score and calibration slope/intercept measure this.  
  *Quick check*: What does a calibration slope < 1 indicate about the model's predicted risk distribution, and how was it addressed for XGBoost?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  Cohort assembly & labeling -> Multimodal feature engineering -> Data splitting -> Model training -> Fixed-horizon risk conversion -> Calibration -> Evaluation (with bootstrapped CIs) -> Subgroup fairness audit.
- **Critical Path**: Preprocessing (train-only) -> Train model with validation tuning -> Convert to fixed-horizon risk -> Calibrate if needed (XGBoost) -> Evaluate on held-out test with bootstrapped CIs -> Subgroup fairness audit. Key constraint: No information from validation/test can influence preprocessing or training parameter choices.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **CoxNet vs. XGBoost**: CoxNet offers interpretable coefficients, inherent probability calibration from survival function, but assumes linear log-hazards. XGBoost models nonlinearities/interactions but requires post-hoc isotonic calibration for probability alignment and is less interpretable.
  - **Missing modality handling**: Stress test simulates deployment with incomplete omics; performance drop informs redundancy but the framework does not dynamically impute—masking is a one-time evaluation.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Calibration failure**: Calibration slope distant from 1 (e.g., XGBoost raw slope 0.305) or high ECE (>0.1). Indicates unreliable absolute risks.
  - **Fairness red flag**: >0.05 difference in AUROC or calibration slope across clinically critical subgroups, or threshold-based TPR/FPR disparity beyond sampling noise.
  - **Reproducibility breach**: Test performance varies >2% across different random seeds without changed hyperparameters, suggesting data leakage or unstable splits.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Baseline reproducibility check**: With fixed seed, run entire pipeline (CoxNet) on a small subset (e.g., 100 patients). Verify exact replication of splits and test AUROC within 0.01 across two runs.
  2. **Calibration diagnosis**: Plot uncalibrated XGBoost risk scores vs. observed 5-year event rate (quantile bins). Expect significant misalignment; then apply isotonic regression and re-plot to confirm improvement.
  3. **Subgroup stress test**: Deliberately stratify test set by a protected attribute (e.g., age ≥70 vs. <50). Compute AUROC and Brier for each. Identify if any subgroup has AUROC drop >0.05 or Brier increase >0.05 relative to overall.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Does the complete-case analysis design (excluding patients censored before 60 months) introduce selection bias that limits the model's transportability to real-world settings where censoring patterns differ?  
  *Basis*: The authors exclude early-censored patients for fixed-horizon evaluation and acknowledge this may cause selection bias.  
  *Why unresolved*: The impact on external validity is unknown; real-world cohorts will have different censoring mechanisms.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Compare performance between complete-case and intention-to-treat analyses (e.g., using inverse probability weighting) in an external cohort.

- **Open Question 2**: To what extent does the proportional hazards assumption hold for the CoxNet model when incorporating high-dimensional transcriptomic and CNA features?  
  *Basis*: The authors note the Cox model assumes proportional hazards and linear effects as a limitation.  
  *Why unresolved*: Multimodal genomic data could violate key CoxPH premises, but this was not formally tested.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Conduct Schoenfeld residual tests for proportionality on key features and compare against time-varying coefficient extensions.

- **Open Question 3**: Could deep survival architectures (e.g., DeepSurv) improve discrimination, calibration, or fairness relative to CoxNet and XGBoost?  
  *Basis*: The authors suggest exploring "nonlinear survival architectures" in future work.  
  *Why unresolved*: Deep models were not evaluated; their ability to capture complex omics interactions might enhance prediction but could compromise calibration.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Implement DeepSurv with identical preprocessing, tuning, and audits on METABRIC and compare all metrics.

- **Open Question 4**: Can fairness-aware regularization reduce subgroup performance disparities more effectively than descriptive auditing, without harming overall discrimination?  
  *Basis*: The authors call for "fairness-aware regularization strategies" as future work, noting they audit but do not mitigate bias.  
  *Why unresolved*: Disparities in calibration or threshold-based metrics were not tested with in-processing interventions.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Integrate fairness constraints (e.g., equalized odds) into training objectives and compare subgroup parity and overall metrics.

- **Open Question 5**: Will the framework maintain performance when prospectively validated on external cohorts with differing demographics, protocols, and feature distributions?  
  *Basis*: The authors emphasize the need for "prospective multi-center validation" to assess transportability.  
  *Why unresolved*: All results derive from METABRIC; external validity across institutions, ethnicities, and assay platforms is uncertain.  
  *Resolving evidence*: Apply the entire pipeline to at least two independent breast cancer survival datasets and report consistency of core metrics.

## Limitations
- Preprocessing hyperparameters (variance/sparsity thresholds, SVD components) and exact hyperparameter search grids are not specified, requiring assumptions for replication.
- Fairness metric definitions and statistical testing for disparity thresholds are not fully detailed; subgroup comparisons rely on unreported confidence intervals.
- The complete-case design excludes early-censored patients, potentially introducing selection bias and limiting transportability to real-world censoring patterns.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Governance pipeline (stratified splits, out-of-sample testing) and CoxNet’s regularization mechanism are explicitly described and theoretically sound.
- **Medium confidence**: XGBoost’s nonlinearity advantage—plausible given model class but lacks direct ablation showing added value over linear terms in this specific multimodal setting.
- **Low confidence**: Fairness stability across subgroups—subgroup metrics reported but definitions (e.g., age bins) and statistical testing for disparity thresholds are not fully specified.
