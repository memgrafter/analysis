# The Geometric Cost of Normalization: Affine Bounds on the Bayesian Complexity of Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27432
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27432
- **References:** 24
- **Primary result:** Normalization layers impose geometric constraints on the data manifold that deterministically reduce the Bayesian complexity (Local Learning Coefficient) of subsequent linear layers; LayerNorm reduces LLC by exactly m/2, while RMSNorm preserves it entirely.

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes how common normalization layers (LayerNorm, RMSNorm) affect the Bayesian complexity of neural networks, quantified by the Local Learning Coefficient (LLC). The core theoretical finding, derived from singular learning theory, is that the LLC reduction Δλ for a single linear layer depends solely on the linear span d_s of the normalized data manifold: Δλ = m(d - d_s)/2. LayerNorm mean-centers data onto a hyperplane (d_s = d-1) causing a full reduction Δλ = m/2, while RMSNorm projects data onto a sphere (d_s = d), preserving LLC. The theory is validated through controlled single-layer teacher-student experiments using SGLD-based LLC estimation. Finite-sample effects introduce a smooth crossover between flat and curved manifolds, and an open problem remains for the "smuggled bias" case with Softmax plus an explicit bias after normalization.

## Method Summary
The work combines theoretical analysis with empirical validation. Theoretically, it uses singular learning theory to prove that for a realizable single linear layer with squared loss, the LLC is determined by the dimension of the symmetry space orthogonal to the data manifold's linear span. Experimentally, it employs a weight-removed LLC (wrLLC) protocol on isolated single-layer teacher-student models: a frozen teacher generates targets, a student linear layer (optionally preceded by a normalization layer) is trained to zero MSE, and the LLC is estimated via SGLD (devinterp). The observed Δλ is compared against the theoretical prediction based on the normalized data's geometry. Tests include Gaussian baselines, flat hyperplanes, curved manifolds (paraboloid, hyperboloid, saddle), and Softmax-simplex data with/without bias.

## Key Results
- For a single linear layer with squared loss, the LLC reduction is Δλ = m(d - d_s)/2, where d_s is the linear span dimension of the input data.
- LayerNorm (mean-centering) yields d_s = d-1 → Δλ = m/2 exactly; RMSNorm (unit-RMS) yields d_s = d → Δλ = 0.
- For Softmax-simplex data combined with an explicit bias term, an affine degeneracy ("smuggled bias") also produces Δλ = m/2.
- Finite-sample LLC estimation shows a binary threshold for manifold curvature: flat hyperplanes yield the full drop, while any non-zero curvature (positive or negative) preserves LLC. The transition smooths with sample size and estimator noise.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Normalization layers impose geometric constraints on the data manifold that directly reduce the LLC of the following weight matrix. LayerNorm forces the mean to zero, embedding data in a hyperplane (codimension 1), so d_s = d-1 and Δλ = m/2. RMSNorm normalizes to unit norm, embedding data on a sphere, which has full linear span d_s = d, so Δλ = 0. [abstract; section 2.3].

### Mechanism 2
The LLC drop arises from a continuous symmetry space in the weight matrix orthogonal to the data manifold's span. If data lies on a manifold M with dim(span(M)) = d_s, then any weight perturbation U satisfying Ux = 0 for all x ∈ M is a symmetry. This symmetry space has dimension m(d - d_s). Under Morse-Bott conditions, the RLCT equals codim(W_0)/2 = m d_s /2, giving Δλ = m(d - d_s)/2. [section 2.5].

### Mechanism 3
At finite sample size, the observed LLC threshold between flat and curved manifolds becomes smooth because weak or localized curvature may not be sampled sufficiently. The crossover amplitude depends on the fraction of data encountering curvature, not the intrinsic geometry. With infinite data, the binary prediction should re-emerge. [section 3.3; Appendix A].

## Foundational Learning
- **Local Learning Coefficient (LLC) / Real Log Canonical Threshold (RLCT)**: The Bayesian complexity measure equivalent to the number of effective parameters. It is the key quantity the paper predicts and measures.
  - *Quick check*: If a weight matrix W ∈ ℝ^{m×d} operates on data confined to a hyperplane of codimension 1, what is the expected Δλ under squared loss? → m/2.
- **Data manifold linear span (d_s)**: The dimension of the linear span of the data manifold M. It is the sole geometric determinant of the LLC reduction Δλ = m(d - d_s)/2.
  - *Quick check*: For LayerNorm with input dimension d, what is d_s? For RMSNorm? → d-1; d.
- **wrLLC (weight-removed LLC) protocol**: An experimental method that freezes all parameters except the target layer's weights to isolate that layer's geometric contribution to the LLC, avoiding interference from other structural symmetries (e.g., GL(r) scalings).
  - *Quick check*: In a multi-layer network, why might measuring the LLC of a single layer directly be misleading? → Interactions with adjacent layers' parameterizations can introduce additional degeneracies that obscure the layer's intrinsic geometric effect.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Normalization layer → geometric constraint on data → reduced linear span d_s → symmetry space S_M in next weight matrix → RLCT reduction Δλ = m(d-d_s)/2.

**Critical path:**
1. Identify the normalization operation (e.g., LayerNorm, RMSNorm, Softmax).
2. Determine the effective data manifold M after normalization (e.g., hyperplane, sphere, simplex).
3. Compute d_s = dim(span(M)). For affine layers (with bias), consider affine span d_a.
4. Predict Δλ = m(d - d_s)/2 (linear) or m(d - d_a)/2 (affine).
5. Validate with wrLLC on a single-layer teacher-student model.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- LayerNorm reduces effective capacity (Δλ = m/2), which may aid generalization but limit expressivity; RMSNorm preserves capacity but may need more regularization.
- The "smuggled bias" phenomenon shows that bias terms can interact with data geometry (e.g., Softmax simplex) to create hidden symmetries and capacity loss.

**Failure signatures:**
- Estimator instability for m≥8 (bimodal Δλ, high variance across seeds).
- Finite-sample crossover: weak curvature may be invisible, yielding false-negative Δλ≈ m/2 on a theoretically curved manifold.
- Poor baseline calibration for non-affine losses (e.g., Post-LN with Softmax), leading to inconsistent Δλ.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Single-layer scaling (vary m)**: Fix d=12, use LayerNorm, measure Δλ vs. m. Should see Δλ ≈ m/2 until estimator fails.
2. **Single-layer scaling (vary d)**: Fix m=4, repeat with varying d. Should see Δλ ≈ 2.0 constant.
3. **Curvature threshold test**: Train on manifolds with zero, positive, and negative curvature (flat hyperplane, paraboloid, hyperboloid, saddle). Expect LLC near Gaussian baseline for curved ones, drop ≈ m/2 for flat.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does the "smuggled bias" degeneracy (Δλ = m/2) also occur when a linear layer with Softmax-simplex input is followed by LayerNorm? (Conjecture 1; SGLD results inconsistent).
- Does the per-layer LLC reduction formula Δλₗ = mₗ(dₗ - dₛ,ₗ)/2 hold in deep networks under the wrLLC protocol? (Remark 3; empirical validation in multi-layer models is future work).
- Does non-trivial top-homology of the data manifold guarantee unconditional LLC preservation (no reduction) even at finite sample sizes, in contrast to local curvature which can fail? (Appendix A).
- Does the LLC drop caused by Pre-LayerNorm correlate with the strength of "attention sinks" in Transformer models? (Conclusion; preliminary experiments found no significant difference).

## Limitations
- SGLD-based LLC estimator becomes unreliable for larger parameter counts (m≥8) and for non-affine loss landscapes (e.g., Post-LN with Softmax).
- Finite-sample effects can mask manifold curvature, leading to false-negative LLC reductions on theoretically curved data.
- The wrLLC protocol's assumption of per-layer additivity in deep networks is untested and conjectural.
- Theory is restricted to realizable settings with smooth, non-degenerate data manifolds and quadratic losses; extensions to other losses or realizability gaps are unknown.

## Confidence
- **High**: Single-layer Δλ formula (Δλ = m(d-d_s)/2) under squared loss, realizable; LayerNorm/RMSNorm outcomes (m/2 drop vs. 0); Softmax+bias "smuggled bias" Δλ = m/2.
- **Medium**: Finite-sample smooth crossover phenomenon (empirically observed but asymptotic theory is binary).
- **Low**: Extensibility to deep networks (additivity under wrLLC) and the Post-LayerNorm case (estimator issues).

## Next Checks
1. **Stability sweep**: Vary output dimension m (2–12) with fixed d=5 and LayerNorm; repeat 10 seeds to map estimator variance and find a reliable m range. Tune SGLD hyperparameters if needed.
2. **Curvature threshold robustness**: On a flat hyperplane, add a Gaussian bump; vary amplitude α and width σ. Measure Δλ to confirm the binary threshold sharpens with sample size N and lower MCMC temperature.
3. **Two-layer additivity test**: Stack two linear layers with a normalization between them. Apply wrLLC separately to each layer and compare the sum of measured Δλ to the total reduction from a fully connected baseline.
