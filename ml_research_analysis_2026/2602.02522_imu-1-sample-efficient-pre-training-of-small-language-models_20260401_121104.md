# IMU-1: Sample-Efficient Pre-training of Small Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02522
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02522
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** The 430M-parameter IMU-1 model achieves an average DCLM benchmark score of 0.574 (with EMA) after training on only 72B tokens, approaching the performance of SmolLM2-360M (0.586 on 4T tokens) and surpassing SmolLM-360M (600B tokens) with 8× fewer tokens.

## Executive Summary
IMU-1 presents a reproducible training recipe for small language models that dramatically improves sample efficiency. By combining architectural interventions (QK-norm attention, per-head gating, value residuals, LayerNorm scaling), an advanced optimizer (NorMuon with cautious weight decay), and a flexible Warmup-Stable-Decay schedule with post-hoc checkpoint averaging, the 430M-parameter model reaches competitive benchmark performance using only 72B tokens. This represents a significant reduction in required training data compared to prior small models, while completing training in under 37 hours on a single 8×H200 node.

## Method Summary
The recipe integrates multiple components: architectural modifications to stabilize training and improve gradient flow, optimization with NorMuon (orthogonalizing weight gradients) and cautious weight decay, and a learning rate schedule that postpones decay until later stages to enable mid-training data adjustments. Training proceeds in three stages with progressively stricter data quality filters, using a mixture of educational and synthetic corpora. The tokenizer is fixed (SmolLM2-360M, vocab 49,152). Hyperparameters are specified (e.g., learning rates: 0.0235 for 2D parameters, 0.007 for 1D; gradient accumulation 2; Z-loss λ=1e-4). Checkpoints from the decay phase are averaged post-hoc (β=0.8 over last 10) to produce the final model.

## Key Results
- IMU-1 (430M) trained on 72B tokens achieves an average DCLM benchmark score of **0.574** (with EMA), compared to SmolLM2-360M's 0.586 on 4T tokens (56× more data) and SmolLM-360M's score on 600B tokens (8× more tokens).
- Ablations on a 70M proxy model show a **5.21% relative reduction in training loss** from the combined interventions.
- Training completes in **under 37 hours** on a single 8×H200 node.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** QK-norm attention prevents training instability caused by unbounded query/key norm growth, which otherwise leads to saturated softmax distributions and high activation kurtosis.
**Mechanism:** RMS normalization applied to queries and keys before dot-product attention rescales their norms, bounding the magnitude of attention logits regardless of parameter growth. This maintains a stable gradient signal through the softmax.
**Core assumption:** Unbounded Q/K norms are a primary driver of attention saturation and training instability in deep transformers.
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract lists "QK-norm attention for stability" as a key intervention; Section 3.2.1 derives the normalization formula and links it to preventing saturation; corpus evidence is tangential but neighboring papers discuss attention stability.
**Break condition:** If monitoring shows activation kurtosis remains low without QK-norm (e.g., with strong gradient clipping or alternative stabilizers), the marginal benefit may diminish.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** NorMuon optimizer with Cautious Weight Decay (CWD) improves gradient quality and regularization efficiency over AdamW, leading to faster convergence.
**Mechanism:** NorMuon orthogonalizes weight matrix gradients via Newton-Schulz iterations, placing updates on the Stiefel manifold to preserve rotational invariance. CWD then applies L2 regularization only when it aligns with the orthogonalized update direction, avoiding destructive interference.
**Core assumption:** AdamW's element-wise adaptive learning rates are suboptimal for weight matrices in transformers, and standard weight decay often conflicts with gradient updates.
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract combines these as optimization advances; Section 3.3.1-3.3.2 details NorMuon and CWD; Section 7.2 ablation shows NorMuon yields -2.88% relative loss improvement over AdamW, and CWD adds -0.97%.
**Break condition:** For very shallow models or extremely low parameter counts, orthogonalization overhead may outweigh benefits; if gradient directions are highly aligned with weight norms later in training, CWD's selective application becomes less impactful.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) schedule with post-hoc EMA enables flexible, multi-stage training and stabilizes final model quality, while matching or exceeding cosine decay performance.
**Mechanism:** WSD maintains a constant "stable" learning rate after warmup, postponing decay until a configurable cutoff. This allows mid-training evaluation and continuation with new data without restarting. Post-hoc EMA over checkpoints from the decay phase smooths weight trajectory, improving generalization.
**Core assumption:** Fixed cosine decay schedules are too rigid for iterative data curation; intermediate checkpoints contain useful information that EMA can harness.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract lists WSD with post-hoc EMA; Section 3.4 explains WSD's flexibility; Section 7.3 shows WSD with 20% decay matches cosine loss exactly; Section 6.1 shows EMA added +0.014 to average benchmark score.
**Break condition:** If total compute budget is fixed and no mid-training data adjustments are planned, WSD's flexibility provides no advantage and may risk under-decay if stable phase is too long.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: RMS Normalization (RMSNorm)**  
  *Why needed here:* QK-norm uses RMSNorm to scale queries/keys; understanding its per-token normalization (vs. LayerNorm's per-channel) is key to grasping why it bounds logit magnitudes without introducing bias.  
  *Quick check question:* How does RMSNorm differ from LayerNorm in terms of computed statistics, and why is bias-less normalization suitable for attention logits?

- **Concept: Orthogonal Gradient Updates (Muon/NorMuon)**  
  *Why needed here:* NorMuon's core innovation is orthogonalizing gradients before applying updates; this requires understanding why orthonormal updates might be beneficial for weight matrices in linear layers.  
  *Quick check question:* What mathematical property of weight matrices does orthogonalization aim to preserve, and why might this accelerate training compared to AdamW's per-parameter scaling?

- **Concept: Learning Rate Schedules with Decay Phases**  
  *Why needed here:* WSD's "stable then decay" pattern contrasts with cosine decay; understanding the role of decay in final convergence and how EMA interacts with it is essential for scheduling decisions.  
  *Quick check question:* Why might a prolonged stable learning rate (no decay) be beneficial in the middle of training, and what risk does post-hoc EMA mitigate during the decay phase?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Data mixture → QK-norm attention → Per-head gating → Value residuals & LayerNorm scaling → NorMuon/CWD → Warmup-Stable-Decay → Post-hoc EMA

### Critical path
1. Implement QK-norm first; it's foundational for stable activation distributions.
2. Switch optimizer to NorMuon (with correct hyperparams for 2D vs 1D params) to leverage higher LR tolerance.
3. Add CWD on top of NorMuon; monitor gradient-weight alignment.
4. Introduce value residuals and LayerNorm scaling; watch for interactions with depth.
5. Implement WSD schedule; validate decay fraction (start with 20% based on ablations).
6. Apply EMA only to final checkpoints from decay phase (β=0.8 over last 10).
**Order matters:** Adding NorMuon before architectural tweaks may change optimal learning rates for latter.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Compute vs. stability:** QK-norm and NorMuon add ~3% and ~10% overhead respectively but enable higher LRs and faster convergence.
- **Complexity vs. flexibility:** WSD requires more careful stage planning but allows mid-training data adjustments; cosine is simpler but rigid.
- **Depth scaling:** LayerNorm scaling becomes more impactful at >20 layers; may be unnecessary for shallow models.
- **EMA timing:** Post-hoc EMA is cheap but requires storing multiple checkpoints; online EMA during training complicates optimization dynamics.

### Failure signatures
- **QK-norm missing:** Monitoring layer-wise kurtosis >200 indicates potential saturation; training loss may plateau or spike.
- **NorMuon misconfiguration:** Using AdamW LR (e.g., 0.001) with NorMuon causes slow convergence; using too high LR (>0.03) may cause divergence even with orthogonalization.
- **CWD over-regularization:** If >50% of weight updates are zeroed out (track `sign(u)==sign(w)` ratio), model capacity may be bottlenecked.
- **WSD decay too short:** Stable phase >90% of training without decay leads to underfitting (loss > cosine baseline at same token count).
- **EMA over-smoothing:** β > 0.9 or too many checkpoints averaged can erase useful individual checkpoint features, hurting benchmark scores.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Proxy validation:** Train 70M model with/without QK-norm for 1B tokens. Measure activation kurtosis (target mean <150) and compare loss curve slope.
2. **Optimizer sweep:** On the same 70M model, compare AdamW (LR 0.002), NorMuon (LR 0.0235 for 2D, 0.007 for 1D), and NorMuon+CWD. Track effective update magnitude (norm of `NeuronNorm(Ortho(G))`).
3. **Schedule test:** Run short WSD runs (10k steps) varying decay onset (70%, 80%, 90% of stable phase). Validate that 80% onset matches cosine loss at step 10k, while earlier decay harms stability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Do the architectural and optimization interventions provide consistent, additive benefits when scaling to larger model sizes or with different data distributions?**  
   *Basis:* The limitations state they have not validated at larger scales or with different data compositions.  
   *Why unresolved:* Only validated on 430M model with specific data mixture.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Systematic scaling study training larger models with identical intervention stack and varied data, measuring sample efficiency against baselines.

2. **What is the precise mechanistic reason for the synergistic interaction between interventions where combined effect exceeds sum of individual parts?**  
   *Basis:* Ablations show all interventions combined yield -1.64% improvement, exceeding sum of individual effects. Paper hypothesizes but provides no analysis.  
   *Why unresolved:* No targeted experiments (activation statistics, gradient flow, representation probing) to isolate why components interact positively.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Analytical experiments measuring gradient norm variance, attention entropy, or CKA across layers for models with single vs. combined interventions; ablations disabling components mid-training.

3. **What is the theoretically optimal or robust "decay fraction" for WSD across different scales, optimizers, and data curricula?**  
   *Basis:* Ablations on 70M show 20% decay fraction matches cosine, but 10% and 30% underperform. Paper adopts 20% based on this single proxy.  
   *Why unresolved:* Optimal decay fraction likely depends on interactions with optimizer and data curriculum; 20% finding may not generalize.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Hyperparameter sweep over decay fractions (5%-50%) across multiple scales and optimizers, measuring loss and benchmark performance.

4. **How does the specific three-stage data mixture interact with the intervention stack? Could a simpler single-stage mixture achieve comparable sample efficiency?**  
   *Basis:* Training uses complex three-stage mixture; ablations isolate interventions on single-stage corpus, not the curriculum itself.  
   *Why unresolved:* Gains from staged data might be orthogonal or multiplicative with architectural/optimization gains; the 5.21% improvement does not account for potential synergy with data curriculum.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Ablation training with interventions but using single-stage high-quality mixture vs. three-stage schedule, controlling for total tokens; testing if interventions reduce need for multi-stage curriculum.

5. **What is the computational overhead and practical scalability limit of NorMuon's orthogonalization step as context lengths and model dimensions increase?**  
   *Basis:* Paper notes NorMuon adds ~3% overhead on 430M model with context up to 1,152. Overhead reported as constant but orthogonalization complexity scales cubically.  
   *Why unresolved:* Overhead on large 2D weight matrices (especially later layers with GQA) may become non-linear and prohibitive; critical for claimed efficiency gains.  
   *What evidence would resolve:* Profiling NorMuon's runtime and FLOPs relative to AdamW across range of scales and context lengths, identifying break-even point.

## Limitations
- Exact data sampling probabilities within each training stage and µP parametrization hyperparameters are unspecified, blocking exact replication.
- Generalization beyond the DCLM benchmark suite is unproven; the paper does not compare with alternative data composition strategies.
- The synergistic interactions between interventions are documented but not mechanistically understood; benefits at larger scales or with different data remain untested.

## Confidence
- **Architectural interventions (QK-norm, per-head gating, value residuals, LayerNorm scaling):** High (5.21% relative loss reduction in 70M proxy ablations).
- **NorMuon + CWD optimization:** Medium (incremental gains shown, but no independent validation; overhead/compute trade-offs not fully characterized).
- **Data efficiency claim (vs. SmolLM2/SmolLM):** Medium (benchmark-specific; token counts and evaluation protocols differ across papers).
- **WSD schedule flexibility & EMA:** Medium (matches cosine decay in loss but flexibility advantage only demonstrated for multi-stage training).
