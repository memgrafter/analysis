# Attack Assessment and Augmented Identity Recognition for Human Skeleton Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24232
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24232
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** Attack-AAIRS improves model robustness by up to 24.0% against Gaussian Noise while maintaining or improving real test accuracy.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the vulnerability of person identification models trained on small, expensive LiDAR skeleton datasets to adversarial attacks. Building on the AAIRS framework that uses GAN-based synthetic data to boost accuracy, the proposed Attack-AAIRS method extends it by training an AT-GAN to generate unconstrained synthetic adversarial examples that specifically exploit weaknesses in the HCN-ID model. These synthetic attacks are used to augment the training set, inoculating the model against unseen attacks. Through ten-fold cross-validation, Attack-AAIRS demonstrates significant robustness improvements across five different attacks (FGSM, PGD, GN, MI-FGSM, BIM) while preserving or even enhancing performance on clean data.

## Method Summary
Attack-AAIRS proceeds in three stages: first, a baseline HCN-ID model is trained on the available real LiDAR skeleton data. Second, an Adversarial Training GAN (AT-GAN) is trained to generate synthetic adversarial skeletons that successfully fool the current HCN-ID model while maintaining high quality, as measured by an HCN-ID Score. Third, these synthetic adversarial examples are combined with the original clean data to retrain (inoculate) the HCN-ID model, yielding a robust identifier. The approach is evaluated using ten-fold cross-validation on a single LiDAR-derived dataset, comparing robustness against five standard attacks and measuring quality of generated samples.

## Key Results
- Robustness (1 - attack success rate) increased by up to 24.0% (against Gaussian Noise) for HCN-ID Model 1 (real-data only).
- Inoculated models achieve equal or higher real test accuracy than non-inoculated baselines, e.g., 72.74% for HCN-ID Model 2 with AAIRS+Attack-AAIRS.
- Generated adversarial samples maintain high quality: HCN-ID Score of 4.104±0.799, close to benign synthetic samples (4.348±0.880).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The AT-GAN learns to produce adversarial skeletons that lie near the decision boundary of the target HCN-ID model. By exposing the model to these boundary examples during retraining, it becomes more resilient to perturbations that would otherwise cause misclassification. The quality constraint (via discriminator) ensures that adversarial skeletons remain within the manifold of plausible human poses, avoiding trivial or unnatural samples that could harm clean accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Real LiDAR skeleton data -> Baseline HCN-ID model -> AT-GAN (Generator + Discriminator) -> Synthetic adversarial skeletons -> Augmented training set (real + synthetic adversarial) -> Robust HCN-ID model
- **Critical path**: 1) Train baseline HCN-ID; 2) Train AT-GAN to attack HCN-ID; 3) Generate adversarial samples; 4) Retrain HCN-ID on mixed data.
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Synthetic quality vs. attack strength: Higher attack success may produce unrealistic samples; 
  - Computational cost: AT-GAN adds training overhead; 
  - Clean accuracy retention: Inoculation must not degrade performance on benign inputs.
- **Failure signatures**: 
  - Low HCN-ID Score for adversarial samples (indicating poor quality) leading to limited robustness gains; 
  - Drop in clean accuracy after inoculation; 
  - Overfitting to synthetic attacks (low transfer to unseen attack types).
- **First experiments**:
  1. Measure baseline HCN-ID accuracy and robustness under the five attacks.
  2. Train AT-GAN and verify generated adversarial samples achieve high attack success rate while maintaining HCN-ID Score ≥ benign samples.
  3. Inoculate HCN-ID and compare robustness and clean accuracy with baseline.

## Limitations
- Synthetic attack transferability to unseen skeleton acquisition methods (e.g., depth vs. inertial) or non-HCN architectures remains untested.
- Real-world attack feasibility (physical perturbations, cross-dataset attacks) is not evaluated; experiments are limited to digital attacks on a single dataset.
- Long-term robustness under adaptive attacks (where attackers know the defense) is unknown.

## Confidence
- Robustness gains on evaluated attacks: **High**
- Quality of synthetic adversarial samples: **Medium**
- Generalization to new domains/models: **Low**

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-dataset replication**: Re-train and inoculate models on at least two additional skeleton datasets (e.g., NTU RGB+D 60/120, Kinetics-Skeleton) to measure robustness transfer.
2. **Transfer attack assessment**: Generate attacks from Attack-AAIRS and test success rate against non-HCN person-ID models (e.g., ST-GCN, PoseTransformer) without retraining.
3. **Physical plausibility audit**: Conduct a human study or pose-metric analysis (e.g., joint angles, temporal smoothness) to verify whether adversarial skeletons remain within natural motion distributions.
