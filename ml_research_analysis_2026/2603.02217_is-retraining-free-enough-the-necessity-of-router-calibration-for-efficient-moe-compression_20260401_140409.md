# Is Retraining-Free Enough? The Necessity of Router Calibration for Efficient MoE Compression

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02217
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02217
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Router Knowledge Distillation (Router KD) recovers performance across pruning, editing, and merging for fine-grained MoEs by updating only the router, while gains are limited for coarse-grained MoEs.

## Executive Summary
Retraining-free compression of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models—via pruning, editing, or merging—is an attractive way to reduce parameter count without costly retraining. However, such compression consistently degrades model performance. This paper identifies the root cause as router–expert mismatch: the router’s parameters are calibrated to the original expert set, and when experts are altered, the router’s routing decisions become suboptimal even if the set of selected experts remains similar. To address this, the authors propose Router Knowledge Distillation (Router KD), which updates only the router parameters by distilling the original model’s next-token distribution on unlabeled data, leaving the compressed experts frozen.

Experiments on the fine-grained Qwen3 (128 experts, top-8) and coarse-grained Mixtral (8 experts, top-2) show that Router KD consistently recovers performance across all compression paradigms on Qwen3—for example, GSM8k accuracy improves from 62.09% to 66.49% after CFES pruning. In contrast, gains on Mixtral are marginal due to its simpler routing space, where the teacher’s distribution is highly peaked. The results demonstrate that router calibration is essential for effective retraining-free MoE compression, particularly for fine-grained architectures, and suggest that the router–expert coupling is a fundamental consideration in MoE efficiency.

## Method Summary
Router Knowledge Distillation (Router KD) is a lightweight calibration procedure applied after retraining-free compression. Given a trained MoE model (the teacher) and a compressed student model obtained by pruning, editing, or merging experts (with student experts frozen), Router KD optimizes only the student’s router parameters to minimize the KL divergence between the student’s output logits and the teacher’s logits on unlabeled data. The student router thus learns to route tokens appropriately over the fixed, compressed expert set so that the overall next-token distribution matches the original model. The method requires only a small calibration set (e.g., 3000 C4 sentences), a single training epoch, and updates a tiny fraction of parameters (~0.04% in Qwen3), making it computationally feasible. Hyperparameters include batch size 2, gradient accumulation 4, learning rate 5e-5, and Adam optimizer. After calibration, the student model is evaluated without further retraining.

## Key Results
- Router KD restores performance across pruning, editing, and merging on the fine-grained Qwen3 model; for instance, CFES pruning’s GSM8k accuracy increases from 62.09% to 66.49% after calibration.
- On the coarse-grained Mixtral, Router KD yields only marginal improvements, highlighting its dependence on routing space complexity.
- Since only the router (≈0.04% of parameters) is updated, Router KD offers an extreme parameter-efficient solution for MoE compression.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mismatch from Router-Expert Coupling
Expert compression (pruning, editing, merging) changes the set of experts and alters the distribution of gate logits, which in turn shifts the normalized routing weights. Even if the same expert indices survive, the renormalization ensures that the router’s output differs from the original (˜g_orig ≠ ˜g_pruned). This router–expert mismatch causes persistent performance degradation because the router’s parameters are tightly coupled to the original expert population. (Evidence: Sec. 3.2.1; related work REAP, EAC-MoE.)

### Router KD Recalibrates the Router
Router KD freezes the compressed experts and trains only the router to minimize KL divergence between student and teacher next-token logits. This indirect objective shapes the router’s gate values to produce a routing policy that best approximates the original model’s behavior given the new expert landscape. The router thus compensates for both expert approximation errors and the initial misalignment. (Evidence: abstract, Sec. 4, Sec. 5.4.)

### Granularity-Dependent Efficacy
The benefit of Router KD strongly depends on MoE granularity. Fine-grained MoEs (e.g., Qwen3: E=128, k=8) have an enormous combinatorial routing space (~1.43×10¹² combinations) and a less peaked teacher distribution, providing rich, graded signals that allow the router to adjust. Coarse-grained MoEs (e.g., Mixtral: E=8, k=2) have a tiny routing space (28 combinations) and a highly concentrated teacher distribution, so gradients vanish once top-1/2 experts match, leaving little signal for improvement. (Evidence: Sec. 5.4, Fig. 3.)

  *Why needed here:* The paper focuses on reducing parameter count (pruning, editing, merging) rather than quantization. This defines the scope of the router-expert mismatch problem.

- **Knowledge Distillation (KD) for Routing**  
  *Why needed here:* Router KD is the core solution: it distills the original model’s next-token distribution to guide the router, not the router outputs directly.  
  *Quick check:* In Router KD, which parameters are updated? If you only updated the experts and left the router frozen, would you recover performance? Why or why not?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Original MoE Model (Experts + Router) → Compression Module (applies pruning, editing, or merging) → Compressed Model (frozen experts) → Router Calibration Module (Router KD) → Updated Compressed Model (trained router, frozen experts).

**Critical path:**  
1. Start with a trained MoE model.  
2. Apply a compression operation to obtain a student model; the router remains uncalibrated.  
3. Performance on benchmarks drops significantly.  
4. Run Router KD: freeze student experts, optimize only the router on unlabeled data (C4) by minimizing KL divergence to the teacher’s logits.  
5. Re-evaluate; performance recovers (especially for fine-grained MoEs).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Compression ratio vs. recoverable performance: higher compression worsens mismatch; Router KD mitigates but cannot fully recover beyond a threshold.  
- Router-only update vs. full fine-tuning: Router KD is extremely parameter-efficient but may fail for catastrophic expert degradation, where full expert tuning might help.  
- Calibration data choice: Domain mismatch between calibration data (C4) and target tasks may limit Router KD effectiveness.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Catastrophic collapse: compression reduces scores to near-zero; Router KD yields no improvement.  
- Coarse-grained MoE with highly peaked teacher distribution: gradients vanish after matching top-1/2 experts, providing little routing signal.  
- Accidental improvement: compression unexpectedly boosts a benchmark; then distilling from the original (weaker) teacher may degrade performance.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Apply a standard pruning method (e.g., CFES) to Qwen3 with 62.5% expert retention. Benchmark without Router KD; expect a significant drop from the original model.  
2. Take the same pruned model, apply Router KD (1 epoch on C4). Benchmark; expect substantial recovery, especially on reasoning tasks (GSM8k, MATH).  
3. Repeat steps 1–2 on Mixtral. Expect much smaller gains from Router KD, confirming the fine-grained vs. coarse-grained hypothesis. Compare absolute GSM8k improvements between Qwen3 and Mixtral.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What causes catastrophic collapse in compressed MoE models, and can router calibration alone recover from it?**  
   The paper notes Router KD fails when scores drop to near-zero but does not investigate the root causes or potential remedies.

2. **How can router calibration be enhanced for coarse-grained MoE architectures?**  
   Given the limited gains on Mixtral, the paper calls for new router objectives tailored to small expert counts (e.g., subset matching, entropy regularization) to close the performance gap.

3. **How does the choice of calibration data (domain, size, diversity) affect Router KD efficacy?**  
   The experiments use C4 exclusively, leaving the impact of calibration data characteristics unexplored.

4. **Can combining router calibration with lightweight expert updates yield further gains?**  
   The paper advocates avoiding expert updates, but the potential synergy of minimal expert tuning (e.g., bias updates) with Router KD remains unknown, especially for challenging scenarios.

## Limitations
- The study evaluates only two MoE models (Qwen3, Mixtral); generalization to other architectures is unclear.
- Reliance on C4 for calibration may not transfer to tasks with different domains (e.g., code, math).
- The procedure for identifying router parameters is model-specific and not detailed in the paper, hindering reproduction.

## Confidence
- **Router–expert mismatch causes degradation:** High confidence (consistent recovery across compression types).
- **Router KD efficacy for fine-grained MoEs:** High confidence (empirical results on Qwen3).
- **Router KD efficacy for coarse-grained MoEs:** Medium confidence (limited gains on Mixtral; may vary with other coarse models).
- **Granularity dependence:** High confidence (supported by combinatorial reasoning and experiments).
