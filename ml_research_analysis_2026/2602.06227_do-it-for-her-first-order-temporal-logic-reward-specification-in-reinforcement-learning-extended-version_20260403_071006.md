# Do It for HER: First-Order Temporal Logic Reward Specification in Reinforcement Learning (Extended Version)

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06227
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06227
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** CRM-HER achieves higher success rates and lower cumulative regret than CRM, HER, and plain DDPG baselines in continuous control tasks, particularly excelling in more complex tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the expressiveness limitations of logic-based reward specifications in reinforcement learning, which traditionally rely on Boolean predicates and manual labeling functions. It introduces LTLfMT, an extension of Linear Temporal Logic over finite traces where atomic propositions are replaced by first-order formulas over arbitrary decidable theories. These formulas are evaluated directly via a Satisfiability Modulo Theories (SMT) solver, eliminating the need for hand-crafted encoders. To mitigate reward sparsity inherent in such tasks, the authors combine Counterfactual Experiences for Reward Machines (CRM) with Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) into a CRM-HER approach. Experiments in continuous control environments (Parking and Reacher) demonstrate that CRM-HER consistently outperforms baseline methods, achieving superior success rates and lower regret, especially on more challenging tasks.

## Method Summary
The method centers on LTLfMT, which augments LTLf with first-order predicates over a chosen background theory (e.g., non-linear real arithmetic). At each timestep, an SMT solver evaluates these predicates on the environment state, effectively serving as a universal labeling function. The LTLfMT formula is syntactically propositionalized—replacing each first-order atom with a fresh propositional letter—yielding a standard LTLf formula that is converted to a deterministic finite automaton (DFA). A product Markov decision process (MDP) is constructed by combining the base environment state with the DFA state; the reward is 1 when the DFA state is accepting, else 0. Training uses deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) on this product MDP. To address sparse rewards, CRM generates counterfactual transitions by swapping DFA states, while HER relabels goals using the final state of each trajectory. The integrated CRM-HER method adds up to 2|Q| synthetic experiences per real transition, where |Q| is the number of DFA states.

## Key Results
- CRM-HER consistently achieves higher success rates than CRM, HER, and plain DDPG across all tested tasks.
- It yields significantly lower cumulative regret compared to all baselines.
- Performance advantages are most pronounced in more complex tasks (e.g., parking_2), where other methods fail to learn successful policies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LTLfMT replaces manual Boolean labeling functions with a universal Satisfiability Modulo Theories (SMT) solver to evaluate first-order predicates directly on environment state.
- **Mechanism:** Atomic propositions in LTLf are replaced by first-order formulas over a decidable background theory. At each timestep, the current state is fed to an SMT solver, which decides the truth value of each first-order atom. These truth values are mapped to propositional letters, enabling standard LTLf automata construction.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "predicates are first-order formulas over arbitrary theories... evaluated via a universal Satisfiability Modulo Theories (SMT) solver, without the need to explicitly encode it as a propositional predicate."
  - Section 4.1: Formal definition of Σ-terms and T-state; evaluation of formulas via (σ, ξ, i)⊨φ.
  - Section 4.2: Propositionalization via substitution of atoms/λ with propositional letters, resolved by a decision procedure for the theory.
  - Corpus: Related work on decidable fragments of LTLf modulo theories supports the need for the restricted fragment.
- **Break condition:** Using an undecidable theory (e.g., full arithmetic with quantifiers) or lookahead operators ⃝/∼⃝ on Σ-variables may cause non-termination. The user must select a decidable quantifier-free fragment or a theory with quantifier elimination.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A syntactic propositionalization process translates a lookahead-free LTLfMT formula and its traces into a standard LTLf formula and trace, enabling reuse of existing LTLf-to-DFA tools.
- **Mechanism:** Each first-order atom and each quantified formula λ is replaced by a fresh propositional letter. The LTLfMT trace (sequence of T-states) is converted to an LTLf trace by querying the SMT solver at each timestep to assign truth values to these letters. The resulting LTLf formula φ′ is then translated into a DFA using standard algorithms (e.g., LTLf2DFA).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 4.3: Detailed substitution procedure for atoms and λ.
  - Section 4.3: "The SMT solver plays the role of an extremely general labeling function."
  - Section 5: Example illustrating the user only writes a logical formula, no manual encoder.
  - Corpus: Neighbor "FORM: Learning Expressive and Transferable First-Order Logic Reward Machines" also moves beyond propositional atoms.
- **Break condition:** DFA size may explode if the original formula is complex, as the number of propositional letters equals distinct first-order subformulas. Slow SMT queries per timestep can bottleneck training.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Combining Counterfactual Experiences for Reward Machines (CRM) with Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) mitigates reward sparsity inherent to logic-specified tasks in continuous control.
- **Mechanism:** CRM enriches the replay buffer by, for each real transition ⟨sₜ, qₜ⟩→⟨sₜ₊₁, qₜ₊₁⟩, generating |Q| counterfactual transitions where qₜ is replaced by every other q∈Q and the reward recomputed. HER further relabels achieved goals in all trajectories (real and counterfactual) by using the final state’s ⟨s_T, q_T⟩ (where q_T is predecessor of an accepting DFA state) as a new goal. The combined CRM-HER generates up to 2|Q| artificial experiences per real transition.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "To mitigate reward sparsity, they combine Counterfactual Experiences for Reward Machines (CRM) with Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) into a CRM-HER approach."
  - Section 5.1: Detailed description of adapting HER to the product MDP state and combining with CRM.
  - Section 6: Experimental results showing CRM-HER "consistently performs well across all tasks… especially in the more challenging tasks."
  - Corpus: Neighbors like "Temporal Logic Specification-Conditioned Decision Transformer" and "Shielded Deep Reinforcement Learning" also address sparsity via logic.
- **Break condition:** HER’s effectiveness depends on the goal space being structured such that relabeling with past states yields useful signals. If the task involves complex, non-state-based goals or the DFA has many states, synthetic experience generation may introduce noise or overhead.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Linear Temporal Logic over finite traces (LTLf) semantics**
  - *Why needed here:* LTLf forms the temporal layer of LTLfMT; understanding operators like until (U), next (X), eventually (F) is essential to grasp how formulas induce automata and non-Markovian rewards.
  - *Quick check:* Given trace σ = [{p}, {p,q}, {q}], what is truth value of `F p ∧ F q` at time 0? What about `p U q` at time 0?
- **Concept: Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and product construction**
  - *Why needed here:* The logic-defined non-Markovian reward is transformed into a Markovian reward on a product MDP where states are augmented with automaton states. This reduction allows applying standard RL algorithms.
  - *Quick check:* If an LTLf automaton has 5 states and base MDP has 1000 states, how many states in the product MDP? What is the reward function R' on the product MDP?
- **Concept: Experience Replay and Hindsight Relabeling**
  - *Why needed here:* HER is a core component of CRM-HER for sparse rewards. Understanding replay buffers and how hindsight goals are generated is key to implementation.
  - *Quick check:* In goal-conditioned RL, if an episode fails to reach goal g but ends in state s'ₜ, how does HER create a new transition using s'ₜ? What assumption makes this effective?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  User Input (LTLfMT formula φ) -> Propositionalization Engine (SMT solver calls) -> Automaton Constructor (LTLf2DFA) -> Product MDP Builder (state = (s, q)) -> RL Agent Core (DDPG) -> Replay Buffer Modifier (CRM-HER: counterfactuals + HER relabeling)
- **Critical path:** Formula → SMT labeling → LTLf trace → DFA → Product MDP state → RL step → (CRM → HER) → Buffer → Policy update. Most sensitive nodes: SMT solver calls per step and DFA size (|Q|).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Expressiveness vs. Decidability: Powerful theories increase expressiveness but risk undecidability; design restricts to lookahead-free and preferably quantifier-free fragments.
  - Automation vs. Performance: SMT solver removes manual encoder writing but introduces per-step computational cost; efficiency depends on theory complexity.
  - Sparsity Mitigation Scope: CRM-HER leverages DFA structure and terminal goal definition; works for tasks with clear terminal goals but may fail for ongoing/non-terminal specifications.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - SMT solver timeout/failure: Propositionalization step fails; evidenced by exceptions or long pauses per environment step.
  - DFA state explosion: φ′ leads to exponentially large Q; symptoms: memory errors or slow DFA transition lookups.
  - HER goal mismatch: Relabeling with final product state yields unreachable or meaningless goals; success rate plateaus at random.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Unit test propositionalization: On a simple MDP (e.g., 2D point mass), use tiny LTLfMT formula (e.g., `F (x>0 ∧ y>0)`). Verify SMT labeling correctness and LTLf trace matches expectation.
  2. Automaton sanity check: Generate DFA with LTLf2DFA. Manually check accepting condition corresponds to original formula intent. Simulate trace through DFA using propositionalization output.
  3. Baseline comparison on sparse task: Run plain DDPG, DDPG+CRM, DDPG+HER, DDPG+CRM-HER on minimal "go to A then B" task (e.g., parking task_1). Observe CRM-HER learns faster, confirming synergy.

## Limitations
- Decidability constraints (no lookahead over Σ-variables) limit formula expressiveness; practical impact unknown.
- Per-step SMT solver overhead unmeasured; could hinder real-time applications.
- DFA state explosion risk for complex formulas; scalability untested.
- CRM-HER assumes terminal goals; may not generalize to non-terminating LTLf tasks.

## Confidence
- C1 (LTLfMT with SMT as universal labeling): Medium. Theoretical soundness plausible, but efficiency unverified in practice.
- C2 (Syntactic propositionalization correctness): High. Formal reduction ensures correctness for the restricted lookahead-free fragment.
- C3 (CRM-HER synergy for sparse rewards): Medium. Positive results in limited tasks; broader validation needed across more domains.
- C4 (Superiority on complex tasks): Medium. Supported by two tasks (Parking, Reacher); lacks extensive benchmarking.
