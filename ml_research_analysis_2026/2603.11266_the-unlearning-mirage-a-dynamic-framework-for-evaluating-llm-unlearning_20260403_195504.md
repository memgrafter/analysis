# The Unlearning Mirage: A Dynamic Framework for Evaluating LLM Unlearning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11266
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11266
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A dynamic, model-specific probing framework reveals that current unlearning methods fail to eliminate residual knowledge accessible via multi-hop reasoning or aliasing, with multi-hop accuracy remaining as high as 33.9% post-unlearning.

## Executive Summary
Current evaluations of LLM unlearning rely on static benchmarks that fail to detect residual knowledge recovered through query variations like multi-hop reasoning or aliasing. This paper introduces a dynamic framework that automatically constructs a model-specific knowledge graph from the pre-unlearning model’s outputs, enabling the generation of structured probes (single-hop, multi-hop, alias-based) without manual curation. Results show the framework achieves high coverage of existing benchmarks (78% on RWKU, 66% on TOFU) and exposes critical failures: unlearning methods successfully suppress dominant single-hop pathways but leave alternative pathways for multi-hop queries intact, leading to significant residual recovery. The analysis also identifies aliasing as an additional vulnerability, increasing recovery by 2.4%. A new harmonic mean score balances forgetting and retention, identifying ULD as the most robust method.

## Method Summary
The framework consists of four stages: (1) **Elicitation**, where the pre-unlearning model is queried for atomic facts about seed entities; (2) **Graph Construction**, which parses facts into triplets (e1, r, e2) and expands them via breadth-first search with exponential decay to control graph size, while filtering irrelevant nodes and resolving aliases; (3) **Probe Generation**, which traverses the graph to create single-hop, multi-hop (up to 3 hops), and alias-based natural language queries; (4) **Evaluation**, where an unlearned model is scored on these probes using three metrics: Multi-hop Forgetting Score (average accuracy over 1-/2-/3-hop queries), Retention Score (average accuracy on retention and relationship retention queries), and their harmonic mean. The process is model-specific, automatic, and scalable, shifting evaluation from static test sets to outcome-level robustness.

## Key Results
- Framework covers 78% of RWKU and 66% of TOFU benchmarks, with Spearman’s ρ > 0.75 alignment to prior method rankings.
- Multi-hop residual accuracy remains high post-unlearning (e.g., 33.9% at 3-hop vs. 20.1% at 1-hop, averaged across methods).
- Activation analysis: unlearning disrupts dominant middle-layer pathways for single-hop queries but preserves bifurcated, late-layer pathways used for multi-hop resolution.
- Aliasing increases residual recovery by 2.4% beyond multi-hop queries.
- Unintended forgetting is highest for facts directly adjacent (1-hop away) to the unlearned entity.
- Harmonic mean score identifies ULD as most robust (80.2% on RWKU).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Model-specific knowledge graphs enable automatic, high-coverage probe generation that matches manually curated benchmarks.  
**Mechanism:** The framework queries the pre-unlearning model to extract atomic triplet facts (e1, r, e2), expands these via BFS with exponential decay to control graph size, and resolves aliases. Probes are generated by graph traversal, ensuring semantic alignment with the model’s internal knowledge structure.  
**Core assumption:** The model’s pre-unlearning outputs reliably capture its factual knowledge for the target entity.  
**Evidence anchors:** Coverage metrics (78% RWKU, 66% TOFU); BFS with decay (Eq 1–2) and alias resolution via LLM judge (section 4.1).  
**Break condition:** If model elicitation fails for low-salience or expert knowledge (e.g., WMDP threats), graph construction yields incomplete nodes, reducing benchmark replication.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Unlearning methods disrupt dominant single-hop activation pathways but leave alternative pathways for multi-hop queries intact, causing residual knowledge recovery.  
**Mechanism:** Activation analysis (PatchScopes) shows single-hop queries resolve in intermediate layers, while multi-hop queries bifurcate: first-hop resolved early, second-hop in deeper layers. Gradient-based unlearning primarily alters middle-layer pathways, disabling direct retrieval but preserving indirect chains.  
**Core assumption:** Unlearning interventions target the most salient (dominant) computational pathways associated with the forget set.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 5.3: single-hop uses dominant pathways disrupted by unlearning; multi-hop uses alternative pathways. Figure 3 shows two-stage resolution for 2-hop vs. concentrated middle-layer for 1-hop.  
**Break condition:** If unlearning uniformly affects all layers (e.g., layer-wise perturbation), multi-hop residual recovery might decrease.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Surface-form variations (aliasing) further expose unlearning brittleness, increasing residual recovery by 2.4%.  
**Mechanism:** Alias resolution merges entity variants (e.g., “Stephen King” ≡ “Stephen Edwin King”) in the knowledge graph. Probes using aliases test whether unlearning is surface-form agnostic. Models retain knowledge through alternate surface forms not explicitly suppressed.  
**Core assumption:** Unlearning methods fail to generalize across lexical variations of the target entity.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 5.2: aliasing increases residual recovery by 2.4%. Section 4.1: LLM judge merges alias nodes.  
**Break condition:** If unlearning incorporates synonym/alias adversarial training, aliasing no longer increases recovery.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** LLM Unlearning Objectives (Removal vs. Retention Trade-off)  
  **Why needed:** Framework scores directly measure forgetting vs. retention, explaining why methods like GA harm retention while ULD balances both.  
  **Quick check:** If a method scores low on multi-hop forgetting (high accuracy) but high on retention, is it effective? Why?

- **Concept:** Knowledge Graphs for LLM Knowledge Elicitation  
  **Why needed:** Core of framework is building an entity-specific graph via BFS. Understanding triplet extraction, expansion control, and relevance filtering is key to implementation.  
  **Quick check:** How does exponential decay factor α in Eq 1 prevent combinatorial explosion during BFS expansion?

- **Concept:** Multi-Hop Reasoning as an Adversarial Evaluation  
  **Why needed:** Multi-hop queries are the primary stress test; understanding why they recover residual knowledge (distributed pathways) explains the framework’s value.  
  **Quick check:** Why might a model correctly answer a 2-hop query about an unlearned entity even after failing the 1-hop query?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Elicitation Module -> Triplet Extractor -> Graph Builder -> Probe Generator -> Evaluation Engine

**Critical path:**  
Pre-unlearning model -> Elicitation -> Graph construction -> Probe generation -> Post-unlearning probing -> Scoring.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Graph depth (d_max) vs. cost: Deeper graphs increase coverage (Fig 4) but raise API calls (Eq 2). Paper uses d_max=3 for RWKU.  
- Decay factor (α) vs. completeness: Lower α (e.g., 0.6) expands more nodes but risks generic facts; paper uses α=0.8.  
- Static KG vs. dynamic re-elicitation: KG built once per (model, entity) but assumes model knowledge stable across unlearning methods; re-eliciting per method would capture method-specific shifts but multiplies cost.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Multi-hop accuracy remains >20% even for top methods (Table 2: ULD 3-hop=28.1%).  
- 1-hop retention drops sharply for facts adjacent to unlearned entity (Table 2: 1-fact away=61.7% vs. Rel. Ret.=76.5%).  
- Aliasing increases recovery by >2% (Table 3).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Coverage validation:** Compare generated probes against RWKU/TOFU; report % overlap (appendix A.6).  
2. **Multi-hop robustness benchmark:** For each method, compute Avg. Multi-hop (1/2/3-hop) and retention scores; table like Table 2.  
3. **Activation pathway analysis:** Use PatchScopes to decode entity resolution layers for single vs. two-hop queries pre/post-unlearning; replicate Figure 3 pattern.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question:** How can the "chicken-and-egg" paradox in evaluating unlearning for low-salience or expert-level knowledge be resolved? The framework's elicitation step requires the model to correctly output facts to build probes, but for domains like WMDP (cybersecurity, novel proteins), the model itself is unreliable at eliciting its own knowledge, preventing probe generation and thus evaluation.  
**Basis in paper:** Elicitation and Multi-hop queries create a paradoxical scenario where we demonstrate unlearning failures through effective elicitation of residual knowledge using Multi-hop queries, yet, elicitation itself is difficult for certain kinds of information.  
**Why unresolved:** The core mechanism of model-informed graph construction fails when the model's pre-unlearning knowledge is sparse, inconsistent, or incorrect for the target domain.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A study that systematically measures unlearning method efficacy on a low-salience benchmark without relying on model elicitation, perhaps by using external, high-confidence knowledge sources or developing alternative probe generation strategies that do not depend on the model's own outputs.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How should benchmarks and evaluation metrics be designed for unlearning targets where the forget set and the retain set have significant semantic or factual overlap (e.g., MUSE-Books, where the goal is to forget Harry Potter books but retain fan wiki content)?  
**Basis in paper:** Elicitation alone becomes insufficient, and external knowledge sources or additional manual intervention is often required for accurate disambiguation for information to be removed and retained.  
**Why unresolved:** The proposed framework assumes a clean boundary between what must be forgotten and what must be retained. Overlapping content makes it ambiguous which facts in the elicited graph belong to the forget set vs. retain set, confounding the measurement of both removal and unintended forgetting.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A detailed case study on an overlapping benchmark (like MUSE-Books) that defines precise, reproducible criteria for labeling graph nodes/edges as "forget" or "retain" and demonstrates that different labeling choices lead to significantly different conclusions about method effectiveness.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Can evaluation frameworks move beyond static graphs to incorporate dynamic, adaptive, or adversarial probing that actively seeks the model's remaining knowledge pathways rather than replaying a fixed set of queries?  
**Basis in paper:** The created knowledge graph is non-deterministic, and moreover, once a knowledge graph is created, it remains static for a given evaluation. Future work can explore evolving evaluation paradigms... for true adversarial testing.  
**Why unresolved:** A static graph probes only the knowledge structure present at the time of elicitation. It cannot adapt to find novel, unexpected, or emergent residual pathways the model might use after unlearning, limiting the stress test's thoroughness.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** A follow-up work that implements an iterative probing loop: (1) run initial unlearning evaluation, (2) analyze surprising successes/failures to infer new query templates or graph structures, (3) generate new probes based on this inference, (4) re-evaluate. This would demonstrate whether such adaptivity uncovers more severe failure modes.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** What architectural or algorithmic changes to unlearning methods are necessary to disrupt the alternate pathways that multi-hop queries exploit, given that single-hop queries rely on dominant intermediate-layer pathways while multi-hop queries use bifurcated, late-layer resolutions?  
**Basis in paper:** From activation analysis: single-hop queries predominantly resolve in intermediate layers; two-hop queries show bifurcated resolutions where the second-hop entity resolves distinctly later. Since current methods disrupt dominant pathways effectively, the inference is they are not designed to target the distributed pathways used for compositional reasoning.  
**Why unresolved:** The paper diagnoses the cause of multi-hop brittleness (pathway divergence) but does not propose or test specific modifications to unlearning objectives aimed at disrupting late-stage or distributed activation patterns.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study modifying common unlearning losses (e.g., GA, ULD) to include a term that explicitly penalizes or distorts activations in later transformer layers (e.g., using a layer-wise contrastive objective), and then measuring if multi-hop forgetting scores improve without disproportionate retention loss.

## Limitations
- Graph construction parameters (decay α, depth, node limits) are chosen empirically; optimal values for new models/entities are unclear.
- Exact prompt templates and LLM judge thresholds for triplet extraction, relevance filtering, and alias resolution are missing from the main text (appendix only).
- Unlearning hyperparameter details (e.g., retain set sampling for RWKU) are incompletely specified, risking inconsistent method comparisons.
