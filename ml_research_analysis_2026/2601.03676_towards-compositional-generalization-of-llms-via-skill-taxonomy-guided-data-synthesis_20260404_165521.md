# Towards Compositional Generalization of LLMs via Skill Taxonomy Guided Data Synthesis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03676
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03676
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: STEPS with 4K samples significantly outperforms baselines (e.g., Instruct-SkillMix, Alpaca52k) on instruction-following (WildBench, MT-Bench) and agent (SkillBench) benchmarks across multiple model architectures.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the data bottleneck in compositional generalization for LLMs, where models struggle with complex skill combinations due to scarcity of multi-skill training examples. It introduces STEPS, a framework that constructs a hierarchical skill taxonomy from co-occurrence data using structural entropy minimization and then synthesizes training data by selecting skill combinations that maximize marginal structural entropy within this taxonomy, guided by a recursive "sweet spot" constraint to maintain semantic coherence. Evaluations on WildBench, MT-Bench, and SkillBench show that with only 4,000 synthetic samples, STEPS substantially improves performance over strong baselines for models like Llama-3-8B, Qwen-2.5, and Mistral-7B. Key findings include the existence of a compositional performance threshold when introducing multi-skill examples, the effectiveness of a taxonomy-guided curriculum, and the necessity of the coherence constraint to avoid incoherent skill pairings.

## Method Summary
STEPS operates in two stages. First, it induces a hierarchical skill taxonomy from a seed corpus of instructions annotated with atomic skills. This is done by building a weighted skill co-occurrence graph and applying a bottom-up agglomerative merging process that minimizes structural entropy, thereby clustering strongly co-occurring skills into a compact hierarchy. Second, it synthesizes multi-skill training data by selecting skill combinations that maximize marginal structural entropy gain within the taxonomy. A greedy sequential procedure chooses skills one by one, but with a recursive subtree constraint: each new skill is first selected from the local subtree of the previously chosen skill, then from parent communities, ensuring semantic coherence while exploring diverse branches. The selected tuples are used to prompt a powerful LLM (e.g., GPT-4.1) to generate coherent instruction-response pairs.

## Key Results
- STEPS with 4K samples consistently outperforms baselines (Instruct-SkillMix, Alpaca52k) on instruction-following (WildBench, MT-Bench) and agent (SkillBench) benchmarks across Llama, Qwen, and Mistral model families. For example, Llama-3-8B Instruct achieves a WildBench score of 36.3 vs. 30.8 for the baseline.
- Introducing multi-skill examples yields a compositional performance threshold; mixing different k-tuple complexities (k=1 to 6) is more effective than using any single k-level alone.
- The "sweet spot" recursive constraint is critical: unconstrained entropy maximization harms instruct-tuned models due to semantic incoherence, while the constrained search ensures high-quality synthetic data. Performance on agent tasks decays with increasing skill depth, highlighting a limit on combinatorial complexity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1**
- **Claim:** Inducing a hierarchical skill taxonomy via structural entropy minimization uncovers latent skill dependencies, providing a structured representation of the combinatorial skill space.
- **Mechanism:** Construct a weighted skill co-occurrence graph from annotated data. Apply a bottom-up agglomerative merging process where communities are merged to minimize the overall structural entropy of the resulting tree. This favors groupings where strong co-occurring edges are contained within local clusters, yielding a compact hierarchy.
- **Core assumption:** Skill relationships exhibit a hierarchical community structure (dense intra-group, sparse inter-group connections) that can be captured by minimizing a measure of graph coding cost.
- **Evidence anchors:** Paper abstract and Section 3.1 formalize the objective using structural entropy. Neighboring works (e.g., Skills-in-Context) highlight the need to model skill relationships, but STEPS uses a hierarchical information-theoretic approach.
- **Break condition:** If the co-occurrence graph is extremely sparse or noisy, the entropy minimization may produce a degenerate taxonomy (e.g., near-linear chain) that fails to capture meaningful dependencies.

**Mechanism 2**
- **Claim:** Selecting skill combinations by maximizing conditional/marginal structural entropy within the induced taxonomy efficiently identifies compositionally informative and diverse k-tuples.
- **Mechanism:** Use a greedy sequential selection procedure. Starting from an initial skill, each next skill is chosen to maximize the conditional structural entropy, which quantifies the novel structural information it contributes relative to already selected ones. This prioritizes combinations that connect different, information-rich regions of the taxonomy.
- **Core assumption:** The marginal gain in structural entropy, computed relative to the taxonomy, is a principled proxy for the "learning utility" of a new skill combination.
- **Evidence anchors:** Paper abstract and Section 3.2 describe the greedy procedure. Compared to random pairing in baselines like Instruct-SkillMix, STEPS's explicit information-maximization within a structure yields better combinations.
- **Break condition:** If the taxonomy misrepresents true skill dependencies (due to annotation bias or limited data), the entropy scores will be misaligned, potentially selecting combinations that are structurally informative according to the taxonomy but semantically incoherent.

**Mechanism 3**
- **Claim:** A recursively constrained search ("sweet spot" constraint) balances diversity from entropy maximization with semantic coherence, avoiding incoherent compositions.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of global maximization over all skills, the selection of each subsequent skill is first confined to the local subtree of the previously selected skill. The search scope then expands upward to parent communities in a controlled, bottom-up manner. This ensures skills remain within a coherent hierarchical context while still spanning diverse branches.
- **Core assumption:** Pure entropy maximization can combine skills from semantically unrelated branches, creating unnatural instructions that harm learning. The "sweet spot" is found by exploring within expanding local neighborhoods of the hierarchy.
- **Evidence anchors:** Paper abstract mentions "preserving semantic coherence." Section 3.3 details the constraint. Section 4.5 ablation shows unconstrained selection underperforms compared to the recursive framework, confirming that unconstrained diversity introduces semantic noise.
- **Break condition:** If the taxonomy's granularity is too coarse, the constraint may be too permissive; if too fine, it may be overly restrictive, preventing sufficiently diverse combinations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Structural Entropy (as a graph coding measure)**
  - **Why needed:** It is the core objective function for both taxonomy induction (minimizing) and combination selection (maximizing marginal gain). It quantifies the "compressibility" of the skill co-occurrence graph under a hierarchical partition.
  - **Quick check:** Given a small co-occurrence graph, why would a community with many strong internal edges and few external edges have lower structural entropy than one with the opposite pattern?
- **Concept: Skill Co-occurrence Graph Construction**
  - **Why needed:** It grounds the abstract taxonomy induction in observable data patterns from real instructions.
  - **Quick check:** How would you construct the edge weight between two skill nodes from a dataset of instructions, each labeled with a set of skills? What are potential pitfalls (e.g., sparsity, noise)?
- **Concept: Conditional Structural Entropy & Chain Rule**
  - **Why needed:** It enables the greedy, sequential selection of a multi-skill combination by measuring the unique contribution of each new skill given the context of already selected ones.
  - **Quick check:** For a set of skills X, explain how the joint structural entropy HT(G;X) can be decomposed into a sum of conditional terms HT(G;x_i | X<i). What does each term represent?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Input Processor -> Graph Builder -> Taxonomy Inducer -> Combination Planner -> Data Synthesizer -> Synthetic Dataset
- **Critical path:** Annotated Corpus -> Graph Builder -> Taxonomy Inducer -> Combination Planner -> Data Synthesizer -> Synthetic Dataset. The taxonomy is the central artifact that informs the combination planning.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Taxonomy Depth vs. Granularity: Deeper trees offer finer control for the "sweet spot" search but risk overfitting to noisy co-occurrences. Shallower trees are more robust but may group distinct skills.
  - Entropy Objective vs. Synthesis Cost: Maximizing marginal entropy may select rare, hard-to-synthesize combinations that the LLM synthesizer cannot render coherently, wasting compute.
  - Corpus Dependency: Quality heavily depends on initial skill annotation quality and coverage; biased or incomplete seed sets yield flawed taxonomies.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Performance: No gain or drop on multi-skill benchmarks compared to random skill mix or original model.
  - Data Quality: Synthesized instructions are semantically incoherent, repetitive, or trivial (skills not genuinely combined).
  - Taxonomy: Induced taxonomy is nearly flat or has a "star" shape, indicating lack of hierarchical signal in co-occurrence data.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Taxonomy Validation:** Train two models with identical 8K synthetic data; one uses taxonomy-guided curriculum (simple to complex per tree depth), the other random mixture of tuples. Compare on complex multi-skill benchmark. Expectation: Guided curriculum outperforms.
  2. **Sweet Spot Ablation:** Ablate recursive subtree constraint. Generate two 4K datasets: one with unconstrained global entropy maximization, one with full STEPS constraint. Train identical models and compare on general (MT-Bench) and complex (WildBench) evaluations. Expectation: Full constraint wins, especially for instruct models.
  3. **Compositional Threshold Scaling:** Fix total data to 4K. Vary proportion of high-k tuples (k>=3). Evaluate on tasks requiring different composition depths. Expectation: Performance increases with some high-k data but may peak/drop if proportion too high, identifying optimal k-distribution curve.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What is the optimal proportion of different k-tuple complexities (k∈[1,6]) in the training dataset to maximize compositional generalization performance?**
   - Basis: The limitations section states they have not yet investigated the optimal ratio or distribution of k-tuples within the training set.
   - Why unresolved: The paper shows mixing k-tuples outperforms any single k, but the specific mixture ratio that maximizes scaling efficiency across model architectures is unknown.
   - What evidence would resolve: Systematic experiments varying the proportion of samples at each k-level while holding total data constant, measuring performance on WildBench and SkillBench across multiple model families.

2. **Does the hierarchical skill taxonomy induced by structural entropy minimization consistently capture true skill dependencies across diverse model architectures, or is its effectiveness model-specific?**
   - Basis: The taxonomy-guided curriculum learning experiment is only conducted on Llama-3-8B, while STEPS is also evaluated on Qwen and Mistral. The paper claims the taxonomy is a "functional roadmap," but its generalizability to other architectures is untested.
   - Why unresolved: Curriculum gains are demonstrated only for Llama; it is unclear if the same hierarchical structure benefits models with different pre-training or scaling properties.
   - What evidence would resolve: Replicating the curriculum learning experiment on Qwen-2.5-7B and Mistral-7B-v0.3, comparing WB-Score against standard SFT to verify if the taxonomy-guided sequence always yields superior learning.

3. **How sensitive is the "sweet spot" constraint (recursive subtree expansion) to model type (base vs. instruct) and scale? Could alternative constraint strategies yield better results for certain models?**
   - Basis: Figure 4 shows unconstrained entropy maximization harms instruct-tuned models but helps base models. The paper attributes this to semantic incoherence but does not explore adjusting constraint parameters.
   - Why unresolved: The fixed recursive expansion may not be optimal for all model types; instruct models might require tighter constraints, while larger models could tolerate looser ones.
   - What evidence would resolve: Ablating expansion depth/breadth in the recursive search for different model families (e.g., smaller vs. larger instruct models) and measuring compositional generalization on WildBench.

4. **Does the choice of LLM used for synthetic data generation (e.g., GPT-4.1 vs. open-source models) significantly impact the quality and downstream effectiveness of STEPS-synthesized data?**
   - Basis: The synthesis step uses GPT-4.1 without comparing to other synthesizers. The paper assumes GPT-4.1's reasoning ensures coherence, but this is not validated.
   - Why unresolved: If a cheaper or more accessible model were used, would the synthesized data still provide the same learning signal? This affects STEPS's practicality and cost.
   - What evidence would resolve: Generating identical skill combinations using different synthesizer models (e.g., GPT-4.1, Claude-3, Llama-3-70B), fine-tuning with each set, and comparing performance on WildBench and SkillBench.

## Limitations
- **Seed corpus and skill annotations are undisclosed:** Annotation quality directly impacts taxonomy validity; biased or sparse labels produce flawed structures.
- **Hyperparameters are unspecified:** Details for structural entropy minimization (merge thresholds, stopping) and recursive search expansion are missing, hindering exact replication.
- **Evaluation reliance on proprietary components:** Use of GPT-4.1 for synthesis and possibly non-public benchmarks raises questions about transferability to open-source LLMs or other domains.

## Confidence
- Taxonomy utility: Medium–High
- Entropy-guided selection + constraint: Medium
- Overall benchmark gains: Medium
