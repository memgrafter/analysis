# PSF-Med: Measuring and Explaining Paraphrase Sensitivity in Medical Vision Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.21428
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21428
- **Reference count**: 32
- **Primary result**: Medical VLMs exhibit 8–58% answer-flip rates on rephrased chest X-ray questions; a mechanistic intervention on a single feature (3818) in MedGemma 4B reduces flips by 31% relative with minimal accuracy cost.

## Executive Summary
Medical vision-language models (VLMs) often give contradictory answers when clinicians rephrase the same question, undermining reliability. This study constructs PSF-Med, a large benchmark of 19,748 chest X-ray questions with ~92,000 meaning-preserving paraphrases. Evaluating six medical VLMs reveals high flip rates (8–58%), indicating poor paraphrase robustness. Analysis via sparse autoencoders on MedGemma 4B identifies a single feature (3818 at layer 17) that activates for formal clinical phrasing and drives conservative answer shifts. Causal intervention (clamping) on this feature recovers decision margins and reverses many flips while reducing reliance on text-only priors. The work demonstrates that paraphrase consistency and visual grounding are separable properties and that targeted mechanistic interventions can improve VLM reliability.

## Method Summary
The authors first built PSF-Med, a dataset of chest X-ray yes/no questions from MIMIC-CXR, each paired with multiple meaning-preserving paraphrases generated via controlled transformations (e.g., lexical substitution, syntax changes). Six medical VLMs (MedGemma 4B/27B, LLaVA-Rad, RadFM, CheXagent, etc.) were evaluated on this benchmark, measuring flip rates when answering original vs. paraphrased questions. For mechanistic analysis, they applied sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to MedGemma 4B’s layer 17 to isolate interpretable features. Feature 3818, which strongly activates for formal clinical language, was identified via correlation with register and conservative answer shifts. Causal patching ( ablation) and clamping ( intervention at inference) quantified its causal influence on flip rates and text-prior reliance.

## Key Results
- Flip rates across six medical VLMs range from 8% to 58%, with no model below 10%.
- Feature 3818 in MedGemma 4B correlates with formal clinical phrasing and conservative answer shifts.
- Causal patching (removing Feature 3818) recovers 44.8% of decision margin on average and fully reverses 15% of flips.
- At inference, clamping Feature 3818 reduces flip rates by 31% relative (1.3 percentage-point accuracy cost) and decreases text-only agreement, indicating reduced language prior reliance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The inconsistency stems from models over-relying on textual registers (formal vs. casual phrasing) rather than visual features. Feature 3818 acts as a "register detector": it activates in response to formal clinical phrasing and pushes the model toward conservative ("yes") answers. This linguistic bias causes flips when a paraphrase shifts the register, even if the visual content is unchanged. Causal patching and clamping interventions suppress this feature’s contribution, forcing the model to rely more on visual signals and less on language priors. The success of clamping—improving consistency with minor accuracy loss—shows that a single feature can disproportionately drive non-robust behavior and that targeted mitigation is possible.

## Foundational Learning
* **Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)**: Decompose model activations into interpretable, linearly represented features. *Why needed*: To isolate and mechanistically interpret the internal causes of paraphrase sensitivity. *Quick check*: Can a feature be reliably associated with a concept (e.g., formal phrasing) across multiple inputs?
* **Causal Patching / Interpolation**: Ablates or substitutes a feature’s activation during forward passes to estimate its causal effect on outputs. *Why needed*: To move from correlation to causation in feature attribution. *Quick check*: Does removing a feature’s contribution change the model’s prediction in the expected direction?
* **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**: Architectures that process visual and textual inputs jointly, often using a fusion module. *Why needed*: PSF-Med targets this class of medical AI. *Quick check*: Does the model integrate image and text signals, or does one modality dominate?
* **Paraphrase Robustness**: Consistency of model outputs under meaning-preserving linguistic variations. *Why needed*: Critical for reliability when clinicians rephrase questions. *Quick check*: What percentage of paraphrases trigger answer changes?
* **Visual Grounding**: The degree to which a model’s answer depends on image content rather than textual priors. *Why needed*: High grounding is essential for clinical validity. *Quick check*: How does performance change when the image is removed or shuffled?
* **Clinical Language Register**: Variation in formality, terminology, and syntax (e.g., “Is there consolidation?” vs. “Consolidation present?”). *Why needed*: PSF-Med’s transformations target this variation. *Quick check*: Are flip rates correlated with register shifts in the paraphrase pairs?

## Architecture Onboarding
The studied VLM (MedGemma 4B) processes an image and text question through a vision encoder and text decoder, with cross-attention fusion layers. Sparse autoencoders are applied post-hoc to intermediate layer activations (layer 17) to extract features.

Component map: Image -> Vision Encoder -> [Fused with Text Tokens via Cross-Attention] -> Transformer Layers (1...N) -> Output Logits. SAE operates on Layer 17 activations: Layer17_activations -> SAE Encoder -> Sparse Latents -> Feature 3818.

Critical path: Input question (original/paraphrase) and image → model forward pass → logits → answer. Feature 3818’s activation magnitude along this path predicts conservative shifts.

Design tradeoffs: Accuracy vs. consistency (clamping reduces flips with slight accuracy drop). Model-specificity (SAEs are trained per model/layer).

Failure signatures: High flip rate, high text-only agreement (ignoring images), strong activation of Feature 3818 for formal phrasing.

First three experiments:
1. **Replication**: Apply the same SAE training and causal patching pipeline to another medical VLM (e.g., LLaVA-Rad) to test for analogous register features.
2. **Generalization**: Generate paraphrases for a non-chest-X-ray dataset (e.g., retinal images) and evaluate flip rates; inspect if Feature 3818 analogs exist in models trained on such data.
3. **Intervention scope**: Clamp Feature 3818 and measure changes in calibration metrics (Expected Calibration Error) and error types (false positive/negative rates) to check for unintended bias.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
Do similar prompt-framing features exist in other medical VLMs beyond MedGemma 4B, and if so, do they exhibit the same causal influence on paraphrase sensitivity? *Basis*: Analysis limited to one model at one layer; other models likely have different mechanisms. *Why unresolved*: No comparative analysis across architectures.

### Open Question 2
What mechanisms account for the remaining ~55% of flip cases not explained by Feature 3818, and do they involve different types of paraphrase transformations? *Basis*: Feature 3818 ablation reverses only 15% of flips; other features/mechanisms contribute. *Why unresolved*: The paper identifies top-5 features by delta but does not characterize their functions or combined effects.

### Open Question 3
Does the observed trade-off between paraphrase robustness and visual grounding reflect an inherent limitation of current VLM architectures, or can training objectives/architectural changes decouple these properties? *Basis*: Correlation found between lower flip rates and higher text-only agreement; interventions improve consistency while reducing text-prior reliance, suggesting trade-off is not absolute. *Why unresolved*: Causality of correlation is unclear; no experiments varying training or architecture.

### Open Question 4
Why are flip cases largely model-specific (low cross-model correlation), and can ensemble or consistency-training methods leverage model-specific failures to improve overall reliability? *Basis*: Mean pairwise flip correlation is r=0.07; authors suggest this could inform ensembles. *Why unresolved*: Causes of model-specificity not investigated; ensembles not tested.

### Open Question 5
Do the mechanistic findings (Feature 3818) generalize to other medical imaging modalities (e.g., CT, MRI) and non-binary question types, and does clamping such features affect calibration or error types? *Basis*: Focus restricted to chest X-ray yes/no questions; calibration effects unevaluated. *Why unresolved*: Scope limited to one modality and question type.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability**: Findings restricted to chest X-rays and MedGemma 4B; unknown if Feature 3818 or similar mechanisms exist in other VLMs or modalities.
- **Clinical Relevance**: No clinician validation; impact of flips on diagnostic decisions unverified.
- **Benchmark Scope**: PSF-Med uses synthetic paraphrases; real-world clinician rephrasing may differ in distribution and error profile.
- **Causal Interpretation**: SAE interpretations and patching effects may be confounded by model-specific artifacts; claims are inferential.
- **Reproducibility**: Limited community validation (low citation count among neighbors); code/data availability unclear.

## Confidence
- **High**: Measurement of paraphrase flip rates on a large, curated benchmark (19,748 questions, ~92,000 paraphrases) across six VLMs.
- **Medium**: Identification of Feature 3818 and its correlation with formal phrasing/conservative shifts via SAEs and patching in MedGemma 4B.
- **Low**: General claim that paraphrase robustness and visual grounding are distinct properties, and that clamping Feature 3818 universally reduces text-prior reliance—evidence is model-specific.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-model replication**: Apply identical SAE-based causal patching to at least two other medical VLMs (e.g., BLIP-2 Med, MedCLIP) to test for analogous register features and flip-reversal effects.
2. **Multimodal validation**: Construct paraphrase benchmarks for other modalities (e.g., retinal fundus, histopathology) and evaluate whether Feature 3818 analogs exist, and whether clamping transfers.
3. **Clinical impact study**: Conduct a clinician evaluation where experts assess model consistency on real rephrased queries, measuring correlation of flips with diagnostic disagreement or potential patient harm.
