# Do LLMs Need Inherent Reasoning Before Reinforcement Learning? A Study in Korean Self-Correction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05459
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05459
- **Reference count:** 20
- **Primary result:** Neuron-tuning followed by RL yields +4.3% on GSM8K-KOR and 60% self-correction, while preserving English performance; RL alone on vanilla models is ineffective.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) excel at reasoning in high-resource languages like English but underperform in low-resource languages such as Korean. This study investigates whether reinforcement learning (RL) alone can bridge this gap for Korean mathematical reasoning and self-correction. The authors find that applying RL directly to unaligned models yields negligible improvements. Instead, they first identify and fine-tune a small set of Korean-specific neurons in early layers using a curated code-switching dataset (English → mixed → Korean), which aligns the model’s internal translation process. Only after this targeted alignment does RL (via GRPO) produce substantial gains in Korean reasoning and self-correction, without degrading English performance. The core conclusion: RL requires pre-aligned inherent reasoning; it cannot inject new reasoning capabilities from scratch.

## Method Summary
The method hinges on a two-stage intervention. First, using Parallel Language-Specific Neuron Detection (PLND), the authors identify a sparse subset of neurons in early layers (e.g., layers 0–11) that are critical for processing Korean inputs. These neurons are then fine-tuned on a self-generated code-switching dataset where reasoning traces begin in English, transition to mixed English-Korean, and conclude in Korean. This curriculum explicitly scaffolds the internal translation from Korean to the model’s English-centric reasoning engine. Second, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is applied to the neuron-tuned model, using rewards for correct final answers and proper reasoning format (e.g., `<THINK>` tags). Baselines include applying RL directly to vanilla models and alternative fine-tuning strategies like LoRA and DPO.

## Key Results
- RL on vanilla models (e.g., Llama3.1-8B) produces minimal gains: +1.1% on GSM8K-KOR and only 4% self-correction success.
- After neuron-tuning, RL yields substantial improvements: +4.3% on GSM8K-KOR, 60% self-correction success, with no loss in English MMLU/GPQA scores.
- Other fine-tuning methods (LoRA, DPO, continual pre-training) either fail to improve Korean reasoning or degrade English performance significantly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LLMs internally translate low-resource language inputs (like Korean) into a high-resource language (like English) in early layers before reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** Language-specific neurons in early layers map Korean input representations to English-centric latent spaces, enabling the model to leverage its primarily English-trained reasoning pathways in middle layers.
- **Core assumption:** The model's core reasoning "engine" is trained predominantly on English data; non-English inputs must be converted to this internal language for effective reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] " LLMs tend to internally translate multilingual inputs into English (or another dominant pre-training language), reason in that language, and then generate outputs in the original input language."
  - [section 2.2] Figure 1 shows the ratio of Korean vs. English tokens decoded from hidden states, indicating a shift toward English representations in early layers.
  - [corpus] Weak direct support; corpus neighbors discuss multilingual LLMs and code-switching but do not specifically validate this internal translation mechanism for Korean reasoning.
- **Break condition:** If internal translation fails (e.g., for complex reasoning tasks), the model cannot access its English-reasoning capabilities, leading to poor performance regardless of downstream techniques like RL.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Tuning Korean-specific neurons in early layers with a structured code-switching curriculum aligns the internal translation process specifically for reasoning and self-correction tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Fine-tuning a small, identified subset of early-layer neurons using a dataset that transitions from English reasoning to mixed to Korean output strengthens the Korean->English translation pathway for problem-solving contexts, without overwriting the model's core English reasoning knowledge.
- **Core assumption:** The model already possesses strong English reasoning capabilities; the bottleneck is the misalignment of the Korean input processing pathway to trigger those capabilities correctly for math/self-correction.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "aligning the model’s internal reasoning processes with Korean inputs—particularly by tuning Korean-specific neurons in early layers—is key to unlocking RL's effectiveness."
  - [section 2.3] "Our goal is to align LLMs by enhancing internal translation through the tuning of language-specific neurons."
  - [section 3.2.3] Table 4 shows that neuron-tuning improves Korean reasoning/self-correction while preserving English performance, whereas other methods (LoRA, DPO) degrade English capabilities.
- **Break condition:** If the tuning dataset does not follow the English-first curriculum (e.g., Korean→Mixed→English), the alignment signal is weakened, leading to poorer RL readiness (per [section 4.1] Figure 6).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Reinforcement Learning (via GRPO) can then effectively amplify and solidify the now-aligned reasoning capabilities for Korean, but it cannot create alignment from a poorly aligned base.
- **Mechanism:** RL optimizes the policy based on rewards for correct final answers and proper reasoning format. It fine-tunes the entire model but is most effective when the underlying reasoning trace (accessed via the aligned early layers) is already competent; it steers and refines rather than building from scratch.
- **Core assumption:** RL is a process of preference optimization and test-time scaling on an existing behavioral distribution; it does not fundamentally alter the model's internal knowledge representation or create new reasoning pathways.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "RL alone yields limited improvements when applied to models lacking inherent Korean reasoning capabilities."
  - [section 3.2.1] Table 1 shows negligible gains (+1-3%) and low actual self-correction rates (2-10%) when applying RL to vanilla models.
  - [section 3.2.4] Table 5 shows significant gains (+4-9%) and high self-correction rates (53-71%) after neuron-tuning + RL.
- **Break condition:** If the model's base Korean reasoning is not pre-aligned (i.e., internal translation for reasoning tasks is weak), RL leads to distribution shift or behavior collapse, not genuine capability gain.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Conditioned/ Direct Answer Score (CAS/DAS)**
  - **Why needed here:** To disentangle whether poor Korean performance is due to inherent text generation difficulty or task misunderstanding. Shows reasoning tasks have a disproportionately larger Korean gap.
  - **Quick check question:** If CAS and DAS gaps between English and Korean are large for math but small for QA, what does that suggest about the nature of the deficiency?
- **Concept: Language-Specific Neuron Detection (PLND)**
  - **Why needed here:** To identify the precise, sparse subset of parameters (in early self-attention/FFN layers) responsible for processing Korean input. Provides a surgical intervention point.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is deactivating early-layer Korean-specific neurons more damaging to reasoning than deactivating middle-layer ones?
- **Concept: Code-Switching Curriculum for Alignment**
  - **Why needed here:** To create a training signal that explicitly teaches the model: "When you see Korean, first think in English, then reflect, then output in Korean." This scaffolds the internal translation process.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might a dataset structured as Korean→Mixed→English be less effective than English→Mixed→Korean for this alignment goal?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Processor (Early Layers) -> Reasoning Engine (Middle Layers) -> Output Generator (Late Layers); Reward Model (External) provides outcome and format signals.
- **Critical path:** Identify Korean-specific neurons (PLND) → Tune them with code-switching self-correction data → Apply GRPO using format+outcome rewards.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Neuron Tuning vs. Continual Pre-training: Neuron tuning is parameter-efficient (<0.1% of params) and preserves English skills; pre-training uses more data/compute and risks overwriting general knowledge.
  - Curriculum Direction: English→Mixed→Korean provides a clear translation target; reverse may degrade data quality.
  - RL Reward Design: Outcome-only reward may not incentivize format; combined reward is used but adds complexity.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - RL on vanilla model: minimal accuracy gain (<3%), self-correction rate near random (<10%).
  - Neuron tuning with wrong layers or too many samples: English performance drop (>2% on MMLU/GPQA), self-correction plateaus post-RL.
  - LoRA/DPO on full model: significant drop (>3%) in English MMLU/GPQA performance.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Validate internal translation: For a small set of Korean math problems, decode hidden states across layers (as in Fig 1/3) to confirm shift toward English token probabilities in early layers.
  2. Establish necessity of early neurons: Deactivate top 100 early-layer Korean-specific neurons vs. random middle-layer neurons on a held-out Korean math set. Expect drastic drop (>50%) only for early-layer deactivation.
  3. Prove concept of alignment before RL: Train 3 models: (a) vanilla, (b) neuron-tuned, (c) LoRA-tuned. Apply GRPO to all. Expect (b) to show significant gains in both math accuracy and self-correction rate, while (a) and (c) show minimal improvement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why does reinforcement learning (RL) fail to improve Korean reasoning in vanilla models, and what specific internal mechanism is missing?
2. Is tuning early-layer Korean-specific neurons *necessary* for unlocking RL benefits, or could alternative fine-tuning strategies (e.g., different layers, broader parameter updates) achieve similar results without degrading English performance?
3. To what extent does the proposed code-switching curriculum (English→mixed→Korean) drive the success of neuron tuning, versus simpler bilingual or monolingual training data?
4. Does the neuron-tuning approach generalize beyond Korean and mathematical reasoning to other low-resource languages and diverse reasoning domains (e.g., commonsense, dialogue)?
5. Why does neuron tuning preserve English performance while other fine-tuning methods degrade it, and what does this imply about the modularity of language-specific vs. language-agnostic reasoning circuits?

## Limitations
- **Neuron localization validity:** PLND identifies "Korean-specific" neurons, but it's unclear if these are truly language-specific or task/format proxies for math reasoning.
- **Dataset curation bias:** The self-correction code-switching dataset is generated by GPT-4o/4.1; its quality and potential English reasoning shortcuts are unknown.
- **GRPO implementation fidelity:** Critical hyperparameters and prompt formatting for GRPO are not fully specified, which could alter the reported RL gains.

## Confidence
- Internal translation hypothesis: **Medium** (indirect evidence, alternative explanations not ruled out).
- Neuron tuning efficacy: **High** (strong experimental control and ablations).
- RL dependency on pre-alignment: **High** (consistent across models/datasets).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate neuron tuning layers:** Restrict targeted neurons to middle layers (e.g., 12–20) instead of early layers; expect performance degradation.
2. **Reverse curriculum test:** Train a neuron-tuning model with `Korean → Mixed → English` order; expect self-correction rate to drop >20% vs. published order.
3. **GRPO behavior analysis:** Compare GRPO optimization trajectories on vanilla vs. neuron-tuned models by tracking variance of generated reasoning traces early in training; expect vanilla to show high variance/poor format adherence from step 1.
