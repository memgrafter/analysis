# Cognitively Diverse Multiple-Choice Question Generation: A Hybrid Multi-Agent Framework with Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03704  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03704  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** ReQUESTA (GPT-5) generates MCQs with significantly higher psychometric quality (difficulty, discrimination, point-biserial) and better distractor quality than zero-shot GPT-5.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents ReQUESTA, a hybrid multi-agent framework for generating multiple-choice questions (MCQs) with targeted cognitive demands. The system decomposes the MCQ authoring process into specialized subtasks—planning, generation, evaluation, and post-processing—orchestrated by rule-based controllers to combine the creativity of large language models (LLMs) with deterministic control. Using GPT-5 across a pipeline that includes a Planner, specialized Generators, an Evaluator with iterative revision, and an Option-Shortening Module, ReQUESTA produces questions that test recall, inference, and main idea comprehension from academic expository texts. In a large-scale study (N=8,440 participant responses), ReQUESTA items outperformed a GPT-5 zero-shot baseline on key psychometric metrics (item difficulty, discrimination, point-biserial correlation) and were rated by experts as more relevant with higher-quality distractors, especially for inferential questions. The results show that structured agentic orchestration, not just model scaling, can substantially improve the reliability and cognitive targeting of automated MCQ generation.

## Method Summary
ReQUESTA processes short academic passages (~400 words) through a fixed modular pipeline. A rule-based Preprocessor segments the text, and a Planner LLM (with chain-of-thought prompting) creates a structured JSON plan that assigns content to one of three question types (text-based, inferential, main idea). A rule-based Controller parses the plan and dispatches each segment to a corresponding specialized Generator agent, each with a distinct persona and few-shot prompts. Generators perform self-critique before passing drafts to an Evaluator agent, which uses a checklist rubric to approve items or request revisions (up to a termination limit). An optional hybrid Option-Shortening Module then balances answer option lengths using both LLMs and rule-based selectors. Finally, a Formatter shuffles options and standardizes labels. The framework is evaluated against a GPT-5 zero-shot baseline using psychometric analysis on thousands of human responses and expert rubrics covering topic relevance, clarity, and distractor quality.

## Key Results
- ReQUESTA items were significantly more challenging (lower accuracy) and more discriminative (higher discrimination index) than baseline items.
- ReQUESTA items showed higher point-biserial correlation with overall reading comprehension, indicating better alignment with targeted ability.
- Expert raters gave ReQUESTA higher scores for topic relevance and distractor quality (linguistic consistency, semantic plausibility), particularly for inferential questions; baseline items were rated slightly clearer.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Decomposition & Controlled Execution
- **Claim:** Breaking MCQ generation into specialized, sequential subtasks (planning, generation, evaluation) with deterministic controllers reduces variance and improves alignment with targeted cognitive demands compared to monolithic prompting.
- **Mechanism:** A rule-based **Preprocessor** segments text, a **Planner** (LLM) creates a structured JSON plan assigning content to question types, a rule-based **Controller** routes tasks to specialized **Question Generator** agents (text-based, inferential, main idea), and an **Evaluator** (LLM) provides iterative feedback. This separation isolates stochastic generation to defined scopes.
- **Core assumption:** Cognitive intent can be encoded in intermediate representations (the plan) and enforced by deterministic coordination, rather than relying on a single LLM's implicit reasoning across all stages.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "decomposes MCQ authoring into specialized subtasks and coordinates LLM-powered agents with rule-based components to support planning, controlled generation, iterative evaluation, and post-processing."
  - [section 3.1]: Details the Preprocessor → Planner → Controller → Generators → Evaluator → Formatter pipeline.
  - [corpus]: Weak direct support; neighboring papers focus on hallucination or distractor generation, not multi-agent orchestration for cognitive control.
- **Break condition:** If the Planner's intermediate representations fail to capture necessary nuance for a given text, downstream agents will operate on flawed scopes regardless of their individual quality, leading to misaligned questions.

### Mechanism 2: Iterative Refinement via Embedded Evaluation Loops
- **Claim:** Incorporating automatic, checklist-based evaluation with revision feedback between specialized agents prevents propagation of low-quality items and systematically improves distractor plausibility and stem clarity.
- **Mechanism:** After generation, an **Evaluator** agent assesses each MCQ against criteria (clarity, alignment, distractor plausibility). Items failing thresholds are returned to the originating Generator with specific feedback for revision. This creates a bounded retry loop before final formatting.
- **Core assumption:** LLM outputs in a single pass are unreliable for complex constraints; embedding a verification step that leverages the same or a different LLM with a structured rubric can catch and correct common failure modes (e.g., ambiguous stems, implausible distractors).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "iterative evaluation" and "combining the generative strengths of LLMs with the precision and consistency of rule-based logic."
  - [section 3.1]: "Questions that satisfy these criteria are approved for output, whereas those that do not are automatically returned to the relevant Question Generator along with targeted revision suggestions."
  - [section 5.2]: Expert ratings show ReQUESTA's significant advantage in "distractor linguistic features" and "distractor semantic plausibility," particularly for inferential questions, suggesting refinement improved these aspects.
- **Break condition:** If the Evaluator's rubric is misaligned with expert judgment or if revision loops become infinite or excessively long, system efficiency degrades without quality gains. The paper notes a termination condition exists but does not specify its parameters.

### Mechanism 3: Hybrid Control (LLM Generativity + Rule-Based Enforcement)
- **Claim:** Assigning constraint enforcement (e.g., option length balancing, shuffling, format standardization) to rule-based modules while reserving LLMs for open-ended interpretation and generation yields more reliable and structurally sound MCQs than using LLMs alone for all steps.
- **Mechanism:** The **Controller** orchestrates task assignment based on the plan. The **Option-Shortening Module** uses a rule-based **Length Determiner** and **Candidate Selector** alongside an LLM **Candidate Generator** to ensure answer options are linguistically parallel. The **Formatter** shuffles and labels options deterministically.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs are poor at consistently adhering to stylistic and structural constraints (e.g., uniform option length) even when prompted, whereas simple rule-based checks are reliable and cheap. Decoupling constraint enforcement from content generation prevents the LLM from being "distracted" by formatting rules during creative generation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "combining LLM-powered agents (for planning, generation, evaluation) with rule-based components (for control and post-processing) to enforce constraints."
  - [section 3.1]: Describes the Option-Shortening Module's four collaborating agents, highlighting the rule-based Length Determiner and Candidate Selector.
  - [section 3.2]: "By separating planning (probabilistic) from execution control (deterministic), ReQUESTA ensures that downstream generation remains predictable and auditable."
  - [section 5.2]: ReQUESTA scored significantly higher than GPT-5 on "distractor linguistic features," implicating the shortening module.
- **Break condition:** If the rule-based components are too rigid (e.g., length thresholds that truncate meaningful content) or poorly calibrated, they could degrade semantic quality even as they improve surface consistency.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. Preprocessor: Rule-based sentence splitter.
  2. Planner: LLM agent (GPT-5) with CoT prompting → outputs structured JSON plan (segment summaries, key concepts, question-type assignments).
  3. Controller: Rule-based Python script → parses plan, assigns text segments to specific Generator agents.
  4. Generators (x3): Specialized LLM agents (text-based, inferential, main idea) with persona & few-shot prompts → output draft MCQ.
  5. Self-Critique (embedded): Prompt step within each Generator → internal revision before external evaluation.
  6. Evaluator: LLM agent with checklist rubric → approves or returns for revision.
  7. Option-Shortening Module (optional loop): Collaborating agents (Syntax Analyzer LLM, Length Determiner rule, Candidate Generator LLM, Candidate Selector LLM+rule) → balances option length.
  8. Formatter: Rule-based → shuffles options, applies labels.

- **Critical path:** Input Text → Preprocessor → Planner → Controller → [Generator (with Self-Critique) → Evaluator? (revise loop)] → (Option-Shortening if needed) → Formatter → Output MCQ Set.
  - *Key handoff:* Planner's JSON plan to Controller; Controller's routed tasks to Generators; Evaluator's feedback to Generator.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Modularity vs. Latency:** Decomposition and loops improve quality but add API calls and coordination overhead. The parallel generation of different question types mitigates some latency.
  - **LLM Flexibility vs. Rule Rigidity:** Rule-based control ensures consistency (e.g., option formatting) but can be brittle. LLM components are flexible but prone to drift from constraints.
  - **Evaluation Cost:** Mixed-effects psychometric analysis requires hundreds of participant responses (N=8,440), making full system validation expensive. Expert rubric is cheaper but subjective.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Planner failure:** Invalid/empty JSON, ambiguous content assignments, missing key concepts for a question type.
  - **Generator failure:** Consistently low expert scores on "writing clarity" or "topic relevance" for a specific agent, indicating prompt inadequacy.
  - **Evaluator failure:** High rejection rate without improvement after several loops, or approvals of low-quality items flagged by experts.
  - **Option-Shortening failure:** Semantic drift in shortened options (check via embedding similarity) or inability to find candidates that meet length criteria.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Isolation & Sanity Check:** Run each major agent (Planner, one Generator, Evaluator) in isolation on 5 sample passages with gold-standard inputs/outputs. Verify Planner produces complete, logical plans. Verify Generator produces a complete MCQ (stem+4 options) with a clear key. Verify Evaluator correctly identifies known flaws in deliberately corrupted MCQs.
  2. **Pipeline Integration with Logging:** Run the full pipeline on 1 passage. Enable verbose logging at every agent handoff. Inspect logs to trace the plan, generator inputs, evaluator feedback, and final output. Confirm the Controller correctly interpreted the plan and routed tasks.
  3. **Constraint Stress Test:** Generate 20 MCQs targeting one question type (e.g., inferential) with the Option-Shortening Module disabled. Manually measure answer option length variance (std dev in words). Then, run with the module enabled and compare variance. Also, check that shortening did not reduce distractor semantic plausibility scores in a quick expert spot-check of 5 items.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can a fully automated, validated evaluation component be designed to replace ReQUESTA's current checklist-based Evaluator agent while maintaining or improving alignment with expert human judgments?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state in Future Work: "One immediate direction is the development of a more robust automated MCQ evaluation component that is explicitly validated against expert judgments, replacing the current evaluator that relies primarily on checklist-based prompting."
- **Why unresolved:** The current Evaluator uses prompt-based self-critique and a predefined checklist, which may not capture nuanced quality dimensions as reliably as human experts. A validated automated module is needed for scalable, consistent quality control without human-in-the-loop.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop an automated evaluator (e.g., fine-tuned model or hybrid rule-based system) trained on expert-rated MCQ data, then integrate it into ReQUESTA. Compare the correlation between its scores and expert scores (for topic relevance, distractor quality, etc.) against the current Evaluator's correlation. Additionally, measurewhether final question quality (via expert ratings) improves when using the new evaluator in the iterative refinement loop.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent can ReQUESTA's architecture be extended to reliably generate MCQs targeting higher-order cognitive skills beyond the three current types (e.g., application, analysis, evaluation per Bloom's taxonomy), and do such items exhibit similarly strong psychometric properties?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors note in Future Work: "future iterations of the framework will expand the range of targeted cognitive categories to include application- and evaluation-level questions." In Limitations, they acknowledge the three categories "do not exhaust the space of possible task demands."
- **Why unresolved:** The current system only targets text-based, inferential, and main idea questions. Generating items that require applying concepts to new contexts or evaluating arguments introduces greater complexity, and it is unclear if the agentic decomposition and control mechanisms can reliably produce high-quality items at these levels without degrading difficulty, discrimination, or distractor plausibility.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend the Planner and specialized Question Generators to target, for example, application and evaluation questions. Generate MCQs across these new categories using the same source texts and baseline. Conduct psychometric analysis (difficulty, discrimination, point-biserial) and expert evaluations (distractor plausibility, topic relevance) comparing the new categories to the existing three. Assess whether the cognitive targeting is successful and maintains the quality advantages over the GPT-5 baseline.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does ReQUESTA's superiority over single-pass prompting generalize to text types and genres beyond short academic expository passages (e.g., narrative texts, scientific reports, multi-document inputs), and how does text characteristics interact with the framework's effectiveness?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Limitations section states: "texts from other disciplines, genres, or with substantially different lengths may interact differently with the framework and influence relative performance compared to a single-pass baseline." The study used 400-word OpenStax passages.
- **Why unresolved:** The framework's decomposition (e.g., sentence-level segmentation, key concept extraction) and cognitive targeting may behave differently with texts that have different structures (e.g., stories with plot, dense technical writing) or lengths. Generalizability is crucial for real-world deployment across subjects.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Re-run the experimental design using diverse text corpora: (a) narrative fiction passages, (b) lengthy scientific journal articles (or chapters), (c) multi-source documentary sets. For each, generate MCQs with ReQUESTA and the GPT-5 baseline. Compare psychometric outcomes (item difficulty, discrimination) and expert ratings (topic relevance, distractor quality) across text types. Use mixed-effects models to test for interactions between text type/generation method and item quality.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What is the incremental contribution of specific ReQUESTA modules (e.g., the Option-Shortening Module, iterative Evaluator feedback loops) to the overall improvement in item quality, and could certain modules be simplified or removed without significant loss?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper describes ReQUESTA as modular and notes the Option-Shortening Module was added later to address a specific failure mode. However, no ablation study isolates the effect of individual components. The Discussion emphasizes orchestration but does not disentangle which control mechanisms are most critical.
- **Why unresolved:** While the full system outperforms the baseline, it is unclear which aspects of the workflow (planning, parallel specialized generators, evaluation/iteration, post-processing) contribute most to the gains in difficulty, discrimination, and distractor quality. This knowledge is essential for efficient system design and resource allocation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct systematic ablation experiments: run ReQUESTA with key modules disabled or replaced (e.g., without iterative Evaluator feedback, without Option-Shortening, with a single unified generator instead of three specialized ones, without the Planner's structured plan). For each ablated version, generate MCQs and evaluate psychometric properties and expert ratings. Compare these to the full ReQUESTA and baseline to quantify the marginal contribution of each module. Additionally, analyze process logs to see which modules most frequently trigger revisions or corrections.

## Limitations
- Exact replication is blocked by the non-disclosure of verbatim prompts and rule-based component parameters (e.g., length thresholds, similarity cutoffs).
- The claimed novelty over prior MCQ generation work is weakly supported by the citation network (low FMR, zero citations).
- Generalizability is limited by the reliance on GPT-5 and evaluation on short academic expository texts only.

## Confidence
- ReQUESTA produces more challenging, discriminative, and aligned MCQs: **Medium**
- The described mechanisms (decomposition, iterative loops, hybrid control) drive the gains: **Medium**
- The framework represents a novel contribution over prior MCQ generation work: **Low**
