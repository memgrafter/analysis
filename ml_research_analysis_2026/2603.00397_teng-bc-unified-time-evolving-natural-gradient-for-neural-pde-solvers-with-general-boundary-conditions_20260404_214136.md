# TENG-BC: Unified Time-Evolving Natural Gradient for Neural PDE Solvers with General Boundary Conditions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.00397
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00397
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Unified boundary-aware natural gradient framework achieves solver-level accuracy (relative L2 errors ~1e-6) without penalty tuning across Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin, and mixed boundaries.

## Executive Summary
TENG-BC introduces a novel framework for neural PDE solvers that simultaneously enforces interior dynamics and general boundary conditions via a joint least-squares update. By linearizing the neural network representation and its normal derivative, it assembles a unified linear system that yields a natural gradient step respecting the L2 metric induced by the PDE's functional space. This eliminates the need for manual penalty weighting. Benchmarks on heat, transport, and Burgers equations demonstrate high accuracy and stable long-time error accumulation, outperforming PINN and FEM baselines.

## Method Summary
TENG-BC advances the neural network solution in small time steps. At each step, it constructs a target field using a time integrator (Euler, Heun, or RK4) and solves a unified least-squares problem over fixed interior and boundary point sets. The neural network output $u_θ$ and its normal derivative $\partial_n u_θ$ are linearized around current parameters; their Jacobians $J$ and $K$ are combined with boundary coefficients to form a single linear system $AΔθ = R$. The update $Δθ$ solves this regularized least-squares problem, corresponding to a natural gradient. Implementation uses random subsets of parameters per iteration to reduce computational cost, and fixed spatial sampling to maintain reproducibility.

## Key Results
- Relative L2 errors as low as 3.08e-06 on heat equation with Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin, and mixed boundaries at T=2.
- Burgers equation at T=4: error ~2.24e-04, beating PINN and FEM baselines and exceeding high-resolution FEM accuracy.
- Stable error accumulation over long-time simulations, including advection-dominated transport and shock-forming Burgers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** TENG-BC enforces general boundary conditions and interior PDE dynamics simultaneously at each time step without manual penalty weighting.
- **Mechanism:** It formulates a single least-squares problem over the domain closure (interior + boundary) that jointly fits the neural representation to the time-evolved target field and the boundary constraints. This is achieved by linearizing $u_θ$ and $\partial_n u_θ$ around current parameters, assembling their Jacobians ($J$ for $u_θ$, $K$ for $\partial_n u_θ$) with boundary coefficients ($a, b$), and solving a unified linear system.
- **Core assumption:** The local parameter update $Δθ$ can accurately approximate the continuous PDE evolution when the neural representation is expressive and $Δt$ is small enough for first-order linearization.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "jointly enforces interior dynamics and boundary conditions... without delicate penalty tuning." [section 3.2] Equation (1) and derivation for mixed boundary show unified least-squares term. [section 3.3] Equation (2) combines interior and boundary terms.
- **Break condition:** The unified Jacobian becomes ill-conditioned or rank-deficient due to contradictory constraints or sparse sampling, causing unstable updates.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The unified least-squares update corresponds to a natural-gradient step, providing geometry-aware optimization that respects the L2 metric induced by the PDE’s functional space.
- **Mechanism:** The normal equations yield a parameter metric $G(θ)$ that is the sum of the interior Gram matrix $∫ JᵀJ dx$ and the boundary Gram matrix $∫ (aJ + bK)ᵀ(aJ + bK) dx$. Solving $GΔθ = -∇_θL$ gives the update, automatically scaling gradient components according to sensitivity across interior and boundary.
- **Core assumption:** The L2 norm over the closure is appropriate for functional error, and the parameterization manifold is smooth enough for natural gradient to approximate steepest descent in function space.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section B] Derivation shows update $Δθ = -G^{-1} ∇_θL$ with $G$ defined in Equation (5). [section 3.3] "This formulation can be equivalently viewed as a unified least-squares problem over the domain closure... admits a natural-gradient interpretation."
- **Break condition:** Boundary contribution to $G$ dominates or misaligns with interior, misrepresenting solution manifold geometry, causing slow convergence or overshooting.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Time-evolving updates with higher-order temporal discretization (Heun, RK4) reduce long-time error accumulation by providing more accurate per-step targets.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of a global space-time loss, TENG-BC advances step-by-step. The target field $u_{target}$ is constructed via integration schemes (e.g., RK4 uses multiple intermediate evaluations of $Lu$). The same boundary-aware least-squares stepper fits this more accurate target, reducing local truncation error.
- **Core assumption:** Local errors propagate linearly or sub-linearly; improving per-step accuracy directly improves global stability.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "stable time evolution without delicate penalty tuning." [section 3.3] Describes TENG-Euler, TENG-Heun, TENG-RK4. [section 4.2] Table 1 shows Heun/RK4 achieve ~10⁻⁶ errors vs ~10⁻⁴ for Euler. [section 4.4] Figure 4d shows stable error vs rising FEM error.
- **Break condition:** For stiff or highly nonlinear PDEs, higher-order schemes may need small $Δt$ or implicit steps; explicit least-squares may become unstable if target changes abruptly.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Natural Gradient in Parameter Space**
  - Why needed: TENG-BC’s update is derived as a natural gradient step, accounting for the curvature of the loss landscape induced by the neural network’s parameterization and the L² error norm. Without this, a steepest-descent update in Euclidean space would ignore non-uniform sensitivity of the solution to parameter changes.
  - Quick check question: Why does the natural gradient use the metric $G(θ) = ∫ JᵀJ dx$ (and its boundary-augmented version) instead of just the identity matrix?
- **Concept: Least-Squares Formulation for PDE Residuals**
  - Why needed: The core update solves a linearized least-squares problem to make the neural function’s derivative match the PDE’s time derivative. This projects the continuous evolution onto the finite-dimensional parameter manifold optimally in L², avoiding nonlinear optimization at each step.
  - Quick check question: How does linearizing $u_θ$ and $\partial_n u_θ$ around current parameters turn enforcing $u_t = Lu$ and boundary conditions into a linear system in $Δθ$?
- **Concept: Boundary Conditions as Linear Constraints in Parameter Space**
  - Why needed: Unlike penalty methods that add a soft loss term (requiring weight tuning), TENG-BC incorporates boundary constraints directly into the linear system by linearizing the boundary residual. This forces the update to respect boundaries exactly within the linear approximation, avoiding heuristic weighting.
  - Quick check question: For a Robin condition $a u + b \partial_n u = v$, how are the Jacobians $J$ (for $u$) and $K$ (for $\partial_n u$) combined in the unified residual?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Neural network $u_θ(x)$ → Compute Jacobians $J$ ($∂_θ u_θ$) on interior and boundary, and $K$ ($∂_θ \partial_n u_θ$) on boundary → Assemble unified least-squares: residual vector $R = [Δu; Δv]$ and design matrix $A = [J_{interior}; a J_{boundary} + b K_{boundary}]$ → Solve min $||R - A Δθ||^2$ with regularization (truncated SVD) → Update $θ$ (using active parameter subset) → Time stepping controller selects $u_{target}$ via Euler/Heun/RK4.

Critical path:
1. Sample fixed interior grid points $\{x_i\}$ and boundary points $\{x_j\}$.
2. Forward pass: compute $u_θ(x_i)$ and $u_θ(x_j)$; compute $\partial_n u_θ(x_j)$ via gradient.
3. Compute residuals: $Δu = u_{target} - u_θ$ on interior; $Δv = v - (a u_θ + b \partial_n u_θ)$ on boundary.
4. Compute Jacobians: $J_{interior} = ∂_θ u_θ(x_i)$; $J_{boundary} = ∂_θ u_θ(x_j)$; $K_{boundary} = ∂_θ(\partial_n u_θ)(x_j)$.
5. Assemble unified system: residual vector $R = [Δu; Δv]$, design matrix $A = [J_{interior}; a J_{boundary} + b K_{boundary}]$.
6. Solve min$_{Δθ} ||R - A Δθ||^2$ with regularization (truncate small singular values).
7. Update $θ ← θ + Δθ$ (partial parameter masking).
8. Repeat for next time step, recomputing $u_{target}$ and boundary $v(t)$ as needed.

Design tradeoffs:
- Partial parameter updates: reduce cost and improve conditioning, but may need more steps and introduce stochasticity.
- Fixed spatial sampling: simple and reproducible, but may miss evolving features; adaptive sampling could help.
- Time scheme order: higher-order (Heun/RK4) reduces error accumulation but increases function evaluations per step.
- Singular-value truncation: stabilizes solution, but excessive truncation loses information and biases updates.

Failure signatures:
- Increasing per-step least-squares residual (should be stable and small).
- Boundary violation: significant deviation of $u_θ$ or $\partial_n u_θ$ from prescribed $v$ after update.
- Oscillatory parameter updates: spikes or inconsistent signs in $Δθ$ norm.
- Diverging error accumulation: relative L2 error grows super-linearly in time.

First 3 experiments:
1. Heat equation on unit disk with Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin, and mixed boundaries; verify accuracy (~1e-6) and stability over T=2.
2. Transport equation on unit disk with inflow Dirichlet boundary; test advection-dominated regime and long-time stability.
3. Burgers equation on [0,2π]² with periodic boundaries; test nonlinear shock formation and compare to high-resolution FEM/spectral references.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can TENG-BC solve Cauchy-type boundary value problems (over-specified data)? Current framework only supports disjoint boundary segments; over-specification requires regularization.
2. Can TENG-BC handle stochastic, parametric, and coupled PDEs? Only deterministic single-equation PDEs tested; extensions need new handling of randomness, parameters, or multiple fields.
3. How does network architecture affect TENG-BC's accuracy and Jacobian conditioning? Only tanh MLPs used; different architectures produce different Jacobians, impacting natural gradient and partial updates.

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters (number of interior/boundary points, singular-value truncation threshold) are not specified, hindering full reproducibility.
- Framework assumes linear boundary conditions; nonlinear boundary conditions (e.g., flux ∝ u²) are not supported without modification.
- No direct ablation of the natural gradient component to isolate its contribution to performance.

## Confidence
- Unified boundary handling without penalty tuning: High
- Natural gradient metric as geometry-aware update: Medium
- Solver-level accuracy and stable long-time accumulation: Medium
