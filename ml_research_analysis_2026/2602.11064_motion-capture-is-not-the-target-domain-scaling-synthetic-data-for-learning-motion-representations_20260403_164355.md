# Motion Capture is Not the Target Domain: Scaling Synthetic Data for Learning Motion Representations

## Quick Facts
- **Arasti ID:** 2602.11064
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11064
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** Synthetic pretraining scaled to 8× the size of real motion capture data outperforms real pretraining in fine-tuned k-shot HAR settings, but lags in 0-shot transfer; mixing synthetic and real data further improves generalization, especially in higher-shot regimes, revealing a fundamental domain mismatch between mocap and wearable IMU signals.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of pretraining motion representation models for wearable-based Human Activity Recognition (HAR) when collecting large-scale real-world data is infeasible. The authors propose using synthetic IMU signals generated by text-to-motion models (trained on motion capture data) as a scalable alternative for contrastive pretraining. Evaluated across 18 HAR datasets, they find that purely synthetic pretraining, when scaled to eight times the volume of real motion capture data, achieves higher macro-averaged F1 scores than real-mocap pretraining in fine-tuned k-shot settings (1–10 shots), though it still trails in 0-shot transfer. Mixing synthetic and real data during pretraining improves generalization, particularly for higher-shot regimes. Notably, scaling real motion-capture pretraining yields only marginal gains, indicating a fundamental misalignment between mocap-based joint position representations and wearable IMU signal characteristics.

## Method Summary
The method begins by generating synthetic motion data using text-to-motion models (MDM, T2M, MotionDiffuse) conditioned on text prompts from the HumanML3D motion capture dataset. These generated motions (in A_mocap, joint positions) are converted to IMU signals (A_imu, 6D orientation/acceleration per joint) via inverse kinematics and differentiation. A contrastive pretraining framework then aligns a spatio-temporal GCN motion encoder (4.94M parameters) with a CLIP text encoder using the synthetic and/or real paired data. Augmentations include random rotations and joint masking. The pretrained encoder is evaluated on 18 downstream HAR datasets: in 0-shot mode (frozen encoder, nearest-text classification) and k-shot fine-tuning (adding a linear head trained on k examples per class). Experiments vary the scale of synthetic data (1×–8× real size) and mixing ratios of real and synthetic pairs (e.g., 50/50). Performance is measured by macro-averaged F1 score, averaged across datasets.

## Key Results
- Synthetic pretraining scaled to 8× real mocap volume surpasses real-mocap pretraining in fine-tuned k-shot (1,2,3,5,10) but remains inferior in 0-shot transfer.
- Mixing synthetic and real data (e.g., 50/50) improves generalization, with notable gains in higher-shot regimes (3,5,10 shots).
- Scaling real motion-capture pretraining produces marginal improvements, suggesting a fundamental domain mismatch between mocap joint-position representations and wearable IMU signals that limits transferability regardless of data volume.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Synthetic Data Scaling Improves k-But Not 0-Shot
**Claim:** Scaling purely synthetic pretraining data improves downstream HAR performance in fine-tuned (k-shot) settings, eventually surpassing real-mocap pretraining, but does not close the 0-shot gap.  
**Mechanism:** The larger, more diverse synthetic corpus expands the motion manifold covered during pretraining, enabling the contrastive encoder to learn richer features that adapt well when limited labeled target data is available for fine-tuning. The 0-shot gap persists because the synthetic distribution remains anchored in the mocap-derived representation space and may not perfectly align with the semantic structure of activity label texts.  
**Core assumption:** Text-to-motion models produce motions that, when converted to IMU signals, are sufficiently plausible and diverse to substitute for real wearable data during pretraining.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Table 2 show synthetic 8× yields higher k-shot scores than real baseline but lower 0-shot; conclusion notes synthetic data’s potential to surpass real mocap in fine-tuned regimes.  
**Break condition:** If scaling synthetic data leads to diminishing or negative returns beyond a certain volume due to degrading quality (e.g., unrealistic dynamics), the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 2: Mixing Synthetic and Real Data Boosts Generalization
**Claim:** Combining synthetic and real data during pretraining improves generalization compared to using either alone, especially in higher-shot fine-tuning.  
**Mechanism:** Real data anchors the representation in the true wearable signal distribution, while synthetic data augments coverage of underrepresented motions and semantics. The mixture prevents overfitting to real mocap idiosyncrasies and yields more robust representations that require less adaptation on new HAR datasets.  
**Core assumption:** The synthetic and real data distributions are partially overlapping or complementary in a way that benefits representation learning.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Table 1 show mixed pretraining (e.g., MotionDiffuse mixed) often exceeds real-only and synthetic-only baselines in 3,5,10-shot settings; introduction highlights synthetic data’s controllability and diversity.  
**Break condition:** If synthetic data introduces harmful semantic distortions or sensor noise, mixed pretraining could underperform real-only, particularly in low-shot regimes.

### Mechanism 3: Motion Capture Has Fundamental Domain Mismatch with Wearables
**Claim:** Large-scale pretraining on motion capture data yields only marginal gains for downstream HAR due to a fundamental domain mismatch.  
**Mechanism:** Representations learned from A_mocap (joint positions) are optimized for 3D pose reconstruction/generation, not for the specific dynamics and sensor noise of IMU signals. The mapping from A_mocap to A_imu via inverse kinematics is lossy and may not preserve discriminative features for activity recognition as experienced by real wearables.  
**Core assumption:** The transformation from joint positions to IMU signals does not fully capture the complexity of actual wearable outputs (e.g., device-specific noise, placement variations).  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and section 4.2.3 show scaling real-world (mocap) data yields only marginal gains, contrasting prior work that used real wearable data; section 3.1 defines A_mocap and A_imu as distinct action spaces.  
**Break condition:** If a more accurate physics-based or noise-injected simulation from mocap to IMU is developed, the domain gap could be reduced, undermining the claim of a fundamental, uncorrectable mismatch.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Contrastive Representation Learning**  
  *Why needed here:* Enables alignment of motion and text embeddings without instance-level labels, forcing the motion encoder to capture activity-relevant semantics.  
  *Quick check question:* Why might contrastive learning between text and synthetic IMU sequences still be effective even if the synthetic IMU signals are imperfect?

- **Concept: Action Space Formalization (A_mocap vs. A_imu)**  
  *Why needed here:* Distinguishes between motion capture joint positions (A_mocap) and wearable IMU signals (A_imu), clarifying the domain mismatch that limits transferability.  
  *Quick check question:* What are the key mathematical differences between A_mocap and A_imu that could cause a sim2real gap even after inverse kinematics?

- **Concept: Text-to-Motion Generation as a Data Source**  
  *Why needed here:* Provides a scalable way to generate diverse motion samples conditioned on text, enabling large-volume synthetic pretraining.  
  *Quick check question:* If text-to-motion models are trained on mocap data (HumanML3D), how does that impose a ceiling on the diversity of synthetic IMU data generated for HAR?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Text Prompt -> Synthetic Data Generator (MDM/T2M/MotionDiffuse) -> A_mocap -> Signal Simulator (Inverse Kinematics/Differentiation) -> A_imu -> Motion Encoder (Spatio-temporal GCN) -> Projection Head -> Shared Embedding Space (contrastive with CLIP Text Encoder). Downstream: Pre-trained Motion Encoder -> Linear Classification Head -> HAR Labels.

**Critical path:** Sample text prompts from HumanML3D → Generate synthetic motions → Convert to IMU signals → Apply augmentations (random rotations, joint masking) → Compute contrastive loss (InfoNCE) → Train motion and text encoders → Save motion encoder weights → For each HAR dataset: Align sensor channels to 24-joint template → Fine-tune linear head on k examples per class (or evaluate 0-shot frozen) → Compute macro-F1 across 18 datasets.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Mocap simulation fidelity vs. scalability: Inverse kinematics is cheap and leverages existing text-to-motion models but may introduce a crude mapping; direct IMU synthesis could be more accurate but less mature.
- Synthetic volume vs. mixture: Scaling pure synthetic data risks quality degradation; mixing with real data provides stability but requires balancing.
- Pre-training data diversity vs. domain purity: Generating motions from out-of-distribution prompts might increase diversity but could yield kinematically invalid samples.

**Failure signatures:**
- 0-shot collapse: Synthetic-pretrained model fails (F1 ~0.1) on 0-shot evaluation.
- Non-monotonic scaling: Increasing synthetic data volume leads to decreased k-shot performance.
- Sensor placement brittleness: Performance drops sharply on datasets with non-standard sensor placements.
- Real-mocap plateau: Full real-mocap pretraining shows no significant improvement over a 40% subset.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Establish baseline: Pretrain on full real-world HumanML3D (24k pairs); evaluate 0-shot and k-shot on all 18 HAR datasets.
2. Synthetic scaling: For one generator (e.g., MDM), pretrain on 1×, 2×, 4×, 8× synthetic data; plot macro-F1 vs. volume for each shot setting.
3. Mixed data ratio sweep: Hold total data at 24k pairs; vary real:synthetic mix (100% real, 75/25, 50/50, 25/75, 100% synthetic); evaluate 5-shot and 10-shot across datasets.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How does expanding the diversity of text prompts beyond those derived from motion capture datasets affect the transferability of synthetically pretrained motion representations to wearable HAR tasks?  
  *Basis:* Conclusion explicitly mentions exploring richer prompt distributions as a promising direction.  
  *Why unresolved:* Current synthetic data is limited to HumanML3D prompts, restricting diversity and potentially underrepresenting real activity patterns.  
  *What would resolve:* Generate synthetic motions using broad, out-of-distribution text prompts, pretrain, and compare downstream HAR performance.

- **Question 2:** What explains the persistent 0-shot transfer gap between real-world and synthetic pretraining, even when synthetic data is scaled to 8× the size of motion capture data?  
  *Basis:* Results show synthetic 8× still trails real baseline in 0-shot.  
  *Why unresolved:* The specific factors (e.g., distributional shifts, noise profiles, motion semantics) causing the gap are not identified.  
  *What would resolve:* Compare embedding spaces; systematically vary synthetic data properties (noise, smoothness, activity distribution) to isolate determinants.

- **Question 3:** Why does scaling synthetic pretraining data not yield strictly monotonic improvements in downstream HAR performance, in contrast to the consistent gains observed when scaling real-world pretraining data?  
  *Basis:* Paper notes non-monotonic trends in Table 2 and states the cause is unclear, possibly due to stochastic generation.  
  *Why unresolved:* Without understanding root causes (generator randomness, prompt effects, quality-diversity trade-offs), optimizing synthetic scaling is difficult.  
  *What would resolve:* Controlled experiments with fixed seeds across generators; correlate downstream performance with quantitative synthetic data metrics (fidelity, diversity, semantic consistency).

- **Question 4:** Which specific representational aspects of motion capture data contribute to the domain mismatch with wearable IMU signals, thereby limiting transferability?  
  *Basis:* Core finding is that mocap pretraining yields marginal gains due to domain mismatch, but the mismatch is not decomposed.  
  *Why unresolved:* Pinpointing factors is essential for designing synthetic pipelines that bridge the sim2real gap.  
  *What would resolve:* Create synthetic mocap variants by incrementally introducing wearable-realistic characteristics (noise, placement effects, motion distortions), pretrain on each, and evaluate transfer.

## Limitations
- **IMU simulation fidelity:** The inverse kinematics/differentiation pipeline for converting mocap to IMU signals is not fully specified (coordinate frames, sampling rate, noise modeling).
- **Downstream alignment protocol:** Details on aligning 18 diverse HAR datasets to the fixed 24-joint encoder template are missing, which is critical for interpreting generalization claims.
- **Training hyperparameters:** Complete augmentation distributions (rotation angles, masking ratios), pretraining schedule (warmup, scheduler), and fine-tuning details (epochs, LR) are not provided, hindering exact replication.

## Confidence
- Synthetic scaling > real mocap in k-shot: **Medium**
- Mixed pretraining benefits: **Medium**
- Fundamental mocap-IMU domain mismatch: **Medium**
