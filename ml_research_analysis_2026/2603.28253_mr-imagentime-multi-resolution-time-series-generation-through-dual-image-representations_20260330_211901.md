# MR-ImagenTime: Multi-Resolution Time Series Generation through Dual Image Representations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.28253
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28253
- **Reference count**: 30
- **Primary result**: MR-CDM achieves 6-10% lower MAE/RMSE than state-of-the-art baselines across four real-world datasets

## Executive Summary
MR-ImagenTime (MR-CDM) is a multi-resolution conditional diffusion model for time series forecasting that addresses variable-length sequences and complex temporal dynamics through a novel hierarchical framework. The approach combines multi-scale trend decomposition, adaptive image-based representations, and conditional diffusion to generate accurate forecasts. By leveraging dual image representations and a diffusion-based generation process, MR-CDM captures both long-term trends and short-term variations in time series data. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements over established methods like ARIMA, LSTM, and CSDI across diverse domains including energy, weather, and traffic data.

## Method Summary
MR-CDM employs a hierarchical framework that first decomposes input time series into multi-scale trends using empirical mode decomposition. These trends are then transformed into adaptive image representations that capture temporal patterns at different resolutions. The core conditional diffusion model learns to generate future time series by iteratively denoising these image representations while conditioning on historical data. The model handles variable-length sequences through its adaptive representation scheme and produces forecasts by reversing the diffusion process. A key innovation is the fusion of multiple image representations to capture both global trends and local variations, enabling superior performance across diverse time series characteristics.

## Key Results
- MR-CDM reduces MAE by approximately 6-10% compared to state-of-the-art baselines on ETTh1, synthetic, weather, and traffic datasets
- The model demonstrates strong generalization across different domains with consistent performance improvements
- Ablation studies confirm that trend decomposition, conditional diffusion, and image fusion components each contribute critically to overall performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MR-CDM's effectiveness stems from its ability to decompose complex time series into interpretable multi-scale components, then represent these components in image space where diffusion models excel at capturing spatial-temporal patterns. The conditional aspect allows the model to incorporate historical context during generation, while the multi-resolution approach ensures both coarse trends and fine details are preserved. By transforming time series into image representations, the model leverages powerful diffusion techniques that have proven successful in computer vision, adapting them to the unique characteristics of temporal data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD)**: Decomposes signals into intrinsic mode functions to capture multi-scale trends; needed because raw time series often contain overlapping patterns at different frequencies; quick check: verify decomposition produces meaningful trend components
- **Diffusion Models**: Iterative denoising process for generating complex data distributions; needed because they excel at capturing high-dimensional data structure; quick check: assess sample quality and diversity in generated sequences
- **Image-based Time Series Representation**: Transforms temporal patterns into 2D images for spatial processing; needed because standard neural architectures are optimized for grid-structured data; quick check: validate that image transformations preserve temporal dependencies
- **Conditional Generation**: Conditions generation on historical context; needed because forecasting requires incorporating past information; quick check: verify conditioning effectively influences generated outputs
- **Hierarchical Processing**: Multi-scale decomposition and reconstruction; needed because real-world time series exhibit patterns at multiple temporal resolutions; quick check: evaluate performance across different trend timescales

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Data Preprocessing -> EMD Decomposition -> Image Transformation -> Conditional Diffusion Model -> Forecast Generation -> Post-processing
**Critical Path**: The EMD decomposition and image transformation stages are critical as errors here propagate through the entire generation process; the conditional diffusion model represents the core computational bottleneck
**Design Tradeoffs**: Multi-scale decomposition provides interpretability but adds computational overhead; image representations enable spatial processing but require careful alignment with temporal dynamics; conditional generation improves accuracy but increases model complexity
**Failure Signatures**: Poor decomposition quality leads to distorted representations; inadequate conditioning results in trend drift; resolution mismatches between components cause information loss
**First Experiments**: 1) Validate EMD decomposition quality on synthetic signals with known components; 2) Test image transformation preservation of temporal correlations; 3) Benchmark conditional vs unconditional diffusion performance on simple forecasting tasks

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several open questions including the need for better uncertainty quantification in generated forecasts, scalability to extremely long time series, and extension to multivariate settings with complex inter-series dependencies. The authors also highlight the need for more efficient training procedures and investigation of alternative decomposition methods beyond EMD.

## Limitations
- Evaluation focuses on point forecasts rather than probabilistic forecasting quality, limiting assessment of uncertainty quantification capabilities
- Performance comparisons don't include recent transformer-based approaches that have emerged in the forecasting literature
- Ablation studies demonstrate component importance but don't explore the full architectural design space or alternative decomposition methods

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Superior performance on established benchmark datasets (ETTh1, weather, traffic) with multiple runs and statistical validation
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about handling variable-length sequences and cross-domain generalization supported but would benefit from more diverse dataset coverage
- **Medium confidence**: Effectiveness of dual image representation and adaptive diffusion theoretically sound but could be further validated through additional ablation studies
