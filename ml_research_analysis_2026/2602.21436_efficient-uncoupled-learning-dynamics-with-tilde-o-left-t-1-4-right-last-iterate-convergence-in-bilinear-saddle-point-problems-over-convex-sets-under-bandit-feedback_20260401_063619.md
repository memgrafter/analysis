# Efficient Uncoupled Learning Dynamics with $\tilde{O}\!\left(T^{-1/4}\right)$ Last-Iterate Convergence in Bilinear Saddle-Point Problems over Convex Sets under Bandit Feedback

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.21436
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21436
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: The algorithm achieves high-probability last-iterate convergence to an $\varepsilon_k$-approximate Nash equilibrium with $\varepsilon_k = \mathrm{poly}(n,m,\log(k/\delta)) \cdot k^{-1/4}$, the first such guarantee under bandit feedback for convex sets.

## Executive Summary
We consider bilinear saddle-point games where two players have compact convex action sets and only observe the payoff of their chosen actions (bandit feedback). Prior work either provided only average-iterate convergence or required richer feedback. This paper introduces an uncoupled learning dynamic that achieves high-probability last-iterate convergence to a Nash equilibrium with a $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/4})$ rate (up to polynomial factors in dimensions). The method combines a phase-based reduction that transforms average-iterate guarantees into last-iterate ones, an experimental design-based unbiased reward estimator using a volumetric spanner for exploration, and an Optimistic FTRL update with a regularizer derived from an ellipsoidal approximation of the symmetrized action set. The algorithm requires only a linear optimization oracle over the action sets and is computationally efficient.

## Method Summary
The algorithm proceeds in phases indexed by $t$. At the start of phase $t$, the row player maintains an average strategy $\bar{x}_t = \frac{1}{t}\sum_{\ell=1}^t \tilde{x}_\ell$ from past OFTRL outputs $\tilde{x}_\ell$. In each of the $B_t = \lceil \ln(8t^2/\delta) \cdot t^3\rceil$ rounds of phase $t$, the player mixes: with probability $1/2$ plays $\bar{x}_t$; with probability $1/2$ samples $z$ from a fixed exploration distribution $D_X$ (uniform over a volumetric spanner $S_X \subset X$) and plays $(1-\lambda_t)\bar{x}_t + \lambda_t z$, where $\lambda_t = t^{-2}$. The column player does analogously. After data collection, the row player constructs an unbiased estimator $\hat{\theta}^x_t$ of the mean utility vector by pairing exploitation rounds with exploration rounds (using the transformation $\tilde{r} = (r_{\text{expl}} - (1-\lambda_t) r_{\text{explo}})/\lambda_t$) and solving a least-squares problem with the precomputed covariance $V_X = \frac{1}{|S_X|}\sum_{z\in S_X} zz^\top$. The phase utility is $\hat{u}^x_t = t\hat{\theta}^x_t - (t-1)\hat{\theta}^x_{t-1}$. The OFTRL update then computes $\tilde{x}_{t+1} = \arg\max_{x\in X} \langle x, \sum_{\ell=1}^t \hat{u}^x_\ell \rangle - \frac{1}{\eta} \phi(x)$ with step size $\eta=1/6$ and regularizer $\phi(x) = \frac{1}{2}x^\top H_X x$. The matrix $H_X$ comes from an ellipsoid $E$ tightly enclosing the symmetrized set $K = \operatorname{conv}(X\cup(-X))$; this ensures $\phi$ is 1-strongly convex in the primal norm $\|z\|_X = \max_{\|y\|_{*,X}\le 1} \langle y,z\rangle$ (dual norm $\|y\|_{*,X} = \max_{x\in X} |\langle x,y\rangle|$) and has bounded Bregman divergence. The process repeats. After $T$ phases, the current iterate $(x_k, y_k)$ satisfies the desired bound.

## Key Results
- First high-probability last-iterate convergence guarantee for bilinear saddle-point problems under bandit feedback with convex action sets.
- Achieves an $\varepsilon_k = \mathrm{poly}(n,m,\log(k/\delta)) \cdot k^{-1/4}$ approximation to Nash equilibrium, improving upon the previous expected $O(T^{-1/6})$ rate.
- The algorithm is computationally efficient, requiring only a linear optimization oracle over the action sets and using a regularizer derived from an ellipsoidal approximation of the symmetrized set.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Claim: The phase-based framework transforms average-iterate convergence to last-iterate convergence by playing strategies that track the running average of OFTRL outputs.
Mechanism: Each phase $t$ runs for $B_t$ rounds. With probability $1/2$, play the current average $\bar{x}_t$; with probability $1/2$, play a mixed point $(1-\lambda_t)\bar{x}_t + \lambda_t z$ where $z \sim D_X$ (exploration). This keeps the actual iterate near the equilibrium estimate while enabling utility estimation through paired observations. The mixing parameter $\lambda_t = t^{-2}$ vanishes slowly enough that played strategies are $O(\lambda_t)$-close to $\bar{x}_t$, and the phase utility estimate $\hat{u}^x_t = t\hat{\theta}^x_t - (t-1)\hat{\theta}^x_{t-1}$ is sufficiently accurate with high probability.
Core assumption: The batch size $B_t$ scales as $\Theta(t^3)$ and $\lambda_t$ decays as $t^{-2}$; the estimator error is $O(1/\sqrt{B_t}) = O(t^{-3/2})$, which is dominated by $\lambda_t$.
Evidence anchors: Algorithm 1 lines 2-6; Section 3 analysis (Eq. (5) and subsequent bounds).
Break condition: If $\lambda_t$ decays faster than $O(1/t^2)$ (e.g., $1/t^3$), the exploration component vanishes too quickly, causing estimation error accumulation and breaking the $O(T^{-1/4})$ guarantee. If $B_t$ is too small, concentration fails.

### Mechanism 2
Claim: An unbiased estimator $\hat{\theta}^x_t$ of the true utility vector $\bar{\theta}_x_t = A \bar{y}_t$ concentrates in the primal-norm $\|\cdot\|_X$ with high probability, using a volumetric spanner for exploration.
Mechanism: In each phase, pair rounds where $x = \bar{x}_t$ (noise-only) with rounds where $x = (1-\lambda_t)\bar{x}_t + \lambda_t z$ ($z$ uniform over a fixed set $S$). Form transformed rewards $\tilde{r} = (r' - (1-\lambda_t)r)/\lambda_t$, which are $\langle z, \theta\rangle$ plus independent noise. Ordinary least squares on these pairs yields $\hat{\theta}^x_t$. The set $S$ satisfies $\sup_{x\in X} x^\top V^{-1} x \le 2n^2$, where $V = \frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i=1}^{|S|} x_i x_i^\top$.
Core assumption: $X$ admits a volumetric spanner $S$ (polynomial-time computable via linear optimization oracle). The noise is independent and subgaussian (bounded rewards imply subgaussian).
Evidence anchors: Section 2.1, Lemma 2.1: $\Pr( \sup_{x\in X} |\langle x, \hat{\theta}^x_t - \bar{\theta}_x^t\rangle| \le 48\sqrt{n^3/t^3}) \ge 1-\delta/(4t^2)$. Appendix A.1 Eq. (1) and construction of pairs.
Break condition: If the spanner condition fails (e.g., $X$ has extreme aspect ratio), the constant in the bound could be larger, or the bound might not hold. If rewards are not subgaussian, concentration degrades.

### Mechanism 3
Claim: The regularizer $\phi(x) = \frac{1}{2}x^\top H x$, derived from an ellipsoid $E$ tightly enclosing the symmetrized action set $K = \operatorname{conv}(X\cup -X)$, is 1-strongly convex in the primal norm $\|\cdot\|_X$ and has polynomially bounded Bregman divergence.
Mechanism: Define primal norm $\|z\|_X = \max_{\|y\|_{*,X}\le 1} \langle y,z\rangle$ with dual $\|y\|_{*,X} = \max_{x\in X} |\langle x,y\rangle|$. Compute ellipsoid $E = \{x: x^\top H x \le 1\}$ such that $E \subseteq K \subseteq \alpha E$, $\alpha = \sqrt{d(d+1)}$. Then $\phi$ is 1-strongly convex w.r.t. $\|\cdot\|_X$ and for any $x,y\in X$, $D_\phi(x,y) \le 2\alpha^2 \le 2d(d+1)$.
Core assumption: $K$ is well-approximated by an ellipsoid (true for any convex body via John’s theorem). Linear optimization over $K$ reduces to two linear optimizations over $X$, so the oracle remains efficient.
Evidence anchors: Section 2.2.1: Lemma 2.2 shows $\|\cdot\|_X$ and $\|\cdot\|_{*,X}$ are primal-dual with unit ball $K$. Lemma 2.3: $\phi$ is 1-strongly convex in $\|\cdot\|_X$. Proposition 2.4: $D_\phi(x,y) \le 2d(d+1)$.
Break condition: If the ellipsoid approximation factor $\alpha$ is large (e.g., $K$ is very non-elliptical), the strong convexity constant in the proof may degrade, weakening the RVU bound. If $H$ cannot be computed exactly, numerical errors could violate the 1-strong convexity.

## Foundational Learning
### Unbiased reward estimation under bandit feedback
Why needed: Players only observe $\langle x_k, A y_k\rangle$ for their chosen $x_k$. To apply a linear optimization algorithm, they need an estimate of the full utility vector $A \bar{y}_t$. The algorithm ingeniously uses paired observations to cancel the opponent’s average effect.
Quick check: How does the transformation $\tilde{r} = (r_{\text{expl}} - (1-\lambda_t) r_{\text{explo}})/\lambda_t$ produce an unbiased estimate of $\langle z, \theta\rangle$? Which terms cancel?  
Answer: Write $r_{\text{explo}} = \langle (1-\lambda_t)\bar{x}_t + \lambda_t z, \theta\rangle + \xi_{\text{explo}}$, $r_{\text{expl}} = \langle \bar{x}_t, \theta\rangle + \xi_{\text{expl}}$. Then $\tilde{r} = (\langle (1-\lambda_t)\bar{x}_t + \lambda_t z, \theta\rangle + \xi' - (1-\lambda_t)(\langle \bar{x}_t, \theta\rangle + \xi))/\lambda_t = \langle z, \theta\rangle + (\xi' - (1-\lambda_t)\xi)/\lambda_t$. The terms involving $\bar{x}_t$ cancel.

### Optimistic FTRL (OFTRL) and the RVU property
Why needed: OFTRL is the average-iterate engine. The RVU (Regret-Variance-Undershooting) property bounds the sum $\langle u_t, x - \tilde{x}_t\rangle$ in terms of regularizer diameter, utility variation, and iterate movement—even when $u_t$ is estimated. This stability is key for last-iterate guarantees.
Quick check: What are the three terms in the RVU inequality? Which term penalizes large changes between iterates, and why is that important for last-iterate convergence?  
Answer: The RVU bound (e.g., from Syrgkanis et al.) states $\sum_{t=1}^T \langle u_t, x - \tilde{x}_t\rangle \le \frac{D_\phi(x,\tilde{x}_1)}{\eta} + \frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T \|u_t\|_*^2 + \sum_{t=1}^T D_\phi(\tilde{x}_t, \tilde{x}_{t+1})$. The last term penalizes large changes between iterates; controlling it ensures that the average iterate does not drift far from any fixed point, facilitating conversion to last-iterate convergence.

### Geometry-matched primal-dual norms
Why needed: The estimator’s concentration is in $\max_{x\in X} |\langle x, \text{error}\rangle| = \|\text{error}\|_{*,X}$. The regularizer must be strongly convex in the dual norm’s primal ($\|\cdot\|_X$) to control the OFTRL regret. These norms are defined directly from $X$ via its symmetrization $K$.
Quick check: Show that the unit ball of $\|\cdot\|_X$ is exactly $K = \operatorname{conv}(X\cup -X)$. How does this connect to the definition $\|z\|_X = \max_{\|y\|_{*,X}\le 1} \langle y,z\rangle$?  
Answer: By definition, $\|z\|_X = \max_{x\in X} |\langle x,z\rangle|$ because $\|y\|_{*,X} = \max_{x\in X} |\langle x,y\rangle|$ and the dual norm’s unit ball is $\{y: \max_{x\in X} |\langle x,y\rangle| \le 1\}$. The set $\{z: \|z\|_X \le 1\} = \bigcap_{x\in X} \{z: |\langle x,z\rangle| \le 1\}$ is the intersection of symmetric half-spaces, which equals $\operatorname{conv}(X\cup -X)$ if $X$ is convex and contains the origin (or after symmetrization). More precisely, the polar of $\{y: \|y\|_{*,X}\le 1\}$ is $\{z: \|z\|_X \le 1\}$, and that polar is $\operatorname{conv}(X\cup -X)$ because the polar of $\operatorname{conv}(X\cup -X)$ is $\{y: \sup_{x\in X} |\langle x,y\rangle| \le 1\}$.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Exploration sampler -> Phase manager -> Strategy selector -> Estimator builder -> Phase utility calculator -> OFTRL updater (with Regularizer generator) -> Phase manager

Critical path:
- Phase $t$ starts with $\bar{x}_t$ from previous OFTRL output.
- Run $B_t$ rounds: sample mixture, observe rewards, record pairs.
- Build estimator $\hat{\theta}^x_t$, compute phase utility $\hat{u}^x_t$.
- Update OFTRL to get $\tilde{x}_{t+1}$.
- Increment $t$, repeat.
- After total $T$ phases, total rounds $\approx \sum_{t=1}^T B_t \sim T^4$.

Design tradeoffs:
- Batch size $B_t \sim t^3$: Larger batches reduce estimation variance ($\propto 1/B_t$) but delay OFTRL updates. The $t^3$ scaling balances with $\lambda_t \sim t^{-2}$ to achieve $\varepsilon_k \sim \mathrm{poly}(n,m,\log(k/\delta)) \cdot k^{-1/4}$.
- Mixing $\lambda_t \sim 1/t^2$: Must dominate estimation error ($O(1/\sqrt{B_t}) \sim 1/t^{3/2}$) to keep played strategies close to $\bar{x}_t$, yet vanish to ensure last-iterate convergence.
- Regularizer choice: Using Euclidean norm ($I$) would not be 1-strongly convex in $\|\cdot\|_X$ unless $X$ is a Euclidean ball. The ellipsoidal $H$ matches the primal norm, keeping strong convexity constant $=1$ and $D_\phi \le O(d^2)$. Any other regularizer risks weakening the RVU bound.

Failure signatures:
- Estimation breakdown: $\hat{\theta}^x_t$ deviates from $\bar{\theta}_x^t$ by more than $O(\sqrt{n^3/t^3})$. Check by logging $\sup_{x\in S} |\langle x, \hat{\theta}^x_t\rangle|$ and comparing across phases; sudden spikes indicate spanner or noise issues.
- Regularizer invalidation: If $H$ is computed with numerical error, the smallest eigenvalue of $\nabla^2\phi$ might be $<1$ in the $\|\cdot\|_X$ metric. Verify by sampling points and approximating $\|\nabla\phi(u)-\nabla\phi(v)\|_{*,X}/\|u-v\|_X$.
- Phase length violation: If actual $B_t$ is smaller than prescribed, concentration may fail. Monitor $B_t$ implementation against $t^3$ scaling; a 10% shortfall could degrade the rate.

First 3 experiments:
1. Ablate batch size scaling: Implement $B_t = c\cdot t^\alpha$ with $\alpha\in\{1,2,3\}$, fix $\lambda_t = 1/t^2$. Measure $\varepsilon_k$ over many runs. Expect $\alpha=3$ matches theory; $\alpha<3$ leads to larger constants and possible high-probability violations; $\alpha>3$ may improve constants but increase total rounds.
2. Test exploration distribution: Replace uniform over spanner $S$ with uniform over $X$ (if feasible) or a Gaussian-like distribution. Compare estimation error $\|\hat{\theta}^x_t - \bar{\theta}_x^t\|_{*,X}$ and final $\varepsilon_k$. Expect significantly larger errors and degraded convergence, illustrating the necessity of the volumetric spanner condition.
3. Regularizer ablation: Use $\phi(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|x\|_2^2$ (Euclidean) instead of ellipsoidal. Run algorithm and observe if last-iterate convergence still occurs and at what empirical rate. Expect slower convergence because $D_\phi$ may be larger for points in $X$, weakening the movement penalty term in RVU.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What is the tight lower bound on the last-iterate convergence rate for bilinear saddle-point problems under bandit feedback, and can we design uncoupled learning dynamics that achieve this rate? The current $\tilde{O}(T^{-1/4})$ rate improves on a previous expected $O(T^{-1/6})$, but the known lower bound is only $\Omega(T^{-1/3})$ for probability simplices; the tight bound for general convex sets remains open.
- Does there exist a simpler dynamics that applies optimistic FTRL in each round and attains last-iterate convergence under bandit feedback, rather than relying on phased updates and the involved sampling procedure? The proposed algorithm is complex; a simpler per-round variant is unknown.
- Can these results be generalized to convex-concave functions and monotone games, achieving last-iterate convergence under bandit feedback? The paper focuses on bilinear games; extension to broader classes is an open challenge.

## Limitations
- Ellipsoid construction for the symmetrized set $K$ is only sketched; for general convex sets it may require solving an SDP or iterative methods, introducing numerical approximations and potential errors.
- The fallback to a zero utility estimator when phase sample counts are insufficient is unanalyzed; this could cause large transient errors or divergence, especially in early phases.
- Total rounds scale as $\sum_{t=1}^T B_t \approx T^4$ due to $B_t = \Theta(t^3)$, making the algorithm impractical for large $T$ despite its asymptotic rate.

## Confidence
- Last-iterate convergence rate ($\tilde{O}(T^{-1/4})$ high-probability): Medium (relies on correct instantiation of spanner, estimator, regularizer; full proof not provided).
- Unbiased estimator concentration (Lemma 2.1): High (standard least-squares concentration with volumetric spanner; bound explicit).
- Regularizer properties (1-strong convexity w.r.t. $\|\cdot\|_X$, bounded Bregman divergence): Medium (ellipsoid approximation factor $\alpha = \sqrt{d(d+1)}$ holds by John’s theorem, but for poorly conditioned sets $\alpha$ may be large; exact $H$ computation may be numerically delicate).
- Computational efficiency (only a linear-optimization oracle): High (OFTRL update reduces to linear optimization over $X$, matching standard assumptions).
