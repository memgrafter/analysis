# Understanding Annotation Error Propagation and Learning an Adaptive Policy for Expert Intervention in Barrett's Video Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.21855  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21855  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** L2RP improves mean Dice: mask prompts from 0.7371 to 0.8436 on Barrett's dataset and from 0.5466 to 0.7307 on SUN-SEG benchmark (statistical significance p<10⁻⁶).  

## Executive Summary
Solving video segmentation via interactive prompting (e.g., SAM2) is hindered by temporal error propagation: small inaccuracies in an initial mask can accumulate as the model processes subsequent frames, eventually requiring costly manual correction. This paper introduces Learning-to-Re-Prompt (L2RP), a cost‑aware framework that learns an adaptive deferral policy. The policy decides *if* and *when* to request an expert correction during annotation, trading off segmentation accuracy against human effort via a tunable parameter λ_corr. Extensive evaluation on a private Barrett’s endoscopy dataset and the public SUN-SEG benchmark shows L2RP significantly outperforms naive strategies (e.g., always correcting at the midpoint or random frames) across mask, box, and point prompts, demonstrating the value of learned intervention for efficient human‑AI collaboration.

## Method Summary
L2RP freezes the base video segmentor (SAM2) and introduces a trainable deferral model Dθ (a R(2+1)D network) that scores candidate correction frames. Given a video clip and an initial prompt (mask, box, or point) on frame 0, SAM2 first propagates the mask to all frames, producing initial masks ˆM^(0). Dθ then processes a sparse subset of these frames (typically 10 evenly spaced) together with their propagated masks. It outputs a score for deferring at each frame; a fixed score of 0 represents “no correction”. During inference, the frame with the lowest positive score is selected if it beats the no‑defer score, triggering an expert correction at that frame. The correction prompt (assumed correct) is combined with the initial prompt, and SAM2 re‑propagates to yield the final masks. During training, Dθ is optimized with a surrogate loss (LL2RP‑MAE) derived from a cost‑sensitive objective that combines the segmentation Dice loss after correction with a penalty λ_corr for human effort. The loss is differentiable and enables end‑to‑end learning of the deferral policy.

## Key Results
- **Mask prompts:** Dice increased from 0.7371→0.8436 (Barrett’s) and 0.5466→0.7307 (SUN‑SEG); both improvements are statistically significant (p<10⁻⁶).  
- **Other prompt types:** L2RP also yields substantial gains for box and point prompts on both datasets.  
- **Effort‑accuracy trade‑off:** Varying λ_corr (e.g., 0.01, 0.06, 0.08) smoothly adjusts the number of corrections and the final Dice, confirming the framework’s cost awareness.  
- **Temporal drift reduction:** By strategically inserting corrections, L2RP mitigates the rapid error growth characteristic of mask prompts and stabilizes segmentation over long video sequences.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Selective correction via cost‑sensitive learning.** The deferral model is trained to minimize an expected cost that balances segmentation error (computed after re‑propagation with a correction) against a tunable human‑effort penalty λ_corr. The surrogate loss (Equation 3) explicitly compares the cost of acting at any candidate frame versus acting never. This objective encourages the model to request corrections at frames where the improvement in overall video‑level Dice per unit of expert input is highest, leading to a learned policy that outperforms heuristics.
2. **Prompt‑specific propagation dynamics are implicitly captured.** The paper’s analysis reveals that mask prompts start with higher accuracy but suffer quickly from boundary drift due to motion and appearance changes; box prompts degrade more gradually; point prompts are initially coarse but more stable over time. The deferral model receives propagated masks from the initial prompt as part of its input, enabling it to detect these dynamics and adjust timing accordingly—for instance, placing corrections earlier for masks and later (or less often) for points.
3. **Tunable human‑cost parameter shifts the operating point.** Increasing λ_corr raises the penalty for asking a correction, making the policy more conservative; decreasing it encourages more deferrals. Empirically (Section 5.3), Dice declines monotonically as λ_corr grows, confirming that λ_corr effectively controls the accuracy‑effort trade‑off.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Video clip + initial prompt p0 → SAM2 → initial propagated masks ˆM^(0) → Deferral model Dθ (R(2+1)D) processes a subset J of {I_k, ˆM_k^(0)} → scores [d1,…,dT] (d0=0 fixed) → decision: if min(d1..dT) < d0, set k* = argmin(d1..dT); else k*=0 → if k*>0, obtain correction p_k* (GT for training, expert for deployment) and re‑run SAM2 with {p0, p_k*} → final masks; else final masks = ˆM^(0).

**Critical path**  
1. Precomputation: For each training clip, run SAM2 once with p0 to obtain all ˆM^(0).  
2. Feature extraction: Sample J frames (e.g., 10) and gather their images and ˆM^(0).  
3. Scoring: Forward pass through Dθ to produce [d1,…,dT].  
4. Decision: Choose k* based on scores.  
5. Conditional re‑propagation: If k*>0, simulate or obtain p_k* and re‑run SAM2 to get final masks for loss calculation.  
6. Loss computation: Compute LL2RP‑MAE based on final Dice over all frames and λ_corr; backpropagate to update Dθ.  

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Sparse frame evaluation*: Using only |J| sampled frames reduces compute but risks missing temporal events if sampling is too coarse.  
- *Fixed SAM2*: Keeps training stable but prevents the base segmenter from adapting to the deferral policy; joint training could help but complicates optimization.  
- *Surrogate loss (MAE)*: Chosen for gradient stability; alternatives (e.g., cross‑entropy) might converge faster but could be sensitive to class imbalance.  
- *Prompt consistency*: Using the same prompt type for initial and correction simplifies the problem but may not be optimal if different prompts have distinct cost‑accuracy profiles.  

**Failure signatures**  
- Deferral model collapses: always outputs k*=0 (no deferral) or a constant value.  
- High variance in Dice across clips for a fixed λ_corr, indicating poor generalization of the trade‑off.  
- No improvement over baseline policies (Random, Midpoint).  

**First three experiments**  
1. **Propagation profiling** – On a held‑out set, run SAM2 with initial prompts only and plot Dice vs. frame for each prompt type; verify that mask degrades fastest, point most stable.  
2. **λ_corr sanity check** – With a pretrained Dθ, run inference across λ_corr values; confirm that increasing λ_corr yields fewer or later deferrals (oracle Dice expected to drop).  
3. **Frame subset sensitivity** – Train Dθ with varying |J| (e.g., 5, 10, 20) on a small subset; compare validation Dice and training stability to select an efficient |J|.  

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How do different prompt types mechanistically influence the rate and pattern of error propagation in SAM2?**  
   *Basis:* The introduction states that the effect of prompt types on temporal error propagation is unclear.  
   *Why unresolved:* The analysis remains descriptive; no probing of SAM2’s internal dynamics (e.g., attention, feature matching) explains why masks drift faster than points.  
   *What would resolve:* Ablations isolating SAM2 components, error analysis stratified by video characteristics (motion, occlusion), or attention‑map studies.  

2. **Does the L2RP deferral policy implicitly learn different intervention strategies depending on the initial prompt type?**  
   *Basis:* The paper reports results averaged over prompt types but does not analyze deferral behavior per prompt.  
   *Why unresolved:* The focus is on final Dice, not on the distribution of deferral frames or frequencies across prompt types.  
   *What would resolve:* Comparing deferral statistics (e.g., average number of deferrals, selected frame indices) across prompt types, or training separate policies per prompt and measuring performance differences.  

3. **Can the cost parameter λ_corr be automatically optimized per video based on content‑specific error drift rates?**  
   *Basis:* λ_corr is a static, manually tuned hyperparameter balancing effort and accuracy.  
   *Why unresolved:* Videos differ in drift propensity; a single λ_corr may be suboptimal across heterogeneous endoscopic scenes.  
   *What would resolve:* Quantify correlation between λ_corr sensitivity and measurable video features (e.g., optical flow magnitude, texture complexity) and develop a meta‑model that predicts optimal λ_corr from coarse video attributes.  

## Limitations
- Exact R(2+1)D architecture details (depth, normalization) are unspecified, forcing guesswork for replication.  
- The simulation of correction prompts is ambiguous: for points, how GT masks are converted to points is unclear and may artificially inflate performance.  
- Training cost: full‑video SAM2 re‑propagation for each candidate correction is expensive; the paper does not detail caching or approximation strategies.  
- Heavy reliance on the private Barrett’s dataset limits external verification; SUN‑SEG results still depend on SAM2’s fixed weights.
