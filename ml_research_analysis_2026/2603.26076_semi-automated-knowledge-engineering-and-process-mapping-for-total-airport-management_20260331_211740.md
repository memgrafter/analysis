# Semi-Automated Knowledge Engineering and Process Mapping for Total Airport Management

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26076
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26076
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduced a scaffolded fusion framework that combines expert-curated Knowledge Engineering with LLMs, achieving high-fidelity triple extraction (Precision 0.961–0.967, Recall 0.971–0.982, F1 0.966–0.975) and provable source provenance for Total Airport Management.

## Executive Summary
Airport operations are hindered by data silos and semantic inconsistencies, which impede Total Airport Management. This paper presents a semi-automated knowledge engineering framework that fuses expert-curated ontologies (derived from NASA ATM and Changi Airport Petri nets) with large language models (LLMs) to extract machine-readable, auditable knowledge triples from unstructured operational manuals. Using the LangExtract library and deterministic string alignment, every extracted triple is traced back to its source sentence. Evaluated on the EUROCONTROL A-CDM manual, the approach achieves near-perfect precision, recall, and F1 scores. Notably, processing the entire document at once improves recall by 38.5% by capturing non-linear procedural dependencies, enabling automated generation of stakeholder-attributed swimlane diagrams and supporting real-time airport decision-making.

## Method Summary
The core method is a scaffolded symbolic fusion: an expert-built ontology defines classes (Procedure, Sequenced Item, Stakeholder) and relations (hasNext, hasStakeholder), which are used to generate a KG schema. This schema guides the construction of system prompts and few-shot examples for an LLM (gemini-2.5-flash) via the LangExtract library. The LLM processes the source document (either page-wise or as a whole) and outputs structured triples with cited source sentences. A deterministic string alignment step (`text.find()` and `difflib.SequenceMatcher`) verifies that each triple can be mapped to a specific sentence, ensuring an audit trail. The validated triples populate an OWL knowledge graph (using `owlready2`), and a topological sort algorithm produces swimlane diagrams. Reproduction requires the A-CDM manual, recreation of the exact ontology (unavailable), and implementation of the alignment logic.

## Key Results
- **High-fidelity extraction:** Precision 0.961–0.967, Recall 0.971–0.982, F1 0.966–0.975 on the A-CDM manual.
- **Recall gain from document-level context:** 38.5% improvement over page-level processing by capturing non-linear dependencies.
- **Full provenance:** Every triple is provably traceable to its source sentence, enabling auditability and automated swimlane diagram generation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Expert-curated Knowledge Engineering (KE) structures guide LLM prompts to produce semantically aligned knowledge triples, improving precision and recall over unstructured LLM extraction.
- **Mechanism:** A manually built ontology (from NASA ATM and Changi Petri nets) defines classes and relations. This schema generates few-shot examples and system prompts, constraining the LLM's output to domain-valid entities and relationships. The scaffold reduces hallucination by aligning the model's probabilistic generation with deterministic logical structures.
- **Core assumption:** The expert-built ontology accurately captures the core semantics of airport operations and is sufficiently comprehensive to cover the target document's (A-CDM manual) key concepts.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Scaffolded fusion strategy in which expert-curated KE structures guide LLM prompts to facilitate the discovery of semantically aligned knowledge triples."
  - [section III.A] Details the ontology layers and class schema (Procedure, Sequenced Item, Stakeholder) used to guide extraction.
  - [corpus] *No direct evidence from provided corpus neighbors; support inferred from literature review on KE/LLM fusion.*
- **Break condition:** If the source ontology is incomplete or misaligned with the document's specific jargon/processes, the prompts will constrain extraction toward an incorrect schema, reducing recall for document-specific entities.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Deterministic string alignment (e.g., `text.find()`, `difflib.SequenceMatcher`) fused with the LLM's output ensures every extracted triple has verifiable, sentence-level source provenance.
- **Mechanism:** After the LLM proposes a triple (subject-predicate-object), the system programmatically searches the source text for verbatim or fuzzy matches to the generated strings. This anchors each assertion to a specific location, creating an audit trail and mitigating "black-box" risks.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM's output strings are textually similar enough to the source text to be found via substring or sequence matching. Complex paraphrases may fail this check.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Every extracted triple is provably traceable to its source text."
  - [section IV] "LangExtract successfully mapped all extracted triples to their respective source sentences... via text.find() and difflib.SequenceMatcher()."
  - [corpus] *Weak; corpus focuses on datasets/prediction, not provenance techniques.*
- **Break condition:** The LLM generates triples with significant lexical deviation from the source text (e.g., synonym substitution, rephrasing), causing the alignment algorithms to fail and breaking the provenance chain.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Document-level context processing (vs. page-level chunks) improves recall by 38.5% by capturing non-linear, inverted, or dispersed procedural dependencies.
- **Mechanism:** Airport process descriptions often state an effect before its cause (e.g., "Once the aircraft is de-iced, the doors may be opened" might be written as "Doors may be opened after de-icing"). Processing the entire document in one context allows the LLM to see the full logical flow and reorder events correctly. Chunking fragments these dependencies.
- **Core assumption:** The essential logical dependencies for a given procedure span more than a single page and are written in a non-linear order.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Contrary to expectations, document-level processing improved recall by 38.5% by capturing non-linear procedural dependencies."
  - [section IV] "The primary driver of FNs... is the inversion of logical flow... long-context window mitigates this by providing the broader context."
  - [corpus] *No direct evidence; neighboring papers focus on prediction/datasets.*
- **Break condition:** If the document's procedural descriptions are strictly linear and self-contained within single pages, the larger context window introduces unnecessary noise ("lost-in-the-middle") without recall benefit, potentially harming precision.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Knowledge Graph (KG) Ontology Design
- **Why needed here:** The entire extraction scaffold depends on a correct, domain-specific ontology (classes, properties). An engineer must understand how to define a schema that balances expressiveness (capturing airport processes) with tractability (for LLM prompting). They must also grasp modular disclosure for multi-stakeholder data silos.
- **Quick check question:** Given the classes `Procedure`, `Sequenced Item`, and `Stakeholder`, and relations `hasNext` and `hasStakeholder`, how would you model a process step where the *Ground Handler* "loads baggage" *after* "aircraft arrival"? What would be the subject, object, and predicate for the sequence and the stakeholder assignment?

### Concept: LLM Prompting with Deterministic Constraints
- **Why needed here:** The system uses the KG schema to build few-shot examples and system prompts that force structured output (triples). An engineer needs to craft prompts that convert a flexible LLM into a reliable extractor without destroying its ability to generalize from the examples.
- **Quick check question:** If your few-shot example shows `(Aircraft, hasNext, Deicing)`, but the source text says "Deicing follows aircraft arrival," how should the prompt be designed to ensure the LLM extracts the correct `(AircraftArrival, hasNext, Deicing)` triple and not invent a new entity "Aircraft"?

### Concept: Context Window Trade-offs in Long Documents
- **Why needed here:** The paper's key finding is that longer context improved recall for this specific document type. An engineer must understand the "lost-in-the-middle" problem and when to prioritize recall (long context) vs. precision/attention density (short chunks). This affects system configuration and cost.
- **Quick check question:** What is the "lost-in-the-middle" phenomenon, and why might it be less severe for a procedural document describing an airport turnaround compared to a narrative novel?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (unstructured text) -> KE Scaffolding (ontology → prompts) -> Extraction Engine (LangExtract) -> KG Population (owlready2) -> Artifact Synthesis (swimlane generator)
- **Critical path:** Manual ontology design → prompt generation → LangExtract execution → provenance-validation → KG construction → swimlane rendering. Failure in ontology design propagates to all downstream steps.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Precision vs. Recall: Short-context (page-level) may have higher precision (less noise) but lower recall for cross-page dependencies; long-context (document-level) inverses this.
  - Automation vs. Auditability: Fully automated schema generation would reduce expert effort but sacrifice the "scaffolded" control and initial semantic alignment; the chosen tradeoff favors auditability.
  - Generality vs. Fidelity: Using a general LLM (Gemini) with prompts is more adaptable than fine-tuning a domain model (e.g., AviationGPT), but relies on prompt engineering for fidelity.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low provenance rate: Many extracted triples fail `text.find()`/`difflib` checks → indicates LLM hallucination or major lexical divergence.
  - High False Negatives in long-context: If document-level processing yields *worse* recall than page-level, the "lost-in-the-middle" degradation is overpowering any benefit from non-linear capture.
  - Swimlane logic errors: Incorrect topological ordering in diagrams (e.g., a "hasNext" step placed before its predecessor) signals errors in the extracted `hasNext` relations.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate Table I:** Run LangExtract on the A-CDM manual in both short-context (page) and long-context (document) modes. Verify Precision/Recall/F1 scores match the paper's ~0.96-0.98 range. This validates the core extraction pipeline.
  2. **Provenance Stress Test:** Take a sample of extracted triples classified as `MATCH FUZZY` and manually verify the source sentence correspondence. Check if the alignment algorithms are too lenient (accepting weakly related text) or too strict (rejecting valid paraphrases).
  3. **Swimlane Sanity Check:** Generate a swimlane diagram for a single, well-defined milestone. Manually confirm that 1) steps are ordered correctly according to `hasNext`, 2) each step is in the correct stakeholder lane (`hasStakeholder`), and 3) clicking a node reveals the exact source sentence.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can low-level sensor detections be automatically mapped to high-level KG entities for real-time procedural deviation detection?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section V identifies "entity resolution and data association; specifically, mapping low-level sensor detections to high-level KG entities" as a critical challenge for the proposed real-time verification framework.
- **Why unresolved:** The current pipeline processes only unstructured text; multimodal sensor integration and entity resolution are not addressed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A system that ingests video and transponder streams, links detections to KG entities with high precision/recall, and demonstrates effective deviation flagging in an airport testbed.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can the KG be continuously refined using live operational data (e.g., deviation logs) while preserving provenance and avoiding manual re-annotation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section V describes a feedback loop where "recurring deviations and novel operational patterns ... can inform refinements to both documented procedures and the KG itself," but leaves implementation for future work.
- **Why unresolved:** The presented pipeline is batch-oriented and lacks mechanisms for incremental, provenance-preserving updates.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An automated refinement module that suggests KG edits from deviation data, maintains versioned provenance, and is validated by domain experts for accuracy and utility.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the scaffolded fusion strategy maintain high-fidelity extraction across the broader, more heterogeneous corpus of airport documentation (e.g., landside procedures, proprietary multi-stakeholder specifications)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Evaluation was restricted to the 16-page A-CDM manual; while the framework claims extensibility, it has not been tested on the diverse, fragmented documents typical of the full airport ecosystem.
- **Why unresolved:** Performance on a single structured manual may not generalize to noisier, less-standardized texts with complex cross-references.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A replication study on a diverse set of airport operational documents (e.g., baggage handling, fueling) showing similar precision, recall, and F1 scores (≥95%).

## Limitations
- Exact version of the EUROCONTROL A-CDM manual used is unknown.
- Complete expert-curated ontology and prompt templates are not provided, hindering exact replication.
- Evaluation is limited to a single manual; generalizability to other airport documents or domains remains unproven.

## Confidence
- Core extraction performance (P/R/F1 >0.95) on the specific A-CDM document: **High**
- Provenance guarantee via deterministic string alignment: **Medium**
- Generalizability to other airport documents or domains: **Low**
