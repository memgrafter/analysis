# Uncertainty Quantification for Named Entity Recognition via Full-Sequence and Subsequence Conformal Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.16999
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16999
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Conformal prediction yields finite-sample coverage guarantees for NER prediction sets; nc1-based scores are most efficient across full-sequence, subsequence, and integrated settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces conformal prediction methods to produce uncertainty-aware prediction sets for named entity recognition (NER) with finite-sample coverage guarantees. It proposes three complementary strategies: full-sequence sets over complete labelings, subsequence (entity-level) sets with class-conditional coverage, and an integrated approach combining subsequences via Šidák correction for sentences with multiple entities. Extensive evaluation across three benchmarks and four models demonstrates that full-sequence sets achieve approximately 95% coverage, subsequence sets remain small (average 1.2–2.5 labels) while satisfying conditional coverage, and integrated sets maintain coverage for multi-entity sentences. The study also finds that out-of-domain degradation primarily affects entity classification accuracy rather than span identification.

## Method Summary
The method builds on CRF-based NER models that generate top-K label sequences via beam search. Non-conformity scores (nc1, nc2, nc3) are computed on a held-out calibration set to determine thresholds τ. For full-sequence prediction, all top-K sequences with score ≤ τ are included, with adaptive rounding to match coverage. Subsequence prediction aggregates probabilities of full sequences containing a given span and applies class-specific thresholds to yield entity-level sets. The integrated method filters full-sequence candidates by requiring that all their entity spans appear in the respective subsequence sets, using a Šidák correction on confidence levels when multiple entities are present. Stratification by language and sentence length during calibration ensures valid conditional coverage across heterogeneous inputs.

## Key Results
- Full-sequence conformal sets achieve ~95% coverage; nc1-based scores yield the smallest average set sizes across benchmarks.
- Subsequence (entity-level) sets have small average sizes (1.2–2.5 labels) and satisfy class-conditional coverage guarantees.
- Integrated sets with Šidák correction successfully maintain coverage for sentences containing multiple entities, where naive integration under-covers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Full-Sequence Conformal Prediction via Non-Conformity Scores
The method constructs prediction sets over complete label sequences by thresholding non-conformity scores computed from the CRF's top-K decoded sequences. For a test input, the model produces K ranked sequences with probabilities; a score such as nc1 = 1 − p(y|x) is assigned to each. The threshold τ is set as the (1−α)-quantile of these scores on an exchangeable calibration set. Sequences with scores ≤ τ form the prediction set, guaranteeing coverage under exchangeability. Adaptive rounding via the adaptive conformal prediction (ACP) procedure fine-tunes τ to achieve exact finite-sample coverage. The core assumption is that the CRF's top-K approximation captures most of the true probability mass (K=100 used experimentally). Evidence appears in the abstract's coverage guarantee claim, Section 4.1's score definitions, and Section 4.2's ACP details. Failure occurs if the true sequence lies outside the top-K or if calibration/test distributions differ.

### Mechanism 2: Subsequence-Level Calibration for Entity-Class Conditional Coverage
This mechanism achieves class-conditional coverage for individual entity spans. For each contiguous subsequence y_a:a+b, its probability is the sum of probabilities of all top-K full sequences that contain it. Entity-specific non-conformity scores (e.g., nc1_ent) are defined, and class-wise thresholds τ_w are derived from calibration entities of class w. A predicted span returns class w if its score ≤ τ_w, ensuring coverage conditional on the span's true class. The approach relies on IOB2 tagging to identify entity boundaries and assumes that span probabilities from top-K sequences approximate the true distribution. Formalized in Equations 18–22 and backed by Theorem 4's coverage guarantee. Break conditions include incorrect IOB2 tagging or the true span never appearing in the top-K set, yielding zero probability and missed coverage.

### Mechanism 3: Integrated Prediction Sets with Šidák Correction for Multi-Entity Sentences
The integrated method assembles coherent full-sequence predictions by filtering candidates using the subsequence prediction sets. A full sequence is included only if every entity span it contains is present in the corresponding subsequence set. To control the family-wise error rate when multiple entities are present, the per-entity confidence level is adjusted to 1 − α^(1/ŝ), where ŝ is the number of entities in the top-ranked sequence (Šidák correction). Optional index-based filtering (C_idx) can cap low-probability additions. The method assumes that ŝ reasonably estimates the true number of entities and that span-level non-conformity scores are conditionally independent. Described in Section 6 and Equations 28–33; Table 5 shows Šidák restores coverage for multi-entity sentences. Dependence between span scores or severe misestimation of ŝ can lead to under-coverage.

## Foundational Learning
- **Conditional Random Fields (CRFs)**: Why needed? The NER model uses a linear-chain CRF to capture dependencies between adjacent labels, enabling beam search for top-K sequences. Quick check: How do the emission score W*(y_j,:)·x_j and transmission score W(y_{j-1}, y_j) differ in the CRF formulation, and why are both necessary for decoding?
- **Conformal Prediction Quantiles**: Why needed? Coverage guarantees depend on estimating the (1−α)-quantile of non-conformity scores from an exchangeable calibration set. Quick check: Why does the formula ⌈(1−α)(1+|I_cal|)⌉ produce a valid finite-sample quantile?
- **IOB2 Tagging Scheme**: Why needed? It encodes entity spans via B- and I- prefixes, allowing subsequence prediction sets to operate on contiguous spans of matching class. Quick check: For the label sequence [B-PER, I-PER, O], what is the entity span and class? How would the algorithm in Section 5 evaluate a candidate subsequence y_1:2?

## Architecture Onboarding
Pre-trained encoder -> BiLSTM -> CRF layer -> Calibration module -> Prediction set generator.
Critical path: Train CRF → calibrate thresholds per stratification (language/length) → for test input: decode top-K sequences → compute non-conformity scores → apply threshold τ → optionally apply integrated/Šidák/index filters → output set. Thresholds must be recomputed if model or stratification changes.
Design tradeoffs: nc1 gives small sets but risk under-coverage; nc2 often yields very large sets; nc3 provides fixed-size sets. More strata improve conditional calibration but require more calibration data; binning trades precision for stability. Integrated sets control class-conditional coverage at the cost of larger set sizes.
Failure signatures: Coverage below target on specific strata (e.g., long sentences) indicates insufficient calibration data or poor quantile estimation; average subsequence set size >>1 suggests model uncertainty; nc2 producing huge sets (>10× nc1) calls for switching to nc1 or RAPS.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can conformal prediction be adapted to large language model (LLM) based NER systems, which often use autoregressive generation instead of CRF-based sequence labeling?
- **Basis in paper**: The conclusion states: "The techniques and considerations introduced in this paper can be adapted and extended to these emerging architectures, offering significant potential for future work."
- **Why unresolved**: The proposed method relies on CRF decoding via beam search and a fixed label set; LLMs have different output spaces and probability structures, requiring new nonconformity scores and calibration strategies.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Development of a conformal prediction procedure for seq2seq NER that maintains finite-sample coverage guarantees, evaluated on multiple benchmarks with comparable efficiency to CRF-based approaches.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How should uncertainty from conformal prediction sets in NER be propagated through downstream NLP tasks (e.g., named entity disambiguation, question answering) to improve overall pipeline robustness?
- **Basis in paper**: The conclusion identifies that "research remains sparse on how this uncertainty can be effectively propagated through downstream NLP tasks."
- **Why unresolved**: Downstream components typically consume deterministic entity spans or classes; integrating set-valued outputs while preserving statistical guarantees is non‑trivial and may require joint modeling or custom aggregation rules.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Empirical studies on end‑to‑end pipelines showing that incorporating NER uncertainty into downstream decisions leads to measurable gains in accuracy, calibration, or error detection compared to point predictions.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What is the optimal hyperparameter configuration for the proposed hybrid conformal strategies (naive, conditional, RAPS) and how sensitive are they to the choice of α, β, λ, and index thresholds?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors note: "We did not analyze the optimization aspect of the conditional +nc1 score versus the RAPS +nc1 score and the effect of the hyperparameter grid‑search used in this paper."
- **Why unresolved**: The current experiments used coarse grid searches; the relative efficiency and stability of each method under fine‑grained tuning remain unknown, impacting practical adoption.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A systematic empirical analysis across datasets and models, comparing grid‑search, random search, or Bayesian optimization for selecting α, β, λ, and reporting sensitivity curves for set size and coverage.

## Limitations
- CRF top-K approximation may exclude the true label sequence, causing coverage failure regardless of threshold.
- Exchangeability assumption between calibration and test data is sensitive to distribution shifts.
- Stratification demands large calibration sets; bins for low-resource languages or extreme lengths can be unstable.
- Šidák correction relies on conditional independence of span-level non-conformity; dependence risks under-coverage.
- Evaluation limited to CRF-based NER; generalizability to other architectures (e.g., seq2seq) is unvalidated.

## Confidence
- Coverage guarantees hold theoretically and empirically (~95%): High
- nc1 non-conformity score yields most efficient sets: High
- Stratification improves conditional coverage but limited strata tested: Medium
- Out-of-domain degradation pattern (entity classification vs. span identification): Low
- Extension to non-CRF NER systems: Low
