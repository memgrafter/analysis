# Empirical Stability Analysis of Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks in Hard-Constrained Recurrent Physics-Informed Discovery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09988
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09988
- **Reference count:** 3
- **Primary result:** Small KANs (~120 parameters) achieve discovery R² up to 0.862 on the Duffing oscillator, matching MLP efficiency, but exhibit catastrophic instability on the multiplicative Van der Pol oscillator, with most configurations yielding negative R²; only very shallow KANs under BPTT reach R² up to 0.785, while MLPs consistently outperform (R² up to 0.965 Duffing, 0.898 Van der Pol).

## Executive Summary
This study integrates Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) into hard-constrained recurrent physics-informed networks (HRPINN) to learn residual dynamics in two oscillatory systems. On the Duffing oscillator, where the residual is a separable univariate cubic term, compact KANs perform comparably to MLP baselines. However, on the Van der Pol oscillator—which involves multiplicative state coupling—KANs suffer severe hyperparameter fragility and optimization instability, with most wide or deep configurations failing catastrophically (negative R²). Backpropagation through time (BPTT) partially stabilizes very shallow KANs but does not close the performance gap with MLPs. The results indicate that KAN’s inherent additive inductive bias, derived from the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation theorem, is ill-suited for efficiently representing multiplicative interactions within a hard-constrained recurrent optimization loop.

## Method Summary
The HRPINN framework fixes a physical integrator (e.g., RK4) and known physics terms, isolating learning to a residual branch \(R_\theta\) that predicts the unknown part of the dynamics. The recurrent update is \(x_{t+1} = \text{Integrator}(f_{\text{known}}(x_t) + R_\theta(x_t))\). Two training regimes are used: single-step teacher forcing (TF) and full-trajectory backpropagation through time (BPTT). Residual branches are either KANs (using univariate B-splines) or MLP ReLU networks, with architectures spanning a range of parameter counts (~120 to ~4400). Performance is evaluated via Discovery R², measuring correlation between \(R_\theta\) and the true residual over a 100×100 phase-space grid, and test MSE on held-out trajectories.

## Key Results
- **Duffing (univariate residual):** Small KANs achieve median Discovery R² up to 0.862, matching MLP efficiency, and reproduce the cubic shape in 38% of seeds.
- **Van der Pol (multiplicative residual):** Most KAN configurations fail, yielding negative R²; deeper/wider KANs are catastrophically unstable. Only very shallow KANs under BPTT reach R² up to 0.785.
- **MLP superiority:** MLPs scale robustly, achieving R² 0.965 (Duffing) and 0.898 (Van der Pol under BPTT), with consistent convergence across hyperparameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Additive Inductive Bias for Separable Univariate Terms
- **Claim:** KANs efficiently recover residual dynamics that are separable univariate functions (e.g., a single state’s polynomial term).
- **Mechanism:** KANs replace neurons with learnable univariate B-spline functions. For a Duffing-like residual \(f(x)\), this directly parameterizes a sum \(\sum \psi(x)\) without cross-variable interactions, aligning with the Kolmogorov-Arnold theorem’s additive decomposition for univariate contributions.
- **Core assumption:** The unknown physics manifests as a sum of independent univariate nonlinearities.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes small KANs (~120 parameters) achieved Discovery R² up to 0.862 on Duffing. Section 2 reports that the median small KAN accurately reproduces the cubic shape and captured it in 38% of seeds.

### Mechanism 2: Compositional Instability for Multiplicative Coupling
- **Claim:** The original KAN’s additive bias forces it to approximate multiplicative interactions (\(x \cdot v\)) through deep composition of univariate splines, which becomes unstable under recurrent error accumulation.
- **Mechanism:** Representing a product requires multiple layers to compute identities like \((x+y)^2 - (x-y)^2\). In a hard-constrained recurrent loop, errors at each integration step compound; gradient flow through these composed spline layers becomes ill-conditioned, leading to divergent training or poor manifold recovery.
- **Core assumption:** Recurrent optimization amplifies any optimization instability in the residual branch’s composition depth.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2 states most Van der Pol configurations yield negative R² values indicative of diverging solutions, and deeper KANs remain catastrophically unstable. Section 3 notes deeper vanilla configurations prove highly unstable during recurrent optimization, exacerbated by rapid error accumulation.

### Mechanism 3: Partial Mitigation via BPTT for Shallow KANs
- **Claim:** Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) provides longer-horizon supervision that can stabilize very shallow KANs for weak multiplicative terms, but not enough to surpass MLP performance.
- **Mechanism:** BPTT propagates error through multiple integration steps, offering a smoother learning signal that may help coordinate spline outputs needed for a rough multiplicative approximation. However, the fundamental additive bias still limits expressivity-efficiency trade-offs.
- **Core assumption:** Longer temporal unrolling improves gradient information for learning coupled dynamics but cannot overcome architectural bias.
- **Evidence anchors:** Table 2 shows “Very Small” KAN Van der Pol R² improves from \(0.464 \pm 0.166\) (TF) to \(0.743 \pm 0.061\) (BPTT). Section 3 states switching to full recurrent training with BPTT partially mitigates issues for shallow KANs.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Hard-Constrained Recurrent Physics-Informed Networks (HRPINN)**
  - *Why needed here:* The experiment isolates the residual branch’s learning; the integrator and known physics are fixed, so failures originate from the KAN/MLP, not the overall framework.
  - *Quick check question:* If the known physics were incorrect, how would the residual branch’s learned manifold appear relative to the true residual?

- **Concept: Kolmogorov-Arnold Representation Theorem**
  - *Why needed here:* It is the theoretical foundation for KAN’s additive design. The paper tests its practical utility for multivariate functions, which the theorem guarantees exist but does not specify an efficient architectural decomposition.
  - *Quick check question:* Why does the theorem not guarantee that a shallow, additive network can efficiently learn \((1 - x^2)v\)?

- **Concept: Recurrent Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) Instability**
  - *Why needed here:* KAN fragility is exacerbated by the recurrent loop. Understanding that BPTT gradients vanish/explode in standard RNNs provides an analog for why composed splines might fail under long unrolling.
  - *Quick check question:* How might the sensitivity of spline derivatives affect gradient magnitude over many BPTT steps compared to a ReLU MLP’s constant derivative (in active regions)?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Fixed Physical Integrator -> Residual Branch \(R_\theta\) (KAN or MLP) -> Recurrent Update -> Loss (MSE between predicted and true states)

**Critical path:** Design and training of \(R_\theta\). All systemic behavior hinges on its ability to learn the residual stably within the recurrent loop.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *KAN:* Potentially better parameter efficiency for separable univariate terms; suffers severe hyperparameter sensitivity (grid size \(G\), order \(k\)) and depth instability for multiplicative terms.  
- *MLP:* Robust across hyperparameters; inherently models interactions via first-layer weights; consistently superior scaling in experiments.  
- *Key tradeoff:* Inductive bias (KAN’s additive splines) vs. optimization stability (MLP’s dense linear pre-activations).

**Failure signatures:**  
1. Negative Discovery R² scores → residual manifold anti-correlated with truth, indicating divergent learned dynamics.  
2. High variance across random seeds (large confidence intervals) → hyperparameter fragility.  
3. Failure under width/depth increase → compositional instability.  
4. Surfacing linear/incorrect forms in qualitative plots → inability to capture multiplicative modulation.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Configuration Ablation (Stability Sweep):** For a minimal KAN (1 hidden layer, 10 neurons), systematically vary grid_size (\(G=3,5,10\)) and spline_order (\(k=3,5\)). Train 100 seeds per config on Duffing and Van der Pol under teacher forcing. Record Discovery R² distribution to identify a stable baseline KAN config that avoids catastrophic failure on Van der Pol.  
2. **Parameter Scale Efficiency (One-Step TF):** Using the stable config from Exp 1, vary KAN width/depth to achieve ~100, 200, 500, 2000 parameters. Create identically-sized MLP baselines. Train 100 seeds each on both oscillators with teacher forcing only. Compare median Discovery R² and variance to replicate that small KANs compete on Duffing but fail on Van der Pol.  
3. **Recursive Stability with BPTT:** Take the smallest KAN and MLP from Exp 2. Train with BPTT (unroll length = full trajectory) on both oscillators. Compare performance change from Exp 2 to confirm partial BPTT stabilization for shallow KANs on Van der Pol but persistent MLP superiority. Monitor variance in deeper KANs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** What specific gradient dynamics cause optimization instability in deep KANs for multiplicative dynamics, and can gradient regularization (e.g., spectral normalization, clipping) mitigate this without harming expressivity?  
  *Basis:* Instability is evident in Table 2 and Discussion notes "optimization challenges" and suggests "gradient conditioning" as future work.  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper identifies instability but provides no diagnostic analysis of gradient behavior or tests of stabilization techniques.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Monitor gradient norms/spectra during BPTT on Van der Pol; systematically apply regularization (e.g., spectral norms, gradient clipping) and measure convergence success rate, R² variance, and final accuracy.

- **Open Question 2:** Can hybrid KAN architectures (e.g., operator chaining to separately represent \(1−x^2\) and \(v\) before multiplicative interaction) overcome the additive inductive bias limitation and achieve stable, accurate recovery of multiplicative terms in HRPINN?  
  *Basis:* Discussion states: "hybrid approaches such as operator chaining... remain promising directions."  
  *Why unresolved:* Only vanilla KANs were evaluated; hybrid designs for state coupling remain unimplemented and unevaluated.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Implement a hybrid KAN with parallel branches for polynomial and linear components combined via a product layer; train on Van der Pol under BPTT and compare R², stability (success rate over seeds), and parameter efficiency against vanilla KANs and MLPs.

- **Open Question 3:** How does automatic symbolic extraction from KANs (via spline pruning) compare to established methods like SINDy in recurrent physics-informed discovery, in terms of symbolic accuracy, noise robustness, and computational cost?  
  *Basis:* Abstract and Discussion propose "comparisons against established symbolic methods such as SINDy" and highlight KAN’s "unique structural advantage" for symbolic recovery.  
  *Why unresolved:* The study used a unified candidate-fitting approach, bypassing KAN’s symbolic pruning; practical trade-offs between accuracy and interpretability are unknown.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Apply KAN pruning and SINDy to residual data from HRPINN (identical trajectories); evaluate symbolic fidelity (e.g., exact equation recovery, sparsity) under varying noise levels and sample sizes, and compare runtime.

## Limitations
- Exact ODE parameters (α, δ for Duffing; μ for Van der Pol) and normalization are unspecified.
- KAN architectural details (grid size, spline order, depth/width per parameter count) are missing; stability is sensitive.
- Training hyperparameters (learning rate, early stopping, integrator) are not fully reported.
- Only two oscillator systems evaluated; broader applicability untested.

## Confidence
- **KAN successfully learns separable univariate residuals (Duffing) with small networks:** Medium confidence. Evidence from one cubic case; other forms not validated.
- **KAN exhibits catastrophic instability on multiplicative coupling (Van der Pol), worsened by depth/width:** High confidence. Supported by extensive seed sweeps, negative R², and clear failure patterns.
- **BPTT partially stabilizes shallow KANs, but MLPs remain superior:** High confidence. BPTT improves R² for tiny KANs; MLPs scale robustly across tasks.

## Next Checks
1. Test on additional systems with multiplicative interactions (e.g., Lotka–Volterra, Lorenz) to confirm KAN fragility.
2. Evaluate modified KAN architectures with explicit product units or tensor-product layers to address the additive bias limitation.
3. Conduct a systematic hyperparameter sweep (grid sizes, orders, optimizers) with ≥1000 seeds per configuration to map stability boundaries and identify robust settings.
