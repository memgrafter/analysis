# ImmSET: Sequence-Based Predictor of TCR-pMHC Specificity at Scale

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26994
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26994
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A transformer-based joint encoder with auxiliary losses that robustly predicts TCR-pMHC activation under a strict holdout (test peptides ≥4 edits from training), outperforms fine-tuned ESM2 (AUROC, p<5e-13) and AlphaFold pipelines (accuracy, >10,000x faster), and scales predictably with data.

## Executive Summary
ImmSET is a machine learning model designed to predict whether a given T cell receptor (TCR) will recognize a specific peptide-MHC (pMHC) complex. It addresses a key failure mode in prior work—where models memorize superficial TCR motifs and appear accurate on peptides similar to the training set—by using a joint transformer architecture that forces interaction between all input segments (TCRα, TCRβ, peptide). This is achieved through special tokens, input order randomization, and auxiliary masked language modeling losses. The model demonstrates strong performance on a rigorously held-out test set, scales favorably with more unique peptides, and generalizes non-randomly across HLA alleles without being explicitly trained on their sequences.

## Method Summary
The task is binary classification of TCR-pMHC activation for HLA-A*02:01-restricted complexes. Inputs are amino acid sequences for CDR regions of TCRα (CDR1α, CDR2α, CDR3α), TCRβ (CDR1β, CDR2β, CDR3β), and the peptide. A transformer encoder with Rotary Positional Embeddings processes a concatenated sequence where segments are delimited by special tokens and their order is randomized during training. Three independent CLS tokens are used for classification. Auxiliary losses enforce cross-segment dependencies: masked language modeling (MLM) is applied independently to each segment, and complete segment masking (CSM) is applied to groups (CDR1α+CDR2α; CDR1β+CDR2β). The total loss is a weighted sum (λ_cls=64, λ_mlm=1, λ_csm=4). Training uses Adam (weight decay 0.01), a batch size of 256, uniform peptide sampling, and a 1:1 positive:negative ratio. Checkpoints are selected by a composite score (AUROC + 5*AUCROC, α=14). Final predictions are an ensemble average from 9 models (3 seeds × top 3 checkpoints). Evaluation uses a strict holdout where test peptides are at least 4 Levenshtein edits from any training/validation peptide, and negatives are sampled exclusively from those test peptides.

## Key Results
- ImmSET shows robustness under the strict ≥4-edit holdout where baseline models fail due to TCR motif memorization.
- It achieves higher AUROC than fine-tuned ESM2 (statistically significant, p<5e-13) with 4x faster inference (1.1ms vs 5ms).
- Against AlphaFold2/AlphaFold3 pipelines on HLA-A*02:01, ImmSET outperforms when trained on sufficient data and is >10,000x faster (10ms vs minutes).
- Model scaling follows a power law (AUROC ∝ P^α T^β). Increasing unique peptides (α=0.078) has a larger impact than adding more TCRs per peptide (β=0.036).
- Models trained solely on A*02:01 exhibit non-random cross-HLA generalization to other class I alleles (e.g., B*40:01), with per-peptide AUROC ranging widely (0.336–0.832).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The joint encoding with order randomization and auxiliary losses (MLM, CSM) prevents the model from relying on isolated TCR motifs or single segments. By masking entire segment groups (CSM), the model must use information from other segments to reconstruct the masked parts, explicitly learning cross-segment dependencies. This design mitigates the shortcut of predicting based on TCR features correlated with activation in the training set, which fails when presented with truly novel peptides. The strict evaluation protocol (≥4 peptide edits) confirms this robustness, as performance drops sharply for models without these constraints.

## Foundational Learning
- **Transformer Encoder with RoPE**: Processes variable-length sequential inputs with relative positional awareness. *Why needed:* Captures long-range interactions between residues in different chains. *Quick check:* Does attention weight variance change with distance? Should decay slowly.
- **Special Segment Tokens & Randomization**: Delimits CDR groups and shuffles their order during training. *Why needed:* Forces the model to learn segment identity and cross-segment relationships invariant to order. *Quick check:* Ablate randomization; performance should drop on strict holdout.
- **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)**: Reconstructs masked tokens within a segment. *Why needed:* Learns robust intra-segment representations. *Quick check:* MLM loss should decrease steadily during training.
- **Complete Segment Masking (CSM)**: Masks entire CDR1/2 groups for reconstruction from other segments. *Why needed:* Explicitly enforces inter-segment dependency learning. *Quick check:* CSM loss should be high initially and decrease; removing CSM should harm generalization.
- **Scoring Function (AUROC + 5*AUCROC)**: Selects checkpoints balancing overall ranking and extreme class separation. *Why needed:* Prevents selection of models that are good on average but poor at identifying clear positives/negatives. *Quick check:* Compare this score against AUROC alone on validation set correlation with test holdout.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input Segments (TCRα CDRs, TCRβ CDRs, Peptide) -> Tokenization (AA + Special Tokens) -> Randomized Concatenation -> Transformer Encoder (with RoPE) -> Three Independent CLS Heads -> Loss Aggregation (λ-weighted sum of classification, MLM, CSM) -> Training Loop (Adam, batch sampling) -> Checkpoint Scoring -> 9-Model Ensemble -> Final Prediction.

Critical path: Data loading -> segment tokenization -> order randomization -> forward pass through encoder -> compute three losses -> backward pass -> checkpoint evaluation based on score -> ensemble averaging.

Design tradeoffs:
- Joint encoding complexity vs. separate twin networks: Joint encoding increases parameter interaction but risks interference; CSM loss mitigates this.
- Auxiliary loss weighting: Heavy λ_cls (64) prioritizes main task but may under-train auxiliary objectives if too high; values were tuned empirically.
- Ensemble size (9 models): Improves stability but increases inference cost 9x; authors chose a balance.

Failure signatures:
- Dataset leakage: Test peptide edit distance <4 from train/val; per-peptide AUROC uniformly high (overfitting).
- Shortcut learning: No-peptide baseline shows non-random performance; performance decays sharply with peptide edit distance.
- Imbalanced sampling: Poor convergence if batch sampling does not maintain uniform peptide and 1:1 class balance.

First three experiments:
1. Validate data split: Compute minimum Levenshtein edit distance between train/val and test peptides; all must be ≥4. Verify test negatives come only from test peptides.
2. Train no-peptide baseline: Remove peptide segment, keep TCR only. Evaluate on strict holdout; should perform at random (AUROC ~0.5). If not, evidence of TCR shortcut.
3. Plot performance vs. peptide edit distance: For a trained model, bin test peptides by minimum edit distance to train set. Expect sharp drop from distance 3 to 4 for a memorizing model; ImmSET should maintain robustness after distance 4.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What are the precise biological or structural mechanisms that enable ImmSET's cross-allelic generalization from HLA-A*02:01-trained models to other class I alleles (e.g., B*40:01), despite no explicit HLA sequence input?
- **Open Question 2:** How does ImmSET's specific architectural components (joint encoding with order shuffling, CSM loss) quantitatively contribute to robustness against the TCR motif memorization failure mode compared to separate encoding architectures (e.g., twin networks) or models without CSM?
- **Open Question 3:** What are the practical data requirements and diminishing returns for achieving a target AUROC (e.g., >0.85) on truly unseen peptides (≥4 edits) in a clinically relevant allele like A*02:01, according to the observed scaling laws (α=0.078, β=0.036)?
- **Open Question 4:** Can the attention mechanisms of ImmSET be systematically interpreted to predict atomic-level TCR-pMHC contacts (e.g., which CDR loops contact which peptide positions), and does this correlate with known structural biology or AlphaFold-predicted contacts?
- **Open Question 5:** Does incorporating the full HLA (or pseudo-sequence) as an additional input segment to ImmSET improve cross-allelic generalization and per-peptide AUROC stability compared to the current context-free (A*02:01-only) training?

## Limitations
- Proprietary data specifics: Exact sequences, counts, and downsampling grid for scaling experiments are unavailable, blocking exact replication of α and β exponents.
- Missing training hyperparameters: Details like learning rate schedule, total epochs, and negative sampling procedures are not fully specified, introducing variability.
- Baseline implementation transparency: Comparisons to AlphaFold2/AlphaFold3 and ESM2 rely on authors' custom setups; independent verification is limited without full code/specs.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1** (ImmSET vs ESM2: higher AUROC, 4× faster): Medium – strong statistical claim but depends on unreported training choices.
- **Claim 2** (ImmSET vs AlphaFold pipelines: better accuracy, >10,000× faster): Medium – speed advantage is clear; accuracy relies on proprietary pipeline details.
- **Claim 3** (Cross-HLA generalization: non-random, AUROC 0.336–0.832): Medium-Low – promising but based on internal data; generalization to unseen alleles uncertain.
- **Claim 4** (Scaling exponents α=0.078, β=0.036): Low – derived from non-public data, no external reproducibility.
- **Claim 5** (Robustness under ≥4 peptide edit distance): High – evaluation protocol is rigorous and results show a distinct failure pattern in baselines.

## Next Checks

2. Cross-allele generalization test: Compile a multi-HLA public dataset, train on A*02:01 only, and evaluate on other alleles to reproduce the observed per-peptide AUROC range and non-random signal.
