# Click it or Leave it: Detecting and Spoiling Clickbait with Informativeness Measures and Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18171
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18171
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** A hybrid model combining transformer embeddings with 15 linguistic features achieves 91% F1-score on clickbait detection, outperforming pure embedding and LLM-prompt baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of clickbait detection, which harms online information quality and user trust. The authors propose a hybrid model that merges transformer-based text embeddings with a curated set of 15 linguistically motivated features (e.g., second-person pronouns, superlatives, numerals) using an XGBoost classifier. Evaluated on a unified clickbait dataset, the model achieves a 91% F1-score, surpassing several baseline approaches. The explicit feature set also provides model interpretability, highlighting the stylistic cues most indicative of clickbait.

## Method Summary
The method involves a two-pronged feature extraction process: (1) generating dense semantic representations from a transformer model (RoBERTa), and (2) computing 15 hand-crafted linguistic features designed to capture common clickbait tactics. These heterogeneous features are concatenated and fed into an XGBoost classifier for binary classification. The approach is evaluated against baselines using traditional vectorization (TF-IDF, Word2Vec, GloVe), a fine-tuned RoBERTa model, and prompt-based large language models.

## Key Results
- The proposed hybrid model achieves a 91% F1-score on a unified clickbait dataset.
- It outperforms all compared baselines, including fine-tuned RoBERTa and LLM prompt-based methods.
- An ablation study confirms the 15 linguistic features contribute a 4.5-point F1 gain over embeddings alone, and the feature set enhances model interpretability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hybrid model succeeds by combining complementary signal types: transformer embeddings capture deep contextual semantics, while the explicit linguistic features encode surface-level stylistic patterns characteristic of clickbait (e.g., emotional language, numerals, personal pronouns). XGBoost effectively integrates these heterogeneous inputs, learning interactions that pure embedding models or simple feature-based models might miss. This synergy leads to superior performance and provides direct insight into the model's decision-making through the weighted linguistic features.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Text Input -> Transformer Embedding Extraction -> Linguistic Feature Extraction -> Feature Concatenation -> XGBoost Classifier -> Clickbait Probability

**Critical Path**: The integration of semantic embeddings and stylistic features prior to classification is the pivotal design choice.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- *Hybrid vs. Pure Deep Learning*: Adds feature engineering overhead but boosts performance and interpretability versus an end-to-end transformer.  
- *Feature Generalizability*: Linguistically motivated features may not transfer well to non-English or culturally distinct clickbait.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- Significant performance drop on non-English or highly informal web text not represented in training data.  
- Over-reliance on specific features (e.g., numerals) causing false positives on legitimate informative headlines containing numbers.

**First Experiments**:  
1. Train and compare three models: (a) embeddings-only, (b) linguistic features-only, (c) full hybrid, to quantify synergy.  
2. Apply SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) to the XGBoost model to rank and visualize the most influential linguistic features.  
3. Evaluate the trained model on a small, manually labeled set of non-English clickbait to初步 probe cross-lingual effectiveness.

## Limitations
- The construction and preprocessing details of the "unified clickbait dataset" are unspecified, creating uncertainty about generalizability and potential bias.
- The strength of claimed outperformance depends on the tuning of baseline models; inadequate baseline hyperparameter optimization could exaggerate the reported gap.
- The 15 linguistic features, curated for English, have unknown effectiveness on non-English or cross-cultural clickbait.
- The 4.5-point F1 gain from the ablation study lacks statistical significance testing (e.g., confidence intervals), so its reliability is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Hybrid Model Performance (91% F1)**: Medium (specific to one non-publicly-documented dataset; external validity unproven).
- **Feature Contribution (+4.5 F1)**: Medium (ablation design is sound but lacks statistical validation).
- **Model Interpretability via Features**: Low-Medium (explicit features offer some transparency, but XGBoost's internal interactions and embedding signals limit full explainability).
