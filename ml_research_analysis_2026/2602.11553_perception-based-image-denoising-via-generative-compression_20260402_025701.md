# Perception-based Image Denoising via Generative Compression
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11553
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11553
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** A generative compression framework that enforces low-complexity structure via entropy-coded latents and recovers realistic textures with perceptual losses, achieving state-of-the-art perceptual quality on synthetic and real noise.

## Executive Summary
Image denoising methods that minimize distortion (e.g., MSE) often oversmooth textures, degrading perceptual quality. This paper proposes a generative compression framework that addresses the oversmoothing problem by first encoding a noisy image into an entropy-coded latent representation, which enforces low-complexity structure and suppresses noise. The latent is then decoded using a generative model—either a conditional WGAN (CGanDeCompress) or a conditional diffusion model (DiffDeCompress)—trained with perceptual losses (LPIPS, Wasserstein distance) to restore realistic details. Theoretical guarantees are provided for the compression-based maximum-likelihood denoiser under additive Gaussian noise, yielding non-asymptotic bounds on reconstruction error and decoding probability. Extensive experiments on standard datasets with synthetic Gaussian noise (σ ∈ {15,25,50}) and real-world noise (FMD, SIDD) demonstrate consistent improvements in perceptual metrics (FID, LPIPS, PI) while maintaining competitive distortion (PSNR, SSIM). The approach establishes a new state-of-the-art in perceptual image denoising and offers a rigorous rate-distortion-perception (RDP) trade-off.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a two-stage compression-based denoising framework. First, an encoder compresses a noisy image into a quantized latent code with a limited bit-rate, effectively projecting the observation onto a low-entropy manifold of plausible clean images. Second, a generative decoder reconstructs the denoised image from this latent. Two decoders are explored: (1) CGanDeCompress, a conditional WGAN that directly optimizes a combined MSE+LPIPS loss and an adversarial Wasserstein loss, enabling explicit RDP control; (2) DiffDeCompress, a conditional diffusion model that iteratively refines a noise sample conditioned on the latent, leveraging a diffusion prior to synthesize textures. Theoretical analysis interprets compression-based denoising as a structured maximum-likelihood estimator and derives bounds on the expected distortion and decoding error probability under AWGN. The framework is trained end-to-end on clean images with synthetic noise, and at test time the encoder and decoder operate on unseen noisy inputs.

## Key Results
- Synthetic Gaussian noise (σ=25) on DIV2K: CGanDeCompress achieves best FID (14.67) and LPIPS (0.0567); DiffDeCompress attains lowest Perception Index (2.93).
- Real-world noise on SIDD: CGanDeCompress reduces LPIPS from 0.646 to 0.093 and increases PSNR from 26.35 to 32.70 dB, showing strong robustness.
- Across KODAK, DIV2K, and COCO2017 with various noise levels, the proposed methods consistently outperform prior distortion-driven and perceptual denoisers in FID/LPIPS/PI while remaining competitive in PSNR/SSIM.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Entropy-coded latent representations enforce a low-complexity signal class that suppresses high-frequency noise while preserving structured content.
- **Mechanism:** The encoder compresses a noisy image into a quantized latent code with limited bit-rate (R). The decoder reconstructs from this codebook, effectively projecting the noisy observation onto a set of plausible clean signals. Since natural images admit compact representations whereas noise is unstructured, this projection removes noise by construction.
- **Core assumption:** Clean natural images can be represented with lower entropy than noisy observations; the learned codebook approximates the manifold of clean images.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “reconstructing from entropy-coded latent representations that enforce low-complexity structure”
  - [section 2.2] “compression-based denoising can be interpreted as a structured maximum-likelihood (ML) estimator… clean images admit more compact representations than noisy observations”
  - [corpus] Related work (DeCompress [10,11]) implements similar compression-based denoising; corpus FMR scores are moderate (avg 0.49) indicating topical relevance but limited direct validation.
- **Break condition:** If the latent rate R is too high (insufficient compression), the codebook becomes overly dense and may include noise-like codewords, reducing denoising effectiveness. If R is too low, the codebook underfits natural image diversity, causing blur.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Generative decoders trained with perceptual losses (LPIPS, Wasserstein distance) recover realistic textures that distortion-optimized decoders oversmooth.
- **Mechanism:** The decoder is regularized not by pixel-wise MSE alone but by a perceptual loss that measures feature-space discrepancies (LPIPS) and a conditional adversarial loss (WGAN) that ensures reconstructed distributions match clean images. This explicitly optimizes for the perception index P in the rate-distortion-perception trade-off.
- **Core assumption:** LPIPS and WGAN losses correlate with human perceptual realism and can guide synthesis of fine textures absent in MSE-optimal solutions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “generative decoders recover realistic textures via perceptual measures such as LPIPS loss and Wasserstein distance”
  - [section 3.1] “reconstruction distortion d(x, ˆx) = k_MSE(x, ˆx) + k_P LPIPS(x, ˆx)” and conditional WGAN objective
  - [corpus] Conditional GANs and LPIPS are standard in perceptual restoration; corpus includes conditional generative denoisers (e.g., paper 171296) but evidence is indirect (FMR up to 0.60).
- **Break condition:** If perceptual loss weight (k_P or β) is excessive, the decoder may generate hallucinated textures not present in the original (“perceptual exaggeration”). If too low, oversmoothing persists.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Conditional diffusion models, guided by compressed latents, perform iterative refinement that combines global consistency (from compression) with local detail synthesis (from diffusion prior).
- **Mechanism:** A stochastic encoder produces a latent z from the noisy image. A diffusion decoder initializes from noise and iteratively denoises conditioned on z. The diffusion process models p(x₀|z) and is trained to reverse a noising schedule while staying anchored to the latent’s semantic content.
- **Core assumption:** Diffusion models can generate diverse, high-fidelity details when conditioned on a compact representation that captures global structure; the latent sufficiently constrains the diffusion trajectory.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “conditional diffusion-based reconstruction strategy that performs iterative denoising guided by compressed latents”
  - [section 3.2.2] “Conditioned on z, the decoder models p(x₀|z) through a diffusion process”
  - [corpus] Diffusion-based compression (e.g., Turbo-DDCM [72482], I²SB [129153]) shows related ideas; corpus FMR up to 0.63 suggests relevance but no direct citation of this exact combination.
- **Break condition:** If the latent z is too compressed (low rate), it may insufficiently guide diffusion, leading to drift or loss of structural fidelity. If the diffusion prior is weak (e.g., few steps), texture quality degrades.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Rate-Distortion-Perception (RDP) Trade-off
- **Why needed here:** The paper’s framework explicitly navigates the RDP triangle. Understanding that distortion (MSE) and perception (distributional similarity) are conflicting objectives at fixed rate explains why MSE-optimized denoisers oversmooth and why generative methods are necessary.
- **Quick check question:** In the RDP curve, what happens to the minimum achievable distortion D(P) if the allowed perception level P increases beyond the MMSE estimator’s perception index P*?

### Concept: Conditional Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) Training
- **Why needed here:** CGanDeCompress uses conditional WGAN for stable adversarial training and a meaningful perceptual loss via Wasserstein distance. Knowing WGAN’s gradient penalty and critic training avoids common GAN pitfalls.
- **Quick check question:** Why does WGAN use a critic (discriminator) that outputs a real-valued score instead of a probability, and how does this affect the loss?

### Concept: Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM)
- **Why needed here:** DiffDeCompress builds on diffusion models. Understanding the forward noising process, reverse process parameterization, and the noise prediction objective is essential to grasp how diffusion serves as a decoder.
- **Quick check question:** In DDPM, what is the purpose of the variance schedule {βₙ} and how does it affect the distribution of xₙ?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
CGanDeCompress: Encoder E(y) → quantized latent z → rate estimator P(z) → Decoder G(z) → ˆx. Discriminator D(ˆx, z) for adversarial loss. LPIPS computed between ˆx and clean x.
DiffDeCompress: Encoder E(y) → latent z (conditioning). Diffusion decoder: forward process parameters, reverse denoising network Mθ(xₙ, z, n) trained to predict ϵ. LPIPS integrated into final loss weighting.

### Critical Path
For both methods, the core pipeline is: Noisy input y → (Encoder + Quantization) → Latent z → (Generative Decoder) → Denoised output ˆx. The latent z is the bottleneck that enforces compression.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Rate (λ) vs. quality:** Higher λ (bit-rate) reduces compression, preserving more details but risking noise retention. Lower λ increases compression, enhancing denoising but potentially losing content.
- **CGan vs. Diffusion:** CGan is faster (single forward pass) but may suffer from mode collapse or unstable training. Diffusion is iterative (slow) but offers better mode coverage and texture quality.
- **Perceptual loss weighting (k_P, ρ):** Must balance texture realism (high weight) and structural fidelity (low weight). Mis-weighting yields either blurry or hallucinated outputs.

### Failure Signatures
- **Blurry outputs:** LPIPS weight too low or rate λ too high (insufficient compression), allowing noise structure to survive and be averaged out.
- **Hallucinated textures / artifacts:** Perceptual loss weight too high or discriminator overfitting (CGan); diffusion steps too many with weak conditioning (DiffDeCompress).
- **Structural distortion / color shifts:** Encoder not trained to preserve semantic features; latent dimensionality too low; or divergence in diffusion conditioning.
- **Decoding failure (high Pe):** Codebook size (2ᴿ) too small for signal complexity, causing misclassification of codewords (see Theorem 2’s error bound).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the non-asymptotic error bounds for compression-based maximum-likelihood denoising (Theorem 2) be extended to non-Gaussian noise models, and what form would these bounds take?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Theorem 2 explicitly assumes additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN); the paper does not discuss generalization to other noise distributions.
- **Why unresolved:** Real-world denoising often involves signal-dependent or colored noise; without theoretical guarantees for such settings, the framework's applicability and performance bounds remain unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Derive analogous bounds for specific non-Gaussian noise models (e.g., Poisson, speckle) and validate them empirically on synthetic non-Gaussian datasets, showing agreement between theory and practice.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What are the comparative trade-offs between the proposed CGanDeCompress (conditional WGAN) and DiffDeCompress (conditional diffusion) in terms of perceptual quality, distortion, computational cost, and robustness to hyperparameters?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper introduces both methods but provides limited direct comparison beyond aggregate metric tables; deeper analysis of their complementary properties is absent.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding these trade-offs is crucial for selecting the appropriate method in practical applications and for guiding future improvements.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct controlled experiments varying computational budgets, network architectures, and training regimes; measure not only final metrics but also convergence stability, sensitivity to rate constraints, and failure modes.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the choice of perceptual loss function (e.g., LPIPS, Wasserstein distance, DISTS, no-reference metrics) influence the attainable distortion-perception (DP) curve in the generative compression framework?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The framework employs LPIPS and Wasserstein distance but does not explore alternatives or their impact on the RDP trade-off.
- **Why unresolved:** Different perceptual losses capture different aspects of human vision; selecting an optimal loss could further improve perceptual quality without sacrificing distortion.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train models with various perceptual losses under identical rate constraints and compare their DP trade-offs; analyze correlations with human perceptual studies.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does DiffDeCompress perform on real-world noisy images (e.g., FMD, SIDD) compared to CGanDeCompress, and does its iterative diffusion process offer advantages in handling complex noise distributions?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The experimental evaluation of real noise only reports results for CGanDeCompress; DiffDeCompress is evaluated solely on synthetic Gaussian noise.
- **Why unresolved:** Without real-noise evaluation, it is unclear whether the diffusion-based approach generalizes as effectively to practical denoising scenarios where noise characteristics differ from synthetic assumptions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply DiffDeCompress to the same real-noise datasets (FMD, SIDD) using the same training protocol and compare metrics against CGanDeCompress and other baselines; additionally, visualize qualitative differences in texture recovery.

## Limitations
- Theoretical bounds assume additive white Gaussian noise; extension to non-Gaussian or spatially correlated noise is not established.
- Rate-distortion-perception trade-off is manually controlled; automatic or content-adaptive rate selection is unexplored.
- Computational cost is high, especially for diffusion-based decoding, limiting practical deployment.
