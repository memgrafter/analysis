# WorldTravel: A Realistic Multimodal Travel-Planning Benchmark with Tightly Coupled Constraints

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08367
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08367
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Frontier models achieve ≤32.67% feasibility on multimodal travel planning, revealing severe limitations in long-horizon temporal reasoning.

## Executive Summary
WorldTravel introduces a novel benchmark for travel itinerary planning that tightly couples temporal constraints with realistic web-based parameter extraction. The multimodal environment comprises 150 tasks across 5 cities and over 2000 rendered webpages; agents must extract constraints from raw screenshots rather than structured data. Evaluation of 10 frontier models shows poor performance: GPT-5.2 reaches only 32.67% feasibility in text-only and 19.33% in multimodal settings, with a 13.34 percentage point perception gap. Feasibility collapses beyond ~10 constraints, falling below 4% on harder tasks. Even with perfect constraint extraction (Gold-θ), top models manage only 36.67%, identifying long-horizon temporal reasoning as the core bottleneck independent of perception.

## Method Summary
The benchmark constructs realistic travel-planning scenarios by rendering web interfaces that users would encounter when booking trips. Each task defines a set of constraints (e.g., opening hours, travel times, activity durations) that are only available as visual content in the screenshots. Agents must process the multimodal input, extract structured constraints, and generate a feasible itinerary that respects all temporal couplings. The tasks span five major cities and vary in complexity by the number of constraints. Performance is measured by the feasibility of the produced itineraries. A gold-θ ablation provides perfect constraint parameters to isolate planning from perception errors.

## Key Results
- GPT-5.2 achieves 32.67% feasibility in text-only and 19.33% in multimodal settings, with a 13.34 pp perception gap.
- Feasibility collapses beyond ~10 constraints, dropping below 4% for harder tasks.
- Even with perfect constraint extraction (Gold-θ), top models reach only 36.67%, highlighting long-horizon temporal reasoning as the primary bottleneck.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The benchmark effectively isolates the challenges of multimodal travel planning by forcing agents to derive constraints directly from visual web content, mirroring real-world usage. The tight coupling of temporal constraints (e.g., business hours, travel durations) creates a long-horizon planning problem that is computationally demanding. By decoupling perception (via the Gold-θ ablation) and measuring the drop, the benchmark reveals that the planning component itself is the dominant failure mode. The realistic rendering and diverse city contexts prevent models from relying on superficial memorization.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal Perception:** Extracting structured data from raw screenshots is essential because the input mimics real web pages. *Why needed:* Without accurate extraction, downstream planning is fundamentally limited. *Quick check:* Can the agent reliably identify opening hours, prices, and locations from a given screenshot?
- **Constraint Extraction:** Parsing the extracted text into formal temporal and logical constraints. *Why needed:* Planning requires a precise representation of restrictions. *Quick check:* Does the agent convert "Museum open 9 AM–5 PM" into a time window constraint?
- **Temporal Reasoning:** Propagating constraints across a sequence of activities over multiple days. *Why needed:* Activities are interdependent; a delay in one affects others. *Quick check:* Given a set of activities with durations and travel times, can the agent detect conflicts?
- **Long-Horizon Planning:** Constructing a feasible schedule that satisfies all constraints over many steps. *Why needed:* Travel itineraries involve dozens of decisions across days. *Quick check:* Does the agent generate a complete itinerary without violations when given perfect constraints?
- **Feasibility Verification:** Evaluating whether a proposed itinerary meets all constraints. *Why needed:* The benchmark's primary metric is feasibility, requiring self-consistency. *Quick check:* Can the agent accurately score its own plan?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Screenshot -> Vision Encoder -> Text Extractor (OCR) -> Constraint Parser -> Planner -> Itinerary

Critical path: The agent first processes the screenshot to extract raw text (OCR). The constraint parser converts this text into a set of temporal and logical constraints. The planner then reasons over these constraints to produce a sequence of activities (itinerary). Feasibility is checked implicitly by the benchmark's evaluation.

Design tradeoffs: End-to-end models (e.g., large multimodal LLMs) simplify integration but sacrifice interpretability and efficiency. Modular pipelines allow targeted improvements but require careful handoff between components. The benchmark favors modular designs because it isolates perception and planning errors.

Failure signatures: Common failures include missed constraints (e.g., ignoring museum closure days), infeasible schedules (overlapping activities), and hallucinated details (inventing times not in the screenshot). The steep feasibility drop with constraint count indicates a scalability limitation in the planning module.

First experiments:
1. **Baseline assessment:** Run a simple rule-based agent (e.g., greedy scheduling) on a single-city, low-constraint task to establish a performance floor.
2. **Perception isolation:** Compare text-only (oracle text) vs multimodal inputs to quantify the perception gap.
3. **Constraint scaling:** Incrementally increase the number of constraints in controlled tasks to pinpoint the collapse threshold and characterize the planning degrade.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to 10 frontier models without open‑source baselines, and no statistical significance testing.
- Fixed benchmark across only 5 cities risks overfitting and limits generalizability.
- Gold‑θ ablation uses unrealistic perfect perception; analysis lacks per‑constraint breakdown.

## Confidence
- WorldTravel dataset scale and multimodal design: High
- Reported performance numbers for GPT‑5.2 and other models: High
- Perception gap (13.34 pp) between text‑only and multimodal: Medium
- Feasibility collapse beyond ~10 constraints: Medium
- Identification of long‑horizon temporal reasoning as primary bottleneck: Medium

## Next Checks
- Re-run the benchmark with a diverse set of open‑source models (e.g., Llama‑3, Claude‑3) including multiple random seeds to compute confidence intervals for feasibility scores.
- Conduct a fine‑grained ablation varying the number of extracted constraints and measuring feasibility drop, specifically fitting a regression to pinpoint the exact constraint threshold and its variance.
- Perform a qualitative error analysis on model outputs to classify failures into perception errors, temporal reasoning errors, and other planning flaws, confirming that the bottleneck indeed lies in long‑horizon reasoning beyond perception.
