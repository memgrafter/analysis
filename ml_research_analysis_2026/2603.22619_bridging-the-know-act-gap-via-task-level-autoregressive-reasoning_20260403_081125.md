# Bridging the Know-Act Gap via Task-Level Autoregressive Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22619
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22619
- **Reference count:** 11
- **Primary result:** Introducing task-level autoregression with control tokens and self-distillation closes the know-act gap on FaultyScience, boosting accuracy from 10.8% to 67.8% without harming general reasoning.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) often answer questions they internally recognize as flawed, revealing a "know-act gap" between discriminative knowledge and generative behavior. This paper identifies token-level autoregression as the root cause, as it entangles the decision to validate or answer with the content generation itself. To bridge this gap, the authors propose DeIllusionLLM, which introduces two special control tokens—[D] for discriminative diagnosis and [G] for generative answering—and trains the model via self-distillation on the FaultyScience benchmark. This forces an explicit "validate-then-answer" decision within a single autoregressive pass. Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-72B with this method dramatically improves performance on flawed questions (from 10.8% to 67.8% accuracy) while maintaining comparable results on standard reasoning benchmarks BBH and GPQA, demonstrating a substantial reduction in answer-despite-error failures without degrading general capability.

## Method Summary
DeIllusionLLM modifies the standard autoregressive training objective by adding two special tokens to the vocabulary: `[D]` (diagnose) and `[G]` (generate). For each training question, two target sequences are created: one starting with `[D]` followed by a diagnostic JSON output (from a `P_Dis` prompt), and one starting with `[G]` followed by a direct answer (from a `P_Gen^{-hint}` prompt). The model is trained with a mode-specific loss that assigns a dedicated learning signal to the first token (the control token) and then applies a standard next-token loss to the remainder of the sequence. Crucially, a reweighting hyperparameter `α` is used to prioritize learning from the `[D]` examples, addressing the inherent imbalance between valid and faulty questions. At inference, given a natural prompt (no hint), the model's first generated token commits it to either the diagnostic or generative path, thereby explicitly separating task selection from content generation.

## Key Results
- On the FaultyScience benchmark under natural prompting (`P_Gen^{-hint}`), DeIllusionLLM (fine-tuned from Qwen2.5-72B) improves accuracy from 10.8% (base model) to 67.8%.
- Performance on standard reasoning benchmarks remains stable: BBH (41.7 vs. 42.8) and GPQA (33.9 vs. 34.2).
- The model selects the `[D]` diagnostic path on 54.1% of FaultyScience questions, and high mode alignment (72.5% aligned cases) is achieved, indicating the control token effectively steers generation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Token-Level Entanglement of Task and Content
- **Claim:** The know-act gap arises because standard token-level autoregression forces an immediate commitment to generating answer content, preventing the model from invoking its latent discriminative knowledge to first decide whether validation is needed.
- **Mechanism:** The next-token prediction objective does not provide a learnable "pause point" for emitting a high-level task decision (validate vs. answer). The model's internal discriminative knowledge exists but is not accessible as a steering signal for the generative trajectory because task selection and content generation are entangled from the first token.
- **Evidence:** The stark performance disparity between `P_Dis` (~90% FMR) and `P_Gen^{-hint}` (>96% FMR) on FaultyScience shows the model's behavior diverges based on prompt, despite identical underlying knowledge. Section 4.1.2 finds models "prioritize surface-level mathematical computation over basic validity checks."
- **Break condition:** If fine-tuning with explicit task tokens does not improve `P_Gen^{-hint}` performance on FaultyScience while preserving BBH/GPQA scores, the claim that token-level entanglement is the primary cause is weakened.

### Mechanism 2: Control Tokens as an Explicit Task Gate
- **Claim:** Adding `[D]` and `[G]` tokens and training with a dedicated first-token loss creates a learnable gate that forces an explicit, inspectable task selection decision before any content is generated.
- **Mechanism:** The loss in Equation 1 provides a non-diluted learning signal for the first token. The model must learn to predict `[D]` or `[G]` based solely on input features related to solvability. Once the control token is emitted, subsequent generation is conditioned on that choice, structuring autoregression into two sequential subtasks.
- **Evidence:** Table 6 and Figure 2 show the model selects `[D]` on 54.1% of FaultyScience questions, and aligned cases ([D]+diagnostic) dominate misaligned ones (72.5% vs. 27.5%). Ablation (Table 7) shows removing task-level autoregression harms performance on all benchmarks, confirming the control token's critical role.
- **Break condition:** If analysis shows frequent misalignment (e.g., `[D]` followed by answer text), the mechanism of a clean task gate fails.

### Mechanism 3: Self-Distillation Transfers Latent Discriminative Knowledge
- **Claim:** Self-distillation, using the base model's own `P_Dis` (diagnostic) and `P_Gen^{-hint}` (generative) outputs as targets, effectively transfers its latent discriminative knowledge into the generative process without requiring a stronger external teacher.
- **Mechanism:** The model's `P_Dis` outputs reveal its ability to recognize faults. By training to generate both `y(G)` and `y(D)` conditioned on the control token, the model learns to associate problematic features with the `[D]` path. Loss reweighting (`L_D + αL_G` with `α<1`) prevents the discriminative objective from being overwhelmed by the longer, more frequent generative examples.
- **Evidence:** Table 4 shows self-distilled Qwen improves FaultyScience `P_Gen^{-hint}` from 10.8% to 67.8%, while BBH/GPQA remain stable. Ablation in section 5.2.3 confirms that removing reweighting (`α=1.0`) degrades performance.
- **Break condition:** If self-distillation fails to improve `P_Gen^{-hint}` for multiple base models or causes significant regression on standard benchmarks, the claim is undermined.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Token-level Autoregressive Generation**
  - **Why needed:** The paper's diagnosis centers on standard next-token prediction as the architectural source of the know-act gap.
  - **Quick check:** In standard autoregressive decoding, what determines the probability of the next token? How does this objective prevent a high-level "validate first" strategy from emerging naturally?
- **Concept: Discriminative vs. Generative Modes**
  - **Why needed:** The know-act gap is defined by the discrepancy between these two modes (`P_Dis` vs. `P_Gen`). The solution unifies them.
  - **Quick check:** If a model correctly identifies a question as flawed under a discriminative prompt but answers it under a generative prompt, what does this imply about the relationship between its internal knowledge and its generation policy?
- **Concept: Knowledge Distillation (Self-Distillation)**
  - **Why needed:** The training method is a form of distillation, transferring knowledge from the model's own outputs under different prompts.
  - **Quick check:** What are the two distinct "teacher" outputs (`y(G)` and `y(D)`) used for, and how do they map to the student's control tokens?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Question -> Pre-trained Backbone LLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-72B) -> Extended Vocabulary (adds [D], [G]) -> Autoregressive Transformer Head -> Output Sequence (first token decides path: [D] -> Diagnostic JSON; [G] -> Answer Text)
- **Critical path:**
  1. **Data Preparation:** For each question in FaultyScience train split, query the teacher model with `P_Gen^{-hint}` and `P_Dis` to obtain `y(G)` and `y(D)`. Prepend `[G]` and `[D]` respectively to create two training instances.
  2. **Model Modification:** Add `[D]` and `[G]` tokens to the tokenizer/vocabulary. Optionally apply LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning.
  3. **Training Loop:** For each instance `(x, [T] ⊕ y(T))`, compute mode-specific loss `L_mode = L_ctrl + (1/N) * Σ L_i` (Equation 1). Weight the `[D]` loss more heavily: total batch loss `L_batch = L_D + α * L_G`. Backpropagate.
  4. **Inference:** Given a new question, prompt naturally. The model autoregressively generates; the first token `[D]` or `[G]` commits it to the corresponding mode for the rest of the generation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Simplicity vs. Control:** Adding two tokens and a modified loss is architecturally simple vs. multi-agent or external verifier systems, but control is limited to a binary, first-token decision.
  - **Generative Fidelity vs. Safety:** Reweighting (`α<1`) prioritizes the diagnostic mode, risking minor degradation on valid questions (seen in small BBH/GPQA drops). `α` is a tunable performance-safety knob.
  - **Self-Distillation vs. External Teacher:** Self-distillation is scalable and free but is capped by the base model's latent `P_Dis` quality. An external teacher (e.g., GPT-4) could provide better targets but may alter style or knowledge.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Mode Collapse:** Model almost always predicts `[G]`. Indicates `L_ctrl` signal is too weak or `[D]` path is under-incentivized.
  - **High Misalignment:** `[D]` token followed by a direct answer, or `[G]` followed by diagnostic JSON. Shows the model hasn't learned to switch generation style based on the first token.
  - **Catastrophic Forgetting:** Significant drop (>5 points) on BBH/GPQA. Indicates reweighting or `[D]` data overwhelmed general reasoning.
  - **Option-Forcing Persistence:** On multiple-choice faulty questions, model selects an option after emitting `[D]`. Shows diagnostic mode is not robust to the "must pick an option" prior.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Control Token Emergence:** Train with only `L_ctrl` (zero content loss) for a few steps. Check if first-token accuracy on FaultyScience (should predict [D]) vs. BBH (should predict [G]) becomes significantly above random (50%). Isolates if the task decision is learned at all.
  2. **Mode Consistency Stress Test:** On 100 FaultyScience examples where the model predicts `[D]`, manually replace the first token with `[G]` and regenerate. Does the content shift to a direct answer? A strong yes confirms the first token's causal steering role.
  3. **Ablation of α (Reweighting):** Train three models with `α = {0.2, 0.6, 1.0}`. Evaluate on: a) FaultyScience `P_Gen^{-hint}` (safety), b) BBH/GPQA (general capability). Expect lower α improves (a) but may harm (b). Find α that maximizes the safety-performance Pareto front.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Q1:** Is the observed "know-act gap" specific to the FaultyScience benchmark, or is it a general phenomenon in LLM reasoning? How does it manifest across different domains (e.g., legal, medical) and question types?
- **Q2:** What is the exact nature of the discriminative knowledge that the model possesses but fails to activate during generative decoding? Is it represented in a specific layer or attention pattern?
- **Q3:** Can the proposed task-level autoregression framework be extended to more than two modes (e.g., multi-step validation with different gate tokens)? Would a hierarchical control token sequence (e.g., `[D-Premise]`, `[D-Logic]`) yield further gains?
- **Q4:** How does the choice of base model affect the effectiveness of self-distillation? Are there models for which the base `P_Dis` is too weak to serve as a useful teacher?
- **Q5:** What are the socio-ethical implications of building models that can "pretend" to answer flawed questions? Does reducing the know-act gap increase the risk of models generating plausible but incorrect explanations for false premises?

## Limitations
- **Benchmark Narrowness:** All evidence is from the single, specialized FaultyScience dataset. Generalization to other domains with flawed questions (e.g., legal, medical) is unproven.
- **Binary Task Assumption:** The solution forces a binary `[D]/[G]` decision. Complex inputs may require multi-stage validation, which a single first token cannot encode.
- **Teacher Quality Ceiling:** Self-distillation cannot surpass the base model's own `P_Dis` accuracy. The reported ~90% `P_Dis` on FaultyScience implies the 67.8% `P_Gen^{-hint}` ceiling may be partially due to imperfect teacher labels.

## Confidence
- **Know-Act Gap Mechanism (Token-Level Entanglement):** Medium. The conceptual argument is strong and supported by the stark `P_Dis` vs. `P_Gen^{-hint}` disparity, but it is inferred from behavior, not proven via direct architectural intervention.
- **Control Token Gate Efficacy:** Medium. The emergence of first-token selection and high mode alignment support the mechanism. The assumption that the first token is a sufficient proxy for a high-level decision is validated behaviorally but not neuroscientifically (e.g., via internal representation analysis).
- **Self-Distillation as a Teacher:** Medium-High. Results on FaultyScience are compelling and the `α` ablation confirms its role. The major limitation is dependency on the base model's latent `P_Dis` quality; a weak or noisy base model caps the student's ceiling.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-Model & Cross-Domain Generalization:** Train DeIllusionLLM from three diverse base models (e.g., Llama-3, Mistral, GPT-2) and evaluate on two new benchmarks of flawed questions (e.g., logic puzzles with hidden contradictions, factual QA with common misconceptions). *Pass criterion:* Consistent `P_Gen^{-hint}` improvement (>20 percentage points) and no BBH/GPQA regression (>2 points) across all models and benchmarks.
2.  **Mechanistic Probe of First-Token Decision:** For a fixed, trained DeIllusionLLM, collect internal hidden states immediately after processing the input but *before* first-token prediction. Train a linear probe to predict the eventual `[D]` vs. `[G]` choice from these states. *Pass criterion:* Probe accuracy significantly (>15 percentage points) above random (50%), confirming the decision is grounded in input-specific representations.
3.  **Stress-Test Binary Assumption with Hierarchical Tokens:** Modify the control token set to `[D-Premise]`, `[D-Logic]`, `[D-Calculation]`, `[G]`. Train with the same self-distillation framework on FaultyScience. *Pass criterion:* The multi-token system does **not** significantly outperform the binary `[D]/[G]` system on `P_Gen^{-hint}` (improvement <5 percentage points), suggesting the binary gate captures the primary failure mode. A significant gain would indicate a need for finer-grained validation stages.
