# Neural Collapse Dynamics: Depth, Activation, Regularisation, and Feature Norm Threshold

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00230
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00230
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: Neural collapse onset is predicted by mean feature norm crossing a model-dataset-specific threshold fn*, with architecture effects showing strong non-additivity across datasets.

## Executive Summary
The paper identifies a feature norm threshold fn* that reliably predicts neural collapse onset across various training conditions. Through systematic experiments on MLPs and ResNets, they show that fn* is invariant within a model-dataset pair, and its crossing precedes collapse by an average of 62 epochs. Key regularities include three regimes for weight decay, non-monotonic depth effects, and activation-dependent fn* values. Architecture-dataset interactions are non-additive: switching from MLP to ResNet increases fn* by 458% on MNIST but only 68% on CIFAR-10. These findings suggest a unified dynamic governing neural collapse driven by feature norm decay.

## Method Summary
The authors employ a two-phase training protocol: first cross-entropy (CE) loss until ≥99% training accuracy, then mean squared error (MSE) loss with one-hot targets to induce neural collapse. Experiments vary depth (2-7 layers), width (128-1024 units), activation functions (ReLU, GELU, Tanh), and weight decay (10⁻⁵ to 5×10⁻⁴) on MLPs with MNIST, and ResNet-20 with CIFAR-10. Neural collapse is measured via NC1 (within-class covariance trace ratio), with threshold NC1<0.01 for ReLU and NC1<0.05 otherwise. Mean feature norm (fn) from penultimate layer features is tracked each epoch; collapse epoch T_NC is first epoch below threshold, and fn* is fn at T_NC. The protocol ensures CE alone does not cause collapse; MSE drives fn down to fn*.

## Key Results
- Mean feature norm threshold fn* predicts collapse onset with a mean lead of 62 epochs (MAE 24).
- fn* is highly invariant across training conditions within a model-dataset pair (coefficient of variation <8%).
- Weight decay displays a three-regime phase diagram: too low slows collapse, an optimal range accelerates it, and too high prevents collapse entirely.
- Activation function determines both collapse speed and fn*: ReLU yields lowest fn* (~1.06), GELU highest (~2.13) with high variance, Tanh fastest.
- Architecture effects are dataset-dependent: ResNet-20 vs MLP-5 increases fn* by 458% on MNIST but only 68% on CIFAR-10.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Neural collapse onset is predicted by the mean penultimate-layer feature norm (fn) crossing a model-dataset-specific threshold fn*.
**Mechanism:** During Phase 2 (MSE loss), fn decays monotonically. When fn drops below fn* (typically ~1.0 for MNIST MLP, ~1.5 for CIFAR ResNet), NC1 collapses within a predictable lead time (~62 epochs). fn* acts as an attractor in the gradient flow.
**Core assumption:** The NC transition is governed by a single scalar feature-norm dynamics separable from other metrics.
**Evidence:** [abstract] NC occurs when fn reaches fn*; fn crossing below fn* predicts T_NC with mean lead 62 epochs. [section 5.4] Within each pair, fn* concentrates tightly (CV<8%); fn crossing below fn* precedes T_NC in all 8 width-sweep runs (mean gap 62 epochs). [corpus] Weak direct support; neighboring papers study BN/weight decay but do not identify predictive norm threshold.
**Break condition:** If two-phase protocol (CE→MSE) is not used, fn may not decay toward fn*.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Weight decay (λ) controls the rate of fn decay toward fn* but not fn* itself, within a collapsing regime.
**Mechanism:** λ scales implicit regularisation strength. Too low (λ=10⁻⁵) slows decay; too high (λ=5×10⁻⁴) over-regularises and prevents collapse by pinning fn above fn*. Optimal λ (5×10⁻⁵ for MNIST MLP) yields fastest decay.
**Core assumption:** fn* is set by the (model, dataset) pair; λ modulates gradient flow speed on fn trajectory.
**Evidence:** [abstract] Weight decay defines three-regime phase diagram. [section 5.3] Table 4 & Fig. 5 show T_NC shifts by 90 epochs across collapsing λ while fn at T_NC clusters (CV=6.4%). [corpus] Pan & Cao (2024) show BN and weight decay are enabling conditions for NC, consistent with regime effect.
**Break condition:** Architecture changes alter regime boundaries as fn* changes.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Activation function jointly determines both collapse speed and the value of fn*.
**Mechanism:** ReLU (positive homogeneous) yields lower fn* (~1.06) and slowest collapse; Tanh (bounded) yields intermediate fn* (~1.33) and fastest collapse; GELU (smooth non-homogeneous) yields highest fn* (~2.13) and intermediate speed, plus high seed sensitivity.
**Core assumption:** Activation shape influences equilibrium geometry of collapsed state, changing attractor fn*.
**Evidence:** [abstract] Activation sets both speed and fn*. [section 5.2] Table 3: ReLU (fn*=1.077), GELU (2.128), Tanh (1.325) at different T_NC; GELU CV=21% vs ReLU CV<3%. [corpus] Limited; prior work does not compare activations' effect on fn*.
**Break condition:** If activation is piecewise-linear and homogeneous (ReLU), fn* is lower; if non-homogeneous (GELU), fn* is higher and more variable.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Models (configurable MLP, fixed ResNet-20) -> Datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10) -> Training (two-phase CE→MSE) -> Metrics (NC1, fn, T_NC, fn*). Hyperparameters: depth (2-7), width (128-1024), activation (ReLU, GELU, Tanh), weight decay λ (10⁻⁵ to 5×10⁻⁴).
- **Critical path**:
  1. Establish baseline (MLP-5/ReLU/λ=1e-4 on MNIST): confirm fn decay in Phase2, measure fn* and T_NC.
  2. Vary one factor at a time (depth, activation, λ, width) while holding others constant. Record T_NC and fn at collapse.
  3. Complete (architecture×dataset) grid: add ResNet-20 on MNIST to expose interaction effects.
  4. Validate protocol: CE-only run to confirm insufficiency.
  5. Intervention: scale fn at Phase1 end to test fn* as attractor.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Depth vs. speed: Deeper (>3) slows collapse non-monotonically; shallow (2-3) may be faster but less expressive.
  - Weight decay: Must find Goldilocks zone; too high (λ≥5e-4) prevents collapse entirely.
  - Activation: ReLU gives low fn* but slow; GELU gives high fn* with seed sensitivity; Tanh fastest but less stable on complex data (NC1 ceiling higher).
  - Architecture-dataset interaction: Cannot extrapolate fn* from marginal effects; must measure per pair.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - No collapse: λ too high (≥5e-4), or using CE only (Phase 2 omitted).
  - Divergence/instability: GELU with some seeds (NC1→∞); excluded by pre-registered criterion.
  - Measurement error: Non-ReLU activations require higher NC1 threshold (0.05 vs 0.01) due to empirical ceiling; misapplying thresholds invalidates comparisons.
  - Non-convergence: ResNet-20 on CIFAR-10 takes 660 epochs; insufficient training time masks T_NC.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Baseline dynamics**: Train MLP-5/ReLU/λ=1e-4 on MNIST with two-phase protocol. Plot NC1, fn vs epoch. Verify 100% accuracy by epoch 10 but NC1 collapse only in Phase2. Record fn*≈1.06 at T_NC≈310.
  2. **Depth sweep**: Vary MLP depth ∈ {2,3,5,7} (ReLU, λ=1e-4). Measure T_NC and fn*. Confirm non-monotonic T_NC (depth-3 fastest) and fn* stability within depth group (CV<8%).
  3. **Activation sweep**: Fix MLP-5, vary activation ∈ {ReLU, GELU, Tanh} (λ=1e-4). Using appropriate NC1 thresholds, compare T_NC and fn*. Observe Tanh fastest (T_NC=220) but fn*=1.33; ReLU slowest (T_NC=310) but fn*=1.06; GELU highest fn*≈2.13 with 21% CV.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** What theoretical principles determine the feature norm threshold fn* and produce the strongly non-additive interaction between architecture and dataset?
  - *Basis:* Section VI.B reports ResNet-20 vs MLP-5 changes fn* by +458% on MNIST but only +68% on CIFAR-10; dataset effects reverse sign with architecture; effects do not sum additively.
  - *Why unresolved:* Paper provides empirical interaction but no predictive model for fn* on new pairs.
  - *Resolving evidence:* Analytical expression for fn* as function of loss geometry, Jacobian, dataset statistics; validation across diverse architectures/datasets; ablation isolating architectural factors.
- **Question 2:** Why is fn* a stable gradient-flow attractor, and can its value be derived in closed form from training objective?
  - *Basis:* Section VI.C intervention shows fn self-corrects to fn* from perturbations; fixed point lacks theoretical derivation.
  - *Why unresolved:* Attractor property confirmed but fixed point not derived; understanding descriptive, not predictive.
  - *Resolving evidence:* Mathematical derivation of fn* under MSE + weight decay, accounting for feature map Jacobian and dataset covariance; proof of convergence; empirical verification across conditions.
- **Question 3:** Can fn* be predicted from Phase 1 training dynamics, enabling prospective forecasting of neural collapse onset?
  - *Basis:* Section VI.D identifies open question: whether fn* can be predicted from Phase 1 alone.
  - *Why unresolved:* fn* currently measured after Phase 2 collapse begins; prediction would allow planning.
  - *Resolving evidence:* Strong correlations between early Phase 1 metrics (e.g., initial fn trajectory, Hessian eigenvalues, gradient norms) and eventual fn*; predictive model validated across architectures/datasets; demonstration that predicted fn* yields accurate T_NC forecasts.

## Limitations
- Two-phase protocol (CE→MSE) is required; unknown if collapse occurs with CE only.
- Experiments limited to MLP/MNIST and ResNet/CIFAR-10; other architectures/datasets may differ.
- Theoretical explanation for fn* mechanism is missing; could be dataset/architecture artifact.
- Hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size) not varied; fn* stability under different optimizers unclear.

## Confidence
- **fn* predictor (A):** High – low CV across sweeps, attractor intervention.
- **Weight decay three-regime (B):** Medium – λ range limited; may not generalize.
- **Activation effect (C):** Medium – clear patterns but GELU instability; hypothesis tentative.
