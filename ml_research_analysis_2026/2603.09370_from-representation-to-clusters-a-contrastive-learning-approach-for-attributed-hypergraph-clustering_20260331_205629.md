# From Representation to Clusters: A Contrastive Learning Approach for Attributed Hypergraph Clustering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09370
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09370
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CAHC achieves state-of-the-art attributed hypergraph clustering, with relative improvements of 10.3% in NMI and 17.1% in ARI on the Pubmed dataset compared to prior methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CAHC, an end-to-end framework for clustering attributed hypergraphs that directly incorporates clustering objectives into contrastive representation learning. Existing methods lack explicit clustering supervision, leading to suboptimal embeddings. CAHC addresses this by combining node-level and hyperedge-level contrastive losses to learn cluster-friendly embeddings, followed by a joint optimization stage that aligns soft cluster assignments with hard pseudo-labels. Evaluated on eight benchmark datasets, CAHC consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, with significant gains on challenging datasets like Pubmed. Ablation studies validate the necessity of each component, while sensitivity analyses offer practical hyperparameter guidance.

## Method Summary
CAHC operates in two stages. First, it performs **representation learning** by augmenting the input hypergraph via node feature masking and hyperedge membership masking, then encoding both views with a multi-head attentive hypergraph neural network (HGNN). Two contrastive objectives are optimized: a node-level InfoNCE loss that discriminates different nodes, and a hyperedge-level loss that distinguishes real hyperedges from negative ones created by random node replacement. Second, **cluster assignment learning** initializes cluster centroids via k-means on the learned embeddings, then jointly optimizes the encoder and a clustering loss that minimizes divergence between soft assignments (based on embedding-centroid similarity) and hard pseudo-labels (nearest centroid). This end-to-end scheme refines embeddings to be more separable while producing final cluster assignments.

## Key Results
- CAHC achieves relative improvements of **10.3% in NMI** and **17.1% in ARI** on Pubmed over prior state-of-the-art.
- It **consistently outperforms** baselines across eight datasets, with the largest gains on citation networks (Cora-A, Citeseer, Pubmed).
- Ablation studies confirm that **removing any core component** (dual contrastive losses, clustering loss, or attention mechanism) significantly degrades performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper proposes three synergistic mechanisms. First, **dual-level contrastive learning** (node- and hyperedge-level) produces cluster-friendly embeddings by preserving individual node discriminability and high-order structural coherence within hyperedges. Ablation shows that dropping either loss damages performance, confirming their complementarity. Second, **joint optimization of embeddings and clustering** provides direct supervision, aligning representations with the final partition goal rather than generic similarity; the soft/hard assignment alignment creates a self-reinforcing loop. Ablation removing this joint stage (“w/o cl”) underperforms the full model. Third, the **multi-head attention mechanism** in the HGNN adaptively weights node contributions within hyperedges, capturing nuanced importance beyond uniform averaging. Removing attention (“w/o mu”) causes significant performance drops, especially on datasets with varied node roles.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data Augmentation (M_f, M_m) → Two augmented views → Shared Encoder (Attentive HGNN) → Node embeddings Z → Projection Head g_φ → Node-level contrastive loss L_node  
Z pooled per hyperedge → Hyperedge scorer → Hyperedge-level loss L_hyper  
L_rep = L_node + L_hyper → Optimizer (Adam) updates encoder & projection (Stage 1)  
After T1 epochs: k-means on Z_init → Cluster centroids c  
Stage 2: Z recomputed → Soft assignments Q (Eq 13) & hard pseudo-labels l (Eq 14) → Clustering loss L_clus → Total loss L = L_rep + L_clus → Optimizer updates *all* parameters (encoder, projection, and possibly c? unclear) → Final labels via Hungarian algorithm.

**Critical path:**  
1. Input hypergraph (X, H) → augment to two views via feature and membership masking.  
2. Encode both views with shared attentive HGNN → embeddings Z^(1), Z^(2).  
3. Compute L_node: contrast Z^(1) vs Z^(2) for same nodes (instance discrimination).  
4. Compute L_hyper: pool nodes per hyperedge, score real vs. negative hyperedges (random node replacement).  
5. Update encoder & projection head using L_rep for T1 epochs.  
6. Fix Z_init, run k-means to initialize centroids c.  
7. For each epoch (Stage 2): recompute Z with current encoder, compute Q and l using c, compute L_clus, minimize L = L_rep + L_clus, update all parameters.  
8. After T2 epochs, apply Hungarian algorithm to final soft assignments for cluster labels.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Two-stage vs. end-to-end:* Two-stage (separate pre-training then joint fine-tuning) improves stability but adds complexity; purely end-to-end from random may collapse.  
- *Negative hyperedge sampling:* Random single-node replacement is cheap but ineffective for large hyperedges (e.g., 20NewsW100), where more sophisticated perturbations may be needed at higher computational cost.  
- *Attention complexity:* Multi-head attention captures nuanced node importance but increases parameters and computation versus standard HGNN averaging.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Poor cluster separation:* t-SNE shows overlapping clusters; likely cause is insufficient clustering loss weight (γ too low) or poor augmentation.  
- *Degenerate assignments:* All nodes assigned to one cluster; indicates representation learning failed (L_rep not optimized or augmentation too harsh).  
- *High runtime on large hyperedges:* Negative sampling becomes less discriminative for hyperedges with >100 nodes; attention computation also scales.  
- *Sensitivity to augmentation rates:* Performance peaks at intermediate p_f, p_m (~0.2–0.7); too low/too high destroys informative views.

**First 3 experiments:**

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **OQ1:** How can negative hyperedge sampling be improved for hypergraphs with large hyperedges (e.g., 20NewsW100), where random node replacement yields nearly identical negatives?  
- **OQ2:** To what extent does the multi-head attention mechanism capture higher-order structural dependencies beyond simple averaging, and how does this interact with the contrastive objectives?  
- **OQ3:** Can k-means initialization of centroids be avoided or improved within the end-to-end framework to reduce bias and enhance convergence?  
- **OQ4:** How do the hyperparameters τ_n (node contrastive temperature) and τ_c (clustering temperature) interact, and what is their optimal balance for different hypergraph properties?  
- **OQ5:** Is the joint optimization of representation and clustering loss susceptible to collapsing to trivial solutions without careful regularization, and how does its optimization stability compare to two-stage methods?

## Limitations
- **Negative hyperedge construction details are unspecified:** The number of replaced nodes, sampling distribution, and validity checks (e.g., minimum hyperedge size) are not described, critically impacting L_hyper.
- **Projection head architecture is undefined:** The exact MLP layers and dimensions after the encoder used for L_node are omitted, affecting node-level contrast.
- **Centroid update mechanism is ambiguous:** It is unclear whether cluster centers c are learnable parameters or fixed after k-means initialization; this is key to the claimed joint optimization.
- **Scalability to large hyperedges is problematic:** Negative sampling becomes ineffective for hyperedges with high cardinality, as explicitly noted for 20NewsW100.

## Confidence
- **Dual-level contrastive learning yields cluster-friendly embeddings:** Medium (ablation shows both losses are necessary, but hyperedge-negative efficacy is uncertain for large hyperedges).
- **Joint embedding-clustering optimization provides direct supervision:** Medium (ablation and comparisons support it, but centroid update semantics remain ambiguous).
- **Multi-head attention improves representation:** High (consistent and significant ablation gains across datasets).
- **Overall superiority over SOTA:** Medium (strong gains on most datasets, but RAGC wins on Mushroom; performance is hyperparameter-sensitive).
