# Knowledge, Rules and Their Embeddings: Two Paths towards Neuro-Symbolic JEPA

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13265
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13265
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RiJEPA achieves perfect zero‑shot logical accuracy (100%) on a clinical heart disease dataset and clean separation between valid and invalid rule regions in a synthetic 3D task (ID energy ~0.006, OOD ~12.7), whereas Classic JEPA fails.

## Executive Summary
RiJEPA is a neuro‑symbolic framework that bridges data‑driven self‑supervised learning (JEPA) with symbolic logic. It injects structured inductive biases via Energy‑Based Constraints (EBC) to geometrically sculpt the latent space, creating low‑energy basins for valid rule mappings and high‑energy regions for invalid ones. A multi‑modal dual‑encoder architecture projects continuous observations and discrete symbolic rules into a shared semantic space, enabling zero‑shot logical inference by simple geometric proximity. Moreover, the continuous relaxation of symbolic rules allows generative rule discovery through gradient‑guided Langevin diffusion, bypassing combinatorial search. Evaluations on a synthetic 3D energy landscape and a real‑world heart disease dataset confirm that EBC effectively separates rule validity (ID ~0.006 vs. OOD ~12.7 vs. Classic JEPA’s OOD ~0.15) and that the shared manifold yields 100% zero‑shot accuracy without downstream classifiers, supporting robust forward and abductive reasoning.

## Method Summary
RiJEPA extends the Joint‑Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) with rule‑informed training. Separate encoders process raw data and association rules, but both modalities are projected into a common latent space. A shared predictor `g` operates in this space and is trained with three losses: the standard JEPA loss (predicting future data embeddings), an Energy‑Based Constraint (EBC) loss that pulls valid rule pairs together while pushing invalid pairs apart by a margin, and an anchor loss (for real data) that aligns data predictions with fixed embeddings of target symbolic poles (e.g., “high risk”). The target encoder is maintained via an exponential moving average. The learned energy function defines a differentiable manifold on which Langevin diffusion can generate new rule embeddings, which are then mapped to symbolic rules. The method requires pre‑mined association rules (e.g., via FP‑Growth) and careful negative sampling for EBC.

## Key Results
- Synthetic 3D experiment: RiJEPA yields near‑zero energy for valid rules (mean 0.006) and high energy for invalid rules (12.713), while Classic JEPA fails to separate (OOD energy 0.150).
- Clinical heart disease dataset: RiJEPA attains 100% zero‑shot logical accuracy by aligning patient data embeddings with symbolic risk poles, eliminating the need for downstream supervised classifiers.
- Generative rule discovery: Langevin diffusion produces clinically coherent rules, e.g., abductive inference from “High Risk” recovers antecedents like “exercise angina=1.0, downsloping ST, senior age”.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Energy‑Based Constraints Shape Latent Geometry
- **Claim:** EBC geometrically sculpts the JEPA latent space, creating separable low‑energy basins for valid rules and high‑energy regions for invalid ones.
- **Mechanism:** The EBC loss `L_EBC = Σ_valid E(A,C) + λ Σ_invalid max(0, m - E(A,C_invalid))` with `E(A,C)=||g(f_c(A)) - f_t(C)||²` pulls valid pairs together and pushes invalid pairs apart by margin `m`.
- **Core assumption:** Valid symbolic rules correspond to stable low‑energy attractors; invalid ones can be separated by an explicit margin.
- **Evidence:** Synthetic experiment shows RiJEPA yields ID energy ~0.006 and OOD energy ~12.7, while Classic JEPA fails (OOD ~0.15).
- **Break condition:** Insufficient margin or uninformative negative sampling leads to entangled latent spaces; success depends on sampling invalid rules from distinct distributions.

### Mechanism 2: Multi‑Modal Dual‑Encoder Alignment
- **Claim:** Separate data and rule encoders project inputs into a shared latent space, enabling zero‑shot logical inference via geometric proximity.
- **Mechanism:** Two pairs of encoders (`f_c^data`, `f_t^data`; `f_c^rule`, `f_t^rule`) map each modality to a common `d=32` space. A single predictor `g` operates in this space, trained on both data and rule losses, enforcing alignment. At inference, data embeddings are compared to fixed symbolic poles.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic alignment between raw data and abstract symbols is achievable by forcing embeddings into a common predictive space.
- **Evidence:** t‑SNE on clinical data shows data points cluster around anchoring poles for “High Risk” and “Low Risk”, yielding 100% zero‑shot accuracy.
- **Break condition:** Inadequate latent capacity (`d`) or encoder expressiveness causes representational collapse or interference; the architecture avoids concatenation, processing modalities independently through `g`.

### Mechanism 3: Continuous Relaxation Enables Generative Rule Discovery
- **Claim:** Relaxing discrete rules into a differentiable energy manifold allows bypassing combinatorial search and using gradient‑guided Langevin diffusion to generate rules.
- **Mechanism:** The learned energy `E(z_c,z_t)=||g(z_c)-z_t||²` defines a continuous distribution `p(z_c,z_t) ∝ exp(-E)`. Langevin dynamics on this manifold yields new rule embeddings, which can be decoded (or nearest‑neighbor matched) to symbolic rules.
- **Core assumption:** The energy landscape is well‑shaped with clear basins, as ensured by EBC.
- **Evidence:** Clinical experiments produce coherent rules from abductive (from High Risk) and forward (from patient vitals) generation.
- **Break condition:** Poorly shaped landscape leads to invalid or oscillatory generations; temperature parameter `T` must balance exploration vs. staying in valid basins.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Joint‑Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)**
  - Why needed here: RiJEPA builds directly on JEPA’s latent‑to‑latent prediction paradigm, which filters low‑level noise to focus on high‑level semantic features. The entire framework modifies JEPA’s standard loss and architecture.
  - Quick check question: What is the core difference between JEPA’s prediction target (latent vectors) and an autoencoder’s target (input reconstruction)?

- **Concept: Metric Learning & Contrastive Objectives**
  - Why needed here: EBC is fundamentally a contrastive loss (pull valid pairs, push invalid pairs with margin). Understanding triplet/contrastive loss mechanics is essential to grasp how latent space geometry is shaped.
  - Quick check question: How does a contrastive margin `m` in a loss function differ from a simple mean‑squared error regression loss in terms of forcing spatial separation?

- **Concept: Association Rule Mining (ARM)**
  - Why needed here: The paper uses ARM (FP‑Growth) to extract the initial symbolic rule set (`IF antecedent→THEN consequent`) that provides the structural inductive bias. Without rule extraction, there is no symbolic supervision.
  - Quick check question: If you have a transactional dataset, what does a “support” threshold control in ARM, and what is a major downside of setting it too strictly?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
1.  **Data Encoders:** `f_c^data`, `f_t^data` (MLPs) map high‑dimensional clinical vitals to 32‑D latents.
2.  **Rule Encoders:** `f_c^rule`, `f_t^rule` map discrete clinical logic strings (e.g., “age>50”) to 32‑D latents.
3.  **Shared Predictor `g`:** Non‑linear head (32→64→32) that takes *any* 32‑D context (data or rule) and predicts a 32‑D target.
4.  **Loss Calculator:** Computes `L_JEPA` (data→data), `L_EBC` (rule valid/invalid pairs), and `L_anchor` (data prediction vs. fixed rule poles).
5.  **EMA Updater:** Keeps `f_t^data` stable via momentum update from `f_c^data`.
6.  **Langevin Diffusion Module:** Uses `∇_z E(z)` to navigate the energy manifold for generative rule discovery.

**Critical path:** Raw patient vitals `x` → `f_c^data` → `z_c^data` → `g` → `ẑ_t^data` (predict future state) & `⟂` `f_t^rule("High Risk")` (zero‑shot classification). Training path also loops through rule extraction → rule encoders → `g` → `L_EBC`.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **EBC Weight (`β`/`α`):** Too low → rules don’t shape manifold; too high → model ignores raw data correlations, harming reconstruction (`Fig 9` shows trade‑off).
- **Latent Dimension (`d`):** Too small → cannot separate clusters; too large → sample inefficiency and risk of overfitting rule noise.
- **Negative Pair Sampling:** Must generate *meaningful* OOD invalid rules (e.g., from separate Gaussians) to define a boundary; random corruption is less effective.
- **EMA Momentum (`τ`):** Critical to prevent collapse of `f_t^data`; standard value `0.99` used.

**Failure signatures:**
1.  **Poor Clustering (t‑SNE/UMAP):** Data points from opposite classes (e.g., High/Low Risk) remain entangled, not anchored to poles.
2.  **Low Zero‑Shot Accuracy:** High `L2` distance between `g(f_c_data(x_test))` and correct rule pole, even if linear probe accuracy is 100%.
3.  **High OOD Energy in Synthetic Test:** Classic JEPA‑like behavior where invalid rules get low energy (`≤0.15` instead of `>> m`).
4.  **Gradient Instability in Langevin:** Generated rules are nonsensical or oscillate wildly, indicating a poorly shaped energy landscape.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Synthetic 3D Topology (Section 5.2):** Train Classic JEPA vs. RiJEPA on simulated data with known rule centers. Visualize learned energy landscape and compute ID/OOD energy. **Goal:** Confirm EBC carves separable basins (RiJEPA ID~0, OOD~12 vs. Classic JEPA ID~0, OOD~0.15).
2.  **Clinical Manifold Alignment (Section 5.3):** Train RiJEPA on UCI Heart data with FP‑Growth rules. Visualize t‑SNE of test patient embeddings vs. fixed rule poles. Compute Zero‑Shot Logical Accuracy (proximity to pole). **Goal:** Achieve 100% accuracy and clear visual separation.
3.  **Generative Langevin Validation (Section 5.3):** Fix a test patient’s vitals, run Langevin on `z_t` space (abductive) or `z_c` space (forward), decode nearest symbolic rule. **Goal:** Produce clinically coherent rules (e.g., “High Risk” → “senior age, exercise angina”). Assess via clinical plausibility or a simple consistency metric against a rule database.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does integrating contrastive triplet loss enhance the geometric separation and compositional structure of the rule‑informed latent space compared to the current anchor and pairwise constraints?
- **Basis:** [explicit] The paper’s Future Work states: “Future work will explore this contrastive extension, evaluate the practical computational limitations … and further refine robust latent‑to‑symbolic rule decoding mechanisms.” (Section 6, Discussion)
- **Why unresolved:** The current RiJEPA uses anchor loss (absolute centroid alignment) and energy‑based pairwise constraint. Triplet loss could impose relative margin constraints that better capture hierarchical rule relationships. This remains untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement triplet loss (anchor, positive, negative) in RiJEPA, train on the same datasets, and compare: (a) latent space clustering quality (e.g., silhouette score), (b) zero‑shot logical accuracy, (c) performance on generative tasks (validity rate of discovered rules). A statistically significant improvement across metrics would support the hypothesis.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the dimensionality of the latent space affect the computational cost and quality of joint Langevin diffusion for rule generation?
- **Basis:** [explicit] The paper notes: “evaluate the practical computational limitations of joint Langevin diffusion in higher‑dimensional semantic spaces.” (Section 6)
- **Why unresolved:** Experiments used a 32‑dimensional latent space. The performance of Langevin dynamics may degrade in higher dimensions due to slower mixing, increased noise, and curse of dimensionality. This critical scaling property is not characterized.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct systematic experiments varying latent dimension (e.g., 32, 64, 128, 256). Measure (a) convergence speed (steps to reach low energy), (b) quality of generated rules (validity rate, semantic coherence via human evaluation), and (c) computational resources (time, memory). Identify threshold where performance drops and propose mitigations (e.g., dimensionality reduction, adaptive step sizes).

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can a structured decoder reliably map continuous latent embeddings back to human‑interpretable symbolic rules without loss of logical integrity?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Section 4.3 highlights the need: “a robust latent‑to‑symbolic rule decoding projection mechanism is required” to avoid “nonsensical mixtures”. Also Future Work mentions refining such decoding.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not implement or evaluate a specific decoder; it remains an open design challenge. Without it, the generative rule discovery cannot produce trustworthy symbolic outputs, limiting interpretability and trust.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Design a decoder (e.g., a neural network with structured output layers corresponding to the rule tuple components) and integrate it with the trained RiJEPA. Evaluate by: (i) decoding a large set of latent vectors (from random sampling and from Langevin diffusion), (ii) having domain experts rate logical coherence and plausibility, (iii) measuring reconstruction accuracy of known rules. High success rates would demonstrate feasibility.

## Limitations
- **Reproduction blockers:** Unclear rule encoding (one‑hot vs. embedding), synthetic invalid sampling distribution, data preprocessing specifics, and pole construction impede faithful replication.
- **Dataset scope:** Evaluation limited to a single clinical dataset (UCI Heart, n=303), raising generalizability concerns.
- **Generative validation:** Rule discovery via Langevin diffusion lacks quantitative evaluation; only anecdotal examples are provided.

## Confidence
- **EBC latent shaping:** Medium (strong synthetic evidence, indirect real‑world support via clustering)
- **Zero‑shot logical accuracy:** Medium (100% on small dataset, no cross‑dataset confirmation)
- **Generative rule discovery:** Low (only qualitative examples, no systematic metrics)
- **Overall neuro‑symbolic integration:** Medium (novel architecture, narrow evaluation)
