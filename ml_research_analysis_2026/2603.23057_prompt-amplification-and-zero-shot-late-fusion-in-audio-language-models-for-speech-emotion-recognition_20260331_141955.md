# Prompt Amplification and Zero-Shot Late Fusion in Audio-Language Models for Speech Emotion Recognition

## Quick Facts
-   **arXiv ID:** 2603.23057
-   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23057
-   **Reference Count:** 30
-   **Primary Result:** A late-fusion method (ZS-Fuse) combining fine-tuned speech Foundation Models with frozen zero-shot Audio-Language Models improves Speech Emotion Recognition UAR, with performance gains strongly dependent on the choice of ALM and the use of a novel "prompt amplification" technique.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces **ZS-Fuse**, a method for Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) that fuses a fine-tuned specialist speech Foundation Model (FM) like WavLM with a frozen zero-shot dual-encoder Audio-Language Model (ALM) like CLSP. The fusion occurs at the feature level: the FM's penultimate layer representation is concatenated with the ALM's normalized zero-shot similarity scores for emotion classes. To improve the reliability of the ALM's zero-shot estimates, the authors use a simple ensemble of text prompts and a novel "prompt amplification" trick that repeats the audio signal and text query. Evaluated on three SER datasets, ZS-Fuse consistently outperforms FM-only baselines, with the CLSP ALM providing the most robust gains. However, prompt amplification is unstable: while the best configuration can yield substantial improvements, the worst can degrade performance below the baseline.

## Method Summary
ZS-Fuse employs a dual-branch architecture. The **FM branch** fine-tunes a pre-trained speech encoder (WavLM/HuBERT) on the target SER dataset by adding and training a linear classification head. The **ALM branch** remains entirely frozen. For a given audio input, it generates a set of text prompts (e.g., "angry speech", "a person speaking angrily") for each emotion class. The core innovation is **prompt amplification**: the raw audio waveform is repeated `a` times (concatenated), and each `<emotion>` placeholder in the prompt is replaced with `t` comma-separated copies. All `(audio_repeat, text_repeat)` pairs are processed by the ALM's audio and text encoders. The resulting cosine similarities for each emotion class are averaged across the prompt ensemble and normalized. The FM's feature vector `h` and the ALM's score vector `s` are concatenated and fed to a new linear classifier, trained end-to-end while keeping the FM and ALM frozen. The optimal `(a,t)` configuration is selected via validation set performance.

## Key Results
-   ZS-Fuse improves Unweighted Average Recall (UAR) over FM-only baselines, but the magnitude and even the sign of the improvement depend heavily on the ALM used. The CLSP ALM yields gains in 9/12 evaluated FM-dataset pairs, while CLAP and ReCLAP show mixed or negative results.
-   Prompt amplification can significantly boost the *best-case* zero-shot performance of an ALM (e.g., CLSP on RAVDESS), leading to larger final UAR gains in ZS-Fuse. However, the *worst-case* amplification configuration can degrade performance below the FM-only baseline, highlighting high instability.
-   Using the optimal amplification configuration with WavLM-Large and CLSP, the method achieves state-of-the-art UAR of 0.8711 on RAVDESS and 0.7184 on IEMOCAP.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
-   **Claim:** Late fusion of zero-shot ALM scores with fine-tuned FM representations reliably improves UAR over FM-only baselines for SER, but benefits are ALM-dependent and dataset-specific.
-   **Mechanism:** The FM branch learns acoustic-to-emotion mappings via fine-tuning, while the frozen ALM branch provides a semantically grounded zero-shot prior via audio-text similarity. Their concatenation (`[h; LN(s)]`) allows the final classifier to weight evidence from both sources. The ALM's zero-shot scores act as a form of semantic regularization or prior, potentially correcting FM overfitting to spurious acoustic correlations.
-   **Core assumption:** The ALM's zero-shot emotion estimates contain non-redundant, complementary information to the FM's learned representations. This assumes the ALM has sufficient alignment between its audio embedding space and the emotion class textual descriptions.
-   **Evidence anchors:**
    -   [abstract] "ZS-Fuse, a late-fusion method that combines zero-shot emotion estimates from a dual-encoder ALM with specialist FMs... report improvements over SOTA baselines."
    -   [section II.A] "We use LayerNorm (LN) and concatenation to do the late fusion... which is then fed to the classification output layer."
    -   [Table I] Shows CLSP (ALM) improves WavLM-Large UAR on RAVDESS (0.8438→0.8711) and IEMOCAP (0.7051→0.7184), while CLAP and ReCLAP show mixed results.
    -   [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., PALM, TSPE) explore prompt engineering for ALMs, suggesting zero-shot score reliability is a recognized challenge; this work's fusion is a mitigation.
-   **Break condition:** The fusion degrades performance when the ALM's zero-shot estimates are systematically misaligned with the FM's learned decision boundaries. Evidenced by CLAP/ReCLAP degrading performance for some FM-dataset pairs (e.g., WavLM-Large+ReCLAP on all three datasets in Table I).

### Mechanism 2
-   **Claim:** "Prompt amplification"—repeating audio and textual queries—can substantially enhance the *best-case* zero-shot capability of a dual-encoder ALM, which can then benefit downstream fusion, but it introduces high instability and risk of degradation.
-   **Mechanism:** Audio repetition (`a×`) extends the effective audio duration/energy, potentially helping the ALM's audio encoder extract stronger features. Text repetition (`t×`) reinforces the textual prompt embedding, effectively "amplifying" the query representation in the contrastive space. This combination (`a×t×`) is a test-time, non-parametric way to modulate the confidence and discriminability of the zero-shot similarity scores `s`.
-   **Core assumption:** The dual-encoder ALM's similarity function is suboptimal for the SER decision boundary; repeated inputs can push the audio and text embeddings into regions of the joint space with higher signal-to-noise ratio for the target classes.
-   **Evidence anchors:**
    -   [abstract] "prompt amplification... repeats audio and text queries to discover stronger zero-shot capabilities."
    -   [section II.B] "it simply repeats the signal, while in text, it replaces `<emotion_j>` speech with N instances of `<emotion_j>` speech."
    -   [Fig. 2] Shows clear performance variation across the 16 `a×t×` combinations, with best configurations (e.g., CLSP on RAVDESS: a1×t4, UAR≈0.742) substantially outperforming the default (a1×t1). Lighter colors (higher performance) tend toward more text repeats.
    -   [Table II] "Besta×t substantially improves over the default a1×t1 as well as the no ALM baseline. The worst a×t counterintuitively degrades performance."
-   **Break condition:** Excessive or improper repetition leads to embedding degradation. For CLSP on RAVDESS, the worst `a×t×` (a3×t2, UAR=0.8008) is below the FM-only baseline (0.8438). The paper notes CLSP degrades with audio repeats due to an implementation limit (6s audio cap), suggesting model-specific input length sensitivity is a key break condition.

### Mechanism 3
-   **Claim:** The identity of the ALM relative to the FM is a primary determinant of whether ZS-Fuse yields a net gain, with CLSP showing the most consistent compatibility, likely due to its specialized training for fine-grained speech-text alignment.
-   **Mechanism:** Specialist FMs (WavLM, HuBERT) are optimized for speech representation via self-supervision. ALMs trained with broader audio-text data (CLAP) or improved caption data (ReCLAP) may not align as well with the emotional speech subspace. CLSP, which incorporates "fine-grained supervision via external captioning models and agentic verification," likely produces audio embeddings more sensitive to paralinguistic cues relevant to emotion, creating more complementary information for the FM.
-   **Core assumption:** Complementarity in ZS-Fuse is not just about individual model strength but about the *alignment* between the ALM's semantic grounding and the FM's acoustic feature space for the specific task (SER).
-   **Evidence anchors:**
    -   [abstract] "CLSP yielded the most gains (e.g., WavLM-Large+CLSP: RAVDESS UAR up to 0.8711, IEMOCAP up to 0.7184)."
    -   [Table I] "Across the 12 runs, CLAP improved UAR in 5/12, ReCLAP in 3/12, and CLSP in 9/12." CLSP consistently improves across WavLM models and datasets (except MSP-Podcast for WavLM-Large).
    -   [section IV.B] "CLSP performs best on RAVDESS (UAR=0.742, a1×t4 config), even surpassing the WavLM-Base-Plus supervised baseline."
    -   [corpus] Neighbors like "Scaling Ambiguity..." and "Steering Language Model..." focus on ALM limitations in SER, implying not all ALMs are equally suitable; this paper's result pattern supports that CLSP's training is uniquely beneficial.
-   **Break condition:** ALM-FM incompatibility, where the ALM's zero-shot scores are either uncorrelated or anti-correlated with the correct labels relative to the FM's output. Evidenced by ReCLAP degrading performance in 3/12 cases and CLAP degrading in 2/12 cases (Table I).

## Foundational Learning

-   **Concept: Zero-Shot Classification with Dual-Encoder ALMs**
    -   **Why needed here:** The ALM branch of ZS-Fuse relies entirely on pre-computed, frozen embeddings from a dual-encoder model (CLAP, CLSP). Engineers must understand that performance is determined by the pre-trained audio-text alignment and the *inference-time* textual prompts, not by any fine-tuning on the emotion labels.
    -   **Quick check question:** Given a frozen dual-encoder ALM and a new set of emotion classes, what are the two steps to obtain zero-shot predictions for an audio sample? (Answer: 1) Compute audio embedding. 2) For each emotion class, compute similarity between audio embedding and the text embedding(s) of the prompt template(s) for that class. The class with highest similarity is the prediction.)

-   **Concept: Late Fusion in Multimodal Classifiers**
    -   **Why needed here:** ZS-Fuse's core innovation is a simple, non-differentiable fusion at the *feature* level (concatenation of FM's penultimate layer output `h` and normalized ALM score vector `s`). This requires understanding that the two information streams are combined *after* each has produced its own representation/prediction, avoiding the complexity of end-to-end training the ALM.
    -   **Quick check question:** What is a key architectural difference between late fusion (as in ZS-Fuse) and early/mid-level fusion for combining a frozen model (ALM) with a fine-tuned model (FM)? (Answer: Late fusion combines model outputs/features *after* independent processing, allowing the fine-tuned FM to adapt to the ALM's output format via the final classification layer rather than merging modalities earlier in the network.)

-   **Concept: Prompt Engineering and Ensembling for Audio**
    -   **Why needed here:** The ALM's zero-shot score is highly sensitive to the exact wording of the text prompt. This work uses a simple ensemble of three template styles ("emotion+speech", "speaker-centric", "voice-attribute") per class to hedge against this. The "prompt amplification" trick is an extreme form of prompt modification (repetition).
    -   **Quick check question:** If an ALM shows high variance in zero-shot SER performance across different prompt templates for the same emotion class, what two strategies from this paper could you employ to stabilize performance? (Answer: 1) Use a prompt ensemble (average scores across multiple valid templates). 2) Use prompt amplification (systematically vary audio/text repeats) and select the best configuration on a validation set, though this introduces a hyperparameter search.)

## Architecture Onboarding

-   **Component map:**
    FM Branch (frozen speech encoder -> fine-tuned linear head) -> `h` (D-dim)
    ALM Branch (frozen audio encoder & text encoder) + Prompt Manager (templates) + Amplification Controller (`a`,`t`) -> Zero-Shot Scorer (similarity, average, LayerNorm) -> `s` (E-dim)
    Fusion Layer (concatenate `[h; LN(s)]`) -> Classifier Head (new linear layer) -> Output logits.

-   **Critical path:**
    1.  Input audio -> FM branch -> `h`.
    2.  Input audio & prompt templates -> Amplification Controller -> (repeated audio, repeated text).
    3.  Repeated audio -> ALM audio encoder.
    4.  Repeated text per class -> ALM text encoder.
    5.  Compute cosine similarities for all classes -> average over prompts -> `s`.
    6.  Apply LayerNorm to `s`.
    7.  Concatenate `h` and `LN(s)` -> Classifier head -> Output.

-   **Design tradeoffs:**
    -   **ALM Choice vs. Compute:** CLSP/ReCLAP are larger and slower than CLAP. CLSP was most effective but had implementation quirks (degradation with audio repeats). CLAP is more robust but less task-specialized.
    -   **Amplification Knob:** `(a,t)` is a discrete, test-time hyperparameter. Sweeping 16 combinations adds validation cost but can yield large gains. Default (1,1) is safe but suboptimal. Choosing `a>1` risks hitting model input length limits.
    -   **Prompt Ensemble Complexity:** Using more templates could improve robustness but increases inference cost linearly. The paper's minimal 3-template ensemble is a pragmatic tradeoff.
    -   **Fusion Simplicity:** Concatenation + new linear head is simple and effective but could be suboptimal compared to more complex fusion (e.g., attention, gating).

-   **Failure signatures:**
    1.  **Amplification Degradation:** Best `(a,t)` on validation does not replicate on test, or worst `(a,t)` performs below FM-only baseline. Indicates instability for that ALM-FM-dataset triplet.
    2.  **ALM-Induced Harm:** FM-only baseline > ZS-Fuse (with default prompts). Suggests ALM's zero-shot scores are misleading or anti-correlated. Common with ReCLAP and CLAP on some datasets.
    3.  **Training Instability:** High variance across random seeds for ZS-Fuse configuration, more than FM-only baseline. Could indicate poorly conditioned fused feature space.
    4.  **Inference Bottleneck:** ALM inference time dominates, especially with amplification (`a*t` forward passes). For long audio, `a>1` may exceed the model's max input length.

-   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Reproduce FM-only Baseline:** Fine-tune each FM (WavLM-Base+, WavLM-Large, HuBERT-Base, HuBERT-Large) on each dataset using standard linear probing. Report mean±std UAR over 3 seeds.
    2.  **ZS-Fuse with Default Prompts (a=1, t=1):** Implement late fusion for each FM+ALM pair using the 3-template ensemble without amplification. Train fused classifier. Compare UAR to Experiment 1 baseline. Verify improvement pattern, especially CLSP superiority.
    3.  **Prompt Amplification Sweep:** For the most promising FM+ALM pair (e.g., WavLM-Large+CLSP), sweep all `(a,t)` ∈ {1,2,3,4}² on the validation set. Identify best and worst configs. Evaluate these fixed configs on the test set. Validate that best beats default and worst dips below FM-only baseline.

## Limitations
-   Exact prompt templates for each of the 8 emotion classes across all datasets are not fully specified; replication relies on inferred template structures.
-   Feature extraction details for FMs are ambiguous (e.g., which layer's output, pooling method).
-   Generalizability beyond the three evaluated SER datasets remains untested.

## Confidence
-   **Late fusion reliability improves UAR:** **Medium**. Table I shows consistent gains only for CLSP; CLAP/ReCLAP often degrade, supporting ALM/dataset dependence but undermining "reliable".
-   **Prompt amplification provides best-case boost with high risk:** **Medium**. Strong best-case improvements and worst-case degradation demonstrated (Fig. 2, Table II), but the underlying mechanism (repetition amplifying embeddings) is speculative.
-   **ALM identity (CLSP) is a key determinant of success:** **High**. Clear pattern: CLSP improves in 9/12 cases vs. CLAP (5/12) and ReCLAP (3/12). The explanation (specialized fine-grained alignment) is logical but not directly proven.

## Next Checks
1.  **Baseline replication:** Reproduce FM-only UAR and ZS-Fuse (a1×t1) numbers in Table I for all FM+ALM+dataset combinations. Confirm the improvement pattern, especially CLSP's consistent superiority.
2.  **Amplification sweep validation:** For the top FM+ALM pair (WavLM-Large+CLSP), exhaustively test all `(a,t)`∈{1,2,3,4}² on the validation set. Then evaluate the identified best and worst configurations on the test set. Verify the pattern: best beats default and FM-only, worst degrades below FM-only.
3.  **Break condition diagnosis:** Test CLSP's sensitivity to audio repetition while strictly controlling for input duration (e.g., pad/crop to a fixed 6-second length). Isolate whether degradation stems from exceeding the model's maximum input length or from a fundamental flaw in how repeated audio is processed by the encoder.
