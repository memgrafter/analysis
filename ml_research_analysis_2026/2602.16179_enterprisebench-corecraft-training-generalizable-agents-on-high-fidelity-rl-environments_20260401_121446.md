# EnterpriseBench Corecraft: Training Generalizable Agents on High-Fidelity RL Environments
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16179
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16179
- **Reference count:** 3
- **Primary result:** Training GLM 4.6 on CoreCraft for one epoch increased held-out task pass rate by 11.39 percentage points and yielded positive transfer (+4.5% to +7.4%) to three out-of-distribution benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether training an agent on a single, high-fidelity enterprise simulation (CoreCraft) improves generalizable problem-solving skills applicable to unseen, diverse benchmarks. CoreCraft is a realistic, stateful environment with expert-defined tasks and rubric-based rewards. Using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), the authors trained GLM 4.6 for one epoch. The model’s performance on a held-out CoreCraft test set improved significantly. More notably, this training transferred positively to three external, out-of-distribution tool-use benchmarks (BFCL Parallel, τ²-Bench Retail, Tool Decathlon), suggesting that exposure to complex, realistic, and diverse professional workflows fosters broadly applicable competencies rather than environment-specific memorization.

## Method Summary
The core method involves training an LLM agent (GLM 4.6) via reinforcement learning on a new environment called CoreCraft. CoreCraft is a Docker-contained, stateful simulation of enterprise operations with 2,500+ entities and 23 tools. Agent actions (tool calls) are routed through the Model Context Protocol (MCP) to modify the environment state. Each training task comes with an expert-authored, granular rubric. After an episode, an LLM judge evaluates the final trajectory against the rubric criteria to produce a dense reward signal (proportion of satisfied criteria). The policy is updated using GRPO, which computes group-relative advantages without a critic, using a buffer of trajectories collected per prompt. The process iterates over a fixed set of 1,000 training tasks for a single epoch before evaluation.

## Key Results
- Held-out CoreCraft pass rate increased by 11.39 percentage points (from 25.37% to 36.76%) after one epoch of GRPO training.
- Positive transfer to out-of-distribution benchmarks: +4.5% on BFCL Parallel (Pass@1), +7.4% on τ²-Bench Retail, and +6.8% on Tool Decathlon.
- Results indicate that high-fidelity, diverse enterprise environments can train agents with skills that generalize beyond their training distribution.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Task Diversity Fosters Flexible Strategies
- **Claim:** Training on environments with high task diversity fosters generalizable problem-solving strategies rather than narrow heuristics.
- **Mechanism:** Exposure to a wide range of task types (information retrieval, communication, reasoning, multi-step workflows) forces the policy to develop a flexible repertoire. The agent must diagnose task type and select appropriate tool and reasoning sequences instead of relying on a single pattern.
- **Core assumption:** Skill diversity in the training distribution correlates with robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "task-centric world building that optimizes for diverse, challenging tasks"; [section 3.1] "Our central design principle is that high-quality, diverse tasks provide a useful training signal."
- **Break condition:** If gains collapse when removing task variety (e.g., training on only information retrieval tasks), the mechanism is falsified.

### Mechanism 2: Granular Rubrics Provide Dense, Reliable Rewards
- **Claim:** Expert-authored, granular rubrics provide a dense, reliable reward signal that guides learning toward nuanced success criteria, preventing reward hacking.
- **Mechanism:** Rubrics decompose success into verifiable sub-criteria (completeness, correctness, constraint satisfaction, format). The LLM judge computes a continuous reward from these, providing frequent, shaped feedback. This encourages satisfying all facets of a professional task rather than optimizing for a single, gameable metric.
- **Core assumption:** Fine-grained reward decomposition is necessary for learning complex, multi-faceted professional behaviors that generalize.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "expert-authored rubrics enabling reliable reward computation"; [section 5.4] "Rubric criteria are designed to be granular and objective... This provides a dense reward signal."
- **Break condition:** Training with a single binary reward (pass/fail) should yield significantly lower in-distribution and transfer performance.

### Mechanism 3: Realistic Workflows Teach Robustness
- **Claim:** Realistic enterprise workflows with interconnected state and "noise" force agents to develop robust, context-aware policies that transfer.
- **Mechanism:** The environment simulates real operational backends with interdependent entities and tools. Tasks require navigating imperfect information (e.g., inferring truncated results from pagination limits). This teaches persistence, contextual cue recognition, and comprehensive tool exploration—skills directly applicable to other long-horizon tool-use benchmarks.
- **Core assumption:** Realism in state dynamics and edge cases is key; synthetic or simplified environments fail to train these robustness mechanisms.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "enterprise workflows that reflect realistic professional patterns"; [section 3.2] "The environment includes the 'noise' of real enterprise data: conflicting timestamps, incomplete records, pagination limits..."
- **Break condition:** A "sanitized" version of CoreCraft should show reduced transfer to benchmarks with similar real-world noise.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (GRPO)
  - **Why needed:** The entire training loop uses GRPO to update the policy from rubric-based rewards without a value network. Understanding group-relative advantages is key to replicating the infrastructure.
  - **Quick check:** How does GRPO compute advantages without a critic, and why might this benefit memory-intensive LLM training?
- **Concept:** Model Context Protocol (MCP) for Tool Integration
  - **Why needed:** All agent-tool interactions route through the MCP server in the Docker container. Replication requires implementing compatible tool schemas and routing.
  - **Quick check:** What are the tradeoffs of using a standardized protocol like MCP versus custom tool-call parsers?
- **Concept:** Rubric-Based Automated Evaluation
  - **Why needed:** The reward signal derives from an LLM-as-a-judge scoring against expert rubrics. This non-standard design requires careful handling to avoid bias.
  - **Quick check:** What makes a rubric criterion "verifiable" here, and how does that support stable RL training?
- **Concept:** Stateful Enterprise Simulation
  - **Why needed:** CoreCraft's transactional state and interdependent entities create realistic operational complexity. This realism is proposed as key for transfer.
  - **Quick check:** How does maintaining persistent, causal state across tool calls differ from stateless tool-use environments in terms of learned policy behavior?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Task Prompt -> Rollout Engine (SGLang) <-> Environment Container (Docker + MCP) -> Final Response -> Reward Module (LLM Judge) -> Data Buffer -> Training Loop (Megatron + GRPO) -> Updated Weights -> Rollout Engine

**Critical path:** Generate rollout with stateful tool calls via MCP, compute rubric-based reward via LLM judge, store trajectory, update policy with GRPO, sync weights to rollout engine.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Stateful Docker vs. Stateless Simulation:** Docker ensures transactional consistency and real backend complexity but is slower to reset. Stateless sims are faster but may miss cascading bugs.
- **LLM Judge vs. Programmatic Reward:** LLM judge handles nuanced rubrics but introduces cost, latency, and inconsistency. Programmatic checks are cheap but limited to simple assertions.
- **Single Epoch Training:** Used for initial results; likely underfits. Extended training risks overfitting to CoreCraft’s specific entity/tool distributions.

**Failure signatures:**
- **In-distribution:** Held-out pass rate < baseline (25.37%) after training indicates reward signal or loop misconfiguration.
- **Transfer:** Gains on BFCL/τ²-Bench/Toolathlon < 1-2 pp suggests learned skills are environment-specific or training was insufficient.
- **Toolathlon Pass@1 volatility:** High standard deviation (>2%) across runs indicates policy inconsistency; may need more rollouts per update or different KL penalty.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Reproduce In-Distribution Gain:** Train GLM 4.6 for 1 epoch on the published 1,000-task split. Verify held-out CoreCraft pass rate reaches ~36-37%.
2. **Measure Transfer Magnitude:** Evaluate the trained model on BFCL Parallel, τ²-Bench Retail, and Tool Decathlon. Confirm positive transfer (>+3 pp) on each.
3. **Ablate Rubric Granularity:** Create a "binary reward" variant where the judge outputs only 1/0 for full pass/fail. Train for same steps and compare in-distribution performance and transfer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does agent performance scale with additional training epochs on CoreCraft and associated transfer benchmarks?
- **Open Question 2:** Are the generalization improvements from CoreCraft training consistent across different model architectures and parameter scales?
- **Open Question 3:** What is the relative contribution of each CoreCraft design principle—task-centric design, expert-authored rubrics, and realistic workflows—to the observed transfer performance?
- **Open Question 4:** Does training on a curriculum of multiple high-fidelity enterprise environments yield synergistic improvements across domains or cause interference that reduces performance?

## Limitations
- Exact GRPO hyperparameters, LLM judge configuration, and Docker reset procedures are not fully disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- Only a single training epoch was performed; the trajectory of performance with more epochs (underfitting vs. overfitting) remains unknown.
- No statistical significance testing or confidence intervals are reported, so the robustness of the observed improvements is uncertain.
- The proposed mechanisms (task diversity, rubric granularity, realism) lack direct experimental validation (ablations not performed).

## Confidence
- **Generalizability claim:** Medium. Gains are positive but based on one model, one epoch, and without error bars.
- **Task diversity mechanism:** Low. No ablation isolating diversity; improvements may be confounded.
- **Granular rubric reward:** Medium. Theoretical justification is solid, but empirical evidence absent.
- **Realism/noise mechanism:** Low. Sanitized environment test missing; realism might not be the active ingredient.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sweep and replication:** Vary GRPO hyperparameters (learning rate, clipping ε, group size) across multiple seeds to establish performance distribution and reproducibility.
2. **Rubric granularity ablation:** Train with binary pass/fail rewards vs. full rubrics; compare in-distribution pass rates and transfer to external benchmarks.
3. **Realism ablation:** Create a sanitized CoreCraft without conflicting timestamps or implicit truncation; evaluate transfer impact on noisy benchmarks (Toolathlon, τ²-Bench Retail).
