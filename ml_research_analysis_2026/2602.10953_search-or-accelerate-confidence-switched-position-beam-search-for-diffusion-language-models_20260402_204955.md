# Search or Accelerate: Confidence-Switched Position Beam Search for Diffusion Language Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10953
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10953
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SOAR improves accuracy by up to +7.3% over greedy decoding while maintaining comparable or better inference speed on GSM8K, MBPP, and HumanEval.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) typically use greedy decoding, which selects the most confident token positions to unmask at each step. However, this can lock into suboptimal unmasking sequences. The authors propose SOAR, a training-free decoding algorithm that dynamically switches between position-space beam search (to explore alternatives when confidence is low) and parallel decoding (to accelerate when confidence is high). Evaluations on GSM8K, MBPP, and HumanEval with DREAM-7B and LLaDA-8B show that SOAR significantly improves accuracy (up to +7.3%) while achieving speedups of up to 1.63× relative to greedy decoding, effectively balancing quality and efficiency.

## Method Summary
SOAR operates within the iterative denoising framework of DLMs. At each step, it computes confidence scores for all masked token positions. If any position exceeds a threshold τ, SOAR enters parallel decoding mode: it simultaneously unmaks all high-confidence positions and collapses the beam to size one. Otherwise, it performs position beam search (PBS) with beam width K: it expands the beam by exploring up to K distinct single-position unmasking candidates, scored by the average confidence of the selected positions. After generating candidates, the size of the beam for the next step is determined by the origin of the top candidate (parallel → size 1, beam search → size K). This adaptive switching allows SOAR to widen search when needed and accelerate when safe. Hyperparameters τ (0.9 for DREAM-7B, 0.95 for LLaDA-8B) and K=2 are set per model. The method is training-free and applicable to any DLM.

## Key Results
- Accuracy gains: On GSM8K, SOAR yields +5.5% absolute improvement over greedy with DREAM-7B; on HumanEval-256, it improves by +7.3% with LLaDA-8B.
- Speed: SOAR achieves up to 1.63× speedup relative to greedy on DREAM-7B HumanEval-256, while pure positional beam search (PBS) slows down by ~2× (0.57× speedup).
- Trade-off: SOAR matches or exceeds greedy speed while delivering higher accuracy; it outperforms PBS in efficiency and provides a Pareto improvement over greedy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Position-space exploration
- **Claim:** Exploring alternative unmasking position orders (beyond greedy selection) can generate higher-confidence sequences, leading to improved output accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** Position Beam Search (PBS) maintains a beam of candidate sequences, each exploring different positions to unmask at each step. It selects position sets based on token confidence scores, scoring candidates by average confidence of chosen positions. This widens the search in position space rather than token space.
- **Core assumption:** The cumulative confidence of a decoding trajectory correlates with final sequence quality, and greedy selection of highest-confidence positions at each step can prematurely commit to suboptimal orderings.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "greedy rule... can lock into suboptimal unmasking sequences"; [section 4.4] PBS improves accuracy by +1.9% on GSM8K and achieves higher average token confidence (Figure 6a). Positive correlation between accuracy and average confidence is demonstrated.
- **Break condition:** If the model's confidence scores are poorly calibrated or uncorrelated with accuracy, position-space exploration would not yield gains.

### Mechanism 2: Dynamic confidence-switched decoding
- **Claim:** Dynamically switching between position-space search (exploration) and parallel decoding (exploitation) based on per-step confidence balances quality and speed.
- **Mechanism:** At each step, if any masked position has confidence > τ, SOAR enters parallel decoding mode: all high-confidence positions are unmasked simultaneously, and the beam collapses to size 1. If no position exceeds τ, it enters beam search mode (PBS with beam width K) to explore alternatives. The candidate size N_t resets based on which mode produced the top candidate.
- **Core assumption:** Model uncertainty (low confidence) indicates risk of premature commitment, warranting search; high confidence indicates safe acceleration via parallel unmasking.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "When confidence is low, SOAR briefly widens the search... when confidence is high, it collapses the search and decodes many positions in parallel."; [section 3.3.2] Formal rule and complexity analysis O(T·N̄); [section 4.2] SOAR matches or exceeds greedy speed while improving accuracy, whereas pure PBS is much slower.
- **Break condition:** If confidence thresholds are poorly chosen, the switch may either forfeit search opportunities or incur unnecessary search overhead.

### Mechanism 3: Mitigating global AR-ness bias
- **Claim:** Reducing the "Global AR-ness" (left-to-right unmasking bias) of the decoding sequence correlates with higher accuracy; SOAR’s position beam search mitigates this bias.
- **Mechanism:** Greedy decoding in DLMs often exhibits a left-first pattern due to positional bias. PBS explores non-leftmost positions, leading to less AR-like schedules. SOAR preserves this exploration when needed.
- **Core assumption:** The inherent left-first bias in masked diffusion decoding limits exploration of potentially better sequences; less AR-like unmasking improves quality.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.4] Definition of Global AR-ness@k and Figure 6b showing a negative correlation between accuracy and Global AR-ness across methods. PBS and SOAR have lower AR-ness and higher accuracy than greedy.
- **Break condition:** If the AR-ness metric is not meaningfully linked to performance for a given task or model, reducing it would not improve accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Diffusion Language Model (DLM) iterative denoising.**  
  *Why needed:* SOAR operates on the core DLM loop: start fully masked, predict all tokens each step, select a subset to commit based on confidence, re-mask the rest. Understanding this "predict-all, commit-subset" cycle is essential to grasp position-space decisions.  
  *Quick check:* How does a DLM’s parallel prediction at each step differ from autoregressive decoding, and why does this make the *unmasking order* a free variable?

- **Concept: Confidence metrics for token selection.**  
  *Why needed:* SOAR’s switching logic hinges on a per-token confidence score (e.g., max softmax probability, margin, negative entropy). Knowing these metrics and their calibration is key to setting the threshold τ.  
  *Quick check:* What are the three confidence metrics discussed (softmax max, margin, negative entropy), and what are the trade-offs between them?

- **Concept: Beam search adaptation from token to position space.**  
  *Why needed:* PBS redefines beam search’s expansion dimension from "which token?" to "which *positions*?". This conceptual shift is central to SOAR’s exploration mechanism.  
  *Quick check:* In standard AR beam search, what is expanded in the beam? How does PBS change this, and what scoring function does it use for position sets?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Confidence Scorer -> Mode Switch Controller -> (Parallel Decoder OR Position Beam Search Module) -> Dynamic Candidate Sizer -> Sequence Manager -> new beam

**Critical path:**
1. For each candidate sequence in current beam, run DLM forward pass → get token distributions.
2. Compute confidence for all masked positions.
3. If any confidence > τ → Parallel Decoding: unmask all high-confidence positions, score = avg confidence of those positions, mark origin = PARALLEL.
4. Else → PBS: rank masked positions by confidence, generate K top single-position candidates (n=1), score each by its position’s confidence, mark origin = BEAMSEARCH.
5. Pool all new candidates from all beam members, sort by score.
6. Identify top candidate’s origin → set next beam size N_t = 1 (if PARALLEL) or K (if BEAMSEARCH).
7. Prune to top N_t candidates → new beam.
8. Repeat until all positions decoded.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Beam size (K): Larger K improves exploration but linearly increases compute. Paper finds K=2 optimal.
- Confidence threshold (τ): Higher τ favors parallel decoding (faster) but risks missing uncertain positions; lower τ increases search (slower). τ tuned per model (0.9 for DREAM-7B, 0.95 for LLADA-8B).
- Parallel decoding token count (n): SOAR uses n = |H_t| (all high-confidence positions). Fixed n (as in PBS-Parallel) harms accuracy.

**Failure signatures:**
- No speed gain: If τ is too low, beam rarely collapses, N_t stays near K → no speedup vs. greedy.
- Accuracy drop: If τ is too high, parallel decoding unmaks low-confidence tokens prematurely, or beam too narrow → suboptimal exploration.
- Instability: If confidence estimates are noisy, switching erratic → oscillatory behavior.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline replication: Implement standard greedy decoding on DREAM-7B/LLADA-8B for GSM8K/MBPP/HumanEval. Record accuracy and SpeedUp.
2. PBS isolation test: Implement PBS with single-token decoding (n=1, beam K=2). Verify accuracy gain over greedy and confirm ~2× slowdown.
3. SOAR integration: Implement full SOAR with τ and K=2. Validate accuracy ≥ PBS-single and speed ≥ greedy. Perform threshold sweep (τ ∈ [0.8, 0.98]) to observe trade-off curve.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** Can the confidence threshold τ be made adaptive per decoding step or per sample to further optimize the speed-accuracy trade-off?  
*Basis:* The paper uses fixed thresholds after a coarse ablation; the long tail in token confidence scores suggests sample-dependent thresholds might be beneficial.  
*Why unresolved:* A fixed threshold does not account for varying uncertainty across samples or steps; an adaptive scheme could improve the Pareto frontier.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Evaluate dynamic τ selection strategies (e.g., median-based, percentile-based, or learned from a validation set) and compare accuracy vs. speed curves against the fixed-τ baseline.

**Open Question 2:** Does allowing the candidate size N_t to vary continuously between 1 and K lead to a better balance?  
*Basis:* Section 3.3.2 describes a binary rule: N_t becomes 1 if the best new candidate originates from parallel decoding, otherwise it resets to K. This heuristic lacks fine-grained control.  
*Why unresolved:* The binary switch may be suboptimal; intermediate N_t values might yield a smoother trade-off.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Design a continuous adjustment mechanism (e.g., N_t proportional to the confidence margin) and measure its impact on accuracy and speed for different beam sizes and thresholds.

**Open Question 3:** How well does SOAR generalize to other diffusion language model architectures, larger scales, and non-reasoning tasks?  
*Basis:* Experiments are restricted to DREAM-7B and LLADA-8B on GSM8K, MBPP, and HumanEval. No evaluation on significantly larger models or diverse text generation tasks is reported.  
*Why unresolved:* The confidence-accuracy correlation on reasoning tasks might not hold for open-ended generation; scaling could affect cost-benefit.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Apply SOAR to a larger DLM (e.g., 70B) and to tasks like summarization (CNN/Daily Mail) or dialogue (MultiWOZ), reporting accuracy and speed relative to greedy and PBS baselines.

## Limitations
- **External validity:** Claims are validated only on two specific DLMs and three reasoning-focused benchmarks; generalization to other architectures, tasks, or sequence lengths remains unclear.
- **Confidence metric calibration:** SOAR relies on a fixed threshold τ and the max-softmax confidence; the paper does not analyze calibration quality or compare alternative metrics, which could break the mode-switching logic.
- **Reporting granularity:** Batch size for speed measurements is unspecified, and exact prompt formatting details are missing, making full reproduction challenging.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Position-space exploration):** High – Supported by ablations showing accuracy gains and a positive correlation between average confidence and final accuracy.
- **Mechanism 2 (Dynamic switching):** Medium – Empirical results show the trade-off, but the claim depends heavily on well-calibrated confidence and tuned τ; no robustness analysis provided.
- **Mechanism 3 (AR-ness reduction):** Medium/Low – The negative correlation between AR-ness and accuracy is correlational; causal inference is weak, and corpus support is lacking.

## Next Checks
1. **Threshold and beam sweep:** Run SOAR across a grid of τ ∈ [0.85, 0.98] and K ∈ [1, 4] on all three benchmarks. Plot accuracy vs. SpeedUp to characterize the Pareto frontier and identify robustness plateaus.
2. **Confidence calibration audit:** Compute reliability diagrams and expected calibration error (ECE) for max-softmax confidence on held-out GSM8K/MBPP data. Correlate miscalibration with drops in SOAR’s accuracy relative to PBS.
3. **Cross-dataset generalization:** Evaluate SOAR on a non-reasoning task (e.g., XSum summarization) using LLADA-8B with same hyperparameters. Test if accuracy gains persist and whether the AR-ness correlation holds.
