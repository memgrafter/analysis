# Explain in Your Own Words: Improving Reasoning via Token-Selective Dual Knowledge Distillation

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.13260
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13260
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** A student-centric distillation method improves reasoning accuracy by up to 54.4% over baselines and up to 40.3% over prior SOTA, with students sometimes exceeding teachers.

## Executive Summary
Traditional knowledge distillation (KD) forces small student models to mimic the entire output distribution of large teacher models. This is problematic for complex reasoning tasks, as the teacher's full distribution often contains noisy or irrelevant tokens that overwhelm the student's limited capacity, causing a distribution mismatch. The proposed method, Token-Selective Dual Knowledge Distillation (TSD-KD), addresses this by making distillation student-centric. It combines two phases: an indirect phase where the teacher ranks the student's own candidate tokens to guide early "opener" token generation, and a direct, selective phase where distribution matching is applied only on tokens where the student is uncertain but the teacher is confident. An entropy regularization term helps the student maintain its own confident predictions.

## Method Summary
TSD-KD implements a dual-phase, selective distillation strategy. First, in the **indirect distillation** phase, the teacher model evaluates and ranks the top tokens already proposed by the student (e.g., from its top-k sampling). This provides a learned signal for the student on which of its own plausible candidates are most promising, particularly targeting high-entropy tokens that appear early in a reasoning chain ("opener" tokens). Second, in the **direct distillation** phase, the standard KL divergence loss is applied **selectively**. It is only computed for tokens where a confidence gap exists: the teacher is highly confident (low entropy) while the student is uncertain (high entropy). This avoids penalizing the student for already-correct or confidently wrong predictions that match the teacher's uncertainty. Finally, an **entropy regularization** term is added to the student's loss to prevent over-smoothing and encourage it to maintain confident predictions where appropriate, balancing the selective matching objective.

## Key Results
*   TSD-KD achieves state-of-the-art results on 10 challenging reasoning benchmarks, with absolute accuracy improvements of up to **54.4%** over baseline student models and up to **40.3%** over the previous best distillation method.
*   Remarkably, student models distilled with TSD-KD **surpass their own teacher models** on four of the ten benchmarks, with a peak gain of **20.3%** on the MATH dataset.

    --> Path B (Direct): Token Probabilities + Teacher Probabilities --> Selective KL Loss (Condition: Teach Confident & Stud Uncertain) --> Update Student
Entropy of Student Outputs --> Entropy Regularization Loss --> Update Student
Total Loss = (Indirect Loss + Selective Direct Loss + Entropy Reg Loss)

**Critical Path:** The training loop iterates: 1) Student generates outputs and candidate tokens. 2) Teacher scores student candidates (indirect) and provides full distribution (for direct). 3) Compute three loss components. 4) Backpropagate total loss to update student. The teacher is frozen.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   **Selectivity vs. Coverage:** Being overly selective (high thresholds) might discard valuable teacher knowledge for tokens where the student is slightly confident. The entropy regularization helps mitigate this.
*   **Indirect Cost:** Requiring the teacher to rank student candidates adds computational overhead compared to standard KD, where the teacher only produces a forward pass.
*   **Complexity:** The dual-phase and conditional logic increase implementation complexity and hyperparameter sensitivity (e.g., confidence/uncertainty thresholds, entropy regularization weight).

**Failure Signatures:**
*   Student performance degrades on tasks requiring high factual recall or standard language generation, indicating over-regularization or loss of baseline capabilities.
*   No improvement or performance drop on benchmarks where the teacher is not significantly stronger than the student, suggesting the method relies on a substantial teacher-student capability gap.
*   Training instability or divergence, potentially from incorrect implementation of the selective mask or unbalanced loss weighting.

**First Three Experiments (Validation):**
1.  **Ablation - Remove Indirect Phase:** Disable teacher ranking of student candidates. Measure performance drop to quantify indirect phase's unique contribution.
2.  **Ablation - Remove Selectivity:** Apply direct KL loss universally (standard KD) instead of selectively. Compare to full TSD-KD.
3.  **Sensitivity Analysis:** Vary the confidence threshold for the teacher and the uncertainty threshold for the student. Plot performance against these thresholds to find robust operating ranges and identify failure points.
