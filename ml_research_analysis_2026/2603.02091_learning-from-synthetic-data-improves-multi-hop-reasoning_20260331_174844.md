# Learning from Synthetic Data Improves Multi-hop Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02091
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02091
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RL fine-tuning on rule-generated synthetic data yields 56–131% relative F1 improvements on real-world multi-hop QA benchmarks despite zero factual overlap.

## Executive Summary
This study tackles the high cost and scarcity of reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning data for multi-hop reasoning in LLMs. It proposes using free, scalable, and fully verifiable synthetic data generated by rules (e.g., PhantomWiki, GSM-∞). Models fine-tuned with RL on such data transfer significantly to real-world benchmarks like HotpotQA and MuSiQue, learning a generalizable "knowledge composition" skill. In contrast, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the same synthetic data improves in-domain performance but fails to transfer. Results show monotonic scaling with synthetic data volume and generalization to out-of-distribution question difficulties.

## Method Summary
The core method employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a variant of RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR), using outcome-only supervision. Synthetic datasets are generated via rule-based systems: PhantomWiki creates fictional multi-hop questions from a grammar over invented entities; GSM-∞ produces mathematical reasoning chains; ReasoningGym family covers logic puzzles. Training uses a base LLM (e.g., Qwen3-0.6B, Phi-4-mini-reasoning) with prompts containing few-shot chain-of-thought examples. The reward is computed by comparing the model's final answer (extracted from `<answer>` tags) to the ground truth via exact match or F1 score. Ablations include SFT on synthetic solution traces and an RL variant rewarded only for correct tag formatting.

## Key Results
- RL fine-tuning on synthetic data achieves 56% to 131% relative F1 score improvements on five real-world multi-hop QA benchmarks (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue, CofCA, SynthWorlds-RM).
- SFT on synthetic data improves performance on the in-distribution synthetic test set but shows no meaningful transfer to real-world benchmarks.
- Performance scales monotonically with the volume of synthetic training data, and models generalize to out-of-distribution question difficulties (e.g., higher hop counts).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Rule-generated synthetic data teaches a domain-independent "knowledge composition" skill that transfers to real-world multi-hop question-answering.
- **Mechanism:** RLVR reinforces correct chaining of information across multiple steps. Synthetic data uses fictional entities and simple templates, preventing memorization and forcing the model to learn sequential integration of provided information.
- **Core assumption:** Multi-hop reasoning fundamentally requires the same compositional inference mechanism regardless of domain facts or linguistic complexity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "models learn to compose knowledge—a fundamental and generalizable reasoning skill."
  - [section 4.2] Despite zero factual overlap, models trained on PhantomWiki and GSM-∞ show 56%–131% relative F1 gains on real benchmarks.
  - [corpus] Primary empirical evidence is from the paper's own transfer experiments.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Outcome-only supervision on synthetic data improves both final answer accuracy and intermediate reasoning groundedness in real-world tasks.
- **Mechanism:** GRPO updates the policy based on final answer rewards. Correct final answers on synthetic compositions require correct intermediate reasoning, leading to emergent reliable internal steps that generalize.
- **Core assumption:** Models that succeed under outcome-only RL implicitly develop sound reasoning processes, not just answer heuristics.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.5] Figure 4: fraction of model generations containing ground-truth intermediate answers increases on MuSiQue and CofCA during RL.
  - [section 4.2] Gains scale monotonically with synthetic data volume, indicating progressive skill acquisition.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Synthetic data scaling and precise difficulty control enable bootstrapping of knowledge composition beyond the training distribution.
- **Mechanism:** Rule-based generators create examples with controllable difficulties (e.g., hop counts). Exposure to this diverse spectrum provides a curriculum, helping models adapt the core chaining skill to both harder and simpler OOD questions.
- **Core assumption:** Knowledge composition is a skill that can be developed through varied problem complexities and can extrapolate.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3] Models improve on held-out test sets with seen difficulties and on OOD difficulties (e.g., >9 hops).
  - [section 5] "Performance scales monotonically with synthetic data volume, with no sign of overfitting."

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**
  - *Why needed here:* Enables scalable RL fine-tuning without human/LLM verifiers; uses fast, rule-based reward computation.
  - *Quick check:* How does GRPO normalize advantages within groups of sampled rollouts to reduce variance?

- **Concept: Multi-hop Reasoning Decomposition**
  - *Why needed here:* The target capability; requires integrating multiple evidence pieces, distinguishing from single-step QA.
  - *Quick check:* What makes a question "multi-hop" and why does it challenge LLMs more than single-hop retrieval?

- **Concept: Synthetic Data Generation via Templates and Logic Programs**
  - *Why needed here:* Provides free, infinite, fully verifiable data with controllable difficulty and zero factual overlap.
  - *Quick check:* How does PhantomWiki's context-free grammar ensure every question is answerable and verifiable by construction?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Base LLM (Qwen3, Phi-4) -> Rule-based synthetic data generator (PhantomWiki, GSM-∞, ReasoningGym) -> RL fine-tuning pipeline (GRPO via TRL) -> Reward function (binary/F1) -> Evaluation benchmark suite (HotpotQA, MuSiQue, etc.)
- **Critical path:** Generate synthetic dataset -> Format prompts with CoT examples -> Run GRPO fine-tuning with on-policy rollouts -> Periodically evaluate checkpoints -> Analyze performance by difficulty.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Synthetic vs. real data: Synthetic is free/verifiable but less linguistically diverse; real data is rich but scarce.
  - RL vs. SFT: RL teaches general skills but is unstable; SFT is stable but risks overfitting to distribution.
  - Model size: Smaller models (0.6B) show steep gains; larger models (4B+) may need tuned hyperparameters.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - No transfer to real benchmarks despite in-domain synthetic improvement.
  - Performance saturates quickly without scaling with more synthetic data.
  - Intermediate step accuracy does not improve, indicating only final-answer hacking.
  - Poor OOD generalization (e.g., fails on higher hop counts not seen during training).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce core transfer: RL fine-tune Qwen3-0.6B on PhantomWiki (10K samples) and evaluate on five real-world benchmarks. Expect 56–131% relative F1 improvement.
  2. Contrast RL vs. SFT: SFT on GSM-∞ solution traces should yield high GSM-∞ accuracy but no HotpotQA improvement; RL should improve both.
  3. Ablate reason for transfer: RL fine-tune with binary reward solely for using `<answer>` tags. For Qwen3/Phi-4, this should not improve real-world performance, confirming knowledge composition drives transfer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- To what extent does synthetic-to-real transfer hold across varying model sizes, architectures, and synthetic complexities? The paper shows transfer for 0.6–4B models but notes differential malleability; boundary conditions are unmapped.
- How do synthetic training data and real-world knowledge interact during RL fine-tuning? Do they compete, complement, or interfere? Current work trains exclusively on one or the other; mixing effects are unknown.
- How does the initial knowledge and reasoning capability of a base model influence RL fine-tuning effectiveness? Smaller models improve more steeply than larger ones, suggesting initialization matters, but specific properties driving this are unidentified.
- What are the precise boundary conditions—data distribution shifts, task complexity, algorithmic choices—under which synthetic-data RL succeeds or fails? The paper focuses on multi-hop QA; transfer to other reasoning types (causal, counterfactual) and interactions with other objectives (e.g., SFT) are unexplored.

## Limitations
- Random seeds for synthetic data generation and training are unspecified, introducing variability.
- Test subsets (500 examples per benchmark) were randomly drawn but seeds/indices not provided, hindering exact replication.
- Reward implementation details (e.g., multi-answer F1 normalization) are incomplete.

## Confidence
- **Transfer of RL-synthetic gains to real benchmarks:** High (consistent improvements across five datasets, with SFT ablation).
- **Knowledge composition as the learned skill:** Medium (intermediate step accuracy improves, but causality not proven).
- **Monotonic scaling with synthetic volume:** Medium (limited scaling points; long-term trend unconfirmed).
- **OOD generalization to unseen hop counts:** Medium (tested on moderate OOD; extreme cases untested).
- **SFT fails to transfer:** High (clear contrast with RL in experiments).

## Next Checks
1. **Exact replication:** Use the official codebase with fixed seeds to generate 10K PhantomWiki samples, GRPO-train Qwen3-0.6B, and evaluate on the same 500-test splits (or replicate random sampling). Confirm the reported 56–131% relative F1 gains.
2. **Intermediate reasoning probe:** On MuSiQue and CofCA, automatically extract intermediate answers from model generations; compare correctness rates pre- and post-RL. A significant increase would directly support the knowledge composition claim.
3. **Reward structure ablation:** Train models with outcome-only reward versus stepwise reward (using synthetic intermediate labels). If outcome-only yields similar real-world gains, it suggests emergent intermediate reasoning; divergence would indicate dependence on explicit step supervision.
