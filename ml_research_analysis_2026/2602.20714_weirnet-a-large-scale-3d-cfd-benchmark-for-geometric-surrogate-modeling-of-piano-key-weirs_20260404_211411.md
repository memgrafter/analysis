# WeirNet: A Large-Scale 3D CFD Benchmark for Geometric Surrogate Modeling of Piano Key Weirs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.20714
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20714
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: WeirNet releases a benchmark of 3,794 feasible 3D geometries and 71,387 CFD simulations, enabling surrogate models that infer in milliseconds with high accuracy (R² up to 99.67%).

## Executive Summary
WeirNet addresses the scarcity of large-scale datasets for surrogate modeling of Piano Key Weirs by providing a comprehensive benchmark. The dataset comprises 3,794 distinct 3D geometries, each simulated across 19 discharge conditions using a consistent OpenFOAM workflow, yielding 71,387 labeled simulations. The benchmark includes multiple data representations (parametric descriptors, surface meshes, point clouds) and standardized tasks. Tree-based regressors achieve near-perfect in-distribution accuracy (R² up to 99.67%), while point- and mesh-based neural networks remain competitive without requiring explicit parameterization. All surrogates infer in milliseconds, contrasting with hours-long CFD simulations, thus enabling orders-of-magnitude speedups. Out-of-distribution tests reveal that geometry shifts are the primary failure mode, not discharge extrapolation. Data efficiency experiments show diminishing returns beyond 60% of the training set.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed the WeirNet dataset by parametrically generating feasible Piano Key Weir geometries and simulating each across a range of discharges using OpenFOAM. Each geometry is represented via (i) 8 geometric parameters (excluding constant Wu) plus discharge Q, (ii) a normalized point cloud of 5,000 surface points concatenated with Q, and (iii) a triangular surface mesh with vertex coordinates and Q as node features. The primary task is predicting the discharge coefficient cD, with additional tasks like rating curve reconstruction and OOD generalization. Baselines include tree-based regressors (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost, LightGBM) for parametric data and geometric deep learning models (PointNet, RegDGCNN, Mesh-GCN) for point clouds and meshes. Tree models are trained with gradient boosting learning rate 5e-2; geometric networks use Adam (lr=1e-3, batch 32) with early stopping and MSE loss. Data splits are geometry-wise (80/10/10) to prevent leakage.

## Key Results
- Dataset scale: 3,794 geometries × 19 discharges = 71,387 CFD simulations; representations include parametric descriptors, point clouds, and surface meshes.
- Tree-based regressors achieve superior in-distribution accuracy (R² up to 99.67%) and millisecond inference; geometric models (PointNet, RegDGCNN, Mesh-GCN) are competitive and parameterization-agnostic.
- Out-of-distribution tests identify geometry shifts as the primary failure mode, not discharge extrapolation; data efficiency plateaus beyond 60% of the training set.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The surrogate models effectively learn the mapping from geometry and discharge to discharge coefficient because they are trained on a large, diverse dataset of high-fidelity CFD simulations. Tree-based regressors excel with the compact parametric representation, capturing nonlinear relationships with high accuracy. Geometric deep learning models (PointNet, RegDGCNN, Mesh-GCN) process raw geometry directly, avoiding the need for handcrafted parameters and thus generalizing across different parameterizations. The dataset's scale and consistent simulation workflow ensure statistical reliability, while the speedup from surrogate inference enables practical applications.

## Foundational Learning
To engage with WeirNet, one should understand:
- **Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)**: Why needed: Generates ground-truth discharge coefficients via numerical simulation. Quick check: Familiarity with OpenFOAM or similar tools.
- **Surrogate Modeling**: Why needed: Replaces expensive simulations with fast approximators. Quick check: Know regression basics and model evaluation metrics (MSE, R²).
- **Piano Key Weirs**: Why needed: Specific hydraulic structure with key geometric parameters. Quick check: Identify parameters like overhang length, key width, etc.
- **Point Clouds**: Why needed: Unstructured 3D data representation for geometry. Quick check: Understand coordinate normalization and point sampling.
- **Mesh Graph Convolutions**: Why needed: Process triangular surface meshes. Quick check: Know how to construct graphs from meshes (vertices as nodes, edges from adjacency).
- **Tree-Based Regression**: Why needed: Strong baseline for tabular data. Quick check: Understand Random Forest and Gradient Boosting.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Geometry generation -> CFD simulation (OpenFOAM) -> Data storage (parametric/point cloud/mesh) -> Model training (tree or geometric network) -> Inference (cD prediction).

Critical path: (1) Acquire dataset, (2) split by geometry ID (80/10/10), (3) preprocess (normalize point clouds, optionally scale Q), (4) train selected model (tree or neural network) with specified hyperparameters, (5) evaluate on test set using MAE, R², and inference time.

Design tradeoffs: Parametric models offer high accuracy and fast training but require explicit parameter extraction; geometric models are parameterization-agnostic and can handle raw geometries but may be less accurate and more computationally demanding. Tree models are robust to hyperparameter variations; geometric networks require careful architecture selection.

Failure signatures: (a) Geometry leakage: same geometry in train and test splits leads to overfitting. (b) Incorrect input representation: e.g., unnormalized point clouds, missing Q, or wrong graph construction for meshes. (c) Hyperparameter mismatch: using default settings far from reported values may degrade performance.

First three experiments:
1. Train a Random Forest (parametric baseline) on the 8 geometric parameters plus discharge.
2. Train a PointNet on the normalized point cloud representation (5,000 points plus discharge) to process raw geometry.
3. Train a Mesh-GCN on the triangular surface mesh representation with vertex coordinates and discharge as node features.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can surrogate models be made more robust to out-of-distribution geometry shifts, which are identified as the primary failure mode?
- Given the observed data efficiency plateau beyond 60% of the training set, does collecting additional geometries yield significant performance gains?
- Can the benchmark be extended to other hydraulic structures to assess transferability of learned surrogates?
- What is the impact of CFD simulation choices (e.g., turbulence models, mesh density) on surrogate accuracy?
- How can uncertainty quantification be integrated into these fast surrogates to support risk-aware engineering decisions?

## Limitations
- The dataset and models are specialized for Piano Key Weirs; results may not generalize to other weir geometries.
- CFD simulations assume steady, incompressible flow with a fixed turbulence model; real-world conditions may include unsteady effects or different fluid properties.
- Tree-based regressors require explicit geometric parameters that must be extracted from new designs, which may be non-trivial for arbitrary 3D shapes.
- Geometric deep learning models currently lag behind tree models in accuracy, indicating a performance gap.
- Out-of-distribution performance degrades for geometry families not represented in the training set.
- The dataset provides only surface representations; internal flow fields are not included.
- Discharge conditions are limited to 19 steady-state values; dynamic operating conditions are not covered.

## Confidence
- In-distribution predictions are highly accurate (R² up to 99.67%) and extremely fast (milliseconds), giving strong confidence for design exploration within the covered geometry and discharge ranges.
- The large-scale, consistently generated dataset enhances statistical reliability of the benchmark.
- However, confidence is lower for geometry shifts outside the training distribution, as the paper documents substantial error increases, cautioning against blind extrapolation.
- The use of standard evaluation metrics (MAE, R²) and geometry-wise splits further validates the reported performance.

## Next Checks
- Validate the surrogate models against physical experiments on real Piano Key Weirs to assess real-world accuracy.
- Extend the dataset to include other weir types (e.g., broad-crested, labyrinth weirs) to test transferability.
- Implement uncertainty estimation techniques (e.g., Monte Carlo dropout, ensembles) to provide prediction intervals.
- Explore advanced geometric deep learning architectures (e.g., point transformers, mesh convolutional networks) to close the accuracy gap with tree-based methods.
- Conduct sensitivity analyses on CFD simulation parameters to understand their effect on surrogate learning.
- Evaluate the surrogates on a wider range of discharge values, including extreme conditions, to test extrapolation limits.
