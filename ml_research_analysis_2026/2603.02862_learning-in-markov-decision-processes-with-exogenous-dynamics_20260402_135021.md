# Learning in Markov Decision Processes with Exogenous Dynamics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.02862
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02862
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces Partially Controllable MDPs (PCMDP) where state splits into controllable and exogenous components; proposes EXAVI (model-based) and EXAQ (model-free) that achieve regret scaling only with exogenous state size (e.g., EXAVI: Õ(H²√(S•K) + H³S•²); EXAQ: Õ(√(H⁵ S•K))) and demonstrate orders-of-magnitude sample efficiency improvements in empirical domains.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents Partially Controllable MDPs (PCMDP), a framework that factorizes the state into controllable and exogenous components. The controllable (endogenous) transition kernel is assumed known, while the exogenous (action-independent) kernel must be learned. Two algorithms are introduced: EXAVI, a model-based method that estimates only the exogenous transitions and runs value iteration without optimism, and EXAQ, a model-free method that performs counterfactual Q-updates across all controllable state-action pairs when an exogenous transition is observed. Both exploit the independence structure to obtain regret bounds that depend solely on the size of the exogenous state space, in contrast to standard methods that scale with the full joint state space. Experiments on Taxi, Elevator, and optimal execution domains confirm dramatic reductions in sample complexity, with EXAQ converging within ~100 episodes versus thousands for Q-learning.

## Method Summary
In a PCMDP, the transition factorizes as \(p_h(s'|s,a) = p^h_\star(s'_\star|s,a) \cdot p^h_\bullet(s'_\bullet|s_\bullet)\). The agent knows the endogenous kernel \(p_\star\) but must learn the exogenous kernel \(p_\bullet\). EXAVI aggregates counts over all controllable states \(s_\star\) to estimate \(p_\bullet\) for each exogenous context \(s_\bullet\), constructs the full model by combining with \(p_\star\), and runs finite-horizon value iteration to produce a greedy policy; no exploration bonus is used. EXAQ maintains a Q-table and, upon observing \(s_{\bullet,h+1}\), computes for every \((s_\star,a)\) with the previous \(s_\bullet\) a target \(r_h(s,a) + \sum_{s'_\star} p_\star^h(s'_\star|s,a) V_{h+1}(s'_\star,s_{\bullet,h+1})\), updating Q-values with a learning rate \(\alpha_t = \frac{H+1}{H+t}\) where \(t = n^h_k(s_\bullet)\). The regret analysis shows that errors depend only on exogenous visitation counts, enabling sublinear regret that avoids factors of \(S_\star\) and \(A\).

## Key Results
- **Regret bounds:** EXAVI: \(\tilde{O}(H^2\sqrt{S_\bullet K} + H^3 S_\bullet^2)\) vs UCBVI’s \(\tilde{O}(H^2\sqrt{S_\bullet S_\star A K} + H^3 (S_\bullet S_\star)^2 A)\). EXAQ: \(\tilde{O}(\sqrt{H^5 S_\bullet K})\) vs Q-learning’s \(\tilde{O}(\sqrt{H^5 S_\bullet S_\star A K})\). Bounds scale only with exogenous state size \(S_\bullet\).
- **Empirical performance:** On a factorized Taxi domain, EXAVI reaches near-optimal return within ~100 episodes while UCBVI requires >2000; EXAQ achieves similar performance in ~200 episodes compared to >5000 for Q-learning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Decoupled Transition Estimation
- **Claim:** PCMDP structure allows learning only the exogenous transition kernel \(p_\bullet\) separately from the known endogenous kernel \(p_\star\), reducing the effective state space in estimation.
- **Mechanism:** In a standard MDP, estimating the full transition \(p(\cdot|s,a)\) requires visiting all \((s,a)\) pairs. In PCMDP, the transition factorizes as \(p_h(s'|s,a) = p_\star^h(s'_\star|s,a) p_\bullet^h(s'_\bullet|s_\bullet)\). Given known \(p_\star\), the agent estimates only \(p_\bullet^h(\cdot|s_\bullet)\) for each exogenous context \(s_\bullet\). The estimator aggregates samples across all endogenous states \(s_\star\) sharing the same \(s_\bullet\), concentrating samples on the exponentially smaller \(S_\bullet\) space rather than the full \(S = S_\bullet \times S_\star\) space.
- **Core assumption:** The endogenous dynamics \(p_\star^h\) are fully known and independent of exogenous transitions. The exogenous state \(s_\bullet\) evolves without action influence.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “regret bounds scale only with the size of the exogenous state space.” [section 3.1] “EXAVI needs to estimate only the part of the transition corresponding to \(p_\bullet\)… EXAVI works without any bonus term.” [section 2.1] “\(p_\bullet\)… its transition does not depend on the agent’s actions. … The agent has full knowledge of \(p_\star^h\) for every \(h\).” Prior work explores similar decompositions but lacks the specific sample-efficiency claim.
- **Break condition:** If the endogenous dynamics are misspecified or the factorization is violated (e.g., \(s_\star\) influences \(s_\bullet\) transitions), the estimator becomes biased and the bound degrades to standard MDP regret.

### Mechanism 2: Counterfactual Updates in Model-Free Learning
- **Claim:** EXAQ performs synchronous Q-value updates across all endogenous states \(s_\star\) and actions \(a\) when observing an exogenous transition \(s_\bullet \to s'_\bullet\), effectively simulating unvisited state-action pairs.
- **Mechanism:** Standard Q-learning updates only the visited \((s,a)\). EXAQ defines an empirical operator \(\hat{P}^k_h[f](s,a) = \sum_{\bar{s}_\star} f(\bar{s}_\star, s_{\bullet,h+1}) p_\star^h(\bar{s}_\star|s,a)\). When \(s_{\bullet,h+1}\) is observed, the target \(r_h + \hat{P}^k_h[V_{h+1}]\) is computed for every \((s_\star, a)\) with the same \(s_\bullet\) (loop over \(S_\star \times A\)). This reuses exogenous samples to update \(S_\star A\) Q-values, mitigating the curse of dimensionality on \(S_\star\).
- **Core assumption:** The reward \(r_h(s,a)\) and endogenous transition \(p_\star^h\) are known and independent of the exogenous sample.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “EXAQ achieves \(\tilde{O}(\sqrt{H^5 S_\bullet K})\) vs QL’s \(\tilde{O}(\sqrt{H^5 S_\bullet S_\star A K})\).” [section 3.2] “EXAQ leverages the independence of the exogenous signal to perform synchronous updates across the entire controllable subspace for the observed exogenous transitions. This effectively acts as a form of counterfactual reasoning.” [section 3.2] “the learning rate \(\alpha_t = \frac{H+1}{H+t}\) where \(t = n_k^h(s_\bullet)\).”
- **Break condition:** Requires known \(p_\star\) to compute \(\hat{P}^k_h\). If \(p_\star\) is unknown, updates are invalid. Also assumes full observability of \(s_\bullet\); partial observability breaks reuse.

### Mechanism 3: Elimination of Optimism via Exogenous Independence
- **Claim:** No exploration bonus is needed for either EXAVI or EXAQ because the uncertainty resides entirely in the exogenous process, which is passively observed regardless of policy.
- **Mechanism:** In standard RL (e.g., UCBVI, QL), optimism/pessimism bonuses encourage visiting uncertain \((s,a)\) pairs. The concentration error bounds (Lemmas 2–3) show the estimation error of the empirical operator \(\hat{P}^k_h\) depends only on \(n_k^h(s_\bullet)\), not on \(n_k^h(s_\star,a)\). Since \(s_\bullet\) exploration is unavoidable (it is sampled regardless of actions), the estimators concentrate without needing to “visit” all \((s,a)\). Thus, algorithms can follow the greedy policy without artificial optimism.
- **Core assumption:** Exogenous dynamics \(p_\bullet\) are action‑independent. This makes visit counts \(n_k^h(s_\bullet)\) independent of policy choice.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.1] “EXAVI works without any bonus term: no optimism is needed. Intuitively, this is because the epistemic uncertainty resides entirely within the exogenous dynamics \(p_\bullet\). Since the evolution of \(s_\bullet\) is independent of the agent’s actions, ‘active’ exploration is unnecessary.” [section 3.2] “EXAQ works without any explicit exploration bonus.” [proof of Theorem 2] “The denominator of [UCBVI’s estimator] depends on the agent’s actions; therefore, exploration is needed. This is the reason why UCBVI needs optimism, while our EXAVI does not.”
- **Break condition:** If the exogenous process has hidden state or the factorization is incomplete (some action‑dependence remains in \(s_\bullet\)), optimism may again be necessary.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Markov Decision Process (MDP) and Bellman Operators**  
  Why needed: The paper refines the MDP framework; understanding standard MDPs (state, action, transition, reward, horizon) and Bellman operators is prerequisite to grasp the PCMDP decomposition and the adapted algorithms.  
  Quick check: How does the Bellman optimality operator \(T_h f(s,a) = r_h(s,a) + \mathbb{E}_{s'\sim p_h(\cdot|s,a)}[\max_{a'} f(s',a')]\) change when transitions factorize as \(p_h = p_\star^h p_\bullet^h\)?

- **Concept: Regret in Online Reinforcement Learning**  
  Why needed: The central performance metric is cumulative regret \(R^K = \sum_{k=1}^K (V_1^*(s_1^k) - V_1^{\pi_k}(s_1^k))\). Understanding why sublinear regret (\(R^K = o(K)\)) implies convergence to optimality is key to interpreting the theoretical guarantees.  
  Quick check: Why does the regret bound scaling only with \(S_\bullet\) (not \(S_\star\)) imply a dramatic improvement when \(S_\star\) is large?

- **Concept: Concentration Inequalities (Hoeffding, Bernstein)**  
  Why needed: The regret proofs rely on bounding deviations of empirical transition estimates from true exogenous probabilities. Bernstein’s inequality is used to obtain tighter, variance‑aware bounds.  
  Quick check: In Lemma 3, why does the bound depend on \(n_k^h(s_\bullet)\) rather than on \(n_k^h(s_\star,a)\), and what role does the boundedness of rewards play?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
PCMDP specification (state \(S = S_\star \times S_\bullet\), actions \(A\), known endogenous kernel \(p_\star\), unknown exogenous kernel \(p_\bullet\), rewards \(r\)) → EXAVI (collect exogenous counts → estimate \(p_\bullet\) → combine with \(p_\star\) to form full model \(\hat{p}\) → finite‑horizon value iteration → greedy policy)  
PCMDP specification → EXAQ (initialize Q‑table; for each step observe \(s_{\bullet,h+1}\); compute target using known \(p_\star\); update all \(Q_h(s_\star,a)\) with same \(s_\bullet\); learning rate based on \(n(s_\bullet)\); greedy policy)

**Critical path:**  
1. Initialize: EXAVI sets empirical \(p_\bullet\) uniformly or arbitrarily; EXAQ sets \(Q_h(s,a)=H-h+1\).  
2. For each episode \(k=1..K\):  
   a. For \(h=1..H\):  
      - Choose action \(a^k_h = \pi^k_h(s^k_h)\) (greedy w.r.t. current estimates).  
      - Execute, observe next exogenous state \(s_{\bullet,h+1}\) (and endogenous part \(s_{\star,h+1}\)).  
   b. Update:  
      - EXAVI: increment count for \((h, s_{\bullet,h}, s_{\bullet,h+1})\), recompute \(\hat{p}_\bullet\), reconstruct \(\hat{p}(s'_\star,s'_\bullet|s,a)=p_\star(s'_\star|s,a)\hat{p}_\bullet(s'_\bullet|s_{\bullet})\). Run backward value iteration to compute new \(\hat{Q}^k\) and policy for next episode.  
      - EXAQ: for all \((s_\star,a)\) with previous \(s_{\bullet}=s_{\bullet,h}\), compute target \(t = r_h(s_\star,a) + \sum_{s'_\star} p_\star(s'_\star|s_\star,a) V_{h+1}(s'_\star,s_{\bullet,h+1})\); update \(Q_h(s_\star,a)\) with \(\alpha_{n_k^h(s_\bullet)}\); set \(V_h(s_\star,s_{\bullet,h}) = \max_a Q_h(s_\star,a)\).  
3. Policy for episode \(k+1\) is greedy w.r.t. latest estimates.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **EXAVI** offers a better regret scaling (no \(S_\star A\) factor) but requires full model planning per episode (cost \(O((S_\star+S_\bullet)S_\star A)\)) and exact knowledge of \(p_\star\).  
- **EXAQ** avoids planning overhead, updating \(Q\) in \(O(S_\star A)\) per step, but its bound contains a higher polynomial in \(H\) (\(H^5\)) and it stores a full table \(O(H S_\star S_\bullet A)\). Both rely strongly on known \(p_\star\) and full observability of \(s_\bullet\).  
- Tradeoff between theoretical optimality and computational practicalities: EXAVI is preferable when planning is cheap and \(p_\star\) is accurate; EXAQ is more lightweight but may suffer larger constants and memory use.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Theoretical: Regret scaling with \(S_\star\) or \(A\) in leading terms indicates violation of assumptions (e.g., coupling between \(s_\star\) and \(p_\bullet\) or misspecification of \(p_\star\)).  
- Empirical: Algorithms fail to converge or plateau at suboptimal returns (as seen with baselines in Fig. 1) → environment may not adhere to PCMDP structure or may require exploration bonuses due to hidden confounding factors.  
- Implementation: For EXAQ, using a fixed learning rate or deviating from \(\alpha_t = \frac{H+1}{H+t}\) breaks the theoretical guarantee and can cause instability. For EXAVI, insufficient counts for some \(s_\bullet\) lead to inaccurate \(p_\bullet\) estimates and poor planning.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Synthetic PCMDP sanity check:** Build a tiny tabular instance (\(S_\bullet=2\), \(S_\star=3\), \(A=2\), \(H=5\)) with known deterministic \(p_\star\) and random \(p_\bullet\). Run EXAVI, EXAQ, UCBVI, Q-learning for \(K=1000\) episodes over 10 seeds. Expect EXAVI/EXAQ to achieve near-zero regret quickly while baselines exhibit higher persistent regret.  
2. **Ablation on \(p_\star\) knowledge:** Intentionally perturb the known \(p_\star\) (e.g., flip 1% of transitions randomly) and measure regret of EXAVI/EXAQ as perturbation magnitude grows. Degradation toward standard MDP regret bounds would confirm sensitivity to endogenous model error.  
3. **Counterfactual update efficacy:** In EXAQ, record the number of Q‑updates performed per observed \(s_{\bullet,h+1}\). Baseline Q‑learning updates only one \((s,a)\) per step; EXAQ should update approximately \(S_\star A\) entries. Compare total update counts to quantify information reuse.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Continuous state/action extension:** How to generalize EXAVI/EXAQ to continuous domains via function approximation while preserving the favorable regret scaling? Tabular analysis relies on exact visitation counts; approximation introduces bias that complicates concentration.  
- **Relaxing known \(p_\star\) assumption:** Can the algorithms jointly learn the controllable dynamics \(p_\star\) alongside \(p_\bullet\) without losing the multiplicative separation benefit? Current methods exploit exact \(p_\star\) for unbiased counterfactual updates; joint estimation adds error that may affect the regret bound.  
- **Partial observability of exogenous state:** What if the agent only observes a noisy or latent version of \(s_\bullet\)? The current counterfactual updates require precise \(s_\bullet\); extending to a POMDP setting with belief states or filters is an open challenge.  
- **Computational efficiency via approximation:** Can the \(O(S_\star A)\) per‑step cost of EXAQ or the planning overhead of EXAVI be reduced using approximate dynamic programming (e.g., prioritized sweeping) while still guaranteeing sublinear regret? Approximation may introduce bias that weakens theoretical bounds.

## Limitations
- **Strong assumption of known endogenous dynamics:** The algorithms require exact knowledge of the controllable transition kernel \(p_\star\), which is unrealistic in most practical problems; learning \(p_\star\) jointly would likely erase the regret advantage.  
- **Strict factorization requirement:** The decomposition \(p_h(s'|s,a)=p_\star^h(s'_\star|s,a)p_\bullet^h(s'_\bullet|s_\bullet)\) assumes independence between controllable and exogenous components; many real‑world systems exhibit subtle couplings that would invalidate the theory.  
- **Reproducibility and tuning gaps:** The experimental environments (Taxi, Elevator, optimal execution) are described only informally, lacking crucial details (e.g., exact choke‑point coordinates, passenger respawn rules). Moreover, baseline exploration bonuses are not specified precisely, making independent verification of the reported sample‑efficiency gains difficult and potentially exaggerating the relative performance.

## Confidence
- Theoretical regret bounds under stated assumptions: **High** confidence (proofs appear sound).  
- Empirical sample‑efficiency improvements: **Medium** confidence (results plausible but suffer from reproducibility issues and possible baseline tuning effects).  
- No exploration bonus needed within PCMDP: **High** confidence (exogenous state is passively observed).  
- Generalizability to real‑world problems: **Low** confidence due to strong assumptions (known \(p_\star\), strict factorization, full observability).

Compare sample efficiency against UCBVI/QL to test whether the benefits extend beyond the paper’s custom synthetic domains.
