# Data-driven discovery of roughness descriptors for surface characterization and intimate contact modeling of unidirectional composite tapes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.20418
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20418
- Reference count: 28
- Primary result: Rank Reduction Autoencoders learn compact roughness descriptors that enable near-perfect tape classification and accurate degree-of-contact prediction with as few as five latent modes.

## Executive Summary
Thermoplastic composite tapes exhibit surface roughness that influences both classification and intimate contact during consolidation. This work addresses the need for unified roughness descriptors that support both tasks by introducing Rank Reduction Autoencoders (RRAEs). RRAEs extend standard autoencoders with a singular value decomposition (SVD) layer that truncates the latent space to a linear subspace, yielding robust and physically meaningful features. Trained on a dataset of 1011 roughness profiles (1000 points each) and simulated degree of intimate contact (DIC) curves, the method attains near-perfect classification accuracy with a single latent mode for full profiles and four modes for micro-roughness. Only five latent modes are required to simultaneously classify tapes and predict DIC evolution, achieving an average test error of ~2% and reconstruction error of ~5%. The RRAE also outperforms standard autoencoders in parameter efficiency, demonstrating its suitability for surface characterization and contact modeling in composite manufacturing.

## Method Summary
The authors tackle the dual objectives of classifying 12 types of thermoplastic composite tapes and predicting the time evolution of the degree of intimate contact (DIC) from surface roughness profiles. The dataset comprises 1011 measured roughness profiles (each with 1000 points) and corresponding DIC curves generated by a 2D cellular automaton compaction simulation. Macro-roughness is separated to isolate micro-roughness (µ), which is then normalized to [0,1] and standardized using training set statistics.

The core model is a Rank Reduction Autoencoder (RRAE). The encoder consists of two 1D convolutional blocks (1→16 channels, kernel 5, padding 2; then 16→32, kernel 3, padding 1) each followed by ReLU and max pooling (factor 2), flattening, and a linear layer projecting to a high-dimensional latent space of size 200. Instead of using these 200 dimensions directly, a custom SVD truncation layer computes the top k_max left singular vectors (U_k) from the encoder output matrix Y (batch dimension) and projects Y onto this subspace via α = Y U_k, yielding low-dimensional coefficients (k_max). These coefficients serve as roughness descriptors.

Three heads consume the coefficients α: a decoder (linear → reshape → two transposed convolution layers) reconstructs the standardized micro-roughness; a five‑layer MLP classifier outputs 12 class probabilities; and another five‑layer MLP regressor predicts the 352‑step DIC curve. The total loss is a weighted sum of relative L2 errors for reconstruction (L_r), classification (one‑hot MSE, L_c), and DIC prediction (L_DIC). Equal weights are used in the main experiments.

Training proceeds on 911 profiles (test set: 100) for 30,000 epochs with Adam (initial learning rate 1e‑3, decayed by 10 every 10,000 epochs). No early stopping is employed.

## Key Results
- A single latent mode (k_max=1) yields near‑perfect classification accuracy on full roughness profiles; four modes are needed for micro‑roughness.
- With five latent modes, the RRAE simultaneously achieves classification accuracy >99% and an average DIC prediction error δ_DIC ≈ 2% on the test set, while maintaining a reconstruction error of ~5%.
- Compared to a standard encoder‑decoder with the same number of latent dimensions, the RRAE requires significantly fewer latent dimensions for comparable performance, underscoring the efficiency of rank reduction and the robustness of the learned descriptors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RRAE’s effectiveness stems from the synergy between representation learning and explicit rank reduction. The encoder learns a mapping that captures the most salient variations in the roughness profiles. By then projecting onto the top singular vectors of the encoder output across the training batch, the SVD layer selects an orthogonal basis that maximizes explained variance in a linear subspace. This basis acts as a set of optimal roughness modes. Because the latent coefficients are linear combinations of these modes, they become directly interpretable and highly informative for both discrimination (classes exhibit distinct mode amplitudes) and physical response (DIC evolution correlates with the same modal contributions). The autoencoder reconstruction loss ensures that the selected subspace retains all necessary information to regenerate the input, while the multi‑task losses align the subspace with the downstream objectives. The linearity also mitigates overfitting, allowing a compact set of modes (k_max=5) to generalize well to unseen tapes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Autoencoders**: Unsupervised neural networks that learn efficient data encodings by reconstructing inputs. *Why needed*: Provide the baseline framework (encoder‑decoder) and reconstruction loss that preserves information. *Quick check*: Can a simple autoencoder with 5 latent dimensions reconstruct roughness profiles with <10% relative L2 error?
- **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)**: Matrix factorization yielding orthogonal modes and singular values. *Why needed*: Enforces a linear latent subspace and quantifies the importance of each mode. *Quick check*: After training the encoder, does the SVD of its output matrix show a clear decay in singular values, indicating a low‑rank structure?
- **Rank Reduction**: Keeping only the top k singular vectors to approximate a matrix. *Why needed*: Compresses information into a few interpretable descriptors while discarding noise. *Quick check*: With k=5, does the truncated approximation retain >95% of the Frobenius norm (i.e., variance) of the full encoder output?
- **Multi-task Learning**: Jointly optimizing a single model on multiple objectives using shared representations. *Why needed*: Enables simultaneous classification and DIC prediction, aligning the latent space with both tasks. *Quick check*: When combining the three losses with equal weights, do all three losses decrease during training, or does one dominate at the expense of others?
- **1D Convolutional Neural Networks**: Apply convolution operations to sequential data. *Why needed*: The encoder processes 1D roughness profiles with local receptive fields and translation equivariance. *Quick check*: Do the first-layer conv filters exhibit edge‑detecting or smoothing patterns after training?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Input roughness profile (1×1000) → Encoder (Conv1d(1→16,k=5,p=2) → ReLU → MaxPool(2) → Conv1d(16→32,k=3,p=1) → ReLU → MaxPool(2) → Flatten → Linear(8000→200)) → Latent matrix Y (batch×200) → SVD Truncation (compute top k_max left singular vectors U_k from Y, then α = Y U_k) → α (batch×k_max) branches to:
  - Decoder (Linear(k_max→200) → Reshape(32,250) → ConvTranspose1d(32→6) → ReLU → ConvTranspose1d(6→1) → reconstruction)
  - Classifier MLP (Linear(k_max→k_max)×5 with ReLU → Linear(k_max→12))
  - DIC Predictor MLP (Linear(k_max→k_max)×5 with ReLU → Linear(k_max→352))

- **Critical Path**: The core flow follows Input → Encoder → SVD → α. The SVD layer is the pivotal component that converts high‑dimensional encoder outputs into low‑rank coefficients. All three heads depend on α, making it the shared descriptor.

- **Design Tradeoffs**:
  - *Latent dimension k_max*: Small values yield compact descriptors but risk underfitting; larger values increase capacity and may overfit. The paper finds k_max=5 sufficient.
  - *Loss weighting*: Equal weights ω=1 balance tasks; adjusting ω_c, ω_r, ω_DIC can prioritize one over others but may harm overall performance.
  - *SVD frequency*: Computing SVD per batch introduces stochasticity and may destabilize training; per‑epoch (full‑dataset) SVD is more stable but computationally heavier. The paper’s exact approach is unspecified.
  - *Encoder capacity*: More convolutional layers or channels could improve reconstruction but increase parameters and risk overfitting on the limited dataset.
  - *Data standardization*: Sensitive to dataset statistics; using population vs. sample stats affects reproducibility.

- **Failure Signatures**:
  - Reconstruction error >10% on test set indicates insufficient k_max or inadequate encoder/decoder capacity.
  - Classification accuracy <90% suggests the latent space does not separate classes well; check preprocessing of micro‑roughness and ensure SVD returns meaningful modes.
  - DIC prediction curves appear oscillatory or far from the reference (δ_DIC >10%) may point to mis‑weighted losses, insufficient k_max, or improper DIC target smoothing.
  - SVD singular values show no significant decay, implying the encoder outputs are not low‑rank; consider stronger regularization or adjust encoder architecture.

- **First Experiments**:
  1. **Baseline Autoencoder**: Train a standard autoencoder (no SVD) with exactly 5 latent dimensions using the same encoder/decoder and reconstruction loss. Compare reconstruction error and feature utility (e.g., linear classifier on latents) to the RRAE. This isolates the benefit of rank reduction.
  2. **Ablation on k_max**: Sweep k_max from 1 to 10 while keeping other hyperparameters fixed. Plot classification accuracy, DIC error (δ_DIC), and reconstruction error to identify the minimal k achieving the reported ~2% DIC error and near‑perfect classification.
  3. **Loss Weight Sensitivity**: Train RRAE with different combinations of loss weights (e.g., (ω_c, ω_r, ω_DIC) = (1,1,1), (2,1,1), (1,2,1), (1,1,2)). Evaluate tradeoffs between reconstruction quality, classification accuracy, and DIC prediction to understand the sensitivity to weighting and confirm the chosen equal weighting is appropriate.

## Limitations
- Access to the original roughness profiles and DIC simulation data is required for exact replication; their absence may lead to deviations from the reported numbers.
- The method for extracting micro‑roughness (e.g., polynomial order, wavelet criteria) is not precisely described, introducing ambiguity in preprocessing.
- Several training details (batch size, Adam β1/β2, weight decay) are omitted, which could affect convergence and final performance.

## Confidence
- Single latent mode achieves near‑perfect classification: Medium
- Five latent modes suffice for simultaneous classification and DIC prediction: Medium
- RRAE requires fewer latent dimensions than standard autoencoders for comparable accuracy: Medium
