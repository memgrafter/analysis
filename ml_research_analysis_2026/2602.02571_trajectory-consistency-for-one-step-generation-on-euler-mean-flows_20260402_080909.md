# Trajectory Consistency for One-Step Generation on Euler Mean Flows

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.02571  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02571  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** EMF achieves state-of-the-art one-step generation across multiple domains (ImageNet FID 7.2 vs. MeanFlow 11.1) with ~50% reduction in training time/memory and improved optimization stability.

## Executive Summary
Enforcing trajectory consistency for one-step generation in flow models is challenging: progressive methods accumulate discretization error, while continuous-equation approaches require expensive Jacobian-vector products (JVP). Euler Mean Flows (EMF) overcome these issues by linearizing the semigroup consistency constraint via a local Euler approximation, yielding a learnable surrogate that can be directly supervised from data without JVP. This unified framework supports both velocity (u-) and endpoint (x₁-) prediction, reducing memory and computation while enhancing stability. Experiments on image synthesis, pixel-space generation, SDF, and point cloud tasks demonstrate consistent quality gains under fixed compute budgets, establishing new SOTA for one-step generation.

## Method Summary
EMF approximates the exact flow map composition ϕᵗ→ʳ = ϕˢ→ʳ ∘ ϕᵗ→ˢ by linearizing around a small time step Δt from t to s = t+Δt. This yields an Euler-based loss: the long-range velocity uᵗ→ʳ is supervised as  
uᵗ→ʳ(x) ≈ uᵗ→ᵗ(x) + (r−t−Δt)/Δt (uᵗ⁺ᵟ→ʳ(x⁺) − uᵗ→ʳ(x)),  
where x⁺ = x + Δt·uᵗ→ᵗ(x) and uᵗ→ᵗ is the conditional velocity from Flow Matching. Stop-gradient on the short-term predictions eliminates the need for JVP. For domains where u-prediction fails (e.g., pixel space, SDF), EMF adopts an x₁-prediction variant with analogous linearization and time-weighting. Training samples (x₀, x₁, t, r), computes required forward passes, assembles the loss (with optional classifier-free guidance and an auxiliary branch for memory reduction), and updates the backbone network.

## Key Results
- **ImageNet latent:** 1-step FID 7.2 (vs. MeanFlow 11.1).  
- **Pixel-space CelebA-HQ:** 1-step FID 30.6 vs. 56.8; **SDF generation:** Chamfer 0.046 vs. 0.060.  
- Across domains, EMF reduces training memory/time by ~50% and exhibits significantly more stable optimization compared to MeanFlow.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
EMF replaces the intractable semigroup consistency constraint with a learnable linear surrogate via local Euler approximation. For interval [t, r] and intermediate s=t+Δt, the exact equality ϕᵗ→ʳ = ϕˢ→ʳ ∘ ϕᵗ→ˢ is linearized: ϕᵗ→ˢ(x) ≈ ϕᵗ→ᵗ(x) + ∂ϕᵗ→ˢ/∂s|ₛ₌ₜ (s−t). This leads to the Euler surrogate (Equation 10):  
uᵗ→ʳ(x) ≈ uᵗ→ᵗ(x) + (r−t−Δt)/(Δt) (uᵗ⁺ᵟ→ʳ(x⁺) − uᵗ→ʳ(x)),  
with x⁺ = x + Δt·uᵗ→ᵗ(x). The short-range term uᵗ→ᵗ(x) is replaced by the conditional velocity u(x|x₁) = (x₁−x)/(1−t), enabling direct supervision from data. The core assumption is sufficient smoothness (bounded gradients/derivatives) and Δt small enough for O(Δt²) error. If the flow is highly nonlinear or Δt is not sufficiently small, the approximation error grows, causing drift.

### Mechanism 2
EMF eliminates the need for JVP during training, reducing memory and computation while improving optimization stability. The loss (Equation 11) uses stop-gradient (sg) on uᵗ⁺ᵟ→ʳ(x⁺) and uᵗ→ʳ(x) when computing the difference term. This avoids backpropagation through the composed flow maps. EMF requires three forward passes (two with sg) versus MeanFlow’s one JVP + two forward passes, cutting memory by ~50%. The paper shows ∇Lₑ ≈ ∇L꼰 and ∇Lₑ ≈ ∇Lᴄ under regularity, indicating the sg approximation remains effective. If model capacity heavily relies on gradient information from composed maps (very deep networks), expressiveness might be limited; further, some sparse libraries (e.g., TorchSparse) do not support JVP at all, making EMF necessary.

### Mechanism 3
EMF’s unified formulation supports both u-prediction and x₁-prediction. For x₁-prediction, define ˜xᵗ→ʳ(x) = (1−t)uᵗ→ʳ(x) + x, which satisfies an analogous consistency. Linearizing yields loss Lₑ′ (Equation 18) with target ˜x(x|x₁) = x₁. The x₁-prediction variant incorporates time weighting 1/(1−t)² to better fit the data manifold, as u-prediction can suffer from variance collapse in pixel-space and SDF tasks. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.4) still requires boundedness conditions (Assumption 2). The x₁-prediction loss also requires careful time clamping (1−t, 1−r ≥ 0.02) to avoid instability near t=1.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**  
Data pool (x₀, x₁) → Time sampler (t, r) → xₜ interpolation → Backbone (predicts u^{t→t} and optionally u^{t→r}) → Euler Loss Module (computes x⁺ₜ via u^{t→t}, second forward for u^{t+Δt→r}, assembles loss with stop-gradient and conditional target) → Optional auxiliary branch (lightweight head for u^{t→t}) → Backward pass. CFG wrapper mixes conditional/unconditional predictions during training and applies guidance at inference.

**Critical path:**  
1. Sample x₁ from dataset, x₀ ~ N(0,I).  
2. Sample times t, r ~ uniform or log-normal (swap if t>r); fraction α have r=t.  
3. Compute xₜ = (1−t)x₀ + t x₁.  
4. Predict u^{t→t}(xₜ, C) [and u^{t→t}(xₜ, C₀) for CFG].  
5. For u-pred: target u(x|x₁) = (x₁−x)/(1−t); for x₁-pred: target ˜x(x|x₁)=x₁.  
6. Compute x⁺ₜ = xₜ + Δt · u^{t→t}(xₜ, C) [using CFG-mixed prediction if needed].  
7. Predict u^{t+Δt→r}(x⁺ₜ, C) and u^{t→r}(xₜ, C).  
8. Assemble loss:  
   || u^{t→r}(xₜ, C) − sg( target + (r−t−Δt)⁺/Δt · (u^{t+Δt→r}(x⁺ₜ) − u^{t→r}(xₜ)) ) ||².  
   For x₁-pred, replace accordingly and apply weighting 1/(1−t)².  
9. Backpropagate; update model parameters.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Δt: Smaller Δt improves linearization accuracy but may amplify x⁺ₜ prediction error; default Δt=0.1.  
- Auxiliary branch: reduces memory for large models with minor compute overhead.  
- CFG scale w: higher w improves sample quality but may reduce diversity; paper uses w=2.5 for ImageNet.  
- Mixing α: fraction of (t, r=t) samples; crucial for learning accurate instantaneous velocity; default α=0.1–0.2.

**Failure signatures**:
- Training: loss spikes or monotonic increase (MeanFlow instability) → check Δt or learning rate; EMF should be stable.  
- Architecture: variance collapse in x₁-prediction (constant outputs) → indicates missing time-weighting 1/(1−t)² or improper CFG.  
- Sample quality: 1-step FID much worse than multi-step → suggests u^{t→t} under-trained; increase α or lower learning rate.  
- Memory OOM: for large models without auxiliary branch → add aux head.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Latent-space ImageNet:** DiT-B/2, 256×256, 400K steps, batch 256, log-normal time sampling, CFG w=2.5, k=0.1. Validate 1-step FID <8.  
2. **Pixel-space CelebA-HQ:** JiT-B/16, 256×256, 600K steps, batch 64, x₁-prediction with time-weighting. Verify u-prediction fails (high FID) while x₁-prediction succeeds.  
3. **SDF generation:** Functional Diffusion on ShapeNet with 64 conditioning points, 1-step Chamfer Distance <0.05. Confirm x₁-prediction variant required; u-prediction collapses.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can EMF scale to video generation and billion-parameter models?  
  *Basis:* Conclusion calls for extension to broader tasks and larger models.  
  *Why unresolved:* Evaluations limited to images and small 3D shapes.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Apply EMF to video datasets (e.g., UCF101) with large transformers; measure FVD.

- **Open Question 2:** Why does u-prediction fail in pixel-space and SDF generation while x₁-prediction works?  
  *Basis:* Paper resorts to x₁-prediction but does not analyze the failure mode.  
  *Why unresolved:* Understanding could improve one-step generator design for high-dimensional outputs.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Compare gradient norms, loss curvature, and data manifold properties between u- and x₁-prediction.

- **Open Question 3:** Do the theoretical assumptions (Mg, Mx, Mt) hold universally across architectures and datasets?  
  *Basis:* Assumptions only verified on DiT-B/2 on CelebA-HQ; theory relies on them.  
  *Why unresolved:* If violated, the surrogate loss may not approximate the original objective well.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Estimate these quantities for various models (CNNs, transformers) on multiple datasets; characterize necessary conditions.

- **Open Question 4:** How does the Euler step size Δt affect the trade-off between approximation error and numerical stability?  
  *Basis:* Derivation uses small fixed Δt, but impact not analyzed.  
  *Why unresolved:* An optimal Δt could improve one-step sample quality and might be task-dependent.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Sweep Δt over a range (e.g., 1e-3 to 0.1) and measure FID; derive theoretical bounds on error in terms of Δt.

## Limitations
- Smoothness assumptions (bounded gradients/derivatives) may be violated in practice, especially near data manifolds.  
- Hyperparameters (adaptive loss c,p; α; time sampler details) not fully disclosed, hindering exact replication.  
- Stop-gradient approximation could limit model capacity; theoretical gradient equivalence relies on strong regularity conditions.  
- One-step focus; multi-step behavior of EMF remains unstudied.  
- Dependence on pre-trained VAE for latent-space experiments introduces external variability.

## Confidence
- **Linear surrogate:** Medium (theory sound but sensitive to Δt and smoothness).  
- **Memory/stability gains:** High (direct measurements confirm ~50% reduction and stable loss).  
- **Unified u/x₁-prediction:** High (empirical cross-domain evidence; x₁-prediction essential for pixel/SDF).  
- **SOTA one-step quality:** Medium (results depend on undisclosed hyperparameters and pre-trained components).
