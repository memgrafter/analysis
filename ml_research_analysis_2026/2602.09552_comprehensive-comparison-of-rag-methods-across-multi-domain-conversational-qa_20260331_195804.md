# Comprehensive Comparison of RAG Methods Across Multi-Domain Conversational QA

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09552  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09552  
- **Reference count:** 13  
- **Primary result:** Straightforward methods (Hybrid BM25, Reranker, HyDE) consistently outperform Vanilla RAG, with HyDE achieving top retrieval on five datasets and tripling performance on INSCIT; several advanced techniques fail to improve and sometimes degrade below No-RAG.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents a systematic empirical comparison of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methods for multi-turn conversational question answering. Evaluating vanilla and six advanced RAG methods across eight datasets using Llama 3 8B Instruct, the study finds that simple methods like Hybrid BM25, Reranker, and HyDE consistently outperform Vanilla RAG, with HyDE achieving the best retrieval on five datasets and a threefold improvement on INSCIT. Conversely, several advanced methods underperform, sometimes falling below the No-RAG baseline. The results highlight that dataset characteristics and dialogue length critically influence RAG effectiveness, and that aligning retrieval strategy with dataset structure is more important than method complexity.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates a suite of RAG methods—Vanilla, Hybrid BM25, Reranker, Query Rewriting, HyDE, HyDE+Reranker, Summarization, and SumContext—on eight multi-turn conversational QA datasets from ChatRAG-Bench. Using a fixed generator (Llama 3 8B Instruct) and a dense retriever (Sentence Transformers all-MiniLM-L6-v2) with optional BM25 and cross-encoder reranking, the authors measure retrieval quality via MRR@5 and answer generation via F1 score. All experiments run in zero-shot mode with dataset-specific prompts, and results are logged via MLflow. The unified setup enables direct comparison across datasets and methods.

## Key Results
- Hybrid BM25, Reranker, and HyDE consistently outperform Vanilla RAG across most datasets.
- HyDE achieves top retrieval on five datasets and triples performance on INSCIT.
- Several advanced techniques (Query Rewriting, Summarization, SumContext) fail to improve and sometimes degrade below No-RAG.
- Dataset characteristics and dialogue length strongly influence effectiveness, with performance varying widely across the eight datasets.
- The correlation between retrieval (MRR) and generation (F1) is dataset-dependent: strong for CoQA and SQA, weak or negative for DoQA and SQA.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hybrid Retrieval Balances Lexical and Semantic Matching
- **Claim:** Combining sparse (BM25) and dense (vector) retrieval (Hybrid BM25) consistently outperforms pure dense retrieval (Vanilla RAG) across most datasets by improving recall of relevant contexts.
- **Mechanism:** BM25 captures exact keyword matches and term importance, which complements the semantic similarity focus of dense embeddings. The hybrid approach merges scores from both retrievers, often via a weighted sum, to produce a ranked list that benefits from both precision (dense) and recall (sparse).
- **Core assumption:** Relevant documents may not always be top-ranked by semantic similarity alone due to embedding space limitations or query ambiguity; lexical matching provides a robust fallback.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "robust yet straightforward methods, such as reranking, hybrid BM25, and HyDE, consistently outperform vanilla RAG."
  - [section 5.4] "for all datasets except for SQA and DoQA, the combination of sparse and dense encoders in Hybrid BM25 leads to better results than Vanilla RAG."
  - [corpus] Weak direct corpus support; neighboring papers focus on multi-turn benchmarks and query rewriting, not hybrid retrieval analysis.
- **Break condition:** May degrade performance if the corpus is highly semantically structured and lexical variance is low, or if the hybrid weighting is poorly tuned for a specific domain.

### Mechanism 2: Hypothetical Answer Generation (HyDE) Aligns Queries with Target Answer Distribution
- **Claim:** Generating a hypothetical answer for a query (HyDE) and using it as the retrieval query significantly improves retrieval (MRR) on diverse datasets, particularly those with complex or shifting intents (e.g., INSCIT).
- **Mechanism:** The LLM generates a plausible answer snippet conditioned on the query and dialogue history. This hypothetical answer is embedded and used to retrieve contexts, effectively moving the query representation closer to the distribution of target answer-containing passages.
- **Core assumption:** The gap between a user's conversational query (often elliptical or contextual) and the language used in knowledge documents is substantial; a generated "answer-like" query bridges this gap better than the original query or a rewritten query.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "HyDE achieving top retrieval on five datasets and tripling performance on INSCIT."
  - [section 5.4] "Among the advanced RAG methods, HyDE ranks clearly ahead, achieving the best performance on five of eight datasets... for INSCIT, HyDE triples the performance of Vanilla RAG."
  - [section 5.6] HyDE and HyDE Reranker often appear in top-right clusters of Figure 3 (high MRR, high F1) for multiple datasets.
- **Break condition:** Less effective or harmful when the generated hypothetical answer is factually incorrect or stylistically mismatched with the target corpus (e.g., highly structured tables in SQA, or formal vs. informal answer styles as in DoQA).

### Mechanism 3: Retrieval-Generation Mismatch Due to Answer Format and Context Complexity
- **Claim:** A high retrieval score (MRR) does not guarantee high generation quality (F1); the correlation is dataset-dependent and weakens when answers are non-factoid, verbose, or require synthesis beyond extraction.
- **Mechanism:** The generator LLM must distill the retrieved context(s) into the target answer format. If the ground-truth answer is very concise (e.g., CoQA) or requires precise extraction, strong retrieval directly aids generation. If answers are long, explanatory, or involve reasoning (e.g., INSCIT), or if the context contains distracting information, even a correctly retrieved context may not yield a high F1 match against a verbose reference.
- **Core assumption:** F1 score is highly sensitive to answer length and style; a short, precise answer from a good retrieval is easy to match, while a long, multi-sentence answer from a good retrieval is harder to match token-for-token.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.6] "For CoQA and SQA, Reranker appears to perform best... In contrast, QuAC and Doc2Dial show only marginal differences... This disconnect is most pronounced for DoQA, where MRR exceeds 90% but F1 remains below 40%."
  - [section 5.6, Table 3] Spearman’s ρ is low/negative for SQA (0.383) and DoQA (-0.157). The text attributes this to "variations in answer formats" and that "many answers take the form of follow-up questions" in DoQA.
  - [section 4.2] Notes answer length variance: CoQA (4.43 tokens) vs. INSCIT (45.32 tokens).
- **Break condition:** The correlation strengthens when answer formats are uniform and extraction-focused (e.g., SQuAD-style) and weakens with heterogeneous, generative, or conversational answer styles.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: RAG Pipeline Composition**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper evaluates distinct strategies (e.g., preprocessing like HyDE, postprocessing like Reranker) within a standard RAG loop: query → retrieval → context → generation. Understanding this pipeline is essential to see *where* a method intervenes.
  - *Quick check question:* For the "Query Rewriting" method, does it modify the query before or after the initial retrieval step?

- **Concept: Conversational QA Challenges**
  - *Why needed here:* Performance is heavily modulated by dataset structure (topic switching, coreference, dialogue length). A foundational grasp of these challenges explains *why* method effectiveness varies.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might retrieval performance degrade over many turns in a topic-switching dataset like TopiOCQA?

- **Concept: Evaluation Metrics for RAG**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper uses MRR@5 (retrieval ranking quality) and F1 (generation token overlap). Their often weak correlation (Section 5.6) is a key insight. Knowing what each metric captures prevents misinterpreting results.
  - *Quick check question:* What does a high MRR@5 but a low F1 suggest about the retrieved context and the answer format?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Query Processor (with optional HyDE/Rewriting) -> Retriever (Dense + Sparse) -> [Optional: Reranker] -> [Optional: Summarizer] -> Generator (Llama 3 8B Instruct)

**Critical path:**  
History + Query → [Optional: Preprocessing (HyDE/Rewriting)] → Dense+Sparse Retrieval → [Optional: Reranker] → [Optional: Summarization] → LLM Generation → Answer.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Hybrid BM25 vs. Vanilla:** Adds sparse retrieval index and score fusion logic. Increases setup complexity and slight latency, but improves recall robustly.
- **Reranker:** Requires a cross-encoder model and a larger initial retrieval set (e.g., top-100). Increases compute cost significantly but can improve ranking precision.
- **HyDE:** Adds one full LLM call per turn to generate hypothetical answer. Costly but often yields the best retrieval gains, especially on complex datasets.
- **Summarization:** Adds LLM call(s) to condense contexts. Risks losing critical details; benefit is context length reduction for the generator's limited context window.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Method fails below No-RAG:** Indicates the pipeline introduces noise (e.g., irrelevant contexts from bad retrieval, confusing context from summarization) that misleads the generator more than no context does.
- **High MRR, low F1:** Suggests retrieved context is correct but answer format mismatch (long vs. short answers) or generator cannot extract/format from the context properly.
- **Performance collapse on later turns:** Classic sign of ineffective dialogue history handling. The method cannot cope with accumulated context or topic shifts (see INSCIT/TopiOCQA in Figure 4).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline Replication:** Run Vanilla RAG on CoQA and INSCIT. Verify you reproduce relative performance gaps (CoQA high F1, INSCIT low F1) to confirm setup integrity.
2. **Hybrid BM25 Sanity Check:** Implement Hybrid BM25 (dense + BM25, reciprocal rank fusion or weighted sum). Measure MRR@5 improvement over Vanilla RAG on QReCC. Expect ~+0.7-1.0 MRR.
3. **Turn-wise Analysis Probe:** For a single dataset (e.g., QuAC), plot MRR@5 by turn number for Vanilla RAG and HyDE. Expect to see HyDE maintaining more stable performance across turns if it better handles context.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** How can standardized evaluation frameworks be developed to overcome the dataset-specific heterogeneity (prompt formats, answer structures, context representations) that currently complicates cross-dataset RAG method comparisons?  
**Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Methodological and Dataset Heterogeneity. This study was hindered by the wide variety of RAG methods and datasets, which required extensive, dataset-specific preprocessing due to differing prompt formats, answer structures, and context representations."  
**Why unresolved:** The paper's comprehensive analysis was limited by the need for custom preprocessing per dataset, preventing deeper, uniform comparisons. A standardized framework would enable more generalizable conclusions about RAG method performance.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Development and adoption of a unified conversational QA benchmark with consistent schema (query, context, answer formats, evaluation prompts) across diverse domains and dialogue structures.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** To what extent can mitigating retrieval challenges from large, fragmented context collections (e.g., via metadata-based grouping or iterative retrieval) improve performance on datasets with high context-to-question ratios like TopiOCQA and INSCIT?  
**Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Retrieval Challenges from Large and Fragmented Contexts... This issue could be addressed by grouping contexts during pre-processing using metadata such as titles or by adopting iterative retrieval or generation strategies..."  
**Why unresolved:** The paper identifies high Ctx/Q ratios as a key cause of low retrieval performance but only hypothesizes solutions (grouping, iterative retrieval) without empirical validation.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments comparing the proposed mitigation strategies (e.g., clustering contexts by title/topic, multi-hop retrieval) against the baselines on the problematic datasets, measuring impact on MRR@5 and F1.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** What specific properties of dialogue history (e.g., coreference chains, topic shifts, turn-taking patterns) cause the divergent effects of conversation depth on retrieval performance—improving in CoQA/SQA but degrading in INSCIT/TopiOCQA?  
**Basis in paper:** [explicit] "CoQA and SQA benefit from more context and improve performance with each turn... INSCIT and TopiOCQA exhibit low performance that steadily decreases with the number of turns, primarily due to the high Ctx/Q ratio... designed to support topic and interaction entity switching."  
**Why unresolved:** The paper correlates performance trends with dataset characteristics (Ctx/Q ratio, topic switching) but does not disentangle the precise conversational phenomena (e.g., how coreference resolution failure or abrupt topic changes specifically harm retrieval).  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Fine-grained error analysis on turn-level retrieval failures, annotated for dialogue phenomena (e.g., presence/absence of pronouns, topic shift detection), coupled with ablation studies simulating controlled history truncation or reformulation.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Is the observed superiority of simple, robust methods (HyDE, Hybrid BM25, Reranker) over more complex advanced RAG techniques consistent across a broader range of LLM architectures, retrieval corpora, and conversational QA datasets beyond the eight studied?  
**Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Our findings indicate that effective conversational RAG depends less on method complexity than on alignment between the retrieval strategy and the dataset structure... Overall, our findings indicate that effective conversational RAG depends less on method complexity..."  
**Why unresolved:** The conclusion is based on a fixed generator (Llama 3 8B Instruct) and a specific set of eight datasets. The generalizability to other model scales, architectures (e.g., encoder-decoder), or unseen domain datasets is not established.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Replication studies using multiple LLMs (e.g., larger instruction-tuned models, domain-specific models), different vector stores/embedding models, and expanding the evaluation to additional multi-turn conversational benchmarks (e.g., conversational search logs, task-oriented dialogues).

### Open Question 5
**Question:** What underlying factors (answer format, refusal mechanisms, context reliance) cause the weak or negative correlation between retrieval (MRR) and generation (F1) performance in datasets like DoQA and QuAC, where strong retrieval does not translate to strong generation?  
**Basis in paper:** [explicit] "In contrast, QuAC and Doc2Dial show only marginal differences across methods, and the correlation is weaker... This disconnect is most pronounced for DoQA, where MRR exceeds 90% but F1 remains below 40%... These differences can be attributed to dataset specific problems, particularly relating to variations in answer formats."  
**Why unresolved:** The paper speculates on answer format, informal responses, and generator refusals but does not quantitatively isolate or measure the contribution of each factor (e.g., by analyzing error types: retrieval failure vs. generation failure given correct context).  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Targeted case studies on DoQA/QuAC samples with high MRR but low F1, categorizing generation errors (e.g., incorrect extraction, refusal, over-generation). Controlled experiments using oracle retrieval for these datasets to measure the pure generation bottleneck ceiling.

## Limitations
- Implementation details for advanced methods (HyDE prompts, reranker model, query rewriting logic) are unspecified, threatening reproducibility.
- Dataset preprocessing steps (normalization, filtering) are not fully documented, risking dataset mismatch and non-comparable results.
- Evaluation is confined to a single LLM (Llama 3 8B Instruct) and one embedding model; generalizability across model scales, architectures, and corpora remains unknown.
