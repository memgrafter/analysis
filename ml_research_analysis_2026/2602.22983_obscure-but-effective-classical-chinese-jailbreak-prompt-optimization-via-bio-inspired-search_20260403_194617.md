# Obscure but Effective: Classical Chinese Jailbreak Prompt Optimization via Bio-Inspired Search

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22983
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22983
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** CC-BOS achieves near 100% attack success rate across six state-of-the-art LLMs with dramatically fewer queries than existing methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CC-BOS, a framework that exploits the obscurity and conciseness of classical Chinese to bypass safety constraints in large language models. It formalizes jailbreak strategies into an eight-dimensional space and uses a fruit fly optimization algorithm to automatically generate and refine adversarial prompts. Evaluated on six black-box LLMs, CC-BOS attains near-perfect attack success rates with high query efficiency and strong cross-model transferability, significantly outperforming prior state-of-the-art jailbreak methods.

## Method Summary
CC-BOS encodes a harmful query into a classical Chinese jailbreak prompt by sampling from eight strategic dimensions (role, behavior, mechanism, metaphor, expression, knowledge, context, trigger pattern). A fruit fly optimizer then iteratively perturbs this strategy vector using smell search (local adaptation), vision search (attraction to the best solution), and Cauchy mutation (escape stagnation). Each generated prompt is sent to a target LLM; the response is translated via a two-stage module (classical Chinese → modern Chinese → English) before a fitness score is computed from keyword matches and a semantic compliance judgment. The process terminates upon reaching a fitness threshold or exhausting iterations.

## Key Results
- Near 100% attack success rate on Gemini-2.5-flash, Claude-3.7, GPT-4o, Deepseek-Reasoner, Qwen3, and Grok-3.
- Superior query efficiency, e.g., average query count of 1.28 on GPT-4o, far below baselines like PAIR and TAP.
- Strong cross-model transferability, with transfer attack success rates up to 96%.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical contributions of the classical Chinese context and the bio-inspired optimization algorithm.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Classical Chinese text exploits a safety alignment blind spot in LLMs, increasing jailbreak success.
- **Mechanism:** The semantic compression, rich metaphors, and inherent ambiguity of classical Chinese reduce the effectiveness of safety guardrails trained primarily on modern language patterns. The model's comprehension capability remains high, but its safety classifiers fail to accurately map the obscured malicious intent to prohibited content.
- **Core assumption:** Safety alignment mechanisms in LLMs are not equally robust across all linguistic contexts, especially for historically attested but rarely used in modern safety training contexts like Classical Chinese.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Owing to its conciseness and obscurity, classical Chinese can partially bypass existing safety constraints, exposing notable vulnerabilities in LLMs."
  - [section 1] "the asymmetry in semantic correspondence ... heightens the risk of security vulnerabilities when models perform cross-lingual interpretation and generation."
  - [corpus] Weak direct support; corpus shows general multilingual vulnerability but does not specifically isolate Classical Chinese as a distinct factor.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Formalizing jailbreak strategies into an eight-dimensional search space enables systematic and combinatorial generation of effective adversarial prompts.
- **Mechanism:** By decomposing successful manual jailbreak patterns into discrete, orthogonal dimensions (Role, Behavior, Mechanism, Metaphor, Expression, Knowledge, Context, Trigger), the framework can explore a vast and structured space of prompt variations. This moves beyond fragmented, single-strategy attacks to multi-strategy synergy.
- **Core assumption:** Jailbreak success is not a monolithic property but an emergent outcome of interacting strategic choices across multiple linguistic and contextual facets.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] "We abstract jailbreak methods into eight core dimensions... formalized as a Cartesian product." This defines the strategy space `S`.
  - [abstract] "formulates an eight-dimensional strategy space... and employs a fruit fly optimization algorithm to automatically generate and refine prompts."
  - [corpus] Huang et al. (2025) propose expanding the jailbreak policy space, supporting the value of a multi-dimensional, combinatorial approach.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The fruit fly optimization algorithm (FOA) efficiently navigates the high-dimensional strategy space by balancing stochastic exploration (smell search, Cauchy mutation) with exploitation (vision search) under black-box constraints.
- **Mechanism:** Smell search performs adaptive local perturbations with decaying step size. Vision search directs individuals toward the current best solution with increasing probability. Cauchy mutation provides heavy-tailed jumps to escape local optima upon stagnation. Hash-based deduplication prevents redundant queries.
- **Core assumption:** Black-box prompt optimization can be framed as a population-based heuristic search problem where fitness is derived from model responses, and a bio-inspired algorithm offers a better exploration-exploitation trade-off than generic random or purely greedy search.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] Detailed algorithms for `Φsmell`, `Φvision`, and `Φcauchy` operators. "This design enables efficient exploration of the search space."
  - [section 4.2] Results show CC-BOS uses dramatically fewer queries (e.g., Avg.Q=1.28 on GPT-4o) than baselines like PAIR/TAP/CL-GSO, indicating high search efficiency.
  - [corpus] No direct support for FOA in jailbreaking; corpus shows other optimizers (e.g., genetic algorithms in CL-GSO, Bayesian optimization). The evidence for superiority is internal empirical comparison, not external validation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multilingual & Cross-Lingual Vulnerabilities in LLMs**
  - *Why needed:* The paper's core premise is that Classical Chinese presents a unique vulnerability vector. Understanding prior work on non-English/low-resource language jailbreaking is essential to differentiate this work from simply exploiting "low-resource" languages and to ground the argument about a "safety blind spot" for a high-resource classical language.
  - *Quick check:* What is the key distinction between the vulnerability in low-resource languages (e.g., Swahili) and the vulnerability in Classical Chinese, according to the paper?
- **Concept: Black-Box Prompt Optimization**
  - *Why needed:* CC-BOS is a black-box attack. Understanding the challenges (no gradients, limited queries) and common paradigms (evolutionary, reinforcement learning, LLM-guided search) is necessary to appreciate the design choices in the FOA and its comparison to PAIR, TAP, AutoDAN, CL-GSO, ICRT.
  - *Quick check:* What are the primary trade-offs between population-based searches (like FOA/GA) and sequential, attacker-LLM-guided searches (like PAIR/TAP) in a black-box setting?
- **Concept: Evaluation of Jailbreak Success**
  - *Why needed:* The paper uses a two-stage evaluation (keyword matching + semantic consistency scorer via a judge model). Grasping the metrics (ASR, Avg.Score, Avg.Q) and the potential pitfalls of evaluation (e.g., judge model bias, translation artifacts) is critical for interpreting the results, especially the ablation on the translation module.
  - *Quick check:* Why does the paper include a classical Chinese-to-English translation module before evaluating the target LLM's response, and what does its ablation study imply about evaluation robustness?

## Architecture Onboarding
Strategy Encoder -> Bio-Inspired Optimizer (FOA) -> Fitness Evaluator -> Termination Controller
(Translation Module is embedded within Fitness Evaluator)

**Critical path:** Initialization -> (Smell Search -> Evaluation -> Vision Search -> Evaluation) * N_iters -> (if stagnation: Cauchy Mutation) -> Update best -> Check termination.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Obscurity vs. Readability:** Classical Chinese provides attack stealth but necessitates a translation step for reliable evaluation, introducing potential evaluation noise.
- **Exploration vs. Query Cost:** The FOA parameters control the balance. The aggressive query efficiency (Avg.Q ~1.2) suggests a bias toward rapid exploitation, possibly at the cost of thorough space coverage.
- **Generality vs. Specificity:** The 8D space is broad, but its elements are hand-crafted based on observed tactics and literary conventions. It may not generalize to novel linguistic attack surfaces.

**Failure signatures:**
- Low ASR across all target models (<80%) indicating an ineffective strategy space or optimizer.
- High query counts (Avg.Q > 10) approaching the iteration limit without reaching threshold τ, suggesting poor search guidance or stagnation.
- High duplication rate tracked by the hash set, indicating a poorly configured search operator or an overly constrained strategy space.
- The translation module causing systematic under-scoring (e.g., ASR drops significantly when removed), pointing to evaluation bias rather than attack failure.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Baseline Replication:** Run CC-BOS on standard benchmarks (AdvBench/CLAS/StrongREJECT) against all six target models. Verify the claim of ~100% ASR and superior query efficiency vs. PAIR, TAP, AutoDAN-Turbo-R, CL-GSO, ICRT.
2.  **Ablation Studies:** Systematically remove components: (a) Classical Chinese context, (b) the 8D strategy space, (c) the FOA, (d) the translation module. This isolates the contribution of each claimed innovation.
3.  **Cross-Model Transferability:** Generate adversarial prompts using one source model (e.g., GPT-4o) and test them on all other target models. This tests the robustness of the generated prompts beyond the specific model used for fitness evaluation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What is the primary cause of classical Chinese jailbreak effectiveness: safety alignment blind spots or data scarcity in training corpora? The paper contrasts classical Chinese's "relatively complete linguistic system" with guardrails failing due to "obscurity and conciseness," suggesting a blind spot, but does not isolate the cause.
- **Open Question 2:** Is the proposed eight-dimensional strategy space exhaustive, or are there unexplored dimensions that could further improve attack success? The dimensions are a fusion of existing strategies and classical properties, but completeness is not claimed or evaluated.
- **Open Question 3:** To what extent does the two-stage translation module introduce biases that affect the evaluation of jailbreak success? The module is designed for reliability, but no experiments test whether translation distorts the perceived harmfulness or compliance of responses.
- **Open Question 4:** What factors explain the observed variations in cross-model transferability of classical Chinese jailbreak prompts? Transfer ASR ranges from 76% to 96%, but the paper provides no analysis linking success to model architectures, training data, or alignment techniques.

## Limitations
- The initial attraction strength β₀ for vision search is not specified, requiring assumption for reproduction.
- The exact few-shot prompts for the translation module and compliance judge are underspecified, potentially affecting evaluation fidelity.
- The reported Classical Chinese advantage may be confounded by general multilingual effects not isolated in experiments.
- The GPT‑4o judge may introduce bias against ambiguous classical text.
- The eight‑dimensional space is manually curated and may omit critical, unexplored strategy factors.
- Benchmarks (AdvBench, CLAS, StrongREJECT) have limited diversity, possibly constraining generalizability.

## Confidence
- **Classical Chinese bypass:** Medium (plausible mechanism, but confounded by general multilingual factors).
- **8D strategy utility:** Medium (empirically effective, but space completeness is unproven).
- **FOA efficiency:** High (clear empirical superiority in query count).
- **Near‑100% ASR:** High (in-paper), Low (externally, due to evaluation dependencies and benchmark limits).
- **Cross‑model transferability:** Medium (empirical evidence but no correlation analysis).
