# A Spectral Revisit of the Distributional Bellman Operator under the Cramér Metric
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12576
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12576
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** Prove √γ contraction and fixed-point existence on CDF space; construct regularised spectral Hilbert spaces via exact conjugation, realizing the Cramér geometry as a singular limit of stable Hilbert geometries.

## Executive Summary
This paper revisits the distributional Bellman operator in reinforcement learning, analyzing its contraction properties under the Cramér metric. The authors shift the perspective to cumulative distribution functions (CDFs), where the Bellman update acts affinely, enabling direct structural analysis. They introduce a spectral lifting via exact conjugation, embedding CDF differences into a regularized Hilbert space defined through characteristic functions with a damping factor. This construction yields a bounded linear operator whose induced metric converges to the Cramér metric as the regularization vanishes. The results provide the first operator-theoretic spectral framework for distributional RL with Cramér geometry, while recovering the native metric as a stable singular limit.

## Method Summary
The method consists of two main steps: (1) A CDF-level formulation of the distributional Bellman operator, which for a fixed policy acts linearly on centered CDF differences, and (2) a spectral lifting that conjugates this affine operator into a bounded linear operator on a Hilbert space. The Hilbert space is built from square-integrable functions with a weighted inner product using the reciprocal of (ω²+ε), where ε>0 regularizes the spectrum. An exact conjugation via an isometric identification map V ensures that the operator’s norm equals the Cramér contraction factor. As ε→0, the induced metric on the Hilbert space converges to the Cramér metric, thus providing a numerically stable spectral representation that asymptotically recovers the original geometry.

## Key Results
- The CDF-level Bellman operator is √γ-contractive under the Cramér metric and possesses a unique fixed point in the space of CDF fields with finite Cramér distance to a reference Dirac distribution.
- A regularized spectral Hilbert space H_ε can be constructed such that the conjugated Bellman operator is bounded and linear on its range, with norm at most √γ.
- The regularized spectral metric converges to the Cramér metric as the regularization parameter ε ↓ 0, recovering the native geometry as a singular limit.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism hinges on two insights. First, by working with CDFs instead of distributions, the Bellman operator becomes affine: the CDF of the updated return is a sum of shifted CDFs scaled by reward probabilities. Second, by centering CDFs relative to a reference (Dirac at 0) and embedding them via the transport V, which combines a centering transform U and an isometric identification, the operator’s action becomes linear in the spectral domain. The regularized inner product ⟨f,g⟩_ε = ∫ (f̂(ω) ĝ(ω))/(ω²+ε) dω/(2π) yields a Hilbert space where the norm corresponds to the damped Cramér distance. The conjugation V T_cdf V⁻¹ inherits the √γ contraction because V is an isometry up to the regularization. The singular limit ε→0 is well-defined because the regularizing term vanishes, and the metric converges to the Cramér distance, which is the L²-based distance on centered CDFs.

## Foundational Learning
**Cramér metric** – A probability metric defined as the L² distance between CDFs. Needed to measure distributional relationships; quick check: it satisfies triangle inequality and symmetry for distributions with finite second moment.

**Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF)** – Encodes probabilities P(X≤x). Needed because Bellman updates become affine on CDFs; quick check: verify monotonicity, right-continuity, and limits 0/1 at ±∞.

**Affine operator** – Combination of linear map and translation. The Bellman update on CDFs is affine; this fact simplifies spectral analysis after centering.

**Spectral lifting** – Embedding distributions into a Hilbert space via characteristic functions. Allows linear operator analysis using spectral theory; quick check: ensure the embedding is well-defined (finite integral for characteristic functions).

**Regularized Hilbert space** – A weighted L² space with kernel 1/(ω²+ε). Needed to control unbounded operators; quick check: verify inner product positivity and completeness.

**Exact conjugation** – A similarity transformation that preserves operator properties. Here V conjugates T_cdf to a linear T on the spectral space; quick check: confirm T = V T_cdf V⁻¹ on the range.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: MDP (S,A,P,R,γ) -> CDF field F: S×A→Γ_F -> Bellman T_cdf (affine) -> Centering U (F → F - F_δ0) -> Isometric identification → Spectral embedding Φ (CDF → char func) -> Regularized space H_ε (weighted L²) -> Conjugated operator T^π = V T_cdf V⁻¹ -> Metric d_{H,ε} induces contraction.

Critical path: The key proof steps are: (i) show T_cdf is √γ-contractive in Cramér metric; (ii) define V = Φ ∘ U and H_ε; (iii) prove T^π is bounded and linear; (iv) show d_{H,ε} → d_Cr as ε→0.

Design tradeoffs: Using a regularizer ε stabilizes the spectral representation at the cost of an additional hyperparameter; choosing too small ε exacerbates numerical integration errors; piecewise-constant PMF representation is simple but may grow support rapidly.

Failure signatures: 
- After T_cdf, CDFs fail monotonicity or limit checks → indicates bug in CDF construction.
- Contraction ratio > √γ → likely error in Cramér distance computation or Bellman step.
- d_{H,ε} does not converge as ε decreases → insufficient integration range or quadrature error.
- Discrepancy between V(T_cdf(F)) and direct spectral iteration → problem in U or Φ implementation.

## Limitations
- The analysis requires bounded rewards and finite Cramér distance (Γ_F), which may not hold for heavy-tailed or continuous return distributions without further assumptions.
- Numerical integration for the spectral metric becomes delicate for very small ε due to oscillatory integrands and requires careful truncation.
- The framework is developed for policy evaluation with a fixed policy; extending to off-policy or greedy improvement is non-trivial and not covered.

## Confidence
- Cramér contraction under d_Cr: Medium
- Spectral lifting and metric recovery: Medium
- Boundedness of linear operator on centered CDFs: Low-Medium
