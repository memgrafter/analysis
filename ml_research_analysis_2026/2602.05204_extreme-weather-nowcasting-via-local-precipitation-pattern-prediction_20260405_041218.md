# Extreme Weather Nowcasting via Local Precipitation Pattern Prediction
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05204
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05204
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Deterministic 32M-parameter model achieves state-of-the-art CSI scores while reducing computational cost >20× versus diffusion-based approaches.

## Executive Summary
Accurate and efficient extreme precipitation nowcasting is limited by the high computational cost of generative models and dataset biases toward common or storm events. This paper introduces exPreCast, a deterministic framework that uses a Video Swin Transformer for efficient local spatiotemporal attention, a Cubic Dual Upsampling decoder to preserve fine-scale rainfall textures, and a Temporal Extractor for flexible forecasting horizons. A new balanced radar dataset from Korea Meteorological Administration (KMA) is constructed to mitigate rainfall distribution bias. Evaluated on KMA, SEVIR, and MeteoNet, exPreCast attains superior CSI scores across multiple thresholds and pooling sizes, matches or exceeds the accuracy of diffusion-based CasCast, and reduces floating-point operations by over 20×. The model demonstrates strong long-term prediction capabilities and robust generalization across diverse rainfall regimes.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a deterministic encoder-decoder architecture built on a 3D Swin Transformer backbone. The encoder processes multi-frame radar sequences via shifted windows to capture local spatiotemporal dependencies efficiently. The decoder employs Cubic Dual Upsampling (CDU) blocks that fuse trilinear interpolation with 3D pixel-shuffle to maintain high-frequency rainfall textures while avoiding checkerboard artifacts. A Temporal Extractor (TE) module post-processes the decoder output to enable flexible prediction horizons (e.g., 1-hour or 6-hour forecasts) without retraining the entire network. Training uses a Fourier Amplitude Correlation Loss (FACL), a linear combination of amplitude and correlative components, scheduled to transition from frequency matching to spatial correlation. The study introduces a balanced KMA radar dataset (2014–2023, 256×256 at 4 km resolution, 10-minute intervals) to address class imbalance. Optimization uses AdamW with a cosine decay schedule, and models are selected based on validation CSI-M.

## Key Results
- **Computational efficiency:** 32 million parameters, 200–550 GFLOPs, representing a >20× reduction versus diffusion-based CasCast.
- **State-of-the-art accuracy:** On KMA, achieves CSI-M 0.4841 (pool16) and HSS 0.4042; on SEVIR, CSI-219 0.2910 (pool16); on MeteoNet, CSI-M 0.4446 (pool16).
- **Generalization:** Demonstrates consistent performance across three datasets with distinct rainfall biases (balanced, storm-biased, normal-rain-biased) and superior long-term (6-hour) forecasting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Video Swin Transformer:** Provides local spatiotemporal attention with linear complexity, enabling the model to capture motion patterns and atmospheric evolution efficiently without the computational burden of global attention.
- **Cubic Dual Upsampling:** Mitigates checkerboard artifacts typical of pure pixel-shuffle by blending trilinear interpolation (smooth background) with pixel-shuffle (sharp details), preserving extreme precipitation textures.
- **Temporal Extractor:** Allows flexible forecasting horizons by adjusting temporal resolution after decoding, avoiding the need for multiple fixed-horizon models.
- **Fourier Amplitude Correlation Loss:** FACL guides the network to match the frequency distribution of precipitation (via amplitude loss) and maintain spatial coherence (via correlation loss), which is critical for structured extreme events.
- **Balanced KMA Dataset:** Reduces model bias toward non-precipitating or light-rain samples, improving detection of rare extreme events that are underrepresented in public datasets.

## Foundational Learning
- **3D Swin Transformer (Video Swin):** *Why needed:* Captures spatiotemporal patterns in radar sequences with hierarchical shifting windows. *Quick check:* Verify input patch shape (T×H×W) and window shifting logic in attention blocks.
- **Pixel-shuffle vs. Interpolation artifacts:** *Why needed:* Pure transposed convolution or pixel-shuffle produce checkerboard patterns; interpolation oversmooths. *Quick check:* Visualize decoder output for uniform regions; look for grid-like artifacts.
- **Fourier domain losses for precipitation:** *Why needed:* Precipitation intensity distributions are heavy-tailed; frequency-domain alignment helps model fine-scale structure. *Quick check:* Compute and compare power spectra of predictions vs. ground truth.
- **Z-R relationship in evaluation:** *Why needed:* CSI thresholds are in reflectivity (dBZ) but often applied to precipitation rate (mm/h) via Z=200R¹.⁶. *Quick check:* Confirm conversion is applied before thresholding metrics.
- **Class-balanced dataset construction:** *Why needed:* Prevents model from ignoring minority extreme classes. *Quick check:* Plot rainfall intensity histograms of KMA vs. SEVIR/MeteoNet.
- **Temporal upsampling factor:** *Why needed:* TE adjusts frame rate; must align with decoder’s temporal output. *Quick check:* For 6-hour output with 10-min input stride, verify decoder outputs 60 frames and TE downsamples to 36.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input (7 frames) -> Video Swin Encoder -> Cubic Dual Upsampling Decoder -> Temporal Extractor -> Output (6 or 36 frames)

**Critical path:** The encoder extracts multi-scale spatiotemporal features; the decoder progressively upsamples spatial and temporal resolution using CDU blocks; the TE resamples the temporal dimension to match the desired forecast horizon.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Deterministic vs. probabilistic: Deterministic design (exPreCast) is computationally efficient but cannot estimate forecast uncertainty.
- CDU complexity: Fusing two upsampling paths increases parameters and FLOPs slightly but avoids severe artifacts that degrade CSI for extreme events.
- Fixed vs. flexible horizon: TE adds minimal overhead but requires correct configuration per horizon; training from scratch may be needed for very long forecasts.

**Failure signatures:**
- Checkerboard artifacts in output: Likely incorrect CDU implementation (missing 3D conv before pixel-shuffle, wrong fusion).
- Low CSI scores despite training convergence: Possible FACL scheduling error, incorrect data normalization, or missing Z-R conversion in evaluation.
- Out-of-memory during training: Model size (Swin stages + CDU) requires multi-GPU; reduce batch size or enable AMP.
- Degraded long-term prediction: TE may not compress temporal dimension correctly; verify resampling factor and fine-tuning strategy.

**First three experiments:**
1. Train on a single SEVIR sequence (short horizon) and qualitatively inspect predictions for checkerboard artifacts; validate CDU block by ablating one branch.
2. Log FACL mixing coefficient P and component losses (FAL, FCL) during early training to confirm linear decay schedule and stable loss combination.
3. Evaluate short-term (1h) vs. long-term (6h) predictions on a held-out extreme event; ensure TE configuration matches paper (e.g., temporal upsampling factor 2 for 6h from 1h model).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can predictive uncertainty be quantified in deterministic precipitation nowcasting models like exPreCast?
- Basis in paper: The paper explicitly identifies that exPreCast, being deterministic, cannot provide uncertainty estimates, which is a limitation for early warning systems (Section 5).
- Why unresolved: Deterministic forecasts lack measures of confidence or variability, hindering probabilistic decision-making in weather risk management.
- What evidence would resolve it: Integration of probabilistic components (e.g., Bayesian neural networks, ensembles), evaluation using proper scoring rules for probabilistic forecasts, and deployment studies in operational contexts.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What strategies can address data scarcity and ill-posedness to improve long-term extreme rainfall forecasting?
- Basis in paper: The authors note that long-term extreme event prediction is challenging due to scarcity of extreme events and the ill-posed nature of forecasting from local radar data, suggesting a need for extrapolation or interpolation methods (Section 5).
- Why unresolved: Rare extreme events limit training data, and predicting fine-scale details over long horizons from limited observations is fundamentally difficult.
- What evidence would resolve it: Data augmentation with synthetic extreme events, incorporation of external data sources (e.g., NWP models), and development of models that can handle sparse data for long-term extrapolation.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How generalizable are models trained on the KMA dataset to other regions, and what biases persist in current precipitation datasets?
- Basis in paper: While the KMA dataset is presented as balanced, the paper compares it with biased datasets (SEVIR and MeteoNet), implying concerns about dataset bias and generalisation, but does not fully test cross-region performance.
- Why unresolved: Meteorological conditions vary by region; a dataset balanced for South Korea may not represent global diversity, and unseen biases could affect model robustness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation on multiple regional datasets, statistical analysis of dataset characteristics, and creation of standardised, globally balanced datasets with diverse rainfall regimes.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can the Cubic Dual Upsample (CDU) block be improved or replaced for better preservation of extreme precipitation fine-scale patterns?
- Basis in paper: CDU is designed to mitigate artifacts from pixel shuffle and interpolation, but only a few alternatives are compared; more advanced upsampling techniques might enhance detail preservation, especially for extreme events.
- Why unresolved: The current CDU design is a specific fusion approach; its optimality for all precipitation intensities and structures is not proven, and performance might be suboptimal for certain cases.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extensive comparison with state-of-the-art upsampling methods, ablation studies on CDU components, and qualitative/quantitative evaluation on extreme event samples.

## Limitations
- **KMA dataset access barrier:** The central balanced dataset is not publicly released, preventing independent replication of the primary claims on KMA.
- **Configuration ambiguity:** Exact per-dataset model configurations (stage depths, channel scaling) are not fully detailed, requiring inference from code.
- **Generalization scope:** Evaluation remains within three datasets with known biases; performance on entirely new geographical regions or radar systems is unverified.

## Confidence
- **Computational efficiency claim (20× reduction, 32M params):** High – Concrete architecture specifications directly verifiable.
- **Accuracy claims (CSI/HSS scores):** Medium – Reported results depend on correct data preprocessing, loss implementation, and evaluation metrics.
- **Generalization & long-term prediction claims:** Low–Medium – Depend on successful training and TE functionality; KMA results unconfirmed due to data unavailability.
