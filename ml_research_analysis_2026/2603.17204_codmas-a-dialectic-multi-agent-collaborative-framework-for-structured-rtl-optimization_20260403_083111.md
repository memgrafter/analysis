# CODMAS: A Dialectic Multi-Agent Collaborative Framework for Structured RTL Optimization
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.17204
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17204
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: CODMAS achieves up to 25.5% timing improvement for pipelining and 21.7% power reduction for clock gating with GPT-4o, maintaining failure rates below 25% across models, significantly outperforming prompting and agentic baselines.

## Executive Summary
CODMAS introduces a dialectic multi-agent framework for automated RTL optimization targeting power, performance, and area (PPA). By separating transformation planning (Articulator) and outcome prediction (Hypothesis Partner) from code generation (Domain-Specific Coding Agent) and deterministic evaluation (Code Evaluation Agent), it mimics expert design collaboration and reduces failures. On the RTLOPT benchmark, CODMAS attains up to 25.5% timing improvement for pipelining and 21.7% power reduction for clock gating, with failure rates under 25% across multiple LLMs. Ablations confirm that the dialectic separation and iterative refinement are crucial for robustness and metric gains.

## Method Summary
CODMAS operates as a closed-loop system without model training. The Articulator produces a stepwise transformation plan (e.g., inserting pipeline registers) and surfaces latent assumptions. The Hypothesis Partner predicts functional behavior and PPA impacts, then reconciles deviations after evaluation. The Domain-Specific Coding Agent (DCA) generates Verilog edits using a prompt enriched with the plan, predictions, and a dataflow graph extracted by Pyverilog. The Code Evaluation Agent (CEA) uses Icarus Verilog and Yosys to provide deterministic feedback on syntax, functional equivalence, and PPA metrics. Feedback updates the Articulator and Hypothesis Partner, guiding subsequent iterations until goals are met or a maximum number of iterations is reached. This dialectic structure, combined with domain-specific knowledge injection and deterministic EDA tools, enables reliable automation of complex RTL optimizations.

## Key Results
- Pipelining: up to 25.5% timing improvement; Clock gating: up to 21.7% power reduction (GPT-4o).
- Failure rates consistently below 25% across GPT-4o, Llama-3, and other models.
- Ablations: removing dialectic agents or merging roles reduces gains (e.g., pipelining timing drops from 25.5% to 12.9%) and increases failure rates, confirming the necessity of role separation and iterative refinement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Articulator agent's explicit stepwise transformation planning reduces synthesis and functional failures by decomposing complex optimizations (e.g., pipelining) into ordered, verifiable operations.
- **Mechanism:** Inspired by rubber-duck debugging, the Articulator verbalizes the optimization intent and structural changes (e.g., "insert pipeline registers between partial products"), surfacing latent design assumptions. This plan serves as a stable semantic scaffold for subsequent code generation and hypothesis testing, preventing uncoordinated edits.
- **Core assumption:** That a deterministic, human-interpretable plan can guide an LLM more reliably than an unconstrained code-generation prompt, especially for domain-specific structural transformations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Articulator... articulates stepwise transformation plans and exposes latent assumptions."
  - [section 3.1.1] "Procedural reasoning allows the Articulator to decompose complex transformations into ordered operations while surfacing latent design assumptions."
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., VeriAgent, RTLSquad) also explore multi-agent RTL design, but CODMAS uniquely isolates planning as a distinct dialectic role; direct citation evidence for this specific mechanism is limited to the paper itself.
- **Break condition:** If the Articulator generates overly generic or incorrect plans (as noted in Limitations), the system may require excessive iterations or fail to converge, indicated by persistently high failure rates in ablation studies (w/o DA in Table 3).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Hypothesis Partner's predictive and abductive reasoning, conditioned on the Articulator's plan, guides targeted refinements by anticipating PPA outcomes and reconciling deviations from evaluation feedback, thereby improving metric gains.
- **Mechanism:** Before code generation, the Hypothesis Partner formulates expected functional behavior and PPA impacts. Upon receiving CEA feedback (syntax errors, functional mismatches, suboptimal metrics), it attributes failure causes and revises hypotheses, suggesting specific adjustments (e.g., "expected timing improvement, risk of latency bugs").
- **Core assumption:** That predictive, model-based reasoning about circuit behavior can be effectively verbalized by an LLM and used to steer code generation more precisely than reactive correction alone.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Hypothesis Partner... predicts outcomes and reconciles deviations between expected and actual behavior to guide targeted refinements."
  - [section 3.1.2] "The Hypothesis Partner operates... leveraging abductive and model-based reasoning to anticipate the effects... revises prior hypotheses, attributes failure causes, and suggests targeted refinements."
  - [section 5.2] Ablation removing dialectic agents (w/o DA) shows large drops in timing/power gains (e.g., GPT-4o pipelining T drops from 25.5% to 12.9%) and increased FR, supporting the partner's role in predictive guidance.
- **Break condition:** If the Hypothesis Partner's predictions are systematically inaccurate or too vague, the refinement loop may chase incorrect transformations, leading to oscillating PPA metrics or stagnation (as seen in hard tasks in Figure 4 after peak iterations).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The dialectic separation between Articulator (planning) and Hypothesis Partner (prediction/critique) is necessary for robust optimization; combining these roles into a single agent degrades performance and increases failure rates.
- **Mechanism:** The two agents maintain complementary cognitive roles: the Articulator provides a stable, consistent plan, while the Hypothesis Partner dynamically evaluates outcomes. This structure prevents premature commitment to suboptimal transformations and enables systematic hypothesis testing under a unified circuit model.
- **Core assumption:** That explicitly separating "design articulation" from "predictive inference" creates a more robust reasoning loop than a monolithic agent attempting both, particularly for multi-objective trade-offs.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Ablations confirm the necessity of dialectic separation and iterative refinement for robustness and metric gains."
  - [section 5.2.1 & Figure 3] Shared-memory multi-agent (SMA) ablation, where roles are collapsed, shows reduced timing/power gains and higher FR (e.g., GPT-4o pipelining T: 25.5% → 19.2%, FR: 19.5% → 35.7%).
  - [section 5.2] Table 3 shows w/o DA (dialectic agents) causes the largest performance drop across models.
- **Break condition:** If the two agents enter a conflict loop (e.g., plan vs. hypothesis deadlock), the system may fail to converge, signaled by repeated syntactic/functional failures despite iterations, potentially requiring external arbitration or heuristic overrides.

## Foundational Learning
- **Dialectic Reasoning in Multi-Agent Systems**: *Why needed:* CODMAS relies on structured debate-like interaction between specialized agents to mimic expert designer collaboration. Understanding how to separate planning from critique is key to interpreting its advantage over single-agent prompting. *Quick check:* "How might the system behave differently if the Articulator and Hypothesis Partner roles were swapped or merged?"
- **Domain-Specific Knowledge Injection (DKI)**: *Why needed:* The DCA uses a multi-faceted prompt (role prompting, examples, dataflow graphs) to ground LLM generation in hardware architecture (e.g., pipeline stages, gating opportunities). Without this, LLMs produce syntactically plausible but physically invalid RTL. *Quick check:* "Why is a dataflow graph from Pyverilog more effective than raw RTL code as context for pipelining?"
- **Deterministic EDA Feedback Loops**: *Why needed:* CEA uses open-source tools (Icarus Verilog, Yosys) for unambiguous syntax, functional, and PPA evaluation. This contrasts with purely LLM-based reasoning, where feedback may be hallucinated or inconsistent. *Quick check:* "What are the trade-offs of using simulation/synthesis versus formal verification for functional equivalence in this loop?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Articulator -> Hypothesis Partner -> Domain-Specific Coding Agent (DCA) -> Code Evaluation Agent (CEA) -> (feedback to Articulator & Hypothesis Partner). The dataflow graph is generated by Pyverilog and injected into the DCA prompt.  
**Critical path:** Articulator produces a stepwise optimization plan; Hypothesis Partner predicts expected PPA and functional changes; DCA generates Verilog using the plan, predictions, and dataflow graph; CEA evaluates syntax, function, and PPA metrics; failures and metric gaps are reported back to update the plan and hypotheses, repeating up to a maximum iteration limit.  
**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Iteration depth vs. compute cost: More iterations can improve PPA but increase expensive synthesis runs; early iterations yield most gains, suggesting adaptive stopping.  
- Dialectic overhead: Two reasoning agents add latency compared to single-agent prompting, but reduce failure rates and improve final metrics.  
- DKI complexity: Dataflow extraction via Pyverilog adds preprocessing but is essential for architecture-aware edits; removing it severely degrades performance.  
**Failure signatures:**  
- High failure rate (>30%): Likely dialectic breakdown (plan/hypothesis mismatch) or DCA ignoring DKI context.  
- Synthesis errors: DCA generating invalid syntax or unsupported constructs; possibly due to unrealistic Articulator plan.  
- Functional failures: Hypothesis Partner mispredicting control logic changes, or testbench misalignment.  
- Poor PPA despite no errors: Hypothesis Partner not exploring aggressively, or Articulator plan too conservative.  
**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Run zero-shot and ReAct baselines on RTLOPT to catalog failure distributions (syntax ~40%, functional ~35%, PPA ~25%) establishing problem severity.  
2. **Ablation - w/o Dialectic Agents:** Disable Articulator and Hypothesis Partner; use DCA+CEA only. Expect large drop in PPA gains and higher failure rates, confirming dialectic necessity.  
3. **Dialectic variant comparison:** Compare shared-memory multi-agent (SMA) variant (combined roles) vs. full CODMAS. Expect degraded performance in SMA, validating role separation; monitor iteration-wise convergence for instability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. To what extent can the CODMAS framework generalize to other RTL optimization techniques beyond pipelining and clock gating, such as retiming, resource sharing, or FSM restructuring?
2. How does the computational cost and tool runtime of CODMAS scale with the complexity and hierarchical size of industrial-scale RTL designs, and what architectural modifications are needed to maintain efficiency?
3. Can the specificity and architectural awareness of the Articulator's transformation plans be improved to reduce reliance on iterative refinement, particularly for complex or ambiguous RTL patterns?
4. What is the minimum viable size and diversity required in an RTL optimization benchmark like RTLOPT to reliably train or evaluate models for real-world EDA workflows?
5. How can deterministic verification in CODMAS be strengthened beyond simulation-based testbenches to formally guarantee functional equivalence and detect corner-case hardware vulnerabilities after optimization?

## Limitations
- Exact LLM prompts and interaction protocol are undisclosed, hindering direct replication and requiring reverse engineering.
- The update mechanisms for the Articulator and Hypothesis Partner (`ArticulatorUpdate`, `HypoPartnerUpdate`) are unspecified, leaving the internal reasoning loop ambiguous.
- Scalability to large industrial designs remains unproven due to tool runtime and repeated synthesis queries, potentially limiting practical adoption.

## Confidence
- Dialectic separation necessity: High
- Hypothesis Partner predictive guidance: Medium
- Articulator plan stability: Medium
- Domain Knowledge Injector (DKI) efficacy: High
- Code Evaluation Agent (CEA) deterministic feedback loop: High
