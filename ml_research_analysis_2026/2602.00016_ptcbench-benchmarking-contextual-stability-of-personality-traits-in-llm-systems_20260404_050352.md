# PTCBENCH: Benchmarking Contextual Stability of Personality Traits in LLM Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.00016  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00016  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: PTCBENCH reveals that situational contexts (e.g., unemployment) induce significant personality trait changes in LLMs, with agentic systems showing amplified instability, and trait shifts correlate with reasoning performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces PTCBENCH, a benchmark for quantifying the contextual stability of personality traits in large language models (LLMs). Leveraging the NEO-FFI inventory, the authors evaluate 39,240 records across foundation models and agentic systems under 12 situational contexts (locations and life events). Results demonstrate that contexts trigger measurable trait changes, notably in Conscientiousness and Neuroticism, with agentic frameworks exhibiting greater instability. Moreover, context-induced shifts in Openness and Neuroticism correlate with reasoning accuracy on AGIEval, suggesting that personality dynamics can impact LLM capabilities. The work establishes a foundation for building psychologically robust LLM systems.

## Method Summary
PTCBENCH uses a two-stage pipeline. First, a prompt synthesis module combines a predefined context (e.g., "You are at a bar") with a preset personality configuration (High/Medium/Low/None for the five Big Five traits). Second, the model completes the NEO-FFI questionnaire both before and after context exposure. To ensure reliability, responses are collected over three runs with temperature 0.2, and response options are shuffled per item per run to mitigate positional bias. A history decay mechanism compresses prior interactions to simulate realistic context retention. Trait changes are quantified via standardized mean differences (dE) and regression coefficients (B), while inter-rater reliability is assessed using ICC(3,1). The benchmark is applied to multiple LLMs and configurations, and trait shifts are correlated with performance on the AGIEval reasoning benchmark.

## Key Results
- Situational contexts induce significant trait changes; for example, High preset Conscientiousness decreases by -1.76 (B coefficient) in the "Bar" context.
- Agentic systems (AutoGen, CAMEL) show amplified instability compared to foundation models, especially for Conscientiousness and Extraversion.
- Preset personality levels modulate change magnitude, with higher baselines buffering against decreases but sometimes exaggerating increases.
- Context-driven shifts correlate with reasoning: increased Openness improves AGIEval accuracy by ~20%, while elevated Neuroticism degrades it.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PTCBENCH's design yields reliable stability measurements through several safeguards. The NEO-FFI is a validated psychometric instrument that maps succinctly to the Big Five traits. The pre/post context design isolates situational influence while controlling for baseline personality. Multi-run averaging with low temperature reduces stochastic variance. Option shuffling eliminates positional bias, and history decay prevents artificial memory artifacts. The use of ICC(3,1) confirms internal consistency, and the broad model coverage strengthens generalizability. Together, these elements allow detection of subtle but systematic trait changes with statistical confidence.

## Architecture Onboarding
*Component map*:  
ScenarioBank -> ContextSelector -> ContextInjector -> PersonalityConfigurator -> PromptSynthesizer -> LLMInterface -> NEOFFIQuestioner -> ResponseCollector -> Scorer -> StatisticsEngine  

*Critical path*:  
1. Select context and preset personality from configuration.  
2. Generate baseline NEO-FFI prompts, collect 3 runs.  
3. Inject context (via summary or interactive session) into LLM.  
4. Re-administer NEO-FFI, collect 3 runs.  
5. Compute pre/post differences, effect sizes (dE), and coefficients (B).  
6. Repeat across contexts/presets for statistical power and compute ICC for reliability.  

*Design tradeoffs*:  
- Single-turn context injection is efficient but may oversimplify multi-turn dynamics; multi-turn is realistic but introduces memory management complexity.  
- Full option shuffling per item per run ensures fairness but increases prompts and API calls.  
- History decay implementation (e.g., exponential weighting) balances recency vs. context length; overly aggressive decay may lose relevant information.  

*Failure signatures*:  
- ICC(3,1) < 0.8 in baseline indicates poor personality adherence or high randomness.  
- Extreme trait changes (|dE| > 2) in control (no-context) runs suggest prompt injection or scoring errors.  
- Non-uniform option distribution (e.g., all "Strongly Agree") signals model misunderstanding or saturation.  

*First experiments*:  
1. Baseline ICC replication for GPT-4o-mini across all presets (3 runs, temp=0.2); validate ICC > 0.8.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What are the specific mechanisms within LLM pretraining data or alignment processes that give rise to stable native personality baselines versus context-sensitive variations? The paper identifies this as future work, asking whether stability arises from pretraining on social data and how training/alignment shape it.  
2. How can LLM system architectures be designed to actively control or bound context-induced personality trait changes, especially in agentic frameworks? The paper calls for personality-aware design to set explicit bounds on trait change.  
3. Why do agentic systems like AutoGen exhibit amplified personality instability compared to foundation models, and what specific architectural components (memory, tool use, multi-agent orchestration) are responsible? The paper shows amplification but does not isolate causes.  
4. Does the observed correlation between context-induced personality shifts (e.g., increased Openness, elevated Neuroticism) and reasoning performance imply a causal relationship, and can personality be strategically modulated to enhance capability? The analysis is correlational; intervention studies are needed.  
5. How can personality assessment for LLMs move beyond static questionnaires like NEO-FFI to capture dynamic, interaction-based personality expression in long-horizon conversations? The paper notes limitations of single-turn prompts and suggests longitudinal metrics.

## Limitations
- Incomplete documentation of key implementation details (history decay, option shuffling, AGIEval mapping) hinders exact replication.
- Evaluation heavily focuses on GPT-4o-mini and a few agentic systems; findings may not generalize across model families.
- Potential residual response variance and positional bias despite mitigation strategies; external ICC verification lacking.

## Confidence
- Contextual trait changes: Medium (strong statistical evidence but dependent on correct pipeline implementation).  
- Agentic system instability: Low (limited model diversity and unclear replication details).  
- Preset personality modulation: High (clear baseline gradients across presets).  
- Trait‑reasoning correlation: Medium (reported effects but selection of extreme contexts lacks transparency).
