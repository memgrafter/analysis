# Would a Large Language Model Pay Extra for a View? Inferring Willingness to Pay from Subjective Choices

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09802
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09802
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Large LLMs produce economically meaningful willingness-to-pay estimates from choice data but show systematic biases, notably overvaluing club access (2–4× human) and exhibiting persona-driven extremes like rigid price minimization.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether large language models (LLMs) can make subjective decisions aligned with human preferences by analyzing their implied willingness to pay (WTP) for hotel room attributes. Using 240 binary choice dilemmas, the authors fit multinomial logit (MNL) models to choices from three advanced LLMs (Llama 3.3 70B, GPT-4o, Gemini-3-Pro). While all models yielded meaningful WTP estimates with high pseudo-R² values (0.63–0.90), they exhibited consistent attribute-level deviations: overvaluing club access and undervaluing view and floor level relative to human benchmarks. LLMs generally overestimated human WTP, particularly when shown expensive examples or given a business persona. Some models, like Gemini-3-Pro under a "student" persona, collapsed to a perfect price-minimization heuristic. The findings highlight both the potential of LLMs for subjective decision support and critical limitations tied to prompt design, model selection, and persona representation.

## Method Summary
The study uses a discrete choice experiment where LLMs select between two hotel room options described by seven attributes (e.g., view, floor, club access, price). From 240 such dilemmas, the LLM's deterministic (temperature=0) choices are aggregated. A multinomial logit (MNL) model is fitted to the choice data, with standardized attribute coefficients estimated via maximum likelihood. The marginal WTP for a non-monetary attribute is calculated as the ratio of its coefficient to the price coefficient, scaled by the ratio of their standard deviations in the original (non-standardized) data. Human benchmark WTP values are drawn from a prior study. The experimental design manipulates user information via persona prompts (e.g., "business trip," "budget student") and in-context learning (ICL) examples (cheap vs. expensive). This protocol is repeated for each LLM and condition.

## Key Results
- All large models produced valid MNL fits (pseudo-R² 0.63–0.90) with negative price coefficients, but all overvalued hotel club access (2–4× human WTP) and undervalued view and floor attributes.
- In-context learning with cheap examples reduced WTP overestimation and improved alignment with human benchmarks; expensive examples exacerbated inflation.
- Persona prompting induced extreme, economically irrational behaviors: Gemini-3-Pro under a "student" persona always chose the cheapest option, making MNL estimation impossible.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LLM choices in paired hotel room dilemmas can be modeled using a multinomial logit (MNL) to extract economically meaningful willingness-to-pay (WTP) estimates for room attributes.
- **Mechanism:** The LLM is presented with binary choice tasks between alternatives described by attributes. Its consistent selection pattern across 240 dilemmas is treated as revealed preference. An MNL model is fitted where utility is a linear combination of attribute values plus an error term. The WTP for attribute *k* is derived from the ratio of its standardized coefficient to the standardized price coefficient, scaled by the standard deviations of the original price and attribute.
- **Core assumption:** The LLM's deterministic choices reflect a stable, closed-set utility function that adheres to random utility maximization (RUM), with IID errors and an adequate linear functional form.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "analyzing their responses using multinomial logit models to derive implied willingness to pay (WTP) estimates."
  - [section 3.4] Description of MNL fitting and WTP calculation formula: WTPₖ = βₖ·σ_price / (β_price·σₖ).
- **Break condition:** The MNL fails if the LLM exhibits strong order bias (e.g., always picks the first option) or uses non-compensatory heuristics violating RUM assumptions, leading to perfect prediction or sign-inverted price coefficients.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The description length and framing of an attribute systematically biases the LLM's implied WTP, making more verbose, positively framed attributes disproportionately salient.
- **Mechanism:** The "hotel club access" attribute is described with an extended, elaborated clause ("which includes extra services such as breakfast and evening cocktails..."), while other attributes are terse. This textual disparity increases cognitive salience in the LLM's attention and prediction process, inflating its perceived utility and derived WTP.
- **Core assumption:** LLM comparative choice is sensitive to surface-form features like text length and emotional valence, not just semantic feature vectors. The model does not perfectly normalize for elaboration.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1] "One possible explanation is the relatively long and detailed description of this feature in the prompt..."
  - [section 4.1] Empirical test: shortening the club description for Llama 3.3 70B reduced its WTP from 1873.66 to 863.88 HKD.
- **Break condition:** The bias disappears if all attribute descriptions are normalized for length and framing. If WTP for club remains inflated under uniform descriptions, the mechanism is not primarily textual.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Persona-based prompting (especially for budget-conscious personas) can induce extreme, economically irrational behavior in some LLMs, such as perfect price-minimization that ignores all other attributes.
- **Mechanism:** A persona like "a student that wants to travel around the world as budget-friendly as possible" is placed in the system prompt. For Gemini-3-Pro, this triggers an overriding heuristic where the sole decision rule is "select the cheapest alternative," collapsing the multi-attribute trade-off into single-attribute optimization.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs can interpret persona descriptions as rigid constraints or dominant objectives rather than soft contextual preferences. This behavior is model-specific.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2.2] "It is not possible to estimate a model for Gemini-3-Pro under the student persona. In this setting, the model’s choices are perfectly predicted by consistently selecting the lowest-priced room."
  - [section 5.1] "Persona-based prompting can also induce less human-like behavior, as illustrated by the student persona condition."
- **Break condition:** The extreme price-only behavior does not occur if the persona is more nuanced or if prompts include explicit trade-off instructions. The mechanism breaks if the LLM shows any sensitivity to non-price attributes under the student persona.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Discrete Choice Modeling (Multinomial Logit)**
  - **Why needed here:** The entire analysis fits an MNL to LLM choices to derive WTP. A new engineer must understand the random utility maximization (RUM) foundation, the IIA property, coefficient interpretation, and the WTP calculation formula to reproduce results or diagnose model failures (e.g., negative price coefficient).
  - **Quick check question:** What does a statistically significant, negative coefficient for "price per night" in the MNL output imply about the LLM's preferences, and how is that used to calculate WTP for other attributes?

- **Concept: Willingness to Pay (WTP) in Economics**
  - **Why needed here:** WTP is the core outcome metric. It translates model coefficients into a monetary value (HKD) comparable to human benchmarks. Understanding that WTP is a marginal rate of substitution between a non-monetary attribute and price is crucial.
  - **Quick check question:** If the standardized coefficient for "view" is 2.0 and for "price" is -3.0, and the standard deviation of price is 400 HKD while for view it is 0.5 (binary), what is the WTP for a harbour view vs. city view? Why is the sign of the price coefficient always expected to be negative?

- **Concept: Persona Prompting & In-Context Learning (ICL)**
  - **Why needed here:** Persona descriptors and few-shot examples are the primary experimental manipulations. The engineer must grasp how these steer LLM output. Misimplementation (e.g., poorly constructed few-shots) would invalidate the manipulation.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the key difference between the "persona" and "ICL" conditions in this study? How might providing three examples where the user always chose the cheaper option be expected to affect the derived WTP, based on the paper's findings?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  Dilemma Generator -> Prompt Composer -> LLM API Client -> Response Parser & Dataset Builder -> MNL Estimator (xlogit) -> WTP Calculator -> Comparison & Visualization Engine

- **Critical path:** Dilemma Generation → Prompt Composer (for a specific persona/model) → LLM API Client (240 API calls) → Response Parser → MNL Estimator → WTP Calculator → Comparison. A failure in any step (e.g., API quota, MNL non-convergence) blocks the pipeline for that configuration.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Model Scale vs. Cost/Throughput:** Larger models (70B+) yield meaningful pseudo-R² and logical coefficients but are expensive and slow. Smaller models often produce order bias or inverted price coefficients, making them unusable. Tradeoff: empirical validity vs. computational budget.
  - **Prompt Fidelity vs. Control:** The "Club Access" attribute has a longer description. To isolate attribute effect, one must create a "short description" variant, but then comparability to the human study's stimuli is lost. Tradeoff: internal validity (clean attribute comparison) vs. external validity.
  - **Condition Realism vs. Interpretability:** Combining Persona + ICL creates a more "realistic" agentic scenario but can produce extreme, non-human-like WTP inflation. Simpler conditions are more interpretable but less ecologically valid.

- **Failure signatures:**
  1.  **MNL non-convergence or extreme coefficients:** Check if LLM shows >95% choice of one alternative. This indicates a condition (e.g., student persona for Gemini) collapsed to a heuristic. Solution: characterize the heuristic instead of forcing MNL.
  2.  **Negative or positive price coefficient:** If β_price > 0, the model prefers higher prices, violating rational theory. Check for order bias (always pick A) or accidental price reversal in prompts.
  3.  **Statistically insignificant intercept:** For larger models, the intercept is often insignificant, indicating no strong prior preference for option A/B after controlling for attributes. A *significant* intercept suggests residual order bias.
  4.  **Negative WTP for desirable attributes:** In persona conditions (student), this may occur due to extreme price dominance, a modeling artifact where the partial effect of a desirable attribute appears negative.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Baseline Replication (No Info):** Run the 240 dilemmas with the neutral prompt (no persona, no ICL) for all three main LLMs. Fit MNL, compute WTP, compare to human benchmark [9]. Goal: Establish each model's base alignment and identify systematic biases.
  2.  **ICL Sensitivity Test (3 Cheap vs. 3 Exp Examples):** For each model, run two conditions: three prior examples where the user always chose the cheaper option, and three where they always chose the more expensive. Compute WTP deviations. Goal: Test if cheap examples improve alignment and expensive ones worsen it.
  3.  **Persona Extremity Test (Student vs. Business):** Run the student and business persona prompts for each model. For Gemini-3-Pro, the student persona will likely fail to produce an MNL (perfect price minimization). Goal: Identify persona-induced extreme behavior and model-specific sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** Can more nuanced user persona representations mitigate the extreme valuation behaviors (e.g., rigid price focus) observed in LLMs like Gemini-3-Pro?
  - **Basis:** The authors explicitly state in their future work: "we would like to investigate the inclusion of more nuanced persona representations to see if they mitigate the extreme behavior witnessed in the results of Gemini-3-Pro."
  - **Why unresolved:** Current persona prompts (e.g., "student" or "business person") lead to unrealistic decision patterns, such as always selecting the cheapest option, which do not capture the complexity of human preferences.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Conduct experiments with detailed, multi-attribute personas that reflect real user heterogeneity, and compare WTP estimates and choice consistency to human benchmarks, assessing if alignment improves.

- **Question:** Is the overestimation of human WTP by LLMs a generalizable finding across different travel-related decision contexts?
  - **Basis:** The authors call for research to "investigate whether LLMs systematically overestimate individuals’ willingness to pay in other travel agent settings, such as booking a hotel, or whether this effect is specific to the hotel room case study."
  - **Why unresolved:** The study is limited to a single hotel room attribute scenario; travel decisions involve varied products (flights, activities) with different attribute structures and price sensitivities.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Apply the same discrete choice and WTP estimation methodology to other travel domains (e.g., airline seat classes, tour packages) and benchmark against human WTP data from those contexts.

- **Question:** How does embedding LLMs in complete travel-agent pipelines affect their subjective decision-making compared to isolated choice tasks?
  - **Basis:** The authors identify "an important avenue for further work is the explicit integration of LLMs into a travel-agent pipeline, to assess whether our findings fully generalize to real-world agentic settings."
  - **Why unresolved:** The current experiments use static, one-off choice dilemmas; real agentic systems involve sequential decisions, user interactions, and dynamic contexts that may alter model behavior.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Implement LLM-based travel agents in simulated or real environments, track decision outcomes over time, and evaluate alignment with user preferences, consistency, and practical utility.

## Limitations
- The study is confined to a single hotel room choice context; generalizability to other product categories or travel services is unproven.
- The attribution of club overvaluation to its longer textual description rests on a single-model test, giving that causal claim low confidence.
- Proprietary model access hinders exact replication and extended testing across more model families or smaller open-source variants.

## Confidence
- **Claim:** Large LLMs can be modeled via MNL to produce economically meaningful WTP estimates (pseudo-R² 0.63–0.90). **Label:** Medium (validated by fit statistics, but lacks independent replication).
- **Claim:** LLMs systematically overvalue club access (2–4× human) and undervalue view/floor. **Label:** High direction, Medium magnitude (consistent pattern across models, but magnitude may be context-specific).
- **Claim:** Persona and ICL manipulations significantly alter derived WTP. **Label:** Medium (empirically shown, but mechanistic understanding is partial).
- **Claim:** The "club access" inflation is primarily caused by its longer textual description. **Label:** Low (based on a single ablation test for one model).
- **Claim:** Gemini-3-Pro under a "student" persona exhibits perfect price-minimization, rendering MNL estimation impossible. **Label:** High (directly observed and reported).

## Next Checks
