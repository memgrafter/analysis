# Symmetry in language statistics shapes the geometry of model representations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15029  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15029  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Translation symmetry in token co-occurrence statistics induces Fourier-style embedding manifolds (e.g., circular months, linear year encodings) that are robust to co-occurrence ablation and allow linear coordinate decoding with provable error bounds.

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that the structured internal geometries observed in language models—such as circular arrangements of months and linear encodings of years—are a direct consequence of translation symmetry in the normalized co-occurrence statistics of words. By analytically diagonalizing matrices that respect this symmetry, the authors derive predictions about the eigenstructure of embeddings, including sinusoidal waveforms and Lissajous curves. These predictions match empirical findings in static word embeddings (Wikipedia) and transformer activations (Gemma-2B). Moreover, the geometry persists even when direct co-occurrences (e.g., month-month pairs) are removed, thanks to a collective mechanism where many words share a latent variable. Finally, the paper establishes theoretical bounds on decoding continuous coordinates (e.g., years) from principal components, showing error scaling as \(r^{-1/D}\), and validates this experimentally.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a normalized co-occurrence matrix \(M^*\) from a large corpus (Wikipedia) using a context window of size 16 and a linear decay weighting. \(M^*_{ij} = (P_{ij} - P_iP_j) / (0.5(P_{ij} + P_iP_j))\) approximates pointwise mutual information and is bounded in \([-2,2]\). Word embeddings are obtained by factorizing \(M^*\) (or its absolute value) via singular value decomposition, yielding \(W = \Phi \sqrt{|\Lambda|}\). To study specific concepts (months, years, US states), subsets of embeddings are extracted, centered, and analyzed using principal component analysis (PCA). The core theoretical insight is that if \(M^*\) depends only on the distance between words along a latent semantic continuum (translation symmetry), then \(M^*\) (or \(|M^*|\)) becomes a Toeplitz or circulant matrix. Such matrices are diagonalized by complex exponentials, so the real PCA modes are sinusoidal. The authors fit an exponential kernel \(C(\Delta) = e^{-|\Delta|/\sigma}\) (or its periodized version) to the empirical \(M^*\) and derive closed-form expressions for the eigenfrequencies and amplitudes. They then test robustness by ablating month-month entries and show that the circular geometry remains due to many other words sharing the seasonal latent variable. For coordinate decoding, they train linear probes on the top-\(r\) principal components to predict years and verify the theoretical mean-squared error decay.

## Key Results
- The eigenstructure of \(M^*\) under translation symmetry predicts Fourier modes that match observed sinusoidal geometries in both static and contextual embeddings (Figures 1, 3).
- Ablating direct co-occurrences (month-month) does not destroy the circular arrangement of months; the signal is recovered from a collective set of seasonal helper words (Figure 4).
- Decoding years from embeddings using linear probes yields test error scaling as \(r^{-1}\) for 1‑D years and \(r^{-1/D}\) in general, confirming the theoretical bound (Figure 2).
- Quantitative agreement between predicted and empirical eigenfrequencies/amplitudes is achieved by fitting an exponential kernel to the co‑occurrence matrix.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Translation symmetry in co-occurrence statistics induces Fourier geometry
**Claim:** When the normalized co-occurrence matrix \(M^*\) (or its absolute value) depends only on the distance between words along a latent continuum, its eigenvectors are Fourier modes, yielding sinusoidal embeddings that form circles (periodic) or open curves (aperiodic) in principal component space.  
**Mechanism:** The symmetry makes \(M^*\) (or \(|M^*|\)) a Toeplitz (open boundaries) or circulant (periodic) matrix. Such matrices are exactly diagonalized by complex exponentials \(e^{ikx}\); the real-valued PCA modes are cos/sin combinations with amplitudes given by the Fourier transform of the co‑occurrence kernel \(C(\Delta x)\).  
**Core assumption (Assumption 3.1):** \(M^*_{ij} = C(\text{dist}(x_i, x_j))\) with a well‑behaved kernel \(C\), and the latent coordinates \(\{x_i\}\) lie on an evenly spaced lattice.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract states that the probability of co‑occurrence depends only on distance along a latent continuum and that the derived eigenstructure matches empirical embeddings. Proposition 1 gives the analytical result for periodic lattices; Figures 1 and 3 show quantitative agreement for months and years.  
**Break condition:** Unevenly spaced latent coordinates, a non‑smooth kernel, or strong confounding features can break the Toeplitz/circulant structure and thus the Fourier prediction.

### Mechanism 2: Collective latent variable explains robustness to co‑occurrence ablation
**Claim:** The geometry persists even when direct co‑occurrences (e.g., month‑month) are removed because many other words share the same latent variable (e.g., seasonality), providing redundant signal that reconstructs the same eigenmodes.  
**Mechanism:** If a latent variable \(t\) modulates many words, the joint PMI matrix factors as \(\text{PMI}(i,j) = K(t_i - t_j) + \text{noise}\). Removing a small subset of entries (the month‑month block) adds a low‑rank perturbation. In the limit of large vocabulary size \(N\), the leading eigenvectors (governed by the global circulant \(K\)) are robust (Weyl/Davis‑Kahan theorems).  
**Core assumption (Section 4.1):** Many words have conditional probabilities \(P(i|t)\) that depend on a shared latent \(t\), making the PMI approximately circulant in the latent shift. The embedding dimension \(d \ll V\) but the signal‑to‑noise ratio for top modes remains high because eigenvalues scale with \(N\).  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract explicitly mentions the collective model. Section 4.1 derives the circulant structure; Figure 4 (left) shows circular months survive ablation, while Figure 4 (right) and Figure 16 demonstrate that even a handful of helper words suffice to reconstruct ordering.  
**Break condition:** Helper words must be sufficiently modulated by the latent; if they are not (e.g., random words), the geometry degrades. Also, if the embedding dimension \(d\) is too low relative to the rank of the perturbation, the top modes may be affected.

### Mechanism 3: Linear coordinate decoding bound from mode amplitude decay
**Claim:** Numerical coordinates (e.g., year, latitude) can be linearly decoded from the top‑\(r\) principal components with error scaling as \(r^{-1/D}\), because the amplitudes of Fourier modes decay monotonically with wavenumber.  
**Mechanism:** Under monotonic amplitude decay \(a_k \downarrow\) with wavenumber \(k\), the optimal linear probe projects onto the top‑\(r\) modes. The mean‑squared error in reconstructing the continuous coordinate is bounded by the tail sum of squared basis functions, which scales as \((r/\mathrm{Vol}_D)^{1/D}\).  
**Core assumption (Proposition 4):** The embedding geometry arises from a translation‑symmetric kernel with monotonic eigenvalue decay \(\lambda_\mu \downarrow\) with \(|k_\mu|\); the lattice is large.  
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract notes the bound and its empirical validation. Proposition 4 derives the bound; Figure 2 (right) shows test error decaying as \(r^{-1}\) for 1‑D years after the interpolation threshold.  
**Break condition:** Non‑monotonic amplitude decay (e.g., due to competing semantic factors) or misalignment between top modes and the coordinate direction can violate the scaling.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept 1: Co‑occurrence statistics and normalized matrix \(M^*\)**  
- *Why needed:* All mechanisms derive from properties of \(M^* = (P_{ij} - P_iP_j) / (0.5(P_{ij}+P_iP_j))\), which approximates PMI. Its eigenstructure governs learned embeddings in factorized word models.  
- *Quick check:* Given a corpus, how would you compute \(P_{ij}\) (co‑occurrence) and \(P_i\) (unigram) to construct \(M^*\)? What does a positive entry in \(M^*\) indicate about word pairs?

**Concept 2: Toeplitz/circulant matrices and Fourier diagonalization**  
- *Why needed:* Translation symmetry makes \(M^*\) locally Toeplitz (open) or circulant (periodic). These are diagonalized by Fourier modes, predicting sinusoidal PCA directions.  
- *Quick check:* For a circulant matrix \(C\) of size \(L \times L\), what is the form of its eigenvectors? How does the cyclic boundary condition affect the allowed wavenumbers?

**Concept 3: SVD/PCA of factorized embeddings**  
- *Why needed:* The visualized geometry is the PCA projection of embeddings. The theory connects this to eigenvectors of \(|M^*|\) via \(W W^\top \approx |M^*|\) for asymmetric factorizations.  
- *Quick check:* If you have word embeddings \(W \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times d}\), how do you extract the principal components for a subset \(S\) (e.g., months)? Why does centering matter?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**  
Co‑occurrence module → Normalization module → Embedding extraction → PCA projector → Linear probe module → Ablation handler  

**Critical path (steps):**  
1. Prepare corpus (tokenize, lowercase, filter vocabulary).  
2. Compute co‑occurrence counts \(C_{ij}\) with window \(L=16\) and weight \(f(d)=L+1-d\); normalize to \(P_{ij}\) and \(P_i\).  
3. Construct \(M^*\) (and optionally \(|M^*|\)).  
4. Obtain embeddings: either factorize \(M^*\) via SVD (word2vec‑style) or extract pretrained model (e.g., Gemma) activations for target tokens.  
5. For a subset \(S\) (months/years/states), center embeddings and compute PCA; visualize top 2‑3 PCs and Gram matrix.  
6. Fit theoretical kernel \(C(\text{dist})\) to empirical \(M^*_S\) (e.g., exponential) and compute predicted eigenmodes (analytical or numerical diagonalization).  
7. Test robustness: zero out entries of \(M^*\) corresponding to \(S \times S\) (e.g., month‑month), refactorize, check if geometry persists.  
8. For coordinate decoding: split \(S\) into train/test; for each rank \(r\), project onto top‑\(r\) PCs; train linear probe (minimum‑norm) on training set; compute test MSE vs. \(r\).  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Embedding dimension \(d\):* Higher \(d\) preserves more eigenvectors but may overfit to noise; moderate \(d\) (e.g., 1000) still captures top modes due to robustness. Trade‑off: model size vs. interpretability.  
- *Context window \(L\) and decay \(f(d)\):* Affect the scale of \(P_{ij}\) and kernel \(C(\cdot)\). Must be consistent across theory and empirical setup; larger \(L\) captures longer‑range dependencies but may dilute local symmetry.  
- *Theoretical kernel choice:* Exponential \(e^{-|\Delta|/\sigma}\) fits time; for cyclic phenomena a periodized version is needed. Fitting \(\sigma\) to empirical \(M^*\) is crucial for quantitative match.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. **No sinusoidal patterns** in PCA plots → violation of translation symmetry (check whether \(M^*_S\) is Toeplitz/circulant).  
2. **Geometry destroyed after ablation** even with helper words → helper words may not share the latent sufficiently (verify affinities).  
3. **Decoding error decays slower than \(r^{-1/D}\)** → eigenvalue spectrum not monotonic; additional semantic factors distort ordering.  
4. **Theoretical prediction mismatch** (wrong frequencies) → poor kernel fit; re‑estimate \(\sigma\) or consider alternative kernel.

**First three experiments:**  
1. *Reproduce Figure 1 (months/years):* Compute \(M^*\) from Wikipedia, factorize at \(d=1000\), extract month/year embeddings, perform centered PCA, and compare PC trajectories to theoretical Fourier curves.  
2. *Ablation robustness (Figure 4 left):* Zero month‑month entries in \(M^*\), refactorize (vary \(d\)), and verify that months still form a circle in PCA, even at low \(d\).  
3. *Linear decoding scaling (Figure 2 right):* For years 1700‑2020, randomly split 60/61 repeatedly; for each rank \(r\), train a linear probe on top‑\(r\) PCs; plot average test MSE vs. \(r\) on log‑log; confirm \(r^{-1}\) trend after interpolation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Context‑dependent representations:** How can we explain geometry changes under disambiguating context? The current static theory fails for polysemous words like “May” when context corrects its position.  
   - *What evidence would resolve:* Measure representation manifolds across many contexts; develop a model where context acts as an additional latent variable modulating the co‑occurrence kernel; predict context‑sensitive geometry and test against layer‑wise activations.

2. **Unified framework for binary, continuous, and hierarchical attributes:** The lattice‑based theory covers discrete uniform (periodic) and continuous (open) attributes, but hierarchical aspects (e.g., taxonomy) may involve multiple scales.  
   - *What evidence would resolve:* Characterize manifolds for hierarchical concepts in various models; design a generative co‑occurrence model with hierarchical latent variables; test eigenmode predictions empirically.

3. **Symmetry in biological neural codes:** Could similar symmetry‑based arguments explain grid cells in the entorhinal cortex?  
   - *What evidence would resolve:* Analyze animal trajectory data for translation‑invariant co‑occurrence patterns; train networks on trajectory prediction using co‑occurrence objectives; check if grid‑cell‑like representations emerge and match theoretical Fourier predictions.

## Limitations
- The kernel parameter \(\sigma\) fitted to co‑occurrence data may be corpus‑dependent, limiting generalizability.  
- Helper word selection (top‑100 by Fourier affinity) is heuristic and not theoretically justified.  
- Excluding the word “May” from month‑centering is ad‑hoc and could bias PCA results.  
- Assumptions of infinite lattices and monotonic eigenvalue decay may not hold in finite, noisy datasets, weakening theoretical guarantees.

## Confidence
- **High:** Translation symmetry → Fourier geometry. Empirically validated across static embeddings (Wikipedia) and Gemma‑2B with quantitative agreement in eigenfrequencies and amplitudes.  
- **Medium:** Collective latent variable explains ablation robustness. Theory assumes many helper words share the latent; empirical test uses seasonal words but robustness to other helper sets or languages is unverified.  
- **Medium:** Linear decoding error bound \(r^{-1/D}\). Validated for 1‑D years; extension to higher dimensions (e.g., US states) is suggested but not rigorously tested.  
- **Low:** Universality of symmetry principle. Claims apply to any latent continuum; only tested on calendar months, historical years, and US states. Generalization to abstract semantics (e.g., word analogies) remains speculative.

## Next Checks
1. **Toeplitz structure verification:** For multiple corpora (multilingual Wikipedia, news), compute \(M^*_S\) for months/years and quantify deviation from Toeplitz/circulant form (e.g., off‑diagonal variance). Confirm translation symmetry holds across contexts.  
2. **Helper set diversity test:** Ablate month‑month co‑occurrences using helper words selected by (a) frequency, (b) PMI with months, (c) random sets. Measure circularity preservation (e.g., PCA ellipse eccentricity) to test robustness to helper quality.  
3. **Decoding in higher dimensions:** For US state embeddings, decode 2‑D geographic coordinates from top‑\(r\) PCs. Verify test MSE scales as \(r^{-1/2}\) and compare to the theoretical prediction for \(D=2\). Repeat across models (GPT-2, Llama-2) to check consistency.
