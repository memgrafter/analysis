# The Stability of Online Algorithms in Performative Prediction
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.24207
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24207
- **Reference count**: 6
- **Primary result**: For any no-regret online algorithm, the uniform mixture over its iterates is ε-performatively stable with ε equal to the average regret (Regret(T)/T), without requiring any assumptions on the distribution map D(·).

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles performative prediction, where model deployment influences future data distributions, creating feedback loops. Traditional approaches require Lipschitz continuity of D(·) and suffer from computational hardness. This work introduces an unconditional reduction from no-regret online learning: by randomizing over models via a uniform mixture and employing a martingale argument, the need for continuity assumptions is eliminated. The core result shows that any no-regret algorithm yields a mixture whose stability gap is bounded by its cumulative regret divided by T. Instantiations with standard online convex optimization algorithms recover or improve known rates—including O(√T/T) for convex losses and O(log T/T) for strongly convex losses—even for non-smooth or weakly convex losses, and sidestep PPAD-hardness issues.

## Method Summary
The method is a reduction: run any no-regret online algorithm on the performative loss sequence ℓ_t(θ)=ℓ(z_t,θ) where each sample z_t is drawn from D(θ_t). After T rounds, take the uniform mixture μ over the models θ_1,…,θ_T. The stability gap E_{θ∼μ, z∼D(θ)}[ℓ(z;θ)−min_{θ'} E_{θ∼μ, z∼D(θ)}[ℓ(z;θ')] is decomposed into an average regret term plus a martingale difference sum that has zero expectation. Thus ε ≤ Regret(T)/T. This works for arbitrary D(·), bounded losses, and conditioned i.i.d. sampling; no continuity is needed. The framework translates any known regret bound (e.g., for online gradient descent, follow-the-leader) directly into an ε-stability rate, matching or improving prior results that assumed Lipschitz D(·).

## Key Results
- The uniform mixture of any no-regret online algorithm’s iterates is ε-performatively stable with ε = Regret(T)/T, without assumptions on D(·).
- For online gradient descent with convex losses and bounded gradients: ε = O(1/√T).
- For follow-the-leader with strongly convex losses: ε = O(log T/T).
- These rates hold even when D(·) is discontinuous or the loss is non-smooth, overcoming limitations of prior work.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Reduction from No‑Regret to Stable Mixtures
The stability gap is expressed as  
(1/T)∑_{t=1}^T (ℓ(z_t;θ_t)−ℓ(z_t;θ⋆)) + (1/T)∑_{t=1}^T V_t,  
where θ⋆ minimizes the expected performative risk under μ, and V_t is a zero‑mean martingale difference. The first term equals the average regret of the online algorithm, which is sublinear by design. The second term vanishes in expectation because (V_t) forms a martingale difference sequence under boundedness and conditional independence of z_t given θ_t. Hence the expected stability gap is at most Regret(T)/T. No continuity of D(·) is invoked.

### Mechanism 2: Martingale Argument for Adaptive Sampling
Define V_t = (E_{z∼D(θ_t)}[ℓ(z;θ_t)−ℓ(z;θ⋆)] − (ℓ(z_t;θ_t)−ℓ(z_t;θ⋆))). Conditional on the past, θ_t is fixed and z_t is drawn independently from D(θ_t), so E[V_t | history] = 0. Bounded loss differences ensure integrability, making (V_t) a martingale difference sequence. Consequently, E[∑_{t=1}^T V_t] = 0, which neutralizes the adaptivity of D(·). This is a standard online‑to‑batch conversion adapted to the performative setting.

### Mechanism 3: Translating Regret Bounds into Explicit Rates
Because the reduction is algorithm‑agnostic, any established regret bound from online convex optimization directly yields an ε‑stability rate. For instance:
- Follow‑the‑leader with γ‑strong convexity: regret ≤ (G²/(2γ))(log T+1) → ε = O(log T/T).
- Online gradient descent for convex losses: regret ≤ (3/2)GD√T → ε = O(1/√T).
- Online Newton step for α‑exp‑concave losses: regret ≤ 2(1/α+GD)d log T → ε = O(d log T/T).
These match or improve upon previous results that required D(·) to be Lipschitz.

## Foundational Learning
**Performative Prediction**: Studies settings where the data distribution D depends on the deployed model θ. Why needed here: The entire problem is framed within performativity, so understanding performative risk and stability is essential. Quick check: What is the difference between the performative risk PR(θ)=E_{z∼D(θ)}ℓ(z;θ) and the decoupled performative risk DPR(θ,D(θ′))=E_{z∼D(θ′)}ℓ(z;θ)? Why does the dependence on D(·) matter for stability?
Answer: PR(θ) measures expected loss under the distribution induced by deploying θ (i.e., D(θ)), capturing the feedback loop. DPR(θ, D(θ′)) evaluates θ on a fixed distribution without deployment effects. The dependence on D(·) creates a coupling between model and data-generating process; stability requires that performance not degrade when the model is deployed relative to the best model under the induced mixture.

**No‑Regret Learning in Online Convex Optimization**: An online algorithm selects a model θ_t each round and suffers loss ℓ(z_t,θ_t). Regret measures cumulative loss minus that of the best fixed θ in hindsight. Why needed: The main reduction transforms stability into a no‑regret guarantee. Quick check: Define external regret of an online algorithm over T rounds. Give one algorithm (e.g., online gradient descent) and its regret bound for convex losses with bounded gradients.
Answer: The external regret over T rounds is Regret(T) = ∑_{t=1}^T ℓ(z_t;θ_t) - min_{θ∈Θ} ∑_{t=1}^T ℓ(z_t;θ). One algorithm is online gradient descent (OGD) with step size η_t = D/(G√t), where D is the diameter of Θ and G bounds the gradient norm ∥∇ℓ(·)∥. For convex losses with bounded gradients, OGD achieves Regret(T) ≤ (3/2)GD√T.

**Martingale Theory (Martingale Difference Sequences)**: A sequence (M_t) where E[M_t | F_{t-1}] = 0 is a martingale difference; its sum has expectation zero. Why needed: The proof that the mixture is stable hinges on showing that the sum of V_t (above) is a martingale difference, so its expectation cancels despite adaptive sampling. Quick check: If (M_t) is a martingale difference sequence with |M_t| ≤ B almost surely, what is E[∑_{t=1}^T M_t]? How does this property enable the proof of Theorem 3?
Answer: E[∑_{t=1}^T M_t] = 0 because expectation of each M_t is zero. In the stability proof, V_t forms such a sequence under boundedness and conditional independence. Hence the martingale sum has zero mean, so the expected stability gap equals the expected average regret term, i.e., E[ε] ≤ E[Regret(T)]/T. This holds even though the sampling distribution D(θ_t) adapts to the algorithm.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Online learner → Distribution oracle (D(θ)) → Loss oracle (ℓ(z,θ)) → Online learner (update). After T steps: all iterates {θ_t} → Mixture builder (uniform μ) → Stability estimator (computes ε and estimates stability gap).

Critical path:
1. Choose an online algorithm A with a known regret bound for the loss family.
2. Initialize θ₁.
3. For t = 1,…,T: deploy θ_t, draw z_t ∼ D(θ_t), compute loss ℓ(z_t,θ_t), update A to get θ_{t+1}.
4. Build μ = uniform(θ_1,…,θ_T).
5. Theoretical guarantee: ε = Regret(T)/T. Optionally estimate the stability gap via Monte‑Carlo sampling.
6. If ε is below tolerance, stop; else increase T or switch algorithm.

Design tradeoffs:
- Algorithm: OGD yields O(1/√T) for convex ℓ; FTL gives O(log T/T) but requires strong convexity; ONS gives O(d log T/T) for exp‑concave ℓ but needs second‑order information.
- Horizon T: larger T reduces ε but increases cost; diminishing returns due to logarithmic factors.
- Mixture vs single model: The reduction requires the uniform mixture; a single deterministic model may be unstable when D is discontinuous.
- Step‑size tuning: Schedules like η_t = D/(G√t) are necessary to achieve advertised regret; mis‑tuning can cause linear regret.

Failure signatures:
- **Regret not sublinear**: Empirical average regret per round does not decrease; ε plateaus.
- **Unbounded losses**: If ℓ can take extreme values, martingale argument fails; enforce boundedness or truncation.
- **Stateful D**: If D depends on past θ’s, conditional independence breaks; dynamic regret may be needed.
- **Implementation bugs**: Incorrect sampling from D, wrong update rule, non‑uniform mixture, omitted projection.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Discontinuous D (Example 1)**: Use squared loss and follow‑the‑leader on the binary switch distribution. For T=1000,10000, compute exact Regret(T) and estimate stability gap of μ. Verify gap ≤ Regret(T)/T and both decay as O(1/T). Observe that individual iterates oscillate between 0 and 1, while μ converges.
2. **Convex non‑strongly convex case**: ℓ(z;θ)=|z−θ| with z∼Uniform[θ−1, θ+1]. Run online gradient descent. Show ε ~ O(1/√T) and note that the final iterate drifts, whereas the mixture stabilizes.
3. **Exp‑concave high‑dimensional case**: Logistic loss on linearly separable data; D(θ)=Normal(Aθ,I) with random A∈ℝ^{d×d}. Use online Newton step. Confirm ε ~ O(d log T / T) and compare to OGD’s slower rate.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Under which structural conditions on D(·) do performatively stable mixtures also achieve performative optimality?
2. In multiplayer performative prediction, if each agent deploys a no‑regret algorithm and randomizes over their models, does the joint outcome constitute a stable equilibrium?
3. Does the no‑regret to stability reduction extend to stateful performative prediction, where D depends on the full history of deployed models, and can dynamic regret guarantees ensure stability?
4. Can "lazy" online algorithms—those that update models infrequently—achieve performative stability while reducing the number of distinct deployed models?

## Limitations
- The proof requires bounded loss differences and conditional independence of samples; unbounded losses or stateful D(·) break the martingale cancellation.
- The guarantee applies only to the uniform mixture of iterates, not necessarily to a single deployed model; for point‑decision applications, the mixture may be less practical.
- The achieved ε depends on the online algorithm achieving sublinear regret; hyperparameter mis‑tuning can lead to linear regret and non‑vanishing ε.
