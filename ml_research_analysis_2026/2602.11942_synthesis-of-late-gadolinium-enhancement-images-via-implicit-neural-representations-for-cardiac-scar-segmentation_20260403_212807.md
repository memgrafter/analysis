# Synthesis of Late Gadolinium Enhancement Images via Implicit Neural Representations for Cardiac Scar Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.11942
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11942
- **Reference count**: 18
- **Primary result**: Augmenting nnU-Net training with 200 synthetic LGE volumes improved fibrosis segmentation Dice score from 0.509 to 0.524 (+9.5% on apical slices) on 133 cardiac MRI scans.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the scarcity of annotated late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) cardiac MRI data for automatic scar segmentation. It introduces a framework that synthesizes paired LGE images and segmentation masks via implicit neural representations (INRs) compressed into a latent space (INR2VEC) and generated using diffusion models. Training a standard nnU-Net on real data augmented with 200 synthetic volumes yields a modest but consistent improvement in fibrosis segmentation Dice (0.509 → 0.524), with particularly strong gains in apical slices. The work demonstrates the potential of INR-based synthesis for medical image augmentation, though with limitations in reproducibility and quantitative plausibility assessment.

## Method Summary
The method comprises four stages: (1) A joint SIREN-based implicit neural representation is trained to map 3D coordinates to image intensity and two binary masks (myocardium, fibrosis) for each real volume, using a composite loss (L2 for image, BCE for masks). The shared backbone enforces spatiotemporal consistency between image and masks. (2) Trained INR parameters (~260K dimensions) are compressed into 512-dimensional latent vectors by an autoencoder (INR2VEC). (3) An Elucidated Diffusion Model (EDM) learns the distribution of these latent codes. (4) New synthetic volumes are generated by sampling a latent code from the diffusion model, decoding it to INR weights, and querying the INR on a grid to produce paired image-mask data. These synthetic volumes augment the training set for a nnU-Net segmentation model, which is then evaluated on held-out real scans.

## Key Results
- Fibrosis segmentation Dice improved from 0.509 (real-only) to 0.524 when adding 200 synthetic volumes.
- Relative Dice improvement of 9.5% observed on apical slices.
- Generated LGE images and masks were qualitatively anatomically plausible, with satisfactory mask-image alignment (PSNR/SSIM reported).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Joint INR training preserves anatomical consistency between LGE images and masks by encoding them as a single continuous function.
- **Mechanism**: A multi-task SIREN network with a shared backbone and separate heads for image intensity and binary masks is optimized with composite loss (L2 for image, BCE for masks). This forces the shared feature space to encode common anatomical structures, ensuring that spatial variations in the mask correspond to coherent intensity patterns.
- **Core assumption**: Spatial correspondence between myocardium/fibrosis boundaries and image gradients is strong enough for a shared representation to learn their coupling without explicit spatial regularization.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract] "Our approach first trains INRs to capture continuous spatial representations... enables implicit weight-sharing for anatomical structures."
  - [section 3.1] "We model LGE images and their segmentation masks jointly within a single implicit neural representation framework... leverages anatomical correlations through a shared backbone."
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors show similar joint synthesis approaches.
- **Break condition**: If the shared backbone fails to sufficiently constrain the joint representation, generated masks may be anatomically plausible but misaligned with image features (e.g., fibrosis placed in blood pool). The paper notes "simplified texture patterns in regions outside the myocardium" suggesting outside-mask regions are less constrained.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Latent compression via INR2VEC creates a low-dimensional, manipulable representation that retains essential anatomical variation.
- **Mechanism**: A shallow encoder with pooling reduces flattened INR weights (~260K) to a 512-D latent vector; a decoder reconstructs INR parameters, enabling operations in latent space.
- **Core assumption**: The manifold of anatomically plausible INR parameters is low-dimensional and can be captured by autoencoder compression without significant fidelity loss.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract] "These INRs are then compressed into compact latent embeddings, preserving essential anatomical information."
  - [section 3.2] "The encoder consists of 4 linear layers... reducing dimensionality from∼260K parameters to a 512-dimensional latent vector while preserving essential anatomical information."
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "LGESynthNet" use latent diffusion for LGE synthesis.
- **Break condition**: Over-compression or poor encoder training could collapse anatomical diversity, leading to unrealistic shape variations.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Latent diffusion models generate novel, diverse INR latent codes that decode to anatomically consistent image-mask pairs, augmenting training data.
- **Mechanism**: EDM operates on the 512-D latent vectors, learning a denoising process that models the distribution of real latent codes. Sampling yields new z, which are decoded to INRs and queried to produce (I, M1, M2).
- **Core assumption**: The latent space formed by INR2VEC is smooth and semantically structured such that diffusion can interpolate meaningfully between real codes while staying within plausible anatomical manifold.
- **Evidence anchors**: 
  - [abstract] "A diffusion model operates on this latent space to generate new representations, which are decoded into synthetic LGE images with anatomically consistent segmentation masks."
  - [section 3.3] describes diffusion pipeline.
  - [section 4.1] shows "anatomically plausible cardiac structures" and "fibrosis regions... generated at anatomically realistic locations."
- **Break condition**: Latent space holes or non-smoothness could cause diffusion to sample invalid codes producing incoherent outputs (e.g., fibrosis in impossible locations). Not explicitly measured.

## Foundational Learning
- **Implicit Neural Representations (INRs)**: Continuous, coordinate-based function approximators that model complex 3D structures at arbitrary resolution without grid limitations, crucial for capturing fine scar boundaries. *Why needed*: Discretized grids limit resolution and continuity; INRs provide smooth representations. *Quick check*: Why might a multi-layer perceptron with ReLU fail to capture high-frequency details compared to SIREN with periodic activations? (Answer: ReLU exhibits spectral bias, requiring many layers to approximate high-frequency functions; SIREN's sine activations naturally represent high-frequency content.)

- **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs)**: Generative models that learn to denoise data from random noise, avoiding mode collapse. *Why needed*: Used here to generate diverse latent codes for INR, providing a richer set of synthetic anatomies than GANs. *Quick check*: How does Elucidated Diffusion Model (EDM) differ from standard DDPM in noise schedule and training, and why might this matter for medical latent spaces? (Answer: EDM employs a continuous‑time formulation with variance‑preserving diffusion and a different noise scaling, improving sample quality and training stability, which is beneficial for complex medical latent manifolds.)

- **nnU-Net**: A self-configuring segmentation framework that automatically adapts to dataset properties. *Why needed*: Serves as a robust, state-of-the-art benchmark to evaluate whether synthetic data improves real-world segmentation. *Quick check*: What are key adaptive design principles of nnU-Net (e.g., preset configuration, cascade, deep supervision) that make it suitable for varied medical imaging tasks? (Answer: It automatically selects architecture (3D U‑Net with residual blocks), resolution strategy, data augmentation, and uses deep supervision and a cascaded approach to handle diverse datasets.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Real volumes (I, M1, M2) → Joint INR (SIREN) → INR parameters → INR2VEC Encoder → Latent vectors → EDM (training/sampling) → INR2VEC Decoder → INR parameters → Query grid → Synthetic (I, M1, M2). Synthetic data + real data → nnU-Net → Segmentation predictions on test scans.

### Critical path
1. Train joint INR on real (image, mask) pairs.
2. Fit INR2VEC autoencoder on INR parameters to obtain latent vectors.
3. Train EDM on the latent vectors.
4. Generate synthetic volumes by sampling latents, decoding to INR, and querying.
5. Augment real training data with synthetic volumes.
6. Train nnU-Net on augmented set; evaluate on held-out real scans.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Fidelity vs. Diversity**: Strong INR reconstruction loss ensures masks align with images but may reduce diversity; diffusion introduces diversity but risks anatomical implausibility if latent space is poorly structured.
- **Compression ratio**: 512-D captures anatomy but may be too small for subtle scar texture; higher dimension increases diffusion computational cost.
- **Mask granularity**: Binary masks simplify training but discard uncertainty; may limit representation of diffuse fibrosis.

### Failure signatures
- Anatomical inconsistency: Synthesized myocardium has discontinuous walls across slices or unrealistic volumes (e.g., holes, extreme thickness variation).
- Mask-image misalignment: Fibrosis mask overlaps blood pool or epicardium, or appears without corresponding intensity change.
- Degenerated diversity: All synthetic volumes show near-identical scar patterns (mode collapse in latent space).
- Segmentation degradation: nnU-Net performance drops on real data when synthetic data is too unrealistic (observed for myocardium when adding 200 samples).

### First 3 experiments
1. Reproduce INR training: Train the joint SIREN on 2–3 real volumes; verify it reconstructs masks (Dice > 0.9 on training coordinates) and that myocardium mask boundaries align with image gradients.
2. Latent space sanity check: Encode 20 real INR weight sets; reduce to 2D with PCA or t-SNE. Check clustering by patient characteristics (e.g., scar presence/location) to see if latent codes capture meaningful anatomical variation.
3. Synthetic quality control: Generate 5 synthetic volumes; have a clinician inspect for: (a) continuous myocardium across slices, (b) fibrosis only within myocardium, (c) plausible scar heterogeneity. Compute mask Dice between two independently generated synthetics (should be low, indicating diversity).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does adversarial training improve boundary sharpness in synthetic LGE images? (Suggested as future work.)
2. Can conditioning the diffusion process on clinical parameters yield more targeted fibrosis synthesis? (Unresolved.)
3. Why does synthetic data augmentation improve fibrosis segmentation but not myocardium segmentation? (Asymmetry unexplained.)
4. Do task‑specialized segmentation architectures better leverage synthetic LGE data than generic nnU‑Net? (Needs evaluation.)
5. How much manual annotation effort can be saved in low‑data regimes by synthetic augmentation, and what are the diminishing returns? (Systematic low-data tests proposed.)

## Limitations
- Architectural details for INR2VEC and EDM are under‑specified, hindering exact replication.
- Quantitative evaluation of anatomical plausibility (mask‑image alignment, slice continuity) is lacking; reliance on qualitative assessment.
- Segmentation improvement is modest (Dice +0.015) and inconsistent (myocardium segmentation degraded with augmentation).

## Confidence
- Joint INR preserves anatomical consistency: Medium
- INR2VEC retains essential variation: Medium
- Latent diffusion generates novel, diverse pairs: Medium
- Augmentation improves fibrosis segmentation: Medium
