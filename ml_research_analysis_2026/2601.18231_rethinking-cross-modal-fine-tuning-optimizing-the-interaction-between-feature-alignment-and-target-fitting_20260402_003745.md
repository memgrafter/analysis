# Rethinking Cross-Modal Fine-Tuning: Optimizing the Interaction between Feature Alignment and Target Fitting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.18231
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18231
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: RECRAFT outperforms SOTA on 15/18 tasks (avg rank 1.3 on NAS-Bench-360, 1.25 on PDEBench) by jointly minimizing feature alignment and feature-label distortion.

## Executive Summary
Cross-modal fine-tuning adapts pre-trained models to novel data modalities but lacks theoretical guidance on how to balance feature alignment (distribution matching) with target fitting (task-specific learning). This paper derives a generalization bound that decomposes the target error into four components: source error, feature alignment, feature-label distortion (measuring semantic gap), and target fitting. The bound shows that minimizing feature alignment alone does not guarantee good generalization; controlling feature-label distortion is equally essential. Guided by this insight, the authors propose RECRAFT, a two-stage algorithm. Stage 1 learns a target feature map that simultaneously minimizes alignment (e.g., distribution discrepancy) and distortion (using pseudo-labels to preserve semantics). Stage 2 then learns the predictor on the fixed feature map to minimize target fitting. Empirical evaluations on NAS-Bench-360 (10 tasks) and PDEBench (8 tasks) demonstrate that RECRAFT consistently surpasses state-of-the-art methods ORCA, PARE, and MoNA, achieving the best performance on 8/10 tasks (average rank 1.3) in NAS-Bench-360 and 7/8 tasks (average rank 1.25) in PDEBench.

## Method Summary
RECRAFT operationalizes the theoretical bound through a decoupled two-stage optimization. Given a pre-trained source model, the algorithm first generates pseudo-labels for the target data using the source classifier. It then learns a neural feature map that transforms target inputs into a feature space where two objectives are minimized: (i) alignment loss, which reduces the discrepancy between the transformed target features and the source features (e.g., using a Wasserstein distance), and (ii) distortion loss, which ensures that the transformed features retain semantic information by requiring a classifier (trained on the same pseudo-labels) to achieve high accuracy. The two losses are combined with a trade-off parameter λ. After the feature map converges, it is frozen. In the second stage, a task-specific predictor (e.g., a linear head) is trained on the frozen transformed features using the available target labels (or pseudo-labels if necessary) to minimize the final task loss. This separation avoids gradient conflicts between alignment and distortion and yields stable convergence and strong empirical results.

## Key Results
- RECRAFT achieves state-of-the-art performance on 15 of 18 tasks across NAS-Bench-360 and PDEBench, with average ranks of 1.3 and 1.25 respectively, significantly outperforming ORCA, PARE, and MoNA.
- The generalization bound reveals that both feature alignment and feature-label distortion must be optimized; focusing on alignment alone is insufficient.
- The two-stage optimization strategy leads to stable training and superior adaptation compared to joint optimization attempts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The derived bound indicates that the total target error is the sum of source error (beyond control), feature alignment mismatch, feature-label distortion (semantic gap), and target fitting. By explicitly minimizing both alignment and distortion in the first stage, RECRAFT constructs a feature space that is statistically close to the source domain while preserving the semantic structure of the target task. This reduces the irreducible parts of the bound, leaving only the target fitting error to be minimized in the second stage. The decoupled approach prevents competing gradients that would arise if all terms were optimized jointly, leading to more reliable convergence and better final performance.

## Foundational Learning

- **Feature Alignment**  
  Why needed: Narrows the domain shift between source and target modalities, a prerequisite for effective knowledge transfer.  
  Quick check: Compute a distribution similarity metric (e.g., Wasserstein distance, MMD) between source and target features; it should decrease during Stage 1.

- **Feature-Label Distortion**  
  Why needed: Quantifies the semantic degradation caused by feature transformation; alignment without semantic preservation harms task performance.  
  Quick check: Train a simple classifier on the transformed target features using pseudo-labels; distortion decreases as classification accuracy increases.

- **Target Fitting**  
  Why needed: Directly optimizes the final task objective on the target domain; the ultimate goal of fine-tuning.  
  Quick check: Monitor the task-specific loss (e.g., cross-entropy, MSE) on a validation set of target data during Stage 2.

- **Two-Stage Optimization**  
  Why needed: Separates potentially conflicting objectives (alignment vs. distortion) to ensure stable gradient dynamics and convergence.  
  Quick check: Compare loss trajectories of joint vs. two-stage optimization; two-stage should exhibit smoother descent and fewer oscillations.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**  
Pre-trained Source Encoder → Source Features  
Target Raw Inputs → Pseudo-Label Generator → Pseudo-Labels  
Source Features, Target Raw Inputs, Pseudo-Labels → Alignment & Distortion Module → Target Feature Map (f_t)  
Target Feature Map + (Target Labels or Pseudo-Labels) → Target Predictor Module → Final Model (f_t + g)

**Critical Path:**  
1. Extract source features using the frozen pre-trained encoder.  
2. Generate pseudo-labels for target data by feeding it through the source classifier.  
3. Train the target feature map (f_t) to minimize alignment loss (between f_t(target) and source features) and distortion loss (pseudo-label classification on f_t(target)).  
4. Freeze f_t.  
5. Train the target predictor (g) on f_t(target) to minimize the task loss.  
6. Combine f_t and g for inference.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- λ trade‑off: Higher λ emphasizes alignment (risk of semantic loss); lower λ emphasizes distortion (risk of misalignment).  
- Metric choice: Using Euclidean distance for alignment is analytically convenient but may be less expressive than a learned Wasserstein metric.  
- Two‑stage vs. joint: Joint optimization could, in principle, minimize the bound more directly but is prone to instability; two‑stage sacrifices theoretical optimality for robustness.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Feature collapse: Alignment loss → 0 while distortion loss remains high → feature map aligned but semantically meaningless → poor target performance.  
- Pseudo‑label noise amplification: Inaccurate pseudo‑labels yield misleading distortion gradients, causing the feature map to learn misaligned representations.  
- Overfitting to small target sets: If λ is too low, the feature map overfits the target distribution without preserving semantic correspondence with the source.  
- Instability in joint training: Divergence or oscillating losses when attempting to optimize all terms simultaneously.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Alignment & Distortion Monitoring** – Track alignment loss and distortion (pseudo‑label classification accuracy) over Stage 1 for various λ values to observe the trade‑off.  
2. **Ablation of Two‑Stage Design** – Implement a joint optimization version that combines Stage 1 and Stage 2 losses; compare convergence stability and final accuracy against the two‑stage baseline.  
3. **Pseudo‑Label Quality Impact** – Replace pseudo‑labels with ground‑truth target labels (where available) for distortion supervision and measure performance change; this isolates the effect of pseudo‑label noise.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does learning the Wasserstein cost metric (instead of Euclidean) yield a tighter generalization bound and better cross-modal transfer?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 6 identifies the Euclidean metric limitation and proposes metric learning as future work.
- Why unresolved: The fixed Euclidean metric may not optimally tighten the bound for all modality pairs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Learn δ as a parameter, compute empirical bound tightness and target error across multiple datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the gap between true FLD and its conditional entropy approximation be made vanishingly small with more pseudo-labels?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 6 explicitly questions whether the FLD bound gap vanishes as pseudo-label samples increase.
- Why unresolved: The sample complexity of the pseudo-label approximation remains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematically vary pseudo-label count, measure bound gap and its correlation with target error.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does RECRAFT's performance advantage generalize to much larger foundation models (e.g., >1B parameters)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Appendix K notes current evaluation on ~100M parameter models and suggests scaling as future direction.
- Why unresolved: Larger models may exhibit different representation geometries affecting feature alignment and distortion.
- What evidence would resolve it: Apply RECRAFT to state-of-the-art large FMs (e.g., GPT-4, ViT-Huge) on cross-modal benchmarks, comparing against fine-tuning.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Is the two-stage optimization (feature map then predictor) necessary, or can joint minimization of the bound terms achieve better performance with stable optimization?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Section 3 adopts two-stage to avoid instability of joint minimization; optimality is not analyzed.
- Why unresolved: The bound terms are coupled; decoupling may be suboptimal.
- What evidence would resolve it: Design a stable joint optimization scheme, compare final error and convergence against the two-stage approach.

## Limitations
- **Benchmark diversity:** Evaluation is limited to NAS-Bench-360 and PDEBench; it is unclear whether results generalize to other cross-modal scenarios (e.g., image-text, audio-visual).
- **Theoretical assumptions:** The bound relies on standard assumptions (bounded loss, Rademacher complexity) that may not hold for deep networks; the tightness of the bound is not empirically quantified.
- **Distortion proxy:** Feature‑label distortion is approximated via pseudo‑label classifier accuracy, a heuristic that may not consistently capture semantic gaps across all modalities.

## Confidence
- Generalization bound decomposition: High
- Feature alignment alone insufficient: High
- RECRAFT effectiveness: High
- Two‑stage optimization necessity: Medium
