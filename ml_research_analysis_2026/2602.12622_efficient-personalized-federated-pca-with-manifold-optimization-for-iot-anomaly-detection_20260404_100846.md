# Efficient Personalized Federated PCA with Manifold Optimization for IoT Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12622
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12622
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** FedEP consistently outperforms FedPG in accuracy and F1-score on IoT anomaly detection datasets, e.g., 83.31% accuracy and 88.97% F1-score on UNSW-NB15, with up to 2× computational speedup on NSL-KDD.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents FedEP, a personalized federated principal component analysis (PCA) method tailored for anomaly detection in IoT networks. Standard federated PCA struggles with non-IID data across devices, leading to suboptimal global models. FedEP introduces two complementary sparsity penalties—element-wise ℓ1-norm and row-wise ℓ2,1-norm—applied to local projection matrices and an auxiliary error term, respectively. The ℓ1 sparsity enables local feature selection (personalization), while the ℓ2,1 sparsity discards entire corrupted features (robustness). Optimization is performed on the Stiefel manifold via an ADMM algorithm with convergence guarantees.
Extensive experiments on three IoT datasets (TON-IoT, UNSW-NB15, NSL-KDD) demonstrate that FedEP achieves superior accuracy and F1-score compared to the baseline FedPG. For instance, on UNSW-NB15 it reaches 83.31% accuracy and 88.97% F1-score, versus 80.93% and 87.20% for FedPG. Ablation studies confirm the contribution of both sparsity components. FedEP also yields sparser projections and up to 2× speedup on NSL-KDD, highlighting its efficiency for resource-constrained IoT deployments.

## Method Summary
FedEP formulates federated PCA as an optimization problem where each client i learns a local projection matrix \(W_i\) under orthonormality constraint (\(W_i^\top W_i = I\)) to minimize reconstruction error of its data \(X_i\) after subtracting an anomaly residual \(S_i\). The objective adds an ℓ1 penalty on \(S_i\) and an ℓ2,1 penalty on \(W_i\), along with a consensus constraint \(W_i = V\) linking local and global models. The resulting non-convex, non-smooth problem is solved via an ADMM framework that introduces auxiliary variables \(U_i\) and Lagrange multipliers.
The algorithm proceeds in alternating steps: each client updates \(W_i\) using a manifold proximal gradient method (with a semi-smooth Newton direction and retraction), updates \(S_i\) via soft-thresholding, and updates \(U_i\) by a closed-form Sherman-Morrison solution; the server aggregates the updated \(W_i\) to compute the consensus variable \(V\); and multipliers are updated accordingly. The augmented Lagrangian is guaranteed to be non-increasing under mild conditions.

## Key Results
- **Performance:** Outperforms FedPG across TON-IoT, UNSW-NB15, and NSL-KDD; e.g., on UNSW-NB15 accuracy 83.31% vs 80.93% and F1-score 88.97% vs 87.20%.
- **Sparsity & Efficiency:** Produces sparser projection matrices and achieves up to 2× computational speedup on NSL-KDD.
- **Ablation:** Studies show both ℓ1 (element-wise) and ℓ2,1 (row-wise) sparsity components are necessary for optimal results.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Personalized local modeling via element-wise ℓ1 sparsity
- **Claim:** Personalized local modeling is achieved via element-wise ℓ1-norm sparsity on the local projection matrix \(W_i\), allowing each IoT client to adapt to its unique data distribution.
- **Mechanism:** The ℓ1 penalty induces sparsity at the individual coefficient level in \(W_i\), effectively performing feature selection pertinent to the local device's traffic patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Non-IID IoT data contains device-specific salient features; a one-size-fits-all global projection matrix is suboptimal.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract states: "achieves personalization through introducing local representations with the ℓ1-norm for element-wise sparsity."
  - Section III.A discusses the ℓ1-norm for filtering fine-grained noise, but the formulation (6) includes \( \beta \|W_i\|_{2,1} \). *Note: There is ambiguity: the ℓ2,1 norm is applied to \(W_i\) for row-wise sparsity, while the ℓ1 norm may be used on an auxiliary variable \(S_i\). The personalization is primarily attributed to the existence of local \(W_i\) rather than strict global consensus.*
  - Literature on sparse PCA supports element-wise sparsity for feature selection.
- **Break condition:** If all local data distributions are statistically identical (IID), the personalization benefit diminishes, and the ℓ1 penalty may unnecessarily remove globally useful features.

### Mechanism 2: Robustness via row-wise ℓ2,1 sparsity
- **Claim:** Robustness to noise and outliers is achieved via row-wise ℓ2,1-norm sparsity on the local projection matrix \(W_i\), which zeros out entire rows (features).
- **Mechanism:** The ℓ2,1 norm sums the ℓ2 norms of rows of \(W_i\). Setting an entire row to zero discards all loadings for that original feature, robustly eliminating features corrupted by noise/outliers across all samples locally.
- **Core assumption:** Noise/outliers affect entire feature dimensions (rows) in local IoT data matrices; eliminating corrupted features improves subspace estimation.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract states: "maintaining robustness via enforcing local models with the ℓ2,1-norm for row-wise sparsity."
  - Section II.B and III.A explicitly link ℓ2,1-norm to structural robustness and resilience to corrupted features.
  - Neighboring works on robust PCA also use ℓ2,1 sparsity.
- **Break condition:** If noise is purely element-wise (affecting individual samples, not entire features), ℓ2,1 may be less effective than an ℓ1 penalty on an error matrix.

### Mechanism 3: Efficient manifold ADMM optimization
- **Claim:** The non-convex, constrained optimization problem is solved efficiently and with convergence guarantees via a manifold ADMM algorithm that alternates between local manifold gradient steps and global variable aggregation.
- **Mechanism:** The Stiefel manifold constraint \(W_i^\top W_i = I\) is handled via retraction operations. ADMM decomposes the problem into local subproblems (for \(W_i, S_i, U_i\)) and a global subproblem (for \(V\)), with Lagrange multipliers synchronizing consensus. The local \(W_i\) update uses a manifold proximal gradient method with a semi-smooth Newton step for the descent direction.
- **Core assumption:** The ADMM splitting and manifold optimization procedures can reach a stationary point; subproblems admit efficient closed-form or fast solvers.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract mentions "optimized via a manifold ADMM algorithm with convergence guarantees."
  - Section III.B details Algorithm 1 and the semi-smooth Newton (SSN) method for \(W_i\) updates.
  - Section III.C provides Theorem 3.1 proving the augmented Lagrangian sequence is non-increasing.
  - Prior work on Riemannian ADMM exists, but the paper notes its assumptions do not directly apply; thus the combination is novel.
- **Break condition:** Poor choices of penalty parameters (\(\mu, \nu\)) or step size may cause slow convergence or practical failure despite the theoretical guarantee (which only ensures non-increasing augmented Lagrangian, not full parameter convergence).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Local Client (per IoT gateway) → Global Server → Local Client. Clients hold local data \(X_i\), compute updates for \(W_i, S_i, U_i\), maintain local multipliers \(\Lambda_i, \Pi_i\), and send \(W_i\) to server. Server maintains consensus variable \(V\), aggregates \(W_i\) to update \(V\), broadcasts \(V\) and \(\Pi_i/\nu\) to clients.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Server initializes \(V^0\).
  2. **Local Loop (per client):** Receive \(V^k, \Pi_i^k\). Compute \(Z_i^k = V^k - \Pi_i^k/\nu\). Update \(W_i\) via manifold proximal gradient (SSN descent direction, retraction with line search). Update \(S_i\) via soft-thresholding (19). Update \(U_i\) via closed-form (22). Update multipliers \(\Lambda_i, \Pi_i\).
  3. **Global Aggregation:** Server averages \(\{W_i^{k+1}\}\) to obtain \(V^{k+1}\).
  4. Repeat local loop and aggregation until convergence (augmented Lagrangian decrease below tolerance or max iterations reached).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Personalization vs. Consensus:* Looser consensus (personalized \(W_i\)) adapts to non-IID data but may lose shared global structure. The paper retains \(W_i=V\) consensus but achieves personalization via sparsity patterns.
  - *Computation vs. Communication:* Heavy local \(W_i\) update (manifold optimization) vs. cheap communication (only \(W_i\) and \(V\)).
  - *Sparsity vs. Information Loss:* Higher penalties \(\alpha,\beta\) increase sparsity (robustness, interpretability) but risk removing useful signal.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Non-convergence of \(W_i\) update:** SSN inner loop fails; check line search and initial point.
  - **Poor sparsity:** \(\|W_i\|_{2,1}\) or \(\|S_i\|_1\) remains high; verify \(\alpha,\beta\) and proximal operators.
  - **Divergent multipliers:** \(\|\Lambda_i\|, \|\Pi_i\|\) grow; reduce \(\mu,\nu\).
  - **No performance gain over FedPG:** Ablation shows both sparsity terms needed; likely hyperparameter mismatch or data where one sparsity harms.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation Validation:** Run the four cases (I: no sparsity; II: only ℓ1 on \(S_i\); III: only ℓ2,1 on \(W_i\); IV: full FedEP) on UNSW-NB15. Confirm Case IV outperforms others, proving both sparsity contributions.
  2. **Manifold ADMM Convergence:** Track augmented Lagrangian per outer iteration; ensure non-increasing per Theorem 3.1. Also monitor primal residuals \(\|X_i - S_i - U_i\|_F\) and \(\|W_i - V\|_F\) dropping below tolerance \(\varepsilon\).
  3. **Sparsity Visualization:** Compute feature importance \(\sum_j |W_i^{(j)}|\) per client and plot. Verify FedEP yields heavy-tailed distribution (few features dominant, many near zero) contrasting FedPG's flatter distribution, demonstrating ℓ2,1's row-wise sparsity effect.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to nonlinear models:** The paper suggests exploring kernel-based techniques or deep learning to handle nonlinear relationships while maintaining personalization, robustness, and efficiency. Nonlinear extensions may break convergence guarantees or exceed IoT resource limits.
2. **Compression of personalized models:** Personalized models may increase heterogeneity, making compression challenging. Investigating pruning, quantization, or distillation to reduce communication overhead without sacrificing detection performance is needed.
3. **Convergence analysis for non-convex, non-smooth objective:** The current guarantee (nonincreasing augmented Lagrangian) is limited; a rigorous analysis for ADMM on the Stiefel manifold with non-smooth regularizers (ℓ1, ℓ2,1) is required to understand reliability and parameter selection.
4. **Robustness to extreme non-IID and temporal drift:** The personalization mechanism's effectiveness under severe distribution shifts or evolving attack patterns is unclear. Evaluating FedEP under synthetic non-IID splits with varying Dirichlet concentrations and simulating concept drift would test its adaptability.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter values (α, β, µ, ν) and their optimal ranges per dataset are not fully reported; only "grid search" is mentioned.
- Exact client splitting protocol for creating 20 non-IID subsets from the 'dst_bytes' feature is unspecified, hindering reproducibility of the non-IID setting.
- Convergence guarantee is limited to a non-increasing augmented Lagrangian sequence; full convergence to a stationary point for the non-convex, non-smooth problem is not proven.
- Baselines are restricted to FedPG; no comparison with other personalized or robust federated PCA methods.
- Reported speedup (up to 2× on NSL-KDD) lacks hardware and implementation details.

## Confidence
- ℓ1 element-wise sparsity for personalization: **Low** (ambiguity: ℓ1 may actually be applied to Si, not Wi; personalization more due to local Wi existence).
- ℓ2,1 row-wise sparsity for robustness: **Medium** (mechanism clear and literature-backed, but isolated contribution not quantified).
- Manifold ADMM algorithm: **Medium** (theoretical guarantee for augmented Lagrangian decrease, but practical sensitivity and full convergence not validated).
- Performance improvement over FedPG: **Medium** (consistent gains on three datasets, but no statistical significance tests reported).
- Sparsity and speedup claims: **Low** (insufficient details on sparsity metrics and timing methodology).
