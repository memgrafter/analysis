# MoD-DPO: Towards Mitigating Cross-modal Hallucinations in Omni LLMs using Modality Decoupled Preference Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.03192
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03192
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MoD-DPO++ improves accuracy on AVHBench to 88.19% (from 84.15%) and perception accuracy on CMM to 89.2% (from 86.4%), outperforming DPO and OmniDPO baselines.

## Executive Summary
Omni-modal large language models (LLMs) often produce cross-modal hallucinations, generating content inconsistent with the relevant input modality due to spurious correlations and over-reliance on language priors. MoD-DPO addresses this by extending Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with modality-decoupled regularization: it enforces invariance to corruptions in irrelevant modalities and sensitivity to corruptions in relevant ones, and adds a penalty to suppress text-only responses. Using an automatically generated 18k-sample preference dataset with hard negatives that incorporate spurious cross-modal information, MoD-DPO++ (with language-prior debiasing) trains an omni LLM to be more modality-faithful. Evaluations on AVHBench and CMM show significant gains in accuracy and hallucination resistance, demonstrating the method’s effectiveness under comparable training budgets.

## Method Summary
MoD-DPO builds upon DPO by introducing two KL divergence regularizers that explicitly decouple modality-specific reasoning. The invariance term penalizes the KL divergence between the model’s output distribution on clean inputs and on inputs where the irrelevant modality is corrupted, encouraging the prediction to remain unchanged when the irrelevant modality is degraded. The sensitivity term encourages a larger KL divergence when the relevant modality is corrupted, ensuring the model relies on it. Corrupted inputs are processed with gradients stopped to maintain a closed-form DPO derivation. MoD-DPO++ further adds a language-prior debiasing (LPD) penalty, subtracting γ_LPD · log π_ref(y|x) from the reward to discourage responses that could be generated from language alone. The training dataset of 18,112 samples is generated automatically: GPT-4o produces modality-specific queries and hard-negative responses that inject spurious information from the irrelevant modality. The final loss combines the standard DPO term, the invariance and sensitivity KLs, and the LPD penalty. Training alternates between audio-related and video-related prompts, uses a frozen reference model, and employs a corruption module (e.g., diffusion-based with 500 steps) to produce a′ and v′.

## Key Results
- AVHBench: Overall accuracy improves to 88.19% from baseline 84.15% (Qwen 2.5 Omni).
- CMM: Perception accuracy rises to 89.2% from 86.4%, with hallucination resistance also improving.
- The approach outperforms DPO and OmniDPO across multiple audiovisual benchmarks with similar training compute.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MoD-DPO’s effectiveness stems from three synergistic mechanisms:

**1. Modality-decoupled regularization** reduces spurious cross-modal correlations by forcing the model to ignore irrelevant modalities. Two KL divergence terms are added to the DPO loss: an invariance term (β_inv) penalizes the KL between the policy’s distribution on clean inputs and on inputs with the irrelevant modality corrupted; a sensitivity term (β_sens) encourages a larger KL when the relevant modality is corrupted. Corruptions (e.g., noise, random replacement, or diffusion) are applied with gradients stopped. This mechanism assumes that hallucinations arise from relying on spurious correlations; explicit decoupling forces reliance only on the task-relevant modality. Evidence: The paper’s abstract and Section 4.1 derive Eq. (5) and discuss invariance/sensitivity. A break condition: if corruptions are too weak to degrade the irrelevant modality, invariance fails; for tasks requiring both modalities, decoupling harms performance.

**2. Language-prior debiasing** reduces over-reliance on text-only priors by penalizing responses that could be generated from language alone. A penalty term −γ_LPD · log π_ref(y|x) is subtracted from the reward, where π_ref is a frozen reference model. This makes text-only outputs less favorable during preference optimization, pushing the model to incorporate audiovisual evidence. The core assumption is that pretrained omni LLMs have strong language priors that cause them to ignore audiovisual inputs. Evidence: Abstract and Section 4.2 define rLPD and its integration. Break condition: excessive γ_LPD over-penalizes legitimate language reasoning, degrading performance; if π_ref already uses modalities, the penalty may be misapplied.

**3. Hard-negative cross-modal preference data** teaches the model to avoid hallucinations by contrasting modality-grounded and cross-modal responses. In the automatic data pipeline, rejected responses are generated to include incorrect information from the irrelevant modality (e.g., for a visual query, the rejected response wrongly incorporates audio-derived details). This is done via GPT-4o prompts that produce an answer opposite to the ground truth and justify it using the other modality’s caption. The assumption is that hallucinations often involve using the wrong modality; explicit cross-modal hard negatives help the model distinguish correct modality use. Evidence: Section 4.3 and Fig. 4 describe the pipeline. Break condition: if GPT-4o fails to generate plausible cross-modal hallucinations, the preference pairs become uninformative; errors in modality classification can invalidate QA pairs.

## Architecture Onboarding
The MoD-DPO training pipeline involves several interacting components and design choices.

**Component map:** 
Preference dataset provides batches with modality labels (audio-related or video-related) and chosen/rejected response pairs. Based on the label, the modality corruption module generates a corrupted version of the irrelevant modality (for invariance) and the relevant modality (for sensitivity). The policy model (π_θ) processes clean inputs to compute log-probabilities for chosen and rejected responses, and also processes corrupted inputs (with gradients stopped) to compute log-probabilities for KL terms. The reference model (π_ref) processes text-only inputs to compute the language-prior debiasing penalty. The loss module aggregates the DPO loss, the invariance and sensitivity KL terms, and the LPD penalty into L_MoD++ and updates π_θ.

**Critical path:** 
1. Split each batch into audio-related and video-related prompts according to modality labels.
2. For each sample, generate corrupted inputs: if audio-related, corrupt video (irrelevant) and audio (relevant); if video-related, corrupt audio and video respectively.
3. Perform forward pass through π_θ on: (a) clean inputs for chosen/rejected logits (gradients enabled), (b) corrupted inputs for KL terms (gradients stopped).
4. Compute language penalty via π_ref on the text-only prompt.
5. Combine all terms into L_MoD++ and backpropagate to update π_θ.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Corruption method: Diffusion-based corruption (500 steps) yields best performance but is slower; simpler methods like noise or random input are computationally cheaper.
- Hyperparameters: β_sens (0.05–0.1), β_inv (~0.02), γ_LPD (~0.05) require careful tuning; values outside these ranges degrade performance.
- Training budget: MoD-DPO++ achieves comparable or better results with fewer epochs (1 vs. 4) due to the additional forward passes providing richer gradients.
- Data composition: Including a mix of matched and mismatched audiovisual contexts improves robustness to spurious correlations.

**Failure signatures:**
- No improvement on AVHBench or CMM compared to vanilla DPO.
- Performance drops on general multimodal benchmarks (DailyOmni, MVBench, MMAU).
- Training loss diverges or fails to converge.
- The model shows low invariance (remains sensitive to irrelevant modalities) or low sensitivity (insensitive to relevant modalities).
- Audiovisual attention (measured in the paper) does not increase after LPD.

**First 3 experiments to run:**
1. Ablation study: Remove each component (invariance, sensitivity, LPD) individually to verify their individual contributions to performance.
2. Hyperparameter sweep: Vary β_sens, β_inv, γ_LPD across a range to identify optimal values and understand sensitivity (see Fig. 9).
3. Corruption method comparison: Train with different corruption strategies (noise, random, diffusion at varying steps) to validate the effectiveness of strong corruptions (Tab. 4).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper highlights several open questions and directions for future work:

- How should modality corruption be designed to effectively enforce invariance and sensitivity without overly distorting the input? The current implementation uses diffusion with 500 steps, but the optimal corruption strength and type is unclear.
- What is the impact of automatic preference data generation on model performance? The use of GPT-4o to create hard negatives may introduce subtle biases or errors; understanding the robustness of MoD-DPO to dataset noise is an open question.
- Can the modality-decoupled regularization be applied to early-fusion omni models? The method relies on separate modality streams; extending it to architectures with joint representations could broaden its applicability.
- What are the interactions between the KL penalties (β_inv, β_sens) and the language-prior debiasing weight (γ_LPD)? A systematic study of their combined effects across tasks is needed.
- How does MoD-DPO scale with larger omni-LLMs and more diverse multimodal data? The current experiments are limited to a specific model and dataset; scalability and potential performance ceilings remain to be explored.

## Limitations
- Corruption implementation details (e.g., diffusion model parameters, audio corruption steps) are not fully specified, risking inconsistent regularization.
- Automatic preference data generation relies on GPT-4o; sampling parameters and verification retry logic are missing, potentially introducing dataset noise.
- The method assumes separate modality streams and may not transfer to early-fusion omni architectures; optimal mixing ratio for mismatched audiovisual contexts is unknown.

## Confidence
- Modality-decoupled regularization: Medium.
- Language-prior debiasing: Medium.
- Hard-negative cross-modal data: Medium.
- Overall performance gains: High.
