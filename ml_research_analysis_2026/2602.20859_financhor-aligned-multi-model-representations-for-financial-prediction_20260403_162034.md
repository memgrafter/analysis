# FinAnchor: Aligned Multi-Model Representations for Financial Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20859
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20859
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** FinAnchor consistently achieves the highest Accuracy and F1 scores across five financial tasks, outperforming single-model LLM baselines (e.g., Conference Call F1: 0.741 vs. 0.731; Stock Movement F1: 0.693 vs. 0.673).

## Executive Summary
FinAnchor tackles the challenge of financial prediction from long documents by combining heterogeneous large language model (LLM) embeddings without fine-tuning. It selects one LLM as an anchor and learns linear ridge mappings to align other models' representations into the anchor space. The aligned embeddings are aggregated (mean) and fed to a lightweight classifier. Across five diverse financial tasks—earnings call transcripts, 10-Q MD&A, financial news, stock movement, and FOMC policy stance—FinAnchor yields systematic gains in Accuracy and F1 scores. The method achieves high alignment quality (R² > 0.97) and reduces false positives by amplifying shared bearish cues. This demonstrates that simple linear alignment and aggregation can effectively harness complementary signals from multiple LLMs for financial forecasting.

## Method Summary
FinAnchor operates in three stages: alignment, aggregation, and readout. First, an anchor LLM (e.g., Gemma) is chosen. For each source LLM, a ridge regression (regularization α=10) is trained on the training split to map the source’s standardized embeddings to the anchor’s standardized embeddings. The learned linear transformation is applied to validation and test embeddings. Second, the anchor embedding and all aligned source embeddings are combined via element-wise mean (or optionally concatenation) and standardized using training statistics. Third, a multilayer perceptron (three hidden layers of 256 units with dropout 0.5) is trained on these aggregated features to predict the financial outcome. All LLMs remain frozen; no fine-tuning occurs.

## Key Results
- FinAnchor achieves the highest Accuracy and F1 scores on all five tasks, with margins up to +0.02 F1 over the best single-LLM baseline.
- Alignment quality is high: training R² exceeds 0.97 across all source models.
- Pairwise error overlap between single-model readouts is low (0.78–0.84), indicating complementary error patterns.
- FinAnchor notably reduces false positives, converting 71 false positives into true negatives in one analysis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** FinAnchor enables meaningful combination of heterogeneous LLM representations by aligning them into a common anchor space using linear ridge mappings.
- **Mechanism:** Choose one encoder as the anchor. For each source encoder, fit a ridge regression (with L2 regularization) that maps the source’s training embeddings to the anchor’s embeddings. The learned linear map is applied to validation and test embeddings to obtain aligned representations in the anchor coordinate system.
- **Core assumption:** The mapping between different LLM embedding spaces can be approximated by a linear transformation; ridge regularization prevents overfitting on high‑dimensional features.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  • [abstract]: “selects an anchor embedding space and learns linear mappings to align other models' representations into it.”  
  • [section 2]: Full description of “Linear Alignment to an Anchor Space” and Equation 1 (ridge objective).  
  • [corpus]: Corpus of related work shows interest in representation alignment (e.g., “LATTE: Learning Aligned Transactions…”) but does not directly evaluate linear ridge alignment for financial LLMs—this is a novel application.  
- **Break condition:** If the training R² of the ridge fit is low (e.g., < 0.90), the linear approximation is poor, and aligned representations may become incompatible, causing degradation when aggregated.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Aggregating the aligned representations (mean in the anchor space) combines complementary signals from multiple LLMs, yielding higher predictive accuracy than any single model or naïve concatenation.
- **Mechanism:** After alignment, treat the anchor’s own representation and each aligned source representation as views in the same space; compute their element‑wise mean (or optionally concatenate). The aggregated vector is standardized and fed to a lightweight readout classifier.
- **Core assumption:** Different LLMs make non‑identical errors on financial documents; their aligned embeddings therefore provide partially independent evidence that becomes stronger when combined.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  • [abstract]: “aggregating aligned features without fine‑tuning… consistently achieves the highest Accuracy and F1 scores.”  
  • [section 3.3, Table 2]: Pairwise error overlap between single‑model readouts is 0.78–0.84, well below 1, confirming non‑identical error patterns.  
  • [section 3.3, Figure 2]: FinAnchor converts many false positives into true negatives (FP→TN count = 71), demonstrating concrete error correction.  
  • [corpus]: Related works on multi‑modal representation (e.g., “Robust Visual Representation Learning with Multi‑modal Prior Knowledge”) show benefits of fusion, but FinAnchor’s specific linear alignment + mean aggregation is not directly studied.  
- **Break condition:** If error overlap approaches 1 (models make nearly identical mistakes) or alignment quality is poor, aggregation provides little benefit and may even average out useful signals.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** FinAnchor reduces false positive predictions by amplifying shared bearish financial cues (e.g., margin pressure, demand softness) across LLMs in the aligned space, leading to more conservative and accurate forecasts.
- **Mechanism:** Linear alignment rewights input features so that dimensions corresponding to salient bearish phrases—identified as important by multiple source models—receive larger coefficients in the aggregated representation. This is observable via sentence‑level occlusion: removing a bearish sentence changes the up‑logit more in FinAnchor than in a single‑model baseline.
- **Core assumption:** Complementary bearish evidence exists across different LLMs; alignment brings these cues into a common space where their contributions sum constructively.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  • [section 3.3]: “FinAnchor exhibits asymmetric shifts… converts a sizable number of false positives into true negatives (FP→TN: 71).”  
  • [section 3.4, Table 4]: Occlusion study on a corrected false‑positive example shows FinAnchor assigns substantially larger |Δzi| to bearish sentences (e.g., “gross margin rates decreased”) compared to Gemma‑only.  
  • [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence; the corpus contains papers on context compression and embedding interpretation but does not address false‑positive reduction in financial prediction.  
- **Break condition:** If the anchor model is biased toward bullish interpretations or if alignment over‑emphasizes non‑diagnostic features, false positives may not decrease; additionally, in tasks where false negatives are more costly, this shift might be undesirable.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Encoders (multiple frozen LLMs) → Aligners (ridge matrices per source) → Aggregator (mean in anchor space) → Standardization (train stats) → Readout (MLP classifier)

- **Critical path:**  
  For each input document:  
  a. Extract embeddings x^(a) from anchor LLM and x^(s) from each source LLM.  
  b. Apply aligner: x̂^(s→a) = x^(s) W_s.  
  c. Aggregate: z = (1/M)(x^(a) + Σ_{s≠a} x̂^(s→a)).  
  d. Standardize z using training statistics.  
  e. Forward pass through readout g_θ to obtain prediction ŷ.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Anchor selection:* Usually the best single‑model performer, but not guaranteed optimal; influences the target space.  
  - *Ridge regularization α:* Balances alignment fidelity vs generalization; higher α reduces overfitting risk but may underfit.  
  - *Aggregation operator:* Mean preserves dimension and is simple; concatenation increases feature count and readout capacity but may add noise.  
  - *Linearity:* Linear alignment is lightweight and interpretable; non‑linear mappings could capture richer relationships but would increase parameters and require careful tuning.  
  - *No fine‑tuning:* Keeps base LLMs unchanged, preserving general knowledge and avoiding catastrophic forgetting, but limits task‑specific adaptation.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Alignment training R² < 0.95 → poor compatibility, likely harming downstream performance.  
  - FinAnchor’s test F1/Accuracy lower than best single‑LLM baseline → alignment/aggregation not providing benefit.  
  - Error overlap > 0.9 across LLMs → models too similar; little complementary signal to exploit.  
  - Readout overfits rapidly (validation loss increases early) → aggregated features may be too noisy or high‑dimensional.  
  - Occlusion or interpretability analysis shows no reweighting of salient financial cues → alignment may be trivial or anchor‑dominated.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. *Main result replication:* Train FinAnchor on Conference Call dataset with Gemma‑2‑9B as anchor and Llama‑3.1‑8B, Qwen‑3‑8B as sources; report Accuracy/F1 and compare against Table 1 values.  
  2. *Alignment necessity ablation:* Replace the alignment step with direct concatenation of raw embeddings (“Concatenation (no alignment)”) and show performance drop relative to FinAnchor (as in Appendix C.2).  
  3. *Hyperparameter sensitivity:* Vary ridge α (e.g., 0.1, 1, 10, 100) and plot validation F1; identify a robust range and observe that too low α leads to overfitting (high train R² but lower val F1), while too high α underfits (low alignment quality).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does FinAnchor generalize to non-finance domains and a broader range of model families beyond the specific LLMs evaluated?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The "Limitations" section states: "the generality of our findings should be further validated on a broader range of tasks, languages, and model families."
- **Why unresolved:** The study exclusively uses finance-oriented datasets and a narrow set of decoder-only LLMs (Gemma, Llama, Qwen). It is unknown whether the alignment method transfers to other domains (e.g., biomedical, legal) or to diverse architectures (e.g., encoder-only, multimodal models).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct experiments on standard non-finance NLP benchmarks (e.g., GLUE, SuperGLUE) using varied model families (BERT, RoBERTa, mBERT). Compare FinAnchor's gains against single-model baselines and measure alignment quality (R²) to test if linear compatibility holds across domains.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the sensitivity of FinAnchor's performance to its implementation choices, particularly ridge regularization strength (α) and the aggregation strategy?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The "Limitations" section identifies "additional implementation choices and hyperparameters (e.g., alignment strength and aggregation strategy)" and calls for "a more systematic sensitivity study."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper uses a fixed α=10 and mean aggregation without justification or ablation. The impact of these choices on robustness, especially under distribution shift or with noisy embeddings, is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform a controlled sensitivity analysis varying α over a logarithmic range (e.g., 10⁻³ to 10³) and comparing aggregation operators (mean, max, weighted sum with learned attention). Report performance stability (variance across splits) and alignment metrics to identify optimal or robust configurations.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What principled criteria should determine the selection of the anchor model, and does the optimal anchor vary with task characteristics?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method arbitrarily selects Gemma as the anchor without comparison, while the introduction notes that "the optimal LLM for generating embeddings varies across tasks and time periods."
- **Why unresolved:** The anchor choice is a critical design decision, yet no systematic study compares different anchors. It is unclear whether anchor selection should be based on intrinsic model properties (e.g., size, training data), task-specific validation performance, or alignment compatibility (R²).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Run a "leave-one-out" anchor selection experiment: for each task, evaluate FinAnchor using each available LLM as anchor while aligning others to it. Correlate anchor choice with downstream performance, alignment quality (R²), and initial single-model accuracy to identify predictive factors for optimal anchor selection.
