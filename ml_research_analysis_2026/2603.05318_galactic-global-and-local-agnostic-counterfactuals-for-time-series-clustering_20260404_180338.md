# GALACTIC: Global and Local Agnostic Counterfactuals for Time-series Clustering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.05318
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05318
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: First unified counterfactual explainability framework for unsupervised time-series clustering, achieving higher effectiveness, lower flipping cost, greater sparsity, and superior efficiency vs. adapted baselines on 30 UCR datasets.

## Executive Summary
GALACTIC introduces a unified counterfactual explainability framework for unsupervised time-series clustering, addressing the critical gap where existing methods fail to explain transitions across cluster boundaries. It generates locally sparse, discriminative perturbations by identifying key temporal segments via permutation-based importance, and produces a global concise summary through a Minimum Description Length (MDL) objective with proven theoretical guarantees. Extensive experiments on 30 UCR datasets demonstrate its superiority over adapted baselines in effectiveness, cost, sparsity, and computational efficiency.

## Method Summary
The method comprises two stages. Locally, for each instance, it computes segment importances by permuting segments within its cluster/subgroup and measuring the drop in surrogate classifier accuracy. A binary mask derived from these scores constrains gradient-based optimization, ensuring updates only affect high-importance segments, yielding sparse and valid counterfactuals. Globally, it formulates the selection of a representative set of counterfactuals as an MDL optimization, balancing coverage and complexity; this objective is proven supermodular, enabling greedy selection with a (1-1/e) approximation guarantee. For scalability, a hierarchical variant first selects within subgroups before full-cluster refinement, reducing runtime while maintaining quality.

## Key Results
- Higher flipping effectiveness and lower perturbation cost compared to adapted baselines (k-NN, TSEvo, Glacier, GLOBE-CE*).
- Greater sparsity, measured by fewer changed temporal segments and timesteps in local counterfactuals.
- Superior computational efficiency, especially with hierarchical variants, enabling practical use on large time-series corpora.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Local sparsity via permutation-importance masking
GALACTIC identifies discriminative temporal segments by permuting them within an instance’s cluster/subgroup and measuring the resulting drop in surrogate classifier accuracy. The importances are thresholded into a binary mask that hard-constrains gradient-based optimization to modify only these regions. This ensures perturbations are sparse and aligned with segments that define cluster identity. Core assumption: segments whose permutation significantly degrades accuracy are discriminative. Break condition: if permutation analysis yields uniformly low importances, the mask becomes permissive, leading to non-sparse or invalid counterfactuals.

### Mechanism 2: Global summary via MDL supermodular optimization
The global selection is framed as a Minimum Description Length problem: the model code length penalizes perturbation cardinality, sparsity, and magnitude, while the data code length penalizes uncovered instances. This combined objective is proven supermodular under “admissible additions” (newly covering instances), making its reduction monotone submodular. Thus, greedy selection achieves a (1-1/e) approximation to the optimum. Core assumption: the MDL encoding correctly captures the trade-off between explanation set complexity and population coverage. Break condition: if additions are not admissible (e.g., redundant candidates), the monotonicity and guarantee may fail.

### Mechanism 3: Scalability through hierarchical refinement
To handle large candidate pools, GALACTIC decomposes global selection into subgroup-level and cluster-level phases. First, within each subgroup (derived from segmentation-based clustering), it selects local MDL-optimal or greedy perturbations under a proportional budget. The winners form a reduced candidate pool, which is then refined at the full cluster level via another MDL selection. This reduces computational cost while preserving effectiveness. Core assumption: subgroup structures approximate global patterns adequately. Break condition: if subgroups are poorly defined (e.g., due to noisy segmentation), hierarchical decomposition may miss globally optimal perturbations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Counterfactual Explanations (CEs):** Needed to provide actionable insights into cluster membership by identifying minimal temporal perturbations that flip assignments. Quick check: What two additional criteria beyond proximity and sparsity must local CEs satisfy for clustering, and why? (Answer: Validity and cluster discriminativeness; they must successfully change the cluster assignment and highlight segments that define cluster boundaries.)
- **Minimum Description Length (MDL):** Needed to resolve the multi-objective trade-off in global counterfactual selection without manual weighting by encoding both model and data complexities as bit lengths. Quick check: How does the MDL data code length approximate the cost of uncovered instances, and why is this efficient? (Answer: It uses the number of uncovered instances as a proxy, avoiding enumeration of all subsets.)
- **Submodular Optimization:** Needed to provide theoretical guarantees for greedy selection in NP-hard subset problems. Quick check: What is the approximation guarantee for greedy maximization of a monotone submodular function under a cardinality constraint? (Answer: (1-1/e) ≈ 63% of the optimal value.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Surrogate classifier (g) -> Segmentation module (NNSegment) -> Subgroup discovery -> Importance scorer -> Local generator (Galactic-L) -> Candidate pool generator (MMD-Critic) -> Global selector (Galactic-G)
- **Critical path:** Data → surrogate training → segmentation/subgrouping → local CE generation for coreset → candidate pool → global MDL selection → final explanations.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Local: Mask strategy (source/target/combined) trades sparsity vs. validity; combined aligns both but may increase segment edits.
  - Global: Greedy vs. optimal trades runtime for quality; hierarchical reduces search space but may lose global coherence.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - Local: Low effectiveness (<80% flip rate) indicates poor target selection or overly restrictive mask; high changed timesteps suggests mask too lenient.
  - Global: High MDL cost with low coverage implies redundant candidates; greedy stagnation early suggests poor candidate pool.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Target selection analysis: Compare “second possible” vs. random policies for local CE effectiveness and cost.
  2. Importance weighting: Evaluate source, target, combined masks on sparsity (changed segments/timesteps) and validity.
  3. Local benchmarking: Test Galactic-L against k-NN, TSEvo, Glacier on UCR datasets for effectiveness, cost, sparsity, runtime.

## Limitations
- Permutation importance may fail to identify sufficiently discriminative segments, leading to non-sparse or invalid counterfactuals.
- MDL supermodularity relies on “admissible additions”; adding redundant candidates can break the (1-1/e) guarantee.
- Evaluation is limited to UCR datasets with adapted baselines; real-world applicability and robustness remain uncertain.

## Confidence
- Local counterfactual sparsity & validity: Medium
- Global MDL near-optimal guarantee: High
- Scalability via hierarchical refinement: Medium
- Overall empirical superiority over baselines: Medium

## Next Checks

2. **Admissible additions violation test:** Build candidate pools with high redundancy (many covering same instances); compare greedy MDL performance to optimal to quantify degradation of the (1-1/e) guarantee.
