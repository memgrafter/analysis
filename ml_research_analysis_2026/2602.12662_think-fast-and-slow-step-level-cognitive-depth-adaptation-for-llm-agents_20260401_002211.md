# Think Fast and Slow: Step-Level Cognitive Depth Adaptation for LLM Agents

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.12662  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12662  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** CogRouter (Qwen2.5-7B) achieves 82.3% average success rate on ALFWorld and ScienceWorld, outperforming GPT-4o (+40.3%), OpenAI-o3 (+18.3%), and GRPO (+14.0%) while consuming 62% fewer tokens.

## Executive Summary
LLM agents typically employ fixed reasoning depth—either always shallow or uniformly deep—across all steps of long-horizon tasks, which is inefficient when step complexity varies. This paper introduces CogRouter, a framework that dynamically adapts cognitive depth on a per-step basis, inspired by ACT-R theory’s hierarchy of cognitive levels. CogRouter uses a two-stage training pipeline: Cognition-aware Supervised Fine-tuning (CoSFT) builds a balanced dataset with random level assignments to instill stable, level-specific patterns, followed by Cognition-aware Policy Optimization (CoPO), which employs confidence-aware advantage reweighting to select appropriate depth. Evaluations on ALFWorld and ScienceWorld show CogRouter (7B model) achieving 82.3% success, surpassing much larger models like GPT-4o and using far fewer tokens than RL baselines. The work demonstrates that step-level cognitive adaptation yields significant gains in both performance and efficiency.

## Method Summary
CogRouter maps ACT-R’s four-level cognitive hierarchy (instinctive to strategic) to a structured output format `<level>...</level><think>...</think><action>...</action>`. In CoSFT, expert demonstration steps are randomly assigned one of four levels, and the model is fine-tuned to generate the corresponding structured output; this ensures the policy can execute all levels reliably. CoPO then performs reinforcement learning without a critic. For each successful trajectory, it creates a cognitive group: four variants sharing the same observation and final action but differing in level and thinking. Action prediction confidence for each variant is computed as the average log-probability of the executed action tokens. The trajectory-level advantage is redistributed across the group via temperature-scaled softmax of normalized confidence, amplifying updates for levels that produced confident, correct actions. Failed trajectories are excluded to avoid misleading signals. This step-level credit assignment prevents the uniform deep-thinking collapse seen in trajectory-level RL and enables adaptive level distributions aligned with step complexity.

## Key Results
- CogRouter (Qwen2.5-7B) achieves 82.3% average success rate on ALFWorld and ScienceWorld, setting a new state-of-the-art for models of its size.  
- It outperforms strong baselines: GPT-4o by +40.3%, OpenAI-o3 by +18.3%, and GRPO by +14.0% (relative gains).  
- It consumes 62% fewer tokens than GRPO while maintaining an adaptive level distribution (e.g., ~55% L1, ~12% L4 on ALFWorld), demonstrating substantial efficiency improvements.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Cognition-aware Supervised Fine-tuning (CoSFT) establishes stable, level-specific patterns
**Claim:** CoSFT prevents format leakage and mode collapse during subsequent RL training by providing a balanced initial distribution across cognitive levels.  
**Mechanism:** CoSFT constructs a dataset where each expert demonstration step is randomly assigned one of four ACT-R inspired levels (L1–L4). The model is trained to output a structured format `<level>...</level><think>...</think><action>...</action>`, forcing distinct reasoning templates for each depth.  
**Core assumption:** A balanced initial distribution is necessary for the RL stage to explore appropriate level selection without bias.  
**Evidence:** The abstract and section 3.3 describe dataset construction and format enforcement; ablations show that without cold start or with expert-chosen levels (biased), performance degrades.  
**Break condition:** Unbalanced SFT data or loose format enforcement leads to implicit biases, causing RL divergence or collapse.

### Mechanism 2: Cognition-aware Policy Optimization (CoPO) enables step-level credit assignment via confidence-aware advantage reweighting
**Claim:** CoPO prevents the uniform deep-thinking collapse observed in trajectory-level RL by distributing advantages according to per-step confidence.  
**Mechanism:** For each successful trajectory, CoPO expands it into a cognitive group of four counterfactual variants (same observation and final action, different thinking). It computes action prediction confidence for each as the average log-probability of the executed action tokens. The trajectory advantage is reweighted across the group using temperature-scaled softmax of normalized confidence, boosting levels that yield confident, correct actions.  
**Core assumption:** An appropriate cognitive depth yields higher confidence in predicting the ground-truth action.  
**Evidence:** Section 3.4 defines the algorithm; section 4.2 shows GRPO collapses to >76% L4 while CoPO maintains adaptive distribution; comparisons to alternatives (max/min/entropy) perform worse.  
**Break condition:** If confidence metric does not correlate with step appropriateness (e.g., due to action distribution quirks), reweighting becomes misleading and degrades performance.

### Mechanism 3: Two-stage pipeline (balanced CoSFT → CoPO) is necessary for adaptive cognitive allocation
**Claim:** Neither RL alone nor SFT alone suffices; the cold start followed by fine-grained RL learning is essential.  
**Mechanism:** CoSFT provides a “cold start” with balanced cognitive patterns ensuring all levels are executable. CoPO then refines level selection online using confidence-aware advantages. This separation avoids the credit assignment problem of trajectory-level RL and the distributional bias of expert-led SFT.  
**Core assumption:** Learning requires both a stable foundation (all levels) and a fine-grained learning signal.  
**Evidence:** Ablations: direct RL (no cold start) collapses to extreme L4 and underperforms (86% vs 92.5% SR); SFT-only uses more tokens with lower SR; CoSFT with expert-selected levels inherits teacher bias and underperforms.  
**Break condition:** If CoSFT fails to produce a balanced, executable policy, CoPO cannot recover adaptability, leading to collapse or poor performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP)**  
  *Why needed:* Agent tasks involve long horizons, partial observability, and sparse rewards. The paper models this in §2 and §3.1, where the policy conditions on interaction history rather than full state.  
  *Quick check:* How does the POMDP formulation in this paper differ from a standard MDP, and why is it essential for multi-turn agent tasks?

- **Concept: Group-based Policy Optimization (GRPO)**  
  *Why needed:* CoPO builds upon GRPO’s group-based advantage estimation (no critic) but extends it to step-level. Understanding GRPO’s trajectory-level advantage assignment is prerequisite.  
  *Quick check:* Why does assigning the same trajectory-level advantage to all steps cause cognitive rigidity, as seen in GRPO/GiGPO?

- **Concept: ACT-R Theory**  
  *Why needed:* The four cognitive levels (L1–L4) are directly mapped to ACT-R’s hierarchy from procedural to declarative processing. This psychological grounding defines the depth spectrum.  
  *Quick check:* What ACT-R principles correspond to each cognitive level, and how do they translate to LLM reasoning patterns?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  `Policy network (LLM base) → Cognitive level selector → Thinking generator → Action predictor → Confidence scorer → Advantage reweighter → Training loop`

- **Critical path:**  
  Observation → sample level → generate thinking → predict action → environment step. On success, expand trajectory into a cognitive group (for each level, regenerate thinking while keeping action fixed) → compute confidence for each level → softmax-normalize to reweight trajectory advantage → policy gradient update with KL penalty.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Structured format vs. efficiency: Enforcing `<level>/<think>/<action>` tags increases token count (~40% more than plain ReAct) but enables credit assignment.  
  - Confidence metric: Average log-probability balances sensitivity and robustness; using max/min/entropy degrades performance.  
  - Cold-start balance: Random level sampling in CoSFT avoids teacher bias but may not reflect realistic level distributions; RL must recover adaptability.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Cognitive collapse: >90% L4 usage across all steps, identical to GRPO.  
  - Token explosion: High token count without SR gain (e.g., AdaptThink uses 1343 tokens vs CoPO’s 1641 with lower SR).  
  - Distribution skew: Extreme level percentages (e.g., <1% L1 in w/o cold start) indicate failed exploration.  
  - Format leakage: Missing or malformed tags during RL, causing Rformat=0 penalties.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Reproduce CoPO vs. GRPO collapse: Train Qwen2.5-7B with CoSFT, then apply GRPO and CoPO for 150 iterations. Verify GRPO collapses to >75% L4 while CoPO remains adaptive.  
  2. Ablate confidence metric: Replace average log-prob with max/min/entropy in CoPO. Expect performance drop, confirming confidence’s role.  
  3. Test cold-start necessity: Initialize RL from (a) random model (no SFT), (b) CoSFT with expert-selected levels (bias), (c) balanced CoSFT. Only (c) should achieve adaptive distribution and highest SR.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How does CogRouter generalize to other agentic domains such as code generation or web interaction?  
*Basis in paper:* The authors explicitly state that future work should include "exploring adaptive mechanisms for broader ranges of tasks."  
*Why unresolved:* Evaluation is limited to ALFWorld and ScienceWorld, leaving open whether adaptive depth provides benefits on tasks with different action spaces, observation structures, or temporal dependencies.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Strong performance gains across a diverse suite of agent benchmarks (e.g., SWE-bench, WebArena) compared to fixed-pattern baselines.

**Open Question 2:** Can the cognitive hierarchy be self-evolved rather than manually fixed to four levels?  
*Basis in paper:* The conclusion proposes investigating "self-evolving cognitive hierarchies" as a future research direction.  
*Why unresolved:* A fixed four-level scheme may not capture the optimal granularity for all tasks or environments; dynamic adjustment could improve adaptability.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* A method that automatically discovers or adjusts the number and definitions of cognitive levels during training, while maintaining or improving performance and efficiency.

**Open Question 3:** Is confidence-based reweighting fundamentally unsuitable for failed trajectories, or can alternative formulations extract learning from them?  
*Basis in paper:* Ablation results show that applying confidence reweighting to failed trajectories caused collapse to L1, leading to the design choice to exclude failures.  
*Why unresolved:* The observed failure indicates that confidence patterns in unsuccessful trajectories are misleading, but it remains unclear whether a different per-step credit assignment signal could successfully leverage failures without harming performance.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Developing a new weighting strategy for failed trajectories that yields stable learning and improved outcomes compared to ignoring them.

**Open Question 4:** What theoretical mechanism causes standard RL methods to collapse to uniform deep thinking in adaptive settings?  
*Basis in paper:* The authors attribute collapse to "coarse credit assignment" that cannot distinguish appropriate depth, but they provide no formal analysis.  
*Why unresolved:* Without a theoretical foundation, designing algorithms that avoid collapse remains heuristic; understanding the root cause could lead to more robust solutions.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* A formal model (e.g., in a simplified MDP) proving that trajectory-level advantages induce a bias toward deeper reasoning, supported by empirical studies that vary reward structures and group sizes.

## Limitations
- Robustness depends on balanced CoSFT data and strict enforcement of structured output tags; sensitivity to data imbalance or format leakage is not fully established.
- The confidence metric (average log-probability) assumes a correlation between high confidence and appropriate cognitive depth, but this link lacks direct validation (e.g., against human annotations or alternative metrics).
- Evaluation is confined to two simulation environments (ALFWorld, ScienceWorld), limiting claims about generalizability to real-world tasks or other domains.

## Confidence
- **CoSFT establishes stable, level-specific patterns**: Medium. Ablation shows performance drops without cold start, but the necessity of random level sampling versus expert-selected levels remains under-explored.  
- **CoPO’s step-level confidence reweighting prevents uniform deep thinking**: High. Strong empirical support (Table 2, Figure 3) shows CoPO maintains adaptive distributions while GRPO collapses, and the mechanism is clearly articulated.  
- **Two-stage pipeline is necessary and sufficient for adaptive cognition**: Medium. Results vs. SFT-only and RL-only baselines are compelling, yet limited to a single model (Qwen2.5-7B) and two benchmarks; broader validation is needed.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain generalization:** Test CogRouter on at least two additional tasks (e.g., MiniWoB++ for web navigation, HumanEval for code generation) and multiple LLM scales (Llama-3-8B, GPT-4o) to confirm adaptive level selection and token efficiency hold beyond the reported settings.  
2. **Confidence-appropriateness correlation:** Collect human judgments of appropriate cognitive depth for a sample of steps; compute correlation between these ratings and the model’s average log-prob confidence. Also compare against alternative confidence definitions (entropy, margin) to verify the chosen metric’s superiority.  
3. **Robustness to CoSFT imbalance:** Deliberately skew the level distribution in the SFT data (e.g., 80% L4, 10% L1–L3 each) and train with CoPO. Measure whether the policy recovers an adaptive distribution and whether success rates degrade, thereby testing sensitivity to initial bias and the mechanism’s corrective capacity.
