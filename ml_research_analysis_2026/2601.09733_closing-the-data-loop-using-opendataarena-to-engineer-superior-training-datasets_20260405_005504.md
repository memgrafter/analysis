# Closing the Data Loop: Using OpenDataArena to Engineer Superior Training Datasets

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.09733
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09733
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ODA-Math-460k (460k samples) achieves 64.1% average across math benchmarks; ODA-Mixture-500k (506k samples) sets a new SOTA 65.6% on Qwen2.5-7B, outperforming larger open-source datasets with less data.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a closed-loop framework for constructing high-quality Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) datasets using OpenDataArena (ODA). Instead of heuristic aggregation, ODA’s multi-dimensional leaderboard rankings guide the selection and engineering of training data. Two instantiations—ODA-Math-460k (targeted mathematics) and ODA-Mixture (multi-domain)—achieve state-of-the-art performance on Qwen2.5-7B while being significantly more data-efficient than existing open datasets. The results demonstrate a data-centric paradigm where transparent evaluation directly informs superior dataset composition.

## Method Summary
The ODA-Math-460k pipeline starts by aggregating top-ranked mathematics datasets from the ODA leaderboard, followed by deduplication and benchmark decontamination. Problems are filtered for domain relevance and validity, then canonical answers are extracted. A two-stage difficulty selection (Pass@4=0 with a small Qwen3 model, then Pass@5>0 with a stronger reasoner) isolates learnable yet challenging items. Responses are generated by AM-Thinking-v1 and verified by Compass-Verifier-7B; only verified traces are retained. The ODA-Mixture pipelines anchor on the efficient LIMO dataset and supplement with specialist sources (e.g., AM-Thinking-distilled math/code, SYNTHETIC-2-SFT, math-gpt-4o-200k). After standard cleaning, embeddings are clustered and sampled either by difficulty-priority (for 100k) or random (for 500k) within clusters. Both final datasets are used to fine-tune Qwen2.5-7B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base with fixed hyperparameters (LlamaFactory/DeepSpeed) and evaluated on the full ODA benchmark suite via OpenCompass/vLLM.

## Key Results
- ODA-Math-460k (460k samples) attains 64.1% average score across math benchmarks on Qwen2.5-7B, surpassing larger open datasets like OpenThoughts3 (1.2M samples).
- ODA-Mixture-500k (506k samples) sets a new overall SOTA of 65.6% on the same model, nearly 6 points above OpenThoughts3 with less than half the data.
- Both datasets exhibit superior data efficiency, confirming the effectiveness of the closed-loop ODA-guided engineering approach.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### 1. Value-Anchored Selection
ODA’s cross-benchmark rankings serve as an actionable signal to identify high-value source datasets. The framework assumes that datasets performing well across multiple ODA evaluators contribute more to downstream SFT. Evidence: top-ranked math and general datasets are aggregated into the final mixtures. Break condition: if rankings do not correlate with actual SFT gains, the selection mechanism collapses.

### 2. Difficulty-Aware Curriculum
A two-stage pass-rate filter isolates problems in the “learnable but challenging” band. Stage 1 removes trivial problems (Pass@4=0 with a small non-thinking model). Stage 2 removes unsolvable ones (Pass@5>0 with a stronger thinking model). This aligns data with the target model’s capability frontier. Evidence: Pass-Rate sampling outperforms alternative metrics (44.7% avg vs 39.2% for Deita-Complexity) in a 100k ablation. Break condition: mis-calibrated thresholds degrade performance, especially on high-difficulty benchmarks.

### 3. Verifier-Backed Distillation
Teacher model (AM-Thinking-v1) generates reasoning traces, which are automatically checked by Compass-Verifier-7B against ground-truth answers. Only verified correct traces enter the SFT dataset. This ensures trace quality, not just final answer correctness. Evidence: verified traces yield a +2.0% absolute improvement over unverified outputs on 100k samples. Break condition: verifier inaccuracies (false positives/negatives) harm data efficiency.

## Foundational Learning
- **Data Valuation via Benchmarking**: Understanding how ODA quantifies dataset value across multiple evaluators is prerequisite to grasping the selection logic. *Why needed*: It shifts dataset construction from heuristic aggregation to evidence-based selection. *Quick check*: How does a dataset’s performance on ODA’s multi-dimensional leaderboard differ from its performance on a single benchmark like GSM8K, and why is the former a better signal for constructing a general-purpose SFT mixture?

- **Difficulty-Aware Curriculum Selection**: The two-stage filtering embodies curriculum learning by isolating problems at the learnable frontier. *Why needed*: It maximizes training efficiency and avoids noise from trivial or unsolvable samples. *Quick check*: If the upper-bound filter (Pass@5 > 0) were removed, what specific failure modes might emerge based on the paper’s rationale?

- **Verify-then-Distill for Trace Quality**: Automatic verification of step-by-step reasoning traces ensures the reasoning process is correct. *Why needed*: Flawed traces can mislead the model even if the final answer is right. *Quick check*: Why might training on unverified CoT traces (final answer correct but steps flawed) harm learning dynamics?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
ODA Leaderboard & Tool -> Curation Pipeline -> Selection Engine -> Distillation & Verification Unit -> Training & Evaluation Harness

### Critical Path
Leaderboard signal → Source aggregation → Basic cleaning (dedup/decon) → Domain/validity filtering → **Difficulty selection** (key branching point for math vs. mixture) → Distillation → Verification → Final dataset → SFT training → ODA benchmark evaluation (loop closure)

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Quality vs. Scale**: Two-stage difficulty filter reduces data volume dramatically (11.4M → 459.6k for math) but may discard diverse problem types.
- **Verification Cost vs. Trace Fidelity**: Using a verifier adds overhead; it may reject marginally correct but educationally valuable traces, depending on verifier accuracy.
- **Difficulty-Priority vs. Diversity Sampling**: For ODA-Mixture-100k, difficulty-priority boosts early gains but risks homogeneity; for 500k, random within clusters promotes breadth but may dilute hard-sample density.

### Failure Signatures
1. Poor performance on high-difficulty benchmarks indicates mis-calibrated difficulty distribution (e.g., AIME/HMMT).
2. Benchmark contamination from inadequate decontamination inflates scores on specific tests, invalidating the closed-loop claim.
3. Verifier bottleneck: weak verifier introduces false positives/negatives, harming data efficiency.
4. Domain imbalance: skewed subject distribution from source aggregation causes weaknesses in underrepresented areas.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Ablation: Selection Strategy Comparison.** Sample 100k problems from curated pool using Random, Diversity, Deita-Complexity, Answer-Prob, Thinking-Prob, and Pass-Rate. Train identical models on ODA math suite. Expect Pass-Rate to achieve highest average (44.7%), validating difficulty-frontier hypothesis.
2. **Ablation: Impact of Verification.** Train two models on 100k samples: one with verified CoT traces, one with unverified raw teacher outputs. Evaluate on Olympiad-level benchmarks. Expect verified traces to show a clear performance gap (e.g., +3.8% on Olympiad).
3. **Scaling Study: Intra-Cluster Sampling Strategy.** For a fixed domain (e.g., AM-Thinking math), sample 100k, 300k, and 500k instances using difficulty-priority (longer sequences) vs. random within embedding clusters. Track ODA overall average. Expect difficulty-priority to win at 100k but random to scale better beyond 300k, justifying the two-track design.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can OpenDataArena be extended to cover additional domains and languages beyond math, code, general reasoning, and English?
2. How can automated verification be improved beyond final-answer matching to ensure high-quality reasoning traces?
3. How can dataset mixtures be systematically optimized under explicit compute and data budgets?
4. Does pass-rate based selection using a single teacher model introduce dataset bias that harms generalization to other model families and scales?

## Limitations
- ODA ranking predictive power: The core assumption that leaderboard rankings correlate with SFT marginal value lacks causal validation; confounders like dataset size or overlap may influence rankings.
- Verifier reliability: Compass-Verifier-7B’s accuracy on trace correctness is not independently quantified; false positives/negatives could distort dataset quality.
- Generalization scope: Results are confined to math/code/reasoning/general domains; performance on niche or factual domains remains unknown.

## Confidence
- Predictive power of ODA leaderboard rankings for SFT value: Medium
- Reliability of automated verifier for trace correctness: Low
- Generalization of findings to new domains/languages: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Ranking signal falsification**: Construct an SFT dataset from mid-ranked ODA sources (e.g., 50th percentile) using the same pipeline. If performance matches top-ranked datasets, the selection mechanism is not predictive.
2. **Verifier audit**: Sample 500 verified/unverified traces; human-annotate step-level correctness; compute verifier precision/recall. If recall < 90%, significant valid traces are being discarded.
3. **Out-of-domain evaluation**: Test ODA-Mixture-500k on non-ODA benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, TruthfulQA, science QA). A sharp drop (>10% absolute) would indicate overfitting to ODA’s evaluator suite.
