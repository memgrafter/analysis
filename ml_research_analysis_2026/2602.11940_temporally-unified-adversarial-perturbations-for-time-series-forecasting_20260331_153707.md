# Temporally Unified Adversarial Perturbations for Time Series Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11940  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11940  
- **Reference count:** 36  
- **Primary result:** MI-TGAM achieves average MSE degradation of 10.9% (ETT), 25.7% (Electricity), and 9.5% (Traffic) in white-box attacks, and nearly doubles the degradation of the strongest baseline in black-box transfer settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles a critical flaw in existing adversarial attacks for time series forecasting: temporal inconsistency. Standard methods generate overlapping samples via sliding windows, but their perturbations assign different values to the same historical timestamp across samples—an unrealistic scenario since a past observation cannot be altered differently per sample. The authors propose Temporally Unified Adversarial Perturbations (TUAPs) to enforce identical perturbations per timestamp. To generate TUAPs efficiently, they introduce the Timestamp-wise Gradient Accumulation Method (TGAM), with MI-TGAM as a momentum-based iterative variant. Extensive experiments on three datasets and four forecasting models confirm that MI-TGAM substantially outperforms baselines in both white-box and transfer attacks, demonstrating both effectiveness and practicality.

## Method Summary
MI-TGAM operates at the timestamp level rather than the sample level. It maintains a shared perturbation vector for each global timestamp `t`. During each iteration, it computes per-sample gradients for all overlapping samples under current perturbations, then aggregates contributions for each timestamp by summing the gradient slices corresponding to that timestamp across all samples that include it. The aggregated gradient per timestamp informs a momentum-based update (similar to MI-FGSM) to iteratively refine the shared perturbations. After `K` iterations, the timestamp-level perturbations are sliced into the sliding windows to produce the final adversarial examples. This approach inherently satisfies the TUAP constraint while leveraging the redundancy in overlapping samples to produce more transferable attacks.

## Key Results
- **White-box attacks:** Average MSE degradation: 10.9% (ETT), 25.7% (Electricity), 9.5% (Traffic). MAE degradation: 6.7%, 16.7%, 9.8% respectively.  
- **Black-box transfer attacks:** MI-TGAM nearly doubles the degradation of the strongest baseline and surpasses unconstrained methods.  
- **Robustness:** Consistently outperforms baselines across three datasets and four forecasting models (FreTS, SegRNN, TimesNet, iTransformer).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Temporal Unification Constraint (TUAPs)
Ensures a single, consistent perturbation per timestamp across all overlapping samples, making attacks physically realizable. TUAPs redefine the adversarial perturbation space by adding constraints: for any two samples sharing a timestamp `t`, their perturbations at that position are identical (`δn,l = δi,j = pt`), and each variable’s perturbation magnitude is bounded by a percentage of the original value (`|pd_t| ≤ ϵ·|vd_t|`). This directly addresses the "temporally inconsistent perturbations" problem in sliding-window forecasting.  
*Break condition:* The constraint becomes trivial for non-overlapping windows (stride ≥ input length). It may also limit white-box attack strength compared to unconstrained methods.

### Mechanism 2: Timestamp-wise Gradient Accumulation (TGAM)
Efficiently generates TUAPs by aggregating gradient information from all overlapping samples to compute a unified update direction per timestamp. Instead of optimizing perturbations per sample, TGAM optimizes per timestamp `t`. For each iteration, it collects sample-level gradients from all samples containing `t` (set `T_t`), then sums their contributions at the corresponding position to form a global gradient `g_t`. This aggregated gradient updates the shared perturbation `p_t`. The core assumption is that the optimal perturbation direction to fool all overlapping samples can be derived from the combined gradient, leveraging redundancy.  
*Break condition:* Effectiveness relies on sufficient overlaps; if `T_t` contains few samples, the aggregated gradient becomes noisy. Also, incorrect timestamp-to-sample mapping breaks unification.

### Mechanism 3: Momentum-based Iterative Update (MI-TGAM)
Enhances attack effectiveness and transferability by integrating momentum stabilization into TGAM’s iterative refinement. MI-TGAM adds a per-timestamp momentum term `ω_t`. At each iteration, momentum updates as `ω_t^{k+1} = μ ω_t^k + g_t^k / ||g_t^k||_1`, and perturbation updates as `p_t^{k+1} = clip(p_t^k + α sign(ω_t^{k+1}) * v_t, ϵ·v_t)`. This smooths the optimization path, helping escape sharp local maxima and yield more transferable perturbations.  
*Break condition:* Requires careful tuning of `μ` and `α`; poor choices cause overshooting or projection violation. Also introduces memory overhead per timestamp.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Data (sliding windows) -> Timestamp indexing (build `T_t` maps) -> Gradient computation (per-sample loss/backprop) -> Accumulation (sum gradients into `g_t`) -> Momentum & update (per-timestamp `ω_t`, `p_t`) -> Output (slice `p_t` into samples).

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Initialize `p_t=0`, `ω_t=0` for relevant timestamps.  
  2. For `k=1..K`:  
     a. For each sample: form `x′_n = x_n + p[n:n+L]`; compute loss; backprop to get `c_n`.  
     b. For each timestamp `t`: accumulate `g_t = Σ_{n: n ≤ t < n+L} c_n[t-n]`.  
     c. Update momentum: `ω_t = μ ω_t + g_t / (||g_t||_1 + ε)`.  
     d. Update perturbation: `p_t = clip(p_t + α sign(ω_t) * v_t, ϵ·|v_t|)`.  
  3. Generate adversarial inputs `X_adv[n] = x_n + p[n:n+L]`.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - TUAP constraint vs optimization freedom: trade some white-box strength for realism.  
  - Aggregation scope: using all overlapping samples maximizes gradient diversity but increases compute.  
  - Iterations vs speed: more iterations improve potency but linearly increase runtime.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Inconsistent output: check that for any shared timestamp, applied perturbations are identical.  
  - No degradation: suspect errors in gradient accumulation (misaligned indices, missing samples in `T_t`).  
  - Numerical instability: NaN losses indicate `α` too large or clipping error.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. White-box attack: compare MI-TGAM against baselines on three datasets and four models, measuring MSE/MAE degradation.  
  2. Transfer attack: use each model as surrogate to attack the other three; evaluate average degradation.  
  3. Sensitivity analysis: vary key hyperparameters (`ϵ`, `K`, input length `L`) on ETT with SegRNN.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to classification and defenses:** How can TUAPs be extended to time series classification tasks, and what are effective defense mechanisms against such attacks? The paper explicitly states future work on classification and robust defense.
2. **Reducing performance gap under constraints:** How can the performance gap between TUAP-constrained attacks and unconstrained attacks be reduced while maintaining temporal consistency? The paper notes a trade-off but does not address it.
3. **Generalization to alternative sampling schemes:** To what extent does TGAM generalize to forecasting setups with strided sampling, hierarchical windows, or irregular time intervals? The method derivation assumes non-stride sliding windows.
4. **Theoretical analysis of transferability:** What theoretical properties of timestamp-wise gradient accumulation explain its advantage in transferability, and can we formalize the connection? The paper provides empirical claims but no formal analysis.

## Limitations
- **Normalization ambiguity:** The perturbation bound `ϵ·|v_t|` is ambiguous whether applied to raw or normalized data, affecting effective strength.
- **Incomplete training details:** Hyperparameters for the four forecasting models are not provided, forcing reliance on external sources and introducing variability.
- **Generalization scope:** Evaluation covers only three datasets; performance on other domains or non-sliding-window setups remains unclear.

## Confidence
- **Performance superiority (MI-TGAM vs baselines):** High – consistent, significant improvements across datasets and models.
- **Physical realizability via temporal unification:** Medium – argument is sound and supported by comparisons, but real-world feasibility not empirically validated.
- **Effectiveness of TGAM/MI-TGAM mechanisms:** High – ablations show clear added value of gradient accumulation and momentum.
