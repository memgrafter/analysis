# PanoAffordanceNet: Towards Holistic Affordance Grounding in 360° Indoor Environments
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.09760
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09760
- **Reference count**: 38
- **Primary result**: Achieves state-of-the-art one-shot performance on 360‑AGD, significantly lowering KLD (1.306 vs. 2.853/3.067) and raising SIM (0.474 vs. 0.124/0.097) on the Hard Split.

## Executive Summary
PanoAffordanceNet addresses the lack of holistic affordance grounding in 360° indoor environments. It introduces two key architectural components: a Distortion‑Aware Spectral Modulator (DASM) to mitigate latitude‑dependent geometric distortion from equirectangular projection (ERP), and an Omni‑Spherical Densification Head (OSDH) to recover continuous functional regions from sparse initial activations. The model is trained under a one‑shot paradigm with multi‑level constraints—pixel-wise BCE, distributional KL, and region‑text contrastive losses—to suppress semantic drift. Evaluated on the newly collected 360‑AGD dataset, PanoAffordanceNet sets a new state‑of‑the‑art, showing robust generalization to perspective data and real‑world scenarios.

## Method Summary
The method employs a dual‑encoder backbone: DINOv2 for visual features and CLIP for text, with LoRA (rank 16) for parameter‑efficient adaptation. An ERP image and affordance text are fed into this backbone. DASM then calibrates distortion by decomposing features into high‑frequency (edges) and low‑frequency (structure) branches, applying latitude‑specific modules (HFEM for equator, LFSM for poles), and fusing them via gated mechanisms with global context re‑aggregation. A transformer decoder generates an initial affordance heatmap $A_{\text{init}}$. OSDH densifies this map by constructing a spherical affinity matrix from normalized tokens, selecting top‑k high‑confidence seeds, computing a confidence‑scaled propagation, and producing $A_{\text{refined}}$. Training combines three losses: binary cross‑entropy ($L_{\text{BCE}}$), KL divergence ($L_{\text{KL}}$), and region‑text contrastive ($L_{\text{RTC}}$) using InfoNCE. The framework operates in a one‑shot setting: after training on base affordance classes, a novel class is adapted using a single support image.

## Key Results
- **Hard Split**: KLD 1.306 (vs. 2.853/3.067 for baselines), SIM 0.474 (vs. 0.124/0.097).
- **Easy Split**: Consistent improvement over comparators.
- **Generalization**: Robust performance on the perspective AGD20K dataset without retraining.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Distortion‑Aware Spectral Modulator (DASM)**
- **Claim**: Mitigates latitude‑dependent geometric distortion from ERP by adaptively calibrating high‑frequency (boundary) and low‑frequency (structural) components.
- **How**: Cross‑modal injection of text into visual features; decomposition into high‑freq (Laplacian) and low‑freq (Gaussian) branches; latitude‑specific enhancement (HFEM) and stabilization (LFSM); hybrid gated fusion; global context re‑aggregation (MHSA+FFN).
- **Core assumption**: ERP distortion causes a non‑uniform frequency imbalance—edges are preserved near the equator but stretched and fragmented near the poles. Separating and compensating frequencies per latitude isolates task‑relevant geometry from projection artifacts.
- **Break condition**: If spherical convolution baselines (without explicit spectral decomposition) achieve comparable performance on Hard Split, DASM’s frequency‑specific design is unnecessary.

**Mechanism 2: Omni‑Spherical Densification Head (OSDH)**
- **Claim**: Recovers topologically continuous affordance regions from sparse initial activations by leveraging spherical self‑similarity as a structural prior.
- **How**: Project features onto unit hypersphere; build symmetric cosine affinity matrix $S$; select top‑k seeds $K$ from $A_{\text{init}}$; compute confidence map $C$; perform propagation $A_{\text{refined}} = A_{\text{init}} + \alpha \cdot \max_{j\in K}(S_{ij} \cdot C_j)$.
- **Core assumption**: In ERP panoramas, functionally coherent regions share visual similarity preserved under spherical projection, allowing sparse seeds to densify via affinity propagation despite non‑uniform sampling.
- **Break condition**: If a simple morphological closing (e.g., elliptical kernel) on $A_{\text{init}}$ yields similar improvements, OSDH’s spherical affinity approach is over‑engineered.

**Mechanism 3: Multi‑Level Training Constraints**
- **Claim**: Jointly suppress semantic drift under one‑shot supervision by aligning predictions across spatial, distributional, and semantic dimensions.
- **How**: $L_{\text{BCE}}$ enforces pixel accuracy; $L_{\text{KL}}$ aligns predicted vs. GT heatmap distributions to preserve global shape; $L_{\text{RTC}}$ (InfoNCE) aligns pooled region features with text embeddings to disambiguate multiple affordances (e.g., “sit” vs. “rest_arm”) on the same object.
- **Core assumption**: Single‑loss supervision is insufficient; BCE ignores shape, KL ignores semantics, and RTC alone lacks spatial precision. Their combination is necessary for robust alignment in complex, multi‑affordance scenes.
- **Break condition**: If ablating $L_{\text{RTC}}$ while keeping $L_{\text{BCE}}+L_{\text{KL}}$ yields no SIM drop on Hard Split, semantic drift is not primarily a cross‑modal alignment issue.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Equirectangular Projection (ERP) distortion**  
Why needed: ERP stretches polygons near poles, distorting local geometry and breaking convolutional equivariance assumed by standard CNNs/ViTs.  
Quick check: Why does a “sit” region on a ceiling‑mounted chair appear geometrically distorted near the pole in ERP, and how does that affect a model’s ability to recognize it?

**Concept: Spectral decomposition in vision**  
Why needed: DASM treats distortion as a frequency‑domain problem—high‑freq edges are compressed at poles, low‑freq textures are stretched—requiring frequency‑specific correction.  
Quick check: If you apply a Laplacian filter to an ERP image, how would response strength vary from equator to pole for the same physical edge?

**Concept: Contrastive loss for multi‑label segmentation**  
Why needed: $L_{\text{RTC}}$ uses InfoNCE to align each affordance’s region representation with its text embedding, preventing a “sit” embedding from activating a “lie” region when both exist on a sofa.  
Quick check: In a scene with a sofa having both “sit” (cushion) and “lean_back” (armrest) affordances, why might $L_{\text{BCE}}$ alone fail to separate them?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input (ERP image + affordance text) → Backbone (DINOv2 + CLIP with LoRA, rank 16) → DASM (cross‑modal injection, dual‑frequency branches with HFEM/LFSM, gated fusion, global context) → Transformer decoder → Initial map $A_{\text{init}}$ → OSDH (spherical affinity, top‑k seeds, confidence map, propagation) → Refined map $A_{\text{refined}}$ → Multi‑level loss ($L_{\text{BCE}}$, $L_{\text{KL}}$, $L_{\text{RTC}}$) during training.

**Critical path**: ERP input → DASM → decoder → OSDH → multi‑loss training. Skipping DASM causes pole artifacts; skipping OSDH causes fragmented predictions.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- DASM complexity adds ~15% FLOPs but is necessary for Hard Split; can be approximated with fewer channels for real‑time use.
- OSDH top‑k sensitivity: performance stable for $k\in[5,20]$; too low loses coverage, too high adds noise; default $k=10$.
- LoRA rank $r=16$ optimal; too low underfits distortion, too high overfits one‑shot data.

**Failure signatures**:
- Pole fragmentation without DASM (e.g., predictions on ceiling become scattered).
- Semantic confusion without $L_{\text{RTC}}$ (different affordances on same object activate similar regions).
- Sparse activations without OSDH ($A_{\text{init}}$ remains clustered; KLD increases).

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Ablation: Remove DASM and measure KLD/SIM drop on Hard Split, especially on pole‑distorted samples.
2. Visualization: Compare $A_{\text{init}}$ vs. $A_{\text{refined}}$; confirm sparse seeds propagate to full functional regions.
3. Hard Split stress test: Train on Easy Split, test on Hard Split; expect large gap without DASM+OSDH, but PanoAffordanceNet maintains low KLD increase (<0.1).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1: Temporal affordance reasoning**  
The paper notes future work on dynamic scenes; current model is static. Resolution requires video‑based extension with temporal consistency losses and evaluation on a dynamic affordance dataset.

**Open Question 2: Generalization to outdoor panoramas**  
360‑AGD covers only indoor scenes; outdoor environments differ in layout, lighting, and functional regions. Evidence needed: an outdoor panoramic affordance dataset and adaptation of DASM for non‑uniform outdoor distortions.

**Open Question 3: Comparison with few‑shot or fully supervised settings**  
The one‑shot paradigm may limit performance in complex scenes with multiple sparse affordances. Evidence needed: experiments varying number of training shots (1,5,10,full) and analyzing performance vs. scene complexity.

**Open Question 4: Real‑world robustness across diverse conditions**  
Real‑world evaluation used a limited wearable setup; factors like night scenes, crowds, camera height may affect robustness. Evidence needed: long‑term deployments in varied environments and systematic stress tests under adverse conditions.

## Limitations
- Unknown one‑shot protocol and base/novel class split prevent exact replication.
- Incomplete architectural details for DASM’s HFEM/LFSM and OSDH’s gates require design choices that may deviate.
- Novelty of DASM and OSDH lacks external validation; related works address ERP distortion differently.
