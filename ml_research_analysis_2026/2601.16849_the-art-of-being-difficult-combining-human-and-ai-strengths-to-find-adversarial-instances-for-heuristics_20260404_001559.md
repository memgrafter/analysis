# The Art of Being Difficult: Combining Human and AI Strengths to Find Adversarial Instances for Heuristics
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.16849  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16849  
- Reference count: 28  
- Primary result: Co-FunSearch combines LLM-driven evolutionary search with human simplification to generate mathematically rigorous adversarial instances, improving lower bounds for several long-standing heuristic analysis problems.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents Co-FunSearch, a collaborative framework that integrates FunSearch—an LLM-based program evolution method—with human expert refinement to discover adversarial instances for combinatorial optimization heuristics. The approach evolves programs that output problem instances, then humans simplify these to reveal scalable, provably hard constructions. It achieves state-of-the-art lower bounds for four classic problems: a super-polynomial bound for the Nemhauser-Ullmann knapsack heuristic, an improved bin-packing ratio of 1.5, the first price-of-hierarchy bound for hierarchical k-median clustering (~1.618), and a disproof of a 2-approximation conjecture for the generalized gasoline problem (factor 4.65). The work demonstrates how human-AI synergy can tackle open problems in theoretical computer science.

## Method Summary
Co-FunSearch proceeds in a loop. First, FunSearch uses an LLM to iteratively mutate programs that generate combinatorial instances; each program’s output is scored by evaluating the target heuristic (e.g., approximation ratio). High-scoring programs are retained for further mutation. Second, human experts inspect top programs, remove redundancies (e.g., repeated constants), and generalize to parametric families. These simplified programs are either directly analyzed to prove lower bounds or re-seeded into FunSearch to explore nearby patterns. This marries the LLM’s brute-force exploration with human abstraction and proof.

## Key Results
- Knapsack (Nemhauser-Ullmann): Instance proving non-output-polynomial runtime with super-polynomial lower bound \(n^{O(\sqrt{n})}\).
- Bin Packing (Best-Fit random-order): Improved absolute ratio lower bound from 1.3 to 1.5.
- Hierarchical k-Median: First non-trivial price-of-hierarchy lower bound of the golden ratio (~1.618).
- Generalized Gasoline: Disproved 2-approximation conjecture for iterative rounding with factor 4.65.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **LLM-based evolutionary search** discovers high-scoring programmatic patterns by mutating structured programs rather than raw numerical vectors. The LLM generates syntactically valid, scalable instances from parent programs, guided by a scoring function. Evidence: [abstract] notes LLM-provided interpretable patterns; [section 2.1] models instances as program outputs.
2. **Human simplification** transforms fragile, instance-specific LLM outputs into generalizable constructions by identifying and removing redundancies, replacing constants with parameters. This reveals the core adversarial structure. Evidence: [section 3.3.1-3] detail simplification for knapsack, bin packing, and clustering.
3. **Iterative feedback loop** uses simplified programs as new seeds for FunSearch or as the basis for theoretical proofs. This bridges empirical high scores and rigorous bounds. Evidence: [section 2.1 & Figure 1] shows manual refinement feeding back; [section 3.3.1] describes re-seeding or proving.

## Foundational Learning
- **Pareto-Optimal Set** (Knapsack): The Nemhauser-Ullmann heuristic’s runtime depends on intermediate Pareto set size. An adversary must create large disparity between an intermediate and final set. Quick check: Why would items with similar profit/weight ratios cause a large intermediate Pareto set?
- **Bin Packing Approximation Ratio & Random-Order Model**: Goal: construct an instance where Best-Fit (on random permutations) uses far more bins than optimal. Understanding the deterministic upper bound (1.7) and need for randomized lower bounds is key. Quick check: In instance `[m+1]*m + [m]*(m+1)`, why is optimal exactly two bins, and why is a bin containing both `m` and `m+1` wasteful?
- **Price of Hierarchy in Clustering**: Measures loss when forcing nested clusterings. Adversarial instance must force a bad trade-off at some hierarchy level. Quick check: Why does a point of infinite weight force it to be a singleton cluster at level `d+1`?
- **Linear Programming Relaxation & Iterative Rounding** (Gasoline): The iterative rounding algorithm’s approximation factor is analyzed by constructing instances where greedy rounding choices yield poor solutions. Understanding the ILP formulation is necessary. Quick check: In the 2D instance, what intuitive roles do the two vector components play in fooling the algorithm’s LP-based decisions?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: FunSearch Core -> Scoring Engine -> Human Refinement Interface -> Feedback Loop Controller
- **Critical path**:
  1. Seed with trivial program (e.g., simple constant list).
  2. Run FunSearch (~500–1000 LLM prompts) to generate program database.
  3. Manually inspect top 10–20 programs for interpretable patterns.
  4. Simplify: remove redundancy, generalize constants to parameters.
  5. Analyze: either prove simplified program’s score or re-seed FunSearch with it.
  6. Iterate until proof or stagnation.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - LLM model size: smaller models (e.g., gpt-4.1-nano) for exploration, larger for reasoning.
  - Initial program structure: hardcoded lists for quick gains vs. loop-based skeletons for scalable patterns.
  - Human time vs. automation: allocate expert time only to programs with clear repetitive structure after several trials.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - LLM stagnates early (<200 prompts) with only hardcoded variations.
  - Top programs are long inscrutable lists (no parametrization).
  - Simplified program’s score collapses asymptotically.
  - Search converges to known instances without improvement.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Baseline replication: Run bin packing with initial program from Figure 2a; verify rediscovery of 1.497 score within 1000 prompts.
  2. Structure test: Compare hardcoded vs. loop-based seeds for hierarchical clustering; measure if loop-based finds `infinite_weight` + `-c*e_i` pattern faster.
  3. Simplification sanity check: Simplify top knapsack program (Figure 4b) to (Figure 4c); validate same score for same `n`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Why does Co-FunSearch fail to generate improved lower bounds or replicate known results for certain heuristics (e.g., page replacement, k-means hierarchical clustering, asymptotic Best-Fit), despite succeeding on others like knapsack and bin-packing?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The conclusion explicitly lists these heuristics as cases where Co-FunSearch did not yield generalizable results or improve the state of the art.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper identifies these failures but does not analyze their underlying causes, leaving unclear whether the issues stem from problem structure, objective function properties, or limitations in the FunSearch evolution process.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A comparative study across a broad set of heuristics, measuring characteristics (e.g., landscape ruggedness, scalability of instance encodings) and correlating them with Co-FunSearch success rates, combined with targeted ablations on initial program templates and scoring functions for the failing problems.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can the proof strategy for the generalized gasoline problem's lower bound be extended to handle the changing optimal LP value at each iterative rounding step, thereby establishing asymptotic approximation factors (e.g., approaching 4, 6, 8 for d=2,3,4) as suggested by computational trends?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper states that the proof strategy from Lorieau (2024) "does not apply here, as the optimum value of the relaxed Linear Program changes at each step," preventing asymptotic guarantees.
- **Why unresolved**: The current analysis relies on a fixed LP optimum; the dynamic nature of the iterative rounding algorithm breaks this assumption, and no alternative analytic approach is proposed.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Develop a new invariant or amortized analysis that tracks LP optimum changes across rounding steps, and verify it on the constructed instance family to prove the observed scaling behavior holds asymptotically.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What specific structural or representational properties of an adversarial instance generation task make it amenable to Co-FunSearch (e.g., allowing concise, parameterized programs), and can these be formalized to predict suitability before search?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper highlights successful cases where human simplification distilled LLM outputs into compact, generalizable constructions (e.g., bin-packing's `[1.0/a]*a + [1.0/b]*b`), while failures (e.g., k-means) lacked such clear patterns, suggesting a link between problem encodability and success.
- **Why unresolved**: The work demonstrates the phenomenon anecdotally but offers no theoretical framework to characterize which problems admit low-complexity, high-impact programs versus those requiring inscrutable vectors.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Analyze Kolmogorov complexity of known adversarial instances across diverse problems; correlate success with metrics like symmetry, scalability, or ability to express via small code snippets. Test by applying Co-FunSearch to a designed set of problems varying these metrics.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: To what extent can the human simplification and generalization step be automated, and what are the trade-offs in result quality and proof-assistance when replacing expert refinement with algorithmic pattern extraction?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The Co-FunSearch framework positions human simplification as essential for transforming LLM outputs into rigorous proofs (e.g., removing redundant elements, identifying parameters), but the paper does not explore automating this stage, treating it as a qualitative expert skill.
- **Why unresolved**: The collaboration's bottleneck is unclear: is simplification primarily about recognizing mathematical structure, or can it be reduced to syntactic transformations? Automation could democratize the approach but risks losing insight.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Implement automated simplification modules (e.g., symbolic regression, redundancy elimination, parameter synthesis) and run them on the raw FunSearch outputs from the paper's problems. Compare the resulting instances' scores, generalizability, and proof readability against the human-simplified versions.

## Limitations
- Exact prompt engineering, database sampling hyperparameters, and human simplification heuristics are not disclosed, hindering direct replication.
- Reliance on expert intuition for generalization may not scale to other domains or problem types.
- Ablation studies are limited; claims about systematic improvement over pure LLM or human-only approaches have medium confidence.

## Confidence
- **High**: Empirical results (adversarial instances and scores) within the reported experimental setup.
- **Medium**: Claim that Co-FunSearch systematically improves over baselines (limited ablations and failure cases noted).
- **Low**: Broad applicability beyond the four studied problems without further validation across diverse heuristics.

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt/seed sensitivity test**: Re-run bin packing with 5 varied initial program structures (hardcoded lists vs. loop-based) and report score distribution convergence to verify robustness.
2. **Simplification fidelity check**: For knapsack, evaluate the human-simplified program’s intermediate Pareto set ratio for \(n = 10^3, 10^4\) to confirm the claimed super-polynomial lower bound holds asymptotically.
3. **Search baseline comparison**: Implement a non-LLM evolutionary program search (e.g., genetic programming with integer vectors) for bin packing; compare best-found score and program interpretability against Co-FunSearch to isolate the LLM’s contribution.
