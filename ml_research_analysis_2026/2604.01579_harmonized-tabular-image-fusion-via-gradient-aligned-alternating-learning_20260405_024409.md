# Harmonized Tabular-Image Fusion via Gradient-Aligned Alternating Learning
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.01579
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01579
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: GAAL achieves SOTA multimodal accuracy on DVM (0.9917), SUNAttribute (0.8668), and CelebA (0.8273), with unimodal improvements.
## Executive Summary
GAAL tackles gradient conflicts in multimodal tabular-image fusion by proposing an alternating learning framework with a shared classifier. It decouples unimodal encoder gradients and employs uncertainty-based cross-modal gradient surgery, using quadratic programming to project conflicting gradients onto directions from high-uncertainty samples. This promotes synergistic learning while avoiding interference. Evaluated on DVM, SUNAttribute, and CelebA, GAAL achieves state-of-the-art multimodal accuracy and robustness to test-time tabular missingness, demonstrating the effectiveness of its harmonic fusion approach.
## Method Summary
GAAL’s training loop alternates between updating one modality’s encoder and the shared classifier while freezing the other encoder. For the active modality, the encoder gradient updates its parameters, while the gradient for the shared classifier is modified via cross-modal gradient surgery: the current modality’s classifier gradient is projected onto a direction derived from high-uncertainty (hard) samples of the other modality. This projection, solved by a quadratic program, aligns conflicting gradients without discarding synergy. The shared classifier thus receives updates that benefit both modalities. Uncertainty is measured by prediction entropy, and a margin hyperparameter controls alignment strength.
## Key Results
- Multimodal accuracy: DVM 0.9917 (↑0.0186 vs. prior best 0.9731), SUNAttribute 0.8668, CelebA 0.8273.
- Unimodal performance also boosted.
- Robust under test-time tabular missing conditions.
## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Alternating unimodal learning decouples encoder gradients, preventing mutual interference during optimization.
- **Mechanism:** The training loop updates only one modality's encoder and the shared classifier at each step. The other modality's parameters are frozen. This isolates the gradient computation for each unimodal encoder (`θ_I` or `θ_T`) from the other, so the unified loss gradient `∇_θ L(x, y)` is never applied to both encoders simultaneously. The shared classifier `ψ` is updated based on gradients from the active modality, but these are separated from the frozen modality's contribution.
- **Core assumption:** Gradient conflicts primarily originate from simultaneously applying a single unified loss gradient to all modality-specific parameters. Decoupling the encoder updates removes this source of conflict for the unimodal feature extractors.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "GAAL adopts an alternating unimodal learning and shared classifier to decouple the multimodal gradient and facilitate interaction."
  - [section III.B]: "By utilizing alternating learning, we can successfully address gradient conflicts in the tabular and image encoders."
  - [corpus]: Weak. Neighbors discuss alternating training but do not provide direct evidence for decoupling as the primary conflict solution (e.g., "Multimodal Representation Learning by Alternating Unimodal Adaptation" uses alternating but for a different purpose).
- **Break condition:** If conflicts persist within the shared classifier `ψ` updates, as noted in the text, decoupling alone is insufficient.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Uncertainty-based cross-modal gradient surgery resolves conflicts at the shared classifier by projecting the current modality's gradient onto a direction derived from the other modality's hard samples.
- **Mechanism:** For the modality being updated (current), its gradient `g` w.r.t. the shared classifier `Θ` is computed. Separately, the other modality (previous) is used to identify high-uncertainty (hard) samples via prediction entropy (Eq. 6). The gradient `g_p` is computed as the average classifier gradient from these hard samples of the previous modality. A quadratic program (Eq. 4) projects `g` onto `g_p` (Eq. 5), producing a modified gradient `ẽg` that is aligned with `g_p` (cosine similarity ≥ ϵ). This `ẽg` is used to update `Θ`.
- **Core assumption:** Gradients from hard samples (`g_p`) contain more transferable, synergistic learning signals that, when used as a projection target, can steer the current modality's update (`g`) to be beneficial for both modalities and avoid detrimental conflict.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section III.C]: "we design Uncertainty-based Gradient Guidance, which selects the gradient direction of high-uncertainty cross-modal samples as the projection direction... Learning hard samples effectively enhances the model’s attention to edge cases."
  - [section III.B]: "We aim to project `g` with the constraint to obtain the modified gradient `ẽg` that is aligned with `g_p`."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence. Corpus neighbors focus on gradient modulation (OGM, MMPareto) or orthogonalization (MLA), not uncertainty-guided surgical projection.
- **Break condition:** If the uncertainty estimation (entropy) is poorly calibrated, `g_p` may represent easy or misinformative samples, degrading performance. The paper shows sensitivity to `λ` (sample selection rate), implying this is a risk.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The shared classifier, while enabling cross-modal interaction, is a locus for gradient conflict; aligning its gradients via the surgery promotes synergistic learning rather than independent or conflicting updates.
- **Mechanism:** During alternating steps, the shared classifier `ψ` is updated with gradients derived from the currently optimized modality. Without intervention, this gradient `g` may conflict with the direction that would help the other modality (represented by `g_p` from its hard samples). The surgery modifies `g` to `ẽg` to respect alignment, thus making the classifier's update a compromise that benefits both. This contrasts with prior works (e.g., MLA) that force orthogonalization, which the authors argue discards potential overlap and synergy.
- **Core assumption:** Modality objectives are not strictly orthogonal; they share a synergistic component that can be captured by aligning gradients in the shared parameter space towards directions beneficial for the other modality's hard samples.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "steering the shared parameters to benefit all modalities."
  - [section III.B]: "Unlike forcibly orthogonalizing all modal gradients, our method targets only conflicting gradients without enforcing orthogonality, which promotes multimodal synergy."
  - [section I]: "MLA uses gradient orthogonalization... neglecting overlaps and synergy among modal objectives."
- **Break condition:** If the modalities have fundamentally contradictory objectives with minimal synergy, forcing alignment (even selectively) could harm performance. The method assumes some shared beneficial direction exists, supported by unimodal performance boosts reported.
## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Gradient Conflict Quantification**
  - **Why needed here:** GAAL's core premise is that negative cosine similarity between modality gradients (`S(g, g_p) < 0`) indicates conflict that harms joint optimization. Understanding this metric is prerequisite to grasping the problem statement and the surgery's alignment goal (`S(ẽg, g_p) ≥ ϵ`).
  - **Quick check question:** What does a negative cosine similarity between two gradient vectors imply about their optimization directions?

- **Concept: Alternating/Multitask Optimization**
  - **Why needed here:** The alternating paradigm is the structural foundation. The engineer must understand why updating one modality at a time (with a shared head) is chosen over standard joint training and how it changes the gradient flow topology.
  - **Quick check question:** In an alternating setup with a shared classifier, what gradients affect the unimodal encoder parameters versus the shared classifier parameters?

- **Concept: Uncertainty as Sample Difficulty**
  - **Why needed here:** The mechanism selects `g_p` from high-entropy predictions. This requires understanding that entropy `H(p_i)` is a proxy for model uncertainty/difficulty, and that hard samples are prioritized for cross-modal guidance.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might gradients from high-uncertainty (high-entropy) samples be more informative for guiding another modality's update than gradients from confident samples?
## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Modality encoders (`ϕ_I` for images, `ϕ_T` for tabular) extract unimodal features `u^I`, `u^T` -> Shared classifier `ψ(Θ, [u^I : u^T])` produces logits. Alternating controller toggles active encoder per iteration. Uncertainty-based gradient guidance module computes entropy `H(p_i^{other})` on the other modality's batch, selects top-`λ^{other}` hard samples, and averages their classifier gradients to get `g_p`. Cross-modal gradient surgery (QP solver) takes current classifier gradient `g` and guidance `g_p`, solves `ẽg = g + v * g_p` with `v = max(0, (ϵ - g^⊤g_p)/||g_p||^2)`, and updates `Θ` with `ẽg`.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Forward pass with active modality `m` (encoder `ϕ_m`) and frozen other modality; compute loss `L^m`.
  2. Backward: `∇_θ_m L^m` updates encoder `θ_m`; compute classifier gradient `g = ∇_Θ L^m`.
  3. Compute entropies `H(p^{other})` from a pass over the other modality's current batch.
  4. Select top-`λ^{other}` hard samples from other modality, compute their average classifier gradient `g_p`.
  5. Solve QP: `ẽg = g + v * g_p` with `v = max(0, (ϵ - g^⊤g_p)/||g_p||^2)`.
  6. Update shared classifier `Θ ← Θ - η * ẽg`.
  7. Switch active modality, repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Pros: Explicitly targets conflict at interaction point; uses hard-sample gradients for potentially more robust guidance; maintains unimodal encoder independence.
  - Cons: Computational overhead from two forward/backward passes per batch? (One for entropy on other modality, one for the active modality's loss). QP solve is cheap (closed-form). Hyperparameters `λ^I, λ^T` and `ϵ` require tuning; `ϵ=0` induces orthogonalization (per Fig 5), non-zero encourages alignment.
  - vs. MLA: MLA orthogonalizes all gradients, likely destroying useful overlap. GAAL aligns only *conflicting* gradients (`cosα<0`) and uses a soft constraint (`ϵ`), preserving synergy.
  - vs. Joint Learning + OGM/MMPareto: Alternating decouples encoder updates, which OGM/MMPareto struggle with in joint training.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Unimodal collapse: One modality's unimodal accuracy drops significantly below its standalone baseline. Could indicate excessive alignment (`ϵ` too large) or poor `g_p` (wrong `λ` or misestimated uncertainty).
  - Unchanged gradient conflict: Post-surgery cosine similarity between `g` and `g_p` remains negative on average. Check if `g^⊤g_p` is often >> `ϵ`, making `v=0` (no modification). Implies `ϵ` is too large or conflict severity is underestimated.
  - Convergence instability: Loss curves show oscillating or increasing unimodal losses during alternating steps. Suggests `ẽg` is oscillatory; may need smaller learning rate for `Θ` or adjustment of `λ` (too few samples in `g_p`).
  - Sensitivity to `λ`: Performance drops sharply when `λ^I, λ^T` move away from optimal. Indicates brittleness to hard-sample selection rate.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Gradient Alignment Verification:** Instrument training to log `S(g, g_p)` before and after surgery for the shared classifier across batches. Verify median/mean cosine similarity shifts from negative to near/above `ϵ`. Also log for encoder gradients to confirm they remain decoupled (should be unaffected by surgery).
  2. **Ablation of Uncertainty Guidance:** Replace the entropy-based `g_p` selection with (a) random sample selection from the other modality, (b) *certain* (low-entropy) sample selection. Expected: entropy-based (high-uncertainty) yields best performance, validating the "hard sample" hypothesis.
  3. **Constraint Margin (`ϵ`) Sweep:** Systematically vary `ϵ` (e.g., 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0 as in Fig 5) on a held-out validation set. Measure both multimodal and unimodal accuracies. Expected: non-zero `ϵ` improves over `ϵ=0` (orthogonalization), but too high `ϵ` harms by forcing alignment even on non-conflicting gradients. Find the sweet spot.

## Limitations
- Alternating learning alone insufficient; gradient conflicts persist in the shared classifier, requiring surgery.
- Sensitivity to hyperparameters (`λ`, `ϵ`) and entropy-based uncertainty calibration; performance drops sharply with suboptimal settings.
- Evaluation limited to three datasets (DVM, SUNAttribute, CelebA); generalizability to other modalities (e.g., audio, text) or missing modalities beyond tabular is unknown.
## Confidence
- Alternating decouples encoder gradients: Medium (partial evidence, conflict persists in classifier).
- Uncertainty-based gradient surgery resolves conflicts: Medium (compelling results but hyperparameter-sensitive).
- Overall multimodal accuracy gains: High (SOTA results on three datasets, but limited domain diversity).
## Next Checks
1. Log cosine similarity between `g` and `g_p` pre/post surgery; verify shift to ≥ `ϵ` and encoder gradients unaffected.
2. Ablate uncertainty guidance: replace entropy-based selection with random or low-entropy samples; expect performance drop.
3. Sweep `ϵ` on validation set; identify optimal range balancing alignment and synergy, and check if too high `ϵ` harms unimodal performance.
