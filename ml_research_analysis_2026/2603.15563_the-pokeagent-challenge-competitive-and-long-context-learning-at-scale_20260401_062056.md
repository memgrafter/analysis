# The PokeAgent Challenge: Competitive and Long-Context Learning at Scale

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.15563  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15563  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Dual-track benchmark (20M+ battle trajectories); RL agents achieve ~80% GXE in Gen1 OU battling, speedrunning winner completes first gym in 40:13, and Pokémon battling shows near-orthogonality to standard LLM benchmarks (max Spearman ρ=0.77, mean |ρ|=0.45).

## Executive Summary
The PokeAgent Challenge introduces a large-scale, dual-track benchmark built on Pokémon to simultaneously stress partial observability, game-theoretic reasoning, and long-horizon planning. The Competitive Battling track adapts the standard Pokémon Showdown engine, providing over 20 million battle trajectories and a suite of RL, heuristic, and LLM baselines. The RPG Speedrunning track supplies an open-source multi-agent orchestration system for controlling Pokémon Emerald via an emulator, with milestones such as defeating the first gym leader. The benchmark underpinned a NeurIPS 2025 competition attracting over 100 teams. Results reveal a persistent gap: specialist RL agents dominate harnessed LLMs in battling, while speedrunning remains unsolved for raw frontier models without extensive scaffolding. The benchmark’s orthogonality to existing LLM evaluations highlights its unique value for measuring advanced reasoning.

## Method Summary
The challenge infrastructure comprises two standardized tracks. Competitive Battling runs on a modified Pokémon Showdown server, using a dataset of 4 million human and 18 million synthetic battles. Agents receive textual/graphical state representations and act under partial information about the opponent; performance is aggregated via FH‑BT and Glicko‑1 ratings into a GXE score (expected win rate vs. random). RPG Speedrunning employs a multi-agent orchestrator that dispatches sub‑agents (battle, reflection, verification) and tools (game control, A* pathfinding, knowledge base, milestone tracker) to a VLM backend (Gemini/OpenRouter) which controls the mGBA emulator. Evaluation measures wall‑clock time and step count to complete the first gym. Both tracks release baselines: Metamon RL and PokeChamp LLM harness for battling; scripted policy distillation (SPD) and PPO for speedrunning.

## Key Results
- Top RL agents reach ~80% GXE in Gen1 OU and ~71% in Gen9 OU, comparable to strong humans but not superhuman.
- The winning speedrunning agent (SPD) completes the first gym in 40:13, roughly double the human world record; no raw frontier model succeeds without a harness.
- Pokémon battling performance is nearly orthogonal to 49 standard LLM benchmarks (max Spearman ρ=0.77, mean |ρ|=0.45), underscoring the benchmark’s distinct evaluation axis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The PokeAgent Challenge’s impact stems from three interconnected factors. First, its dual-track design uniquely targets partial observability, game‑theoretic reasoning, and long‑horizon planning simultaneously. Battling forces belief tracking and adversarial decision‑making under hidden information; speedrunning demands sequential decisions over thousands of steps with sparse rewards. This pairing ensures that progress on one track does not trivially transfer to the other, creating a multifaceted stress test. Second, standardized infrastructure—20M+ trajectories, curated team datasets, an open‑source multi‑agent orchestration system, and robust rating engines—removes implementation heterogeneity and enables fair comparisons across RL, LLM, and hybrid approaches. Third, orthogonality analysis shows that performance on standard LLM benchmarks poorly predicts battling success (low correlations), revealing that the benchmark measures strategic reasoning under adversarial partial information that most existing suites do not capture.

## Foundational Learning
- **Partial‑Observation Reasoning**  
  *Why needed:* In the Battling Track, the opponent’s team and move pool are hidden. Agents must maintain belief distributions and infer opponent state from observed actions and damage rolls.  
  *Quick check:* What techniques (e.g., damage roll grouping, usage statistics) does the paper employ to infer opponent sets, and how do they reduce sample complexity?

- **Long‑Horizon Credit Assignment**  
  *Why needed:* The Speedrunning Track involves thousands of actions with only milestone‑based rewards (e.g., gym badge). Agents must learn temporally extended behaviors without dense supervision.  
  *Quick check:* How does the winning scripted policy distillation agent overcome sparsity, and why is additional RL refinement still necessary?

- **Benchmark Standardization**  
  *Why needed:* Consistent evaluation protocols are essential to compare diverse methods (RL, LLM, hybrid) fairly.  
  *Quick check:* What are the three pillars of standardization (datasets, baselines, evaluation system) and how do they prevent common evaluation pitfalls?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Battling Track: Showdown server ↔ replay dataset ↔ baseline agents (Metamon RL, PokeChamp LLM harness) ↔ rating engines (FH‑BT, Glicko‑1) → live leaderboard.  
Speedrunning Track: Multi‑agent orchestrator → sub‑agents/tools (battle, reflection, verification, game control, A* pathfinding, knowledge base, milestone tracker) → VLM backend (Gemini/OpenRouter) → emulator interface → milestone‑based evaluator.

**Critical path**  
- Speedrunning: Orchestrator initializes long‑term plan → per step parse visual frame + partial state → dispatch to appropriate sub‑agent/tool → execute action via button press → update memory & milestone status → compact context if needed.  
- Battling: Agent connects to Showdown → selects team → per turn observe full state (with private info as perspective) → infer opponent state → select action (move/switch) → receive outcome → update.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Real‑time vs. deliberative: Battling timer favors efficiency; extended timer for LLMs increases wall‑clock but improves decision quality. Speedrunning measures wall‑clock, penalizing slow inference.  
- State representation: Speedrunning agents may choose raw pixels vs. parsed state; baselines use parsed state to focus planning, while pure VLMs struggle.  
- Rating metrics: FH‑BT is robust for small agent pools but requires many battles; Glicko‑1 supports online updates but may diverge from batch MLE.

**Failure signatures**  
- Battling (LLM): Panic behavior after bad turn, timeouts, excessive fallback to heuristics, recursive verification loops in CoT.  
- Speedrunning: Navigation loops (oscillating between tiles), milestone confusion, context overflow, excessive token usage.  
- Common: Catastrophic forgetting in RL, overfitting to specific team archetypes.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Run Metamon SyntheticRL‑V2 (Gen1 OU) and the PokéAgent harness (Gemini Flash, speedrunning) to recover reported metrics (~80% GXE, ~40:13 gym time) and validate evaluation scripts.  
2. **Orthogonality check:** For 10–20 models, collect GXE scores and existing LLM benchmark scores; compute Spearman and SVD to verify the orthogonality claim holds.  
3. **Harness ablation:** In speedrunning, disable the reflection sub‑agent or context compaction; measure impact on completion rate and wall‑clock time to isolate each component’s contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **VLM‑SLAM for RPGs:** How can we develop robust localization, action‑distance estimation, and objective detection through language‑vision interfaces? Current VLMs lack internal spatial models, causing navigation failures.  
- **Hybrid LLM+RL in imperfect‑information games:** Can hybrid approaches close the ~10% GXE gap between specialist RL and harnessed LLMs in battling? RL’s sample efficiency and precise probability estimation remain advantages.  
- **Open‑source full‑game speedrunning:** Can open‑source models (≤70B) complete the full Pokémon Emerald story using the standardized framework? All current successes rely on proprietary frontier models.  
- **Approaching human speedrun times:** What architectural improvements in navigation efficiency, obstacle avoidance, and objective sequencing are needed to reach within 1.5× of the human world record? The best agent is 2.2× slower.

## Limitations
- Environment specifications (exact Pokémon Emerald ROM version, required patches) are not fully specified, hindering exact offline replication.  
- LLM harness details (prompt templates, inference settings) are under‑specified; small variations may cause large performance differences.  
- Critical resources (large model checkpoints) are not publicly hosted, and reliance on live evaluation servers may prevent fully offline benchmarking.  
- Post‑competition infrastructure maintenance and updates remain unclear, potentially limiting long‑term adoption.

## Confidence
- Dual‑track design & standardization infrastructure: **High** (concrete implementation, strong community uptake).  
- Orthogonality of Pokémon battling to LLM benchmarks: **Medium** (supported by current data but may shift with new model architectures).  
- Superhuman performance gap remains: **Medium** (RL approaches strong human but not superhuman; human baselines not fully detailed).  
- Open‑source model progress on speedrunning: **Low** (no open‑source success yet, dependent on future breakthroughs).
