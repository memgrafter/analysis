# Learning Recursive Multi-Scale Representations for Irregular Multivariate Time Series Forecasting
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21498
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21498
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** ReIMTS achieves an average 27.1% reduction in MSE compared to baseline models across five IMTS datasets, outperforming 26 competitive methods.

## Executive Summary
Irregular multivariate time series (IMTS) forecasting is challenged by variable sampling intervals and multi-scale dependencies. Existing multi-scale approaches often resample to create coarser series, disrupting valuable sampling pattern information. This paper introduces ReIMTS, a recursive multi-scale model that avoids resampling by recursively splitting each sample into subsamples with progressively shorter time periods while preserving original timestamps. Backbone encoders process each scale to capture dependencies, and an irregularity-aware fusion mechanism adaptively combines global-to-local representations based on sampling patterns. Tested on five IMTS datasets (MIMIC-III, MIMIC-IV, PhysioNet’12, Human Activity, USHCN) with six backbone models (PrimeNet, mTAN, TimeCHEAT, GRU-D, Raindrop, GraFITi), ReIMTS yields an average error reduction of 27.1% in MSE over the respective baselines, outperforms 26 competitive methods, and maintains comparable training efficiency with minimal memory overhead.

## Method Summary
ReIMTS operates in three stages. First, given an irregular multivariate time series with variable timestamps, it recursively decomposes the series into a set of subsamples covering progressively shorter time windows while strictly preserving the original timestamps to retain irregularity information. This recursive splitting yields a hierarchy of scales from the full series down to finer periods. Second, for each scale, a backbone encoder processes the subsample to learn representations that capture temporal dependencies relevant to that temporal granularity; the encoders are model-agnostic, allowing integration with various state-of-the-art architectures. Third, an irregularity-aware fusion module combines the representations from all scales, adaptively weighting contributions based on the observed sampling patterns (e.g., density, gaps). The fused representation is then used for forecasting. By avoiding resampling, ReIMTS maintains the integrity of the original irregular sampling, and the multi-scale approach effectively models both long-term trends and short-term dynamics.

## Key Results
- Average MSE reduction of 27.1% over six backbone models.
- Outperforms 26 competitive methods on five datasets.
- Comparable training efficiency with negligible memory overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The recursive decomposition ensures that the original sampling irregularity is retained at every scale, allowing the model to learn from the true temporal distribution of observations. Multi-scale processing captures dependencies across different time horizons: coarser scales reveal long-term trends, while finer scales capture short-term variations. The irregularity-aware fusion adapts to the sampling density, effectively down-weighting unreliable intervals and up-weighting dense observations, leading to robust representations. This combination addresses the key challenges of IMTS forecasting—variable intervals and multi-scale dependencies—without the information loss caused by resampling.

## Foundational Learning
- **Irregular Multivariate Time Series (IMTS):** Time series with multiple variables where observations are recorded at irregular timestamps. *Why needed:* Standard models assume regular intervals; ignoring irregularity biases learning. *Quick check:* Does the dataset have variable time gaps between consecutive recordings?
- **Recursive Multi-Scale Decomposition:** Splitting a time series into subsamples of progressively shorter durations while preserving original timestamps. *Why needed:* Generates multiple temporal scales without resampling, maintaining irregularity. *Quick check:* Can we repeatedly partition the series by halving the time window?
- **Irregularity-Aware Fusion:** Mechanism that weights representations from different scales based on sampling pattern characteristics. *Why needed:* Not all scales contribute equally; fusion must account for data density and gaps. *Quick check:* Does the fusion module incorporate metrics like observation frequency per scale?
- **Backbone Encoders:** Base temporal models (e.g., GRU, Transformer) used to encode each scale. *Why needed:* Leverage powerful sequence modeling architectures tailored to each scale’s resolution. *Quick check:* Can the backbone handle variable-length sequences with missing values?
- **Model-Agnostic Framework:** ReIMTS can incorporate various backbone models without architectural change. *Why needed:* Allows flexibility and reuse of existing strong encoders. *Quick check:* Does adding a new encoder require only plugging it into the scale-wise processing step?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Input IMTS → Recursive Splitter → Scale Encoders (multiple) → Fusion Module → Forecast Output.

**Critical Path:** Data begins as the original irregular series. The Recursive Splitter generates a set of subsamples at increasing time resolutions (e.g., whole series, half-period, quarter-period). Each subsample is fed into its corresponding Scale Encoder (backbone) to produce a representation. All representations are then passed to the Fusion Module, which weighs and aggregates them into a unified representation. Finally, a predictor head maps the fused representation to the forecast values.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- Depth of recursion: More levels increase multi-scale coverage but raise computational cost and memory usage.  
- Preservation of timestamps vs. abstraction: Keeping original timestamps maintains irregularity information but may complicate handling at coarser scales; the model balances by operating directly on irregular inputs at each scale.  
- Fusion complexity: A more sophisticated fusion can better adapt to sampling patterns but adds parameters and risk of overfitting.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Degraded performance when recursion depth is too low (insufficient scale diversity) or too high (diminishing returns, overfitting).  
- Inconsistent weighting in the fusion module if sampling patterns are extreme (e.g., extremely sparse data) leading to dominance of one scale.  
- Memory overflow when processing many scales or long sequences due to storing intermediate subsamples.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. Ablate recursive decomposition by replacing it with fixed resampling at multiple regular intervals (e.g., hourly, daily) and compare performance to isolate the benefit of preserving irregularity.  
2. Replace the irregularity-aware fusion with a simple averaging or concatenation of scale representations to assess the contribution of adaptive weighting.  
3. Vary the maximum recursion depth (e.g., 1, 2, 3, 4) to study the tradeoff between multi-scale coverage and computational overhead, identifying the optimal depth for different dataset characteristics.
