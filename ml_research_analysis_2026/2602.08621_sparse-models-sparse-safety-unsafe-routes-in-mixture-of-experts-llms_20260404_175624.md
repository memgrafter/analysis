# Sparse Models, Sparse Safety: Unsafe Routes in Mixture-of-Experts LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08621
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08621
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Sparse safety in MoE LLMs: manipulating a few critical routers can drastically increase harmful output generation (ASR up to 0.98).

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates safety in Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) large language models and discovers that safety is sparse: only a small number of routers need to be manipulated to trigger harmful outputs. The authors introduce RoSais, a metric that identifies safety-critical routers by measuring the increase in affirmative token probability when a router is masked. Experiments on DeepSeek-V2-Lite show that masking just five high‑RoSais routers raises attack success rate on JailbreakBench from 0.15 to 0.79. Furthermore, they develop F‑SOUR, a fine‑grained stochastic optimization that searches token‑layer‑wise routes, achieving average ASR of 0.90 on JailbreakBench and 0.98 on AdvBench across four MoE families. These results reveal a novel attack surface in MoE routing and underscore the need for safety‑aware router training and route auditing.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two main contributions: the RoSais metric and the F‑SOUR attack method. RoSais (Router Safety Significance) quantifies the importance of each router for safety by masking the router and measuring the change in probability of affirmative tokens (e.g., “yes”) in response to harmful prompts. Higher increases indicate that the router contributes to refusing harmful requests. F‑SOUR (Fine‑grained Stochastic Optimization for Unsafe Routes) is an attack that optimizes the selection of experts at the token and layer level to maximize the likelihood of generating harmful content. It uses stochastic gradient‑based search to find routes that bypass safety‑critical routers identified by RoSais. The methods are evaluated on multiple MoE models using jailbreak benchmarks.

## Key Results
- Masking only five high‑RoSais routers in DeepSeek-V2‑Lite increases ASR on JailbreakBench from 0.15 (baseline) to 0.79.
- F‑SOUR achieves average ASR of 0.90 on JailbreakBench and 0.98 on AdvBench across four MoE model families.
- Only a small fraction of routers (often <5%) are responsible for safety‑critical decisions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Safety in MoE models is concentrated in a few routers. RoSais identifies these routers by masking each router and observing the increase in affirmative token probability when processing harmful prompts. This indicates that the masked router was previously contributing to a safe refusal. F‑SOUR then uses stochastic optimization to search for token‑layer‑wise routes through the MoE that avoid safety‑critical routers and instead select experts that lead to harmful outputs. By bypassing the routers that enforce refusals, the model can be coerced into generating dangerous content. The sparsity of safety makes the attack highly efficient, requiring manipulation of only a handful of routers.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Token -> Router (per layer) -> Selected Experts -> Aggregation -> Next Layer (repeat) -> Output Head -> Token Prediction.
- **Critical path:** Routing decisions at each layer; manipulating specific routers can redirect the token flow through harmful pathways.
- **Design tradeoffs:** MoE balances model capacity (more experts) against inference cost (sparsity). Safety is rarely a design objective for routers, leading to unconstrained routing that can be exploited.
- **Failure signatures:** High RoSais scores indicate safety‑critical routers; masking these leads to large ASR increases. F‑SOUR successfully finds routes that avoid these routers and yield high affirmative token probabilities.

## Limitations
- Model diversity limited: evaluated on only a small subset of MoE architectures; unknown if sparsity holds across router types and sparsity levels.
- Benchmark narrowness: only two adversarial benchmarks used; safety on other dimensions (truthfulness, bias, toxicity) remains unexplored.
- White‑box assumption: RoSais and F‑SOUR require full model access; effectiveness under black‑box or transfer attacks is uncertain.

## Confidence
- Sparse safety (few routers critical): Medium
- RoSais metric identifies critical routers: Medium
- F‑SOUR achieves high ASR (0.90/0.98): Medium
- Novel attack surface in MoE routing: Medium
