# Explanation Generation for Contradiction Reconciliation with LLMs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.22735
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22735
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Most LLMs struggle with reconciliatory explanation generation; proprietary models achieve ~31–40% success, top open-weight models ~26%.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces reconciliatory explanation generation (REG), a task requiring models to produce explanations that make two contradictory statements compatible, moving beyond traditional NLI arbitration. By repurposing existing NLI datasets with human label disagreements, the authors construct an evaluation framework where LLM judges assess explanation effectiveness and coherence. Testing 18 LLMs shows widespread difficulty, with proprietary models (GPT‑5) reaching 31–40% success and the best open-weight model (Llama‑3.3‑70B) only ~26%. Enabling chain‑of‑thought (“thinking”) improves performance for mid‑sized models but not smaller ones, and a weak negative correlation suggests that contradictions harder for humans are also harder for models. The results highlight a significant reasoning gap in current LLMs.

## Method Summary
The authors transform NLI datasets containing human disagreement into REG instances, where each input consists of two contradictory statements and the model must generate an explanation that reconciles them. Explanation quality is evaluated automatically using LLM judges that output NLI‑style judgments (entailment, contradiction, neutral) to measure effectiveness and coherence. This scalable metric avoids costly human evaluation while approximating reconciliation success. The study tests 18 LLMs of varying sizes and types, with and without chain‑of‑thought prompting, to analyze performance on this reasoning task.

## Key Results
- LLMs perform poorly on REG; top models (GPT‑5) achieve only 31–40% success, and the best open‑weight (Llama‑3.3‑70B) reaches ~26%.
- Chain‑of‑thought prompting benefits mid‑sized models but plateaus or harms smaller models.
- A weak negative correlation between human disagreement and model performance indicates that contradictions more challenging for people are also harder for models to reconcile.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
REG forces models to reason about both statements simultaneously and craft a narrative that resolves the conflict, rather than simply classifying the relation. The LLM‑as‑judge evaluation leverages the same models’ ability to assess logical consistency, providing a scalable proxy for explanation quality. The task’s design, based on real human disagreements, ensures that successful explanations must capture nuanced reasoning that goes beyond surface‑level pattern matching.

## Foundational Learning
**Contradiction & Reconciliation**  
*Why needed*: REG hinges on understanding logical opposition and constructing a resolution.  
*Quick check*: Can a model identify when two statements cannot both be true under common knowledge?  

**Natural Language Inference (NLI)**  
*Why needed*: REG repurposes NLI data and uses NLI judgments for evaluation.  
*Quick check*: Does the model correctly classify entailment, contradiction, and neutral relations?  

**Explanation Generation**  
*Why needed*: The task requires producing coherent, natural language explanations, not just labels.  
*Quick check*: Are generated explanations fluent, on‑topic, and logically structured?  

**LLM‑as‑Judge**  
*Why needed*: Automatic evaluation scales to large datasets while approximating human judgment.  
*Quick check*: Do LLM judges correlate with human assessments of explanation quality?  

**Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT)**  
*Why needed*: Explicit reasoning steps (“thinking”) may improve reconciliation ability.  
*Quick check*: Does prompting the model to think step‑by‑step increase success rates?  

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Statement Pair -> Explanation Generator (LLM) -> NLI Judge (LLM) -> Score  

**Critical Path**  
1. Input: Two contradictory statements.  
2. Generator: LLM produces an explanation.  
3. Judge: LLM evaluates the explanation’s consistency with both statements (NLI judgment).  
4. Scoring: Judgment mapped to success (e.g., entailment or neutral counts as reconciled).  

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- LLM judges vs. human evaluators: Scalability vs. validation.  
- Dataset conversion: Real human disagreements provide difficulty but may include noise.  
- Model size vs. prompting: Larger models generally stronger, but CoT benefits may vary.  

**Failure Signatures**  
- Explanation ignores one statement or fails to resolve conflict.  
- Judge outputs inconsistent or contradictory judgments.  
- Performance drops for smaller models when CoT is enabled.  

**First Experiments**  
1. Baseline evaluation of 18 LLMs on REG dataset without CoT.  
2. Introduce CoT prompting for each model and compare success rates.  
3. Analyze correlation between model performance and human disagreement level on NLI items.

## Limitations
- NLI‑to‑REG conversion may introduce noise into the evaluation data.  
- LLM judges lack human validation and may exhibit bias.  
- The study covers only 18 models and provides no human baseline, making absolute scores uncertain.

## Confidence
- LLM judge metric: Low  
- Most LLMs struggle: Medium  
- Proprietary > open‑weight: Medium  
- “Thinking” helps mid‑sized only: Medium  
- Negative correlation: Low
