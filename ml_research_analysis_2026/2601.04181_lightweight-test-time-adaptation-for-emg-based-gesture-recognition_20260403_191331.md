# Lightweight Test-Time Adaptation for EMG-Based Gesture Recognition

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04181
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04181
- **Reference Count:** 32
- **Primary Result:** Test-time adaptation lifts inter-session EMG gesture recognition accuracy from a baseline 56.61% to up to 80.30% with meta-learning, while maintaining a lightweight ~50k parameter footprint.

## Executive Summary
Inter-session signal drift severely degrades EMG-based gesture recognition when models are deployed on new recording days. This paper tackles the problem with a test-time adaptation (TTA) framework built on a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) backbone. Three adaptation strategies are introduced: (1) causal adaptive batch normalization (AdaBN) for unsupervised statistical alignment, (2) Gaussian Mixture Model alignment combined with experience replay (GMM+DER) to prevent catastrophic forgetting, and (3) meta-learning (MAML) with low-rank adaptation for rapid few-shot calibration. Evaluated on NinaPro DB6, the baseline TCN achieves ~85% intra-session but only 56.61% inter-session accuracy. TTA yields substantial gains: AdaBN reaches 68.93%, GMM+DER 69.78% (with stable adaptation under limited data), and MAML achieves 80.30% in few-shot regimes—all with minimal computational overhead.

## Method Summary
The authors train a TCN (~47k parameters) on source sessions (first 5 of NinaPro DB6). For test-time adaptation, they explore three methods: (1) **AdaBN** updates batch normalization statistics online from a streaming prefix of unlabeled target data, blending the target moments with source EMA statistics via a mixing coefficient that grows with observed samples. (2) **GMM+DER** fits a Gaussian Mixture Model to source features from an intermediate layer; at test-time, only a low-rank LoRA module inserted in one TCN block is updated using a Sliced Wasserstein Discrepancy loss between target features and GMM samples, regularized by Dark Experience Replay (DER) that stores source exemplars to maintain previous knowledge. (3) **MAML** meta-learns a LoRA initialization such that a few gradient steps on a small labeled support set from a new session produce strong performance; DER can be added to stabilize. All approaches operate during inference with negligible compute cost relative to the forward pass.

## Key Results
- Baseline TCN: ~85% intra-session, **56.61%** inter-session accuracy.
- AdaBN: **68.93%** inter-session accuracy (unsupervised, continuous updates).
- GMM+DER: **69.78%** inter-session accuracy with robust adaptation under very limited target data.
- MAML: **80.30%** inter-session accuracy in few-shot regimes (requires brief labeled calibration).
- All methods preserve a lightweight footprint (~50k parameters) and low test-time overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Causal adaptive batch normalization improves inter-session accuracy by online alignment of feature distribution statistics.
- **Mechanism:** The frozen pre-trained TCN uses source-domain EMA BN statistics during normal operation. At test-time on a new session, a streaming prefix of unlabeled EMG data is used to compute online per-channel mean and variance for each BN layer via Welford’s incremental method. These target statistics are blended with the source EMA using a mixing coefficient α(t)=min(1, β·t/N) that increases with observed samples. The blended statistics replace the static EMA for subsequent normalization.
- **Core assumption:** The primary cause of inter-session degradation is a distribution shift in the BN layer activations, which can be corrected by adjusting normalization moments. The shift is slowly varying and can be estimated from a small prefix.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “causal adaptive batch normalization for real-time statistical alignment”; Section II-D1 derives causal BN update equations (Eq. 1-10) and blending schedule.
- **Break condition:** If the initial prefix is unrepresentative (e.g., mostly rest), early target statistics are misleading. Excessive β causes over-correction, degrading performance on both current and held-out sessions. The method only aligns first-order statistics (mean/variance).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Replay-regularized statistical alignment via GMM and LoRA yields stable cross-session robustness under limited data by preventing catastrophic forgetting of source representations.
- **Mechanism:** A small fixed-size buffer `Der` stores source-domain exemplars (data and model outputs). At test-time on a new session, a low-rank LoRA module is injected into one intermediate TCN block. The model extracts features `z` from a streaming buffer `Z_τ`. A GMM is fitted to source features (`G_src`); samples are drawn and their distribution matched to `Z_τ` using Sliced Wasserstein Discrepancy (SWD). The alignment loss `L_align` plus a DER term `L_er` (penalizing deviation of current outputs on `Der` from stored outputs) updates only the LoRA parameters `ϕ`.
- **Core assumption:** (1) A low-rank subspace suffices for adaptation, avoiding full-network overfitting. (2) Storing source outputs (dark experience) regularizes updates enough to maintain performance on all previously seen session distributions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) alignment combined with experience replay (DER)”; Section II-D2 formulates LoRA injection, GMM/SWD loss, and DER loss (Eq. 11-16).
- **Break condition:** If `Der` is too small or non-representative, adaptation over-specializes to the current session, harming held-out performance. The GMM assumption of multimodally Gaussian source features may not hold for complex EMG latent spaces.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Gradient-based meta-learning (MAML) with LoRA enables rapid few-shot calibration by learning an initialization adaptable to new sessions with minimal labeled data.
- **Mechanism:** During meta-training, each source session is a task. The model (with frozen backbone `θ` and LoRA `ϕ`) takes `K` gradient steps on a *support* set of few labeled examples to obtain adapted parameters `ϕ'_s`. The meta-loss on the corresponding *query* set updates the shared initialization `(θ, ϕ)` via meta-gradients. At test-time on a new session, only `K` steps on a few labeled calibration examples adapt the LoRA from the meta-initialization. DER can be added to stabilize.
- **Core assumption:** Session-to-session variability is learnable via gradient-based updates from a few examples; inner-loop adaptation dynamics are consistent across sessions, so optimizing for fast adaptation on source sessions transfers to unseen targets.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract cites “meta-learning (MAML) for rapid, few-shot calibration”; Section II-D3 details the MAML algorithm (Eq. 17-20) and combination with DER.
- **Break condition:** Requires sparse labels at test-time. If the few-shot support set is unbalanced or noisy, meta-adaptation can overfit to idiosyncrasies. Second-order MAML is sensitive to step count and may overshoot if hyperparameters are poorly chosen.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Test-Time Training (TTT) / Test-Time Adaptation (TTA)
- **Why needed here:** Adaptation must occur *during* inference because EMG signals drift due to electrode shift and muscle fatigue. Offline transfer learning cannot capture the continuous non-stationarity encountered in real-world prosthetic use.
- **Quick check question:** If you update the model’s BN statistics online using the first 10 seconds of a new session’s data, are you performing TTT or traditional transfer learning? What’s the key distinction?

### Concept: Catastrophic Forgetting & Replay (DER)
- **Why needed here:** Any parameter update risks erasing knowledge from the diverse source sessions. Experience replay (storing source examples/activations) provides a regularization signal to maintain performance on *all* previously seen session distributions, not just the current one.
- **Quick check question:** In the DER loss `L_er = ||ŷ(x_er) - ŷ_er||^2`, what do `ŷ_er` (stored outputs) and `ŷ(x_er)` (current outputs) represent, and why is matching them helpful?

### Concept: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for Efficient Updates
- **Why needed here:** Full-network gradient updates on a streaming, resource-constrained wearable are infeasible. LoRA constrains adaptation to a small low-rank matrix added to weight updates, drastically reducing memory and compute while preserving expressivity.
- **Quick check question:** If a LoRA module has rank `r=4` and the original convolutional weight matrix is `[out, in, kernel]`, what is the approximate parameter reduction factor? What layer(s) did the paper inject LoRA into for best performance?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input (raw 14‑channel sEMG stream) -> TCN backbone (M residual causal‑conv blocks with dilation) -> Output head (linear projection to 8 classes). Adaptation injection points: all BN layers (AdaBN); one intermediate block (4th) for LoRA (GMM+DER/Meta); LoRA parameters only (others frozen). Streaming buffer accumulates recent samples/features for online statistics or alignment. Replay buffer `Der` stores source exemplars (x, y, ŷ).

**Critical path (inference with adaptation):**  
1. Receive new sample `x_t`.  
2. Forward pass through TCN. If AdaBN: update causal BN stats (mean/var) from all seen samples `x_{1:t}` and blend with source EMA.  
3. If using statistical alignment/meta: accumulate features `Z_τ` from the target intermediate layer into a buffer; periodically (every K steps or after N samples) compute alignment loss (SWD for GMM) on `Z_τ` vs. source GMM samples, add DER loss, and update only LoRA parameters via one gradient step.  
4. Output gesture prediction from final layer.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Adaptation strength vs. forgetting:** Aggressive updates (large β for AdaBN, high alignment weight α) improve current-session accuracy faster but degrade held‑out session performance. Replay buffer size |Der| trades memory for stability.  
- **Compute vs. data efficiency:** Meta‑learning gives best few‑shot gains but requires inner‑loop backpropagation through the full network (higher MMAC). AdaBN is cheapest but needs a longer streaming prefix for stable statistics. GMM+DER is mid‑cost and best for low‑data/unbalanced streams.  
- **Supervision requirement:** AdaBN/GMM+DER are unsupervised. Meta‑learning requires a brief labeled calibration phase.  
- **LoRA placement:** Injecting into a single intermediate block (after the 4th block) performed best; injecting into early layers or multiple blocks increased cost without accuracy gain.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **AdaBN:** Error rate initially drops below baseline with very few adaptation samples. Oscillations in online error for high β. Severe drop on held‑out sessions with large α.  
- **GMM+DER:** If `Der` is empty or tiny, performance on non‑adapted sessions decays as adaptation proceeds. If SWD computation is unstable due to poor GMM fit, alignment loss may not be meaningful.  
- **Meta:** Without DER, accuracy on held‑out sessions can plummet after a few adaptation steps. High variance across few‑shot runs, especially with 1–2 repetitions. Second‑order MAML may diverge with incorrect learning rate.  
- **General:** All methods assume the target session drift lies within the manifold of source sessions; a truly novel electrode configuration or muscle injury may not be correctable.

**First 3 experiments (to validate implementation):**  
1. **Baseline sanity check:** Train TCN on source sessions (first 5 of NinaPro DB6). Report intra‑session (test within same session) and inter‑session (test on remaining 5 sessions) majority‑voted active gesture accuracy. Expect ~85% intra, ~56% inter.  
2. **AdaBN functionality:** Implement causal BN update (Welford’s method) and blending schedule. For a fixed target session, vary β and adaptation prefix length (e.g., 2, 4, 7, 14 repetitions). Plot error rate on current session vs. prefix length. Expect a U‑shaped curve: improvement after sufficient data, degradation with over‑aggressive β/short prefix.  
3. **GMM+DER vs. no‑DER:** Implement LoRA (rank 4) in the 4th TCN block; fit source GMM on features from that block; use SWD loss. Compare adaptation with and without a DER buffer (store 50 source exemplars) on both current and held‑out target sessions after adapting with 4 repetitions. Expect DER to maintain held‑out accuracy while both variants improve current‑session performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Long‑term performance:** How do the adaptation strategies perform over extended periods (e.g., months) considering natural electrode degradation and muscle changes? The dataset only covers 5 days, limiting evaluation to short‑term drift. Longitudinal studies are needed.
2. **Coordination of mechanisms:** What is the optimal policy for combining continuous AdaBN updates with periodic replay‑regularized fine‑tuning to balance responsiveness and long‑term stability? A dynamic coordination mechanism (e.g., triggers based on divergence or performance) is required.
3. **OOD signalling:** Can divergence between source‑domain statistics and test‑time estimates reliably trigger adaptation and signal out‑of‑distribution conditions? Its sensitivity and specificity are unknown; controlled experiments correlating divergence metrics with performance drops are necessary.
4. **Replay buffer design for meta‑learning:** How does replay buffer size, class balance, and replay frequency affect the trade‑off between current‑session accuracy and retention on previous sessions when using meta‑learning? Systematic ablations are needed to optimize buffer design.

## Limitations
- **Unspecified details:** Exact TCN architecture (number of blocks, channel dimensions, dropout) and training hyperparameters (learning rate, epochs) are not reported, hindering exact replication.
- **TTA hyperparameters:** Critical values (AdaBN β schedule, GMM components, DER buffer size and loss weights, MAML inner/outer learning rates, number of inner steps) are absent, forcing search.
- **Dataset scope:** All claims are based solely on NinaPro DB6; generalization to other datasets, real‑time streaming, or Electrode shifts beyond training sessions remains untested.
- **Assumption limits:** All methods assume that target‑session drift lies within the source manifold; truly novel muscle conditions or hardware may cause catastrophic failures.

## Confidence
- **TTA improves inter‑session accuracy:** Medium – internal experiments show large gains, but reproducibility depends on unreported hyperparameters.
- **Mechanisms (AdaBN/GMM+DER/MAML) as described:** Medium – theoretical reasoning is sound, but lack of independent verification and sensitivity to unspecified parameters reduces certainty.
- **Lightweight footprint:** High – parameter count (~50k) is explicitly stated and computationally plausible.
