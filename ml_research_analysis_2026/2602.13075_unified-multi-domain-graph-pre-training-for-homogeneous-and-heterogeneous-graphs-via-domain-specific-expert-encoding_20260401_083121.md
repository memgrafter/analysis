# Unified Multi-Domain Graph Pre-training for Homogeneous and Heterogeneous Graphs via Domain-Specific Expert Encoding
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13075
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13075
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** GPH2 unifies homogeneous and heterogeneous graph pre-training via domain-specific expert encoding and achieves state-of-the-art few-shot node classification, outperforming the strongest baseline by 4.59% F1 on homogeneous graphs and 5.98% F1 on heterogeneous graphs.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces GPH2, a unified framework for multi-domain graph pre-training that handles both homogeneous and heterogeneous graphs without type-specific architectural modifications. It employs a multi-view construction to transform any graph into a set of adjacency matrices processed by a generic GNN, and uses independent domain-specific experts to avoid negative cross-domain interference during pre-training. For downstream tasks, a task-oriented fusion module with class-wise attention adaptively integrates expert predictions. Evaluated on nine datasets across few-shot node classification, GPH2 consistently surpasses previous state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating the effectiveness of its unified approach and adaptive fusion.

## Method Summary
GPH2 addresses the challenge of pre-training on a mixture of homogeneous and heterogeneous graphs by first constructing unified multi-view representations: homogeneous graphs are augmented via edge dropping, while heterogeneous graphs are rewired along meta-paths. Each source graph is assigned a separate expert encoder (e.g., a 2-layer SGC) that is pre-trained independently using a DGI-style Infomax objective on its views, thus isolating domain-specific knowledge. For a target downstream task, all experts encode the target graph (frozen), and their embeddings are aligned to a common space via linear projections. A shared classifier produces logits per expert, and a learnable class-attention mechanism computes adaptive weights for each expert-class pair, with orthogonality regularization to encourage diversity. The final prediction is a weighted sum of expert logits, trained on the few-shot labels.

## Key Results
- GPH2 achieves average Micro-F1 gains of 4.59% over the strongest homogeneous baseline (SCOPE) and 5.98% over the strongest heterogeneous baseline (HGPrompt) on few-shot node classification.
- Ablation studies confirm that domain-specific expert isolation and the alignment module are critical; removing either causes significant performance drops.
- The method shows robust transfer across graph types and domains, with class-wise attention effectively routing to the most relevant experts per class.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Unified Multi-View Graph Construction enables a single GNN encoder to process both homogeneous and heterogeneous graphs without type-specific architectural modifications.
- **Mechanism:** Homogeneous graphs are transformed into multiple augmented views via edge dropping, creating varied topological patterns. Heterogeneous graphs are transformed via meta-path-based rewiring, where each meta-path defines a semantically meaningful view. Both yield a set of adjacency matrices {𝐴(𝑝)} fed into the same GNN. Final embeddings are view-aggregated using attention.
- **Core assumption:** The multi-view abstraction removes explicit type-dependent operations from the encoder; the GNN only sees node features and adjacency matrices, making it agnostic to whether the original graph was homogeneous or heterogeneous.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "propose a Unified Multi-View Graph Construction that simultaneously encodes both without explicit graph-type-specific designs."
  - [section 3.2] "reformulates both homogeneous and heterogeneous graphs into a unified multi-view form... any GNN model can be employed as the encoder."
- **Break condition:** If the constructed views fail to preserve critical type-specific semantics (e.g., meta-paths in heterogeneous graphs do not capture meaningful paths, or edge dropping in homogeneous graphs destroys essential structural motifs), the unified encoder cannot learn effective representations for one or both graph types.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Domain-specific expert encoding prevents negative cross-domain interference during pre-training by isolating knowledge acquisition per source graph.
- **Mechanism:** Each pre-training graph (or set of similar graphs) is assigned an independent expert encoder (𝑓𝑖) with non-shared parameters. Experts are pre-trained in parallel on their respective graphs using a self-supervised objective (e.g., Infomax). No parameter sharing occurs across experts.
- **Core assumption:** Distribution shifts between domains (in node features, topology, semantics) are so severe that a single model trained on all domains would suffer from conflicting gradients and suboptimal convergence. Isolated training allows each expert to specialize.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "introduce domain-specific expert encoding. Each expert is independently pre-trained on a single graph... shielding the pre-training encoder from adverse effects of cross-domain discrepancies."
  - [section 3.3] "each expert is trained independently and in parallel, allowing it to specialize... without being affected by distribution shifts from others."
- **Break condition:** If domain discrepancies are minor or if the number of source graphs per domain is too small, expert isolation may waste capacity and miss opportunities for positive transfer; a shared encoder could perform as well or better.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Task-oriented expert fusion with class-wise attention adaptively integrates expert predictions based on per-class discriminative strength, mitigating the "one-size-fits-all" problem of uniform fusion.
- **Mechanism:** Downstream, all experts encode the target graph (frozen). Their embeddings are aligned via expert-specific linear projections. For a C-class task, C learnable class-attention vectors {𝑞𝑐} compute attention scores 𝛼(𝑐) 𝑖 ∝ exp(Z𝑖⊤𝑞𝑐) for each expert i and class c. Final class logits are weighted sums of expert logits. Orthogonality regularization on {𝑞𝑐} encourages diversity.
- **Core assumption:** Different experts excel at discriminating different subsets of classes (e.g., an expert pre-trained on citation networks is stronger for academic topic classes). Class-wise attention allows the model to route to the best expert per class.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.4] "experts often exhibit varying discriminative strengths across classes... adaptively select the most informative experts for each class."
  - [section 4.5] "experts pre-trained on academic networks had substantially higher attention weights than Freebase expert on ACM task," showing selection based on semantic compatibility.
- **Break condition:** If all experts perform similarly across all classes, or if the downstream task has very few classes, the attention mechanism may confer no benefit and could introduce instability. Orthogonality regularization might also force unnecessary separation if class semantics are inherently correlated.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multi-View Graph Construction**
  - **Why needed here:** To bridge the fundamental incompatibility (C1) between homogeneous graphs (single relation type) and heterogeneous graphs (multiple types/semantics) into a unified input format for a generic GNN.
  - **Quick check question:** How do edge dropping and meta-path rewiring produce analogous "views" that a GNN can process identically, despite originating from graph types with different semantic richness?

- **Concept: Domain-Specific Expert Isolation**
  - **Why needed here:** To mitigate amplified cross-domain shifts (C2) when mixing homogeneous and heterogeneous graphs, which have starkly different topological distributions (e.g., power-law vs. multi-type imbalanced degrees).
  - **Quick check question:** Why would training a single model on a mixture of homogeneous and heterogeneous graphs lead to worse representations than training separate experts? What specific gradients would conflict?

- **Concept: Class-Wise Attention Routing**
  - **Why needed here:** Because experts trained on different domains capture complementary semantics; a fixed fusion rule (averaging) would dilute the contribution of strong experts for certain classes. The attention mechanism is a soft, learnable routing policy.
  - **Quick check question:** In the fusion equation ℓ𝑣(𝑐) = ∑𝑖 𝛼(𝑐)𝑖 ℓ𝑖,𝑣(𝑐), what does a high 𝛼(𝑐)𝑖 for expert i on class c empirically indicate about expert i's pre-training graph relative to class c?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  1. **Unified Multi-View Constructor:** Takes raw graph (hom/het) → outputs {𝐺(𝑝)} (multiple adjacency matrices).  
  2. **Expert Encoders:** 𝑓𝑖: {𝐺(𝑝)} → 𝐻𝑖 (node embeddings). Independent parameters 𝜃𝑖.  
  3. **Expert-Specific Aligner:** 𝑊𝑖: 𝐻𝑖 → 𝑍𝑖 (shared embedding space).  
  4. **Task-Oriented Fusion Module:** Class-attention vectors {𝑞𝑐} compute 𝛼(𝑐)𝑖 from {𝑍𝑖}; aggregates logits ℓ𝑖,𝑣 to final ℓ𝑣.
- **Critical path:** Pre-training: For each source graph, construct views → train expert 𝑓𝑖 (parameters 𝜃𝑖, 𝑞𝑖) with Infomax. Downstream: Construct views for target → get frozen {𝑓𝑖(𝐺)} → align {𝑍𝑖 = 𝑊𝑖𝐻𝑖} → compute class-wise attention 𝛼(𝑐)𝑖 from {𝑍𝑖} and {𝑞𝑐} → fuse logits → train {𝑞𝑐, 𝑊𝑖, 𝜙} with cross-entropy + ortho.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Expert isolation vs. shared encoder:* Isolation avoids negative transfer but multiplies parameters and requires storing M experts. Shared encoder reduces footprint but risks catastrophic interference on heterogeneous+homogeneous mixes.
  - *View construction:* For homogeneous graphs, edge dropping is simple but may not capture higher-order structures; for heterogeneous, meta-path selection is manual and domain-knowledge intensive.
  - *Orthogonality regularization:* Encourages class-attention diversification but may over-constrain if classes are semantically similar.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Pre-training:** Experts show no specialization (e.g., homogeneous graphs' experts perform similarly on heterogeneous validation).
  - **Downstream:** Attention weights collapse to a single expert across all classes; or orthogonality loss plateaus at high value without improvement.
  - **Generalization:** Performance degrades sharply when target graph type (hom/het) is absent from pre-training set (e.g., only het pre-trained → hom downstream).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation: remove expert isolation (w/o Expert).** Train one shared encoder on all pre-training graphs. Expect significant drop (observed in Fig 4), confirming C2 is real and isolation helps.
  2. **Hyper-parameter: number of views (homogeneous) / meta-path choice (heterogeneous).** Vary views 2–6; expect stable performance (Fig 6a) but watch for drop if views too few (lost diversity) or too many (noisy/similar).
  3. **Attention analysis on 50-shot classification.** Inspect 𝛼(𝑐)𝑖 weights. Expect experts pre-trained on semantically close domains (e.g., citation graphs for ACM) dominate (Fig 5,7). If weights are uniform or random, fusion is not learning meaningful routing.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter ambiguity in the paper's tables prevents exact replication (layer counts per dataset, learning rates, weight decay). Downstream protocol details (validation split size, early stopping) are underspecified.
- The reliance on handcrafted meta-paths for heterogeneous graphs may limit applicability to domains without such domain knowledge.
- Claims about class-wise attention routing are supported by empirical analysis but risk overfitting on few-shot labels; orthogonal regularization may force artificial separation.

## Confidence
- **Unified Multi-View Construction:** High. Mechanically clear and empirically validated via ablations.
- **Domain-Specific Expert Isolation:** Medium. Ablations show benefit, but the hypothesis of negative transfer is not systematically probed across varying domain similarity; potential capacity waste is noted.
- **Task-Oriented Fusion with Class-Wise Attention:** Medium-Low. Evidence is empirical but may be attributed to overfitting; orthogonality regularization could introduce instability.

## Next Checks
1. **View Construction Stress Test:** Replace handcrafted meta-paths for heterogeneous graphs with automatically generated ones (e.g., via random walks) and measure performance to quantify reliance on domain knowledge.
2. **Gradient Conflict Analysis:** During shared-encoder pre-training on mixed domains, compute gradient similarity (cosine angle) between batches from homogeneous vs. heterogeneous graphs to substantiate negative transfer claims.
3. **Attention Stability under Imbalance:** Evaluate with extreme few-shot class imbalance (e.g., 1:100 shot ratios) to test whether attention weights collapse or become noisy, indicating brittleness.
