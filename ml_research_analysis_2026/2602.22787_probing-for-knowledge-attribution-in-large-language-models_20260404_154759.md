# Probing for Knowledge Attribution in Large Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22787
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22787
- **Reference count:** 33
- **Primary result:** Linear probes achieve up to 0.96 Macro-F1 in classifying whether an LLM's output derives from provided context or its parametric memory, and generalize to out-of-domain QA benchmarks (0.94–0.99 Macro-F1) without retraining.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method to determine whether a large language model's response is based on information provided in the input context (contextual) or retrieved from its internal training data (parametric). The core contribution is **AttriWiki**, a self-supervised pipeline that automatically creates training data by manipulating Wikipedia passages to force the model to rely on one source or the other. Linear probes trained on the model's internal activations can classify the knowledge source with high accuracy. The study finds that when the model confuses these sources (an "attribution mismatch"), its error rate increases substantially (by up to 70%), demonstrating a direct link between source confusion and unreliable outputs. However, correct attribution alone does not guarantee the answer is factually correct.

## Method Summary
The authors develop **AttriWiki**, a pipeline to construct a labeled dataset for probing knowledge attribution. It selects entities from Wikipedia passages, tests the target model's ability to recall facts about each entity without any context (to establish parametric knowledge), and then creates two variants of the passage: one where the entity is removed (forcing parametric retrieval) and one where a different entity is removed while the target remains (forcing contextual retrieval). For each variant, a completion sentence is generated. The model's hidden states are extracted at specific token positions during generation. These labeled hidden states are used to train linear probes (logistic regression with layer aggregation) to predict whether the completion relied on context or parameters. The method is evaluated in-domain on a held-out AttriWiki test set and out-of-domain on SQuAD and WebQuestions without any fine-tuning.

## Key Results
*   Linear probes achieve up to **0.96 Macro-F1** on the in-domain AttriWiki test set across Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, and Qwen-7B.
*   The probes generalize effectively to out-of-domain QA benchmarks (**0.94–0.99 Macro-F1** on SQuAD and WebQuestions) without retraining.
*   **Attribution mismatches** (using the wrong knowledge source) are strongly correlated with increased error rates, with relative risk increases of approximately **30% to 70%**.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Causal Isolation via Controlled Manipulation
The AttriWiki pipeline creates examples where the knowledge source is the deterministic cause of the model's completion. For a given entity, it first establishes via a knowledge test whether the fact is stored in the model's parameters. For entities deemed "known," all mentions are removed from the passage, forcing the model to rely on parametric memory. For "unknown" entities, a different entity is removed, leaving the target in the context and forcing contextual retrieval. This yields paired prompts that differ *only* in the presence of the entity in the context, isolating the knowledge source as the causal variable.
*Evidence:* [abstract], [section 3.3]
*Break condition:* High false-negative rate in the initial knowledge test would label a parametric fact as "unknown," contaminating the "contextual" training signal with parametric examples.

### Mechanism 2: Linear Separability in Mid-to-Upper Layers
The internal activations of the model during generation encode whether it is using contextual or parametric knowledge. PCA analysis shows that representations for the two source types overlap in early layers but diverge from the middle layers onward. A linear classifier (probe) trained on these activations can successfully predict the source because the two modes occupy distinct, linearly separable regions in the high-dimensional space. The probe's strong out-of-domain performance suggests it captures a robust, generalizable signal about provenance rather than dataset-specific artifacts.
*Evidence:* [section 4.3], [section 4.1], [section 4.4]
*Break condition:* If the separation is an artifact of the specific dataset or model architecture, the probe would fail to generalize, which is contradicted by the OOD results but could occur on very different architectures or domains.

### Mechanism 3: Attribution Mismatch as a Causal Contributor to Errors
When a model uses the wrong knowledge source, it is more likely to produce an incorrect or unfaithful answer. For instance, ignoring provided evidence (relying on parametric knowledge when context is required) leads to contradictions with the source (faithfulness violation), while relying on a misleading context over known facts causes hallucinations (factuality violation). The observed strong statistical association (p ≪ 0.001) between attribution mismatch and error, with risk increases up to ~70%, supports that source confusion itself is a significant driver of unreliability.
*Evidence:* [abstract], [section 4.4]
*Break condition:* The correlation might be driven by a confound like question difficulty, where harder questions both increase errors and create more ambiguous attribution signals. The controlled experimental design (forcing a specific source requirement) attempts to isolate causality but cannot fully eliminate such confounds.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Transformer Hidden States as Representation Space**
    *   **Why needed:** The entire probing approach relies on the premise that a model's internal activation vectors at different layers are structured and encode abstract properties like the source of knowledge being used.
    *   **Quick check:** Based on the PCA divergence, how might the direction of the hidden state vector for a contextual example differ from a parametric one at a middle layer?
*   **Concept: Probing as a Diagnostic Tool**
    *   **Why needed:** Probing uses a simple classifier (e.g., logistic regression) on frozen model representations to test if a specific property (knowledge source) is decodable, providing an interpretation method without altering the model.
    *   **Quick check:** Why does aggregating signals from multiple layers (Layer-LR) significantly outperform using only the final layer (Final-LR)? What does this imply about the *timing* of the attribution decision in the forward pass?
*   **Concept: Knowledge Conflicts in LLMs**
    *   **Why needed:** The paper's scenario (parametric vs. contextual) is a specific type of knowledge conflict. Understanding this taxonomy (faithfulness vs. factuality violations) frames why accurate attribution is crucial for trustworthy systems like RAG.
    *   **Quick check:** In a RAG system where the retrieved document contradicts the model's memory, what are the two primary error types from a user's perspective, and how could an attribution probe help diagnose each?
*   **Concept: Self-Supervised Data Construction for Probing**
    *   **Why needed:** Manually labeling thousands of examples for knowledge source is infeasible. The AttriWiki pipeline's automated, controlled manipulation is key to creating a large-scale, high-quality training set.
    *   **Quick check:** What is the core assumption behind using the model's own recall (the "knowledge test") as a ground-truth proxy for "parametric knowledge," and what is a potential pitfall?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Wikipedia Passage Sampler -> NER & Entity Filtering -> Knowledge Tester (model query) -> Passage Rewriter (entity removal) -> Completion Cue Generator -> Target Model Generation & Hidden State Extraction (at FTG, LTE) -> Probe Training (Layer Aggregation + Classifier) -> Evaluation (In-domain & OOD)
*   **Critical path:** Data Generation (ensuring causal isolation) → Hidden State Extraction (correct token positioning) → Probe Training (balanced loss, early stopping) → In-Domain Evaluation → OOD Generalization Test → Ablation & Mismatch Analysis. A failure in generating cleanly isolated examples (e.g., poor entity removal) propagates and poisons the probe.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Linear vs. MLP Probes:** Linear probes (Layer-LR) generalize better and resist lexical shortcuts; MLPs (Layer-MLP) give marginal in-distribution gains but overfit to dataset artifacts (see decoy ablation collapse).
    *   **Token Choice:** FTG (first generated token) captures initial response intent; LTE (last token of entity) captures integrated entity semantics. Both are used; FTG is critical for analyzing incorrect responses.
    *   **Knowledge Proxy:** Using model recall as a "parametric" label is efficient but imperfect; it may mislabel fragile facts learned during pre-training.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   Probe accuracy near random on OOD datasets → pipeline artifacts or failure to capture a generalizable signal.
    *   MLP probe collapses on decoy variants → classifier is learning superficial patterns (entity string presence) instead of attribution.
    *   High mismatch-error correlation does not replicate on a new domain → attribution signal may not transfer or error mechanisms differ.
    *   PCA shows no layer-wise separation → probe must rely on noisy early-layer signals, likely reducing accuracy.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  Replicate AttriWiki generation for a small subset (e.g., 100 passages). Manually verify: (a) fluency of rewritten passages after entity removal, (b) consistency of knowledge test labels, (c) that "parametric" variant completions do not accidentally use the entity from the context.
    2.  Train Final-LR (last layer only) and compare to Layer-LR on the same data. Expect a 7-11 pp F1 gain from layer aggregation. Inspect learned layer weights to confirm concentration in mid-to-upper layers.
    3.  Take a Layer-LR probe trained on AttriWiki and evaluate it directly on the SQuAD (contextual) and WebQuestions (parametric) sets (2000 samples each). Expect >0.94 accuracy. If lower, check for data leakage (e.g., Wikipedia passages overlapping with QA datasets) and filtering protocol adherence.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1.  **Integration into Live Systems:** How can attribution signals be integrated into retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and chatbot systems to dynamically improve reliability? The paper suggests using probes to detect ignored evidence and trigger verification or re-retrieval, but this is unimplemented.
2.  **Cross-Architecture Alignment:** Can attribution-relevant signals be aligned across different model architectures via shared, model-agnostic feature spaces? The probe generalizes across datasets but not necessarily across fundamentally different architectures (e.g., Llama vs. GPT).
3.  **Sequence-Level Attribution:** How can attribution classifiers move beyond token-level signals to handle open-ended generation with no clear entity span? The current method is limited to factual questions with a single named entity; multi-entity or non-entity outputs require new aggregation strategies.
4.  **Domain & Language Generalization:** Do attribution signals generalize beyond Wikipedia to domains like legal text, dialogue, or noisy web data, and to multilingual settings? AttriWiki's controlled, English encyclopedic domain may not reflect the complexity of other contexts.
5.  **Beyond Attribution for Correctness:** Why does correct knowledge attribution not guarantee factual correctness, and how can attribution be combined with other diagnostics? A significant fraction of errors persists even with correct source alignment, indicating the need for multi-signal frameworks incorporating reasoning checks, confidence, etc.

## Limitations
*   **Reliance on GPT-4o-mini:** The pipeline uses GPT-4o-mini for entity selection, rewriting, and cue generation. Its label quality, biases, and cost are unverified and could affect dataset integrity and reproducibility.
*   **Underspecified Matching:** The details of the cross-encoder (0.6 threshold) for entity/answer span matching and synonym generation are not fully specified, leaving room for implementation variance.
*   **Causal Claim Strength:** While the mismatch-error correlation is statistically significant, residual confounds (e.g., inherent question difficulty affecting both attribution ambiguity and error rates) cannot be fully ruled out.

## Confidence
*   **In-domain attribution accuracy (F1 ≤0.96):** **High** - Direct, controlled experimental results with title-disjoint splits.
*   **Out-of-domain generalization (SQuAD/WebQ F1 ≤0.99):** **Medium** - Strong numbers but limited to two related QA benchmarks; the magnitude and nature of the domain shift are not deeply characterized.
*   **Attribution mismatch → increased error rate (causal):** **Low** - The association is robust (p≪0.001) and the design attempts isolation, but the causal claim remains vulnerable to unmeasured confounds like entity difficulty.

## Next Checks
1.  **Audit AttriWiki generation:** Manually review a sample of 100 generated examples to verify the quality of entity removal (fluency, no residual mentions), the plausibility of knowledge test labels, and that "parametric" variants truly lack the entity in context.
2.  **Validate answer matching implementation:** On 50 test examples, manually confirm that the cross-encoder (0.6 threshold) correctly identifies answer spans across paraphrased model completions and that the entity span extraction is accurate.
3.  **Test mismatch effect on a new domain:** Re-run the mismatch-error correlation analysis on a different OOD dataset (e.g., a trivia QA set) to confirm the effect holds beyond Wikipedia/standard QA contexts.
