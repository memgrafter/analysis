# Convergence of Byzantine-Resilient Gradient Tracking via Probabilistic Edge Dropout

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.00449
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00449
- **Reference count**: 30
- **Primary result**: GT-PD and GT-PD-L achieve linear convergence under Byzantine attacks; GT-PD-L improves test accuracy by up to 4.3% over coordinate-wise trimmed mean, especially against stealth ALIE attacks.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses Byzantine-resilient distributed optimization where adversarial agents may send arbitrary messages. GT-PD introduces two defenses: a universal self-centered projection that clips incoming messages to an ℓ₂-ball around the receiver’s current state, bounding adversarial influence while preserving doubly stochastic mixing; and a decentralized probabilistic edge dropout driven by a dual-metric trust score that evaluates deviations in both parameter and gradient tracking channels. Under complete isolation of Byzantine agents, GT-PD converges linearly to a stochastic noise floor with no extra consensus penalty. For partial isolation, GT-PD-L adds a leaky integrator to prevent tracking error accumulation, yielding linear convergence to a bounded neighborhood whose size depends on the clipping-to-leak ratio. Experiments on MNIST under Sign Flip, ALIE, and Inner Product Manipulation attacks show GT-PD-L outperforms coordinate-wise trimmed mean by up to 4.3 percentage points in test accuracy, particularly under the stealthy ALIE attack.

## Method Summary
GT-PD combines a deterministic projection with a stochastic dropout mechanism. Each honest agent first projects all received messages (both parameter and gradient tracker values) onto an ℓ₂-ball centered at its own state; this "self-centered" step limits the maximum per-message corruption without altering the doubly stochastic nature of the mixing matrix. Next, agents compute a dual-metric trust score for each neighbor by combining normalized deviations in parameters and gradient trackers. Retention probabilities are set to exp(-λ·score) (capped at 1 for low scores), and a Bernoulli dropout mask is sampled. The base mixing weights are scaled by the mask, and self-loops absorb dropped weight to maintain row sums. The agent then performs a gradient tracking update: the decision variable is a weighted sum of projected neighbor states minus a step-size scaled tracker; the tracker is updated with the projected neighbor trackers plus the local stochastic gradient difference. GT-PD-L modifies the tracker update by a leaky integrator (factor 1-β) to dampen accumulated tracking violations when Byzantines are partially isolated.

## Key Results
- **Theoretical**: Under p_b=0 (complete isolation), GT-PD converges linearly to a stochastic noise floor. For p_b>0, GT-PD-L converges linearly to an ϵ-neighborhood with ϵ proportional to ζ²/(αμβ²), where ζ bounds the clipped adversarial perturbation.
- **Empirical**: On MNIST with Sign Flip, ALIE, and Inner Product Manipulation attacks, GT-PD-L achieves up to 4.3% higher test accuracy than coordinate-wise trimmed mean; it is robust even when the dropout layer alone would fail (e.g., ALIE).
- **Mechanistic**: The projection preserves doubly stochastic mixing; the dual-metric trust score drops Byzantine edges with high probability while ensuring a uniform lower bound on honest edge retention.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Universal Self‑Centered Projection
**Claim**: Bounding every incoming message to an ℓ₂‑ball of radius τ around the receiver’s state limits adversarial impact while preserving doubly stochastic mixing because the projection acts on message values only, not on mixing weights.  
**Mechanism**: For a received message ˜x_j, agent i computes x'_j = x_i + min(1, τ/∥˜x_j - x_i∥)·(˜x_j - x_i). If ∥˜x_j - x_i∥ ≤ τ, the message passes unchanged; otherwise it is clipped to the ball boundary.  
**Core assumption**: Under complete Byzantine isolation (p_b=0) and sufficiently large τ, honest messages from other honest agents are never clipped (Proposition 3). Under partial isolation, any honest clipping error is bounded by the consensus error O(C_k) (Lemma 17).  
**Break condition**: If τ is too small relative to the growing honest consensus error, honest clipping occurs and injects bias. This increases the coupling coefficient Φ; if Φ ≥ 1‑ρ (where ρ is the spectral gap), the Lyapunov recursion may fail to contract, harming convergence.

### Decentralized Probabilistic Edge Dropout with Dual‑Metric Trust
**Claim**: Probabilistic dropout based on a composite trust score that incorporates both parameter and gradient tracker deviations drops Byzantine edges with high probability while almost surely preserving doubly stochasticity of the mixing matrix.  
**Mechanism**: Agent i computes for neighbor j scores S^x_ij = ∥x_i^{k‑1} - x_j^{k‑1}∥/(n_H·std_x), S^y_ij = ∥y_i^{k‑1} - y_j^{k‑1}∥/(n_H·std_y), and S_ij = S^x_ij + S^y_ij. Retention probability is p_ij = exp(-λ S_ij), with a threshold S₀: if S_ij ≤ S₀ then p_ij=1. The dropout mask ξ_ij ~ Bernoulli(p_ij) scales the base weight W_ij; the diagonal is adjusted to keep row sums 1.  
**Core assumption**: Honest edges exhibit small deviations in both channels (S_ij low) and are retained with high probability (p_min = e^{-8λ}); Byzantine edges have large deviation in at least one channel and are heavily penalized.  
**Break condition**: Too low λ fails to sufficiently penalize Byzantines; too high λ may drop honest edges that momentarily deviate, reducing connectivity and spectral gap. A high tolerance S₀ also risks retaining Byzantines.

### Leaky Integrator for Gradient Tracking Stability
**Claim**: The leaky integrator in GT‑PD‑L prevents unbounded accumulation of the tracking invariant violation s_k = ȳ_k - ȯ_k, which occurs under partial isolation when Byzantine perturbations enter the tracker update.  
**Mechanism**: The tracker update becomes y_i^{k+1} = (1‑β) Σ_j [W^k]_{ij} P_{τ,i}(ŷ_j) + g_i^{k+1} - (1‑β) g_i^k. The factor (1‑β) geometrically discounts historical tracker states and perturbations.  
**Core assumption**: β ∈ (0, ½] ensures s_k remains bounded (∥s_k∥ ≤ (1‑β)ζ/β) and contracts over time. The coupling between honest clipping errors and s_k is controlled by Φ < 1‑ρ (Theorem 18).  
**Break condition**: β too small → s_k grows, dominating steady‑state error ϵ_Byz ~ ζ²/(αμβ²). β too large → excessive forgetting of past gradients, slowing convergence. The step size α must satisfy the coupled condition (56) and Φ<1‑ρ.

## Foundational Learning
- **Gradient tracking**  
  *Why needed*: Maintains local estimators that track the global gradient, enabling exact convergence in decentralized optimization.  
  *Quick check*: What is the key invariant? (Answer: the average local tracker equals the average stochastic gradient: ȳ_k = (1/n_H) Σ_{i∈H} g_i^k.)
- **Doubly stochastic mixing matrix**  
  *Why needed*: Guarantees that consensus averaging preserves the mean of iterates; essential for unbiased convergence in decentralized algorithms.  
  *Quick check*: Why does breaking double stochasticity cause bias? (Answer: The consensus average drifts from the true mean due to uneven weighting, leading to a biased convergence point.)
- **Byzantine resilience**  
  *Why needed*: Models adversarial agents that can send arbitrary, possibly malicious messages; the algorithm must isolate their influence.  
  *Quick check*: How do Byzantine failures differ from random communication failures? (Answer: Byzantine failures are adversarial and coordinated; random failures are independent and non‑malicious.)

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Messages (¬x_j, ¬y_j) -> Self‑centered projection (P_τ) -> Trust score calculator (S_ij) -> Dropout mask generator (ξ_ij) -> Mixing weight composer (W^k) -> Gradient tracking update core (x_{k+1}, y_{k+1}) -> Next iteration.

### Critical Path
1. At iteration k, each honest agent i receives raw messages ˆx_j, ˆy_j from neighbors.  
2. Apply self‑centered projection: compute x'_j = P_{τ,i}(ˆx_j), y'_j = P_{τ,i}(ˆy_j) for all j.  
3. Compute dual‑metric trust scores S_ij using local parameters x_i^{k‑1}, x_j^{k‑1} and gradient trackers y_i^{k‑1}, y_j^{k‑1}.  
4. Compute retention probabilities p_ij = exp(-λ S_ij); if S_ij ≤ S₀ set p_ij=1.  
5. Sample dropout mask ξ_ij ~ Bernoulli(p_ij) (symmetric per edge).  
6. Construct dropout mixing matrix W^k: off‑diagonal W^k_{ij}=W_{ij}·ξ_ij; diagonal W^k_{ii}=1‑Σ_{j≠i} W^k_{ij}.  
7. Update decision variable: x_i^{k+1} = Σ_j W^k_{ij} x'_j - α y_i^k.  
8. Update gradient tracker:  
 - GT‑PD: y_i^{k+1} = Σ_j W^k_{ij} y'_j + g_i^{k+1} - g_i^k.  
 - GT‑PD‑L: y_i^{k+1} = (1‑β) Σ_j W^k_{ij} y'_j + g_i^{k+1} - (1‑β) g_i^k.  
9. Increment k and repeat.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Projection radius τ**: Larger τ reduces honest clipping but increases adversarial bound ζ = δ_B^{max} τ; smaller τ may cause honest clipping and raise coupling Φ. Under p_b=0, τ does not affect convergence rate but must satisfy honest non‑clipping with high probability.
- **Leak parameter β**: Larger β reduces steady‑state Byzantine error ϵ_Byz but increases coupling coefficient Φ (∝ 1/β²); if Φ ≥ 1‑ρ convergence degrades. Smaller β improves tracking continuity but risks unbounded s_k.
- **Sensitivity λ**: High λ penalizes deviations strongly, dropping Byzantines more aggressively; too high may remove honest edges with natural variance, harming connectivity and spectral gap.
- **Tolerance S₀**: Lower S₀ retains more edges (including possible Byzantines) but maintains connectivity; higher S₀ drops more edges but risks isolating honest agents.

### Failure Signatures
- GT‑PD (β=0) under partial isolation: tracking invariant violation s_k grows without bound, leading to accuracy collapse (observed in ALIE experiment for GT‑PD).
- τ too small: increases honest clipping error, causing Φ to approach or exceed 1‑ρ; Lyapunov recursion fails to contract → divergence or large steady‑state error.
- Dropout ineffective: trust scores misclassify Byzantines as honest, leading to high ζ and elevated ϵ_Byz.
- Step size α too large: violates condition (56); Lyapunov coefficients fail, causing divergence.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Sign Flip attack**: Byzantines send negated and scaled honest mean. Validates projection and dropout isolation; expects GT‑PD‑L to outperform CWTM and unprotected GT.
2. **ALIE attack**: Stealth attack mimicking honest gradient distribution. Dropout alone may fail; expects GT‑PD‑L to converge via projection + leaky integrator, demonstrating defense‑in‑depth.
3. **Inner Product Manipulation attack**: Attack maximizes inner product with true gradient to cause slow divergence. Expect dual‑metric trust to detect deviations in both channels, leading to effective isolation; compare GT‑PD‑L vs. CWTM.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Adaptive projection radius**: Can a τ that contracts with the consensus error tighten the steady‑state error floor beyond (δ_B^{max})²τ²/β²? The current proof assumes fixed τ; an adaptive rule would require a coupled induction to avoid circularity.
2. **Adaptive tolerance S₀**: How to automate S₀ based on observed honest disagreement while preserving the uniform lower bound on retention probabilities critical for spectral contraction? An adaptive S₀ makes dropout state‑dependent, complicating the uniform p_min analysis.
3. **Time‑varying directed graphs and asynchrony**: Does the probabilistic dropout framework extend to directed topologies or asynchronous updates? The analysis relies on symmetric doubly stochastic mixing and synchrony; directed/asynchronous settings may require push‑sum or bounded delay assumptions.

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis assumes static doubly stochastic mixing and exact network knowledge; dynamic or directed graphs may invalidate guarantees.
- The coupling condition Φ < 1‑ρ for GT‑PD‑L is restrictive and highly sensitive to τ, β, and step size; practical tuning guidelines are lacking.
- Experiments are limited to MNIST with three attack models; broader evaluation across datasets, larger networks, and diverse attacks is needed for generalizability.

## Confidence
- **Convergence proofs (Theorem 18, Proposition 2)**: High – mathematically sound under stated assumptions.
- **Dual‑metric dropout efficacy**: Medium – mechanism is novel and partially validated, but theoretical detection guarantees and doubly stochasticity claim need verification.
- **Empirical superiority over trimmed mean**: Medium – results promising but based on narrow experimental scope.
