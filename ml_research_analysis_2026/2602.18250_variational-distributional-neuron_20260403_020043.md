# Variational Distributional Neuron

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18250
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18250
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** EVE validates a primitive for observable, tunable probabilistic computation at the unit level; internal uncertainty metrics (e.g., out-of-band fraction) correlate with test MSE (r=0.567), enabling regime selection.

## Executive Summary
Neural units typically propagate deterministic scalars, yet uncertainty is intrinsic to computation. This paper introduces EVE, a variational distributional neuron that treats each unit as a 1D variational autoencoder (VAE) brick with an internal latent variable. Each neuron parameterizes an amortized posterior \(q(z|h)\), samples via reparameterization, and is regularized by a local KL divergence term with a standard normal prior. Additionally, EVE incorporates steerable constraints such as a \(\mu^2\) energy band and optional per-unit autoregressive (AR) dynamics, with diagnostics like KL and out-of-band fraction to monitor internal regimes.

On the LongHorizon time series forecasting benchmark, different controllers reveal trade-offs: homeostasis improves stability (lower out-of-band fraction) on Weather and Exchange, while unconstrained variants achieve lower MSE on ECL and TrafficL. The out-of-band fraction correlates strongly with test MSE (\(r=0.567, p=2.3\times10^{-6}\)), enabling early regime selection. Compared to a deterministic baseline with matched capacity, EVE does not systematically reduce MSE but demonstrates the feasibility of observable, tunable probabilistic computation at the elementary neuron level.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates EVE on multivariate time series forecasting using the LongHorizon benchmark (input length 336, prediction horizon 96) across four datasets: ECL, TrafficL, Weather, and Exchange. The model consists of layers of EVE units, each being a 1D VAE: given pre-activation \(h\), it computes parameters \(\mu(h)\) and \(\sigma(h)\) of a Gaussian posterior \(q(z|h)\), samples \(z = \mu + \sigma \epsilon\) (\(\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\)), and propagates \(z\) (or \(\mu\)) forward. The global loss combines the task MSE, a scaled mean KL divergence \(\beta \cdot \text{KL}_{\text{mean}}\) over units, and optionally an autoregressive (AR) penalty \(\alpha_{AR} L_{AR}\) that encourages each latent to follow an AR(1) prior \(p(z_t|z_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}(\phi z_{t-1}, \sigma_{AR}^2)\).

Three control regimes are studied: (i) *homeo* (homeostasis) uses a soft penalty to keep the per-unit expected squared mean \(\mathbb{E}[\mu^2]\) within a band \([l,u]\), (ii) *projON* applies a hard multiplicative projection onto the band after each optimizer step, and (iii) *projOFF* imposes no band constraints. At initialization, each neuron samples its own band \([l_j,u_j]\) from a shared central range with some overlap, providing steerability. Training uses Adam/AdamW with early stopping; model selection employs a composite validation score that penalizes deviation from a target \(\mu^2\) range. A deterministic baseline with identical architecture but no latent variable and no KL term serves as a reference.

## Key Results
- Homeostasis (homeo) improves stability (lower out-of-band fraction) on Weather and Exchange, while unconstrained variants (projOFF) minimize MSE on ECL and TrafficL.
- The out-of-band fraction correlates strongly with test MSE (\(r=0.567\), \(p=2.3\times10^{-6}\)), enabling early regime selection via internal diagnostics.
- Against a deterministic baseline, EVE does not systematically lower MSE but validates a primitive for observable, tunable probabilistic computation at the unit level.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A neuron can locally perform variational inference, making its internal uncertainty explicit and observable.
- **Mechanism:** Each unit parameterizes an amortized posterior `q(z|h) = N(µ(h), σ(h)²)` over a scalar latent `z`, samples `z` via reparameterization, and is regularized by a local KL term `β KL(q(z|h)||p(z))` with a standard normal prior. This turns the unit from a deterministic scalar propagator into a distributional compute brick with a micro-ELBO.
- **Core assumption:** Local variational inference at the unit level is composable and does not destabilize network-wide training when properly constrained.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Each neuron parameterizes a posterior, propagates a reparameterized sample and is regularized by the KL term of a local ELBO — hence, the activation is distributional."
  - [section 3.1.2] Formal definition of local objective `L_unit = L_task + β KL_mean` and reparameterization.
  - [corpus] The nearest neighbor "Exploring the Dimensions of a Variational Neuron" (arXiv:2603.13849) describes an identical EVE primitive, suggesting conceptual alignment in the emerging literature. *Corpus evidence is weak: this is a single related pre-print without citations.*
- **Break condition:** If `σ(h)` collapses to near-zero everywhere, the unit reverts to deterministic (`z ≈ µ(h)`), losing its distributional nature. The paper monitors this via `KL_mean` and `µ²` bands.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Steerable constraints (e.g., `µ²` energy band) make the local latent regime stable, observable, and tunable per neuron.
- **Mechanism:** A band `[ℓ_j, u_j]` on the expected squared mean `µ²_j = E[µ_j(h)²]` is enforced via multiplicative projection after each gradient step. This acts as a local informational budget, controlling latent activity amplitude. Combined with homeostasis (soft regulation) or hard projection, it prevents collapse (low `µ²`) and explosion (high `µ²`).
- **Core assumption:** `µ²` is a controllable proxy for the latent's informational contribution when `σ` is fixed or slowly varying, as derived from the analytic KL expression `KL ≈ ½ µ² + const`.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3.5] Derivation linking KL to `µ²` and definition of the band constraint.
  - [section 3.3.6] Hard projection formula `s_j = sqrt(ℓ_j/µ²_j)` or `sqrt(u_j/µ²_j)`.
  - [section 4.2] Tables 3–4 show `out` (fraction of units outside band) varies systematically with controller type (`homeo`, `projON`, `projOFF`), demonstrating steerability.
- **Break condition:** If the band is too narrow relative to the task's required latent expressivity, the projection will permanently correct `µ`, leading to a clamped regime (high `Band` penalty, e.g., TrafficL `homeo` in Table 4) and degraded MSE.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Per-neuron autoregressive (AR) dynamics introduce local memory/inertia without centralizing the latent state, creating a "micro-dynamics" axis separable from budget control.
- **Mechanism:** Each unit's scalar latent follows an AR(1) prior `p(z_t|z_{t-1}) = N(ϕ_j z_{t-1}, σ²_AR)`, implemented as a penalty `L_AR` on `(µ_t - ϕ_j µ_{t-k})`. This defines a neuron-specific time constant `τ_j = -Δt / log(ϕ_j)`.
- **Core assumption:** Local trajectories can encode temporal persistence independently of global state-space dynamics, and their effect is measurable via `ar_share` (fraction of objective from `L_AR`).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3.8] Definition of per-unit AR prior and `ar_share` metric.
  - [section 4.5] Table 5 shows AR `ON`/`OFF` changes `ar_share` and `out` without monotonically improving MSE (degrades on ECL, slightly improves on Exchange), confirming AR as a distinct regime axis.
  - [corpus] "Variational Neurons in Transformers for Language Modeling" (arXiv:2603.28219) explores similar per-unit stochasticity but in a different architecture. *Corpus suggests growing interest but no direct validation of EVE's AR mechanism.*
- **Break condition:** If `α_AR` (AR loss weight) is too high or `ϕ_j` poorly tuned, the unit may over-prioritize temporal smoothness, increasing `out` (see ECL `AR ON` in Table 5) and harming task performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Variational Inference (VI) and the ELBO.
  - **Why needed here:** EVE is a 1D VAE brick. Understanding the trade-off between reconstruction (`L_task`) and KL regularization (`β KL`) is essential to grasp why the unit carries a distribution and what the local objective optimizes.
  - **Quick check question:** If you set `β=0` in an EVE unit, what happens to the latent distribution `q(z|h)` during training? (Answer: It may collapse to a deterministic point or ignore the prior, losing the "distributional" property.)

- **Concept:** Reparameterization Trick.
  - **Why needed here:** Enables gradient flow through the stochastic sampling `z = µ(h) + σ(h)ε`. Without it, backpropagation through the random node would be biased or high-variance.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is `ε ~ N(0,1)` sampled independently of the network parameters, while `µ(h), σ(h)` are functions of the input `h`? (Answer: To make the expectation `E_q[·]` differentiable w.r.t. `ϕ`.)

- **Concept:** KL Divergence as a Regularizer and Budget.
  - **Why needed here:** The KL term `KL(q||p)` penalizes deviation from the prior, preventing collapse and controlling information rate. The paper further approximates it via `µ²` when `σ` is stable, enabling a direct energy-band constraint.
  - **Quick check question:** In the analytic `KL ≈ ½ µ² + const` approximation, what assumption about `σ` is made? (Answer: `σ` is fixed or nearly constant across samples/units.)

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
1. **Input `h`**: Pre-activation from previous layer (or raw input `x`).
2. **EVE unit**: Computes `(µ, σ)` via a small MLP (or linear layer), samples `z`, applies `µ²` band constraint (if enabled), and emits `a` (often `µ` itself or a transformed sample).
3. **Aggregation**: Layer output `ˆy` is typically a scaled sum of `µ` values: `ˆy = w⊤µ / sqrt(N) + b`.
4. **Loss**: Global `L = L_task(ˆy, y) + β KL_mean + α_AR L_AR`, with optional hard projection step.
5. **Diagnostics**: Per-unit `KL_i`, `µ²_i`, `out` (band violation fraction), `Band` penalty, `ar_share`.

### Critical path
1. Initialize network with EVE units (latent dim `k=1`), set prior `N(0,1)`.
2. During forward: compute `(µ, σ)` → sample `z` → emit `a` (usually `a = µ` for readout simplicity).
3. During backward: compute `L_task` + `β KL_mean` + `α_AR L_AR`; apply gradients.
4. If hard projection: after optimizer step, for each unit, if `µ²_j < ℓ_j` or `> u_j`, scale `(a_j, b_j)` by `s_j` to exactly hit the bound.
5. Log all internal metrics (`KL`, `µ²`, `out`, `Band` penalty, `ar_share`) every few steps.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Budget (`ℓ, u`) vs. Capacity:** Too tight bands cause clamping (high `Band` penalty, low effective latent use); too loose bands risk instability (high `out`).
- **AR dynamics vs. Stability:** AR introduces memory but can increase `out` if not tuned (`α_AR`, `ϕ_j`, `σ_AR`), as seen in ECL Table 5.
- **`k=1` vs. `k>1`:** `k=1` isolates the inference mechanism; `k>1` adds capacity but complicates calibration (multivariate `µ²`, anisotropic collapse).
- **Homeostasis (soft band penalty) vs. Hard Projection:** Homeostasis (`homeo`) uses a differentiable band penalty; hard projection (`projON`) enforces exact bounds. Homeostasis may be smoother but harder to tune; projection is exact but may disrupt gradients if overactive.

### Failure signatures
- **Collapse:** `KL_mean → 0`, `µ² → 0`. Indicates latent is ignored; unit behaves deterministically.
- **Saturation/Explosion:** `µ²` consistently above `u_j`, `out` high (especially `frac_high`). Latent activity is unbounded.
- **Clamped regime:** Very high `Band` penalty (e.g., >2.0 in Table 4) with `out` still moderate. Projection is active almost every step, starving the unit of adaptive latent range.
- **AR-dominant:** `ar_share` near 1.0, `out` high. The AR penalty overwhelms the task loss.
- **Inconsistent `σ`:** If `σ` varies wildly, the `µ²`-KL proxy becomes unreliable; monitor `E[|z-µ|]` as a sanity check.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Sanity check: "Living neuron" existence.** Train a single EVE layer (wide, e.g., 1024 units) on a simple regression (e.g., sine wave). Verify `KL_mean > 0.1` and `0.01 < µ² < 10` at convergence. If `KL_mean ≈ 0`, increase `β` or adjust band.
2. **Band sensitivity sweep.** Fix `homeo` variant. Sweep central band width (e.g., `µ²` in {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}) on a small dataset (ECL subset). Plot MSE vs. `out` and `Band`. Expect a U-curve: too narrow → high `Band`/MSE; too wide → high `out`/MSE.
3. **AR isolation.** Train `homeo` with `AR OFF` and `AR ON` on Exchange (where AR helped slightly). Compare `ar_share` and `out`. Confirm `AR ON` increases `ar_share` and may increase `out` without hurting MSE much (Exchange) or hurts MSE (ECL). Tune `α_AR` to balance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can probabilistic constraints be combined at the unit level in a stable, interpretable, and controllable manner?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The introduction states: "how can we combine probabilistic constraints (variational experts) in a stable, interpretable, and controllable manner?"
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only realizes a simple additive KL with a µ² band; advanced compositional mechanisms (e.g., Product of Experts, message passing) are discussed conceptually but not implemented or validated.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement and evaluate these composition schemes in networks of EVE units across tasks, using internal diagnostics (KL, out, drift) to measure stability, interpretability (e.g., disentangled factors), and controllability.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Should the primitive compute unit be deterministic or distributional, and under what task conditions does each paradigm excel?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The introduction asks: "should the primitive unit remain a deterministic neuron, or become a distributional neuron?" The experiments show trade-offs: distributional units with regulation improve stability on Weather/Exchange, while deterministic baselines win on ECL/TrafficL.
- **Why unresolved:** The study is confined to one forecasting protocol; broader conditions (noise regime, horizon length, dataset characteristics) are unknown. General design principles for choosing unit type remain elusive.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic comparison on diverse sequential tasks (generation, control, RL), correlating internal regime metrics (e.g., out, KL) with external performance to identify predictors of when distributional units are beneficial.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can the variational distributional neuron be extended to latent dimensions k>1 while preserving stability, calibration, and interpretable diagnostics?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5.7: "A natural extension is to study k>1 at the unit level... raises new questions about calibration (informational budget per dimension), stability (richer collapse modes), and instrumentation."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper fixes k=1 to isolate the inference mechanism; scaling up introduces multivariate challenges (e.g., anisotropic collapse, covariance budgeting, richer diagnostics) that are not addressed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Propose and test extensions: per-dimension or full-covariance budget constraints; collapse detectors (volume/orientation of latent distribution); experiments showing k>1 improves performance only when these controls are properly designed.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can internal budgets (µ² bands) be made adaptive, i.e., learned or driven by data, to automate regime selection and reduce hyperparameter dependence?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5.8 lists "adaptive budget control (learned or driven bands)" as an immediate perspective. The current work uses static bands and manual sweeps.
- **Why unresolved:** Static bands require per-dataset tuning; adaptive control could dynamically adjust budgets based on internal signals (KL, out), potentially improving robustness and simplifying deployment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop an adaptive controller (e.g., a small network or rule-based system) that adjusts bands during training; demonstrate that it matches or exceeds hand-tuned baselines across datasets with fewer hyperparameters and stable internal metrics.

## Limitations
- Band initialization (ℓ, u) details are vague; exact sampling ranges impact reproducibility.
- Deterministic baseline lacks precise parameter matching, confounding interpretation of distributional benefits.
- The µ²–KL analytical link assumes constant σ; without σ statistics, the proxy's reliability is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Local variational inference (Mechanism 1):** Medium confidence.
- **Steerable µ² constraints (Mechanism 2):** Low‑Medium confidence.
- **Per‑unit AR dynamics (Mechanism 3):** Low confidence.

## Next Checks
1. **Band width ablation:** Train EVE with ℓ, u sampled from a grid and measure out vs. MSE to test robustness of band‑based stability.
2. **σ profiling:** Log per‑unit σ² during training; compute mean/variance and correlation with µ². If σ varies significantly, the µ²‑KL approximation may be invalid.
3. **Matched‑capacity deterministic baseline:** Construct a deterministic network whose total parameter count matches the EVE variant; compare test MSE under identical training to isolate distributional benefit.
