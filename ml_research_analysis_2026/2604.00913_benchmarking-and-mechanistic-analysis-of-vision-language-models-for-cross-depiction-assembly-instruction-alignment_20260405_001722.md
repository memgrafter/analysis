# Benchmarking and Mechanistic Analysis of Vision-Language Models for Cross-Depiction Assembly Instruction Alignment

## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID:** 2604.00913
* **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00913
* **Reference Count:** 30
* **Primary Result:** Assembly instruction understanding is recoverable via text descriptions (+23.6 pp), but adding text actively harms diagram-to-video alignment (−3.1 pp); video understanding remains a hard ceiling (max ~71.1%).

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the "depiction gap"—the fundamental mismatch between schematic assembly diagrams and photorealistic assembly videos—which breaks standard vision-language models (VLMs) for intelligent assembly guidance. The authors build **IKEA-Bench**, a benchmark with 1,623 questions across six tasks derived from 29 IKEA products, and evaluate 19 VLMs under three alignment strategies. Crucially, they find that providing text descriptions of diagrams dramatically improves instruction comprehension but *degrades* cross-depiction matching by diverting the model's attention away from visual inputs. Mechanistic analysis reveals that the visual encoder (ViT) produces nearly disjoint feature spaces for diagrams and video (CKA ≈ 0), making direct alignment infeasible. The primary bottleneck is not the alignment itself but poor video understanding, which remains a strategy-invariant ceiling.

## Method Summary
The authors construct **IKEA-Bench** using the IKEA Manuals at Work dataset. For 29 furniture products, they align assembly videos (frame-sampled) with corresponding wordless manual diagrams. Six diagnostic tasks are derived: three for cross-depiction alignment (T1: step recognition, T3: progress tracking, T4: next-step prediction) and two for unimodal baselines (D1: video discrimination, D2: instruction ordering). VLMs are evaluated zero-shot under three input conditions: **Visual** (diagrams only), **Visual+Text** (diagrams + structured descriptions), and **Text-Only** (descriptions only). Mechanistic probes extract activations at three layers: Layer 1 (ViT/merger features), Layer 2 (LLM hidden states), and Layer 3 (attention patterns in Qwen3-VL-8B) to diagnose the source of failures.

## Key Results
* Text descriptions recover diagram-only instruction comprehension (D2) by +23.6 percentage points but degrade diagram-video alignment (T1) by −3.1 pp, revealing a tradeoff.
* Diagram and video frames occupy disjoint subspaces in the ViT (CKA ≈ 0.001–0.101), causing near-zero cross-modal retrieval.
* Video understanding (D1) is the invariant performance ceiling, peaking at 71.1% for proprietary models; it shows negligible improvement when text is added.
* Model architecture family (e.g., Qwen3.5 vs. older dense models) predicts performance more strongly than parameter count.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** The visual encoder (ViT) fails to create a shared representational space for schematic diagrams and photorealistic video frames.  
**Mechanism:** The ViT’s feature extraction is specialized to the low-level statistics (texture, color, edges) of its natural-image training data. Diagram and video inputs map to fundamentally different, non-overlapping regions in this high-dimensional space (CKA ≈ 0). Downstream alignment therefore compares essentially unrelated features.  
**Core Assumption:** Cross-depiction alignment requires a visual front-end that maps semantically equivalent content across depictive styles to nearby points in feature space.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Adding textual descriptions of diagrams induces a "distraction" effect, shifting the model's internal reasoning from visual to text-mediated processing, which harms diagram-video matching.  
**Mechanism:** Text tokens provide a rich, high-dimensional alternative information channel. The LLM’s attention and hidden state formation reallocate computational resources toward this channel, reducing the influence and attention share of visual tokens. This is evidenced by drops in diagram token influence (−59% in Qwen3-VL-8B) and halved visual attention share when text is present.  
**Core Assumption:** Optimal cross-depiction alignment requires sustained focus on visual inputs; text competes for the same representational bandwidth within the transformer decoder.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Video understanding—the ability to extract fine-grained procedural semantics from unstructured video—is a hard, invariant bottleneck that limits overall cross-depiction performance, regardless of alignment strategy or model scale.  
**Mechanism:** The visual encoder and/or early fusion layers lack capacity to parse part states, actions, and spatial relationships in video. The isolated D1 task shows consistently low, strategy-invariant scores (even the best proprietary model reaches only 71.1%), indicating this unimodal capability is the binding constraint.  
**Core Assumption:** Cross-depiction alignment is a conjunction of (A) video understanding and (B) diagram comprehension; D2 is text-recoverable but D1 is not, making (A) the limiting factor.

## Foundational Learning
* **Concept: Cross-Depiction Recognition**  
  **Why needed:** The problem is a specific instance of recognizing the same entity/scene across different visual styles (schematic vs. photorealistic). This explains why standard vision models fail and motivates specialized robustness.  
  **Quick Check:** If an object’s silhouette matches in a diagram and photo, but texture/color/shading differs, can an ImageNet-trained classifier reliably recognize them as the same? Why?

* **Concept: Mechanistic Probing (CKA & Attention Analysis)**  
  **Why needed:** The paper’s core insight comes from analyzing *how* models fail. Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) and attention distribution localize failure to specific layers and mechanisms (ViT vs. LLM, attention routing).  
  **Quick Check:** If two input types (diagrams, video) produce activations with CKA ≈ 0, what does that imply about the network’s ability to perform a task requiring their direct comparison?

* **Concept: Diagnostic Task Isolation (D1 vs. D2)**  
  **Why needed:** The strategic design of D1 (video-only) and D2 (diagram-only) tasks decouples and identifies independent bottlenecks in video parsing vs. instruction comprehension. This is key for complex multimodal diagnosis.  
  **Quick Check:** To diagnose whether a VLM’s failure on diagram-video matching stems from poor video parsing or poor diagram parsing, what two minimal test tasks would you design?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Visual Encoder (ViT) -> Vision-Language Projector (Merger) -> LLM Decoder (transformer). Special tokens demarcate vision spans.

**Critical Path:** The visual encoder to projector output is the critical path for cross-depiction alignment. Evidence (Layer 1 CKA, retrieval) shows failure originates here; the LLM receives already-disjoint visual representations, making recovery via reasoning alone impossible.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
* **Text Bridge Utility:** Adding text massively boosts diagram instruction comprehension (D2 +23.6pp) by offloading parsing to the LLM but degrades diagram-video matching (T1 −3.1pp) by distracting attention from visual inputs.
* **Architecture vs. Scale:** Newer dense architectures (Qwen3.5) at ~8B consistently outperform older families and larger MoE models (~30B active). Architectural innovations matter more than raw parameter count.

**Failure Signatures:**
* T1/T3 scores <45% under Visual setting.
* D1 score is ≤ T1 score (video understanding is worse than alignment).
* V+T setting improves D2 but reduces T1 compared to Visual.
* Layer 1 CKA(diagram, video) < 0.1 and video temporal probe accuracy ~50%.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. **Representational Diagnostic:** Extract ViT/merger features for matched diagram-video frame pairs. Compute CKA and train a logistic probe on video frame pairs (same/diff step). Expect CKA ≈ 0 and probe ~50% if the core bottleneck holds.
2. **Strategy A/B Test:** Run T1 (step recognition) and D2 (instruction ordering) under Visual and Text-Only conditions. Calculate Δ(Text Only − Visual). Expect positive Δ for D2 and negative Δ for T1.
3. **Attention Distraction Probe (Qwen models only):** For T1 questions under Visual and V+T, extract last-token attention weights from multiple decoder layers. Sum attention per modality (diagram, video, text). Expect significant drop in visual token attention share when text is added.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
* **Generalization:** Do the video bottleneck and text distraction effects generalize beyond IKEA furniture to other assembly domains (e.g., electronics, machinery)?
* **Architectural Fix:** What modifications can enable visual encoders to produce shared representations for diagrams and video, given current ViTs yield near-zero CKA?
* **Underlying Cause:** Why does adding text degrade cross-depiction alignment while improving instruction comprehension? Is this inherent to VLM fusion mechanisms or mitigable via design?

## Limitations
* The disjoint subspace finding (CKA ≈ 0) is sensitive to pooling and layer choice, though reported across multiple layers.
* The text distraction effect is strongly evidenced in Qwen3-VL-8B but may not generalize to other fusion architectures.
* The "hard ceiling" for video understanding is inferred from D1 task invariance; causality is not proven by intervention.

## Confidence
* **Mechanism 1 (Disjoint Subspaces):** High. Directly supported by CKA and cross-modal retrieval across four models.
* **Mechanism 2 (Text Distraction):** High. Supported by consistent drops in diagram influence and visual attention share, especially in Qwen3-VL-8B.
* **Mechanism 3 (Video Bottleneck):** Medium. Strong correlative evidence from D1 invariance, but causality is inferred.
* **Diagnostic Task Isolation (D1/D2):** High. Clean separation of abilities via task design is a major strength.
* **Architecture > Scale:** Medium. Correlation is clear but confounding factors (training data, objectives) prevent a pure scale claim.

## Next Checks
1. **Subspace Generalization Probe:** For a new model family (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision), extract Layer 1 visual features for the same matched diagram-video pairs and compute CKA. Expected: CKA remains <0.1.
2. **Text Bridge Quality Ablation:** Re-run V+T strategy using three description sources: human annotators, an older LLM (e.g., Llama-3-8B), and Claude Opus. Expected: T1 degrades relative to Visual *regardless* of description quality; D2 recovery magnitude correlates with fidelity.
3. **Targeted Video Understanding Intervention:** Continue pre-training a base VLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B) only on assembly video frames with a temporal contrastive objective (e.g., frame order). Re-evaluate D1 and T1. Expected: >10pp D1 improvement with minimal D2 change confirms video parsing as the binding constraint.
