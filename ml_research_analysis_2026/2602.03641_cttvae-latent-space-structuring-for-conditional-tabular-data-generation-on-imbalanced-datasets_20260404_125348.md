# CTTVAE: Latent Space Structuring for Conditional Tabular Data Generation on Imbalanced Datasets

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.03641
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03641
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: CTTVAE+TBS achieves the strongest minority-class downstream utility (MLE scores with CatBoost), ranking best in 5 of 6 datasets and often exceeding performance on original imbalanced data.

## Executive Summary
CTTVAE addresses synthetic tabular data generation for imbalanced datasets by extending the Transformer-based VAE (TTVAE) with two key innovations: a class-aware triplet margin loss that restructures the latent space for sharper intra-class compactness and inter-class separation, and a training-by-sampling (TBS) strategy that adaptively increases exposure to minority classes during training. The combined CTTVAE+TBS method generates utility-aligned samples for minority groups while maintaining competitive fidelity and enhanced privacy. Across six binary classification benchmarks, CTTVAE+TBS consistently outperforms baselines in minority-class downstream utility, demonstrating the effectiveness of latent space structuring and adaptive sampling.

## Method Summary
CTTVAE builds upon TTVAE, a transformer-based variational autoencoder for tabular data. It introduces a class-aware triplet margin loss applied to the latent means, which pulls same-class samples together and pushes different-class samples apart using semi-hard negative mining. Additionally, the training-by-sampling (TBS) mechanism modifies minibatch construction to blend the original class distribution with a uniform distribution via a convex combination parameter λ, thereby boosting the frequency of minority examples. After training, conditional generation for a target class is performed by filtering the latent space to that class, identifying k-nearest neighbors within the class, and synthesizing new latent points through stochastic triangle interpolation before decoding to tabular data. Hyperparameters (α, β, λ) are tuned via Optuna, and the method is evaluated primarily by machine learning efficacy (MLE) on a CatBoost downstream model, alongside fidelity and privacy metrics.

## Key Results
- CTTVAE+TBS achieves the highest minority-class MLE scores in 5 out of 6 benchmark datasets, often surpassing the performance of models trained on the original imbalanced data.
- The method maintains competitive fidelity (low Wasserstein Distance and Jensen-Shannon Divergence) and provides enhanced privacy (higher Nearest Neighbour Distance Ratio) relative to baseline generative models.
- Ablation studies confirm that both the triplet loss (latent structuring) and TBS are essential; removing either leads to a significant drop in minority utility.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Class-Aware Triplet Margin Loss for Latent Structuring
- **Claim:** The class-aware triplet margin loss restructures the latent space to produce tighter intra-class clusters and wider inter-class separation, improving the quality and class fidelity of conditionally generated minority samples.
- **Mechanism:** For each anchor sample in a batch, a semi-hard positive (same class) and a semi-hard negative (different class) are mined based on latent distances. The loss directly minimizes the anchor-positive distance while pushing the anchor-negative distance beyond a margin m. This geometric constraint is added to the TTVAE loss and acts on the latent mean vectors µ.
- **Core assumption:** A latent space with well-separated class manifolds enables reliable interpolation within a class-specific region without blending samples from other classes, yielding authentic and class-accurate synthetic data.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states the triplet loss restructures the latent space; Section 3.2 explains it eliminates blending of unrelated samples; the broader literature trend supports latent-space structuring for utility.
- **Break condition:** If triplet mining fails to find valid semi-hard negatives (e.g., in extremely high-dimensional or already well-separated spaces) or if the margin m is poorly tuned, the loss may provide no gradient or force artificial separation, harming reconstruction.

### Mechanism 2: Training-by-Sampling (TBS) for Minority Exposure
- **Claim:** TBS adaptively oversamples minority classes during training, increasing the model's exposure to underrepresented groups and preventing the generator from ignoring them.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of uniform minibatch sampling, TBS constructs batches by sampling data points according to a softened class distribution: PMF[c] = λ * P_orig[c] + (1-λ) * P_uniform[c]. This ensures minority classes appear more frequently without completely discarding the original data distribution.
- **Core assumption:** Standard training on naturally imbalanced data biases density estimation toward majority regions. Artificially increasing minority example frequency allocates more gradient updates to learning the minority class manifold.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions training-by-sampling adaptively increases exposure; Section 3.3 details the batch construction; prior work (CTGAN) validates sampling-level intervention for imbalance.
- **Break condition:** If λ is too low (near 0), training becomes dominated by uniform sampling, ignoring the true distribution and causing mode collapse. If λ is too high (near 1), the original imbalance persists and minority exposure remains inadequate.

### Mechanism 3: Class-Conditional Interpolation within Structured Latent Subsets
- **Claim:** Class-conditional generation via triangle interpolation within the class-separated latent subsets leverages the structured latent space to produce utility-aligned samples for a target class without crossing class boundaries.
- **Mechanism:** After training, for a target class c, the latent space is filtered to S_c = {(z_i, h_i): y_i = c}. For a base point z_i ∈ S_c, its k nearest neighbors within S_c are found. A synthetic point ẑ is created via weighted interpolation: ẑ = z_i + Σ wr * u_r * (ν_{i,r} - z_i), where weights decrease with neighbor rank and u_r ~ U(0,1) adds stochasticity. The decoder then reconstructs ẑ with the corresponding h_i.
- **Core assumption:** If the latent space is well-structured by the triplet loss, S_c forms a connected, convex-like region. Interpolating within this local neighborhood yields points that remain on the true class manifold.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes conditional generation that is both representative and utility-aligned; Section 3.2 describes the interpolation process; literature shows latent interpolation is common, but CTTVAE uniquely combines it with triplet-structured space and class-conditioned neighbor search.
- **Break condition:** If the triplet loss failed to create a well-separated latent space, nearest-neighbor search within S_c may retrieve points from overlapping class regions, causing interpolation to blend classes and generate low-utility minority samples.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Raw tabular input -> Preprocessor (per-feature embeddings) -> Transformer Encoder (produces contextual embeddings h and latent parameters µ, σ) -> Latent Sampler (z ~ N(µ, diag(σ²))) -> (Training) Triplet Mining Module (computes L_triplet on µ) -> Loss Aggregator (combines reconstruction loss, MMD, triplet loss) -> Decoder (reconstruction). For conditional generation: Stored (z, h) pairs -> Class filter to obtain S_c -> k-NN search within S_c -> Triangle interpolation to get ẑ -> Decoder with h_i -> Synthetic sample.

### Critical Path
1. During training, the TBS sampler feeds batches to the model.  
2. For each batch, the encoder produces (µ, σ, h).  
3. Triplet mining operates on µ to compute L_triplet.  
4. Total loss L_CTVAE backpropagated to update encoder and decoder.  
5. Post-training, for conditional generation of class c: filter stored (z, h) to S_c, then for each anchor z_i ∈ S_c, interpolate with its k-NN in S_c to get ẑ, and decode with the corresponding h_i.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Triplet loss weight α vs. reconstruction/MMD:** High α enforces strong class separation but may distort the latent manifold, harming reconstruction fidelity. Low α yields weaker class structure.  
- **TBS parameter λ vs. distribution fidelity:** λ → 0 (uniform) boosts minority exposure but risks generating samples far from the original distribution. λ → 1 (original) preserves distribution but may neglect minorities.  
- **Neighbor count k in interpolation:** Larger k increases diversity but risks crossing subtle sub-cluster boundaries within a class; smaller k yields safer but less diverse samples.  
- **Computational cost:** Triplet mining is O(batch_size²), increasing training time.

### Failure Signatures
- **Latent space visualization:** No visible class separation (triplet loss ineffective) or extreme fragmentation (over-regularization).  
- **Ablation utility drop:** Removing triplet loss (CTVAE vs. TTVAE+TBS) or TBS (CTVAE vs. TTVAE+TBS) leads to significant minority MLE drop.  
- **Conditional generation artifacts:** Synthetic minority samples with features inconsistent with the target class (e.g., fraud cases with typical transaction amounts) indicate poor class conditioning.  
- **Privacy-fidelity-utility imbalance:** Very high NNDR paired with very low WD/MLE may indicate synthetic data drifting unrealistically far from real data.  
- **Training instability:** Loss spikes when α is too high or when triplet mining fails to find valid triplets (many anchors skipped).

### First Three Experiments
1. **Ablation of components:** Train TTVAE, TTVAE+TBS, CTVAE, CTVAE+TBS. Compare minority-class MLE (primary), WD/JSD (fidelity), and NNDR (privacy). Expect CTVAE+TBS to dominate minority utility.  
2. **Fidelity-utility-privacy tradeoff scatter plot:** For each method, compute geometric mean of (minority MLE / Real MLE) vs. (minority NNDR / Real NNDR) and vs. (minority WD / Real WD). Expect CTVAE+TBS to cluster near (1, high NNDR, low WD).  
3. **Sensitivity analysis of λ (TBS) and α (triplet weight):** Fix other hyperparameters, sweep λ ∈ [0.3, 0.9] and α ∈ [0.5, 5]. Plot minority MLE vs. λ and vs. α. Expect optimal λ < 1 (e.g., 0.5-0.7) and moderate α (e.g., 1-2), with performance degrading at extremes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Self-adaptive Training-by-Sampling:** Can a self-adaptive version of TBS dynamically adjust class sampling probabilities during training based on training dynamics or dataset properties? The current TBS uses a fixed λ blending; manual tuning is required.  
2. **Rigorous Privacy Evaluation:** How does CTTVAE perform under Membership Inference Attacks (MIA) compared to existing baselines? Current privacy metrics are distance-based proxies; MIA offers a more realistic threat model.  
3. **Reducing Triplet Loss Overhead:** Can the computational overhead of triplet loss be reduced via approximate nearest neighbor mining or caching without degrading the latent space structuring benefits? The O(batch_size²) cost limits scalability.  
4. **Multi-Class Imbalance Generalization:** How well does CTTVAE generalize to multi-class imbalanced scenarios? The paper focuses on binary targets; the triplet loss's ability to separate numerous classes, especially rare ones, remains untested.

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters (λ for TBS, α for triplet loss, MMD β) used in final reported results are not disclosed; only search spaces are provided, hindering full replication.  
- Base TTVAE implementation details (e.g., MMD kernel specifics, early stopping criteria) are opaque, requiring reliance on an external codebase with potential undocumented choices.  
- The computational overhead of triplet mining is noted but not quantified in wall-clock time, limiting assessment of scalability to large datasets.
