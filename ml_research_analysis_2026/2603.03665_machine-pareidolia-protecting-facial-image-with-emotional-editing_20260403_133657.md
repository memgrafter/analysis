# Machine Pareidolia: Protecting Facial Image with Emotional Editing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03665
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03665
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** MAP achieves up to 11% higher black-box protection success rates than makeup baselines and 38% over noise-based methods, with average PSR gains of 73.76% and high perceptual quality (FID 24.21).

## Executive Summary
Facial recognition (FR) systems pose significant privacy risks. This paper introduces MAP (Machine Pareidolia), a method that protects facial images by subtly editing the subject's emotional expression to disguise their identity. Unlike makeup-based approaches that can introduce demographic bias, MAP uses emotion edits as a demographic-agnostic "cover" for adversarial identity noise. It fine-tunes a diffusion model with two core objectives: making the protected image mimic a target identity (to fool FR systems) and aligning the visual change with a specified emotion (to ensure naturalness). These potentially conflicting objectives are resolved via a synergistic gradient projection strategy and stabilized with Laplacian smoothness regularization on facial landmarks. Evaluated on CelebA-HQ and LADN, MAP substantially outperforms prior art in protection success, perceptual quality, and robustness to commercial APIs like Face++, while demonstrating applicability across diverse demographics and photographic styles.

## Method Summary
MAP begins with a pretrained score-based diffusion model. To generate a protected image \(x_p\) from a source image \(x_o\) and a target identity \(x_t\), it first adds noise to \(x_o\) and then denoises it via a deterministic DDIM reverse process guided by the fine-tuned model. The model is trained end-to-end with a composite loss combining: (1) an **identity loss** \(L_A\) that uses multiple surrogate FR models to pull the embedding of \(x_p\) toward that of \(x_t\) via cosine similarity; and (2) an **emotion loss** \(L_E\) that uses CLIP to align the visual change from \(x_o\) to \(x_p\) with the semantic change from "face" to a target emotion text (e.g., "surprised face"). A **synergistic gradient projection** mechanism adjusts the minibatch gradients of \(L_A\) and \(L_E\) at each layer by removing components that exacerbate conflict with the other task's historical gradient direction (approximated via an exponential moving average). This guides the model toward a shared optimum. **Perceptual regularization** includes a Laplacian smoothness loss \(L_LS\) on facial landmarks to preserve natural geometry during emotion edits, plus \(\ell_1\) and LPIPS losses. The diffusion model's own score-matching loss \(L_D\) is also included and alternated during training. The total weighted loss is backpropagated to update the model parameters.

## Key Results
- MAP achieves superior black-box protection success rates (PSR), with gains up to 11% over makeup baselines and 38% over noise-based methods, averaging 73.76% PSR improvement across evaluated FR models.
- It produces high-fidelity protected images (FID 24.21) and significantly reduces confidence scores of the Face++ API by 8–10% compared to unprotected images.
- The method shows strong, consistent performance across different demographics (age, gender, ethnicity) and challenging photographic styles (e.g., monochrome, Rembrandt lighting, backlighting), highlighting its universal applicability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dual-Objective Loss Coupling
- **Claim:** Jointly optimizing an adversarial identity objective and an emotion editing objective within a fine-tuned diffusion model yields protected images that both fool FR systems and appear natural.
- **Mechanism:** The score network is trained to minimize two losses simultaneously: (1) \(L_A\) uses surrogate FR models to pull \(x_p\)'s embedding closer to \(x_t\)'s embedding via cosine similarity. (2) \(L_E\) uses CLIP's text and image encoders to align the visual change (\(\Delta_{\text{vis}}\)) from \(x_o\) to \(x_p\) with the textual change (\(\Delta_{\text{txt}}\)) from "source" to "target" emotion. This couples the identity-mimicking transformation with a semantically coherent edit.
- **Core assumption:** The two objectives share a compatible optimization landscape; a solution exists within the model's capacity and the natural image manifold. The emotion edit provides a plausible cover for the adversarial noise.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states MAP "employs human emotion modifications to disguise original identities" and fine-tunes for "dual objectives, target identity and human expression." Section "Dual Objectives" describes \(L_A\) and \(L_E\) and their goal to "inject adversarial noises into the emotional change."
- **Break condition:** If the required identity-mimicking features cannot be plausibly incorporated into the chosen emotion edit (e.g., target identity differs in non-emotional, structural ways), the coupled optimization may fail, degrading perceptual quality or attack success.
