# Alternating Diffusion for Proximal Sampling with Zeroth Order Queries

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19633
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19633
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A zeroth-order proximal sampler achieving ~100× wall-clock speedup and reducing KL divergence to 0.02 on a 5D Gaussian-Lasso mixture, with deterministic runtime and successful multi-mode exploration.

## Executive Summary
Proximal sampling offers strong theoretical convergence under log-Sobolev conditions but practical implementations rely on rejection-based algorithms (RGO) that require small step sizes, gradient access, and have variable runtime. This paper introduces an alternating diffusion method that replaces the backward diffusion step with a particle-based Gaussian mixture score estimator. Only zeroth-order queries to the potential are needed. The algorithm deterministically simulates forward heat flow (Gaussian convolution) and approximates backward diffusion via Monte Carlo over a mixture built from current particles. Theory shows geometric contraction when the target satisfies a log-Sobolev inequality and score error is bounded. Experiments on synthetic distributions demonstrate significant speedups and effective exploration of separated modes.

## Method Summary
The algorithm operates on N particles. Each outer iteration consists of: (1) a forward step adding Gaussian noise (exact convolution) to each particle, and (2) a backward diffusion step simulated over T sub-steps. The drift for the backward step is an estimate of the score ∇log π(z) obtained by constructing a Gaussian mixture surrogate from all current particles and their forward-noisy versions. For a given particle's position z_t during diffusion, the mixture parameters are derived from the posterior distribution under the Gaussian convolution. Monte Carlo samples are drawn from this mixture, the potential f evaluated, and a weighted average forms the drift estimate. No rejection sampling occurs. Key hyperparameters are step size h, number of diffusion sub-steps T, number of particles N, and number of Monte Carlo proposals M per drift evaluation.

## Key Results
- Theoretical: Provides a proximal sampling interpretation via alternating forward/backward SDEs and proves a contraction factor of (1+h/C_LSI)^{-O(1)} per outer iteration under LSI and bounded score error.
- Experimental: On a 5D Gaussian-Lasso mixture, using N=100, h=1/10, T=10 for 100 iterations matches the quality of 950 RGO iterations (h=1/135) with ~100× wall-clock speedup, reducing KL from 1.4 to 0.02.
- Multi-modal exploration: On disjoint 3D tori, the method finds both modes while the In-and-Out sampler stagnates, demonstrating acceleration from particle interactions.
- Offers deterministic runtime, full parallelization, and flexibility in step size choice.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Alternating Diffusion Implements Proximal Sampling without Rejection
The algorithm interprets a proximal iteration as alternating between a forward SDE (exact Gaussian convolution) and a backward SDE (reverse-time diffusion from the convolved distribution). Instead of using rejection sampling to simulate the backward step (as in RGO), it approximates the required score ∇log(π(z)) via a Monte Carlo estimate. This estimate is built from a Gaussian mixture surrogate derived from the current particle set and their forward-noisy counterparts. The forward step is exact and cheap; the backward step becomes deterministic and parallelizable. The core assumption is that the target π satisfies a log-Sobolev inequality (LSI) with constant C_LSI, and the score estimation error remains bounded. If the score error Λ exceeds a threshold proportional to the step size h and LSI constant, the geometric convergence guarantee degrades.

### Mechanism 2: Particle Gaussian Mixture Score Estimation Enables Interaction and Larger Steps
The score estimator constructs an intermediate distribution q̂_{k+1/2} as a Gaussian mixture centered at the forward-noisy particles. For a query point z during the backward diffusion, the posterior under this mixture yields weights that reweight particles inversely by their density under q̂_{k+1/2}. This down-weights overpopulated regions and encourages exploration of sparse, separated modes. The interaction between particles—the fact that the score at one particle's location depends on the entire cloud—accelerates mixing compared to independent chains. This mechanism allows the use of larger step sizes h, limited only by global geometry (e.g., inter-mode distances) rather than local smoothness constraints that bind rejection samplers. If the particle cloud becomes degenerate (e.g., all particles collapse to one mode), the mixture may lose diversity and the interaction benefit diminishes.

### Mechanism 3: Time-Discretization Error is Controlled via Sub-Stepping
The backward diffusion is simulated using T Euler-Maruyama sub-steps of size η = h/T. The drift used in each sub-step is held constant (frozen) from the previous sub-step's location. Lemma 2 bounds the one-step KL increase due to this discretization error as O(η² L⁴), where L bounds the smoothness of intermediate distributions. Choosing T = O(h) makes this error negligible compared to the geometric decay factor from the continuous-time proximal sampler, which is roughly (1+h/C_LSI)^{−c}. Thus, the larger h (which reduces the number of outer iterations) does not incur a prohibitive discretization penalty, as long as T scales appropriately. If the potential f is extremely ill-conditioned (large local Lipschitz constants), the required T may grow and offset gains from fewer outer iterations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Log-Sobolev Inequality (LSI)**: A functional inequality that controls the convergence of distributions under Langevin-type dynamics. In this paper, the constant C_LSI directly appears in the contraction factor. If π satisfies LSI with constant C, then the relative entropy H_π(μ) decays exponentially under the proximal sampler with rate (1+h/C_LSI)^{-1} in continuous time.

- **Proximal Sampling as Alternating SDEs**: The proximal update x_{k+1} = argmin_x [ h f(x) + (1/2)||x - (x_k + √h ξ)||² ] can be represented by alternating a forward SDE that adds Gaussian noise and a backward SDE that reverses this diffusion conditioned on the target. This SDE interpretation is key: the forward step is exact convolution, the backward step uses the score of the convolved target.

- **Score Estimation via Gaussian Mixture Posterior**: The posterior distribution of the original point x given a forward-noisy observation y and the current particle cloud (X_k) is a Gaussian mixture. Its normalized weights and means can be computed in closed form using only the potential evaluations at the particles. This allows Monte Carlo approximation of the score ∇log π(z) at any z by sampling from this mixture and evaluating f, without requiring gradients.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Initial particles X₀ → Forward module (add N(0,hI)) → Y_{k+1/2} → Score estimator module (compute Gaussian mixture parameters from (X_k, Y_{k+1/2}), draw M proposals, evaluate f) → Backward diffusion module (T Euler-Maruyama steps using estimated drift) → Updated particles X_{k+1} → (repeat)
- **Critical path**:
  1. Initialize N particles X₀ (e.g., from N(0,I)).
  2. For each outer iteration k:
     a. Forward: Perturb each x ∈ X_k by N(0, hI) to get Y_{k+1/2}.
     b. For each particle i in X_k and each diffusion sub-step t from T down to 1:
        i. Compute mixture means m_{i,j} and normalized weights w_{i,j} for the query point z_t (the current state of particle i's diffusion).
        ii. Draw M proposals from the mixture ∑_j w_{i,j} N(m_{i,j}, σ_t² I).
        iii. Evaluate f on proposals, compute weighted average as drift estimate.
        iv. Euler-Maruyama update: z_{t−1} = z_t + η * drift + √η * N(0,I).
     c. Set X_{k+1} to the final z₀ values.
  3. Repeat for K iterations; monitor convergence via KL estimate.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Step size h: Larger h reduces number of outer iterations (better geometric rate) but requires larger T (more sub-steps) to control discretization error. Empirically, h=1/10 works for 5D Gaussian-Lasso; h=1 works for disjoint tori.
  - N vs. M: N controls fidelity of the Gaussian mixture surrogate to the target; M controls Monte Carlo variance of the score estimate. Total computational cost scales with N×M; Figure 6 suggests total proposal count matters more than the split.
  - Noise schedule: σ_t² linearly interpolated from σ_min² (e.g., 0.01) to h. Small σ_min ensures score estimator remains accurate near t=0.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - KL divergence plateaus or increases: score estimation error Λ too large; increase N, M or decrease h.
  - Particles collapse to a single mode: mixture surrogate degenerate; increase N, add regularization to weights, or check interaction logic.
  - High variance across random seeds: Monte Carlo noise dominant; increase M or use antithetic sampling.
  - Out-of-memory: N and M multiply memory footprint; reduce both while maintaining product if needed.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Gaussian Lasso mixture (d=5)**: Verify KL contraction and compare wall-clock speedup vs. RGO baseline. Check that N=100 interacting particles converges faster than N=1 independent chains (same total particles).
  2. **Disjoint 3D tori (uniform on two separated tori)**: Test ability to explore both modes. Visualize particle cloud at iterations k=3,10,200; should fill both components. In-and-Out should fail to cross the gap.
  3. **Ablation over step size h**: Run with h ∈ {1/20, 10/135, 1/10, 2/10, 1} keeping N, M fixed. Observe larger h accelerates early progress (as in Figure 5) but may require tuning T.

## Limitations
- **LSI assumption**: The theoretical guarantee requires the target π_X to satisfy a log-Sobolev inequality with finite constant. Many real-world posteriors (heavy-tailed, high-barrier multimodal) may violate this, breaking the contraction guarantee.
- **Score error scaling**: The dependence of the score error Λ on dimension d, number of modes, and inter-mode distances is not quantified. In high dimensions, Λ may grow, potentially degrading the geometric rate.
- **Benchmark scope**: Evaluation is limited to two synthetic distributions (Gaussian-Lasso mixture, disjoint tori). Performance on standard Bayesian inference tasks or high-dimensional neural network posteriors remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **High**: The algorithmic mechanism (alternating diffusion with particle Gaussian mixture score estimator) is clearly described, internally consistent, and ablation experiments confirm the benefit of particle interaction.
- **Medium**: The theoretical contraction result (Theorem 1, Lemma 2) is sound under its assumptions (LSI, smoothness, bounded error), but the paper does not analyze how violations of these assumptions affect practical convergence rates.
- **Low**: Claims about "100× wall-clock speedup" and deterministic runtime are specific to the reported experimental setups; generalization to other hardware (GPU vs. CPU), problem classes, or higher dimensions is not established.
