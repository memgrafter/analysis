# On Robustness and Chain-of-Thought Consistency of RL-Finetuned VLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.12506
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12506
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: RL fine-tuning improves VLM accuracy on visual reasoning benchmarks but reduces robustness to textual perturbations and erodes chain-of-thought consistency.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates the impact of reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning on vision-language models (VLMs). While RL fine-tuning boosts performance on visual reasoning tasks, it introduces a trade-off: models become more vulnerable to simple text perturbations (misleading captions or incorrect CoT prefixes) and exhibit degraded consistency between their reasoning traces and final answers. Through controlled stress tests across several open-source RL-finetuned VLMs and comparisons with closed-source models, the authors uncover that RL's reward-only-correctness objective compresses the output distribution, making models overconfident and overly reliant on language priors, which harms robustness. Adversarial data augmentation helps with caption perturbations but does not fix CoT faithfulness; adding a faithfulness-aware reward can align reasoning and answers yet may induce shortcut learning when combined with augmentation. The study concludes that evaluation must extend beyond accuracy to include robustness and faithfulness metrics, and future training protocols should jointly optimize for all three goals.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate robustness by applying perturbations—Stop-Think (suppressing reasoning), Wrong-Caption (misleading description), and Wrong-Think (incorrect CoT prefix)—to prompts from eight visual reasoning benchmarks. For RL fine-tuning, they employ GRPO on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct using a mixture of SAT2 and Pixmo‑Count datasets. Optionally, Geometry3K data are included. Adversarial augmentation is applied to 40% of rollouts: a wrong/correct caption or think prefix is prepended (10% each). The base reward is binary based on final answer correctness; optionally a faithfulness reward from Qwen3‑32B verifies consistency between `<think>` and `<answer>`. They compare open-source fine-tuned variants (SpaceR, Video‑R1, Vision‑R1, VLAA‑Thinker, ViGoRL‑Spatial) with closed models (o3, o4‑mini, Gemini‑2.5‑Pro). Throughout training, they monitor output entropy and P(Correct Letter) to quantify reasoning behavior.

## Key Results
- RL fine-tuning introduces an accuracy–faithfulness trade‑off: higher benchmark scores come at the cost of reduced CoT consistency and lower robustness to context shifts.
- Simple textual perturbations (misleading captions, incorrect CoT prefixes) cause large accuracy drops and suppress the probability of the correct answer token, often while increasing output entropy.
- Open-source RL‑finetuned VLMs are significantly more fragile than closed-source models like o3 and Gemini, which maintain better robustness and reasoning faithfulness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: RL fine-tuning narrows output distribution
- **Claim**: RL fine-tuning that optimizes only final answer correctness can inadvertently narrow the model’s output distribution and increase overconfidence on textual priors, thereby reducing reasoning faithfulness and robustness.
- **Mechanism**: RL with verifiable rewards (e.g., GRPO) progressively lowers token‑level entropy across both clean and perturbed prompts. This compression makes predictions more peaky; when a misleading textual cue appears, the model’s confidence in its textual prior is not balanced by uncertainty, leading to brittleness.
- **Evidence**: The abstract states the accuracy–faithfulness trade‑off; Section 3.2/Figure 9 show entropy suppression during RL; neighboring work “The Cost of Reasoning: Chain‑of‑Thought Induces Overconfidence” links over‑confident reasoning to safety issues.
- **Break condition**: Adding explicit penalties for CoT–answer inconsistency (e.g., faithfulness‑aware reward) and careful regularization could mitigate the trade‑off, though the paper notes potential instabilities.

### Mechanism 2: Textual perturbations hijack decision
- **Claim**: Misleading captions or incorrect CoT prefixes cause substantial accuracy drops and actively suppress the probability assigned to the correct answer token.
- **Mechanism**: VLMs heavily rely on provided textual context, often at the expense of visual grounding. When text contradicts the image, language priors bias the generation, decreasing P(Correct Letter) even if entropy rises.
- **Evidence**: The abstract notes perturbation‑induced drops and increased entropy; Section 2.3/Figures 6‑7 show entropy up and P(Correct) down under adversarial prompts; neighboring titles like “Visual CoT Makes VLMs Smarter but More Fragile” address related vulnerabilities.
- **Break condition**: Training the model to detect and disregard misleading cues could help; adversarial augmentation partially mitigates but does not fully prevent suppression.

### Mechanism 3: Shortcut learning with augmentation + faithfulness reward
- **Claim**: Combining adversarial data augmentation with a faithfulness‑aware reward can collapse training onto shortcuts, where the model copies superficially helpful captions or think strings instead of genuinely reasoning.
- **Mechanism**: The faithfulness reward (from an LLM judge) checks alignment between `<think>` and `<answer>`. With augmentation, some prompts include correct captions or think prefixes; reproducing them yields high reward without reconciling visual evidence, incentivizing mimicry.
- **Evidence**: The abstract warns of shortcut risk; Section 3.3/Figure 16 show augmented+faithfulness models copying misleading captions verbatim while augmentation‑only models reset; neighboring “The Synergy Dilemma of Long‑CoT SFT and RL” reports similar instability.
- **Break condition**: Augmentation that prevents literal copying (e.g., transforming captions) or explicit visual‑grounding modules might avoid shortcuts; closed‑source models appear to possess such inductive biases.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Vision encoder → Visual tokens → Multimodal fusion (project/interleave with text) → LLM backbone (decoder‑only transformer with `<think>` and `<answer>` tokens) → Generation of `<think>` and `<answer>` → Reward modules (correctness checker, optional faithfulness judge) → GRPO update.

**Critical path**  
1. Sample a training instance: image, question, optional augmentation (wrong/right caption or think string).  
2. Encode the image → visual tokens.  
3. Format the prompt: `<image>` placeholder, optional caption, question, possibly pre‑filled `<think>` seed.  
4. Model rollout: generate `<think>` tokens, then `<answer>` tokens.  
5. Parse answer token; compare to ground truth → correctness reward.  
6. If faithfulness reward enabled, invoke LLM judge with (question, full response) → binary consistency signal.  
7. Combine rewards and perform GRPO update using collected rollouts.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Correctness‑only reward maximizes accuracy but ignores reasoning fidelity; adding faithfulness aligns answer and reasoning but may incentivize shallow copying when augmentation is used.  
- Including both correct and incorrect augmentations teaches the model to discriminate trustworthy cues, but adds noise and can destabilize training.  
- Zero KL coefficient gives policy freedom to deviate from reference, enabling rapid gains but risking output distribution collapse and overconfidence.  
- Augmentation improves robustness to Wrong‑Caption but not to Wrong‑Think, indicating hijacking the reasoning chain is harder.  
- The strong textual prior suggests visual grounding must be explicitly reinforced, not left to emerge implicitly.

**Failure signatures**  
- Large accuracy drop on perturbed prompts despite high clean accuracy.  
- Entropy increases under adversarial prompts while P(Correct Letter) drops sharply.  
- Unfaithful generations: final answer contradicts the content within `<think>`.  
- Shortcut behavior under Aug+Faithfulness: model copies misleading caption or think verbatim and fails to reason.  
- Monotonic entropy decrease during RL training, signaling overconfidence that harms robustness.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Perturbation sensitivity study: Evaluate base and Stop‑Think conditions across models; measure accuracy and entropy to see how thinking suppression affects confidence.  
2. Augmentation ablation: Compare three RL training regimens (no augmentation, +geometry data, +augmentation) on Base, Wrong‑Think, and Wrong‑Caption performance over training steps.  
3. Reward intervention: Fine‑tune with faithfulness‑aware reward (with and without augmentation) to assess trade‑offs between consistency and robustness; manually inspect generations for shortcut copying (Boxes 22‑23).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can training protocols simultaneously improve accuracy, robustness to text perturbations, and CoT faithfulness in VLMs?  
- *Basis in paper*: “motivate training and assessment protocols that jointly emphasize correctness, robustness, and the faithfulness” (Abstract)  
- *Why unresolved*: Existing RL fine‑tuning yields accuracy while harming robustness/faithfulness; separate fixes (augmentation, faithfulness rewards) are insufficient.  
- *What evidence would resolve it*: New reward formulations that incorporate all three goals; benchmarks measuring them; empirical demonstrations of simultaneous improvement.

**Open Question 2:** Why do open‑source RL‑finetuned VLMs show larger robustness and faithfulness failures than closed‑source models, and which specific training choices cause this gap?  
- *Basis in paper*: “the gap reflects a shortcoming in current open‑source RL fine‑tuning rather than an inherent limitation” (Sec 2.2)  
- *Why unresolved*: No controlled study isolates responsible factors (e.g., data scale, RL algorithm, model size) between open and closed models.  
- *What evidence would resolve it*: Ablations varying only one training aspect; probing internal representations; reproducing closed‑model robustness with open recipes.

**Open Question 3:** Why does combining faithfulness‑aware rewards with adversarial data augmentation cause shortcut learning, and how can this instability be avoided?  
- *Basis in paper*: “combining faithfulness enforcement with data augmentation… risks collapsing onto shortcut strategies” (Sec 3.3)  
- *Why unresolved*: Mechanistic understanding lacking; no successful joint training reported.  
- *What evidence would resolve it*: Analysis of reward gradients; design of stabilizing techniques (e.g., curriculum, regularization); training runs that avoid collapse while maintaining both robustness and faithfulness.

## Limitations
- **Incomplete reporting**: Details of augmentation generation (model version, sampling) and evaluation decoding settings (temperature, stop tokens) are not fully disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- **Closed‑source opacity**: API specifics for closed models (o3, Gemini) are undisclosed, making comparisons less controlled and potentially biased.
- **Reproducibility gaps**: RL training lacks seed and log documentation; faithfulness judge (Qwen3‑32B) may be biased with no human validation; experiments only on one base model; no checks for data leakage in augmentation strings.

## Confidence
- **C1**: RL fine‑tuning reduces robustness and CoT consistency. **High** – multi‑benchmark results, entropy suppression, consistent patterns.
- **C2**: Accuracy–faithfulness trade‑off exists. **High** – directly observed across RL runs.
- **C3**: Open‑source models fail more than closed‑source. **Medium** – based on few closed models; possible selection bias.
- **C4**: Adversarial augmentation improves caption robustness but not faithfulness drift. **Medium** – partial mitigation shown, but training instability may confound.
- **C5**: Faithfulness‑aware reward + augmentation leads to shortcut learning. **Medium** – qualitative examples exist, needs broader validation.
