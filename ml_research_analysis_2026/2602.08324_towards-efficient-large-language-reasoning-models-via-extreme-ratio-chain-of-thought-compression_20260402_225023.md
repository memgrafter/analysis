# Towards Efficient Large Language Reasoning Models via Extreme-Ratio Chain-of-Thought Compression
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08324
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08324
- Reference count: 35
- Primary result: Achieves 73% token reduction on MATH-500 with a 0.6% accuracy gain over SOTA, and 80.2% accuracy on GSM8K at a 0.2 compression ratio, outperforming baselines under extreme compression.

## Executive Summary
Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning improves LLM problem-solving but incurs high token costs. Existing compression methods degrade accuracy at high compression ratios due to loss of logical fidelity. This paper introduces Extra-CoT, a three-stage framework for extreme-ratio CoT compression. It first trains a semantically-preserved, question-aware compressor using formula-aware annotations to generate high-fidelity compressed CoTs. Then, mixed-ratio supervised fine-tuning teaches the model to obey variable compression budgets via a policy token. Finally, Constrained and Hierarchical Ratio Policy Optimization (CHRPO), a reinforcement learning algorithm with hierarchical rewards, explicitly pushes for ultralow token usage while preserving correctness. On Qwen3-1.7B, Extra-CoT reduces tokens by 73% on MATH-500 while slightly improving accuracy, and achieves 80.2% accuracy on GSM8K at a 0.2 compression ratio where baselines fail. Across three mathematical benchmarks, it consistently offers a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off.

## Method Summary
The method comprises three stages. Stage 1: A compressor is trained to condense full CoTs while preserving semantics, using question-aware inputs and formula-aware annotations to maintain logical structure. Stage 2: The base model (Qwen3-1.7B) undergoes mixed-ratio supervised fine-tuning on compressed CoTs generated by the compressor at various compression ratios; a policy token is introduced to indicate the target compression budget. Stage 3: CHRPO, a reinforcement learning algorithm, fine-tunes the model with hierarchical rewards that separately incentivize correctness and ultralow token usage, enabling extreme compression without sacrificing accuracy. The final model (Extra-CoT) thus produces highly compressed CoTs that retain problem-solving efficacy.

## Key Results
- On MATH-500, Extra-CoT reduces token usage by 73% compared to full CoT while achieving a 0.6% accuracy gain over the state-of-the-art compression method.
- On GSM8K, it reaches 80.2% accuracy at a 0.2 compression ratio, where prior methods (TokenSkip, Thinkless) collapse entirely.
- Across three mathematical reasoning benchmarks, Extra-CoT consistently yields a better accuracy-efficiency trade-off than baselines, especially under extreme compression.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works because it combines high-fidelity compression with explicit budget conditioning and hierarchical optimization. The compressor's semantic preservation and question-awareness prevent logical degradation during compression. Mixed-ratio SFT teaches the model to generate CoTs at diverse compression levels, making it robust to extreme budgets. CHRPO then pushes the model to ultralow ratios by rewarding token reduction without compromising correctness, thanks to a hierarchical reward structure that separates these objectives. This staged design ensures that each component addresses a specific challenge: fidelity, adaptability, and extreme optimization.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Original full CoTs -> Compressor (semantic-preserving, question-aware) -> Compressed CoTs at multiple ratios -> Mixed-ratio SFT on base model (with policy token) -> Initial compressed model -> CHRPO (RL with hierarchical rewards) -> Final Extra-CoT model

**Critical Path**  
The pipeline's success hinges on: (1) high-fidelity compression that retains logical steps, (2) diverse ratio conditioning during SFT, and (3) CHRPO's ability to further compress without accuracy loss via hierarchical rewards.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Compressor design balances semantic fidelity and compression ratio; higher ratio risks losing steps.  
- Mixed-ratio SFT must cover a wide range of ratios (including extreme) to prepare for CHRPO; more ratios increase training complexity.  
- CHRPO reward weighting: overemphasizing token reduction may harm correctness; underemphasizing may limit compression gains.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Compressor produces incoherent or incomplete reasoning, leading to accuracy collapse at high compression.  
- Model after SFT fails to respect policy token, generating CoTs of wrong length.  
- CHRPO degenerates: either token count drops but accuracy plummets, or accuracy remains but token reduction stalls.

**First 3 Experiments**  
1. Evaluate compressor alone: measure compression ratio vs. semantic similarity (e.g., BLEU, ROUGE) on a validation set to ensure high fidelity.  
2. After mixed-ratio SFT, test the model's ability to hit target compression ratios (e.g., 0.1, 0.2, 0.5) and measure accuracy on a held-out set.  
3. Run CHRPO with varying reward weights (accuracy vs. token reduction) and select the configuration that maximizes the accuracy-efficiency trade-off on a validation set.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to a single model scale (Qwen3-1.7B) and three math-focused benchmarks; generalizability to other models and domains is uncertain.
- Compressor training relies on formula-aware annotations, which may require domain-specific engineering and are not universally available.
- No statistical reporting (means, variances) for accuracy numbers, raising reproducibility concerns and risk of overfitting to evaluation sets.
- Training overhead (compressor tuning, RL fine-tuning) is not quantified; net efficiency gains in practice may be offset by these costs.
