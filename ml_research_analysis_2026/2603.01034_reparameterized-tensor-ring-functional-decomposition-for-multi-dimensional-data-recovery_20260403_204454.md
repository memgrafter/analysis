# Reparameterized Tensor Ring Functional Decomposition for Multi-Dimensional Data Recovery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv:2603.01034
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01034
- References: 40
- Primary result: Reparameterized Tensor Ring Functional Decomposition (RepTRFD) mitigates spectral bias of INRs by reparameterizing TR factors, achieving up to +2 dB PSNR gains in inpainting and consistent improvements across multi-dimensional data recovery tasks with moderate computational cost.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a key limitation of Tensor Ring (TR) decomposition: its restriction to discrete grid representations. The authors propose a continuous functional extension using Implicit Neural Representations (INRs), enabling TR-based models to handle continuous coordinates. Through frequency-domain analysis, they reveal that the spectral content of TR factors directly bounds the high-frequency components of the reconstructed tensor, and that the inherent spectral bias of INRs impedes learning these details. To overcome this, they introduce Reparameterized TRFD, where each TR factor is expressed as a structured product of a learnable latent tensor (generated by an INR) and a fixed basis. This reparameterization theoretically amplifies gradient responses for high frequencies, facilitating better capture of fine details. The method is supported by principled Xavier-style initialization for the fixed basis and Lipschitz continuity guarantees, ensuring stable training. Comprehensive experiments on image inpainting, denoising, super-resolution, and point cloud recovery demonstrate consistent superiority, e.g., up to +2 dB PSNR in inpainting, ~1 dB in denoising/SR, and lower NRMSE in 3D recovery, with only moderate increase in computational cost.

## Method Summary
RepTRFD reparameterizes each Tensor Ring factor \( G^{(k)} \) as \( G^{(k)} = C^{(k)} \times_3 B^{(k)} \), where \( C^{(k)} \) is a learnable latent tensor produced by an INR (a multi-layer perceptron with a shared sinusoidal frequency embedding) and \( B^{(k)} \) is a fixed basis matrix. The INR takes a normalized coordinate \( v_k \) as input, applies sinusoidal embedding \( z_k = \sin(\omega_0 (W v_k + b)) \), and passes through an MLP (1-2 layers, 256 hidden units) to output a slice of \( C^{(k)} \). The fixed basis \( B^{(k)} \) is initialized with a Xavier uniform distribution scaled by the dimensions and then kept constant during training. The overall reconstruction is obtained by contracting all factors \( G^{(k)} \) via the Tensor Ring trace operation. The model is trained with coordinate sampling, using task-specific losses that combine data fidelity (e.g., L2) with total variation or sparse total variation regularization (except for point clouds, which use pure L2). Key hyperparameters include the TR ranks \( r_k \) (typically 16-20), the expansion factor \( \beta \) (with \( R_k = \beta r_k \), usually 3-10), and the frequency parameter \( \omega_0 \) (90-240). Optimization uses Adam with a learning rate of \( 3 \times 10^{-4} \).

## Key Results
- Achieves up to +2 dB PSNR improvement on image inpainting compared to state-of-the-art tensor-based and INR methods.
- Delivers approximately +1 dB PSNR gains in denoising and super-resolution tasks.
- Reduces NRMSE on 3D point cloud recovery, while maintaining moderate computational overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Spectral Bound of TR Factors
- **Claim:** The spectral content of Tensor Ring (TR) factors directly bounds the high-frequency components of the reconstructed tensor, limiting the model's ability to capture fine details.
- **Mechanism:** Using mode‑k Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT), the frequency components of each TR factor propagate to the corresponding dimension of the reconstructed tensor via the TR contraction operation. Theorem 1 mathematically shows that high‑frequency attenuation in TR factors leads to similar attenuation in the output tensor, with the bound constant depending on other cores' magnitudes.
- **Core assumption:** TR decomposition structure holds, and DFT properties apply to tensor operations; factors' frequencies are representative of the reconstruction's spectral behavior.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: "frequency-domain analysis reveals that the spectral content of TR factors directly bounds the reconstructed tensor’s high frequencies"  
  - Section 3.3, Theorem 1 provides the formal proof using mode‑k DFT and trace properties.
- **Break condition:** This mechanism fails if TR decomposition is not used, or if data is inherently low‑frequency with no high‑frequency details, making the analysis irrelevant.

### Mechanism 2: Gradient Amplification via Reparameterization
- **Claim:** Reparameterizing TR factors as a structured product of a learnable latent tensor and a fixed basis amplifies gradient responses for high‑frequency components, improving training dynamics and enabling better capture of fine details.
- **Mechanism:** Each TR factor is reparameterized as \( G^{(k)} = C^{(k)} \times_3 B^{(k)} \), where \( C^{(k)} \) is a learnable latent tensor generated by an INR and \( B^{(k)} \) is a fixed basis. Theorem 2 shows that for an appropriately chosen \( B^{(k)} \), the absolute ratio of gradients for high‑frequency to low‑frequency components is increased relative to the original parameterization, making optimization more sensitive to high frequencies.
- **Core assumption:** Existence of a basis \( B^{(k)} \) that amplifies gradients as per Theorem 2; loss landscape has separable frequency components; gradient‑based optimization is used.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: "We introduce Reparameterized TRFD, where each factor is a structured product of a learnable latent tensor and a fixed basis, theoretically amplifying high-frequency gradient responses."  
  - Section 3.3, Theorem 2 and equation (6) define the reparameterization and gradient amplification property.
- **Break condition:** If the fixed basis is poorly initialized or if gradient amplification does not translate to practical improvement due to noise, poor conditioning, or conflicting loss terms.

### Mechanism 3: Initialization and Lipschitz Guarantees
- **Claim:** A principled Xavier‑style initialization for the fixed basis and Lipschitz continuity guarantees ensure stable and effective training of the reparameterized model, preventing divergence and improving convergence.
- **Mechanism:** Theorem 3 derives a variance‑preserving initialization scheme for \( B^{(k)} \) by balancing forward and backward pass variances, using a uniform distribution with scale based on dimensions. Theorem 4 proves that under conditions (Lipschitz activations, bounded spectral norms of MLP weights, bounded outputs), the overall mapping \( g_\phi(v) \) is globally Lipschitz continuous, ensuring bounded sensitivity to input changes.
- **Core assumption:** MLPs use Lipschitz continuous activation functions (e.g., ReLU, sigmoid); weight matrices have bounded spectral norms; outputs are bounded; fixed basis \( B^{(k)} \) is constant.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: "A principled Xavier-style initialization and Lipschitz continuity guarantee are provided."  
  - Section 3.3, Theorems 3 (initialization) and 4 (Lipschitz continuity) with detailed proofs.
- **Break condition:** If activation functions are not Lipschitz (e.g., unbounded periodic functions without clipping), or if weight norms grow unbounded during training, Lipschitz continuity may be violated, causing instability.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
- Shared sinusoidal frequency embedding for all modes.
- Mode‑specific MLP branches generating latent tensor slices \( C^{(k)}(:v_k:) \).
- Fixed basis matrices \( B^{(k)} \) for each mode.
- TR contraction operation \( \Phi \) combining factors \( G^{(k)} = C^{(k)} \times_3 B^{(k)} \).

### Critical Path
Input coordinate \( v = [v_1, ..., v_d] \) → for each \( v_k \): sinusoidal embedding → MLP → \( C^{(k)}(:v_k:) \) → multiply by \( B^{(k)} \) to get \( G^{(k)}(:v_k:) \) → contract all \( G^{(k)} \) via trace operation to output scalar.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Fixed vs. learnable basis:** Fixed reduces overfitting and stabilizes training but might limit expressiveness; learnable adds flexibility but risks instability (see ablation in Section C.1).
- **Latent dimension expansion factor \( \beta \):** Larger \( \beta \) increases capacity and high‑frequency modeling but raises computational cost; tradeoff studied in Fig. 9.
- **Frequency parameter \( \omega_0 \):** Controls initial spectral bias; higher values promote high‑frequency learning but may affect convergence.

### Failure Signatures
- Blurry or smooth reconstructions lacking edges/textures indicating insufficient high‑frequency capture.
- Training loss plateauing at high values or diverging, suggesting poor basis initialization or violation of Lipschitz conditions.
- Overfitting to noise if basis is too expressive or regularization is insufficient.

### First 3 Experiments
1. Image and Video Inpainting: Reconstructing missing regions in color images, multispectral images, hyperspectral images, and videos under various sampling ratios.
2. Multispectral Image Denoising: Removing Gaussian noise from MSIs and HSIs at different noise levels (SD=0.1 to 0.3).
3. Image Super‑Resolution: Upscaling low-resolution images to high-resolution (×3, ×4, ×6) using DIV2K dataset.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can the reparameterization strategy be extended to other tensor decompositions such as Tucker and CP?**  
   *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The authors state in the conclusion: "Future work includes extending the framework for Tucker decomposition and block‑term tensor decomposition."  
   *Why unresolved:* The current work focuses exclusively on Tensor Ring decomposition; other decompositions have different factor interactions and may require novel reparameterization schemes.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Developing analogous reparameterized functional decompositions for Tucker and CP, with theoretical analysis of frequency propagation and experimental validation on recovery tasks.

2. **Is it possible to design a data‑adaptive fixed basis that maintains stability while further improving high‑frequency component learning?**  
   *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The paper uses a random fixed basis with Xavier initialization and shows that a learnable basis leads to overfitting and degradation. This suggests that while fixed basis is crucial, its random nature might not be optimal.  
   *Why unresolved:* The basis is chosen randomly without considering data statistics; a principled data‑driven basis could potentially enhance performance.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Propose a method to construct the basis from data (e.g., via PCA or learned initialization) and demonstrate consistent improvement over random initialization across tasks.

3. **How does RepTRFD's reparameterization compare with other spectral bias mitigation techniques (e.g., Fourier features, alternative activations) in implicit neural representations?**  
   *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The paper compares with various INR‑based methods (PEMLP, SIREN, Gauss, WIRE, FINER) but these use different architectures; the specific contribution of reparameterization versus other design choices is not isolated.  
   *Why unresolved:* The relative effectiveness of reparameterization within the TR structure versus other INR enhancements remains unclear.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled experiments that decouple reparameterization from other factors (e.g., using the same base network with and without reparameterization, and with other bias mitigation techniques) to measure impact on frequency learning.

4. **What is the theoretical relationship between the TR rank, expansion factor, and the maximum representable frequency in RepTRFD?**  
   *Basis in paper:* [inferred] Theorem 1 links factor spectra to reconstructed tensor frequencies, and Theorem 2 shows gradient amplification. However, no explicit formula relates model capacity (ranks, β) to frequency bandwidth.  
   *Why unresolved:* Understanding this relationship would guide hyperparameter selection for tasks requiring fine details.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Derive a bound on the highest frequency that can be represented given \( r_k \) and \( \beta \), and verify it experimentally by synthesizing data with known frequency content.

## Limitations
- **Reproduction details are incomplete:** Training schedule, batch size, coordinate sampling strategy, exact TV/SSTV formulations, and SR downsampling operators are not described, hindering exact replication.
- **Theoretical guarantees rely on strict assumptions:** The bounds assume fixed basis after init, bounded weight spectral norms, and Lipschitz activations, which are not enforced during training; drift may invalidate guarantees.
- **Evaluation scope is narrow:** Results are limited to four tasks on specific datasets; generalisation to other domains (e.g., medical volumes, light fields) or large‑scale data remains unexplored.
