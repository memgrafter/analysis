# ViCLIP-OT: The First Foundation Vision-Language Model for Vietnamese Image-Text Retrieval with Optimal Transport

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22678
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22678
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Achieves 67.34% average Recall@K on UIT-OpenViIC, improving CLIP baseline by 5.75 percentage points.

## Executive Summary
ViCLIP-OT is the first foundation vision-language model designed specifically for Vietnamese image-text retrieval, addressing the suboptimal performance of existing models in low-resource language settings. It combines CLIP-style contrastive learning with a novel Similarity-Graph Regularized Optimal Transport (SIGROT) loss to enhance global cross-modal consistency and reduce the modality gap. On the UIT-OpenViIC benchmark, ViCLIP-OT achieves a 5.75-point gain in average Recall@K over CLIP, and in zero-shot evaluation on Crossmodal-3600 it surpasses CLIP by 11.72 points. Embedding-space analysis confirms improved alignment and a smaller modality gap, validating the effectiveness of the SIGROT mechanism.

## Method Summary
ViCLIP-OT uses a dual-encoder architecture: an image tower based on DINOv3-pretrained ViT-B/16 and a text tower using a Vietnamese Sentence-BERT model, both projecting to a 768-dimensional shared space with L2 normalization. The core innovation is the SIGROT loss, which regularizes standard contrastive losses (CLIP or SigLIP) via a precomputed cross-modality similarity graph. This graph is built offline using embeddings from Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B on the training set. During training, for each batch, unbalanced optimal transport (via Sinkhorn) computes a soft matching plan between the current image and text embeddings, and a KL divergence minimizes the discrepancy between this plan and the normalized similarity graph. The total loss is a weighted sum λ·L_contrastive + L_SIGROT. Training employs gradient accumulation, mixed precision, and careful hyperparameter tuning on the UIT-OpenViIC dataset, with near-duplicate removal using SSCD.

## Key Results
- On UIT-OpenViIC, ViCLIP-OT achieves 67.34% average Recall@K, a 5.75-percentage-point improvement over the CLIP baseline.
- Zero-shot on Crossmodal-3600, it outperforms CLIP by 11.72 percentage points.
- Embedding analysis shows higher alignment scores and lower modality gap compared to baselines, confirming the SIGROT loss's efficacy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Global Structure Injection via Similarity-Graph Regularized OT
**Claim:** Adding a SIGROT loss to CLIP-style training improves cross-modal alignment by aligning not just matched pairs but the global relational structure among all samples in a batch.  
**Mechanism:** For a training batch, precomputed embeddings from a robust model (e.g., Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B) generate intra- and inter-modal similarity matrices, forming a "similarity graph" G_cross. During training, the model's current embeddings produce a cost matrix. Unbalanced OT solves for a transport plan γ* between image and text distributions. The SIGROT loss minimizes the KL divergence between this OT-derived distribution and the normalized similarity graph distribution, forcing the model's batch-level matching to respect the relational structure encoded in G_cross.  
**Core assumption:** The precomputed similarity graph captures meaningful, stable semantic relationships (e.g., similar scenes, synonymous captions) that guide the model's embedding learning.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [section 4.2] Detailed formulation of graph construction and OT-based loss.  
- [section 6.3] Table 3 shows SIGROT variants achieve higher Alignment scores and lower Modality gap than baselines.  
- [section 6.1] Table 1 shows ViCLIP-OT/ViSigLIP-OT outperform pure CLIP/SigLIP by ~5-6% avg R@K.  
- [corpus] Analogous to OT use for distribution alignment in other multimodal works [18, 26].  
**Break condition:** If the precomputed embedding model (e.g., Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B) is poorly aligned to the Vietnamese domain, the graph may introduce misleading relational structure, harming performance. Ablation in Section 7.3 confirms cross-modality graph is critical; single-modality graphs harm performance.

### Mechanism 2: Vietnamese-Specific Encoder Initialization for Low-Resource Adaptation
**Claim:** Using a Vietnamese-pretrained text encoder (SBERT) and a strong vision backbone (DINOv3) provides a better initialization for Vietnamese retrieval than using models pretrained on high-resource language data.  
**Mechanism:** The text tower uses a Sentence-BERT model previously fine-tuned on large-scale Vietnamese corpora, providing robust Vietnamese language understanding from the start. The image tower uses DINOv3, a self-supervised ViT pretrained on diverse images, providing strong visual features. This avoids the need to learn fundamental language or visual representations from scratch on limited Vietnamese data.  
**Core assumption:** A monolingual or strongly Vietnamese-adapted text encoder reduces the "English-centric" bias of standard CLIP and better captures Vietnamese linguistic nuances than a multilingual model fine-tuned on small Vietnamese data.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [section 4.1] Explicitly states text encoder is a "pretrained Vietnamese Sentence-BERT model" and image encoder is "DINOv3-based ViT".  
- [section 6.1] ViCLIP-OT (Vietnamese-adapted) significantly outperforms zero-shot multilingual models like Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B (Table 1, 67.34% vs 55.40% avg R@K), suggesting Vietnamese-specific initialization is key.  
- [corpus] Analogous to challenges in transferring natural CLIP to specialized domains in "Unifying Biomedical Vision-Language" and "S-CLIP".  
**Break condition:** If the Vietnamese SBERT has poor coverage of specific visual domains in UIT-OpenViIC (e.g., niche cultural concepts), its textual representations may be misaligned. Section 7.1 ablation shows some fine-tuning of the image encoder is still necessary, indicating the vision backbone also needs domain adaptation.

### Mechanism 3: Hybrid Loss Balancing for Stable Optimization
**Claim:** The hybrid objective λ·L_CLIP + L_SIGROT (or SigLIP variant) yields better performance than either loss alone by combining strong pairwise discrimination with global structural consistency.  
**Mechanism:** The contrastive loss (CLIP/SigLIP) provides a stable, instance-level learning signal. The SIGROT loss adds a regularizer that operates on the batch-level distribution, encouraging embeddings to respect the relational graph. The hyperparameter λ (typically 0.1-0.2) controls the trade-off.  
**Core assumption:** The two losses are complementary and not strictly conflicting; the contrastive loss prevents the OT solution from collapsing to trivial solutions, while OT prevents the contrastive loss from overfitting to batch-specific negatives.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [section 4.3] Formal definition of hybrid objectives (Eq. 19-20).  
- [section 7.2] Figure 5b and Tables G.7-G.8 show peak performance at λ=0.1 (ViSigLIP-OT) and λ=0.2 (ViCLIP-OT), with λ=0 performing worst.  
- [section 6.3] Table 3 shows both Alignment and Modality gap improve with SIGROT on top of the contrastive baseline.  
- [corpus] "Post-pre-training for Modality Alignment" discusses similar gap mitigation but without graph+OT; ViCLIP-OT's empirical result validates hybrid combination.  
**Break condition:** Poor λ selection: too low (λ→0) leads to unstable training from pure OT regularization; too high (λ>>0.5) reduces SIGROT's effect, reverting to baseline performance (Figure 5b).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE / CLIP loss)**  
  Why needed: Provides the core cross-modal alignment objective by pulling embeddings of matched image-text pairs together and pushing mismatched pairs apart using in-batch negatives. Without this, the model lacks a clear discriminative signal for retrieval.  
  Quick check: What is the effect of batch size on the number of "negative" samples in standard CLIP loss, and why might this be problematic for small datasets?

- **Concept: Optimal Transport (OT) & Sinkhorn Algorithm**  
  Why needed: SIGROT uses entropic-regularized unbalanced OT to compute a soft, many-to-many matching plan between image and text embeddings in a batch, respecting a cost matrix derived from similarity. This provides a distribution-level alignment objective complementary to instance-level contrastive learning.  
  Quick check: How does the KL divergence term in unbalanced OT help handle noisy or incomplete image-text pairs, and what parameters control this relaxation?

- **Concept: Modality Gap**  
  Why needed: A known phenomenon where image and text embeddings occupy distinct regions in the shared space, even for matched pairs. This harms retrieval. ViCLIP-OT aims to reduce it (measured by centroid distance) via SIGROT's global regularization.  
  Quick check: According to Liang et al. [22], what two main factors contribute to the emergence of the modality gap during CLIP training? How might SIGROT's batch-level OT objective counteract these?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Image Tower (DINOv3 ViT-B/16) -> mean pooling -> linear projection -> L2-normalized Z_image  
  Text Tower (Vietnamese SBERT) -> mean pooling -> optional linear projection -> L2-normalized Z_text  
  Precomputed Similarity Graph Module (using Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B) -> G_cross (average of 4 similarity matrices)  
  SIGROT Loss Module: cost C = 1 - Z_image * Z_text^T -> unbalanced OT (Sinkhorn, ε=0.05, τ_m=0.5) -> transport plan γ* -> L_SIGROT = KL(N·γ* || softmax(G_cross))  
  Hybrid Loss Combiner: L_total = λ·L_CLIP/SigLIP + L_SIGROT

- **Critical path:** Precompute G_cross on full training set. For each batch: forward pass encoders -> compute L_contrastive -> fetch precomputed embeddings for current batch indices -> build G_cross -> solve OT -> compute L_SIGROT -> sum losses -> backward.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Graph freshness vs. cost: Precomputed graph is fast but static; online features might be adaptive but add computation.  
  - Hybrid loss weight λ: Low λ (~0.1) leverages SIGROT without overwhelming contrastive signal; high λ (>0.5) degrades SIGROT effect.  
  - OT parameters (ε, τ_m): ε controls smoothness; τ_m controls relaxation. Larger batch improves OT approximation but increases memory.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Poor retrieval if λ ≈ 0: Model relies solely on OT, lacks instance discrimination.  
  - No improvement from SIGROT if G_cross is noisy/low-quality: Check precomputed embedding model's validation performance.  
  - Training instability if temperature τ or bias b poorly initialized: Use defaults (τ=0.07 for CLIP, τ=0.06, b=-9 for SigLIP).  
  - Modality gap persists if G_cross uses only intra-modal graphs: Ablation shows cross-modality graph is essential.

- **First 3 experiments (validation set):**  
  1. Baseline reproduction: Train CLIP and SigLIP-only models (λ=0) on UIT-OpenViIC; confirm base R@K ~61.6%.  
  2. SIGROT integration test: Add SIGROT with λ=0.1; expect immediate R@K jump and modality gap drop; verify L_SIGROT decreases.  
  3. Ablation - Graph strategy: Swap G_cross for arithmetic mean or caption-only; expect performance drop, confirming cross-modality graph necessity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can the similarity graph in SIGROT be learned end-to-end rather than using precomputed embeddings? (Conclusion mentions this; current static approach may limit adaptability.)
- Can the SIGROT objective be effectively extended to other multimodal tasks beyond image-text retrieval, such as visual question answering or multimodal reasoning? (Conclusion suggests it; requires non-trivial architectural modifications.)
- What large-scale pretraining strategies would most effectively enhance ViCLIP-OT's performance for Vietnamese and other low-resource languages? (Unresolved due to small dataset size; scaling strategies unexplored.)
- To what extent does SIGROT generalize to other low-resource languages beyond Vietnamese, and what language-specific factors influence its effectiveness? (Focused on Vietnamese; Crossmodal-3600 zero-shot shows gains but no per-language analysis.)

## Limitations
- Unspecified Vietnamese SBERT variant and its pretraining details.
- SIGROT implementation details (Sinkhorn iterations, tolerance, graph recomputation) omitted.
- No provided train/val/test splits for UIT-OpenViIC, hindering exact reproducibility.
- Dependence on Qwen3-VL-Embedding-2B for graph; its Vietnamese alignment unknown.
- "First foundation VLM" claim not systematically verified.

## Confidence
- **SIGROT improves cross-modal alignment**: High (Table 3 shows increased alignment, decreased modality gap; ablation confirms necessity).
- **Hybrid loss weight λ is optimal**: High (Fig 5b, Tables G.7-G.8 show clear peak at λ=0.1/0.2; λ=0 underperforms).
- **Vietnamese-specific initialization helps**: Medium (Table 1 shows ViCLIP-OT > zero-shot Qwen; but text encoder not isolated; fine-tuning still needed).
- **Global OT mechanism is effective**: Medium (break condition sensitive to graph quality; cross-modality graph ablation critical but causal link not fully dissected).
- **ViCLIP-OT is the first foundation VLM for Vietnamese**: Low (no prior work survey; neighbor corpus includes other retrieval models but not specifically Vietnamese VLMs).
