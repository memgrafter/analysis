# Bound to Disagree: Generalization Bounds via Certifiable Surrogates
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23128
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23128
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces a disagreement-based framework that yields substantially tighter, non-vacuous generalization bounds for deep learning models by transferring certificates from simple surrogates using unlabeled data.

## Executive Summary
Generalization bounds for deep neural networks are often vacuous, non-computable, or limited to specific architectures. This paper proposes a novel disagreement-based framework that certifies a target model's generalization by bounding its true risk relative to a simpler surrogate model with provable guarantees. The key insight is that the risk gap between two predictors can be controlled by their disagreement on an unlabeled dataset. This allows the tight, computable generalization bounds of the surrogate—derived from sample compression, model compression, or PAC-Bayes theory—to be transferred to the complex target without modifying it. The approach yields the first non-vacuous bounds for large language models and dramatically improves bound tightness across vision and NLP tasks, all with high probability confidence using only unlabeled data.

## Method Summary
The method involves three steps: (1) Train a complex target model on a full training set. (2) Obtain or train a simpler surrogate model (e.g., via compression or stochasticity) that enjoys a tight, computable generalization bound. (3) Measure the disagreement between target and surrogate on a separate unlabeled dataset. The target's true risk is upper-bounded by the surrogate's bounded risk plus a disagreement term, with high probability. For zero-one loss, disagreement is the classification disagreement rate; for Lipschitz losses like cross-entropy, it is a soft disagreement based on output probability differences. The framework requires no modifications to the target's training and only uses unlabeled data for the final certificate.

## Key Results
- On MNIST, zero-one loss bounds range from 1.78% to 4.83% vs. 86.55% (partition-based) and ~3×10⁸% (norm-based).
- On CIFAR10, zero-one bounds are 17.36%–35.06% vs. 90.58% and ~3×10²¹%; smoothed cross-entropy bounds drop from ~8.34 to as low as 0.6858.
- Provides the first certifiable non-vacuous bounds for large language models (DistilBERT, GPT-2) on NLP tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Disagreement-Bounded Risk Transfer via Zero-One Loss
- **Claim:** The true zero-one risk of a target predictor *f* is bounded by the true risk of a surrogate *h* plus a term based on their disagreement on an unlabeled set, with high probability.
- **Mechanism:** Uses the binomial test-set bound on Yang et al.'s result that the absolute risk difference is upper-bounded by the expected disagreement rate. Observed disagreement *k* on set *U* is converted via `Bin(k, m, δ)` to a confidence interval for the true disagreement rate.
- **Core assumption:** The surrogate *h* has a tight, computable generalization bound (from sample compression, model compression, or PAC-Bayes) independent of *f*'s complexity.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract], section 3.1 (Theorem 4, Eq. 7, Theorem S8), corpus context on PAC-Bayes certificates.
- **Break condition:** Bound vacuous if surrogate's bound is loose or measured disagreement is high.

### Mechanism 2: Disagreement-Bounded Risk Transfer via Lipschitz Losses
- **Claim:** For a Lipschitz loss ℓ, *f*'s risk is bounded by the expected risk of a surrogate distribution *Q* plus a term involving average ℓ₁-disagreement of softmax outputs, scaled by the Lipschitz constant.
- **Mechanism:** Applies the Chernoff bound for unit-interval variables to `(1/2) E_{h~Q} ||σ(f(x)) - σ(h(x))||₁`. The empirical average is inverted via `KL⁻¹(q, ϵ)` to bound its expectation.
- **Core assumption:** Loss ℓ is Lipschitz; surrogate class/distribution admits a tight PAC-Bayes or similar bound; softmax outputs are stabilized (e.g., clamped).
- **Evidence anchors:** section 3.2 (Theorem 5), section C.1 (Corollaries S17, S18), corpus on PAC-Bayes learning.
- **Break condition:** Bound degrades with large Lipschitz constant or high softmax divergence.

### Mechanism 3: Disagreement-Bounded Risk Gap via Labeled Loss Differences
- **Claim:** For any bounded loss ℓ, the true risk gap between *f* and *h* can be bounded using the average absolute loss difference on a held-out labeled set *L*, with high probability.
- **Mechanism:** Uses the Chernoff bound directly on normalized absolute loss differences `|ℓ(f(x_i), y_i) - ℓ(h(x_i), y_i)| / λ_ℓ`. The empirical mean is inverted to bound the true expectation.
- **Core assumption:** A small labeled validation set *L* is available; *h*'s true risk is bounded separately.
- **Evidence anchors:** section 3.3 (Theorem 6), section C.1 (Corollary S16), corpus contrast with labeled-data bounds.
- **Break condition:** Requires expensive labeled data; bound loose if empirical loss differences are unrepresentative.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sample Compression Theory:** Provides tight bounds for predictors that depend only on a small subset (compression set) of training data. Used here to certify surrogates without assumptions on the target. Quick check: If a model's output can be reconstructed from a small training subset, what does sample compression theory say about its generalization gap?
- **PAC-Bayes Theory:** Bounds expected risk of a stochastic predictor by empirical risk plus a KL divergence term from a prior. Used for tight surrogate bounds and distributional surrogates. Quick check: How does the KL divergence between a posterior *Q* and prior *P* reflect the complexity of a stochastic neural network in a PAC-Bayes bound?
- **Model Compression (Kolmogorov Complexity):** Bounds true risk by code length needed to describe the model (e.g., via pruning, quantization). Used to create compact surrogates for large models. Quick check: Why does a shorter code length for a neural network suggest better generalization according to model compression theory?

## Architecture Onboarding
Target model (*f*) -> Surrogate model (*h* or *Q*) -> Unlabeled disagreement set (*U*)

**Critical path:**
1. Train target *f* on full training set *S* (standard, unmodified).
2. Train/obtain surrogate *h* or *Q* using a certified framework (sample compression, model compression, PAC-Bayes).
3. Compute disagreement metric on unlabeled set *U* (or labeled *L* for Mechanism 3).
4. Combine surrogate's risk bound (e.g., Binomial or KL-inverted) and disagreement term.
5. Report final bound with confidence δ (typically 0.99).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Surrogate type: Sample compression yields tightest test-set-style bounds but offers little disagreement control; model compression/PAC-Bayes allow tunable surrogates but may have looser intrinsic bounds.
- Data: Mechanisms 1–2 use cheap unlabeled *U*; Mechanism 3 requires costly labeled *L*.
- Cost: PAC-Bayes needs MC sampling; model compression requires quantization/coding; sample compression needs subset selection.

**Failure signatures:**
- Surrogate's empirical risk >> target's → disagreement dominates.
- Surrogate's bound is vacuous (large KL/code length/compression set).
- Measured disagreement >10-20% → bound loose/trivial.
- Cross-entropy without clamping → Lipschitz constant huge → bound explodes.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **MNIST zero-one with random coreset:** Train target CNN, select random coreset from 20% unlabeled *U*, train surrogate CNN on coreset. Compute *d₀⁻¹_U*. Combine with test-set bound → ~1.78% bound.
2. **CIFAR10 cross-entropy with SubLoRA:** Train target ResNet18. Pretrain surrogate on 50% *S*, add SubLoRA adapter, train on rest, quantize. Compute disagreement on *U* with smoothed cross-entropy → ~1.7851% bound.
3. **NLP (DistilBERT) zero-one via distillation:** Train target DistilBERT on Amazon polarity. Generate pseudo-labels for *U* (85% of remaining). Train SubLoRA surrogate on *S ∪ U* with pseudo-labels. Compute disagreement on *U* → ~21.35% bound.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can appropriate surrogate models be identified and trained for highly complex target models to ensure tight generalization bounds? The paper acknowledges finding a surrogate for a very complex model is challenging.
- **Question 2:** Why does random coreset selection produce the tightest bounds despite not explicitly minimizing disagreement? This counterintuitive result suggests current disagreement minimization may be suboptimal.
- **Question 3:** To what extent can the framework be generalized to non-classification tasks and unbounded loss functions? Current application is limited to classification with bounded losses.

## Limitations
- Incomplete reproducibility: exact architectures, hyperparameters, and disagreement computation details are not fully specified.
- Bound tightness hinges on surrogate quality; a too-complex surrogate yields vacuous certificates.
- Requires additional unlabeled (or labeled) data beyond standard training splits.
- Residual looseness: even best bounds (e.g., 17–35% on CIFAR10) far exceed typical test errors.

## Confidence
- Theoretical soundness: High.
- Empirical improvements over prior bounds: Medium.
- Generality across tasks and losses: Medium.
- Practical non-vacuity: Medium.
