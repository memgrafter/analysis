# FP8-RL: A Practical and Stable Low-Precision Stack for LLM Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID:** 2601.18150
* **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18150
* **Reference Count:** 25
* **Primary Result:** An FP8 quantization stack for LLM reinforcement learning rollout achieves up to 44% higher throughput while maintaining learning trajectories comparable to BF16 baselines, using blockwise W8A8 quantization, FP8 KV-cache with per-step recalibration, and importance-sampling correction.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models (LLMs) is computationally bottlenecked by rollout generation, especially with long contexts where attention and KV-cache memory dominate. Applying FP8 precision is challenging due to dynamic policy weight changes (requiring frequent quantization) and train-inference mismatch (causing instability). This paper introduces a practical FP8 rollout stack that combines blockwise W8A8 quantization for linear layers, an FP8 KV-cache with per-step QKV scale recalibration, and importance-sampling-based rollout correction (TIS/MIS). Implemented in the veRL framework and compatible with common inference backends (vLLM, SGLang), the system supports both dense (Qwen3-8B) and MoE (Qwen3-30B-A3B) models. Across evaluations, FP8 rollout delivers significant throughput gains (up to 44%) while preserving training stability and accuracy through targeted technical mitigations.

## Method Summary
The proposed FP8-RL system accelerates the RL rollout phase by quantizing the inference engine while maintaining training stability. The core method involves three integrated components: (1) **Blockwise W8A8 Quantization**: Linear layer weights are quantized per 128×128 block to FP8 (E4M3), with activations dynamically quantized per tensor. Updated BF16 weights from the training backend are quantized and synchronized to the inference engine each RL step. (2) **FP8 KV-Cache with Recalibration**: The KV-cache is stored in FP8 to halve memory usage. Because policy weights change every step, QKV projection scales are recalculated either on the inference side (vLLM/SGLang) or trainer side (NeMo-RL) to maintain attention accuracy. (3) **Importance-Sampling Correction**: To correct for distribution shift between the quantized rollout policy (π_FP8θ) and the training policy (πθ), token-level Truncated Importance Sampling (TIS) reweights each token's contribution by the likelihood ratio, clipping at C=2 to control variance. This correction is applied during PPO updates. The system is implemented in veRL, enabling end-to-end RL training with dynamic weight loading and compatibility across major inference engines.

## Key Results
* FP8 rollout (with TIS) matches BF16 baseline training accuracy on AIME24 while achieving 10–20% speedup for 8B models and up to 44% for 30B MoE models under long-context settings (20K tokens).
* Ablation studies show that FP8 without importance-sampling correction causes severe accuracy degradation, demonstrating TIS's critical role in stabilizing training under train-inference mismatch.
* The FP8 KV-cache alone yields ~38% speedup; combined with blockwise quantization and TIS, the full stack reaches the maximum throughput gain without compromising learning trajectories.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Blockwise W8A8 Quantization for Linear Layers
* **Claim:** Blockwise FP8 W8A8 quantization of linear layers reduces compute and memory during rollout while preserving accuracy, enabling higher throughput.
* **Mechanism:** The rollout engine uses quantized weights (E4M3 FP8) and dynamically quantized activations. Weights are quantized per-block (128×128) with per-block scaling factors, balancing error and efficiency. The training backend syncs updated weights each RL step; these are quantized and loaded into the inference engine (vLLM/SGLang). Using hardware FP8 tensor cores (DeepGEMM) accelerates matrix multiplications and reduces memory traffic.
* **Core Assumption:** Blockwise quantization provides sufficient accuracy for rollout generation; the per-block scaling can adapt to weight distributions better than per-tensor.
* **Evidence Anchors:**
  * [abstract] “blockwise W8A8 quantization for linear layers”
  * [section 2.1.1] “We employ blockwise quantization with block size 𝐵 = 128 × 128”
  * [section 2.2.2] Experimental results show training accuracy comparable to BF16 baseline and 10–20% speedup.
* **Break Condition:** *Assumption:* If block size is too large, quantization error increases; if too small, overhead dominates. Also, if activations contain extreme outliers not captured by dynamic quantization, accuracy may drop.

### Mechanism 2: FP8 KV-Cache with Per-Step QKV Scale Recalibration
* **Claim:** FP8 quantization of the KV-cache doubles effective cache capacity, reduces memory pressure, and increases throughput in long-context rollouts, while per-step QKV scale recalibration maintains accuracy despite dynamic weight updates.
* **Mechanism:** The KV-cache stores keys/values in FP8 (1 byte vs 2 bytes for BF16), effectively doubling capacity. Because RL updates policy weights every step, the QKV projection scales must be recalculated to match new weights. Two approaches: Inference-side (vLLM recalibrates on first forward pass after weight load) or Trainer-side (training backend computes scales and syncs). Both ensure attention computations remain accurate with the current policy.
* **Core Assumption:** The KV-cache is the primary memory bottleneck in long-context generation; doubling capacity significantly increases concurrency and reduces preemptions.
* **Evidence Anchors:**
  * [abstract] “FP8 KV-cache with per-step QKV scale recalibration”
  * [section 2.3.1] Describes both calibration strategies.
  * [section 2.3.2] KV-cache FP8 only yields 38% speedup; Full FP8 yields 44% speedup for the 8B model, with training accuracy aligned.
  * [figure 8] Shows performance gains from KV-cache quantization.
* **Break Condition:** *Assumption:* If the model’s attention logits require higher precision (e.g., due to large magnitude variations), FP8 may cause noticeable accuracy loss. Recalibration overhead (2–3%) may become significant if rollout steps are very short.

### Mechanism 3: Importance-Sampling-Based Rollout Correction (TIS/MIS)
* **Claim:** Token-level truncated importance sampling (TIS) mitigates train-inference mismatch caused by FP8 rollout, stabilizing training and preserving accuracy.
* **Mechanism:** The training policy πθ (BF16) and rollout policy π_FP8θ (quantized) differ. Importance sampling reweights each token’s contribution by w(a|s)=πθ(a|s)/π_FP8θ(a|s). TIS clips weights to a threshold C=2 to control variance. This correction is applied during PPO updates, effectively accounting for the off-policy distribution shift.
* **Core Assumption:** The likelihood ratio can be accurately estimated from token-level probabilities; clipping at C=2 is sufficient to balance bias and variance.
* **Evidence Anchors:**
  * [abstract] “importance-sampling-based rollout correction (TIS/MIS)” and “TIS effectively mitigating mismatch.”
  * [section 2.1.3] Details TIS formula and clipping.
  * [section 2.2.2] Ablation: FP8 without TIS shows accuracy degradation; with TIS matches BF16.
  * [section 2.3.2] Full FP8 with TIS maintains stability despite high mismatch KL.
* **Break Condition:** *Assumption:* If the mismatch KL exceeds the clipping threshold substantially, TIS may under-correct, leading to bias. If variance remains high, more advanced methods (MIS, RRR) may be needed.

## Foundational Learning
* **Concept: Quantization for Deep Learning**
  * *Why Needed:* FP8-RL relies on low-precision formats to accelerate computation and reduce memory. Understanding quantization trade-offs (dynamic range vs. precision) and schemes (per-tensor vs. per-block) is crucial.
  * *Quick Check Question:* “In FP8 E4M3 format, what do the 4 and 3 bits represent, and why might it be preferred over E5M2 for forward passes in LLMs?”
* **Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and PPO**
  * *Why Needed:* The paper targets RL post-training. The rollout phase generates data, and PPO updates the policy. The mismatch arises because the rollout policy differs from the training policy; importance sampling corrects this.
  * *Quick Check Question:* “In PPO, why is the importance sampling ratio used, and what problem does it address when rollouts come from a different policy?”
* **Concept: Transformer Inference and KV-Cache**
  * *Why Needed:* Rollout generation uses autoregressive decoding with a KV-cache; its memory usage scales with context length, making it a key bottleneck. Quantizing the cache directly addresses this.
  * *Quick Check Question:* “During autoregressive decoding, what information is stored in the KV-cache, and how does quantizing it to FP8 affect both memory usage and attention accuracy?”

## Architecture Onboarding
* **Component Map:**
  Training Backend (FSDP/Megatron-LM) -> Quantization Module -> Inference Engine (vLLM/SGLang) -> Rollout Coordinator (veRL) -> Correction Module -> Training Backend
* **Critical Path (per RL step):**
  1. Training backend updates policy weights (BF16).
  2. Quantization: Blockwise quantize weights to FP8; optionally compute QKV scales (trainer-side).
  3. Load quantized weights/scales into inference engine.
  4. Inference engine performs rollout generation using FP8 weights and KV-cache.
  5. Rollout outputs (tokens, logprobs) sent back to trainer.
  6. Trainer recomputes logprobs under current BF16 policy (or uses FP8 if end-to-end).
  7. Compute importance weights (TIS) and run PPO update.
  8. Loop.
* **Design Tradeoffs:**
  * Block size: Larger blocks reduce overhead but may increase error; 128×128 is empirically chosen.
  * Calibration location: Inference-side avoids data movement but needs engine support; trainer-side adds ~2–3% overhead but offers control.
  * Quantization aggressiveness: More components (KV, attention) yield higher speedups but require stronger correction (TIS/MIS).
  * Precision alignment: End-to-end FP8 reduces mismatch but needs careful scaling and FP8 training support.
  * Correction strength: TIS clipping threshold C=2 balances bias/variance; larger C may increase variance.
* **Failure Signatures:**
  * Accuracy collapse: Check if TIS is enabled, if lm_head is quantized, or if mismatch KL spikes.
  * No speedup: Ensure KV-cache FP8 is active, DeepGEMM is used, and monitor preemption rates.
  * High mismatch KL: May indicate stale QKV scales or kernel differences; consider MIS or RRR.
  * Out-of-memory: Despite quantization, reduce batch size or concurrency.
  * Slow weight sync: Profile blockwise quantization and loading; consider async pipelines.
* **First 3 Experiments:**
  1. **Baseline:** Run BF16-only RL (no quantization). Record accuracy, reward, time-per-token.
  2. **FP8 W8A8 + TIS:** Enable blockwise W8A8 and token-level TIS (C=2). Verify accuracy within margin of baseline and observe ~10–20% speedup on 8B model.
  3. **FP8 KV-Cache:** Add KV-cache FP8 (inference-side or trainer-side). Expect further throughput gains (~38% total for 8B) and check stability via validation accuracy and mismatch KL.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
* **Question 1:** Can NVFP4 format be stably applied to LLM RL without causing training collapse or severe performance degradation?
  * *Basis in paper:* Section 4 mentions future work exploring more aggressive formats like NVFP4, noting reported instability from accumulated quantization error.
  * *Why unresolved:* Its interaction with RL-specific train-inference mismatch and long-context generation is unexplored.
* **Question 2:** Under what precise conditions does token-level TIS fail for MoE models, requiring more advanced corrections like MIS or RRR?
  * *Basis in paper:* Section 2.2.3 notes MoE mismatch KL increases over time and suggests MIS/RRR for more severe instability, but TIS succeeded in their setup.
  * *Why unresolved:* Thresholds or architectural factors (expert count, router sensitivity) that overwhelm TIS's variance clipping are not defined.
* **Question 3:** What are the systematic trade-offs between Inference-Side and Trainer-Side QKV scale calibration for FP8 KV-cache in RL?
  * *Basis in paper:* Sections 2.3 and Appendix B describe both implementations but provide no direct quantitative comparison.
  * *Why unresolved:* The two approaches are evaluated in separate frameworks (veRL vs NeMo-RL), so relative strengths remain unclear.
* **Question 4:** How do FP8 rollout speedups scale with model architecture, context length, and hardware generation beyond the evaluated H100 setups?
  * *Basis in paper:* Results report 10-20% speedup for 8B dense and 30-50% for 30B MoE, with gains larger at longer contexts, but lack a scaling study.
  * *Why unresolved:* The dependence on arithmetic intensity, memory bandwidth, and KV-cache pressure is discussed qualitatively.

## Limitations
* DeepGEMM kernel availability across vLLM/SGLang versions is unknown; speedup may not reproduce without it.
* Trainer-side QKV recalibration overhead (2–3%) and data selection are not detailed; could vary in practice.
* TIS clipping (C=2) only tested up to moderate KL; under larger policy shifts it may under-correct.

## Confidence
* Blockwise W8A8: High (ablations show accuracy/speed).
* KV-cache FP8 + recalibration: Medium (speedup clear but calibration sensitivity unexamined).
* TIS: High (ablation proves necessity).
* Overall 44% speedup: Medium (conditional on DeepGEMM and H100).
