# RbtAct: Rebuttal as Supervision for Actionable Review Feedback Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09723
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09723
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RbtAct generates more actionable review feedback than baselines, achieving 3.46/5 human-rated actionability and 3.38/5 LLM-as-judge actionability.

## Executive Summary
AI-generated peer reviews often lack concrete, actionable guidance. RbtAct addresses this by training a model to produce focused critique segments conditioned on a target perspective (e.g., Experiments, Writing). It leverages author rebuttals as an implicit supervision signal: review segments that elicit concrete revisions or specific plans in the rebuttal are treated as more actionable. The authors construct the RMR-75K dataset from ICLR 2024 OpenReview data, mapping review spans to rebuttal spans and labeling them with perspective and impact categories. A Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct model is first fine-tuned on segment generation (SFT) and then optimized via Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) using pairs ordered by rebuttal impact. Evaluations on held-out data show RbtAct surpasses strong baselines in actionability and specificity while maintaining groundedness and relevance.

## Method Summary
RbtAct’s pipeline consists of two main stages. First, the RMR-75K dataset is created: review texts are segmented into atomic weakness/question spans, which are automatically aligned to corresponding rebuttal spans using a heuristic anchor matcher followed by an LLM-based matcher with confidence scoring. Each pair is labeled with one of seven perspectives and one of five impact categories (e.g., Concrete Revision Performed, Defend Without Change). Low-confidence or low-quality pairs are filtered out, yielding 13,300 SFT examples and 22,000 DPO preference pairs.
Second, a Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct model is trained. The SFT stage fine-tunes the model with LoRA (rank 8) to generate a single critique given a paper and a target perspective. The DPO stage then uses the impact ordering (CRP > SRP > VCR > DWC > DRF) to construct preference pairs within the same paper and perspective. The model is optimized to increase the likelihood of higher-impact segments relative to lower-impact ones, with a small mixing of the SFT loss (λ=0.1) to prevent drift. The final model operates under a 32k token context to accommodate full papers.

## Key Results
- **Higher actionability:** RbtAct scores 3.46/5 (human) and 3.38/5 (LLM-as-judge) on actionability, significantly outperforming baselines.
- **Improved specificity:** The model produces more specific feedback without sacrificing groundedness or relevance.
- **Dataset contribution:** The RMR-75K dataset provides 75K review-rebuttal segment pairs with fine-grained labels, enabling future research.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RbtAct’s gains stem from three interconnected mechanisms:

1. **Rebuttal as an implicit reward signal:** Author rebuttals reveal which review comments led to concrete revisions (CRP, SRP) versus those merely defended (DWC, DRF). By mapping review segments to rebuttal responses and ordering them by impact, the system creates a large-scale preference signal for actionability without costly human annotation. Human validation shows the automatic alignment achieves token-level F1=0.91, supporting the signal’s reliability. *Break condition:* If authors make vague or disingenuous rebuttal promises, or if the mapping is noisy, the preference signal degrades.

2. **Perspective-conditioned segment generation:** Instead of generating full reviews, the model is constrained to produce one focused critique for a specified perspective (e.g., Experiments). This decomposition isolates actionable points and aligns each generated segment directly with a rebuttal-aligned training example, preventing the model from hiding generic advice among multiple points. *Break condition:* If rebuttal responses address multiple review points or perspective labels are inconsistent, the segment-level supervision becomes misaligned.

3. **Preference learning directly for actionability:** DPO optimizes the model’s policy to favor review segments that historically provoked stronger author commitments (higher rebuttal impact). The impact ordering (CRP > SRP > VCR > DWC > DRF) serves as a ranking criterion, bypassing the need for a separate reward model. This directly encodes the target attribute rather than generic quality. *Break condition:* If the impact ordering does not correlate with human-judged actionability (e.g., SRP not perceived as more actionable than VCR), the DPO signal misaligns.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Review-Rebuttal Alignment as a Noisy Supervision Signal**  
- *Why needed here:* The entire method hinges on automatically created, point-to-point mappings between review weaknesses and rebuttal responses. The quality of this alignment directly limits downstream performance.  
- *Quick check:* Given a review's enumerated weaknesses and a concatenated rebuttal text, can you design a heuristic (e.g., anchor phrase matching) and an LLM-based matcher that reliably pair them? What are the primary failure modes for each method (e.g., ambiguity, ellipsis)?

**Concept: Perspective Taxonomy for Atomic Critique Generation**  
- *Why needed here:* To generate actionable feedback, the model must be constrained to a single, specific critique viewpoint. This requires a robust classifier or labeling scheme to disambiguate categories like *Experiments* (baselines/ablations) and *Evaluation* (metrics/analysis).  
- *Quick check:* How would you label the point: "The paper compares against Method X but not the stronger baseline Y, and the reported accuracy lacks confidence intervals."? Which perspective is primary, and why might this be ambiguous?

**Concept: Preference Learning for Actionability (Not Just Quality)**  
- *Why needed here:* Standard supervised fine-tuning teaches the model to imitate good reviews. DPO teaches it to prefer a specific *flavor* of good: one that provokes revision. The engineer must distinguish between generic quality (fluency, relevance) and targeted utility (actionability).  
- *Quick check:* If you had two positive review segments—one vague ("The experimental section is weak") and one specific ("Please add an ablation study in Section 5 comparing against [Specific Baseline] using the same hyperparameters as Table 2")—which would win in a DPO pair built from rebuttal impact? ## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data Pipeline (yields REVIEWSEG-SFT-13K and REVIEWPREF-DPO-22K) → SFT Module (fine-tunes base Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with LoRA) → DPO Module (optimizes with preference pairs and a frozen reference) → Inference.

**Critical path:**  
Build RMR-75K → Train RbtAct-SFT → Create REVIEWPREF-DPO-22K preference pairs → Train RbtAct-DPO → Evaluate on held-out test set (human & LLM-as-judge).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **LoRA rank:** SFT uses rank 8, DPO uses rank 16, indicating preference learning may require more capacity. Lower rank saves memory but might limit nuanced updates.  
- **One-to-one alignment constraint:** Enforces clean review-rebuttal mapping but may drop valid review points addressed polyphonously. Tradeoff: precision vs. recall.  
- **Impact granularity:** Five categories provide an ordering but risk label noise; binary actionable/not would be simpler but lose gradient signal.  
- **Context length:** 32k tokens accommodates full papers but demands gradient checkpointing, quantization, and increases cost.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **High DPO training loss with low actionability:** Likely poor-quality preference pairs (misaligned mappings or incorrect impact labels). Check confidence scores and human validation samples.  
- **Model ignores perspective constraint:** Degrades to generic weaknesses. Diagnose SFT model’s perspective adherence first; if poor, increase SFT data quality or training steps. During DPO, ensure λ is sufficient to regularize.  
- **Specificity drop while actionability rises:** Model learns to suggest revisions without specifics. Could indicate impact categories are too coarse (e.g., SRP vs. VCR not sensitive to specificity) or that training data lacks concrete examples.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Span alignment audit:** Randomly sample 500 papers, have humans annotate review-rebuttal segment correspondences, compute token-level F1 against the automatic mapper. Target F1 ≥ 0.85 to confirm mapping reliability.  
2. **SFT sanity check:** Train RbtAct-SFT on REVIEWSEG-SFT-13K. Evaluate its perspective adherence and fluency on a held-out set. It should outperform zero-shot prompting on generating on-perspective critiques.  
3. **Impact order ablation:** Train a DPO model with reversed impact ranking (DWC > DRF). The main RbtAct model should achieve significantly higher actionability, validating that the rebuttal-derived ordering drives improvements.

## Limitations
- **Alignment noise:** The review-rebuttal mapping relies on heuristic and LLM matching; incorrect pairings corrupt the DPO supervision signal.  
- **Impact ordering validity:** The assumed ranking CRP > SRP > VCR > DWC > DRF may not perfectly correlate with human-perceived actionability, requiring validation.  
- **Perspective label ambiguity:** Overlap between categories (e.g., *Experiments* vs *Evaluation*) can introduce noise in the SFT and DPO pairs.

## Confidence
- **Rebuttal-based preference signal is reliable:** **Medium** (depends on mapping quality and impact ordering).  
- **Perspective decomposition improves focus:** **High** (well-defined and empirically supported).  
- **DPO directly increases actionability:** **Medium** (contingent on correct preference construction).  
- **Overall reproducibility given omitted hyperparameters:** **Medium** (key settings can be inferred or standard choices suffice).

1. **Span alignment audit:** Annotate 500 review-rebuttal pairs; compute token-level F1. Target >0.85.  
2. **Impact order ablation:** Train DPO with reversed impact ranking; expect a significant drop in actionability compared to the main model.  
3. **Perspective drift probe:** During DPO training, periodically evaluate perspective accuracy on a validation set. If accuracy falls below 90%, increase the SFT loss mixing weight λ or reduce DPO epochs.
