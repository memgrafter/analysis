# Uniform Spectral Growth and Convergence of Muon in LoRA-Style Matrix Factorization
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06385
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06385
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Muon optimizer with LoRA induces uniform spectral growth of the product AB; spectral gradient flow (SpecGF) reveals equal‑rate dynamics (smallest‑first learning) and almost sure convergence to the best rank‑r approximation, with ℓ₂ regularization guaranteeing global convergence.

## Executive Summary
The paper studies the Muon optimizer (spectral gradient descent) in LoRA fine‑tuning, where the adapters A and B are updated via orthogonalized gradients. They observe that, despite separate orthogonalization, the singular values of the composite AB grow uniformly during training. To explain this, the authors analyze spectral gradient flow (SpecGF), a continuous‑time analogue, in a simplified matrix factorization setting. Under small initialization and a smoothed orthogonalization map Tβ, they prove “equal‑rate dynamics”: all active singular values of AB increase at nearly identical speeds, causing the smallest ones to converge first. Moreover, they show that SpecGF almost always converges to the global minimum (the best rank‑r approximation of the target), and adding ℓ₂ regularization guarantees convergence for any initialization. Experiments on synthetic factorization and LLM fine‑tuning corroborate the uniform growth and faster convergence relative to standard gradient flow.

These findings provide a theoretical foundation for the efficacy of Muon in LoRA and suggest design principles such as careful initialization and potential regularization.

## Method Summary
The study focuses on minimizing the Frobenius loss between a low‑rank product AB and a target matrix Y, with A ∈ ℝ^{m×r}, B ∈ ℝ^{r×n}. The Muon optimizer replaces the raw gradient g with Tβ(g), an orthogonalization that normalizes singular values (exactly: T(g)=UVᵀ from SVD). In the continuous limit (SpecGF), the dynamics are analysed after transforming to “core” variables X and Z that isolate the dominant subspace. Under assumptions: A(0)=0, B(0) is Gaussian with small scale γ, and β is tuned appropriately (β = O(ε³) for target accuracy ε), the core product G = XZ remains nearly diagonal (δ‑alignment). Consequently, the diagonal entries d_i (which approximate the singular values σ_i(AB)) obey d√i/dt ≈ 1, i.e., all √σ_i grow linearly in time until saturation. The proof leverages stability of the diagonal manifold, Lipschitz bounds, and the strict saddle geometry of the loss. ℓ₂ regularization directly bounds the factor norms, eliminating the possibility of divergence. Empirical evaluation uses synthetic matrices with known spectral structure and LoRA fine‑tuning of RoBERTa/LLaMA models, showing that SpecGD (discrete Muon) reproduces the predicted uniform spectral growth and converges faster than gradient flow.

## Key Results
- **Uniform spectral growth:** Singular values of AB increase in a synchronized manner under Muon/SpecGF, in contrast to gradient flow which emphasizes large singular values first.
- **Equal‑rate dynamics:** For small initialization and smoothed orthogonalization, all active singular values rise at nearly identical speeds (Theorem 5.7), leading to smallest‑first learning.
- **Global convergence:** SpecGF almost surely converges to the best rank‑r approximation from almost all initializations, provided factor norms stay bounded; ℓ₂ regularization ensures convergence unconditionally (Theorem 6.2).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Equal‑Rate Dynamics via Approximate Decoupling
**Claim:** Under SpecGF with small initialization, all active singular values of AB grow at nearly identical rates, causing smallest‑first learning.  
**Mechanism:** Orthogonalized updates on A and B induce an approximate decoupling of the product’s dynamics. After a change of variables (X = Uᵣᵀ A, Z = B Vᵣ), the core product G = XZ becomes nearly diagonal (δ‑alignment). In √dᵢ coordinates (dᵢ are diagonal entries of G), the dynamics satisfy d√ᵢ/dt = 1 + O(ε¹⁴). This synchrony stems from Tβ normalizing gradients, making growth rates independent of current scale.  
**Core assumptions:** Initialization scale γ = O(ε¹ᐟ²), smoothing β = O(ε³), δ‑alignment holds throughout (Proposition 5.4), and the smallest diagonal entry stays bounded away from zero (Lemma 5.5).  
**Evidence:** [abstract]; [Section 5.2.2] Lemma 5.6 (square‑root dynamics); [Section 5.2.3] Theorem 5.7 (uniform growth); [Section 3] Figure 2 (experimental uniform SV growth).  
**Break condition:** If γ is too large, δ‑alignment fails; if β is not tuned, the square‑root approximation loses accuracy.

### Mechanism 2: Alignment Preservation via Small Initialization
**Claim:** Starting from A(0)=0 and small Gaussian B(0), the trajectory stays close to the diagonal manifold (XZ nearly diagonal), so σᵢ(AB) are well approximated by the diagonal entries dᵢ.  
**Mechanism:** The SpecGF vector field is Lipschitz near the invariant manifold where G is diagonal. This bounds the off‑diagonal and out‑of‑subspace components: initially O(γ), they remain O(γ) as long as the solution stays in a compact set.  
**Core assumptions:** Gaussian initialization, existence of a finite termination time T (or bounded iterates up to T), and a Lipschitz constant on a containing compact set.  
**Evidence:** [Section 5.2.1] Proposition B.3: sup_{t≤T} ‖Off(G(t))‖_F, ‖X(t)Z⊥(t)‖_F = O(γ); [Section 5.1] Lemma 5.2 (dᵢ approximates σᵢ within O(δ)); [Section 7] Figure 4 (diagonal entries track σᵢ).  
**Break condition:** Non‑Gaussian or large B may produce persistent off‑diagonals; leaving the compact set breaks Lipschitz bounds.

### Mechanism 3: Convergence to Global Minima via Strict Saddle Geometry
**Claim:** SpecGF with μ=0 almost surely converges to the best rank‑r approximation of Y, provided factor norms stay bounded; ℓ₂ regularization guarantees global convergence.  
**Mechanism:** The loss L(A,B) is analytic. Its critical points are either global minima (best rank‑r approx) or strict saddles (Proposition D.11). Monotonic decrease of L and the Łojasiewicz gradient inequality imply that SpecGF either converges or diverges. Almost all initial points avoid saddles (Theorem D.13), so bounded iterations converge to a global minimum. Regularization directly bounds norms, preventing divergence.  
**Core assumptions:** Analyticity of Tβ, boundedness of (A,B) (or ℓ₂ reg), and distinct target singular values (for exponential rate ½).  
**Evidence:** [abstract]; [Section 6.2] Theorem 6.2 (almost sure convergence/divergence and regularized global convergence); [Section D.3] Proposition D.12, Theorem D.13 (saddle avoidance a.s.); [Section 7] Figure 3 (loss convergence).  
**Break condition:** Unbounded norms may cause divergence; unregularized boundedness remains conjectural.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Pretrained weight W⁰ (frozen) → LoRA adapters A (m×r) and B (r×n) → product AB (added to W⁰) → loss ℓ → Muon optimizer (compute grads, apply Tβ) → updates to A and B.

**Critical path:**  
1. Initialize A = 0, B ∼ N(0, γ²I) with small γ.  
2. Forward: compute Ŷ = AB (plus W⁰), evaluate loss ℓ.  
3. Backward: obtain ∇_A ℓ, ∇_B ℓ.  
4. Orthogonalize: apply Tβ to gradients (or momentum‑buffered gradients).  
5. Update: A ← A − η·Tβ(∇_A ℓ), B ← B − η·Tβ(∇_B ℓ).  
6. Repeat until convergence criterion met.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- T (exact SVD) vs Tβ (smoothed): Tβ yields analyticity and avoids finite‑time degeneracies; T can be approximated by Newton‑Schulz iterations for efficiency.  
- β scaling: must be O(ε³) relative to target accuracy ε to preserve square‑root dynamics.  
- Initialization scale γ: too small slows progress; too large breaks δ‑alignment (need γ ≤ O(ε^{1/2})).  
- LoRA rank r: if r > rank(Y), uniform growth still occurs but may learn unnecessary components; ℓ₂ regularization can suppress them.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Loss plateaus above zero → possible convergence to a saddle or suboptimal local minimum; check rank(AB).  
- Singular values of AB deviate from parallel √ growth → alignment violation, likely due to large γ or β.  
- Norms ∥A∥_F + ∥B∥_F → ∞ → unbounded SpecGF; indicate need for ℓ₂ reg or smaller learning rate.  
- Effective rank (e.g., number of singular values above a threshold) does not stabilize near r → failure to capture intended low‑rank structure.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Synthetic uniform growth:** Generate Y with distinct singular values (e.g., [8,5,3,1.5,0.7]). Set γ=10⁻³, β=10⁻⁸. Run SpecGD (μ=0) for 2500 steps, record σᵢ(AB) every 10 steps. Expect √σᵢ curves to be nearly parallel (Theorem 5.7). Compare with standard gradient descent (GD) which shows largest‑first growth.  
2. **Alignment test:** Track δ(t) = ‖Off(G(t))‖_F + ‖X(t)Z⊥(t)‖_F. Sweep γ ∈ {10⁻⁴, 10⁻³, 10⁻²}. For small γ, δ should stay ≤ Cγ; for larger γ, δ grows and uniform growth breaks.  
3. **Regularization effect:** Add ℓ₂ term λ(‖A‖_F²+‖B‖_F²) with λ ∈ {0, 10⁻⁴}. Compare convergence: unregularized may diverge for some γ; regularized should always converge to global minimum (Theorem 6.2). Verify final rank of AB via SVD.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Global convergence without ℓ₂ regularization** – Does SpecGF always converge to a global minimum from any initialization, or can it diverge? Boundedness of factor norms remains conjectural.
- **Existence of non‑constant polynomial invariants** – The authors conjecture that no such invariants exist for SpecGF, based on scalar case analysis; a general proof (or counterexample) is open.
- **Extension to discrete‑time and deep LoRA** – Does equal‑rate dynamics hold for practical Muon (with Newton‑Schulz) and for LoRA on networks with multiple layers (product of >2 factors)?
- **Impact of rank misspecification (r* < r)** – When the true low‑rank structure is smaller than the chosen LoRA rank, uniform growth may over‑fit; understanding the trade‑off between implicit bias and generalization is an open problem.

## Limitations
- Theoretical results rely on smoothed orthogonalization (Tβ) and Gaussian small initialization, which are idealizations of practical Muon (which uses approximate Newton‑Schulz and momentum).
- LLM fine‑tuning experiments omit key hyperparameters (momentum μ, exact learning rate, number of Newton‑Schulz iterations), limiting reproducibility and confidence in the observed uniform spectral growth.
- Convergence guarantees for unregularized SpecGF depend on a boundedness assumption that remains unproven; ℓ₂ regularization, while effective, is not standard in LoRA pipelines and may affect performance.

## Confidence
- **Claim 1:** Muon/SpecGF yields uniform spectral growth in LoRA. → **High** (consistent empirical evidence across synthetic and LLM settings).
- **Claim 2:** Equal‑rate dynamics (smallest‑first) arise from approximate decoupling. → **Medium** (proof requires δ‑alignment and small init; holds under stated assumptions but may fail if assumptions violated).
- **Claim 3:** SpecGF converges globally to the best rank‑r approximation (with ℓ₂ reg; almost surely otherwise under boundedness). → **Medium** (unregularized boundedness is conjectural; ℓ₂ reg guarantees but not always used).
