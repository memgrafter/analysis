# MLDocRAG: Multimodal Long-Context Document Retrieval Augmented Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.10271
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10271
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: MLDocRAG achieves 47.9% accuracy on MMLongBench-Doc and 50.8% on LongDocURL, consistently outperforming multiple baseline approaches.

## Executive Summary
Long-context document question answering across modalities (text, images, layout) faces challenges of cross-modal heterogeneity and cross-page reasoning. MLDocRAG introduces a framework centered on a Multimodal Chunk-Query Graph (MCQG). The graph is built using MDoc2Query, which generates answerable queries from heterogeneous document chunks and explicitly links each query to its source content. This query-centric structure enables selective retrieval and structured evidence aggregation across modalities and pages. Evaluated on MMLongBench-Doc and LongDocURL, MLDocRAG attains 47.9% and 50.8% accuracy respectively, surpassing text-only, image-to-text, multimodal embedding, page-level vision, and graph-based baselines. The results highlight the effectiveness of bridging dispersed multimodal evidence for improved retrieval and answer generation.

## Method Summary
MLDocRAG operates in two phases: offline graph construction and online retrieval-augmented generation. During preprocessing, each multimodal document is segmented into heterogeneous chunks (e.g., text blocks, image regions). For every chunk, the MDoc2Query module generates multiple answerable queries that are grounded in the chunk’s content. These queries are added as nodes to a graph, with edges linking each query node to its originating chunk node. The resulting Multimodal Chunk-Query Graph (MCQG) captures the relationship between potential questions and their supporting evidence. At inference time, a user question is used to retrieve the most relevant query nodes from the graph. The corresponding source chunks are then aggregated—potentially spanning multiple pages and modalities—to form a rich evidence set. Finally, a generative answer model conditions on this evidence and the original question to produce the final response. This approach replaces traditional linear or embedding-based retrieval with a query-centric graph, allowing precise matching to pre-computed intents and enabling multi-hop reasoning over distributed multimodal content.

## Key Results
- Achieved **47.9%** accuracy on MMLongBench-Doc and **50.8%** on LongDocURL.
- Consistently outperformed a range of baselines, including text-only RAG, image-to-text conversion, multimodal embedding retrieval, page-level vision models, and prior graph-based methods.
- Demonstrated superior ability to retrieve and aggregate evidence across different modalities and document pages, leading to more accurate answers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core innovation of MLDocRAG is its query-centric graph, which transforms static document content into a dynamic network of searchable intents. By generating answerable queries for each chunk, the system creates a set of “pre-fabricated” retrieval targets that already embed the information needed to answer questions about that chunk. This bypasses the ambiguity of embedding-based similarity search, especially in multimodal contexts where text and visual features are heterogeneous. The explicit linking of queries to source chunks ensures that retrieved evidence always points to its origin, enabling traceable and structured aggregation. Furthermore, the graph naturally supports cross-page reasoning: queries from different chunks (even on separate pages) can be co-retrieved if they are relevant, and their linked content can be combined without losing modality-specific details. This design directly addresses the challenges of cross-modal heterogeneity (by using modality-appropriate query generation) and cross-page reasoning (by allowing non-local evidence collection). The empirical results confirm that this mechanism yields higher retrieval precision and better final answer accuracy compared to methods that treat the document as a flat sequence of tokens or as independent pages.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multimodal Document Understanding**  
  *Why needed*: Real-world documents combine text, images, tables, and layout; reasoning must integrate these signals.  
  *Quick check*: Can the system answer a question that requires both reading a figure caption and interpreting a chart?

- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**  
  *Why needed*: External retrieval reduces hallucination and grounds generation in verifiable evidence.  
  *Quick check*: Does the retriever fetch passages that actually contain the answer before the generator runs?

- **Graph-based Representations**  
  *Why needed*: Complex relationships between document fragments (e.g., cross-page references) are more naturally modeled as a graph than a linear sequence.  
  *Quick check*: Does the graph connect related chunks across pages through shared query nodes?

- **Query Generation for Chunks**  
  *Why needed*: Turning static content into potential questions creates targeted retrieval anchors that match user intents more precisely than raw embeddings.  
  *Quick check*: For a given chunk, does MDoc2Query produce plausible, answerable questions that cover its key information?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Document → Chunking → MDoc2Query → MCQG Construction → Graph-based Retriever → Evidence Aggregator → Answer Generator.  
The graph is built offline; during inference, the retriever matches the user question to query nodes in the MCQG, fetches linked chunks, and passes them to the aggregator and generator.

**Critical path**:  
1. Ingest a multimodal document.  
2. Split into topic- and modality-homogeneous chunks.  
3. For each chunk, use MDoc2Query to generate multiple queries.  
4. Add chunk and query nodes to the graph with appropriate edges (link each query to its chunk).  
5. At query time, encode the user question and retrieve top‑k query nodes via approximate nearest neighbor search on the graph.  
6. Collect all chunks linked to retrieved queries, de‑duplicate, and order by relevance.  
7. Feed the evidence set to a large language model (LLM) to generate the final answer.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Graph size vs. retrieval speed*: More chunks and queries improve coverage but increase memory and latency.  
- *Chunk granularity*: Too fine chunks may lose context; too coarse may mix modalities and reduce precision.  
- *Modality-specific query generation*: Separate models per modality add complexity but better capture unique features (e.g., visual attributes).  
- *Static vs. dynamic graph*: A static prebuilt graph is efficient for repeated queries on the same document but cannot adapt to new query patterns.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Low recall of relevant chunks indicates poor query generation or sparse graph connectivity.  
- High irrelevant retrieval suggests noisy queries or insufficient filtering during matching.  
- Inability to answer cross-page questions may expose limited graph edges between distant pages or inadequate aggregation heuristics.  
- Poor performance on certain modalities (e.g., figures) signals imbalanced MDoc2Query training or representation issues.

**First three experiments to onboard**:  
1. **Graph inspection**: Load a sample document through the pipeline, visualize the MCQG (nodes and edges), and verify that each query is correctly linked to its source chunk.  
2. **Retrieval sanity check**: Provide a question with a known answer, retrieve top‑k queries, and confirm that the linked chunks contain the supporting evidence.  
3. **End‑to‑end accuracy test**: Run the full system on a small benchmark suite (e.g., 10 examples from MMLongBench-Doc) and compare the generated answers against ground truth; log failure cases for analysis.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility**: No official code or data release; independent replication impossible without the MDoc2Query generator and MCQG implementation.  
- **Benchmark Specificity**: Results are limited to two long‑document QA datasets; performance on other document genres (e.g., legal contracts, scientific papers) or noisy scanned inputs remains unknown.  
- **Component Ablation**: The relative contribution of the MDoc2Query module versus the graph retrieval structure is not disentangled, making it unclear which part drives the observed gains.
