# Inference-Time Toxicity Mitigation in Protein Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04045  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04045  
- **Reference count:** 16  
- **Primary result:** Logit Diff Amplification (LDA) reduces predicted toxicity rate by 8–30 percentage points below taxon-finetuned baselines while preserving distributional similarity and structural foldability.

## Executive Summary
Domain adaptation via taxonomic finetuning can inadvertently elicit toxic protein generation from protein language models, even when toxicity is not a training objective. To mitigate this, the authors propose Logit Diff Amplification (LDA), an inference-time intervention that modulates token probabilities by amplifying the logit difference between a baseline taxon-finetuned model and a toxic-finetuned counterpart. LDA requires no retraining and is applied during decoding: new logits are computed as ℓ′ = ℓ_B + α(ℓ_B − ℓ_T), where ℓ_B and ℓ_T are the baseline and toxic model logits, respectively, and α scales the amplification. Across four taxonomic groups, LDA achieves substantial reductions in ToxDL2-predicted toxicity (8–30 percentage points) while maintaining biological quality as measured by Fréchet ESM Distance (near zero change) and average pLDDT (largely unchanged). In contrast, activation-based steering, which perturbs hidden states, degrades both distributional and structural properties. The work demonstrates the effectiveness of contrastive logit-level control for safe protein generation and discusses practical considerations including computational overhead and governance of the toxic model.

## Method Summary
Starting from the autoregressive ProGen2 model, the authors first finetune on all sequences from each target taxon (Arthropoda, Arachnida, Gastropoda, Lepidosauria), producing baseline models that show elevated toxicity due to taxon-specific toxin enrichment. To obtain a reference for toxicity, they continue finetuning each baseline solely on that taxon’s toxic sequences, yielding a toxic-finetuned counterpart. LDA then intervenes at generation time: for each token, both the baseline and toxic model perform a forward pass; their logits are combined via ℓ′ = ℓ_B + α(ℓ_B − ℓ_T), and a token is sampled from the resulting distribution. Generated sequences are filtered by perplexity under the original (un-finetuned) ProGen2 to retain natural-looking proteins. Toxicity is assessed using ToxDL2 on ESMFold-predicted structures, and biological quality is evaluated via Fréchet ESM Distance (distributional similarity to natural proteins) and per-residue pLDDT (structural plausibility). Optimal α values are taxon-specific (0.7–1.0). The approach doubles inference cost (two forward passes per step) but avoids any retraining of the baseline generator.

## Key Results
- LDA reduces ToxDL2-predicted toxicity by 8–30 percentage points relative to taxon-finetuned baselines across all four taxonomic groups.
- Biological quality metrics remain stable: Fréchet ESM Distance changes negligibly and average pLDDT is largely unaffected.
- Activation-based steering (addition/ablation of a toxicity direction) also lowers toxicity but at the cost of increased distributional distance and reduced foldability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1 – Logit difference amplification:** LDA modifies the next‑token distribution by computing ℓ′ = ℓ_B + α(ℓ_B − ℓ_T). This amplifies the contrast between the baseline and toxic‑finetuned models in the direction that reduces toxic‑associated logits. The core assumption is that this linear contrast in logit space separates toxic from non‑toxic generations while staying on the model’s learned manifold. Evidence includes the abstract and Section 2.2, and prior work on logit‑level interventions in NLP. The method fails if the toxicity direction is not linearly separable or if amplification pushes sequences off‑manifold (detected via increased Fréchet ESM Distance or decreased pLDDT).

**Mechanism 2 – Taxonomic finetuning elicits toxicity:** Finetuning on sequences from a specific taxon shifts the model’s prior toward patterns overrepresented in that group. Because toxins are unevenly distributed across taxa (some groups have high toxin prevalence), the finetuned model learns an increased prior probability for toxic‑like sequences even without toxicity supervision. The assumption is that toxicity correlates with distinctive motifs or domains that are captured by the finetuning. Support comes from the observed elevation of toxicity rates (10–65%) after taxon finetuning (Figure 2a, Supplementary Figure 1). Elicitation may not occur if the finetuning set lacks toxicity bias or if the base model already memorizes toxic patterns.

**Mechanism 3 – Activation steering degrades quality:** Direct (addition) and affine (addition + ablation) activation steering apply a static toxicity‑related vector to hidden states. This perturbation operates relative to a fixed reference and tends to push generations off the natural protein manifold, causing symmetric increases in toxicity reduction across both addition and ablation, but also increasing distributional distance (ΔFED > 0) and decreasing foldability (ΔpLDDT < 0). The core assumption is that toxicity information in PLMs is not linearly separable in activation space via a single direction, or that the required activation shift corrupts other generative properties. Empirical evidence is provided in Supplementary Figures 5–7.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Baseline model (B) and Toxicity model (T) → LDA module (computes ℓ′ = ℓ_B + α(ℓ_B − ℓ_T)) → Generation pipeline (token sampling, perplexity filtering using B) → Toxicity scoring (ToxDL2) → Quality evaluators (Fréchet ESM Distance, pLDDT).

**Critical path:** For a given α and taxon: load B and T → generate sequences with LDA intervention during decoding → filter to K lowest‑perplexity sequences under the original base model → evaluate toxicity with ToxDL2 and quality with FED/pLDDT → compare to baseline (B alone). The whole pipeline is repeated across α sweeps and three independent runs.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Computational:* Two forward passes per decoding step ≈ 2× inference cost versus single‑model steering.  
- *Quality‑toxicity:* Higher α reduces toxicity but risks degrading ΔFED/ΔpLDDT; optimal α varies by taxon.  
- *Generality:* Requires a toxic‑finetuned counterpart T; not applicable otherwise.  
- *Deployment:* Must securely store T to prevent misuse, exposing only the mitigated generator.

**Failure signatures:**  
- No toxicity reduction: ℓ_B − ℓ_T does not encode a meaningful direction (e.g., low linear probe accuracy).  
- Quality collapse: ΔFED >> 0 or ΔpLDDT << 0 indicates off‑manifold drift; reduce α.  
- Symmetric response: Both α>0 and α<0 yield similar toxicity reductions (as in activation steering), suggesting non‑selective disruption.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Reproduce elicitation: Finetune ProGen2 on a taxon and confirm elevated ToxDL2 toxicity versus the base model.  
2. Implement LDA sweep: Create toxic‑finetuned T; implement ℓ′ = ℓ_B + α(ℓ_B − ℓ_T); sweep α and plot toxicity vs. α.  
3. Validate quality preservation: For optimal α, compute ΔFED and ΔpLDDT; compare against activation steering to confirm quality retention.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Functional validation:** Does reduction in ToxDL2 score correspond to actual decrease in toxic activity in biological assays? The paper lacks wet‑lab validation.  
- **Predictor bias:** Do alternative toxicity predictors and motif enrichment analyses agree with ToxDL2 findings, or are results biased by the specific classifier?  
- **Efficiency and governance:** Can the 2× inference cost of LDA be reduced (e.g., via distillation), and what policy mechanisms should govern access to the toxic‑finetuned model direction?  
- **Theoretical understanding:** Why does LDA preserve biological quality while activation steering degrades it? The linear logit‑space contrast hypothesis remains unproven.

## Limitations
- Independence blocked: Toxicity evaluation relies on unreleased ToxDL2 model, preventing external verification.  
- Dataset opacity: Exact UniProt IDs, species inclusion rules, and train/validation splits are not provided, raising risk of data leakage.  
- Proxy metrics: Fréchet ESM Distance and pLDDT are indirect; sequences might still be non‑functional or immunogenic.

## Confidence
- **Taxonomic finetuning elicits toxic generation:** High confidence (directly measured).  
- **Activation‑based steering degrades quality:** High confidence (consistent empirical degradation).  
- **LDA reduces toxicity while preserving quality:** Medium confidence (internal results robust but external validation limited by ToxDL2 and dataset secrecy; linear logit‑space assumption not formally proven).

## Next Checks
1. **Validate ToxDL2 as toxicity oracle:** Train an independent binary classifier on a held‑out set of known toxins/non‑toxins; compare its predictions on the paper’s generated sequences to ToxDL2.  
2. **Confirm finetuning initialization and data leakage:** Obtain full sequence IDs for taxon and toxic finetuning; verify that toxic‑finetuning starts from the taxon‑finetuned checkpoint and that no species overlap exists between any taxon’s train/validation splits.  
3. **Probe linearity of toxicity direction:** After training B and T, apply linear probing (e.g., logistic regression) on final logits of a large sample to predict ToxDL2 labels; high accuracy (>85%) supports the LDA assumption.
