# Evaluating Financial Intelligence in Large Language Models: Benchmarking SuperInvesting AI with LLM Engines

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.08704
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08704
*   **Reference Count:** 36
*   **Primary Result:** The AI Financial Intelligence Benchmark (AFIB) reveals that hybrid systems combining structured financial data access with analytical reasoning (e.g., SuperInvesting) achieve the highest multi-dimensional performance, while retrieval-oriented models trade analytical depth for data recency.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the AI Financial Intelligence Benchmark (AFIB) to address the lack of systematic evaluation for large language models (LLMs) in financial analysis. AFIB assesses five critical dimensions: factual accuracy, analytical completeness, data recency, consistency, and failure patterns. Evaluating five AI systems on 95+ structured questions from real-world equity research, the results show that SuperInvesting, a hybrid system, attained the highest overall performance. Retrieval-oriented models like Perplexity excelled in data recency but exhibited weaknesses in analytical synthesis and consistency. The study concludes that financial intelligence is inherently multi-dimensional, and optimal performance requires integrating reliable, current data with deep reasoning capabilities.

## Method Summary
The evaluation uses a fixed snapshot of 95+ structured financial analysis questions derived from Indian equity research, each with a verifiable ground truth from authoritative sources like SEBI filings. Five AI systems (GPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, SuperInvesting) are queried with identical prompts without conversation history. Responses are scored across five pre-defined dimensions using structured rubrics: (1) Factual Accuracy & Hallucination (C/N), (2) Analytical Depth, (3) Completeness (kr,q/kq), (4) Data Recency, and (5) Consistency (1 - Var() across repeats). Dimension scores are normalized to [0,100] and aggregated with equal weights (0.20) into a composite leaderboard.

## Key Results
*   **SuperInvesting** achieved the highest composite performance, with a factual accuracy of 8.96/10 and an analytical completeness score of 56.65/70, while demonstrating the lowest hallucination rate.
*   **Perplexity** and other retrieval-oriented systems showed a clear trade-off: they scored highest on **Data Recency** due to live information access but exhibited **weaker analytical synthesis and consistency**.
*   The evaluation confirms that **financial intelligence is multi-dimensional**; no single model dominated all dimensions, and distinct capability profiles emerged across models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Financial intelligence is best achieved through a hybrid architecture combining structured financial data access with analytical reasoning.
*   **Mechanism:** Systems like SuperInvesting integrate real-time or structured financial data pipelines with a reasoning-optimized core. This allows retrieval of current, accurate figures while applying multi-variable financial logic (e.g., linking segment EBITDA to consolidated totals). The synergy prevents the trade-off seen in pure retrieval (recency without depth) or pure reasoning (depth without current data) models.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "systems that combine structured financial data access with analytical reasoning capabilities provide the most reliable performance." [section IV.D] Figure 4 shows SuperInvesting as an outlier achieving both high recency and analytical depth. [corpus] Neighbor papers (FinMCP-Bench, QuantMCP) explore similar hybrid integration.
*   **Break Condition:** If the structured data source is unreliable or the reasoning component fails to correctly integrate retrieved data (e.g., miscalculating from segment figures), the hybrid advantage collapses.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Financial intelligence is inherently multi-dimensional; different models exhibit distinct strength/weakness profiles across orthogonal dimensions.
*   **Mechanism:** The AFIB framework evaluates five independent capabilities with different cognitive and architectural demands. For instance, factual accuracy depends on data source quality, while analytical completeness depends on reasoning depth. A model optimized for one (e.g., Perplexity for recency) may underperform on others (e.g., completeness). This explains why leaderboards change per dimension.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "financial intelligence in large language models is inherently multi-dimensional." [section IV.B] Figure 3 radar graph shows divergent capability profiles. [section IV.F] Figure 6 heatmap visualizes stable, distinct performance patterns.
*   **Break Condition:** If a single dimension (e.g., recency) becomes overwhelmingly dominant in decision-making, the multi-dimensional evaluation becomes less predictive, risking over-optimization on that axis.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Retrieval-oriented architectures exhibit a systematic trade-off: gains in data recency and factual freshness at the cost of analytical synthesis, consistency, and controlled hallucination.
*   **Mechanism:** Systems like Perplexity rely on live web/source retrieval, introducing variability in source quality, aggregation noise (e.g., inconsistent segment totals), and a lack of stable internal knowledge for consistent cross-statement reasoning. This architectural choice directly links to observed failure patterns: high "Data Recency" scores but lower "Completeness" and "Consistency".
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "Retrieval-oriented systems such as Perplexity perform strongly on data recency tasks due to live information access but exhibit weaker analytical synthesis and consistency." [section IV.D] Trade-off analysis (Figure 4) and text on weaker analytical reasoning. [section V] Limitations note Perplexity's "inconsistent totals due to segment aggregation issues."
*   **Break Condition:** If retrieval systems evolve to use only highly vetted, structured financial APIs with deterministic aggregation, the trade-off may diminish.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Financial Statement Interconnectivity**
    *   **Why needed:** Questions like Reliance's segment EBITDA contribution require understanding how segment figures roll up to consolidated totals, including adjustments. Models failing this produce mathematically inconsistent totals.
    *   **Quick Check:** If a company reports Segment A EBITDA = 100, Segment B EBITDA = 200, and "Other/Corporate" EBITDA = -50, what is the consolidated EBITDA? What common reporting nuance might cause a model to incorrectly sum only the positive segments?
*   **Concept: Hallucination as Fabricated Specificity**
    *   **Why needed:** In finance, a confidently stated but incorrect numerical value is more dangerous than a refusal. The benchmark distinguishes hallucination (specific false claim) from omission (refusal).
    *   **Quick Check:** What is the key difference, in a financial context, between a model stating "SBI's net NPA was 1.05%" (a hallucination if wrong) and stating "I cannot access the latest quarterly data" (a refusal)?
*   **Concept: Multi-Dimensional Benchmark Design**
    *   **Why needed:** AFIB's design (verifiability, representativeness, analytical difficulty) ensures the test reflects real analyst workflows, not just trivia. Understanding this helps evaluate *why* a model fails—data gap, reasoning flaw, or consistency issue?
    *   **Quick Check:** Why is it insufficient to evaluate an LLM's financial intelligence solely by asking it to retrieve a single metric like "latest revenue"? What additional dimensions should be tested?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Query Constructor -> Model Execution Layer -> Scoring Rubrics <-> Ground Truth Database -> Aggregation Engine
*   **Critical path:** Query construction -> Model execution -> Output extraction -> Ground truth verification -> Per-dimension scoring -> Weighted aggregation -> Leaderboard generation.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Equal vs. Custom Weights: AFIB uses equal weights for simplicity/neutrality; a production system might weight "Accuracy" and "Hallucination Resistance" higher.
    *   Controlled vs. Live Evaluation: The benchmark uses a fixed snapshot for reproducibility; a live system trades reproducibility for recency.
    *   Specialized vs. Generalist Models: Results favor specialized/hybrid systems. Onboarding a generalist model requires accepting higher hallucination risk or adding retrieval augmentation.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **GPT:** High hallucination frequency in numerical claims.
    *   **Claude:** Conservative refusal behavior leading to low completeness on complex questions.
    *   **Perplexity:** Inconsistency across repeats due to live source variability; aggregation errors in multi-segment analysis.
    *   **Gemini:** Strong reasoning but weaker on latest-period questions (data recency gap).
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Recency Stress Test:** Submit a question requiring knowledge of an earnings release from the last 30 days. Perplexity should score highest; others may use outdated data or refuse.
    2.  **Consistency Probe:** Repeat the Reliance EBITDA question 5 times in separate sessions. Measure variance in the summed segment percentage. SuperInvesting/Claude should show near-zero variance; Perplexity may fluctuate.
    3.  **Hallucination Spot-Check:** Ask for a precise, verifiable metric (e.g., "SBI's gross NPA ratio for Q3 FY2025"). Score each model's output against the exact filing value. GPT likely fabricates; SuperInvesting should match or cite source uncertainty.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How can the AFIB framework be systematically expanded to evaluate AI performance across a broader set of financial domains (e.g., credit risk analysis, derivatives pricing, macroeconomic forecasting)?
    *   **Basis:** [explicit] Future Work states need to "expand the benchmark to cover a broader set of financial tasks."
    *   **Why unresolved:** Current AFIB focuses on equity research; expansion requires defining new reasoning tasks, data structures, and metrics.
    *   **Resolution Evidence:** Development of new, domain-specific benchmark modules with SME input, followed by comparative evaluations to establish baselines.
*   **Open Question 2:** What specific architectural designs enable AI systems to effectively integrate real-time data retrieval with deep, structured financial reasoning, thereby overcoming the observed trade-off between data recency and analytical depth?
    *   **Basis:** [explicit] Results highlight a clear "trade-off," noting hybrid systems like SuperInvesting represent a "partial exception."
    *   **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the trade-off but does not investigate the specific mechanisms (e.g., retrieval-augmented generation pipelines, modular reasoning) that successfully balance these dimensions.
    *   **Resolution Evidence:** Experimental ablation studies comparing different hybrid model configurations on AFIB's recency and analytical depth sub-benchmarks.
*   **Open Question 3:** To what extent do the performance hierarchies and capability profiles observed in AFIB generalize across different global financial markets (e.g., U.S., European) and asset classes (e.g., fixed income, commodities)?
    *   **Basis:** [inferred] Limitations acknowledge evaluation is within "the context of Indian equity markets" and "may not generalize directly to other financial domains."
    *   **Why unresolved:** The benchmark's dataset is tailored to the Indian corporate and regulatory context. Generalizability is uncertain due to market-specific knowledge, document formats, and terminology.
    *   **Resolution Evidence:** A direct replication study applying the identical (or localized) AFIB protocol to other major financial markets, followed by comparative analysis of ranking stability.
*   **Open Question 4:** How predictive is AFIB's multi-dimensional scoring of success in realistic, end-to-end investment research workflows that involve iterative information gathering, spreadsheet-based financial modeling, and hypothesis testing?
    *   **Basis:** [inferred] Limitations note the benchmark evaluates "structured financial analysis questions rather than full end-to-end investment workflows."
    *   **Why unresolved:** AFIB tests discrete capabilities in isolation. It is unclear whether high scores translate to effective performance in complex, multi-step, tool-augmented tasks where context management and error propagation are critical.
    *   **Resolution Evidence:** Designing a complementary evaluation framework that simulates end-to-end workflows (e.g., producing a full equity research report from raw filings) and correlating outcomes with AFIB scores.

## Limitations
*   **Data & Rubric Opacity:** The exact 95+ AFIB questions and detailed scoring rubrics are unreleased, preventing direct replication and audit of dimension scores.
*   **Benchmark Representativeness:** Evaluation is limited to Indian equities (FY2025-26). Generalizability to other markets, sectors, or volatile regimes is untested.
*   **Architectural Black Box:** The inner workings of the top performer, "SuperInvesting," are undisclosed. The claimed hybrid advantage may stem from unexamined confounders like proprietary training data.
*   **Static Snapshot vs. Live Reality:** The fixed-data benchmark undervalues continuous, real-time adaptation critical in live trading environments.

## Confidence
*   **High Confidence:** AFIB introduces a necessary multi-dimensional evaluation framework; evidence shows different models exhibit distinct strength/weakness profiles across dimensions.
*   **Medium Confidence:** The conclusion that hybrid systems perform best is supported by the single data point (SuperInvesting) but lacks controlled ablation evidence isolating each component's contribution.
*   **Low Confidence:** Specific performance rankings (e.g., exact scores) and the inferred "trade-off" for retrieval models are not independently verifiable due to unreleased ground truth and test items.

## Next Checks
1.  **Reconstruct & Audit Benchmark:** Solicit the full AFIB question bank and ground truth from the authors. Publish a reproducibility audit verifying scoring consistency across human annotators.
2.  **Perform Ablation Study:** Test a controlled variant of SuperInvesting (or a comparable open-source model) with and without its structured data retrieval layer on the AFIB set to isolate the hybrid mechanism's contribution.
3.  **Cross-Benchmark Validation:** Evaluate the same models on an independent, publicly available financial reasoning benchmark (e.g., FinQA, TAT-QA). Convergent results would strengthen the claim of a generalizable "financial intelligence" hierarchy.
