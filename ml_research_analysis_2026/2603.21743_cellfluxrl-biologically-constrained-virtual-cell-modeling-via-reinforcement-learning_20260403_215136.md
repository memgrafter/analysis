# CellFluxRL: Biologically-Constrained Virtual Cell Modeling via Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21743
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21743
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Reinforcement learning post-training with seven biologically meaningful rewards significantly improves mode-of-action classification, structural validity, and morphology metrics over the CellFlux baseline while maintaining competitive image quality scores.

## Executive Summary
Existing virtual cell models, such as CellFlux, generate cell images that violate basic biological constraints (e.g., nuclei positioned outside the cytoplasm). This paper introduces CellFluxRL, a method that post-trains a pretrained flow-matching generative model using reinforcement learning. The approach uses a suite of seven non-differentiable reward functions—derived from domain knowledge and off-the-shelf evaluators—to steer the model toward biologically plausible outputs. The rewards cover mode-of-action classification, structural validity (e.g., nuclear containment), and morphology. Combined with test-time best-of-N selection, CellFluxRL achieves substantial gains on all biological metrics, demonstrating a viable path for incorporating complex scientific constraints into generative models.

## Method Summary
CellFluxRL builds upon the CellFlux flow-matching model and the DiffusionNFT reinforcement learning algorithm. The core procedure involves: 1) loading a pretrained CellFlux velocity field and initializing seven reward modules (a MoA classifier, Cellpose for segmentation, and population statistics for morphological features); 2) generating multiple rollouts per training sample using a lagging copy of the policy; 3) computing a weighted sum of rewards per rollout and normalizing them to obtain advantages; 4) applying a contrastive loss that implicitly defines positive (high-reward) and negative (low-reward) target velocity policies, with a KL penalty to prevent excessive deviation from the pretrained model; 5) updating the policy via gradient descent and an EMA update of the lagging copy. At inference, best-of-N selection chooses the highest-reward sample from N rollouts. Key hyperparameters (e.g., KL weight β=1, reward weights, group size m) are chosen empirically. Exact values for the normalization constant Z_c, EMA decay, and m are not fully specified.

## Key Results
- Mode-of-action classification accuracy increased from 0.56 (CellFlux) to 0.66 (CellFluxRL with best-of-N=4).
- Nuclear containment (nucleus inside cytoplasm) improved from 0.88 to 0.97.
- Combined overall reward rose dramatically from -2.44 to 3.15.
- Frechet Inception Distance (FID) and Kernel Inception Distance (KID) remained competitive with the baseline, indicating maintained image quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Reinforcement learning post-training steers the generative distribution toward biologically valid samples by increasing the likelihood of high-reward rollouts and decreasing the likelihood of low-reward rollouts, without requiring differentiable reward functions.
**Mechanism:** The method adapts DiffusionNFT’s contrastive framework. For a batch of generated images from a fixed control and condition, it computes normalized optimality probabilities from combined biological rewards. It then constructs implicit positive and negative velocity policies by mixing the current policy with a lagging copy. The contrastive loss fits the positive policy to the flow matching target for high-reward samples and the negative policy for low-reward samples, while a KL penalty prevents excessive deviation from the pretrained model. This implicitly increases probability mass in high-reward regions of the generation path.
**Core assumption:** Reward functions are meaningful proxies for biological correctness; the flow matching forward process provides a tractable way to estimate likelihood ratios; within-group rollout diversity is sufficient for a useful positive/negative split.
**Break condition:** If rewards are biased, noisy, or fail to correlate with true biological plausibility (e.g., reward hacking), the RL update optimizes the wrong objective, potentially degrading biological relevance despite higher reward scores.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** A suite of seven complementary reward functions—covering mode of action, structural validity, and morphology—provides multi-scale supervision that explicitly enforces physical and biological constraints missing from the pixel-level flow matching loss.
**Mechanism:** Rewards are derived from domain knowledge and evaluators: (1) `r_MoA` uses a pretrained classifier to encourage correct perturbation-induced signatures; (2) `r_Nuc-in-Cyto` and `r_Roundness` enforce geometric constraints via segmentation (Cellpose) and shape statistics; (3) four morphological rewards (`NucSize`, `CytoSize`, `NucCount`, `CytoCount`) penalize deviation from MoA-conditioned population statistics. The linear combination `r = Σ w_k r_k` directs optimization toward balanced improvement.
**Core assumption:** The true perturbed distribution satisfies these constraints; optimizing them moves the generated distribution closer to the biological distribution along dimensions underweighted by flow matching.
**Break condition:** If reward definitions are incomplete or misaligned (e.g., missing a critical constraint, using statistics that vary within MoA classes), the model may overfit to specified rewards while still generating implausible images in unmeasured aspects.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Test-time scaling via best-of-N selection, using the same reward functions as verifiers, yields monotonic improvements in biological correctness by selecting the highest-reward sample from multiple rollouts, with RL post-training improving the base distribution to make selection more efficient.
**Mechanism:** At inference, for a given control and condition, generate N candidate images and select the one with the highest combined reward. Because RL training already shifts the distribution toward higher-reward regions, even small N yields good samples; increasing N extracts additional gains. This is complementary to training and scales with compute.
**Core assumption:** Reward functions are reliable verifiers at the individual sample level; the generation process produces sufficiently diverse candidates; the post-trained distribution contains a higher fraction of high-reward samples than the pretrained model.
**Break condition:** If reward model errors are high or the generative distribution has low variance (e.g., mode collapse), best-of-N yields diminishing returns. Computational cost may also be prohibitive.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Flow Matching for Distribution Transformation**
  - **Why needed here:** CellFlux uses flow matching to transform an unpaired control distribution to a perturbed distribution conditioned on the same-batch control, addressing batch effects without pointwise correspondences.
  - **Quick check:** How does flow matching differ from diffusion models in its training objective, and why is it suitable for unpaired perturbation data?

- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning for Non-Differentiable Objectives**
  - **Why needed here:** Biological reward functions (e.g., MoA classifier, segmentation metrics) are non-differentiable, so standard backpropagation cannot optimize them. RL provides a framework to optimize expected reward via sampling and contrastive updates.
  - **Quick check:** In the DiffusionNFT-style contrastive loss, what is the role of the implicit positive and negative velocity policies, and how does the KL regularization prevent reward hacking?

- **Concept: Biological Constraints in Cellular Imaging**
  - **Why needed here:** Generated cell images must obey fundamental biological facts (nucleus inside cytoplasm, MoA-specific morphology, reasonable cell sizes/counts). Understanding these constraints is necessary to design meaningful reward functions and interpret results.
  - **Quick check:** Give an example of a structural constraint and a morphological statistic used in the rewards, and explain why each is biologically plausible.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Pretrained velocity field v_θ (CellFlux) -> Biological reward modules (MoA classifier, Cellpose, morphological stats) -> RL training loop (rollout buffer, advantage normalization, contrastive loss with implicit v⁺_θ and v⁻_θ, KL penalty) -> Updated velocity field v_θ -> Test-time best-of-N selection -> Final output

- **Critical path:**
  1. Load pretrained CellFlux model v_ref_θ and initialize reward functions.
  2. For each RL iteration: sample (x₀, c) pairs; generate m rollouts using v_old; compute combined reward r per rollout; normalize to r_adv; store in buffer.
  3. For each mini-batch: sample t, compute forward-noised x_t and target v; construct v⁺_θ, v⁻_θ; compute contrastive loss + KL; update v_θ.
  4. Update v_old via EMA.
  5. At inference: for given (x₀, c), generate N candidates with v_θ, compute reward for each, select best.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **KL weight β_KL:** Higher values preserve pretrained visual quality and prevent reward hacking but limit biological improvement; lower values allow larger distribution shifts but risk degradation in FID/KID or unrealistic generations.
  - **Reward weights w_k:** Empirically tuned; changing weights can bias improvement toward specific aspects but may harm others.
  - **Group size m:** Larger m provides better positive/negative split but increases memory/computation per iteration.
  - **Test-time N:** Larger N improves results but scales inference cost linearly; RL post-training improves base distribution so moderate N already yields gains.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Reward hacking:** Sudden spike in reward metrics without visual improvement (e.g., nuclei artificially enlarged). Monitor FID/KID alongside rewards.
  - **Mode collapse:** Reduced diversity in rollouts (all generated cells look similar). Visualize multiple rollouts.
  - **KL divergence collapse:** If β_KL is too low, model rapidly diverges from v_old, leading to degenerate images. Monitor D_KL(v_θ ∥ v_old).
  - **No scaling with N:** If best-of-N does not improve metrics, reward model may be unreliable or base distribution saturated.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reward sanity check:** Apply each reward to real perturbed images (BBBC021) and CellFlux baseline outputs. Verify real images score higher and rewards discriminate known failure modes.
  2. **Single-reward RL ablation:** Post-train CellFlux with each reward individually. Plot each metric vs. training steps to see which rewards are easier to optimize and whether optimizing one harms others.
  3. **Test-time scaling curve:** For both pretrained and RL models, run best-of-N with N=1,2,4,8,16 on a fixed test set. Plot each reward metric vs. N. Expect monotonic improvement and RL curve above baseline at all N.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Can LLMs automatically generate biologically meaningful reward functions for virtual cell models?** Manual rewards require expertise and do not scale; future work proposes leveraging LLMs to translate literature into executable rewards.
- **Does the CellFluxRL framework transfer to other scientific domains with non-differentiable constraints?** The approach is claimed to be domain-agnostic (e.g., material science, medical imaging) but is only tested on BBBC021.
- **How can we automatically determine optimal weights for multiple biological rewards without manual tuning?** Authors manually set weights based on empirical difficulty; no automatic method is proposed.

## Limitations
- Reward functions lack independent biological validation and may overlook critical constraints or inadvertently reward artifacts.
- Hyperparameters (group size m, EMA decay, normalization constant Z_c) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- Evaluation is limited to the BBBC021 dataset; generalization to other cell types, stains, or perturbations is unknown.
- FID/KID remain competitive but not improved, suggesting a trade-off between visual quality and biological validity.
- The relative impact of RL post-training versus test-time best-of-N is unclear; RL-only metrics are not explicitly reported.

## Confidence
- **RL post-training improves biological metrics:** Medium – Evidence is strong but depends on unreported hyperparameters and reward design; independent replication needed.
- **Reward suite covers necessary constraints:** Low–Medium – Rewards are plausible but lack external validation; may miss important failure modes.
- **Best-of-N selection yields monotonic gains:** Medium – Shown on BBBC021; scalability to other settings untested.
- **Flow matching + RL is a viable framework:** Medium – The combination works on one dataset but domain transfer is unproven.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation of reward components:** Zero out or reweight each reward, retrain RL, and evaluate all metrics (including FID/KID). Identifies essential rewards and conflicts.
2. **Cross-dataset generalization:** Apply CellFluxRL to a hold-out dataset (e.g., CP-GEMM) without retraining. Compare biological metrics against the pretrained baseline.
3. **Human expert evaluation:** Conduct a blinded study with biologists ranking pretrained, RL-only, and best-of-N samples. Correlate expert scores with automated rewards.
