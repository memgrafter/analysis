# Ontology-Guided Neuro-Symbolic Inference: Grounding Language Models with Mathematical Domain Knowledge

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.17826
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17826
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: Ontology-guided context improves LLM accuracy on the MATH benchmark only when retrieval quality is high; irrelevant OpenMath definitions actively degrade performance.

## Executive Summary
Language models often hallucinate and lack formal grounding in specialist domains such as mathematics. This paper investigates whether formal domain ontologies (OpenMath) can enhance reliability through retrieval-augmented generation. The authors build a neuro-symbolic pipeline that retrieves relevant mathematical definitions and injects them into prompts for open-source models (Gemma2-2B/9B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B) evaluated on the MATH benchmark. Results show that accuracy gains are conditional on retrieval quality: high-quality context improves performance, while noisy context harms it. Optimal threshold filtering (0.3–0.5) balances noise reduction and coverage, but only 24.2% of problems achieve high OpenMath coverage. The retrieval bottleneck is identified as the fundamental limitation, and efficiency trade-offs reveal both reduced attempts for mid-difficulty problems and paradoxical "false confidence" and "false exploration" effects.

## Method Summary
The pipeline consists of five phases. First, a knowledge base is constructed by parsing OpenMath Content Dictionaries and normalizing symbol descriptions via regex and a large language model (qwen2-math:7b), yielding 1,040 indexed symbols. Second, concepts relevant to each MATH problem are extracted using qwen2-math:7b. Third, hybrid retrieval combines BM25 (lexical) and dense embeddings (qwen3-embedding:4b) using Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) to produce a unified top-50 ranking. Fourth, a cross-encoder reranker (Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B) rescoring the candidates filters low-relevance symbols based on a score threshold. Fifth, the selected OpenMath definitions are appended to the problem prompt, and the model generates answers in either greedy (temperature 0.0) or best-of-n (temperature 0.6, up to 5 attempts) mode. Answer correctness is evaluated via exact string or SymPy match against the ground truth.

## Key Results
- Conditional accuracy: Qwen2.5-Math-7B shows consistent positive delta across thresholds; Gemma2-2B degrades in greedy mode but improves in best-of-n via context recovery. High retrieval quality is necessary for gains.
- Optimal threshold: Filtering with reranker scores between 0.3 and 0.5 maximizes accuracy by reducing noise while retaining sufficient coverage. Only 24.2% of MATH problems achieve high OpenMath coverage (score ≥0.5), with severe gaps in Geometry (14.6%) and Number Theory word problems.
- Retrieval bottleneck: The fundamental limitation is the quality of retrieved context; irrelevant definitions actively harm performance, making the first-stage retrieval the key to any improvement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The neuro-symbolic approach grounds the LLM’s reasoning in formally defined mathematical symbols retrieved from OpenMath. The hybrid retrieval and reranker ensure that only highly relevant definitions are presented, which reduces hallucination by providing precise semantic anchors. However, because only a minority of problems have high-coverage definitions, the system is inherently constrained by retrieval quality. When relevant context is included, the model can correctly apply definitions; when irrelevant context is injected, it introduces noise that misleads the model. Threshold filtering empirically balances this trade-off, confirming that the mechanism of improvement is conditional upon context relevance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**: Why needed: To supplement parametric knowledge with external, authoritative sources, reducing hallucinations. Quick check: How is retrieved content integrated? (Answer: Concatenated with the original query into the model prompt.)
- **OpenMath Content Dictionaries**: Why needed: Provide a structured ontology of mathematical symbols with precise semantics, examples, and formal descriptions. Quick check: What does CMP stand for and what is its role? (Answer: Content MathML, a formal semantic representation of a symbol’s meaning.)
- **Hybrid Retrieval (BM25 + Dense Embeddings)**: Why needed: Combines lexical exactness (BM25) with semantic similarity (embeddings) to capture both term matches and conceptual equivalence. Quick check: What is Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)? (Answer: A technique that merges rankings by summing the reciprocals of item ranks.)
- **Cross-Encoder Reranking**: Why needed: To more accurately assess query-document relevance through joint encoding, filtering out false positives from first-stage retrieval. Quick check: How does a cross-encoder differ from a bi-encoder? (Answer: Cross-encoder processes query and document together, allowing deep interaction; bi-encoder encodes separately for efficiency.)
- **Best-of-N Sampling**: Why needed: To explore multiple reasoning paths and increase the chance of finding a correct answer, especially when augmentation might help recover from errors. Quick check: What is the stopping condition in this work? (Answer: Up to 5 attempts; likely stops early if a correct answer is found.)
- **Threshold Filtering**: Why needed: To control the trade-off between context coverage and noise by discarding low-scoring reranker results. Quick check: What is the effect of setting the threshold too high? (Answer: Very few symbols are retained, potentially missing needed definitions and hurting accuracy.)

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Knowledge Base -> Concept Extraction -> Hybrid Retrieval (BM25 + Dense) -> RRF Fusion -> Cross-Encoder Reranking -> Threshold Filtering -> Augmented Prompt -> Inference (Model) -> Answer Extraction

Critical path: The pipeline's sequential dependencies are: Knowledge Base must be built before retrieval; Concept Extraction produces queries; Hybrid Retrieval relies on the knowledge base and queries; RRF merges rankings; Reranking requires candidates; Threshold Filtering uses reranker scores; Prompt construction and Model Inference depend on filtered symbols; Answer Extraction completes the loop. Any failure in an early stage propagates forward.

Design tradeoffs:
- BM25 is computationally cheap but limited to lexical overlap; dense embeddings capture semantics but require a separate model—hybrid mitigates both.
- RRF parameter k=60 was chosen without tuning; different k values could alter which symbols advance, affecting final selection.
- Cross-encoder reranking improves precision but adds latency; serving via vLLM helps but still a bottleneck.
- Threshold selection balances coverage and noise; the found sweet spot (0.3–0.5) likely varies with domain or model.
- Greedy inference is deterministic but may miss solutions; best-of-n explores alternatives at the cost of more compute.

Failure signatures:
- Low retrieval quality: average maximum reranker score substantially below 0.27 indicates poor symbol-problem matching.
- Accuracy degradation in augmented vs baseline: suggests noise from irrelevant context overwhelming benefits.
- High answer extraction failure (>20%): indicates model not following \boxed{} format; fallbacks needed.
- Coverage discrepancy: fewer than 24.2% of problems above threshold 0.5 points to issues in concept extraction or reranker calibration.
- Best-of-n attempts not decreasing for mid-difficulty problems: may reflect ineffective sampling or absence of recovery effects.

First experiments:
1. Validate retrieval quality by computing the distribution of max reranker scores across the MATH subset and checking that the mean matches the reported 0.2715.
2. Re-run accuracy evaluation with Qwen2.5-Math-7B across threshold values (0.0–0.9) to reproduce the U-shaped curve and identify the optimal threshold range (0.3–0.5).
3. Compare greedy and best-of-n modes for Gemma2-2B to confirm the reported degradation in greedy and improvement in best-of-n, and verify that average attempts decrease for mid-difficulty problems.

## Limitations
- Underspecified retrieval pipeline parameters (BM25 tokenization, RRF k, embedding/reranker versions) hinder exact replication.
- Evaluation setup lacks precise details (MATH500 split, best-of-n stopping logic, generation parameters beyond temperature).
- Answer extraction relies heavily on \boxed{} with limited fallbacks, risking data loss and skewed metrics.

## Confidence
- Conditional accuracy gains: Medium
- Coverage and threshold optimization: Low
- Retrieval bottleneck as fundamental limitation: High
- Efficiency trade-offs (false confidence/exploration): Low
