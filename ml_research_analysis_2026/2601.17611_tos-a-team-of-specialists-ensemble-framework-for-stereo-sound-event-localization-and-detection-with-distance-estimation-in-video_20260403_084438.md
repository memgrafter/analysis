# ToS: A Team of Specialists ensemble framework for Stereo Sound Event Localization and Detection with distance estimation in Video

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17611  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17611  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art on DCASE2025 Task 3 with 40.2% F1 score, 16.7° DOAE, and 29.6% RDE.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ToS (Team of Specialists), an ensemble framework for 3D sound event localization and detection (SELD) using stereo audio and video. It decomposes the joint spatio-temporal-linguistic reasoning task into three complementary sub-networks—spatio-linguistic, spatio-temporal, and tempo-linguistic—each applying a unique pooling strategy on shared visual embeddings to emphasize specific dimension pairs. A majority voting rule requiring agreement from at least two specialists fuses predictions, filtering false positives. Benchmarking on the DCASE2025 Task 3 development set shows significant gains over prior methods and individual specialists.

## Method Summary
The framework processes stereo log-mel spectrograms, inter-channel level difference (ILD), and video frames. A frozen OWL-ViT model extracts a per-frame spatial grid of visual embeddings. Three specialist networks share a ResNet18-based SELD encoder and Cross-Modal Conformer (CMC) fusion layers but differ in how they pool the visual grid: temporal average (spatio-linguistic), channel average (spatio-temporal), or spatial average (tempo-linguistic). Spatio-linguistic and tempo-linguistic specialists also incorporate frozen CLAP audio embeddings. Each specialist predicts a multi-ACCDDOA output per frame (azimuth, distance, on/off-screen). The ensemble requires at least two specialists to detect an event with direction-of-arrival within 20°; final direction and distance are averaged, and on-screen is accepted if any specialist predicts it.

## Key Results
- F1 score (F≤20°/1/on) of 40.2%, a 3.3-point gain over the best specialist and ~5-point gain over previous SOTA audio-visual model.
- Direction of arrival error (DOAE) of 16.7°.
- Relative distance error (RDE) of 29.6%.
- On/off-screen accuracy of 80.3%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Specialization via dimension-pair-focused pooling**  
   - *Claim:* Each specialist’s unique pooling on shared visual embeddings yields complementary error profiles.  
   - *Mechanism:* Spatio-linguistic uses temporal average pooling (preserves space+semantics), spatio-temporal uses channel average (preserves space+time), tempo-linguistic uses spatial average (preserves time+semantics).  
   - *Core assumption:* Isolating dimension pairs creates non-redundant error patterns.  
   - *Evidence anchors:* [abstract], [section 2.1-2.3] describe pooling strategies; neighbor works on cross-domain fusion support integration importance.  
   - *Break condition:* If specialists converge to similar errors despite different pooling, complementarity fails.

2. **Majority voting with spatial thresholds filters false positives**  
   - *Claim:* Requiring ≥2 specialists to agree within 20° DOA improves precision by rejecting independent errors.  
   - *Mechanism:* An event is detected only if multiple specialists predict it with consistent direction.  
   - *Core assumption:* True events are more consistently detected than false positives across specialists.  
   - *Evidence anchors:* [abstract], [section 2.4] define voting; [section 3.3] pairwise ensembles with looser rule underperform.  
   - *Break condition:* Highly correlated specialist errors would make voting ineffective or harmful.

3. **Frozen pre-trained language-aligned encoders preserve transferable knowledge**  
   - *Claim:* Freezing OWL-ViT and CLAP maintains semantically rich embeddings while task-specific layers learn alignment.  
   - *Mechanism:* Frozen encoders provide stable, high-quality visual/audio semantics; trainable SELD encoder and CMC adapt to spatial/temporal labels.  
   - *Core assumption:* Pre-trained knowledge is generally applicable; distortions would harm performance.  
   - *Evidence anchors:* [section 2.1], [section 3.2] confirm frozen weights; gains over other frozen-encoder models support.  
   - *Break condition:* If SELD requires novel semantics absent from pre-training, frozen encoders limit all specialists.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision-Language Models (e.g., OWL-ViT)**  
  - *Why needed:* Provides spatially fine-grained, semantically aligned visual embeddings; pooling operates directly on its grid output.  
  - *Quick check:* If OWL-ViT outputs a grid of patch embeddings, what does each embedding represent, and how does averaging across rows/columns/sequence alter spatial or semantic content?

- **Audio-Language Models (e.g., CLAP)**  
  - *Why needed:* Supplies a semantically aligned audio embedding space that bridges sound events to natural language for linguistic specialists.  
  - *Quick check:* How does a contrastive language-audio pretraining objective ensure that an embedding of a "dog bark" is close to the text embedding for "dog bark"?

- **Multi-ACCDDOA Representation**  
  - *Why needed:* The output format for up to N overlapping sound sources, containing class, azimuth, distance, and on/off-screen flags per frame.  
  - *Quick check:* What does a multi-ACCDDOA vector contain for a single active source at 45° azimuth, 2m distance, on-screen? How does it differ for two simultaneous sources?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Raw inputs (stereo log-mel, ILD, video) -> Frozen OWL-ViT (per-frame spatial grid) -> Specialist-specific pooling (temporal/channel/spatial average) -> Cross-Modal Conformer (CMC) fusion with audio branch (ResNet18 SELD encoder + optional CLAP) -> Linear head -> per-specialist multi-ACCDDOA prediction -> Ensemble module (majority voting + DOA threshold + averaging) -> Final SELD output.

- **Critical path:**  
  Visual grid extraction -> Dimension-pair pooling -> Audio-visual fusion in CMC -> Temporal modeling via Conformer -> Frame-level prediction -> Voting-based fusion.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Computation: ToS triples inference cost vs. single model (acceptable for research; distillation needed for deployment).  
  - Information loss: Each pooling discards one dimension; success depends on compensation by other specialists.  
  - Threshold selection: Fixed 20° DOA threshold in voting; may need dataset-specific tuning.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *Dominant specialist:* One specialist always correct in ensemble → poor specialization.  
  - *Voting failure:* Too many true events rejected (low recall) or false positives accepted (low precision) → wrong threshold/calibration.  
  - *Pooling catastrophe:* Specialist’s pooling destroys task-relevant cues → pairwise ensemble including it degrades performance.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Train/evaluate each specialist in isolation; expect reasonable but not SOTA performance with dimension-specific weaknesses.  
  2. Build all three pairwise ensembles with "either predicts" rule; expect improved recall but lower precision vs. individuals; check DOAE/RDE.  
  3. Implement full ToS with ≥2 voting and 20° DOA threshold; verify it exceeds best specialist by >3 F1 points and improves DOAE/RDE; compute proportion of predictions from exactly 2 vs. 3 specialists.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the individual specialists be strengthened through tailored tasks, training data, or augmentation strategies?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The abstract states future work will extend the proof of concept "by strengthening the specialists with appropriate tasks, training, and pre-training curricula."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper presents ToS as a proof-of-concept ensemble but does not explore specific training enhancements or auxiliary tasks for each specialist, leaving the optimal way to improve each sub-network unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablation studies varying data augmentation, multitask objectives, or loss functions for each specialist, measuring individual and ensemble performance on validation sets.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What pre-training curricula best exploit the complementary strengths of the three specialists?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion notes "It would be interesting to investigate specialist pre-training curricula to fully exploit their complementarity."
- **Why unresolved:** Specialists are trained from scratch on the same dataset without staged or modality-specific pre-training; the potential for curricula that enhance dimension-specific reasoning (space, time, semantics) is unexamined.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments with staged pre-training (e.g., self-supervised learning on large audio-visual corpora) for each specialist, evaluating convergence speed, generalization, and final metric gains.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Would fine-tuning the frozen CLAP and OWL-ViT encoders improve specialist performance, or does it risk overfitting and loss of generalization?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper freezes CLAP and OWL-ViT weights (Sections 2.1-2.3) without analysis, implicitly assuming frozen features are optimal for the SELD task.
- **Why unresolved:** Fine-tuning could adapt these encoders to the SELD domain but may degrade robustness; this trade-off is not investigated, limiting understanding of feature reuse.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative experiments with frozen vs. fine-tuned encoders for each specialist, using validation performance, robustness to acoustic diversity, and computational cost metrics.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does the Tempo-Linguistic Specialist’s omission of explicit spatial audio features (relying only on ILD embedded in spectrograms) limit its spatial accuracy, and would integrating direct spatial cues (e.g., GCC-PHAT) improve its DOA estimation?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The Tempo-Linguistic Specialist excludes dedicated spatial features from its SELD encoder input (Section 2.3), acknowledging implicit directional cues; it achieves the highest DOAE among specialists (Table 1).
- **Why unresolved:** The design trade-off between temporal-semantic focus and spatial precision is not quantified; adding explicit spatial features might narrow the performance gap but could distract from temporal-semantic goals.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation study augmenting Tempo-Linguistic’s audio input with explicit spatial features, measuring changes in DOAE, F1 score, and whether its specialization profile shifts.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How sensitive is the ToS ensemble’s performance to the voting threshold parameters (20° DOA convergence threshold and majority vote rule), and are these thresholds optimal for varying event densities or acoustic conditions?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The ensemble uses fixed thresholds (20° angular, majority vote) without sensitivity analysis (Section 2.4), assuming these values are generally effective.
- **Why unresolved:** Thresholds are chosen empirically; their impact on precision-recall balance, false positives, and robustness to challenging scenes (e.g., overlapping events) is unknown, potentially limiting applicability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Parameter sweep experiments varying the DOA threshold and voting rule (e.g., 2-of-3 vs. strict consensus), plotting performance metrics across diverse test conditions to identify optimal and robust settings.

## Limitations
- Unknown exact mapping of the 156-dimensional multi-ACCDDOA output to class/azimuth/distance/on-screen flags hinders faithful reproduction.
- Cross-Modal Conformer hyperparameters (e.g., feed-forward size, dropout placement) are not fully specified.
- Fixed 20° DOA voting threshold lacks tuning or sensitivity analysis; optimal values may vary with scene density.
- Frozen CLAP and OWL-ViT encoders may not generalize to unseen sound sources or domains.

## Confidence
- *Pooling specialization yields complementary errors:* Medium (pairwise ensembles improve, but error correlation unmeasured).  
- *Majority voting filters false positives:* Medium (ablation shows benefit, but threshold arbitrary).  
- *Frozen encoders preserve transferable knowledge:* High (standard practice; gains support).  

## Next Checks
