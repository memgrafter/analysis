# Stabilizing Rubric Integration Training via Decoupled Advantage Normalization
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.26535
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26535
- Reference count: 37
- Primary result: Process-Aware Policy Optimization (PAPO) achieves 51.3% accuracy on OlympiadBench with Qwen2.5-7B, compared to 46.3% for outcome reward models (ORM), while reducing the zero-advantage ratio from 69% to 44%.

## Executive Summary
Outcome reward models (ORM) provide a sparse correctness signal but lack reasoning quality feedback and suffer from signal exhaustion when all sampled responses become correct. Process reward models (PRM) offer fine-grained evaluation but induce reward hacking, causing verbosity inflation and eventual collapse. PAPO introduces a decoupled advantage computation that isolates the outcome and process signals. It computes an outcome advantage normalized over all responses to anchor correctness, and a process advantage normalized only among correct responses to differentiate reasoning quality. This design prevents PRM from incentivizing verbosity while sustaining a dense learning signal. On challenging math benchmarks, PAPO significantly outperforms ORM baselines and maintains improvement where ORM plateaus, without exploding response lengths.

## Method Summary
PAPO modifies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) by splitting the advantage into two components. For each prompt, eight responses are sampled. A binary outcome reward \(r_{out}\) indicates answer correctness. A process reward \(r_{proc}\) from a rubric-based PRM (GPT-OSS-20B) scores reasoning quality, but is set to zero for incorrect answers. The outcome advantage \(A_{out}\) is computed by normalizing \(r_{out}\) over all eight responses (full-group). The process advantage \(A_{proc}\) is computed only over the subset of correct responses \(C\) (if \(|C|\ge 2\); otherwise zero) by normalizing \(r_{proc}\) with the mean and standard deviation of \(r_{proc}\) over \(C\). The total advantage is \(A_{total}=A_{out}+A_{proc}\) and is fed into the GRPO objective. Training uses the NuminaMath-1.5-RL-Verifiable dataset (20k problems stratified by pass rate) and evaluates on OlympiadBench, MATH-500, AIME, GPQA-Diamond, and HumanEval. Decoupling prevents verbosity reward hacking (since \(A_{proc}=0\) for incorrect responses) and mitigates signal exhaustion (because \(A_{proc}\) can vary among uniformly correct groups).

## Key Results
- OlympiadBench accuracy: PAPO 51.3% vs ORM 46.3% (Qwen2.5-7B), with the gap widening after ~750 training steps.
- Zero-advantage ratio reduced from 69% (ORM) to 44% (PAPO), indicating more samples contribute informative gradients.
- Response length remains moderate and does not explode, confirming effective mitigation of PRM-induced reward hacking.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Claim: Decoupling advantage normalization prevents PRM-induced reward hacking by isolating the process signal’s influence to correct responses only.
Mechanism: A_proc is computed exclusively over the subset of responses that are correct (r_out = 1). This ensures incorrect responses (r_out = 0) receive A_proc = 0, preventing them from gaining positive advantage via inflated verbosity scores from the PRM. The outcome component A_out, normalized over all responses, independently anchors the gradient on final correctness.
Core assumption: The PRM’s bias toward verbosity is primarily an issue when its score directly influences advantage for incorrect answers. By restricting its normalization scope, this bias cannot corrupt the outcome signal.
Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Aproc normalized exclusively among correct responses... prevents PRM from incentivizing verbosity."; [section 4.2 & 4.3] The definition of A_proc: "normalized only among correct responses" and "incorrect responses are not rewarded solely for strong reasoning traces."; [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., GDPO) explore multi-reward decoupling but do not directly evidence this specific correct-subset normalization design; the paper’s own ablation (Fullnorm) provides primary evidence.
Break condition: If the PRM’s scoring rubric itself becomes biased to penalize concise but correct reasoning, A_proc could suppress desirable efficiency even within correct responses. The current 3-tier rubric is assumed to assess reasoning quality independently of length.

### Mechanism 2
Claim: Correct-subset normalization maintains gradient signal density longer than standard ORM by providing non-zero advantages in uniformly correct groups.
Mechanism: When all responses in a group are correct, A_out = 0 due to zero standard deviation in full-group normalization. However, A_proc continues to differentiate among these correct responses based on rubric scores, as its normalization is over the correct subset (which may still have score variance). This yields a non-zero total advantage for at least some responses, sustaining learning.
Core assumption: As training progresses, the proportion of uniformly correct response groups increases. The process signal’s contribution is expected to grow correspondingly, as evidenced by the rising "process signal active ratio" (Fig 9b).
Evidence anchors: [section 3.1] Describes signal exhaustion: "when all G responses... are correct... advantage is set to zero."; [section 4.3] "Aproc remains active in this case, differentiating responses... and providing non-zero gradients."; [section 5.3 & Fig 4a] Empirical evidence: zero-advantage ratio reduced from 69% (ORM) to 44% (PAPO).
Break condition: If the model quickly achieves uniformly high rubric scores across all correct responses (σ_proc_C → 0), the process signal would also exhaust. This is mitigated by the rubric’s inherent discriminative power (0, 0.5, 1.0) and the persistence of reasoning flaws.

### Mechanism 3
Claim: The process component actively penalizes poor reasoning within correct responses, not just rewarding good reasoning.
Mechanism: Because A_proc is normalized to zero mean and unit variance within the correct subset, responses scoring below the mean receive a negative advantage. This creates a direct pressure against "lucky" or "sloppy" correct answers, which receive identical advantage to rigorous ones under ORM.
Core assumption: The rubric-based PRM reliably distinguishes reasoning quality (e.g., fatal errors vs. minor omissions) independently of answer correctness, as its scoring rules are designed to do.
Evidence anchors: [section 4.3] "correct responses with poor derivations receive negative advantage and are suppressed."; [Appendix B.3 & Fig 8a] Shows min advantage among correct responses is ≈ -1.49 under PAPO vs. 0.0 under ORM.; [section 6] Ablation Fullnorm underperforms PAPO, suggesting that penalizing incorrect responses with A_proc (which would be negative) disrupts the clean separation provided by A_out.
Break condition: If the PRM’s scoring becomes inconsistent or uncorrelated with human-judged reasoning quality, the penalization signal becomes noise. The paper assumes GPT-OSS-20B provides a stable, high-quality rubric evaluation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**
  - *Why needed here:* PAPO directly modifies GRPO’s advantage computation. Understanding the baseline—group-level normalization over sampled responses for a prompt—is essential to see what PAPO decouples.
  - *Quick check question:* What happens to the advantage when all G sampled responses for a prompt have the same reward under standard GRPO?
- **Concept: Outcome vs. Process Reward Models**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire method hinges on distinguishing these two reward types and their complementary failure modes (signal exhaustion vs. reward hacking).
  - *Quick check question:* Why does a binary ORM fail to differentiate between a lucky guess and a rigorous proof, and why does a continuous PRM score encourage verbosity?
- **Concept: Reward Hacking in RL**
  - *Why needed here:* The core motivation is to avoid the "three-phase collapse" (section 3.2) that occurs when PRM is naively integrated.
  - *Quick check question:* Describe the positive feedback loop that leads from verbosity to PRM score inflation to accuracy collapse.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Prompt x → Sample G responses → Compute r_out (binary) and r_proc (rubric; 0 for incorrect) → Compute A_out (full-group normalization) and A_proc (normalization over correct subset C if |C|≥2 else 0) → Sum A_total → Input to GRPO loss.
Critical path: The logic diverges at the normalization scopes: full-group for A_out, correct-subset for A_proc; the summation assumes both are normalized to comparable scales.
Design tradeoffs:
- *Correct-subset vs. full normalization for A_proc:* Correct-subset prevents incorrect responses from receiving process-based gradients (avoiding hacking) but requires a sufficient number of correct responses per group (|C|≥2) to compute variance.
- *No learned weighting:* The method assumes independent normalization makes the scales comparable, avoiding a heuristic weight hyperparameter.
Failure signatures:
- *Reward hacking resurgence:* If A_proc is inadvertently normalized over all responses (Fullnorm), verbosity may explode again. Monitor response length and training reward.
- *Signal exhaustion in A_proc:* If correct responses all receive identical rubric scores (σ_proc^C→0), A_proc vanishes, mimicking ORM within correct groups.
- *Gradient instability:* When |C|=1, A_proc defaults to 0, which degrades to standard GRPO; this may be problematic if process signal is most needed for rare correct answers.
First 3 experiments:
1. **Zero-advantage ratio curve:** Plot fraction of samples with A_total=0 during training. Expect PAPO ~44% vs ORM ~69% on Qwen2.5-7B.
2. **Response length monitoring:** Track average response length. PRM-only should grow explosively; PAPO should remain comparable to ORM (moderate growth from genuine reasoning).
3. **Ablation: Correct-subset vs Full normalization:** Implement Fullnorm variant. Expect consistent underperformance on benchmarks (e.g., OlympiadBench gap ~1-2 points), confirming importance of restricting A_proc to correct responses.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Dynamic weighting:** How can we dynamically weight outcome and process advantages in PAPO to adapt to different training stages or task requirements? Basis: explicit; yet unresolved because current sum uses equal contribution. Needed evidence: experiments with adaptive weighting mechanisms compared to fixed PAPO.
2. **Domain transfer:** Does decoupled advantage normalization transfer effectively to non-mathematical reasoning domains (e.g., scientific reasoning, general QA)? Basis: explicit extension mentioned; unresolved due to evaluation only on math benchmarks. Needed evidence: application to diverse benchmarks like SciQ, ARC, coding tasks with appropriate process rubrics.
3. **Judge model sensitivity:** How sensitive is PAPO's performance to the choice of rubric-based PRM judge model, and can biases in different judges affect training stability? Basis: limitations note use of only GPT-OSS-20B; effect of different judges unexplored. Needed evidence: replicate with multiple judge models (GPT-4, Claude 3, Llama 3) and analyze variance in accuracy, reward distribution, verbosity.
4. **Architecture transfer:** Is PAPO compatible with model architectures beyond Qwen (e.g., Llama, Gemma)? Basis: limitations state experiments only on Qwen families; transferability unverified. Needed evidence: implement PAPO on non-Qwen models and verify similar gains on math benchmarks.

## Limitations
- Exact stratification method (reference model for pass rates) is unspecified, potentially altering training difficulty distribution.
- PRM implementation details (system prompt, few-shot setup) are not fully described, risking scoring inconsistencies.
- For prompts with very few correct responses (|C| < 2), A_proc defaults to zero, weakening the process signal precisely where it might be most needed.

## Confidence
- **Verbosity Prevention via Decoupling:** Medium (supported by Fullnorm ablation but hinges on rubric’s length-agnosticism)
- **Sustained Gradient Signal:** High (demonstrated by reduced zero-advantage ratio and widening accuracy gap)
- **Penalizing Poor Reasoning:** Medium (empirically shown via advantage distributions but assumes rubric quality)

## Next Checks
1. **Stratification Sensitivity Test:** Re-run PAPO training using alternative reference models (e.g., a smaller base) for pass-rate stratification and measure variance in final OlympiadBench accuracy and active ratio.
2. **PRM Rubric Audit:** Conduct human evaluation on a sample of GPT-OSS-20B’s rubric scores to quantify inter-rater reliability and check for length/verbosity bias; also test robustness to minor prompt changes.
3. **Low-Correct-Group Robustness:** Analyze PAPO’s performance on training prompts where |C| < 2 for most steps; compare accuracy and A_proc activity to prompts with consistently high |C|.
