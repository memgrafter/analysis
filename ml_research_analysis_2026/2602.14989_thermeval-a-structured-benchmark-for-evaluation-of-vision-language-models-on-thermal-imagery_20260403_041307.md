# ThermEval: A Structured Benchmark for Evaluation of Vision-Language Models on Thermal Imagery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14989  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14989  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Thermal understanding remains a distinct challenge for VLMs; supervised fine-tuning on thermal data yields significant improvements, with a small model matching or surpassing human performance and outperforming a 235B parameter model.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ThermEval, a structured benchmark designed to evaluate vision-language models on thermal imagery. The benchmark comprises ThermEval‑B (∼55 000 VQA pairs across seven tasks of increasing difficulty) and ThermEval‑D (1 000+ thermal images with dense per‑pixel temperature maps and body‑part annotations). Evaluation of 25 VLMs reveals systematic failures: models struggle with temperature‑grounded reasoning (accuracies 0.37–0.74 vs. human 0.84), degrade under colormap transformations, and often default to language priors (e.g., outputting 37°C). Temperature estimation errors exceed 3.5°C for large models, far above the human baseline (~2.7°C). Notably, supervised fine‑tuning of Qwen‑VL 2.5 8B on ThermEval‑D yields dramatic gains, matching or exceeding human performance on several tasks and surpassing a 235B model. The work demonstrates that dedicated thermal supervision—not mere scale—is critical for thermal understanding.

## Method Summary
ThermEval‑B assembles thermal visual question answering pairs from existing FLIR‑ADAS and LLVIP datasets plus newly collected ThermEval‑D. The seven tasks progress from simple modality identification (T1–T2) and human counting (T3) to colorbar interpretation (T4), thermal reasoning (T5), and absolute temperature estimation at varying distances (T6–T7). ThermEval‑D provides dense per‑pixel temperature maps and body‑part masks for fine‑tuning. Models are evaluated zero‑shot using fixed prompt templates; outputs are normalized via an LLM‑based parser (Gemini) validated on a gold set. Supervised fine‑tuning (LoRA) on ThermEval‑D’s training VQAs isolates the effect of thermal‑specific supervision.

## Key Results
- VLMs fail on thermal imagery despite strong RGB performance; temperature‑grounded reasoning accuracies range 0.37–0.74 versus human 0.84.
- Temperature estimation MAE exceeds 3.5°C for large models, while humans achieve ~2.7°C.
- Supervised fine‑tuning of Qwen‑VL 2.5 8B yields substantial gains, matching/surpassing human performance on several tasks and outperforming a 235B parameter model.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** VLMs fail on thermal imagery because their pretraining on RGB images does not teach them to map visual colors to physical temperature values.
- **Mechanism:** Thermal images represent temperature via false colormaps; RGB‑pretrained models interpret colors as semantic features rather than as encodings of a continuous physical quantity, so they cannot parse colorbars or reason about temperature magnitudes.
- **Core assumption:** The visual representation space of VLMs is optimized for object recognition in RGB, not for estimating physical quantities from pseudocolor representations.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 1, Section 5.5; models default to prior biases.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Performance degradation under colormap transformations reveals that models rely on specific color statistics instead of modality‑invariant temperature understanding.
- **Mechanism:** Different colormaps reassign colors to identical temperature distributions. Models that have not abstracted the underlying temperature map fail when the palette shifts because their learned features are tied to particular hues or textures.
- **Core assumption:** Models use low‑level color histograms or texture patterns as shortcuts for modality identification or temperature estimation, rather than learning the mapping between colormap and temperature.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 5.1, Tables 2 & 8 show accuracy drops on complex colormaps (e.g., Summer, Spring) versus sequential maps (e.g., Magma, Viridis).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** When visual grounding is insufficient, VLMs default to language priors such as canonical body temperatures (e.g., 37°C), ignoring the thermal evidence.
- **Mechanism:** The language component of VLMs generates highly probable responses based on training data; without strong visual signals to override these priors, the output becomes stereotypical numeric values.
- **Core assumption:** The visual‑to‑language grounding mechanism is weak for thermal signals, so the language prior dominates decision making.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 5.5 (MiniCPM and InternVL occasionally ignore thermal inputs), Section 5.7 (counterfactual evaluation shows predictions collapse to a small set of preferred values).

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Thermal Image Encoding
- **Why needed:** To understand why thermal images differ fundamentally from RGB and why standard CV models fail; thermal images encode temperature via false colormaps, not visible light reflectance.
- **Quick check:** What does each pixel in a raw thermal image represent before applying a colormap?

### Concept: Vision‑Language Model Fusion
- **Why needed:** To interpret how VLMs combine visual features and language; weak fusion leads to language prior dominance, a central failure mode in thermal reasoning.
- **Quick check:** In a typical VLM, how are image features integrated with the language model to produce answers?

### Concept: Evaluation by Prerequisite Tasks
- **Why needed:** ThermEval’s task hierarchy (T1–T7) diagnoses capability gaps by ordering tasks from simple to complex; failures in early tasks (e.g., colorbar interpretation) causally predict later failures.
- **Quick check:** Which task in ThermEval is a prerequisite for successful temperature estimation?

### Concept: Supervised Fine‑Tuning as Diagnostic
- **Why needed:** The dramatic gains from fine‑tuning demonstrate that pretraining lacks thermal grounding rather than model capacity; this insight directs future pretraining efforts.
- **Quick check:** What does the improvement from fine‑tuning tell us about the nature of the thermal understanding gap?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Thermal images (false-colored) → VLM (vision encoder + language model) → Raw text/numeric response → LLM parser (Gemini) → Normalized answer; Ground truth labels → Evaluation metrics; ThermEval‑D → Supervised fine‑tuning (LoRA) → Updated VLM.

### Critical Path
1. Acquire and verify datasets (FLIR, LLVIP, ThermEval‑D).  
2. Implement unified inference pipeline supporting multiple open‑ and closed‑source VLMs.  
3. Develop task‑specific prompt templates and answer‑parsing logic.  
4. Run zero‑shot evaluation across all tasks and aggregate results.  
5. Perform error analysis to identify failure patterns.  
6. *(Optional)* Fine‑tune a representative model (e.g., Qwen‑VL 2.5 8B) with LoRA.  
7. Compare fine‑tuned performance against zero‑shot and human baselines.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **False‑colored vs. raw thermal inputs:** False‑colored images work with existing VLMs but encode colormap‑specific biases; raw tensors would remove the confounder but require architectural changes.  
- **Zero‑shot vs. fine‑tuning:** Zero‑shot reveals out‑of‑the‑box generalization; fine‑testing tests domain adaptation but risks overfitting to the benchmark distribution.  
- **LLM parser vs. structured generation:** LLM parser is flexible and model‑agnostic but adds cost and potential parsing errors; structured output constraints are cleaner but not universally supported.  
- **Task granularity:** Finer‑grained tasks offer richer diagnostics but demand denser annotations and stricter evaluation.

### Failure Signatures
- Poor performance (<70% accuracy) on T4 (colorbar interpretation) strongly predicts failure on T5–T7.  
- High variance in T2 across colormaps indicates reliance on superficial color cues.  
- Repetitive numeric outputs (e.g., constant 37.0) across many images signal language prior collapse.  
- MAE >3.5°C on T6 even for large models; little improvement with scale.  
- Declining accuracy on T7 with increasing subject distance, reflecting sensitivity to thermal resolution.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline Zero‑Shot Replication:** Evaluate Qwen‑VL 2.5 8B (and optionally InternVL 14B) on all ThermEval‑B tasks using the published prompts and parsing pipeline; confirm that results match Table 2, 3, 4 within expected margins.  
2. **Prompt Ablation Study:** For the same models, compare standard prompts with the detailed contextual prompts from Appendix B.7; quantify the change in accuracy/MAE per task to separate modality confusion from deeper thermal reasoning deficits.  
3. **Counterfactual Temperature Perturbation:** Before fine‑tuning, run the perturbation experiment (add/subtract ±1, ±2, ±3°C to body‑part regions) to confirm prior bias. Then fine‑tune on ThermEval‑D and repeat; expect reduced bias and MAE improvements on T6/T7.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can VLMs be trained to perform human‑level thermal reasoning without relying on language priors?**  
   *Basis:* VLMs fail on thermal reasoning tasks, often outputting fixed values like 37°C. Unresolved because models lack grounding; a model achieving >0.84 accuracy without constant output would resolve it.

2. **How can VLMs maintain accurate thermal interpretation across varied colormap representations?**  
   *Basis:* Models degrade on complex colormaps (e.g., Summer, Spring) in Task T2, indicating dependence on color statistics. Robustness across colormaps is needed for deployment; evidence: >95% accuracy on all colormaps.

3. **What architectural changes are necessary to give VLMs intrinsic thermal domain grounding, beyond scaling and fine‑tuning?**  
   *Basis:* Authors state thermal understanding needs architectural innovation, not just parameter growth. Evidence: new pretraining objectives that incorporate raw thermal signals achieving high ThermEval scores with minimal adaptation.

## Limitations
- False‑colored images limit transfer to raw thermal data and non‑human domains.  
- LLM parser (Gemini) is a black box; its errors may bias standardization.  
- Closed‑source models lack transparency; API changes could affect results.

## Confidence
- **RGB‑pretraining gap:** Medium  
- **Colormap degradation:** Medium  
- **Language prior collapse:** High  
- **SFT gains & scale irrelevance:** High  

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑architecture SFT:** Fine‑tune at least two other VLMs (e.g., LLaVA, BLIP‑2) on ThermEval‑D; if gains hold, thermal supervision is generally effective.  
2. **Parser audit:** Manually label 1 000 outputs; compare to Gemini parser. If error >5%, re‑evaluate metrics with corrected gold standard.  
3. **Colormap‑invariant pretraining:** Pre‑train a VLM on thermal images with multiple colormaps; reduced sensitivity would confirm abstraction failure in current models.
