# Attention Frequency Modulation: Training-Free Spectral Modulation of Diffusion Cross-Attention
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.28114
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28114
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** Training-free Fourier-domain modulation of cross-attention logits, with entropy gating as adaptive gain, achieving strong perceptual edits while preserving alignment.

## Executive Summary
Latent diffusion models lack principled, training-free control over the multi-resolution dynamics of cross-attention. This paper introduces Attention Frequency Modulation (AFM), an inference-time intervention that edits token-wise pre-softmax cross-attention logits in the Fourier domain. AFM reweights low- and high-frequency bands according to a denoising-progress-aligned schedule, optionally modulated by token-allocation entropy. Experiments on Stable Diffusion v1.5 show that AFM reliably suppresses late-stage high-frequency fragmentation in encoder cross-attention (negative ∆ρ<sub>late</sub> in 98–99% of prompt-seed pairs) and induces substantial perceptual edits (paired LPIPS 0.237±0.138 without entropy, 0.409±0.131 with entropy) while largely preserving text-image alignment (CLIP cosine similarity distributions overlap baseline). Entropy primarily amplifies the frequency-based edit rather than acting as an independent control axis.

## Method Summary
AFM builds on a spectral characterization of diffusion cross-attention. The authors convert token-softmax attention weights into token-agnostic concentration maps (top-K mean) on the latent grid, then apply 2D FFT and radially bin the power. This yields a high-frequency ratio ρ<sub>s</sub> that monotonically increases over denoising steps—a stable coarse-to-fine fingerprint across prompts and seeds. The intervention targets the encoder’s cross-attention modules (attn2 in the downsampling path). For each token, the pre-softmax logits are reshaped into a 2D spatial map, FFT-transformed, and low/high radial frequency bands are scaled by α<sub>LF</sub> and α<sub>HF</sub>. These factors follow a schedule on normalized time u (e.g., α<sub>LF</sub> = 1 + λ(1−u), α<sub>HF</sub> = 1 + λu) and optionally depend on token-allocation entropy Ȟ<sub>tok</sub> (which multiplies λ). After inverse FFT and flattening, the modified logits are passed to the softmax. The method is training-free and applied during inference. Evaluation on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with DDIM (50 steps) uses ∆ρ<sub>late</sub> (last 20% steps), paired LPIPS, band-wise LPISP, and CLIP cosine similarity, comparing against SAG and FreeU.

## Key Results
- **Spectral suppression:** Encoder cross-attention shows negative ∆ρ<sub>late</sub> in 98–99% of prompt-seed pairs.
- **Perceptual edits:** Paired LPIPS = 0.237±0.138 (AFM-curve) and 0.409±0.131 (with entropy gating).
- **Alignment preserved:** CLIP cosine similarity distributions remain largely overlapping with baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Diffusion cross‑attention exhibits a stable coarse‑to‑fine spectral progression
- **Claim:** The spatial distribution of token competition in cross‑attention follows a predictable time‑frequency trajectory across denoising steps, consistent across prompts and seeds.
- **Mechanism:** Token‑agnostic concentration maps (top‑K mean of attention weights across tokens) are treated as spatial signals. After 2D FFT, radially binned power yields a high‑frequency ratio ρ<sub>s</sub> that increases monotonically from low to high radial frequencies over time.
- **Core assumption:** The top‑K concentration map is a valid proxy for token competition spatial structure; its spectral evolution is stable enough to schedule interventions.
- **Evidence:** Figures 2/3 and Section 3.2 show consistent trajectories across multiple prompts and seeds; the abstract states "yielding a stable time–frequency fingerprint."
- **Break condition:** If the coarse‑to‑fine trend is not monotonic or varies widely across models, samplers, or prompts, the fingerprint assumption fails.

### Mechanism 2: Pre‑softmax logit editing in Fourier domain changes token competition
- **Claim:** To alter the token competition pattern, one must modify the token‑wise pre‑softmax logits in a token‑dependent way; post‑softmax edits are invariant to constant shifts.
- **Mechanism:** For each token, reshape its logit vector to a 2D spatial map, apply FFT, reweight low/high bands, inverse FFT, and flatten back before softmax. This exploits that softmax row‑wise normalization is unaffected by constant offsets but responds to token‑dependent changes.
- **Core assumption:** Frequency‑based edits to the logit spatial map lead to intended changes in the post‑softmax attention distribution.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 3.3 derive the softmax invariance (Eq.13) and motivate logit‑space editing; experimental ∆ρ shifts confirm spectral effect.
- **Break condition:** If the softmax nonlinearity scrambles the edits or other U‑Net normalizations nullify effects, the intervention would be ineffective.

### Mechanism 3: Entropy gating acts as adaptive gain, not an independent axis
- **Claim:** Token‑allocation entropy primarily scales the amplitude of the frequency‑based edit rather than providing a separate control dimension.
- **Mechanism:** Entropy (mean token‑wise entropy across spatial queries) multiplies the α factors: α<sub>LF</sub> = 1 + λ(1−u)(1+β Ȟ<sub>tok</sub>), α<sub>HF</sub> = 1 + λu(1+γ(1− Ȟ<sub>tok</sub>)). Higher entropy increases the effective λ.
- **Core assumption:** Entropy correlates with contexts where stronger frequency modulation is beneficial or effective.
- **Evidence:** Entropy‑only (λ=0) yields zero effect (Table 2); enabling entropy increases LPIPS without proportionally harming CLIP similarity; the distributions remain overlapping baseline.
- **Break condition:** If entropy gating independently shifts CLIP scores or alters the spectral trajectory shape rather than amplitude, the adaptive gain hypothesis fails.

## Foundational Learning
### Fourier Analysis for Spatial Signals
- **Why needed:** AFM operates on 2D spatial maps derived from attention logits; understanding FFT, radial binning, and power spectra is essential to grasp how "frequency" is defined and manipulated.
- **Quick check:** If a spatial map has most of its FFT power in low radial frequencies, what does that imply about the map’s visual structure (e.g., smooth blobs vs. fine speckles)?

### Cross‑Attention in Latent Diffusion U‑Nets
- **Why needed:** AFM targets cross‑attention layers (attn2) that inject text conditions. Knowing the U‑Net structure (encoder/middle/decoder), the role of queries (from latents) and keys/values (from text), and the per‑step application is necessary to understand intervention scope and diagnostics.
- **Quick check:** Why is cross‑attention considered a "natural carrier of stage‑dependent signals" in diffusion models?

### Entropy as a Measure of Dispersion in Attention Distributions
- **Why needed:** Entropy quantifies how spread out or concentrated the token attention weights are across spatial locations for a given query (or averaged). It’s used as an optional gating signal in AFM.
- **Quick check:** Would a high mean token entropy across spatial queries indicate that text tokens are being assigned to many diverse spatial regions, or that a few tokens dominate large regions?

## Architecture Onboarding
#### Component Map
Stable Diffusion v1.5 U‑Net → Encoder (downsampling input_blocks) → Cross‑attention modules (attn2) → Instrumented hooks (capture L<sub>s</sub>, compute entropy, apply AFM, replace logits, logging)

#### Critical Path
For each denoising step *s* and each targeted attn2 head:
- Capture pre-softmax logits *L<sub>s</sub>* (shape HW×T).
- If entropy gating is enabled, compute post-softmax attention *A<sub>s</sub>* and mean token entropy Ȟ<sub>tok</sub>.
- Determine scaling factors α<sub>LF</sub> and α<sub>HF</sub> from the schedule (progress *u = s/(S‑1)*, possibly using Ȟ<sub>tok</sub>).
- For each token *j*:
  * Reshape *L<sub>s</sub>[:,j]* to 2D spatial map *Z<sub>s,j</sub>*.
  * Compute FFT, apply radial band masks (LF/HF), scale coefficients by α<sub>LF</sub>/α<sub>HF</sub> (preserving DC).
  * Inverse FFT to get *Z’<sub>s,j</sub>*, flatten to *L’<sub>s</sub>[:,j]*.
- Reassemble *L’<sub>s</sub>*, apply softmax to obtain *A’<sub>s</sub>*, continue forward pass.

#### Design Tradeoffs
- **Target block:** Encoder cross‑attention chosen for stable spectral trajectory; middle/decoder show downstream effects but weaker consistency.
- **Schedule design:** Curve schedule (α<sub>LF</sub> = 1 + λ(1−u), α<sub>HF</sub> = 1 + λu) aligns LF boost early, HF boost late; must be monotonic in *u*.
- **Entropy gating:** Parameterized by (β,γ); modulates amplitude of frequency schedule multiplicatively; requires tuning per model/sampler.
- **Mask sharpness:** Hard radial mask vs. smooth transition; smooth masks reduce ringing artifacts.

#### Failure Signatures
- **No change:** LPIPS ≈ 0, ∆ρ<sub>late</sub> ≈ 0. Likely due to editing post‑softmax, incorrect *u*, wrong block, or mask error.
- **Artifacts (ringing, blur):** Caused by harsh binary mask, excessive λ, or forgetting to preserve DC.
- **Inconsistent spectral shift:** Encoder ∆ρ<sub>late</sub> not negative in ≥98% pairs; may indicate instability for that model/sampler.
- **Misinterpretation:** Treating ρ<sub>s</sub> (attention‑level HF ratio) as direct image frequency content.

#### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline fingerprint verification:** On SD v1.5/DDIM (50 steps), record encoder cross‑attention ρ<sub>s</sub> for multiple prompts/seeds. Confirm monotonic increase and low variance.
2. **AFM‑curve spectral intervention test:** Apply AFM‑curve (λ=0.2, entropy off) only to encoder. Compute ∆ρ<sub>s</sub>; expect negative ∆ρ<sub>late</sub> (last 20% steps) in ≥98% of pairs. Check downstream middle/decoder shifts.
3. **Entropy gating validation:** Compare AFM‑curve (entropy off) vs. AFM‑curve+entropy (β=20, γ=4). Verify (a) LPIPS increases (~0.24 vs ~0.41), (b) CLIP cosine distributions overlap baseline, (c) entropy‑only (λ=0) yields zero LPIPS.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: How do cross‑attention spectral metrics correlate with direct image Fourier power?
- **Basis:** Attention‑derived concentration maps are proxies, not direct measures of image Fourier content.
- **Why unresolved:** Empirical correlation between attention metrics and image‑level spectra remains unmeasured.
- **What would resolve it:** For paired baseline/AFM images, compute per‑image FFT and regress against per‑step attention metrics (ρ<sub>s</sub>) while controlling for prompts/seeds.

### Open Question 2: Is the coarse‑to‑fine spectral progression universal beyond latent diffusion?
- **Basis:** Analysis limited to Stable Diffusion v1.4/v1.5; generality to other architectures (pixel‑diffusion, DiT) not evaluated.
- **Why unresolved:** Different architectures may have distinct attention dynamics, limiting AFM’s applicability.
- **What would resolve it:** Replicate the Fourier‑based attention analysis on diverse models (e.g., DALL‑E 2, Imagen, SD3) and compare trajectories.

### Open Question 3: Why does entropy act as adaptive gain rather than independent control?
- **Basis:** Authors state entropy mainly amplifies the frequency‑based edit but do not explain the underlying mechanism.
- **Why unresolved:** Theoretical understanding is lacking; this hinders designing better gating strategies.
- **What would resolve it:** Analytical study of entropy’s effect on logit distributions under synthetic frequency masks; derive relationships between entropy, softmax, and band reweighting.

## Limitations
- **Prompt and layer details undisclosed:** Exact prompts and precise selection of encoder cross‑attention layers not specified, which may affect reproducibility of reported metrics.
- **Generality untested:** The stable coarse‑to‑fine fingerprint and AFM effectiveness are only validated on SD1.5/DDIM; other samplers (e.g., DPM‑Solver) or newer architectures (SDXL, DiT) may behave differently.
- **Spectral‑image link heuristic:** AFM uses attention‑level frequency measures as proxies; the quantitative relationship to actual image Fourier content is not established.

## Confidence
- **Stable coarse‑to‑fine spectral progression:** *Medium*. Evidence strong for tested setup but risk of failure on other models/samplers.
- **Pre‑softmax logit Fourier editing effectiveness:** *High*. Supported by theory (softmax invariance) and empirical ∆ρ shifts/perceptual changes.
- **Entropy as adaptive gain:** *Medium*. Entropy‑only no‑op and LPIPS amplification are persuasive, but orthogonality tests are limited; could interact non‑multiplicatively.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑architecture spectral fingerprinting:** Replicate the coarse‑to‑fine trajectory on SDXL and a non‑Stable Diffusion model (e.g., DALL‑E 2). Divergence would invalidate Mechanism 1’s generality.
2. **Logit vs. weight editing ablation:** Directly compare AFM on pre‑softmax logits versus post‑softmax attention weights (with matched scaling). Only logit editing should yield the intended spectral shift.
3. **Entropy gating orthogonality test:** Run AFM with a fixed α schedule (no entropy) and then manually scale λ by (1 + β H̄<sub>tok</sub>) using recorded baseline entropy. Matching results of endogenous entropy gating would confirm the adaptive gain hypothesis.
