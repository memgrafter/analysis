# Causal Prosody Mediation for Text-to-Speech: Counterfactual Training of Duration, Pitch, and Energy in FastSpeech2

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11683  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11683  
- **Reference count:** 24  
- **Primary result:** Causal prosody mediation with counterfactual training achieves superior emotional TTS: MOS 4.45, emotion accuracy 94%, WER 3.1%, content consistency 0.96.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a causal prosody mediation framework for emotional text-to-speech (TTS) that models prosody (duration, pitch, energy) as the mediator through which emotion influences speech. Extending FastSpeech2, the method incorporates emotion conditioning and two counterfactual training losses: an Indirect Path Constraint to eliminate direct emotional effects on the acoustic spectrum, and a Counterfactual Prosody Constraint to enforce distinct prosody per emotion while preserving linguistic content. Trained on multi-speaker emotional corpora, the model outperforms strong baselines in naturalness, emotion expressiveness, intelligibility, and content consistency. Ablations confirm the necessity of both losses for disentangling content and emotion.

## Method Summary
The approach builds on FastSpeech2 by adding emotion embeddings that condition the variance adaptor (duration, pitch, energy predictors) but are excluded from the mel decoder to enforce a causal graph where emotion only affects speech via prosody. Two auxiliary losses are introduced during fine-tuning on emotional data: (1) Indirect Path Constraint (IPC) compares the original speech with a counterfactual where emotion is swapped but prosody is held fixed, penalizing any spectral difference; (2) Counterfactual Prosody Constraint (CPC) generates a full counterfactual (swap emotion and re-predict prosody) and combines a content preservation term (low WER) with an emotion classification loss to push prosody toward the target emotion. Training proceeds in two stages: pre-training on neutral corpora (LibriTTS, VCTK) with base losses, then fine-tuning on EmoV-DB with the full objective.

## Key Results
- Mean opinion score (naturalness): 4.45 vs 4.21 for baseline.
- Emotion classification accuracy: 94% vs 80%.
- Word error rate: 3.1% vs 4.0%.
- Content consistency score: 0.96.
- Ablations show both IPC and CPC are necessary for content preservation and emotion expressiveness respectively.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Emotion influences speech exclusively through intermediate prosodic features (duration, pitch, energy), not via direct spectral or timbral alterations.
- **Mechanism:** The paper posits a Structural Causal Model (SCM) where emotion (E) is a parent node of prosody (M), which in turn is a parent of the speech output (Y). This creates an indirect path E → M → Y, with the dashed direct path E → Y intended to be zero.
- **Core assumption:** All emotion-relevant information for synthesis is captured by the three prosody variables; any residual direct effect would indicate model entanglement.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "models prosody ... as the mediator through which emotion influences speech" and "Indirect Path Constraint (IPC) to eliminate direct emotion effects on the spectrum".
  - [section 3] "We hypothesize that emotion influences speech primarily by modulating prosody" and formal graph in Figure 1.
  - [corpus] Weak support: neighbor papers focus on prosody modeling but not causal mediation; no direct corpus evidence for full mediation claim.
- **Break condition:** If ablation shows that removing IPC reduces content consistency (CCS) or speaker similarity, it signals the model was using a direct E→Y pathway (e.g., by altering decoder activations with emotion embedding).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Indirect Path Constraint (IPC) loss enforces that changing emotion while holding prosody constant does not change the generated mel-spectrogram.
- **Mechanism:** During training, for a sample (X, S, E, Y), the model runs a counterfactual forward pass with emotion E' ≠ E but using the original ground-truth or predicted prosody features. IPC loss is the L1 distance between this counterfactual output and the original output. Minimizing it forces the decoder to ignore any residual emotion signal not carried by prosody.
- **Core assumption:** The prosody features (duration, pitch, energy) are sufficient to lock the acoustic output; the decoder does not require any other emotion-dependent information.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "Indirect Path Constraint (IPC) to eliminate direct emotion effects on the spectrum".
  - [section 4.2] Detailed derivation: "We feed the model with this E' only in the decoder, while keeping the prosody fixed... Any difference... can be attributed to the direct influence."
  - [corpus] No direct corpus validation; relies on experimental ablations.
- **Break condition:** If IPC loss weight β_ipc is set to zero, the model may start to use direct emotion information in the decoder (e.g., via skipped connections or latent emotion trails), degrading content consistency when emotions are switched.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The Counterfactual Prosody Constraint (CPC) ensures that when emotion changes, the predicted prosody changes in an emotion-distinct way while preserving linguistic content.
- **Mechanism:** CPC combines (a) a content consistency term (1 - WER between original and counterfactual outputs) to lock lexical content, and (b) an emotion classification loss on the counterfactual output to push it toward the target emotion label. This forces the prosody predictors to output emotion-specific patterns.
- **Core assumption:** An external emotion classifier can serve as a proxy for human-perceived emotional expressiveness; prosody patterns are the primary lever to achieve this.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "Counterfactual Prosody Constraint (CPC) to maximize distinct prosody for different emotions while keeping content identical."
  - [section 4.3] "L_CPC = ... L_content(Y, Ŷ_cf) + λ_emo · L_emo-cls(Ŷ_cf, E')" and explanation that content must stay fixed, so only prosody can vary to satisfy the classifier.
  - [corpus] Limited: evaluation uses a pretrained emotion classifier (not described in corpus signals); no external corpus validation of CPC's design.
- **Break condition:** If CPC is removed, emotion classification accuracy drops sharply (as shown in ablation), and subjective DMOS for emotion similarity falls, indicating prosody is not sufficiently differentiated by emotion.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Structural Causal Models (SCMs) in ML**  
  - Why needed here: Provides formal framework to encode the assumption that emotion affects speech only via prosody, enabling derivation of IPC and CPC losses.  
  - Quick check question: What does it mean for a direct path E → Y to be zero in the graph, and how would you test for it empirically?
- **Concept: Counterfactual reasoning for training**  
  - Why needed here: Allows simulation of "what-if" scenarios (same text/speaker, different emotion) without needing real paired data, which is crucial for enforcing mediation.  
  - Quick check question: How is the counterfactual in IPC different from that in CPC in terms of which variables are held fixed?
- **Concept: FastSpeech2 variance adaptor**  
  - Why needed here: The model builds on FS2's explicit duration/pitch/energy predictors; understanding their role is key to seeing how emotion conditioning is injected and how prosody is manipulated.  
  - Quick check question: In FS2, where are the prosody features (duration, pitch, energy) actually used in the architecture?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (X, S, E) → Encoder (adds S, E) → H → Variance Adaptor (conditions on E) → M (duration, pitch, energy) → Length Regulator (expands H with M) → H_expanded → Decoder (adds S only) → mel-spectrogram Y  

**Critical path:** The emotion signal must route only through the variance adaptor's predictions; the decoder explicitly excludes emotion to prevent direct effects.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Excluding emotion from decoder improves causal compliance but may limit expressiveness if prosody features are insufficient.  
- Counterfactual passes double compute; mitigated by sharing encoder states where possible.  
- Pre-training on neutral data (LibriTTS/VCTK) then fine-tuning on emotional (EmoV-DB) prevents overfitting to small emotional corpus.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High WER and low CCS indicate content isn't preserved when emotions change (CPC failure).  
- Low emotion accuracy (<85%) but good naturalness suggests prosody isn't distinct enough (CPC under-pressure).  
- Drop in speaker similarity when transferring emotion indicates speaker timbre entangled with emotion (IPC failure or speaker embedding weak).  
- Training instability when adding causal losses – monitor L_IPC and L_CPC values and use gradient clipping.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Reproduce baseline FastSpeech2 on LibriTTS+VCTK (neutral) to verify basic multi-speaker synthesis quality (WER ~3.5%, MOS >4.0).  
2. Add naive emotion conditioning to FS2 (feed emotion to encoder, variance adaptor, and decoder) without IPC/CPC. Expect emotion accuracy ~80% but possible speaker/content degradation.  
3. Incremental loss addition: from naive emotion model, first add IPC loss only – monitor CCS and speaker similarity; then add CPC loss only – monitor emotion accuracy and DMOS; finally combine both.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the causal prosody mediation framework be extended to include acoustic features beyond duration, pitch, and energy (e.g., voice quality, spectral characteristics) to capture fuller emotional expression?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state in Section 4.5 and Section 8 that "some aspects like voice quality (breathiness, roughness) or spectral tilt can change with emotion but are not reflected in pitch/energy/duration alone" and that this is a known limitation they leave for future work.
- **Why unresolved:** The current mediator M is restricted to three handcrafted prosodic features, which may not fully account for all emotion-related acoustic variations, potentially leading to incomplete or less nuanced emotional rendering for certain affect states.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Integrate additional acoustic descriptors (e.g., spectral moments, harmonic-to-noise ratio) into M, retrain with the same IPC/CPC losses, and evaluate whether emotion classification accuracy and subjective naturalness improve, particularly for emotions like disgust that may rely on non-prosodic cues.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Is the assumption of zero direct effect (emotion affecting speech only via prosody) optimal, or could allowing a small direct pathway improve expressiveness for certain emotions without harming content preservation?
- **Basis in paper:** In Section 8, under "Assumption of No Direct Effect," the authors note that "completely eliminating direct effects might not be optimal" because some emotional nuances might bypass the simplified prosody features.
- **Why unresolved:** The strict enforcement via IPC loss may overly constrain the model, possibly suppressing subtle emotional cues that are not easily captured by duration, pitch, and energy, thereby limiting the authenticity of some expressions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically relax the IPC constraint by varying β_ipc and measure trade-offs between emotion accuracy, content consistency (CCS), and MOS; use probing classifiers to detect residual direct emotion signals in decoder activations and correlate with perceived naturalness.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can continuous or multi-dimensional emotion representations (e.g., arousal-valence) be integrated into the causal mediation framework while maintaining content invariance and prosody disentanglement?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors mention in Section 8 that "emotions are more nuanced (there are degrees of intensity, combinations, etc.)" and that their method "would benefit from continuous or multi-dimensional emotion descriptors."
- **Why unresolved:** The current model uses coarse categorical labels, which cannot represent the gradient or co-occurrence of emotional states; extending to continuous spaces may require redesigning the conditioning and CPC loss to handle regression or multi-target settings without entangling factors.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replace categorical embeddings with continuous emotion vectors (e.g., arousal and valence scores), adapt the emotion classifier in CPC to output continuous predictions (e.g., via MSE loss), and evaluate on datasets with dimensional labels, checking if content consistency and emotion controllability improve for intermediate styles.

## Limitations
- Implementation details are missing: the emotion classifier used in CPC and evaluation, the exact mechanism for conditioning the decoder on emotion in IPC, and the counterfactual sampling strategy.
- The central assumption that prosody fully mediates emotional influence lacks direct validation; residual direct effects could persist.
- The mediator is restricted to three handcrafted prosodic features, which may not capture all emotional cues (e.g., voice quality, spectral tilt).

## Confidence
- Causal mediation (emotion → prosody → speech): Medium  
- IPC eliminating direct emotion effects: Medium  
- CPC achieving distinct prosody with content preservation: High
