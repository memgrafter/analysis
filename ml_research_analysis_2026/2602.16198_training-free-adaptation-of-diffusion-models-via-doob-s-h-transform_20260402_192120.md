# Training-Free Adaptation of Diffusion Models via Doob's $h$-Transform

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16198  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16198  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** DOIT achieves state-of-the-art among inference-time methods with 87.6 average normalized score on D4RL locomotion, surpassing prior SOTA by 1.5 points.

## Executive Summary
We propose DOIT, a training-free framework for adapting pre-trained diffusion models to non-differentiable reward functions. By leveraging Doob’s \(h\)-transform, DOIT modifies the reverse diffusion process with a dynamic correction term that steers samples toward high-reward regions without retraining the score network. The correction \(\nabla \log h\) is estimated via Monte Carlo rollouts using a Tweedie surrogate to avoid extra score evaluations. Theoretically, we prove high-probability convergence to the target conditional distribution. Empirically, DOIT sets a new state-of-the-art on D4RL offline RL benchmarks (average normalized score 87.6) and improves results on ImageReward, while also boosting resampling-based methods.

## Method Summary
DOIT applies Doob’s \(h\)-transform to the reverse stochastic differential equation of a pre-trained diffusion model. The transform adds a drift term \(\nabla \log h(x,t)\), where \(h(x,t) = P(E|\bar{X}_t = x)\) is the probability of the reward event \(E\) given state \(x\) at time \(t\). This tilts the process so that its terminal distribution becomes \(P_\theta(\cdot|E)\). Since \(h\) is unknown, DOIT approximates \(\nabla \log h\) using Monte Carlo rollouts: at selected timesteps, it samples future trajectories under the base diffusion kernel, approximates terminal states via a Tweedie formula (reusing the already-computed score), and forms an empirical estimate of the gradient. The method requires only a non-differentiable reward evaluator at \(t=0\) and keeps the score network frozen.

## Key Results
- On D4RL offline RL locomotion tasks, DOIT achieves an average normalized score of 87.6, outperforming prior inference-time SOTA by 1.5 points.
- On the ImageReward dataset, DOIT improves reward scores and further enhances resampling-based methods such as BoK and BFS.
- The Tweedie surrogate reduces computational overhead by ~23× compared to full trajectory simulation while matching performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Doob’s \(h\)-transform modifies the diffusion reverse SDE to sample from the conditional distribution \(P_\theta(\cdot|E)\) without retraining.
- **Mechanism:** Define \(h(x,t) = P(E|\bar{X}_t = x)\). The tilted process \(d\bar{X}_t^h = [-\frac{1}{2}\bar{X}_t^h - s_\theta(\bar{X}_t^h, t) - \nabla \log h(\bar{X}_t^h, t)]dt + dW_t\) has terminal distribution \(P_\theta(\cdot|E)\).
- **Core assumption:** \(P_\theta(E) > 0\) (non-degeneracy) and \(s_\theta\) is sufficiently accurate.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We leverage Doob’s \(h\)-transform to realize this transport, which induces a dynamic correction to the diffusion sampling process."
  - [section 3.2] Formal derivation of the tilted process and Lemma 3.2 proving the conditional distribution.
  - [corpus] Strong: neighbor papers (e.g., "Inference-Time Alignment for Diffusion Models via Doob's Matching," "Infinite-dimensional generative diffusions via Doob's h-transform") establish Doob’s \(h\)-transform as a valid measure-transform tool for diffusion.
- **Break condition:** When \(P_\theta(E)\) is extremely small, the correction term \(\nabla \log h\) becomes large and unstable, leading to high variance in Monte Carlo estimation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** \(\nabla \log h\) can be approximated via Monte Carlo rollouts without requiring the reward function to be differentiable.
- **Mechanism:** Lemma 4.1 expresses \(\nabla h(x,t_l)\) as an expectation over backward trajectories: \(\nabla h = \mathbb{E}[h(\bar{X}_0,0) \nabla_{x} \log \phi_\theta(\bar{X}_{t_l-1}|\bar{X}_{t_l})\,|\,\bar{X}_{t_l}=x]\). The gradient is taken only on the Gaussian transition \(\phi_\theta\), not on \(h\). The ratio \(\nabla \hat{h}/\hat{h}\) approximates \(\nabla \log h\).
- **Core assumption:** The Monte Carlo samples are generated from the pre-trained diffusion kernel; \(h\) is bounded; and \(s_\theta\) has bounded gradient (Assumption 5.1).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The correction term \(\nabla\log h\) is approximated via Monte Carlo rollouts with a Tweedie surrogate for efficiency."
  - [section 4] Derivation of the sample average approximation (Eq. (9)) and note that it “does not require differentiability in the reward function.”
  - [section 5] Theorem 5.4 provides a high-probability convergence bound that decays with \(M^{-1/6}\).
- **Break condition:** With insufficient Monte Carlo samples (\(M\) too small), the approximation error grows, especially when the variance of \(h\) across trajectories is high. Truncation \(\eta_{t_l}\) mitigates explosion but may bias results when \(h\) is small.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A Tweedie formula surrogate avoids additional score evaluations during rollout, making the method computationally efficient.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of simulating full backward trajectories to recover \(\bar{X}_0\), DOIT approximates \(\hat{x}_0^{(m)} = e^{t_{l-1}/2}(x_{t_{l-1}}^{(m)} + (1-e^{-t_{l-1}})s_\theta(x_{t_l},t_l))\). This reuses the score already computed at \(x_{t_l}\) and requires no extra network calls.
- **Core assumption:** The score varies slowly between timesteps; the surrogate error is bounded (empirically validated in Table 1).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The correction term \(\nabla\log h\) is approximated via Monte Carlo rollouts with a Tweedie surrogate for efficiency."
  - [section 6.1] Equation (10) and the statement: “By substituting \(x_0^{(m)}\) with \(\hat{x}_0^{(m)}\), DOIT incurs zero additional NFEs.”
  - [table 1] Surrogate achieves similar aesthetic score improvements as full simulation but with ~23x speedup.
- **Break condition:** The surrogate may degrade if the score changes rapidly between \(t_l\) and \(t_{l-1}\); this is more likely at small noise scales (late denoising steps).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Pre-trained diffusion model:** Score network \(s_\theta(x,t)\), time schedule \(\{t_l\}\), and transition parameters \((\mu_\theta, \sigma_{t_l}^2)\).
  2. **Correction module:** For timestep \(t_l\) (if \(l \le l^*\)), it:
     - Generates \(M\) lookahead states \(x_{t_{l-1}}^{(m)}\) using the base kernel.
     - Computes terminal approximations \(\hat{x}_0^{(m)}\) via Tweedie surrogate.
     - Evaluates \(h(\hat{x}_0^{(m)},0) \propto \exp(r(\hat{x}_0^{(m)})/\tau)\).
     - Computes \(\hat{\nabla h} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_m h(\hat{x}_0^{(m)},0) \nabla \log \phi_\theta(x_{t_{l-1}}^{(m)}|x_{t_l})\) and \(\hat{h} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_m h(\hat{x}_0^{(m)},0)\).
     - Forms \(\nabla \log \hat{h} = \hat{\nabla h}/(\hat{h} \vee \eta_{t_l})\).
  3. **Modified sampler:** The score used for the next step is \(s_\theta + \gamma \nabla \log \hat{h}\).
  - **Output:** \(x_0\).

- **Critical path:**
  The computational bottleneck is the Monte Carlo rollouts at each correction step. The critical loop iterates \(l = L \to 1\); for \(l \le l^*\) it performs \(M\) lookahead computations. Efficiency hinges on the surrogate (Eq. 10) to avoid extra score evaluations.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - \(\gamma\) (correction strength): Higher \(\gamma\) steers more aggressively but risks collapse.
  - \(\tau\) (temperature): Lower \(\tau\) intensifies reward emphasis; requires lower \(\gamma\) for stability (see Figure 2).
  - \(l^*\) (cutoff): Earlier correction (larger \(l^*\)) increases adaptation but may harm sample quality if applied too late (high noise).
  - \(M\) (rollouts): Higher \(M\) reduces variance but increases runtime linearly.
  - \(\eta\) (truncation): Prevents division by near-zero \(\hat{h}\); too large \(\eta\) weakens correction.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Sample collapse:** Extremely low rewards (or high variance) when \(P_\theta(E)\) is tiny; check if \(\hat{h}\) is often below \(\eta\).
  - **No improvement:** If the reward signal is too weak (high \(\tau\)) or \(\gamma\) too low, the correction term is negligible.
  - **Numerical instability:** Large \(|\nabla \log \hat{h}|\) when \(\hat{h} \approx 0\); assess clipping of \(\nabla \log \hat{h}\) or adaptive \(\eta\).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation on \(\tau,\gamma\):** Violin plots (Figure 2) show the trade‑off; optimal region \(\tau\in[0.3,0.4], \gamma\in[4.0,8.0]\).
  2. **Surrogate validation:** Table 1 confirms surrogate matches full simulation in reward but is ~23x faster.
  3. **Integration with BoK/BFS:** Table 2 shows DOIT boosts resampling methods, indicating orthogonality to population‑level strategies.

## Limitations
- Sensitivity to rare events: When the target event probability \(P_\theta(E)\) is extremely small, the correction term becomes large and Monte Carlo variance spikes, potentially destabilizing sampling.
- Hyperparameter dependence: Optimal values for temperature \(\tau\), correction strength \(\gamma\), and cutoff \(l^*\) vary across reward functions and datasets; the paper’s ablations are limited to specific benchmarks.
- Theoretical bound practicality: The proven convergence rate \(M^{-1/6}\) is high‑probability but may be loose; the paper does not empirically measure the gap versus the number of rollouts \(M\).

## Confidence
- **Doob’s \(h\)-transform validity:** High (theoretically grounded, supported by neighboring literature).
- **Monte Carlo \(\nabla\log h\) approximation:** Medium (Lemma 4.1 is sound, but Assumption 5.1’s bounded gradient may not hold universally; bound tightness unknown).
- **Tweedie surrogate efficiency:** Medium (empirically validated but lacks theoretical error analysis; risk at small \(t_l\)).
- **Empirical SOTA claims:** Medium (results on D4RL/ImageReward are strong but lack cross‑benchmark diversity and statistical tests; possible overfitting to evaluated tasks).

## Next Checks
1. **Stress‑test on rare‑event conditioning:** Evaluate DOIT on rewards where \(P_\theta(E) \ll 0.01\) (e.g., extreme image semantics or low‑reward RL trajectories). Monitor \(\|\nabla\log\hat{h}\|\) and success rate vs. \(M\).
2. **Ablate Monte Carlo sample count \(M\):** Systematically vary \(M\) (e.g., 10, 50, 200) on D4RL tasks; measure (a) variance of final rewards, (b) wall‑clock time, and (c) gap to full simulation (no surrogate). This tests the \(M^{-1/6}\) rate practically.
3. **Cross‑dataset generalization & sensitivity:** Apply DOIT to unseen benchmarks (e.g., humanoid manipulation in D4RL, or text‑to‑image rewards beyond ImageReward). Perform grid search over \(\tau, \gamma, l^*\) and report performance ranges—this checks robustness to hyperparameter choices.
