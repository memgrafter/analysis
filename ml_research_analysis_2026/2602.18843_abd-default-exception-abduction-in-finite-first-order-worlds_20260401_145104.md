# ABD: Default Exception Abduction in Finite First Order Worlds

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18843
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18843
- **Reference count:** 10
- **Primary result:** Frontier LLMs achieve >98% training validity on default-exception abduction but leave a parsimony gap of 1.0–1.6 exceptions per world; holdout tests reveal parsimony inflation in Full/Partial regimes and validity brittleness in Skeptical.

## Executive Summary
The ABD benchmark evaluates large language models on default-exception abduction in first-order logic. Given a background theory with abnormality predicates and multiple finite relational worlds, the task is to output a formula that defines abnormality to restore satisfiability while minimizing exceptions. The benchmark uses exact SMT verification across 600 instances and 11 frontier LLMs, with three regimes (closed-world, existential, universal completion) to test different reasoning modes.

Results show that top models attain high validity (>98%) on training worlds but fail to reach optimal parsimony, leaving a gap of 1.0–1.6 extra exceptions per world compared to a solver lower bound. GPT-5.4 reduces the gap at the cost of larger formulas and lower validity. Holdout evaluation uncovers distinct failure modes: parsimony inflation (gap increase ~1) in Full and Partial settings, and validity brittleness in Skeptical where many formulas break on new worlds. The benchmark’s adversarial world generation and multi-dimensional metrics aim to steer models toward general relational patterns rather than trivial shortcuts.

## Method Summary
ABD constructs abduction instances from seven default theories, each with rules of the form ∀x(ϕ(x) ∧ ¬Ab(x) → ψ(x)). Worlds are finite domains (size 9–12) with interpretations of unary predicates P, Q and binary predicates R, S. The three regimes are: ABD-Full (closed-world, all atoms known), ABD-Partial (existential completion), and ABD-Skeptical (universal completion). The model must output α(x) in S-expression syntax, interpreted as Ab(x) ↔ α(x). Validity requires that under the regime’s completion semantics, the completed theory is satisfiable in every world. Parsimony is the total number of abnormal objects across worlds, minimized.

Instances are generated by sampling worlds, then hardening via an adversarial process that iteratively adds worlds to invalidate a pool of known shortcut formulas (competitors). A cheater filter rejects instances where a shortcut matches the gold cost. For evaluation, each model’s output is parsed and verified exactly using Z3: the grounded theory plus Ab↔α is checked for satisfiability; for partial regimes, optimization computes best- or worst-case abnormal counts. A free-Ab lower bound (independent Ab assignments per object) provides a normalized gap. Holdout worlds (5 per instance) are sampled without adversarial filtering to test generalization.

## Key Results
- Training validity exceeds 98% for top models, but parsimony gaps of 1.0–1.6 extra exceptions per world remain versus the solver lower bound.
- GPT-5.4 achieves a smaller gap (~0.4) at the cost of larger formulas (AST 65.9) and lower training validity (85%).
- Holdout assessment shows parsimony inflation (gap increase ~1) in ABD-Full and ABD-Partial, and validity brittleness in ABD-Skeptical where many formulas fail on new worlds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Exact SMT verification removes ambiguity and enables precise, reproducible scoring of both validity and parsimony cost.
- **Mechanism:** Each finite world is translated into a ground logical theory; α(x) is evaluated by checking satisfiability under completion semantics and counting abnormal elements via Z3 optimization.
- **Core assumption:** Small domains (9–12 elements) permit complete grounding, making validity and min/max cost decidable.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "exact SMT-verification across 600 instances with 11 frontier LLMs"
  - [section 4] Formalization of validity and cost queries for all three regimes
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers lack this level of mechanical checkability; ABD’s FMR score likely stems from solver integration.
- **Break condition:** Domain growth beyond several dozen elements causes grounding explosion; verification would require approximations or sampling, losing exact guarantees.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Adversarial world addition hardens training sets against trivial case-splitting shortcuts, pushing models toward portable relational patterns.
- **Mechanism:** A pool of known short formulas (competitors) is tested on current worlds; new worlds are sampled to invalidate survivors until the pool is eliminated or a world budget is exhausted.
- **Core assumption:** Shortcuts are syntactically compact (AST ≤ 15) and can be falsified by extending the world set.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "control difficulty... eliminate shortcut hypotheses via a lightweight, counterexample-guided (CEGIS-like) procedure"
  - [section 5.3] Describes competitor pool and iterative world addition
  - [appendix D.3] Provides pseudocode for GENERATE_INSTANCE
- **Break condition:** If the shortcut space is not finite or the world budget is too low, some competitors may survive; also, over-hardening could produce training worlds that are atypical of the distribution.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Multi-dimensional metrics (validity, gap-to-lower-bound, AST size) jointly expose overfitting; holdout tests reveal whether low train cost comes from general rules or brittle memorization.
- **Mechanism:** Besides binary validity, report normalized gap to a solver-computed free-Ab lower bound and group formulas by AST; holdout worlds (same distribution but not adversarially filtered) show whether parsimony generalizes.
- **Core assumption:** Large AST indicates memorized case-splits; free-Ab gap correlates with semantic suboptimality; holdout ∆Gap measures out-of-filter robustness.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "cost-based scoring... size-conditioned variants... holdout evaluation reveals distinct failure modes"
  - [section 6.5] Size-conditioned metrics show longer formulas have lower ∆Gap but worse holdout validity
  - [table 10] Paired comparison: formulas longer than gold have 28% holdout validity vs 85% for shorter ones
- **Break condition:** Free-Ab baseline relaxes single-formula constraint; gap may overestimate suboptimality. Also, AST alone does not prove brittleness (some long formulas could be genuinely complex).

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Default logic with abnormality predicates  
  *Why needed here:* The background theory uses rules of the form ϕ(x) ∧ ¬Ab(x) → ψ(x); understanding that Ab(x) blocks defaults is key to the task.  
  *Quick check question:* "In theory T1 (∃y R(x,y)∧P(y) → Q(x)), what condition makes an element x require Ab(x) to be true?"

- **Concept:** Completion semantics (existential vs universal)  
  *Why needed here:* Dictates whether α must work for some (ABD-Partial) or all (ABD-Skeptical) assignments to unknown atoms; changes cost computation and failure modes.  
  *Quick check question:* "How does the per-world cost for a valid α differ between ABD-Partial and ABD-Skeptical?"

- **Concept:** Parsimony as optimized exception minimization  
  *Why needed here:* Primary secondary objective; validity is easy by marking everything abnormal, so cost gap distinguishes meaningful solutions.  
  *Quick check question:* "Why is a formula with gap 0.5 better than one with gap 2.0, even if both are valid?"

- **Concept:** First-order logic with restricted output language  
  *Why needed here:* Models must output α(x) in S-expression syntax with exactly one free variable; quantifier depth and connectives affect expressiveness and brittleness.  
  *Quick check question:* "Why might a model use (forall y (or (not (R x y)) (P y))) instead of (exists y (and (R x y) (not (P y))))?"

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** World Sampler: draws domain sizes, predicate densities, masks unknowns (Appendix D.1). Theory Bank: stores seven default theories (T1–T7) with allowed/forbidden predicates (Appendix C). SMT Verifier (Z3): encodes grounded theory + Ab↔α + completion; checks satisfiability and optimizes abnormal count (Appendix E). Competitor Pool & Eliminator: manages shortcut formulas and adds adversarial worlds (Appendix D.3). Metrics Engine: computes validity, normalized gap (vs free-Ab), gold-relative gap, AST size, holdout ∆Gap (Section 4, 6). Holdout Generator: samples distribution-matched worlds without competitor elimination (Appendix F).

**Critical path:** Instance generation → competitor elimination → holdout sampling → model inference (zero-shot prompt) → parse formula → run SMT checks per world → aggregate metrics → failure-mode classification.

**Design tradeoffs:** Finite domains enable exact verification but invite extensional case-splitting; adversarial training reduces shortcuts but may shift distribution; free-Ab lower bound is solver-cheap but conservative (relaxes single-formula constraint).

**Failure signatures:**
- High train validity + high AST → likely memorization (case-split)
- High train validity + large ∆Gap on holdout → parsimony inflation (overfit to training set)
- Low holdout validity despite high train validity → brittle formula (uniqueness constraints, accidental coincidences)
- Parse errors or missing parentheses → output format instability

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Gold sanity check: For a seeded instance, confirm α∗ achieves 0 gap on training worlds and valid AST ≈15–30.
2. Competitor elimination audit: Verify that a known shortcut (e.g., P(x)) is invalidated after ≤3 added worlds.
3. Holdout brittleness probe: Run a model with high AST on holdout; confirm validity drop and inspect formulas for exists-only or uniqueness patterns (Appendix H.3).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Does solver-in-the-loop training with counterexamples improve LLM performance on ABD?**  
   *Basis:* Future work suggests "solver-in-the-loop refinement that supplies counterexample worlds or counterexample completions."  
   *Why unresolved:* No experiments have tested this interactive approach.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Fine-tune models with counterexample feedback and compare validity, cost gaps, holdout performance against zero-shot; improvement would support the hypothesis.

2. **Can we compute a tighter lower bound for parsimony that respects the single-formula constraint, and would it change the assessment of LLM performance?**  
   *Basis:* The free-Ab baseline "relaxes the single-formula constraint, so gap-to-opt is a conservative overestimate... it would be valuable to tighten the lower bound."  
   *Why unresolved:* Such a bound is currently unavailable.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Develop an algorithm (e.g., ILP) to approximate the true single-formula optimum on a subset of instances; compute new gaps. A significant reduction would indicate current gaps are partly an artifact of the baseline.

3. **How does increasing domain size (e.g., 20–30 objects) affect LLM performance on ABD, and does scaling require new methods?**  
   *Basis:* Future work aims to "scale beyond small worlds via approximate verification or sampling-based evaluation." Also, limitations state "domains are small to permit exact SMT verification."  
   *Why unresolved:* It is unclear whether current LLMs can handle larger, more complex relational worlds without performance degradation.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Generate larger ABD instances using approximate verification, evaluate frontier LLMs, and track changes in validity, cost gaps, and formula sizes. A sharp drop would indicate scalability limits.

4. **Why do LLMs exhibit parsimony inflation in Full/Partial settings but validity brittleness in Skeptical settings, and how can these failures be reduced?**  
   *Basis:* The paper reports distinct failure modes: "parsimony inflation in ABD-Full/Partial" and "validity brittleness in ABD-Skeptical" (Section 6.4).  
   *Why unresolved:* The underlying causes (e.g., quantifier reasoning, overfitting) are not identified.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Conduct fine-grained error analysis (categorize formulas, controlled variations) and test interventions (chain-of-thought, robustness regularization). Successful mitigation would reveal key factors behind each failure mode.

## Limitations

- Unknown: competitor pool composition (Tier-1 shortcuts, mined patterns) and elimination margin; holdout filtering thresholds; AST measurement rules.
- Evaluation limited to small domains (9–12 objects); scalability to larger worlds remains untested and may require approximate verification.
- Parsimony gap based on free-Ab lower bound relaxes the single-formula constraint and may overestimate suboptimality.

## Confidence

- SMT verification: High (well-specified, exact)
- Training validity >98%: Medium (aggregated, no raw data per model)
- Parsimony gap 1.0–1.6: Medium (depends on free-Ab baseline)
- Adversarial elimination effectiveness: Medium (pool unspecified)
- Holdout failure modes: Medium (thresholds approximate, but pattern consistent)
