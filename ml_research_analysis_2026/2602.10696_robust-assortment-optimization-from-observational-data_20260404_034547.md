# Robust Assortment Optimization from Observational Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.10696  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10696  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Introduces a KL‑divergence‑based distributionally robust optimization framework for data‑driven assortment optimization, providing polynomial‑time algorithms for the known‑model case and Pessimistic Robust Rank‑Breaking (PR2B) algorithms that achieve near‑minimax optimal sample complexity under a robust item‑wise coverage condition, with a √K gap between uniform‑revenue and general‑revenue settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies data‑driven assortment optimization—selecting a subset of products to offer—with the goal of maximizing worst‑case expected revenue when historical data are generated by a nominal multinomial logit (MNL) choice model but preferences may shift. The authors develop a KL‑divergence‑based distributionally robust optimization (DRO) framework that maximizes the minimum expected revenue over all choice models lying within a KL‑ball centered at the nominal MNL. For a known nominal model, the robust optimization is shown to be polynomial‑time solvable via convex duality. In the data‑driven setting, they propose Pessimistic Robust Rank‑Breaking (PR2B) algorithms that combine rank‑breaking estimation of MNL parameters with lower confidence bounds to simultaneously address statistical error and model uncertainty. Theoretical analysis yields matching upper and lower bounds on sample complexity, revealing a √K gap between uniform‑revenue and general‑revenue cases. Numerical experiments confirm that PR2B outperforms non‑robust baselines in both sample efficiency and robustness under distributional shifts.

## Method Summary
The proposed method operates in two stages. First, the MNL attraction parameters \(v_j\) are estimated via rank‑breaking: for each item \(j\), compute the empirical ratio \(\hat{p}_j = \tau_j / \tau_{j0}\), where \(\tau_j\) is the number of times \(j\) was chosen and \(\tau_{j0}\) the number of times it was offered (including no‑purchase observations). A lower confidence bound \(\hat{p}_j^{\text{LCB}}\) is derived using Hoeffding‑type inequalities, then transformed to \(\hat{v}_j^{\text{LCB}} = \hat{p}_j^{\text{LCB}} / (1 - \hat{p}_j^{\text{LCB}})\). Second, a distributionally robust optimization (DRO) problem is solved to choose an assortment \(S\) that maximizes the worst‑case expected revenue over all choice distributions \(Q\) with KL‑divergence \(D_{\text{KL}}(Q \| P_v(\cdot|S)) \leq \rho(S)\). Two variants are considered: (i) constant \(\rho\) (PR2B‑C) and (ii) varying \(\rho\) that depends on the no‑purchase probability, using known total attraction \(v_{\text{tot}}\) (PR2B‑V). The DRO is reformulated via a dual variable \(\lambda\), turning the inner minimization into a sup over \(\lambda\) and yielding a polynomial‑time saddle‑point algorithm (e.g., water‑filling plus bisection). The pessimism from the LCB parameters and the robust DRO together yield high‑probability guarantees. The overall algorithm is termed Pessimistic Robust Rank‑Breaking (PR2B).

## Key Results
- **Polynomial‑time robust optimization**: For both constant and varying KL‑ball radii, the robust expected revenue maximization problem is solvable in polynomial time via convex duality and specialized solvers.
- **Optimal sample complexity**: PR2B attains near‑minimax rates; the sample complexity is governed by the minimum number of observations of any item in the optimal robust assortment (robust item‑wise coverage). A √K gap separates uniform‑revenue from general‑revenue cases.
- **Empirical superiority**: Simulations show PR2B substantially reduces suboptimality gaps and maintains higher revenue under distributional shifts compared to non‑robust plug‑in methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: KL‑DRO Dual Reformulation
- **Claim**: The KL‑divergence‑based DRO models preference shifts by optimizing worst‑case expected revenue over a KL‑ball centered on the nominal MNL.
- **Mechanism**: The inner infimum over choice models is replaced via convex duality (introducing a dual variable \(\lambda\)), transforming the min‑max problem into a saddle‑point problem that is polynomial‑time solvable.
- **Core assumption**: Preference shifts are bounded in KL‑divergence from the nominal MNL; the nominal model is correctly specified as MNL.
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 2.2 (Proposition 2.1, Examples 2.1/2.2) and Section 4 use the dual to define \(\tilde{H}(S,\lambda;v)\).
- **Break condition**: Guarantees fail if true shifts exceed the KL‑ball or if the nominal model is not MNL.

### Mechanism 2: Double Pessimism (PR2B)
- **Claim**: PR2B handles coupled statistical error and model uncertainty by nesting pessimistic estimators: first compute lower confidence bounds on MNL parameters, then solve the robust DRO with these pessimistic estimates.
- **Mechanism**: Rank‑breaking yields per‑item estimates \(\hat{p}_j\); Hoeffding bounds give \(\hat{p}_j^{\text{LCB}}\) and hence \(\hat{v}_j^{\text{LCB}}\). Because the robust objective \(R^\rho(S;v)\) is monotone in \(v\), solving with \(\hat{v}^{\text{LCB}}\) ensures a high‑probability lower bound on the true robust revenue.
- **Core assumption**: Valid confidence regions for \(p_j\); monotonicity of \(R^\rho(S;v)\) w.r.t. \(v\) (Lemma D.1 for constant \(\rho\), Lemma D.3 for varying \(\rho\)).
- **Evidence**: Section 4.1, Lemmas D.1/D.3.
- **Break condition**: If rank‑breaking fails (e.g., some items rarely appear) or monotonicity does not hold for the chosen \(\rho\) form, the pessimism wrapper may break.

### Mechanism 3: Robust Item‑Wise Coverage
- **Claim**: The minimal data requirement for sample‑efficient robust learning is “robust item‑wise coverage”: each item in the optimal robust assortment must appear in enough historical assortments.
- **Mechanism**: Rank‑breaking decouples estimation; the suboptimality bound scales as \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n_i})\) for the worst‑exposed item in the optimal assortment. Thus, only items in the optimal assortment matter.
- **Core assumption**: Historical data are i.i.d. from the nominal MNL; an optimal robust assortment exists and satisfies coverage.
- **Evidence**: Theorem 5.1 (constant \(\rho\)) and Theorem 5.3 (varying \(\rho\)) provide explicit bounds; matching lower bounds in Theorems 5.2 and 5.4.
- **Break condition**: If some \(i \in S^\star\) has \(n_i\) too small, the guarantee degrades; large \(v_{\max}\) also weakens the bound.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Distributionally robust optimization (DRO).  
  *Why needed*: Converts the min‑max robust revenue problem into a tractable saddle‑point problem via dualization, enabling polynomial‑time algorithms (Section 3).  
  *Quick check*: How does the dual variable \(\lambda\) regulate the trade‑off between revenue and robustness?
- **Concept**: Rank‑breaking for MNL estimation.  
  *Why needed*: Decouples estimation of each item’s attraction parameter \(v_j\) from pairwise comparisons \(p_j = v_j/(1+v_j)\), avoiding joint MLE over exponentially many mix‑and‑match choice distributions.  
  *Quick check*: Why does rank‑breaking only require item‑specific data exposure rather than full assortment observations?
- **Concept**: Partial/coverage conditions in offline policy learning.  
  *Why needed*: The “robust item‑wise coverage” condition is the minimal data property that enables sample‑efficient learning; it generalizes the item‑wise coverage from non‑robust assortment learning (Han et al., 2025) to DRO.  
  *Quick check*: How does the double pessimism principle allow relaxing uniform coverage over all assortments to item‑wise coverage?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  1. **Data Ingestion Module**: Consumes historical data \(\{(S_k, i_k)\}_{k=1}^n\) and preprocesses into counts \(\tau_j\) (times item \(j\) chosen) and \(\tau_{j0}\) (times item \(j\) is offered and either chosen or no‑purchase observed).
  2. **Pessimistic Estimator Module**: For each item \(j\), computes empirical estimate \(\hat{p}_j = \tau_j / \tau_{j0}\), then forms lower confidence bound \(\hat{p}_j^{\text{LCB}}\) using Hoeffding‑type bounds, and transforms to \(\hat{v}_j^{\text{LCB}} = \hat{p}_j^{\text{LCB}} / (1 - \hat{p}_j^{\text{LCB}})\).
  3. **Robust Solver Module**: For constant \(\rho\) (PR2B‑C) solves \(\max_{|S|\leq K} \sup_{0\leq\lambda\leq r_{\max}/\rho} \tilde{H}(S,\lambda;\hat{v}^{\text{LCB}})\). For varying \(\rho\) (PR2B‑V) solves \(\max_{|S|\leq K} \sup_{\lambda\geq 0} \tilde{H}(S,\lambda;\hat{v}^{\text{LCB}})\) where \(\tilde{H}\) depends on \(\rho_0\) and known \(v_{\text{tot}}\); uses geometric/bisection algorithms (Appendix A.3.1). Both planning subproblems are polynomial‑time (e.g., \(\mathcal{O}(N^2)\)).
  4. **Output Module**: Returns the selected assortment \(\hat{S}\).
- **Critical path**: Raw data → rank‑breaking counts → LCB parameters → robust expected revenue evaluation (via dual) → assortment selection → output. The LCB computation is item‑wise and parallelizable. The robust solver (for non‑uniform revenues and constrained cardinality \(K\)) dominates the computational cost (\(\mathcal{O}(N^2)\) operations).
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Constant vs. varying \(\rho\)**: Constant \(\rho\) (PR2B‑C) is simpler but may be less conservative; varying \(\rho\) (PR2B‑V) adapts robustness to assortment‑specific risk (e.g., larger KL‑ball when the no‑purchase attraction is weak) but requires known \(v_{\text{tot}}\) and a more complex solver.
  - **Dependence on \(v_{\max}\)**: Bounds scale with \((1+v_{\max})\) and \((1+v(S^\star))\). Highly skewed MNL parameters (large \(v_{\max}\)) worsen sample complexity; near‑uniform revenues tighten bounds.
  - **Algorithmic constraints**: \(\mathcal{O}(N^2)\) complexity may become prohibitive for very large \(N\); for \(K\approx N\) the unconstrained case (Proposition 3.1) can be used.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High suboptimality exceeding theoretical bound ⇒ likely violation of robust item‑wise coverage (some \(n_i\) too small) or large \(v_{\max}\) causing excessively wide confidence intervals.
  - Algorithm instability for large \(K\) or \(N\) ⇒ computational cost grows quadratically; consider approximations or unconstrained variant.
  - PR2B‑V sensitivity to \(v_{\text{tot}}\) ⇒ if provided total attraction deviates from \(\sum_j v_j\), the KL‑ball is mis‑calibrated, yielding suboptimal assortments.
  - Rank‑breaking breakdown ⇒ if some items rarely appear (\(\tau_{j0}\) small), LCB may be zero, possibly eliminating the true optimal item.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Sample efficiency**: Compare PR2B‑C/V against non‑pessimistic plug‑in baselines across increasing sample sizes; verify \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{\min n_i})\) decay in suboptimality (Section 6.1, Figures 3–4).
  2. **Robustness under distributional shifts**: Simulate perturbed choice models with known KL‑bounded deviations; evaluate revenue improvement of PR2B assortments over baselines across many shifts (Section 6.2, Figure 5).
  3. **Cardinality effect**: For fixed \(\min_{i\in S^\star} n_i\), plot suboptimality versus \(\log K\); confirm the \(\mathcal{O}(K)\) vs. \(\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{K})\) gap predicted for non‑uniform vs. uniform revenue cases (Section 6.3, Figures 6–9).

## Limitations
- **Algorithmic completeness**: The planning algorithm for the varying‑\(\rho\) case (PR2B‑V) is described only conceptually; full pseudocode and edge‑case handling are missing, hindering reproducibility.
- **Confidence‑bound constants**: Discrepancy between the lower‑confidence bound formula used in the algorithm and the theoretical lemma (different constants) raises questions about tightness and adherence to the proven guarantees.
- **Estimation of total attraction**: PR2B‑V requires the true total attraction \(v_{\text{tot}}\) as input; misspecification can materially affect the size of the KL‑ball and degrade performance. The paper does not address how to estimate \(v_{\text{tot}}\) from data.

## Confidence
- The KL‑DRO reformulation yields polynomial‑time solvable optimization for constant \(\rho\) and (with known \(v_{\text{tot}}\)) for varying \(\rho\). **Confidence: High**.
- The PR2B estimator achieves near‑minimax optimal sample complexity under robust item‑wise coverage. **Confidence: High**.
- Numerical experiments demonstrate PR2B’s superiority over non‑robust baselines in sample efficiency and robustness. **Confidence: Medium**.
