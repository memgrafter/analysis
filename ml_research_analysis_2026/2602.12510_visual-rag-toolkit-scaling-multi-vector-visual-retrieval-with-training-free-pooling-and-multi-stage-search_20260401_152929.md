# Visual RAG Toolkit: Scaling Multi-Vector Visual Retrieval with Training-Free Pooling and Multi-Stage Search

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.12510  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12510  
- **Reference count:** 7  
- **Primary result:** Achieves ~4× higher query throughput via training-free spatial pooling and multi-stage retrieval while preserving NDCG and Recall@5/10 within ±0.01 of the uncompressed baseline; recall at large cutoffs (R@100) degrades moderately.

## Executive Summary
Multi-vector visual retrievers such as ColPali produce thousands of patch embeddings per document page, leading to quadratic search costs and unwieldy indexes. The Visual RAG Toolkit tackles this scalability bottleneck by (1) compressing page embeddings into dozens of compact vectors through static, model-aware spatial pooling, and (2) employing a multi-stage retrieval pipeline that uses these pooled vectors for fast candidate generation followed by exact reranking with the original full embeddings. Combined with robust preprocessing—token hygiene and optional margin cropping—the approach reduces inner-product computations quadratically and yields a ~4× throughput gain on the ViDoRe v2 benchmark (3006 pages) while keeping NDCG and Recall@5/10 within 0.01 of the uncompressed baseline. The toolkit operates without additional training and targets consumer‑hardware deployment.

## Method Summary
Images are generated from PDFs and optionally cropped to remove empty margins. A vision‑language model (VLM) encodes each page into a grid of patch embeddings. Non‑visual tokens (special symbols, padding) are filtered out (token hygiene) to avoid spurious similarities. Model‑specific spatial pooling then reduces the grid to a small set of tile‑level or global vectors: row‑wise averaging with a 1D convolution for ColPali, tile‑level mean for ColSmol, and row‑mean plus Gaussian smoothing for ColQwen2.5. Both the full (“initial”) patch embeddings and the pooled vectors are stored as named vectors in a Qdrant collection. At query time, the query is encoded, and a single API call invokes a two‑stage cascade: first, MaxSim on the pooled vectors retrieves a top‑K candidate set; second, exact MaxSim reranking is performed on the full vectors of those candidates. Evaluation on ViDoRe v2 shows the trade‑off between speed and recall, particularly at large cutoffs.

## Key Results
- **Throughput:** Approx. 4× higher queries‑per‑second (QPS) on the union dataset (~3000 pages) compared to a one‑stage baseline.
- **Quality preservation:** For the 3B‑parameter models (ColPali, ColQwen2.5), NDCG and Recall@5/10 remain within ±0.01 of the baseline; the main degradation is at R@100, which drops by 0.07–0.30.
- **Small model limitation:** The 500M‑parameter ColSmol suffers larger quality drops under aggressive pooling, indicating a capacity threshold.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The toolkit’s scalability arises from three interrelated mechanisms.

**Training‑free spatial pooling** reduces the number of vectors per page by 32–64× without any learning. Model‑specific operations (row‑mean for ColPali, tile‑mean for ColSmol, Gaussian smoothing for ColQwen2.5) exploit the 2D spatial coherence of document embeddings, summarising them into compact tile‑level and global reps. This works because late‑interaction MaxSim tolerates a loss of fine‑grained detail as long as broader spatial patterns remain. The compression fails for models with limited representational capacity: ColSmol‑500M shows up to 0.30 drop in R@100, signalling that spatial summaries become overly lossy.

**Multi‑stage retrieval** turns vector‑count reduction into quadratic computational savings. The pooled vectors support a fast prefetch stage that pulls a small candidate set (top‑K) from the whole corpus. Exact MaxSim reranking is then applied only to those K candidates using the original full embeddings. The expensive inner products drop from O(N) to O(K) for the second stage, yielding an empirical ~4× QPS boost on N≈3000. The trade‑off is that when a relevant document lies outside the prefetch window, recall at large cutoffs (e.g., R@100) suffers; this is the primary observed failure.

**Preprocessing (token hygiene and cropping)** removes spurious signals and reduces raw token count. Non‑visual tokens (special symbols, padding) act as high‑similarity attractors in MaxSim, while empty margins contribute noisy embeddings. Filtering these improves both quality and the reliability of subsequent pooling. Token hygiene alone can even exceed published ViDoRe scores in some cases.

## Foundational Learning
- **Late‑Interaction Retrieval (MaxSim)**  
  *Why needed here:* The entire toolkit assumes ColBERT‑style late interaction: a document is a set of token embeddings, and similarity is the maximum over token‑pair dot products. This paradigm makes vector count per document a direct driver of computational cost, and it underpins the two‑stage rerank design.  
  *Quick check:* If a document has D vectors and a query has Q vectors, how many dot products are computed per document? How does reducing D affect this count?

- **Spatial Pooling without Learning**  
  *Why needed here:* Core innovation uses static arithmetic (means, weighted averages) over embedding grids to achieve dramatic compression while retaining spatial semantics (rows → reading order, tiles → regions). Understanding why this works for some VLMs (e.g., fixed grids) but not others (e.g., those with built‑in PatchMerger) is essential to applying the method.  
  *Quick check:* Why would a 2×2 learned PatchMerger (in ColQwen2.5) make a uniform conv1d pooling ineffective, requiring instead a gentle, same‑length Gaussian smoothing?

- **Multi‑Stage (Retrieve‑then‑Rerank) Pipelines**  
  *Why needed here:* The two‑stage cascade is the efficiency engine: a cheap approximate first stage (using compact vectors) narrows N to K, enabling an expensive exact second stage (full vectors) without O(N) cost. The design space—prefetch‑K, choice of stage‑1 vectors—directly impacts the speed‑recall trade‑off.  
  *Quick check:* If you double the prefetch‑K value, what happens to recall (especially at large k) and latency? Why is QPS improvement expected to grow with corpus size N?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
PDF Ingestion → Preprocessing (token hygiene, optional cropping) → VLM Encoding → Pooling → Index Layer (Qdrant collection storing multiple named vectors: full and pooled). Query Pipeline: Query Encoding → Prefetch (MaxSim on pooled vectors) → Rerank (exact MaxSim on full vectors) → Results. Evaluation Harness runs end‑to‑end metrics.

**Critical path:**  
PDF → image conversion → (crop) → VLM encode → token filter → pooling → store in Qdrant. Query: encode query → single API call `prefetch+query` → results.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Model‑specific pooling vs generality:* Tailored pooling per backbone maximises quality but requires per‑model implementation; the modular SDK allows adding new strategies.  
- *Prefetch‑K vs Recall:* Larger K improves recall (especially R@100) but increases rerank cost and reduces net QPS gain; default K=256 balances tight metrics.  
- *Storage vs compute:* Pooled vectors enable smaller indexes (dozens vs thousands per page), but full vectors must still be stored for reranking; total storage reduction is modest (~2–4×, not 32×).

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Quality:* >0.05 drop in N@5/N@10 or large R@100 drop with small prefetch‑K.  
- *Throughput:* <2× QPS improvement on union scope (N≈3000) suggests prefetch‑K too large or pooling ineffective.  
- *Model‑specific:* ColSmol‑500M consistently underperforms; expect >0.1 degradation on some metrics.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Reproduce the 1‑stage baseline with hygiene/cropping: index all pages using only full vectors after token filtering and default cropping; verify NDCG/Recall@5/10 match or exceed published ViDoRe scores.  
2. Compare pooling strategies per model: for each model (ColPali, ColQwen2.5, ColSmol), index with its recommended pooled vector and run 2‑stage retrieval (prefetch‑K=256, rerank on full vectors); confirm N@5/N@10 within ±0.01 of baseline and QPS increases by ~3–4× on union scope.  
3. Vary prefetch‑K on union scope: with best pooled vector fixed, run 2‑stage retrieval with prefetch‑K in {64,128,256,512,1024}; plot R@100 and QPS vs K; expect sharp recall gain until K≈256 then diminishing returns, and QPS dropping linearly; identify the knee point for this corpus.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Can 1‑stage retrieval using only pooled embeddings be viable?** The authors propose that improving pooling quality might eliminate the need for storing full vectors, but this remains to be evaluated. Current evidence is limited to the two‑stage setup.  
- **Can lightweight learned pooling close the gap for small models?** Static pooling causes large quality drops for the 500M‑parameter ColSmol; exploring trainable adapters or other learned compression is suggested but untested.  
- **What multiplicative gains arise from combining with orthogonal techniques?** The paper hints that vector‑count reduction could be combined with quantisation and HNSW pruning for further efficiency, but their interaction is unexplored.

## Limitations
- **Technical detail omissions:** Exact cropping parameters (thresholds, page‑number stripping) and interpolation methods for adaptive row binning are unspecified, hindering exact reproduction. Qdrant collection configuration (HNSW, quantisation) is also not fully detailed.  
- **Model‑specific efficacy boundaries:** Aggressive pooling fails for models with limited capacity (e.g., ColSmol‑500M); the boundary between effective and lossy compression for new architectures is unclear.  
- **Recall at large cutoffs:** The multi‑stage design inherently sacrifices R@100 (0.07–0.30 drop) because the prefetch window cannot cover all relevant documents; this trade‑off may be unacceptable for some applications.  
- **Generalisability:** Evaluation is restricted to ViDoRe v2 (3006 pages) and three specific VLMs; performance on other document types, languages, or newer model families is unverified.

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** The multi‑stage pipeline achieves a quadratic reduction in inner‑product computations and yields ~4× QPS improvement on corpora of ~3000 pages; the mechanism and throughput gains are clearly described and measured.  
- **Medium confidence:** Model‑specific training‑free pooling preserves NDCG and Recall@5/10 within ±0.01 for the two 3B models; evidence is strong but the success of the same handcrafted strategies on unseen architectures remains uncertain.  
- **Low confidence:** Token hygiene and cropping universally improve quality; the paper demonstrates gains over a no‑hygiene baseline, but the isolated contribution of cropping parameters and their optimality across document types is not characterised.
