# Effective Dataset Distillation for Spatio-Temporal Forecasting with Bi-dimensional Compression

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10410
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10410
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** STemDist enables joint spatio-temporal dataset distillation, yielding up to 6× faster training, up to 8× lower GPU memory usage, and up to 12% lower prediction error versus nine state-of-the-art baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces STemDist, the first dataset distillation method designed specifically for spatio-temporal time series forecasting. Existing approaches compress only the temporal dimension, leaving the spatial dimension (number of locations) uncompressed, which leads to high training costs. STemDist addresses this by jointly compressing both temporal and spatial dimensions through three core components: a location encoder that induces model generalization to unseen locations, clustering to reduce spatial distillation cost, and subset-based granular distillation to capture all spatial regions effectively. Evaluations across five real-world datasets demonstrate significant improvements in training efficiency and prediction accuracy, with the method generalizing across forecasting models and scaling sublinearly with dataset size.

## Method Summary
STemDist processes spatio-temporal data by first encoding location metadata to make the forecasting model inductive to the number of locations. This allows training on a distilled subset of locations while maintaining inference capability over a larger set. To reduce the computational burden of distilling many locations, a clustering module groups similar locations. Within each cluster, a subset-based granular distillation procedure generates synthetic representations that preserve both temporal dynamics and spatial characteristics. The resulting compressed dataset—synthesizing both dimensions—is then used to train forecasting models, dramatically cutting resource requirements without sacrificing accuracy.

## Key Results
- Training speed improved by up to 6× compared to baselines.
- GPU memory usage reduced by up to 8×.
- Prediction error lowered by up to 12% on benchmark datasets.
- Method generalizes across different forecasting architectures and exhibits sublinear scaling with dataset size.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
STemDist succeeds because it directly tackles the dual compression challenge in spatio-temporal data. The location encoder breaks hard spatial dependencies by embedding locations into a shared representation space, enabling models trained on a distilled spatial subset to generalize to new locations. Clustering reduces the effective number of spatial points that must be distilled, lowering computational cost while maintaining coverage through representative groups. Subset-based granular distillation ensures that within each cluster, diverse spatial patterns are captured in the synthetic data, preventing loss of local dynamics. This combination allows the synthetic dataset to be much smaller in both dimensions yet still informative for training accurate models.

## Foundational Learning
- **Dataset Distillation:** Compressing a large dataset into a small synthetic set that trains models comparably to the full dataset. *Needed to reduce computational burden of model training.* Quick check: Measure model accuracy when trained solely on synthetic data versus original data.
- **Spatio-Temporal Forecasting:** Predicting future values across both time and space, involving complex inter-dependencies. *Needed because many real-world systems (traffic, climate) are inherently spatio-temporal.* Quick check: Validate spatial and temporal autocorrelation in residuals.
- **Inductive Bias via Location Encoding:** Using metadata (e.g., coordinates, features) to embed locations so models can extrapolate to unseen ones. *Needed to separate spatial scale from model parameters.* Quick check: Test model performance on held-out locations not seen during training.
- **Clustering for Dimensionality Reduction:** Grouping similar locations to treat them as a single unit during distillation. *Needed to avoid linear scaling of distillation cost with location count.* Quick check: Assess cluster homogeneity using domain-relevant metrics.
- **Granular (Subset-based) Distillation:** Distilling separate synthetic subsets per cluster or region rather than a global set. *Needed to preserve local heterogeneity that global synthesis might smooth over.* Quick check: Compare error rates across different spatial regions.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:** Location Encoder -> Clustering Module -> Subset Selection -> Granular Distillation -> Synthetic Spatio-Temporal Dataset -> Forecasting Model Training -> Inference on Expanded Locations
- **Critical Path:** 1. Encode all locations. 2. Cluster encoded locations. 3. For each cluster, select representative temporal subsets and distill their spatial-temporal patterns. 4. Combine cluster-level synthetic data into one compressed dataset. 5. Train forecasting model using location encoder; model now accepts any location embedding and generalizes beyond training locations.
- **Design Tradeoffs:** Clustering reduces spatial dimension but risks merging dissimilar locations; subset-based distillation improves spatial coverage but increases synthesis complexity; inductive encoder enables scaling but may overlook unique location-specific signals.
- **Failure Signatures:** Degraded performance when cluster assignments are poor (e.g., high intra-cluster variance); synthetic data failing to capture extreme events or local anomalies; model failing to generalize to geographically or functionally distinct held-out locations.
- **First Three Experiments:** 1. Ablate the number of clusters on a single dataset, measuring training cost, memory, and accuracy vs. full-data training. 2. Compare STemDist against a baseline that only compresses temporally (no spatial clustering) to isolate spatial compression benefits. 3. Train on a subset of locations (e.g., 50%) and test inference on completely held-out locations to quantify inductive spatial generalization.

## Limitations
- The claim of being the first joint spatio-temporal dataset distillation method requires verification against very recent or concurrent work.
- Reported efficiency gains (training speed, memory) likely exclude the one-time cost of the distillation process itself; total end-to-end resource trade-offs are unclear.
- Evaluation spans only five datasets; their diversity may not represent all spatio-temporal forecasting scenarios, and absence of ablation studies prevents assessing each component's individual contribution.

## Confidence
- **Novelty (first joint compression):** Medium – plausible but unconfirmed without comprehensive literature review.
- **Performance (speed, memory, error reduction):** Medium – empirical results on limited benchmarks; statistical robustness and total cost not established.
- **Generalization (across models and sublinear scaling):** Medium – requires validation on more datasets, models, and scales.
