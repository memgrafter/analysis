# PISCO: Precise Video Instance Insertion with Sparse Control

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08277  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08277  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** PISCO significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on video instance insertion, reducing whole-video FVD from 371 to 204 for the strongest baseline, with monotonic gains as more sparse control frames are provided.

## Executive Summary
PISCO is a video diffusion framework for inserting a visual instance into a background video using only sparse user keyframes (e.g., first and last frame). It automatically propagates the instance’s appearance, motion, and physical interactions while preserving background dynamics. The method introduces three core techniques: Variable-Information Guidance (VIG) to handle arbitrary sparsity, Distribution-Preserving Temporal Masking (DPTM) to stabilize training under pretrained VAEs, and depth-aware conditioning for physically plausible compositing. Evaluated on a new benchmark, PISCO achieves state-of-the-art performance with clear improvements as additional sparse controls are given.

## Method Summary
PISCO reformulates precise video instance insertion as a conditional denoising task. Given a background video, a segmented instance cutout (with mask and depth), and an availability mask indicating which frames are user-provided keyframes, the model generates the full output video. The pipeline preprocesses inputs to produce depth maps and applies augmentations (amodal completion, relighting). The key innovation is DPTM: missing frames are filled in pixel space by nearest-frame interpolation to create a natural-looking clip that passes through the pretrained temporal VAE without distribution shift; then, tokens corresponding to originally missing frames are masked in latent space to inform the denoiser. Conditioning is injected via a modified VACE context adapter that ingest multiple channels (RGB, depth, mask, availability). Training uses VIG, where the availability mask is randomly sampled to expose the model to a hybrid distribution of supervision densities, enabling robust generalization to any sparse pattern at test time without retraining.

## Key Results
- PISCO outperforms all baselines on PISCO-Bench, achieving whole-video FVD as low as 204 (vs. 371 for the best prior method).
- Both reference-based (FVD, LPIPS) and reference-free (VBench) metrics show clear monotonic improvement as the number of provided sparse keyframes increases.
- Ablations confirm the importance of DPTM (prevents flickering), depth conditioning (improves occlusion/shadows), and VIG (enables flexible sparsity handling).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Variable-Information Guidance (VIG)
- **Claim:** Training with dynamically sampled availability masks enables a single model to robustly handle arbitrary sparse keyframe control without retraining.
- **Mechanism:** During training, the model sees a hybrid distribution of annotation densities (single keyframe, sparse, dense). The availability mask modulates which instance-side signals are active, forcing the denoiser to learn two coupled skills: (1) inferring plausible object motion and appearance from minimal cues by leveraging background context, and (2) strictly adhering to provided cues when they are dense, preventing identity drift.
- **Break condition:** If the provided keyframes describe physically impossible trajectories (e.g., teleportation), the model will produce spatial morphing artifacts due to lack of geometric prior.

### Mechanism 2: Distribution-Preserving Temporal Masking (DPTM)
- **Claim:** Pixel-space nearest-frame interpolation followed by token-space masking prevents the severe distribution shift that occurs when feeding sparsely masked tensors into a pretrained temporal VAE, stabilizing training and inference.
- **Mechanism:** The temporal VAE is pretrained on continuous video distributions. Naively applying the availability mask in pixel space creates discontinuous inputs. DPTM first fills missing frames with the nearest available instance frame, creating a temporally smooth clip that preserves VAE input statistics. Then, after VAE encoding, tokens corresponding to originally missing frames are explicitly masked so the denoiser knows which signals are interpolated fillers.
- **Break condition:** If the instance undergoes a rapid appearance change between keyframes, nearest interpolation creates an incorrect transitional plateau that the model may fail to override promptly.

### Mechanism 3: Depth-Aware Scene-Aware Conditioning
- **Claim:** Joint conditioning on background depth and instance depth enables physically plausible compositing (occlusion, shadows, scale).
- **Mechanism:** The context adapter ingests RGB, depth, mask, and availability channels. The two depth maps provide a relative depth ordering signal. During denoising, cross-attention and feature modulation use these cues to correctly layer the instance, infer shadow regions, and maintain consistent scale relative to scene geometry.
- **Break condition:** If the depth estimator produces severely erroneous background depth, the instance may be incorrectly occluded or cast shadows in physically impossible locations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Video Latent Diffusion Models**  
  *Why needed here:* PISCO is built on Wan, a video diffusion model operating in the latent space of a pretrained temporal VAE. Understanding the VAE’s compression and the denoising U‑Net’s conditioning interface is essential to grasp DPTM and the context adapter’s role.  
  *Quick check:* “How does the temporal compression factor Ct of the VAE affect the design of the availability channel in DPTM?”
- **Concept: Spatio‑Temporal Attention in Video Transformers**  
  *Why needed here:* The Wan backbone uses spatio‑temporal attention. The VACE context adapter injects conditions via cross‑attention. Debugging PISCO requires understanding how spatial mask and availability tokens interact with query/key/value projections.  
  *Quick check:* “If the availability mask A is poorly aligned with the token grid after reshaping (Ct into channels), what specific artifact might emerge?”
- **Concept: Amodal Completion & Relighting**  
  *Why needed here:* Training augmentations assume a complete (amodal) instance cutout, but data often contains occluded (modal) instances. The model must learn to remove the amodal instance per mask and depth, and adapt illumination via relighting augmentation.  
  *Quick check:* “Without amodal completion augmentation, what failure occurs when a user provides a fully visible cutout but the model was trained only on occluded segments?”

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input preprocessor -> DPTM module -> Context adapter (VACE) -> Diffusion backbone (Wan) -> Temporal VAE decoder -> Output video  
- **Critical path:** `(V, Î, M, A)` -> DPTM (pixel completion -> VAE encode -> token mask) -> Context adapter (merge with DV) -> Wan U‑Net (denoise) -> VAE decode -> `V'`. The availability mask A and depth signals must flow correctly through DPTM reshaping and adapter injection.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - VIG density range: Too wide may slow sparse-regime convergence; too narrow risks failure on unseen sparsity.  
  - DPTM interpolation: Nearest‑frame is simple but may blur transitions; flow‑based risks hallucination.  
  - Depth reliance: Noisy depth estimates degrade occlusion; dropping depth harms physical consistency.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. Temporal flickering: token masking misalignment or VAE distribution shift.  
  2. Identity drift under sparse control: VIG failed to propagate appearance cues.  
  3. Occlusion/shadow artifacts: ineffective depth conditioning or erroneous depth maps.  
  4. Background hallucination: model fails to preserve input background.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Ablate DPTM: train two models identical except DPTM; evaluate under sparse keyframes (frames 1,25,49) for flicker and FVD/LPIPS.  
  2. Control density scaling: fix model, vary provided keyframes (1,3,5,all); plot foreground FVD/LPIPS vs. number of keyframes.  
  3. Depth ablation: remove depth inputs; compare occlusion/shadow consistency metrics and qualitative inspection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can PISCO be adapted to support other instance‑centric video editing tasks (e.g., background replacement, instance speed modification) beyond insertion?  
  *Basis:* Section 6 invites extensions; the paper only evaluates insertion.  
  *Resolution:* Implement each task with minimal modifications, curate data if needed, and evaluate on appropriate benchmarks or user studies.
- **Open Question 2:** How does the density ratio γ in Variable‑Information Guidance affect robustness when test‑time keyframe sparsity differs from the training distribution?  
  *Basis:* VIG samples availability masks with γ during training, but sensitivity to γ and generalization to unseen sparsity patterns are not analyzed.  
  *Resolution:* Train models with varying γ; evaluate on a range of sparsity patterns; analyze performance to determine optimal γ or need for adaptive guidance.
- **Open Question 3:** Can DPTM be improved by replacing nearest‑frame interpolation with more sophisticated temporal completion (e.g., flow‑based, generative inpainting)?  
  *Basis:* DPTM uses nearest interpolation to preserve distribution; no comparison to alternatives.  
  *Resolution:* Substitute nearest interpolation with optical flow warping or video inpainting models; compare using temporal coherence metrics and qualitative inspection.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter specifics (γ, learning rates, batch sizes) are not fully disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- The method depends on external components (pretrained Wan VAE, depth estimator, amodal completion model); failures in these propagate to the final output.
- Break conditions: VIG fails on physically impossible trajectories; DPTM may delay rapid appearance changes; erroneous depth maps yield impossible composites.

## Confidence
- VIG robustness: Medium (supported by internal ablation only, weak corpus evidence)
- DPTM stabilization: Medium (qualitative improvement shown, no quantitative ablation)
- Depth‑aware compositing: High (consistent with prior work, ablations imply benefit)
- Overall SOTA performance: High (PISCO‑Bench shows clear gains)
