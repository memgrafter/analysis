# CARE: Towards Clinical Accountability in Multi-Modal Medical Reasoning with an Evidence-Grounded Agentic Framework
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.01607
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01607
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: CARE-Flow (10B) achieves 74.9% accuracy on medical VQA benchmarks, improving 10.9% over same-size SOTA; CARE-Coord reaches 77.5%, outperforming 32B Lingshu by 5.2%.

## Executive Summary
CARE addresses the lack of accountability in black-box medical vision-language models (VLMs) by introducing an evidence-grounded agentic framework. It decomposes clinical reasoning into three staged sub-modules: an entity proposal VLM, expert ROI segmentation, and evidence-grounded VQA that utilizes zoom-in, mask, and global hints. The VLMs are fine-tuned with reinforcement learning using verifiable rewards to align answers with provided evidence. An optional VLM coordinator plans tool invocations and reviews consistency across specialist outputs. On four medical VQA benchmarks, CARE-Flow (10B) achieves 74.9% accuracy, a 10.9% improvement over same-size SOTA, while CARE-Coord reaches 77.5%, beating 32B Lingshu by 5.2%. Human evaluation shows an 82.1% pass rate versus 73.9% for GPT-4o coordinator, indicating enhanced accountability. The framework demonstrates that emulating clinicians' evidence-based workflows with decoupled specialists yields more accurate and trustworthy medical AI.

## Method Summary
The CARE framework operationalizes clinical accountability through a multi-stage pipeline. First, an entity proposal VLM identifies key anatomical structures or pathologies. Then, an expert ROI segmentation module isolates relevant regions. Next, an evidence-grounded VLM processes the image with zoom-in (local patch), mask (segmented region), and global hints (full image) to answer the question, trained via RL to maximize reward only when answers align with verifiable evidence. An optional coordinator VLM decides whether to invoke segmentation and reviews consistency among module outputs. This decoupled design ensures each step's output is interpretable and auditable, contrasting with end-to-end black-box VLMs.

## Key Results
- CARE-Flow (10B) achieves 74.9% accuracy on medical VQA benchmarks, improving 10.9% over same-size SOTA.
- CARE-Coord reaches 77.5%, outperforming 32B Lingshu by 5.2%.
- Human evaluation yields 82.1% pass rate vs. 73.9% for GPT-4o coordinator.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CARE improves accountability by structuring reasoning around verifiable evidence. The staged modules force the system to produce intermediate outputs (entity proposals, segmentation masks, evidence clues) that can be inspected and validated. RL with verifiable rewards ensures the final answer must be supportable by the generated evidence, reducing hallucination. The optional coordinator adds a layer of consistency checking, mimicking a senior clinician's review. By specializing each component, the framework avoids the opacity of monolithic VLMs and aligns more closely with clinical workflows where diagnosis is built from observable findings.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Agentic Framework**: Decomposes complex tasks into specialized, sequential modules. *Why needed*: Enables targeted fine-tuning and interpretable intermediate outputs. *Quick check*: Each agent has a well-defined input/output and success metric.
2. **Verifiable Rewards**: RL rewards based on whether the answer is supported by generated evidence (e.g., segmentation mask covers relevant region). *Why needed*: Aligns model behavior with evidence-based reasoning rather than surface-level pattern matching. *Quick check*: Can a human verify the evidence-answer link automatically?
3. **ROI Segmentation**: Isolates regions of interest from medical images. *Why needed*: Provides focused visual evidence for local detail tasks. *Quick check*: Does the mask accurately cover the pathology according to a clinician?
4. **Evidence-Grounded VQA**: VLM that answers questions using explicit visual clues (zoom-in, mask, global). *Why needed*: Forces reliance on provided evidence, reducing reliance on spurious correlations. *Quick check*: Does the VLM's answer change if irrelevant clues are provided?
5. **RL Fine-tuning**: Optimizes VLM policies using rewards from evidence verification. *Why needed*: Learns to generate answers that are consistent with evidence, not just correct labels. *Quick check*: Does performance drop when evidence is randomized?
6. **Clinical Accountability**: Ability to trace and justify decisions. *Why needed*: Critical for safety and trust in medical AI. *Quick check*: Can a clinician follow the evidence chain to understand the answer?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Entity Proposal VLM -> Expert ROI Segmentation -> Evidence-Grounded VQA -> [Optional Coordinator]
- **Critical path**: Entity proposal always runs; segmentation may be skipped by coordinator; evidence-grounded VQA always runs with available clues; coordinator optionally reviews final answer and evidence consistency.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Segmentation adds latency but is necessary for local detail tasks; coordinator improves consistency but may overwrite correct expert answers (∼3-5% error) and introduces opacity; dynamic clue selection (zoom-in, mask, global) is dataset-dependent and can harm performance if miscombined.
- **Failure signatures**: Coordinator hallucination leading to incorrect edits; ROI invoked for global-context queries producing irrelevant evidence; evidence combination (e.g., using both zoom-in and mask) degrading accuracy; smaller coordinators performing worse due to limited reasoning capacity.
- **First experiments**: 1) Process a global-context question (e.g., "What imaging modality is used?") to observe whether ROI segmentation is skipped and how evidence is used. 2) Disable the coordinator and compare raw evidence-grounded VQA outputs to coordinated outputs on a sample to quantify coordinator's impact. 3) Provide conflicting evidence (e.g., zoom-in suggests one answer, mask suggests another) to test coordinator's conflict resolution strategy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can the CARE framework be adapted for medical VQA tasks that rely primarily on global image context rather than local details?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors state: "Our method is mainly designed for diagnosis tasks that require local detail in the medical image, rather than general tasks like diagram analysis or global related tasks" and plan to extend to general tasks.
- **Why unresolved**: The current pipeline always invokes ROI segmentation, which may be unnecessary or detrimental for global queries, and the coordinator's decision to skip segmentation needs exploration.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Evaluation of CARE on global-context benchmarks; analysis of coordinator's ability to detect and bypass ROI steps; potential redesign of evidence types.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can hallucination in the coordinator model, particularly during iterative answer review, be minimized to improve trustworthiness?
- **Basis in paper**: "As discussed in Sec. D.12, our method still suffers from model hallucination, especially from the coordinator. Developing a system that is more resistant to hallucination will be our next target."
- **Why unresolved**: Coordinator edits sometimes overwrite correct expert VLM answers (∼3-5% error), and smaller coordinators perform worse, indicating a trade-off between capability and reliability.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Detailed error analysis of coordinator reviews; incorporating confidence scores from expert VLMs into review decisions; training coordinator with consistency-focused rewards.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What is the optimal strategy for selecting and combining multiple visual evidence types (zoom-in, mask, global) to maximize accuracy without excessive computational cost?
- **Basis in paper**: Ablations reveal that using both zoom-in and mask at inference can harm performance, and majority voting does not consistently beat a dynamic coordinator; input sequence length is a suspected factor.
- **Why unresolved**: Relative benefits of each clue type are dataset-dependent; dynamic selection adds latency; unclear if a lightweight learned selector could match GPT-5's performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Per-question-type analysis of clue effectiveness; developing an efficient clue-selection module; measuring accuracy-latency trade-offs.

## Limitations
- **Benchmark generalizability**: Results are on four curated VQA benchmarks; unknown if performance holds for rare conditions, diverse imaging modalities, or real-world clinical workflows.
- **Coordinator black-box risk**: The optional VLM coordinator reintroduces opacity; its decision-making process and failure modes are not analyzed.
- **Human evaluation scope**: Pass rate (82.1%) is defined by unspecified clinical criteria; unknown if this correlates with actual diagnostic utility or safety.

## Confidence
- **High**: Reported accuracy gains on specific benchmarks (CARE-Flow 74.9%, CARE-Coord 77.5%) relative to named SOTA counterparts.
- **Medium**: Claim that "decoupled specialists" improve accountability; supported by architecture but lacking ablation of each module's isolated contribution.
- **Low**: Broader assertion that the framework yields "more trustworthy medical AI"; generalizability beyond test sets and to clinical deployment remains unevidenced.

## Next Checks
1. **Out-of-distribution stress test**: Evaluate CARE on a held-out medical specialty (e.g., dermatology or pathology) with no overlap in training data to probe specialization limits.
2. **Module ablation study**: Disentangle contributions of entity proposal, ROI segmentation, and evidence-grounded VQA by removing each component to quantify their individual impact on final accuracy and evidence alignment.
3. **Coordinator transparency audit**: Analyze the VLM coordinator's internal attention maps and decision logs to verify that its planning and consistency reviews are clinically interpretable and not heuristic shortcuts.
