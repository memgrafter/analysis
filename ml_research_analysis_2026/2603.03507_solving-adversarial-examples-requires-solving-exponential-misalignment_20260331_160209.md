# Solving adversarial examples requires solving exponential misalignment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.03507
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03507
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Adversarial examples exist because machine perceptual manifolds (PMs) are exponentially misaligned with human concepts: their extremely high dimensionality (e.g., ~3060 on CIFAR10) causes them to nearly fill the input space, making any point arbitrarily close to a misclassification region.

## Executive Summary
The paper argues that adversarial vulnerability arises from a fundamental geometric mismatch: neural networks learn class-specific perceptual manifolds that occupy nearly the full ambient dimension of the input space, whereas human visual concepts reside on much lower-dimensional structures. By measuring the dimensionality of these manifolds across 18 models on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, the authors show that more robust models have lower-dimensional manifolds but still far exceed human-like dimensions (~10–20). This "exponential misalignment" means that high-dimensional manifolds fill the space, so tiny perturbations can push inputs into wrong-class regions. The work suggests that achieving true adversarial robustness requires training models with perceptual manifolds aligned to human-level dimensionality.

## Method Summary
The authors measure class-conditional perceptual manifolds (PMs) defined as the set of inputs assigned a confidence above a threshold. They sample PM points via projected gradient ascent from random noise, then estimate intrinsic dimension using participation ratio (PR) from the covariance of samples and two-nearest-neighbors (2NN). For 18 pretrained CIFAR-10 models (from RobustBench) and ImageNet models, they compute these dimensions and correlate with adversarial robustness measured by AutoAttack (L∞). They also compute typical squared Euclidean distance from uniform random points to the PM, and visualize PM samples to assess semantic content. Human PM dimensions are estimated from natural image statistics as a baseline.

## Key Results
- Machine PM dimensions are orders of magnitude higher than human concept dimensions (~3060 vs ~10–20 on CIFAR-10), meaning they nearly fill the input hypercube.
- Adversarial robustness (robust accuracy) correlates negatively with PM dimension across models: more robust models have lower-dimensional PMs.
- The squared distance from random inputs to the PM increases as PM dimension decreases, confirming that high-dimensional PMs cause vulnerability by filling ambient space.
- Even state-of-the-art robust models remain exponentially misaligned; their PM dimensions (~250) are still far from human scales, and their PM samples lack semantic coherence.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper presents three linked mechanisms:

1. **Exponential misalignment causes vulnerability**: In high-dimensional spaces, volume grows exponentially. A PM with dimension close to the ambient dimension (D) occupies almost the entire hypercube. The expected squared distance from a random point to a d-dimensional ellipsoid scales with D−d; for nearly full-dimensional PMs, this distance is vanishingly small. Thus, an arbitrarily tiny perturbation can cross into a wrong-class PM. This relies on concentration of measure.

2. **Robust training compresses PM dimensionality**: Adversarial training reduces the PM's effective dimension by eliminating directions sensitive to worst-case perturbations. Empirically, across 18 models, robust accuracy negatively correlates with both PR and 2NN estimates, showing that lower-dimensional PMs are necessary for robustness.

3. **Semantic alignment emerges only at human-like dimensions**: When PM dimension approaches human scales (~10–20), random samples from the PM become recognizable objects, indicating perceptual agreement. High-dimensional PMs yield noise-like samples; low-dimensional ones (from robust models) produce coherent parts. Power spectral density of robust PM samples also trends toward natural 1/f² decay.

## Foundational Learning
- **Perceptual Manifold (PM)**: The set of inputs confidently assigned to a class: PM = {x | p(c|x) > p₀}. *Why needed*: Central construct that defines the decision region for a class; allows geometric comparison between models and humans. *Quick check*: For a given classifier and class, how is the PM formally defined? (Answer: {x | p(c|x) > p₀} for some confidence threshold p₀.)
- **Dimensionality Metrics (PR, 2NN)**: Participation Ratio = (tr(C))²/tr(C²) from covariance C; 2NN uses log-linear regression on neighbor distance ratios. *Why needed*: Provide numerical estimates of PM intrinsic dimension for comparison. *Quick check*: Given eigenvalues λ_j of the covariance matrix, compute PR = (∑λ_j)² / ∑λ_j².
- **Adversarial Robustness**: Measured as robust accuracy under AutoAttack (L∞ with ε=8/255). *Why needed*: The practical failure mode to be explained and correlated with PM dimension. *Quick check*: What does robust accuracy measure? (Fraction of test images that cannot be misclassified by any ℓ∞ perturbation ≤ ε.)
- **High-Dimensional Volume Scaling**: In D dimensions, expected squared distance between two random points in [0,1]^D is D/6; distance to a d-dimensional subset scales with D−d. *Why needed*: Underlies the exponential misalignment hypothesis; explains why high-d manifolds fill space. *Quick check*: How does expected squared distance to a d-dimensional ellipsoid scale with D−d? (≈ (D−d)/6 plus other terms.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: ModelLoader → RobustEvaluator → PMSampler → DimEstimator → DistanceCalculator → Analyzer
- **Critical path**:
  1. Select models spanning robust accuracies; download weights.
  2. Compute robust accuracy on clean test set using AutoAttack.
  3. For each model and class, sample PM with PMSampler (ensure convergence to confidence threshold p₀).
  4. Compute PR and 2NN from class sample sets.
  5. Compute average squared distance from uniform random points to nearest PM sample.
  6. Aggregate metrics, plot dimension vs robustness and vs distance.
  7. Repeat for other datasets with adjusted hyperparameters.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - PM sampling: More iterations/smaller steps improve success for robust models but increase compute; Adam optimizer may help.
  - Sample size: PR needs many samples (≥10⁵) for stable covariance; 2NN requires large N (up to 10⁶) but may underestimate dimension.
  - Distance precision: More random starts improve estimate but cost more; fixed budget (e.g., 100 starts) suffices for relative comparisons.
  - Confidence threshold p₀: Must be high enough to exclude natural images (e.g., 0.9 for CIFAR) but not so high that PM is unreachable.
  - High-dimensional covariance: For ImageNet (D=150528), full covariance infeasible; use random projection or trace estimators.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - PGA fails to reach p₀ for many starts (common in robust models). Remedy: increase max steps, reduce step size, switch to Adam, add Gaussian noise.
  - Dimensionality estimates (PR/2NN) vary widely or are unrealistically low. Check convergence plots; if 2NN rises with sample size, true dimension is higher.
  - Robust accuracy differs from reported values; could be gradient masking. Verify AutoAttack configuration matches model training.
  - Memory overflow when computing covariance. Use dimensionality reduction or trace estimators.
  - PM samples are pure noise despite low reported dimension. Ensure diverse random starts; avoid saturation at decision boundary.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Replicate Figure 2**: For standard WideResNet-28-10 (0% robust), sample PMs for all CIFAR-10 classes (p₀=0.9) with N≥50,000 samples per class. Compute PR/2NN; expect machine PR ~3000 vs natural images ~10.
  2. **Replicate Figure 6**: For all 18 RobustBench CIFAR-10 models, compute average PM dimensions (PR and 2NN) and plot against reported L∞ robust accuracy. Verify negative correlation; compute Pearson coefficient.
  3. **Replicate Figure 7**: For each class, compute typical squared distance from 10⁴ random points to PM. Plot distance vs PR; expect distance to increase as dimension decreases. Also compare distance from natural images to validate proxy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can we develop explicit training objectives that directly minimize PM dimensionality?**  
   *Basis*: The paper shows robust training indirectly lowers PM dimension but even the best models remain misaligned (human dimensions ~10–20 vs model ~250+).  
   *Why unresolved*: No known objective explicitly penalizes PM dimension during training.  
   *Resolving evidence*: A training procedure that achieves PR ≤ 25 on CIFAR-10 while maintaining ≥85% clean accuracy and significantly improved robust accuracy under strong attacks.

2. **Does exponential misalignment persist in language models?**  
   *Basis*: The discussion suggests extending to language; for a given next token, many high-probability gibberish sequences might exist.  
   *Why unresolved*: PMs for text are not defined or measured; LLM architectures differ from vision models.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Define and estimate "token prediction manifold" for specific next tokens in LLMs, and show these manifolds occupy exponentially more dimensions than human-written continuations, correlating with adversarial prompt success.

3. **Which components of adversarial training causally reduce PM dimensionality?**  
   *Basis*: The paper analyzes 18 diverse robust models and finds a negative correlation, but models vary in architecture, loss, augmentation, etc.  
   *Why unresolved*: No ablation isolating factors like loss type, augmentation strength, architecture.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Systematic ablation on a fixed architecture (e.g., ResNet-50) varying one component at a time while measuring PM dimensionality.

4. **What architectural modifications could reduce PM dimension to human-like levels?**  
   *Basis*: The best models still have PR ~250, far from ~10–20; human-level dimension may be necessary for alignment.  
   *Why unresolved*: No proposals or tests of architectures specifically targeting dimensional alignment.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Design a vision model (e.g., with low-dimensional latent spaces, capsules) achieving PR < 30 on ImageNet while maintaining ≥75% clean accuracy and >90% robust accuracy under AutoAttack with ε=8/255.

5. **Which geometric properties beyond global dimension predict robustness?**  
   *Basis*: The paper measures eigenvector alignment and power spectral density (PSD); robust models show "sparks of alignment" but not as strongly correlated as global dimension.  
   *Why unresolved*: It's unclear whether improving PSD or eigenalignment would improve robustness or are just consequences of lower dimension.  
   *Resolving evidence*: Train models with explicit regularization for PSD decay or eigenvector alignment, and test if they achieve higher robust accuracy at equivalent PM dimension compared to standard adversarial training.

## Limitations
- **PM sampling fidelity**: Projected gradient ascent may not fully converge to the true decision boundary, especially for robust models, potentially under-estimating PM dimension and volume.
- **Human PM dimension anchor**: The human concept dimension (~10–20) is inferred from prior natural image statistics and may not directly correspond to class-conditional PMs; it is dataset- and task-specific.
- **Causality vs. correlation**: The negative correlation between PM dimension and robustness is strong but does not prove that reducing dimension alone is sufficient; other factors like margin or feature smoothness may confound.
- **Distance metric validity**: The claim that distance to PM predicts adversarial vulnerability assumes the PM is the closest misclassification region, which may not hold for all inputs or threat models (e.g., targeted attacks).

## Confidence
- **Claim 1 (Exponential misalignment mechanism)**: Medium. The geometric argument is theoretically sound, but its causal role depends on accurate PM sampling and distance measurements.
- **Claim 2 (Negative correlation between PM dim and robustness)**: High. Supported by extensive empirical results across 18 models and two datasets; likely robust to sampling variations.
- **Claim 3 (Semantic alignment via low-dim PM samples)**: Low-Medium. Recognizability assessment is qualitative and not human-validated; spectral trends are suggestive but not conclusive.

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary PGA hyperparameters (step size, optimizer, max steps, confidence threshold p₀) for a subset of models (most and least robust). Quantify impact on PR/2NN and check convergence to stable dimension estimates.
2. **Distance-to-PM vs. empirical perturbation size**: For a diverse set of natural images, compute both the reported squared distance to the PM and the actual minimum ℓ∞ perturbation needed to cause misclassification (via binary search). Validate the predictive linear relationship between D−d and required perturbation magnitude.
3. **Human evaluation of PM samples**: Conduct a blinded study where human participants label random PM samples from models across the robustness spectrum. Quantify recognizability (e.g., % labeled as the target class) and correlate with measured PM dimension to test alignment objectively.
