# $\mathcal{X}$-KD: General Experiential Knowledge Distillation for Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.12674
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12674
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** X-KD consistently outperforms baseline KD methods (GKD, MiniLLM, SKD, SeqKD) across summarization, translation, and reasoning tasks, with gains up to ~0.5% in ROUGE-2/BLEU and improved performance-diversity trade-off.

## Executive Summary
Large language model (LLM) knowledge distillation typically ignores the teacher model’s original learning environment, leading to suboptimal student imitation. This paper introduces $\mathcal{X}$-KD (General Experiential Knowledge Distillation), which adopts the Approximated Variational Reward Imitation Learning (AVRIL) framework to jointly model the teacher’s unknown reward function and distill the student policy. By adding an experiential regularization term to standard KD losses (sequence-level, forward-KL, or GKD), $\mathcal{X}$-KD ensures the student aligns with the teacher’s environmental reward structure. Experiments on abstractive summarization (XSum), machine translation (WMT), and arithmetic reasoning (GSM8k) show consistent improvements over baselines, better performance-diversity trade-offs, and improved data efficiency.

## Method Summary
$\mathcal{X}$-KD formulates knowledge distillation within a Markov Decision Process (MDP) where the teacher’s optimal policy $\pi$ is assumed to maximize an unknown reward function $R_\pi$. Using Bayesian inverse reinforcement learning (AVRIL), the method learns a variational posterior $q_\phi(R)$ over rewards given the teacher’s demonstrations, while simultaneously training a student Boltzmann policy $B_\theta$ (derived from a Q-value decoder $Q_\theta$). The training objective augments any standard KD loss $L_{kd}$ (e.g., sequence-level, forward-KL, or generalized Jensen-Shannon) with an experiential regularization term $L_{ex}$ that enforces consistency between the student’s TD-errors and the learned reward posterior. This unified ELBO-based approach allows the student to maximize the teacher’s original reward rather than merely mimicking behavior. The method is applicable to both white-box (teacher logits available) and black-box (only sampled outputs) settings.

## Key Results
- On large→base distillation (T5 models), G-XKD (with GKD base) achieves higher ROUGE-2 (16.24 vs 16.18), BLEU (27.61 vs 27.43), and GSM8k accuracy (13.63 vs 13.48) than GKD.
- $\mathcal{X}$-KD variants consistently outperform competitive baselines (MiniLLM, SKD, SeqKD) across all three tasks.
- In black-box distillation (Llama-2-7b → T5-large), SeqXKD attains a 48.3% win rate vs SeqKD on XSum by GPT-4 evaluation, and requires ~25% less training data to match baseline performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
$\mathcal{X}$-KD’s gains stem from three interconnected mechanisms. First, it adopts Bayesian IRL (AVRIL) to jointly infer the teacher’s latent reward function $R_\pi$ and train the student policy, moving beyond simple behavior cloning. The Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) combines maximizing the likelihood of teacher behaviors under the student’s Boltzmann policy and regularizing the reward posterior toward a prior, with a TD-error term linking the two. Second, the experiential regularization $L_{ex}$ enforces that the student’s Q-values (and thus its policy) are consistent with the recovered reward structure, not just the teacher’s action distributions. The term includes a KL divergence to keep the reward posterior well-behaved and a negative log-likelihood of the TD-error under the posterior, which approximates the Bellman constraint. Third, the formulation is composable: any KD loss expressible as a distribution divergence (e.g., $D_{KL}$, $D_{JS}$) can be extended by adding $L_{ex}$, yielding supervised or generalized X-KD losses that retain the original optimization landscape while incorporating reward awareness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Markov Decision Process (MDP) for text generation:** Text generation is modeled as an MDP with state $(x, y_{1:t})$, action $y_{t+1}$, and reward $R(x,y)$. This formalism is essential to define the teacher’s “original learning environment” and derive the Bellman/TD constraints.  
  *Quick check:* Given a prompt $x$ and partial output $y_{1:t}$, what is the state $s$ and action $a$? How does the teacher’s optimal policy relate to $R_\pi$?
- **Knowledge Distillation (KD) variants:** Understanding sequence-level (e.g., $D_{KL}(\pi||B_\theta)$) vs. divergence-based (forward-KL, Jensen-Shannon) KD losses is crucial because $\mathcal{X}$-KD can extend any of them by adding $L_{ex}$. Sequence-level KD minimizes negative log-likelihood of teacher-generated sequences, while divergence-based methods align token distributions.  
  *Quick check:* What distinguishes sequence-level KD from supervised token-wise KD? How does GKD combine on-policy and off-policy data?
- **Bayesian Inverse Reinforcement Learning (BIRL) and AVRIL:** BIRL infers the posterior $p(R|\pi)$ over rewards given a policy. AVRIL provides a practical variational approximation via an ELBO that includes a TD-error term to connect reward and Q-function. $\mathcal{X}$-KD directly leverages this to recover $R_\pi$.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Teacher policy $\pi$ (fixed) → provides demonstration data $(x,y)$ → trains student policy $B_\theta$ and reward approximator $q_\phi$. The student policy is derived from a Q-value decoder $Q_\theta$ (computed from student logits); $Q_\theta$ and $q_\phi$ are coupled via the TD-error constraint in $L_{ex}$.

**Critical path (white-box):**  
1. Initialize student $p_\theta$ (SFT) and reward network $q_\phi$.  
2. For each iteration, sample a prompt $x$. With probability $\alpha$, sample on-policy $y \sim p_\theta(\cdot|x)$; otherwise sample off-policy $(x,y)$ from SFT data or teacher (if available).  
3. For the generated $(x,y)$, compute token-wise student logits, derive $Q_\theta$ via $Q_\theta((x,y_{1:t}), y_{t+1}) = \log \text{softmax}(\tau' \cdot p_\theta(y_{t+1}|x,y_{1:t}))$, and compute TD-errors $\delta_t(\theta) = Q_\theta(s_t,a_t) - \gamma Q_\theta(s_{t+1},a_{t+1})$.  
4. Pass each state-action $(s_t,a_t)$ to $q_\phi$ to obtain reward distribution $q_\phi(\cdot|s_t,a_t)$. Compute $L_{ex}$ as the sum over tokens of $D_{KL}[q_\phi(\cdot|s_t,a_t)||p(R)] - \lambda \log q_\phi(\delta_t(\theta)|s_t,a_t)$.  
5. Compute base KD loss $L_{kd}$ (e.g., $D_\beta(\pi||B_\theta)$ or $L_{seq}$).  
6. Total loss $L = L_{kd} + L_{ex}$; update $\theta$ and $\phi$ via gradient descent. Repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Experiential weight $\lambda$: Controls regularization strength; too low → no benefit, too high → performance collapse (optimal ~0.001 in experiments).  
- Boltzmann temperature $\tau'$: Scales $Q_\theta$ relative to $p_\theta$; default 1.0 but affects $L_{ex}$ magnitude.  
- Reward prior $p(R)$: Typically Gaussian; its variance influences $D_{KL}$ term.  
- On-policy weight $\alpha$ (for GKD): Balances on-policy vs. off-policy data; higher $\alpha$ improves diversity but may add noise.  
- Computational overhead: $L_{ex}$ requires extra forward passes through $q_\phi$ and computing $Q_\theta$ per token, increasing memory by ~2-3× over standard GKD.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Training instability (loss spikes/NaN) often due to $\lambda$ too high or $q_\phi$ producing degenerate distributions; monitor $L_{ex}$ and $q_\phi$ stats.  
- No improvement or degradation vs. baseline when $\lambda$ mis-set or reward prior mis-specified; sweep $\lambda$.  
- Poor performance on small student models (e.g., large→small) may indicate capacity bottleneck for learning $q_\phi$.  
- Black-box distillation fails if teacher trajectory set is too small/noisy; ensure diverse demonstrations.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Sanity check:** Train G-XKD with $\lambda=0$ on a small subset (e.g., 100 XSum examples). Verify loss equals GKD and performance matches baseline. Then increase $\lambda$ to 0.001; confirm $L_{ex}>0$ and improved performance.  
2. **$\lambda$ ablation:** Train SeqXKD, S-XKD, G-XKD for $\lambda \in \{0, 0.0005, 0.001, 0.002\}$ on XSum (large→base). Plot ROUGE-2 vs. $\lambda$; expect peak near 0.001. Identify failure at higher $\lambda$.  
3. **TD-error necessity:** Train G-XKD but remove the $-\lambda \log q_\phi(\delta_\theta)$ term from $L_{ex}$, keeping only $D_{KL}[q_\phi||p]$. Performance should drop significantly, proving the TD constraint’s importance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Automatic setting of $\lambda$:** How can the experiential weight be tuned automatically or adapted during training to reduce hyperparameter sensitivity? The paper notes sensitivity but provides no adaptive scheme.  
- **Black-box distillation scalability:** How does $\mathcal{X}$-KD perform across a wider range of tasks, teacher models, and data scales in true black-box settings? Current experiments are limited to a single teacher (Llama-2-7b) and small data.  
- **Interpretability of learned reward:** Does the reward function $q_\phi(R)$ correspond to meaningful, task-aligned features? The paper never analyzes the posterior; it is treated as a latent mechanism.  
- **MDP design choices:** How do design decisions (token-level rewards, discount factor $\gamma=1$, TD-error formulation) affect performance, especially for tasks with long-range dependencies? Could alternative MDP formulations (e.g., sequence-level rewards) yield improvements?

## Limitations
- **Implementation details underspecified:** Exact architectures and training of $q_\phi$ and $Q_\theta$, reward prior specifics (dimensionality, variance), and on-policy sampling procedure are not fully described, hindering reproduction.  
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Performance is sensitive to $\lambda$ and to a lesser extent $\tau'$; optimal values may vary across tasks and model sizes, requiring extensive tuning.  
- **Limited black-box evaluation:** The method’s applicability in black-box settings is only preliminarily explored with a single teacher and small data; practical utility at scale remains unproven.  
- **Computational overhead:** The experiential term roughly triples memory consumption compared to standard GKD, which may be prohibitive for very large models.

## Confidence
- AVRIL-based reward modeling: **Medium** (relies on assumptions of optimality and Bellman equation; limited empirical validation of recovered rewards).  
- Experiential regularization ($L_{ex}$): **Medium** (effective but mechanism not fully dissected; TD-error term critical but approximate).  
- Composability across KD losses: **High** (derivation is solid and general).  
- Performance gains on standard tasks: **Medium** (results are consistent but modest; improvements small and may be task-dependent).  
- Data efficiency & diversity: **Medium** (demonstrated but not deeply analyzed).

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sweep:** Systematically sweep $\lambda \in [10^{-4}, 10^{-2}]$, $\tau' \in [0.5, 2]$, and on-policy weight $\alpha \in \{0,0.25,0.5\}$ on large→base distillation for T5 models on XSum and WMT to identify robust defaults and interactions.
2. **Ablation of $L_{ex}$ components:** Isolate the TD-error term by training G-XKD with $L_{ex}$ = (only $D_{KL}[q_\phi||p]$) and with fixed $q_\phi$ (e.g., constant Gaussian) to quantify each component’s contribution.
3. **Extension to decoder-only models:** Reproduce $\mathcal{X}$-KD with decoder-only LLMs (e.g., GPT-2, Llama-2) on diverse tasks (dialogue, instruction following) and benchmarks (e.g., MT-Bench) to test generalizability beyond encoder-decoder architectures.
