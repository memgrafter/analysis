# Assessing Electricity Demand Forecasting with Exogenous Data in Time Series Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05390
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05390
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** Among evaluated time-series foundation models, Chronos-2 achieves the best zero-shot performance in leveraging exogenous features for electricity demand forecasting, but a simple RevIN-LSTM baseline frequently outperforms all foundation models in Singapore's climatically stable context for short-term horizons.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates the capability of recent time-series foundation models to incorporate exogenous data (e.g., weather) for electricity demand forecasting. Using datasets from Singapore and Australia, the authors compare several foundation models—MOIRAI, MOMENT, TinyTimeMixers (TTM), ChronosX, and Chronos-2—against a RevIN-LSTM baseline. The results reveal that model architecture critically determines how effectively exogenous features are used. Chronos-2, with its grouped attention mechanism, performs best among foundation models, particularly in zero-shot settings. However, the simple LSTM baseline often surpasses foundation models in Singapore's climatically stable environment for forecasts up to 48 hours. The geographic variability of the target market emerges as a key factor, with foundation models showing advantages primarily in Australia's more volatile climate. These findings challenge the assumption of universal superiority for foundation models in energy forecasting and highlight the need for domain-specific architectural considerations.

## Method Summary
The study uses hourly and daily electricity demand data from Singapore (2016–2022) and Australia’s ACT region (2015–2023). Each dataset includes demand, calendar features, and weather variables; the Australian data also incorporates air-quality metrics. After a fixed 60/20/20 train/validation/test split, sliding windows of context length 512 are generated to produce samples for forecast horizons ranging from 1 hour/day up to 336 hours/365 days. Three feature configurations are constructed: all available variables (~30), a selected subset of 7–10 features based on Spearman correlation, and target-only. Models evaluated include a RevIN-LSTM baseline and five foundation models (MOMENT, MOIRAI, TTM, ChronosX, Chronos-2). MOMENT, MOIRAI, TTM, and ChronosX are fine-tuned on the training data using a standardized training protocol (MSE loss, Adam optimizer with learning rate 0.001, maximum 100 epochs, early stopping with patience 10, batch size 16). MOIRAI is also evaluated in a zero-shot setting, as is Chronos-2, which was not fine-tuned. Performance is measured primarily via Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) aggregated over the test set for each horizon.

## Key Results
- Chronos-2 achieves the best performance among the foundation models in zero-shot settings, with its grouped attention architecture providing consistent benefits from exogenous features.
- The RevIN-LSTM baseline frequently outperforms all foundation models in Singapore's climatically stable context for short-term horizons (up to 48 hours), indicating that simple models can suffice when exogenous influences are minimal.
- Architectural design is critical for exogenous feature utilization: TTM's channel-mixing during fine-tuning and Chronos-2's grouped attention show clear advantages, whereas models like MOIRAI and MOMENT exhibit inconsistent or negative impacts from added covariates.
- Geographic context is a major determinant: foundation models demonstrate their largest advantages in Australia's variable climate, while benefits diminish in stable Singapore.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Chronos-2's Grouped Attention for Cross-Channel Modeling
Chronos-2 employs a grouped attention mechanism that partitions input channels into groups and applies attention within and across these groups. This architecture natively supports multivariate forecasting with covariates without requiring fine-tuning, allowing it to capture both intra-group and inter-channel dependencies. The core assumption is that exogenous features contain predictive information that manifests through cross-channel interactions. Evidence shows Chronos-2 achieves the lowest MAPE among foundation models in zero-shot evaluations, and its design is explicitly intended for covariate-informed forecasting.

### Mechanism 2: TTM's Fine-Tuning Channel-Mixing
TinyTimeMixers (TTM) uses channel-independent pre-training but activates a channel-mixer block (based on MLP-Mixer) exclusively during fine-tuning. This allows the model to learn domain-specific interactions between the target series and exogenous variables in the target market. The mechanism relies on fine-tuning to adapt the pre-trained representations to local cross-channel relationships. Evidence indicates that enabling channel-mixing during fine-tuning improves forecast accuracy when exogenous features are informative, though performance can degrade if the mixer introduces noise or is poorly tuned.

### Mechanism 3: Simplicity Wins in Stable Climates
In climatically stable environments like Singapore, electricity demand is driven primarily by strong, predictable temporal patterns (daily, weekly cycles) with minimal modulation from exogenous factors such as temperature. A simple model like RevIN-LSTM, which captures these autoregressive and periodic dynamics through instance normalization and recurrent layers, is both effective and efficient. The added complexity of foundation models, optimized for diverse and variable patterns during pre-training, provides little to no benefit and may even overfit. This is evidenced by the LSTM baseline consistently achieving lower errors than foundation models for short horizons in Singapore.

## Architecture Onboarding
Data Layer -> Model Layer -> Experiment Layer -> Evaluation Layer -> Visualization Layer

**Critical path:**
1. Acquire and preprocess raw data (resampling, merging weather, handling missing values) for Singapore and Australia, generating three feature sets.
2. Set up environment and load model weights for RevIN-LSTM and all foundation models (MOMENT, MOIRAI, TTM, ChronosX, Chronos-2).
3. Create sliding window samples with context length 512 and defined horizons for each dataset and feature configuration.
4. Fine-tune models that require it (MOMENT, MOIRAI, TTM, ChronosX) using standardized hyperparameters (Adam lr=0.001, epochs=100, early stopping patience=10, batch size 16).
5. Run zero-shot inference for models that support it (MOIRAI-ZS, Chronos2-ZS) directly on test samples.
6. Evaluate predictions against true values using MAPE for each horizon and feature set.
7. Aggregate results, compute comparative statistics, and generate tables/figures.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Feature set selection (all vs. selected) balances information against noise; correlation-based selection may miss nonlinear relationships.
- Model selection is limited to those supporting exogenous inputs; univariate foundation models are excluded, potentially overlooking simpler alternatives.
- MAPE is chosen as the primary metric for its interpretability, though it can be unstable near zero demand values.
- A single fixed train/val/test split (60/20/20) is used instead of time-series cross-validation due to computational constraints.
- Fine-tuning strategies vary (e.g., TTM requires horizon-specific revisions, Chronos-2 was not fine-tuned), which may affect comparability.

**Failure signatures:**
- Inconsistent performance across horizons for a single model (e.g., ChronosX with exogenous features at horizon 1) suggests architectural limitations in handling certain forecast lengths or feature sets.
- RevIN-LSTM outperforming foundation models in a variable climate (Australia) would indicate foundation models failing to leverage exogenous signals effectively.
- Large variance across random seeds (not reported) would point to training instability, especially in fine-tuning.
- Extremely high MAPE values often signal data preprocessing errors (e.g., mismatched timezones, incorrect feature alignment).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline check: Train and evaluate RevIN-LSTM on Singapore hourly data with all features vs. target-only. Expected outcome: Small or negative improvement from adding features.
2. Foundation model sanity check: Run Chronos-2 zero-shot on Australia daily data with selected features. Expected: Best MAPE among foundation models and improvement over target-only.
3. Architectural ablation: For TTM on Australia hourly, compare fine-tuning with channel-mixing enabled vs. disabled. Expected: Channel-mixing yields lower MAPE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Why do foundation models underperform simple LSTM baselines in stable climates?** The paper shows this phenomenon empirically in Singapore but does not diagnose whether it stems from pretraining data distribution, architectural inductive biases, or an overparameterization issue.
2. **Which architectural mechanisms most reliably enable exogenous feature use?** While TTM's channel-mixing and Chronos-2's grouped attention show benefits, the comparison is between full model families, making it unclear which component is causal.
3. **Can models adapt reliance on exogenous features based on context volatility?** The paper implies foundation models may be optimized for variable markets, but it does not test whether models can internally detect stability and adjust feature usage.
4. **What design principles ensure adapter-based models (e.g., ChronosX) transfer univariate knowledge to multivariate tasks?** ChronosX exhibits unpredictable performance with covariates; the study does not isolate the role of adapter placement, capacity, or training regime.
5. **What pretraining data composition is optimal for energy-domain foundation models?** The paper suggests generalist pretraining pools may not represent global energy market diversity adequately, but it does not analyze existing pretraining corpora or propose a targeted strategy.

## Limitations
- Exact RevIN-LSTM architecture (layers, hidden units) and regularization details are unspecified.
- Fine-tuning specifics for foundation models (e.g., learning rate schedules, adapter configurations) rely on default repository settings that may change.
- Data acquisition pipelines for weather and air-quality sources (APIs, interpolation methods, timezone handling) are not fully documented, hindering exact replication.
- The precise lists of "selected" features per dataset are not provided; only summary counts are given.

## Confidence
- **Chronos-2 superiority among foundation models:** Medium (based on zero-shot results; fine-tuning might alter ranking).
- **LSTM baseline outperforms in Singapore short-term:** Medium (limited to one stable region and short horizons).
- **Architecture determines exogenous feature utilization:** Medium (plausible but entangled with training details).
- **Geographic variability as a key factor:** Medium (based on only two regions).
- **Challenge to universal foundation model superiority:** Medium (premature without broader benchmarks).

## Next Checks
1. **Full replication:** Implement the RevIN-LSTM with a documented architecture and rerun all models on identically preprocessed data (same splits, feature engineering) to verify reported MAPE values.
2. **Fine-tuning sensitivity:** Systematically vary learning rates, epochs, and early-stopping patience for TTM and ChronosX to assess whether performance gaps with the LSTM baseline persist or narrow under optimized tuning.
3. **Geographic extension:** Apply the same pipeline to a third region with a distinct climate (e.g., a temperate zone) to test whether the observed pattern—foundation models better in variable climates, LSTM better in stable climates—holds more generally.
