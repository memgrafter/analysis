# Deadline-Aware, Energy-Efficient Control of Domestic Immersion Hot Water Heater

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.18123
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18123
- **Reference count**: 5
- **Primary result**: PPO reduces energy consumption by up to 69% compared to bang‑bang control under a 2‑hour heating horizon.

## Executive Summary
Domestic immersion hot water heaters often operate wastefully by heating continuously without regard to demand deadlines or thermal losses. This paper introduces a reinforcement learning approach using a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent trained in a Gymnasium environment that models first-order thermal dynamics. The agent learns to schedule heating only as needed to meet a target temperature by a specified deadline, thereby minimizing energy use. Evaluated against time-optimal bang‑bang and zero‑shot Monte Carlo Tree Search baselines, PPO achieves the lowest energy consumption, with savings of 26–69% over bang‑bang and up to 33% over MCTS. After training, inference cost is negligible, making the approach practical for low‑cost embedded controllers.

## Method Summary
The authors built a custom Gymnasium environment simulating a domestic immersion heater with first‑order thermal dynamics, where the state includes current water temperature and remaining time until deadline. Actions are discrete (heater on/off) taken every 120 seconds. Three control strategies were compared: a time‑optimal bang‑bang controller, a zero‑shot MCTS planner that uses the model for lookahead, and a PPO agent trained via reinforcement learning. The PPO agent was trained across a sweep of initial temperatures, deadlines, and target temperatures to ensure robustness. Training used a standard advantage estimator and clipped objective to stabilize updates. The environment’s reward function penalized energy consumption while encouraging meeting the deadline with minimal overshoot.

## Key Results
- For a 60‑step (2‑hour) horizon targeting 60°C, PPO consumed 3.23 kWh versus bang‑bang’s 4.37–10.45 kWh and MCTS’s 4.18–6.46 kWh.
- PPO achieved energy savings of 26% to 69% compared to bang‑bang and up to 33% compared to MCTS.
- The learned policy delays heating until necessary, avoiding overshoot and standby losses.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The PPO agent learns to postpone heating until the last possible moment while still guaranteeing the target temperature by the deadline. By maximizing reward that penalizes energy use, the policy avoids unnecessary heating cycles and reduces thermal losses that occur when the heater is on longer than needed or when the water cools before use. The discrete action interval (120 s) provides sufficient granularity to control the temperature trajectory efficiently, and the first‑order dynamics capture the essential heat transfer behavior, enabling the agent to discover a near‑optimal schedule.

- **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)**: A model‑based planner that simulates trajectories to select actions. *Why needed*: Offers a strong zero‑shot baseline for discrete‑action problems. *Quick check*: Does the planner rely on a learned or known model?

- **Discrete Action Control**: The heater can only be fully on or off. *Why needed*: Matches the binary nature of typical immersion switches. *Quick check*: Is the action space {0,1}?  

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: `ThermalEnv (state) -> PPO policy network -> On/Off action -> ThermalEnv (next state)`  
- **Critical path**: During training, the agent observes the current temperature and time remaining, selects an action via the policy, the environment updates temperature via first‑order dynamics and computes a reward; trajectories are buffered and used for PPO updates. During deployment, only the forward pass of the policy network is needed.  
- **Design tradeoffs**: Using a simplified first‑order model reduces simulation speed but sacrifices realism; the 120‑second action interval limits control precision but matches typical heater cycles; training requires many interactions but yields a lightweight inference model.  
- **Failure signatures**: Overshooting the target temperature, missing the deadline (final temperature below target), excessive energy consumption relative to baselines, and training instability (e.g., sudden loss spikes or divergence).  
- **Three first experiments**:  
  1. **Seed sensitivity** – train PPO with at least 5 random seeds and compare mean and variance of energy consumption.  
  2. **Baseline comparison** – evaluate PPO, MCTS, and bang‑bang across a grid of deadlines and initial temperatures to confirm consistent superiority.  
  3. **Action frequency ablation** – test control intervals of 60 s, 120 s, and 300 s to quantify the impact on energy savings and policy stability.

## Limitations
- Relies on a simplified first‑order thermal model, ignoring stratification, variable ambient conditions, and stochastic user draws.
- Evaluation horizon limited to 2 hours; long‑term performance over day‑long cycles is unknown.
- Results are simulation‑only; no physical validation on real hardware.

## Confidence
- PPO achieves lower energy consumption than baselines under tested conditions: **High**  
- PPO learns to delay heating until necessary, avoiding overshoot and standby losses: **High**  
- Findings generalize to real domestic immersion heaters: **Low**  
- PPO inference cost is negligible after training: **High**
