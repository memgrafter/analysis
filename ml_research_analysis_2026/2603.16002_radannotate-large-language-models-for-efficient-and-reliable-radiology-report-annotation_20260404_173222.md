# RadAnnotate: Large Language Models for Efficient and Reliable Radiology Report Annotation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16002
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16002
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** An LLM-based framework that trains entity-specific classifiers, uses RAG-synthetic data to boost performance on rare entities in low-resource settings, and applies confidence-based selective automation to auto-annotate 55–90% of reports with high precision, reducing expert effort by 55–60%.

## Executive Summary
RadAnnotate is a framework for automating radiology report annotation according to the RadGraph schema using large language models. It addresses the high cost of manual labeling by training separate binary classifiers for four entity types (anatomy, observations with and without certainty, and uncertain observations), generating synthetic training data for scarce entities via retrieval-augmented generation, and implementing a selective automation pipeline that routes low-confidence predictions for expert review. Evaluated on the RadGraph corpus, the gold-trained classifiers achieve an aggregate F1 of 0.8747, with uncertain observations (OBS-U) being the hardest at 0.7500. Synthetic augmentation improves OBS-U F1 from 0.61 to 0.70 under a low-resource setting (50 gold reports). By setting entity-specific confidence thresholds, the system can automatically annotate 55–90% of reports at an entity match score of 0.86–0.92, thereby reducing required expert effort by an estimated 55–60% on large corpora.

## Method Summary
RadAnnotate processes radiology reports by first splitting them into individual sentences. For each of the four RadGraph entity types, it fine-tunes a separate Qwen2.5-7B model using QLoRA, converting the task into binary presence classification per sentence. To address data scarcity for rare entities, especially uncertain observations (OBS-U), the framework employs a retrieval-augmented generation pipeline: it extracts medical keywords from the training corpus, retrieves semantically similar gold sentences using FAISS with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings, and prompts Mixtral-8x7B to generate synthetic sentences along with token-level RadGraph labels. A Qwen2.5-32B judge validates and corrects the synthetic annotations to ensure quality. The synthetic data can then augment gold training examples. For deployment, each entity-specific classifier outputs a confidence score (sigmoid probability). Thresholds for auto-acceptance are determined by sweeping the validation set to find the minimum confidence that achieves a target entity match score (TP/(TP+FP+FN)). At inference, a report is automatically annotated only if all its predicted entities exceed their respective thresholds; otherwise, the report is deferred for expert review. Conflicting entity predictions across models are resolved by selecting the prediction with the highest confidence margin.

## Key Results
- Gold-trained entity-specific classifiers achieve an aggregate F1 of 0.8747; OBS-U remains the hardest category with F1 0.7500.
- In a low-resource scenario (50 gold reports), augmenting with synthetic data improves OBS-U F1 from 0.61 to 0.70; synthetic-only models perform within 1–2 F1 points of gold-only models for other entities.
- Confidence-based selective automation enables auto-annotation of 55–90% of reports at an entity match score of 0.86–0.92, reducing expert effort by an estimated 55–60%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Retrieval-augmented synthetic reports improve rare-entity learning
- **Claim:** Retrieval-augmented synthetic reports can improve performance for rare entity types (e.g., OBS-U) under severe data scarcity.
- **Mechanism:** A RAG pipeline retrieves semantically similar gold reports using medical keywords as queries. These few-shot examples guide an LLM generator (Mixtral-8x7B) to produce synthetic radiology sentences with token-level RadGraph-style entity labels. An LLM judge (Qwen2.5-32B) validates and corrects the annotations, yielding a clean synthetic dataset used to augment scarce gold training examples.
- **Core assumption:** Synthetic reports preserve the lexical and contextual patterns of real radiology text for the target entity, providing valid supervision even when gold positives are very few.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract reports that synthetic augmentation is especially helpful for uncertain observations in a low-resource setting, improving F1 from 0.61 to 0.70. Table 3 in the section "Incremental Synthetic Augmentation Under Low-Resource Conditions" shows monotonic F1 gains for OBS-U as synthetic data increases from 0% to 100% relative to 50 gold reports. Corpus signals show related work on synthetic data but not on RAG for RadGraph-style annotation.
- **Break condition:** If synthetic generation fails to capture key uncertainty cues (e.g., "possible", "likely") or introduces systematic boundary errors, augmentation would harm rather than help performance on rare entities.

### Mechanism 2: Entity-specific confidence thresholds enable selective automation
- **Claim:** Entity-specific confidence thresholds enable selective automation by filtering high-certainty predictions while routing ambiguous cases for expert review.
- **Mechanism:** Each entity-specific classifier outputs a probability via sigmoid. Thresholds are learned on a validation set by sweeping confidence levels and recording the minimum threshold achieving a target entity match score (TP/(TP+FP+FN)). At deployment, a report is auto-accepted only if its predicted entities all exceed their entity-specific thresholds; otherwise it is deferred.
- **Core assumption:** Model confidence well-calibrates with annotation correctness for each entity type, and thresholds learned on a validation set generalize to the full corpus.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states that the system can automatically annotate 55–90% of reports at 0.86–0.92 entity match score while routing low-confidence cases for expert review. Tables 6(a) and 6(b) in the section "Confidence Scoring and Threshold Discovery" show coverage and match scores at different threshold configurations; stricter thresholds reduce coverage but increase automated match score. Neighbors mention LLM calibration challenges; RadAnnotate's post-hoc isotonic calibration improved ECE, supporting the assumption that raw logits may be overconfident.
- **Break condition:** If confidence is poorly calibrated (e.g., overconfident on false positives) or thresholds do not transfer across institutions/settings, automation could introduce unacceptable error rates in the auto-accepted set.

### Mechanism 3: Entity-specific classifiers avoid dominance by high-frequency categories
- **Claim:** Training separate entity-specific classifiers avoids dominance by high-frequency categories and improves learning on rare entities compared to a unified model.
- **Mechanism:** Four independent Qwen2.5-7B models are instruction-tuned, each with binary labels (presence/absence) for one entity type. This eliminates competition between entities during training and allows each model to see a natural distribution of positive and negative examples tailored to its label.
- **Core assumption:** RadGraph entity types are semantically distinct enough that specialized models capture category-specific linguistic patterns more effectively than a multi-class or joint model.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract notes that they train entity-specific classifiers and that uncertain observations are hardest to learn. The section "Entity-Specific Model Training" explains the highly imbalanced label distribution (ANAT-DP: 2033, OBS-U: 309) and rationale for separate models to prevent overfitting to frequent entities. Table 1 in "Results RQ1" shows varied per-entity F1 (ANAT-DP 0.937, OBS-U 0.750), indicating that performance is entity-dependent, consistent with tailored modeling.
- **Break condition:** If entities share substantial lexical overlap or context (e.g., an anatomy mentioned within an observation), separate models may produce conflicting predictions that are difficult to merge reliably without a joint decoding step.

## Foundational Learning

### Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
- **Why needed here:** To generate clinically plausible synthetic radiology reports with accurate entity labels by conditioning generation on retrieved gold examples, avoiding free-form hallucination.
- **Quick check question:** How does the RAG pipeline ensure that synthetic reports reflect the distribution of real reports for rare entities like OBS-U?

### Confidence Calibration for Selective Automation
- **Why needed here:** Raw model logits may not reflect true probability of correctness; calibrated confidence scores are necessary to set reliable thresholds that balance automation coverage and accuracy.
- **Quick check question:** Why did post-hoc isotonic calibration reduce automatic acceptance rates at the 0.80 target match level, and what does that imply about the original confidence estimates?

### Instruction Tuning with Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (QLoRA)
- **Why needed here:** To adapt a large base LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) for entity extraction tasks with limited gold data while avoiding catastrophic forgetting and excessive compute.
- **Quick check question:** How does QLoRA preserve the base model's knowledge while learning task-specific extraction patterns from the RadGraph-style instruction data?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
- Sentence Splitter: Segments reports into independent sentences.
- Four Entity-Specific Classifiers: Qwen2.5-7B models fine-tuned via QLoRA for ANAT-DP, OBS-DP, OBS-DA, OBS-U.
- RAG Synthetic Generator: Keyword extractor → FAISS retriever (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) → Mixtral-8x7B prompt generator.
- LLM Judge (Qwen2.5-32B): Validates and corrects synthetic annotations.
- Confidence Scorer & Threshold Learner: Sweeps thresholds on validation set per entity.
- Automation Merger: Applies entity thresholds to full reports, resolves conflicts via highest confidence margin, and decides accept/review.

### Critical path
Gold report preprocessing → sentence splitting → train entity classifiers (gold-only) → generate synthetic data (if needed) → learn validation thresholds → evaluate automation on held-out test reports.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Entity-specific vs. unified models:** Specialization improves rare-entity recall but requires conflict resolution at merge.
- **Synthetic vs. gold-only training:** Synthetic scales to low-resource regimes but may miss subtle contextual patterns (e.g., missing labels per lexicon check).
- **Threshold permissiveness:** Higher coverage (90% accept) yields lower precision; stricter thresholds (95% accept) reduce expert effort but require more review capacity.

### Failure signatures
- Low recall on OBS-U/OBS-DA even after augmentation.
- High false positive rate in automated set (check precision at chosen thresholds).
- Synthetic corpus lacks diversity (high internal coherence but poor cross-set similarity to gold).
- Thresholds on validation set do not achieve any target match score (as with OBS-U), forcing fallback to suboptimal cutoffs.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline gold models:** Train four classifiers on the 2,425 sentence training set and evaluate per-entity F1 on the presence-conditioned test set (as in Table 1). Expect OBS-U to be lowest.
2. **Synthetic fidelity check:** Train synthetic-only models on equal-sized synthetic subsets; compare F1 to gold-trained counterparts (Table 2). Expect within 1–2 points for ANAT-DP/OBS-DA, slightly larger gap for OBS-DP.
3. **Threshold sweep:** On the 256-sentence validation split, for each entity, sweep thresholds from 0.0 to 1.0, compute entity match score and coverage. Record the minimum threshold achieving target scores (0.80, 0.85, 0.90, 0.95). Expect OBS-U to never reach targets.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can RadGraph-style relation extraction (edges) be reliably integrated with the proposed entity annotation framework?
- **Basis in paper:** The abstract and methodology state that the study focuses solely on entity labeling (graph nodes) and explicitly leaves relation extraction (edges) to future work.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper's core pipeline and evaluation are designed for independent, entity-specific classifiers. Adding relation extraction requires modeling dependencies between entities, which could conflict with their current confidence-based, per-entity thresholding approach.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An extended framework that jointly or sequentially predicts entities and relations, followed by an evaluation of how relation extraction accuracy impacts the overall RadGraph graph completeness and the stability of the confidence-based automation workflow.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent do biases from the source RadGraph corpus propagate into synthetic reports, and how can this be mitigated for cross-institutional deployment?
- **Basis in paper:** The Discussion notes that "synthetic data may inherit source-corpus biases" and that "confidence thresholds learned from internal data likely require new environment recalibration." This implies a known but unquantified bias transfer problem.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates distributional similarity (t-SNE, cross-set similarity) between synthetic and real reports but does not measure bias in terms of demographic, linguistic, or clinical finding distributions that vary across hospitals. The recalibration need is stated but not investigated.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A bias audit comparing key variables (patient demographics, disease prevalence, phrasing patterns) between the source RadGraph corpus, the generated synthetic corpus, and reports from a different institution. Follow-up experiments would test if fine-tuning synthetic data on a small target-institution sample reduces the need for full recalibration.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can confidence thresholds be made adaptive or self-calibrating to reduce the need for expert-labeled validation sets in new deployment environments?
- **Basis in paper:** The methodology relies on a held-out validation set to sweep thresholds for entity match scores. The Discussion concludes that thresholds "likely require new environment recalibration," highlighting a dependency on local labeled data.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper uses static thresholds derived from one test split. It does not explore methods like unsupervised uncertainty estimation, semi-supervised calibration with unlabeled data, or domain adaptation techniques that could adapt thresholds without a new labeled validation set.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and evaluation of calibration methods (e.g., using prediction entropy, clustering of embeddings, or small sets of unlabeled reports with heuristic pseudo-labels) against the standard supervised threshold sweep. Performance would be measured by the resulting entity match score and coverage on a new institution's test set.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What is the fundamental cause of OBS-U's failure to reach acceptable automation match scores, and can targeted data augmentation resolve it?
- **Basis in paper:** Table 5 shows OBS-U never attains the 0.80 target match score even at the lowest threshold, leading to all its predictions being routed for review. This occurs despite synthetic-only models achieving comparable F1 to gold (Table 2) and synthetic augmentation helping in low-resource settings (Table 3).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies OBS-U as "hardest to learn" due to ambiguity but does not diagnose whether the automation failure stems from (a) irreducible semantic ambiguity in the text, (b) inconsistent or sparse gold labels for uncertainty cues, or (c) a mismatch between the model's confidence estimates and true reliability for ambiguous spans. The synthetic data helps overall F1 but does not fix the confidence calibration for automation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A detailed error analysis of OBS-U predictions at various confidences, comparing model outputs against multiple expert annotations to measure inter-observer agreement. This would clarify if low model confidence aligns with high human disagreement (inherent ambiguity) or if the model is miscalibrated. Then, targeted augmentation focused on ambiguous patterns, coupled with confidence re-calibration, could be tested.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Does RadAnnotate's selective automation workflow lead to measurable improvements in downstream clinical NLP tasks, or does it introduce subtle errors that propagate?
- **Basis in paper:** The Conclusion states: "the effect of selective automation on downstream clinical utility remains unassessed." This is a direct acknowledgment of a missing evaluation.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper evaluates only the immediate annotation quality (entity match score) of the automated vs. reviewed subsets. It does not train a downstream model (e.g., a RadGraph-based diagnosis classifier or a cohort retrieval system) on the automatically annotated corpus and compare its performance to a fully gold-annotated corpus.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A downstream task experiment where a model is trained using (1) 100% gold annotations, (2) annotations from RadAnnotate's automation pipeline (with expert review only for routed cases), and (3) a hybrid where all automated annotations are included. The performance gap between (1) and (2) on a clinical task (e.g., predicting a specific pathology from the extracted graph) would quantify the practical utility or cost of the selective automation approach.

## Limitations
- Prompt design for generation/judging is unreported; synthetic quality may vary.
- Confidence thresholds learned on a single validation split may not generalize.
- Entity-specific models risk inconsistent merged outputs if entities co-occur.

## Confidence
- RAG synthetic augmentation for rare entities: Medium
- Confidence-based selective automation: High
- Entity-specific classifiers vs. unified model: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Synthetic fidelity audit:** Perform a lexicon-based check on generated sentences for OBS-U to quantify preservation of uncertainty markers (e.g., "possible", "cannot exclude") versus gold.
2. **Threshold transfer test:** Apply learned thresholds to an external radiology corpus (e.g., MIMIC-CXR) to measure drop in entity match score; if >0.05, apply isotonic calibration.
3. **Conflict analysis:** On a sample of merged predictions, compute rate where entity-specific models produce overlapping spans with conflicting types; high conflict rate (>10%) would indicate a need for joint decoding.
