# Learning from Limited Labels: Transductive Graph Label Propagation for Indian Music Analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03626
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03626
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: For instrument classification, label propagation achieved 84.6% accuracy on a held-out labeled set and 91.7% on an unlabeled gold test set, significantly exceeding supervised baselines (48.3% and 63.2%). For raga identification, it improved file-level F1-score to 0.82 from 0.77.

## Executive Summary
Indian Art Music (IAM) suffers from a scarcity of labeled data, hindering robust music analysis. This paper introduces a transductive graph-based label propagation (LP) approach that leverages a small labeled subset to annotate a large unlabeled corpus via audio embeddings. The method constructs a k-nearest neighbor graph from embeddings produced by a neural network (ResNet for instruments, CNN-LSTM for raga) and propagates labels by solving a linear system on the normalized graph Laplacian. An iterative training loop combines supervised loss, pseudo-label loss from LP predictions, and consistency loss on augmented inputs, progressively refining both the model and pseudo-labels. Evaluated on two tasks—20-class instrument classification and 42-class raga identification—the approach drastically reduces labeling overhead, achieving 84.6% and 91.7% accuracy (held-out and gold sets) for instruments and 0.82 file-level F1 for ragas, far surpassing supervised baselines. The work demonstrates the promise of graph-based semi-supervised learning for scarce-label music domains.

## Method Summary
The approach comprises an iterative transductive framework. First, a neural network is trained on the limited labeled data to generate discriminative embeddings. A k-NN graph is built from these embeddings using dot-product similarity, yielding an adjacency matrix W. The normalized affinity matrix S = D^{-1/2} W D^{-1/2} (where D is the degree matrix) defines the graph structure. Label propagation then solves (I - αS)Z = Y to obtain soft labels Z for all nodes, where Y contains one-hot labels for the initially labeled samples. Pseudo-labels are produced by taking the argmax of Z. The network is retrained with a combined loss: L = L_s (supervised cross-entropy on labeled data) + λ_p L_p (cross-entropy on all data using pseudo-labels) + λ_u L_u (consistency loss between predictions on original and augmented inputs). Steps (extract embeddings, build graph, propagate, retrain) are repeated for several iterations, creating a virtuous cycle that improves both embeddings and pseudo-labels.

## Key Results
- Instrument classification: LP achieved 84.6% accuracy on a held-out labeled subset and 91.7% on a manually annotated gold test set, versus supervised baselines of 48.3% and 63.2%.
- Raga identification: LP improved file-level F1-score to 0.82 from the supervised baseline of 0.77, with gains also at the chunk level.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of label propagation hinges on three linked mechanisms. First, the initial supervised training yields discriminative audio embeddings that cluster by class; constructing a k-NN graph using dot-product similarity captures these local affinities, laying the groundwork for smooth label spread. Second, transductive diffusion via the normalized graph Laplacian solves (I - αS)Z = Y, which mathematically enforces that nearby nodes in the graph share similar label distributions—this is the manifold smoothness assumption in action. Third, the iterative refinement loop uses the propagated pseudo-labels, together with a consistency regularizer on augmented inputs, to retrain the network. This process gradually improves the embedding space (making clusters tighter) and cleans the pseudo-labels, creating a positive feedback cycle. The approach assumes that the graph is well-connected (no isolated components) and that early pseudo-labels contain sufficient signal; violations could cause noise propagation, but the reported results suggest these assumptions largely hold in the Indian music corpora.

## Foundational Learning
- **Transductive Learning**: Inference is performed directly on the given unlabeled set rather than learning a model to generalize to unseen data. Why needed: The goal is to efficiently annotate a fixed, large archive (Prasar Bharati), not to deploy a predictive model for new distributions; transductive LP leverages the specific test points for propagation. Quick check: Why would an inductive classifier be less suitable for annotating a fixed corpus?
- **Graph-Based Semi-Supervised Learning**: SSL uses unlabeled data alongside limited labels by constructing a graph that encodes relationships among all samples. Why needed: The scarcity of manual annotations in IAM makes it essential to exploit the unlabeled corpus; the graph explicitly models pairwise affinities, enabling label diffusion under smoothness priors. Quick check: How does a graph over embeddings differ from simply training a classifier on the labeled set and then predicting unlabeled instances?
- **Manifold Smoothness Assumption**: The hypothesis that data lies on a low-dimensional manifold where nearby points are likely to share the same label. Why needed: LP’s propagation depends on the idea that the k-NN graph approximates the manifold; if the embedding space respects class boundaries, then smoothness yields accurate label spreads. Quick check: What could cause the manifold assumption to fail in the instrument classification task given the noted class imbalance?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Feature Extractor (h_θ1) -> Classifier (g_θ2) -> Graph Builder -> Diffusion Solver -> Pseudo-Labels -> Loss Aggregator (Ls, Lp, Lu) -> Network Training -> (repeat)

Critical path:
1. Train network on labeled subset only (supervised baseline).
2. Extract embeddings for all data (labeled + unlabeled) using the current network.
3. Build k-NN graph from embeddings; compute normalized affinity S.
4. Solve (I - αS)Z = Y via conjugate gradient to obtain soft labels Z for all nodes.
5. Convert Z to pseudo-labels (argmax) for the unlabeled nodes.
6. Retrain the network using the combined loss L = L_s + λ_p L_p + λ_u L_u, where L_s is on labeled data, L_p on all data using pseudo-labels, and L_u enforces consistency between predictions on original and augmented inputs.
7. Repeat steps 2–6 for a fixed number of iterations or until pseudo-label stability.

Design tradeoffs:
- Graph sparsity (k): small k yields a sparse graph with less noise but risks disconnected components; large k introduces more inter-class edges.
- Computational cost: solving large linear systems requires memory; using conjugate gradient with sparse matrices mitigates but still scales with n.
- Pseudo-label thresholding: using argmax includes all unlabeled points; thresholding confidence could reduce noise but discards data.

Failure signatures:
- Degenerate pseudo-labels: majority of unlabeled samples assigned to few classes, visible in distribution histograms.
- Convergence to poor local minima: training loss decreases while validation accuracy plateaus at low values, indicating reinforcement of wrong patterns.
- Graph connectivity issues: many nodes have zero degree or belong to tiny components, preventing label diffusion.

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline replication: Train the specified architectures fully supervised on an 80/20 split of the labeled set; report accuracy/F1 on held-out labeled and gold sets.
2. Sensitivity to graph hyperparameters: Fix all else, vary k and α, and measure performance on the gold set to identify stable operating ranges.
3. Gold set validation of pseudo-labels: After final LP iteration, compute precision/recall/F1 between LP pseudo-labels and the manual gold annotations to directly assess pseudo-label quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Adaptive or dynamic graph construction**: How can adaptive or dynamic graph construction strategies improve label propagation for Indian Art Music tasks compared to the static k-NN graph used? Basis: Future Work states "exploring adaptive or dynamic graph construction". Why unresolved: The current study relies on a fixed k-NN graph; it is unknown whether adaptivity yields better label propagation quality for the complex audio manifolds in IAM. What evidence would resolve: Systematically vary graph construction parameters (k, edge weight functions, density-adaptive thresholds) and evaluate propagation accuracy on held-out gold sets for both instrument and raga tasks.
2. **Integration of domain-specific temporal and structural musical features**: Can the integration of domain-specific temporal and structural musical features (e.g., tonic, rhythmic cycles) into the label propagation process enhance the accuracy of raga identification? Basis: Future Work mentions "incorporating temporal and structural musical features into the propagation process". Why unresolved: The paper uses generic audio embeddings; the potential of musicological knowledge (tonic normalization, tala patterns) to improve discriminability for subtle raga distinctions remains unexplored. What evidence would resolve: Extract known musicological features (using CompIAM or similar) and fuse them with deep embeddings (concatenation or attention). Train the LP framework with these hybrid features and perform an ablation study to isolate the contribution of each feature type. Measure chunk- and file-level F1 improvements over the generic embedding LP.
3. **Open-set recognition**: How can label propagation be extended to open-set recognition where unseen ragas or instruments emerge during propagation, rather than collapsing them into an "Others" category? Basis: Future Work states "extending the framework to support open-set recognition, where previously unseen labels are automatically detected and modeled rather than being grouped under a generic 'Others' category". Why unresolved: The current method forces unknown classes into a single bucket, limiting real-world applicability in archives where new classes constantly appear. No mechanism exists to detect and incorporate novel categories during propagation. What evidence would resolve: Develop a novelty detection module (e.g., outlier scoring in embedding space or low-confidence flagging) that highlights candidates for new classes. Manually verify a small subset to assign new labels, then iteratively augment the label set and re-run propagation. Evaluate the system's ability to recover new ragas/instruments from unlabeled Prasar Bharati recordings, measuring precision/recall of novel class discovery.
4. **Sensitivity to initial pseudo-label noise**: How sensitive is the label propagation performance to the quality of initial pseudo-labels generated from the small labeled set, and what mitigation strategies exist for label noise? Basis: Inferred; the method first trains a classifier on the labeled subset, then generates pseudo-labels for unlabeled data before propagation, risking error accumulation. Why unresolved: The paper does not analyze the effect of initial pseudo-label errors on final propagation quality. In low-resource settings, the initial model may be weak, potentially leading to catastrophic error propagation. What evidence would resolve: Conduct controlled experiments by injecting known label noise (e.g., flipping a percentage of initial pseudo-labels) and measuring propagation accuracy on a clean gold test set. Test mitigation strategies such as confidence-thresholded pseudo-labeling, early stopping based on validation consistency, or robust loss functions during the joint training phase. Report sensitivity curves and best practices for reliable LP under noise.

## Limitations
- Missing key hyperparameters (k, α, loss weights) and training configuration details (optimizer, learning rate, batch size) hinder exact reproduction.
- Lack of analysis on graph connectivity, embedding discriminability, and the impact of class imbalance on pseudo-label bias.
- Small gold test sets (e.g., 200 files for instruments) limit statistical reliability and may overstate performance stability.

## Next Checks
1. Ablate the iterative loop: Train with only one LP pass (using initial pseudo-labels) without retraining; compare to full iterative method to quantify the contribution of the refinement cycle.
2. Hyperparameter sensitivity: Vary k (number of neighbors) and α (propagation strength) on a validation split; assess performance stability and identify optimal ranges for replication.
3. Pseudo-label error tracking: On a held-out labeled subset, compute precision/recall of pseudo-labels at each iteration; monitor error accumulation and its correlation with final performance.
