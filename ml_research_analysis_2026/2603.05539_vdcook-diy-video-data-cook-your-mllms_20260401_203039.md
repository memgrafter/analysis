# VDCook:DIY video data cook your MLLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05539
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05539
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** A configurable, self-evolving system that produces a 100M+ video clip corpus with rich multi-dimensional metadata, enabling low-barrier domain-specific dataset construction.

## Executive Summary
VDCook tackles the high cost and technical challenges of creating domain-specific video datasets. Instead of static one-off builds, it introduces a continuous "cooking" process: videos are ingested persistently, enriched with extensive metadata (scene segmentation, motion scores, OCR, captions), and stored in a searchable index. Users then specify natural language queries and parameters to flexibly assemble tailored data packages, optionally blending real retrievals with synthetically generated clips. The system’s closed-loop design feeds synthetic data back into the corpus, progressively covering rare phenomena. A 100M+ clip corpus has been built, and qualitative validation on a Chinese ink‑wash subset shows improved stylistic fidelity after fine‑tuning Wan‑1.3B.

## Method Summary
The pipeline comprises four main stages. First, an MCP‑based ingestion layer continuously crawls and uploads videos from diverse sources. Second, the Model Annotation Center processes every clip with a suite of models to extract structured attributes: temporal scene boundaries, motion intensity, OCR regions, dense captions, style tags, etc. These metadata are stored alongside the video in an indexed repository. Third, a query optimizer translates a user’s natural language request into expanded retrieval templates; the Dual Source Engine then operates in parallel to fetch relevant real videos and to generate synthetic variants conditioned on retrieved seeds and extracted attributes. Finally, a cooking stage applies user‑defined policies (e.g., thresholds on motion, OCR area) to filter and assemble the final dataset package, which includes a full provenance manifest. Synthetic outputs can be re‑ingested, creating a self‑bootstrapping loop that amplifies coverage of long‑tail distributions.

## Key Results
- Corpus scale: >100 million clips, average caption length 266 words, 32.7% at 1080p resolution, enriched with per‑clip metadata (OCR area, motion intensity, etc.).
- Qualitative validation: Fine‑tuning Wan‑1.3B on a curated Chinese ink‑wash subset yields a model with superior stylistic fidelity and coherent traditional composition compared to the base model.
- The system’s flexible “annotate‑first, filter‑later” approach enables on‑demand construction of specialized datasets without rebuilding the entire pipeline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Shifting from static, one‑time dataset construction to a continuous, configurable “cooking” process reduces cost and technical barriers.
- **Mechanism:** The pipeline decouples enrichment from hard filtering; metadata are retained as indexable fields. Users later specify queries and parameters to flexibly select and combine clips from an enriched pool. Continuous ingestion via MCP allows the corpus to evolve.
- **Core Assumption:** Domain data needs are dynamic; a fixed pre‑filtered dataset cannot adapt to new queries without rebuilding.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract mentions “configurable, self‑evolving system” and “query optimization, concurrently running real video retrieval and controlled synthesis.” Section 3.2 describes preserving metadata for flexible downstream selection.
- **Break Condition:** If enrichment fails to scale or metadata signals are inconsistent across domains, the flexibility promise collapses.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** An “annotate‑first, filter‑later” strategy with multi‑dimensional metadata maximizes information utility for diverse downstream tasks.
- **Mechanism:** All ingested clips are uniformly processed to generate rich attribute records. Basic structural filters are applied early, but semantic and quality signals are retained for post‑hoc policy definition during cooking.
- **Core Assumption:** Optimal filtering criteria for a downstream task are unknown during ingestion and must be discoverable later.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract lists “multi‑dimensional metadata enrichment (scene segmentation, motion scoring, OCR ratio, automatic captioning).” Section 3.2 states that metadata attributes are kept as flexible indexing signals.
- **Break Condition:** If the metadata dimensions are insufficiently discriminative for key tasks (e.g., no fine‑grained style classifier), cooked datasets will not meet specialized needs.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A closed‑loop “data–model co‑evolution” process, where synthetic data is re‑ingested and re‑annotated, progressively amplifies coverage of long‑tail phenomena.
- **Mechanism:** For domains with scarce real data, the controllable synthesis engine generates new clips conditioned on retrieved seeds and extracted attributes. These synthetic clips are fed back into the pipeline for annotation, evaluated on benchmarks, and can seed future retrieval/synthesis cycles.
- **Core Assumption:** Iterative refinement of synthetic data guided by real‑world attributes can augment real distributions without catastrophic shifts.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Section 3.6 describes the self‑bootstrapping loop. Section 5.4 notes that synthetic augmentation is important for the artistic domain, and Section 5.5 shows qualitative improvement.
- **Break Condition:** If synthetic data introduces artifacts or biases not caught by annotation models, re‑injection could degrade corpus quality.

## Foundational Learning
- **Modular Pipeline Design (Decoupled Stages):**  
  *Why needed:* VDCook’s flexibility stems from separating ingestion, enrichment, indexing, and cooking. Engineers must understand how to modify one stage (e.g., swap a captioning model) without breaking others.  
  *Quick check:* To add a “violence detection” filter as an optional cooking policy, at which stage should it be injected and what metadata format would it output?

- **MCP (Model Context Protocol) for Ingestion:**  
  *Why needed:* MCP is central to the “self‑evolving” claim; it standardizes how new video sources plug into the system. Understanding its trade‑offs (automation vs. noise) is crucial.  
  *Quick check:* What are the trade‑offs of relying on an external protocol like MCP versus building a proprietary crawler for video ingestion?

- **Controllable Video Synthesis as Data Augmentation:**  
  *Why needed:* The closed‑loop mechanism depends on a synthesis engine that can be conditioned on extracted attributes (pose, style). Engineers need to grasp what “controllable” entails (e.g., ControlNet‑like inputs).  
  *Quick check:* If the synthesis module only accepts text prompts (no visual conditioning), how would that limit the system’s ability to generate variants of a retrieved clip?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input/Query Interface → Query Optimizer → Dual Source Engine (MCP retrieval + controllable synthesis) → Model Annotation Center → Index & Storage → Cooking/Assembly & Filter → Data Package (with Notebook & Provenance). Baseline Eval Hub optionally fine‑tunes/evaluates. Re‑injection Loop feeds synthetic outputs back into MCP ingestion.

**Critical path:** User query → query optimization → parallel retrieval/synthesis → unified enrichment → indexing → policy‑based filtering/assembly → final package + manifest.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Annotation cost vs. flexibility:* Enriching every clip with many models is expensive but enables maximal downstream flexibility.  
- *Synthetic fidelity vs. long‑tail coverage:* Higher synthesis freedom risks artifacts; the system relies on baseline evaluation to catch failures.  
- *Open MCP vs. curated sources:* MCP enables automation but may introduce noisy sources; user‑contributed data is cleaner but requires manual effort.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Corruption in loop: Synthetic artifacts pass annotation, re‑ingested, polluting corpus (detectable via drops in baseline performance).  
- Query‑cooking gap: Query optimizer fails to find relevant candidates, yielding packages misaligned with user intent.  
- Metadata skew: Enrichment models underperform on new domains (e.g., OCR on stylized text), rendering metadata‑based filtering ineffective.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Sanity‑Check Cooking:* Issue a broad query (“people walking”) with defaults; verify relevance, metadata completeness, manifest reproducibility.  
2. *Metadata Verification:* Pick a signal (e.g., motion score); manually spot‑check high‑ and low‑score clips for correlation with subjective motion.  
3. *Synthesis Loop Test:* Request a niche‑domain package with high synthesis ratio (e.g., 80% synthetic); compare real vs. synthetic quality; re‑ingest synthetic set and confirm its appearance in a subsequent identical query.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Minimizing Temporal Artifacts in Automated Synthesis
**Basis:** Conclusion identifies temporal artifacts as a key limitation.  
**Why unresolved:** Artifacts degrade video quality and could harm downstream tasks; resolving is essential for trustworthy synthetic data.  
**Evidence needed:** Quantitative artifact metrics (e.g., flow consistency scores) and ablation studies linking artifact reduction to downstream performance gains, plus qualitative user evaluations.

### Automating Provenance and Licensing Checks
**Basis:** Conclusion notes that fine‑grained provenance and licensing checks remain engineering‑intensive.  
**Why unresolved:** Manual checks hinder scalability and legal compliance; automation is necessary for large‑scale deployment and reproducibility.  
**Evidence needed:** Implementation of an automated provenance logging system and integrated license detection tools, validated by case studies on compliance rates and manual‑effort reduction.

### Standardized Quantitative Benchmarks for VDCook Datasets
**Basis:** Conclusion states that rigorous quantitative benchmarks across diverse downstream tasks are an ongoing effort.  
**Why unresolved:** Current validation is qualitative (e.g., ink‑wash case); without benchmarks, claims of generalizability are weak.  
**Evidence needed:** A multi‑task benchmark suite (e.g., action recognition, captioning) applied to VDCook datasets from multiple domains, with statistically significant comparisons against baseline datasets.

### Formal Automated Quality Scoring and Uncertainty Estimation
**Basis:** Future work includes formalizing automated quality scoring and uncertainty estimation for per‑clip selection.  
**Why unresolved:** Without this, dataset curation lacks reproducibility and optimization; scoring and uncertainty are key for reliable selection.  
**Evidence needed:** A scoring model combining metadata and model confidences, validated by correlation with human quality judgments or downstream performance improvements.

### Improving the Model Annotation Center with Domain Adapters
**Basis:** Future work aims to expand the Model Annotation Center with stronger domain adapters and calibrated confidence outputs.  
**Why unresolved:** Current annotation models may be inaccurate for niche domains (medical, artistic), reducing dataset utility.  
**Evidence needed:** Integration of domain adaptation techniques (e.g., fine‑tuning) and calibration methods (e.g., temperature scaling), evaluated on annotation accuracy metrics for target domains like ink‑wash or medical videos.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed details of MCP, synthesis engine, and quality thresholds impede faithful replication.
- Metadata extraction may be noisy on specialized domains (e.g., OCR on stylized ink‑wash text), compromising filtering reliability.
- Re‑injection of synthetic data risks corpus corruption if temporal artifacts or biases escape annotation.

## Confidence
- Cost reduction via continuous “cooking”: **Medium** confidence (plausible design, but no empirical cost comparison).
- Metadata‑first flexibility: **Medium** confidence (rich metadata shown, but direct task gains not demonstrated).
- Closed‑loop long‑tail amplification: **Low** confidence (qualitative improvement on artistic subset exists; synthetic fidelity risks and quantitative tail coverage unverified).

## Next Checks

3. Quantify long‑tail gain: Measure occurrence frequency of rare events/ styles in the corpus before and after synthetic re‑injection, using a fixed annotation pipeline.
