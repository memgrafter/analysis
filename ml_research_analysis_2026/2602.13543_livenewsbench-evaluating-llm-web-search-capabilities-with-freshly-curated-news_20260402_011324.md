# LiveNewsBench: Evaluating LLM Web Search Capabilities with Freshly Curated News

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.13543
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13543
*   **Reference Count:** 36
*   **Primary Result:** LiveNewsBench is a fresh, multi-hop news QA benchmark that successfully reduces memorization (no-internet accuracy 3.5%-21.5%) and demonstrates strong, budget-dependent discriminative power for evaluating agentic web search.

## Executive Summary
LiveNewsBench introduces a novel benchmark to evaluate the web search capabilities of LLM agents. It addresses a key challenge in the field: disentangling an agent's ability to retrieve and reason with fresh information from its internal, memorized knowledge. The authors achieve this by automatically generating question-answer pairs from a continuously updated corpus of recent news articles, ensuring questions are genuinely beyond a model's training cutoff and require multi-source synthesis. The benchmark includes a human-verified test subset for quality control. Evaluations show that state-of-the-art models perform poorly without internet access, confirming low memorization, and that performance scales with allowed search budgets, validating the multi-hop design. This provides a robust, frequently refreshable tool for measuring real-world agentic search proficiency.

## Method Summary
The benchmark construction is an automated pipeline. It starts by identifying recent news events from the Wikipedia Current Events archive. For each event, it retrieves a cluster of relevant, full-text news articles from a vetted allowlist of sources via the archive.today service. A reasoning LLM (GPT-5.1 Thinking) is then prompted to generate a question-answer pair that must synthesize information from multiple articles within the cluster, with a self-consistency check to verify answer correctness. The generated items undergo automated guideline adherence filtering (e.g., "Google-Proof" requirement) and a human audit of a test subset. For evaluation, a custom ReAct-style agent framework is used, enforcing a strict budget of search queries and full-page visits (default 5 each). Model answers are judged for factual correctness by an LLM-as-a-judge (GPT-4.1). The authors commit to quarterly updates to maintain freshness.

## Key Results
*   **Low Memorization:** No-internet accuracy on the human-verified test set ranges from only 3.5% to 21.5% across leading models, significantly lower than prior time-sensitive benchmarks like FreshQA (28.1%-82.9%).
*   **Strong Discriminative Power:** Under a controlled search budget of 5 queries/visits, model accuracy spans a wide range from 11.0% to 90.0%, effectively differentiating agent capabilities.
*   **Budget Sensitivity:** Ablation studies show performance consistently improves with increased search budgets (e.g., 1→7 queries), with gains ranging from 4% to 64.5%, confirming the multi-hop question design forces iterative search.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
*   **Mechanism 1 (Freshness & Disentanglement):** Automated generation from *freshly sourced* news articles, retrieved post-training cutoffs, minimizes memorization. The pipeline clusters articles around a single event and uses a reasoning LLM to create Q&A pairs that explicitly require synthesizing multiple sources. Evidence: Low no-internet scores (Table 2) vs. prior benchmarks; generation prompt mandates multi-article citation.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Multi-Hop Enforcement):** Questions are designed to be "Google-Proof," meaning a single search snippet is insufficient. The evaluation's strict action budget (Search/Click) tests if agents can chain actions to gather dispersed information. Evidence: Performance gains from increased budgets (Table 5); observed agent over-reliance on snippets suggests the design is challenging but not always overcome.
*   **Mechanism 3 (Quality at Scale):** Full automation enables frequent updates and large scale, while a human-verified test subset (200 samples) and extrinsic validation (comparable model performance on full vs. human set) ensure integrity. Evidence: Minimal accuracy difference between full and human-verified sets (Table 4); 92% human annotator agreement; commitment to quarterly updates.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Agentic Search / ReAct Paradigm**
    *   *Why needed:* The benchmark evaluates an *agent* that interleaves reasoning with discrete actions (Search, Click, Finish), not a static RAG system. Understanding the think-act loop is key to analyzing failure modes like tool misuse or premature termination.
    *   *Quick Check:* What is the functional difference between an LLM generating a search query string and executing a "Click" action on a specific URL in a ReAct agent?
*   **Concept: Benchmark Contamination & Memorization**
    *   *Why needed:* The core goal is to separate search capability from internal knowledge. One must understand how static benchmarks become "contaminated" as models train on web data, and why temporal sensitivity alone (e.g., FreshQA) may not suffice if questions are simple enough to answer from pre-training.
    *   *Quick Check:* Why might a model score 47.4% on FreshQA without internet but only 21.5% on LiveNewsBench without internet?
*   **Concept: Multi-Hop Question Design**
    *   *Why needed:* The benchmark's discriminative power hinges on questions where the answer is not present in any single document but requires chaining information across sources. This tests deeper agentic reasoning.
    *   *Quick Check:* For the example question about a Guatemalan state of emergency, what specific pieces of information from different articles are needed, and why would a single search query's snippet likely fail?
*   **Concept: LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluation**
    *   *Why needed:* Model outputs are scored by GPT-4.1. This common practice raises questions about judge reliability, bias, and alignment with human judgment, which impacts confidence in reported accuracy scores.
    *   *Quick Check:* What are potential failure modes of using an LLM judge for factual correctness on temporally sensitive news questions?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:**
    Wikipedia Current Events Archive -> Event Selection & Article Clustering (Brave Search API + allowlist) -> Full-Text Retrieval (archive.today) -> Q&A Generation & Verification (GPT-5.1 Thinking + self-consistency + rule-based filter) -> Human Audit (Test Subset) -> Chronological Train/Test Split -> Custom Agentic Web Search Framework (ReAct with Search/Click/Finish, budget-enforced) -> Answer Scoring (GPT-4.1 judge) -> Leaderboard.
*   **Critical Path:** News Event Identification -> Relevant Article Cluster Acquisition -> Multi-article Q&A Generation -> Guideline Filtering -> Human Verification (Test) -> Agent Execution with Budget -> LLM Judge Scoring -> Results Aggregation.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   *Automation vs. Quality:* Full automation enables scale and quarterly updates but requires stringent filters and a human audit to catch systematic guideline violations.
    *   *Cost vs. Budget:* Higher search/click budgets improve model performance (Table 5) but linearly increase evaluation cost and runtime; the default 5/5 is a practical compromise.
    *   *Controlled Agent vs. Official APIs:* The custom agent framework ensures fair, reproducible comparisons by standardizing the search engine and action space. Using official APIs (e.g., GPT-5.2 Search) introduces variability due to undisclosed prompting and retrieval choices.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   *Data Pipeline:* Low yield of usable articles per event (~5.3), high human rejection rate (>15% of auto-passed items), or generator warnings about temporal instability.
    *   *Agent Execution:* High rate of tool-calling format errors (e.g., Kimi K2: 44% failure), consistently exceeding the action budget, or very low click-through rates (indicating snippet over-reliance).
    *   *Evaluation:* Large performance gap (>10%) between the human-verified and full test sets, suggesting automated data quality issues; or judge prompt mismatches with expected answer format.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  **Memorization Sanity Check:** Run top models (GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro) on the human-verified test set with `internet_access: false`. Expected accuracy: 3.5%-21.5% (per Table 6). Higher scores indicate a flaw in event recency or question design.
    2.  **Snippet-Only Ablation:** Re-run the evaluation but strictly enforce that the agent may only use search result snippets (zero full-page visits allowed). A minimal drop in accuracy compared to the full budget would contradict the multi-hop necessity claim.
    3.  **Human-Automated Parity:** Compute accuracy, average search/click counts, and model rankings for 3-5 models on both the full test set and the 200-sample human-verified subset (replicating Table 4). Expect <5% absolute accuracy difference and preserved rankings. Significant divergence indicates quality drift in the automated set.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How can we design and evaluate benchmarks for temporally sensitive questions whose answers may change as events unfold? *Basis:* The Conclusion states future work will target such dynamic questions, as the current paper uses static Q&A pairs.
*   **Open Question 2:** To what extent does relying on GPT-5.1 Thinking in the automated pipeline introduce hidden contamination or bias, and can alternative (e.g., open-source) generation methods maintain benchmark quality and memorization resistance? *Basis:* The pipeline depends on a single proprietary model for generation and verification, a choice not analyzed for its own contamination risks.
*   **Open Question 3:** Why do LLM-based search agents preferentially use search result snippets over full-page visits, and how can agent architectures be modified to encourage deeper page interaction when necessary? *Basis:* Section 5.2 reports a consistent pattern of agents issuing more searches than clicks, suggesting a snippet-reliant strategy.
*   **Open Question 4:** How reliable is GPT-4.1 as an automatic grader for factual correctness on LiveNewsBench, and to what extent does its evaluation align with human judgments, particularly on borderline cases? *Basis:* No human judgment data is reported for model *predictions*; only the test Q&A pairs were human-verified. LLM-as-judge biases are a known concern.

## Limitations
*   **Freshness Guarantee:** Reliance on `archive.today` snapshots may have inherent delays; if articles were crawled before their public release or during model training, memorization contamination is possible.
*   **Multi-Hop Enforcement:** Observed agent over-reliance on snippets (low click rates) suggests many questions may be answerable with surface-level retrieval, potentially weakening the claim that all items consistently force deep multi-hop synthesis.
*   **Generalization Scope:** The source events are drawn solely from Wikipedia Current Events, which may introduce geopolitical and topical biases and may not cover all domains where web search is needed.

## Confidence
*   **Reduced Memorization Claim:** **Medium**. Low no-internet scores are strong evidence, but absolute confidence requires precise verification that all source article timestamps are post-training-cutoff for all evaluated models.
*   **Multi-Hop Discriminative Power:** **Medium**. The budget ablation shows performance gains, but the persistent snippet-reliance behavior indicates the benchmark may not uniformly force the intended deep synthesis across all items.
*   **Automated Data Quality:** **Medium**. Human-automated performance parity is reassuring, but the small human audit size (200 samples) and 15% rejection rate leave room for undetected systematic flaws in the majority of auto-generated items.

## Next Checks
1.  **Timestamp Contamination Audit:** For a stratified random sample of test items, verify the original publication dates of all source articles are definitively after the final training cutoffs of all models in the leaderboard.
2.  **Snippet-Only Ablation Study:** Re-run the full evaluation with a modified agent that is *prohibited* from executing any full-page visits (clicks). A negligible accuracy drop compared to the standard budget would contradict the core multi-hop design premise.
3.  **Scale Human Verification:** Increase the human-annotated test subset to at least 1,000 samples. Categorize rejection reasons (e.g., "answer findable in first snippet," "temporal instability") to quantify the prevalence of specific failure modes in the automated pipeline.
