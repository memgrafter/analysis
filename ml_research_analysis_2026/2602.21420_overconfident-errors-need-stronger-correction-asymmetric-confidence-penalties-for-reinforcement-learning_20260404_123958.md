# Overconfident Errors Need Stronger Correction: Asymmetric Confidence Penalties for Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.21420
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21420
- **Reference count**: 22
- **Primary result**: ACE improves Pass@k across models; e.g., ACE-DAPO on Qwen2.5-Math-7B achieves Pass@32 96.1% (+1.5pp) on MATH-500 and 38.6% (+1.5pp) on AIME 2025 while matching Pass@1.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is effective for improving LLM reasoning but often narrows the reasoning boundary due to uniform penalization of all errors, allowing overconfident errors to dominate. This paper identifies the problem and introduces ACE (Asymmetric Confidence-aware Error Penalty), which modulates the penalty for incorrect rollouts based on a confidence shift metric derived from the ratio between policy and reference probabilities. By strongly penalizing overconfident errors while leaving exploratory and self-correcting errors untouched, ACE preserves diversity and expands the reasoning boundary.

ACE extends standard RLVR algorithms (GRPO, DAPO) and is shown theoretically and empirically to reduce overconfident error fraction and token entropy decay. Extensive experiments across multiple LLM families and benchmarks demonstrate consistent improvements in Pass@k, especially at large k, without sacrificing Pass@1. The approach provides a simple yet powerful mechanism to enhance the robustness of RL-trained reasoning models.

## Method Summary
ACE modifies the advantage computation in GRPO/DAPO. For each incorrect rollout (reward = 0), it computes a confidence shift \(c_i = (\sum \log \pi_\theta(y_i^{(t)}|x) - \sum \log \pi_{\text{ref}}(y_i^{(t)}|x)) / T_i\), where \(T_i\) is the sequence length. The standard negative advantage \(\hat{A}_i^-\) is then scaled by \((1 + \alpha \cdot \text{Softplus}(c_i))\), amplifying the penalty when the policy is more confident than the reference (\(c_i>0\)). Correct rollouts retain the unmodified advantage. The loss combines this clipped surrogate objective with a fixed KL penalty \(\beta D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_\theta\|\pi_{\text{ref}})\). Training uses \(\alpha=1.0\) and \(\beta=0.001\) on the DAPO-Math-17K dataset, integrated within the VERL framework.

## Key Results
- ACE-DAPO on Qwen2.5-Math-7B: Pass@32 = 96.1% (+1.5pp) on MATH-500 and 38.6% (+1.5pp) on AIME 2025, with Pass@1 unchanged.
- ACE-GRPO yields +3.0pp Pass@32 on MATH-500.
- ACE reduces overconfident error fraction and preserves token entropy, confirming mitigation of diversity collapse.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Overconfident errors as a root cause
Overconfident errors (\(c_i>0\)) are a key pathology in RLVR that narrows reasoning boundaries. The confidence shift \(c_i = \log(\pi_\theta(y_i|x)/\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_i|x))\) signals when the policy has become spuriously confident on an incorrect output. Overconfident errors accumulate during RL training and monopolize probability mass, suppressing diverse reasoning. Evidence: The paper highlights uniform penalization as the overlooked cause (abstract, Sec 3); Section 5.3 tracks OEF and mean overconfidence, showing ACE reduces them.

### Mechanism 2: Asymmetric penalty via confidence modulation
ACE modulates negative advantages with \((1+\alpha\cdot\text{Softplus}(c_i))\), strongly penalizing overconfident errors while minimally affecting exploratory/self-correcting errors. For incorrect rollouts, the penalty factor grows with \(c_i\) when \(c_i>0\) and remains near 1 for \(c_i\le0\). Evidence: Equation (5) and Figure 1 define the modulation; design rationale in §4.2; prior works (SimKO, DAPO) lack this confidence-aware asymmetry.

### Mechanism 3: Gradient decomposition and theoretical guarantee
ACE’s gradient decomposes into a selective regularizer over overconfident errors plus a residual, providing theoretical grounding. The difference from standard GRPO equals \(-\alpha \nabla R_{\text{sel}} + \alpha E(\theta)\), where \(R_{\text{sel}}\) is a KL-like term restricted to overconfident incorrect outputs, and \(E(\theta)\) tempers correction strength. Evidence: Theorem 1 (Sec 4.4, Appendix A) and Theorem 2 (improved gradient quality ratio). No neighboring work offers such a decomposition.

  Why needed: ACE relies on the confidence shift \(c_i\), which assumes the reference \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\) is well-calibrated; overconfidence on incorrect outputs signals spurious reinforcement.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:  
Rollout Generator → Verifier → Advantage Calculator → Loss Module → Optimizer

**Critical path**:  
1. For each prompt in a batch, generate G rollouts from \(\pi_\theta\).  
2. Compute rewards via verifier; calculate group mean \(\hat{\mu}_x\) and std \(\hat{\sigma}_x\).  
3. Compute standard advantages: \(\hat{A}_i = (r_i - \hat{\mu}_x) / (\hat{\sigma}_x + \epsilon)\).  
4. For each incorrect rollout (\(r_i=0\)), compute \(c_i = (\sum \log \pi_\theta(y_i^{(t)}|x) - \sum \log \pi_{\text{ref}}(y_i^{(t)}|x)) / T_i\).  
5. Apply ACE scaling: \(A_{\text{ACE},i}^- = \hat{A}_i^- \cdot (1 + \alpha \cdot \text{Softplus}(c_i))\); keep \(A_{\text{ACE},i}^+ = \hat{A}_i^+\) for correct rollouts.  
6. Compute loss: \(\mathcal{L} = -\text{mean}_i \min(\rho_i A_{\text{ACE},i}, \text{clip}(\rho_i,1-\epsilon_c,1+\epsilon_c)A_{\text{ACE},i}) + \beta\cdot\text{KL}\).  
7. Backpropagate \(\mathcal{L}\) and update \(\theta\).

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- \(\alpha\): penalty amplification; too low ineffective, too high harms exploration; \(\alpha=1.0\) robust per Appendix D.  
- Modulation function: Softplus vs ReLU; Softplus provides smooth gradients and non-zero for \(c_i\le0\); ReLU is discontinuous and inferior (ablation §5.5).  
- Confidence normalization: Dividing by \(T_i\) ensures scale invariance; may dilute signal for very long CoT.  
- Stop-gradient on \(c_i\): Avoids training instability; including gradient through \(c_i\) can cause oscillations.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Over-penalization: Pass@1 drops with modest Pass@k gains → \(\alpha\) too high or mis-calibrated confidence.  
- Under-penalization: Overconfident error fraction (OEF) remains high → ACE not suppressing harmful errors.  
- Training instability: Loss spikes → aggressive \(\alpha\) or noisy confidence; monitor gradient norms.  
- Reference model mismatch: If \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\) poorly calibrated, \(c_i\) may not correlate; check correlation on validation.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. Baseline replication: Train GRPO/DAPO on DAPO-Math-17K; verify: Pass@1 improves, Pass@k declines, OEF increases.  
2. ACE integration: Apply ACE-GRPO/ACE-DAPO with \(\alpha=1.0\); measure OEF and token entropy dynamics; expect reduced OEF and slower entropy decay.  
3. Pass@k evaluation: Compare GRPO vs ACE-GRPO and DAPO vs ACE-DAPO on MATH-500 and AIME 2025; expect consistent improvements at \(k\ge16\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Reference model dependence**: How does the quality of \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\) affect ACE's ability to identify overconfident errors? If the reference is poorly calibrated, \(c_i\) may not reliably indicate spurious patterns. The paper assumes a well-calibrated reference but does not test robustness to reference variations.
- **Extension to non-binary rewards**: Can ACE handle continuous or partial rewards? The current formulation uses binary rewards; with graded rewards, "overconfident error" becomes ambiguous and requires redefining the penalty trigger.
- **Long CoT normalization**: Does sequence-length normalization \(\bar{c}_i = c_i/T_i\) remain effective for extremely long chain-of-thought outputs (e.g., >10K tokens)? Aggregation may mask localized overconfidence patterns.

## Limitations
- The overconfident-error hypothesis is supported indirectly (OEF, entropy trends) but lacks a direct ablative isolation.
- The reference model \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\) update schedule (frozen vs. moving average) is unspecified, affecting confidence shift stability.
- The DAPO-Math-17K training dataset is not publicly available, hindering faithful replication.
