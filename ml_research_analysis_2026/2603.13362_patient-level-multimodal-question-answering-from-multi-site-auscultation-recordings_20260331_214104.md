# Patient-Level Multimodal Question Answering from Multi-Site Auscultation Recordings

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.13362
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13362
- Reference count: 5
- Primary result: Achieves 0.865 F1-macro and 0.952 BERTScore on CaReSound, outperforming larger foundational audio-language models.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework for patient-level multimodal question answering from multi-site auscultation recordings. It addresses the subjectivity of traditional auscultation diagnosis and the inability of general-purpose audio-language models to capture subtle physiological sounds. The method aligns variable-length multi-site recordings (up to 30 s) with a frozen large language model's embedding space via a trainable gated cross-attention module and a Perceiver Resampler. This preserves the LLM's linguistic priors while grounding it in acoustic evidence. On the CaReSound benchmark, the model achieves state-of-the-art results with an F1-macro of 0.865 and a BERTScore of 0.952, significantly surpassing prior audio-language models. Ablation studies show that lightweight domain-specific audio tokenizers (e.g., raw waveform patches) are effective alternatives to large pretrained encoders, and that aggregating recordings across anatomical sites provides spatial redundancy, mitigating the performance drop caused by temporal truncation.

## Method Summary
The framework, termed Audio-Tuned Speech-Language Model (AudioTSLM), processes a patient’s auscultation clips from multiple sites. Each raw waveform (resampled to 16 kHz, padded/truncated to 30 s) is tokenized into non-overlapping 40 ms patches via a simple 1D convolutional frontend (RawAudioTokenizer) or other encoders. Patch embeddings are projected to the LLM’s hidden dimension and flattened across all clips into a long sequence. A Perceiver Resampler with a fixed number (K) of learnable latent queries cross-attends over this sequence to produce a compact patient-level representation. This representation is injected into a frozen LLaMA-3.2-1B model via gated cross-attention layers inserted periodically among the Transformer blocks; the gate (tanh(αℓ)) controls audio influence to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Input prompts follow a multimodal template: `[Instruction] ... <audio> [Site] ... Question: [Q] Answer: [A]`. Training uses AdamW with component-specific learning rates, balanced sampling across datasets, and cross-validation splits. The objective combines standard language modeling loss with metrics tailored to open-ended and binary QA.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art on CaReSound: 0.865 F1-macro (binary) and 0.952 BERTScore (open-ended).
- Outperforms much larger foundational audio-language models (Audio-Flamingo3, Qwen2-Audio) despite using a 1B-parameter LLM.
- RawAudioTokenizer (domain-specific, raw waveform) matches or exceeds general-purpose pretrained encoders (CLAP, Whisper, Wav2Vec2); multi-site aggregation reduces sensitivity to shorter clips (e.g., 10 s vs 30 s) in multi-instance datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Gated Cross-Attention Alignment**  
A trainable gated cross-attention module aligns multi-site audio with a frozen LLM while preserving linguistic priors. The Perceiver Resampler compresses variable-length audio tokens into a fixed set of latents Z′. These are injected into the LLM via cross-attention (H(ℓ) ← H(ℓ) + tanh(αℓ)·CrossAttn(H(ℓ), Z′)), where the learnable gate tanh(αℓ) regulates the audio influence per layer. Assumption: The LLM’s latent space contains sufficient world knowledge to interpret the compressed acoustic patterns when explicitly conditioned. Evidence: The architecture is described in the abstract and section 3.3.3. Break condition: If the LLM lacks latent concepts for specific auscultation phenomena (e.g., murmurs), the gating may fail to align modalities, leading to incoherent outputs.

**Mechanism 2: Domain-Specific Audio Tokenization**  
Lightweight tokenizers (raw waveform or mel-spectrogram patches) are effective because they capture task-relevant features without extraneous general-audio baggage. Simple CNNs operate directly on short patches (e.g., 40 ms) and learn representations optimized for pathological heart/lung sounds. Assumption: Discriminative features for auscultation (e.g., murmur bands, crackle transients) are efficiently learnable from domain data and not optimally represented by generic speech models. Evidence: Ablations in section 4.2/Table2 show RawAudioTokenizer achieving the highest F1-macro (0.865), outperforming Wav2Vec2 and matching CLAP. Break condition: If long-range temporal dependencies or subtle hierarchies are essential, a lightweight frontend may fail.

**Mechanism 3: Multi-Site Spatial Redundancy**  
Aggregating recordings across sites provides spatial redundancy that compensates for reduced temporal context. The Perceiver Resampler’s cross-attention over the flattened (M clips × N tokens) matrix allows latents to jointly attend across all sites and time, learning co-occurrence patterns (e.g., a murmur audible at multiple valves). Assumption: The same pathology often manifests at multiple sites, offering complementary evidence. Evidence: Section 5.3 shows that multi-instance (MIL) datasets (e.g., SPRSound) suffer less performance drop when truncating to 10 s compared to single-instance (SIL) datasets (e.g., KAUH). Break condition: For site-specific pathologies or poorly synchronized/noisy recordings, spatial aggregation could introduce noise or mislead.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Multi-Instance Learning (MIL)**  
- Why needed: The patient is represented as a “bag” of auscultation clips from different anatomical sites; the model must aggregate evidence from multiple instances to answer patient-level questions, rather than classifying each clip independently.  
- Quick check: How would you design a pooling mechanism that allows a model to learn which sites are most informative for a given question, without knowing the answer beforehand?

**Concept: Gated Modality Fusion**  
- Why needed: To inject audio information into a frozen LLM without destabilizing its pretrained textual representations. The learnable gate modulates audio influence per layer, enabling gradual alignment.  
- Quick check: Why use a learnable gate (tanh(αℓ)) instead of simply adding audio cross-attention outputs to the LLM’s stream?

**Concept: Latent Compression for Long Sequences**  
- Why needed: Auscultation recordings produce very long token sequences (e.g., 30 s @ 16 kHz / 40 ms ≈ 750 patches). Directly processing all tokens in a Transformer is computationally prohibitive; the Perceiver Resampler compresses them into a fixed number (K) of latents.  
- Quick check: What are the trade-offs of using a small K (e.g., 32) vs. a large K (e.g., 256) in the Perceiver Resampler for this task?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Audio Tokenizer → Projection MLP → Perceiver Resampler → LLM Integration (frozen LLaMA-3.2-1B with gated cross-attention) → Prompt Engineering (multimodal formatting) → Text generation.

**Critical path**: Raw multi-site audio clips → patch tokenization → dimension projection → flatten across sites → Perceiver Resampler (produces K latents) → latents attend to LLM hidden states via gated cross-attention at intermediate layers → LLM produces token probabilities for the answer sequence.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Perceiver K size*: Smaller K reduces compute but risks losing fine-grained acoustic details; larger K increases memory and may overfit.  
- *Frozen vs. fine-tune LLM*: Freezing preserves generative fluency and avoids catastrophic forgetting, but limits depth of audio grounding; fine-tuning could improve alignment but risks degrading textual knowledge.  
- *Tokenizer choice*: RawAudioTokenizer is simplest and performed best, but may be less robust to background noise than a pretrained encoder; pretrained encoders (Wav2Vec2, CLAP) bring domain shift from speech/environmental sounds.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Poor cross-site cues: Model ignores site metadata; answers treat multi-site input as a single clip, leading to similar performance on MIL and SIL datasets under truncation.  
- Over-gating: The learned tanh(αℓ) saturates near 0, effectively ignoring audio; outputs resemble zero-shot LLM responses despite audio input.  
- Encoder mismatch: Using generic pretrained encoders (e.g., Wav2Vec2) underperforms because they discard phase information or are tuned to speech phonemes.

**First experiments**:  
1. **Encoder Ablation**: Replace RawAudioTokenizer with Mel, Wav2Vec2, Whisper, CLAP to test domain-specific vs. generic frontends.  
2. **Temporal Truncation**: Reduce max duration from 30 s to 20 s and 10 s, comparing performance drop on MIL vs. SIL datasets.  
3. **Baseline Comparison**: Evaluate against zero-shot audio-language models (Audio-Flamingo3, Qwen2-Audio) and prior fine-tuned model CaReAQA on CaReSound.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Mitigating dataset bias in CaReSound**: The discussion notes that future work must address bias to ensure generalizability. CaReSound aggregates heterogeneous public datasets, potentially overrepresenting certain demographics or devices. External validation on diverse multi-center cohorts and fairness audits are needed.  
2. **Features captured by RawAudioTokenizer**: Despite matching or surpassing pretrained encoders, the paper offers no analysis of the learned representations. Without feature attribution or latent space inspection, the reason for its success remains unknown, hindering principled encoder design.  
3. **Mechanisms of spatial redundancy**: The paper observes that multi-site aggregation mitigates truncation effects but does not dissect how cross-site evidence integrates for different pathologies or locations. Understanding would require attention analysis and controlled studies with known site-specific patterns.

## Limitations
- Key architectural hyperparameters (e.g., RawAudioTokenizer architecture, Perceiver latent count) are omitted, limiting reproducibility.
- The gated cross-attention mechanism (e.g., learned gate values, effect on LLM outputs) is not analyzed.
- Generalizability is uncertain: only CaReSound is used; spatial redundancy may not hold for site-specific pathologies or asynchronous recordings.

## Confidence
- Gated alignment with frozen LLM: Medium
- Lightweight domain-specific tokenizer: High
- Multi-site spatial redundancy: Medium
- SOTA performance on CaReSound: High

## Next Checks
1. Probe the LLM embedding space: Train linear classifiers on frozen LLM token representations (with and without audio conditioning) to predict pathology labels; assess whether audio alignment improves class separability.
2. Gate dynamics analysis: Monitor and visualize the learned tanh(αℓ) values across layers and training; ablate gates to quantify their impact on performance and output coherence.
3. Spatial vs. temporal trade-off test: Collect or synthesize a multi-site dataset with controlled truncation and known site-specific pathologies; evaluate whether multi-site aggregation reliably compensates for reduced temporal context.
