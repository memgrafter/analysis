# MedCoRAG: Interpretable Hepatology Diagnosis via Hybrid Evidence Retrieval and Multispecialty Consensus

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05129  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05129  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art diagnostic performance on hepatic diseases (81.32% precision, 79.18% recall, 79.12% F1) via hybrid retrieval and multi-agent consensus.

## Executive Summary
MedCoRAG is a hybrid retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system for interpretable hepatic disease diagnosis from EHRs. It constructs patient-specific evidence by jointly retrieving and pruning paths from the UMLS knowledge graph and clinical guidelines. A router agent dynamically dispatches specialty agents (e.g., Hepatology, Oncology) to iteratively reason over the shared evidence, triggering targeted re-retrieval when needed. The system distills complex multi-agent reasoning into a deployable 8B model. Evaluated on MIMIC-IV, it outperforms existing medical LLMs and agent frameworks, with strong ablation evidence for its core mechanisms.

## Method Summary
The pipeline operates in three stages. First, abnormal findings are extracted from the clinical narrative and standardized to UMLS concepts. Initial diagnostic hypotheses are generated. Second, for each hypothesis, the system retrieves raw UMLS multi-hop paths (≤3 hops) and top guideline excerpts. An LLM prunes the paths using the full narrative and guidelines to form a coherent evidence package. Third, a router assesses case complexity; for complex cases, it dynamically dispatches relevant specialist agents. Specialists iterate, indicating stance, confidence, and evidence sufficiency; if a majority finds evidence insufficient (ρ>0.5), re-retrieval is triggered. A generalist synthesizes the final consensus diagnosis. The teacher model (Qwen3-Max) simulates this loop to fine-tune a student model (Llama-3.1-8B) via LoRA.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art performance on 13 hepatic disease classes: 81.32% precision, 79.18% recall, 79.12% F1.
- Ablation shows multi-agent deliberation contributes a 9.42-point F1 gain; hybrid retrieval is also critical.
- Inference latency: 9.95s for simple cases, 33.36s for complex cases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Hybrid Evidence Pruning
Hybrid retrieval and guideline-informed pruning of UMLS paths reduces spurious evidence. For each candidate diagnosis, the system retrieves raw 3-hop KG paths and guideline excerpts, then uses the full clinical narrative and top guidelines to prune KG paths via an LLM judgment of clinical coherence. This filters out implausible semantic links before agent deliberation. The core assumption is that the LLM can reliably identify guideline-aligned paths when given the complete narrative and authoritative excerpts. Evidence is in the abstract and section 3.2.2. Break condition: if the pruner fails to discriminate due to narrative ambiguity, irrelevant paths remain. This combination of KG and guideline-constrained pruning is novel relative to prior graph-based RAG systems like MedGraphRAG.

### Mechanism 2: Dynamic Specialist Dispatch
A router agent dynamically dispatches specialist agents based on abnormal findings and case complexity, enabling targeted, efficient deliberation. The router computes complexity and activates only context-relevant roles (e.g., Hepatology, Oncology). Specialists iterate on the same pruned evidence package, with re-retrieval triggered only if a majority deem evidence insufficient (ρ(t) > τ_suff). The assumption is that abnormal findings map reliably to specialties and that specialist stances indicate evidence gaps. Evidence is in the abstract and section 3.2.3. Break condition: if the routing heuristic omits a critical specialty (e.g., Nephrology for renal involvement), differentials are incomplete. This dynamic, abnormality-driven dispatch contrasts with static agent teams in systems like ColaCare.

### Mechanism 3: Teacher-Student Distillation of Interaction Patterns
Teacher-student distillation transfers complex multi-agent reasoning to a deployable 8B model without catastrophic loss. The teacher (Qwen3-Max) simulates specialist agent behavior within the MedCoRAG loop to generate training trajectories. The student (Llama-3.1-8B) is fine-tuned via LoRA on these trajectories, learning role-specific prompts, evidence assessment patterns, and consensus formation. The assumption is that the teacher's simulated dialogues capture high-quality, clinically plausible reasoning. Evidence is in sections 4.4 and 5.4. Break condition: if teacher simulations contain systematic biases or hallucinations, the student inherits them. Distilling multi-agent interaction patterns—rather than single-step answers—is less explored in neighboring work.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: UMLS entity standardization and multi-hop path retrieval**  
  Why needed: The entire evidence package depends on finding coherent semantic paths from abnormal findings (e.g., "IgM_elevated") to candidate diagnoses (e.g., "PBC") via intermediate UMLS concepts. Standardization ensures entity matching in the KG.  
  Quick check: If an abnormal finding "elevated ALP" is not standardized to the correct UMLS concept, what is the most likely failure mode in subsequent KG retrieval?

- **Concept: Clinical guideline authority and structure**  
  Why needed: Guidelines (e.g., EASL, AASLD) provide the authoritative clinical constraints used for pruning and final justification. The system must distinguish guideline excerpts from general knowledge and understand their hierarchical role (diagnostic criteria vs. treatment).  
  Quick check: Why are guidelines used *after* initial KG path retrieval, rather than as the sole evidence source?

- **Concept: Multi-agent coordination under uncertainty**  
  Why needed: Specialists operate with incomplete evidence, expressing stance, confidence, and sufficiency. The system aggregates these signals (Eq. 12, 13) to decide when to stop or re-retrieve. Understanding the iterative debate loop and thresholds (τ_suff=0.5, τ_high=0.9) is crucial.  
  Quick check: What happens if all specialists report "Neutral" stance but collectively judge evidence "Insufficient"? How should the Router interpret this?

## Architecture Onboarding
The pipeline flows sequentially: EHR -> Clinical Narrative -> Abnormal Findings & Initial Hypotheses -> Hybrid Retrieval & Pruning -> Evidence Packages -> Router -> (if complex) Specialist Agents -> Generalist Consensus. The critical path is: standardized abnormal entities -> accurate KG retrieval -> correct LLM pruning -> appropriate specialist dispatch -> reliable evidence sufficiency signal. A failure at any link corrupts the shared evidence base.

Design tradeoffs include: hybrid retrieval increases evidence quality but costs ~2x latency vs. single-source; dynamic dispatch reduces unnecessary agent calls for simple cases (9.95s vs 33.36s) but adds routing complexity and potential mis-routing; pruning aggressively risks losing valid paths, leniently risks noise.

Failure signatures: (1) Low recall on certain diseases (e.g., LF: 15.79%) suggests the evidence package misses key paths for ambiguous presentations; (2) Ablation without multi-agent drops F1 by 9.42 points—if specialist stances are correlated or neutral, the benefit vanishes; (3) If ρ(t) never exceeds τ_suff in complex cases, re-retrieval never triggers, leading to premature consensus on insufficient evidence.

First three experiments:  
1. Build and validate the Hybrid RAG module in isolation: For 50 diverse hepatic cases, manually inspect pruned KG paths and guideline excerpts. Aim for >90% clinician agreement on package relevance.  
2. Test the Router's complexity classifier: Use a held-out set of cases labeled "simple"/"complex" by clinicians. Measure ϕ_comp accuracy; target ≥85%.  
3. Run a "single specialist" ablation: For complex cases, force dispatch of only the Generalist Agent. Compare F1 to full MedCoRAG; expect drop >10% to confirm role-based deliberation necessity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can MedCoRAG incorporate longitudinal EHR data for time-aware diagnosis?**  
   *Basis:* Lacks modeling of longitudinal signals (Sec.5.6). *Why unresolved:* Current static snapshot ignores disease progression. *Resolving evidence:* Implement temporal encoding and evaluate on a longitudinal dataset for improved accuracy and earlier detection.

2. **Can MedCoRAG's multi-agent architecture be generalized to other medical specialties?**  
   *Basis:* Future work includes extending to broader clinical domains (Sec.5.6). *Why unresolved:* Agents and evidence sources are hepatology-tailored. *Resolving evidence:* Deploy in two new specialties with appropriate agent configurations and report comparable performance to domain-specific baselines.

3. **Will MedCoRAG maintain effectiveness when prospectively evaluated in live clinical workflows?**  
   *Basis:* All evaluations are retrospective, not validated in live workflows (Sec.5.6). *Why unresolved:* Real-world deployment introduces data heterogeneity and clinician interaction factors. *Resolving evidence:* Conduct a prospective trial measuring diagnostic accuracy, decision time, satisfaction, and adverse events.

## Limitations
- Undisclosed prompt templates and few-shot examples risk implementation drift.
- Opaque router specification (ϕ_comp, ψ) limits generalizability and reproducibility.
- Knowledge source gaps (UMLS/guideline) may harm rare diseases, as seen in low LF recall (15.79%).

## Confidence
- **Hybrid retrieval mechanism:** Medium (novel but unevaluated in isolation).
- **Multi-agent coordination:** High (supported by strong ablation results).
- **Teacher-student distillation of interaction patterns:** Medium (transfer of complex dialogue patterns is unverified).
