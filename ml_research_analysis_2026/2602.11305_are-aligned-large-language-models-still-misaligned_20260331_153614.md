# Are Aligned Large Language Models Still Misaligned?
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11305
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11305
- **Reference Count:** 7
- **Primary Result:** Single-dimension fine-tuned models show high Coverage (up to 97.6%) but suffer False Failure Rates >50% under joint-dimension constraints, while general-purpose aligned models maintain higher Alignment Scores (81.0–81.6%) by balancing constraints.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Mis-Align Bench and the SAVACU dataset (382,424 samples across 112 domains) to evaluate large language model alignment under joint safety, value, and cultural constraints. Benchmarking reveals that models fine-tuned on a single dimension achieve high detection coverage but incur excessive false failures when multiple dimensions must be satisfied simultaneously. In contrast, general-purpose aligned models maintain better overall alignment by balancing trade-offs. The work demonstrates that misalignment emerges from the interaction of dimensions, which isolated benchmarks fail to capture, and provides an automated pipeline for constructing multi-dimensional alignment test beds.

## Method Summary
The study proceeds in two stages. First, it constructs SAVACU via a two-module pipeline: Module I uses Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 for multi-label classification of ~309k prompts into 112 safety/value/cultural domains, expands low-resource domains with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and deduplicates with SimHash (Hamming threshold τ=10). Module II pairs each query with a high-quality aligned response (satisfying all three dimensions) and a misaligned response (violating at least one) using two-stage rejection sampling with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as judge. Second, it benchmarks various models (including dimension-specific LoRA fine-tunes and general-purpose aligned models) zero-shot on the test split, generating responses and evaluating them with the same judge to compute Coverage, False Failure Rate (FFR), and Alignment Score (harmonic mean).

## Key Results
- Single-dimension fine-tuned models achieve Coverage up to 97.6% on their target domain but show FFR exceeding 50% on joint-dimension subsets and overall Alignment Scores of only 63–66%.
- General-purpose aligned models maintain higher Alignment Scores (81.0–81.6%) by balancing detection with lower false positives.
- Cross-domain analysis confirms that misalignment is most severe when prompts require navigating multiple dimensions simultaneously.
- Human-model agreement for the automated classification pipeline is within 3% (84.6% human vs. 81.6% model accuracy on a 300-sample validation set).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Fine-tuning on a single alignment dimension causes overfitting to that constraint, leading to poor performance under joint-dimension conditions.
**Mechanism:** The model learns a biased decision boundary optimized for one loss signal, which fails to account for trade-offs with other dimensions (e.g., value vs. cultural), resulting in excessive rejection of valid multi-constraint responses (high FFR).
**Evidence:** [abstract], [section 4.1, Table 2] show safety-specific models with 97.6% Coverage but 53.6%+ FFR on cross-domain aligned samples.
**Break condition:** Training on a balanced, multi-objective dataset representing all dimension interactions equally could mitigate the overfitting.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Misalignment failures are interactional—most frequent and severe when prompts involve multiple dimensions.
**Mechanism:** Real-world queries inherently combine constraints; models not explicitly trained on these conjunctions lack policies to resolve normative tensions (e.g., cultural appropriateness vs. universal safety), defaulting to over-cautiousness or blind spots.
**Evidence:** [abstract], [section 4.1, Table 3] show dimension-specific models’ Coverage gains are accompanied by FFR spikes on aligned cross-domain samples, while general-purpose models stabilize.
**Break condition:** If joint-dimension constraints were perfectly separable (no tension), single-dimension performance would transfer; results contradict this.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The SAVACU construction pipeline (multi-label classification + controlled generation + rejection sampling) effectively creates a dataset exposing joint-dimension misalignment.
**Mechanism:** 1) Unified taxonomy forces prompts to be labeled for all applicable dimensions. 2) Two-stage rejection sampling generates high-quality aligned/misaligned pairs per prompt. 3) SimHash deduplication ensures diversity without leakage.
**Evidence:** [section 3.1] details the pipeline; [section 4.1, Table 4] shows human-model agreement within 3%, validating automated labeling.
**Break condition:** Systematic evaluator bias (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct in non-Western contexts) would invalidate labels; human validation is limited (n=300).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multi-Label Classification**
  **Why needed:** Prompts often belong to multiple safety/value/cultural domains simultaneously. The pipeline uses an LLM as a zero-shot multi-label classifier with a threshold (δ=0.5) to assign all applicable domain labels.
  **Quick check:** If a prompt is classified with scores [0.6, 0.4, 0.7] for three domains and δ=0.5, which domains are included? What if δ=0.9?

- **Concept: Rejection Sampling with Feedback-Guided Resampling**
  **Why needed:** To obtain a high-quality (aligned, misaligned) pair per prompt, the pipeline first filters candidates, then appends diagnostic feedback to the query if a pair is missing, resampling. This iteratively refines the query to elicit the required response types.
  **Quick check:** Why is feedback appended to the *query* rather than used to edit the candidate response directly? What risk does this mitigate?

- **Concept: SimHash for Leakage-Safe Deduplication**
  **Why needed:** To expand low-resource domains without introducing near-duplicate prompts that could cause training/data leakage. SimHash produces bit fingerprints; a Hamming distance threshold (τ=10) prunes duplicates.
  **Quick check:** If using cosine similarity instead of SimHash, which metric (e.g., Cross-Split Leakage Rate) would likely degrade, and why?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  LLM-PROMPT-DATASET -> Module I (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 classifier + Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct expansion + SimHash dedup) -> Balanced query set Q -> Module II (Q + F_gen pool: Gemma-3-27B, Phi-3-14B, Qwen-2.5-32B -> Candidate responses) -> Stage 1 filter (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct judge) -> If missing pair: feedback + resample -> Final paired dataset SAVACU -> Benchmarking (Model f_θ zero-shot on test split -> judge evaluation -> Cov/FFR/AS metrics).

- **Critical path:** Dataset quality hinges on: (a) classifier accuracy (Mistral-7B), (b) diversity of F_gen, (c) reliability of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct as judge, (d) SimHash threshold τ preventing over-pruning.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Automation vs. Cost: Fully automated pipeline enables scale but risks evaluator bias; human validation is limited (n=300).
  - Generality vs. Depth: 112 domains cover breadth but rely on static taxonomies (BEAVERTAILS, VALUECOMPASS, UNESCO); evolving norms may be missed.
  - Pair Quality: Two-stage rejection sampling aims for high-quality pairs but discards ~<8% of queries where pairs cannot be found.

- **Failure signatures:**
  1. High FFR in dimension-specific models: indicates failure to satisfy non-target dimensions when target is met.
  2. Low Coverage in open-weight models: indicates under-sensitivity; model rejects too few misaligned outputs.
  3. SimHash over-pruning (τ too high): loses semantically distinct prompts, hurting long-tailed domain coverage.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate classification agreement: Sample 300 new instances, have 3 human annotators label domains, compute agreement with Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3; expect ≤3% gap as in Table 4.
  2. Threshold sensitivity sweep: Vary δ (0.1–0.9) in Module I; plot FP/FN rates for multi-label assignment; validate δ=0.5 is optimal.
  3. Evaluator reliability check: For 100 random SAVACU instances, have humans score alignment per dimension; compare with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct; compute per-dimension agreement; if <80% on Cultural, evaluator bias is likely.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does misalignment manifest across languages and cultural contexts beyond English, and can interactional failure patterns be generalized to multilingual settings? *Basis:* Limitations state SAVACU is English-only. *Resolution:* Build non-English versions and compare Alignment Scores.
- **Open Question 2:** Do alternative or evolving normative frameworks expose additional joint-dimension failure modes not captured by predefined taxonomies? *Basis:* Limitations note reliance on static taxonomies. *Resolution:* Redesign taxonomy from dynamic sources, regenerate SAVACU, compare error distributions.
- **Open Question 3:** To what extent can LLM-based automated evaluators reliably replace human judgment for joint-dimension alignment, and what are their systematic biases? *Basis:* Limitations cite dependence on LLM-based assertions; human-model gap is 3%. *Resolution:* Large-scale multi-annotator human evaluation on a stratified subset, with error analysis of discrepancies.
- **Open Question 4:** What training/architectural modifications can achieve joint robustness without the prohibitive cost of general-purpose aligned models? *Basis:* Limitations and Table 5 show efficiency-robustness trade-off. *Resolution:* Design and evaluate novel paradigms (e.g., parameter-efficient multi-task learning) optimizing for harmonic mean of Coverage/(1–FFR) while measuring throughput/memory.

## Limitations
- **Evaluator bias:** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct’s judgments may not generalize to non-Western contexts; human validation sample is small (n=300) and potentially domain-skewed.
- **Unreleased models:** Results for MARL-FOCAL and TRINITYX cannot be externally verified due to lack of public implementation or checkpoints.
- **Test split opacity:** Exact query distribution across joint-dimension subsets is not publicly itemized, risking replication mismatches.

## Confidence
- **High:** Joint-dimension interactions cause severe misalignment (consistent patterns in Tables 2–3).
- **Medium:** SAVACU construction pipeline reliably captures multi-dimensional alignment (human-model agreement ~84.6%, but validation sample is small).
- **High:** General-purpose aligned models better balance constraints vs. single-dimension fine-tuned models (direct benchmark results).
- **Low:** Claims about multilingual generalization or alternative normative frameworks (unresolved open questions).

## Next Checks
1. Replicate human-model agreement on a stratified sample of 1,000 instances across all 112 domains with diverse annotators; report per-dimension kappa to expose evaluator blind spots.
2. Train a model on a balanced multi-objective dataset (mixing safety/value/cultural examples equally) and measure whether Coverage/FFR gaps narrow on SAVACU’s joint subsets, testing the interactional tension hypothesis.
3. Run a cross-cultural robustness test: Replace the judge with a culturally specific model (e.g., Yi-34B-Chat) and measure divergence from Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct’s scores on Cultural-domain samples.
