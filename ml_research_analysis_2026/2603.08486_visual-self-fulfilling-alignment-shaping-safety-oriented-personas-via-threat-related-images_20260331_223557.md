# Visual Self-Fulfilling Alignment: Shaping Safety-Oriented Personas via Threat-Related Images
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.08486
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08486
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: VSFA reduces attack success rate by ~60% (e.g., 38.77% → 14.18% on Qwen3-VL-8B), improves constructive response quality (CS 0.11 → 0.50), and keeps over-refusal low (1.82% vs 29.36% with prompting).

## Executive Summary
Multimodal large language models (VLMs) suffer from safety misalignment where images can trigger harmful outputs. Existing defenses require explicit safety labels, but safety is abstract and hard to visualize. This paper proposes Visual Self-Fulfilling Alignment (VSFA), a fine-tuning method that uses neutral VQA pairs built around threat-related images—no safety supervision. Repeated exposure to such visuals is hypothesized to instill vigilance and caution, shaping a safety-oriented persona. Across four VLMs and three jailbreak benchmarks, VSFA cuts attack success rate substantially (e.g., from 38.77% to 14.18% on Qwen3-VL-8B) and raises constructive response quality, while keeping over-refusal on benign queries minimal (as low as 1.82%) and preserving multimodal performance. Mechanistic analysis with sparse autoencoders uncovers a latent strongly linked to vigilance that activates after VSFA and whose steering bidirectionally controls safety behavior, confirming internal persona shaping.

## Method Summary
The VSFA pipeline begins with AI safety abstracts from arXiv, transformed into image prompts using GPT-4o-mini. These prompts, enriched with threat-related modifiers and diverse visual styles/scenes, generate 700 images via the Doubao API. For each image, 6 neutral VQA pairs are created using 16 question templates across descriptive, open-ended, analytical, and contextual categories. Answers from GPT-4o-mini are filtered for neutrality, clarity, and consistency (score ≥6.0). The resulting 4,200 pairs fine-tune a target VLM with LoRA (rank 128, lr=2e-5, 5 epochs, batch 16), freezing the visual encoder and updating only the language model. Training runs on a single NVIDIA L20 GPU for 3–6 hours. Evaluation uses GPT-4o as a judge on three jailbreak benchmarks (FigStep, MM-SafetyBench, SPA-VL) to compute Attack Success Rate (ASR) and Constructive Score (CS), and on MM-Vet to measure over-refusal. Mechanistic analysis applies sparse autoencoders (SAE) to middle-layer activations to identify a vigilance-associated latent.

## Key Results
- Significantly reduced attack success rate (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B: 38.77% → 14.18%) and improved constructive response quality (CS 0.11 → 0.50).
- Minimized over-refusal on benign queries (as low as 1.82% vs 29.36% with prompting) while preserving general multimodal capabilities.
- Mechanistic analysis uncovered a vigilance-associated latent; steering it bidirectionally controls safety behavior, confirming internal persona shaping.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
VSFA operates on the principle of implicit, self-fulfilling alignment: repeated exposure to threat-related imagery—even without explicit safety labels—encourages the model to internalize a cautious, vigilant persona. The neutral VQA context ensures the model engages with the images descriptively rather than reactively, allowing safety associations to form at a representational level. Mechanistically, sparse autoencoder analysis reveals a specific latent dimension that becomes strongly active after fine-tuning; this latent correlates with concepts of vigilance and threat assessment. Steering this latent empirically toggles safety outputs, demonstrating that VSFA shapes the model’s internal decision landscape rather than merely adding refusal rules.

## Foundational Learning
- **Neutral VQA Construction**: Non-safety-laden question-answer pairs that describe or analyze images.  
  *Why needed*: Enables implicit learning without explicit supervision, preventing shortcutting.  
  *Quick check*: Evaluate a sample with GPT-4o using the Appendix B rubric; ≥90% of pairs should score ≥6.0.

- **Threat-Related Imagery**: Visuals containing warning signals, surveillance, or ominous atmospheres derived from AI safety abstracts.  
  *Why needed*: Acts as subtle cues that trigger vigilance associations during fine-tuning.  
  *Quick check*: Human annotators rate a subset for presence of threat cues; aim >80% agreement on “menacing” or “dystopian” elements.

- **LoRA Fine-Tuning**: Low-rank adaptation that updates only the language model’s weights while freezing the vision encoder.  
  *Why needed*: Efficiently injects new behaviors without catastrophic forgetting of visual features.  
  *Quick check*: Verify training logs exclude vision encoder parameters and that validation loss decreases smoothly.

- **Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) Analysis**: Unsupervised decomposition of model activations to find interpretable latents.  
  *Why needed*: Provides interpretable evidence that VSFA alters internal representations toward vigilance.  
  *Quick check*: Train SAE on base and fine-tuned models; a vigilance-related latent should show increased activation on safety-sensitive inputs.

- **Over-Refusal Constraint**: The requirement that improved safety does not degrade performance on benign queries.  
  *Why needed*: Balances safety with usability, a key practical consideration.  
  *Quick check*: Measure Refusal Rate on MM-Vet; aim for <5% while ASR improves.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
arXiv abstracts → GPT-4o-mini (prompt generation) → Doubao API (image generation) → Images → GPT-4o-mini (VQA generation) → Filtered dataset → LoRA fine-tune (LM only) → Evaluation (GPT-4o judge on benchmarks) → SAE training (activations) → Vigilance latent identification.

**Critical Path**:
1. Data pipeline (abstracts → prompts → images → VQA) must produce diverse, threat-related visuals and neutral QAs.
2. LoRA fine-tuning must converge without overfitting (monitor loss, validation ASR).
3. Evaluation prompts and judge must be consistent to ensure fair comparison across defenses.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- Threat imagery vs abstract safety: using concrete visuals simplifies data collection but may not cover all safety nuances.
- LoRA rank and epochs: higher rank/epoch may improve safety but risk over-refusal; selection balances ASR reduction and benign usability.
- Freezing vision encoder preserves general vision capabilities but limits adaptation of visual representations to safety concepts.

**Failure Signatures**:
- ASR remains high after fine-tuning → dataset threat semantics weak or LoRA under-powered.
- Refusal Rate spikes on MM-Vet → training data may have inadvertently included refusal instructions or too few neutral examples.
- SAE finds no clear vigilance latent → insufficient training data or inappropriate layer selection.
- Training loss diverges → incorrect LoRA application or hyperparameter mismatch.

**First Three Experiments**:
1. Validate dataset quality: Build a small VSFA set (e.g., 50 images, 300 QAs). Have humans rate threat cues in images and neutrality of QAs; target >80% positive rates.
2. Replicate LoRA fine-tune on a small model (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B) with a subset (500 examples) for 3 epochs. Check loss curve and run a quick ASR probe on one benchmark to confirm downward trend.
3. Reproduce SAE analysis: Train a TopK SAE (expansion=4, k=100) on activations from the base model’s middle layer, then on the fine-tuned model. Identify top-activated latent on safety prompts and test steering by adding/subtracting its vector; observe ASR change.

## Limitations
- Doubao API configuration (key, region, parameters) is unreported, making image generation potentially non-reproducible.
- Neutrality scoring (for VQA) and GPT-4o judge prompts are missing, hindering consistent quality control and metric computation.
- SAE training hyperparameters (expansion factor, k-value, activation sampling) are unspecified, blocking exact mechanistic replication.
