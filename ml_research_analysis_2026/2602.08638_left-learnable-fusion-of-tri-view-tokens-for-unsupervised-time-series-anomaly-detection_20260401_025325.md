# LEFT: Learnable Fusion of Tri-view Tokens for Unsupervised Time Series Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08638
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08638
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Achieves state-of-the-art unsupervised time series anomaly detection on seven benchmarks, improving average VUS-ROC by ~3% and VUS-PR by ~6% over prior best, while reducing computational cost by over 80% FLOPs and speeding up training ~8×.

## Executive Summary
LEFT introduces a novel unsupervised time series anomaly detection framework that models anomalies as inconsistencies across three complementary views: time domain, frequency domain, and multi-scale resolutions. Its core is a tri-view token architecture with a bidirectional time-frequency cycle consistency loss and a cross-path constraint aligning multi-scale reconstructions. This design captures both local temporal dynamics and global periodic patterns while maintaining computational efficiency through selective, directed fusion and a Nyquist-constrained filterbank. Evaluated on seven public benchmarks, LEFT sets new performance records while being significantly more lightweight than existing state-of-the-art methods.

## Method Summary
LEFT processes input time series windows through three parallel tokenization pathways. Time-domain tokens use stacked 1D convolutions to capture local dynamics. Frequency tokens are generated via a differentiable Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) followed by a 2D convolutional encoder. Multi-scale tokens employ a learnable, Nyquist-constrained filterbank to produce stable downsampled resolutions without aliasing. These tokens interact via lightweight, directed cross-attention (time↔frequency, multi-scale←time) over shared blocks. Two prototype banks (time/frequency) provide memory-augmented representations via soft assignment with an uncertainty gate. A bidirectional cycle decoder enforces mutual reconstruction between time and frequency views. A multi-scale decoder reconstructs across all scales under a weighted SmoothL1 loss. A cross-path constraint aligns the multi-scale reconstruction with the cycle reconstruction. The final anomaly score fuses point-wise reconstruction errors from both pathways, optionally smoothed with a moving average.

## Key Results
- **SOTA Performance:** On seven benchmarks (SMD, MSL, SMAP, SWaT, PSM, GECCO, SWAN), LEFT achieves the highest average VUS-ROC and VUS-PR, surpassing the best prior method by ~3% and ~6% respectively.
- **Efficiency:** Reduces FLOPs by over 80% and speeds up training approximately 8 times compared to strong baselines like Anomaly Transformer, while using fewer parameters.
- **Ablation Validation:** Removing the bidirectional cycle consistency degrades performance by ~0.2 average VUS-ROC, confirming its critical role. Directed cross-attention outperforms all-pairs fusion.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's central hypothesis is that anomalies manifest as *view-inconsistencies*—patterns that are normal in one representation but abnormal in another. This is operationalized through three mechanisms:
1.  **Tri-view Complementarity:** Time tokens capture abrupt changes; frequency tokens reveal periodic disruptions; multi-scale tokens detect anomalies at different temporal granularities. Modeling all three provides a more complete abnormal signature.
2.  **Bidirectional Cycle Consistency:** By forcing a time-series representation to reconstruct the STFT and vice versa, the model learns a shared latent that must preserve information faithful to *both* domains. Anomalies, which are inherently inconsistent across domains, yield higher reconstruction errors in this cycle.
3.  **Selective Fusion:** Instead of dense all-pairs attention (costly and noisy), LEFT uses a sparse directed graph (T↔F, M←T). This focuses learning on the most informative interactions (time-frequency mutual reconstruction, multi-scale refinement with time) while minimizing irrelevant mixing that could dilute anomaly evidence.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept 1: Tri-view Tokenization**
    - *Why needed:* To encapsulate distinct data characteristics (local dynamics, periodicity, scale-dependent patterns) into discrete, interactive units.
    - *Quick check:* Verify output token sequences from each encoder have compatible dimension `D` and length `L` (e.g., `L≈window_length/stride`).
- **Concept 2: Differentiable STFT**
    - *Why needed:* To embed frequency information as a learnable token sequence, enabling gradient flow for joint time-frequency optimization.
    - *Quick check:* Confirm `Ŝ = W_θ(X)` and `X̂_rec = W_θ^{-1}(Ŝ)` are invertible for a random batch (reconstruction loss near zero for clean data).
- **Concept 3: Prototype Banks & Uncertainty**
    - *Why needed:* To provide a compressed, cluster-based memory for normal patterns, where assignment sharpness indicates confidence. Low confidence (high entropy) signals potential anomalies.
    - *Quick check:* Track assignment entropy `H(p)` on a clean validation set; it should be low and stable.
- **Concept 4: Cycle Consistency Loss**
    - *Why needed:* To enforce that the shared latent representation is invertible across domains, making deviations (anomalies) cause high error in at least one direction.
    - *Quick check:* With cycle loss disabled, `L_cyc` should be high; with it enabled, `L_cyc` must decrease faster than single-direction losses.
- **Concept 5: Nyquist-Constrained Filterbank**
    - *Why needed:* To generatealias-free multi-scale representations by ensuring each learnable band mask respects the theoretical Nyquist limit for its downsampling factor.
    - *Quick check:* After training, sum of learned band masks across scales should be ≈1 for all frequencies; no mask should extend beyond `f_Nyquist / r_k`.
- **Concept 6: Cross-Path Constraint**
    - *Why needed:* To align the multi-scale reconstruction (which may focus on coarse patterns) with the cycle reconstruction (which captures finer time-frequency details), ensuring both pathways agree on normal data.
    - *Quick check:* `L_cons = |X̂_ms - X̂←f|` should be low on normal validation data and spike on injected anomalies.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input Window -> Time Encoder -> Time Tokens  
Input Window -> STFT -> Frequency Tokens  
Input Window -> Filterbank -> Multi-scale Tokens  
Time Tokens <-> Frequency Tokens (via Cycle Decoder)  
Multi-scale Tokens -> Time Tokens (via Cross-Attention)  
[Time, Frequency, Multi-scale Tokens] -> Prototype Banks -> Fused Latent `z` -> [Time Decoder, Frequency Decoder, Multi-scale Decoder] -> Reconstructions (X̂, Ŝ, X̂_ms) -> Anomaly Score `A(t)`

**Critical Path:** The core training loop minimizes `L = λ_ms L_ms + λ_cyc L_cyc + λ_cons L_cons`. The bidirectional cycle between time and frequency is the primary driver for cross-view anomaly capture. The multi-scale pathway provides complementary evidence, aligned via `L_cons`.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- *Lightweight Fusion vs. Expressiveness:* Directed cross-attention reduces FLOPs but may miss some cross-view interactions. The paper shows all-pairs fusion underperforms, suggesting over-mixing is harmful.
- *Learnable Filterbank vs. Fixed Wavelets:* Learnable masks adapt to data but risk aliasing without the Nyquist constraint. The constraint adds complexity but ensures stable multi-scale tokens.
- *Prototype Memory vs. Capacity:* Small prototype banks (M=256) are efficient but may underfit complex normal distributions. Larger banks could improve normality modeling at a cost.

**Failure Signatures:**
- *Cycle Collapse:* `L_cyc` stagnates or increases; VUS-ROC drops sharply. Indicates one direction of the cycle is broken (e.g., STFT gradient vanished).
- *Multi-scale Aliasing:* Learned band masks overlap or extend beyond Nyquist zones; coarse-scale tokens appear noisy; poor performance on high-frequency anomalies.
- *Over-fusion:* Using more cross-attention layers (`L_f > 1`) or all-pairs attention degrades VUS-PR, as shown in ablation studies.
- *Threshold Misalignment:* SPOT threshold yields excessive false positives on test set; often due to fitting on training scores instead of validation, or ignoring the uncertainty gate `g(t)`.

**First Experiments:**
1.  **Tokenization Sanity:** Pass a batch through each encoder. Check: (a) No NaNs/Infs in tokens. (b) Token sequence lengths are as expected (e.g., for SMAP, time tokens length ~12). (c) Frequency token dimensionality matches `2 * n_fft` channels projected to `D`.
2.  **Cycle Loss Functionality:** Train for 10 steps with and without `L_cyc`. Expected: Model with `L_cyc` shows lower `|X̂ - X|` and `|Ŝ - S|` on a held-out normal batch. Without `L_cyc`, frequency reconstruction may remain poor.
3.  **Filterband Partitioning:** After minimal training (1 epoch), extract learned band edges `e_k` and masks `b_k`. Verify: (a) Edges are monotonically increasing. (b) Masks are non-zero only within `[0, f_Nyquist/r_k]`. (c) Sum of masks across scales is near-constant (~1) across frequencies.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **When does cross-view inconsistency most strongly signal anomalies?** The paper shows overall effectiveness but does not analyze which anomaly types (point vs. contextual, spectral shifts) benefit most from the tri-view framework.
- **How can detected anomalies be interpreted?** LEFT outputs aggregate scores without attributing them to specific views or resolutions, limiting actionable insights for operators.
- **How can hyperparameters be set automatically?** Sensitivity analysis shows performance varies sharply with consistency weights (`λ_cyc`, `λ_cons`) and multi-scale kernel sizes, yet the paper uses manual tuning. An unsupervised, adaptive mechanism is needed.
- **What theoretical guarantees exist?** While Lemmas 3.1–3.3 bound aliasing and reconstruction errors, a general statistical analysis (e.g., false positive rates under noise) is lacking, limiting understanding of failure modes.

## Limitations
- **Unreported Hyperparameters:** Critical values like prototype bank size `M`, assignment temperature `γ`, multi-scale mixing coefficients (`β_res`, `β_int`), loss weights (`ω_k`, `α_*`), and STFT parameters (`n_fft`, `hop_length`) are omitted, making exact replication low-confidence.
- **Vague Training Protocol:** Epoch count, early stopping, optimizer weight decay, gradient clipping, and training window overlap are unspecified, undermining claims about training speed and convergence stability.
- **Thresholding Ambiguity:** SPOT parameters (e.g., `q`, `level`) and how per-timestamp scores aggregate to window-level decisions are not detailed, creating medium-low confidence in the reported detection performance.

## Confidence
- **Tri-view Fusion Innovation:** High. The directed cross-attention and cycle consistency design are clearly articulated and ablated.
- **SOTA Performance:** Medium. Results on 7 benchmarks are strong, but unreported hyperparameters and thresholding leave room for performance variance in independent replication.
- **Efficiency Gains:** Medium-Low. Claims of >80% FLOPs reduction and 8× speedup depend on unspecified baseline comparison setups (hardware, batch size, epoch count).
- **Nyquist-Constrained Filterbank:** High. The mathematical constraint is well-defined, though implementation edge cases are sparse.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cycle Consistency Ablation:** Systematically disable either the time→freq→time or freq→time→freq direction. A drop in average VUS-ROC >0.1 would confirm the necessity of bidirectionality.
2.  **Filterbank Aliasing Inspection:** Visualize learned band masks post-training. Verify they partition the spectrum cleanly, respect Nyquist limits for each scale (`r_k`), and exhibit minimal spectral leakage (`ε_k` norm).
3.  **Efficiency Replication Under Controlled Conditions:** Implement LEFT and the strongest baseline (e.g., Anomaly Transformer) on identical hardware (NVIDIA L40), with the same batch size (128), input window length, and epoch count. Measure FLOPs/sample and total training time. A deviation >20% from reported gains suggests unreported optimizations or conditional dependency on specific hyperparameters.
