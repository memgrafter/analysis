# Traceable Latent Variable Discovery Based on Multi-Agent Collaboration

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.14456
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14456
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: TLVD framework achieves average improvements of 32.67% in Accuracy, 62.21% in Causal Accuracy, and 26.72% in Evidence Citation over state-of-the-art baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of discovering latent variables and interpreting their semantics within causal graphs—a task complicated by traditional algorithms' assumptions of no latent confounders and their inability to infer variable meanings. The proposed Traceable Latent Variable Discovery (TLVD) framework integrates three key components: (1) data-driven causal structure learning via RLCD to produce a graph with latent variables, (2) a multi-LLM collaboration system (MALLM) modeled as an incomplete-information game seeking Bayesian Nash Equilibrium to hypothesize latent variable semantics, and (3) validation by retrieving supporting evidence from web sources like arXiv and Wikipedia. Evaluated on five datasets (including three de-identified hospital datasets), TLVD substantially outperforms existing baselines, demonstrating superior accuracy, causal precision, and evidence traceability.

## Method Summary
TLVD operates in three stages. First, the RLCD algorithm processes observational data to identify a Markov equivalence class that may incorporate latent variables, yielding a causal graph and the Markov blanket for each latent candidate. Second, a multi-agent LLM system (MALLM) with a coordinator and multiple executors infers plausible semantics for these latent variables. Executors maintain belief networks (GRU/MLP) that generate prompt embeddings (temperature, penalty) based on local histories; a mixing network aggregates Q-values, and training via temporal-difference loss with multi-component rewards (action likelihood, uncertainty reduction, collaborative contribution, evidence reliability) guides policies toward a Bayesian Nash Equilibrium. Third, for each proposed latent variable, LLMs query arXiv and Wikipedia to retrieve relevant documents; evidence is assessed for relevance and reliability, fed back to update agent strategies, and contributes to the final output. The process iterates across latent nodes until convergence.

## Key Results
- Average Accuracy improvement: 32.67%
- Average Causal Accuracy improvement: 62.21%
- Average Evidence Citation improvement: 26.72%
- On WCHSU-Cancer (n=12), TLVD achieves ACC 0.833 vs. the next best baseline (MiniMax) at 0.467.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: RLCD Graphical Scaffolding**
- Claim: RLCD constructs a causal graph that incorporates latent variables, providing structural scaffolding for subsequent inference.
- Mechanism: Apply RLCD to observational data to obtain a Markov equivalence class that may contain latent variables; this graph guides the identification of Markov blankets for each latent variable.
- Core assumption: RLCD’s theoretical guarantees (e.g., identifiability under linear non‑Gaussian models or appropriate rank constraints) hold for the data generating process, and the sample size is sufficient.
- Evidence anchors: abstract: “first employ a data‑driven approach to construct a causal graph that incorporates latent variables.”; section: Section 3.1: “RLCD [11] is currently the most advanced algorithm with theoretical guarantees, and thus, we use it to identify latent causal structures.”; corpus: Neighbor papers focus on latent causal discovery, providing moderate support for the general approach.
- Break condition: If the data violates RLCD assumptions (e.g., incorrect noise distributions) or the sample size is too small, the recovered graph may be inaccurate, leading to poor downstream latent variable proposals.

**Mechanism 2: Multi-LLM BNE Coordination**
- Claim: Modeling the inference process as a game with incomplete information and seeking its Bayesian Nash Equilibrium effectively coordinates diverse LLMs to infer plausible latent variable semantics.
- Mechanism: A coordinator LLM distributes queries; execution LLMs maintain belief networks that map local histories to belief states, which generate prompt embeddings (temperature, penalty) controlling LLM outputs. A mixing network aggregates beliefs to compute global Q‑values. Training via TD loss with rewards (action likelihood, uncertainty reduction, collaborative contribution, evidence reliability) drives policies toward BNE.
- Core assumption: The DEC‑POMDP formulation accurately captures agent interactions; agents can learn policies that converge to BNE; reward components align with correct latent variable inference.
- Evidence anchors: abstract: “modeling this process as a game with incomplete information and seeking its Bayesian Nash Equilibrium (BNE) to infer the possible specific latent variables.”; section: Section 3.2 details MALLM architecture, belief networks, mixing network, reward design; Section 3.2.3 provides theoretical analysis of BNE existence and convergence; corpus: Neighbors include multi‑agent LLM systems, indicating the promise of multi‑agent collaboration.
- Break condition: Agents may fail to converge due to high variance, insufficient training, or poor reward design; too many agents (N>6) cause coordination breakdown (empirically observed); heterogeneous LLMs struggle to reach BNE.

**Mechanism 3: Web Evidence Validation**
- Claim: Retrieval and integration of causal evidence from multiple web data sources (arXiv, Wikipedia, databases) validates inferred latent variables, ensuring traceability and improving reliability.
- Mechanism: After initial inference, LLMs query web sources to retrieve relevant documents; evidence is assessed for relevance and reliability; the evidence is fed back to update executor and coordinator strategies; evidence reliability contributes to the reward signal.
- Core assumption: Publicly available web sources contain accurate, domain‑relevant causal knowledge; LLMs can effectively retrieve and interpret such evidence.
- Evidence anchors: abstract: “to validate these variables across multiple real‑world web‑based data sources, we leverage LLMs for evidence exploration to ensure traceability.”; section: Section 3.3: “leverage data sources (e.g., arXiv, Wikipedia, databases, and patients’ anonymized personal text reports) for verification, and feed the retrieved evidence back to the execution LLMs and the coordinator LLM to update their strategies and beliefs.”; corpus: Neighbors include information retrieval and knowledge grounding works, supporting the general idea of evidence‑based validation.
- Break condition: If web sources lack information on the specific latent constructs (e.g., novel or proprietary domains) or retrieval returns irrelevant/noisy data, validation may be ineffective or even misleading.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Data -> RLCD Module -> Causal Graph G' (with latents) -> Markov Blanket Extraction -> MALLM System (Coordinator LLM + Executor LLMs with Belief Networks) -> Evidence Retrieval Module (arXiv/Wikipedia) -> Validation & Strategy Update -> Training Loop (TD loss, mixing network)

**Critical path:**
1. Input observational data to RLCD.
2. For each latent node in G', extract its Markov blanket.
3. Coordinator sends queries (Markov blanket + task) to executors.
4. Executors use belief networks to propose latent variables and semantics.
5. Coordinator aggregates proposals.
6. Evidence retrieval module queries web sources for supporting documents.
7. Retrieved evidence is assessed for relevance and reliability.
8. Rewards computed based on action likelihood, uncertainty reduction, collaboration, and evidence reliability.
9. Belief networks and mixing network updated via TD loss; strategies refined.
10. Repeat until convergence; final coordinator output gives discovered latent variables with evidence.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Executor count (N): Optimal at 4–6; higher N increases token usage and coordination difficulty.
- LLM homogeneity: Homogeneous models converge more easily to BNE; heterogeneous models may bring diverse knowledge but face alignment challenges.
- Reward composition: Each component (action likelihood, uncertainty reduction, collaborative contribution, evidence reliability) is necessary; ablations show performance drops when omitted.
- BNE training vs. simpler voting: BNE models strategic interactions explicitly, achieving better coordination and lower token usage than multi‑round debate.
- Evidence sources: arXiv provides academic depth; Wikipedia is broad but less technical; excluding arXiv hurts performance significantly.

**Failure signatures:**
- Low ACC, CAcc, ECit metrics.
- MAST failure analysis highlights Specification Issues (vague prompts, unclear roles) and Task Verification failures (insufficient validation).
- Token consumption spikes indicate excessive communication, often from too many agents or poor belief network design.
- Parameter sensitivity: N>6 degrades performance; low discount factor (γ) yields short‑sighted policies.
- Heterogeneous LLMs underperform homogeneous ones in BNE convergence.
- Evidence retrieval failure (no relevant documents) reduces ECit.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Comparative evaluation (RQ1):** Run TLVD and baselines on five datasets; report ACC, CAcc, ECit. Expect TLVD to outperform, e.g., on WCHSU-Cancer (n=12) TLVD ACC 0.833 vs MiniMax 0.467.
2. **Ablation study (RQ3):** Remove multi-agent collaboration (use voting or single LLM) or ablate reward components; compare to full TLVD to demonstrate BNE’s contribution.
3. **Parameter sensitivity (RQ4):** Vary executor count N (2,4,6,8) and discount factor γ (0.9,0.95,0.99) on WCHSU-Cancer and WCHSU-Pain; record metrics; expect peak performance at N=4–6 and higher γ up to a point.

## Limitations
- RLCD’s theoretical guarantees (identifiability under specific conditions) may not hold for real‑world hospital data with unknown noise distributions.
- BNE convergence is challenging with heterogeneous LLMs and degrades when executor count exceeds 6, limiting scalability.
- Web evidence validation relies on sources containing accurate causal knowledge; performance drops for novel or proprietary domains with scarce documentation.

## Confidence
- RLCD graphical scaffolding: Medium (strong on benchmarks, uncertain on clinical data due to unknown noise distributions).
- MALLM BNE convergence: Medium (theoretically sound but empirical convergence depends on LLM consistency and agent count; heterogeneous models lack guarantees).
- Web evidence generalization: Low (performance sensitive to source availability; not tested in low-resource or out-of-domain scenarios).

## Next Checks
1. **RLCD assumption audit:** Re-run RLCD on hospital datasets with statistical tests for non‑Gaussianity (e.g., Henze‑Zirkler test) and tensor rank estimation; report violation rates and correlate with accuracy drops.
2. **BNE scalability stress test:** Incrementally increase executor count (N=2–10) with homogeneous and heterogeneous LLMs; measure convergence iterations and reward variance to map breakdown thresholds beyond N=6.
3. **Evidence source ablation:** Remove arXiv or Wikipedia separately; supplement with domain‑specific databases (e.g., UMLS for medical data); quantify ECit changes to assess robustness to source bias.
