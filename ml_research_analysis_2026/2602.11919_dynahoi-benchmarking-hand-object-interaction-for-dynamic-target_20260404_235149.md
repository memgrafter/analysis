# DynaHOI: Benchmarking Hand-Object Interaction for Dynamic Target

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11919  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11919  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** ObAct baseline improves dynamic capture, achieving 36.0% localization success and 30.7% grasping success, outperforming prior diffusion policies and VLMs.

## Executive Summary
DynaHOI addresses a critical gap in hand-object interaction (HOI) research by focusing on *dynamic* targets, whereas existing benchmarks assume static objects. The authors introduce DynaHOI-Gym, an online closed-loop simulation platform with parameterized motion generators and rollout-based evaluation, and DynaHOI-10M, a large-scale dataset containing 10M frames and 180K trajectories across 8 motion categories and 22 subcategories. Their observe-before-act baseline (ObAct) integrates spatiotemporal attention over a short observation window to condition a diffusion policy. Evaluations reveal that dynamic capture is extremely challenging: the best prior policy achieves only 27.90% localization success, and general-purpose VLMs attain ≤7%. ObAct improves localization success by 8.1% over the strongest diffusion baseline and outperforms all prior diffusion policies across seven metrics, including lower errors, better trajectory quality, and faster completion.

## Method Summary
The task (dynamic capture) requires controlling an 18-DoF hand to intercept and grasp a moving target based on egocentric vision and a language instruction. DynaHOI-Gym provides a unified interface where a Unity-based simulator runs in real time, streaming RGB images and proprioceptive hand states to a policy and stepping the physics engine upon receiving actions. Object trajectories are generated by a suite of parametric motion generators (e.g., straight lines, circular arcs, harmonic motions) that expose semantic parameters for systematic variation. The DynaHOI-10M dataset comprises 180K episodes collected in this environment. The ObAct baseline processes a window of *s* observation frames together with the current hand state and language command using a vision-language backbone and spatiotemporal attention; the resulting representation conditions a diffusion policy head (adapted from GR00T-N1.5) to produce 18-D actions. Minimal fine-tuning on the training split yields strong performance.

## Key Results
- Best prior policy attains 27.90% localization success; VLMs achieve ≤7%.
- ObAct improves localization success by 8.1% over the strongest diffusion baseline and outperforms all prior diffusion policies across seven metrics (lower errors, better trajectory quality, faster completion).
- ObAct obtains 36.0% localization success and 30.7% grasping success.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Parameterized Motion Generators
- **Claim:** Parameterized motion generators enable controlled, reproducible evaluation of dynamic hand-object interaction across a diverse space of target motions.
- **Mechanism:** The DynaHOI-Gym synthesizes object trajectories using a vocabulary of kinematic primitives (e.g., straight line, circular arc, harmonic motion) and physics-constrained dynamics (e.g., projectile, pendulum). Each motion type exposes semantic parameters (radius, frequency, launch angle, etc.) that can be varied systematically to create episodes with ground-truth motion properties. This contrasts with static benchmarks where target motion is absent or uncontrolled.
- **Core assumption:** These primitives sufficiently cover the manifold of time-critical motions encountered in real-world interception tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "parameterized motion generators"; [section] Page 3, “Parametric Motion Synthesis” (Section 3.2); [corpus] No direct evidence in provided neighboring papers; they focus on static or demonstration-captured datasets.
- **Break condition:** If real-world moving objects exhibit motions outside the primitive space (e.g., chaotic, human-driven irregularities), the benchmark’s coverage becomes incomplete and evaluation may not reflect practical challenges.

### Mechanism 2: Closed-Loop Online Evaluation
- **Claim:** Closed-loop online evaluation with rollout-based metrics yields a realistic assessment of policy performance under temporal constraints and physics interaction.
- **Mechanism:** DynaHOI-Gym operates as a real-time middleware: it streams visual and proprioceptive observations to the policy, receives actions, steps the Unity physics engine, and records outcome metrics from full episode rollouts (success, errors, quality scores). This closed-loop setup enforces temporal alignment between inference and simulation, unlike offline frame-wise alignment to motion-capture ground truth.
- **Core assumption:** That simulation-based rollouts with enforced clock synchronization approximate the demands of real-time robotic control.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "online closed-loop platform" and "rollout-based evaluation"; [section] Page 3, “Unified Evaluation Interface” and “Observe-Before-Act Mechanism” (Section 3.2); [corpus] Table 1 shows prior benchmarks lack online eval; DynaHOI-Gym’s “✓” distinguishes it.
- **Break condition:** Simulation–reality gaps (e.g., inaccurate contact models, rendering nondeterminism Appendix E) could cause policies to overfit to simulator quirks and fail in deployment.

### Mechanism 3: Spatiotemporal Attention in Observe-Before-Act
- **Claim:** Spatiotemporal attention over a short observation window allows a policy to infer target motion dynamics and improve interception timing (“observe-before-act”).
- **Mechanism:** ObAct encodes egocentric images and text into tokens, then applies attention across multiple historical frames plus the current frame. This yields a motion-aware representation that conditions a diffusion policy’s action generation, implicitly capturing velocity/acceleration cues absent from single-frame methods.
- **Core assumption:** That a brief, fixed-length observation window suffices to predict near-future target motion for interception.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "spatiotemporal attention to integrate short-term observations for action prediction"; [section] Page 5, “Observe-Before-Act Baseline” and Figure 5 (Section 3.4); [corpus] Table 3 shows VLMs struggle with motion-aware localization, underscoring the need for temporal modeling.
- **Break condition:** For targets with long-term dependencies or stochastic transitions (e.g., sudden direction changes), the fixed observation window may not provide predictive signal, leading to systematic anticipation errors.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Motion Generator -> Unity Simulator (physics + rendering) -> produces egocentric RGB images and hand state -> Policy Interface (receives observations, sends actions) -> simulator steps -> Metrics Module records outcomes; Data Pipeline stores synchronized episodes.

**Critical path:**  
Load episode config → initialize simulator → enter observation phase (hand fixed, object moves) → after *s* frames, policy starts producing actions → simulator steps repeatedly until termination (success/failure) → metrics recorded.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Physics realism vs. determinism:* Unity offers realistic contacts but introduces nondeterminism from rendering/IO (Appendix E). Testing uses a fixed-step, manually-advanced clock to ensure temporal consistency.  
- *Parameterized motion vs. data-driven diversity:* Primitives give explicit control and interpretability but may miss nuanced real-world motions.  
- *Observe-before-act vs. direct act:* The observation window allows temporal reasoning but adds latency; direct act is simpler but unlikely to succeed on dynamic targets.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low Sloc (<25% for best models) indicates failure in motion anticipation.  
- Low Sgra (≤4%) despite decent Sloc shows poor grasp execution or timing mismatch.  
- Low Qsmooth/Qline suggests jerky or indirect trajectories (common in diffusion models).  
- High Rtime (near 0) means the policy is slow to act.  
- Discrepancy between per-frame grasp success (Figure 7) and episode-level Sgra reveals temporal consistency issues.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline reproduction:** Run ObAct and all policy models (Table 2) on the test split to confirm reported numbers. Use fixed seeds and identical simulator settings.  
2. **Observation window ablation:** Vary the number of observation frames (1,3,5,7,9) to reproduce Figure 6 and validate that longer context improves Sloc/Eloc.  
3. **Stratified evaluation:** Group test episodes by motion category (e.g., Circular vs. Linear) and run models to reproduce periodicity-stratified trends (Figure 11, Appendix H). This helps diagnose whether a model exploits periodic patterns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can current models accurately forecast where a moving target will be in the near future and carry out the manipulation instructed by a human?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors directly pose this question in the Introduction: "can current models accurately forecast where a moving target will be in the near future and carry out the manipulation instructed by a human?"  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper's evaluations reveal that the best policy model attains only 27.90% localization success, and general-purpose VLMs achieve ≤7%, demonstrating that current models fail to reliably perform dynamic capture.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of new models that incorporate robust temporal reasoning (e.g., extended observation windows, explicit motion prediction) and achieve substantially higher success rates (e.g., >50% localization) on the DynaHOI-10M benchmark.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can dynamic capture be decomposed into explicit stages to improve the coupling between localization and grasping?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] In Appendix C (Limitations and Future Work), the authors observe that "grasping frequently fails even when localization succeeds" and recommend "decompose dynamic capture into explicit stages, where successful localization triggers a dedicated grasping phase."  
- **Why unresolved:** The study evaluates only end-to-end policies; no staged architecture is implemented, leaving the weak coupling between spatial alignment and contact-rich grasp execution unaddressed.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementation of a two-stage system with a localization-triggered grasping module, along with experimental results showing improved conditional grasping success rates on DynaHOI-10M compared to end-to-end baselines.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can general-purpose vision-language models (VLMs) be enhanced with motion-aware anticipation to improve performance on dynamic hand-object interaction?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The abstract and Section 4.3 highlight that VLMs "struggle, with localization success ≤7%, highlighting the need for motion-aware anticipation."  
- **Why unresolved:** VLMs are not inherently designed for temporal motion forecasting; their poor performance indicates a significant gap in integrating short-horizon dynamics into HOI tasks.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Architectural extensions to VLMs (e.g., spatiotemporal attention, motion tokenization) or training on dynamic HOI trajectories that lead to markedly higher localization and grasping success on the DynaHOI-10M benchmark.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps:** VLM backbone identity, training hyperparameters, data split ratios, and diffusion head details are unspecified, limiting exact replication.  
- **Simulation–reality gap:** Unity physics and rendering nondeterminism may cause overfitting to simulator quirks; the primitive motion space may not cover real-world chaotic or irregular targets.  
- **Observation window constraints:** Fixed short context may fail for long-term or stochastic motions; performance on non-primitive dynamics remains untested.

## Confidence
- **Parameterized motion generators:** Medium – primitives provide systematic variation but real-world motion diversity is uncertain.  
- **Closed-loop online evaluation:** High – platform clearly implements real-time rollouts; simulation limitations are acknowledged but implementation is solid.  
- **Observe-Before-Act with spatiotemporal attention:** Medium – strong benchmark results, yet reproducibility and generalization depend on missing hyperparameters and motion repertoire.
