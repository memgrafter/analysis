# DISCO: Document Intelligence Suite for COmparative Evaluation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23511
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23511
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** OCR parsing beats VLM on handwriting and multi-page docs; VLM parsing beats OCR on multilingual/visually-rich layouts; direct VQA beats OCR-based QA on single-page datasets, but OCR-based QA wins on multi-page.

## Executive Summary
DISCO introduces a stage-wise evaluation framework that separately measures text parsing (OCR vs VLM) and question answering across diverse document types. By isolating these stages, the suite uncovers nuanced performance patterns: OCR-based pipelines achieve lower character error rates on handwritten text and multi-page documents, while vision-language models (VLMs) handle multilingual scripts and visually-rich layouts more effectively. For question answering, direct VQA (VLM end-to-end) yields higher accuracy on single-page datasets (DocVQA, InfographicVQA), whereas OCR-based pipelines remain superior on multi-page DUDE. Task-aware prompting shows mixed benefits, sometimes degrading performance. These findings provide empirically grounded guidance for selecting document-processing strategies based on document characteristics.

## Method Summary
DISCO decouples document processing into two independent stages: parsing (text extraction) and question answering. For parsing, it compares traditional OCR engines (e.g., Azure, Mistral) against VLM-based extraction (using models like GPT-5, Claude). For QA, it evaluates three strategies: OCR-pipeline (using extracted text), VLM-two-stage (VLM parsing then LLM QA), and direct VLM-QA (end-to-end image+question). This design isolates the impact of parsing quality on downstream QA and reveals error propagation effects. The suite is evaluated on established VQA datasets (DocVQA, InfographicVQA, DUDE) representing different document complexities. Metrics include character similarity for parsing and multiple QA metrics (GT-in-Pred, ANLS, EM, cosine similarity) to capture various answer qualities. The stage-wise approach yields diagnostic insights that conventional end-to-end benchmarks obscure.

## Key Results
- **Parsing:** OCR achieves lower character error rates than VLM on handwritten text (0.087 vs 0.171) and multi-page documents; conversely, VLMs excel on multilingual scripts (CER 0.073 vs 0.553) and visually-rich layouts.
- **QA:** Direct VQA (VLM end-to-end) yields higher ground-truth-in-prediction on single-page datasets: DocVQA (0.908 vs 0.876) and InfographicVQA (0.785 vs 0.754), while OCR-based pipelines are superior on multi-page DUDE (0.562 vs 0.493).
- **Prompting:** Task-aware prompting shows mixed benefits, improving performance on some document types but degrading it on others, with effects varying across model families.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DISCO’s guidance emerges from three causal mechanisms revealed by its stage-wise design.

**Mechanism 1: Document structure dictates pipeline choice.** Complex layouts (multilingual scripts, infographics) demand holistic visual reasoning provided by VLMs, while text-heavy or multi-page documents benefit from OCR’s explicit text grounding and reduced context length. Evidence shows OCR excels on handwriting and long contexts because its specialized extractors maintain fidelity; VLMs handle diverse scripts and visual elements better due to pretrained visual understanding. However, when documents combine high script diversity with long length, the trade-off becomes unpredictable—a break condition not covered in current datasets.

**Mechanism 2: Task-aware prompting effects are heterogeneous.** Prompts that over-specify output format can interfere with a model’s pretrained priors, especially when document characteristics deviate from prompt assumptions. For instance, prompting for structured extraction on free-form handwriting may degrade performance. The stage-wise isolation shows prompting utility is highly task- and data-dependent; it helps in some scenarios (e.g., certain OCR tasks) but harms others (e.g., VLM parsing on specific layouts). This indicates model capabilities are not infinitely steerable via prompts.

**Mechanism 3: Two-stage VLM pipelines suffer error propagation ceilings.** In a VLM-two-stage approach (VLM parsing → LLM QA), the intermediate linear text representation loses spatial layout cues and introduces formatting noise, degrading QA performance. Direct VQA avoids this by preserving the full image context, yielding higher scores on single-page documents. OCR pipelines mitigate propagation via domain-optimized extractors that maintain fidelity for text-heavy tasks. Nevertheless, on multi-page documents requiring cross-page reasoning, both approaches face limits: OCR may fail if layout analysis is poor, and direct VQA may hit context window constraints.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Document complexity dimensions**  
  *Why needed:* To apply DISCO’s heuristics, engineers must classify documents along axes such as script diversity, layout density, page count, and handwriting prevalence.  
  *Quick check:* For a 50-page Arabic annual report with embedded charts, which dimension is most salient? (Answer: script diversity and layout density; DISCO suggests VLM parsing for multilingual/visually-rich content, but multi-page length may favor OCR—trade-off requires testing.)

- **Concept: Stage-wise error attribution**  
  *Why needed:* DISCO separates parsing and QA metrics, enabling diagnosis of whether failures stem from perception or reasoning. High parsing error does not always doom QA if the QA model is robust.  
  *Quick check:* On DUDE, OCR parsing had 50.5% GT-in-Extracted-Text but QA GT-in-Pred was 51.4%. Does this indicate perfect error propagation? (Answer: No; the gap suggests QA model can sometimes recover correct answers despite missing text, or parsing metric may not capture all relevant extracted content.)

- **Concept: Metric ecology**  
  *Why needed:* DISCO employs multiple QA metrics (GT-in-Pred, ANLS, EM, cosine similarity). Discrepancies reveal answer quality nuances; e.g., high GT-in-Pred with low ANLS indicates verbosity rather than incorrect content.  
  *Quick check:* If a VLM achieves S_GT-in-Pred=0.95 but S_ANLS=0.30 on InfographicVQA, what is the likely failure mode? (Answer: The model often includes the correct substring within a verbose, partially correct answer; need to prompt for conciseness.)

## Architecture Onboarding
Document Ingestion -> Feature Extraction -> Parser Selector -> Parsing Stage -> QA Stage -> Metric Engine -> Decision Module
**Critical path:** Document ingestion → Feature extraction (script, length, layout density) → Parser selection → Parsing → QA → Metric calculation → Guidance synthesis. The most failure-sensitive node is feature extraction, as misclassifying document characteristics leads to suboptimal pipeline choice.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Accuracy vs. latency:** Direct VQA is fastest on single-page docs; OCR+LLM is slower but more reliable on multi-page.
- **Cost vs. robustness:** OCR APIs are cheaper per page but may require re-extraction for new questions; VLM direct charges per image+question but amortizes if questions fixed.
- **Generality vs. specialization:** Task-aware prompts can boost performance on narrow domains but harm generalizability across diverse document types.

**Failure signatures:**
- High S_GT-in-Pred, low S_ANLS/S_EM: Model finds correct answer but wraps it in verbose explanation. Fix: add “respond with only the answer” to prompt.
- OCR parsing S_CS high but QA S_GT-in-Pred low: Parsing preserved semantics but lost critical numeric values or IDs; check field-level recall (e.g., RxPad analysis).
- VLM-2stage underperforms both OCR and direct VQA: Intermediate representation loses layout/spatial cues; consider switching to direct VQA or adding layout tokens.
- Mistral OCR 3 underperforms Mistral OCR 2 across datasets: Indicates model regression; do not assume newer versions are better—benchmark on your data.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Parsing sanity check:** Run P_OCR and P_VLM-base on a small handwritten sample (IAM-like) and a multilingual sample (ICDAR-like). Expect OCR to win handwriting, VLM to win multilingual. If not, check image resolution and prompt.
2. **QA pipeline comparison:** On a single-page form (DocVQA-like), run QA_OCR (Azure) and QA_VLM-direct with the same VLM (e.g., gpt-5-mini). Expect direct VQA to have higher S_GT-in-Pred. If not, verify OCR text completeness (GT-in-Extracted-Text) >80%; if low, OCR provider is the bottleneck.
3. **Prompt sensitivity test:** For a visually rich document (InfographicVQA-like), run QA_VLM-direct with generic vs. task-aware prompts. Expect mixed results—task-aware may degrade if it overconstrains output format. Measure S_GT-in-Pred vs. S_ANLS to detect verbosity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How do vector-based text retrieval and vision-based page selection compare for long-document QA?**  
  DISCO did not evaluate retrieval components, which are needed for real long documents. Future work should assess retrieval-augmented pipelines, comparing vector-based text retrieval against vision-based page selection, reporting accuracy and efficiency.

- **How well do OCR and VLMs parse non-Latin scripts and mixed-script documents?**  
  DISCO focuses on English/French Latin scripts; multilingual performance remains unmeasured. Future benchmarks should systematically evaluate non-Latin scripts, mixed-script documents, and culturally specific layouts.

- **What evaluation metrics can capture semantic equivalence of extracted text while remaining deterministic?**  
  Current metrics (substring match, ANLS, EM) penalize semantically correct but lexically varied outputs. Future work could explore hybrid approaches that maintain reproducibility whilst better capturing semantic equivalence.

## Limitations
- **Dataset coverage:** Evaluation confined to existing VQA datasets (DocVQA, InfographicVQA, DUDE) that may not represent extreme document characteristics (very long, highly mixed scripts, poor image quality).
- **Reproducibility:** Insufficient implementation details; no public code release limits independent verification.
- **Model generality:** Findings tied to specific commercial OCR/VLM providers; rapid evolution of models may shift trade-offs.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (document structure determines optimal pipeline): **High**
- Mechanism 2 (prompting heterogeneity): **Medium**
- Mechanism 3 (error propagation ceiling in two-stage VLM): **Medium**
