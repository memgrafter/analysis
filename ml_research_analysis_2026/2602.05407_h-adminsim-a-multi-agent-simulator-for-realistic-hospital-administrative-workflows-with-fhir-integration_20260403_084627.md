# H-AdminSim: A Multi-Agent Simulator for Realistic Hospital Administrative Workflows with FHIR Integration

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05407
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05407
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Tool-based scheduling achieves up to 99.8% success; intake remains bottleneck with Gemini 2.5 Flash leading at 88.9% success.

## Executive Summary
H-AdminSim addresses the lack of realistic simulation environments for evaluating LLM-based automation in complex hospital administrative workflows, which prior work oversimplified. The framework introduces a multi-agent environment featuring synthetic data generation, dialogue-driven patient intake, tool-augmented appointment scheduling, and seamless FHIR integration to support scalability across hospital sizes.

Evaluation shows that the scheduling agent attains near-perfect success rates (up to 99.8%) thanks to deterministic tool usage, while the intake agent’s performance varies by hospital level and model, with Gemini 2.5 Flash achieving the highest intake success (88.9%). Intake remains the primary bottleneck, particularly in tertiary care settings where department ambiguity is higher. The platform serves as a standardized testbed for future LLM-driven administrative automation research.

## Method Summary
The core of H-AdminSim is a configurable multi-agent simulator that generates synthetic patient profiles and simulates administrative workflows. The intake agent engages in a text-based conversation to elicit symptoms, medical history, and preliminary complaints, then predicts the appropriate clinical department. The scheduling agent leverages tool-calling capabilities to interact with a mock appointment system, checking availability and booking slots under varying resource constraints. All data are structured according to FHIR resources, enabling realistic interoperability with hospital information systems. The simulator supports different hospital tiers (primary, secondary, tertiary) by adjusting the number and specificity of departments and the complexity of patient cases.

## Key Results
- Tool-based scheduling achieves a success rate of 99.8% in the simulated environment.
- Intake success peaks at 88.9% with Gemini 2.5 Flash; intake is the main performance bottleneck, especially in tertiary hospitals.
- Department assignment accuracy improves with longer dialogues and when prior diagnoses are available; errors increase with higher department ambiguity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The scheduling agent’s high success stems from converting the problem into deterministic tool calls, eliminating ambiguity present in free-form generation. The intake agent’s lower performance is due to the open-ended nature of mapping patient-described symptoms to specific departments, a task that requires nuanced clinical reasoning and is sensitive to dialogue length and prior information. The synthetic data, while broad, may lack rare edge cases that could further challenge the system. FHIR integration ensures that data representations are consistent and portable, which is essential for cross-hospital scalability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multi-Agent Systems**: Decompose complex workflows into specialized agents (intake, scheduling), enabling focused evaluation and parallelism. *Why needed*: Separates language understanding from structured action execution. *Quick check*: Are agents encapsulated with clear inputs/outputs and no hidden state dependencies?
- **Tool-Augmented LLMs**: Allow models to perform reliable, deterministic operations (e.g., database lookup, booking) instead of generating potentially incorrect structured data. *Why needed*: Increases reliability for tasks requiring exact adherence to schemas. *Quick check*: Do tool calls correctly handle errors and return structured results in a consistent format?
- **FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources)**: Standardized data model for healthcare information exchange. *Why needed*: Ensures that simulated patient records, conditions, and appointments align with real-world EHR systems, facilitating future real deployments. *Quick check*: Are all entities (Patient, Condition, Appointment) represented as valid FHIR resources with required fields?
- **Synthetic Data Generation**: Produces large volumes of diverse patient profiles without privacy concerns. *Why needed*: Enables training and evaluation at scale while controlling for specific variables (e.g., hospital level). *Quick check*: Does the generated data mirror real-world distributions for demographics, conditions, and visit reasons?
- **Dialogue Systems**: Manage interactive information gathering with patients via natural language. *Why needed*: Captures the variability and ambiguity of real patient intake conversations. *Quick check*: Does the dialogue agent ask relevant follow-up questions and avoid missing critical information?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Synthetic Patient Generator -> Intake Agent -> Scheduling Agent -> FHIR Interface -> Simulated Hospital DB

**Critical Path**  
1. Generate synthetic patient profile.  
2. Conduct intake dialogue to collect symptoms and history.  
3. Predict department based on dialogue.  
4. Query appointment availability via scheduling agent's tool calls.  
5. Book appointment and update FHIR resources.  
6. Log outcome for evaluation.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Synthetic data vs real data: Synthetic data allows unlimited, privacy-safe generation but may not capture rare conditions; real data improves validity but introduces privacy and scale challenges.  
- Multi-agent separation vs monolithic pipeline: Separation simplifies debugging and allows independent model upgrades, but may increase inter-agent communication overhead.  
- Deterministic tools vs free-form generation for scheduling: Tools ensure high reliability but limit the model’s flexibility in handling edge cases; free-form could adapt but introduces errors.  
- FHIR compliance vs performance: Strict adherence to FHIR standards ensures interoperability but adds serialization overhead and complexity.

**Failure Signatures**  
- High error rates in department assignment for tertiary hospitals, indicating difficulty with ambiguous mappings.  
- Long dialogues leading to context window overflow, causing truncated information and misclassification.  
- Scheduling conflicts arising from simultaneous booking attempts or insufficient resource checking.  
- Tool call failures due to malformed parameters or missing data in synthetic profiles.

**Three First Experiments**  
1. **Model Comparison**: Evaluate intake success rates across multiple LLMs (Gemini, GPT-4, Claude) on primary, secondary, and tertiary hospital configurations to benchmark performance differences.  
2. **Scalability Stress Test**: Increase the number of simulated hospitals from 1 to 50, interconnected via shared referral pathways, to assess the framework’s ability to maintain scheduling success and FHIR consistency under load.  
3. **Human Baseline**: Have human administrative staff perform the same intake and scheduling tasks on a subset of synthetic cases to establish a human performance baseline for comparison with LLM agents.

## Limitations
- Synthetic patient data may not fully represent real-world complexities, including rare conditions and atypical presentations, limiting external validity.
- Tool-based scheduling success is measured in a controlled simulation; real-world deployment could face integration failures, time constraints, and human overrides.
- Intake performance generalizability is uncertain, as results depend on specific LLMs and synthetic dialogue distributions; comparison with rule-based or human-only systems is lacking.

## Next Checks
1. Real-World Data Pilot: Collect de-identified EHR intake forms and appointment logs to create a held-out validation set; compare H-AdminSim's department assignment accuracy and scheduling success against this real data to quantify distributional gaps.
2. Human-in-the-Loop Benchmark: Implement the intake and scheduling tasks using human administrative staff on the same synthetic patient cases to provide a direct performance baseline and identify augmentation opportunities.
3. Scalability Stress Test: Simulate a multi-hospital network (50+ facilities) with interconnected referrals and shared resources to test the simulator's performance and the LLM agents' success rates under scale, validating the interoperability claim.
