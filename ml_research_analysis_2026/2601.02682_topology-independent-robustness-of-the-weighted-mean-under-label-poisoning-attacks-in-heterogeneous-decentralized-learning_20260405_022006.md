# Topology-Independent Robustness of the Weighted Mean under Label Poisoning Attacks in Heterogeneous Decentralized Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.02682
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02682
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Under label poisoning attacks, the weighted mean aggregator in decentralized gradient descent yields a topology-independent learning error of O(δ²A²) and can outperform state‑of‑the‑art robust aggregators (e.g., TriMean, FABA, IOS) when data heterogeneity is high and the global contamination rate δ is smaller than the local contamination rate δ_max, or when the regular network is disconnected or sparse.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines decentralized learning under label poisoning attacks, where a small subset of agents corrupt their local labels. Contrary to the common belief that simple weighted mean aggregation is vulnerable, the authors prove that its convergence error is O(δ²A²) and independent of network topology. In contrast, state‑of‑the‑art robust aggregators (TriMean, FABA, CC, CG, IOS, etc.) exhibit errors that depend on the spectral gap λ and non‑doubly‑stochasticity β of the communication graph. Under high data heterogeneity (A ≈ ξ), weighted mean provably outperforms majority‑dominant robust aggregators if δ < δ_max, or if the regular network is disconnected or sparse. Extensive experiments on MNIST and CIFAR100 with diverse topologies (two‑castle, line, fan) confirm these theoretical findings, revealing a surprising robustness of the weighted mean in challenging decentralized settings.

## Method Summary
The study analyzes decentralized gradient descent (D‑GD) over a network of agents. Each agent updates its local model using its gradient and aggregates neighbors’ models. Label flipping attacks corrupt one or more agents by flipping all labels. The core comparison is between two aggregator families: (i) weighted mean (using a doubly stochastic mixing matrix E) and (ii) robust aggregators (TriMean, FABA, CC, CG, IOS, RFA, LFighter) that filter out malicious updates. Theoretical analysis expresses learning error in terms of contamination rate δ, gradient disturbance bound A, heterogeneity ξ, and topology metrics λ and β. Empirical validation uses non‑i.i.d. data partitions (Dirichlet α=1 or exclusive class assignments) and graphs (two‑castle, line, fan). Results support the theory: weighted mean is topology‑independent and dominates robust methods under high heterogeneity and δ < δ_max; robust aggregators suffer on disconnected or sparse networks.

## Key Results
- Weighted mean’s learning error scales as O(δ²A²) and is independent of network topology, while robust aggregators’ errors contain explicit λ‑ and β‑dependent terms.
- Under high data heterogeneity (A = Θ(ξ)), weighted mean provably outperforms majority‑dominant robust aggregators when (i) δ < δ_max, (ii) the regular network is disconnected, or (iii) the regular network is sparse with high δ_max.
- Experiments confirm: on disconnected/sparse graphs or when δ < δ_max, weighted mean achieves higher test accuracy than robust methods like IOS, TriMean, and FABA; CC and CG often fail due to lack of majority‑dominance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1 (Weighted Mean Topology Independence)
Weighted mean employs a doubly stochastic mixing matrix E. The convergence analysis separates error into a dominant term O(δ²A²) arising from poisoned gradient disturbances and a transient term involving λ′ (related to spectral gap) that vanishes as iterations increase. Thus the asymptotic error is unaffected by network topology. Key assumptions: E is doubly stochastic, label poisoning yields bounded gradient disturbance (A finite), and loss gradients are Lipschitz.

Evidence: Theorem 2 provides bound (13) with O(δ²A²); λ′ appears only in vanishing terms.

### Mechanism 2 (Robust Aggregators’ Topology Dependence)
Robust aggregators satisfy an (ρ, M)-robustness property: their output approximates a convex combination of regular inputs with error ≤ ρ·max‖y_v−ȳ‖. The virtual mixing matrix M (over regular agents) has spectral gap λ and non‑doubly‑stochasticity β. The analysis (Theorem 1) yields error terms O(ρ²ξ²/(1−λ−4ρ)²) and O(β²ξ²), both topology‑dependent. If λ ≥ 1 (disconnected regular network) or ρ ≥ 1−λ/4, convergence guarantees break.

Evidence: Theorem 1 bounds (10) and (11) explicitly contain λ and ρ.

### Mechanism 3 (When Weighted Mean Wins)
If data heterogeneity is high such that A ≈ ξ (typical for label poisoning in non‑i.i.d. settings), weighted mean’s error scales as Θ(δ²ξ²). Optimal majority‑dominant robust aggregators achieve a lower bound Ω(δ_max²ξ²). Since δ is often smaller than δ_max, weighted mean has a smaller constant. Additional win conditions: regular network disconnected (λ ≥ 1) or sparse with high δ_max, where robust aggregators’ topology‑dependent terms blow up.

Evidence: Theorem 3 gives lower bounds; experiments on two‑castle (δ<δ_max) and fan (sparse) graphs confirm superiority.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**:
Agent Network → Local State (model, data) → Compute Gradient → Half‑step Update → Neighborhood Exchange → Aggregator (Weighted Mean or Robust) → New Model

**Critical Path** (per iteration):
1. Each agent w computes its local gradient ∇f_w(x_w^k) (or poisoned ∇̃f_w).
2. Half‑step update: x_w^{k+½} = x_w^k − γ·∇f_w(x_w^k).
3. Exchange x_w^{k+½} with neighbors N_w; receive neighbors’ half‑step models.
4. Aggregate: x_w^{k+1} = Aggregator({x_v^{k+½}: v∈N_w ∪ {w}}). For weighted mean: weighted sum with doubly stochastic E; for robust aggregators: filtering (e.g., trimming, iterative clipping).
5. Repeat for K iterations; final evaluation uses average of regular agents’ models.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- *Weighted Mean*: Simple (O(degree) per agent), memory‑efficient, no hyperparameters; but vulnerable if poisoning level is high or data heterogeneity low.
- *Robust Aggregators*: Higher computational cost (often O(W log W) due to sorting/clipping), may require knowledge of δ_max; performance varies with topology (λ, β). IOS (doubly stochastic M) often best among majority‑dominant methods.
- *Step size*: Theory uses constant γ = 1/√K; practice often uses diminishing γ = γ_0/√k for faster convergence.

**Failure Signatures**
- Robust aggregators diverge on disconnected regular networks (λ ≥ 1) or when ρ ≥ 1−λ/4.
- Strong topology sensitivity for robust aggregators (e.g., accuracy drop on fan graph) while weighted mean remains stable.
- Weighted mean unexpectedly outperforms robust aggregators suggests δ < δ_max and/or high heterogeneity.
- CC/CG failing majority‑dominance: unanimous regular inputs still corrupted if poisoned inputs differ.
- Mismatch A << ξ (unlikely under strong label poisoning) could let robust aggregators win.

**First 3 Experiments**
1. Two‑castle graph, non‑i.i.d., δ=0.1 < δ_max≈0.111: Verify weighted mean surpasses robust methods (e.g., Figure 3, Figure 7). Regular agents have 8 regular + 1 poisoned neighbor → δ_max = 1/9.
2. Line graph, non‑i.i.d.: Regular network disconnected (λ ≥ 1). Observe robust aggregators’ severe accuracy drop while weighted mean remains robust (Figure 6).
3. Fan graph, non‑i.i.d., δ_max=1/3 sparse regular network: Test condition (iii). Verify weighted mean dominates as regular network is sparse (line topology among regulars) and δ_max high (Figure 8).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Computational Complexity Trade‑offs**: What are the precise per‑iteration time, memory, and communication overhead of weighted mean versus each robust aggregator (TriMean, FABA, CC, CG, IOS, RFA, LFighter) in practical decentralized systems?
2. **Stochastic Extensions**: Do the theoretical topology‑independent advantage and scaling results extend to stochastic decentralized optimization with mini‑batch gradients? Does weighted mean still dominate in that regime?
3. **Stronger Attacks**: How does weighted mean fare under attacks beyond label poisoning (e.g., arbitrary model poisoning with unbounded vectors)? What are its exact failure modes?
4. **Unified Phase Diagram**: Can we derive an exact threshold formula in terms of network sparsity λ, local contamination δ_max, and heterogeneity ratio A/ξ that precisely predicts when weighted mean outperforms the optimal majority‑dominant robust aggregator?
5. **Algorithmic Enhancements**: How do common enhancements (local SGD steps, momentum, adaptive step sizes, gradient clipping) interact with the robustness trade‑off? Can they strengthen or weaken weighted mean’s advantage?

## Limitations
- The exact placement of the poisoned agent in each topology is unspecified, which may affect δ_max calculations and experimental outcomes.
- Implementation details for several robust aggregators (RFA, LFighter, IOS variants) are missing, which could lead to inconsistent comparisons.
- The key assumption that A = Θ(ξ) under label poisoning in highly non‑i.i.d. settings lacks comprehensive empirical verification across varying heterogeneity levels.
- Theoretical guarantees require doubly stochastic mixing matrices and bounded gradient disturbances; real‑world systems may violate these assumptions.
- Results are limited to label‑flipping attacks; extension to other attack models (e.g., sign‑flipping, arbitrary manipulation) remains open.

## Confidence
We assign confidence levels based on theoretical and empirical support:

- **Topology independence of weighted mean error (O(δ²A²))**: High (Theorem 2, extensive experiments)
- **Topology dependence of robust aggregators’ errors (via λ, β)**: High (Theorem 1, validated across graphs)
- **Weighted mean outperforms robust aggregators under high heterogeneity when δ < δ_max**: Medium (relies on A=Θ(ξ) and specific experimental conditions; more comprehensive validation needed)
- **Robust aggregators fail on disconnected regular networks or when ρ ≥ 1−λ/4**: High (theoretical condition and empirical confirmation)
- **CC and CG lack majority‑dominance**: High (analytical proof in Lemma 4/5, empirical failure)
