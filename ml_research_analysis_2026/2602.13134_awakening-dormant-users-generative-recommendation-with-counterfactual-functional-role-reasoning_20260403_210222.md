# Awakening Dormant Users: Generative Recommendation with Counterfactual Functional Role Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.13134
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13134
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RoleGen improves Recall@1 by 6.2% offline and order volume by 7.3% online on Kuaishou, while enhancing long‑tail item exposure and GMV growth.

## Executive Summary
Dormant e‑commerce users—those who browse but rarely purchase—pose a significant challenge for recommender systems because traditional models estimate item intrinsic value in isolation, ignoring the instrumental effect where items trigger latent intents along conversion trajectories. This paper introduces RoleGen, a framework that combines a generative LLM‑based Conversion Trajectory Reasoner with a lightweight Generative Behavioral Backbone. The reasoner infers context‑dependent functional roles of items (e.g., complementarity, substitution) and employs counterfactual intent intervention to explore diverse conversion pathways. Its outputs, including candidate Semantic IDs (SIDs) and role statistics, guide the backbone, which retrieves items by generating hierarchical SID tokens. The two modules co‑train in a closed‑loop “Reasoning‑Execution‑Feedback‑Reflection” manner. Extensive offline experiments on Kuaishou show a 6.2% improvement in Recall@1 for dormant users, and online A/B tests confirm a 7.3% increase in order volume. RoleGen also mitigates the Matthew effect by improving long‑tail item exposure and generalizes to out‑of‑distribution scenarios, driving incremental GMV growth on large‑scale platforms.

## Method Summary
RoleGen consists of two core components. The **Conversion Trajectory Reasoner** is an LLM (e.g., Qwen‑8B) fine‑tuned on multi‑task instruction data that includes SID alignment, behavior prediction, and step‑by‑step reasoning with Functional Role Chain‑of‑Thought (FR‑CoT). It also performs counterfactual role intervention (beam = 25, temperature = 0.95) to generate diverse candidate SIDs and role statistics. The **Generative Behavioral Backbone** is a decoder‑only Transformer (~0.1B parameters) that autoregressively generates the three‑level hierarchical SID tokens. The backbone is first pre‑trained on all user interaction logs and then fine‑tuned on data augmented with reasoning guidance: the reasoner’s candidate list and coarse‑SID frequency statistics are injected into the backbone’s input context via a reasoning‑injection module. The two modules are co‑trained in a closed loop: the reasoner runs weekly on aggregated dormant‑user conversion logs, produces counterfactual candidate sets, and caches reasoning guidance features; during online serving, the backbone retrieves these features, merges them with real‑time user context, and generates the final SID tokens with a large beam (1024) to produce top‑K recommendations. Online exposure feedback (clicks, purchases) is collected to create reflection examples that periodically fine‑tune the reasoner, aligning it with the backbone’s collaborative signals.

## Key Results
- **Offline:** 6.2% relative improvement in Recall@1 for dormant users on the Kuaishou dataset (22 M training sequences, 7 M+ test pairs).
- **Online:** A/B testing with Kuaishou’s production system shows a 7.3% lift in order volume for the dormant user segment.
- **Additional benefits:** RoleGen significantly improves long‑tail item exposure, mitigates the Matthew effect, generalizes to out‑of‑distribution scenarios, and drives incremental GMV growth.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RoleGen overcomes the item‑centric limitation of conventional recommenders by explicitly modeling the **Functional Role Trajectory** of items within a user’s conversion journey. The LLM‑based reasoner infers the role each item plays (e.g., complement, substitute, decision‑cost reducer) in a given context and uses counterfactual intent intervention to explore alternative pathways that a dormant user might follow. These high‑level role signals, together with candidate Semantic IDs, are injected into the **Generative Behavioral Backbone**, which retrieves items via hierarchical Semantic IDs. Decomposing the item space into a three‑level codebook reduces the output vocabulary and allows the backbone to focus on generation rather than scoring millions of items. The closed‑loop “Reasoning‑Execution‑Feedback‑Reflection” process continuously aligns the backbone’s behavior with the reasoner’s broader intent exploration, while reflection from online outcomes calibrates the reasoner again. This synergy enables accurate and diverse recommendations even for users with extremely sparse historical interactions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Functional Role Trajectory**  
  **Why needed here:** It replaces item‑centric prediction with a structured intent‑evolution signal, enabling the system to reason about how each interaction contributes to future conversion.  
  **Quick check question:** Can you explain why modeling an item’s complementarity or substitution relation to a user’s interest profile is more effective than predicting click probability in isolation?

- **Concept: Counterfactual Intent Intervention**  
  **Why needed here:** Dormant users exhibit highly sparse and biased histories; plain maximum‑likelihood generation overfits to a few observed items and reinforces filter bubbles. Counterfactual role changes expose the model to alternative intent pathways.  
  **Quick check question:** How does intervening on a low‑cardinality role space differ from standard beam search or temperature scaling in terms of exploration?

- **Concept: Semantic ID Generative Retrieval**  
  **Why needed here:** Decomposing the massive item space into a hierarchical semantic codebook reduces the output vocabulary and allows the backbone to generate items token‑by‑token, which improves scalability and facilitates injection of semantic guidance (e.g., role‑level statistics).  
  **Quick check question:** Why is generating a three‑level SID (coarse to fine) easier for a language model than selecting directly from millions of item IDs?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  - Raw logs → preprocessing → user sequences, item metadata.  
  - Conversion Trajectory Reasoner (LLM fine‑tuned with FR‑CoT and counterfactual inference) outputs candidate SIDs and role statistics.  
  - Reasoning Injection module converts these into features (candidate list, coarse SID frequencies) that augment the backbone’s context.  
  - Generative Behavioral Backbone (decoder‑only, ~0.1B params) generates final SID tokens via lazy cross‑attention.  
  - Online serving pipeline: pre‑computed Reasoner features per dormant user are cached; at request time the backbone retrieves them, merges with real‑time context, and produces candidates (beam size 1024) within P99<53ms.  
  - Reflection loop: online exposure data (which recommended items led to clicks/purchases) is fed back to fine‑tune the Reasoner weekly.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. **Near‑line:** Reasoner updates on weekly aggregated logs of successful dormant‑user conversions → produces counterfactual candidate sets → caches Reasoning Guidance Features.  
  2. **Online:** incoming request → fetch cached Reasoner features for user → create backbone input (user profile + history + guidance) → backbone auto‑regressive generation → send top‑K items to ranking → expose.  
  3. **Post‑exposure:** log user feedback (clicks, orders) → store in data lake → for next Reasoner update and Reflection.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - LLM vs backbone accuracy/latency: The Reasoner provides high‑level intent but is too slow for real‑time serving; the backbone offers low‑latency CF signals. Injecting guidance bridges the gap.  
  - Exploration depth: Counterfactual beam size=25 gives diversity without prohibitive cost; larger would increase computational load.  
  - Semantic ID granularity: 3‑level hierarchy balances expressiveness and generation complexity; more levels increase generation steps and potential error.  
  - Training data mix: Using all users for backbone pre‑training preserves general CF patterns; over‑specializing on dormant data too early would degrade coverage.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Decline in long‑tail item exposure ratio (Fig. 5) indicates Reasoner’s counterfactuals are not expanding diversity.  
  - Rising training loss on the backbone after guidance injection suggests mis‑alignment between semantic signals and collaborative patterns.  
  - Online metrics: stagnating or decreasing DAC/order volume for dormant segment; high overlap between recommended items and historical clicks (interest collapse).  
  - Reflection loop failure: if the Reasoner’s performance on held‑out validation degrades after reflection updates, the feedback signal may be noisy or the back‑propagation schedule needs adjustment.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Offline ablation: remove Reasoning Injection and Reflection separately to measure impact on Recall@1 and MRR for dormant users (as in Table 2).  
  2. Counterfactual role diversity test: vary the candidate role set size and measure coverage of high‑popularity vs long‑tail buckets (replicating Fig. 5).  
  3. Online A/B test: allocate a small percentage (e.g., 5%) of dormant‑user traffic to RoleGen vs production baseline; monitor DAC, CTCVR, order volume over one week to detect statistically significant lifts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can functional roles be learned automatically from interaction data?
- **Basis:** [inferred] The paper uses fixed role dimensions (Sec. 3.4.2) and does not explore learning them.
- **Why:** Manual definitions may not capture all relevant semantics and hinder adaptation.
- **Evidence:** Replace hand‑crafted roles with latent factors from a VAE or clustering; compare RoleGen’s performance and interpretability.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does LLM size impact RoleGen’s trade‑off between accuracy and latency?
- **Basis:** [inferred] Only Qwen‑8B is used; no scaling study.
- **Why:** Deploying large LLMs is costly; understanding scaling informs resource planning.
- **Evidence:** Benchmark multiple LLM sizes (e.g., 1B, 8B, 70B) on offline recall and online response time.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Is the closed‑loop “Reasoning‑Execution‑Feedback‑Reflection” stable over repeated updates?
- **Basis:** [inferred] The paper describes the loop but evaluates only one iteration.
- **Why:** Feedback loops can amplify errors; long‑term stability is unclear.
- **Evidence:** Conduct a multi‑week A/B test with regular Reasoner updates; monitor metric variance and perform error analysis on drift.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does RoleGen generalize to other e‑commerce platforms?
- **Basis:** [inferred] Evaluation solely on Kuaishou; platform‑specific components (SIDs, knowledge graph) may not transfer.
- **Why:** Cross‑platform applicability is necessary for broader impact.
- **Evidence:** Port the framework to a different dataset (e.g., Amazon), adapting only necessary components, and compare with local strong baselines.

## Limitations
- **Knowledge graph construction specifics:** The algorithm for deriving semantic/behavioral relations between categories (edge thresholds, weighting, sparsity) is not detailed, which is critical for replicating contextual role definitions.
- **Curriculum & sampling thresholds:** Exact thresholds for the high‑quality item subset (“orders > 5”) and down‑sampling ratios for view events are unspecified, affecting data distribution and training dynamics.
- **Inference cost of weekly Reasoner updates:** Computational resources and latency of running the LLM across millions of dormant users are not quantified, raising scalability concerns.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** Offline Recall@1 improvement (6.2%) and online order volume lift (7.3%) are strongly supported by large‑scale experiments on Kuaishou.
- **Medium Confidence:** Mitigation of the Matthew effect (improved long‑tail exposure) and generalization to out‑of‑distribution scenarios are backed by presented figures/tables but lack detailed statistical testing.
- **Low Confidence:** The hypothesis that modeling functional roles is strictly superior to isolated item‑value prediction lacks a direct ablation against a strong non‑role‑based sequence model (e.g., SASRec) using the same SID backbone.

## Next Checks
- **KG Ablation:** Re‑train the Reasoner using knowledge graphs constructed with varying edge sparsity thresholds (e.g., top 1%, 5%, 10% most frequent category co‑occurrences) and measure impact on offline Recall@1 to isolate KG contribution.
- **Guidance Feature Ablation:** Remove specific injected features (coarse SID frequency vs. candidate list) from the backbone input to quantify which reasoning signals drive the gain.
- **Long‑Term Online Stability:** Extend the A/B test for at least 4 weeks, monitoring DAC, order volume, item catalog coverage, repeat exposure, and user retention to assess persistence of gains and potential filter‑bubble effects.
