# Retrieval-aligned Tabular Foundation Models Enable Robust Clinical Risk Prediction in Electronic Health Records Under Real-world Constraints

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01841
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01841
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** AWARE, a retrieval-aligned framework, improves AUPRC by up to 12.2% under extreme outcome imbalance and recovers performance losses from naive retrieval in heterogeneous EHRs.

## Executive Summary
Clinical risk prediction using electronic health records (EHRs) is hampered by high-dimensional, heterogeneous features, severe class imbalance, and distribution shifts. Standard tabular in-context learning (TICL) and retrieval-augmented methods degrade when using naive distance-based retrieval in raw feature space. This paper proposes AWARE, a framework that aligns the retrieval space with the downstream task via supervised metric learning (Soft Nearest Neighbor loss) and a lightweight adapter to bridge distribution gaps between retrieved contexts and a frozen prior-fitted network. Evaluations across multiple EHR cohorts show that AWARE substantially improves robustness, particularly for rare outcomes, and identifies retrieval quality and retrieval-inference alignment as critical bottlenecks for clinical deployment.

## Method Summary
AWARE is a two-stage framework for robust retrieval-augmented TICL in EHRs. First, it learns a task-specific embedding space using an attention-based encoder optimized with the Soft Nearest Neighbor (SNNL) loss, which clusters same-label patients and separates different-label patients to produce clinically relevant neighborhoods. Retrieval is then performed in this aligned space. Second, a lightweight adapter (a trainable linear projection) is fine-tuned on bootstrapped prompts constructed from SNNL-based retrievals; this adapter aligns the retrieved context distribution with the expectations of a frozen pretrained Prior-Fitted Network (PFN) backbone, correcting for distribution shift without altering the backbone. The system uses an ensemble of K SNNL-trained encoders for stable embeddings and a fixed context size (k=1024) for in-context inference.

## Key Results
- Naive distance-based retrieval in raw EHR feature space degrades performance as feature heterogeneity and class imbalance increase, with poor neighborhood label purity.
- SNNL-based alignment consistently improves retrieval quality and downstream AUPRC across datasets and tasks, with relative gains scaling with training size (0.3–4%), dimensionality (up to 7%), and imbalance severity.
- Under extreme outcome imbalance (imbalance ratio > 500), AWARE improves AUPRC by up to 12.2% compared to baseline RA-TICL models.
- The adapter provides additional stabilization, effectively bridging the distribution gap between synthetic pretraining data and real EHR retrieval contexts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Naïve distance-based retrieval in raw EHR feature space selects contextually irrelevant neighbors due to high-dimensional heterogeneity and severe class imbalance.
- **Mechanism:** Euclidean or cosine distance is dominated by sparse, weakly informative features and majority-class prevalence, producing neighborhoods with low label purity. Retrieved examples are mathematically proximal but clinically non-comparable, degrading in-context learning.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "naïve distance-based retrieval ... leads to degradation as heterogeneity and imbalance increase."; [section 3.4] "high-dimensional and heterogeneous clinical features can distort similarity metrics, leading to retrieved neighbors that are mathematically close but not clinically comparable."
- **Break Condition:** Retrieval precision@k and AUPRC plateau or decline as feature dimensionality or imbalance ratio increases beyond dataset-specific thresholds.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Supervised metric learning via SNNL reshapes the embedding space to cluster same-label patients and separate different-label patients, improving neighborhood quality for retrieval.
- **Mechanism:** SNNL minimizes intra-class distances while maximizing inter-class separation in the embedding space, explicitly aligning similarity with the downstream task label. This counteracts noise from irrelevant features and majority-class dominance.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [section 4] "Minimizing Eq. 6 encourages embeddings from the same outcome class to form compact local neighborhoods while separating embeddings from different classes."; [section 6.1] "SNNL consistently produces positive gains... indicating that explicit alignment of neighborhood geometry with task labels is critical."
- **Break Condition:** SNNL loss fails to decrease or embedding visualization (e.g., t-SNE) shows no cluster separation by label, indicating poor alignment or incorrect temperature T.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Lightweight adapter fine-tuning aligns the representations from the task-aligned retrieval space with the frozen prior-fitted network backbone, stabilizing inference under the new context distribution.
- **Mechanism:** A small trainable projection layer maps retrieved (SNNL-aligned) support and query instances into a representation space compatible with the pretrained Transformer's expectations. Trained on bootstrapped prompts, it corrects for distribution shift between synthetic pretraining data and real EHR retrieval contexts without altering backbone weights.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [section 4] "The adapter learns a dataset-specific correction that aligns the model with the empirical prompt distribution induced by SNNL-based retrieval."; [section 6.1] "Subsequent refinements, including adapter fine-tuning, provide additional... improvements."
- **Break Condition:** Adapter training loss does not converge, or performance degrades versus SNNL-only retrieval, suggesting the correction is harmful or the prior is already well-aligned.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Metric Learning (e.g., SNNL)**
  - **Why needed here:** To transform high-dimensional, sparse EHR features into a low-dimensional space where Euclidean distance reflects clinical outcome similarity, not just raw feature overlap.
  - **Quick Check:** Does minimizing SNNL in a batch actually increase the ratio of same-label to different-label neighbors within a fixed radius in the embedding space?
- **Concept: Prior-Fitted Networks (PFNs)**
  - **Why needed here:** To enable inference-time adaptation without retraining, crucial for rapidly deploying on new EHR tasks/cohorts where labeled data is scarce.
  - **Quick Check:** Does the PFN's in-context prediction performance improve when conditioned on a task-aligned retrieved context versus a random context?
- **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (Adapters)**
  - **Why needed here:** To bridge the distribution gap between the synthetic pretraining distribution of the PFN and the real, retrieval-augmented EHR data distribution, without catastrophic forgetting or excessive compute.
  - **Quick Check:** Does a frozen backbone + trained adapter on synthetic bootstrapped prompts match the performance of a fully fine-tuned backbone on the target task (with far fewer parameters)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:** Input Pipeline -> AWARE Encoder (with SNNL) -> Embedding Space -> Nearest Neighbor Retrieval -> Prompt Construction -> Adapter -> Frozen PFN Backbone -> Prediction
- **Critical Path:** Raw EHR data is encoded via an SNNL-trained encoder; embeddings are used to retrieve top-k neighbors; a prompt is built; the adapter processes the prompt; the frozen PFN backbone produces the final prediction. The SNNL-trained encoder must yield meaningful clusters before adapter training; adapter assumes retrieval context is already task-aligned.
- **Design Tradeoffs:**
  - **Retrieval k-size:** Larger k increases context diversity/compute but risks including irrelevant neighbors; smaller k risks insufficient context.
  - **SNNL temperature T:** Low T yields sharp clusters but may overfit; high T yields smooth neighborhoods but less separation.
  - **Adapter capacity:** Too small cannot correct distribution shift; too large risks overfitting on small bootstrap samples and losing prior benefits.
- **Failure Signatures:**
  - Degraded AUPRC on rare outcomes despite AWARE: Check if Precision@k for positive class remains low (< majority class baseline), indicating persistent retrieval bias.
  - High variance across AWARE K-folds: Indicates unstable embedding learning; likely due to insufficient SNNL convergence or extreme imbalance not handled by balanced sampling.
  - Adapter training loss diverges: Mismatch between synthetic pretraining distribution and AWARE retrieval space is too large; may need more adapter capacity or different initialization.
- **First 3 Experiments:**
  1. **SNNL Ablation:** Train AWARE encoder with/without SNNL on a high-imbalance task; evaluate retrieval Precision@k and downstream AUPRC to isolate SNNL's contribution.
  2. **Feature Heterogeneity Stress Test:** Incrementally add low-SHAP features to a fixed dataset; measure AUPRC degradation slope for baseline vs. AWARE-enhanced models.
  3. **Adapter Bootstrapping Validation:** Train adapter on synthetic bootstrapped prompts from the target dataset; evaluate if it closes the gap between SNNL-retrieval + frozen PFN and a fully supervised model on a small held-out set.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can retrieval and inference be jointly optimized in RA-TICL to improve clinical prediction?  
  *Basis:* Authors state: "Future work may therefore explore tighter coupling between retrieval and prediction, such as jointly optimizing embedding and in-context inference or propagating gradients from the downstream PFN model into the retrieval space."  
  *Why unresolved:* Current methods treat retrieval and inference as disjoint stages; joint training could align context construction with prediction but might compromise efficiency or stability and remains unexplored.
- **Question 2:** How can RA-TICL achieve robust performance under extreme outcome imbalance and institutional distribution shift?  
  *Basis:* Authors identify: "Extreme imbalance and distribution shift remain open challenges for retrieval-based inference in clinical settings."  
  *Why unresolved:* AWARE mitigates but does not eliminate degradation for rare events (e.g., IR > 500) and cross-site scenarios; minority-class retrieval neighborhoods remain unstable.
- **Question 3:** What is the prospective clinical utility and workflow impact of RA-TICL models compared to traditional supervised approaches?  
  *Basis:* Limitations: "We do not evaluate downstream decision-making impact, clinician interaction, real-time workflow integration, or prospective performance in live clinical environments."  
  *Why unresolved:* Retrospective benchmarks cannot ascertain whether performance gains translate to improved patient outcomes, clinician trust, or feasible integration.
- **Question 4:** How can longitudinal EHR dynamics be incorporated into tabular in-context learning without sacrificing computational efficiency?  
  *Basis:* Inferred: The study focuses on snapshot-based representations, acknowledging that "clinical data are inherently longitudinal" but summarization may discard temporally critical information.  
  *Why unresolved:* Directly modeling sequences increases token length and computational burden; efficient architectures for time-aware retrieval and context construction remain undeveloped.

## Limitations
- Key hyperparameters (embedding dimension, SNNL temperature, adapter size) and evaluation details (seeds, CV folds, k-tuning) are unspecified or sparse.
- Adapter bootstrapping is heuristic and may overfit synthetic prompts; lacks theoretical justification.
- External validity across diverse healthcare systems is not tested.
- No quantitative breakpoints defining when naive retrieval or SNNL fails (e.g., specific dimensionality/imbalance thresholds).
- No evaluation of clinical decision-making impact or real-time workflow integration.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1** (naïve retrieval fails under heterogeneity/imbalance): Medium. Supported by stress tests, but failure thresholds vague.
- **Mechanism 2** (SNNL aligns embeddings): Medium. Gains consistent, but temperature sensitivity not explored.
- **Mechanism 3** (adapter aligns retrieval with PFN): Low. Modest gains; heuristic; risk of overfitting.
- **Overall AWARE improvement:** Medium. Up to 12.2% AUPRC gain reported; needs independent replication.
- **Scalability claims** (gains with size/dimensionality/imbalance): Medium. Correlative evidence; no controlled ablations isolating each factor.

## Next Checks
1. Systematically vary feature dimensionality and class imbalance; measure per-class Precision@k to quantify failure thresholds for naive vs. SNNL retrieval.
2. Perform grid search over SNNL temperature and embedding dimension; correlate with downstream AUPRC to establish robust settings.
3. Test adapter generalization: train on source cohort, evaluate on unseen target cohort; compare to full fine‑tuning.
