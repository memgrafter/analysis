# Can LLMs See Without Pixels? Benchmarking Spatial Intelligence from Textual Descriptions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03590  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03590  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: SOTA LLMs (e.g., Gemini-3-Flash) reach only 59.46% accuracy on SiT-Bench, well below human performance (74.42%), with especially low scores on global consistency tasks like cognitive mapping (best 8.34% vs. 26.77%).  

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SiT-Bench, a large-scale textual spatial reasoning benchmark comprising 3,892 expert-annotated samples across 17 subtasks. Unlike traditional vision-based evaluations, SiT-Bench provides only coordinate-aware textual scene descriptions, testing whether language models can reason about spatial layouts without pixel input. The study finds that state-of-the-art LLMs still lag behind humans, with the best model (Gemini-3-Flash) achieving 59.46% accuracy versus 74.42% for humans. Performance is particularly poor on tasks requiring global consistency, such as cognitive mapping, where top models score below 10%. The authors also show that explicit reasoning techniques (Chain-of-Thought) markedly improve results, and that vision-language models (VLMs) consistently outperform their LLM backbones, suggesting visual training imparts useful spatial priors. In contrast, specialized spatial models underperform general-purpose ones, highlighting a need for stronger symbolic spatial reasoning in LLM foundations.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate a range of LLMs and VLMs on the SiT-Bench benchmark. Each sample consists of a textual caption describing one or more viewpoints (with absolute or relative coordinates) and a multiple-choice question (4 options) or, for some subtasks like Cognitive Mapping, a structured JSON output requirement. Models are prompted using task-specific templates (detailed in the appendix) and queried with a fixed token budget (max 32,768 tokens, thinking budget 8,192 if applicable). Inference is performed on a server with 8×NVIDIA A100 (80GB) GPUs using vLLM for open-source models; proprietary models are accessed via APIs. The primary metric is accuracy per subtask and overall RankAvg, with secondary measurement of inference latency. Results are compared against a random baseline (~25% for multiple-choice) and human performance obtained via expert annotation.

## Key Results
- Gemini-3-Flash achieves the highest overall accuracy among LLMs at 59.46%, still well below the human benchmark of 74.42%.  
- Cognitive Mapping tasks prove exceptionally difficult: the best model scores only 8.34% versus 26.77% for humans.  
- Enabling Chain-of-Thought reasoning yields significant improvements across most subtasks; VLMs consistently outperform their LLM backbones, indicating that visual training provides valuable spatial priors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The benchmark succeeds because it isolates spatial reasoning from visual perception, forcing models to rely on linguistic representations of space. The substantial gains from Chain-of-Thought suggest that LLMs benefit from explicit symbolic step-by-step reasoning to integrate local spatial relations into global conclusions. The consistent advantage of VLMs over their LLM backbones indicates that visual training instills internal spatial priors (e.g., understanding of perspective, layout, and distance) that transfer to textual tasks. Conversely, the catastrophic failure on cognitive mapping reveals a fundamental limitation: LLMs struggle to construct and maintain a coherent mental map from fragmented descriptions, likely due to insufficient training on multi-view spatial integration and symbolic map construction.

## Foundational Learning
- **Spatial Reasoning**: Ability to understand and manipulate relationships like distance, direction, and layout. *Why needed*: SiT-Bench tests pure textual spatial intelligence. *Quick check*: Given “The book is on the table, the lamp is to the right of the book,” can you determine the lamp’s position relative to the table?  
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT)**: Prompting technique that elicits intermediate reasoning steps. *Why needed*: The paper shows CoT markedly improves spatial task performance. *Quick check*: Does adding “Let’s think step by step” to a spatial QA increase accuracy for a base LLM?  
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**: Models trained on paired image-text data. *Why needed*: VLMs outperform LLM backbones, implying visual training encodes useful spatial priors. *Quick check*: Compare a VLM and its text-only variant on a spatial description task; does the VLM score higher?  
- **Cognitive Mapping**: Constructing a mental representation of an environment from partial views. *Why needed*: This is the most challenging subtask, exposing a key weakness. *Quick check*: From descriptions of multiple room corners, can you sketch a coherent floor plan?  
- **Textual Spatial Descriptions**: Captions that include coordinates or relational terms (e.g., “north of”, “2 meters away”). *Why needed*: They are the sole input in SiT-Bench, replacing pixel data. *Quick check*: Can you parse “The chair is 2m north of the table” to answer “What is east of the chair?”  
- **Benchmarking**: Standardized evaluation suites for model capabilities. *Why needed*: SiT-Bench offers a multi-faceted assessment of spatial intelligence. *Quick check*: Does the benchmark cover diverse spatial skills (counting, perspective shift, mapping) with a sufficiently large sample size?

## Architecture Onboarding
The evaluation pipeline consists of the following components:

SiT-Bench Data -> DataLoader -> PromptTemplate -> ModelEngine -> OutputParser -> MetricsLogger -> Aggregator

- **DataLoader**: Reads JSON samples (caption, question, options, task type).  
- **PromptTemplate**: Applies task‑specific formatting to generate model inputs.  
- **ModelEngine**: Executes inference (vLLM for local, API for remote); supports optional thinking mode.  
- **OutputParser**: Extracts a single option letter (A–D) via regex and fallback heuristics.  
- **MetricsLogger**: Records correctness and timestamps per sample.  
- **Aggregator**: Computes per‑task accuracy, overall RankAvg, and latency statistics.

**Critical path**: Each sample traverses the chain sequentially: load → prompt → generate → parse → log → aggregate.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Textual‑only input reduces compute but eliminates raw visual cues.  
- Multiple‑choice simplifies scoring but limits expressiveness; JSON tasks allow richer outputs but require robust parsing.  
- Local inference (vLLM) offers control and speed; APIs are needed for proprietary models but add latency and cost.  
- Thinking mode (CoT) improves accuracy at the expense of token usage and latency.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Parser returns no valid letter (e.g., verbose output without clear answer) → record as incorrect.  
- Out‑of‑memory on large models → reduce batch size or use tensor parallelism.  
- Latency spikes → network issues for APIs or non‑deterministic kernels.  
- Accuracy below random baseline → likely data contamination or template mismatch.

**First experiments**:  
1. **Sanity check**: Run 5 samples per task with a small model (e.g., Llama‑2‑7B) to validate the entire pipeline, prompt formatting, and parsing logic.  
2. **VLM vs LLM backbone**: Compare Qwen3‑VL and Qwen3 on a single subtask (e.g., Panoramic Counting) with and without CoT to confirm expected gaps.  
3. **Random baseline verification**: Simulate random guessing on multiple‑choice questions and ensure the computed average (~25%) matches the paper’s reported baseline.

## Limitations
- The full SiT‑Bench dataset, evaluation code, and task‑specific prompt templates are not publicly released, preventing exact replication.  
- Checkpoints for specialized spatial models (e.g., Space‑Qwen, SpaceThinker) are inaccessible, so their reported underperformance cannot be independently verified.  
- The method to enable “thinking” mode varies across model families without standardized parameters; human baseline variance is also unreported, undermining reproducibility confidence.

## Confidence
- Benchmark construction (3,892 samples, 17 subtasks): Medium  
- LLM vs. human performance gap (59.46% vs. 74.42%): High  
- Cognitive mapping failure (8.34% vs. 26.77%): High  
- CoT improvement: Medium  
- VLM advantage over LLM backbones: Medium  
- Specialized spatial models underperforming: Low
