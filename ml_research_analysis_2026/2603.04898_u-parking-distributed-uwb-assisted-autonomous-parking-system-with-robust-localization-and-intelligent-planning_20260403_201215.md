# U-Parking: Distributed UWB-Assisted Autonomous Parking System with Robust Localization and Intelligent Planning

## Quick Facts
* arXiv ID: 2603.04898  
* Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04898  
* Reference count: 13  
* Primary result: Achieved a mean Euclidean tracking error of 0.118 m and a normalized DTW mean of 0.133 in real-vehicle demonstrations, significantly outperforming UWB-only (0.240 m) and UWB+EKF (0.190 m) baselines.

## Executive Summary
U-Parking is a distributed system for reliable autonomous parking in indoor environments plagued by UWB multipath and occlusions. It integrates a server-side LLM-assisted planner that leverages parking-lot topology and real-time UWB signal quality to select parking spaces and generate constraining waypoints for an A* search, a three-layer hierarchical UWB-IMU fusion with an innovation-based adaptive EKF (IAEKF) for robust localization during LOS/NLOS transitions, and an MPC-based trajectory tracker that adapts its cost weights to suppress aggressive maneuvers when UWB reliability degrades. Real-world tests in a 30.2 m × 37.9 m indoor lot demonstrated a mean Euclidean tracking error of 0.118 m, confirming the system’s accuracy and robustness.

## Method Summary
The system comprises a parking-lot server and a vehicle node communicating via ROS2. Upon a parking request, the server constructs a structured prompt containing the environmental map, UWB anchor deployment, space status, and current vehicle pose. An LLM (LLaMA-3.3-70B) processes this input to output a target parking-space ID and a sparse set of guiding waypoints. These waypoints constrain the A* graph expansion, drastically reducing unnecessary node explorations. The resulting path is smoothed with B-spline/Bézier curves and extended with a final curve-line maneuver for the parking action. On the vehicle, raw UWB ranges and IMU data are preprocessed through a three-layer hierarchy: first-layer filtering, second-layer 2D position computation, and third-layer IAEKF localization that fuses UWB-derived positions with IMU propagation. The IAEKF monitors the innovation residual; when its norm exceeds a threshold, the measurement noise covariance is inflated to down-weight potentially corrupted UWB updates. Finally, an MPC controller tracks the planned trajectory, adaptively adjusting cost weights—reducing position error weight and increasing control effort penalties—when severe UWB-induced jumps are detected, thereby preventing oscillations.

## Key Results
- Mean Euclidean tracking error: **0.118 m** (vs. 0.240 m for UWB-only, 0.190 m for UWB+EKF)  
- Normalized DTW mean: **0.133**  
- Demonstrated robust operation in real-vehicle indoor parking scenarios with challenging UWB multipath and occlusions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: LLM-Assisted Planning**  
- **Claim:** LLM reasoning over topology and UWB signal quality pre-filters candidate parking spaces and generates guiding waypoints, constraining the A* search space.  
- **Mechanism:** The server feeds a structured prompt (environment map, anchor layout, space IDs/coords, vehicle pose, space status) to an LLM. The LLM outputs a target space ID and sparse waypoints that lie in high-UWB-coverage corridors. These waypoints restrict A* expansion to relevant graph regions, cutting computational effort.  
- **Core assumption:** The LLM can synthesize heterogeneous spatial and signal data to produce a feasible, globally sub-optimal but efficient path initialization that aligns with UWB reliability.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “LLM-assisted planning … constraining A* search space”; Section II details that LLM prioritizes regions with favorable UWB signal quality and outputs waypoints to guide an improved A* planner, reducing unnecessary grid expansions.  
- **Corpus:** Neighboring papers address LLM path planning [3,4] and UWB localization [2,7] separately, but their integration for search-space reduction is not evidenced.  
- **Break condition:** LLM outputs waypoints that are unreachable (e.g., inside obstacles) or fail to reduce A* node expansions compared to a naive global search.

**Mechanism 2: Three-Layer Hierarchical UWB-IMU Fusion with IAEKF**  
- **Claim:** The IAEKF robustly localizes during LOS/NLOS transitions by dynamically down-weighting UWB measurements when innovation residuals indicate corruption.  
- **Mechanism:** Three layers: (1) raw UWB range filtering, (2) initial 2D position computation, (3) IAEKF fusion with IMU. The IAEKF computes the innovation residual `r_k = z_UWB_k - x̂-_k`. When `||r_k||` exceeds a threshold, the measurement noise covariance is inflated, reducing the filter’s reliance on the likely erroneous UWB update.  
- **Core assumption:** Innovation residual magnitude reliably signals NLOS-induced error, and adaptive covariance tuning maintains filter stability during transient faults.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states “three-layer hierarchical UWB-IMU fusion with IAEKF for robust localization during LOS/NLOS transitions”; Section II explains dynamic reduction of UWB weighting when residual norm exceeds threshold.  
- **Corpus:** Prior UWB/IMU fusion works [2,7] lack this specific residual-thresholding mechanism.  
- **Break condition:** Prolonged high residuals cause over-distrust of UWB, leading to IMU drift; or the threshold misses rapid multipath errors.

**Mechanism 3: MPC with Adaptive Weight Adjustment**  
- **Claim:** The MPC tracker adapts cost weights based on estimated localization reliability to suppress aggressive control during UWB-induced position jumps, preventing oscillations.  
- **Mechanism:** The controller monitors for “severe UWB jumps” (e.g., sudden large position deltas). When detected, it reduces the weight on instantaneous position tracking error and increases penalties on velocity and steering rates, encouraging smoother motion at the cost of some tracking accuracy.  
- **Core assumption:** Localization uncertainty (UWB noise bursts) can be mapped to an appropriate control policy via heuristic weight scaling, stabilizing the vehicle without explicit uncertainty quantification.  
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “MPC-based trajectory tracker with adaptive weight adjustment”; Section II describes down-weighting position errors and penalizing excessive variations when jumps are detected.  
- **Corpus:** No direct prior work on MPC weight adaptation for UWB noise in parking.  
- **Break condition:** The jump detector triggers too often (causing sluggish tracking) or too rarely (allowing oscillations) under real glitches.

## Foundational Learning
1. **UWB Multipath & NLOS Error Characteristics**  
   - *Why needed:* The system combats UWB’s weakness in cluttered indoor lots; NLOS causes large biased errors, not just Gaussian noise, and LOS transitions are high-risk.  
   - *Quick check:* If a UWB anchor suddenly reports a range 2 m longer than the previous measurement in an open area, is this more likely a NLOS error or a sensor fault? What would the IAEKF’s residual do?

2. **Innovation Residual in Kalman Filtering**  
   - *Why needed:* The IAEKF adaptation hinges on monitoring the residual between predicted and measured states to detect faulty UWB updates.  
   - *Quick check:* In a standard EKF, a consistent large residual over several steps most directly indicates what? (A) Correct model, (B) Unmodeled dynamics, (C) Measurement fault, (D) Process noise misestimation.

3. **Distributed ROS2 Communication Architecture**  
   - *Why needed:* The server-vehicle collaboration relies on ROS2 for data flow (map, requests, trajectories, poses); understanding topics, services, and QoS is essential for debugging.  
   - *Quick check:* What ROS2 message type would most likely carry the “planned path” from the server to the vehicle? What about the “UWB-derived position measurement” from the vehicle to the server?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Server Node (Parking Lot Server) → LLM Planner (LLaMA API client), Map Manager, A* Planner, Bezier/B-spline Smoother ↔ ROS2 Topics/Services ↔ Vehicle Node → Sensor Drivers (UWB, IMU), Layer1/2 Preprocessors, IAEKF Localization, MPC Tracker, Vehicle Interface.

**Critical path:**  
Parking Request → Server LLM Planning → Constrained A* → Smoothed Trajectory → Vehicle IAEKF Localization → MPC Tracking → Actuation. Any break (LLM timeout, lost UWB packets, IAEKF divergence) halts execution.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *LLM latency vs. planning quality:* Using a 70B model via API adds seconds; smaller models may reduce waypoint quality, negating search-space savings.  
- *IAEKF threshold sensitivity:* Empirically tuned residual threshold; too high → NLOS errors corrupt filter, too low → valid data rejected, increasing IMU drift.  
- *MPC adaptation heuristic:* Jump detector and weight scaling are not learned; poor tuning leads to timid or unstable maneuvers.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *LLM Failure:* Waypoints placed inside walls or occluded zones; A* search space not reduced (check node expansion count).  
- *IAEKF Failure:* Sudden large pose jumps not smoothed within ~1–2 seconds; position covariance exploding.  
- *MPC Failure:* High-frequency steering oscillations or path deviation during UWB glitches; control output saturating.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **LLM Waypoint Validation:** In simulation, feed the LLM a known map and manually induce “bad” UWB quality maps. Verify output waypoints lie on drivable areas and that A* node expansion decreases >X% vs. full grid search.  
2. **IAEKF NLOS Transition Test:** With the vehicle stationary, manually block one UWB anchor to simulate NLOS. Confirm IAEKF position remains stable (drift <Y cm) while UWB-only and UWB+EKF show >Z cm error.  
3. **MPC Weight Tuning Sweep:** On a recorded dataset with known UWB glitches, run MPC with a range of weight adaptation gains. Measure maximum steering angle rate and Euclidean error during glitches to find a Pareto front between smoothness and accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization of LLM-assisted planning:** How well does it handle unseen parking lot layouts and dynamic changes in space availability? Current evaluation uses a fixed demo environment without diverse topologies.  
2. **IAEKF threshold optimality:** Is the empirically tuned innovation residual threshold optimal for all LOS/NLOS transition scenarios? Suboptimal thresholds may cause insufficient noise suppression or excessive measurement discounting.  
3. **MPC reliability metric:** What precise metric quantifies localization reliability for the adaptive weight adjustment, and how robust is it under extreme UWB degradation? The current heuristic (“severe UWB jumps”) lacks formal definition.  
4. **Scalability:** How does the distributed system scale with increasing vehicle count and parking lot size, given LLM server latency and ROS2 communication overhead? Only single-vehicle demonstrations are reported.

## Limitations
- The LLM planning mechanism (prompt design, output parsing, generalizability) lacks direct evidence; its success depends on untested model capabilities.  
- The IAEKF’s residual threshold and covariance inflation law are empirically tuned without sensitivity analysis, creating a potential single point of failure.  
- The MPC adaptation heuristic for detecting “severe UWB jumps” and weight scaling is unspecified and may not optimally balance robustness and tracking accuracy across varying noise conditions.

## Confidence
- **High:** Reported performance metrics (mean Euclidean error 0.118 m, normalized DTW 0.133) from real-vehicle demonstrations; the three-layer fusion and MPC architecture are clearly described.  
- **Medium:** That the proposed system functions as an integrated whole; the hierarchical localization scheme is a sound approach to UWB/IMU fusion.  
- **Low:** The specific causal contributions of each novel mechanism (LLM search-space reduction, IAEKF residual-thresholding, MPC weight adaptation) to the overall performance gain; the “why” relies on untested core assumptions.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation — LLM Waypoint Utility:** Compare full LLM-constrained A* against (a) unconstrained A* and (b) A* constrained by random waypoints. Measure node expansion count and path optimality to isolate the LLM’s contribution.  
2. **Sensitivity Analysis — IAEFK Threshold:** Systematically vary the innovation residual threshold on a recorded dataset with ground truth. Plot tracking error vs. threshold to identify the operational window and quantify degradation at suboptimal values.  
3. **MPC Heuristic Benchmarking:** On datasets with known UWB glitches, run MPC with the proposed adaptation versus (a) fixed weights and (b) an oracle that knows ground-truth localization uncertainty. Quantify trade-off between tracking error and control smoothness (e.g., max steering rate) during glitches.
