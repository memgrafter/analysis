# Bayesian Lottery Ticket Hypothesis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.18825
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18825
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Bayesian winning tickets exist in Bayesian neural networks, matching dense model accuracy up to ~98% sparsity, with pruning effectiveness primarily governed by weight mean magnitude.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores the extension of the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH) to Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) to enable efficient uncertainty quantification. The authors apply iterative magnitude pruning within mean-field variational inference to ResNet, VGG, and Vision Transformer models trained on CIFAR-10. They find that sparse Bayesian subnetworks—Bayesian winning tickets—achieve comparable or superior accuracy and calibration to dense BNNs at sparsities up to approximately 98%. Beyond this, performance degrades. Pruning effectiveness is driven chiefly by the magnitude of weight means rather than uncertainty estimates. The structure of the pruning mask and initialization are particularly important for Transformers. Furthermore, a transplantation approach that initializes BNNs with deterministic lottery tickets preserves accuracy and calibration while reducing training computation by up to 50%.

## Method Summary
The study employs mean-field variational inference (VI) to train BNNs on CIFAR-10 using ResNet, VGG, and Vision Transformer architectures. Iterative magnitude pruning is applied: weights are scored by their mean magnitude (and other criteria in ablations), and a fraction of the smallest magnitudes are pruned iteratively until the desired sparsity is reached. The resulting sparse subnetworks (winning tickets) are evaluated for accuracy, calibration, and computational efficiency. Additionally, the authors investigate a transplantation method where a deterministic lottery ticket (from a pruned dense network) is used as the mean initialization for a BNN, with uncertainty introduced via VI.

## Key Results
- Bayesian winning tickets match or exceed dense BNN accuracy up to ~98% sparsity on CIFAR-10.
- Pruning effectiveness is primarily determined by weight mean magnitude; mask structure and initialization are critical, particularly for Vision Transformers.
- Transplanting deterministic lottery tickets into BNNs preserves accuracy and calibration while reducing training compute by up to 50%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
In mean-field VI, each weight is represented by a Gaussian posterior with a mean and variance. The mean captures the learned direction and strength of the weight, while the variance reflects uncertainty. The paper finds that pruning based on the absolute magnitude of the mean is more effective than using uncertainty. This suggests that the mean magnitude is a more reliable indicator of a weight's contribution to the network's function. Intuitively, weights with large means are highly tuned to the task and their removal would cause significant performance loss, whereas weights with small means are less critical. Uncertainty measures (variances) may be noisy or diffuse, especially early in training, making them less suitable for identifying important weights. Thus, the mechanism underlying successful Bayesian ticket discovery is the selective preservation of weights with large mean magnitudes, which retain the core signal learned by the dense model.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
CIFAR-10 data -> Model (ResNet/VGG/ViT) -> Mean-field VI training -> Iterative magnitude pruning (score = mean magnitude) -> Sparse BNN (winning ticket) -> Evaluation (accuracy, calibration)  
Transplantation: Deterministic lottery ticket -> Initialize BNN means -> Mean-field VI training -> Sparse BNN.

**Critical path**:  
1. Initialize BNN with mean-field prior.  
2. Train model on CIFAR-10 until convergence.  
3. Compute pruning scores (weight mean magnitudes).  
4. Prune a fraction of weights with smallest scores.  
5. Repeat steps 2-4 until target sparsity reached.  
6. Evaluate the final sparse subnet (winning ticket).  
7. For transplantation: use a pruned dense network's weights as the mean initialization for a new BNN, then train with VI.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- **Sparsity vs. accuracy**: Higher sparsity improves efficiency but degrades accuracy beyond ~98%.  
- **Mask structure**: Unstructured pruning is flexible but may not yield hardware speedups; structured pruning (e.g., entire channels) is more practical but can hurt performance, especially for Vision Transformers.  
- **Scoring function**: Mean magnitude works well; using uncertainty or combinations may not improve results and could worsen performance.  
- **Initialization**: For Transformers, the mask structure and initialization are crucial; random initialization may fail to find good tickets.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Accuracy dropping sharply after ~98% sparsity, indicating a fundamental limit.  
- High variance in results for Vision Transformers when mask structure or initialization is altered, leading to inconsistent winning tickets.  
- Poor calibration if the BNN's uncertainty estimates are not preserved through pruning.

**First experiments**:  
1. **Baseline pruning**: Apply iterative magnitude pruning to a ResNet-20 with mean-field VI on CIFAR-10, plot accuracy vs. sparsity, and confirm the existence of a winning ticket up to ~98% sparsity.  
2. **Scoring function ablation**: Compare pruning based on mean magnitude, uncertainty (variance), and their product to verify that mean magnitude alone is the most effective.  
3. **Transplantation validation**: Initialize a BNN with a deterministic lottery ticket (from a pruned dense network) and train with VI; compare its accuracy, calibration, and training compute to a standard BNN trained from scratch.

## Limitations
- The study is restricted to mean-field variational inference on CIFAR-10 and iterative magnitude pruning; other Bayesian methods, datasets, or pruning criteria may yield different outcomes.
- Vision Transformer results are highly sensitive to mask structure and initialization, with the cause unknown.
- Accuracy degrades beyond approximately 98% sparsity, indicating a sparsity limit that may not generalize.

## Confidence
- **Existence of Bayesian winning tickets**: High  
- **Pruning effectiveness driven by weight mean magnitude**: Medium  
- **Mask structure/initialization critical for Transformers**: Medium  
- **Transplanting deterministic tickets preserves accuracy/calibration and reduces compute**: Medium
