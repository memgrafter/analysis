# Internalizing Agency from Reflective Experience
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16843  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16843  
- **Reference count:** 33  
- **Primary result:** LEAFE improves Pass@128 (e.g., +14% absolute on CodeContests) by mitigating distribution sharpening through experience-guided rollback and distillation, outperforming outcome-driven RL (GRPO) and scaling with model size.

## Executive Summary
Outcome-driven post-training (e.g., RL with verifiable rewards) often sharpens an LLM agent's policy toward a narrow set of successful behaviors, limiting expansion of problem-solving capacity in long-horizon tasks as measured by Pass@k. LEAFE addresses this with a two-stage framework: first, it generates corrective experience by reflecting on feedback, identifying suboptimal decision points, and backtracking to explore alternative branches; second, it distills these corrections into the policy via supervised fine-tuning, internalizing recovery agency without runtime experience. Across multiple agentic benchmarks and model scales, LEAFE consistently achieves higher Pass@128 than baselines, demonstrating improved behavioral coverage and out-of-distribution generalization.

## Method Summary
LEAFE operates in two stages. **Stage 1** (experience generation) runs interactive rollouts where the agent periodically reflects—every *K* steps or upon failure—to produce a rollback target τ (a prior suboptimal step) and an experience summary *e* (diagnosis and fix). The environment deterministically resets to the state at τ by replaying prior actions, and a revised action *a*′<sub>τ</sub> is generated conditioned on the history, task query, and *e*. This creates a new branch in an implicit rollback tree managed via BFS. Successful trajectories are stored, and rollback events (history *h*<sub>τ</sub>, corrected action *a*′<sub>τ</sub>) are recorded. **Stage 2** (distillation) fine-tunes the policy on two datasets: Dreh (20% random successful trajectories for behavior rehearsal) and Dcf (counterfactual pairs (*h*<sub>τ</sub>, *a*′<sub>τ</sub>)). The loss *L* = *L*<sub>cf</sub> + β*L*<sub>reh</sub> forces the model to learn corrections intrinsically, removing the need for explicit experience at test time.

## Key Results
- LEAFE significantly improves Pass@128 over base models and outcome-driven baselines (GRPO), with up to +14% absolute gain on CodeContests.
- Gains are larger at high *k* (e.g., Pass@128 vs. Pass@1), indicating expanded behavioral coverage and mitigation of distribution sharpening.
- Performance scales with model size (Qwen2.5 7B→72B, Llama3.1 8B→70B), and LEAFE generalizes better out-of-distribution than GRPO.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Reflective backtracking turns environment feedback into actionable experience, enabling exploration of alternative corrective branches.
- **Mechanism:** At reflection points, the policy outputs τ (suboptimal round) and *e* (diagnosis/fix). The environment resets to *E*<sub>τ</sub> by replaying actions up to τ−1; the policy then generates *a*′<sub>τ</sub> conditioned on *h*<sub>τ</sub>, *q*, and *e*, launching a new BFS-managed branch.
- **Core assumption:** Deterministic, resettable environment; accurate identification of τ and useful *e*.
- **Evidence anchors:** [Abstract] “the agent summarizes environment feedback into actionable experience, backtracks…” [Sec 3.1] Algorithm 1 details. *Memento 2* (arxiv:2512.22716) supports reflective mechanisms.
- **Break condition:** Inexact resets or poor reflection yield unproductive branches.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Distillation internalizes feedback‑grounded recovery, removing test‑time need for explicit experience.
- **Mechanism:** From Stage 1, Dreh (successes) and Dcf (counterfactuals (*h*<sub>τ</sub>, *a*′<sub>τ</sub>)) train the policy with *L* = *L*<sub>cf</sub> + β*L*<sub>reh</sub>. *L*<sub>cf</sub> maximizes likelihood of *a*′<sub>τ</sub> given only *h*<sub>τ</sub>, forcing intrinsic learning of the correction.
- **Core assumption:** *a*′<sub>τ</sub> is a valid improvement for *h*<sub>τ</sub> even without *e*.
- **Evidence anchors:** [Abstract] “distill these experience‑guided corrections…” [Sec 3.2] formal loss definitions. [Sec 4.3] Table 4: adding *L*<sub>cf</sub> boosts Pass@128.
- **Break condition:** *a*′<sub>τ</sub> not truly superior or noisy Dcf degrades policy.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** LEAFE mitigates distribution sharpening, increasing Pass@k by expanding behavioral coverage via experience‑driven corrections.
- **Mechanism:** Outcome‑driven RL (e.g., GRPO) only upweights full successes, biasing toward existing competent modes. LEAFE’s rollback branches generate corrections for failing attempts; distilling these adds novel successful behaviors, raising the probability of drawing at least one success under many samples.
- **Core assumption:** Corrections introduce genuinely novel successes that survive distillation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [Abstract] “outcome‑driven… lead to distribution sharpening… LEAFE achieves higher Pass@k.” [Intro] “RLVR… like distribution sharpening…” [Sec 4.1] Tables 1–2: LEAFE surpasses GRPO in Pass@128. [Sec 4.2] Figure 3: scaling curves dominate as *k* increases.
- **Break condition:** Corrections lack diversity or distillation collapses them into narrow modes.

## Architecture Onboarding
Policy (LLM π<sub>θ</sub>) -> Environment *E* -> Reflection Trigger -> Rollback Engine -> Branching Manager (BFS queue) -> Data Collectors (Dreh, Dcf) -> Training Pipeline (SFT with *L* = *L*<sub>cf</sub> + β*L*<sub>reh</sub>) -> Final policy

**Critical path:**  
1. Initialize π<sub>θ</sub> (base or GRPO‑trained).  
2. **Stage 1 Exploration:**  
   a. Rollout until termination.  
   b. On trigger (every *K* steps or failure), generate (τ, *e*) and enqueue.  
   c. Process queue: reset *E*, replay actions to τ−1, generate *a*′<sub>τ</sub> with *e*, continue rollout.  
   d. Record successes in Dreh; record (h<sub>τ</sub>, *a*′<sub>τ</sub>) in Dcf.  
3. **Stage 2 Distillation:** Build Dreh (20% random successes) and Dcf (counterfactuals); fine‑tune π<sub>θ</sub> for 2–3 epochs with *L*.  
4. Evaluate final policy via standard sampling (no rollback) to compute Pass@k.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Rollback frequency (*K*):** Smaller *K* catches early errors but increases overhead; larger *K* risks error propagation.  
- **Experience quality:** Dependent on policy’s reflection; poor prompts yield noisy τ/*e*.  
- **Rehearsal‑to‑counterfactual ratio (β):** High β preserves skills but may dilute new corrections; low β risks forgetting.  
- **Initialization:** GRPO init improves Pass@1 but may constrain exploration (smaller Pass@128 gains reported).  
- **Determinism requirement:** Exact state resets are assumed; stochasticity requires approximation.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Pass@k scaling matches baseline → experience not expanding coverage (τ/*e* ineffective).  
- Pass@1 drops after distillation → catastrophic forgetting (β too high or Dreh too small).  
- High variance across seeds → unstable branching or nondeterministic resets.  
- Many rollback requests fail (invalid τ) → reflection producing out‑of‑range indices or restoration issues.  
- *L*<sub>cf</sub> stagnates while *L*<sub>reh</sub> decreases → model ignoring counterfactual signal.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Main Results** (Sec 4.1): Compare LEAFE with Base, GRPO, EarlyExp, ACE on five benchmarks; report Pass@1/128. Higher Pass@128 confirms expanded capability.  
2. **Ablation of Experience Distillation** (Sec 4.3): Remove *L*<sub>cf</sub> on ScienceWorld; adding it substantially improves Pass@128, proving counterfactual learning’s necessity.  
3. **Effectiveness of Stage 1 Sampling** (Sec 4.3, Table 3): On CodeContests, compare independent sampling, iterative refinement, and LEAFE’s rollback branching; rollback yields highest Pass@128, demonstrating efficient feedback‑guided exploration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How to handle weak, delayed, or ambiguous environment feedback?** The authors note LEAFE’s benefits may diminish under such conditions; adaptation via temporal credit assignment or hierarchical abstraction is needed.
- **How to perform rollback in non‑deterministic or stateful environments where exact reset is impossible?** The paper assumes deterministic resettability; real‑world settings (e.g., robotics) require approximate methods like world models.
- **How does the quality of self‑generated rollback points and experience summaries affect performance, and can it be improved?** No analysis of reflection accuracy; human evaluation or correctness‑rewarded training could help.

## Limitations
- Reflection prompt design (few‑shots, templates) is unspecified but critical for τ/*e* quality.
- Tree expansion hyperparameters (max depth, branching factor, budget) are missing, hindering resource‑controlled reproduction.
- Robustness to stochastic environments is untested; exact rollback assumption may not hold generally.
- Distillation hyperparameter β is unreported; imbalance risks forgetting or noisy learning.
- Context length management for long histories is not addressed, though long‑horizon tasks may exceed capacity.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (reflective backtracking): Medium** – plausible but depends on reflection quality and deterministic env; no prompt ablation.
- **Mechanism 2 (distillation internalization): Medium** – β tuning and counterfactual noise risks not quantified.
- **Mechanism 3 (distribution sharpening mitigation): Low‑Medium** – inferred from Pass@k curves; lacks direct entropy/coverage measurements.
