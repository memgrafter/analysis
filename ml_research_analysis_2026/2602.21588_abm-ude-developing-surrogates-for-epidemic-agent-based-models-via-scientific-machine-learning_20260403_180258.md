# ABM-UDE: Developing Surrogates for Epidemic Agent-Based Models via Scientific Machine Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21588
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21588
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Combining multiple shooting and observer-based prediction-error correction (PEM) with a neural-parameterized Universal Differential Equation reduces mean squared error by 77% relative to single-shooting, yields near-perfect coverage of ABM uncertainty bands, and achieves ~10⁴× speedup for nightly hospital planning.

## Executive Summary
Agent-based epidemic models (ABMs) are essential for capturing complex stochastic transmission dynamics but are computationally prohibitive for rapid "what-if" analyses needed in operational planning. This paper introduces a surrogate modeling framework based on Universal Differential Equations (UDEs) that embeds a neural network to parameterize the time-varying contact rate within a mechanistic SEIR ODE. Stabilization techniques—multiple shooting to shorten gradient paths and an observer-based prediction-error method (PEM) to anchor predictions—preserve physical constraints and yield accurate, calibrated forecasts. Evaluated on a representative exascale ABM scenario, the combined MS+PEM-UDE reduces MSE by 77% compared to single-shooting and improves uncertainty calibration, while cutting inference time from hours to seconds (~10⁴× speedup). This enables nightly scenario sweeps to support hospital capacity planning.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a UDE where the unknown contact rate is replaced by a bounded neural function `κ_φ(u,t) = κ_max·σ(N_φ(ũ))` that depends on normalized living compartment states (excluding susceptible by mass balance). The mechanistic SEIR ODE structure ensures positivity and conservation of total population. Training from ABM trajectories employs two stabilization strategies: (1) Multiple shooting partitions the time horizon into windows, optimizes per-window initial states, and adds a continuity penalty to mitigate error accumulation across long or discontinuous trajectories; (2) A prediction-error method augments the dynamics with a linear observer correction `K(y-Hu)` and projects each integration step onto the feasible simplex to maintain physical viability while anchoring to ABM data. The combined method is trained via Adam followed by LBFGS, with regularization on observer gains and continuity. The resulting surrogate can be integrated on commodity CPUs in seconds for ~90-day forecasts, offering orders of magnitude speedup over ABMs while preserving interpretability and uncertainty.

## Key Results
- Combined MS+PEM-UDE reduces mean squared error to 2.93, a 77% improvement over single-shooting UDE (13.14) and 20% over MS-UDE (3.75).
- Empirical coverage of ABM 10–90% and 25–75% quantile bands reaches 0.94 and 0.69, indicating well-calibrated uncertainty.
- Inference completes in 20–35 seconds per ~90-day forecast on an Apple M4 MacBook Air, a ~10⁴× speedup over the original ABM.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### 1. Bounded Neural Contact Rate Inside Mechanistic Core
Replacing the unknown contact rate with `κ_φ(u,t) = κ_max·σ(N_φ(ũ))` inside the SEIR ODE preserves physical constraints (positivity, mass conservation) while enabling the surrogate to learn time-varying transmission changes driven by behavioral regime shifts. Because only the scalar coefficient in the infection term is learned, the vector field remains Lipschitz and forward-invariant on the feasible set `X_N`. Core assumption: the dominant missing dynamics relative to the ABM are captured by a bounded, state-dependent contact rate modulation. Evidence: [abstract] notes preservation of mechanistic interpretability; [section 3.4] states that this preserves biological structure and conservation laws. Break condition: insufficient neural capacity or restrictive `κ_max` may fail to capture rapid intervention effects, causing bias around change-points.

### 2. Multiple Shooting Stabilization
Partitioning the trajectory into windows and optimizing per-window initial states with a soft continuity penalty reduces the effective backpropagation horizon, mitigating compounding numerical errors and improving robustness to abrupt regime shifts (e.g., interventions). The continuity penalty `L_cont` softly enforces smooth transitions between windows. Core assumption: error accumulation over long horizons is the primary instability in single-shooting. Evidence: abstract reports that MS+PEM-UDE reduces MSE by 77% relative to single-shooting. Break condition: window size too small risks under-constrained optimization and artificial kinks; too large recovers single-shooting instability. Inappropriate `λ_MS` may over-smooth genuine discontinuities.

### 3. Observer-Based Prediction-Error Correction
Augmenting the UDE with an additive correction term `du/dt = f_θ(u,t) + K(y-Hu)` anchors the state trajectory toward the ABM ensemble mean during integration. A projection `Π_X_N` onto the feasible set ensures physical plausibility after each step. Core assumption: the ABM ensemble mean provides a valid target, and a regularized linear correction can reduce cumulative error without destabilizing the system. Evidence: abstract cites enhanced uncertainty calibration (coverage 0.94/0.69); section 3.5.3 describes the observer and projection. Break condition: overly large or poorly regularized gain `K` can cause oscillations or constraint violations; too small a gain yields insufficient anchoring. High ABM stochasticity may reduce effectiveness.

## Foundational Learning

- **ODE forward invariance and constraint preservation**  
  *Why needed:* The UDE must maintain non-negative compartment counts and constant total population `S+E+I~s+Is+Ia+R+D = N` for biologically plausible forecasts. Training and integration use reparameterization and projection to enforce these constraints.  
  *Quick check:* For the SEIR RHS (Eq. 11), if `u(0) ∈ X_N` and `κ(t) ∈ [0,κ_max]`, prove `u(t) ∈ X_N` ∀t without PEM. How does adding `K(y-Hu)` potentially break invariance, and why is projection necessary?

- **System identification via multiple shooting and prediction error methods**  
  *Why needed:* Single-shooting backpropagation through long stiff ODE trajectories with neural parameters is numerically unstable, especially across intervention-induced discontinuities. MS and PEM are classical control-theoretic stabilizers adapted for scientific ML.  
  *Quick check:* What is the trade-off between the MS continuity penalty weight `λ_MS` and the risk of over-smoothing genuine intervention-induced discontinuities? How does PEM differ from MS in *when* it corrects errors (boundary vs. continuous)?

- **Ensemble-based model uncertainty and coverage metrics**  
  *Why needed:* Operational decisions require calibrated uncertainty, not just point accuracy. The paper evaluates coverage of ABM-generated quantile bands by UDE ensemble trajectories, quantifying how often UDE uncertainty intervals contain the ABM reference.  
  *Quick check:* Distinguish between the two sources of ensembles: (1) ABM stochastic replicates forming reference quantiles `y^(q)(t_k)`, and (2) UDE training ensembles from random initializations forming `û(t_k) ± σ`. Why must coverage be evaluated against the *ABM* ensemble, not the UDE's own spread?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  ABM trajectories → normalization & mass balance → choose UDE variant (MS/PEM) → train (φ, θ, [K], {z_i}) via ADAM+LBFGS with stiff solver and constraint projection → ensemble training (100 seeds) → validation on ABM hold-out / quantile coverage → deploy for rapid forward simulation.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Set up ODE system with neural `κ_φ` and observation model.  
  2. Implement training for Vanilla UDE (single-shooting) to establish baseline.  
  3. Add multiple shooting: partition time into windows, parameterize `z_i`, add continuity penalty.  
  4. Add PEM: define observer correction `K(y-Hu)`, projection `Π_X_N`, gain regularization.  
  5. Combine MS+PEM: apply PEM within each window plus continuity.  
  6. Train ensemble of 100 seeds per method; evaluate MSE and coverage.  
  7. Deploy trained UDE for CPU inference.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **MS window size (`m`):** Smaller windows improve stability near interventions but increase boundary parameters, risking underconstraint; larger windows reduce this benefit.  
  - **PEM gain (`K`):** Diagonal `K` (often `D·H^T`) limits overfitting and maintains interpretability but may be less responsive. Regularization (`λ_K`, `λ_sp`) balances responsiveness and overfitting.  
  - **Neural capacity:** 3×10 Swish network used; deeper/larger networks may capture more complexity but increase instability; smaller may underfit.  
  - **Ensemble size:** 100 seeds used for robust statistics; fewer seeds risk noisy metrics, more increase compute cost.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High MSE post-intervention (especially for Vanilla UDE) indicates inability to capture regime shift; visible drift after day 49.  
  - Poor coverage (<0.8 for 25–75% band) suggests mis-specified uncertainty; under-dispersion often from overfitting or insufficient `K`, over-dispersion from excessive `K` or sparse training.  
  - Solver failures or `NaN` states violated Lipschitz conditions (e.g., `κ_φ` spikes) or projection failed; check bounds on `κ_φ` and solver tolerances.  
  - Slow convergence (>50 epochs without improvement) may indicate poor `z_i` init, inappropriate `K` init, or learning rate too low.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline replication:** Train Vanilla UDE on the ABM dataset (89 days, normalized compartments). Expect MSE ~13–17, ensemble drift after intervention.  
  2. **MS sensitivity sweep:** Train MS-UDE with window sizes `m ∈ {7,14,21}` days and continuity weights `λ_MS ∈ {1e2,1e3,1e4}`. Monitor MSE and `L_cont` to identify best trade-off.  
  3. **PEM gain ablation:** Train PEM-UDE (with or without MS) under different `K` structures (diagonal vs. full) and regularization strengths `λ_K ∈ {0,1e-4,1e-2}`. Evaluate coverage and per-compartment MSE to assess calibration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Does the ABM→UDE pipeline generalize across diverse intervention regimes and ABM configurations beyond the single scenario evaluated? The current study focuses on one ExaEpi scenario due to computational cost; extending to multiple scenarios is needed to establish robustness to varying policies, population structures, and model implementations.

### Open Question 2
How accurately can the UDE surrogate be trained from partially observed epidemiological data (e.g., hospital admissions, ICU occupancy) instead of full compartment counts? Real-world data are incomplete and noisy; the present assumption of full observability is unrealistic. Training under a realistic observation operator `H` and noise model is an important next step.

### Open Question 3
What observer gain matrix structure (diagonal vs. full) and regularization yield the best trade-off between responsiveness and stability in PEM-UDE? The paper uses a diagonal gain for stability but richer structures might improve accuracy if carefully regularized. Systematic ablations under equalized training budgets are required to determine the optimal gain structure.

## Limitations

- The study evaluates only one proprietary ExaEpi scenario without public data or code, limiting reproducibility and generalizability of the reported improvements.
- Hyperparameter values (e.g., `λ_MS`, `λ_K`, window sizes, solver tolerances) are not fully disclosed, raising concerns about tuning bias and the ability to exactly replicate results.
- Confidence in the substantial MSE reduction and coverage gains is **Medium** because they are based on a single dataset; cross-scenario validation is needed. Inference speedup is **High** confidence due to direct benchmarks.

## Confidence

- **Accuracy and calibration improvements** (77% MSE reduction, coverage 0.94/0.69): **Medium** confidence (single-scenario evidence, hyperparameter tuning not fully disclosed).
- **Inference speedup** (~10⁴×): **High** confidence (direct timing measurements on commodity hardware).
- **Theoretical mechanisms** (constraint preservation, stabilization benefits): **Medium** confidence (plausible and partially evidenced, but not validated across varied conditions).

## Next Checks

1. **Full replication:** Reproduce exact results using released code and data (once available), including hyperparameter sweeps and ensemble training.
2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Systematically vary key settings (`λ_MS`, `λ_K`, window size, neural capacity) to confirm robustness and identify optimal ranges.
3. **Cross-scenario testing:** Apply the MS+PEM-UDE pipeline to at least two additional ABM scenarios with different intervention schedules and parameters to assess generalizability of accuracy and calibration.
