# General Proximal Flow Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.00751  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00751  
- **Reference count**: 22  
- **Primary result**: A Gaussian GPFN using squared Wasserstein distance (W²) significantly outperforms the standard BFN baseline on MNIST, achieving aFID 67 at NFE=20 compared to BFN-stoch's 1513.

## Executive Summary
Bayesian Flow Networks (BFNs) rely on fixed KL‑divergence‑based belief updates, which may not align with the geometry of certain data types. General Proximal Flow Networks (GPFNs) generalize this framework by replacing the divergence with an arbitrary distance function \(D\), framing each update as a proximal optimization step. This flexibility allows the use of divergences like the squared Wasserstein distance, which better matches image data geometries. On MNIST, a Gaussian GPFN with \(W_2^2\) drastically outperforms the BFN baseline: at NFE=20, GPFN‑det attains an aFID of 67 versus BFN‑stoch's 1513, and even at NFE=5, GPFN’s aFID (166) surpasses BFN at NFE=100 (aFID 919). The GPFN also achieves superior Sliced Wasserstein Distance and maintains high Precision/Recall, demonstrating better sample quality and diversity with far fewer steps.

## Method Summary
GPFNs extend Bayesian Flow Networks by formulating the belief update as a proximal step: \(p_{t+1} = \arg\min_p \left[ F_t(p, q_{t+1}) + \frac{1}{\eta_t} D(p, p_t) \right]\), where \(F_t\) measures fidelity to the target \(q_{t+1}\) and \(D\) is any divergence or distance. Setting \(D\) to KL and \(F_t\) to expected negative log‑likelihood recovers the standard BFN update. The paper instantiates a Gaussian GPFN with \(D = W_2^2\) and Dirac targets, yielding closed‑form mean/variance updates that coincide with the Euler step of Rectified Flow. Training uses a U‑Net predictor \(F_\theta\) that takes a noisy sample \(x_t \sim p_t\) and time \(t/T\) to predict \(\hat{x}_0\); the loss is mean squared error between \(\hat{x}_0\) and the true data \(x_0\). Sampling can be deterministic (direct update) or stochastic (adding noise to preserve diversity). Baselines include BFN with stochastic and deterministic samplers, the latter applying the same \(W_2^2\) update to BFN’s predictions.

## Key Results
- At NFE=20, GPFN‑det achieves aFID 67 vs BFN‑stoch’s 1513; even at NFE=5, GPFN’s aFID 166 surpasses BFN at NFE=100 (aFID 919).  
- GPFN attains superior Sliced Wasserstein Distance and maintains high Precision/Recall across NFE budgets, indicating both quality and diversity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Each belief update in a GPFN is a proximal optimization step that balances fidelity to a target signal and proximity to the current belief using an arbitrary divergence or distance function \(D\).
- **Mechanism**: The update is defined as \(p_{t+1} = \arg\min_{p} \left[ F_t(p, q_{t+1}) + \frac{1}{\eta_t} D(p, p_t) \right]\), where \(F_t\) measures discrepancy to the target \(q_{t+1}\) and \(\eta_t\) is a step size. This is exactly a proximal‑point method applied in the space of probability distributions.
- **Core assumption**: The chosen \(D\) yields a well‑posed and computationally tractable minimization (e.g., closed‑form or efficiently solvable).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] “GPFNs replace this fixed choice with an arbitrary divergence or distance function, such as the Wasserstein distance, yielding a unified proximal‑operator framework for iterative generative modeling.”
  - [section 2] “The belief is advanced from \(p_t\) to \(p_{t+1}\) by solving a regularized optimization problem … This is precisely the structure of a proximal‑point step from convex optimization.”
  - [corpus] Neighbor paper “Convergence of flow‑based generative models via proximal gradient descent in Wasserstein space” (arxiv:2310.17582) similarly connects flow models to proximal steps in Wasserstein space, reinforcing the general proximal viewpoint.
- **Break condition**: If \(D\) is not lower‑semicontinuous or \(F_t\) is not proper, the infimum may not be attained; if the minimizer is non‑unique or intractable, the update becomes ambiguous or computationally prohibitive.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Setting \(D\) to the Kullback‑Leibler divergence and the fidelity functional to an expected negative log‑likelihood recovers exactly the Bayesian posterior update used in standard Bayesian Flow Networks.
- **Mechanism**: With \(D(p\|p_t) = \mathrm{KL}(p\|p_t)\), \(F_t(p,q) = -\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p}[\ln L(x|y_{t+1})]\) and \(\eta_t=1\), the minimizer satisfies \(p_{t+1}(x) \propto p_t(x) L(x|y_{t+1})\), i.e., Bayes’ rule.
- **Core assumption**: The likelihood \(L(x|y)\) is defined and conjugate to the prior family \(p_t\); the step‑size parameterization matches (\(\eta_t=1\)).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 2] “When \(D(p, p_t) = \mathrm{KL}(p\|p_t)\) and \(F_t\) is the expected negative log‑likelihood … setting \(\eta_t = 1\) yields the closed‑form minimizer \(p_{t+1}(x)\propto p_t(x)L(x|y_{t+1})\). This exactly recovers the Bayesian posterior update of standard BFNs.”
  - [abstract] “recovering the standard BFN update as a special case.”
- **Break condition**: If the likelihood is not conjugate to the prior family, the closed form may not exist; the specialization relies on the specific forward process that guarantees conjugacy in BFNs.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Using the squared Wasserstein distance \(W_2^2\) as \(D\) with a Dirac target induces an optimal‑transport‑based update whose particle dynamics exactly match the Euler step of Rectified Flow, enabling high‑quality generation in few steps.
- **Mechanism**: For a Gaussian belief \(p_t = \mathcal{N}(m_t, v_t I)\) and target \(q = \delta_{x_0}\), the minimizer of \(W_2^2(p, q) + \frac{1}{\eta_t} W_2^2(p, p_t)\) is Gaussian with mean \(m_{t+1} = \tau_t x_0 + (1-\tau_t)m_t\) and variance \(v_{t+1} = (1-\tau_t)^2 v_t\), \(\tau_t = \eta_t/(1+\eta_t)\). The particle update \(x_{t+1} = \tau_t \hat{x}_0 + (1-\tau_t) x_t\) (with \(x_t \sim p_t\)) coincides with Rectified Flow’s Euler step.
- **Core assumption**: The belief is Gaussian with isotropic covariance and the target is a Dirac; the squared Wasserstein distance between such Gaussians admits a closed‑form expression that yields the barycenter.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 2] “When \(D=W_2^2\) … equation (1) computes the Wasserstein barycenter between the current belief \(p_t\) and the clean data. The solution corresponds exactly to McCann’s displacement interpolation, moving \(p_t\) along the optimal transport geodesic towards \(\delta_{x_0}\) with an effective step size \(\tau_t = \eta_t/(1+\eta_t)\).”
  - [appendix A.1] “The \(W_2\) proximal step … induces an optimal transport map on the particles … this particle update perfectly coincides with the Euler integration step of Rectified Flow.”
  - [abstract] “On MNIST, a Gaussian GPFN using the squared Wasserstein distance (W²) significantly outperforms the standard BFN baseline.”
- **Break condition**: If the belief deviates from Gaussian or the target is not a Dirac, the closed form generally fails; computing the Wasserstein barycenter becomes a high‑dimensional optimization that may be intractable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Proximal point algorithms**
  - **Why needed here**: GPFN’s entire update mechanism is a proximal step; understanding proximal operators clarifies how the divergence \(D\) shapes the geometry of belief updates.
  - **Quick check question**: Given a functional \(f(p)\) and divergence \(D\), write the proximal operator of \(f\) at \(p_t\) with step size \(\eta\). (Answer: \(\arg\min_p \left[ f(p) + \frac{1}{\eta} D(p, p_t) \right]\).)

- **Concept: Bayesian posterior updates**
  - **Why needed here**: The BFN special case is a direct application of Bayes’ rule; this connection grounds GPFN in classical probabilistic inference.
  - **Quick check question**: For a prior \(p_t(x)\) and likelihood \(L(x|y)\), what is the posterior \(p_{t+1}(x)\)? (Answer: \(p_{t+1}(x) \propto p_t(x) L(x|y)\).)

- **Concept: Optimal transport and Wasserstein barycenters**
  - **Why needed here**: The \(W_2\)‑GPFN update computes a Wasserstein barycenter; this geometric perspective explains its alignment with image data and its link to Rectified Flow.
  - **Quick check question**: For two isotropic Gaussians \(\mathcal{N}(m_1, v_1 I)\) and \(\mathcal{N}(m_2, v_2 I)\) in \(\mathbb{R}^d\), what is the squared Wasserstein distance? (Answer: \(\|m_1-m_2\|^2 + d\,(\sqrt{v_1}-\sqrt{v_2})^2\).)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Belief state \((m_t, v_t)\) → Sample \(x_t \sim \mathcal{N}(m_t, v_t I)\) → Neural predictor \(F_\theta(x_t, t/T)\) → \(\hat{x}_0\) → Proximal update \(U\) → New belief state \((m_{t+1}, v_{t+1})\). Training additionally computes loss \(D(\hat{x}_0, x_0)\) and backpropagates to \(F_\theta\); sampling iterates until \(T\) and then draws final samples from \(p_T\).
- **Critical path**:
  - **Forward**: \(x_t \sim p_t \xrightarrow{F_\theta} \hat{x}_0 \xrightarrow{U} p_{t+1}\).
  - **Loss**: Compare \(\hat{x}_0\) to true \(x_0\) (or true target) using \(D\).
  - **Backward**: Gradients through \(F_\theta\) only; the belief trajectory is detached.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Choice of \(D\)**: KL gives simple updates but may not match data geometry; \(W_2\) yields better sample quality but restricts belief to Gaussians and requires Dirac targets for closed form.
  - **Belief parametrization**: Gaussian is tractable for \(W_2\); richer families (e.g., mixtures) increase representational capacity but make the proximal step potentially intractable.
  - **Step‑size schedule \(\eta_t\)**: Controls convergence speed and final sample quality; the cosine schedule for \(\gamma_t\) (related to variance) prevents singularities near \(t=0\).
  - **Stochastic vs deterministic sampling**: Stochastic (Ornstein‑Uhlenbeck) adds principled noise to avoid mode collapse; deterministic is faster but may miss modes if the forward process is not straight.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Numerical instability**: Variance becomes negative or near zero; ensure variance parameterization stays positive (e.g., \(\gamma_t^2\) with \(\gamma_t>0\)).
  - **Mode collapse**: As with BFN‑det, deterministic updates using BFN’s forward process yield near‑zero Recall and Diversity (all samples identical). Monitor Precision/Recall and Intra‑set Diversity.
  - **Divergence mismatch**: Poor choice of \(D\) degrades sample quality slowly; watch aFID and SWD across NFE budgets.
  - **Training instability**: Loss does not decrease; verify that the belief trajectory is generated with true targets (not predictions) and that gradient clipping/EMA are used.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Closed‑form verification**: Manually compute the \(W_2\) proximal update for a simple Gaussian belief and Dirac target; check that code output matches the formulas \(m_{t+1} = \tau_t x_0 + (1-\tau_t)m_t\), \(v_{t+1} = (1-\tau_t)^2 v_t\).
  2. **Sampler comparison**: On MNIST, compare GPFN‑det and GPFN‑stoch at NFE=20; expect aFID within 10% of each other and Intra‑set Diversity >0.35.
  3. **Step‑size ablation**: Replace the cosine schedule for \(\gamma_t\) with a linear schedule \(\gamma_t = 1-t/T\); train a GPFN and evaluate aFID at NFE=20 – it should degrade noticeably (e.g., >100) compared to the cosine schedule (~67).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
#### Open Question 1
- **Question**: For a given dataset, what systematic method can be used to select the optimal proximal divergence D (e.g., Wasserstein, KL, others) to maximize generation quality and efficiency?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors emphasize that "adapting the divergence to the underlying data geometry yields measurable improvements" and demonstrate superior results with W² on MNIST, but provide no general guidance for choosing D.
- **Why unresolved**: The framework allows arbitrary D, but no theoretical or empirical principles are offered for matching D to data characteristics, leaving practitioners without a clear strategy.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A comprehensive study evaluating GPFN performance across multiple datasets (e.g., image, text, audio) and divergences, identifying correlations between data properties (e.g., modality, dimensionality, geometry) and optimal D.

#### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Should the divergence used in the training loss (equation 2) be the same as the proximal divergence in the update operator (equation 1), and how does this choice affect sample quality and training stability?
- **Basis in paper**: In the Gaussian GPFN experiments, the training loss uses pixel-wise MSE while the proximal update uses W² distance, yet the paper does not discuss whether this mismatch is necessary or optimal.
- **Why unresolved**: The formulation uses the same symbol D for both, but the implementation diverges, raising questions about design principles and potential conflicts between learning signals and belief dynamics.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Controlled ablations where the training loss divergence is varied independently of the proximal divergence (e.g., KL vs. W² in both roles), measuring impacts on metrics like aFID, precision/recall, and convergence.

#### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can GPFNs be extended beyond axis-aligned Gaussian beliefs to more expressive families (e.g., mixture models, normalizing flows), and what algorithms are needed to solve the proximal update (1) efficiently for general D?
- **Basis in paper**: The paper restricts beliefs to Gaussians for closed-form updates, noting that other choices would require solving optimization problems, but does not explore feasibility or performance.
- **Why unresolved**: Gaussian beliefs may limit modeling capacity for complex, multi-modal data distributions; the framework's generality is untested beyond this simple case.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Implementations of GPFN with non-Gaussian beliefs (e.g., using implicit distributions or variational inference) and evaluations on datasets with known multi-modality, comparing sample quality and computational cost.

#### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Does the performance advantage of GPFNs over BFNs persist on more complex, high-dimensional datasets like CIFAR-10 or ImageNet, and what are the scaling properties in terms of NFE, model size, and training cost?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors highlight "state-of-the-art performance at low NFE" on MNIST but only evaluate on this single, low-resolution dataset, limiting claims about generalizability.
- **Why unresolved**: MNIST is relatively simple; performance on richer datasets with higher entropy and structure remains unknown, which is critical for practical adoption.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Replication of the MNIST experimental protocol on standard benchmarks (CIFAR-10, ImageNet 32x32/64x64), reporting metrics across NFE budgets and analyzing computational efficiency.

#### Open Question 5
- **Question**: What are the theoretical convergence and stability guarantees for GPFNs when using arbitrary divergences D, and how do they relate to properties like sample diversity and mode coverage?
- **Basis in paper**: The paper establishes a formal link to proximal-point methods but does not provide convergence analysis for general D, instead focusing on the W² case where updates coincide with Rectified Flow.
- **Why unresolved**: Without theory, it is unclear why certain D (like W²) lead to better performance (e.g., avoiding mode collapse) or how step-size schedules should be theoretically derived.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Mathematical analysis of the proximal update operator for a class of divergences, proving convergence to the data distribution under assumptions, and empirical validation of predicted behaviors (e.g., diversity metrics vs. step size).

## Limitations
- The GPFN framework requires the divergence \(D\) to be well‑posed and yield a tractable proximal step; if \(D\) is not lower‑semicontinuous or \(F_t\) improper, the update may be ill‑defined or intractable.
- The demonstrated superiority of \(W_2^2\) is restricted to Gaussian beliefs with Dirac targets, limiting generalizability to complex data distributions or more expressive belief families.
- Critical implementation details such as BFN’s discrete‑time forward schedule and loss discretization are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
