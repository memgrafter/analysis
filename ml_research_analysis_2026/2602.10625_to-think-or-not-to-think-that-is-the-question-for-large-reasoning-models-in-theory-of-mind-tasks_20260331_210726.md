# To Think or Not To Think, That is The Question for Large Reasoning Models in Theory of Mind Tasks
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10625
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10625
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Reasoning models do not always outperform non-reasoning models on ToM tasks; they suffer from slow thinking collapse and option matching shortcuts, but interventions like S2F and T2M can yield significant gains.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a systematic comparison of nine large language models—both reasoning and non-reasoning—across three Theory of Mind benchmarks: HiToM (hierarchical belief reasoning), ToMATO (dialogue-based), and ToMBench (broad mental-state coverage). The key finding is that reasoning models frequently underperform relative to non-reasoning counterparts, particularly on complex tasks. Two failure modes are identified: slow thinking collapse, where extended reasoning degrades accuracy; and option matching shortcut, where models rely on superficial pattern-matching against provided choices instead of genuine deduction. To address these, the authors propose two interventions: Slow-to-Fast (S2F), which dynamically limits reasoning length, and Think-to-Match (T2M), which removes options during the thinking phase. Experiments show that S2F improves performance on HiToM (e.g., R1-Distill-Qwen-32B +22.8%) while T2M boosts reasoning model accuracy dramatically (DeepSeek-R1 from 0.549 to 0.691). The results highlight that reasoning strategies successful in formal reasoning are counterproductive for ToM, indicating the need for specialized social reasoning mechanisms.

## Method Summary
Evaluation uses three ToM benchmarks with multiple-choice format. Nine models are tested, including DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-8B-Reasoning, GPT-o3 and their non-reasoning equivalents. Baselines run with CoT prompts; for reasoning models, S2F and T2M are applied. S2F monitors generation for “wait” tokens (a proxy for internal reasoning steps) and injects a preset sentence after three occurrences to truncate reasoning. T2M combines S2F with option removal: the prompt omits multiple-choice choices; the options are only injected after the S2F trigger or when the model emits ``. Accuracy is the primary metric, computed overall and per reasoning order or mental-state category.

## Key Results
- Reasoning models do not consistently outperform non-reasoning models; on complex ToM tasks they often perform worse.
- Two failure modes: (1) slow thinking collapse—accuracy drops as reasoning length increases on HiToM; (2) option matching shortcut—models exploit answer choices instead of deducing from first principles.
- Interventions: S2F (adaptive length limiting) yields large gains on HiToM (e.g., R1-Distill-Qwen-32B +22.8%); T2M (removing options during reasoning) improves reasoning model accuracy on HiToM (DeepSeek-R1 0.549→0.691).
- Reasoning and non-reasoning models show minimal overlap in correct answers on hard HiToM items, indicating complementary strengths.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Slow thinking collapse
Prolonged reasoning in ambiguous social scenarios introduces noise and perspective drift because intermediate mental-state inferences are weakly verifiable. Extending chain length overwrites correct early intuitions, especially on high-order (e.g., 4th) and deceptive tasks.

### Option matching shortcut
Multiple-choice options provide a strong prior, leading models to reverse-engineer justifications that match surface patterns rather than building a causal model of mental states. Removing options eliminates the shortcut, forcing genuine deduction.

### Adaptive reasoning
Performance improves when reasoning effort is moderate and instance-specific. Simple tasks may not need extensive deliberation; complex tasks require some but too much causes collapse. S2F and T2M implement this adaptive balance.

## Foundational Learning

### Theory of Mind taxonomies and orders
Mental states (belief, desire, intention, knowledge) and recursion depth (0th–4th+ order) create a complexity gradient. Failure modes vary: collapse worsens with order; the option shortcut primarily affects complex belief tracking.

### Dual-process theory (System 1 / System 2)
Reasoning models approximate System 2 (slow, deliberative) while non-reasoning models approximate System 1 (fast, pattern-matching). Pure System 2 fails (collapse) and pure System 1 lacks structured deduction; a hybrid strategy is needed.

### Benchmark design pitfalls
Multiple-choice formats enable the option matching shortcut. Designing open-ended ToM tasks or removing options during evaluation better discriminates true reasoning.

## Architecture Onboarding
Prompt Engineering Layer -> Generation Controller (monitors "wait" tokens or ``; applies S2F/T2M logic) -> Token Budget Manager (max_new_tokens=2048) -> Evaluator (accuracy per item)

Critical path:
- Determine task complexity (e.g., HiToM order >2). If high, enable S2F/T2M.
- For T2M, construct prompt without options; stream tokens; at "wait" count ≥3, inject options and continue.
- After generation ends, extract answer (option letter) and compare to ground truth.

Design tradeoffs:
- S2F threshold: too low cuts necessary reasoning; too high risks collapse. Must be tuned per benchmark.
- T2M removes options, which may misalign when the correct answer is not among the injected choices (open-ended settings); best kept for multiple-choice.
- Interventions increase generation complexity and may require injection handling, raising code and compute costs.

Failure signatures:
- Length‑accuracy: error cluster beyond ~8000 characters on HiToM.
- Option removal Δ: accuracy increase >0.05 for reasoning models on HiToM indicates heavy shortcut reliance.
- Complementary sets: low Jaccard index (<0.3) between reasoning and non-reasoning models on hard orders.

First three experiments:
1. Length profiling: bin HiToM responses by character count; compute acc per bin.
2. Option ablation: run baseline vs T2M on HiToM; record Δ for reasoning vs non-reasoning models.
3. S2F threshold sweep: vary "wait" count (2,3,4) on HiToM and ToMATO; identify optimal threshold.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Could adaptive control mechanisms (e.g., learned rather than heuristic-based) improve ToM reasoning?
- How do reasoning failures manifest in open-ended ToM tasks (non-multiple-choice)?
- Do the identified failure modes extend to other social reasoning domains beyond ToM?
- What model architectures or training objectives might foster genuine Theory of Mind capabilities?

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility**: missing prompt templates, GPT “reasoning effort” parameter details, and exact “wait” token handling require assumptions.
- **Scope**: only three ToM benchmarks used; generalization to other tasks and open-ended formats uncertain.
- **Model coverage**: nine models tested; newer architectures may differ.

## Confidence
- Reasoning underperformance: High
- Slow thinking collapse: Medium
- Option matching shortcut: High
- S2F intervention efficacy: High
- T2M intervention efficacy: Medium
