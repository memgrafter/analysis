# WestWorld: A Knowledge-Encoded Scalable Trajectory World Model for Diverse Robotic Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.14392  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14392  
- **Reference Count**: 40  
- **Primary Result**: WestWorld introduces a System-aware Mixture-of-Experts (Sys-MoE) architecture with structural embeddings to achieve state-of-the-art zero-shot trajectory prediction and robust scaling across diverse robots, reducing MAE by up to 40.9% and improving real-world control reward by 10.2×.

## Executive Summary
WestWorld tackles the poor scalability and zero-shot generalization of existing trajectory world models. It proposes a System-aware Mixture-of-Experts (Sys-MoE) backbone that dynamically routes tokens to specialized experts based on a learnable system embedding derived from robot morphology via LCRS structural embeddings. Pre-trained on 89 environments, it significantly lowers prediction error on unseen robots (Walker2d, Hopper, Franka) compared to TrajWorld and maintains stable performance as pre-training diversity grows. In real-world model-based control (MPPI) on a Unitree Go1, it achieves over 10× higher accumulated reward, enabling stable locomotion. This work moves closer to a general-purpose, reusable world model for diverse robotic systems.

## Method Summary
WestWorld treats trajectory prediction as a sequence-to-sequence task. Input consists of partial state trajectories and a robot’s morphological description. The morphology is encoded through a Linearized Tree Canonical Form (LCRS) string, which is processed by a structural encoder to produce a structural embedding. This embedding is transformed into a system embedding that conditions a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) transformer. In each MoE layer, a router uses the system embedding to assign tokens to one of several experts, allowing morphology-specific processing. The output head discretizes future states into 256 bins and predicts categorical distributions. Training uses cross-entropy loss. This decoupling of shared dynamics and morphology-specific factors enables zero-shot transfer.

## Key Results
- Zero-shot MAE reduction: Walker2d -26.6%, Hopper -21.1%, Franka -40.9% versus TrajWorld.  
- Robust scaling: prediction error remains stable as pre-training environments increase from 1 to 30; TrajWorld degrades.  
- Real-world control: MPPI with WestWorld on Unitree Go1 yields 10.2× higher accumulated reward than best baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Sys-MoE architecture dynamically routes tokens to experts based on the system embedding, which captures morphology. This allows the model to maintain a shared backbone for common physical principles while dedicating experts to family-specific dynamics. LCRS structural embeddings provide a permutation-invariant representation of kinematic trees that correlates with behavior patterns. Discretization into bins simplifies optimization and yields natural predictive distributions. Combined with large-scale pre-training, these components give the model strong generalization without retraining.

## Foundational Learning
- **System-aware Mixture-of-Experts (Sys-MoE)**  
  **Why needed**: To allocate model capacity conditionally on robot identity, avoiding interference between morphologies.  
  **Quick check**: Do distinct robots consistently activate disjoint sets of experts across layers?

- **Structural Embedding via LCRS**  
  **Why needed**: To provide a compact, physically meaningful representation of robot morphology for routing.  
  **Quick check**: Does clustering of structural embeddings align with known morphological similarities (e.g., quadrupeds vs. manipulators)?

- **Trajectory Discretization**  
  **Why needed**: To convert regression into classification, easing optimization and yielding calibrated uncertainties.  
  **Quick check**: Are predicted token distributions well-calibrated on held-out trajectories?

- **Zero-shot Generalization**  
  **Why needed**: To avoid per-robot data collection and training, enabling a single model for many robots.  
  **Quick check**: Does performance on unseen robots approach that of models trained specifically on those robots?

- **Model-based Control with MPPI**  
  **Why needed**: To leverage the learned world model for planning without real-world interactions.  
  **Quick check**: Does the model’s multi-step prediction accuracy correlate with control performance?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:
Robot Description -> LCRS Conversion -> Structural Encoder -> System Embedding -> Sys-MoE Transformer -> Categorical Output

Critical path: The robot’s morphological description (e.g., joint tree) is converted to an LCRS string and fed to a structural encoder, producing a structural embedding. This is transformed into a system embedding. For an input trajectory sequence, tokens are embedded and processed by the Sys-MoE transformer; each MoE layer uses the system embedding to route tokens to experts. After expert processing, outputs are combined and passed to the next layer. The final layer projects to categorical distributions over 256 bins per state dimension.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Expert count (P=4)**: Balances specialization and computational overhead; more experts may improve capacity but increase sparsity management cost.
- **Structural embedding complexity**: LCRS plus encoder adds preprocessing but gives morphology-aware routing; simpler embeddings could reduce overhead but lose inductive bias.
- **Discretization resolution (256 bins)**: Affects precision and calibration; coarser bins ease learning but lose detail.
- **System embedding fine-tuning**: Freezing after pre-training preserves stability; fine-tuning may adapt to new morphologies but risks overfitting.

Failure signatures:
- **Routing collapse**: All tokens assigned to a single expert, indicating poor conditioning.
- **High prediction variance within morphology families**: Suggests inadequate structural encoding.
- **Error spikes when adding environments**: Degraded scaling beyond training distribution.
- **Contact event misprediction**: Frequent mismatches leading to unstable MPPI control.

First three experiments:
1. **Baseline replication**: Train WestWorld on 30 environments and compare zero-shot MAE to TrajWorld on Walker2d, Hopper, Franka.
2. **Ablation of structural embedding**: Replace LCRS embedding with random or learned embeddings of equal dimension; measure impact on generalization and routing sparsity.
3. **Expert utilization analysis**: Visualize per-expert activation for different robot families to confirm meaningful specialization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can WestWorld be extended to incorporate visual observations and multi-modal inputs for truly general-purpose world modeling across perception and control?  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The conclusion states: "Despite its remarkable performance, WestWorld currently focuses on trajectory modeling and does not explicitly incorporate visual observations. In the future, we will extend WestWorld into a multimodal world model that fuses both vision and trajectory signals."  
- **Why unresolved**: The paper presents only trajectory-based evaluation. No prototype or experiments demonstrate how to encode images, fuse modalities, or whether visual input actually improves planning compared to pure trajectory modeling. The integration remains an open architectural and training challenge.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A working multimodal extension that ingests both state trajectories and image sequences, showing improved zero-shot generalization or control performance on vision-based robotic tasks, with ablations proving the contribution of visual information.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What are the practical scalability limits of the Sys-MoE design when pretraining across hundreds or thousands of distinct robot morphologies, and does the expert allocation (e.g., 4 experts per layer) remain optimal at extreme scale?  
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper demonstrates stable performance when scaling from 1 to 30 pretraining environments (Fig. 3) and uses 89 total environments. However, the conclusion claims "strong scalability" without defining its boundary, and the number of experts (P=4) is fixed based on a 1% validation slice, not a large-scale stress test.  
- **Why unresolved**: The expert routing and specialization patterns (Fig. 4) are analyzed on only three systems. At larger scale, routing may become less sparse, experts may specialize too narrowly or not enough, and training instability could emerge. The paper provides no scaling law or analysis of expert utilization beyond 30 environments.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic experiments scaling up to hundreds of robot morphologies, measuring validation MAE versus number of robots, analyzing per-expert activation statistics, and possibly adaptive expert allocation or hierarchical routing to maintain performance.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Is the LCRS-based structural embedding necessary and sufficient for encoding robot morphology, or could alternative graph representations (e.g., spectral embeddings, GNNs, or simpler adjacency features) capture the essential physical inductive biases with less complexity?  
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The method uses a specific LCRS transformation with pre/in/post-order traversal indices, justified by the hypothesis that "robots with similar connectivity patterns often exhibit shared high-level dynamical behaviors." However, no alternative morphology encodings are compared, and the paper does not prove that this particular representation optimally captures those shared dynamics.  
- **Why unresolved**: The structural embedding adds noticeable parameters and preprocessing (tree conversion). Simpler alternatives (e.g., binary adjacency, distance matrices, or even learnable per-joint embeddings) might work equally well. The choice may be suboptimal, over-engineered, or sensitive to tree conversion nuances.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A comprehensive ablation study comparing LCRS against several alternative morphology encodings, including no structure, random embeddings, graph neural networks, and spectral methods, with results showing whether the proposed encoding offers a clear, consistent advantage across diverse morphologies.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Does the real-world deployment success on Unitree Go1 transfer to robots with fundamentally different dynamics and contact modes (e.g., manipulators, humanoids), or does the method implicitly overfit to quadruped locomotion?  
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The real-world section ("Real-world deployment on Unitree Go1") reports only one physical robot, despite evaluating on Franka (manipulator) and UR5 (manipulator) in simulation. The paper claims broad applicability but provides no real-world validation beyond quadruped locomotion.  
- **Why unresolved**: The sim-to-real gap for manipulators involves different compliance, friction, and precision requirements. The distilled model’s performance on Go1 may rely on specificities of legged locomotion that don’t generalize. There is no evidence that the same pretrained model could be deployed on a manipulator without substantial re-training or architecture changes.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Successful real-world deployment on at least one manipulator platform (e.g., Franka or UR5) using the same WestWorld framework, with quantitative metrics comparable to simulation results, and analysis of which components (structural embedding, Sys-MoE) were critical for transfer.

### Open Question 5
- **Question**: Does WestWorld produce well-calibrated uncertainty estimates for its multi-step predictions, and can this uncertainty be exploited to improve safety and robustness in model-based control (e.g., risk-sensitive MPPI or fallback strategies)?  
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The model uses categorical predictions over 256 discrete bins, which theoretically allow constructing a predictive distribution. However, the paper reports only point metrics (MAE/MSE) and never discusses calibration, confidence intervals, or out-of-distribution detection. The downstream MPPI uses deterministic rollouts.  
- **Why unresolved**: For real-world deployment, probabilistic predictions are crucial for risk-aware planning. A model that is accurate on average but poorly calibrated could cause catastrophic failures. The paper does not evaluate calibration (e.g., negative log-likelihood, expected calibration error) or test whether high prediction uncertainty correlates with control performance degradation.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Calibration metrics showing that predicted token distributions match empirical frequencies; experiments where MPPI uses the uncertainty (e.g., by sampling from the categorical distribution or penalizing variance) and achieves more robust control, especially under distribution shift or rare events.

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation diversity: Pre-training and zero-shot tests rely on a fixed set of 89 simulated/real environments; generalization to highly novel morphologies remains unproven.
- Real-world validation is confined to one quadruped (Unitree Go1) and a manipulator (Franka) in simulation, with no tests on other platforms like humanoids or aerial vehicles.
- No analysis of training/inference computational costs, which are essential for assessing scalability claims.

## Confidence
- **Zero-shot MAE reduction on benchmark robots**: Medium  
- **Robust scaling with more pre-training environments**: Medium  
- **Real-world control advantage on Unitree Go1**: Low  

## Next Checks
1. **Morphology Generalization Test**: Evaluate WestWorld on at least three entirely novel robot morphologies (e.g., a hexapod, a snake robot, an aerial vehicle) absent from pre-training, measuring zero-shot prediction error and control success.
2. **System Embedding Analysis**: Ablate the structural embedding component and visualize/cluster the learned system embeddings to verify they meaningfully separate robots by morphological features (e.g., joint count, limb topology).
3. **Sys-MoE Routing Study**: For hybrid or unseen systems, log the expert selection patterns and correlation with prediction error to identify whether routing failures drive performance drops.
