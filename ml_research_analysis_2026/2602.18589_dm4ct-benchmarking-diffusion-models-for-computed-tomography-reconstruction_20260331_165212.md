# DM4CT: Benchmarking Diffusion Models for Computed Tomography Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18589
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18589
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Diffusion models achieve higher PSNR/SSIM than classical CT methods but lag behind supervised SwinIR; pixel-space diffusion is more efficient and stable than latent-space, which suffers from artifacts when using only gradient-based data consistency.

## Executive Summary
DM4CT presents a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating diffusion models in computed tomography reconstruction. The study addresses practical challenges—correlated noise, artifacts, geometry dependencies—by systematically comparing ten diffusion-based reconstruction methods against seven established baselines. Evaluation uses controlled medical/industrial CT datasets with varying noise/sparsity and a real high-resolution synchrotron dataset. Results: diffusion outperforms classical methods (PSNR/SSIM) but underperforms supervised SwinIR; pixel-space diffusion is more efficient and stable; latent gradient-only methods produce artifacts; a critical trade-off exists between prior strength and data fidelity. The authors release the dataset and code to foster reproducibility.

## Method Summary
The authors trained separate pixel‑space and latent‑space diffusion models as shared backbones on each dataset (200 epochs, AdamW, LR 1e‑4). Ten diffusion‑based reconstruction algorithms—categorized by data consistency strategy (gradient steering, optimization steps, plug‑and‑play, pseudo‑inverse guidance, variational Bayes)—were evaluated alongside seven baselines (FBP, SIRT, ADMM‑PDTV, FISTA‑SBTV, DIP, INR, R2Gaussian, supervised SwinIR). All used the same forward/backprojection operator defined by consistent CT geometry. Hyperparameters were tuned via grid search on held‑out slices; performance metrics included PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, reconstruction time, and peak GPU memory.

## Key Results
- Diffusion models surpass classical CT methods in PSNR/SSIM but remain inferior to supervised SwinIR; pixel‑space diffusion is more efficient and stable than latent‑space, which produces artifacts with gradient‑only consistency.
- There is a critical trade‑off between prior strength and data fidelity: excessive weighting leads to overfitting and collapse, while too little loses detail.
- Performance degrades significantly on real synchrotron data due to distribution shift and limited training samples; the benchmark dataset and code are publicly released.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Gradient‑based data consistency steering
- **Claim:** Gradient‑based data consistency steering (e.g., DPS) enables diffusion models to incorporate measurement information without fully disrupting the learned prior.
- **Mechanism:** At each diffusion timestep, compute gradient ∇x L(Ax̂₀ − y) on estimated clean image x̂₀, apply update xₜ ← xₜ − η∇x L. This softly nudges sample toward measurement manifold while preserving prior structure.
- **Core assumption:** The gradient provides a locally valid correction direction that minimally interferes with diffusion's denoising trajectory.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] lists "data consistency gradient steering (DC‑grad)" as a reconstruction strategy class. [section 3.1] details gradient‑based methods: "Many methods incorporate a gradient‑based data consistency steering during the reverse denoising steps".

### Mechanism 2: Latent diffusion gradient propagation issues
- **Claim:** Latent diffusion reduces memory/computation but introduces gradient propagation challenges through the VQ‑VAE decoder, causing artifacts.
- **Mechanism:** Perform diffusion in latent space z = E(x). For data consistency, gradients must propagate through decoder D when computing ∇z L(AD(ẑ₀)−y). This through‑model gradient is often less stable than pixel‑space.
- **Core assumption:** Stable gradient flow through discrete latent representations preserves reconstruction quality.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Pixel‑space diffusion is found more efficient than latent‑space, while latent methods relying solely on gradients (e.g., PSLD) produce artifacts." [section 4] "PSLD... produces discontinuities... indicating a structural limitation of gradient‑based enforcement in latent space."

### Mechanism 3: Trade‑off between prior strength and data fidelity
- **Claim:** Optimal CT reconstruction requires balancing prior strength against measurement fidelity; imbalance causes overfitting (noise amplification) or underfitting (loss of detail).
- **Mechanism:** Tune weighting parameter η between prior denoising step and data consistency update. Larger η increases data fidelity but risks overfitting.
- **Core assumption:** An optimal, dataset‑dependent balance exists between prior and likelihood terms in the conditional reverse SDE.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "critical trade‑offs between prior strength and data consistency". [section 4] "Figure 3a illustrates this tradeoff... when η becomes too large, the reconstruction becomes dominated by measurement noise, severely degrading image quality." [section 4] Figure 4 shows null space decomposition: DC‑step methods (ReSample) have smaller null energy than DC‑grad (DPS), reflecting tighter data adherence.

## Foundational Learning
#### Concept 1: Diffusion models as expressive priors
- **Why needed:** Understand that diffusion models learn data distribution p(x) via denoising score matching, providing rich structural priors for ill‑posed CT where y = Ax is underdetermined.
- **Quick check:** If the diffusion model is trained on natural images, why might its prior misalign with CT anatomy?

#### Concept 2: CT forward/backprojection operators
- **Why needed:** Data consistency requires computing Ax and Aᵀ projections efficiently; geometry (sparse views, angles) determines null space size and artifact type.
- **Quick check:** How does increasing projection angle count affect the condition number of AᵀA?

#### Concept 3: Data consistency enforcement strategies
- **Why needed:** Choose between gradient steering (fast, soft), optimization steps (exact but costly), or pseudo‑inverse guidance (data‑aware initialization) based on noise level and hardware constraints.
- **Quick check:** When would a full optimization step (DC‑step) be detrimental compared to gradient stepping?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Input module: Geometry configuration → forward operator A  
Diffusion backbone: Pretrained UNet (pixel) or (VQ‑VAE encode → latent UNet) → score estimator ϵθ  
Data consistency module: Computes L(Ax̂₀ − y) → gradient or optimization update  
SDE solver: DDPM/DDIM integration with DC → reconstructed image x₀

### Critical path
1. Load appropriate pretrained diffusion model (pixel or latent) for your dataset resolution.
2. For t = T‑1 downto 0:
   - Predict ϵₜ₊₁ from xₜ₊₁
   - Estimate clean image x̂₀ via Tweedie or DDPM formula
   - Compute data consistency error L(Ax̂₀ − y)
   - Apply DC update (gradient or optimization)
   - Take denoising step to xₜ
3. Return final x₀.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Pixel vs Latent:** Pixel gives higher fidelity but uses ~10× more GPU memory; latent uses VQ‑VAE compression but may suffer decoder‑induced artifacts.
- **Gradient vs Optimization:** Gradient (DC‑grad) is O(1) per step but soft; optimization (DC‑step) enforces exact fit but requires solving a subproblem and can overfit noise.
- **Prior initialization:** Pseudoinverse‑guided methods (PGDM) blend noise with A†y to start closer to solution but require an approximate A† (e.g., FBP).

### Failure signatures
- **OOM errors:** Latent methods may still OOM on high‑resolution data; reduce batch size or use gradient checkpointing.
- **Ring artifacts persist:** DC strength too weak (η too small) or pseudoinverse approximation biased.
- **Over‑smoothed output:** Excessive DC iterations or too large η in noisy settings amplifies noise.
- **Edge discontinuities:** Latent gradient‑only method (PSLD) fails to propagate through VQ‑VAE decoder.
- **Domain shift collapse:** Poor performance on real synchrotron data despite good synthetic scores → distribution mismatch; consider fine‑tuning on target domain.

### First 3 experiments
1. *Baseline sanity check:* Run FBP and SIRT on one slice to verify geometry and noise level are as expected before expensive diffusion runs.
2. *Hyperparameter sensitivity:* Sweep η for DPS on one slice; locate collapse point where PSNR drops sharply (Figure 3a). Choose η in stable high‑performance region.
3. *Pixel vs latent validation:* Compare DPS (pixel) and PSLD (latent) on same data; if PSLD shows edge artifacts, either increase latent DC iterations or switch to pixel‑space method.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Cross‑system generalization:** How do diffusion‑based reconstruction methods generalize across different CT systems, protocols, and institutions? The benchmark uses only three datasets under controlled conditions; real‑world deployment involves diverse hardware, calibration, and preprocessing that may shift the data distribution.
2. **Optimal prior‑consistency balance:** What is the optimal way to balance prior strength (learned by diffusion models) and data consistency for different noise and sparsity levels? Currently this balance is set by manual hyperparameter tuning; an adaptive mechanism or theoretical guideline is missing.
3. **Latent diffusion artifacts:** Why do latent diffusion models suffer from reconstruction artifacts when relying solely on gradient‑based data consistency, and how can this be mitigated? The exact mechanism (e.g., decoder gradient landscape, latent space conditioning) is not analyzed; alternative latent‑space consistency mechanisms remain underexplored.
4. **Clinical relevance:** Do improvements in pixel‑level reconstruction metrics (PSNR, SSIM) from diffusion models translate into better performance on downstream clinical tasks? The benchmark includes only a preliminary segmentation study and lacks radiologist assessment or organ‑level metrics.

## Limitations
- Data scarcity and domain shift limit real‑world performance; diffusion models trained on limited synthetic CT data underperform on real synchrotron scans.
- Value range misalignment between CT images (normalized to [‑1,1]) and natural image priors may hinder effectiveness.
- Computational overhead from complex geometries and large diffusion models, as well as instability in latent diffusion (edge artifacts), pose practical barriers.

## Confidence
- Diffusion models outperform classical CT methods: High
- Supervised learning (SwinIR) surpasses diffusion models: High
- Gradient steering softly incorporates measurements: Medium
- Latent diffusion artifacts stem from decoder gradient issues: Medium
- Trade‑off between prior strength and data fidelity: High

## Next Checks
1. Fine‑tune diffusion priors on a small real‑CT subset to quantify improvement on real‑world data and assess overfitting risks.
2. Systematically sweep the data consistency weighting parameter η across varying noise and sparsity levels to derive a robust adaptive scheduling rule.
3. Replace the VQ‑VAE with a fully continuous latent space (e.g., a standard autoencoder) in a diffusion model and compare artifact severity to isolate the discrete bottleneck effect.
