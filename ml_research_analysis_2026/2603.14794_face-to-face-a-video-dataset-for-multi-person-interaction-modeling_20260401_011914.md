# Face-to-Face: A Video Dataset for Multi-Person Interaction Modeling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14794  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14794  
- **Reference count:** 29  
- **Primary result:** Introduced a 70-hour talk-show dataset (F2F-JF) and extended a diffusion model with video conditioning, yielding small but consistent reductions in Emotion-FID and FVD on held-out test hosts while preserving lip-speech synchronization.

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the scarcity of datasets capturing sequential dyadic turn-taking for reactive avatar generation. It introduces the Face-to-Face with Jimmy Fallon (F2F-JF) dataset—a 70-hour, 14,123-clip collection curated from talk-show footage via a semi-automatic pipeline that yields aligned host/guest turns with frame-level identity labels. The authors extend the MultiTalk diffusion model with a video modulation pathway to condition host avatar synthesis on the preceding guest video. Quantitative evaluation on held-out test hosts shows modest but consistent improvements in motion and emotion distribution matching (lower FVD, Emotion-FID) with increased video guidance, while lip-speech synchronization metrics remain stable. The paper highlights open challenges in evaluating conversational appropriateness and scaling the pipeline to diverse multi-person scenarios.

## Method Summary
The authors construct F2F-JF by processing ~400 hours of raw talk-show video. A semi-automatic pipeline first filters for segments with exactly two persistent people via multi-person tracking. Audio diarization separates speech feeds, and a bootstrapped Gaussian model on speaker embeddings identifies the recurring host. The host's visual identity is then bound to the audio stream via a small set of human-vetted face crops and ArcFace embeddings. Per-frame identity assignment matches faces to the host reference or clusters non-host faces. Canonical crops are generated for each turn, pairing a guest's first 64 frames as context with the host's subsequent 81-frame response. For modeling, a frozen Wan 2.1 diffusion backbone is extended with a video modulation branch: a frozen I3D encoder processes the guest clip, and MLP projectors generate affine parameters that modulate DiT block activations after audio conditioning. Training is staged: first audio-only, then the video branch from zero initialization.

## Key Results
- Created F2F-JF: 70 hours, 14,123 canonical dyadic turn clips with frame-level identity labels from ~400 hours of raw talk-show footage.
- Extended MultiTalk with video modulation: on held-out test hosts, increasing video CFG scale yielded small but consistent reductions in FVD (e.g., ~115.0 at CFG=5.0 vs ~115.08 at CFG=0) and Emotion-FID.
- Lip-speech synchronization (Sync-C, Sync-D) remained statistically stable across video CFG sweep, indicating preservation of audio-driven lip sync.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A semi-automatic pipeline can convert raw talk-show footage into a clean, large-scale dataset of dyadic conversational turns with frame-level identity labels.
- **Mechanism:** The pipeline (a) filters for two-person co-occurrence via multi-person tracking, (b) identifies the recurring host through audio diarization and a bootstrapped Gaussian classifier on speaker embeddings, (c) binds the acoustic host to a visual identity via a small set of human-vetted face crops and ArcFace embeddings, and (d) performs per-frame identity assignment by matching face embeddings to the host reference and clustering non-host faces.
- **Core assumption:** Talk-show footage contains sufficiently many clean, sustained dyadic exchanges where a consistent host interacts with rotating guests, and these can be isolated via automated signals (co-occurrence, speaker separation) with minimal human verification.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "semi-automatic pipeline combines multi-person tracking, speech diarization, and lightweight human verification"  
  [section 3] Details the four-stage pipeline and human annotation effort (~5 hours for diarization tagging, ~5 minutes for face vetting) enabling scalable processing of 400 hours down to 70 curated hours.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Formulating reactive avatar generation as conditioning host synthesis on the *preceding guest video* provides a supervised signal that can be exploited by diffusion models to improve motion/emotion distribution matching without harming lip-speech synchronization.
- **Mechanism:** The dataset is curated into canonical, temporally aligned guest-context/host-response crop pairs. A diffusion model is extended with a video modulation branch that ingests the guest clip's I3D features and applies affine transformations (shift, scale, gate) to DiT block activations *after* audio conditioning. Classifier-free guidance (CFG) scale on this video branch is swept to control adherence to visual context.
- **Core assumption:** The host's reactive behavior (head motion, affective expression) in the response turn is statistically dependent on the immediately preceding guest turn's visual presentation, and this dependency is learnable from the paired clips.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 4] Describes derivation of paired canonical crops: guest context (first 64 frames) and host response (next 81 frames).  
  [section 6] Table 2/Fig 6 show consistent reductions in Emotion-FID and FVD as video CFG increases, while Sync-C/D remain stable.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Injecting cross-person visual context via a lightweight, trainable modulation module (frozen I3D encoder + MLP projectors) on a frozen diffusion backbone is sufficient to capture reactive dependencies without destabilizing training or sacrificing audio-lip sync quality.
- **Mechanism:** The Wan 2.1 DiT backbone is frozen. A new video pathway: frozen I3D encodes guest clip → MLP projects to per-block [µ, σ, γ] vectors → these modulate normalized activations *after* the audio branch via element-wise operations. Training is staged: first audio-only, then video branch from zero initialization to act as residual experts.
- **Core assumption:** The pre-trained audio-driven avatar model's activations contain subspaces where cross-person visual cues can be injected via low-rank affine transformations, and that this injection does not conflict with the strong audio-lip sync prior.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 5] Details the modulation equations, zero-init staging, and frozen backbone strategy.  
  [section 6] Sync-C/D show no statistically significant degradation across video CFG sweep, while FVD/Emotion-FID improve modestly.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Multi-modal speaker diarization**  
  *Why needed here:* To separate the host's speech from the guest's in the audio track and assign speech intervals to the correct visual identity. The pipeline relies on diarization embeddings to identify the recurring host.  
  *Quick check question:* What are two common failure modes of speaker diarization in multi-talker audio, and how might they affect the dataset's turn-taking alignment?
- **Concept: Face recognition with robust embeddings (ArcFace)**  
  *Why needed here:* To create a high-precision reference set for the host's visual identity and to classify/track faces per-frame. The Gaussian host model and visual verification both depend on embedding similarity thresholds.  
  *Quick check question:* Why might cosine similarity with an ArcFace embedding be preferred over a classifier for this open-set, single-exemplar identification task?
- **Concept: Diffusion models with classifier-free guidance (CFG)**  
  *Why needed here:* The baseline model uses CFG to control the influence of the video modulation branch (λ_video). Understanding CFG is essential to interpret the ablation study where λ_video is swept.  
  *Quick check question:* If you increase the CFG scale for a conditional branch in a diffusion model, what happens to output diversity and fidelity relative to the conditional distribution? How does this relate to the observed Emotion-FID/FVD trends?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Raw video/audio → ffmpeg decoding → Ultralytics person detector+tracker → two-person segment filtering → pyannote.audio diarization → speaker embeddings → Gaussian host model → host speech intervals → DeepFace/ArcFace on host windows → human vetting → host face embedding bank → per-frame face detection/embedding → host/non-host classification → guest clustering → canonical crop generation (guest context, host response) → frozen Wan 2.1 VAE + DiT backbone → frozen I3D (guest) → MLP projectors → per-DiT-block [µ, σ, γ] → modulation after audio branch → noise prediction loss → Stage 1 (audio-only) → Stage 2 (video branch, zero-init) → inference with λ_video sweep.
- **Critical path (training):** Raw clip → tracking & diarization → host ID → canonical crops → (guest_video, host_audio, host_video) tuples → encode guest with I3D, host audio with HuBERT, host video with VAE → DiT forward with modulation → noise prediction loss. *Key dependency:* Clean paired crops must accurately capture the reactive turn boundary.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Crop stability vs. motion capture: Large expansion (130%) with downward shift ensures upper-body motion but may include background; trajectory smoothing removes fine-grained head nods.  
  - Parameter efficiency vs. expressivity: Freezing Wan backbone and using a small video projector keeps params low (<1% of backbone) but limits capacity to learn complex cross-person dynamics.  
  - Metric sensitivity: Emotion-FID/FVD improve with λ_video but Sync-C/D do not, suggesting metrics capture different aspects; no single metric measures conversational appropriateness (e.g., backchannel timing).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Dataset level:** Clips with >30% missing guest frames; extreme crop area ratios (>10:1); guest face cluster ID inconsistent within a clip.  
  - **Model level:** At λ_video > 0, sudden drop in Sync-C (>0.5% absolute) or rise in Sync-D; no Emotion-FID improvement until very high λ_video (e.g., >4.0).  
  - **Training instability:** Loss spikes when enabling video branch if not zero-initialized; model ignores guest context if audio CFG is also >1.0.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Run full dataset construction on 1 raw episode (~1hr). Verify: (a) % frames with 2 people tracked, (b) host speech diarization F1 against 5-min manual transcript, (c) host face verification precision on held-out 50-crop sample.  
  2. On 100 held-out host turns, compare baseline (λ_video=0) vs. best λ_video (e.g., 5.0) via: (a) FVD/Emotion-FID, (b) human A/B test on "does host response seem reactive to guest?" (Likert 1-5).  
  3. Train two Stage 2 models: (a) zero-init projectors, (b) random init. Compare Sync-C/D stability and FVD convergence speed; expect (a) to preserve lip-sync and converge faster.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can we design evaluation protocols and metrics that directly capture conversational reactivity (e.g., backchannel timing, gesture mirroring, affective alignment) rather than relying on proxies like lip-sync and distributional fidelity?
- To what extent do the inherent biases of the talk-show source material (a single recurring host, three-quarter face angles, specific demographic range) limit the generalizability of models trained on F2F-JF to other dyadic scenarios (e.g., multi-cultural conversations, varying poses, multiple recurring partners)?
- Do current model architectures (like the modulated diffusion baseline) effectively exploit the provided cross-person visual context, or are the modest gains in FVD/Emotion-FID primarily due to architecture limitations rather than a lack of signal in the data?
- Can the semi-automatic dataset construction pipeline be made fully automatic and robust enough to scale to diverse, untrimmed multi-person video sources (e.g., reality TV, vlogs, meetings) without significant human verification?

## Limitations
- Dataset construction relies on talk-show structure; generalizability to less constrained interactions is unknown. Human verification was minimal (~5 hours total), so label errors may persist.
- Model improvements in FVD/Emotion-FID are tiny (e.g., ~0.08 FVD points), raising questions about practical significance. Metrics may not capture conversational appropriateness (e.g., backchannel timing, gaze).
- The modulation module's small parameter budget may limit expressivity; it is unclear if larger gains are possible with alternative architectures.

## Confidence
- **Dataset pipeline:** Medium confidence. Pipeline details and verification are provided, but no independent audit of label accuracy or diversity.
- **Reactive generation gains:** Medium confidence. Consistent metric trends exist, but effect sizes are small and human perceptual validation is absent.
- **Modulation architecture adequacy:** Medium confidence. Experiments show stability and marginal improvements, but the claim of sufficiency for reactive dependencies is not strongly supported by alternative designs.

## Next Checks
1. **Human reactivity study:** Conduct a blind A/B test with participants comparing baseline (λ_video=0) vs. best model (λ_video=5.0) responses, rating perceived reactivity on Likert scales.
2. **Dataset label audit:** Annotate a random sample (e.g., 200 clips) for turn boundaries and identity; compute agreement with pipeline outputs to quantify error rates.
3. **Architecture ablation:** Train variants with (a) fully fine-tuned backbone, (b) larger video projectors, and compare FVD/Emotion-FID gains to assess whether current light design is indeed sufficient.
