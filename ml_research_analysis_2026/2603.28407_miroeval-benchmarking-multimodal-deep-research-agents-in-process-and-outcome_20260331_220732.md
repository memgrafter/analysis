# MiroEval: Benchmarking Multimodal Deep Research Agents in Process and Outcome

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.28407
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28407
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Introduces a benchmark of 100 real-world queries and a three-dimensional evaluation framework (adaptive synthesis, agentic factuality, process auditing) to reveal that multimodal tasks are 3-10 points harder and process quality predicts final outcome (r=0.88).

## Executive Summary
MiroEval addresses critical gaps in evaluating deep research agents by introducing a benchmark of 100 authentic queries (70 text-only, 30 multimodal) and a three-dimensional framework assessing synthesis quality, factuality verification, and research process. Evaluating 13 systems shows these dimensions measure complementary skills, that process quality reliably predicts final report quality and exposes hidden weaknesses, and that multimodal inputs impose a significant performance penalty. The benchmark is designed for periodic updates via a refreshable query construction pipeline.

## Method Summary
The benchmark is built from 100 real-world tasks using a dual-path construction: 65 queries are privacy-rewritten patterns from internal use, and 35 are auto-generated from current web trends via Serper API with quality filters. Evaluation is performed across three dimensions using LLM-based agents: (1) **Adaptive Synthesis** generates task-specific rubrics from attachment key facts and scores reports; (2) **Agentic Factuality** decomposes reports, retrieves evidence from the web and attachments (using native multimodal or retrieval-augmented methods), and labels statement consistency as RIGHT, WRONG, CONFLICT, or UNKNOWN; (3) **Process Auditing** structures raw agent logs into atomic units and scores intrinsic quality (breadth, depth, refinement, thinking, efficiency) and alignment between process and final report.

## Key Results
*   The three evaluation dimensions are complementary, measuring distinct agent capabilities with low inter-correlation.
*   Process quality is a strong predictor of overall outcome quality (Pearson r=0.88) and can detect traceability issues missed by output-only metrics.
*   Multimodal tasks are substantially harder, causing a consistent 3-10 point performance drop across all evaluated systems.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Adaptive synthesis quality evaluation captures task-specific nuances in report quality that fixed rubrics miss, improving diagnostic validity.
*   **Mechanism:** For each query, the system generates a customized evaluation dimension space by extracting key facts from attachments (if any) and producing task-specific expertise dimensions with dynamic weights. It then scores the report against these tailored criteria, ensuring attachment-grounded tasks penalize superficial referencing.
*   **Core assumption:** Research tasks vary sufficiently in domain, objective, and modality that a single static rubric cannot adequately measure synthesis quality across diverse queries.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "Comprehensive Adaptive Synthesis Quality Evaluation with task-specific rubrics"; [section]: §3.1 details the adaptive dimension space, key facts extraction, and dynamic weighting process.
*   **Break condition:** If key facts extraction fails for complex attachments (e.g., dense tables or images), generated criteria may be irrelevant or miss critical aspects, leading to misaligned scoring.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Agentic factuality verification ensures claim accuracy by dynamically retrieving and reasoning over heterogeneous evidence (web + attachments), explicitly handling cross-source conflicts.
*   **Mechanism:** The system decomposes reports into atomic statements, uses an agent to retrieve supporting/refuting evidence from both live web searches and task attachments (via native multimodal or retrieval-augmented processing), and labels consistency as RIGHT, WRONG, CONFLICT, or UNKNOWN based on evidence aggregation.
*   **Core assumption:** Factuality in multimodal deep research requires active evidence integration from multiple source types, and binary verification is insufficient when sources conflict.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "Agentic factuality verification via active retrieval and reasoning over both web sources and multimodal attachments"; [section]: §3.2 describes the agentic pipeline, attachment evidence retrieval strategies, and four-way consistency assessment.
*   **Break condition:** When attachments use unsupported formats or evidence retrieval yields no credible sources, verification defaults to UNKNOWN, potentially inflating unverifiable rates without resolution.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Process-centric evaluation reliably predicts outcome quality and exposes traceability gaps by auditing research trajectories, revealing weaknesses invisible to output-only metrics.
*   **Mechanism:** Raw process logs are structured into atomic units with dependency recovery, then assessed on intrinsic dimensions (breadth, depth, refinement, thinking, efficiency) and alignment (Process→Report, Report→Process, contradiction detection), combining them into a process score.
*   **Core assumption:** The quality of the research procedure itself is a leading indicator of final report reliability and can identify issues like insufficient depth or mismatch between findings and report.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "Process-centric evaluation audits how the system searches, reasons, and refines throughout its investigation"; [section]: §3.3 defines process representation, intrinsic metrics, and alignment components; §4.4 shows process predicts outcome (correlation 0.88).
*   **Break condition:** For closed-source systems that do not expose intermediate traces, process evaluation is infeasible, limiting applicability to black-box agents.

    *   Quick check question: *What challenges arise when extracting factual anchors from heterogeneous attachments like charts or scanned documents?*
*   **Concept: Agentic Evaluation**
    *   Why needed here: The factuality and process components rely on autonomous agents (e.g., for evidence retrieval or process structuring), necessitating familiarity with agent design patterns and tool integration.
    *   Quick check question: *How does an agentic factuality verifier differ from a static LLM judge, and why is it better for cross-source conflicts?*
*   **Concept: Process Tracing and Alignment**
    *   Why needed here: Process-centric evaluation depends on reconstructing research trajectories and measuring consistency with final reports, requiring knowledge of dependency graphs and alignment metrics.
    *   Quick check question: *What does a low Report→Process (R→P) score indicate about a system's traceability?*
*   **Concept: Adaptive Rubric Generation**
    *   Why needed here: The synthesis dimension dynamically creates evaluation criteria from task-specific key facts, moving beyond static rubrics.
    *   Quick check question: *How might key fact extraction from a spreadsheet attachment differ from extraction from a research paper PDF?*

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Input Handler -> (Synthesis Evaluator, Factuality Agent, Process Auditor) -> Aggregator
*   **Critical path:** For a new query, the pipeline executes in parallel across dimensions: (1) Synthesis: extract key facts → generate rubric → score report; (2) Factuality: decompose report → retrieve evidence per statement → assign labels; (3) Process: parse logs → structure process → evaluate intrinsic + alignment. Outputs are aggregated for final rankings.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Dynamic rubrics improve relevance but reduce cross-task comparability; fixed dimensions (coverage, insight) provide baselines.
    *   Multimodal evidence retrieval: native processing is accurate but limited to supported formats; retrieval-augmented scales but may lose context.
    *   Process logging requires system cooperation; for non-cooperative systems, process evaluation defaults to unavailable.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   Low P→R alignment: report includes unsupported claims not in process.
    *   High CONFLICT rates in factuality: frequent web-attachment discrepancies.
    *   Low Analytical Depth in process: repetitive searches without follow-up reasoning.
    *   High Efficiency score but low Breadth: narrow but non-redundant search may miss sources.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Integration test:** Run the full evaluation on 5 sample queries (mix of text/multimodal) to verify component interoperability and judge stability.
    2.  **Dimension orthogonality analysis:** Compute rank correlations across synthesis, factuality, and process scores across systems to confirm complementary coverage (expected low correlation).
    3.  **Updateability check:** Re-run the auto-generated query pipeline with current web trends to assess benchmark refresh feasibility and temporal drift.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How can process-centric evaluation be extended to fully closed-source deep research systems that do not expose intermediate reasoning traces?
    *   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Our process evaluation relies on systems exposing their intermediate reasoning traces, which limits applicability to fully closed-source systems that do not provide such access."
    *   **Why unresolved:** Current process evaluation cannot assess major commercial systems that keep their reasoning private, creating a significant blind spot in comparative analysis and preventing process-based diagnostics for a large portion of deployed agents.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of reliable trace inference methods from final outputs and search patterns, or alternative evaluation paradigms that approximate process quality without direct access to intermediate steps.
*   **Open Question 2:** How can agentic factuality evaluation resolve conflicts between heterogeneous evidence sources rather than merely flagging them as CONFLICT?
    *   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "the factuality evaluation currently identifies cross-source conflicts (e.g., between web evidence and user-provided attachments) but does not yet resolve them: the CONFLICT label flags disagreements without determining which source is correct, an important direction for future work."
    *   **Why unresolved:** The CONFLICT label leaves users without guidance for decision-making when authoritative sources disagree, limiting the practical utility of factuality assessment in contested domains.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Designing conflict resolution mechanisms that incorporate source reliability modeling, temporal grounding, domain-specific verification protocols, and possibly user preferences for source trustworthiness.
*   **Open Question 3:** Can the refreshable dual-path query construction pipeline be practically implemented at scale to maintain MiroEval's temporal relevance as knowledge evolves?
    *   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Looking ahead, we plan to leverage the refreshable dual-path construction pipeline to periodically update the benchmark with new queries reflecting evolving user needs and the latest web trends, ensuring that MiroEval remains temporally relevant as a live benchmark."
    *   **Why unresolved:** While the pipeline design supports updates, operational challenges around re-execution frequency, quality control at scale, version management, and ensuring comparability across benchmark iterations remain unaddressed.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Demonstrating a sustained update cycle over multiple time periods with maintained benchmark quality (precision >90%), stable system rankings across versions, and published versioning protocols to prevent data leakage.
*   **Open Question 4:** Is the observed correlation between process quality and outcome quality causal, or are both driven by underlying system capabilities that separately influence each?
    *   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper reports a strong Pearson correlation (r=0.88) and states that "process quality serves as a reliable predictor of overall outcome," but does not establish whether improving process directly improves outcomes or whether both are effects of a third factor.
    *   **Why unresolved:** Without causal understanding, it is unclear whether resources should be invested in improving process mechanisms (e.g., deeper investigation, better traceability) or if process metrics are merely epiphenomena of superior reasoning capacity.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled intervention studies where process quality is artificially enhanced (e.g., through process supervision, forced planning steps) while isolating other system capabilities, measuring resulting changes in outcome quality; or natural experiments comparing systems with similar outcomes but divergent processes.
*   **Open Question 5:** What specific factors within multimodal tasks cause the systematic 3-10 point performance degradation, and are these factors uniform across task types?
    *   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper identifies that "multimodal tasks pose substantially greater challenges" with consistent score drops, but does not isolate whether the bottleneck lies in visual understanding, multimodal integration, report generation constraints, or process adaptations to attachments.
    *   **Why unresolved:** Without mechanistic diagnosis, research efforts may be misdirected; improving vision encoders might not help if the core issue is reasoning over combined textual and visual evidence, or vice versa.
    *   **What evidence would resolve it:** Fine-grained error analysis across systems and multimodal task types; controlled ablations varying attachment complexity, visual information density, and reasoning requirements; and process-trajectory examination to identify where multimodal integration fails.

## Limitations
*   **Data Accessibility:** The full set of 100 queries, especially the 65 user-derived ones, is not public, hindering exact replication.
*   **Evaluation Opacity:** Prompts, hyperparameters (e.g., process score weight α), and judge model specifics are undisclosed, risking score variability.
*   **Process Log Dependency:** Process evaluation requires intermediate system traces, excluding closed-source agents like commercial APIs.

## Confidence
*   **Benchmark Design (Real-world, multimodal, updatable):** Medium (construction methods detailed but data unavailable)
*   **Evaluation Framework (Three dimensions):** High (clearly specified with algorithmic descriptions)
*   **Key Findings (Complementarity, prediction, multimodal drop):** Medium (correlations strong but confounded by judge choice and small N)
*   **Validation Metrics (Human precision 92.0%, robustness τ=0.91):** High (empirically supported in paper)
