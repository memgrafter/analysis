# Detecting RAG Advertisements Across Advertising Styles

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04925
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04925
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Token-level classifiers (ModernBERT) achieve near-perfect detection (F1 up to 0.997) and remain robust across advertising style and LLM shifts, while lightweight models are brittle.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the detection of LLM-generated native advertisements that may attempt to evade detection by varying advertising styles. The authors introduce a two-dimensional taxonomy of advertising styles based on explicitness (covert vs. overt) and appeal type (emotional vs. rational). Using this taxonomy, they simulate advertiser evasion by generating ads with different LLMs and prompts. A suite of classifiers—from traditional lightweight models (random forest, SVM) to transformer-based token classifiers—is evaluated. Results show that token-level classifiers, particularly ModernBERT fine-tuned on BIO tags, achieve exceptionally high detection performance (F1 up to 0.997) and maintain robustness across style and LLM shifts, whereas lightweight models suffer significant performance drops. Overt and emotional ads are easier to detect than covert and rational ones. Token classifiers also demonstrate strong entity recognition (F1 up to 0.798), enabling precise ad localization. The study concludes that fine-grained token-level modeling is key to robust native ad detection.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed the WGNA 25 dataset by inserting LLM-generated ads into organic search responses, with ads adhering to the style taxonomy. Nine additional test sets were created using different LLMs and prompts to simulate style shifts. For detection, they trained and compared three families of models: lightweight models (random forest on bag-of-words, SVM on Word2Vec averages), sentence-level transformers (MiniLM, MPNet, ModernBERT) fine-tuned on a sentence-pair task, and token-level classifiers (BERT, ModernBERT) fine-tuned on BIO tags. Models were evaluated on response-level F1, robustness via odds ratios across test sets, and entity-level seqeval for token classifiers. The experimental pipeline involved standard supervised training/validation splits, followed by prediction on reference and shifted test sets.

## Key Results
- Token classifiers (ModernBERT) achieve F1 up to 0.997 on the reference test set, far surpassing lightweight and sentence-level models.
- Across nine style/LLM-shifted test sets, ModernBERT token classifiers remain robust (non-significant odds ratios), while lightweight models (e.g., SVM) see F1 drop from 0.595 to 0.419.
- Overt ads are detected more easily than covert ads; emotional appeals are easier than rational appeals for most classifiers.
- Token classifiers excel at entity recognition, with F1 up to 0.798, allowing precise localization of ad spans.
- Sentence-level transformers show intermediate performance but still degrade under style shifts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Fine-grained lexical and syntactic cues
- **Claim:** Token-level classification detects ads robustly across style/LLM shifts because it models fine-grained patterns at sub-sentence level, which are less affected by macro-level stylistic variations.
- **Mechanism:** BIO tagging forces the model to learn local cues (e.g., brand names, promotional adjectives) that persist despite rhetorical changes. These cues are embedded in the transformer’s contextual representations, enabling detection even when ads are subtly integrated.
- **Core assumption:** Advertising content, regardless of style, contains distinctive token sequences statistically separable from organic text; contextual attention isolates them without relying on sentence coherence.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports token classifiers achieve F1 up to 0.997 and robustness; Section 5.1 shows token classifiers consistently outperform others and maintain robustness; related work (e.g., "Detecting Generated Native Ads in Conversational Search") also uses token-level features.
- **Break condition:** If LLMs generate highly paraphrased, context-aware ads that systematically alter local token distributions (e.g., synonym substitution), token-level patterns may become indistinguishable.

### Mechanism 2: Contextual relational cues
- **Claim:** Robustness stems from token classifiers leveraging full-response context to resolve ambiguity, while lightweight models rely on style-sensitive sparse features.
- **Mechanism:** Transformers process entire sequences, capturing relational cues (e.g., a positive adjective modifying a brand) that are consistent across styles. Lightweight models (bag-of-words, embedding averages) capture only style-specific vocabulary.
- **Core assumption:** The semantic relationship between ad-related tokens and surrounding text creates a stable "footprint" detectable by contextual models; non-contextual models overfit to surface forms.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 5.2 notes ModernBERT retains effectiveness across all nine new test sets while others degrade; Section 6 emphasizes the importance of context; related work on podcast ad detection shows BERT superiority over SVM+TF-IDF.
- **Break condition:** If ads are generated by an LLM explicitly optimized to mimic the contextual distribution of organic text (e.g., adversarial training), the footprint may be erased.

### Mechanism 3: Entity segmentation anchor
- **Claim:** Entity recognition improves localization and precision because ads have a consistent structural skeleton (brand + attributes) that persists across styles.
- **Mechanism:** BIO-trained token classifiers learn to segment ad components (ITEM, ADVERTISER, AD), forcing recognition of multi-token style-invariant entities. Even covert ads contain these entities, and precise spans enable targeted blocking.
- **Core assumption:** All ads must mention the advertised entity explicitly or implicitly, providing an anchor; BIO tagging helps distinguish entity boundaries from surrounding text.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states token classifiers excel at entity recognition (F1 up to 0.798); Section 5.3 shows entity metrics with similar robustness trends; Section 4.2 describes BIO tags in WGNA 25.
- **Break condition:** If advertisers use implicit references (e.g., pronouns) or split entities across non-contiguous tokens, entity recognition may fail without coreference or long-range dependencies.

## Foundational Learning

### Advertising Style Taxonomy (Explicitness × Appeal)
- **Why needed here:** The paper’s core evaluation manipulates ad generation along these two dimensions. Understanding this taxonomy is essential to interpret why some ads are harder to detect (covert/rational) and to design evasive prompts.
- **Quick check question:** How would you construct a prompt to generate a covert ad with a rational appeal? What linguistic features would distinguish it from an overt-rational ad?

### Token-Level Classification (BIO Tagging)
- **Why needed here:** This is the primary effective detector. It differs from sentence-level classification by assigning labels per token, enabling precise localization and capturing intra-sentence patterns.
- **Quick check question:** Why might a token classifier detecting "B-AD" tags be more robust to style changes than a sentence classifier that labels entire sentences as ad/non-ad?

### Robustness Evaluation via Odds Ratios
- **Why needed here:** The paper’s main claim about robustness is statistical, using odds ratios to compare detection rates across test sets. This method controls for the constant negative examples.
- **Quick check question:** If a classifier’s odds ratio for a new test set is 0.5 (CI excludes 1), what does that indicate about its robustness to that specific change?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Ad Generator → Dataset (WGNA 25 + 9 new test sets) → Detectors [Lightweight: Random Forest (BoW), SVM (Word2Vec); Sentence Transformers: MiniLM, MPNet, ModernBERT (sentence-pair); Token Classifiers: BERT, ModernBERT (BIO)] → Evaluator
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Select detector type and fine-tune on WGNA 25 training/validation splits.  
  2. Predict on the reference test set and the nine style/LLM-shifted test sets.  
  3. Compute response-level F1, odds ratios for robustness, and entity-level seqeval for token classifiers.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Accuracy vs. Efficiency:* ModernBERT (220M) is robust but heavy; SVM/Random Forest are lightweight but brittle.  
  - *Granularity:* Token classification yields precise spans but requires token-level annotations; sentence classification is coarser but can use existing datasets.  
  - *Style coverage:* The taxonomy covers two dimensions but omits others (e.g., moral appeal, hard/soft sell), limiting breadth of robustness evaluation.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Large recall drop on covert-rational ads (e.g., MiniLM recall 0.938 → 0.709).  
  - Low entity recognition F1 (<0.6) on covert styles.  
  - High Jaccard index of false negatives between classifiers of same type, indicating shared failure modes.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Baseline effectiveness: Fine-tune each classifier on WGNA 25, report F1 on reference test. Expect token classifiers > sentence transformers > lightweight.  
  2. Style robustness: For each of the nine shifted test sets, compute odds ratio of detection vs. reference. Identify which classifiers maintain robustness (CI includes 1). Expect only ModernBERT token classifier to be robust.  
  3. Entity recognition: Evaluate token classifiers on entity-level seqeval for each test set. Correlate robustness with entity detection; expect overt/emotional ads to yield higher entity F1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

#### Open Question 1: Efficient robust detectors for low-resource devices
- **Question:** How can ad detectors be made both highly effective and efficient enough to run reliably on low-resource end-user devices, given that current lightweight models (random forest, SVM) are brittle under style and LLM shifts?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors explicitly evaluate lightweight models for practical deployment but find them "brittle under such changes, highlighting the need for further efficiency-oriented research for a practical approach."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper shows transformer-based token classifiers (ModernBERT) are robust and effective but are computationally heavy. No solution is provided for achieving similar robustness with models suitable for mobile/edge devices.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A new class of lightweight models or model compression/optimization techniques (e.g., knowledge distillation, pruning) that maintain ModernBERT-level robustness (F1 > 0.95) across style/LLM shifts while meeting strict memory/inference time constraints.

#### Open Question 2: Impact of additional advertising style dimensions
- **Question:** How do advertising styles beyond the studied rational/emotional and overt/covert dichotomies (e.g., moral appeals, hard-sell vs. soft-sell) impact the robustness and failure modes of ad detectors?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors develop a taxonomy from marketing literature but deliberately "blur out the details" and adopt only the rational/emotional and overt/covert dimensions. They acknowledge other dichotomies exist (Table 1) but do not test them.
- **Why unresolved:** The taxonomy is operationalized for two specific dimensions. The impact of other theoretically valid style distinctions on detection is unknown. Ads using these styles might create new evasion patterns not captured by the current test sets.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Generating and evaluating ads using prompts that operationalize other style dichotomies from marketing literature (e.g., "hard-sell" vs. "soft-sell"). Measuring the change in detector performance (F1, odds ratios) compared to the baseline styles would quantify the impact.

#### Open Question 3: Robustness to token-level distributed ads
- **Question:** To what extent are current ad detectors robust to more integrated, "token-auction" style ad generation where promotional content is interwoven at the token distribution level rather than inserted as a contiguous text segment?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state their work is limited because ads were inserted *after* response generation. They explicitly plan future work to implement "advertising mechanisms that generate the response from an advertiser-biased distribution," citing Dütting et al. [9] and Soumalias et al. [39].
- **Why unresolved:** The paper's evasion simulation only changes the style of a contiguous inserted ad snippet. Token-level bias could produce ads with different statistical and semantic patterns (e.g., subtle priming, dispersed keywords) that might evade token classifiers trained on contiguous ads.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Creating a new test set where ads are generated by sampling from a distribution biased by an advertiser (simulating the token auction models). Evaluating the existing suite of classifiers (especially token-level ones) on this dataset and comparing robustness metrics (odds ratios) to the contiguous-ad baseline.

#### Open Question 4: Style-invariant detectable features and explainable detection
- **Question:** What specific lexical, semantic, or structural features distinguish covert vs. overt and emotional vs. rational ads in a way that is consistently detectable across different LLMs, and can these features be encoded into a more robust, explainable detector?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper demonstrates strong performance differences between styles (covert harder than overt, emotional easier than rational for most classifiers) but does not analyze *why*. The "brittleness" of lightweight models suggests they rely on patterns that shift with LLM/prompt changes.
- **Why unresolved:** The study is primarily empirical (F1 scores, odds ratios). It does not provide a linguistic or feature-based analysis of what makes an ad "detectable" or "evasive." This limits the ability to design detectors that are robust because they target fundamental, style-invariant cues.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study or feature attribution analysis (e.g., using LIME, SHAP) on the robust token classifier (MBERT_T) to identify the tokens or token patterns (e.g., specific POS sequences, sentiment shifts, named entity types) most responsible for correct classifications across all style/LLM shifts. Human validation of these features' marketing plausibility would be key.

## Limitations
- **Evasion realism:** The evasion simulation inserts ads after response generation, not from an advertiser-biased token distribution, which may underestimate more integrated and subtle ad placements.
- **Taxonomy coverage:** Only two style dimensions (explicitness, appeal) are evaluated; other advertising styles (e.g., moral appeals, hard/soft sell) remain untested and could affect detection.
- **Dataset ecology:** Training data is synthetic with artificially inserted ads; real-world platforms have noisier, context-dependent ad placements that may limit generalizability.

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Token classifiers (ModernBERT) achieve near-perfect response-level F1 on the reference test set and significantly outperform other model types. (Evidence: Abstract, Section 5.1, Table 3)
- **Medium confidence:** Token classifiers exhibit statistical robustness (non-significant odds ratios) across the nine LLM+prompt-shifted test sets, though this is tested only against a fixed set of evasion styles and LLMs. (Evidence: Section 5.2, Table 4)
- **Low confidence:** Claims about the generalizability of entity recognition across all advertising styles (especially covert/rational) and about the style-invariance of local cues lack direct validation via ablation or probing studies. (Inferred from results)

## Next Checks
1. **Human-adversarial evasion test:** Recruit human advertisers or use an adversarial LLM agent to generate covert rational ads that explicitly target detector weaknesses (e.g., mimicking surrounding discourse, using pronouns). Evaluate token classifiers; a >10% absolute F1 drop from the reference test set would indicate limited robustness.
2. **Cross-domain zero-shot evaluation:** Train detectors solely on WGNA 25 (simulated conversational/search data) and test zero-shot on authentic platform data containing known sponsored/affiliate content (e.g., Reddit posts with disclosed paid promotions, Amazon reviews with "verified" purchases). This tests distributional shift beyond LLM style variations.
3. **Entity boundary stress test:** Construct a test set where ad entities are referenced implicitly or non-contiguously (e.g., "the service we mentioned" with intervening organic text). Report seqeval F1 for AD spans; a >15% absolute drop from the baseline (explicit contiguous mentions) would highlight fragility in entity recognition.
