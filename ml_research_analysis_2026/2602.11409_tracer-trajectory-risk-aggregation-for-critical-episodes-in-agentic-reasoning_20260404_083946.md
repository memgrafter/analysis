# TRACER: Trajectory Risk Aggregation for Critical Episodes in Agentic Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11409
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11409
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** Improves AUROC by up to 37.1% and AUARC by up to 55% for failure prediction and selective execution, detecting up to 97.4% of failures within the first 20% of trajectory execution.

## Executive Summary
TRACER tackles the problem of estimating uncertainty for AI agents in multi-turn, tool-using interactions where failures often arise from sparse critical episodes like looping or incoherent tool use—phenomena that token-level uncertainty proxies frequently miss. The method combines content-aware normalized surprisal (filtering meaningful content tokens) with situational-awareness indicators (semantic/lexical repetition and tool-grounded coherence gaps). These signals are aggregated per step using a MAX-composite risk, and then at the trajectory level via a tail-focused functional that emphasizes decisive anomalies. Evaluated on the τ²-bench benchmark, TRACER substantially outperforms baselines: AUROC improves by up to 37.1%, AUARC by up to 55%, and it signals up to 97.4% of failures within the first 20% of execution, compared to 82.1% for the strongest baseline. The approach demonstrates the importance of dual-control (agent and user) signals and MAX aggregation for capturing sparse breakdowns.

## Method Summary
TRACER processes a trajectory T = \{(a_t, x_t, o_t)\}_{t=1}^N where a_t is the actor (agent/user), x_t is the emitted event (utterance/tool call), and o_t is the observation. First, per-step uncertainties are computed: (i) **U_t** – content-aware normalized surprisal derived from token probabilities of content-bearing tokens (filtering stop-words, numeric tokens, and low-probability tokens); (ii) **D_rep(t)** – repetition score measuring semantic and lexical similarity over a local window of agent turns; (iii) **D_ao(t)** – action-observation coherence gap; (iv) **D_uo(t)** – user-agent coordination gap. These are weighted and combined into a step risk r_t = \max(r_U^t, r_rep^t, r_ao^t, r_uo^t). Then, the trajectory risk TRACER_θ(T) = (1−w)·TM_k(r) + w·\|r\|_∞, where TM_k is the mean of the top-K highest step risks (tail mean) and \|r\|_∞ is the maximum step risk. Hyperparameters (α,β,γ,k,w) are tuned on a validation set to maximize AUROC for failure prediction. The design ensures sensitivity to rare but critical anomalies while maintaining robustness.

## Key Results
- AUROC improvements up to 37.1% and AUARC up to 55% over strongest baseline on τ²-bench for task failure prediction and selective execution.
- Early failure detection: 97.4% of failures signaled within first 20% of trajectory vs. 82.1% for the strongest baseline.
- Ablations confirm the necessity of both agent and user situational signals and MAX aggregation for capturing sparse breakdowns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Content-aware normalized surprisal isolates epistemic uncertainty
- **Claim:** Filtering tokens to focus on content-bearing ones prevents dilution by predictable structural tokens, isolating epistemic uncertainty.
- **Mechanism:** Define a content-bearing predicate χ(w) via stop-word set, numeric filter, and probability threshold π₀. Per-step uncertainty U_t is the mean negative log-probability over tokens where χ(w)=1 (or a small ϵ if none).
- **Core assumption:** Epistemic mismatch manifests primarily in content tokens; function words and highly predictable tokens contribute little to decisive uncertainty.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section III-B formalizes χ(w) and U_t (Eqs. 5–7); Theorem A.1 decomposes content uncertainty separately from epistemic mismatch.
- **Break condition:** If content-bearing token set is empty (U_t = ϵ) or filtering discards tokens carrying epistemic signal (e.g., domain-specific terms misclassified as stop-words).

### Mechanism 2: Situational-awareness indicators detect trajectory-level breakdowns
- **Claim:** Semantic/lexical repetition and tool-grounded coherence gaps detect looping and misalignment even when token surprisal is low (false confidence).
- **Mechanism:** D_rep(t) uses hybrid similarity (cosine semantic + Jaccard lexical) over a local window of agent turns. D_ao(t) measures semantic distance between agent action and observation. D_uo(t) measures semantic distance between prior agent and user utterances.
- **Core assumption:** Failures in dual-control interaction manifest as increased repetition (looping) or decreased coherence between actions/observations or agent/user messages.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section III-C defines D_rep, D_ao, D_uo (Eqs. 11, 14, 15); Table III ablation shows dual-control signals are necessary.
- **Break condition:** Embedding similarity fails to capture true semantic drift (e.g., paraphrased repetition with low lexical overlap) or tool-grounded gaps are misaligned with task constraints.

### Mechanism 3: Tail-focused aggregation with MAX emphasizes decisive anomalies
- **Claim:** Using MAX-per-step risk and a tail-weighted trajectory function surfaces rare, high-magnitude uncertainty spikes (sparse critical episodes) while preserving sensitivity.
- **Mechanism:** Step risk r_t = ℓ∞-norm of (r_U^t, r_rep^t, r_ao^t, r_uo^t). Trajectory risk combines top-K tail mean (TM_k) and max step risk: TRACER_θ(T) = (1−w)·TM_k + w·\|r\|_∞.
- **Core assumption:** Task failures are driven by sparse critical episodes rather than uniformly elevated uncertainty; MAX preserves single-point failure sensitivity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section III-D (Eqs. 20, 22, 23); Theorem A.2 proves coherence; Theorem A.8 shows risk control under sparse hazards; Table IV ablation shows MAX outperforms additive/multiplicative aggregation; Figure 2 shows early detection gains.
- **Break condition:** Critical episodes are not highest step risks due to noise in signals, or tail fraction k mis-specified to include many non-critical steps.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Trajectory Input -> Stage I: Compute content-aware surprisal U_t per step -> Stage II: Compute situational signals D_rep(t), D_ao(t), D_uo(t) -> Stage III: Form component risks and step risk r_t = max(...) -> Stage IV: Tail aggregation (TM_k and \|r\|_∞) to produce TRACER score.

**Critical path:**  
Embedding φ for all utterances and observations must be computed first (dominates cost). Then per-step signals can be computed in parallel per step. Finally, sorting step risks for tail aggregation requires all r_t.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- MAX vs additive aggregation: MAX emphasizes decisive anomalies; additive dilutes them (Table IV).  
- Tail mean vs worst-case: TM_k captures chronic patterns; \|r\|_∞ captures acute spikes; weight w trades off.  
- Dual-control signals: Including both agent and user signals improves performance; removing either causes significant drop (Table III).

**Failure signatures:**  
- Using only token surprisal U_t: misses looping (high D_rep, low U_t).  
- Replacing MAX with sum: fails to highlight trajectories with a single critical episode.  
- Omitting user signals: cannot detect coordination failures (e.g., user misunderstanding).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Task failure prediction:** Evaluate AUROC for full trajectories using TRACER vs. baselines on τ²-bench.  
2. **Selective task execution:** Evaluate AUARC (accuracy under abstention).  
3. **Early-warning analysis:** For failed trajectories, measure first step where metric crosses threshold; plot cumulative detection vs. normalized progress.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Generalization to real-world deployments:** Does TRACER maintain effectiveness beyond τ²-bench simulation?  
   *Basis:* Evaluation only on τ²-bench (Section IV.A). Needs real user logs.

2. **Utility without token surprisal:** How does performance change when token probabilities (U_t) are unavailable (black-box agents)?  
   *Basis:* Paper notes TRACER can operate without U_t but provides no ablation. Impact unknown.

3. **Validity of theoretical assumptions:** Are the risk-dominance and tail sparsity assumptions (Assumptions A.6-A.7) empirically valid across diverse tasks?  
   *Basis:* Theorem A.8 relies on them; not verified against τ²-bench trajectories.

4. **Sensitivity to embedding model:** How does choice of embedding φ affect TRACER’s performance?  
   *Basis:* Situation indicators depend on cosine similarity; robustness to model choice unexplored.

5. **Adaptation to single-agent settings:** Can TRACER be adapted for tasks without a user counterpart, and how does it compare?  
   *Basis:* Designed for dual-control; user-derived signals absent in single-agent; not explored.

## Limitations

- **Hyperparameter tuning:** Exact search grid ranges and validation set construction are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- **Signal specification:** Embedding model, stop-word set, numeric filter, and surprisal threshold are not detailed; choices may significantly affect signals.
- **Evaluation scope:** Results limited to τ²-bench; generalizability to other agentic domains or LLM backbones is unknown.

## Confidence

- **Content-aware normalized surprisal mechanism:** Medium (strong theory but empirical impact depends on unstated filtering details).
- **Situational-awareness indicators mechanism:** Medium (clear formulas and ablation support, but performance depends on embedding quality).
- **Tail-focused aggregation mechanism:** High (robust ablations, theoretical coherence, clear empirical gains).
