# Optimizing Small Language Models for NL2SQL via Chain-of-Thought Fine-Tuning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22942
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22942
- **Reference count:** 7
- **Primary result:** CoT fine-tuning of a 7B model yields 54.5% execution accuracy on a complex benchmark, a significant improvement over both zero-shot (36.17%) and standard SFT (45.33%), while fine-tuning large models yields negligible or negative returns.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the high inference cost of large language models (LLMs) for NL2SQL tasks in enterprise settings. It investigates whether a small model (Qwen-7B) can be fine-tuned to approach production viability by leveraging Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning traces in its training data. The core finding is that CoT fine-tuning yields a substantial accuracy gain (from 36.17% zero-shot to 54.5% with CoT), while standard fine-tuning of large models (Gemini 2.5 Flash/Lite) leads to performance degradation, possibly due to representation collapse. This demonstrates that transferring reasoning patterns via explicit CoT enables compute-efficient small models to achieve near-production performance, offering significant cost and latency reductions.

## Method Summary
The authors curate an optimized training set of 5,500 NL2SQL examples from Spider by applying a custom complexity score to over-represent hard queries (40% hard, 50% medium, 10% easy), matching the difficulty distribution of a 600-query test benchmark. They then generate CoT reasoning traces for each example, structured into five steps: Query Analysis, Table Selection, Column Selection, Logic & Join Strategy, and Self-Validation. For fine-tuning, they use Qwen-7B-Chat with LoRA (rank 32, 5 epochs, learning rate 2e-5). The baseline uses standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) without CoT. Inference generates full responses (reasoning + SQL) from which the SQL code block is extracted and executed against a shadow database to compute execution accuracy.

## Key Results
- CoT fine-tuning boosts Qwen-7B's execution accuracy from a zero-shot baseline of 36.17% to 45.33% with standard SFT and further to 54.50% with CoT fine-tuning—an 18.33% absolute improvement over zero-shot.
- Fine-tuning large models (Gemini 2.5 Flash/Lite) yields negligible or negative returns; e.g., Flash drops from 73.17% to 72.50% overall, and from 62.5% to 57.5% on the top 40 complex queries.
- The CoT approach reduces "execution failed" errors for Qwen from 223 (zero-shot) to 106 (CoT), indicating improved syntactic validity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces in fine-tuning data teach small models multi-step reasoning, bridging the gap with larger models' emergent reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** The CoT dataset structures the NL2SQL task into discrete, human-like steps (Query Analysis, Table Selection, Column Selection, Logic Strategy, Self-Validation). Fine-tuning forces the small model to internalize this procedural logic, moving beyond shallow input-output mapping to learn a generalizable algorithm for translating intent to syntax.
- **Core assumption:** A small model's capacity is sufficient to learn and replicate a defined reasoning process if that process is made explicit in training, rather than expecting it to infer complex reasoning from final-answer-only pairs.
- **Evidence anchors:** The paper's abstract and section 6.1 describe the CoT structure; the corpus shows neighboring work on task decomposition supports the approach.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on large models causes "representation collapse," where the model abandons its generalized, multi-step reasoning for shallow, dataset-specific pattern matching, harming performance on complex queries.
- **Mechanism:** A highly capable model already possesses robust reasoning pathways. Standard SFT on direct SQL pairs acts as a regularizer that prioritizes fitting the training distribution's surface patterns over maintaining deep, compositional reasoning. This leads to overfitting on common structures and failure on out-of-distribution complex queries.
- **Core assumption:** Fine-tuning is not always additive for saturated models; it can be destructive if the training objective conflicts with the model's pre-trained internal representations and strategies.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 5.1 reports performance drops for Gemini models on complex queries and hypothesizes representation collapse.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Curating a training dataset biased towards complex SQL structures (Hard/Medium) with a custom complexity score maximizes the learning signal for improving on a challenging benchmark.
- **Mechanism:** The source Spider distribution is skewed toward medium-difficulty queries where large models are already saturated. By filtering and re-weighting the training set to emphasize hard queries (nested subqueries, complex joins), the fine-tuning process is forced to confront and learn the challenging reasoning patterns that matter for the target 600-query benchmark.
- **Core assumption:** The bottleneck for small model accuracy is not fundamental capacity but lack of exposure to and training on sufficiently difficult examples; a model can learn complex patterns if the training distribution is optimally aligned with the evaluation goal.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 details the complexity scoring; section 3.4 shows the distribution shift; section 7 ensures the benchmark matches the distribution.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning**
  - *Why needed here:* The core innovation is using CoT not just as a test-time prompt, but as a training signal. The engineer must understand that CoT decomposes a monolithic NL→SQL mapping into a learnable sequence of sub-tasks (schema linking, intent decomposition, operator selection).
  - *Quick check question:* What are the five explicit reasoning steps defined in the CoT training data (Listing 1), and how might each step reduce a specific class of SQL errors?
- **Concept: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**
  - *Why needed here:* Used for efficient fine-tuning of the 7B Qwen model. The engineer must grasp that LoRA updates only a small subset of parameters (adapters), making the process feasible and reducing catastrophic forgetting risks compared to full fine-tuning.
  - *Quick check question:* If LoRA uses a rank of 32 and target modules are `q_proj`, `v_proj`, approximately what percentage of the 7B model's parameters are being updated? (Hint: Parameter count for a standard transformer layer's q/v projections).
- **Concept: Execution Accuracy vs. Exact Match**
  - *Why needed here:* The primary metric is execution accuracy (running SQL and comparing results). The engineer must understand this decouples syntactic correctness from semantic equivalence, forgiving valid alternative SQL formulations that Exact Match would penalize.
  - *Quick check question:* For a query asking for "customers in Berlin," would a generated SQL that uses `LIKE '%Berlin%'` on a `city` column get the same execution accuracy as the ground truth `WHERE city = 'Berlin'`? Why or why not?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Dataset Curation Module -> CoT Data Generator -> Fine-Tuning Engine -> Validation & Evaluation Pipeline
- **Critical path:** Raw Spider Data -> Complexity Scoring -> Optimized Training Set (5.5k) -> CoT Annotation -> LoRA Fine-Tuning (Qwen) -> Generation on Benchmark (600q) -> Execution Accuracy Calculation. The same pipeline with "no CoT" annotation runs for the SFT baseline.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **CoT Token Overhead vs. Accuracy:** Longer outputs increase latency and compute per query (~20-30% more tokens). Tradeoff: Is the ~9% absolute gain (45%→54.5%) worth the added inference cost?
  - **Dataset Size vs. Quality:** The curated set (5.5k) is smaller than full Spider (~7k+). Tradeoff: Smaller set of high-quality, hard examples vs. larger, noisier, easier set.
  - **Schema Detail:** Providing full schema details increases context length but reduces column hallucination. Tradeoff: Context window limits vs. error rate.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **High "Execution Failed" rate:** Indicates syntax errors or invalid references. Monitor this metric.
  2. **Performance drop on Hard subset:** If model accuracy on the benchmark's Hard 40% subset is significantly lower than the overall 54.5%, the CoT training didn't transfer for complex logic.
  3. **CoT traces that are illogical or skip steps:** During generation, inspect CoT outputs for nonsensical reasoning even if final SQL is correct—indicates memorization, not reasoning.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Establish Baseline:** Run zero-shot evaluation of base Qwen-7B-Chat on the 600-query benchmark using the enhanced prompt (schema details + self-correction instruction). Record execution accuracy and failure count.
  2. **Standard SFT Replication:** Fine-tune Qwen on the same 5.5k query set but with direct SQL outputs (no CoT). Use identical LoRA config (or the final CoT config's for pure effect of CoT). Evaluate on benchmark.
  3. **CoT Fine-Tuning:** Fine-tune Qwen on the 5.5k CoT-annotated dataset (LoRA r=32, epochs=5). Evaluate on benchmark. This isolates the effect of the CoT reasoning signal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Does CoT fine-tuning generalize to real-world enterprise databases with messy schemas and ambiguous column names? *Basis:* Evaluation limited to Spider; no production data tested.
- **Open Question 2:** Can we internalize reasoning to reduce CoT token overhead without losing accuracy? *Basis:* Authors state future work will investigate internalized reasoning.
- **Open Question 3:** Why does standard fine-tuning harm large models' performance on complex queries? *Basis:* Representation collapse is hypothesized but not tested.
- **Open Question 4:** What is the optimal training data complexity distribution for small NL2SQL models? *Basis:* No ablation study on distribution ratios.

## Limitations
- The complexity scoring formula and CoT trace provenance (human vs. LLM) are undisclosed, hindering exact replication.
- Benchmark size (600 queries) may limit statistical robustness; only one small model (Qwen-7B) and Gemini family tested.
- CoT inference overhead (~20–30% more tokens) trades off with accuracy gains; cost-benefit analysis is superficial.

## Confidence
- **CoT fine-tuning boosts small model accuracy:** High (large absolute gain, consistent with SFT baseline).
- **Standard fine-tuning harms large models (representation collapse):** Medium (observed degradation on hard subset, mechanism inferred).
- **Difficulty-biased dataset curation is critical:** Medium (distribution alignment argued but scoring formula opaque).
