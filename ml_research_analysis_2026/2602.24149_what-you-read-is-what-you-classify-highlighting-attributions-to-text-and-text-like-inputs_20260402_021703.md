# What You Read is What You Classify: Highlighting Attributions to Text and Text-Like Inputs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.24149  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24149  
- **Reference count:** 24  
- **Primary result:** A mask-based XAI method for token sequences yields contiguous, faithful attributions with high efficiency.

## Executive Summary
Existing explainable AI (XAI) techniques struggle with token sequences (e.g., text, DNA), often producing fragmented or diffuse importance maps. This paper adapts a mask-based approach from image XAI to sequential data by learning a continuous mask that is applied via Hadamard product to the model’s token embeddings. The mask preserves embedding orientation while scaling influence, and is trained with a composite loss that balances classification accuracy, entropy maximization, area regularization, and smoothness. On a large nucleotide taxonomic classifier, masked inputs retain nearly original accuracy, while inverted masks cause drastic drops, demonstrating faithfulness. The method produces human‑readable contiguous explanations and scales orders of magnitude faster than LIME, generating explanations for over 100k sequences in minutes. However, evaluation is limited to a single genomic dataset and lacks cross‑domain validation.

## Method Summary
The authors propose to train an Explainer network that outputs a continuous mask over token embeddings. The mask is applied element‑wise (Hadamard product) to the embeddings, altering their magnitude but keeping orientation unchanged. The underlying classification model remains frozen. The Explainer is optimized with a loss combining four terms: (1) classification accuracy on masked inputs (preserving original predictions), (2) entropy maximization on inputs where the mask is inverted (ensuring the removed tokens are informative), (3) an area regularization to control sparsity, and (4) a total variation smoothness penalty to encourage contiguous masks. This multi‑objective training yields sparse, smooth masks that highlight a small set of contiguous tokens critical for the classification decision.

## Key Results
- Masked sequences retain near‑original accuracy (e.g., superkingdom 98.39% vs. 99.05% unmasked).
- Inverted masks cause a drastic accuracy drop (superkingdom to 46.70%), confirming the mask’s faithfulness.
- The method produces contiguous, human‑readable explanations and is highly scalable: 103,638 sequences explained in 20 minutes, far faster than LIME’s 26 seconds per sequence.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works because scaling embeddings by a learned mask changes their magnitude but not their directional orientation. In high‑dimensional embedding spaces, orientation often encodes semantic meaning, so preserving it while attenuating magnitude reduces the token’s influence without altering its abstract representation. The loss components collaboratively shape the mask: accuracy loss maintains model behavior; entropy loss forces the model to become uncertain when important tokens are removed, indicating the mask captures critical information; area and total variation losses promote sparsity and spatial continuity, yielding masks that align with human intuition of important regions (e.g., DNA motifs). The Explainer network thus learns to identify a minimal set of contiguous tokens whose removal maximally disrupts the prediction.

## Foundational Learning
- **Embedding Orientation:** The direction of a vector in embedding space often represents its semantic meaning; scaling its magnitude (via the mask) modulates influence without changing orientation. Quick check: Compute cosine similarity between original and masked embeddings—should remain close to 1.
- **Hadamard Product:** Element‑wise multiplication of two tensors; here used to apply the mask to the embedding matrix. Quick check: Multiply a dummy embedding matrix by a random mask; verify dimensions match and values are scaled.
- **Total Variation (TV) Loss:** Penalizes differences between neighboring mask values, encouraging smoothness and contiguity. Quick check: Calculate TV as sum of absolute differences between adjacent mask entries; minimization yields smoother masks.
- **Entropy Maximization:** By maximizing the entropy of predictions on masked‑out inputs, the method ensures that removing the masked tokens renders the model uncertain, a signal that those tokens were important. Quick check: Apply an inverted mask and compare prediction entropy to that of the original input; entropy should increase significantly.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
Explainer Network → Mask (continuous) → Hadamard(Embeddings) → Target Model → Predictions  
Loss = L_acc + λ_e L_entropy + λ_a L_area + λ_tv L_tv  
- **Critical path:**  
1. Input tokens are converted to embeddings (frozen).  
2. Explainer generates a mask per sequence.  
3. Masked embeddings are created via Hadamard product.  
4. Model produces predictions.  
5. L_acc compares predictions to true labels; L_entropy measures uncertainty on the masked‑out version; L_area penalizes deviation from a target mask sum; L_tv enforces smoothness.  
6. Combined loss backpropagates only through the Explainer.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Hyperparameters (λ_e, λ_a, λ_tv) balance sparsity, smoothness, and accuracy retention.  
  - The orientation‑preserving assumption may fail if magnitude encodes meaning.  
  - Training an Explainer incurs upfront cost but enables fast explanation generation at inference time.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Accuracy on masked inputs drops below acceptable threshold → mask too aggressive.  
  - Mask remains fragmented despite λ_tv → optimization difficulty or overly strict sparsity.  
  - Inverted mask does not reduce accuracy → mask fails to capture important tokens.  
  - Explainer overfolds training data → poor generalization to unseen sequences.  
- **First experiments:**  
  1. Replicate the NT50m results and compare against LIME/SHAP on fidelity and sparsity.  
  2. Ablate each loss component (set λ=0) to quantify its impact on mask quality and accuracy.  
  3. Apply the method to a text classification task (e.g., AG News with a CNN) to test generalization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do the hyperparameters λ_e, λ_a, and λ_tv in the loss function affect the trade-off between mask sparsity, smoothness, and classification accuracy?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper sets these hyperparameters to 1 without justification or ablation studies, as mentioned: "the hyperparameters λ_e, λ_a, and λ_tv are all 1."
- **Why unresolved:** Without sensitivity analysis, it is unclear if these values are optimal or if different settings could improve mask interpretability or fidelity for various tasks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct experiments varying each hyperparameter independently and measure metrics such as mask continuity (e.g., number of chunks), accuracy on masked inputs, and entropy of masked-out inputs.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the proposed masking approach generalize effectively to transformer models using the [CLS] token for classification and to other architectures beyond the NT50m genomic model?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper addresses an issue with mean pooling by applying an additional mask but notes that for CLS token classification, this may not be necessary. However, it does not test or compare these cases explicitly. Future work mentions applications to text or proteins, implying generalization is untested.
- **Why unresolved:** The method is only evaluated on NT50m with mean pooling; performance on CLS-based models or other architectures is unknown, limiting broader applicability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply the method to standard text classifiers (e.g., BERT for sentiment analysis) and protein classifiers, comparing mask quality, classification accuracy, and interpretability with the original results.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How well do the generated masks align with human-identified important regions in genomic sequences or text documents?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper claims masks are "human-readable" but provides no quantitative validation with human experts or ground truth annotations.
- **Why unresolved:** For XAI methods, human interpretability is critical, but without user studies or comparisons to known biological features (e.g., conserved DNA regions), the claim remains unverified.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct studies where domain experts (e.g., biologists for DNA, linguists for text) rate mask interpretability or compare masks with annotated important regions in benchmark datasets.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Is the assumption that element-wise multiplication of the mask with embeddings preserves orientation while reducing magnitude valid across all embedding spaces, particularly those from large language models?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper states: "changing the magnitude of the embedding vector but keeping the orientation unchanged" and "The abstraction of the meaning of apple is preserved (it is not suddenly an orange), but its influence on the modeling task is diminished."
- **Why unresolved:** This assumption is fundamental but not empirically verified across different models; embeddings may have complex geometries where magnitude changes affect semantic meaning.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze cosine similarity between original and masked embeddings for various models, and correlate magnitude reduction with downstream task performance to confirm orientation preservation.

## Limitations
- Evaluation only on one nucleotide taxonomic dataset; no tests on other domains (e.g., NLP, proteins) or modern architectures.
- Smoothness constraint may force contiguous explanations even when true importance is dispersed, risking biased attributions.
- “Human‑readable” asserted without quantification; lacking user studies or comparison to known motifs.

## Confidence
- Contiguous Explanations: Medium
- Faithfulness (accuracy retention & inversion drop): High
- Efficiency (fast generation for 100k+ sequences): High
