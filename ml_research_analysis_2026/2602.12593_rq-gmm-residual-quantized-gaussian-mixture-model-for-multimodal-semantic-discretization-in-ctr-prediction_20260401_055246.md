# RQ-GMM: Residual Quantized Gaussian Mixture Model for Multimodal Semantic Discretization in CTR Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12593
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12593
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Deployed RQ-GMM achieves a 1.502% lift in Advertiser Value over RQ-VAE and a 3.600% improvement over direct embedding concatenation in online A/B tests.

## Executive Summary
CTR prediction in advertising systems often requires converting continuous item embeddings (e.g., from BERT) into discrete categorical features for efficient feature learning. Existing vector quantization methods (VQ-VAE, RQ-VAE, RQ-KMeans) suffer from low codebook utilization, reconstruction errors, and limited semantic granularity. To address these challenges, this paper introduces RQ-GMM, which combines residual quantization with Gaussian Mixture Models. RQ-GMM learns codebooks via Expectation-Maximization (EM) on the original embeddings without an encoder-decoder network, and uses soft assignment to capture cluster uncertainty and shape. The resulting multi-level semantic IDs serve as stable categorical features.

Offline experiments on Amazon datasets demonstrate notable improvements: lower reconstruction RMSE (e.g., 0.117 vs. 0.121 for RQ-KMeans) and higher codebook utilization (~90% vs. <88%). Downstream CTR models (FNN, IPNN) consistently achieve better AUC and LogLoss compared to baselines. In a large-scale online A/B test on a short-video platform with hundreds of millions of daily users, RQ-GMM yields a 1.502% lift in Advertiser Value over RQ-VAE and a 3.600% improvement over direct embedding concatenation. The method is fully deployed in production.

## Method Summary
RQ-GMM performs multi-level residual quantization by learning a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) for each level directly on the embedding data. Starting from the original embedding x = r^(0), at level l a GMM with K components (means μ_k^(l), diagonal covariances Σ_k^(l), mixing coefficients π_k^(l)) is fitted to the current residual r^(l-1) using the EM algorithm. In the E-step, each residual is assigned posterior probabilities γ_{i,k} to each component; in the M-step, the parameters are updated via weighted averages. For inference, the component with maximum posterior is selected, its mean z_q^{(l)} is subtracted to form the new residual r^{(l)}. After L levels, the quantized vector is the sum of the selected means, and the sample obtains a discrete ID sequence [k_1^*, ..., k_L^*]. These IDs are embedded (e.g., via lookup tables) and concatenated with other features, optionally combined with the original embedding passed through a small MLP, and fed into a CTR backbone such as FNN or IPNN. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy loss. The RQ-GMM training is conducted offline on historical item embeddings, producing codebooks that are then used to generate semantic IDs for all items (including new ones) and stored in a feature store for online serving.

## Key Results
- **Reconstruction & utilization:** Lower RMSE (0.117 vs. 0.121 for RQ-KMeans) and higher codebook utilization (~90% vs. <88%) on Amazon datasets.
- **Offline CTR gains:** Consistent AUC and LogLoss improvements over VQ-VAE, RQ-VAE, and RQ-KMeans on both FNN and IPNN backbones.
- **Online impact:** 1.502% lift in Advertiser Value over RQ-VAE and 3.600% improvement over direct embedding concatenation in a production short-video platform; fully deployed.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** RQ-GMM models each codebook entry as a Gaussian distribution (mean and diagonal covariance) rather than a fixed vector, capturing cluster shape and uncertainty.
- **Mechanism:** For each quantization level, the residual vectors are modeled as a mixture of K Gaussians. The diagonal covariance matrix for each component characterizes the spread of data assigned to that component, allowing adaptive modeling of cluster geometry.
- **Core assumption:** The embedding space (or residual space at each level) can be well-approximated by a mixture of Gaussians with diagonal covariances, and the residuals are i.i.d. samples from this mixture.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Through Gaussian Mixture Models combined with residual quantization, RQ-GMM achieves superior codebook utilization and reconstruction accuracy."
  - [section 3.1.1]: "we model r^(l-1) as a mixture of K Gaussians: p(r^(l-1)) = sum_{k=1}^K π_k^{(l)} N(r^(l-1) | μ_k^{(l)}, Σ_k^{(l)})" and "diagonal covariance: Σ_k^{(l)} = diag(σ_{k,1}^2, ..., σ_{k,D}^2) for efficiency."
  - [corpus]: The related paper "Vector Quantization using Gaussian Variational Autoencoder" (arXiv:2512.06609) explores Gaussian-based quantization, indicating community interest. However, no direct evidence for RQ-GMM's effectiveness from the corpus.
- **Break condition:** If the true data distribution is highly non-Gaussian or has complex correlations not captured by diagonal covariances, the GMM may underfit, leading to poor reconstruction and low codebook utilization. Additionally, if EM initialization is poor, the algorithm may converge to a suboptimal local optimum.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** RQ-GMM employs soft assignment during training (via posterior probabilities) to handle boundary samples smoothly, which improves codebook utilization and reconstruction accuracy compared to hard assignment.
- **Mechanism:** In the E-step of EM, each residual vector is assigned to all K components with probabilities γ_{i,k}. These soft assignments are used to update the Gaussian parameters. However, for residual propagation (to maintain consistency with inference), the hard assignment (argmax) is used.
- **Core assumption:** Soft assignments provide more stable and informative updates for codebook learning, especially for points near cluster boundaries that would be arbitrarily assigned in hard clustering.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "leveraging probabilistic soft assignment to model the statistical structure of embedding spaces."
  - [section 3.1.2]: "We adopt soft assignment via posterior probabilities: γ_k^{(l)} = p(k|r^{(l-1)}) = ..." and "During inference, we select the component with highest posterior."
  - [section 4.2.2]: RQ-GMM achieves codebook utilization ~90% (e.g., 89.5% on Appliances) versus <88% for RQ-KMeans, and lower RMSE (0.117 vs 0.121).
  - [corpus]: Limited; the neighbor "Gaussian Mixture Vector Quantization with Aggregated Categorical Posterior" (arXiv:2410.10180) also uses GMM for VQ, supporting the soft assignment approach. But no direct comparison.
- **Break condition:** If the posterior probabilities become too concentrated (e.g., one component gets near 1 for most points), soft assignment degenerates to hard assignment and loses its advantage. Also, if EM iterations are cut short, posteriors may be inaccurate, harming reconstruction.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** RQ-GMM applies multi-level residual quantization, where each level quantizes the residual from the previous level, enabling hierarchical semantic discretization without an encoder-decoder network.
- **Mechanism:** Starting with r^(0)=x, at each level l, the residual r^(l-1) is quantized using the GMM to produce z_q^{(l)}. The new residual is r^{(l)} = r^{(l-1)} - z_q^{(l)}. After L levels, the final quantized representation is z_q = sum_{l=1}^L z_q^{(l)}. The codebooks are learned via EM directly on the original embeddings (no encoder-decoder).
- **Core assumption:** The residual at each level captures finer-grained information not represented by coarser levels, and the sum of quantized vectors approximates the original embedding. EM can learn codebooks directly from the data distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "RQ-GMM learns codebooks via EM without encoder-decoder networks" and "Residual quantization combined with Gaussian Mixture Models."
  - [section 3.1.3]: "Parameters are learned via EM algorithm on {x_i} with r_i^(0)=x_i, optimizing each level sequentially."
  - [section 3.1.4]: "After L levels, each sample obtains a discrete code sequence: ID(x)=[k_1^*, ..., k_L^*]"
  - [section 4.2.2]: RQ-GMM achieves higher utilization and lower RMSE than RQ-KMeans (which also uses residual quantization but with hard K-means) and RQ-VAE (which uses encoder-decoder).
  - [corpus]: The concept of residual quantization is present in RQ-VAE and RQ-KMeans; RQ-GMM integrates it with GMM. The neighbor "PCR-CA" uses parallel codebooks, but RQ-GMM's residual cascade is distinct.
- **Break condition:** If the number of levels L is too large, residuals become too small and noisy, leading to overfitting or instability. If the EM algorithm fails to converge within reasonable iterations, training may be inefficient.

## Foundational Learning
### Vector Quantization (VQ)
- **Why needed here:** VQ is the foundational technique for mapping continuous vectors to discrete codes; RQ-GMM extends VQ with probabilistic modeling and residuals.
- **Quick check:** What is the main objective of VQ? (Minimize reconstruction error with a fixed codebook, often with commitment losses to prevent codebook mismatch.)

### Residual Quantization (RQ)
- **Why needed here:** RQ allows multi-level discretization, capturing finer semantics; RQ-GMM uses this structure with GMM instead of hard clustering.
- **Quick check:** How does residual quantization differ from standard VQ? (It quantizes residuals sequentially and sums the quantized vectors to approximate the original.)

### Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) and Expectation-Maximization (EM)
- **Why needed here:** RQ-GMM models each codebook entry as a Gaussian and learns parameters via EM; understanding GMM and EM is crucial to grasp the probabilistic assignment.
- **Quick check:** What are the three parameters of a GMM component? (Mean, covariance, mixing coefficient.) How does EM update them? (E-step: compute posterior probabilities; M-step: update parameters using weighted averages.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Multimodal Embedding Extractor → RQ-GMM Quantizer → Semantic ID Generator → Feature Constructor → CTR Model. (Offline Pipeline and Online Serving support the flow.)
- **Critical path:**
  1. **Offline training:** Train RQ-GMM on historical item embeddings via EM until convergence. Save codebooks.
  2. **ID generation:** For all items (including new ones), run RQ-GMM inference to get semantic IDs, store in feature store.
  3. **CTR training:** Construct training samples using stored IDs and other features. Train CTR model with binary cross-entropy.
  4. **Deployment:** Load CTR model and feature store online. For each impression, fetch user, item (including semantic IDs), and context features, run CTR model, and output prediction.
  5. **Monitoring:** Track codebook utilization, reconstruction RMSE (offline), and CTR metrics (online) to detect degradation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  1. **Codebook size (K):** Larger K improves reconstruction and discriminability but increases memory and inference time (O(LKD) per sample). Typical values: 128 per level.
  2. **Number of levels (L):** More levels capture finer semantics but increase feature dimension (L * embedding_dim) and risk overfitting. Paper uses L=2.
  3. **Covariance structure:** Diagonal covariance is efficient but ignores feature correlations; full covariance is more expressive but O(KD^2) memory and computation. Diagonal is chosen for scalability.
  4. **EM iterations (T):** More iterations improve convergence but increase training time. Paper uses up to 30 with convergence threshold.
  5. **Original embedding inclusion:** Adding the original embedding (via MLP) improves performance but increases input dimension. Paper shows consistent gains.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Codebook collapse:** Utilization drops significantly (e.g., <70%). Indicates some codebook vectors are rarely used; may need better initialization or more iterations.
  2. **High reconstruction RMSE:** Suggests GMM is not fitting well; check K, L, or consider increasing EM iterations.
  3. **No CTR lift:** Semantic IDs not helping; verify that IDs are actually being used in CTR model, and that codebook utilization is high.
  4. **EM divergence:** Covariance matrices becoming singular (underflow); add small epsilon to diagonal or reduce K.
  5. **Online performance drop:** Possible train-serving skew; ensure that offline RQ-GMM is trained on up-to-date embeddings and that feature store updates are synchronized.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Discretization quality replication:** On Amazon Appliances dataset, train RQ-GMM with L=2, K=128, diagonal covariance. Compute RMSE and utilization. Expect RMSE ~0.117 and utilization ~89.5% (as in Table 3). Compare against RQ-KMeans (RMSE 0.121, util ~87%).
  2. **CTR performance integration:** Replace a baseline discretization (e.g., RQ-KMeans) with RQ-GMM in an FNN model on the same dataset. With original embedding included, expect AUC improvement of at least 0.01 (e.g., 0.678 vs 0.667) and LogLoss reduction.
  3. **Convergence behavior:** Plot RMSE over EM iterations for RQ-GMM and RQ-KMeans on a held-out validation set. RQ-GMM should converge faster and with less fluctuation, as shown in Figure 1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How to adaptively determine codebook size per quantization level instead of using a fixed value (e.g., 128)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work mentions "adaptive codebook size selection across levels," but the current method uses fixed sizes.
- **Why unresolved:** Fixed sizes may underutilize codebooks for simpler residuals or waste capacity for complex ones, impacting reconstruction and CTR gains.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Propose a variance- or entropy-driven mechanism to adjust K per level, evaluate RMSE, codebook utilization, and CTR metrics across datasets.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can RQ-GMM be extended to jointly model multiple modalities (e.g., image and text) in a unified probabilistic framework rather than discretizing each separately?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work cites "unified probabilistic modeling of multiple modalities," but the current approach processes modalities independently.
- **Why unresolved:** Separate modeling misses cross-modal correlations that could produce more discriminative semantic IDs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Design a multi-modal GMM operating on concatenated or aligned embeddings, compare reconstruction and CTR performance against separate RQ-GMM on multimodal datasets.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What are the individual contributions and interaction effects between semantic IDs and continuous embeddings in the coarse-to-fine CTR model, and how does their fusion impact training dynamics?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper combines both features but lacks ablation studies or gradient analysis to isolate their roles.
- **Why unresolved:** Without this, it is unclear whether gains arise from IDs, embeddings, or synergy, limiting optimal feature engineering.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablate semantic IDs or embeddings, monitor gradient norms and convergence speeds, and test alternative fusion strategies (e.g., attention) in CTR models.

## Limitations
- EM initialization and regularization details are omitted, risking suboptimal or unstable training.
- Exact CTR model configuration unclear, hampering reproducibility.
- Evaluation limited to Amazon datasets and one short-video platform; domain generalization unproven.

## Confidence
- **High:** Improvements in codebook utilization and reconstruction RMSE (Table 3) are well‑supported across datasets.
- **Medium:** Offline and online CTR gains are consistent but lack cross‑domain validation and detailed architecture reporting.
- **Low‑Medium:** Assertions that soft assignment and GMM modeling are the main drivers lack direct ablation.
