# FC-CONAN: An Exhaustively Paired Dataset for Robust Evaluation of Retrieval Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.01350
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01350
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Primary result:** Exhaustive annotation of all HS–CN pairs reveals hundreds of missing appropriate pairs; metrics increase with more positives; SBERT-based rankers achieve highest scores and stability.

## Executive Summary
Sparse annotation of hate speech (HS)–counter-narrative (CN) pairs in existing datasets like CONAN causes underestimation of retrieval system performance, as unlabeled pairs are incorrectly treated as negatives. To address this, the authors created FC-CONAN by exhaustively annotating all 5,805 possible combinations from 45 HS messages and 129 CNs, resulting in four quality-graded partitions (Diamond, Gold, Silver, Bronze). Evaluation across these partitions shows that performance metrics (e.g., Hit Ratio@10, MRR@10) improve as more positive pairs are included, confirming a lower-bound bias in sparsely labeled datasets. Semantic embedding-based rankers (SBERT variants) outperform lexical (TF-IDF, BM25) and LLM-based (GPT-4o) approaches, achieving the highest average scores and greatest stability.

## Method Summary
The method involves constructing an exhaustively labeled dataset from a subset of CONAN. Nine annotators and four validators performed a two-stage process to label all 45×129 = 5,805 HS–CN pairs as “appropriate” or “non-appropriate,” producing four partitions with varying quality thresholds. For evaluation, a fixed candidate pool of 100 CNs (from the Bronze partition) is ranked for each query HS (also from Bronze). Training data is built from CONAN (English) by excluding any HS or CN appearing in the Bronze partition to prevent train/test contamination. Systems are then evaluated by computing retrieval metrics against the appropriateness labels in each partition.

## Key Results
- Exhaustive labeling added hundreds of previously unannotated appropriate CN pairs for given HS messages.
- Performance metrics (Hit Ratio@10, MRR@10) consistently increased from Diamond → Gold → Silver → Bronze partitions, demonstrating underestimation in sparse datasets.
- SBERT-based rankers achieved the highest average scores (~0.32) and lowest coefficient of variation (CV%), indicating greater stability across partitions compared to lexical and LLM-based methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism underlying the results is two-fold. First, sparse annotation in typical datasets treats unlabeled HS-CN pairs as negatives, which are often false negatives. This artificially depresses recall and other retrieval metrics. Exhaustive annotation in FC-CONAN uncovers these missing true positives, causing metrics to rise as more appropriate pairs are included (Diamond→Bronze). Second, semantic embedding-based rankers (SBERT variants) capture nuanced appropriateness better than lexical (TF-IDF, BM25) or LLM-based (GPT-4o) methods because appropriateness often depends on semantic alignment rather than exact word overlap. This explains SBERT’s higher scores and stability across partitions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sparse annotation bias:** Unlabeled pairs in datasets are treated as negatives, artificially lowering metrics. *Why needed:* Understanding this bias explains why exhaustive labeling is necessary for accurate evaluation. *Quick check:* Compare metrics on a dataset before and after adding missing positives.
- **Exhaustive pairwise labeling:** All possible HS–CN combinations are annotated. *Why needed:* Eliminates false negatives and provides a complete ground truth. *Quick check:* Verify total pairs = number of HS messages × number of CNs.
- **Quality-graded partitions (Diamond/Gold/Silver/Bronze):** Different stringency levels for “appropriate” labels. *Why needed:* Allows measuring robustness of metrics to label quality. *Quick check:* Count of “appropriate” pairs should increase from Diamond to Bronze.
- **Retrieval metrics (Hit Ratio@10, MRR@10):** Standard measures for ranked retrieval. *Why needed:* Quantifies how well systems rank relevant CNs highly. *Quick check:* Manually compute for a small set: Hit@10 = 1 if any appropriate CN in top-10; MRR = 1/(rank of first appropriate CN).
- **Embedding-based rankers (SBERT):** Use semantic similarity via pre-trained transformers. *Why needed:* Capture nuanced semantic alignment between HS and CN beyond lexical overlap. *Quick check:* Compute cosine similarity between SBERT embeddings of HS and CN texts.
- **Lexical vs. semantic methods:** Comparison of TF-IDF/BM25 (lexical) with SBERT (semantic) and GPT-4o (LLM). *Why needed:* Highlights the importance of semantic understanding for CN retrieval. *Quick check:* Test if semantic methods retrieve CNs with paraphrased but lexically different text.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Two-stage annotation pipeline (annotators → validators) → Four quality partitions → Fixed candidate pool (100 CNs) → Query HS set (from Bronze) → Training data construction (exclude Bronze IDs) → Model training (on non-Bronze CONAN) → Ranking of candidates → Metric computation against partition labels.

Critical path: Prepare exhaustive labels → Define evaluation splits (candidate pool, query set) → Build non-overlapping training set → Train/apply rankers → Evaluate per partition.

Design tradeoffs: Exhaustive labeling is costly but eliminates bias; fixed 100-CN pool simplifies evaluation but limits generalizability; quality partitions trade off precision vs. recall.

Failure signatures: Training data includes any Bronze HS/CN IDs (contamination); candidate pool differs from paper’s 100 CNs; metrics computed against wrong partition labels.

## Limitations
- Candidate pool limited to 100 CNs from 129 Bronze CNs; selection criteria unspecified.
- No inter-annotator agreement statistics reported for partition labels.
- Training data construction details (exact CONAN split and exclusion logic) not fully specified.

## Confidence
- **Sparse annotation bias:** High – Directly demonstrated via partition comparisons.
- **Added hundreds of new positives:** High – Exhaustive labeling of all pairs necessarily uncovers missing pairs.
- **SBERT superiority and stability:** Medium – Empirical results but depend on unresolved data splits and pool selection; no statistical significance tests.
