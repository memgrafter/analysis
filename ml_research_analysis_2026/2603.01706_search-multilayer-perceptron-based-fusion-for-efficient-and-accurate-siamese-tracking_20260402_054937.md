# Search Multilayer Perceptron-Based Fusion for Efficient and Accurate Siamese Tracking

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01706
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01706
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SEAT achieves state-of-the-art results on four generic (OTB2015, GOT-10K, VOT2019, NFS30) and three aerial (UAV10FPS, UAVDT, VISDRONE) benchmarks, with SEAT_LT reaching 69.7% success on OTB2015 at 80.54 FPS and SEAT_AL achieving 0.593 success on UAV20L on NPU.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the accuracy-efficiency trade-off in Siamese visual tracking. While CNN-based fusion (e.g., cross-correlation) is efficient but operates coarsely at the patch level, Transformer-based fusion achieves pixel-level accuracy at high computational cost. The authors propose SEAT, an MLP-based tracker using a coarse-to-fine fusion framework: a Coarse Fusion MLP (CFM) for early, efficient pixel-level integration and a Refine Fusion MLP (RFM) with Wave-MLP blocks for fine-grained refinement. To manage the quadratic complexity of Wave-MLP, they introduce a hierarchical MLP-based Correlation Architecture Space (MCAS) and a Harmony-Relaxation differentiable neural architecture search (DNAS) strategy. This decouples channel-width optimization from other architectural choices, automatically balancing depth and width. Two variants emerge: SEAT_LT (GPU-focused) and SEAT_AL (NPU-focused), both delivering state-of-the-art accuracy and speed across generic and aerial benchmarks.

## Method Summary
SEAT introduces a novel neck architecture for Siamese tracking based on MLPs. The backbone (e.g., LightTrack_M) extracts template and search features. The **Coarse Fusion MLP (CFM)** merges these features by pooling the template via ROIAlign, broadcasting it, and performing a matrix multiplication with the search features to generate a coarse interaction heatmap—a linear approximation of cross-attention. The **Refine Fusion MLP (RFM)** then iteratively refines this heatmap using stacked Wave-MLP blocks, which employ Phase-Aware Token Mixing for global dependencies. To avoid the quadratic FLOPs of Wave-MLP relative to channel width, the authors construct a hierarchical search space (MCAS) grouping fixed-channel Wave-MLP variants into Harmonization blocks. A **Harmony-Relaxation** DNAS strategy decouples channel-width selection (optimized via β parameters with Adam) from intra-block operation choices (optimized via α parameters with SGD), guiding the search toward efficient architectures. The final tracking head decodes the refined response map into classification and regression outputs for bounding box prediction.

## Key Results
- SEAT_LT (GPU) achieves 69.7% success on OTB2015, 63.6% AO on GOT-10K, and 34.0% EAO on VOT2019 while running at 80.54 FPS (12.4 ms latency).
- SEAT_AL (NPU) reaches 0.593 success on UAV20L, outperforming other NPU-oriented trackers in both speed and accuracy.
- SEAT ranks state-of-the-art on four generic (OTB2015, GOT-10K, VOT2019, NFS30) and three aerial (UAV10FPS, UAVDT, VISDRONE) benchmarks, demonstrating strong generalization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Coarse Fusion MLP (CFM) provides efficient pixel-level co-activation.
The CFM module fuses template and search feature maps at pixel level with computational cost comparable to attention but without softmax and multi-head projections. It pools a template ROI via ROIAlign, duplicates it to match search spatial dimensions, adds a broadcast bias to search features, then multiplies with the reshaped full template feature matrix to produce an interaction map. A linear projection yields a channel-reduced heatmap. This operation approximates cross-attention between search pixels and template tokens plus a self-attention effect from the target region. The core assumption is that a single linear projection after matrix multiplication suffices for pixel-wise similarity modeling. Evidence includes Lemma 2 mathematically equating CFM output to attention terms, though ablation of alternatives is limited. Break condition: ROIAlign failure on extreme aspect ratios, rotations, or occlusions misrepresents the template, leading to noisy interactions.

### Mechanism 2: Refine Fusion MLP (RFM) with Wave‑MLP blocks offers pixel-level refinement within manageable complexity.
RFM stacks Wave-MLP blocks to refine the coarse heatmap into a high-fidelity response map, capturing global dependencies. Each Wave-MLP uses Phase-Aware Token Mixing (PATM) to mix tokens via learned amplitude and phase. Naive stacking yields O(C'²) FLOPs (Lemma 1). MCAS groups fixed-channel Wave-MLP variants into Harmonization blocks, and Harmony-Relaxation uses a β parameter to dynamically choose inter-block channel pathways, decoupling channel width from depth. The core assumption is that linear token mixing is sufficient for fine-grained refinement and that hierarchical, channel-decoupled search optimizes depth-width trade-offs. Evidence includes the quadratic bound proof and search space design; however, comparative NAS studies are absent. Break condition: Harmony-Relaxation collapse to uniform β (losing efficiency) or insufficient channels (losing capacity).

### Mechanism 3: Harmony‑Relaxation Differentiable NAS automatically optimizes the neck architecture.
MCAS with Harmony-Relaxation extends DARTS to decouple channel-width optimization (β) from operation selection (α). In the supernet, each Harmonization block has a fixed output channel (256/320/384). Inter-block connections are weighted by β (Adam optimizer); within each layer, candidate Wave-MLP variants are weighted by α (SGD). Training alternates weight updates (inner loop) and architecture updates (outer loop) with a cost-aware loss including latency/FLOPs. The core assumption is that separate optimization stabilizes search and yields better accuracy-efficiency trade-offs than joint optimization. Evidence includes the formal equations for β/α relaxation and three-stage training; however, external validation (e.g., vs. FBNetV2) is weak. Break condition: training instability (β divergence, α oscillation) prevents convergence to optimal architectures.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Backbone (LightTrack_M or AlexNet) -> Coarse Fusion MLP (CFM) -> Refine Fusion MLP (RFM) [Harmonization Blocks H0–H3] -> Tracking Head (CLS and REG towers).
- **Critical path:** `t,s → Backbone → F_t, F_s → CFM → z → H0 → H1 → H2 → H3 → Head → {cls, reg}`. The neck (CFM+RFM) is searchable; backbone and head fixed during NAS.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Accuracy vs. efficiency: CNN fusion (patch-level, fast) vs. Transformer (pixel-level, heavy); MLP-based (CFM+Wave-MLP) aims for pixel-level with lower overhead but faces quadratic channel cost.
  - Hardware targets: SEAT_LT favors GPU throughput (80+ FPS); SEAT_AL targets NPU low-power (20+ FPS on Horizon X3).
  - Search complexity vs. performance: Larger MCAS increases candidates but prolongs DNAS; Harmony-Relaxation reduces burden by decoupling channel from other choices.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. Illumination sensitivity: Wave-MLP amplitude branch intensity-dependence degrades performance under lighting changes.
  2. NAS instability: β/α divergence or collapse to uniform channel allocation undermines efficiency gains.
  3. Memory bottleneck: Large coarse heatmap `z` may exceed NPU buffers if channels unconstrained.
  4. ROIAlign mismatch: Fixed 1×1 pooled template fails for extreme scaling/rotation/occlusion.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablation of fusion modules: Compare CFM vs. CNN/Transformer pre-fusion and RFM vs. no post-fusion (accuracy/speed on GOT10K/OTB/VOT).
  2. Generic benchmark evaluation: Test SEAT_LT against lightweight GPU trackers on OTB2015, GOT10K, VOT2019, NFS30 (success/EAO/FPS).
  3. Aerial deployment validation: Deploy SEAT_AL on Horizon X3 NPU; evaluate on UAV10FPS, UAVDT, VISDRONE; measure latency/FPS on edge chip.

## Limitations
- CFM robustness depends on ROIAlign; extreme distortions (aspect ratio, rotation, occlusion) may misrepresent template and degrade fusion.
- Harmony-Relaxation NAS risks instability (β/α divergence) or collapse to suboptimal channel allocations.
- Coarse heatmap memory may exceed NPU buffers without tight channel constraints.
- Evidence base for MLP-based fusion in Siamese tracking is weak (limited citations, no direct prior work).
- Wave-MLP amplitude branch is intensity-sensitive, potentially hurting performance under illumination changes.

## Confidence
- CFM efficiency & pixel-level claim: Medium (Lemma 2 support, scarce ablation).
- RFM refinement with Wave-MLP: Medium (quadratic bound proven, search stability not fully evaluated).
- Harmony-Relaxation NAS decoupling: Low (novel, prone to instability, minimal external validation).
- SOTA benchmark results: High (extensive evaluations, though benchmarks may be saturated).
- NPU deployment (SEAT_AL): Medium (FPS reported, power/thermal metrics missing).
