# Online Learning and Equilibrium Computation with Ranking Feedback
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19221
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19221
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Algorithms achieve sublinear regret for time‑average ranking feedback under constant temperature; time‑average joint play converges to an approximate coarse correlated equilibrium; impossibility results hold for instantaneous feedback and very small temperature.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates online learning when an agent receives only rankings (permutations) over actions instead of numeric utilities. Two ranking mechanisms are studied: instantaneous utility rankings (InstUtil Rank) and time‑average utility rankings (AvgUtil Rank), in both full‑information and bandit settings. The core method decomposes the Plackett‑Luce model into pairwise comparisons to estimate utilities from rankings, then feeds these estimates to a stable online learning oracle (e.g., Follow‑The‑Regularized‑Leader). Results include impossibility of sublinear regret for InstUtil Rank and for AvgUtil Rank with very small temperature, positive regret guarantees under sublinear utility variation (Instutil, AvgUtil bandit) or without variation (Avgutil full‑info), and convergence to an ε‑approximate coarse correlated equilibrium (CCE) with exploitability ε = O(1/T) in multi‑player games. Empirical validation on an online LLM routing task shows decreasing average regret over time.

## Method Summary
The method comprises a utility estimator and a no‑regret oracle. The estimator uses a sliding window of m recent rankings; for each pair of actions it counts head‑to‑head occurrences and applies the inverse sigmoid to the Plackett‑Luce pairwise probability to recover utilities relative to a baseline. For instantaneous utilities (InstUtil Rank), this estimate is fed to any full‑information no‑regret algorithm; bandit feedback adds uniform exploration to guarantee all actions appear sufficiently often. For time‑average utilities (AvgUtil Rank), the estimator targets the time‑average of utilities. In full‑information settings, a stable oracle (small change in cumulative utilities leads to small change in strategies) suffices even with arbitrary utility variation. In bandit settings, time is partitioned into blocks of size M; block‑wise empirical average utilities are differenced to obtain an estimate of the time‑average, which is then used by the stable oracle with exploration. Theoretical analysis yields regret bounds and equilibrium Convergence guarantees under conditions on temperature τ, utility variation P(T), and oracle stability.

## Key Results
- Impossibility of sublinear regret for InstUtil Rank and for AvgUtil Rank with very small temperature (τ ≤ O(1/T) or similar).
- Sublinear regret for AvgUtil Rank full‑information without any variation assumption; for Instutil Rank and AvgUtil Rank bandit, sublinear regret holds under sublinear total variation (P(T)=O(T^q), q<1 for Instutil, q<1/3 for bandit) and constant τ.
- In normal‑form games, time‑average joint strategies from these algorithms yield an ε‑approximate CCE with exploitability ε = O(1/T).
- Empirical LLM routing experiment demonstrates decreasing average regret over time.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Pairwise Utility Estimation under Plackett‑Luce
The learner reconstructs utilities from rankings by exploiting the Plackett‑Luce model: for any two actions a and b, P(a ranked before b) = sig((u(a)−u(b))/τ). By maintaining a sliding window of m recent permutations, the learner counts pairwise co‑occurrences, computes the empirical frequency of a beating b, and applies the inverse sigmoid (logit) to estimate the utility difference relative to a baseline (last action fixed at 0). This yields an unbiased estimator provided the window contains enough samples of each pair (mp⁴ ≳ log(1/δ)). The error bound (Theorem 6.1) scales with 1/√m plus the utility variation within the window. The mechanism fails if τ is extremely small (sigmoid near‑step) or large (rankings near uniform), because the pairwise marginals become degenerate or too noisy to invert accurately.

### Mechanism 2: Plug‑and‑Play Learning for InstUtil Rank
For instantaneous‑utility rankings, the estimator (Mechanism 1) is combined with any full‑information no‑regret oracle. At each timestep t, the estimator outputs eu(t) using the last m permutations; the oracle then produces the next mixed strategy π(t+1). In bandit feedback, a uniform exploration mixture (γ/|A|) is blended into the proposal to ensure each action appears with probability at least γ/|A|, guaranteeing sufficient pairwise data. This algorithm (Algorithm 2) guarantees sublinear regret under: (i) sublinear total variation P(T)=∑_{t=2}^T‖u(t)−u(t−1)‖∞ = O(T^q) with q<1, and (ii) constant τ>0. The regret bound (Theorem 6.2) reflects both variation and estimation error. If P(T) grows linearly (q≥1), estimation errors accumulate linearly, causing linear regret (Theorem 5.1). Similarly, very small τ leads to impossibility (Theorem 5.1).

### Mechanism 3: Stable Oracles for AvgUtil Rank
For time‑average utility rankings, the estimator directly targets u_avg(t) = (1/t)∑_{s=1}^t u(s). The key insight is that a small perturbation in cumulative utilities leads to a small change in the strategies of a *stable* online learning oracle (Assumption 7.1). In full‑information settings, feeding the estimated u_avg(t) (from sliding‑window pairwise counts) to such a stable oracle (e.g., FTRL with any strongly convex regularizer) yields sublinear regret *without any assumption on utility variation* (Theorem 7.2), provided τ is a positive constant (τ ≤ O(1/(T log T)) is impossible, Theorem 5.3). In bandit settings, the algorithm partitions time into blocks of size M and uses a block‑differencing trick to estimate the average utility per action within each block; these block‑wise estimates are aggregated to form the time‑average estimate. This approach works with sublinear variation (P(T)=O(T^q), q<1/3) and constant τ (τ ≤ O(1/log T) impossible, Theorem 5.4), and also requires a stable oracle and uniform exploration γ. Stability prevents error accumulation in both settings.

## Foundational Learning
- **External regret**: Measures cumulative performance relative to the best fixed action in hindsight. *Why needed*: It is the primary metric for online learning. *Quick check*: Given utilities u(t) and mixed strategies π(t), write the external regret formula: R(T) = max_{bπ∈ΔA} ∑_{t=1}^T ⟨u(t), bπ - π(t)⟩.
- **Plackett–Luce model**: A generative model for rankings from latent utilities and temperature τ. *Why needed*: It characterizes how observed rankings are produced from underlying utilities. *Quick check*: For two actions a and b, P(a ranked before b) = exp(u(a)/τ) / (exp(u(a)/τ) + exp(u(b)/τ)).
- **No‑regret algorithms (e.g., FTRL)**: Guarantee sublinear external regret under full numeric feedback. *Why needed*: They provide robust decision‑making once utility estimates are available. *Quick check*: What property of the regularizer in FTRL gives no‑regret guarantees? (Strong convexity.)
- **Coarse correlated equilibrium (CCE)**: A solution concept where no player can improve by deviating given a joint distribution over actions. *Why needed*: When all players run no‑regret algorithms, the time‑average joint strategy converges to an approximate CCE; exploitability follows from individual regrets. *Quick check*: Given a joint strategy π, define its exploitability as the maximum over players i of the utility gain from best responding to π.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Estimator → NoRegretOracle; Bandit: Estimator → NoRegretOracle → ExplorationModule; AvgUtil Bandit: Estimator → BlockManager → NoRegretOracle → ExplorationModule.
- **Critical path** per timestep: (1) Propose actions (full set A for full‑info; sample from π(t) for bandit). (2) Receive ranking σ(t). (3) Update sliding window; compute utility estimate eu(t) via Estimator (or for AvgUtil bandit, BlockManager aggregates block‑wise estimates). (4) NoRegretOracle receives eu(t) and outputs π(t+1); for bandit, ExplorationModule blends π(t+1) with uniform (γ/|A|). (5) For AvgUtil bandit, every M steps BlockManager updates block statistics. (6) In multi‑player games, each player runs independently; after T rounds the platform computes the empirical joint distribution.
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Window size m: larger reduces noise but increases bias if utilities drift fast; for Instutil with variation P(T)=Θ(T^q), optimal m = Θ(T^{2/3}).
  - Exploration rate γ (bandit): must scale as γ = Ω((P(T)/T)^{1/5}); too large adds linear term 2γT.
  - Temperature τ: must be a non‑trivial constant; error bounds contain factor 1/(e^{1/τ}+1)^2 ≈ τ² for small τ, making estimation unreliable.
  - Block size M (AvgUtil bandit): larger M reduces variance but requires P(T)=o(T^{1/3}); typical M scales as T^{5/6}/√P(T).
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Linear regret: check τ is not too small (e.g., τ=0.01) and utility variation is sublinear.
  - Poor action coverage (bandit): if some actions rarely appear in the window, increase γ.
  - Instability in AvgUtil: using a non‑stable oracle (e.g., vanilla multiplicative weights without strong convexity) may cause regret to diverge; use FTRL with L2‑regularization.
  - Game exploitability stuck: monitor each player’s external regret; if any is linear, CCE exploitability won’t vanish. Ensure algorithms satisfy stability and variation assumptions.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Estimation accuracy**: Simulate known utilities and PL rankings; run Algorithm 1 with varying m and τ. Plot ‖eu(t)−u(t)‖ vs. m to verify O(1/√m) decay plus variation term.
  2. **InstUtil full‑info learning**: Use smooth drift (e.g., u₁(t)=0.1·sin(2πt/T)). Run Algorithm 2 with Hedge oracle, τ=1. Cumulative regret should be o(T) and match Theorem 6.2.
  3. **AvgUtil full‑info (no‑variation regime)**: Construct highly varying utilities (piecewise constant with random jumps). Run Algorithm 3 with FTRL; sublinear regret should still be achieved (Theorem 7.2) despite linear total variation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Can sublinear regret be achieved for AvgUtil Rank under bandit feedback without Assumption 5.2 (sublinear utility variation), or is constant τ fundamentally hard? The bandit algorithm requires the assumption while full‑info does not; the necessity remains open.
- **Open Question 2**: Do the proposed ranking‑based algorithms succeed on real‑world ranking feedback datasets (e.g., ride‑sharing, dating)? The paper only reports an LLM routing simulation; real data may contain noise, missing information, and human biases.
- **Open Question 3**: What is the precise minimal temperature τ* that permits sublinear regret in AvgUtil Rank (full‑info and bandit)? The paper shows impossibility for extremely small τ and algorithms for constant τ, leaving a substantial gap in the threshold.

## Limitations
- Plackett‑Luce assumption may be violated in real rankings, harming estimation accuracy.
- Temperature τ must be a non‑trivial constant; τ → 0 inevitably causes linear regret.
- Bandit versions require exploration rate γ and block size M with only asymptotic guidance, making finite‑time tuning non‑trivial.
- AvgUtil Rank bandit further restricts utility variation exponent to q < 1/3.
- Empirical validation is limited to a single LLM routing task; broader real‑world testing is needed.

## Confidence
- Theoretical guarantees (impossibility, InstUtil, AvgUtil algorithms): **High** (backed by formal theorems).
- Equilibrium convergence (ε → 0): **High** (direct corollary of no‑regret, Theorem 8.1).
- LLM routing empirical results: **Medium** (single‑task evaluation; hyperparameters from limited grid search; independent replication needed).

## Next Checks
1. **τ‑sensitivity study**: Sweep τ across {0.01,0.1,0.5,1,2} in synthetic stationary and drifting environments to pinpoint thresholds where regret transitions from linear to sublinear.
2. **Robustness to PL violations**: Inject Mallows noise or deterministic perturbations into PL rankings and measure resulting changes in estimation error and regret.
3. **Bandit hyperparameter scaling**: For Avgutil bandit with synthetic sequences of known variation exponent q < 1/3, test the theoretical scaling of block size M (e.g., M ∝ T^{5/6}/√P(T)) against fixed M; confirm that correct scaling yields sublinear regret while deviations cause linear regret.
