# ERPO: Token-Level Entropy-Regulated Policy Optimization for Large Reasoning Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28204  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28204  
- **Reference count:** 33  
- **Primary result:** ERPO improves accuracy (e.g., +1.27% for 7B on average) and reduces generation length (~8%) over GRPO on mathematical reasoning benchmarks by introducing token-level entropy regulation.

## Executive Summary
ERPO addresses a core limitation of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR): uniform sequence-level credit assignment ignores token-level heterogeneity in reasoning chains, leading to entropy collapse and redundant generation. It introduces a token-level optimization framework that (1) identifies Critical Decision Pivots (CDPs) via token entropy, (2) adaptively amplifies exploration at CDPs using an entropy‑aware gating mechanism, (3) normalizes token progress signals via bucket‑based implicit normalization to mitigate difficulty bias across reasoning stages, and (4) synthesizes outcome‑anchored advantages to prevent reward hacking. Experiments across Qwen2.5 models (1.5B, 3B, 7B) on MATH, AMC23, AIME24, and AIME25 show consistent accuracy gains and more concise reasoning paths with comparable training time.

## Method Summary
ERPO modifies GRPO by replacing its uniform token‑level advantage with a dynamically synthesized signal. For each rollout, it computes per‑token entropy \(H_{i,t}\) and an implicit progress signal \(s_{i,t} = \beta_{\text{progress}} (\log \pi_\theta - \log \pi_{\text{ref}})\). Tokens are assigned to relative position buckets, and \(s_{i,t}\) is normalized within its bucket (\(\tilde{s}_{i,t}\)) to remove stage‑specific difficulty bias. A gating weight \(W_{i,t} = \text{sigmoid}(\gamma \cdot \text{Norm}_G(H_{i,t}))\) amplifies gradients at high‑entropy CDPs. A token‑level process reward \(\Psi_{i,t}\) is formed by combining \(W_{i,t}\), the sign of the group advantage, and \(\tilde{s}_{i,t}\), then rescaled to a target standard deviation. The final token advantage \(\hat{A}_{i,t}^{\text{final}} = \text{Norm}_G(\hat{A}_i^{\text{group}} + \eta \Psi_{i,t})\) is plugged into the clipped GRPO surrogate loss with a KL penalty. Training uses LoRA (rank 32, α 64), AdamW (lr 5e‑6), global batch size 16, and a single epoch.

## Key Results
- For Qwen2.5‑7B, average accuracy across benchmarks rises from 19.43% (GRPO) to 20.70% (ERPO).  
- Generation length decreases by ~8% (e.g., AMC23: 776→756 tokens; AIME24: 950→887 tokens).  
- Training time remains comparable to GRPO, offering a better efficiency‑accuracy trade‑off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** ERPO combats entropy collapse by identifying Critical Decision Pivots (CDPs) via token entropy and adaptively amplifying gradient updates at these tokens using an entropy‑aware gating function.  
**Mechanism:** Compute entropy \(H_{i,t}\) per token. Normalize within each prompt group \(G\) to \(\text{Norm}_G(H_{i,t})\), then apply \(\text{sigmoid}(\gamma \cdot \text{Norm}_G(H_{i,t}))\) to obtain gating weight \(W_{i,t}\), which scales the token‑level advantage.  
**Core assumption:** High entropy tokens mark reasoning junctures where the policy is uncertain and exploration is beneficial; low entropy tokens are deterministic steps where redundancy should be suppressed.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "identifying Critical Decision Pivots (CDPs) through token entropy, (2) adaptively amplifying exploration at CDPs via entropy‑aware gating"  
- Paper: Section 2 defines CDPs; perturbation study shows 35.2% accuracy drop when high‑entropy tokens are disturbed.  
- Corpus: Neighbor work (arXiv:2510.09369) notes GRPO’s uniform advantage fails to address "sparse token rewards inherent to chain‑of‑thought reasoning".  
**Break condition:** If entropy estimates are noisy (small batch, low‑probability tokens) or most tokens have high entropy, gating may amplify noise and destabilize training.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** ERPO removes temporal bias by normalizing token‑level progress signals within aligned buckets, ensuring fair comparison across reasoning stages.  
**Mechanism:** Compute relative progress \(\tau_{i,t}=t/|o_i|\) and assign each token to one of \(K\) buckets. For each bucket \(B_k\) and group \(G\), calculate the Z‑score \(\tilde{s}_{i,t} = \text{Norm}_{B_k}(s_{i,t})\) of the implicit progress signal \(s_{i,t} = \beta_{\text{progress}} (\log \pi_\theta - \log \pi_{\text{ref}})\).  
**Core assumption:** Token informativeness is stage‑dependent; a token in planning should not be directly compared to a token in derivation.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "normalizing token progress via bucket‑based implicit normalization to mitigate difficulty bias"  
- Paper: Section 3 states bucket normalization "filters out task variance and provides a pure measure of token‑level contribution".  
- Corpus: Neighbor work (arXiv:2505.23564) highlights drawbacks of flat‑sequence advantage and motivates segment‑level adjustments.  
**Break condition:** If \(K\) is poorly chosen or reasoning chains are non‑linear, bucketing may misalign stages and introduce artifacts.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** ERPO prevents reward hacking by anchoring the token‑level advantage to the final outcome sign, ensuring process rewards cannot diverge from answer correctness.  
**Mechanism:** Form \(\Psi_{i,t} = \sigma_{\text{target}} \cdot W_{i,t} \cdot \text{sgn}(\hat{A}_i^{\text{group}}) \cdot \tilde{s}_{i,t} / (\text{std}(\Psi_{\text{active}})+\delta)\), then rescale to target std \(\sigma_{\text{target}}\). Final advantage \(\hat{A}_{i,t}^{\text{final}} = \text{Norm}_G(\hat{A}_i^{\text{group}} + \eta \Psi_{i,t})\) preserves GRPO’s zero‑sum property.  
**Core assumption:** Pure token‑level signals may be maximized by confident but irrelevant steps; outcome sign acts as a directional guardrail penalizing tokens leading to incorrect answers.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "synthesizing outcome‑anchored advantages to prevent reward hacking"  
- Paper: Section 3 describes outcome anchoring as a "directional guardrail".  
- Corpus: Neighbor work (arXiv:2601.03703) redistributes advantages in tree rollouts, supporting linking token credit to global outcome.  
**Break condition:** If group advantages are based on few samples, \(\text{sgn}(\hat{A}_i^{\text{group}})\) may be high‑variance, causing excessive penalization of legitimate exploratory tokens.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Entropy estimator -> Progress signal generator -> Gating module -> Bucketing & normalization -> Advantage synthesizer -> Loss integration

**Critical path:**  
During each iteration, generate rollouts. For every token, compute entropy \(H_{i,t}\) and implicit progress \(s_{i,t}\). Bucket tokens by relative position \(\tau_{i,t}\). Derive gating weight \(W_{i,t}\) from intra‑group normalized entropy. Form process reward \(\Psi_{i,t}\) using \(W_{i,t}\), \(\text{sgn}(\hat{A}_i^{\text{group}})\), and \(\tilde{s}_{i,t}\); rescale to \(\sigma_{\text{target}}\). Combine with group advantage and apply intra‑group normalization to get \(\hat{A}_{i,t}^{\text{final}}\). Compute GRPO clipped surrogate loss with KL penalty against reference policy. Update parameters \(\theta\) via AdamW.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Efficiency vs. granularity:** Token‑level diagnostics add minimal compute vs. a value network but increase memory for storing intermediate stats.  
- **Exploration vs. stability:** Gating amplifies gradients at CDPs, accelerating learning of uncertain branches, but may cause instability if \(\gamma\) is too large.  
- **Normalization scope:** Intra‑bucket normalization removes cross‑stage bias but assumes linear reasoning progression; non‑linear chains may need more complex partitioning.  
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** \(\eta\), \(\gamma\), \(K\), \(\beta_{\text{progress}}\), \(\sigma_{\text{target}}\) require careful tuning per model scale.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Entropy collapse persists:** \(H_{i,t}\) remains low; gating failed to stimulate exploration.  
- **Sequence length explosion:** Model becomes verbose; suppression ineffective.  
- **Gradient norm spikes or KL saturation:** Outcome‑anchored signal overpowering group advantage.  
- **Stagnant pass@k scaling:** ERPO should improve multi‑sample pass rates; stagnation suggests broken token‑level credit assignment.  
- **AIME25 performance drop on small models:** Initial lag may turn into failure with insufficient training; monitor convergence curves.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablation study:** Train with (a) gating removed, (b) bucketing removed, (c) outcome anchoring removed. Compare accuracy and sequence length against full ERPO.  
2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep:** Vary \(\gamma \in [0.5, 2.0]\), \(K \in [5, 20]\), \(\eta \in [0.1, 1.0]\) on AMC23 (1.5B). Plot accuracy vs. token length.  
3. **Token‑level analysis:** Visualize entropy distributions and gating weights \(W_{i,t}\) over training steps for GRPO vs. ERPO. Verify ERPO maintains higher entropy at later stages and weights concentrate on plausible CDPs.

## Limitations
- Missing exact values for core hyperparameters (\(\gamma\), \(\eta\), \(\beta_{\text{progress}}\), \(\sigma_{\text{target}}\), \(K\)) and ambiguous definition of \(\Psi_{\text{active}}\).  
- No ablation studies reported; contributions of individual components remain unquantified.  
- Evaluation limited to models up to 7B and a single training epoch; scaling behavior unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Medium** for overall accuracy/length gains: Evidence from a single experimental setup with unreported hyperparameters.  
- **Medium** for bucket normalization mitigating difficulty bias: No direct ablation isolates its contribution.  
- **Low** for outcome anchoring preventing reward hacking: Lack of explicit reward‑hacking analysis or breakdowns.
