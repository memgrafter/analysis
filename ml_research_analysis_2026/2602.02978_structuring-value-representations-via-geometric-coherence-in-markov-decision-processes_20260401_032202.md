# Structuring Value Representations via Geometric Coherence in Markov Decision Processes
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.02978
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02978
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GCR-RL improves sample efficiency, final return, and stability in RL by enforcing geometric coherence via poset construction and learned symmetries, outperforming Rainbow and DQN.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning often suffers from instability and poor sample efficiency due to value update loops under function approximation. This paper introduces GCR-RL, which recasts value learning as constructing a partial order (poset) over state-action pairs. It enforces geometric coherence through super-poset refinements: near‑equivariance via learned symmetry transforms and action relabelings, together with TD‑induced partial orders. Two variants are proposed—soft regularization and hard manifold projection—to guarantee reflexivity, transitivity, and antisymmetry, eliminating value circles and improving convergence. Theoretical analysis provides convergence rates and stability guarantees. Empirically, GCR-RL achieves significant gains in sample efficiency, final return, and stability across diverse benchmarks (gridworlds, Atari, non‑transitive chains), reducing Bellman residuals and outperforming strong baselines like Rainbow and DQN.

## Method Summary
GCR‑RL augments standard RL algorithms (e.g., Double‑DQN, Rainbow) with two cooperating branches. The symmetry branch learns K pairs of state transforms T_k and action permutations Π_k, trained with an equivariance consistency loss and group‑structure regularizers to approximate MDP symmetries. The logic‑order branch constructs a directed acyclic graph D from TD residual signs via a greedy add‑edge‑unless‑cycle procedure, using k‑NN candidate edges with confidence weighting. This D defines a partial order; an auxiliary value head is trained to respect it either via a soft squared‑hinge isotonic penalty or via a hard manifold projection that keeps Q‑values within the feasible set. The total loss combines the TD loss, symmetry losses, and order losses, yielding a method that theoretically eliminates value circles and empirically stabilizes learning.

## Key Results
- Significant gains in sample efficiency, final return, and stability across gridworlds, Atari, and non‑transitive chain benchmarks.
- Substantial reduction in Bellman residuals compared to baselines.
- Outperforms strong RL algorithms such as Rainbow and DQN.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Super‑Poset Refinement from TD Evidence
- **Claim**: Constructing a partial order over state‑action pairs using TD‑derived comparisons eliminates value update loops and stabilizes learning by enforcing geometric coherence.
- **Mechanism**: From TD residuals (Eq. 6), confident pairwise comparisons (u should be ≥ v) are selected, and a greedy add‑edge‑unless‑cycle procedure builds a DAG D. The DAG induces a partial order via reachability, guaranteeing reflexivity and transitivity; antisymmetry is handled by quotienting with learned symmetry orbits.
- **Core assumption**: TD push direction signs are consistent enough that a cycle‑free subset of comparisons approximates a true partial order.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "computes a sequence of super-poset refinements … learning additional order relationships from temporal difference signals."
  - [section 2]: "TD evidence provides comparisons that progressively refine an underlying partial order … greedy add‑edge‑unless‑cycle produces a DAG D."
  - Literature: Nearby work on stability via switching MDPs does not directly study poset construction from TD.
- **Break condition**: If TD noise corrupts comparison signs, the greedy DAG may include erroneous edges that either remain cyclic or induce incorrect order, undermining monotonicity enforcement.

### Mechanism 2: Learned Near‑Equivariance with Action Relabeling
- **Claim**: Learning algebraic operators (W_k, Π_k) that approximate MDP symmetries clusters orbits and enforces antisymmetry on the quotient set, reducing variance and preventing value circles.
- **Mechanism**: An equivariance consistency loss (Eq. 8) penalizes deviations between Q(s,·) and Π_k^T Q(T_k s,·). Group‑like regularizers (Eq. 10) push transforms toward a finite group structure (identity, closure, inverse, finite orders). Action permutations Π_k are encouraged via Sinkhorn soft‑perm. The quotient set χ/∼ collapses orbit‑equivalent pairs.
- **Core assumption**: The MDP exhibits a stably approximated symmetry subgroup that holds on a non‑negligible fraction of the empirical distribution; encoder and Q‑head have capacity to realize equivariance.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "enforcing geometric coherence … through near‑equivariance via learned symmetry transforms and action relabelings."
  - [section 3.1]: "equivariance consistency loss … penalizes deviations from the equivariance condition … group‑like regularizations keep learned transforms non‑degenerate and enclose group‑like structure."
  - [section 3.2]: "Definition 2.1 … MDP is G‑invariant … each g ∈ G can be viewed as an automorphism … optimal values preserve."
- **Break condition**: If the environment lacks approximate symmetries, or if learned transforms collapse to identity or violate group axioms, the quotient identification fails and antisymmetry is not guaranteed.

### Mechanism 3: Geometric Coherence Enforcement (Soft vs. Hard)
- **Claim**: Enforcing reflexivity, transitivity, and antisymmetry along the sequence of super‑posets, either via soft regularization or hard manifold projection, eliminates value circles and accelerates convergence.
- **Mechanism**:
  - **Soft**: Adds differentiable regularizers to TD loss: L_sym (equivariance) and L_ord (isotonic projection via squared‑hinge penalty). Shapes Q_θ toward the intersection of equivariant and monotone functions.
  - **Hard**: Maintains a feasible set M defined by edge inequalities and equivariance equalities. Updates follow tangent–normal decomposition: descent in tangent space (Null(J_V)) plus contraction of constraint violations along normal.
- **Core assumption**: Soft: penalty weights/schedule can approximate hard constraints; Hard: batch constraints are jointly feasible.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.4]: "optimize the overall objective L_total = L_TD + λ_sym L_sym + λ_dag L_dag + λ_ord L_ord"
  - [section 4.1]: "Define the batchwise constraint manifold M = { (V, μ): c(V, μ;θ)=0, μ≥0 }"
  - [section 4.2]: "tangent step satisfies J_V Δ_tan = 0" and "contraction reduces violation energy"
- **Break condition**: Soft: inadequate hyper‑parameters leaves residual violations; Hard: infeasible constraints early require careful initialization or fallback.

## Foundational Learning
### Order theory (posets)
- **Why needed**: GCR‑RL recasts value learning as building a target partial order; reflexivity/transitivity/antisymmetry directly map to stability properties.
- **Quick check**: Given a DAG D, does its reachability relation define a valid poset? (Yes.) What happens if antisymmetry is violated? (Value circles appear.)

### Group equivariance
- **Why needed**: MDP symmetries induce equivalence classes; enforcing near‑equivariance collapses orbits and provides antisymmetry on the quotient.
- **Quick check**: If MDP is G‑invariant and Q ≈ Π_g^T Q ∘ T_g, what can you say about Q on the same orbit? (All orbit members have approximately equal Q, reducing variance and ensuring antisymmetry after quotienting.)

### Convex projection / penalty methods
- **Why needed**: Isotonic projection onto a monotone cone removes order violations; the squared‑hinge surrogate enables differentiable training.
- **Quick check**: What is the difference between the isotropic projection onto a cone and the squared‑hinge penalty approximation? (Penalty is soft and differentiable; projection is exact but non‑differentiable; penalty converges to projection as µ → ∞.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
shared encoder ϕ_θ(s) → feature vectors → [Q-head, symmetry branch]
Q-head → Qθ(s,·)
symmetry branch → (T_k, Π_k)
Qθ(s,·) + TD targets → logic‑order branch → {DAG D, isotonic/ranking losses}
symmetry branch → L_sym = L_eq + regularizers + permutation losses
logic‑order branch → L_dag + L_ord
total loss L_total = L_TD + λ_eq·L_sym + λ_dag·L_dag + λ_ord·L_ord

### Critical Path
For each minibatch: (1) encode states to features; (2) compute Q-values and TD residuals; (3) compute confidence weights from residuals; (4) select k‑NN candidate edges, construct D; (5) train auxiliary head ˆV with isotonic/ranking losses; (6) compute symmetry losses on T_k, Π_k; (7) sum all losses; (8) backpropagate to update encoder, Q‑head, symmetry parameters. In the hard variant, Qθ update is replaced by a constrained step on the batch‑wise manifold M.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Soft vs hard**: Soft regularization integrates easily but depends on penalty coefficients; hard projection enforces constraints by construction but requires careful handling of feasibility and Jacobian conditioning.
- **DAG construction**: Greedy algorithm is O(E log E); k‑NN and percentile gating trade off sparsity (faster, fewer edges) against coverage (more order information). Too sparse a DAG weakens isotonic enforcement; too dense may introduce cycles.
- **Symmetry branch**: Number K trades off coverage of symmetries vs. computational overhead. Regularizers (identity, closure, inverse, diversity) prevent collapse to identity or degenerate group‑like structures.
- **Hyperparameters**: λ_eq, λ_dag, λ_ord, µ schedule, τ, k, p, K, and isotonic steps T_iso all require tuning. Over‑regularization can hinder learning; under‑regularization may fail to guarantee geometric coherence.

### Failure Signatures
- **Cyclic DAG**: Detect directed cycles in D; cycles indicate the gating threshold (p) or confidence weight (τ) is too permissive, or TD noise is high.
- **Low symmetry coverage**: Monitor alignment scores α_i,k; values near zero suggest transforms are not aligning. Remedies: increase λ_eq, reduce W_k rank, or boost group regularizer weights. Check orthonormality of W_k and permutation compliance of Π_k.
- **Isotonic constraints ignored**: Mean squared hinge violation remains high; increase µ or λ_ord, or increase T_iso. May also indicate DAG is too sparse; increase k or p.
- **Numerical instability in hard mode**: Violation energy ∥c∥ fails to decrease. Check active set E_act, damping ϵ, and Jacobian conditioning; consider subsampling constraints or increasing damping.
- **Training instability**: TD loss spikes relative to baseline; scale down λ_eq/λ_ord or introduce a gradual ramp‑up schedule.

### First Experiments
1. **Symmetry‑rich task** (e.g., RotMirror‑Grid): Compare sample efficiency and stability against Rainbow/DQN; validate that learned transforms approximate the true symmetries and that Q‑values are consistent across orbits.
2. **Action‑permutation task** (e.g., ButtonPermutation Minigrid): Ablate state transforms to isolate Π_k; confirm Π_k recovers the known action permutation and yields performance gains over a baseline without Π_k.
3. **Cycle‑suppression task** (Noisy‑RPS Chain): Inject non‑transitive loops; measure Bellman residuals and learning‑curve stability; compare against a logic‑only ablation to highlight the benefit of combining symmetry and order mechanisms.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Continuous action spaces**: GCR‑RL assumes discrete actions for permutation matrices. Extending to continuous control requires new formulations of equivariance on continuous action manifolds; theoretical guarantees and empirical validation are open.
2. **Computational trade‑offs between soft and hard variants**: The paper does not quantify wall‑clock overhead or sensitivity of the hard manifold projection relative to soft regularization. Detailed benchmarks across tasks are needed.
3. **Robustness to TD noise**: The theoretical analysis assumes a Massart‑type condition on TD residual signs. In environments with high variance or sparse rewards, this assumption may break; robustness experiments and robust ranking methods are required.
4. **Impact of candidate edge construction**: The k‑NN and percentile gating heuristic for candidate edges is not analyzed. The choice of k, distance metric, and cutoff can affect the learned poset; systematic ablations and adaptive schemes are needed.
5. **Using the learned poset beyond value regularization**: The poset encodes preference relationships that could guide exploration, hierarchical planning, or reward shaping. Exploring these secondary uses remains future work.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Many λ, µ, k‑NN, p‑gate, K, etc. are not fully specified; mis‑tuning can lead to ineffective regularization or instability.
- **DAG construction fragility**: The greedy DAG from noisy TD comparisons may become cyclic or too sparse, jeopardizing partial‑order guarantees.
- **Symmetry branch collapse**: In environments lacking strong symmetries, learned transforms can degenerate to identity or violate group axioms, failing to provide antisymmetry.
