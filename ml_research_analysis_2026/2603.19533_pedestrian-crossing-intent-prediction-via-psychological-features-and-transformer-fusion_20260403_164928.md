# Pedestrian Crossing Intent Prediction via Psychological Features and Transformer Fusion
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19533
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19533
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: Lightweight multi-stream model with uncertainty heads achieves state-of-the-art on PSI 1.0 (F1=0.90, AUC=0.94) and sets a new baseline on PSI 2.0 (F1=0.78), while enabling selective prediction gains of up to 4.7 percentage points.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a lightweight, interpretable architecture for pedestrian crossing intent prediction that fuses four behavioral cues—psychological attention, position, situation, and interaction—via highway encoders and a compact 4-token Transformer. Complementary uncertainty heads (KL divergence and Mahalanobis distance) provide calibrated probabilities and risk scores. On the PSI 1.0 benchmark, the model surpasses recent vision-language approaches using only structured features, achieving 0.90 F1, 0.94 AUC-ROC, and 0.78 MCC. It establishes a new baseline on the more diverse PSI 2.0 dataset. Selective prediction based on Mahalanobis scores further improves accuracy at high coverage. The design is modality-agnostic and efficient, suited for resource-constrained autonomous vehicle platforms.

## Method Summary
The model processes four pre-extracted structured streams per pedestrian instance: psychological attention (gaze, head orientation, hesitation), position (curb proximity, crosswalk location), situation (traffic context, vehicle speed/density), and interaction (pairwise products of the other streams). Each stream is encoded by a highway network (two-layer MLP with gating) to preserve raw features while enabling nonlinear transformation. The four encoded streams are added with learnable stream embeddings, concatenated into a 4-token sequence, and fused via a Transformer encoder. Global self-attention pooling synthesizes the token representations into a unified embedding, which is passed through a residual MLP classifier. Two parallel uncertainty heads are attached: a variational bottleneck that computes KL divergence to a unit Gaussian prior (epistemic uncertainty) and a Mahalanobis distance estimator in the classifier’s latent space (distributional shift). Training uses a composite loss: binary cross-entropy with MixUp augmentation, label smoothing (ε=0.1), and regularization terms for KL divergence, diversity, and temperature. Post-hoc temperature scaling on validation logits improves calibration.

## Key Results
- SOTA on PSI 1.0: F1=0.90, AUC-ROC=0.94, MCC=0.78, outperforming vision-language baselines with only structured features.
- New baseline on PSI 2.0: F1=0.78, AUC-ROC=0.79, demonstrating robustness to greater scene diversity.
- Selective prediction: Using Mahalanobis scores to filter high-risk predictions improves accuracy by up to 4.7 percentage points at 80% coverage on PSI 1.0.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Fusing four semantically distinct behavioral streams (psychological attention, positional, situational, interaction) via highway encoders and a compact 4-token Transformer enables accurate, interpretable intent prediction.
- **Mechanism:** Each stream encodes a specific cognitive or contextual cue. Highway encoders preserve critical raw feature information (e.g., gaze vectors) while allowing nonlinear transformation. The Transformer’s self-attention over the four fixed-order tokens learns cross-stream dependencies (e.g., how gaze attention modifies spatial context). Global attention pooling then synthesizes these interactions into a unified representation for classification.
- **Core assumption:** These four streams capture sufficient, complementary signals of pedestrian intent, and their joint representation is more informative than any single stream or raw visual input.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "fuses four behavioral cues—psychological attention, position, situation, and interaction—using highway encoders and a compact 4-token Transformer." Section IV describes the four input streams, highway encoding, stream embedding, and Transformer fusion. Neighbor papers focus on multimodal fusion and transformers but do not specify this exact 4-stream psychological decomposition; evidence is thematic only.
- **Break condition:** If the situational stream (most influential per ablation) is removed or corrupted, performance collapses (F1 drops from 0.90 to 0.75 on PSI 1.0), invalidating the necessity of this specific multi-stream design.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Complementary uncertainty heads (KL divergence from a variational bottleneck and Mahalanobis distance in the latent space) provide calibrated probabilities and actionable risk scores for selective prediction.
- **Mechanism:** The variational head measures epistemic uncertainty by deviation from a unit Gaussian prior. The Mahalanobis detector measures distance from the training distribution in the classifier’s feature space. High scores on either indicate unreliable predictions; the latter is more strongly correlated with errors (AUROC 0.77 vs. 0.59 on PSI 1.0).
- **Core assumption:** Uncertainty in pedestrian intent prediction can be decomposed into (a) model uncertainty (captured by KL) and (b) distributional shift (captured by Mahalanobis), and both are signaled in the learned latent space.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "Two complementary uncertainty heads (KL divergence and Mahalanobis distance) enable calibrated probabilities and risk scoring." Section IV.F details the variational bottleneck and Mahalanobis computation. Section V.D.2 shows empirical error-detection results: Mahalanobis AUROC 0.77 (PSI 1.0) and selective prediction gains (+4.7 pp at 80% coverage).
- **Break condition:** If neither uncertainty score correlates with classification errors (e.g., AUROC near random), the heads fail to provide useful risk awareness. The paper shows correlation, but a strong counterexample would be a dataset where distributional shift does not impact intent features.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The 4-token cross-stream attention pattern is interpretable and shifts meaningfully under ambiguity, providing insight into model reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** The Transformer’s attention weights over the four stream tokens reveal which behavioral cues the model prioritizes for a given decision. Diffuse attention (~0.25 per token) indicates routine reasoning; concentrated attention on a specific stream (e.g., attention) signals atypical or ambiguous scenes where that cue dominates.
- **Core assumption:** Attention weights over the semantically tagged tokens reflect the relative importance of each behavioral cue in the model’s decision, analogous to human driver reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "Qualitative attention heatmaps further show how the model shifts its cross-stream focus under ambiguity." Section V.F provides qualitative examples: low-risk uniform attention vs. high-risk attention concentration on the attention stream; shifts in crowded/conflicting scenes. Neighbor papers use attention for interpretability but not over a fixed 4-stream psychological token set; limited direct evidence.
- **Break condition:** If attention patterns are uniform or random regardless of scene context and uncertainty, the claimed interpretability and cue-shifting behavior would be invalid.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Highway Networks**
  - Why needed here: They allow adaptive gating between transformed and raw features within each behavioral stream, preserving critical low-level signals (e.g., raw gaze vectors) while enabling deeper nonlinear processing without vanishing gradients.
  - Quick check question: How does the highway gate `g` in equation (1) balance transformation vs. passthrough, and why is this important for interpretable feature streams?
- **Concept: Transformer Cross-Stream Attention**
  - Why needed here: The 4-token sequence requires the model to learn interactions between psychological, positional, situational, and interaction cues. Self-attention provides a direct, content-based mechanism for this fusion without rigid concatenation or late merging.
  - Quick check question: Why are fixed stream embeddings added (equation 3) instead of positional encodings, and how does this help the Transformer distinguish behavioral sources?
- **Concept: Mahalanobis Distance for Anomaly Detection**
  - Why needed here: It detects samples whose latent representations deviate from the training distribution’s covariance structure, indicating novel scenarios (e.g., crowded scenes) where intent prediction is risky.
  - Quick check question: What is the effect of the Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage estimator (equation 18) on the covariance matrix, and why is it used here instead of the empirical covariance?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (4 normalized streams) -> Highway Encoders (per stream) -> Add Stream Embeddings -> Concatenate -> Transformer Encoder (L layers) -> Global Self-Attention Pooling -> Flatten -> Residual MLP -> Sigmoid Output. Parallel branches: From flattened vector `z`, Variational Head (µ, η -> KL) and Mahalanobis Head (compute distance in µ-space).
- **Critical path:** Feature engineering -> Highway encoding -> 4-token Transformer fusion -> Residual classifier -> Uncertainty heads. The Transformer fusion is the pivotal component enabling cross-cue reasoning.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Lightweight structured features vs. vision-language richness: Model uses engineered features (no image backbone) for efficiency and interpretability, sacrificing raw visual nuance. Tradeoff: PSI 1.0 SOTA with 0.90 F1 but may miss subtle visual cues.
  - Fixed 4-token vs. variable-length sequences: Simplicity and efficiency vs. inability to model arbitrary numbers of pedestrians/vehicles dynamically. The interaction stream encodes pairwise products as a proxy.
  - Two uncertainty heads vs. single: Higher computational cost but complementary signals (KL for model uncertainty, Mahalanobis for dataset shift).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High Mahalanobis score (> training distribution percentile) + high KL: Out-of-distribution or highly ambiguous scene; system should defer or flag for human oversight.
  - Uniform attention across all 4 tokens even in clearly ambiguous scenes: Fusion may be failing; check Transformer training or stream embedding quality.
  - Post-hoc temperature scaling dramatically improves calibration (as on PSI 2.0): Indicates base classifier is poorly calibrated; verify loss function and regularization (label smoothing, MixUp).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Stream ablation: Train with each stream removed (as in Table II) to confirm their individual contributions, especially the dramatic drop when removing situational features.
  2. Uncertainty-error correlation: Compute Spearman correlation between Mahalanobis/KL scores and binary correctness on a held-out set; expect strong positive correlation for Mahalanobis.
  3. Attention visualization: For a set of ambiguous PSI 2.0 clips (e.g., conflicting cues, occlusion), extract the 4×4 attention matrix and verify systematic shifts (e.g., increased weight on interaction stream) as shown in Fig. 3.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** How can the structured feature-based model be effectively combined with vision-language embeddings to leverage complementary cues for pedestrian intent prediction?
- **Basis in paper:** The abstract and conclusion state the approach is "modality-agnostic, easy to integrate with vision–language pipelines," but only structured features are used in experiments, leaving integration as a theoretical suggestion.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not implement or evaluate such integration, so the practical benefits and challenges of combining modalities remain unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A hybrid model that processes both structured features and vision-language embeddings (e.g., CLIP) should be trained and tested on PSI datasets, with ablation studies to measure contributions from each modality.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** What explains the significant performance drop when the situational stream is ablated, and does this indicate over-reliance on contextual features that may not always be available or reliable?
- **Basis in paper:** Table II shows F1-score declines sharply from 0.90 to 0.75 when removing the situational stream, highlighting its dominance, but the paper does not analyze why or explore dependencies.
- **Why unresolved:** The ablation confirms importance but offers no insight into whether situational cues are irreplaceable or if other streams could compensate under different conditions (e.g., occlusions).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Further experiments with controlled feature perturbations (e.g., masking situational features) across diverse scenarios, or evaluating feature importance in scenes with limited context, to assess robustness and potential redundancy.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** How reliable is the Mahalanobis distance-based uncertainty estimator for detecting errors in out-of-distribution scenarios, particularly on diverse datasets like PSI 2.0 where its AUROC drops?
- **Basis in paper:** Table VI shows Mahalanobis AUROC decreases from 0.77 on PSI 1.0 to 0.66 on PSI 2.0, suggesting sensitivity to dataset shift, but this limitation is not discussed.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper presents Mahalanobis as effective for selective prediction but does not investigate why performance degrades on more diverse data or how to improve generalizability.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze failure cases on PSI 2.0 to identify specific scene types causing high Mahalanobis scores, test adaptive covariance estimation methods, or benchmark against other OOD detectors on multiple datasets.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** What are the key factors causing the performance decline on PSI 2.0 compared to PSI 1.0, and how can the model be adapted to handle greater diversity in pedestrian behavior and scenes?
- **Basis in paper:** Table III shows lower F1 (0.78 vs. 0.90) and MCC on PSI 2.0, which is described as more diverse, but no analysis of dataset differences or model shortcomings is provided.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper establishes a baseline but does not explore whether the drop is due to distribution shift, feature limitations, or architectural constraints, limiting guidance for improvement.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct statistical comparisons of feature distributions between PSI 1.0 and 2.0, perform domain adaptation experiments, or introduce data augmentation techniques targeting identified gaps (e.g., crowd scenes).

## Limitations
