# Early-Warning Signals of Grokking via Loss-Landscape Geometry

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.16967
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16967
- **Reference count:** 2
- **Primary result:** The commutator defect—a measure of gradient non-commutativity—spikes reliably before grokking across tasks and learning rates, and causal interventions confirm its necessity and partial sufficiency.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether the geometric mechanism of grokking, previously identified in modular arithmetic, extends to more complex algorithmic tasks: SCAN compositional generalization and Dyck sequence learning. The authors use the commutator defect, which quantifies loss-landscape curvature via the Lie bracket of stochastic gradient vector fields. They find that the defect consistently spikes before the generalization transition, with lead times scaling super-linearly with grokking time across learning rates. Causal interventions—amplifying the defect accelerates grokking, while suppressing orthogonal gradient flow delays or prevents it—demonstrate the defect's causal role. Thus, the commutator defect serves as a robust, architecture-agnostic early-warning signal for grokking.

## Method Summary
The study trains transformer models on SCAN (encoder-decoder) and Dyck-1 (causal decoder) tasks using AdamW with weight decay λ=1.0 and gradient clipping. Learning rates are swept over a wide range (SCAN: 1e-5 to 1e-3; Dyck: 3e-5 to 1e-2). Models are trained until memorization and beyond, with periodic checkpointing of weights and evaluation of test accuracy. The commutator defect is computed at each checkpoint by sampling two mini-batches, performing ordered gradient updates with a small step size η_comm=1e-3, and measuring the normalized difference between the resulting weight changes; the median over K=5 repeats is reported. Grokking is defined as the first step where test accuracy reaches ≥0.98 and stays above that threshold for three consecutive evaluations. Defect onset is detected when the median defect exceeds ten times the baseline (median of first three measurements) and also exceeds 20. Lead time Δt = t_grok − t_onset is recorded for each run. To probe mechanisms, principal component analysis (PCA) is applied to weight trajectories to identify an execution manifold, and the orthogonality of defect vectors to this manifold is quantified. Causal interventions modify the gradient update: either amplifying the orthogonal component (1A-kick, 1A-noise) or suppressing it (1B-project, 1B-penalty), and their effect on grokking speed is measured.

## Key Results
- Defect spikes precede grokking in 25 out of 26 runs across SCAN and Dyck, with super-linear scaling of lead time (α≈1.18 for SCAN, α≈1.13 for Dyck, comparable to modular arithmetic's α≈1.27).
- Causal interventions confirm necessity: suppressing orthogonal gradient flow delays or prevents grokking universally. Partial sufficiency: amplifying the defect accelerates grokking (~32% on SCAN, ~50% on Dyck).
- The defect vectors are predominantly orthogonal to the low-dimensional execution manifold (ρ≈1), indicating that curvature accumulates in the normal bundle during confinement. Attention matrices (W_Q, W_K) exhibit a peak in their commutator before grokking, signaling spectral alignment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Defect as early-warning curvature signal
The commutator defect measures local loss-landscape curvature via the Lie bracket of stochastic gradient vector fields. A spike indicates that gradient updates do not commute, reflecting a breakdown of integrability on the execution manifold. This curvature accumulation in the normal bundle precedes escape from the memorization basin into a generalizing solution. Evidence: defect onset precedes grokking in 25/26 runs; lead times scale super-linearly with grokking time.

### Mechanism 2: Transverse confinement on low-dimensional manifold
Networks train within a low-rank subspace (execution manifold) during memorization, as shown by PCA (PC1 variance 70–90%). The commutator defect vectors are almost entirely orthogonal to this subspace (ρ≈1), meaning nonlinearity builds up in directions transverse to the learned manifold. Generalization requires escaping this confinement; the accumulated transverse curvature acts as a barrier that must be overcome. Evidence: high PC1 variance during memorization; defect orthogonality holds across tasks; Dyck shows pre-grok de-concentration while SCAN shows post-grok, but orthogonality persists.

### Mechanism 3: Attention spectral alignment
In transformers, the query and key matrices (W_Q, W_K) start with near-degenerate singular values, causing orientation instability. Their matrix commutator peaks before grokking, indicating a realignment of eigenbases. Weight decay then drives one singular mode to dominate, reducing the commutator and establishing a stable spectral gap. This representational reorganization is temporally ordered: SGD defect spike → matrix commutator peak → grokking → spectral compression. Evidence: matrix commutator peaks in all 6 transformer runs; phase portraits show trajectories from high-commutator/low-gap to low-commutator/high-gap.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) of weight trajectories**
  - Why needed: PCA on weight displacements from initialization defines the execution manifold and quantifies low-dimensional confinement (PC1 variance). It also decomposes defect vectors into components parallel and orthogonal to the manifold.
  - Quick check: If PC1 variance is 85% during memorization, what does that imply about the effective degrees of freedom in the weight trajectory? (Answer: The dynamics are largely confined to a one-dimensional subspace.)

- **Concept: Stochastic gradient descent and loss landscape curvature**
  - Why needed: The commutator defect arises from non-commuting stochastic gradient steps; it serves as a computationally cheap proxy for local nonlinearity (Hessian) without explicit second-order computation.
  - Quick check: If two mini-batch gradient steps commute exactly, what does that say about the loss surface in that region? (Answer: The loss is locally linear (or the gradients are collinear) in the directions of those steps, so the surface is approximately flat or the steps are aligned.)

- **Concept: Transformer attention projections (W_Q, W_K)**
  - Why needed: The spectral geometry analysis focuses on the singular values and commutator of W_Q and W_K; understanding their SVD is necessary to interpret alignment dynamics and gap formation.
  - Quick check: What does a small gap between the top two singular values of W_Q suggest about the stability of its dominant eigenvector? (Answer: The dominant eigenvector is unstable; small perturbations can cause it to rotate, indicating orientation competition.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Weight matrices (W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O, feedforward) -> logged for PCA and spectral analysis; Gradient buffers (from mini-batch pairs) -> commutator defect computation; PCA storage -> execution manifold and orthogonality measure; Intervention modules (1A-kick, 1A-noise, 1B-project, 1B-penalty) -> modify gradient updates.

**Critical path:**
1. Train baseline model with checkpointing (weights every 200–500 steps) and periodic defect measurement (every 100 steps, K=5 repeats).
2. Detect defect onset (10× baseline + absolute floor 20) and grokking step (sustained test accuracy ≥0.98 for 3 evals).
3. Perform PCA on weight displacements to define execution manifold; compute invariance measure ρ and exec/random ratios.
4. For interventions: identify PCA basis from baseline, then retrain from same init with modified update rules (amplify/suppress orthogonal component).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Learning rate sweep must span at least two orders to establish scaling law; too narrow yields misleading α.
- Weight decay λ=1.0 induces grokking but may cause instability at high LR (e.g., Dyck η=1e-2); sustained-accuracy criterion essential.
- Intervention strength requires careful dose–response sweeps; aggressive boosting destabilizes SCAN.

**Failure signatures:**
- No defect spike despite long training and perfect train accuracy → likely never groks.
- PC1 never dominates (>50%) → no clear execution manifold; transverse dynamics may not apply.
- High-LR instability (accuracy oscillations) → grokking detection requires sustained threshold, not single crossing.
- Intervention causing divergence or immediate collapse → architectural sensitivity (e.g., encoder–decoder SCAN with 1A-kick).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Baseline validation: Train SCAN (η=1e-4) and Dyck (η=1e-3) with 3 seeds; confirm defect onset precedes grokking in all runs; record lead times.
2. PCA dissociation test: Compute PC1 trajectories for both tasks; verify Dyck shows pre-grok de-concentration while SCAN shows post-grok (rejecting spectral concentration as universal).
3. Causal intervention pilot: From same init, run baseline, 1A-noise (mild boost), and 1B-project (suppress); if suppression delays grokking and mild boost accelerates, necessity and graded causality are supported.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: What governs the necessity versus sufficiency of the commutator defect for grokking across tasks?
- **Basis:** Section 9.3 explicitly raises this question.
- **Why unresolved:** Suppression universally prevents grokking (necessity), but boosting accelerates only on Dyck and mildly on SCAN, not modular arithmetic. The reason for this spectrum is unknown.
- **Resolution path:** Cross-task intervention studies with quantified solution complexity; theoretical models linking curvature dynamics to inductive biases and solution accessibility.

### Open Question 2: Why does PC1 de-concentration occur before grokking in Dyck/modular arithmetic but after grokking in SCAN?
- **Basis:** Inferred from Section 5.3.1 and Fig 5, which report this dissociation without explanation.
- **Why unresolved:** Architectural differences (encoder-decoder vs. encoder-only/causal) are plausible but not isolated; the role of PC1 turnover remains unclear.
- **Resolution path:** Compare PC1 dynamics on identical tasks with different architectures (e.g., SCAN with encoder-only) or ablate cross-attention.

### Open Question 3: Does the super-linear scaling exponent α of defect lead time depend on weight-decay strength?
- **Basis:** Section 9.4 explicitly asks this.
- **Why unresolved:** Experiments used only λ=1.0, with qualitative consistency at λ=0.1; robustness of α to regularization is unknown.
- **Resolution path:** Perform learning-rate sweeps across multiple weight-decay values (e.g., λ∈{0.1,0.5,1.0,2.0}) and fit α for each to test dependence.

## Limitations
- Unspecified intervention hyperparameters (kick magnitude, noise scale, penalty coefficient) and defect measurement details (batch size, checkpoint interval) hinder exact replication.
- Task diversity is limited (SCAN, Dyck, modular arithmetic); scaling law fits rely on narrow learning rate sweeps.
- PCA analysis scope (all vs. subset parameters) remains ambiguous; weight decay interactions may confound dynamics.

## Confidence
- **Defect as early-warning signal:** Medium (consistent lead times in 25/26 runs, but generalizability to unseen tasks/architectures unproven).
- **Transverse confinement on low-D manifolds:** Medium (high PC1 variance during memorization evident, but timing of de-concentration differs between tasks).
- **Attention spectral alignment:** Medium (commutator peaks in all transformer runs, but may not extend to non-attention models).

## Next Checks
1. **Architecture diversity test:** Replicate defect onset and intervention effects on MLP-based grokking (e.g., modular arithmetic with MLP, parity tasks) to confirm architecture-agnosticity.
2. **Measurement robustness sweep:** Systematically vary defect computation parameters (mini-batch pairs K, step size η_comm, baseline multiplier) and checkpoint interval to establish stable detection thresholds.
3. **Dose-response intervention study:** Quantify grokking speed as a continuous function of defect amplification/suppression strength for both SCAN and Dyck, including extreme suppression to test necessity.
