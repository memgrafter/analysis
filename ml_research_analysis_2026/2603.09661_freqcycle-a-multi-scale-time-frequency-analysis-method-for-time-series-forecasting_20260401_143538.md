# FreqCycle: A Multi-Scale Time-Frequency Analysis Method for Time Series Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09661
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09661
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** FreqCycle achieves state-of-the-art accuracy, ranking first in 10 out of 14 metrics against strong baselines while maintaining superior inference speed.

## Executive Summary
FreqCycle introduces a dual-module framework to address the underrepresentation of mid-to-high frequency components in time series forecasting. The Filter-Enhanced Cycle Forecasting (FECF) module explicitly learns shared low-frequency periodic patterns (e.g., daily/weekly cycles) via a learnable cyclical basis and adaptive frequency-domain filtering. The Segmented Frequency-domain Pattern Learning (SFPL) module enhances mid-to-high frequency energy by segmenting the residual signal, applying STFT-like operations, and adaptively weighting frequency components across segments. For coupled multi-periodicity, MFreqCycle extends this hierarchically, decoupling nested cycles through parallel branches with different temporal scales. Evaluated on seven benchmark datasets, FreqCycle achieves state-of-the-art performance, ranking first in 10 out of 14 MSE/MAE metrics against baselines like DLinear, CycleNet, and Transformers, while offering superior inference speed. Ablation studies confirm both modules are essential; SFPL alone yields up to a 6.91% MSE improvement on non-stationary datasets.

## Method Summary
FreqCycle processes a multivariate time series window of length \(L\) and \(D\) variables. Inputs first undergo per-channel LayerNorm. The FECF module generates a low-frequency periodic component by tiling a learnable basis matrix \(Q\) (period \(W\)) across the lookback and horizon, then filtering via FFT, element-wise multiplication with a learned filter vector \(\theta_c\), and inverse FFT. The residual \(r = x - c\) (with \(c\) aligned to the lookback) is passed to SFPL. SFPL divides \(r\) into \(s\) sub-segments of length \(w_L\). For each segment, FFT is computed; a learnable weight vector \(\theta_F\) (softmax over segments) emphasizes mid-to-high frequencies. The weighted sum of spectra is inverted and processed by a feed-forward network to predict \(r_{\text{hat}}\). The final forecast is \(\hat{x} = c_{\text{filtered}} + r_{\text{hat}}\). For multi-periodic data, MFreqCycle runs two parallel branches: a base-cycle branch (as above) and a weekly-cycle branch that pools the input to a shorter length, applies a linear projection, then the same modules. Predictions are fused via adaptive weighting (Equation 10). Training uses Adam with learning rates in \(\{0.005, 0.01, 0.025, 0.03\}\), batch sizes \(\{64,128,256\}\), and hidden dimensions \(\{128,512\}\). Complexity is \(O(L \log L)\) due to FFT operations.

## Key Results
- FreqCycle achieves state-of-the-art accuracy, ranking first in 10 out of 14 MSE/MAE metrics across seven benchmark datasets.
- It outperforms strong baselines including DLinear, CycleNet, and Transformer-based models while maintaining superior inference speed.
- Ablation studies confirm both FECF and SFPL modules are essential; SFPL alone provides a 6.91% MSE improvement on the non-stationary ETTh2 dataset.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Filter-Enhanced Cycle Forecasting (FECF)
- **Claim:** The Filter-Enhanced Cycle Forecasting (FECF) module explicitly learns shared low-frequency periodic patterns (e.g., daily/weekly cycles) by generating a learnable cyclical basis and applying adaptive low-pass filtering in the frequency domain.
- **Mechanism:** A globally shared, learnable cyclical basis matrix \(Q\) is periodically tiled to match the input length. This learned component is filtered via \(\text{IFFT}(\text{Filter}(\text{FFT}(c)))\) where \(\text{Filter}\) multiplies the FFT output by a learnable vector \(\theta_c\), attenuating mid/high frequencies to prioritize low-frequency periodic patterns. The filtered component is added to a residual prediction.
- **Core assumption:** Dominant low-frequency periodic patterns are stable and shareable across the time series length, and can be separated via a linear (or near-linear) basis plus spectral filtering.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...explicitly learn shared periodic patterns in the time domain, and... SFPL module to enhance mid to high frequency energy..."
  - [section, page 4] "Through periodic replication operations... this basis produces a cyclical component... undergoes processing via adaptive filtering... Filter(ξ)=ξ⊙θ_c..."
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., ReFocus, Dualformer) corroborate the focus on mid-frequency gaps and dual-domain learning, but do not directly anchor FECF's specific parametric cycle basis.
- **Break condition:** If the underlying periodic patterns are non-stationary or highly irregular across the lookback window, the fixed-length basis tiling will fail to align with true cycles.

### Mechanism 2: Segmented Frequency-domain Pattern Learning (SFPL)
- **Claim:** The Segmented Frequency-domain Pattern Learning (SFPL) module enhances the energy contribution of mid-to-high frequency components (capturing aperiodicity/transients) by applying a Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)-like segmentation, learnable spectral weighting, and fusion.
- **Mechanism:** The residual signal is divided into overlapping/adjacent sub-segments. FFT is applied to each, yielding localized spectra. A learnable softmax weight vector \(\theta_F\) is applied across the segment dimension (\(F' = F \odot \Theta'_F\)), adaptively boosting mid/high-frequency bins in specific segments. The weighted sum \(f\) is inverted and passed through an FFN.
- **Core assumption:** Mid/high-frequency information (short-term fluctuations) is time-localized; segmenting the signal improves their spectral representation and allows adaptive selection of which temporal windows' frequency content is most predictive.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...enhance mid to high frequency energy proportion via learnable filters and adaptive weighting."
  - [section, page 5] "By dividing the signal into short sub-segments... constitutes a variant of the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)... achieving higher frequency-domain locality."
  - [appendix A] Explicitly derives STFT and notes that "shorter windows are preferred as they naturally amplify mid to high frequency energy."
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (TFDNet, WaveTuner) use time-frequency decompositions but SFPL's specific segmentation + adaptive weighting strategy is not directly evidenced in the corpus.
- **Break condition:** If the key mid/high-frequency features are not temporally localized or if the segmentation window length is mismatched to the characteristic transient timescale, the STFT-based approach may dilute rather than enhance relevant features.

### Mechanism 3: Hierarchical Multi-Periodicity Decoupling (MFreqCycle)
- **Claim:** The MFreqCycle extension hierarchically decouples coupled multi-periodicity (e.g., nested daily/weekly cycles) through parallel modules with different temporal scales and adaptive fusion, addressing long lookback window challenges.
- **Mechanism:** Two parallel branches process the input: a "base cycle" module (using FECF+SFPL on the full lookback \(L\)) and a "weekly cycle" module (first applying a pooling layer to downsample time, then a Linear projection before FECF+SFPL). Their predictions are fused via learnable weights (\(x_{t+1:t+H} = x_0 \odot \theta'_0 + x_1 \odot \theta'_1\)).
- **Core assumption:** Multi-scale periodicities can be approximately separated by tapering the lookback window via pooling for longer cycles, and that a simple adaptive weighted average of separate scale-specific forecasts is sufficient for final integration.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...coupled multi-periodicity... extend FreqCycle hierarchically into MFreqCycle, which decouples nested periodic features through cross-scale interactions."
  - [section, page 5] "MFreqCycle... comprises two key modules: a base cycle learning... and a weekly cycle learning... These multi-scale predictions are then adaptively integrated..."
  - [corpus] The concept of multi-scale decomposition is common (WaveTuner, Multi-Order Wavelet), but MFreqCycle's specific parallel pooling-before-learning design for long periods is not pre-evidenced.
- **Break condition:** If the multi-periodic components are not truly separable by the chosen coarse pooling operation, or if their interaction is nonlinear and not captured by a linear fusion, performance may degrade vs. a single-scale model.

## Foundational Learning
### Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) & Duality
- **Why needed here:** The entire frequency-domain operation (filtering in FECF, weighting in SFPL) relies on mapping time series to the frequency domain via FFT (efficient DFT) and back via IFFT. The convolution-multiplication duality justifies pointwise spectral filtering as a substitute for circular time-domain convolution.
- **Quick check:** If \(y[n] = (x * h)[n]\) in time, what is the equivalent operation in the frequency domain given DFT pairs \(X[k]\) and \(H[k]\)? (Answer: \(Y[k] = X[k] \cdot H[k]\)).

### Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)
- **Why needed here:** SFPL's segmentation strategy is an STFT variant. Understanding the trade-off between window length (time resolution vs. frequency resolution) is critical to appreciate why shorter windows enhance mid/high-frequency energy.
- **Quick check:** What happens to the frequency resolution (\(\Delta f\)) if you halve the STFT window length while keeping sampling rate constant? (Answer: Frequency resolution decreases (\(\Delta f\) increases), but temporal localization improves).

### Seasonal-Trend Decomposition (STD)
- **Why needed here:** FECF is an STD method where the "seasonal" component is a learned periodic basis. Understanding traditional STD (e.g., moving average) helps interpret the ablation comparisons (DFreq, LDFreq).
- **Quick check:** In moving average decomposition for a daily cycle (\(W=24\)), what is a key limitation compared to a learned basis like FECF's? (Answer: Moving average is a fixed, linear, non-parametric smoother that cannot learn irregular or amplitude-modulated cycle shapes).

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
- **FreqCycle:** Input \(X\) (\(L \times D\)) → LayerNorm → FECF (generates periodic component \(c\)) → Residual \(r = x - c\) → SFPL (processes \(r\) to predict \(r_{\text{hat}}\)) → Sum (\(c_{\text{filtered}} + r_{\text{hat}}\)) → Denorm → Output.
- **MFreqCycle extension:** Two parallel branches: base cycle (as above) and weekly cycle branch (Pool → Linear → FECF+SFPL) → Adaptive fusion (weighted sum or product) → Denorm → Output.

### Critical Path
The residual path (\(x \rightarrow r \rightarrow \text{SFPL} \rightarrow r_{\text{hat}}\)) is critical for capturing non-periodic/transient patterns. A failure in SFPL (e.g., poor weighting, mismatched window size) directly degrades performance on datasets with strong non-stationary characteristics, as evidenced by the 6.91% MSE improvement on ETTh2 when SFPL is included.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **SFPL window length:** Shorter windows improve temporal localization of mid/high frequencies but reduce frequency resolution (Appendix A, Table 9). Optimal length varies per dataset (typically 4–10).
- **Window function choice:** Rectangular window preserves all time-domain energy (best in 85% cases, Table 8) but introduces spectral leakage. Hann/Hamming reduce leakage at the cost of main-lobe width, potentially harming sparse mid-high frequencies.
- **Filter vs. noise amplification:** Adaptive filtering in FECF and weighting in SFPL are learnable but risk amplifying noise if regularization (dropout, weight decay) is insufficient. Ablation shows MLP feed-forward in SFPL helps (Table 11).

### Failure Signatures
- **No SFPL gain:** On highly periodic, stationary datasets (e.g., Traffic), ablating SFPL has minimal impact (Table 3: Traffic 0.04% MAE change). If no gain, the dataset may have negligible mid-high-frequency predictive content.
- **FECF misalignment:** If the learned periodic pattern shape is irregular or period \(W\) mismatched (e.g., daily pattern on weekly data), adjust \(W\) per dataset characteristics.
- **Memory/compute overhead:** FFT operations on long sequences may exceed memory; reduce batch size or sequence length. Complexity \(O(L \log L)\) should scale, but check for inadvertent \(O(L^2)\) operations.
- **Visualization check:** Plot FFT magnitude before/after SFPL; mid/high-frequency bins (indices >10 for \(L=96\)) should be amplified. If spectra are identical, SFPL's weights \(\theta_F\) may have collapsed.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Ablation - Remove SFPL / Replace with MLP:** Replicate Table 3. Expect significant drop on ETTh2 (non-stationary). If no drop, SFPL may not be learning useful frequency masks.
2. **Window Sensitivity:** For SFPL, sweep window length \(w_L \in \{4,6,8,10\}\) with stride \(w_S = w_L\) (Table 9). Confirm optimal length varies by dataset. If performance is flat across lengths, segmentation may be irrelevant.
3. **Frequency Spectrum Visualization:** Plot FFT magnitude before/after SFPL for one channel (Figures 5,7–10). Look for amplified mid/high-frequency bins. If low-frequency only, \(\theta_F\) is not focusing on higher bins.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: Can more sophisticated or adaptive window functions in SFPL's segmentation yield further performance gains?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper states the rectangular window was optimal in over 85% of cases due to energy retention but limits the tested window types to three standard options.
- **Why unresolved:** The optimal window likely depends on the underlying signal's properties; the paper's fixed per-dataset tuning may not capture this dependency.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments with a wider range of window families (e.g., Kaiser, Blackman) or a learnable windowing mechanism that adapts shape and length per segment.

### Open Question 2: How does MFreqCycle perform on time series with multi-periodicities longer than weekly cycles?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper states: "longer-period patterns like annual cycles remain unverified due to dataset limitations."
- **Why unresolved:** Current benchmarks have maximum inherent periods of days or weeks; the design for "coupled multi-periodicity under long lookback windows" cannot be properly validated for truly long cycles.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluation on datasets with strong, verified annual or multi-year cycles (e.g., certain climate, economic, or ecological time series) to test if the cross-scale interaction module successfully decouples and models nested long- and short-term periods.

### Open Question 3: Is the simple adaptive weighting in SFPL's frequency aggregation the most effective method?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] SFPL uses a single softmax over segment-level frequency representations (Equation 9). The paper demonstrates effectiveness against replacing SFPL with a simple LPF or PaiFilter but does not compare against more complex weighting or fusion mechanisms.
- **Why unresolved:** The adaptive weighting is a key innovation but its simplicity might be a bottleneck; it treats all frequency components within a segment equally after the initial filter, potentially missing opportunities to weight which frequency bands are most important from each segment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies replacing the softmax segment weighting with a learned attention mechanism that operates jointly on segment and frequency indices, or a small network that processes the stacked frequency tensor \(F\) before aggregation.

### Open Question 4: What is the precise interaction mechanism between FECF and SFPL across dataset types?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The ablation study (Table 3) shows FECF gives the largest boost on highly cyclic datasets (Traffic, Weather), while SFPL gives the largest boost on more non-stationary datasets (ETTh1, ETTh2). The authors state they show "complementary performance characteristics."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper quantifies individual contributions but does not analyze *why* or *how* they complement each other. For instance, does SFPL's enhancement of residual components consistently avoid degrading the periodic patterns captured by FECF? Is the synergy simply additive, or are there complex, dataset-specific interactions?
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A deeper diagnostic analysis, such as visualizing the time-frequency representations after each module, measuring the correlation between the outputs of FECF and SFPL, or designing experiments where one module's output is perturbed to see the effect on the other's utility.

### Open Question 5: Does FreqCycle's efficiency advantage hold for very high-dimensional multivariate series and long horizons?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The efficiency analysis (Figure 4) is conducted only on the Electricity dataset with \(L=96\), \(H=96\). The theoretical complexity \(O(L \log L)\) is stated, but the practical memory and speed profiles for larger \(L\), \(H\), or \(D\) (e.g., Traffic with 862 variables) are not thoroughly benchmarked against all Transformer-based and MLP-based competitors across scales.
- **Why unresolved:** For high-dimensional data, the cost of FFT/IFFT operations in SFPL and the processing of multiple segments could become significant. The "optimal balance" might shift with scale.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comprehensive scaling experiments varying \(D\) (number of variables), \(L\) (lookback), and \(H\) (horizon) across all benchmark datasets, measuring peak memory, training time, and inference latency to definitively map the efficiency frontier compared to baselines like PatchTST and iTransformer.

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters for SFPL (segment window length, overlap, MLP size) are not fully specified; tuning may be dataset-specific.
- Fusion strategy in MFreqCycle (element-wise product vs weighted sum) is ambiguous, risking incorrect implementation.
- Generalization beyond the seven benchmark datasets (e.g., irregular sampling, high noise) remains untested.
