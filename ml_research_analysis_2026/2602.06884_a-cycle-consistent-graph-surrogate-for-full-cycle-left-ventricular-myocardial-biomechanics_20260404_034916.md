# A Cycle-Consistent Graph Surrogate for Full-Cycle Left Ventricular Myocardial Biomechanics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06884
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06884
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** Proposes a unified graph neural network surrogate (CGFENet) that reduces FEA supervision needs by 70% while achieving high fidelity (displacement RMSE 0.39 mm, pressure R² 0.98) and a 74× speed-up for full-cycle left ventricular biomechanics.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the prohibitive computational cost of patient-specific left ventricular finite-element analysis (FEA) for full-cycle biomechanics. The authors introduce CardioGraphFENet (CGFENet), a unified graph neural network surrogate that integrates a global–local graph encoder, a GRU-based temporal encoder conditioned on volume-time, and a cycle-consistent bidirectional formulation to jointly model forward loading (predicting pressure and displacement from volume) and inverse unloading (recovering zero-pressure geometry). Against FEA ground truth, CGFENet achieves high predictive accuracy and produces physiologically plausible pressure-volume loops. Crucially, the cycle-consistency strategy acts as a self-supervision signal, dramatically reducing the need for paired FEA labels—maintaining loading accuracy down to 30% supervision where models without it collapse—while generating a full-cycle prediction in ~44 seconds (a ~74× speed-up over traditional FEA).

## Method Summary
CGFENet processes unstructured patient-specific LV tetrahedral meshes as graphs. A Graph Fusion Encoder uses stacked GATv2 attention layers for local message passing and a mean-pooled global “chamber token” fused via cross-attention to inject whole-heart equilibrium awareness. A GRU-based Temporal RNN Encoder embeds the prescribed volume–time signal, producing a cycle-coherent latent that conditions predictions across the cardiac cycle. The architecture shares the encoder but employs separate loading (pressure + displacement) and unloading (zero-pressure geometry) decoders. Training employs a cycle-consistency loss: after predicting a loaded state, the unloading branch recovers the unloaded geometry; the loading branch is reapplied to this recovered state, and losses penalize discrepancies between the original and reconstructed loaded states, as well as between recovered and true unloaded states. This enforces approximate invertibility, enabling learning from sparsely labeled FEA data.

## Key Results
- Achieves displacement RMSE of 0.39 mm and pressure R² of 0.98 on held-out test meshes.
- Cycle consistency maintains loading accuracy (vertex accuracy <1 mm ~0.89) at supervision ratios as low as 30%; without it, accuracy collapses at 30% SR.
- Generates a full-cycle prediction in ~44 seconds, yielding a ~74× speed-up over conventional FEA.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Global–Local Graph Encoder:** Stacked GATv2 layers capture local mesh features, while a mean-pooled global chamber token (summarizing cavity volume, wall thickness statistics) is fused back via cross-attention. This injects explicit global awareness (e.g., pressure–volume coupling) without rigid pooling, approximating FEA-like weak-form equilibrium. *Break condition:* May fail on anatomies where simple volume/wall thickness descriptors poorly summarize global mechanical state (e.g., highly trabeculated or pathological shapes).
- **GRU-based Temporal Encoder:** An MLP embeds per-time-step features (target volume, volume offset, phase), and a GRU propagates these across the cycle, capturing history-dependent dynamics (e.g., pressure decay during diastole, isovolumic phases). *Break condition:* May produce non-smooth or incoherent outputs for irregular, non-physiological volume–time signals outside the training distribution.
- **Cycle-Consistency Training:** The load→unload→reload loop forces the forward (loading) and inverse (unloading) branches to learn approximate inverses via reconstruction losses. This provides self-supervision from unlabeled or sparsely labeled data. *Break condition:* In pathophysiological states where the biomechanical mapping is non-invertible or non-unique (e.g., isotropic remodeling, aneurysm), the constraint may bias the inverse solution, especially under limited supervision.

## Foundational Learning
- **Graph Neural Networks for Mesh Processing:** Needed to handle irregular, unstructured tetrahedral meshes without fixed node ordering or registration. *Quick check:* Does the model generalize to meshes with varying node counts and connectivity? (Yes, tested on 67 distinct meshes.)
- **Sequence Modeling with GRUs:** Needed to capture temporal coherence and phase-dependent mechanics from a prescribed volume–time trajectory. *Quick check:* Does replacing the GRU with a per-time-step MLP degrade cycle waveform smoothness and pressure accuracy? (Yes, ablation shows pressure RMSE worsens from 1.70 to 2.43 mmHg.)
- **Cycle Consistency as Regularization:** Needed to reduce dependency on paired FEA labels by treating forward and inverse tasks as mutual inverses. *Quick check:* Does removing cycle-consistency cause a sharper accuracy drop at low supervision ratios? (Yes, loading accuracy collapses at 30% SR without it.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Inputs (LV mesh graph with node coordinates/labels + global descriptors, V–t signal) -> Graph Fusion Encoder (GATv2 blocks + global token pooling + cross-attention) -> Temporal RNN Encoder (MLP embed + GRU) -> Fusion (time-pooled global latent + broadcast local latent) -> Decoders (Loading: Pressure + Displacement; Unloading: Zero-pressure displacement) -> Cycle-Consistency Loop (load→unload→reload losses)

**Critical path:**  
1. Encode each mesh once via Graph Fusion Encoder → obtain Z_local (node-wise) and Z_global (pooled).  
2. For each time step *k* in cycle, embed V_t and phase, run GRU to get T_k, fuse Z_global with T_k → L_global.  
3. Broadcast-fuse Z_local with L_global per node → L_local.  
4. Predict pressure P_t from L_global; predict displacement u_t from L_local.  
5. For cycle loss: from Z_local (unloaded) run LoadingDecoder → predicted loaded state; feed predicted loaded state into UnloadingDecoder → recovered unloaded state; reload from recovered state and compare to first loaded state.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Speed vs. Detail: ~74× speed-up trades exact FEA convergence for ~0.4 mm displacement error.  
- Supervision vs. Generalization: Cycle consistency reduces labeled data need (down to 30% SR) but assumes invertibility.  
- Fixed Material vs. Adaptability: Current model uses fixed Fung parameters; extending to variable stiffness requires retraining.  
- Bidirectional Unity vs. Specialization: Shared encoder may limit per-task capacity, but cycle loss enforces compatibility.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Poor pressure fidelity (low R², high RMSE): Likely global fusion failure or temporal deficiency; check Z_global aggregation and GRU hidden states.  
- Unloading instability (high Hausdorff, poor vertex accuracy): Cycle-consistency loss weight too low or missing; monitor reconstruction errors separately.  
- Non-physiological P–V loops: Discretization too coarse for lumped-parameter coupling, or GRU fails at phase transitions; inspect T_k evolution.  
- Generalization failure on new anatomy: Graph encoder fails on extreme wall thickness/volume beyond training PCA bounds.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Temporal Encoder Ablation (A1): Replace GRU with MLP-per-time-step; expect degradation in pressure cycle smoothness and R².  
2. Global Fusion Ablation (A2): Remove cross-attention global token fusion; expect worsened pressure prediction (loss of global coupling) and possibly displacement.  
3. Supervision Efficiency Test (Table 2): Train with varying supervision ratios (70%, 50%, 30%, 10%) with and without cycle consistency; expect cycle-enabled model to maintain accuracy down to ~30% SR, while no-cycle model collapses by 30% SR.

## Limitations
- Training distribution narrow: Meshes derived from a PCA shape model with coefficients clipped to [−2, 2]; likely fails on extreme anatomies (e.g., severe dilatation, hypertrophy) or pathologies not represented.
- Fixed material properties: All FEA uses identical Fung-type parameters; no evidence of robustness to inter-patient variability in passive stiffness or active tension.
- Invertibility assumption: Cycle consistency presumes approximate bijectivity between loading and unloading; may force biased inverse solutions in non-invertible states (e.g., aneurysms with multiple zero-pressure configurations).

## Confidence
- Predictive accuracy (displacement RMSE 0.39 mm, pressure R² 0.98): **High** (robust held‑out test metrics across 67 meshes).
- Data efficiency via cycle consistency (stable at 30% supervision): **High** (clear large‑effect ablation in Table 2).
- 74× speed‑up over FEA: **Medium** (baseline FEA runtime and hardware unspecified; surrogate runtime may vary).
- Mechanistic interpretations (encoder captures equilibrium, GRU models dynamics, cycle consistency enforces invertibility): **Medium** (component ablations supportive but causal links indirect; break conditions identified but not empirically tested).

## Next Checks
1. **Out‑of‑distribution anatomy test:** Evaluate on LV meshes with PCA coefficients beyond [−2, 2] and on patient‑specific pathological models (e.g., HCM, DCM, aneurysm). Measure degradation in displacement/pressure accuracy and unloading quality to quantify the break condition of the global token.
2. **Material sensitivity analysis:** Generate a test set via FEA with systematically varied passive exponential coefficients and active tension scaling. Train surrogates on a baseline material set and test across the parameter ranges; report accuracy drop‑off to define the material envelope of applicability.
3. **Cycle‑consistency hyperparameter sweep:** Systematically vary the cycle‑loss weight (λ_cycle) and supervision ratio (SR) across low‑SR regimes (e.g., 5%–30%). Plot unloading vertex accuracy and cyclic reconstruction error to map the stability region and identify minimal SR for reliable inverse mapping under different anatomies/materials.
