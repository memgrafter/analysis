# Dynamics-Aligned Shared Hypernetworks for Zero-Shot Actuator Inversion

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.06550
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06550
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** DMA*-SH achieves zero-shot generalization on the Actuator Inversion Benchmark, outperforming domain randomization by 111.8% and a context-aware concatenation baseline by 16.1%.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles a failure mode in contextual reinforcement learning called actuator inversion, where a single action produces opposite effects under different latent contexts (e.g., a motor spinning clockwise vs. counterclockwise). Standard methods fail because they cannot disentangle action effects from the hidden context. The proposed DMA*-SH framework solves this by using a single hypernetwork to generate adapter weights that are shared across the dynamics model, policy, and Q-function. This shared, dynamics-aligned representation, combined with normalization and random input masking, produces stable, directional-concentrated embeddings that allow the agent to infer the context and generalize zero-shot to unseen actuator inversions. The method is validated on a new benchmark, AIB.

## Method Summary
DMA*-SH centralizes learning through a hypernetwork that produces adapter modules. These adapters are injected into three core networks: the learned dynamics model, the policy, and the Q-function. The framework is trained in two stages: first, the dynamics predictor (with hypernetwork-generated adapters) is trained to forecast next states, forcing the hypernetwork to produce context-aware representations. Second, the policy and Q-function, using adapters from the same frozen hypernetwork, are trained via standard RL objectives. Crucially, all networks share input/output normalization statistics and the policy's observations are randomly masked, which the authors show promotes representations that are stable and concentrated along directions relevant to the binary context.

## Key Results
*   DMA*-SH successfully generalizes zero-shot to held-out actuator inversion tasks on the new Actuator Inversion Benchmark (AIB).
*   It outperforms a strong domain randomization baseline by 111.8% and a context-aware concatenation baseline by 16.1% on average return.
*   Ablation studies indicate that the combination of shared hypernetwork adapters, dynamics alignment, and random input masking is critical for stable performance, with each component contributing to the final result.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism is the **dynamics-aligned shared hypernetwork**. Training the dynamics predictor first forces the hypernetwork to generate adapter weights that encode a stable, actionable representation of the latent binary context (e.g., "normal" vs. "inverted" actuator). Because the exact same adapter weights are then used by the policy and Q-function, these value-based modules inherently operate within the correctly inferred context without explicit context labels. Input/output normalization and random input masking act as regularizers that prevent the representation from collapsing or capturing spurious correlations, encouraging the features to concentrate along the true binary context direction. This results in a policy that can reliably distinguish and act under opposite dynamics zero-shot.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Contextual Reinforcement Learning
    *   **Why needed:** Real-world control often involves hidden context variables (e.g., hardware variations) that change transition dynamics. An agent must infer and adapt to these to perform robustly.
    *   **Quick check:** Does the method maintain performance when the latent context switches between training and deployment?
*   **Concept:** Actuator Inversion
    *   **Why needed:** A specific, common failure mode in robotics where the sign of an actuator's effect is flipped (e.g., motor polarity reversed), causing a complete reversal of action outcomes.
    *   **Quick check:** Does a policy trained on one actuator configuration fail catastrophically on its inverse?
*   **Concept:** Hypernetworks
    *   **Why needed:** To generate parameterizations (adapters) for multiple networks (dynamics, policy, Q) in a coordinated way, enforcing a shared, context-sensitive representation.
    *   **Quick check:** Do the generated adapter weights for different networks show high correlation in their context-sensitive subspace?
*   **Concept:** Shared Adapters / modular networks
    *   **Why needed:** Enables knowledge transfer and consistency across the dynamics model and control modules, preventing the policy from learning a dynamics model that contradicts the learned world model.
    *   **Quick check:** Is performance degraded if the policy uses adapters from a different hypernetwork than the dynamics model?
*   **Concept:** Zero-Shot Generalization
    *   **Why needed:** The goal is to deploy in new, unseen context configurations (e.g., a newly installed motor with opposite polarity) without additional training.
    *   **Quick check:** Does the method work on a context configuration never experienced during any training phase?
*   **Concept:** Input/Output Normalization
    *   **Why needed:** Stabilizes training by ensuring consistent scale across different context modes and network components, which is critical for the shared hypernetwork to produce usable adapters.
    *   **Quick check:** Does removing normalization cause training instability or collapse of the learned representation?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** Dynamics Predictor (with Adapter) -> [Training Loss] -> Hypernetwork -> Generates -> Adapter Weights -> Injected Into -> (Dynamics Model, Policy, Q-function). Normalization statistics are shared across all three branches. Random Mask is applied to Policy observations.
*   **Critical Path:** 1) Train Dynamics Predictor (supervised) to convergence, freezing the hypernetwork after initial warm-up. 2) Freeze the trained Hypernetwork and its generated Adapter distribution. 3) Train Policy and Q-function (RL) using the fixed adapters, shared normalization, and random masking.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:** Using a single hypernetwork for all components maximizes parameter sharing and representation alignment but risks underfitting if the hypernetwork capacity is insufficient for the diverse tasks. Random input masking improves representation stability but intentionally discards information, creating a trade-off between robustness and potential performance上限.
*   **Failure Signatures:** 1) **Context Confusion:** Policy performance is near random on the held-out inverted context, indicating the shared representation failed to separate the modes. 2) **Training Instability:** Large variance in policy gradient norms or Q-function values during stage two, suggesting the frozen adapters are incompatible with the RL objective. 3) **Representation Collapse:** PCA or t-SNE of adapter weights or penultimate layer features shows no separation along the known context axis.
*   **First 3 Experiments:** 1) **Replication:** Implement DMA*-SH and reproduce the main results on the AIB suite, verifying the reported 111.8% and 16.1% improvements. 2) **Component Ablation:** Systematically remove/disable: (a) shared hypernetwork (use separate adapters), (b) dynamics predictor pre-training, (c) random input masking, and (d) shared normalization, measuring impact on zero-shot performance. 3) **Context Scalability Probe:** Extend the binary context assumption to a ternary (3-way) actuator inversion setting. ## Limitations
*   **Novel Benchmark Dependency:** Evaluation is primarily on the newly introduced AIB. Its difficulty and generalizability to real-world or other simulated actuator inversion scenarios are not yet established.
*   **Binary Context Assumption:** The theoretical and empirical framework assumes a discrete, binary latent context. Performance and theoretical guarantees may not extend to continuous or higher-cardinality context variations.
*   **Component Interaction:** The ablation study may not fully disentangle the synergistic effects of the proposed components (shared hypernetwork, dynamics alignment, masking, normalization), making precise attribution of cause and effect difficult.
