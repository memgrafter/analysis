# SymTorch: A Framework for Symbolic Distillation of Deep Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.21307  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21307  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Symbolic distillation of MLP layers in Qwen2.5-1.5B via SymTorch achieves 8.3% higher token throughput on Wikitext-2 with perplexity increase of +3.14.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents SymTorch, a PyTorch library that automates symbolic distillation of deep neural network components. Symbolic distillation replaces neural modules with human-readable symbolic expressions derived via symbolic regression. SymTorch lowers engineering barriers by providing a wrapper that handles activation caching, GPU-CPU data transfer, and seamless switching between neural and symbolic forward passes. The framework is validated across multiple domains: it recovers known physical laws from graph neural networks and physics-informed neural networks, decodes arithmetic operations learned by language models, and accelerates transformer inference by substituting MLP layers with symbolic surrogates after PCA dimensionality reduction. In the latter, a proof-of-concept on Qwen2.5-1.5B yields an 8.3% throughput gain with minimal accuracy loss, demonstrating a new Pareto frontier for efficient LLM inference.

## Method Summary
SymTorch operates by wrapping a target neural module (e.g., an MLP layer) with a `SymbolicModel` class. During a data collection phase, forward hooks capture input-output activations on a representative dataset. The framework then applies symbolic regression (via PySR) to these cached activations, fitting separate symbolic models to each output dimension. Users may optionally apply PCA to reduce the dimensionality of the inputs and/or outputs to make SR tractable. After selecting an equation from the SR Pareto front (balancing accuracy and complexity), `switch_to_symbolic` replaces the original module with the compiled symbolic function. The symbolic forward pass bypasses neural computation, potentially accelerating inference if the symbolic expression is cheaper to evaluate. The library also includes utilities like `get_importance` for analyzing input salience, enabling interpretability studies.

## Key Results
- SymTorch successfully recovers known physical interaction laws (e.g., 1/r²) from GNNs trained on particle dynamics and distills closed-form solutions from PINNs (e.g., 1-D heat equation).
- Analysis of LLM-learned arithmetic operations yields simple symbolic approximations (e.g., addition as `x0 + x1`) that closely match the neural computations.
- In an LLM acceleration case study, replacing three MLP layers in Qwen2.5-1.5B with symbolic surrogates (after PCA compression) increases token throughput by 8.3% on Wikitext-2, with perplexity rising from 10.62 to 13.76 (+3.14). The modified model remains on the Pareto front relative to other open-source models of similar size.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** SymTorch lowers the engineering barrier to applying symbolic regression (SR) to deep learning models by automating data collection, transfer, and model switching.
- **Mechanism:** The framework provides a `SymbolicModel` wrapper that uses PyTorch forward hooks to cache input-output activations of any wrapped module or function. It manages GPU-CPU data movement, serialization, and allows seamless toggling between the original neural computation and a user-selected symbolic surrogate from the SR Pareto front.
- **Core assumption:** The primary obstacle to adopting symbolic distillation is practical engineering complexity, not algorithmic limitations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...automates this process by wrapping neural network components, caching input-output activations, managing GPU-CPU data transfer, and enabling seamless switching between neural and symbolic forward passes."
  - [section 3.1]: "SymTorch expands the SR problem... by fitting a separate symbolic model to each output dimension... The `distill` method performs SR... `switch_to_symbolic` replaces the original block..."
  - [corpus]: Weak direct corpus evidence; neighboring papers focus on specific SR applications (e.g., KAN-SR) or logical neural networks, not on lowering engineering barriers for general PyTorch integration.
- **Break condition:** If the SR runtime is intractable for a given component (exponentially scaling with input variables), the automation provides no value despite handling engineering tasks.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Symbolic distillation of individual neural components can recover known physical laws or reveal the functional form of learned operations by approximating local input-output mappings.
- **Mechanism:** SR (via PySR) is performed on the cached activations of a specific module. By constraining the search space (e.g., with variable transforms) and analyzing the Pareto front of equations, one can identify simple, human-readable expressions that approximate the component's behavior, which may correspond to underlying physical or algorithmic rules.
- **Core assumption:** A neural component implementing a known function (e.g., a force law) will have an input-output relationship that is discoverable by SR given sufficient, representative data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2]: "Using SymTorch, we reproduce the symbolic recovery results... successfully distilling the true interaction laws across all systems considered."
  - [section 4.3.1]: "Using SymTorch, we further distilled the trained PINN into a closed-form analytic expression, successfully recovering the solution to the 1-D heat equation."
  - [section 4.3.2]: Table 2 shows SR recovering forms close to expected arithmetic operations (e.g., addition approx. as `x0 + x1`).
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like "KAN-SR" and "Deep Symbolic Optimization" also use SR for discovery, supporting the mechanism's plausibility but not specific to the component-wise approach.
- **Break condition:** If the learned component's function is too complex, discontinuous, or not well-represented by the allowed operator set, SR will fail to find an accurate or interpretable surrogate.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Replacing neural network layers (specifically transformer MLPs) with accurate symbolic surrogates, after dimensionality reduction, can improve inference throughput while maintaining acceptable performance on a target distribution.
- **Mechanism:** PCA is applied to compress the high-dimensional input and output activations of an MLP layer to a lower-dimensional subspace. SymTorch then distills the functional mapping between these compressed spaces. The original MLP is replaced by this symbolic function followed by inverse PCA transforms. Simpler symbolic evaluation and reduced parameter count can accelerate compute-bound matrix operations.
- **Core assumption:** The dominant, performance-critical computation in an MLP can be captured by a low-dimensional symbolic manifold with minimal accuracy loss for a specific task/dataset.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1]: "achieving an 8.3% increase in token throughput... with a perplexity increase of +3.14... the modified model remains on the Pareto front."
  - [section 4.1, Table 1]: The perplexity increase is comparable to PCA compression alone (`+3.11` vs `+3.14`), suggesting the symbolic step adds minimal degradation.
  - [corpus]: Limited; neighboring work on LLM acceleration (e.g., pruning, quantization) does not use symbolic surrogates, so this is a novel, evidence-backed claim within the paper only.
- **Break condition:** If the required dimensionality reduction (PCA) causes prohibitive accuracy loss, or if the symbolic surrogate cannot be efficiently evaluated on GPU (e.g., involves complex non-linearities), the speedup will diminish or reverse.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Symbolic Regression (SR)**
  - **Why needed here:** SR is the core engine that transforms neural activations into equations. Understanding its stochastic, Pareto-optimal nature (searching expression space for accuracy vs. complexity trade-offs) is crucial to interpreting results and setting expectations.
  - **Quick check question:** What does it mean if the "best" equation from the Pareto front (per Equation 2) is not the true underlying law, and the true law appears at higher complexity?
- **Concept: PyTorch Forward Hooks & Module Wrapping**
  - **Why needed here:** SymTorch's automation relies on registering hooks to detour activations for caching without modifying model code. Engineers must understand hook lifecycle and scope to debug data collection.
  - **Quick check question:** If a wrapped module is called multiple times within a forward pass (e.g., in a loop), how might the cached I/O data be affected?
- **Concept: Dimensionality Reduction for SR (PCA)**
  - **Why needed here:** SR complexity scales exponentially with input variables. PCA is presented as a critical preprocessing step to make SR on high-dimensional MLP activations tractable. Its impact on information retention must be balanced.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is compressing the *input* to the MLP (pre-activation) considered more critical for SR tractability than compressing the output?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - `SymbolicModel`: Main wrapper class inheriting from `nn.Module`. Manages the original `block`, activation cache (`self.inputs`, `self.outputs`), SR configuration, and symbolic expression compilation.
  - `distill()`: Orchestrates data preparation, calls PySR, and stores results.
  - `switch_to_symbolic()`: Replaces `self.block` with a compiled symbolic function (from the Pareto front) using `self.outputs` to map dimensions.
  - `switch_to_block()`: Restores the original neural module.
  - `get_importance()`: Utility for identifying key input dimensions (used in GNN case study).
  - *External dependencies:* PySR for evolutionary search, scikit-learn for PCA (used in example applications).
- **Critical path:** `model = SymbolicModel(original_module)` → `model(train_data)` (to cache I/O) → `model.distill(sr_params)` → `model.switch_to_symbolic(complexity)` → `model(eval_data)` (runs symbolic forward).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **SR fidelity vs. compute:** Longer SR runs and larger datasets yield better surrogates but are costly. Tradeoff is managed via `n_iterations`, `population_size`.
  - **Dimensionality vs. accuracy (LLM case):** Aggressive PCA enabled SR but caused most perplexity increase. The symbolic replacement itself added only ~1% (Paper: "the symbolic model captures the MLP’s behavior... with minimal additional degradation").
  - **Interpretability vs. expressivity:** Simpler equations (lower complexity) are more interpretable but may be less accurate. The `score` metric (Eq 2) balances this.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - SR fails to converge or produces high-loss equations: likely insufficient/ non-representative cached data, inappropriate operator set, or component function too complex.
  - Large accuracy drop after `switch_to_symbolic`: indicates PCA removed critical information or SR failed to find a good surrogate for the reduced subspace.
  - Silent hook failure: cached I/O shapes mismatch expected dimensions; check that wrapped module's forward signature matches the data passed.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Minimal sanity check:** Wrap a single `nn.Linear` layer, generate random data, run `distill()` with simple operators (`+,-,*`), and `switch_to_symbolic()`. Verify output numerical similarity to original layer within tolerance.
  2. **GNN edge model replication:** Follow Section 4.2/E. Wrap a GNN's edge MLP, cache I/O from particle dynamics data, apply variable transforms (`dx, dy, r`), run SR with `inv`, and check if recovered force law (e.g., `1/r^2`) appears on Pareto front.
  3. **LLM MLP surrogate prototype:** Replicate Section 4.1 pipeline: for a small transformer (Qwen/Llama), install forward/pre-forward hooks on an MLP block to record pre/post activations. Train PCA on a dataset subset, call `SymbolicModel` on the PCA-compressed function, and benchmark throughput change before/after `switch_to_symbolic()`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can the computational scalability of symbolic regression be improved for high-dimensional neural network components?** The exponential runtime growth with input variables limits application to complex layers without aggressive dimensionality reduction.
- **How can complexity metrics in symbolic regression be refined to prioritize human-interpretable expressions over mathematically equivalent but opaque forms?** Current measures (e.g., node count) may yield expressions that are functionally sound but cognitively impenetrable.
- **Can learned linear projections (beyond PCA) preserve more task-relevant information during dimensionality reduction for symbolic surrogate training?** PCA is unsupervised and may discard directions critical for the model's predictive function.
- **Do symbolic surrogates of neural network components generalize across domains or are they inherently task- and dataset-specific?** The paper's LLM surrogates were trained and evaluated on the same distribution.
- **Which specific layers or positions within a transformer architecture are most amenable to symbolic replacement, and how many can be substituted before performance degrades substantially?** The study arbitrarily replaced three MLP layers; a systematic exploration is needed.

## Limitations
- Exact reproduction of the LLM speedup experiment is blocked by missing details (random seed, specific data subset, full PySR hyperparameters, model variant).
- Generalizability of the 8.3% throughput gain to other architectures, datasets, and layer types is untested.
- The approach relies on PCA dimensionality reduction; the chosen dimensions (32 input, 8 output) may be suboptimal or cause excessive information loss for other models.

- Mechanism 2 (recovering known laws): High
- Mechanism 3 (inference acceleration): Medium
