# Counterfactual Fairness with Graph Uncertainty

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03203
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03203
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: CF-GU introduces an uncertainty-aware counterfactual fairness auditing framework that quantifies graph uncertainty via entropy and provides confidence intervals for fairness metrics.

## Executive Summary
Counterfactual fairness (CF) audits depend on a causal graph, but the correct graph is often uncertain. This paper proposes CF with Graph Uncertainty (CF-GU), a method that addresses this by generating an ensemble of plausible causal DAGs using bootstrapped causal discovery under domain knowledge constraints. It quantifies graph uncertainty via normalized Shannon entropy over edge distributions and propagates the ensemble through the CF pipeline to compute confidence intervals for fairness metrics (Positive/Negative Switch Rates) and individual counterfactual prediction variance. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets (COMPAS, Adult) show that varying domain knowledge significantly affects CF conclusions and uncertainty, and that CF-GU can detect known biases with high confidence even under minimal domain knowledge, thus offering a robust and uncertainty-aware approach to fairness auditing.

## Method Summary
Given training data, test data, a fixed binary classifier, a protected attribute, and domain knowledge constraints (e.g., causal orderings, forbidden edges), CF-GU operates as follows. First, it generates multiple bootstrap samples from the training data. For each sample, a constraint-based causal discovery algorithm (BOSS) produces a CPDAG; all DAGs within each CPDAG’s Markov Equivalence Class are enumerated. The union of these DAGs across bootstraps forms an ensemble representing graph uncertainty. The ensemble’s uncertainty is quantified by normalizing Shannon entropy over edge frequencies, both for the full graph and the subgraph involving descendants of the protected attribute. For each DAG in the ensemble, a linear structural causal model (SCM) is fitted on its corresponding bootstrap sample. Counterfactuals for test instances are created by intervening on the protected attribute and predicting outcomes using the SCM; these counterfactuals are then scored by the fixed classifier. Fairness metrics (Positive Switch Rate, Negative Switch Rate) are computed per DAG and aggregated to obtain means, variances, and confidence intervals, while individual counterfactual score variances are also recorded. Deterministic counterfactuals and a fixed classifier isolate graph uncertainty as the sole source of variation.

## Key Results
- Synthetic experiments demonstrate that the strength of domain knowledge (Low/Medium/High, additional forbidden edges) directly influences counterfactual fairness conclusions and uncertainty measures: stricter constraints reduce total entropy (e.g., from 0.766 to 0.145) and narrow confidence intervals for fairness metrics.
- On COMPAS and Adult datasets, CF-GU successfully detects known racial and gender biases with high confidence, even when only minimal domain knowledge is provided, indicating robustness to causal specification uncertainty.
- The method yields both population-level uncertainty (CI for PSR/NSR) and individual-level uncertainty (variance of counterfactual scores), allowing practitioners to evaluate the reliability of CF audits under different causal assumptions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Generating an ensemble of plausible causal DAGs via bootstrapped causal discovery under domain knowledge constraints captures graph uncertainty.
- **Mechanism:** Multiple bootstrap samples from training data are fed into a constraint-based CD algorithm (BOSS). For each sample, BOSS outputs a CPDAG; all DAGs within each CPDAG's Markov Equivalence Class are enumerated. The union of these DAGs across bootstraps forms the ensemble.
- **Core assumption:** The CD algorithm's output under the given constraints is a reasonable approximation of the set of graphs compatible with the domain knowledge and data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "bootstraps a Causal Discovery algorithm under domain knowledge constraints to produce a bag of plausible Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs)"
  - [section 3] "For each bootstrap sample Xb_train, we apply a CD algorithm to get a CPDAG Cb, then enumerate all DAGs in its Markov Equivalence Class."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: Corpus neighbors focus on bias mitigation or evaluation with single graphs, not ensemble generation for uncertainty. No direct anchor.
- **Break condition:** If the CD algorithm's assumptions (e.g., faithfulness, i.i.d. data from exponential family) are severely violated, the ensemble may not represent plausible graphs, leading to misleading uncertainty estimates.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Normalized Shannon entropy over edge frequencies in the DAG ensemble quantifies the uncertainty of the causal specification for the subgraph relevant to the protected attribute.
- **Mechanism:** For each directed edge *e*, compute its frequency *p_e* across all DAGs in the ensemble. The binary entropy *H_e* = -*p_e* log(*p_e*) - (1-*p_e*) log(1-*p_e*) is calculated. Total normalized entropy *H_G* is the sum of *H_e* over all distinct edges *E*, divided by |*E*| ln 2. A separate entropy *H_GA* is computed for edges in subgraphs rooted at the protected attribute *A*.
- **Core assumption:** The variability in edge presence across bootstrap-derived DAGs meaningfully reflects uncertainty about the true causal structure given the data and constraints.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "quantifies graph uncertainty via normalized Shannon entropy on edge distributions"
  - [section 3] "We then [...] measure the entropy of the bag of DAGs [...] HG = (∑_e H_e) / (|E| ln 2)."
  - [section 4.1] Table 1 shows entropy (HG, HGA) decreases with stricter domain knowledge (Low → Medium → High), aligning with the mechanism's output.
- **Break condition:** If the CD algorithm produces CPDAGs with many undirected edges (high Markov equivalence class size), the DAG enumeration may be computationally intractable or the entropy may become an unreliable summary if the ensemble size is insufficient.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Propagating the DAG ensemble through the counterfactual generation and classification pipeline yields distributions for individual counterfactual outcomes and population-level fairness metrics (PSR/NSR), from which confidence intervals indicate the reliability of the CF audit.
- **Mechanism:** For each DAG *G_m* in the ensemble, fit an SCM *M_m* on the corresponding bootstrap sample. For each test individual *x_i*, generate a counterfactual *x_i^cf* by intervening on the protected attribute *A* using *M_m*. Feed *x_i^cf* to the fixed classifier ϕ to get score *R_i^cf*. Across *M* models, compute mean, variance, and empirical CI for *R_i^cf*, and for PSR/NSR computed over the whole test set.
- **Core assumption:** The variance in counterfactual scores and fairness metrics across different, equally plausible causal models is a valid measure of the audit's sensitivity to causalgraph uncertainty.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "computes confidence intervals for CF metrics (Positive/Negative Switch Rates) and for individual counterfactual prediction variance."
  - [section 3] "We then [...] evaluate the variance of its output scores, as well as the variance of the PSR and the NSR."
  - [section 4.1] Figure 3 shows individual score variance and CI shrink with more domain knowledge; Figure 4 shows PSR/NSR CIs narrow from Low to Medium/High knowledge.
- **Break condition:** If the SCM fitting procedure (e.g., linear functional forms) is misspecified for the true data-generating process underlying a DAG, the counterfactuals *x_i^cf* may be systematically biased, and the ensemble variance may not capture the true audit uncertainty.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Structural Causal Models (SCMs) and do-calculus**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire CF audit is defined on an SCM; counterfactuals are derived via abduction, action, prediction. Understanding exogenous variables, functional assignments, and interventions is essential to grasp what changing *A* entails.
  - *Quick check question:* Given an SCM with DAG A → B ← C, what is the conditional independence relation implied, and how does intervening on A affect B versus C?
- **Concept: Counterfactual Fairness (CF) operationalization**
  - *Why needed here:* CF-GU's output metrics (PSR, NSR) are defined relative to counterfactual predictions. The engineer must understand how individual counterfactuals are aggregated into these rates.
  - *Quick check question:* How are Positive Switch Rate (PSR) and Negative Switch Rate (NSR) calculated from a dataset of original and counterfactual predictions?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (training data, test data, classifier ϕ, protected attribute A, domain knowledge constraints) → Bootstrap & CD Module (generates DAG ensemble via bootstrapped BOSS and MEC enumeration) → Uncertainty Quantification Module (computes normalized Shannon entropy H_G and H_GA) and SCM & Counterfactual Module (fits linear SCMs, generates counterfactuals by intervening on A, obtains scores via ϕ) → CF Metrics Module (aggregates over ensemble to compute means, variances, confidence intervals for PSR, NSR, and individual scores) → Output (entropy measures, CIs, individual variance).
- **Critical path:** Domain knowledge constraints → BOSS causal discovery → DAG ensemble → SCM fitting → counterfactual generation → classifier scoring → PSR/NSR computation → CI calculation. Any failure (e.g., CD non-convergence, SCM non-identifiable) breaks the chain.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Bootstrap replicates (B) vs. computational cost:* Higher B yields more precise entropy/CI but requires fitting B SCMs and generating B counterfactuals per test instance.
  - *Domain knowledge strictness vs. entropy:* Stricter constraints reduce CD search space, lowering entropy and CI width, but may exclude plausible graphs; looser constraints increase coverage but may yield high entropy and wide CI, reducing audit conclusiveness.
  - *Linear SCMs vs. flexibility:* Linear SCMs ensure identifiability and speed but may be misspecified if true relationships are nonlinear.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Extremely high H_G (~1) or H_GA across all knowledge levels: CD algorithm may not converge or constraints may be contradictory/too weak.
  - Very wide CI for PSR/NSR (e.g., covering [0,1]): Ensemble is too diverse; audit conclusion is irrecoverably uncertain given constraints.
  - Inconsistent PSR/NSR across classifiers: May indicate classifier decision boundary interacts with specific causal pathways made plausible by the ensemble.
  - SCM fitting warnings (e.g., non-invertible covariance): DAG structure may be incompatible with linear Gaussian assumptions or data may have multicollinearity.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Synthetic replication:** Use the paper's synthetic DAG (Figure 2) and data generating process. Run CF-GU under Low, Medium, High, Forbid-X1, Forbid-X2 constraints. Verify that Table 1 and Figures 3-4 trends hold: entropy and CI decrease from Low→Medium→High, and Forbid-X2 yields high uncertainty like Low, while Forbid-X1 yields low uncertainty.
  2. **COMPAS sensitivity:** On COMPAS data, run CF-GU with Low vs. High knowledge as in paper. Verify Table 3: total entropy decreases but subgraph entropy (H_GA) increases. Verify Figure 6: under High knowledge, PSR/NSR CIs shrink and point to bias (non-zero rates). Explore a new constraint: forbid race as a parent of any variable (stricter than High) and observe if H_GA further changes and if PSR/NSR remain biased.
  3. **Adult dataset exploration:** On Adult data, replicate Low vs. High results (Table 4, Figures 7-8). Key check: under Low knowledge, NSR for Male→Female has high variance/wide CI (uncertain unfairness). Under High knowledge, NSR increases and CI shrinks (confident unfairness). Test an intermediate causal ordering (e.g., only forbid connections within {age, sex, race}) to see if it already yields confident unfairness, identifying minimal sufficient constraints.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** How can we evaluate the goodness of fit of the domain knowledge constraints (i.e., the causal assumptions) used in CF-GU?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The conclusion states future work includes "evaluating the goodness of fit of knowledge assumptions, given the available data."
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper does not provide a method to validate whether the imposed topological restrictions (e.g., causal orderings, forbidden edges) are consistent with the observed data, leaving practitioners without a measure of confidence in the knowledge assumptions themselves.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* A systematic framework, such as likelihood-based model selection, posterior predictive checks, or cross-validation over different constraint sets, that quantifies how well each set of domain knowledge aligns with the data distribution.
- **Question:** How can we reduce the computational burden of enumerating all DAGs in the Markov equivalence class and fitting SCMs for each, to make CF-GU scalable to large variable sets?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] Future work mentions "optimizing the bag of DAGs, to minimize the computational cost of fitting SCMs and generating counterfactuals for large datasets."
  - *Why unresolved:* The current approach enumerates all DAGs from each CPDAG, which scales super-exponentially with the number of nodes, making it prohibitive for high-dimensional data.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Algorithms that approximate the DAG ensemble (e.g., Monte Carlo sampling), exploit conditional independence symmetries, or use parallelization strategies that retain accurate uncertainty estimates for CF metrics.
- **Question:** Can CF-GU be extended to account for latent (unmeasured) confounders while still quantifying graph uncertainty?
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The paper explicitly compares to prior work and notes that "CF-GU assumes no hidden variables and assumes the graph itself is uncertain." This limitation is not addressed in the methodology.
  - *Why unresolved:* Real-world datasets often contain unobserved variables that induce spurious correlations; ignoring them may lead to biased causal graphs and misleading CF assessments.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Integration of causal discovery algorithms that handle latent variables (e.g., FCI) into the bootstrapping procedure, and definition of appropriate entropy measures over equivalence classes that include latent nodes.
- **Question:** How can individual-level uncertainty in counterfactual generation (e.g., from posterior distributions over exogenous variables) be combined with graph uncertainty to provide more robust fairness conclusions?
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The authors state they use deterministic counterfactuals to isolate graph uncertainty, but note that their procedure is "extensible to probabilistic counterfactuals," implying they have not explored this direction.
  - *Why unresolved:* Deterministic counterfactuals fix the exogenous noise for each individual, ignoring posterior uncertainty about the true noise values; combining this with graph uncertainty would yield a fuller picture of reliability at the individual level.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* A Bayesian formulation where both the causal graph and exogenous variables are treated probabilistically, propagating both sources of variance to confidence intervals for PSR/NSR.

## Limitations
- DAG enumeration from CPDAGs can be computationally explosive; the paper does not report typical Markov equivalence class sizes, raising scalability concerns.
- SCM fitting details are sparse (e.g., exact regression procedure, handling of categoricals, identifiability checks), making faithful reproduction challenging.
- Reliance on linear SCMs and Gaussian assumptions; violations could bias counterfactuals and CF metrics, and the robustness to such misspecification is not thoroughly investigated.

## Confidence
- **Graph uncertainty ensemble generation (bootstrapped BOSS + enumeration):** Medium. Mechanism plausible but lacks validation of enumeration feasibility and sensitivity to CD assumptions.
- **Entropy as graph uncertainty measure:** High. Entropy is mathematically sound and synthetic trends align.
- **Confidence intervals via ensemble variance:** Medium. Intuition sound, but robustness to SCM misspecification and CD failures not fully shown.
