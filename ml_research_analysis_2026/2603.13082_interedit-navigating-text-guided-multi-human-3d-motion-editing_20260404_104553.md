# InterEdit: Navigating Text-Guided Multi-Human 3D Motion Editing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.13082
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13082
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art on TMME benchmark with FID 0.3707 and 5.85% g2t R@1 improvement over baselines.

## Executive Summary
InterEdit tackles text-guided multi-human 3D motion editing, generating a target two-person motion from a source motion and a textual edit instruction while preserving interaction dynamics and non-edited content. The framework introduces a synchronized diffusion model with two novel alignment modules: Semantic-Aware Plan Token Alignment for capturing high-level editing intent, and Interaction-Aware Frequency Token Alignment using DCT to model temporal coupling. Trained on the newly constructed InterEdit3D dataset (5,161 source-target-text triplets), InterEdit achieves state-of-the-art results on the TMME benchmark, demonstrating superior text-motion consistency and interaction realism.

## Method Summary
InterEdit employs a synchronized classifier-free conditional diffusion model with a transformer denoiser. Inputs include a source two-person motion (AMASS features) and a text instruction. The source is encoded by a lightweight Transformer, text by frozen CLIP. The denoiser processes symmetrically interleaved motion tokens for the two persons, ensuring equal treatment and role-switching capability. Two alignment modules are inserted into the transformer: (1) Plan tokens (16) aligned via InfoNCE loss with a frozen contrastive motion teacher to align editing semantics; (2) Frequency tokens (6) derived from DCT band-energy descriptors of average/difference signals to supervise temporal synchronization. Additional losses include diffusion reconstruction, geometric constraints (velocity, foot contact, bone length), and interaction constraints (distance map, relative orientation). At inference, synchronized classifier-free guidance (SCFG) combines conditional and unconditional predictions.

## Key Results
- FID of 0.3707 on TMME, outperforming TIMotion (0.4451)
- Generated-to-target retrieval (g2t R@1) improved by 5.85% over strongest baseline
- Maintains source preservation and interaction realism while achieving high text-motion consistency

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Symmetric interleaving of the two persons' tokens removes order bias and enables role switching. Plan token alignment directly links high-level editing intent to a clean motion embedding via contrastive learning, guiding diffusion toward semantically correct edits. Frequency token alignment uses DCT band descriptors to explicitly enforce temporal coupling and phase synchronization between the two persons, which is critical for realistic interactions. SCFG allows joint conditioning on source and text without quality degradation, while the composite losses ensure physical plausibility through per-person geometry and inter-person interactions.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Source Motion Encoder (Transformer) -> csrc; Text Encoder (CLIP) -> ctext; Denoiser Transformer (interleaved tokens + plan tokens + frequency tokens) -> predicted x0; Localized Pattern Amplification (LPA) per person; Loss aggregator (Ldiff + geometric + interaction + Lplan + Lfreq).

Critical path: Input source motion and text -> encode conditions -> for each diffusion step: sample xt, feed to denoiser with AdaLN -> get x0 prediction -> apply LPA -> compute losses -> backpropagate. At inference, use DDIM with SCFG.

Design tradeoffs:
- Symmetric interleaving vs. separate streams: Improves role-switching but doubles sequence length, increasing memory.
- Plan token alignment vs. direct text conditioning: Adds explicit high-level supervision but depends on quality of frozen teacher encoder.
- Frequency token alignment vs. raw temporal losses: Provides interpretable synchronization control but requires correct DCT band selection and pooling.

Failure signatures:
- High g2s retrieval but low g2t: Plan alignment ineffective (monitor Lplan); increase λp or check teacher encoder.
- Low g2s and unsynchronized motion: Frequency tokens misaligned (monitor Lfreq); adjust λf or DCT parameters.
- Noisy motions or foot sliding: Geometric losses (Lvel, Lfoot) too low or incorrectly implemented; verify thresholds.
- Training instability: Alignment losses causing spikes; reduce λp/λf, add gradient clipping, check normalization.

First three experiments:
1. Train on a minimal subset (10 triplets) for a few epochs; all losses should decrease smoothly.
2. Validate frequency token alignment by computing ground-truth band descriptors g(x0) and comparing to decoded tokens from a frozen checkpoint; low/mid bands should align better than high.
3. Qualitatively inspect edited motions for a fixed set of edits; verify source preservation, semantic edit execution, and interaction continuity.

## Limitations
- InterEdit3D dataset not publicly released; exact reproduction of training data and splits is impossible.
- Ambiguities in geometric/interaction loss formulations (e.g., thresholds for masked distance-map) require inference from other papers.
- Evaluation relies on non-public InterGen retrieval model; independent verification of metrics is hindered.
- Challenging cases (subtle/ambiguous edits) remain failure modes, as noted in the paper.
