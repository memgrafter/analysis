# Hierarchical Successor Representation for Robust Transfer

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12753  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12753  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Hierarchical SR with NMF yields policy-robust, interpretable features that significantly improve transfer efficiency and exploration over standard SR.

## Executive Summary
The Successor Representation (SR) is a powerful predictive feature for reinforcement learning but suffers from policy dependence and spectral diffusion, limiting its robustness for transfer. This paper introduces Hierarchical SR (HSR), which integrates temporal abstractions (options) into SR to create representations that are stable across policy changes. Applying Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) to HSR produces sparse, low-rank bases that capture environment topology, such as bottlenecks. Evaluated on four-room transfer and large procedural mazes, HSR reduces successor matrix change dramatically and improves sample efficiency and exploration coverage compared to SR-based baselines.

## Method Summary
HSR constructs a two-level hierarchy: a high-level policy selects options (temporally extended actions), and a low-level SR is learned over the resulting option-augmented state space. This decouples the representation from specific primitive policies, reducing policy dependence. To derive interpretable features, NMF is applied to the HSR matrix, enforcing non-negativity and sparsity to extract topological bases (e.g., room-like regions). The method assumes pre-defined or pre-trained options; the paper does not implement online option discovery. Experimental protocols compare HSR and HSR-NMF against flat SR variants and SR with intrinsic rewards (SPIE) in grid-world and maze transfer tasks.

## Key Results
- HSR reduced successor matrix change under policy transfer by a significant margin (p<0.001).
- HSR-NMF required far fewer training episodes than all SR-based baselines to achieve comparable performance (p<1e-9).
- HSR-SPIE intrinsic reward achieved higher state coverage in large procedurally generated mazes, whereas standard SR-based exploration degraded.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Temporal abstractions (options) create a hierarchical Markov chain where the high-level dynamics are coarser and less sensitive to changes in primitive action policies. This yields successor features that are more invariant across different policies solving the same task. NMF then decomposes the HSR matrix into additive, non-negative components that align with environmental bottlenecks and subregions, providing interpretable features that generalize better than the full, diffused SR spectrum. The combination stabilizes the representation against policy variations while extracting sparse topological priors.

## Foundational Learning
- **Successor Representation (SR):** Predicts expected future state occupancy under a policy. *Why needed:* Provides predictive features for RL but is policy-dependent. *Quick check:* Compute φ(s) = (I - γP_π)^{-1} e_s for policy π.
- **Options (temporal abstractions)::** Semi-MDP policies with initiation and termination conditions. *Why needed:* Enable hierarchical temporal abstraction to reduce policy sensitivity. *Quick check:* Define option policy π_o, initiation set I_o, termination β_o.
- **Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF):*: Factorizes matrix M ≈ WH with W,H ≥ 0. *Why needed:* Enforces sparsity and parts-based decomposition for interpretable topological features. *Quick check:* Apply NMF to HSR matrix M, inspect basis vectors for spatial clusters.
- **Policy robustness:** Representation invariance across different policies achieving same task. *Why needed:* For reliable transfer without retraining features. *Quick check:* Measure ||φ_π1(s) - φ_π2(s)|| across policies π1, π2.
- **Spectral diffusion:** Smearing of SR eigenvalues across many directions. *Why needed:* Understanding why SR transfers poorly. *Quick check:* Examine eigenvalue spectrum of SR matrix; diffusion = many small-magnitude eigenvalues.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Options -> HSR (hierarchical successor matrix) -> NMF -> Sparse feature basis

**Critical path:** 1) Learn/define options over environment. 2) Collect transitions under a policy using options to build HSR matrix. 3) Apply NMF to HSR to extract sparse basis vectors. 4) Use basis as features for downstream tasks (transfer, exploration).

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- Pre-trained vs. learned options: Pre-trained options simplify HSR but may not adapt to new tasks; learned options add complexity but could improve generality.
- NMF rank: Low rank yields sparser, more interpretable features but risks losing detail; high rank approaches full HSR.
- Hierarchy depth: Paper uses two-level; deeper hierarchies might further abstract but increase approximation error.

**Failure signatures:** 
- Poor option design (e.g., too broad or narrow) leads to HSR that remains policy-sensitive or fails to capture relevant structure.
- NMF rank too high produces features similar to full HSR (no sparsity benefit); too low yields features that cannot discriminate between states.
- Inaccurate HSR estimation (due to limited data) propagates errors to NMF and downstream performance.

**First three experiments:** 
1. Compare flat SR vs. HSR on successor matrix change when switching between policies in four-room domain.
2. Ablate NMF: compare HSR features (full rank) vs. HSR-NMF features (varying ranks) on transfer learning efficiency.
3. Evaluate HSR-SPIE vs. SR-SPIE on state coverage in a large (20x20) procedural maze, measuring sample complexity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can iterative option discovery be integrated into HSR for online adaptation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors identify incorporating iterative option-discovery into learning as a future direction to adjust temporal abstractions in dynamic environments.
- Why unresolved: Current HSR relies on pre-trained options, limiting adaptation when dynamics or tasks change post-training.
- What evidence would resolve it: Algorithms that jointly learn options and HSR during interaction, evaluated on tasks with non-stationary transition dynamics, showing improved robustness over pre-fixed options.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can HSR be effectively applied in deep RL for high-dimensional/continuous domains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose integrating HSR into deep RL systems to leverage its stable properties for representation learning in complex domains.
- Why unresolved: All experiments used linear approximations on discrete grids; deep RL introduces confounding factors like non-stationary features.
- What evidence would resolve it: Deep HSR architectures (e.g., neural SR heads) tested on standard benchmarks (MuJoCo, Atari) demonstrating superior data efficiency or transfer.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does over-complete HSR-NMF improve feature discrimination in aliased environments?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors suggest exploring HSR-NMF in the over-complete regime for environments with repetitive/aliased substructures where spectral methods fail.
- Why unresolved: Paper only considered full-rank NMF; over-completeness could capture repeated patterns but remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluate over-complete HSR-NMF (basis > states) on mazes with identical rooms, measuring feature uniqueness and transfer performance.

## Limitations
- Evaluation confined to discrete grid-worlds and procedural mazes; unknown generalizability to continuous/visual domains.
- Temporal abstractions (options) are not fully described; unclear whether hand-defined or learned, and their scalability impact is unassessed.
- Statistical evidence lacks sample sizes and effect sizes; no ablations to isolate contributions of hierarchy, NMF, and SR; benchmarks only against SR-based methods.

## Next Checks
1. Test HSR on continuous (MuJoCo) and visual (Atari) benchmarks to evaluate generalizability beyond discrete grids.
2. Conduct systematic ablations: flat vs. hierarchical SR, with/without NMF, varying NMF rank/sparsity to disentangle each component's contribution.
3. Compare HSR against non-SR representation methods (e.g., contrastive learning, autoencoders) on transfer and exploration tasks to establish relative advantages.
