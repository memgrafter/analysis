# CausalGDP: Causality-Guided Diffusion Policies for Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09207
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09207
- **Reference count:** 8
- **Primary result:** CausalGDP integrates causal reasoning with diffusion policies to improve performance in high-dimensional RL, achieving superior or competitive results on Gym MuJoCo, Maze2D, AntMaze, Adroit, and Kitchen benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion-based reinforcement learning policies excel at modeling complex action distributions but are limited by their reliance on statistical correlations rather than causal relationships. This can lead to selecting actions that are associated with high returns without truly causing them. CausalGDP overcomes this by first learning a causal dynamical model from offline data to uncover dependencies among states, actions, and rewards (using NOTEARS to obtain sparse causal masks). During online interaction, this causal model is continuously updated and used to compute a guidance gradient that steers the diffusion sampling process toward actions that causally influence future states and rewards. The method combines the expressive power of diffusion policies with causal inductive bias, yielding strong empirical performance across diverse continuous control tasks, especially in challenging high-dimensional environments. Ablation studies confirm that the approach is robust to the choice of causal discovery technique.

## Method Summary
CausalGDP operates in two stages. In the offline phase, a base diffusion policy (noise prediction network ϵ_θ) is trained on the dataset D of transitions (s, a, r, s'). Concurrently, an initial causal dynamical model is learned: linear-Gaussian transition and reward models (f_φ, g_ω) whose inputs are masked by sparse causal masks (C_ss, C_sa, U_sr, U_ar) derived via NOTEARS. In the online phase, for each environment step the agent collects new transitions, updates the causal masks periodically by re-running causal discovery on a recent replay buffer, and computes a causal guidance term ∇a log p(s_{t+1}, r_t | s_t, do(a_t)) using the current causal models. This gradient is incorporated into the diffusion denoising process over K steps via ϵ_cg_θ = ϵ_θ - λ_k / √(1-ᾱ_k) ∇a log p(...), steering action samples. The policy loss combines the denoising loss and a Q-guided loss from a double Q-network, which is updated simultaneously. The result is a diffusion policy biased toward actions that have a causal impact on achieving high rewards.

## Key Results
- Achieves superior or competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art diffusion and offline RL methods on a wide range of benchmarks (Gym MuJoCo, Maze2D, AntMaze, Adroit, Kitchen).
- Gains are particularly pronounced in complex, high-dimensional control tasks.
- Ablation studies demonstrate robustness to different causal discovery techniques.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CausalGDP improves upon standard diffusion policies by explicitly modeling causal effects. The causal dynamical model provides an estimate of the interventional distribution p(s', r | s, do(a)), whose gradient with respect to the action gives a direction that increases the likelihood of desirable outcomes. By injecting this gradient into each step of the diffusion denoising, the sampling process is biased toward regions of the action space that truly cause high returns, rather than merely correlating with them. This guidance acts as a regularizer that encourages the policy to explore causally relevant actions, improving sample efficiency and generalization, especially in environments where spurious correlations are common. The linear-Gaussian assumption keeps the gradient computation tractable while still capturing essential causal relationships, as evidenced by the strong empirical results.

## Foundational Learning
- **Diffusion Models for RL**  
  *Why needed:* Offers a flexible, expressive way to model complex, multi-modal action distributions.  
  *Quick check:* Train the diffusion network on offline data and verify that it can reconstruct noised actions and that sampled actions match the training distribution (e.g., via histograms or KL divergence).  

- **Causal Discovery (e.g., NOTEARS)**  
  *Why needed:* Identifies sparse directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) among state, action, and reward variables, avoiding spurious correlations.  
  *Quick check:* Apply NOTEARS to a small synthetic dataset with known causal structure; ensure the learned adjacency matrix recovers the true DAG and is sparse (most weights near zero).  

- **Causal Dynamical Models (Linear‑Gaussian)**  
  *Why needed:* Parameterizes p(s', r | s, do(a)) for computing the guidance gradient analytically.  
  *Quick check:* Train f_φ and g_ω on offline data and evaluate prediction error (MSE) on a held‑out validation set; errors should be reasonable relative to reward/state scales.  

- **Causal Guidance Integration**  
  *Why needed:* Steers diffusion sampling toward causally influential actions.  
  *Quick check:* During a forward pass, compute the norm of the causal gradient; it should be non‑zero and not explode (e.g., < 100). Compare action distributions with and without guidance to confirm a shift.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Offline dataset D -> NOTEARS -> causal masks (C_ss, C_sa) -> causal dynamical models (f_φ, g_ω). f_φ, g_ω and diffusion policy (ϵ_θ) -> causal guidance (∇a log p(s', r|s, do(a))) -> guided denoising (ϵ_cg_θ) -> action. Online interactions feed into replay buffer -> periodic mask updates; Q‑network updates from policy actions.

### Critical Path
Given current state s_t, the policy iteratively denoises for K steps. At each step k, the noise prediction ϵ_θ(s_t, a_t, k) is adjusted by the causal gradient scaled by λ_k/√(1-ᾱ_k), producing ϵ_cg_θ. After K steps, the final a_t is executed. The causal gradient depends on f_φ and g_ω, which use the current masks.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Linear‑Gaussian vs. flexible models:** Simpler models give closed‑form gradients but may limit accuracy; neural networks could improve fidelity but require approximations (e.g., finite differences) that are costly or unstable.
- **Guidance strength (λ_k):** Large values enforce strong causal effects but risk destabilizing diffusion; careful scheduling (γ_t, β_t) is needed but not specified.
- **Mask update frequency:** Frequent updates adapt to distribution shift but increase computational overhead (NOTEARS on large buffer) and may cause mask oscillations; infrequent updates are cheaper but may lag.
- **Computation vs. performance:** Causal discovery and gradient computations add significant runtime, which could be prohibitive for real‑time control.

### Failure Signatures
- **Masks remain uniform** (all entries ≈ 0.5) after NOTEARS → causal structure not learned; may need more data or sparsity regularization.
- **Action norms explode** during online training → guidance coefficients too large or unweighted; inspect gradient norms.
- **Reward model prediction error high** on validation → causal model inadequate; consider increasing capacity or training longer.
- **Performance worse than unguided diffusion baseline** → guidance may be overriding useful diffusion samples; try ablating λ_k or fixing masks.

### First Experiments
1. **Offline diffusion baseline:** Train ϵ_θ on the offline dataset without causal components; evaluate on test tasks to establish a performance baseline.
2. **Causal model validation:** With masks from NOTEARS, train f_φ and g_ω and assess next‑state and reward prediction accuracy on a held‑out set; also check mask sparsity.
3. **Guided denoising sanity check:** Pre‑train diffusion and causal models, then run a few online steps with fixed causal guidance (small λ_k). Verify that the causal gradient is non‑zero, that sampled actions differ from the unguided policy, and that collected rewards are above random.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter gaps:** Guidance coefficients (γ_t, β_t, λ_k) and their schedules are unspecified, making exact replication difficult.
- **Ambiguous reward term:** The use of “optimal reward r*” in Eq. 5 is undefined; it is unclear whether this refers to the actual environment reward or a proxy, conflicting with standard online RL assumptions.
- **Causal mechanism specifics:** The frequency of causal mask updates during online interaction and the closed‑form gradient derivation under the linear‑Gaussian assumption are underspecified.
