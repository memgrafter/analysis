# Bias In, Bias Out? Finding Unbiased Subnetworks in Vanilla Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05582
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05582
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A structured pruning method (BISE) extracts subnetworks from biased vanilla models that dramatically improve unbiased accuracy (e.g., +24.7% on BiasedMNIST) while reducing computational cost.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether unbiased subnetworks exist within standard, biased deep learning models and can be extracted without additional data or full retraining. The proposed Bias-Invariant Subnetwork Extraction (BISE) method learns binary masks that selectively prune neurons/filters while optimizing for two objectives: a reweighted loss that emphasizes bias-conflicting samples and a mutual information term that suppresses bias-related features. Across five bias-sensitive benchmarks, BISE subnetworks significantly outperform the original models on unbiased test sets and often achieve state-of-the-art debiasing performance with reduced FLOPs.

## Method Summary
BISE applies structured pruning to a frozen, pretrained model. Learnable binary mask parameters are introduced for neurons or convolutional filters. The masks are trained end-to-end using a composite loss: (1) a reweighted cross-entropy loss that upweights samples where the model's initial prediction conflicts with the known bias (bias-conflicting samples), and (2) a mutual information regularization term that uses an auxiliary classifier to minimize the predictiveness of the masked subnetwork's internal representations about the bias attribute. After mask optimization, the pruned subnetwork can be optionally finetuned. The method also has an unsupervised variant that estimates bias labels via clustering.

## Key Results
- On BiasedMNIST (bias ratio 0.997), BISE improves unbiased accuracy from 66.1% (vanilla) to 90.8%, and to 95.9% with finetuning while pruning 35% of parameters.
- Worst-group accuracy on CivilComments rises from 59.6% to 80.4%.
- FLOPs are reduced by up to 82% on Corrupted-CIFAR10 with improved robustness.
- The unsupervised variant using pseudo-bias labels shows promising but less consistent results.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by exploiting the lottery ticket hypothesis within a debiasing framework. The composite loss guides mask learning to retain neurons critical for predicting the true label while suppressing those that encode the bias. The reweighted loss forces focus on hard, bias-conflicting examples. The mutual information term explicitly minimizes the flow of bias-related information through the retained subnetwork, acting as an information bottleneck. Pruning structurally enforces this by removing parameters, which regularizes the model and reduces its capacity to rely on biased features.

## Foundational Learning
- **Structured Pruning:** Removing entire neurons/filters instead of individual weights. *Why needed:* To achieve efficient inference and enforce coarse-grained selection of feature channels. *Quick check:* Does pruning a channel remove all its outgoing weights?
- **Bias-Conflicting Samples:** Samples where the model's initial (biased) prediction is incorrect. *Why needed:* To identify and emphasize examples that contradict the spurious correlation. *Quick check:* Can you compute the disagreement between model prediction and bias label on a validation set?
- **Mutual Information Minimization:** Estimating and reducing the dependence between internal activations and the bias attribute. *Why needed:* To directly suppress learned bias representations. *Quick check:* Is an auxiliary classifier trained to predict bias from activations, and is its error maximized?
- **Reweighted Loss:** Modifying sample weights in the loss function. *Why needed:* To counteract the dominant signal from bias-aligned samples and improve learning on the minority of bias-conflicting ones. *Quick check:* Are sample weights inversely proportional to their predicted bias alignment?
- **Information Bottleneck:** A principle for learning representations that compress input while maximizing prediction of the target. *Why needed:* The MI term implicitly implements this to discard bias information. *Quick check:* Does the objective combine prediction accuracy with a penalty on bias predictability?
- **Lottery Ticket Hypothesis:** The idea that a large network contains smaller subnetworks that can be trained effectively from the same initialization. *Why needed:* Suggests that an unbiased subnetwork might exist within a biased one. *Quick check:* Are masks initialized to be sparse (mostly zero)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Frozen Pretrained Model -> Binary Mask Parameters -> Masked Subnetwork -> (a) Reweighted Cross-Entropy Loss & (b) Auxiliary Bias Classifier -> Composite Loss -> Mask Gradient Update

**Critical Path:** The core loop involves passing data through the masked subnetwork, computing both the main task loss (with sample reweighting) and the bias prediction loss via the auxiliary classifier, combining them, and backpropagating to update only the mask parameters.

**Design Tradeoffs:** 
- Pruning ratio vs. debiasing performance: Aggressive pruning may remove necessary features, while conservative pruning may leave biased pathways intact.
- MI coefficient strength: Too high may distort main task learning; too low may fail to sufficiently suppress bias.
- Dependence on initial bias estimate: The quality of reweighting and unsupervised pseudo-labels hinges on accurately identifying bias-conflicting samples.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Minimal gap between original and extracted subnetwork worst-group accuracy.
- Large drop in overall accuracy after pruning without recovery from finetuning.
- High auxiliary classifier accuracy on the subnetwork's activations, indicating persistent bias information flow.

**First Three Validation Experiments:**
1. **Ablation on Mask Learning Rate:** Sweep the learning rate for mask parameters (e.g., 1e-2 to 1e-4) and measure impact on worst-group accuracy and overall performance across all five datasets.
2. **MI Coefficient Sensitivity:** Systematically vary the weight of the mutual information term in the loss (e.g., λ ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10}) and record the bias-accuracy Pareto frontier.
3. **Comparison to Adversarial Debiasing:** Implement a standard adversarial debiasing method (e.g., with a gradient reversal layer) using the same pretrained model and compare worst-group accuracy, FLOPs, and training stability to BISE.

## Limitations
- Effectiveness depends on the availability of sufficient bias-conflicting samples; extremely rare such samples may hinder debiasing.
- Evaluation is limited to academic benchmarks with synthetic or well-defined biases; generalizability to complex, high-dimensional, intersectional real-world biases is unknown.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (mask learning rate, MI coefficient) is not thoroughly explored, potentially affecting reproducibility.
- The unsupervised variant using pseudo-bias labels may be noisy and less reliable.

## Confidence
- **BISE extracts unbiased subnetworks without additional data:** Medium – Strong benchmark results, but needs independent verification on diverse datasets.
- **Substantial performance gains and FLOP reductions are achieved:** Medium – Improvements are large but may be dataset-specific and dependent on the initial bias strength.
- **Unsupervised setting using pseudo-bias labels is effective:** Low – Presented as preliminary; requires more rigorous validation and comparison to supervised baselines.

## Next Checks
1. Reproduce BISE across all five reported benchmarks with comprehensive hyperparameter sweeps and direct comparisons to state-of-the-art debiasing methods (e.g., adversarial training, group DRO).
2. Test BISE on a real-world, high-stakes dataset with intersectional attributes (e.g., COMPAS for race and gender, or CelebA with multiple sensitive attributes) and evaluate using a suite of fairness metrics (demographic parity, equalized odds).
3. Conduct a detailed ablation study isolating the contributions of the reweighted loss, the mutual information term, and the pruning ratio to understand their individual and interactive effects on the bias-accuracy trade-off.
