# AgentCPM-Report: Interleaving Drafting and Deepening for Open-Ended Deep Research

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06540
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06540
- **Reference count**: 31
- **Primary result**: An 8B-parameter agent using the WARP framework surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro on DeepResearch Bench (Insight 52.64 vs 49.45; Comprehensiveness 50.54 vs 49.51) and achieves state-of-the-art on DeepResearch Gym.

## Executive Summary
Deep research agents typically follow a rigid plan-then-write paradigm, which limits insight generation by requiring a strong initial outline and preventing dynamic revision. This paper introduces **AgentCPM-Report**, which implements the **Writing As Reasoning Policy (WARP)** framework. WARP interleaves two core processes—Evidence-Based Drafting and Reasoning-Driven Deepening—allowing the agent to revise its outline during writing. A multi-stage agentic training strategy (cold-start SFT, atomic skill RL, holistic pipeline RL) stabilizes learning for an 8B backbone model. Evaluations on DeepResearch Bench, DeepConsult, and DeepResearch Gym show the agent outperforms leading closed-source systems, demonstrating that structured interleaving and targeted reinforcement learning can overcome the "insight ceiling" of prior methods.

## Method Summary
The WARP inference policy governs a cyclical loop: it begins with `INITIALIZE` to generate a sparse L1 outline, then alternates between `Evidence-Based Drafting` (a `SEARCH` action conditioned on the current outline and draft, followed by `WRITE`) and `Reasoning-Driven Deepening` (either `EXPAND` to enrich the outline or `TERMINATE` to stop). This dynamic structure allows insights from drafting to directly reshape the research plan.

Training proceeds in three stages: (1) **Cold-Start SFT** imitates ~33k pruned teacher trajectories from 1,200 queries to establish basic action sequencing; (2) **Atomic Skill RL** applies per-action rewards (for planning, retrieval, writing, decision-making) across ~5k actions to stabilize local execution; (3) **Holistic Pipeline RL** optimizes 500 end-to-end trajectories using survey-level rewards (Comprehensiveness, Insight, etc.) to enable deviation from teacher policy and master optimal stopping.

## Key Results
- Surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro on DeepResearch Bench: Insight 52.64 vs 49.45; Comprehensiveness 50.54 vs 49.51.
- Achieves near-perfect scores (~100) on DeepResearch Gym metrics (Depth, Breadth, Insightfulness).
- Ablations confirm WARP's interleaving is intrinsically superior to rigid planning, and that trajectory pruning + reinforcement learning are critical for learning optimal stopping and deepening.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The rigid plan-then-write approach creates an "insight ceiling" because initial outlines cannot incorporate insights that emerge only during the writing process. **WARP breaks this cycle by interleaving drafting with deepening**, allowing retrieval and writing to inform outline expansion. This mimics how human researchers revise plans upon discovering new evidence. The multi-stage training is essential: SFT provides a stable foundation, atomic RL hones individual skills (e.g., faithful writing, precise retrieval), and pipeline RL optimizes the end-to-end trade-off between depth and termination. **Trajectory pruning** resolves the teacher model's poor stopping decisions by selecting the highest-scoring intermediate draft as a optimal-stop demonstration, providing a better learning signal than raw teacher rollouts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Writing As Reasoning Policy (WARP)**: The core framework treating writing as an ongoing reasoning process rather than execution of a fixed plan. *Why needed*: Enables dynamic outline revision based on draft-generated insights. *Quick check*: Verify the action loop allows `WRITE` outputs to influence subsequent `EXPAND` decisions.
- **Evidence-Based Drafting**: Drafting phase where `SEARCH` is conditioned on current outline *and* draft, ensuring retrieval is grounded in emerging content. *Why needed*: Prevents context drift and keeps writing anchored to relevant documents. *Quick check*: Inspect prompts to ensure `q_i,k ← SEARCH(Q, O_i^k, D_i)` is implemented.
- **Reasoning-Driven Deepening**: The `EXPAND` action that enriches the outline based on draft analysis. *Why needed*: Allows the plan to evolve with new angles discovered during writing. *Quick check*: Validate that `EXPAND` prompts reference the current draft's gaps or novel connections.
- **Multi-Stage Training (SFT → Atomic RL → Pipeline RL)**: Progressive curriculum from imitation to fine-grained skill reinforcement to holistic optimization. *Why needed*: Atomic RL prevents catastrophic forgetting of basic skills during end-to-end RL; pipeline RL teaches global trade-offs like optimal stopping. *Quick check*: Compare action distributions across stages—SFT should be conservative, Atomic RL improves retrieval/writing, Pipeline RL increases `EXPAND`/`TERMINATE` diversity.
- **Trajectory Pruning for Optimal Stopping**: Selecting the highest-reward intermediate draft from a long teacher trajectory as the demonstration. *Why needed*: Teachers terminate poorly; pruning extracts optimal-stopping examples. *Quick check*: For a forced-12-step teacher trajectory, confirm the demonstration pick is the draft with max checklist score, not necessarily the final one.
- **Per-Action Reward Functions**: Detailed rewards for `planning`, `retrieval`, `writing`, and `decision-making` (Table 1). *Why needed*: Provides dense, grounded feedback to stabilize RL on a sparse final-reward task. *Quick check*: Implement each reward component (e.g., `Citation Precision`, `Faithfulness`) and ensure they are computed per-action during atomic RL.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: User Query Q -> INITIALIZE -> Outline O0 -> (SEARCH -> WRITE)* -> (EXPAND -> (SEARCH -> WRITE)* | TERMINATE) -> Final Report

**Critical path**: The interleaving cycle between the Drafting stack (SEARCH+WRITE) and the Deepening decision (EXPAND/TERMINATE). Performance hinges on the quality of the `SEARCH` query (conditioned on draft) and the `EXPAND` judgment (identifying valuable new angles vs. redundancy).

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Interleaving vs. Plan-Then-Write*: Interleaving gains insight but risks coherence loss; requires careful state management (context C_i) to maintain thread.
- *Small model (8B) vs. Large teacher*: Efficiency and accessibility, but weaker innate reasoning; compensated by heavy RL and retrieval grounding.
- *Explicit actions vs. end-to-end*: Action scaffolding enables modular rewards but may constrain creativity; pipeline RL is needed to relax teacher policy.

**Failure signatures**:
1. **RL collapse**: Policy resorts to always `TERMINATE` or endlessly `EXPAND`. Indicates reward imbalance or too-aggressive entropy penalty.
2. **Retrieval hallucination**: `WRITE` outputs unsupported claims, low `Citation Precision`/`Faithfulness`. Suggests `WRITE` prompt ignored or retrieval grounding weak.
3. **Suboptimal stopping**: Average deepen steps far from ~9 saturation curve (Figure 6). Points to flawed `TERMINATE` reward or pruning signal.

**First three experiments**:
1. **Action distribution sanity check**: After cold-start SFT, run a batch of rollouts and log action frequencies. Expect predominantly `SEARCH`/`WRITE` cycles with rare `EXPAND`/`TERMINATE`. After atomic RL, `EXPAND` should increase; after pipeline RL, `TERMINATE` should appear with a spread of deepen steps (6–15).
2. **Retrieval-grounding validation**: On a held-out set, compute `Citation Precision` and `Faithfulness` (using reference docs) for `WRITE` actions during atomic RL. Scores should exceed baseline (e.g., >0.7) and not degrade in pipeline RL.
3. **Stopping curve replication**: Force the agent to stop at fixed deepen steps (1,3,6,9,12,15) on 50 queries and evaluate report Insight/Comprehensiveness. The curve should peak around 9, and the self-terminating policy's average deepen steps should cluster near that peak.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**: How can the decoupling of presentation (tables/figures) from content generation be effectively implemented to improve layout quality for small models without sacrificing content depth?  
*Basis in paper*: [explicit] The conclusion states that constructing tabular layouts requires different reasoning than prose, coupling explanations for small models' presentation underperformance, and proposes a "dedicated rendering agent" as a direction.  
*Why unresolved*: The paper does not implement or evaluate such a decoupled architecture; it remains a proposed future direction with unknown trade-offs in coherence, training complexity, and evaluation.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: A system that separates content drafting from layout planning, with ablations showing whether this improves visual quality metrics (e.g., layout coherence, figure-table integration) for small models compared to an end-to-end approach, while maintaining or improving content metrics.

**Open Question 2**: To what extent does the trajectory pruning strategy for optimal stopping generalize across diverse research domains and query complexities, or does it inadvertently bias the policy toward "average" teacher behaviors and away from novel, high-quality stopping points?  
*Basis in paper*: [explicit] The paper introduces trajectory pruning to fix suboptimal teacher termination by selecting the highest-scoring intermediate draft, but notes teacher models "struggle with high-level decision-making."  
*Why unresolved*: Pruning relies on a reward model (LLM-as-judge) that may itself have blind spots; selecting a single "best" draft per trajectory could prune trajectories that contain valuable, non-linear insight development not captured by the checklist.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: An analysis comparing pruned vs. raw teacher trajectories across a wider domain spectrum; measuring whether the pruned data leads to policies that underperform on queries requiring atypical research depth or structure; and experimenting with multi-pruned-path or stochastic pruning to preserve diversity.

**Open Question 3**: What are the fundamental limits of an 8B-parameter model for open-ended deep research under the WARP framework, and which component—planning (Expand), retrieval (Search), or writing (Write)—becomes the primary bottleneck as query difficulty increases?  
*Basis in paper*: [explicit] The paper achieves SOTA with an 8B model but speculates that "the primary bottleneck lies not in model size, but in the design of effective cognitive and planning processes." It also notes small models have "weaker reasoning ability and domain knowledge" than large ones.  
*Why unresolved*: The multi-stage RL training mitigates but does not eliminate size-related gaps; it's unclear whether further algorithmic innovation (e.g., better RL rewards, more sophisticated pruning) can close the gap indefinitely or if a minimum scale exists for certain domains (e.g., highly specialized scientific research).  
*What evidence would resolve it*: A controlled study scaling model size (e.g., 8B, 70B, 135B) with identical WARP+training, measuring performance degradation on queries of increasing abstraction/novelty; isolating failure modes (e.g., faulty Expand decisions, shallow synthesis, retrieval hallucination) via fine-grained metrics.

**Open Question 4**: Does the WARP framework's interleaving of drafting and deepening provide universal benefits across all research paradigms, or are there categories of queries (e.g., fact-summarization, technical tutorials) where a static outline is more efficient and equally effective?  
*Paper basis*: [inferred] The paper positions WARP as superior to plan-then-write for "open-ended" research where insights emerge during writing. However, the benchmarks (DeepResearch Bench, DeepConsult) focus on analytical/survey tasks; it is not tested against more deterministic or expository queries.  
*Why unresolved*: The "insight ceiling" of static outlines is argued but not quantified across query types; some tasks may not benefit from dynamic revision and could suffer from unnecessary cycles.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: A taxonomy of query intents (exploratory vs. synthetic vs. expository) with systematic evaluation of WARP vs. plan-then-write on each, measuring not just quality but also efficiency (steps, cost) and stability (variance in output quality).

**Open Question 5**: How can the agent's decision-making for termination (optimal stopping) be made more robust and less dependent on reward-model scoring, potentially developing an intrinsic "saturation" signal from the draft's textual or retrieval patterns?  
*Basis in paper*: [explicit] The paper identifies "Ambiguous Termination" as a key challenge and uses trajectory pruning + RL to teach optimal stopping, but the termination reward is based on checklist scoring against a reference.  
*Why unresolved*: The current termination signal is extrinsic (from the reward model) and may not capture true informational saturation; it could be gamed or fail on novel topics where reference answers are poor proxies. An intrinsic signal (e.g., retrieval redundancy, semantic entropy, argument graph closure) would be more generalizable.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Development and integration of a learned or heuristic intrinsic signal for "draft maturity" (e.g., diminishing returns in new citation引入, convergence of section embeddings) and measuring whether policies using this match or exceed checklist-based termination, especially on out-of-distribution queries.

## Limitations
- **Critical details omitted**: Exact reward model prompts/rubrics, RL hyperparameters (e.g., PPO clip, KL penalties), and the trajectory pruning algorithm are not specified, hindering exact replication.
- **Evaluation opacity**: Benchmark scoring relies on undisclosed judge prompts and model versions (e.g., Qwen3-32B), risking score incomparability.
- **Limited external validation**: Corpus signals show moderate related-work impact (avg neighbor FMR 0.464), indicating potential benchmark overfitting and unproven generalization.
