# ViewFusion: Structured Spatial Thinking Chains for Multi-View Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06024
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06024
- **Reference count:** 6
- **Primary result:** Achieves a 5.3% absolute accuracy improvement on MMSI-Bench over Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct by enforcing explicit two-stage spatial pre-thinking.

## Executive Summary
Multi-view spatial reasoning remains a challenge for vision-language models, which often rely on single-image shortcuts. ViewFusion addresses this with a two-stage framework: first, an explicit spatial pre-thinking step that infers cross-view relations and transformations into an intermediate workspace; second, question-driven reasoning conditioned on that workspace. Training combines supervised fine-tuning on synthesized, tag-delimited reasoning traces with reinforcement learning via GRPO, using a composite reward that enforces correctness, strict format compliance, and appropriate response length. On multi-view benchmarks, ViewFusion shows significant gains, particularly on examples requiring genuine cross-view alignment, and outperforms models with unstructured thinking, demonstrating the value of enforced structural reasoning.

## Method Summary
The method uses a two-stage generation protocol enforced during training and inference. First, a base multimodal LLM (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct) is supervised fine-tuned on 18K synthetic examples. These examples are created by rewriting existing rationales using a strong teacher model (Qwen3-32B-Instruct) into three mandatory sections: `<spatial_thinking>` (cross-view alignment), `<thinking>` (question reasoning), and `<answer>` (final choice). After SFT, GRPO reinforcement learning is applied to 16K instances (inputs and only final answers). For each input, multiple outputs are sampled and rewarded based on three components: answer correctness, strict adherence to the tag order and completeness, and a length bonus for responses within a token window. The policy is updated using group-relative advantages with a KL penalty to a reference model. At inference, the model is expected to generate all three sections.

## Key Results
- On MMSI-Bench, ViewFusion (Qwen3-VL-4B) achieves 35.4% accuracy vs. 30.1% for the instruct-tuned baseline, a 5.3% absolute gain.
- Largest improvements occur on subcategories requiring cross-view alignment (e.g., Camera–Camera comparisons: 30.1% → 46.2%).
- It also surpasses Qwen3-VL-4B-Thinking, highlighting the benefit of enforced structural reasoning over free-form chains of thought.
- Ablations confirm the necessity of both the strict two-stage output format (via format reward) and GRPO optimization for the gains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Explicit spatial pre-thinking improves cross-view alignment by making it a deliberate first step rather than an implicit byproduct of answering.
- **Mechanism:** The model generates a `<spatial_thinking>` segment describing viewpoint changes, object correspondences, and spatial transformations. This serves as an intermediate workspace for the subsequent `<thinking>` stage.
- **Core assumption:** Cross-view spatial relations can be explicitly captured in natural language, and this structured description aids multi-view integration.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.1 describe the separation of pre-alignment from answering. Table 2 shows largest gains on subcategories requiring genuine cross-view alignment.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Reinforcement learning with GRPO and a composite reward improves answer correctness while stabilizing the intended two-stage generation behavior.
- **Mechanism:** GRPO samples multiple outputs per input, computes group-relative advantages, and uses a reward combining correctness, format compliance, and length regularization. This encourages accurate, well-structured, and sufficiently detailed responses.
- **Core assumption:** Outcome-based RL can refine an SFT-initialized policy to produce both correct answers and disciplined intermediate steps.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.3.3 detail the reward. Figure 4 shows steady increases in total, accuracy, and format rewards during GRPO training, with KL plateauing.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Synthetic supervision with structured reasoning traces provides a high-quality initial distribution that teaches the desired two-stage protocol.
- **Mechanism:** A teacher model (Qwen3-32B-Instruct) rewrites multi-view instance rationales into three mandated sections. Strict filtering removes malformed outputs, yielding a clean 18K-sample SFT corpus.
- **Core assumption:** A strong LLM can generate plausible spatial reasoning traces, and mimicking these instills the decomposition.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 describes corpus construction. Table 3 shows SFT-only (32.4%) already beats baseline (30.1%), and free-form reasoning + RL (33.4%) underperforms full method (35.4%).

## Foundational Learning
- **Multi‑view spatial reasoning**  
  **Why needed:** The core task involves aligning information across different camera viewpoints (e.g., inferring camera motion, object correspondence, occlusion changes). Single‑view shortcuts fail here.  
  **Quick check:** Given two images of the same room from different angles, what visual cues would you use to infer how the camera moved between shots?

- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**  
  **Why needed:** GRPO is the RL algorithm used to stabilize training and enforce the two‑stage output structure via group‑wise advantages and a composite reward.  
  **Quick check:** How does using a group‑wise baseline in GRPO reduce variance compared to computing a single global baseline for each sampled output?

- **Structured chain‑of‑thought with XML‑like tags**  
  **Why needed:** The model’s output is forced into `<spatial_thinking>`, `<thinking>`, and `<answer>` sections. This rigid format is crucial for the reward signal and interpretability.  
  **Quick check:** Why might a free‑form reasoning style be insufficient for multi‑view spatial tasks, even if it contains correct final answers?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Base MLLM (Qwen3‑VL‑4B) -> SFT module -> RL module (GRPO) -> Inference (tag‑delimited generation)

**Critical path:**  
- **Training SFT:** Load base model, process multi‑view images + question, generate structured output, compute token‑wise loss on all sections, update parameters.  
- **Training RL:** For each input, sample K=8 outputs from current policy. Compute reward per output: `r_ans` (binary correctness) + λ·`r_fmt` (binary format) + `r_len` (length bonus). Compute group‑relative advantages, apply PPO‑style clipped objective with KL penalty to reference model, update policy.  
- **Inference:** Autoregressive generation expected to produce `<spatial_thinking>...</spatial_thinking><thinking>...</thinking><answer>...</answer>`.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Reward weights (λ, ω):** Too high λ risks over‑fitting format at expense of accuracy; too low risks losing structure. ω controls length bonus strength.  
- **Length window [ℓmin, ℓmax]:** Must balance allowing necessary detail vs. discouraging verbosity.  
- **SFT vs RL data overlap:** RL set excludes rationales to improve generalization but may require more RL steps.  
- **Two‑stage rigidity:** Strict tag order improves reliability but may limit natural reasoning variations; format reward mitigates this.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Format collapse:** `r_fmt` plateaus below 1.0; model omits or misorders tags.  
- **Shallow spatial thinking:** `<spatial_thinking>` contains view‑local descriptions, not cross‑view relations.  
- **RL instability:** KL divergence spikes or reward crashes; likely LR too high or β too low.  
- **Length gaming:** Tokens cluster at window edges; model repeats phrases.  
- **Answer extraction failure:** Multiple letters or missing `<answer>` tags; parser needs robust fallback.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Main evaluation:** Compare ViewFusion (SFT+RL) against Qwen3‑VL‑4B‑Instruct on MMSI‑Bench, MindCube, ViewSpatial, with breakdown by subcategory.  
2. **Ablation of format reward:** Remove `r_fmt` from composite reward, measure impact on format compliance and accuracy (as in Table 3).  
3. **Training dynamics:** Plot total reward, accuracy reward, format reward, and KL divergence over 1500 GRPO steps (replicating Figure 4).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Direct cross‑view consistency rewards:** How to design rewards that explicitly enforce spatial alignment during RL, beyond outcome correctness and format? The current composite reward lacks a direct consistency signal, leaving open whether such optimization could further improve robustness.
- **Scalability with views and complexity:** Performance with more than two views or complex, cluttered scenes is untested; additional views may introduce noise and amplify shortcuts. The framework’s robustness to view count and scene complexity is an open challenge.
- **Rigid format limitations:** Does the strict three‑part output format constrain more natural or flexible reasoning patterns needed for complex spatial tasks? The trade‑off between structure and expressiveness is unexplored.
- **Intermediate workspace representation:** What is the representational format (implicit neural, symbolic graph, hybrid) and sufficiency of the workspace constructed during `<spatial_thinking>`? The mechanism by which it captures viewpoint transformations remains abstract.

## Limitations
- **Reproduction ambiguity:** Exact SFT/GRPO hyperparameters (batch size, KL β, λ), decoding settings, and full GRPO implementation are unspecified, requiring empirical tuning.
- **Dataset access dependency:** The curated 18K SFT corpus is not released; quality hinges on Qwen3‑32B‑Instruct’s spatial reasoning and the original data’s availability.
- **Generalization scope:** Evaluation limited to existing multi‑view benchmarks; performance on novel viewpoints, occlusion patterns, or real‑world images is unknown.
- **Reward sensitivity:** Impact of composite reward weights and length window not thoroughly explored; optimal configuration may be dataset‑specific.

## Confidence
- **Overall improvement (5.3% on MMSI‑Bench):** Medium. Based on single benchmark without statistical tests; possible overfitting.
- **Mechanism 1 (explicit spatial pre‑thinking):** Medium. Ablations show benefit, but causality may be entangled with format enforcement and RL.
- **Mechanism 2 (GRPO with composite reward):** Medium. Ablations confirm format reward’s role, but optimal weighting and stability across runs are not fully established.
- **Mechanism 3 (synthetic SFT traces):** Low. Teacher model’s spatial accuracy not validated; erroneous traces could propagate.

## Next Checks
1. **Replication with full hyperparameters:** Implement the exact SFT+GRPO pipeline (including reported LRs, KL coefficient, λ) on a held‑out MMSI‑Bench split to confirm gains and training stability.
2. **Qualitative analysis of `<spatial_thinking>`:** Annotate a sample of outputs for explicit cross‑view relational content (viewpoint transformation, object correspondence) to verify the stage captures multi‑view alignment rather than single‑view descriptions.
3. **Reward component ablation:** Systematically vary λ (format strength) and ω (length bonus) and measure effects on format compliance, reasoning length, and accuracy to identify robust settings and trade‑offs.
