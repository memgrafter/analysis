# LLM-Bootstrapped Targeted Finding Guidance for Factual MLLM-based Medical Report Generation

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.00426
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00426
*   **Reference Count:** 27
*   **Primary Result:** Fact-Flow significantly improves clinical efficacy (RadFact F1 from 0.0286 to 0.2831 with Qwen2.5-VL) and natural language metrics over SOTA methods and vanilla MLLM fine-tuning by decoupling visual fact identification from report composition.

## Executive Summary
Fact-Flow is a pipeline designed to combat factual instability—specifically omissions and hallucinations—in multimodal large language model (MLLM)-based medical report generation. It introduces a two-stage process: first, an LLM-bootstrapped pipeline automatically creates a multi-label finding dataset from existing image-report pairs, producing a comprehensive clinical taxonomy and annotations without manual effort. Second, a vision-based multi-label classifier is trained on this data to predict a set of findings for a given image. These predicted findings are then serialized into a prompt that guides a fine-tuned MLLM during report generation, explicitly grounding the narrative in a discrete factual scaffold. Evaluated on chest X-ray (tuberculosis) and ophthalmology datasets, the approach yields substantial gains in both factual accuracy (RadFact) and standard natural language generation metrics.

## Method Summary
The method comprises three sequential stages. **Stage 1 (Bootstrap)** processes a corpus of training reports with an LLM (GPT-5-mini): reports are batched, and the LLM extracts clinical concepts which are iteratively merged into a unified, canonical label set `L` under a token budget. Each report is then annotated against `L`, and a frequency filter (`θ=15`) prunes rare labels to form the multi-label dataset `D_MLC`. **Stage 2 (Classifier)** trains a vision encoder (DINOv3-ConvNeXt) on `D_MLC` to perform multi-label classification. To handle the severe long-tail distribution, logit adjustment (`τ=1`) is applied during training, shifting each logit by `τ * log(p_j/(1-p_j))` where `p_j` is the empirical label frequency. **Stage 3 (Guided Generation)** fine-tunes an MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL) using LoRA. For each sample, the ground-truth labels from `D_MLC` are serialized into a prompt prepended to the generation task, teaching the MLLM to condition its output on the factual scaffold. At inference, the trained classifier's predictions replace the ground-truth labels in the prompt.

## Key Results
*   **Clinical Efficacy (RadFact F1):** Improved from 0.0286 (vanilla fine-tuning) to 0.2831 with Fact-Flow (Qwen2.5-VL), approaching the oracle with ground-truth labels (0.3095).
*   **Natural Language Metrics:** BLEU-4 increased from 0.0265 to 0.2129; CIDEr from 1.2035 to 1.9434.
*   **Taxonomy Quality:** The bootstrapped process achieved 100% coverage on a clinician-verified sample, with 100% LLM-Match Accuracy and 80% of labels rated "valid & decidable."

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: LLM-Bootstrapped Taxonomy & Annotation
*   **Claim:** The LLM-bootstrapped pipeline creates a usable multi-label taxonomy and annotations from existing reports, providing a factual foundation without manual annotation cost.
*   **Mechanism:** An LLM processes batched reports to extract clinical concepts (diseases, anatomy, severity), then iteratively merges synonyms under a token budget to form a canonical label set `L`. Each report is then annotated against `L`. Frequency filtering (`θ`) prunes rare labels.
*   **Core assumption:** A general-purpose LLM (GPT-5-mini) can reliably extract and normalize clinically relevant findings from diverse medical reports to form a coherent, comprehensive taxonomy.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The abstract states it "effectively eliminates the need for expensive manual annotation." Section 2.1 details the extraction/merging process. Table 5 shows 100% coverage and 100% LLM-Match Accuracy on a clinician-verified sample.
*   **Break condition:** If the LLM systematically misses or misclassifies critical, context-dependent findings, the resulting taxonomy `L` will have low coverage or validity, undermining downstream guidance.

### Mechanism 2: Discrete Factual Scaffolding via Classifier
*   **Claim:** The trained multi-label classifier `f_MLC` provides a discrete, factual "fact-flow" that grounds the MLLM's generation, reducing hallucinations and omissions.
*   **Mechanism:** A vision encoder is trained on the bootstrapped `(image, multi-label)` pairs. At inference, its binary predictions `Ŷ` are serialized into a natural language prompt prepended to the MLLM's generation task.
*   **Core assumption:** The classifier can achieve sufficient accuracy (particularly recall) on the clinically relevant label set `L` to provide a reliable factual scaffold. The MLLM can then condition its narrative on this scaffold.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The abstract notes it "separates the process of visual fact identification from the generation of reports... subsequently directs the MLLM." Section 2.3 describes prompt construction. Section 3.4 (Table 4) shows "Label Only (Pred)" substantially improves metrics over "Image Only," proving the predicted labels provide strong guidance.
*   **Break condition:** If `f_MLC` has poor recall on critical but rare findings, the MLLM will be conditioned on an incomplete fact set, propagating omissions. The MLLM may also ignore or contradict the provided labels.

### Mechanism 3: Logit Adjustment for Long-Tail Recall
*   **Claim:** Logit adjustment during classifier training rebalances the decision boundary for the severe long-tail distribution of medical findings, improving recall on rare but critical labels.
*   **Mechanism:** For each label `j`, the raw logit `z_j` is shifted by `τ * log(p_j / (1-p_j))`, where `p_j` is its empirical frequency in the bootstrapped dataset, making the classifier less biased towards head classes.
*   **Core assumption:** The empirical label frequencies `p_j` are a reasonable proxy for true prior probabilities. The hyperparameter `τ=1` optimally balances precision and recall.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Section 2.2 defines the adjusted logit `˜z_j` and sets `τ=1`. Section 3.5 (Table 5) reports Macro-F1 scores (0.5227, 0.7071) that, while lower than Micro-F1, indicate the model retains performance on tail classes.
*   **Break condition:** If bootstrapped label frequencies `p_j` are distorted by LLM annotation noise, the logit adjustment will mis-calibrate, harming performance on both head and tail classes.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Multi-Label Classification**
    *   *Why needed here:* The core guidance signal is a set of binary findings (`Y ∈ {0,1}^k`). The system must learn to predict multiple, non-exclusive clinical signs from a single image.
    *   *Quick check:* What loss function is suitable for predicting multiple independent binary labels from a single image, and how does it differ from cross-entropy for multi-class classification?
*   **Concept: LLM-Based Data Synthesis / Bootstrapping**
    *   *Why needed here:* The entire pipeline hinges on automatically creating a large-scale `(image, multi-label)` dataset from only `(image, report)` pairs.
    *   *Quick check:* What are two specific risks of using an LLM to annotate medical reports (e.g., hallucinated labels, missed contextual findings), and how does the paper's filtering step (`θ`) attempt to mitigate one of them?
*   **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA) for MLLMs**
    *   *Why needed here:* The final stage fine-tunes a large MLLM on domain-specific data. The paper uses LoRA to adapt the decoder, vision encoder, and projector efficiently.
    *   *Quick check:* Why might one apply LoRA to the vision encoder and projector in an MLLM, not just the language decoder, when conditioning on new factual guidance?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Stage1:LLM_Bootstrap -> D_MLC -> Stage2:MLC_Classifier -> Predicted_Labels -> Stage3:MLLM_FineTune -> Generated_Report
*   **Critical path:** Report corpus → Stage 1 LLM pipeline → `D_MLC` → Stage 2 classifier training → Stage 3 MLLM fine-tuning (using `D_MLC` labels) → Inference: image → `f_MLC` → `Ŷ` → MLLM → report.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *LLM Bootstrap vs. Manual Annotation:* Zero cost and scalability vs. risk of label noise/out-of-vocabulary findings. Mitigated by frequency filtering (`θ`) and clinician verification.
    *   *Discrete Labels vs. Free-Form Generation:* Explicit guidance improves factuality but constrains linguistic diversity to the predefined taxonomy `L`.
    *   *Logit Adjustment Simplicity:* Single parameter (`τ`) easily integrated vs. may not fully resolve heavy tails if frequencies `p_j` are extremely low or noisy.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Stage 1:** Clinician review finds low label coverage (<90% on sample) or high invalid label rate (>5%).
    *   **Stage 2:** Classifier Macro-F1 is very low (<0.4) or recall on rare, critical labels is near zero.
    *   **Stage 3:** "Image Only" baseline shows mode collapse, but adding predicted labels does **not** improve metrics, suggesting MLLM ignores the prompt or `f_MLC` predictions are too poor.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  Validate Stage 1 Output: Clinician reviews 50 random reports from `D_MLC` for label coverage, matching accuracy, and label clinical validity.
    2.  Benchmark Classifier Alone: Evaluate `f_MLC` on the test set using Macro-F1/Micro-F1 and per-label recall for the rarest 10% of labels.
    3.  Ablation of Guidance Source: Compare MLLM performance with: (a) image only, (b) predicted labels only, (c) image + predicted labels (full Fact-Flow), and (d) image + ground-truth labels (oracle).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Generalization to Open-Ended Domains:** Can Fact-Flow generalize to medical report generation scenarios with open-ended or non-enumerable finding categories (e.g., full-body CT or neurology reports), or is it fundamentally limited to disease-focused domains with predefined taxonomies? The conclusion states it is "particularly suited to clinical scenarios where reports revolve around targeted and enumerable finding categories," implying a scope limitation.
*   **Cross-Specialty Robustness:** How robust and consistent is the LLM-bootstrapped label taxonomy across different medical specialties, report writing styles, and LLM versions? Stage 1 taxonomy validation involved only one ophthalmologist on one dataset, and medical terminology varies widely across specialties.
*   **Impact of Classifier Error Types:** What is the specific impact of different error types in the multi-label classifier (e.g., false positives vs. false negatives for common vs. rare findings) on the factual accuracy and completeness of the final generated report? Table 4 shows a significant gap between predicted and ground-truth labels, but error types are not analyzed.
*   **Logit Adjustment Trade-offs:** Does the logit adjustment for long-tail optimization inadvertently increase false positive rates for rare findings, thereby introducing new hallucinations, and how does this trade-off affect clinical safety? The method is adapted without explicit discussion of potential precision loss on rare classes, and per-class metrics are not reported.

## Limitations
*   **LLM Prompt Opacity:** The exact prompts used for extraction, merging, and annotation are unspecified (GPT-5-mini may be inaccessible), risking non-reproducible taxonomy quality.
*   **Inference Threshold Unknown:** The classification prediction threshold at inference is unreported; the chosen cutoff strongly influences guidance quality and downstream performance.
*   **Potential Data Leakage:** The bootstrapped `D_MLC` is used for both classifier training *and* MLLM fine-tuning (with GT labels), possibly inflating performance if test samples leak into `D_MLC`.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1 (LLM bootstrap):** **Medium** – Clinician-verified sample supports usability, but prompt opacity prevents certainty on scalability/robustness.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Classifier guidance):** **Medium** – Ablations confirm value of predicted labels, but reliance on threshold and MLLM conditioning fidelity is unquantified.
*   **Mechanism 3 (Logit adjustment):** **Medium** – Theoretically grounded, but effectiveness depends on noise in bootstrapped frequencies `p_j`.

## Next Checks
1.  **Prompt Audit:** Reverse-engineer the LLM prompts by analyzing output examples or contact authors; replicate Stage 1 on a small subset to measure taxonomy stability across LLM versions.
2.  **Threshold Sensitivity:** Sweep sigmoid thresholds (e.g., 0.3–0.7) for `f_MLC`; evaluate impact on RadFact F1 and downstream MLLM performance to identify robust operating point.
3.  **Leakage Test:** Ensure strict separation: train `f_MLC` only on training-set reports, validate/test on held-out reports; re-evaluate MLLM without using GT labels from test reports during fine-tuning.
