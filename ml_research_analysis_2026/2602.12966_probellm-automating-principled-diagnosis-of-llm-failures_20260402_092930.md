# ProbeLLM: Automating Principled Diagnosis of LLM Failures

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12966
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12966
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** ProbeLLM uncovers 3–5× more structured failure clusters than static benchmarks, with lower noise rates (31–37% vs. 45–78%) and cost below $0.10 per high-error test.

## Executive Summary
ProbeLLM automates the discovery of interpretable, structured failure modes in large language models, addressing the static nature of existing benchmarks. It formulates probing as a hierarchical Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) problem, dynamically balancing global exploration (discovering new failure regions) and local refinement (deepening known patterns). Test cases are generated with tool augmentation (web search, Python execution) to ensure factual grounding and verifiable ground truths. Failures are aggregated via failure-aware embeddings and boundary-aware induction into concise, diagnostic clusters. Evaluated across 12 LLMs and five benchmarks, ProbeLLM reveals substantially richer and more reliable failure landscapes than prior automated or static methods, enabling targeted error-focused training without general capability degradation.

## Method Summary
ProbeLLM treats failure discovery as a budget-constrained search. A top-level MCTS controller allocates simulations between two regimes: MACRO (diverse topic generation for broad coverage) and MICRO (local perturbations for pattern refinement). Each regime maintains its own search tree; node selection uses a UCB formula balancing empirical failure rate and visit count. The generator LLM, equipped with tools, synthesizes verifiable test cases and computes ground-truth answers. The target model’s responses are judged by a verifier. Collected failures are embedded using a model conditioned on prompt and error description, clustered via HDBSCAN, and summarized by an inducer LLM that contrasts central cluster failures with nearby non-failures (boundary-aware induction). This pipeline replaces manual benchmark design and adapts to evolving model weaknesses.

## Key Results
- Discovers 3–5× more failure clusters (10–19 vs. 2–6 from static benchmarks) across 12 LLMs and five benchmarks.
- Achieves lower noise rates (31–37%) compared to prior automated methods (45–78%) and tighter cluster-size consistency.
- Maintains high error rates (29–86%) at low operational cost (<$0.10 per high-error test case).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Hierarchical MCTS for Budget Allocation
- **Claim:** Hierarchical Monte Carlo Tree Search efficiently allocates a limited probing budget between discovering new failure regions (MACRO) and refining recurring patterns (MICRO).
- **Mechanism:** The top-level MCTS controller selects between MACRO and MICRO regimes using a UCB-type rule that balances exploitation of high-yield nodes and exploration of under-visited nodes. Each regime maintains its own search tree; expansions generate new test cases conditioned on the selected node’s topic or perturbation history.
- **Core assumption:** LLM failures cluster semantically; exploring broadly first and then refining locally maximizes the discovery of distinct, well-supported failure modes under a fixed budget.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section 3.2] Details the two-level MCTS structure, node statistics (visit count N, failure count E), and UCB selection formula.
  - [Abstract] States the framework "formulates probing as a hierarchical Monte Carlo Tree Search" to balance "global exploration and local refinement."
  - [corpus] Weak – neighboring papers like "L4: Diagnosing Large-scale LLM Training Failures" also use automated log analysis but do not employ hierarchical MCTS for probing.
- **Break condition:** If the UCB balance (β parameter) is mis-specified such that either MACRO or MICRO dominates persistently, the search may either miss coherent failure clusters (over-MACRO) or fail to discover new regions (over-MICRO), leading to stagnant or redundant failure sets.

### Mechanism 2: Tool-Augmented Verification for Reliable Evidence
- **Claim:** Tool-augmented generation and verification restrict probing to verifiable test cases, grounding failure discovery in reliable evidence.
- **Mechanism:** The generator LLM is equipped with tools (web search, Python execution) to construct questions with factual grounding and to compute or verify ground-truth answers y⋆(x). This reduces spurious failures from ambiguous prompts or incorrect references.
- **Core assumption:** LLM-generated test cases without external grounding are prone to hallucinations and semantic drift; tool use enforces factuality and verifiability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section 3.3] Explains that GMACRO and GMICRO can invoke tools "to support factual grounding and verification" and that "answer generation uses a more balanced mix of Python execution and web search."
  - [Abstract] Notes the framework "restricts probing to verifiable test cases and leverages tool-augmented generation and verification."
  - [corpus] Strong – multiple neighbors (e.g., "Using Large Language Models to Support Automation of Failure Management in CI/CD Pipelines") also leverage tools for reliability in failure analysis contexts.
- **Break condition:** If tool outputs are themselves erroneous or if tool calls are consistently disallowed by the environment, the verifier becomes unreliable, inflating false-positive failure rates or rejecting valid model outputs.

### Mechanism 3: Failure-Aware Embedding and Boundary-Aware Induction
- **Claim:** Failure-aware embeddings and boundary-aware induction consolidate isolated failures into interpretable, coherent failure modes.
- **Mechanism:** Failures are embedded using a model trained on prompt concatenated with error description (model output vs. ground-truth mismatch), so similarity reflects shared error patterns. HDBSCAN clusters these embeddings; for each cluster, central failures (near centroid) and boundary failures (paired with nearest non-failures) are used to prompt an LLM for a concise, diagnostic summary.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic similarity of failure *mechanisms* can be captured in an embedding space that incorporates error signals; contrastive boundary cases prevent overgeneralization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section 3.4] Describes failure-aware embeddings ("concatenate the problem description with an error description"), HDBSCAN clustering, and boundary-aware induction using central and boundary cases.
  - [Abstract] Mentions failures are "consolidated into interpretable failure modes via failure-aware embeddings and boundary-aware induction."
  - [corpus] Weak – neighboring work like "Discovering Knowledge Deficiencies..." focuses on harvesting errors but does not use boundary-aware induction to summarize modes.
- **Break condition:** If the embedding model fails to encode error semantics (e.g., clusters group by topic rather than error type), the induced modes become topic summaries rather than mechanism descriptions, losing diagnostic value.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)
  - **Why needed:** Provides a principled, budget-aware search strategy to allocate effort between exploration (new failure regions) and exploitation (refining known patterns).
  - **Quick check:** How does the UCB formula in Equation 7 trade off between a node’s empirical failure rate and its visit count?
- **Concept:** Tool-augmented generation
  - **Why needed:** Ensures test cases have verifiable ground truths and factual grounding, reducing noise from LLM hallucinations during test creation.
  - **Quick check:** What specific tools does ProbeLLM use, and what role does each play in question vs. answer generation?
- **Concept:** Boundary-aware clustering induction
  - **Why needed:** Transforms a set of failure cases into a concise, interpretable failure mode by contrasting failures with nearby successes, avoiding overgeneralization.
  - **Quick check:** Why are boundary failures and their nearest non-failures more informative than only central failures for summarizing a cluster?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Seed dataset → MCTS Controller (MACRO/MICRO trees) → Generator (LLM with tools) → Target Model (fθ) → Verifier (V) → Failure storage → Embedder → HDBSCAN Clusterer → Inducer (LLM)
- **Critical path:** Seed dataset → Initial node creation → Hierarchical MCTS rollouts (select regime → traverse → expand via tool-augmented generator → evaluate → backup stats) → Stop on budget → Embed failures → Cluster → Induce summaries.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Budget vs. coverage (more simulations find more modes but cost more); topic diversity vs. depth (MACRO vs. MICRO allocation); noise vs. granularity (clustering parameters affect cluster purity and count).
- **Failure signatures:** High noise rate (>40%) suggests poor embedding quality or over-fragmentation; very low cluster count (<3) across 100 simulations indicates insufficient MACRO exploration; identical cluster descriptions across benchmarks imply template-based failures.
- **First 3 experiments:** RQ1 (measure error rate and simulation proportions across models/benchmarks); RQ2 (ablate search depth and tool usage); RQ4 (compare cluster count/size with static benchmarks and compute overlap).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can ProbeLLM be effectively extended to handle open-ended, subjective evaluation settings where ground-truth answers are ambiguous or non-existent?**  
  *Basis:* The authors state in Appendix B.2 that the current implementation is restricted to test cases with well-defined ground-truth answers and note extension to open-ended scenarios is future work.  
  *Why unresolved:* Proposed mechanisms for subjective evaluation are undeveloped; reliance on verifiable ground truth limits applicability to creative, ethical, or opinion-based tasks.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* A robust, validated protocol for subjective failure-mode discovery demonstrating consistency, inter-annotator reliability, and diagnostic utility comparable to the verifiable setting.

- **What specific architectural or training changes explain the observed convergence and specialization of failure modes across model generations (e.g., from GPT-3.5 to GPT-5.2)?**  
  *Basis:* RQ8 and Section C.7 analyze that failure modes evolve toward fewer, tighter clusters and retreat into niche domains, but provide only descriptive metrics, not causal mechanisms.  
  *Why unresolved:* The paper identifies the *pattern* of evolution (consolidation, specialization) but does not investigate *why* it occurs (scale, alignment, architecture, data). Findings are correlational.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Ablation studies correlating failure-mode progression with specific model checkpoints, combined with controlled analyses (probing family trees, training data composition) to isolate factors like scale, algorithm changes, or safety fine-tuning.

- **Why do different tool-augmentation strategies (web search vs. Python execution) lead to significantly different error exposure rates during question generation, and what does this imply about the nature of the weaknesses being probed?**  
  *Basis:* RQ2 and Figure 5 show test generation dominated by Python execution yields higher error rates than web-search generation, but the reason is not analyzed.  
  *Why unresolved:* Analysis is descriptive; no hypothesis or test on whether tool usage correlates with specific *types* of weaknesses (computational vs. factual). Understanding this is key to controlling probing strategy.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Fine-grained categorization of failure cases generated under each tool, correlated with tool function (e.g., computational accuracy vs. factual grounding), to build a model of tool-failure mode affinity.

- **How robust is the boundary-aware induction process? Could it introduce systematic biases or over-generalizations when automatically summarizing failure modes, especially for clusters that are borderline in terms of density or separation?**  
  *Basis:* Boundary-aware induction contrasts boundary failures with nearby non-failures to avoid over-generalization, but the paper provides no analysis of its sensitivity to embedding noise, cluster quality, or boundary-point selection.  
  *Why unresolved:* Quality of induced descriptions is crucial for diagnostic utility but depends on an unvalidated clustering-then-induction pipeline; potential failure includes misidentifying the boundary and constructing misleading summaries.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Human evaluations on boundary/non-failure contrast pairs to judge inferred boundary accuracy; sensitivity analyses on clusters of varying quality to see if induction degrades predictably with cluster ambiguity.

## Limitations
- Tool reliance: Errors in web search or Python execution can corrupt ground truth; robustness not evaluated.
- Embedding bias: Failure embeddings may conflate topic with error type; cross-domain validity unknown.
- Parameter sensitivity: No analysis of MCTS β or HDBSCAN thresholds; stability unclear.

## Confidence
- Hierarchical MCTS: Medium (conceptually sound but β sensitivity untested)
- Tool-augmented verification: High (ablation demonstrates clear benefit)
- Failure-aware clustering: Medium (noise reduction shown, but embedding bias may affect interpretability)
- End-to-end superiority: High (consistent gains across 12 models, 5 benchmarks)
