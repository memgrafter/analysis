# Deep Sequence Modeling with Quantum Dynamics: Language as a Wave Function

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.22255
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22255
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Theoretical separation: a quantum-inspired complex unitary model of dimension N solves a family of disambiguation tasks exactly, while any real orthogonal model with affine-softmax readout requires at least N²–2 dimensions.

## Executive Summary
Standard real-valued sequence models with affine-softmax readouts suffer from a representational bottleneck: they cannot efficiently capture pairwise phase correlations required for certain disambiguation tasks. This paper introduces a quantum-inspired alternative where the latent state is a complex unit-norm wave function evolving under a learned time-dependent Hermitian Hamiltonian, with exact norm preservation via Cayley discretization. Token probabilities are extracted via the Born rule—a quadratic measurement that couples magnitudes and relative phases—implicitly lifting the N-dimensional complex state into an N²-dimensional space of Hermitian matrices. The core theoretical contribution is a separation theorem proving that for a constructed family of disambiguation tasks, a complex unitary model of dimension N solves the tasks exactly, while any real orthogonal model with affine-softmax readout requires at least N²−2 dimensions. This quadratic gap arises from the Born rule’s access to O(N²) pairwise phase features inaccessible to linear readouts. The work is purely theoretical; no empirical results are reported.

## Method Summary
The model maintains a complex state |ψ⟩ ∈ ℂ^N with unit norm. The state evolves according to a time-dependent Hermitian Hamiltonian H(t) = H₀ + H_int(t), where H₀ is a fixed diagonal matrix of learned frequencies and H_int(t) is constructed as Φ(t)Φ(t)† + diag(δ(t)) with Φ ∈ ℂ^{N×r} and δ ∈ ℝ^N to guarantee Hermiticity. Discrete-time updates use the Cayley transform (Crank–Nicolson) to ensure exact unitarity regardless of step size Δt. The interaction picture is employed to separate the free evolution under H₀ from the interaction. At each step, given the previous interaction-picture state ψ_I(t−1) and the current token embedding, a neural network g_θ produces Φ and δ. The Cayley update solves (I + iΔt/2 H_int,I) ψ_I(t) = (I − iΔt/2 H_int,I) ψ_I(t−1) efficiently via the Woodbury identity, exploiting the low rank of H_int. After updating ψ_I(t), the Schrödinger picture state is recovered by ψ(t) = ψ_I(t) ⊙ e^{−iλ_j t}. Token probabilities are then computed by the Born rule: p(k) = |⟨m_k|ψ(t)⟩|², where M ∈ ℂ^{N×V} is a measurement matrix with orthonormal rows (M M† = I). The model is trained by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the target tokens, with backpropagation through the Cayley solve performed via implicit differentiation. Two model classes are compared: the Complex Unitary Sequence Model (CUSM) and a Real Orthogonal Sequence Model (ROSM) that uses real-valued orthogonal dynamics and an affine-softmax readout.

## Key Results
- **Representational bottleneck**: Affine-softmax readouts are linear and cannot efficiently capture pairwise phase correlations needed for certain disambiguation tasks.
- **Core method**: Quantum-inspired sequence model with complex wave function, Hermitian Hamiltonian, Cayley discretization for exact unitarity, and Born-rule decoding that accesses O(N²) phase features.
- **Separation theorem**: For a family of disambiguation tasks D_N, a CUSM of dimension N solves them exactly, while any ROSM with affine-softmax requires at least N²−2 dimensions, assuming the target log-probability matrix has full row rank. This establishes a quadratic expressivity gap.
- **Theoretical only**: No empirical validation is provided; the contribution is a rigorous theoretical analysis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Unitary Dynamics via Hermitian Hamiltonian and Cayley Discretization
- **Claim**: The model’s latent state evolves unitarily, preserving the L₂ norm exactly at each discrete step, ensuring valid probability distributions from the Born rule and stable gradient propagation through time.
- **Mechanism**: The Hamiltonian H(t) = H₀ + H_int(t) is constructed to be Hermitian (H_int(t) = ΦΦ† + diag(δ) with arbitrary complex Φ and real δ). Continuous-time Schrödinger evolution i d|ψ⟩/dt = H|ψ⟩ is unitary. Discretization uses the Cayley transform: |ψ(t+1)⟩ = (I + iΔt/2 H_int,I)⁻¹ (I – iΔt/2 H_int,I) |ψ(t)⟩, where H_int,I is the interaction-picture Hamiltonian. This update is exactly unitary for any step size Δt.
- **Core assumption**: The Hamiltonian is Hermitian at every step (by construction) and the step size is fixed (typically Δt = 1 per token).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: “The dynamics are strictly unitary, ensuring that the state norm is preserved exactly at every time step via a Cayley (Crank–Nicolson) discretization.”
  - [section]: Proposition 3.1 proves that the Cayley update W(t) is unitary for any Hermitian H_int,I and any Δt > 0.
  - [corpus]: Other quantum‑inspired neural ODE works use Schrödinger‑like equations but do not address exact discrete unitarity; the Cayley technique is also used in unitary RNNs, but the corpus lacks direct evidence for this exact combination.
- **Break condition**: Numerical instability in the Woodbury solve (if the r×r matrix G = I + iΔt/2 Φ†D⁻¹Φ is ill‑conditioned) can cause deviations from unitarity in floating point. If g_θ produces a non‑Hermitian H_int (e.g., due to bugs), unitarity fails.

### Mechanism 2: Born Rule Decoding as Quadratic Lifting
- **Claim**: The Born rule p(k) = |⟨m_k|ψ⟩|² gives the model access to O(N²) pairwise phase features from an N‑dimensional complex state, creating a representational gap relative to linear readouts.
- **Mechanism**: Expanding the inner product yields p(k) = Σ_j |[m_k]_j|²|c_j|² + 2 Σ_{j<j′} Re([m_k]_j* [m_k]_{j′} c_j* c_{j′}). The cross terms c_j* c_{j′} = r_j r_{j′} e^{i(θ_{j′}−θ_j)} depend on relative phases. This is equivalent to a linear function on the density matrix ρ = |ψ⟩⟨ψ|, which has N² independent real parameters. The measurement vectors {m_k} must be informationally complete to span all Hermitian matrices.
- **Core assumption**: The measurement vectors form an informationally complete set (so V ≥ N² and their outer products span the space of N×N Hermitian matrices). The state is normalized (∥ψ∥ = 1) so probabilities sum to 1.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: “implicitly lifting the N‑dimensional complex state into an N²‑dimensional space of Hermitian matrices.”
  - [section]: Lemma 5.7 shows p(k) = vec(M_k)ᵀ vec(ρ), linear in the vectorized density matrix. Section 3.5 equation (20) displays the expansion with phase‑dependent cross terms.
  - [corpus]: Guidotti & Ferrara (2022) demonstrate Born‑rule effectiveness in text classification, providing empirical support for quadratic measurement in NLP, though not on sequence modeling.
- **Break condition**: If the measurement vectors are not informationally complete (e.g., V < N² or they lie in a low‑dimensional subspace), the model cannot fully utilize phase features. Small amplitude values can make cross terms numerically negligible relative to diagonals, reducing phase sensitivity.

### Mechanism 3: Probability Currents as Internal Diagnostics
- **Claim**: The model exhibits antisymmetric probability currents J_{j←k}(t) between latent dimensions that are driven solely by the interaction Hamiltonian, giving an exact, conservation‑respecting decomposition of amplitude redistribution at each step.
- **Mechanism**: From the Schrödinger equation, derive dp_j/dt = Σ_{k≠j} J_{j←k} with J_{j←k} = 2 Im(H_{jk} c_j* c_k). Hermiticity ensures antisymmetry (J_{k←j} = –J_{j←k}) and zero self‑current. For the discrete Cayley update, the midpoint current formula (36) using the averaged state yields exact discrete changes: Δp_j = Δt Σ_{k≠j} J_{mid,j←k}.
- **Core assumption**: Hamiltonians are Hermitian and the dynamics follow the Schrödinger equation (or Cayley discretization). The currents are valid for any complex state.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section]: Section 4 derives the continuity equation and defines currents; Proposition 4.1 proves antisymmetry, zero self‑current, and global conservation.
  - [section]: Section 4.5 derives the midpoint currents that exactly match discrete changes and inherit all structural properties.
- **Break condition**: Non‑Hermitian Hamiltonians (e.g., from implementation error) break antisymmetry and conservation. The currents are defined on the latent dimensions; if the latent space is entangled, the currents may not correlate with interpretable semantic features.

## Foundational Learning

### Complex Vector Spaces and Unitary Evolution
**Why needed**: The model’s state is a complex unit‑norm vector. Understanding phase (two states with identical magnitudes but different phases interfere differently) is essential for the Born rule’s phase sensitivity and the separation theorem. The distinction between unitary (complex) and orthogonal (real) transformations underlies the quadratic gap.

**Why needed**: The paper’s architectural guarantees (norm preservation, gradient stability) rely on Hermiticity and Cayley discretization. Understanding why explicit integrators fail and why Cayley works is key to appreciating the design choices.
**Quick check**: “Why does the Cayley transform of a skew‑Hermitian matrix yield a unitary matrix? Why is this important for long sequence modeling compared to forward Euler?”

**Why needed**: This output mechanism accesses O(N²) features via the density matrix ρ. It is the source of the representational advantage over linear readouts; the separation theorem rests on this algebraic fact.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Token embedding → [Embed(x_t); Re(ψ_I(t−1)); Im(ψ_I(t−1))] → g_θ (MLP) → (Φ(t), δ(t)) → H_int(t) = ΦΦ† + diag(δ) → Interaction-picture Hamiltonian ÌHint (via H₀ diagonal frequencies) → Cayley update (Woodbury solve) → ψ_I(t) → Schrödinger picture ψ(t) = ψ_I(t) ⊙ e^{−iλ_j t} → Measurement matrix M† → Born rule p(k)=|⟨m_k|ψ⟩|².

### Critical Path (single step)
1. Embed token x_t to vector v ∈ ℝ^d.
2. Form concatenated real vector z = [v; Re(ψ_I(t−1)); Im(ψ_I(t−1))] of length d+2N.
3. Forward through g_θ: output Φ ∈ ℂ^{N×r} and δ ∈ ℝ^N.
4. Compute interaction-picture factor: ÌΦ_{ja} = e^{iλ_j t} Φ_{ja}.
5. Compute v_vec = ÌΦ† ψ_I(t−1) (size r) and u = ÌΦ v_vec (size N).
6. Set b = (I − iΔt/2 diag(δ)) ψ_I(t−1) − iΔt/2 u.
7. Define diagonal D = I + iΔt/2 diag(δ) (elementwise D_{jj}=1+iΔt/2 δ_j).
8. Solve (D + iΔt/2 ÌΦ ÌΦ†) ψ_I(t) = b via Woodbury:
   - y = D⁻¹ b (elementwise)
   - P = D⁻¹ ÌΦ (each column divided by D_{jj})
   - z_vec = ÌΦ† y
   - G = I_r + iΔt/2 ÌΦ† P
   - Solve G w = z_vec
   - ψ_I(t) = y − iΔt/2 P w
9. Recover Schrödinger picture: ψ(t) = ψ_I(t) ⊙ e^{−iλ_j t}.
10. Compute α = M† ψ(t) (size V) and p(k)=|α_k|².

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Rank r of Φ**: Lower r reduces per-step cost (O(N r²)) and parameters but restricts the rank of H_int per step. Too small may force reliance on many steps to build complex interactions.
- **Measurement matrix M**: Enforcing M M† = I via QR every step is expensive (O(V N²)) and may interfere with gradients. Alternatives like Stiefel‑manifold parameterization could be cleaner but require Riemannian optimization.
- **Step size Δt**: Paper uses Δt = 1 per token. Smaller Δt would require sub‑steps per token, increasing cost but potentially improving accuracy; the discrete model’s goal is not exact continuous approximation but stable unitarity.

### Failure Signatures
- **Ill‑conditioned Woodbury matrix**: Monitor condition number of G (the r×r matrix). If κ(G) ≫ 10⁸, the solve may become unstable.
- **Norm drift**: Track ∥ψ(t)∥ over sequence length; should stay within 1 ± 1e−6. Decay or growth indicates lost unitarity.
- **Gradient explosion in g_θ**: While state gradients are preserved, gradients through g_θ are not. Monitor gradient norms of g_θ parameters; clip if necessary (e.g., norm > 1.0).
- **Poor learning on synthetic tasks**: If CUSM fails to learn D_N with dimension N, check that M is informationally complete (M M† ≈ I), that context states are non‑orthogonal, and that loss decreases from random initialization. Adjust learning rate or r if needed.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Synthetic task validation (Theorem 5.10)**: Construct D_N for N=4,8,16 with general‑position contexts (random non‑orthogonal unit vectors and unitaries) and informationally complete measurement (V=N², e.g., random complex matrix with orthonormal rows after QR). Train CUSM (complex unitary) and ROSM (real orthogonal) across dimensions: CUSM at N, ROSM at d ∈ {N, N²/2, N²}. Measure cross‑entropy loss on all N² input pairs. Expected: CUSM achieves near‑zero loss at dim N; ROSM requires d ≥ N²−2 for comparable loss.
2. **Born rule vs. affine‑softmax on language**: Use TinyShakespeare. Build two models with identical unitary dynamics (H₀, g_θ, initial state) but different outputs: one uses Born rule, the other uses affine‑softmax on [Re(ψ); Im(ψ)] (size 2N) followed by softmax. Train both with same hyperparameters and seed. Compare validation perplexity as N increases (e.g., 64, 128, 256). Expect Born rule to show lower perplexity, with gap growing with N.
3. **Probability currents peak at disambiguation**: Train CUSM on SemCor 3.0 (sense‑annotated). For each token position, compute midpoint current magnitude: J_mid(t) = Σ_{j<k} |J_{mid,j←k}(t)|. Label tokens as “disambiguating” if their sense annotation resolves a prior ambiguity (e.g., “bank” followed by “river” or “financial”). Compare J_mid distributions between disambiguating and non‑disambiguating tokens using Wilcoxon rank‑sum test (Bonferroni‑corrected). Prediction: disambiguating tokens have significantly higher J_mid.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question**: Does the Ω(N²) expressivity separation extend to the state‑dependent quantum model?
**Basis in paper**: “Proving a separation for the full state‑dependent architecture is an open problem.” (Section 6.3)
**Why unresolved**: The current proof relies on fixed token→unitary mappings and a rank argument that fails when the Hamiltonian depends on the current state (introducing nonlinearity).
**What evidence would resolve it**: New lower‑bound proof techniques for state‑dependent models, or a counterexample showing a state‑dependent real model achieving the tasks with dimension o(N²).

### Open Question 2
**Question**: Under what conditions does the target log‑probability matrix L* have full row rank N²?
**Basis in paper**: “The lower bound is conditional on a full‑rank assumption on L* … a complete analytic characterization is deferred.” (Remark following Theorem 5.10)
**Why unresolved**: While the probability matrix P* is proved rank N², entrywise logarithms are not rank‑preserving, so rank(L*) may be lower. The genericity claim remains unproven.
**What evidence would resolve it**: Prove that for almost all choices of the task parameters (context states, query unitaries, measurement vectors), L* has full rank; alternatively, circumvent the assumption using a relative log‑odds rank argument.

### Open Question 3
**Question**: Do the five experimental predictions (Section 6.2) hold for trained models on natural language data?
**Basis in paper**: Section 6.2 outlines specific tests; Section 6.3 notes the complete lack of empirical validation.
**Why unresolved**: The work is purely theoretical; none of the predicted phenomena (quadratic scaling, Born‑rule advantage, probability current peaks, frequency correlation, phase cross‑term impact) have been observed in practice.
**What evidence would resolve it**: Implement the architecture, run the proposed protocols on synthetic tasks (Prediction 1), standard language corpora (Predictions 2,4,5), and sense‑annotated data (Prediction 3). Confirming results would strongly support the theory; any major deviation would challenge its practical relevance.

## Limitations
- The paper provides no empirical validation; all claims remain theoretical.
- Critical implementation details are omitted: architecture of g_θ (layers, activations), rank r selection, learning hyperparameters, and initialization schemes.
- The separation theorem relies on a “full‑rank condition” of the target log‑probability matrix, which may not hold for natural language tasks.
- Practical stability of the Cayley solve (conditioning of G) and enforcement of orthonormality for M are not addressed.
- The model’s computational cost scaling (O(N r² + N V)) is given, but optimal choices of r and V are unknown.
