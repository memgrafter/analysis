# Heterogeneous Debate Engine: Identity-Grounded Cognitive Architecture for Resilient LLM-Based Ethical Tutoring

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27404  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27404  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** HDE achieves perfect argumentative coherence (ArCo=1.00) under adversarial perturbations (homogeneous: ArCo=0.06) and increases student argument complexity by an order of magnitude (ΔACS=+2.2).

## Executive Summary
Multi-agent LLM debates for ethical tutoring commonly suffer from semantic drift and consensus collapse. The Heterogeneous Debate Engine (HDE) addresses these failures through Identity-Grounded RAG (ID-RAG), which filters retrieval against immutable doctrinal boundaries to enforce persona fidelity, and Heuristic Theory of Mind (ToM-Lite), which uses static opponent weakness maps to stimulate cross-framework engagement. Crucially, HDE teams are heterogeneous—pitting axiomatically opposed ethical schools (e.g., Deontology vs. Utilitarianism)—to prevent meta-epistemological convergence. Under adversarial perturbations, heterogeneous systems maintain perfect argumentative coherence (ArCo=1.00) while homogeneous systems collapse (ArCo=0.06). A student study (N=22) shows heterogeneous debates boost Argument Complexity Scores by 2.2 points, an order-of-magnitude gain over single-agent baselines. Ablation studies confirm ID-RAG improves doctrinal accuracy by 39% and ToM-Lite increases cross-referencing by 35%. These results establish architectural heterogeneity as essential for resilient, high-fidelity adversarial pedagogy.

## Method Summary
HDE orchestrates a three-phase debate via a LangGraph state machine: Internal Deliberation, Socratic Interrogation, and Inter-Team Debate. Each philosopher agent (e.g., Kant, Mill) comprises two modules: (1) ID-RAG, which retrieves from a philosopher-specific corpus (ChromaDB) and removes retrieved facts that violate immutable negative constraints from the agent’s belief graph; (2) ToM-Lite, which consults a predefined weakness map of the opponent framework to tailor counter-arguments. A neutral Socratic Moderator injects perturbations at Turn 4. Evaluation used ethical dilemmas with three perturbations, measuring System Argumentative Resilience (SysAR), Argumentative Coherence (ArCo), Doctrinal Accuracy (DA), Cross-Referencing (CR), and student learning gains (ACS). Ablations compared Full HDE against conditions lacking ID-RAG or ToM-Lite.

## Key Results
- Heterogeneous teams sustained perfect argumentative coherence (ArCo=1.00) under perturbation; homogeneous teams failed (ArCo=0.06).
- In student trials (N=22), heterogeneous debates achieved ΔACS=+2.2, an order-of-magnitude improvement over single-agent baselines (+0.2/+0.4).
- Ablation: ID-RAG increased doctrinal accuracy by 39%; ToM-Lite raised cross-referencing by 35%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Identity-Grounded RAG (ID-RAG)
ID-RAG enforces doctrinal fidelity by filtering retrieved facts against an immutable belief graph (γ=1.0). Negative constraints (e.g., Kantian “REJECT: Reducing morality to calculation”) delete non-conforming facts from working memory before generation, preventing persona drift. Evidence: Section 4.2 details filtering; ablation shows +39% DA. Break condition: incomplete or incorrect constraints may over-filter or miss violations, suppressing dialectical nuance.

### Heuristic Theory of Mind (ToM-Lite)
ToM-Lite equips agents with a static opponent weakness map (e.g., “Utilitarianism vulnerable to justice criticisms”). During turn preparation, explicit belief injection prompts agents to craft counter-arguments within the opponent’s framework—without recursive simulation—shifting dialogue from parallel monologue to confrontation. Evidence: Section 6.3 reports +35% cross-referencing; the heuristic avoids simulation latency. Break condition: generic or misaligned weakness maps produce strawman attacks, possibly compromising the agent’s own doctrinal boundaries.

### Architectural Heterogeneity
Heterogeneous teams initialize agents with irreconcilable first principles (e.g., Deontology vs. Utilitarianism), making meta-epistemological convergence impossible and forcing sustained object-level engagement. Homogeneous teams (e.g., two Virtue Ethics variants) drift into irrelevant meta-debates (e.g., theological vs. rationalist grounds). Evidence: Resilience tests (ArCo 1.00 vs. 0.06) and student studies (ΔACS +2.2 vs. negative regression in homogeneous condition) show stability arises from axiomatic incongruence. Break condition: heterogeneity must be at the school level; intra-school differences still allow consensus collapse.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Team Coordination Layer (LangGraph State Machine) -> orchestrates debate phases -> interacts with Philosopher-Agent Modules (each with ID-RAG and ToM-Lite) and Socratic Moderator -> injects perturbations.

**Critical path:** Moderator defines topic -> Phase 1: Internal Deliberation (agents intra-team sync) -> Phase 2: Socratic Interrogation (moderator challenges) -> Phase 3: Inter-Team Debate (cyclic turns with ID-RAG/ToM-Lite) -> Turn 4 perturbation -> Post-perturbation observation (Turns 5-10).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Heterogeneity vs. Coherence: Axiomatic conflict prevents consensus collapse but requires careful school selection to avoid incoherence.
- Constraint Rigor vs. Flexibility: ID-RAG’s negative boundaries ensure fidelity but risk over-filtering; system prioritizes pedagogical precision.
- Heuristic ToM vs. Simulation Fidelity: Static weakness maps are efficient but may miss dynamic adaptation; offset by opponents’ fixed identity.

**Failure signatures:**
- Homogeneous teams: post-perturbation drift into meta-debates (ArCo << 1.0, e.g., 0.06), abandoning ethical dilemma keywords.
- Vanilla RAG only: low DA (~0.51) and CR (~0.05), producing generic moral talk.
- Vanilla + ToM (no ID-RAG): CR improves but DA volatile (e.g., Mill DA=0.33 on `push_vs_lever`).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. System Resilience Test: Compare Hetero vs. Homo under three perturbations; measure SysAR and ArCo. Expected: Hetero ArCo=1.00, Homo ArCo<<1.0.
2. Architectural Ablation Study: Within Hetero, test Full, ID-RAG only, ToM only, Neither; measure DA, CR, ArCo. Expected: Full maximizes both; ID-RAG +39% DA, ToM +35% CR.
3. Pedagogical Effectiveness Study (N=22): Pre-test/exposure/post-test comparing B_Chat, B_SingleRAG, Homo, Hetero; measure ΔACS and Quiz Shift. Expected: Hetero ΔACS ~+2.2, Homo ΔACS negative.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Balancing fidelity and flexibility:** How can HDE quantify the trade-off between doctrinal fidelity (via ID-RAG constraints) and generative flexibility for novel arguments? The paper demonstrates improved fidelity but not argumentative novelty or Pareto frontier mapping.
2. **Generalization beyond ethics:** Does architectural heterogeneity stabilize other multi-agent domains (e.g., scientific debate, policy)? Validation is limited to ethical dilemmas.
3. **Mechanism of homogeneous degeneration:** What precise process causes homogeneous systems (e.g., Aristotle vs. Aquinas) to escalate into meta-epistemological debates? The hypothesis of “common axioms leading to conformity concerns” lacks process tracing.
4. **Boundary conditions for ID-RAG:** Over what debate length, topic ambiguity, and perturbation severity does ID-RAG maintain identity coherence? The study used fixed 10-turn debates; long-horizon durability is unknown.

## Limitations
- Belief graph extraction (immutable constraints) and ToM-Lite weakness map definitions are poorly specified, hindering replication.
- Socratic moderator prompts for ontological questioning and perturbation are underspecified.
- Long-term identity coherence under extended, ambiguous interactions remains untested; boundary conditions unknown.

## Confidence
- ID-RAG mechanism: **Medium** (conceptual support and ablation gains, but limited mechanistic transparency).
- ToM-Lite: **High** (strong ablation data and explicit mechanism).
- Architectural heterogeneity necessity: **High** (robust empirical evidence across resilience and student studies).
- Overall claim that heterogeneity is essential for resilient ethical tutoring: **High**.
