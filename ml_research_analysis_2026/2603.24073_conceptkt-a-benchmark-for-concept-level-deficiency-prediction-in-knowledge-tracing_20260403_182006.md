# ConceptKT: A Benchmark for Concept-Level Deficiency Prediction in Knowledge Tracing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24073
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24073
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Concept-aware selection of historical student responses (by concept alignment and semantic similarity) significantly improves LLM-based diagnosis of specific concept deficiencies compared to using all available history.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel task in knowledge tracing (KT): concept-level deficiency prediction. Instead of only predicting whether a student will answer a question correctly, the goal is to diagnose which specific prerequisite concepts the student lacks. To enable this, the authors present ConceptKT, a small expert-annotated dataset linking math questions to required concepts and incorrect responses to missing concepts. They explore using large language models (LLMs) and reasoning models (LRMs) in a zero-shot in-context learning setup. The central finding is that carefully selecting which past student responses to include in the prompt—prioritizing those covering the same concepts and with semantically similar questions—dramatically improves the model's ability to identify missing concepts, while using the entire history often degrades performance.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates in-context learning for concept-level KT without any model training. The core pipeline uses a fixed Chain-of-Thought prompt that instructs the model to (1) identify the target question's concepts, (2) analyze the student's historical mastery from provided examples, and (3) predict both correctness and any lacking concepts. The critical innovation is the **selection strategy** for the historical examples provided in the prompt: (1) **All Responses** (unfiltered history), (2) **Same-Concept Only** (only responses for questions sharing at least one concept with the target), and (3) **Conceptual Semantic Selection** (from the same-concept set, rank by BERTScore semantic similarity to the target and take top-30). Predictions are parsed from a strict output format. Performance is measured on the ConceptKT dataset via correctness accuracy and Macro-F1 for multi-label concept deficiency.

## Key Results
- Filtering historical responses by **conceptual alignment** (Same-Concept Only) yields large gains in concept-level Macro-F1 over using all history (e.g., DeepSeek-R1: 17.40% vs. 14.82%; Llama-4: 10.13% vs. 5.43%).
- Adding **semantic similarity** ranking within the concept-aligned set further improves performance for certain models (e.g., Llama-4 Macro-F1: 13.83% vs. 10.13%).
- Using **unfiltered histories** consistently degrades concept-level diagnosis, with some models' Macro-F1 dropping dramatically.
- DeepSeek-R1 achieves the best overall performance (71.02% accuracy, 16.87% Macro-F1) with Conceptual Semantic Selection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Conceptual Filtering Reduces Noise
- **Claim:** Filtering to include only historical responses that share at least one concept with the target question improves predictive performance by reducing irrelevant context.
- **Mechanism:** The selection removes responses unrelated to the target's knowledge components, decreasing noise and helping the model focus on patterns directly relevant to the student's mastery of those specific concepts.
- **Core assumption:** Student responses on problems covering the same concepts are more predictive of target performance than unrelated responses.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "selecting response histories based on conceptual alignment ... leads to improved performance"; Table 5 shows Macro-F1 gains for DeepSeek-R1 (17.40% vs 14.82% with All Responses) and Llama-4 (10.13% vs 5.43%); neighbor work on concept disentanglement supports concept-aware filtering.
- **Break condition:** If a student has few or no prior responses covering the target concepts, this strategy yields insufficient context and may degrade performance.

### Mechanism 2: Semantic Similarity Refines Within-Concept Selection
- **Claim:** Ranking the concept-aligned responses by semantic similarity to the target question (via BERTScore) provides additional gains by prioritizing contextually similar problems.
- **Mechanism:** Semantic similarity selects examples whose question text is lexically and contextually close to the target, likely involving similar problem contexts, wording, or reasoning steps, enriching the in-context examples with high-fidelity patterns.
- **Core assumption:** Questions with high semantic similarity share underlying reasoning structures even when covering identical concepts.
- **Evidence anchors:** Conceptual Semantic Selection improves Llama-4's Macro-F1 from 10.13% to 13.83% and o3-mini from 10.90% to 12.77% (Table 5, statistically significant); abstract highlights "conceptual alignment and semantic similarity"; neighbor work on concept-level training objectives aligns with this fine-grained approach.
- **Break condition:** If the similarity metric does not correlate well with conceptual relevance (e.g., selects paraphrases of different problems), it may introduce irrelevant context.

### Mechanism 3: In-Context Learning with LLMs/LRMs Enables Zero-Shot Diagnosis
- **Claim:** In-context learning with LLMs/LRMs is effective for concept-level KT because these models can leverage pre-trained knowledge to reason about mathematical concepts and student misconceptions directly from natural language prompts.
- **Mechanism:** The structured Chain-of-Thot prompt guides the model to identify target concepts, analyze history, and predict correctness/missing concepts. The model's internal knowledge of mathematics and common errors supports accurate diagnosis without task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs possess sufficient mathematical and pedagogical knowledge to perform KT in a zero/few-shot manner.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states evaluation of "diagnostic capabilities of various LLMs and LRMs"; DeepSeek-R1 achieves 71.02% accuracy and 16.87% Macro-F1 with Conceptual Semantic Selection; multiple neighboring papers apply LLMs to KT.
- **Break condition:** Models with limited reasoning capacity or poor instruction following may fail to produce structured outputs or misinterpret concepts, as seen with Llama-4's low Macro-F1 (5.43%) when using all responses.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Student History Database -> Concept Annotation Database -> Selection Module -> Prompt Constructor -> LLM Inference Engine -> Output Parser.
  - Student History Database: stores (question, response, correctness) tuples per student.
  - Concept Annotation Database: stores for each question its associated concepts (expert-labeled) and for each incorrect response the missing concepts (expert-labeled).
  - Selection Module: filters student history according to strategy (All, Same-Concept, Conceptual Semantic). Conceptual Semantic computes BERTScore between candidate questions and target.
  - Prompt Constructor: formats selected histories and target into structured prompt (Table 4), including concept list and Chain-of-Thought instructions.
  - LLM Inference Engine: calls chosen LLM/LRM with temperature=0.
  - Output Parser: extracts correctness and missing concepts from textual output; enforces strict format.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Input: student ID, target question q.
  2. Retrieve student’s full history H.
  3. Determine required concepts C_q for q (from annotation or prediction).
  4. Apply selection strategy to H → subset S (size ≤ m, default m=30).
  5. Construct prompt using S and q.
  6. Submit prompt to LLM, get raw output.
  7. Parse output into (correctness, missing concepts).
  8. Return predictions; if incorrect, missing concepts inform remediation.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Selection strategy: Conceptual Semantic adds BERTScore overhead but can yield gains; Same-Concept Only is simpler and nearly as effective. All Responses is trivial but often harms concept-level prediction.
  - Context length: Larger m provides more data but risks noise; even with long-context models, selection remains beneficial, suggesting quality over quantity.
  - Model choice: Larger reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1) outperform smaller ones but at higher computational cost. Model-specific prompting may further improve results but is not explored.
  - Prompt fixedness: A single prompt format is used for all models; tailored prompting could unlock more capability.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Persistently low Macro-F1 (<10%) on concept deficiency: likely due to poor selection (e.g., including many irrelevant responses) or model’s inability to follow output format.
  - Parsing errors: model output deviates from expected pattern (e.g., extra explanations, missing "&&"), indicating weak instruction adherence.
  - High variance across students: some students (e.g., those with unstable performance or sparse histories) yield unreliable predictions; Section 6 notes this as a key challenge.
  - Degradation when augmenting with different-concept responses (Table 6): even similarity-based augmentation lowers performance, confirming the importance of conceptual purity.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline selection comparison:** Evaluate All Responses, Same-Concept Only, and Conceptual Semantic Selection across Gemini-2.0-Flash, Llama-4, o3-mini, DeepSeek-R1 on correctness accuracy and concept-level Macro-F1 (Table 5). Establishes that concept-aware selection improves diagnosis.
  2. **Augmentation analysis:** For Llama-4, when same-concept responses are fewer than 30, augment with random or similarity-based different-concept responses. Results (Table 6) show augmentation harms performance, reinforcing that conceptual alignment outweighs quantity.
  3. **Model-specific analysis:** Compare how different models react to selection strategies. For example, DeepSeek-R1’s Macro-F1 remains high across strategies, while o3-mini benefits more from Conceptual Semantic Selection. Informs model selection for deployment.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
Does using the full student response history consistently degrade model performance for concept-level deficiency prediction, or are there specific student or problem characteristics where it remains beneficial?
- **Basis in paper:** RQ1 asks whether to feed entire history or a subset; findings show unfiltered histories degraded results.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates filtering improves average performance but does not analyze for which subgroups (e.g., students with stable vs. volatile performance) or problem types (e.g., high vs. low concept coverage) the full history might be non-detrimental.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study stratifying results by student consistency (e.g., error rate variance) and problem attributes (e.g., number of associated concepts), comparing "All Responses" vs. filtered strategies within these strata.

### Open Question 2
Beyond conceptual alignment and semantic similarity, what other criteria for historical response selection most effectively predict nuanced, multi-concept deficiencies, especially for students with partial or context-dependent mastery?
- **Basis in paper:** RQ2 asks which records are most informative; authors test Conceptual Semantic Selection and note it further improves performance for certain models.
- **Why unresolved:** The study only evaluates a limited set of selection strategies (concept-matching + BERTScore). It does not explore other potential signals like response complexity, error type history, or temporal recency.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation of novel selection heuristics (e.g., prioritizing records with specific error types, varying time windows, or difficulty gradients) on concept-level Macro-F1, particularly for responses with non-binary understanding.

### Open Question 3
How does the coarse-grained binary annotation of concept deficiency (present/absent) limit a model's ability to capture and predict the continuum of student understanding, and would graded labels improve diagnostic fidelity?
- **Basis in paper:** Limitations state the binary scheme "may oversimplify students’ conceptual states."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies this as a limitation but provides no empirical analysis of its impact. It is unknown whether model performance stalls due to annotation granularity or other factors.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A follow-up study re-annotating a subset with multi-level labels (e.g., "no mastery," "partial," "proficient") and measuring change in model performance and calibration.

### Open Question 4
To what extent do the findings on concept-aware selection generalize beyond the specific MathEDU dataset (secondary school math, 55 concepts) to other educational domains (e.g., programming, language learning) with different concept taxonomies and error structures?
- **Basis in paper:** Discussion notes predicting concept-level deficiencies remains challenging and the dataset is rooted in a specific mathematical curriculum; concept distribution is imbalanced.
- **Why unresolved:** The dataset's narrow scope—single subject, specific student population, expert-defined concepts—means optimal selection strategies may not transfer. The role of "conceptual alignment" could diminish in domains with less hierarchical knowledge components.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replication of the experimental framework on diverse, multi-domain KT datasets (e.g., programming exercises with bug-type concepts, language learning with grammar rules), comparing efficacy of concept-based selection against alternatives like skill embeddings or graph-based relations.

## Limitations
- **Dataset scale & diversity:** ConceptKT is extremely small (4,048 records, 6 students), raising concerns about statistical validity, student population generalizability, and potential overfitting to idiosyncratic histories.
- **Prompt engineering & parsing fragility:** The exact prompt template and strict output parser are critical but incompletely specified. Minor formatting deviations likely cause silent failures, limiting reproducibility.
- **Concept annotation dependency:** The approach assumes high-quality, pre-mapped concept labels for all questions. For new or adaptive question generation, this becomes a separate, unsolved bottleneck.

## Confidence
- **Claim:** Concept-aware history filtering (Same-Concept/Conceptual Semantic) significantly improves concept-level diagnosis vs. using all history.
  - **Confidence: High** (Supported by Table 5 across multiple models; ablation in Table 6).
- **Claim:** Conceptual Semantic Selection (BERTScore) provides additional gains over Same-Concept Only for some models.
  - **Confidence: Medium** (Gains observed for Llama-4, o3-mini but not DeepSeek-R1; effect size moderate and model-specific).
- **Claim:** In-context learning with LLMs/LRMs is an effective paradigm for zero-shot concept-level KT.
  - **Confidence: Medium** (Results are promising but baseline comparisons to traditional KT models are limited; performance (16.87% Macro-F1) remains modest for a diagnostic tool).
