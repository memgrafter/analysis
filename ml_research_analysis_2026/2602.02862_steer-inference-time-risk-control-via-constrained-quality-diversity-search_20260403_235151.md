# STEER: Inference-Time Risk Control via Constrained Quality-Diversity Search

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02862  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02862  
- **Reference count:** 22  
- **Primary result:** STEER constructs a diverse ensemble of persona‑based LLM raters via constrained quality‑diversity search, enabling deterministic, monotonic adjustment of risk tolerance in clinical triage while maintaining high safety and broad behavioral coverage.

## Executive Summary
Large language models aligned via standard RLHF often suffer from mode collapse, eliminating the spectrum of expert disagreement needed for risk‑sensitive applications like clinical triage. STEER addresses this by offline evolving a team of natural‑language personas—each with a distinct, describable bias—while enforcing strict safety, coherence, and stability constraints. At inference time, a user‑specified risk percentile selects the corresponding persona’s decision from a sorted list of ordinal outputs, yielding a smooth trade‑off between overtriage and undertriage. On two clinical benchmarks, STEER outperforms temperature sampling and static persona baselines in coverage (Ordinal AUC) and retains high accuracy on unambiguous urgent cases compared to post‑training alignment methods.

## Method Summary
STEER’s pipeline begins with a set of seed personas (e.g., “Vital Signs Anchor”). For each persona and case, the base LLM produces an ordinal triage label (ESI level 1–5). A linear mixed‑effects model fits the data: \( RC(i,j) = \Theta_i + u_j + \varepsilon_{ij} \), where \( u_j \) is the persona’s bias descriptor (centered intercept) and \( \Theta_i \) the case effect. Personas are evaluated on safety (accuracy on unambiguous urgent cases), coherence (reasoning validity via an LLM judge), and variance (residual instability). Those meeting thresholds form a feasible pool. An evolutionary loop then generates new personas: gap‑filling (interpolating between neighbors in \( u \)) and edge expansion (extrapolating beyond extremes) using a meta‑LLM. After several generations, a final team of \( N \) personas is assembled by uniformly covering the \( u \) spectrum while favoring high safety/coherence and low variance. During inference, for a given case, all team outputs are sorted by urgency; a user‑provided percentile \( P \) selects the label at rank \( \lfloor P/100 \cdot (N-1) \rfloor \), guaranteeing monotonic conservativeness with increasing \( P \).

## Key Results
- **Broader behavioral coverage:** STEER achieves substantially higher Ordinal AUC than temperature sampling and static persona baselines across clinical triage benchmarks.
- **High safety on unambiguous cases:** STEER maintains 80.9% accuracy on urgent unambiguous cases (MIETIC), far above Spectrum Tuning’s 49.6%.
- **Comparable control:** The percentile tuning yields a smooth, monotonic overtriage‑undertriage trade‑off, matching or surpassing specialized alignment methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Constrained quality‑diversity search expands valid behavioral coverage while preserving safety.
**Claim:** The evolutionary loop maximizes entropy of ambiguous‑case outputs subject to feasibility constraints, yielding a diverse yet safe persona pool.  
**Mechanism:** New personas are generated via gap‑filling (interpolation between existing \( u \) neighbors) and edge expansion (extrapolation beyond extremes). Each candidate is evaluated for safety (≥90% on unambiguous urgent), coherence (above 15th percentile of LLM‑judge scores), and variance (below 85th percentile within bias clusters). Only feasible personas enter the next generation. The objective explicitly maximizes \( D(P) \) (mean normalized entropy) over the population \( P \) subject to \( \Phi \) (feasibility).  
**Core assumption:** The valid spectrum of expert disagreement \( P(y|x) \) can be captured by a discrete set of personas without requiring continuous density estimation.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: “constrained quality‑diversity search that promotes behavioral coverage while enforcing minimum safety, reasoning, and stability thresholds.”  
- Section 3.3: “We seek to find a population \( P \subseteq \Phi \) that maximizes Eq. 3 [diversity].”  
- Section 4.1.3: “We apply feasibility constraints sequentially… discarding personas failing a strict accuracy threshold.”  
- Corpus support is indirect; neighbors focus on personas/QD but not constrained evolution for risk control.  
**Break condition:** If constraint thresholds \( \tau \) are set too high, the feasible region \( \Phi \) may become empty or overly small, collapsing diversity entirely.

### Mechanism 2: Inference‑time percentile tuning provides a deterministic, monotonic control parameter for risk tolerance.
**Claim:** Sorting the ensemble’s ordinal outputs and selecting by a user‑specified percentile yields a monotonic adjustment of decision conservativeness.  
**Mechanism:** For a given case, collect ordinal labels from all team personas. Sort them from most urgent (e.g., ESI 1) to least. The percentile \( P \in [0,100] \) maps to rank index \( k = \lfloor P/100 \cdot (N-1) \rfloor \); the output is the label at that position. Because the list is sorted, increasing \( P \) always selects a label that is equal or more conservative (higher ESI number).  
**Core assumption:** The persona biases \( u_j \) consistently translate to output urgency, so that sorting by label order respects the underlying bias ordering.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: “a deterministic percentile‑tuning mechanism maps a user‑specified risk parameter to a selected persona, yielding a monotonic adjustment of decision conservativeness.”  
- Section 4.3: “Because the outputs are sorted, this function guarantees monotonicity, where increasing \( P \) strictly results in an output that is either equal to or more conservative.”  
- Section 6.2: Overtriage/undertriage trade‑off curves are strictly non‑decreasing.  
**Break condition:** If the ensemble’s bias diversity is narrow or personas are inconsistent, the sorted outputs may cluster or not follow a total order, breaking monotonicity.

### Mechanism 3: A linear bias model reduces persona behavior to a scalar descriptor for evolutionary navigation.
**Claim:** Fitting a mixed‑effects model \( RC(i,j) = \Theta_i + u_j + \varepsilon_{ij} \) yields a reliable scalar bias \( u_j \) that can be targeted during generation.  
**Mechanism:** For all collected persona‑case pairs, grand mean is computed; \( u_j \) is the mean label for persona \( j \) minus grand mean; \( \Theta_i \) is the mean label for case \( i \) minus grand mean. Residual variance \( s_j^2 \) is used for the variance constraint. The model assumes ordinal labels (1–K) can be treated as continuous integers for ranking.  
**Core assumption:** Case effects \( \Theta_i \) and persona biases \( u_j \) are separable; disagreements are systematic across many cases, not case‑specific.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Section 3.4: “We model the observed urgency \( RC(i, j) \)… by persona \( j \) as: \( RC(i, j) = \Theta_i + u_j + \varepsilon_{ij} \)… \( u_j \) serves as the behavioral descriptor.”  
- Appendix C.2: High correlation (\( r>0.73 \)) between targeted and actual \( u_j \) indicates the model’s controllability.  
- Section 5.3: Entropy computed over discrete ordinal distributions from persona outputs, not from continuous \( u_j \).  
**Break condition:** If \( \Theta_i \) and \( u_j \) are not separable (e.g., personas disagree only on a subset of cases), the \( u_j \) estimates become noisy, impairing gap‑filling and clustering.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Mode collapse in RLHF**  
  *Why needed:* Standard alignment collapses output distribution to a single mode, eliminating valid disagreement needed for risk tuning.  
  *Quick check:* Why does maximizing average reward in RLHF tend to reduce output diversity on ambiguous inputs?

- **Concept: Quality‑Diversity (QD) algorithms**  
  *Why needed:* STEER’s evolutionary search is a QD process—it explicitly maximizes a diversity metric (entropy) alongside quality constraints, unlike standard evolutionary algorithms that converge to a single optimum.  
  *Quick check:* How does a QD algorithm’s objective differ from a standard genetic algorithm’s objective?

- **Concept: Ordinal decision‑making & ROC operating point**  
  *Why needed:* The task is ordinal triage (levels 1–5). Control means moving the operating point along an overtriage vs. undertriage curve, analogous to adjusting the threshold on a ROC.  
  *Quick check:* In clinical triage, what does moving toward “more conservative” mean in terms of false positives and false negatives?

- **Concept: Mixed‑effects modeling for bias decomposition**  
  *Why needed:* Separating case difficulty from persona bias allows a low‑dimensional descriptor \( u_j \) that guides evolution.  
  *Quick check:* What assumptions are required for \( u_j \) and \( \Theta_i \) to be estimable from the collected data?

- **Concept: Evolutionary generation with meta‑LLM**  
  *Why needed:* New personas are created by prompting a meta‑LLM to generate natural‑language descriptions that target specific bias gaps.  
  *Quick check:* How does the meta‑LLM use the target bias value to shape the persona’s behavior?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Seed personas → (Evolution loop: generate candidates → evaluate safety/coherence/variance → filter → add to pool) × generations → Final feasible pool → Team assembly (bias‑uniform selection) → Inference engine (parallel LLM calls, sort outputs, percentile selector).

**Critical path:** Seed personas → iterative evolution → feasible pool → team assembly → deployment. The team assembly step’s coverage heuristic is crucial; if it fails to uniformly sample the \( u \) spectrum, steerability degrades.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Team size \( N \) vs. coverage:** Larger \( N \) improves diversity but linearly increases inference cost; diminishing returns after ~10 personas.  
- **Constraint strictness \( \tau \) vs. population health:** Tighter constraints prune more but risk annihilating diversity; authors use adaptive percentiles and sequential filtering to avoid empty feasible regions.  
- **Evolution generations vs. compute:** More generations refine the pool but require extra meta‑LLM calls; paper uses 5 generations.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Low Ordinal AUC:** Ensemble diversity insufficient. Diagnose: narrow \( u_j \) range, redundant team selection, or overly strict constraints.  
- **Non‑monotonic tuning:** Percentile increase sometimes reduces urgency. Check: persona inconsistency (high variance), or misalignment between \( u_j \) and label ordering.  
- **Low safety on unambiguous cases:** Many personas fail the safety threshold. Examine: \( \tau_{safe} \) too low, evolution drifted unsafe, or mismatch between training and test safety sets.  
- **High output variance:** Residual variance \( s_j^2 \) large for many personas. Remedy: tighten clustering delta, increase pruning fraction, or enforce deterministic decoding.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline Coverage Test:** Compare STEER‑evolved team (N=10) vs. static random personas on ambiguous cases; compute mean normalized entropy \( D(P) \). Expect STEER > Static.  
2. **Monotonicity Stress Test:** Sweep risk percentile \( P \) from 0 to 100 on fixed ambiguous cases; count how often urgency decreases when \( P \) increases. STEER should show zero violations; high‑temperature baselines may not.  
3. **Constraint Ablation:** Disable each constraint (safety, coherence, variance) in turn. Measure impact on \( D(P) \) and safety accuracy on unambiguous urgent cases; safety ablation should catastrophically drop accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Distillation:** Can the ensemble be compressed into a single steerable model without losing the risk dial and safety guarantees?  
- **Open‑ended generation:** Can STEER’s framework extend to tasks with free‑form outputs (e.g., creative writing) where ordinal metrics are unavailable?  
- **Domain generalization:** How well does STEER transfer to other high‑stakes domains (legal, financial, ethical reasoning) with different ambiguity patterns and safety definitions?  
- **Model update robustness:** If the underlying LLM changes (e.g., API upgrade), can the ensemble be efficiently re‑calibrated to preserve operating characteristics?

## Limitations
- **Bias estimation fidelity:** Paper uses a PyTorch Adam optimizer for the linear mixed‑effects model; a closed‑form solution (via means) may diverge if regularization or weighting is applied, risking inaccurate \( u_j \).  
- **Meta‑LLM dependence:** Persona generation success hinges on the capability of the meta‑LLM (GPT‑5‑mini in the paper); substituting a weaker model may fail to target biases precisely and limit diversity.  
- **Variance clustering heuristic:** The adaptive delta \( \delta \) and DBSCAN validation are incompletely specified; simplified clustering could prune extremes differently, affecting final team composition.  
- **Coherence judge variability:** Using different LLM judges or thresholds could alter filtering outcomes and final safety/coverage trade‑offs.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Constrained QD search):** Medium – Strong textual anchors but weak indirect corpus support; feasible region highly sensitive to thresholds.  
- **Mechanism 2 (Percentile tuning):** High – Deterministic and mathematically proven; empirical monotonicity curves strongly support.  
- **Mechanism 3 (Linear bias model):** Medium – High correlation (\( r>0.73 \)) supports separability assumption, but noise possible if disagreements are case‑specific.
