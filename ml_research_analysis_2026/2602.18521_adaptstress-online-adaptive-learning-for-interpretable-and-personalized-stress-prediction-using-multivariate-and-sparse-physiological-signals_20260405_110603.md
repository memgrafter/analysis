# AdaptStress: Online Adaptive Learning for Interpretable and Personalized Stress Prediction Using Multivariate and Sparse Physiological Signals

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.18521
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18521
*   **Reference Count:** 29
*   **Primary Result:** AdaptStress achieves optimal stress forecasting (5-day history, 1-day horizon) with MSE 0.053, MAE 0.190, and RMSE 0.226, representing improvements of 21.5–36.9% over the best baseline model.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces AdaptStress, an online adaptive learning framework for personalized and interpretable stress prediction from sparse wearable data. It addresses key gaps in existing work—focus on estimation over forecasting, poor cross-user generalization, and lack of explainability—by combining a Transformer-based architecture with feature-level attention, adversarial domain adaptation, and selective test-time adaptation. Evaluated on 16 participants with 10–15 weeks of multivariate physiological data (HRV, activity, sleep), the model demonstrates superior performance and reveals individualized, sometimes opposing, feature effects via SHAP analysis, with sleep metrics emerging as the most consistent predictors.

## Method Summary
AdaptStress is a three-phase framework. **Phase 1 (Training):** A Transformer encoder with feature-level attention processes sliding windows of 15 selected physiological features (history length `win`, forecast length `wout`). The model is trained with a combined loss: a regression loss (MSE) for stress prediction and an adversarial domain confusion loss (cross-entropy) to erase participant-specific biases, using leave-one-out cross-validation (14 train, 1 validation). **Phase 2 (Inference):** For a new user, a composite distribution shift score (`S_dist`) is computed. If the shift exceeds a threshold (0.3) and a quick validation suggests >2% improvement, selective test-time adaptation (TTA) applies 10 epochs of entropy minimization with data augmentation. **Phase 3 (Post-hoc):** Kernel SHAP is used on aggregated prediction windows to generate per-user feature importance and directional effects, validating personalization.

## Key Results
*   **Optimal Forecasting Performance:** Best results with a 5-day history and 1-day prediction horizon (H5-P1), achieving RMSE 0.226, MAE 0.190, and MSE 0.053, with 21.5–36.9% relative improvement over strong baselines (TimesNet, PatchTST, CNN-LSTM).
*   **Interpretability & Personalization:** SHAP analysis shows sleep features (e.g., sleep score, restfulness) have high consistency in importance across users (0.9–1.0), while activity features show high inter-participant variability (0.1–0.2). Crucially, identical features exert opposite effects on stress prediction for different individuals.
*   **Generalization Mechanism:** Adversarial domain adaptation and selective TTA enable stable performance across all 16 test participants, avoiding negative transfer for users with low initial distribution shift.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** The model learns interpretable, compact representations from sparse, multivariate time series via a Transformer encoder with feature-level attention.
*   **Mechanism:** Sliding windows segment continuous data into fixed-length sequences. The Transformer encoder processes these, where feature-level attention weights the 15 physiological features dynamically based on their temporal relevance, allowing the model to focus on the most informative signals despite sparsity.
*   **Core Assumption:** Stress-indicative patterns are captured within an optimal historical window (~5 days) and feature importance varies across time and individuals.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "processes multivariate physiological signals... with a sliding window approach." [section IV.C] details window formulation. [section IV.B] describes "feature-level attention mechanism."
*   **Break Condition:** If the optimal history window size changes drastically for new populations or feature attention weights remain static for a single user, the mechanism fails to capture temporal/personal variability.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Adversarial domain adaptation forces the model to learn features discriminative for stress but invariant to participant identity, improving generalization.
*   **Mechanism:** A domain discriminator is trained to identify the participant (domain) from feature representations, while the main feature extractor and predictor are trained to fool this discriminator. The total loss combines prediction error and adversarial domain confusion, erasing participant-specific idiosyncrasies.
*   **Core Assumption:** The primary barrier to cross-user generalization is distribution shift between participants, which can be aligned adversarially.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "adversarial domain adaptation for cross-participant generalization." [section IV.B.1] formalizes the adversarial objective with distinct domains per individual. [section IV.A] UMAP visualization shows "pronounced domain shifts."
*   **Break Condition:** If the domain discriminator's accuracy remains at chance, adversarial pressure is absent. If main task performance degrades severely despite low domain error, alignment may be harming task-relevant features.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Selective Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) prevents degradation by only adapting when a new user exhibits a significant distribution shift from the training population.
*   **Mechanism:** At inference, `S_dist` (combining MMD, KL Divergence, Variance Ratio) is computed. A hierarchical cascade uses `S_dist`, historical performance change, and a quick entropy-minimization validation test to decide whether to apply TTA (10 epochs with noise augmentation). This avoids unnecessary adaptation for already well-aligned users.
*   **Core Assumption:** Indiscriminate TTA harms users with low distribution shift through overfitting or error accumulation from noisy pseudo-labels.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] "selective test-time adaptation." [section IV.B.2 & B.3] details the cascade and thresholds (`S_dist > 0.3`, >2% improvement). [section V] shows stable performance across all participants.
*   **Break Condition:** Poorly calibrated thresholds cause under-adaptation (skipping needed TTA) or over-adaptation (applying TTA to low-shift users), worsening performance compared to a non-adaptive model.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Adversarial Domain Adaptation
*   **Why needed:** Physiological data distributions differ across participants. A model trained on one set fails on another without adaptation. Adversarial training builds a participant-agnostic feature extractor.
*   **Quick Check:** What is the *minimax* objective—what does the feature extractor try to minimize and maximize simultaneously?

### Concept: SHAP for Time Series Explainability
*   **Why needed:** To validate personalization and clinical utility by demonstrating how features have opposite, individualized effects on stress predictions.
*   **Quick Check:** Why is SHAP (vs. simple permutation importance) critical for proving *individual-specific* feature effects?

### Concept: Sliding Window Segmentation for Forecasting
*   **Why needed:** The problem is framed as sequence-to-sequence learning: given `win` days of features, predict `wout` days of stress. Sliding windows generate training samples from continuous data.
*   **Quick Check:** If the step size changes from 1 day to 7 days, what is the primary trade-off in training sample size vs. temporal correlation?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Raw 23 Garmin Features -> 15 Selected Features -> Imputation -> Sliding Windows -> Transformer Encoder (with Feature-Level Attention) -> Global Avg Pool -> Stress Regression Head (G_y) & Parallel Domain Discriminator (G_d) -> Combined Loss (MSE + Adversarial CE) -> (For Test User) Distribution Shift Check -> if High -> Selective TTA (Entropy Min + Augmentation) -> Final Inference -> SHAP Analysis

### Critical path
Data -> Preprocessing -> **Training with Adversarial Loss** -> [For Test User] **Distribution Shift Check** -> if `S_dist` high -> **Apply TTA** -> **Final Inference** -> **SHAP Analysis**.

### Design tradeoffs
*   Computational cost vs. Personalization: Adversarial training and selective TTA add significant overhead. TTA is skipped for low-shift users to save latency.
*   Window size (history vs. forecast): Longer history may capture more cycles but adds noise; shorter forecast (1-day) is more accurate. Trade-off is model capacity vs. sequence length.
*   Model complexity vs. Baselines: Transformer-based AdaptStress outperforms simpler (LSTM, CNN) and other transformer (Informer, PatchTST) models, indicating the specific innovations (feature-attention, domain-adv, selective TTA) are necessary.

### Failure signatures
*   High domain classifier accuracy (>95%) without main task drop: domain adaptation failed; features remain domain-specific.
*   SHAP consistency (1/CV) near 0 for key features: model hasn't learned stable physiological markers; predictions are spurious.
*   Performance degradation on longer forecast horizons (`wout=7`) for all models: fundamental limitation of wearable data for long-term forecasting.
*   TTA performance drop >5% on a user: selection mechanism should have skipped TTA; indicates threshold miscalibration.

### First 3 experiments
1.  **Ablation - Domain Adaptation:** Train AdaptStress with adversarial loss weight `α=0`. Expect significant cross-participant performance drop, proving the domain classifier's role.
2.  **Ablation - Selective TTA:** Apply TTA indiscriminately to all test participants. Compare average performance to selective TTA. Expect worse average performance, validating the selection cascade.
3.  **SHAP Consistency Check:** Compute the inverse CV (1/CV) of SHAP values for each feature across all 16 participants. Expect sleep features (SS, RS) to have high 1/CV (~0.9-1.0) and activity features (TS, TD) to have low 1/CV (~0.1-0.2), matching the paper's pattern. If all features show high consistency, the personalization mechanism may be broken.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Generalizability:** How does AdaptStress generalize to larger and more demographically diverse populations beyond the 16 participants in this study?
*   **Intervention Impact:** Can real-time stress forecasting using AdaptStress be effectively integrated into lifestyle interventions to demonstrate causal improvements in stress management outcomes?
*   **Activity Variability Source:** What physiological or behavioral mechanisms explain why activity features exhibit high inter-participant variability in their impact on stress predictions?
*   **Window Optimality Basis:** Is the empirically optimal 5-day history window and 1-day prediction horizon grounded in underlying temporal dynamics of stress physiology, or an artifact of the specific dataset and model?
*   **Contextual Integration:** How do non-physiological contextual factors (e.g., psychosocial stressors, environmental conditions) quantitatively enhance accuracy when integrated with wearable signals?

## Limitations
*   Small dataset (16 participants, 10–15 weeks each) limits generalizability and risks overfitting in window/hyperparameter selection.
*   Incomplete architectural details (exact Transformer layers, FFN size, dropout) and stress label derivation prevent faithful reproduction.
*   Selective TTA thresholds (`S_dist > 0.3`, improvement > 2%) appear arbitrary and may not generalize; SHAP on aggregated windows may mask temporal dynamics.

## Confidence
*   **Performance Superiority:** Medium – strong reported improvements but small sample and potential optimistic bias from in-dataset window tuning.
*   **Interpretability & Personalization:** Medium – SHAP provides plausible insights, but approximations and small N reduce reliability of feature consistency/direction claims.
*   **Cross-Participant Generalization:** Medium – mechanism is standard, but absence of ablation numbers in the summary weakens confidence in its actual contribution.
*   **Efficacy of Selective TTA:** Medium – concept is sound, yet arbitrary thresholds and lack of sensitivity analysis leave robustness uncertain.

## Next Checks
1.  **Ablation of Domain Adaptation:** Train AdaptStress without adversarial loss (`α=0`) and compare cross-participant performance to quantify the component's impact.
2.  **Threshold Sensitivity Analysis for TTA:** Vary `S_dist` and improvement thresholds widely; evaluate resulting performance to validate chosen defaults and identify robust operating boundaries.
3.  **Temporal SHAP Analysis:** Compute SHAP values at each timestep within the optimal 5-day window for multiple users; visualize temporal patterns to verify that personalized feature effects are genuine and not aggregation artifacts.
