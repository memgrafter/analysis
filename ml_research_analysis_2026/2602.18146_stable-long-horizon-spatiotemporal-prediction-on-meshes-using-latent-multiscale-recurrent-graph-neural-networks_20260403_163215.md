# Stable Long-Horizon Spatiotemporal Prediction on Meshes Using Latent Multiscale Recurrent Graph Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18146
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18146
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: LM-RGNN reduces MAE from 26.57°C to 6.50°C, MAPE from 6.59% to 2.58%, and mpIoU from 0.67 to 0.85 on long-horizon temperature prediction.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of long-horizon spatiotemporal prediction on complex meshes, focusing on temperature histories in additive manufacturing where errors accumulate over thousands of timesteps. They introduce the Latent Multiscale Recurrent Graph Neural Network (LM-RGNN), which decomposes the problem into two coupled models operating at different timescales: an interlayer model capturing slow heat diffusion between layers, and an intralayer model capturing fast laser-induced dynamics within a layer. Both models use a latent recurrent graph neural network with a variational graph autoencoder (VGAE) to compress the high-dimensional temperature fields into a compact latent representation. Evaluated on simulated powder bed fusion data, LM-RGNN significantly outperforms the Decoupled-RGNN baseline, reducing MAE from 26.57°C to 6.50°C, MAPE from 6.59% to 2.58%, and improving melt-pool IoU from 0.67 to 0.85. Spatial and temporal gradient errors drop by over 60%, while maintaining comparable memory usage and only a 0.97ms/timestep inference overhead. The framework demonstrates stable rollouts across diverse part geometries without degradation over long horizons.

## Method Summary
The task is to predict temperature fields over thousands of timesteps on 2D meshes derived from additive manufacturing simulations. Inputs consist of a mesh graph (node coordinates and fixed adjacency from quadrilateral elements), masks indicating active nodes and material (powder/solid), and global process parameters (laser position and power updates). The authors propose LM-RGNN, a two-stage architecture. First, a variational graph autoencoder (VGAE) with depth-3 pooling compresses full-resolution temperature fields into low-dimensional node features on a coarsened latent mesh (latent dimension 16). The VGAE is pre-trained on reconstruction using a β-VAE loss. Second, two coupled latent recurrent graph neural networks (Latent-RGNNs) operate at different temporal scales: the interlayer model predicts the temperature state at layer completion (slow diffusion dynamics), while the intralayer model predicts the within-layer evolution given the interlayer initial state (fast laser dynamics). Both models employ a GraphGRU cell that replaces linear GRU operations with message-passing graph neural networks, enabling direct modeling on the irregular mesh without grid interpolation. Training uses truncated backpropagation through time (TBPTT) with 8-step chunks. The interlayer model is trained on sequential layer predictions, while the intralayer model is trained on temporally subsampled within-layer sequences (1-in-7) and can be parallelized across layers. During inference, the interlayer model runs sequentially to produce starting latent states for each layer, then all intralayer models run in parallel; their outputs are batched and decoded by the fixed VGAE decoder to full resolution. The approach generalizes across variable part geometries and maintains stability over long rollouts.

## Key Results
- MAE reduced from 26.57°C to 6.50°C, MAPE from 6.59% to 2.58%, and mean maximum error from 170.9°C to 108.7°C.
- Melt-pool intersection over union improved from 0.67 to 0.85; spatial and temporal gradient errors dropped by over 60%.
- Stable rollouts across diverse geometries with only 0.97ms timestep inference overhead and comparable memory usage to baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Temporal Multiscale Decomposition
- **Claim**: Decomposing long-horizon prediction into coupled interlayer (slow diffusion) and intralayer (fast laser-induced) models improves stability and accuracy over thousands of time steps.
- **Mechanism**: The additive manufacturing process naturally separates into two temporal scales. The interlayer model predicts temperature at layer completion, capturing slow heat diffusion and cumulative energy effects. The intralayer model predicts the transient evolution within a layer, focusing on fast, localized laser dynamics. Both are independently trained latent-RGNNs.
- **Core assumption**: Fast and slow dynamics can be effectively decoupled without significant loss of accuracy, and error propagation between scales is manageable.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "temporal multiscale architecture composed of two coupled models operating at complementary time scales"
  - [section 4.6]: Detailed description of interlayer/intralayer split and training procedure
  - [corpus]: Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers like "Wavelet-Inspired Multiscale Graph Convolutional Recurrent Network" and "Interpretable learning of effective dynamics for multiscale systems" confirm multiscale modeling is active research, but not this specific decoupling approach.
- **Break condition**: Would fail if the physical system lacks clear temporal scale separation or if interlayer errors catastrophically contaminate intralayer predictions.

### Mechanism 2: Latent Space Compression via VGAE
- **Claim**: Using a variational graph autoencoder (VGAE) to operate on compact latent representations reduces memory consumption and stabilizes training of long-sequence recurrent models.
- **Mechanism**: The VGAE encodes full-resolution temperature fields into low-dimensional node features on a coarsened latent mesh (depth-3 hierarchy reduces nodes by 8×). The latent-RGNN evolves these compact states, drastically cutting memory for TBPTT while preserving key information through the decoder.
- **Core assumption**: Critical spatiotemporal dynamics are preserved in the latent space despite aggressive spatial compression.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "variational graph autoencoder provides a compact latent representation that reduces memory usage and improves training stability"
  - [section 4.4]: VGAE encoder/decoder architecture with hierarchical pooling
  - [corpus]: Neighbor "Mesh-based Super-resolution..." uses graph representations but not latent compression for temporal stability; limited direct corpus support.
- **Break condition**: Would fail if latent reconstruction error accumulates or if coarsening loses localized features like melt pool gradients.

### Mechanism 3: Graph-native Sequential Modeling
- **Claim**: Recurrent graph neural networks (RGNNs) with message passing directly on simulation meshes maintain geometric fidelity and enable generalization across variable part geometries.
- **Mechanism**: GraphGRU modules replace linear GRU operations with message-passing, allowing the model to learn complex spatiotemporal dependencies on irregular domains without interpolation to fixed grids. Mesh connectivity retains part boundary details crucial for thermal physics.
- **Core assumption**: Graph-based message passing is sufficiently expressive to approximate PDE-driven dynamics on arbitrary meshes.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4.1]: "operate directly on mesh-based data using GNNs, thereby avoiding interpolation onto a fixed snapshot grid, which would introduce additional approximation errors"
  - [section 4.3]: GraphGRU formulation with MPGNNs
  - [corpus]: Multiple neighbors (EAGLE, TGN) explore graph networks for physics, supporting viability but not specific design.
- **Break condition**: Would fail if message-passing depth is insufficient for long-range dependencies or if mesh connectivity poorly represents physics.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Pre-train VGAE (compression encoder/decoder) → Interlayer latent-RGNN (predicts end-of-layer latent states) → Intralayer latent-RGNN (predicts within-layer latent sequences) → Decoder batching (fixed VGAE decoder for full-resolution reconstruction)

### Critical Path
VGAE pre-training → interlayer training (sequential layer predictions) → intralayer training (parallelizable per layer given interlayer outputs) → full LM-RGNN rollout (interlayer sequential, intralayer parallel).

### Design Tradeoffs
- Latent dimension: 16 chosen balances accuracy and compression; higher dims improve MAE/MAPE but reduce memory benefit.
- TBPTT steps: Fixed at 8; longer sequences risk gradient issues, shorter reduce temporal context.
- Model separation: Two-stage training adds complexity but isolates timescales; monolithic full-sequence model fails.

### Failure Signatures
- Training instability: High variance in non-latent interlayer ablation (Table 1, 17.80±9.16 MAE)
- Memory overflow: Full-resolution models force tiny batch sizes (section 5.5)
- Temporal error growth: Baseline Decoupled-RGNN shows increasing t-MAE (Figure 6)
- Poor VGAE reconstruction: Low mpIoU (<0.85) would indicate latent information loss.

### First 3 Experiments

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Does the LM-RGNN framework generalize to three-dimensional geometries and to additive manufacturing processes with varying parameters (e.g., laser power, scan speed, material)?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors state: "the present study is limited to two-dimensional simulations and a single powder bed fusion process with fixed parameters."
- **Why unresolved**: The method was only evaluated on 2D data with fixed parameters; 3D and parameter variations introduce increased geometric complexity, larger state spaces, and potentially different dynamics that could affect stability and accuracy.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Train and test LM-RGNN on 3D simulation datasets with diverse process parameters; compare performance metrics (MAE, t-MAE) against baselines to assess generalization.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can LM-RGNN, trained exclusively on simulated data, reliably predict temperature fields on real-world additive manufacturing experiments?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "the models are trained exclusively on simulated data, and further investigation is needed to assess robustness to process variability and transferability to experimental measurements."
- **Why unresolved**: Simulations rely on idealized models (e.g., material properties, heat transfer coefficients) that may not perfectly match real conditions; domain shift could degrade performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Deploy LM-RGNN on experimental setups with measured temperature data (e.g., via infrared thermography or embedded sensors); quantify prediction errors and temporal stability.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Would a VGAE trained solely on interlayer temperature fields improve the accuracy of the interlayer model compared to using a VGAE trained on all temperature fields?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The authors note: "Training a dedicated VGAE on interlayer temperature fields could therefore further improve interlayer prediction accuracy," implying the current VGAE is general.
- **Why unresolved**: The current VGAE is trained on the full dataset including intralayer states; interlayer states have different statistical properties (cooling phases), so a specialized encoder might capture relevant features more effectively.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Train a separate VGAE on only interlayer temperature fields, integrate it into the LM-RGNN interlayer model, and compare key metrics (MAE, MME, t-MAE) with the original model.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: How does the latent dimensionality of the VGAE affect the trade-off between predictive accuracy (especially for spatial and temporal gradients) and computational efficiency (memory, inference time) in long-horizon LM-RGNN predictions?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper reports VGAE performance for latent dimensions 4,8,16, and selects 16 for best accuracy, but does not fully explore the impact on the full LM-RGNN's efficiency-accuracy trade-off.
- **Why unresolved**: The latent dimension influences compression ratio, information preservation, and downstream recurrent model performance; an optimal balance may differ for multiscale prediction.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Train LM-RGNN models with a range of VGAE latent dimensions; systematically evaluate accuracy metrics (MAE, MAE∇xy, MAE∇t) and resource usage (memory, inference time) across long rollouts.

## Limitations
- Baseline reimplementation details are sparse; exact architecture and hyperparameters for Decoupled-RGNN are unreported, making direct comparison uncertain.
- Graph edge feature encoding (lengths, unit vectors) and whether edges are updated during message passing are unspecified, hindering full reproducibility.
- VGAE coarsening node selection rule and unpooling assignment are not algorithmically defined, leaving implementation ambiguous.

## Confidence
- Performance improvement (MAE/MAPE reductions): Medium – Experimental evidence is strong, but baseline uncertainty prevents definitive verification.
- Temporal multiscale decomposition: Medium – Ablation shows benefit, yet cross-scale error propagation is not quantified.
- Latent compression via VGAE: High – Memory savings are clear; reconstruction metrics indicate adequate fidelity.
- Graph-native modeling for geometric fidelity: Medium – Generalization to variable geometries is shown, but causality to graph structure is not isolated (no grid-based ablation).
