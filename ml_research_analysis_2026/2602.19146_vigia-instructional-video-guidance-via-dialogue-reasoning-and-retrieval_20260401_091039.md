# VIGiA: Instructional Video Guidance via Dialogue Reasoning and Retrieval
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.19146
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19146
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: VIGiA achieves state-of-the-art performance, reaching 94.02% accuracy on plan-aware VQA, 55.66% ROUGE-L on visually-informed step generation, and 30.74% Recall@1 (m=0.5) on conversational video moment retrieval.

## Executive Summary
VIGiA tackles grounded, plan-aware dialogue for multi-step instructional videos by combining multimodal plan reasoning with plan-based video retrieval. Its architecture integrates a vision encoder (SigLIP), an LLM (LLaMa 3), and a learned MLP connector, augmented with special retrieval tokens and projection layers for moment localization. Trained through a four-stage procedure on the InstructionVidDial dataset and several pretraining corpora, the model consistently outperforms prior domain-specific and general-purpose models across plan-grounded QA, step generation, and conversational moment retrieval tasks.

## Method Summary
The system uses LLaMa 3 (8B) as the LLM, SigLIP SO400M as the vision encoder, and a two-layer MLP to bridge visual and language modalities. Special tokens `[RETs]` and `[RETe]` are predicted by the LLM to indicate start and end frames; their embeddings are projected via linear layers and compared against frame-level features (encoded by the vision encoder, transformed with `W_ret` and RoPE positional encoding) using an InfoNCE contrastive loss. Training proceeds in four stages: (1) initialization on image captioning and text-image retrieval (frozen LLM/VE), (2) visual instruction tuning on general VQA datasets (unfrozen LLM/VE, LoRA applied), (3) domain-specific adaptation on cooking/DIY video corpora for step classification and retrieval, and (4) task-specific multi-task tuning on InstructionVidDial (frozen VE). Multi-task batches balance objectives: plan-grounded answer generation (PGAG), plan-aware VQA (pVQA), visually-informed step generation (VSG), and conversational video moment retrieval (CVMR), using token-level NLL loss for generation and contrastive loss for retrieval.

## Key Results
- **Plan-aware VQA accuracy**: 94.02% (majority vote of three judge LLMs)
- **Visually-informed step generation ROUGE-L**: 55.66%
- **Conversational video moment retrieval Recall@1 (m=0.5)**: 30.74% ("adjusted" extraction with τ=0.35)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
VIGiA succeeds by explicitly coupling dialogue reasoning with plan-structured video grounding. The dedicated retrieval tokens allow the LLM to directly query video content at a fine-grained level, while the plan context keeps responses coherent with the instructional sequence. Multi-stage training first equips the model with general vision-language skills, then hones domain and task expertise without losing broader capabilities—a balance critical for handling both open-ended dialogue and precise moment retrieval. The contrastive retrieval objective, combined with generation losses, forces the model to align textual plan references with visual moments, leading to plan-adherent and visually grounded responses.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Video frames → SigLIP → `W_ret` + RoPE (temporal position) → frame embeddings (retrieval space). LLM input: [text history, plan, `<image>` tokens from MLP connector, special `[RETs]`/`[RETe]` tokens]. LLM output: generated text + `[RETs]`/`[RETe]` token embeddings → `W_start`/`W_end` → compare with frame embeddings via cosine similarity → predicted moment.  
**Critical path**: Visual features are encoded and temporally positioned, then aligned with LLM-predicted retrieval tokens; generation losses shape the LLM’s textual output, while retrieval loss aligns `[RETs]`/`[RETe]` with target video segments.  
**Design tradeoffs**: Freezing the vision encoder in Stage 4 preserves pretrained visual features but limits adaptation; LoRA enables efficient LLM tuning but may constrain expressiveness; dual dataloaders ensure task balance but introduce scheduling complexity.  
**Failure signatures**: Sharp MME score drop after Stage 4 indicates forgetting; CVMR performance sensitive to threshold τ; unbalanced task losses suggest multi-task interference; pVQA errors may stem from dataset noise (image-text mismatch, on-screen text).  
**Exactly 3 first experiments**:  
1. Implement the 2-layer MLP connector (hidden dim=4096, GELU) and apply RoPE to `W_ret` output using frame index; validate by measuring CVMR Recall@1 with/without RoPE.  
2. Ablate the N=5 frame-sampling strategy for retrieval targets (random vs. uniform from start/end segments) and compare Recall@1 using τ=0.35.  
3. Sweep τ from 0.1 to 0.5 on dev set for “adjusted” CVMR extraction; confirm performance collapse outside [0.3,0.4].

## Limitations
- **Architecture underspecification**: MLP connector dimensions and RoPE implementation details are inferred, risking replication gaps.
