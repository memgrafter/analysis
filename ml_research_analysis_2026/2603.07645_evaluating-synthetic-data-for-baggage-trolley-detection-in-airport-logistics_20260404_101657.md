# Evaluating Synthetic Data for Baggage Trolley Detection in Airport Logistics
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07645
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07645
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** Mixed training (synthetic data + 40% real annotations) matches or exceeds 100% real-data training, reducing annotation effort by 25–35% and achieving 0.94 mAP@50 and 0.77 mAP@50-95.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles automated baggage trolley detection in airport logistics, where privacy regulations restrict real data collection. It introduces a synthetic data pipeline using a high‑fidelity Digital Twin of Algiers International Airport (via NVIDIA Omniverse) to generate richly annotated oriented bounding box (OBB) data, including complex chained formations. The key finding is that a mixed training strategy—combining the full synthetic dataset with only 40% of real‑world annotations—achieves performance comparable to training on 100% real data (0.94 mAP@50, 0.77 mAP@50-95) while cutting annotation effort by 25–35%. Multi‑seed experiments confirm high reproducibility with negligible variance.

## Method Summary
The authors build two datasets: a real‑world set (1,504 frames, 14,080 OBBs) and a synthetic set (817 frames, 8,616 OBBs) rendered from a Digital Twin. A semi‑automated annotation pipeline—using a YOLO11‑obb proxy model for pre‑labeling followed by manual correction—produces consistent OBB labels in YOLO‑OBB format. Training employs YOLO26‑obb under five strategies: Real‑Only, Synthetic‑Only, Linear Probing (synthetic pretrain, freeze backbone), Full Fine‑Tuning (synthetic pretrain, unfreeze all), and Mixed Training (from scratch on combined data). By varying the percentage of real data (5%–50%) and applying standardized augmentations and hyperparameters, the study evaluates each approach on a held‑out real test set (200 frames) using mAP@50, mAP@50-95, Precision, and Recall. The mixed strategy at 40% real data yields the best trade‑off, matching the 100% real baseline.

## Key Results
- Mixed training (full synthetic + 40% real) achieves 0.94 mAP@50 and 0.77 mAP@50-95, matching 100% real‑data training.
- This reduces required real‑world annotation effort by 25–35%.
- Multi‑seed validation shows high reproducibility with standard deviation <0.01 on mAP@50.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper explains the success of mixed training through three mechanisms:

### Mechanism 1: Synthetic geometric prior for dense chain separation
Claim: Synthetic data provides a geometric prior that enables learning to separate densely overlapping, nested trolleys.
Mechanism: The Digital Twin generates high‑density “chained” formations (12–18 units) with oriented bounding boxes, forcing the model to learn rotation‑invariant features rather than relying on background context.
Core assumption: The geometric distribution of synthetic arrangements is representative of real deployments.
Evidence anchors: Abstract and Section III.B describe capturing complex trolley formations including tightly nested chains.
Break condition: If real chains exhibit new knot types beyond synthetic diversity, performance degrades without further domain randomization.

### Mechanism 2: Mixed training regularizes low‑data regimes
Claim: Synthetic data stabilizes training when real annotations are scarce by preventing overfitting.
Mechanism: Mixed training anchors the feature space with the synthetic distribution while real data fine‑tunes texture/lighting, preserving geometric structure when real samples are insufficient.
Core assumption: The synthetic texture gap is separable from geometry.
Evidence anchors: Section VI.B shows mixed training highest mAP in low data regimes (5%–30%); Section VII.C explains synthetic data acts as an “anchor.”
Break condition: If the synthetic geometric prior is misleading (e.g., wrong hinge mechanics), mixed training could converge to a worse local minimum than real‑only.

### Mechanism 3: Full fine‑tuning after sufficient real data
Claim: With ~50% real data, full fine‑tuning (unfreezing all layers) surpasses mixed training because real data can overwrite synthetic‑biased low‑level features.
Mechanism: Sufficient real samples allow complete adaptation to the target texture distribution.
Core assumption: The domain gap is primarily in low/mid‑level features, not high‑level geometry.
Evidence anchors: Section VI.B reports full fine‑tuning achieves highest recall (91.1%) at 50% real data; Section VIII.A attributes the residual gap to appearance factors.
Break condition: If real data at 50% still lacks extreme occlusion cases, the recall advantage may vanish.

## Foundational Learning
### Oriented Bounding Boxes (OBB) vs. Axis‑Aligned (AABB)
- **Why needed:** Trolleys in chains are diagonally oriented and nested; AABB introduces excessive overlap and background noise, making instance separation impossible. OBB aligns with the object’s principal axis.
- **Quick check:** For a chain of 3 trolleys at 45° angles overlapping, which annotation type would yield a lower average IoU between neighboring ground‑truth boxes?

### Sim2Real Transfer via Mixed Training
- **Why needed:** Direct synthetic‑only training fails (mAP50=0.416). Interleaving synthetic and real data during training is more effective than sequential pretraining/fine‑tuning in low‑data regimes.
- **Quick check:** Given 100 real images, would you allocate 60 to real‑only and 40 to mixed, or 40 real + 100 synthetic? What metric would you track to decide?

### Semi‑Automated OBB Annotation Pipeline
- **Why needed:** Manual OBB annotation is prohibitively slow for dense scenes. The human‑in‑the‑loop approach (manual seed → proxy model → pre‑label → correction) scales dataset creation while maintaining quality.
- **Quick check:** If your proxy model has 80% precision on a held‑out synthetic set, what is the maximum theoretical speedup over pure manual labeling for 10,000 frames?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Digital Twin Generator (NVIDIA Omniverse) → Annotation Engine (Roboflow + YOLO11‑obb proxy model) → Training Orchestrator (Ultralytics YOLO26‑obb with 5 strategies) → Evaluation Bench (200‑frame real test set)

### Critical Path
1. Generate synthetic dataset in Omniverse with phone‑view cameras matching real operational perspective.
2. Annotate real and synthetic sets with OBBs using semi‑automated pipeline.
3. Train baseline Real‑Only (100%) and Synthetic‑Only models to bound performance.
4. Run adaptation strategies (Linear Probing, Full Fine‑Tuning, Mixed) across real‑data percentages (5%–50%) with matched real‑only controls.
5. Perform multi‑seed validation (±3 seeds) on best mixed strategy (40%) to confirm stability.

### Design Tradeoffs
- *Mixed vs. Full Fine‑Tuning:* Mixed is more stable in low‑data regimes (<30% real) but may underutilize abundant real data (>50%). Full fine‑tuning risks catastrophic forgetting of geometric priors with scarce real samples.
- *OBB vs. AABB:* OBB provides precise separation in chains but requires 5 parameters per box (cx, cy, w, h, θ) vs. 4 for AABB, increasing annotation and compute cost marginally.
- *Synthetic Diversity vs. Realism:* Increasing synthetic scenario randomization improves coverage but may widen the texture gap, requiring more real data for adaptation.

### Failure Signatures
- Synthetic‑Only mAP50 < 0.5 → severe domain gap (texture/lighting mismatch).
- Linear Probing underperforming Full Fine‑Tuning by >0.05 mAP50 → backbone features not directly transferable.
- High variance (>0.02 mAP50) across seeds in Mixed Training at 5–10% real → synthetic data not sufficiently diverse to regularize.
- Degraded recall in dense chains → model struggles with extreme occlusion; check for missed trolleys in nested chains.

### First 3 Experiments
1. Establish baselines: Train Real‑Only (100% real) and Synthetic‑Only (100% synthetic) to quantify upper bound and raw Sim2Real gap.
2. Validate OBB necessity: Retrain Real‑Only baseline with converted AABB labels; expect significant mAP50 drop (>0.1) on chained trolleys due to overlap.
3. Test mixed training sweet spot: Run Mixed Training (Strategy C) at 40% real data; if mAP50 ≥ Real‑Only(40%) and variance <0.01, proceed to scale to 30% to find minimal effective real‑data budget.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: Can the synthetic data pipeline generalize to other airports with different trolley designs, lighting conditions, and terminal layouts?
- **Basis in paper:** Section VIII.A notes the Digital Twin is tailored to Algiers International Airport; zero‑shot generalization to other airports remains unverified.
- **Why unresolved:** The study only evaluated on data from Algiers Airport; no experiments have tested transfer to other environments.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Gather real‑world trolley datasets from multiple airports with diverse designs and lighting, and evaluate model performance; or create synthetic Digital Twins of other airports and conduct cross‑domain generalization studies.

### Open Question 2: Can unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) eliminate the need for any real‑world annotations while maintaining detection performance?
- **Basis in paper:** Section VIII.B states future work will explore UDA frameworks to theoretically eliminate the need for real‑world annotations entirely.
- **Why unresolved:** The current best approach (Mixed Training) still requires 40% of real data; UDA methods have not been implemented or evaluated.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement UDA techniques (e.g., adversarial feature alignment, CycleGAN) using only synthetic source data and unlabeled real target data, then compare detection metrics (mAP, Precision, Recall) against the mixed‑training baseline with varying labeled real fractions.

### Open Question 3: To what extent does improving synthetic realism (e.g., physically based rendering, accurate reflections) reduce the simulation‑to‑reality gap and improve model performance?
- **Basis in paper:** Section VIII.A explains the residual gap is dominated by appearance factors and the current rendering does not fully model high‑fidelity PBR phenomena.
- **Why unresolved:** The synthetic dataset was generated with a standard rendering engine; the impact of higher‑fidelity rendering on detection accuracy is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Generate a new synthetic dataset using a PBR‑capable renderer or ray‑tracing to model specular reflections and lens effects, train models on this dataset, and compare performance (especially synthetic‑only and mixed‑training) against the original synthetic data.

### Open Question 4: How can YOLO‑OBB models be optimized for real‑time deployment on edge devices (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson) without significant accuracy loss?
- **Basis in paper:** Section VIII.C proposes researching quantization and optimization for low‑power edge devices to realize a decentralized, privacy‑preserving, real‑time monitoring system.
- **Why unresolved:** Experiments were conducted on a high‑end NVIDIA H100; model size, inference speed, and memory usage on resource‑constrained hardware have not been measured.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply quantization‑aware training, network pruning, or knowledge distillation to compress the model; benchmark inference latency, throughput, and power consumption on edge platforms like Jetson Nano/TX2/Orin; report trade‑offs between accuracy (mAP, Precision, Recall) and resource efficiency.

## Limitations
- Synthetic data generation details (Omniverse scene parameters, randomization protocols) are not fully disclosed, hindering independent reproduction and domain gap analysis.
- Semi‑automated annotation pipeline specifics (proxy model training, correction interface, real subset splits) remain unclear, affecting dataset construction reproducibility.
- Hardware/software stack versions (CUDA, PyTorch, Ultralytics) are unspecified, risking implementation mismatches.

## Confidence
- Synthetic data captures dense trolley formations: **Medium** confidence (evidence includes example formations, but diversity metrics and real‑world representativeness are unverified).
- Mixed training achieves parity with 100% real data using only 40% real annotations: **High** confidence (supported by consistent multi‑seed experiments across data fractions and low variance).
- Annotation effort reduction of 25–35%: **Medium** confidence (derived from performance equivalence threshold; assumes constant per‑image annotation cost and does not account for potential quality differences).

## Next Checks
1. Release the Digital Twin scene and randomization scripts to allow the community to regenerate synthetic data, compare distributions, and assess domain gap quantitatively.
2. Ablate synthetic chain complexity: systematically vary synthetic chain length and occlusion levels during training to determine the minimal synthetic diversity required for robust mixed training, especially in low‑data regimes.
3. Test generalization on an external airport dataset: apply the mixed training pipeline (with 40% real from Algiers) to a different airport’s trolley data (e.g., HPPS) to evaluate transferability beyond the original context.
