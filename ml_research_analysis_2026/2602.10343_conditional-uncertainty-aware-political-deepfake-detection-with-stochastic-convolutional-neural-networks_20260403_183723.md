# Conditional Uncertainty-Aware Political Deepfake Detection with Stochastic Convolutional Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- *ArXiv ID*: 2602.10343
- *Source URL*: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10343
- *Reference count*: 40
- *Primary result*: Stochastic inference (especially single-pass T=1) improves calibration; uncertainty scores conditionally useful for risk stratification in high-confidence bands, but not as a universal trust signal.

## Executive Summary
Political deepfake detection demands high reliability, yet point-prediction detectors often produce overconfident outputs. This paper evaluates uncertainty-aware convolutional neural networks to enhance operational trust. Using a politically-filtered real-synthetic image dataset, fine-tuned ResNet-18 and EfficientNet-B4 are compared under deterministic and stochastic inference regimes. Results reveal near-perfect discrimination (ROC-AUC >0.99) but poor deterministic calibration (Expected Calibration Error up to 0.138). Single-pass stochastic inference (T=1) markedly improves calibration without sacrificing accuracy. Uncertainty metrics (predictive variance/entropy) do not globally rank errors but consistently stratify residual risk within the high-confidence band (0.85–0.95), where higher uncertainty predicts higher error. Under generator-disjoint out-of-distribution evaluation, ensembles modestly boost AUC for EfficientNet-B4 (Δ=0.010, p<0.05). The study concludes that uncertainty offers conditional value for risk‑aware triage but is not a universal remedy for detector unreliability.

## Method Summary
The authors fine‑tune ImageNet‑pretrained ResNet‑18 and EfficientNet-B4 on a politically filtered subset of the OpenFake dataset (4000 balanced images, split 2800/600/600). Training uses Adam (lr 1e‑4 for classification head, 1e‑5 for backbone), weight decay 1e‑4, and early stopping on validation negative log‑likelihood. Inference variants include deterministic (dropout off), single‑pass stochastic (dropout on, T=1), Monte Carlo dropout (T=20), temperature scaling (τ fitted on validation), and an ensemble (K=5 independently trained models). Evaluation covers discrimination (ROC‑AUC), calibration (Expected Calibration Error, Brier score, negative log‑likelihood), uncertainty‑error ranking (AUROC using predictive variance/entropy), and a confidence‑band sweep. Unknowns affecting replication include exact dropout placement/rate, political keyword matching rules, temperature scaling optimization details, and training data augmentations.

## Key Results
- Near‑perfect discrimination (ROC‑AUC >0.99) but poor deterministic calibration (ECE up to 0.138).
- Single‑pass stochastic inference (T=1) significantly improves calibration without sacrificing accuracy.
- Uncertainty scores (variance/entropy) fail to globally rank errors but consistently stratify residual risk within high‑confidence bands (0.85–0.95), with effect sizes up to 0.04.
- Generator‑disjoint OOD evaluation shows ensembles improve EfficientNet‑B4 AUC by 0.010 (p<0.05).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

#### Mechanism 1: Test‑time stochasticity via MC dropout (or single‑pass) improves calibration
**Claim**: Enabling dropout during inference smooths the model’s confidence landscape, reducing overconfidence on ambiguous inputs, and single‑pass (T=1) isolates this smoothing effect from Monte Carlo averaging.  
**Mechanism**: Injecting noise at inference regularizes the predictive distribution, making confidence estimates less extreme. This is an empirical smoothing effect rather than a full Bayesian posterior approximation.  
**Core assumption**: Predictive variance under dropout reflects an uncertainty signal whose calibration value stems from noise‑induced regularization.  
**Evidence**: [abstract] single‑pass improves calibration; [section 3.F] describes MC dropout variants; [section 4.G] reports T=1 yields lower ECE than deterministic for both backbones.  
**Break condition**: If dropout rate or architecture induces excessive variance that degrades accuracy or increases ECE (as seen in ResNet‑18 with high T or p in Tables VII, IX).

#### Mechanism 2: Uncertainty scores provide conditional operational value by stratifying residual risk within high‑confidence bands
**Claim**: Predictive variance/entropy correlates with error only when conditioned on narrow, high‑confidence intervals (e.g., 0.85–0.95), not globally.  
**Mechanism**: In low/mid‑confidence bands, uncertainty is largely a reparameterization of confidence; only in the high‑confidence regime does additional variance signal residual ambiguity.  
**Core assumption**: The utility of uncertainty is regime‑specific; it refines trust among already confident predictions rather than replacing confidence.  
**Evidence**: [section 4.J] shows significant ΔErr only in the 0.90 band; low/mid bands have near‑zero effect sizes with CIs overlapping zero. [section 5.C] states uncertainty stratifies risk only among predictions that would otherwise be treated as highly reliable.  
**Break condition**: If no narrow confidence band exhibits positive, statistically significant ΔErr across variance partitions; or if uncertainty negatively correlates with error in high‑confidence regimes.

#### Mechanism 3: High discrimination (AUC) does not guarantee probabilistic reliability; calibration metrics are essential
**Claim**: Models can achieve near‑perfect ROC‑AUC (>0.99) yet be poorly calibrated (ECE up to 0.138), undermining trust in high‑stakes decisions.  
**Mechanism**: AUC measures ranking separability; calibration measures confidence‑accuracy alignment. Deterministic models often suffer from overconfidence due to excessive likelihood optimization.  
**Core assumption**: Deployment in political contexts requires probabilistic reliability, not just binary separability.  
**Evidence**: [abstract] notes this contrast; [section 4.C] tables show high AUC alongside varying ECE; [section 5.A] states discriminative performance alone is insufficient.  
**Break condition**: If a model achieves both high AUC and low ECE deterministically, reducing the need for uncertainty‑aware adjustments.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:  
Political filtering -> Dataset construction -> Fine‑tuned CNN (ResNet‑18/EfficientNet‑B4) -> Inference variants (deterministic, stochastic T=1, MC T=20, temperature scaling, ensemble) -> Evaluation metrics (AUC, ECE, uncertainty‑error AUROC, confidence‑band sweep)

**Critical path**:  
OpenFake corpus → political keyword filter → fixed train/val/test splits → full fine‑tuning (Adam, lr 1e‑4/1e‑5) → validation NLL checkpointing → inference‑mode switching → score extraction → metric computation

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Resolution/normalization*: Used 380×380 with [0,1] scaling; ablations show ImageNet mean‑std often lowers ECE but may affect accuracy.
- *Stochasticity degree*: Dropout rate (p) and MC sample count (T) non‑monotonically affect ECE; higher p increases variance but may harm accuracy; T>1 does not guarantee better calibration than T=1.
- *Uncertainty metric*: Variance vs. entropy: both show similar conditional trends, but variance is direct from MC samples; entropy derived from mean probability.

**Failure signatures**:
- Poor deterministic calibration (ECE >0.1) despite high AUC.
- No significant ΔErr in the 0.90 confidence band across variance partitions.
- Large AUC drop (>0.05) in generator‑disjoint OOD evaluation.
- Temperature scaling increases ECE, indicating mis‑specified τ or overfitting.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Baseline reliability check: Train deterministic model, report AUC, accuracy, ECE, Brier on test. Expect high AUC (>0.99) but potentially high ECE (up to 0.138 for ResNet‑18).
2. Stochastic vs. deterministic calibration: Run T=1 and T=20 MC dropout on same checkpoint. Compare ECE/Brier to deterministic. Expect T=1 to reduce ECE relative to deterministic (e.g., EfficientNet‑B4: 0.0549 vs. 0.0674).
3. Confidence‑band sweep: Using MC T=20 predictions, compute ΔErr for four confidence bands ([0.55,0.65] to [0.85,0.95]) and four variance partitions (median/top‑bottom 40%/30%/20%). Expect significant positive ΔErr only in 0.90 band (Table V).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Q1**: Under controlled in‑distribution and generator‑disjoint evaluation, do uncertainty‑aware inference procedures improve reliability (calibration, uncertainty‑error correlation) relative to deterministic baselines? *Basis: Explicit central question; unresolved because conditional value only found in high‑confidence bands; precise regimes where uncertainty adds signal remain unmapped.*
- **Q2**: How robust are uncertainty estimates under distribution shifts beyond generator difference? *Basis: Limitations state evaluation restricted to in‑distribution and generator‑disjoint; unresolved because operational deployments face diverse shifts (e.g., unseen identities, platform processing).*
- **Q3**: Do more expressive Bayesian neural network variants yield better calibrated uncertainties than MC dropout/ensembles? *Basis: Paper notes more expressive BNNs unexplored; unresolved because MC dropout improvements may stem from noise‑induced smoothing rather than true epistemic uncertainty.*
- **Q4**: Can sequence‑aware uncertainty estimates improve error detection beyond image‑level uncertainty? *Basis: Limitations highlight uncertainty evaluated only at image level; unresolved because political misinformation often exploits temporal context.*

## Limitations
- Uncertainty signals only help within high‑confidence bands (0.85–0.95), not globally, limiting operational utility.
- Deterministic calibration remains poor (ECE up to 0.138), risking overconfident errors.
- Evaluation is restricted to in‑distribution and generator‑disjoint splits; robustness to other distribution shifts (e.g., unseen identities, adversarial perturbations) remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Medium**: T=1 calibration improvement (strong evidence but dependent on dropout implementation details).
- **High**: Conditional uncertainty utility (robust confidence‑band effects with statistical significance).
- **High**: High AUC does not imply reliability (clear empirical contrast across metrics).
