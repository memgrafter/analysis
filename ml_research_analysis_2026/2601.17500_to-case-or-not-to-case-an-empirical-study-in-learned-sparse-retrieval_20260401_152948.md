# To Case or Not to Case: An Empirical Study in Learned Sparse Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17500  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17500  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Cased backbone models underperform uncased in learned sparse retrieval without lowercasing pre-processing; applying lowercasing eliminates the gap. Post-processing to an uncased vocabulary halves FLOPs with minimal accuracy loss.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the impact of backbone model casing on Learned Sparse Retrieval (LSR). Using SPLADE with BERT and DistilBERT in cased and uncased variants, the authors find that cased models without pre-processing underperform uncased models by approximately 5% relative on MSMARCO. Applying lowercasing to cased inputs closes this performance gap. Additionally, restricting the output vocabulary to uncased tokens reduces computational cost (FLOPs) by nearly 50% while incurring only a minor accuracy drop (∼0.2% MRR@10). In zero-shot evaluation on BEIR, uncased models are more robust, leading on 9 of 14 datasets, while cased models with lowercasing remain competitive. The study highlights the sensitivity of LSR to casing alignment and proposes practical efficiency improvements.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate SPLADE, a learned sparse retrieval framework that produces term-weighted sparse vectors via masked language modeling logits. Experiments use BERT-base and DistilBERT-base in cased and uncased forms, trained on the MSMARCO passage collection. Input pre-processing conditions are: no lowercasing, or lowercasing applied to all text before tokenization. Post-processing strategies include no restriction, limiting outputs to uncased vocabulary, and a cased regularizer during training. Retrieval effectiveness is measured via MRR@10, nDCG@10, and R@1000 on in-domain (MSMARCO, TREC DL) and zero-shot BEIR benchmarks. Efficiency is assessed using FLOPs (Equation 5), which estimates the number of operations needed to aggregate sparse vectors. The implementation builds on the official SPLADE repository and uses SPLADE-v2 hyperparameters, teacher-student distillation (Margin-MSE loss), and FLOPs regularization.

## Key Results
- Cased models without lowercasing underperform uncased counterparts by 4–5% MRR@10 on MSMARCO.
- Lowercasing pre-processing applied to cased models eliminates the performance gap (≤0.3 points).
- Post‑processing to retain only uncased vocabulary reduces FLOPs by nearly 50% with a marginal MRR@10 loss (<0.2 points).
- Uncased models demonstrate stronger zero‑shot robustness on BEIR, leading on 9 of 14 datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Casing of the backbone model vocabulary introduces a mismatch with typical IR query distributions, degrading LSR performance.  
- **Mechanism:** Cased models maintain separate embeddings for capitalized and lowercased tokens (e.g., `Apple` vs. `apple`). MSMARCO queries are predominantly lowercased. When a lowercased query token is processed by a cased model, the model must map it to a vocabulary entry. Due to the vocabulary's cased/uncased split and the model's learned parameters, this mapping may be suboptimal, leading to less effective term weighting and sparse vector construction. The mismatch increases lexical sparsity and reduces overlap between query and document representations.  
- **Core assumption:** The retrieval effectiveness in LSR is sensitive to the alignment between the input text casing and the backbone model's token vocabulary. A misalignment causes information loss during the token-to-vocabulary projection.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "cased models without pre-processing underperform uncased counterparts by approximately 5% relative on MSMARCO"  
  - [section 5.1, RQ1] "cased models without pre-processing underperform their uncased counterparts by approximately 4–5% MRR@10"  
  - [corpus] Neighbors focus on LSR adaptations (long docs, multimodal) but not casing; evidence is primarily from this paper's empirical analysis.  
- **Break condition:** If the query and document corpora have consistent, natural casing that matches the cased vocabulary distribution, the mismatch may not occur. Or if the model is explicitly trained to handle casing mismatches.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Applying lowercasing as pre-processing to cased models eliminates the performance gap with uncased models by forcing the model to operate within the uncased sub‑space of its vocabulary.  
- **Mechanism:** Lowercasing all input text ensures every token fed to the tokenizer corresponds to an uncased vocabulary entry. During fine‑tuning, the cased model's output layers and internal representations adapt to this constrained input space. The token-level analysis (Figure 2) shows that with lowercasing, cased models suppress cased vocabulary items (<1% usage) and behave as if they were uncased models, thus avoiding the vocabulary mismatch penalty.  
- **Core assumption:** A cased model's learned parameters are sufficiently flexible to remap its output distribution when presented with a consistent, lowercased input stream, effectively ignoring the cased dimensions that would otherwise introduce noise.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "applying lowercasing to cased inputs eliminates this performance gap"  
  - [section 5.1, RQ2] "lowercasing pre-processing substantially mitigates the negative impact of casing... reducing the gap to the uncased model to less than 0.3 points"  
  - [section 5.2] "when input lowercasing is applied... both BERT and DistilBERT entirely limit the use of cased tokens to less than 1%"  
- **Break condition:** If the pre‑training of the cased model created rigid, casing‑specific representations that cannot be repurposed via fine‑tuning on lowercased text. The paper's evidence suggests this does not happen.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Post‑processing strategies that restrict the output vocabulary to uncased tokens improve computational efficiency (FLOPs) at a small cost to accuracy.  
- **Mechanism:** By zeroing out logits corresponding to cased vocabulary dimensions during inference, the resulting sparse vector has a significantly lower number of non‑zero entries. This reduces the number of floating‑point operations required for indexing and search (measured as FLOPs). The minor accuracy loss (~0.2% MRR@10) suggests that the cased dimensions contribute little marginal signal for the MSMARCO task, as the model primarily activates uncased terms anyway.  
- **Core assumption:** The effectiveness of LSR for a given task is not critically dependent on the rare activation of cased vocabulary items; their removal simplifies the representation without substantial semantic loss.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "Post‑processing strategies, such as restricting outputs to uncased vocabulary, reduce computational cost (FLOPs) by nearly 50% but with a minor accuracy trade‑off"  
  - [section 5.1, RQ3] "restricting logits reduces FLOPs by nearly 50% (4.1→2.8) with a marginal MRR@10 decrease (<0.2 points)"  
  - [Table 1] Comparing "None" vs. "Uncased Voc. only" post‑processing shows FLOPs drop and stable MRR@10.  
- **Break condition:** For domains where casing is semantically critical (e.g., distinguishing `Apple` the company from `apple` the fruit), discarding cased dimensions could cause significant accuracy degradation.

## Foundational Learning

  *Why needed:* LSR is the core framework being studied; it generates sparse vectors over a model’s vocabulary, making casing decisions impactful.  
  *Quick check:* What is the key difference between LSR and BM25 in term weighting? (Answer: LSR learns weights via neural training; BM25 uses corpus statistics like TF‑IDF.)

- **Vocabulary Mismatch**  
  *Why needed:* The paper’s central mechanism revolves around misalignment between input casing and the backbone model’s token vocabulary, a classic IR problem in neural sparse representations.  
  *Quick check:* Give an example of a vocabulary mismatch that could occur with a cased model on a lowercased query. (Answer: Query token “apple” may not cleanly map to the intended “Apple” if that only exists as a cased token in the vocabulary.)

- **Transformer Tokenization (WordPiece/BPE)**  
  *Why needed:* Casing effects are mediated by the tokenizer; cased and uncased models have different tokenization rules and vocabulary sets, defining the output space of the LSR head.  
  *Quick check:* How does the WordPiece tokenizer in a cased BERT model differ from its uncased counterpart? (Answer: The cased vocabulary contains separate tokens for capitalized forms (e.g., `Apple`, `##apple`), while the uncased vocabulary merges them into a single `apple` token.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Raw Text -> Pre‑processor (optional lowercasing) -> Tokenizer (cased/uncased) -> Backbone LM (BERT/DistilBERT) -> SPLADE Head (MLM logits over |V|) -> Post‑processor (optional uncased‑vocab filter) -> Sparse Vector (|V|) -> Inverted Index / Scoring

- **Critical path:** The casing decision impacts two key points: (1) Tokenizer input casing determines which vocabulary entries are triggered. (2) Final logit space (the |V| dimension) includes both cased and uncased tokens in cased models. The path from tokenized input through the backbone to the MLM head logits is where the casing signal propagates.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Accuracy vs. Efficiency: Using the full cased vocabulary may marginally benefit case‑sensitive tasks but increases FLOPs and index size. Restricting to uncased vocabulary halves FLOPs with minimal accuracy loss on standard benchmarks.
  - Pre‑training vs. Fine‑tuning: Leveraging a strong cased pre‑trained model (e.g., ModernBERT) is desirable, but requires lowercasing pre‑processing to match IR data characteristics. This decouples the benefits of pre‑training from the inefficiency of a large cased vocabulary.
  - Simplicity vs. Performance: The simplest approach is to use an uncased model directly. Using a cased model with lowercasing adds a pre‑processing step but allows access to potentially stronger architectures only released in cased form.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Unexpectedly low recall on a dataset with many proper nouns when using a cased model without lowercasing or when aggressively pruning cased dimensions.
  - High FLOPs (inefficient inference) when using a cased model without post‑processing on a resource‑constrained system.
  - Inconsistent zero‑shot performance across BEIR datasets if casing strategy is not aligned with target corpus characteristics (e.g., Touché‑2020 anomaly where cased‑no‑preprocess won).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Gap Replication:** Train SPLADE with `bert-base-cased` and `bert-base-uncased` on MSMARCO with no pre‑processing. Expect ~5% relative MRR@10 gap (cased worse). Validates RQ1.
  2. **Pre‑processing Ablation:** Take the trained cased model from Exp. 1, apply lowercasing only during *inference* (no re‑training). Expect partial recovery. Then, re‑train the cased model from scratch with lowercasing as a *fixed pre‑processing step*. Expect near‑parity with uncased model. Validates RQ2.
  3. **Post‑processing Efficiency Test:** Take the best performing cased‑lowercasing model from Exp. 2. During indexing/retrieval, zero out all logits corresponding to cased vocabulary tokens (using a hard‑coded list or heuristic). Measure FLOPs reduction and delta in nDCG@10 on MSMARCO Dev. Expect ~50% FLOPs reduction and <0.2 point accuracy drop. Validates RQ3.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **ModernBERT Applicability:** Does the observed performance gap between cased and uncased LSR models persist with modern encoder architectures like ModernBERT, which are only released in cased form? The paper notes this as future work.
2. **Role of Rare Anchor Tokens:** What is the functional role of the rare anchor tokens (e.g., `R` and `##R`) that cased BERT activates under lowercasing, and do they represent a meaningful specialization or an artifact of the vocabulary? The paper identifies this anomaly but does not explain it.
3. **Uncased Robustness Mechanism:** What is the underlying mechanism that makes uncased backbone models more robust for zero‑shot transfer across diverse BEIR datasets compared to cased models, even when lowercasing is applied? The paper documents the phenomenon but not its cause.
4. **Beyond Vocabulary‑Dependent Post‑processing:** Can the efficiency‑accuracy trade‑off imposed by post‑processing (e.g., restricting logits to uncased vocabulary) be improved with methods that do not require explicit knowledge of the backbone model’s vocabulary or add significant implementation complexity? The paper calls for a more general solution.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Key training details (exact hyperparameters, teacher model identity, precise post‑processing logic) are inherited from SPLADE‑v2 without explicit specification and must be sourced from external code.
- **FLOPs calculation nuance:** The method for estimating activation probability `a_i` (Equation 5) is not detailed, which may affect the reported ~50% reduction claim.
- **Scope limited to BERT/DistilBERT:** All experiments use these architectures; findings may not generalize to newer (e.g., ModernBERT), larger, or differently pre‑trained models.
