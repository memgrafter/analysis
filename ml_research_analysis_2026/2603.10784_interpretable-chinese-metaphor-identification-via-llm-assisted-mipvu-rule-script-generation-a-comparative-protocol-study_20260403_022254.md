# Interpretable Chinese Metaphor Identification via LLM-Assisted MIPVU Rule Script Generation: A Comparative Protocol Study

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10784
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10784
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** Four linguistically grounded metaphor identification protocols are implemented as deterministic, auditable rule scripts; protocol choice is the dominant source of variation, with MIP vs. Simile showing near-zero agreement (κ=0.001).

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the interpretability gap in Chinese metaphor identification by operationalizing four established protocols (MIP/MIPVU, CMDAG, Emotion, Simile) as deterministic, human-auditable rule scripts. Using a five-module pipeline with constrained LLM calls, the system processes segmented Chinese text without model training. Evaluated on seven Chinese datasets, the research finds that the choice of identification protocol drives performance far more than model-level variations, with extreme divergence between some protocol pairs (e.g., MIP vs. Simile, κ=0.001) while others agree nearly perfectly (CMDAG vs. Emotion, κ=0.986). All protocols achieve full deterministic reproducibility, and the architecture provides structured rationales for every decision, enabling transparent auditability.

## Method Summary
The core method is a fixed five-module pipeline: Preprocessing (segmentation), Candidate Selection (filtering), Semantic Analysis (LLM-driven extraction of protocol-specific elements like contextual/basic meanings or tenor/vehicle), Classification (deterministic rule application), and Rationale Generation (structured JSON output). The LLM (GPT-4, temperature=0) acts as a constrained subroutine within each protocol's logic, which is encoded in the pipeline configuration. No model training occurs; the "script" consists of the prompts, parsing rules, and decision thresholds. Evaluation uses seven Chinese datasets covering token-, sentence-, and span-level annotations, measuring within-protocol F1/accuracy, cross-protocol Cohen's κ on a common subset, and interpretability metrics (rationale correctness, determinism, editability).

## Key Results
- Protocol A (MIP) achieves a token-level F1-score of 0.472 on PSU CMC.
- Cross-protocol analysis reveals extreme divergence: pairwise Cohen's κ between MIP and Simile is 0.001, while CMDAG and Emotion show near-perfect agreement at 0.986.
- All protocols achieve 100% deterministic reproducibility, with rationale correctness ranging from 0.40 to 0.87 and editability from 0.80 to 1.00.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The architecture succeeds by strictly delimiting the LLM's role to structured, protocol-specific extraction tasks within a fully deterministic pipeline. The LLM is prompted to output only predefined JSON fields (e.g., contextual meaning, basic meaning, tenor, vehicle). These outputs are then processed by unambiguous, hard-coded decision rules that classify each candidate as metaphorical or literal. This design ensures every step is auditable: the rationale for each decision is the combination of the extracted elements and the applied rule. The protocol logic, not the LLM's internal weights, governs outcomes, making the system transparent and reproducible. Variation across protocols stems from their underlying linguistic definitions of metaphor, not from stochastic model behavior.

## Foundational Learning
- **Deterministic Rule Scripts:** Why needed: To eliminate stochasticity and enable perfect reproducibility. Quick check: Can the entire pipeline be re-run on identical input to produce byte-for-byte identical output?
- **LLM as Structured Subroutine:** Why needed: Leverages LLM knowledge while constraining its output to a fixed schema, preventing free-form generation that would break determinism. Quick check: Does the LLM prompt always force valid JSON with exactly the required keys?
- **Cross-Protocol Agreement Metrics:** Why needed: To quantify how much different theoretical definitions of metaphor overlap in practice. Quick check: Are Cohen's κ values computed on exactly the same text units after proper gold-label conversion?
- **Chinese Tokenization for MIP:** Why needed: Protocol A (MIP) operates at the token level, requiring accurate word segmentation. Quick check: Is the same segmentation tool (e.g., `jieba`) used consistently for all Protocol A runs?
- **Rationale Auditability:** Why needed: Allows humans to verify if a decision aligns with the protocol's logic. Quick check: For a sampled decision, can an annotator trace the classification from the LLM's extracted meaning to the final rule?
- **Protocol-Specific Logic Encoding:** Why needed: Each protocol (MIP, CMDAG, Emotion, Simile) has distinct theoretical criteria that must be translated into unambiguous computational steps. Quick check: Are the decision rules for each protocol mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive for their defined candidate set?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Preprocessing -> Candidate Selection -> Semantic Analysis -> Classification -> Rationale Generation

**Critical Path:** The sequential flow is rigid: segmentation must precede candidate selection; selected candidates are fed one-by-one to the Semantic Analysis module; its structured output is the sole input to the deterministic Classification rule; the Classification verdict and supporting data are packaged into the final Rationale. Any failure in an earlier module (e.g., missed candidate) propagates forward and cannot be corrected later.

**Design Tradeoffs:** 
- *Interpretability vs. Performance:* Rigid rules ensure transparency but limit recall compared to end-to-end neural models.
- *LLM Reliability vs. Determinism:* Using `temperature=0` and strict JSON prompts maximizes determinism but may fail if the LLM deviates from the format, requiring robust parsing.
- *Protocol Generality vs. Specificity:* The architecture is protocol-agnostic in structure but requires manual translation of each protocol's theory into the Semantic Analysis prompt and Classification rule, which is non-trivial and influences outcomes.

**Failure Signatures:** 
- Parsing errors: LLM output is not valid JSON or lacks expected keys.
- Incorrect extraction: Semantic Analysis returns an inaccurate `basic_meaning` or fails to identify a tenor/vehicle.
- Rule violation: Classification logic produces an output not defined by the protocol (e.g., labeling a token as metaphor when the comparison is within the same domain for Simile).
- Non-determinism: Two identical runs yield different outputs, indicating API variance or reliance on unsorted data structures.

**First Three Experiments:**
1. **Prompt-Output Validation:** Run the Semantic Analysis module on 10 hand-picked sentences for each protocol. Verify every LLM response matches the required JSON schema and contains plausible, protocol-relevant content.
2. **Single-Sentence Cross-Protocol:** Apply all four protocols to the same 5 sentences. Manually compare the generated rationales and final labels to understand why protocols disagree (e.g., MIP flags a word as metaphorical while Simile does not).
3. **Determinism Stress Test:** Execute the full pipeline on a 100-sentence subset twice using identical seeds and API parameters. Compute a cryptographic hash of the complete output directory for each run; they must be identical.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Do the observed cross-protocol divergences generalize beyond the MIPVU-annotated PSU CMC dataset?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "the cross-protocol comparison is conducted on PSU CMC, which was annotated following MIPVU guidelines—this inherently advantages Protocol A and may not generalise to data annotated under other schemes."
- **Why unresolved:** All cross-protocol evaluations used PSU CMC, so the extreme protocol disagreements might be an artefact of the MIPVU gold standard rather than a universal property of metaphor identification.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the near-perfect agreement between conceptual-mapping (Protocol B) and emotion-based (Protocol C) protocols reflect a genuine cognitive overlap in metaphor processing or merely an LLM reasoning artifact?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "The near-perfect agreement between Protocols B and C... may be an artefact of the LLM’s reasoning patterns—further investigation with human annotators is needed."
- **Why unresolved:** The agreement (κ=0.986) was computed solely from LLM-generated outputs; it is unclear whether human annotators applying these protocols would show the same convergence.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can ensemble methods that combine decisions from multiple protocols achieve broader metaphor coverage without severely compromising precision?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "ensemble protocols that combine decisions from multiple identification procedures could potentially achieve both higher coverage and maintained precision."
- **Why unresolved:** The study evaluates protocols only in isolation; the proposed ensembles remain speculative and untested.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Are the findings on protocol-driven variation generalizable to other languages and figurative language phenomena?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "extending the cross-protocol comparison to additional languages would test the generality of our findings about protocol-driven variation."
- **Why unresolved:** All experiments were conducted on Chinese data; languages with different morphological cues may exhibit different protocol agreement patterns.

## Limitations
- Exact LLM prompts, curated marker lists (e.g., 15 simile indicators), and precise deterministic rule implementations are not disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- The claimed 100% determinism is fragile, relying on perfect LLM prompt adherence and zero API variance; minor deviations would break it.
- Cross-protocol Cohen's κ values are computed on a specific common subset with gold-label conversion from token to sentence level; alternative subsets or conversion methods might yield different agreement scores.
- Interpretability metrics (rationale correctness 0.40–0.87) are based on limited manual sampling (50 decisions per protocol) and subjective judgment, lacking statistical confidence intervals.

## Confidence
- **Full deterministic reproducibility:** Medium — Minor prompt or parsing deviations could break determinism.
- **Extreme cross-protocol divergence:** Medium — Re-evaluation with alternative data subsets or conversion methods might alter κ values.
- **Interpretability audit metrics (rationale correctness, editability):** Low — Human audit variability and sampling bias limit reliability.

## Next Checks

2. Independent human audit: Evaluate ≥200 decisions per protocol via multiple annotators; compute rationale correctness with confidence intervals and inter-annotator agreement.
3. Generalizability test: Apply protocols to a new, independently annotated Chinese metaphor corpus to see if cross-protocol divergence pattern persists.
