# Can an Actor-Critic Optimization Framework Improve Analog Design Optimization?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24714
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24714
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** ACOF improves average top-10 figure of merit by 38.9% and reduces regret by 24.7% compared to the strongest baseline, with peak gains of 70.5% in FoM and 42.2% lower regret.

## Executive Summary
Analog circuit sizing is hampered by expensive SPICE simulations and a vast, sparsely populated search space. Existing optimizers lack human-like judgment to direct search efficiently. This paper introduces the Actor-Critic Optimization Framework (ACOF), which separates proposal (an actor suggests promising design regions) from evaluation (a critic audits and refines proposals before simulation). Across four benchmark amplifiers, ACOF significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines: it boosts the figure of merit, lowers regret, achieves higher physical feasibility, and explores more productively. The gains are especially pronounced on larger, more challenging circuits (21 parameters), demonstrating the value of mimicking a designer’s propose‑then‑audit workflow.

## Method Summary
ACOF iterates in rounds. Each round starts with an LLM‑based actor that, given the circuit topology and a summary of previous results, proposes a search region (parameter subranges). An LLM‑based critic then audits this proposal: it enforces PDK legality, corrects any invalid bounds, and adjusts ranges to balance exploitation (tightening around high‑performing areas) and exploration (widening when progress stalls). Within the vetted region, a region‑constrained Bayesian optimizer (GP‑based) samples candidate designs, which are evaluated via Ngspice simulations. After simulating a batch (e.g., 100 points), a summarizer compresses the round data—including best designs and the critic’s reflective memo—into a structured summary that conditions the next actor proposal. The process begins with an initial calibration set generated by pure BO. The framework is agnostic to the BO backend and LLM choice, though stronger LLMs yield better outcomes.

## Key Results
- Average top‑10 figure of merit improved by 38.9%; regret reduced by 24.7% versus the strongest baseline.
- Peak gains: 70.5% FoM improvement and 42.2% lower regret on the 21‑parameter folded‑cascode amplifier.
- Physical feasibility rates consistently higher (e.g., 85.6% vs. 74.6% for Single‑LLM and 19.3% for Pure‑BO on the 180 nm 21‑parameter benchmark), indicating fewer wasted simulations on invalid designs.
- More productive exploration: ACOF discovers a larger number of distinct high‑performing regions via clustering.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Separation of proposal and evaluation**  
The actor‑critic split introduces designer‑like judgment, stabilizing search in high‑dimensional, sparse spaces. The actor proposes a region based on historical evidence (round summary), while the critic audits this proposal *before* simulation, enforcing PDK/legal constraints and adjusting ranges to balance exploitation (tightening around high‑performing areas) and exploration (widening when progress stalls). This pre‑simulation gatekeeping diverts the budget from invalid or low‑potential regions. Evidence: The abstract and Section III describe this separation; the framework outperforms Single‑LLM (which lacks critic) especially in physical feasibility. Break condition: If the critic’s adjustments are consistently poor (e.g., over‑constraining), the separation becomes detrimental.

**Mechanism 2: Minimal, legality‑preserving repairs**  
The critic corrects the actor’s proposed ranges to maintain strategic intent while ensuring simulation validity. It converts the actor’s tentative ranges into a corrected region that prunes large swaths of invalid or low‑yield points. For example, the critic might tighten R6 from 20k‑35k to 21.5k‑27k based on past round patterns. This correction occurs before BO sampling, ensuring every simulation occurs inside a vetted region. Evidence: Section III and Table I show ACOF’s higher physical feasibility rates (e.g., 85.6% vs. 74.6% Single‑LLM). Break condition: If actor proposals are already highly legal and well‑targeted (simpler circuits), the critic’s overhead may reduce exploration speed.

**Mechanism 3: Iterative reflection with memo**  
Round summaries that include the critic’s reflective memo create persistent memory that guides long‑horizon direction, preventing cycling or premature convergence. After each BO round, the summarizer condenses data (best designs, stats, and the critic’s memo on patterns like stall signals) into a summary that conditions the next actor proposal. This explicit feedback loop accumulates design insights across rounds. Evidence: Section III describes the memo’s role; Section II.C references Reflexion and LEDRO. Break condition: If the summarization fails to extract meaningful, generalizable patterns, the actor receives no actionable insight and the system degrades to a memory‑less version.

## Foundational Learning

1. **Bayesian Optimization (BO) with region constraints**  
   *Why needed:* BO is the core search engine inside the critic‑approved region; understanding how constraining the sampling domain affects the exploration‑exploitation balance is key to seeing why ACOF’s region proposal matters.  
   *Quick check:* How does constraining BO’s sampling domain to a smaller region affect the balance between exploration (high uncertainty) and exploitation (high predicted FoM)?

2. **LLM prompt engineering for structured output**  
   *Why needed:* The actor and critic are LLMs prompted to output numeric ranges and audit memos, not free text. The system’s effectiveness hinges on the prompt’s ability to elicit consistent, interpretable, and legally bounded parameter suggestions and critiques.  
   *Quick check:* What are the risks if the actor’s prompt fails to include the round summary’s key trade‑off insights, or the critic’s prompt lacks clear PDK constraints?

3. **Analog circuit performance metrics and physical feasibility**  
   *Why needed:* The FoM combines Gain, UGBW, Phase Margin, and Power with trade‑offs; the critic’s feasibility checks (positive bandwidth, PM, power; non‑negative gain) are domain‑specific sanity checks.  
   *Quick check:* Why might a design that meets all numerical FoM component targets still be physically infeasible (e.g., due to transistor operating region mismatches not captured by the FoM)?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Actor (LLM) -> Critic (LLM + rule check) -> Region‑constrained BO -> Simulator (Ngspice) -> Round Summarizer -> (feedback to Actor)

**Critical path:**  
Sequential: Actor generates proposal → Critic audits and outputs corrected region → BO samples within that region → Simulator evaluates → Summarizer creates summary → feeds back to Actor.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *LLM choice vs. cost:* Stronger LLMs (GPT‑5 vs. Qwen) improve outcomes but increase inference cost/latency; critic must be simple enough for smaller models.  
- *Region tightness:* Over‑tightening (critic over‑exploits) risks missing global optima; over‑loosening (actor too broad) wastes budget. Reflection memo is the primary control.  
- *BO backend agnosticism:* ACOF works with any BO, but hyperparameters (GP kernel, acquisition) interact with region boundaries.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Critic overrides >50% of actor ranges → actor proposals misaligned with domain; prompt needs redesign.  
- Simulation validity rate drops in ACOF vs. Pure‑BO → critic’s corrections introduce hidden infeasibilities.  
- Regret metric worsens over rounds → reflection memo not capturing stall signals; actor keeps proposing similar regions.  
- Very low number of distinct regions → either actor stuck or critic over‑constraining; check memo for “explore more” triggers.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Sanity check:* Run ACOF on a trivial 1‑parameter circuit (e.g., resistor value). Verify actor proposes a range, critic accepts/rejects, BO finds optimum within that range. Confirm final FoM beats random search.  
2. *Critic override test:* Force actor to propose a deliberately illegal region (e.g., width < min PDK). Confirm critic fully repairs it to legal bounds and BO finds valid points. Measure if repair changes strategic direction (range center).  
3. *Ablation:* Replace critic with a pass‑through (no audit) while keeping actor. Compare FoM/regret/physical feasibility to full ACOF and Pure‑BO. This isolates the value of the audit step.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Scalability to high‑dimensional spaces:** How does ACOF perform with >50 parameters or more complex, densely coupled topologies? The paper tests only up to 21 parameters; the curse of dimensionality may degrade both BO and LLM reasoning about multi‑variable ranges.
2. **Dependence on critic’s prompting and heuristic rules:** The critic uses hand‑crafted adjustments; can a more formal or learnable critique policy (e.g., trained classifier) yield further gains? The current separation’s effectiveness hinges on the critic’s judgment.
3. **Quantitative contribution of the reflection memo:** What is the impact of the iterative summary `en` on long‑horizon stability and convergence, and what is its optimal structure? No ablation isolates this component.
4. **Separation vs. unified LLM agent:** Is the actor‑critic split fundamentally better than a single, more powerful LLM that internally reasons and self‑corrects? The trade‑offs in cost, latency, and reliability need analysis.
5. **Transferability of critic strategies:** Can region adjustment strategies learned on one circuit transfer to another (within same or different technology nodes)? The framework currently treats each circuit independently after shared initialization.

## Limitations
- Prompt/implementation details missing: exact LLM prompts, output parsing, and critic rule‑set are unspecified, risking reproduction drift.
- BO configuration sensitivity: kernel choice, acquisition function hyperparameters, and historical data weighting are omitted; these can strongly affect sample efficiency.
- Generalization scope limited: benchmarks are only four circuits (12–21 parameters) in SKY130/180nm; applicability to larger/mixed‑signal or advanced nodes is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Separation mechanism:** Medium confidence (conceptual + comparative results; no ablation isolating propose vs audit).
- **Critic repair improves feasibility:** High confidence (Table I shows consistent validity/feasibility gains over baselines).
- **Reflection memo aids long‑horizon search:** Medium confidence (inferred from design; no direct experiment varying memo content).

## Next Checks
1. Prompt archaeology: Request authors’ exact actor/critic prompts and parsing logic; re‑run with recovered templates to quantify output variance and impact on results.
2. BO sensitivity sweep: Systematically vary GP kernel (Matérn vs. RBF), acquisition (EI vs. UCB), and historical data weighting; measure FoM/regret stability to identify robust settings.
3. Critic override ablation: Log every critic adjustment (range tightening/expansion and memo reason); correlate override frequency/direction with per‑round FoM gain to isolate strategic vs. legal repairs.
