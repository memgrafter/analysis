# Goodness-of-pronunciation without phoneme time alignment
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.25150
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25150
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Enables speech evaluation without phoneme-aligned ASR, achieving comparable performance to hybrid baselines on English (PCC ~0.75) and superior results on low-resource Tamil (PCC 0.629, MSE 0.569).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of performing automatic speech evaluation (pronunciation accuracy scoring) in low-resource languages where phoneme-aligned ASR models are unavailable. It proposes a fully alignment-free pipeline that leverages a frame-asynchronous model (Whisper) to derive phoneme-level pronunciation features from a confusion network built over N-best hypotheses, and word-level fluency features from attention-based time alignments. These heterogeneous features are fused via a cross-attention mechanism constrained by the estimated word boundaries. The method demonstrates that high-quality evaluation is possible without requiring language-specific forced aligners, with particularly strong results on Tamil where it outperforms a hybrid ASR baseline.

## Method Summary
The system processes an audio recording and its reference transcript through several stages. First, Whisper generates an N-best word hypothesis list. These hypotheses are normalized and converted to phonemes using a lexicon/G2P. A phoneme-level confusion network is constructed from this lattice to compute pronunciation features like CN-GOP (log-posterior of the canonical phoneme), CN-GOP margin, LPP, and LPR. Concurrently, word-level time boundaries are estimated by applying dynamic time warping to Whisper's cross-attention weights between encoder and decoder. From these boundaries, word-level speaking rate (SR) and normalized duration (ND) are calculated and broadcast to constituent phonemes. Frame-level features include SSL embeddings (wav2vec 2.0) and pitch. A transformer decoder layer fuses the phoneme-sequence features (as queries) with the frame-sequence features (as keys/values) via cross-attention. Crucially, attention for each phoneme is restricted to frames falling within its parent word's estimated time boundaries. The fused sequence is processed by additional encoder layers, pooled, and passed through a classifier to predict a sentence-level pronunciation score. Training uses human-annotated scores, but the feature extraction requires no phoneme-aligned ASR model for the target language.

## Key Results
*   On English speechocean762, the proposed method achieves a Pearson Correlation Coefficient (PCC) of ~0.75 with human judgments, comparable to a conventional hybrid ASR baseline that relies on phoneme alignments.
*   On low-resource Tamil, the method significantly outperforms the same hybrid baseline, achieving PCC 0.629 and MSE 0.569 versus the baseline's 0.547 and 0.637, demonstrating its effectiveness where traditional alignment tools fail.
*   Ablation studies confirm that the word-level time constraint on the cross-attention mechanism is essential; removing it causes a substantial performance drop, proving that coarse word boundaries provide necessary weak supervision for feature fusion.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Phoneme-level pronunciation features (GOP, LPP, LPR) can be derived without frame-synchronous ASR alignments by using a phoneme confusion network constructed from an N-best list of a frame-asynchronous ASR model.
*   **Mechanism:** Whisper's N-best word hypotheses are normalized, phonemized via a lexicon/G2P, and formed into a phoneme lattice. A phoneme-level confusion network is computed from this lattice, where each position represents a conditional phoneme probability distribution given the acoustic evidence. GOP-like scores are the log-posterior of the canonical phoneme in each confusion set. The core assumption is that the N-best list captures sufficient acoustic uncertainty and that the confusion network's conditional independence approximation (Equation 8) holds.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Phoneme posteriors are computed by mapping ASR hypotheses to a phoneme confusion network." [section III] Details the 6-step CN construction and CN-GOP definition. [corpus] Neighbors focus on alignment-free GOP or Whisper-based phoneme recognition but do not directly validate this CN construction method; evidence is indirect.
*   **Break condition:** If the N-best list lacks diversity (common in low-resource ASR), the CN may not represent true acoustic uncertainty, degrading pronunciation features.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Speaking rate (SR) and normalized duration (ND) features can be estimated without phoneme alignments by using word-level time boundaries derived from the frame-asynchronous model's attention weights.
*   **Mechanism:** For frame-asynchronous models like Whisper, token time alignments are estimated via dynamic time warping on cross-attention weights between encoder and decoder. These are aggregated to word-level. Word-level SR (inverse duration) and ND (z-normalized duration) are computed and then assigned to each phoneme within the same word. The assumption is that word-level timing is easier to extract reliably than phoneme-level and is sufficiently informative for fluency assessment.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Word instead of phoneme-level speaking rate and duration features." [section III] Describes DTW on attention weights to get token alignments and compute word SR/ND. [corpus] Neighbors like "Segmentation-Free Goodness of Pronunciation" explore alignment-free duration but not specifically this word-level method; evidence is indirect.
*   **Break condition:** If attention weights are uninformative or misaligned (e.g., in poorly adapted low-resource settings), estimated word durations become noisy.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Per-phoneme features (from CN) and per-frame SSL features can be effectively combined without explicit phoneme-time alignment using a cross-attention mechanism constrained by word-level time boundaries.
*   **Mechanism:** A transformer decoder layer uses the phoneme-feature sequence as queries and the frame-level SSL/pitch feature sequence as keys/values. Cross-attention allows each phoneme position to attend to all frames. To prevent over-scattering and incorporate weak supervision, attention is restricted to frames within the word-level time boundaries of the phoneme's parent word. The model learns a soft alignment, while the constraint provides an inductive bias.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "A cross-attention architecture combines ... without alignment, with word-time constraints enhancing performance." [section IV] Describes the constrained attention design. [section V-C] Table V shows word-constrained attention yields better PCC/MSE than unconstrained cross-attention and matches the oracle phoneme-pooling baseline.
*   **Break condition:** If word-level alignments are poor (e.g., due to Whisper segmentation errors), the constraint misguides attention, harming performance.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Frame-synchronous vs. Frame-asynchronous ASR**
  *   *Why needed:* Traditional speech evaluation relies on frame-synchronous models (e.g., HMM-DNN hybrids) that provide precise phoneme boundaries. This work bypasses that requirement using frame-asynchronous models (Whisper). Understanding this distinction is key to the alignment challenge.
  *   *Quick check:* What architectural property of Whisper makes direct phoneme time alignment difficult, and what alternative does the paper propose?
*   **Concept: Confusion Networks for ASR Uncertainty**
  *   *Why needed:* Confusion networks compactly represent competing word sequences from an N-best list as a phoneme-level consensus with probabilities. This is the mechanism to extract phoneme-level posteriors without frame-level alignments.
  *   *Quick check:* How does a phoneme-level confusion network approximate the joint probability of a phoneme sequence, and why is this useful for feature extraction?
*   **Concept: Cross-Attention for Feature Fusion**
  *   *Why needed:* The final model must merge discrete, sequence-aligned phoneme features with continuous, high-rate frame features without shared timestamps. Cross-attention is the learnable mechanism to achieve this fusion.
  *   *Quick check:* In the proposed cross-attention layer, what serves as the Query, Key, and Value, and why is this design chosen?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Whisper Encoder-Decoder (N-best + attention) -> Text Normalization & Lexicon/G2P -> Phoneme Confusion Network Builder -> (parallel) Word Alignment Estimator (DTW on attention) & Frame-Level SSL/Pitch Extractor -> Feature Projection -> Cross-Attention Fusion (with word constraint) -> Transformer Encoders -> Sentence Pooling -> Classifier -> Proficiency Score.
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Whisper decoding (N-best, attention weights).
    2.  Text normalization & phonemization of N-best.
    3.  Phoneme CN construction & CN-GOP feature extraction.
    4.  Word alignment estimation from attention (DTW).
    5.  Word SR/ND feature computation and assignment.
    6.  Frame-level SSL/pitch extraction.
    7.  Cross-attention fusion with word-boundary masking.
    8.  Classification and score prediction.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **CN complexity vs. feature richness:** Using a CN leverages N-best diversity but adds algorithmic complexity and depends on lexicon/G2P quality. Simpler direct confidence scoring might be less expressive.
    *   **Constraint granularity:** Word-level time constraints are a practical compromise. They are easier to obtain from Whisper than phoneme boundaries but provide less precise guidance than ideal phoneme-level masks.
    *   **Feature independence:** Using word-level SR/ND instead of phoneme-level assumes word-level fluency cues are sufficient, potentially missing fine-grained disfluencies.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   High WER on Whisper's N-best indicates poor ASR quality, which will corrupt CN posteriors and word alignments.
    *   Removing the word constraint on cross-attention causes a large performance drop (per Table V), indicating the model cannot learn alignment from scratch and needs the weak supervision.
    *   A performance drop when using pronunciation priors (Table I) suggests acoustic evidence from the CN dominates and static priors may mislead if not conditioned on audio.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Table I & II:** Validate CN-based GOP features. Test impact of multiple pronunciations and beam size on phoneme-level SVR. Conclusion: more pronunciations help; beam size effect small beyond 100; priors may not help.
    2.  **Table IV:** Test word-level SR/ND features (from Whisper word alignments) as a replacement for phoneme-level SR/ND (from hybrid ASR). Conclusion: word-level is comparable, enabling removal of phoneme alignment.
    3.  **Table V:** Core ablation on fusion architecture. Compare phoneme-pooling (requires alignment) vs. cross-attention (no alignment), with/without word constraint. Conclusion: word constraint is necessary to match the phoneme-pooling baseline, validating the core proposal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How can phoneme-level pronunciation priors from a static lexicon be improved by incorporating acoustic evidence from the speech signal itself? The authors note current priors are independent of observed audio.
*   **Open Question 2:** What is the optimal time-constraint granularity (e.g., word, phoneme, or none) for the cross-attention mechanism? The paper shows word-level constraints help, but phoneme-level or other schemes are untested.
*   **Open Question 3:** How can text normalization and script transliteration/romanization be made robust and language-agnostic for low-resource languages with diverse writing systems? For Tamil, hypotheses with non-Tamil characters were omitted due to tooling gaps.
*   **Open Question 4:** Can speech evaluation models be trained without any human-annotated proficiency scores? The method still requires speech-text-score triples, citing scalability as an unresolved issue.

## Limitations
*   The phoneme confusion network construction details (lattice scoring, CN algorithm specifics) are underspecified, relying on interpreting a prior method [29], creating a significant reproducibility gap.
*   The procedure for extracting word-level time boundaries from Whisper's cross-attention ("dynamic time warping on the weights") is underspecified (e.g., which layers, DTW parameters), making this weak supervision step a black box.
*   The entire pipeline's viability depends on Whisper producing a sufficiently diverse and accurate N-best list and meaningful attention weights for the target low-resource language, which is not analytically guaranteed beyond the single Tamil result.

## Confidence
*   **Core Claim (Alignment-Free Evaluation Feasible):** Medium. Validated on two languages, but robustness to broader low-resource conditions (where ASR quality may be very poor) is not established.
*   **Mechanism 1 (CN-Derived Pronunciation Features):** Medium. Phoneme-level SVR results are shown, but no direct comparison to gold-standard alignments on the same data. Evidence is indirect.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Word-Level Duration from Attention):** Low-Medium. Claimed comparable to phoneme-level features, but the baseline phoneme-level features come from a *different* hybrid ASR system, confounding the comparison.
*   **Mechanism 3 (Constrained Cross-Attention Fusion):** High. Ablation clearly demonstrates the necessity of the word-level time constraint to match the oracle alignment baseline, providing strong empirical support.
