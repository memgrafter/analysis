# RAP: Retrieve, Adapt, and Prompt-Fit for Training-Free Few-Shot Medical Image Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.27705  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27705  
- **Reference Count**: 23  
- **Primary Result**: State-of-the-art training-free few-shot medical segmentation; mean Dice scores: 69.16% (Abd-MRI), 69.56% (Abd-CT), 75.27% (Card-MRI), outperforming prior methods by >2%.

## Executive Summary
RAP introduces a novel training-free framework for few-shot medical image segmentation that combines retrieval, geometric adaptation, and prompt engineering to leverage large vision models. Given a query image and a single support (image-mask pair), RAP first retrieves a morphologically compatible support from a database using DINOv3 descriptors. It then adapts the support mask to the query via wavelet-based style transfer, semantic gating, and oriented chamfer matching to produce a coarse pre-segmentation. Finally, it generates geometry-aware prompts through Voronoi tessellation and negative point selection to refine the segmentation with SAM2. Evaluated on three benchmarks, RAP achieves state-of-the-art performance without any training, demonstrating the power of retrieval-augmented prompting for medical segmentation.

## Method Summary
The method operates in three stages. **Retrieve**: global DINOv3 descriptors are extracted from query and support images; the second most similar support (to avoid self-matching) is selected from a database. **Adapt**: the chosen support image undergoes wavelet-based style transfer (replacing the LL subband with that of the query) to align low-level appearance; a semantic gating mask is derived by clustering support foreground DINO features and selecting the top-4 prototypes, thresholding similarity maps to focus on relevant regions; oriented chamfer matching then estimates the optimal scale, rotation, and translation by aligning the support boundary to query edges within the gating region, yielding a transformed mask (M_pre). **Prompt-Fit**: M_pre is used to generate positive points via farthest-point sampling and Voronoi cell centroids, negative points by finding dissimilar exterior pixels within angular sectors, and a tight bounding box. These prompts are fed to SAM2, which produces the final segmentation.

## Key Results
- RAP sets new state-of-the-art for training-free few-shot medical segmentation on Abd-MRI (69.16%), Abd-CT (69.56%), and Card-MRI (75.27%) mean Dice.
- It outperforms the previous best method (MAUP) by over 2% on all benchmarks.
- Ablations confirm that each component (retrieval, adaptation, Voronoi prompts) contributes positively; Voronoi prompts specifically improve fine-detail segmentation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RAP succeeds because it tightly integrates retrieval, geometry-aware adaptation, and SAM2's prompting capabilities. Retrieval ensures that the support mask originates from an anatomically similar structure, providing a plausible shape prior. The adaptation stage aligns this prior to the query through a combination of appearance matching (wavelet transfer), semantic focus (gating), and precise geometric registration (oriented chamfer), yielding a coarse mask that is already well-positioned. Finally, the Voronoi-based prompt design supplies SAM2 with diverse positive points spread across the pre-mask and with negative points that highlight ambiguous boundaries, allowing SAM2 to refine both broad extent and fine details. The synergy avoids any training while effectively bridging the gap between generic foundation models and domain-specific medical segmentation.

## Foundational Learning
- **DINOv3 self-supervised features**: Provide robust, transferable representations for retrieval and semantic gating. *Why needed?* To compare images and cluster foreground without domain-specific training. *Quick check:* Compute nearest-neighbor retrieval on a few samples; clusters should correspond to organ types.
- **SAM2 (Segment Anything Model)**: A powerful foundation model that segments from prompts. *Why needed?* Acts as a universal refinement engine that can interpret diverse point and box prompts. *Quick check:* Run SAM2 on a simple point prompt for a medical image; output should roughly cover the target organ.
- **Oriented Chamfer Matching**: Computes transformation by minimizing distance between boundaries with orientation bins. *Why needed?* To estimate scale, rotation, and translation robustly despite shape variations. *Quick check:* Apply to synthetic rotated/scaled shapes; recovered transformation should align masks.
- **Semantic Gating**: Restricts alignment to semantically relevant regions by thresholding feature similarities. *Why needed?* Prevents interference from irrelevant background structures. *Quick check:* Visualize the gating mask on query; it should cover the organ of interest and exclude clutter.
- **Voronoi Tessellation for Prompting**: Generates positive points spread across the pre-mask. *Why needed?* Provides SAM2 with a set of points that capture both large-scale coverage and local variation, improving fine detail segmentation. *Quick check:* Compare SAM2 outputs with Voronoi points vs. random points; Voronoi should yield smoother and more complete masks.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Query Image → DINOv3 Feature Extraction → Retrieval Module → Selected Support → Wavelet Style Transfer → DINO Feature Clustering → Semantic Gating → Oriented Chamfer Matching → Pre-mask (M_pre) → Voronoi Prompt Generation (positive/negative points + bbox) → SAM2 → Final Segmentation.

**Critical Path**: The pipeline flows sequentially from retrieval to adaptation to prompting; successful execution depends on each stage producing a plausible output: retrieval must pick a compatible support, adaptation must yield a reasonable pre-mask, and prompts must meaningfully guide SAM2.

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Training-free design avoids costly data collection but limits fine-grained domain adaptation.
- Reliance on a support database introduces performance sensitivity to its diversity and size.
- Oriented chamfer matching provides accurate alignment but at high computational cost due to exhaustive search over scales, rotations, and translations.
- SAM2's generalization comes with risk of overfitting to prompt geometry; poor prompts lead to poor segmentation.

**Failure Signatures**:
1. **Retrieval failure**: Selected support is anatomically mismatched (e.g., different organ shape). Visualize the retrieved support alongside the query; if shapes differ greatly, the database may be insufficient.
2. **Adaptation failure**: Gating mask too small/large or oriented chamfer misalignment, resulting in a pre-mask that is mispositioned or incomplete. Overlay M_pre on the query; check if it covers the target organ reasonably.
3. **Prompting failure**: Voronoi points cluster in one region or negative points lie on true foreground, causing SAM2 to under- or over-segment. Inspect prompt distribution; points should be spread and negatives on true background near boundary.

**First Experiments**:
1. **Retrieval validation**: Compute retrieval accuracy (e.g., top-1 descriptor similarity) on a held-out set of support images; ensure that retrieved supports are morphologically similar to queries.
2. **Oriented chamfer calibration**: Test on synthetic transformations with known scale/rotation/translation; measure error in recovered parameters across the search ranges to verify effectiveness of heuristics.
3. **Voronoi prompt ablation**: Replace Voronoi-generated positive points with random foreground points; compare Dice scores on a few samples to quantify the contribution of the specific prompt design.

## Limitations
- DINOv3 feature extraction details (e.g., use of CLS vs. patch tokens, normalization) are unspecified, which may cause deviations from the intended implementation.
- Oriented chamfer hyperparameters (translation range, edge thresholding) are not fully described, relying on heuristics that might be suboptimal.
- The quality and diversity of the support database strongly influence retrieval and downstream performance; small or biased databases can degrade results.
