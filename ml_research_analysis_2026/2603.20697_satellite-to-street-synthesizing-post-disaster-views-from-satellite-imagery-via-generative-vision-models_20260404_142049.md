# Satellite-to-Street: Synthesizing Post-Disaster Views from Satellite Imagery via Generative Vision Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20697
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20697
- **Reference count**: 8
- **Primary result**: Proposed VLM-guided and MoE methods improve perceptual realism but sacrifice semantic consistency compared to ControlNet, revealing a realism-fidelity trade-off.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the scarcity of post-disaster street-level imagery by synthesizing it from satellite images. The authors propose two novel generative approaches: a Vision-Language Model (VLM)-guided method that leverages textual damage descriptions from a VLM, and a damage-sensitive Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) that aggregates severity-specific experts. Both are benchmarked against Pix2Pix and ControlNet on a dataset of 300 hurricane disaster scenes. Using a Structure-Aware Evaluation Framework that combines pixel-level metrics, a ResNet-based semantic classifier, and a VLM-as-a-Judge, the study uncovers a fundamental trade-off: ControlNet achieves the highest semantic consistency (F1=0.71) but often hallucinates structural repairs; in contrast, the VLM-guided and MoE methods produce more texturally plausible and perceptually realistic outputs (top VLM-as-a-Judge scores) at the cost of reduced semantic clarity (F1~0.43), as stochastic damage details confuse the classifier.

## Method Summary
The task is cross-view synthesis from satellite to street-view for post-disaster assessment. The dataset consists of paired satellite and street-view images from Hurricane Ian, with 3,821 training pairs and a balanced test set of 300 images stratified by damage severity (mild, moderate, severe). Four generative paradigms are compared: (A) Pix2Pix (conditional GAN), (B) ControlNet (Stable Diffusion 1.5 with spatial conditioning), (C) VLM-guided (using Gemini-2.5-Flash to extract textual damage descriptions that condition the diffusion model alongside spatial features), and (D) Disaster-MoE (three severity-specific ControlNet experts with a routing network). The evaluation is three-tiered: (1) pixel-level fidelity (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS, FID), (2) semantic consistency through a fine-tuned ResNet-18 damage classifier (reported as F1-score), and (3) perceptual alignment via a VLM-as-a-Judge scoring damage accuracy, structural consistency, and realism. The minimum viable reproduction involves training ControlNet as baseline and implementing the VLM-guided method, but several unknowns impede exact replication: the exact spatial constraint type for ControlNet (e.g., Canny edges vs. depth), MoE routing and expert training details, VLM prompt engineering, and full training hyperparameters. Common failure modes include structural hallucination vs. semantic noise trade-off, classifier dataset shift, and geographic bias.

## Key Results
- Standard ControlNet achieves the highest semantic consistency (F1=0.71) but often hallucinates structurally repaired buildings.
- The VLM-guided and MoE methods improve textural plausibility and perceptual realism (top VLM-as-a-Judge scores) but reduce semantic clarity (F1~0.43) due to stochastic damage details that confuse the ResNet classifier.
- The Structure-Aware Evaluation Framework uncovers a fundamental realism-fidelity trade-off in disaster image synthesis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Incorporating explicit disaster semantics via a Vision-Language Model (VLM) can improve the perceptual realism and textural plausibility of synthesized street-view images compared to vision-only baselines.  
**Mechanism:** The VLM (Gemini-2.5-Flash) processes the satellite image to generate a textual damage description (e.g., "collapsed roof," "debris"). This linguistic prompt is then fed into the diffusion model's conditioning pathway alongside the standard ControlNet spatial features. The hypothesized causal pathway is that the text prompt activates latent knowledge about disaster-related textures and object arrangements within the pre-trained diffusion model, guiding the denoising process toward generating more credible high-frequency damage details (e.g., scattered debris, fractured walls) that are difficult to infer from low-frequency satellite spatial layout alone.  
**Core assumption:** The VLM can generate accurate and relevant damage descriptions from satellite imagery, and the frozen diffusion model's cross-attention layers can effectively map these semantic concepts to appropriate visual patches in the latent space.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "VLM-guided approach... excel in textural plausibility and perceptual realism (via VLM-as-a-Judge)"  
- [section II.B.3] Explicitly defines `p = Φ_VLM(I_sat)` and conditions generation on `(C(I_sat), p)`.  
- [table III] VLM-guided method achieves the highest scores for "Damage Accuracy" (2.04) and "Structural" consistency (1.88) in the VLM-as-a-Judge evaluation.  
**Break condition:** This mechanism breaks down if the VLM's textual extraction becomes unreliable (e.g., hallucinating damage features not present in the satellite image) or if the diffusion model's conditioning on text overrides the spatial constraints from ControlNet, leading to severe geometric misalignment. The paper notes this method reduces semantic clarity (F1~0.43), indicating a trade-off.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** A Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) framework, with experts specializing in distinct damage severity levels, can better model the heterogeneous visual patterns of disaster damage compared to a single generalist model, improving the diversity and specificity of generated damage textures.  
**Mechanism:** A routing network analyzes satellite features to compute gating weights for K severity-specific ControlNet experts (trained on mild, moderate, severe subsets). The final noise prediction is a weighted sum of expert outputs. The causal mechanism is that by isolating learning for different damage modes, each expert develops a sharper, less-confused representation of its target severity's texture and structural failures. The dynamic routing aims to apply the most relevant expert's knowledge for a given input, reducing the model's tendency to "average" across all damage states and thus generating more varied and context-appropriate wreckage.  
**Core assumption:** The damage severity stratification is visually separable and that a single model's objective function cannot simultaneously optimize for all these disparate modes without interference. A learned router can accurately infer the severity from satellite cues.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "damage-sensitive Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) method"  
- [section II.B.4] Details the expert training `{E_k}` and dynamic aggregation `ϵ_θ = Σ wk ϵ_θ^(k)`.  
- [table I] Disaster-MoE has the highest LPIPS (0.688) and FID (134.52), indicating greater pixel-level variance from ground truth, interpreted as "introducing stochastic damage details."  
- [table II & Fig 3] Disaster-MoE shows lower F1 (0.44) and increased confusion between Moderate and Severe classes, consistent with generating more complex, varied damage that challenges a deterministic classifier.  
**Break condition:** The mechanism fails if the routing network makes systematic errors (e.g., routing a severely damaged building to the "mild" expert), resulting in under-generated damage. It also fails if the variance introduced by the experts exceeds acceptable limits for operational use, or if the training data for any severity subset is insufficient for a viable expert.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** A multi-tier, structure-aware evaluation framework that combines pixel-level metrics, ResNet-based semantic verification, and VLM-as-a-Judge is necessary to uncover the inherent realism-fidelity trade-off in post-disaster cross-view synthesis, which would be missed by any single metric.  
**Mechanism:** The causal mechanism here is methodological. Traditional IQA metrics (SSIM, PSNR, FID) primarily capture low-frequency fidelity and distributional similarity. A ResNet classifier trained on real damage labels measures preservation of task-relevant semantics. A VLM-as-a-Judge attempts to model human perception of plausibility and accuracy. By interpreting discrepancies *across* these tiers (e.g., high VLM realism but low ResNet F1), the framework causally identifies the specific nature of model failures: hallucinated repairs (high ControlNet realism/F1) versus semantically noisy realism (lower F1 but high VLM scores for VLM/MoE methods).  
**Core assumption:** No single existing metric fully captures the multi-dimensional utility of an image for post-disaster assessment. Human-like perception (approximated by VLM) and task-specific semantic accuracy (ResNet) are orthogonal dimensions that can conflict with pixel-level fidelity.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "proposed Structure-Aware Evaluation Framework... integrates (1) pixel-level... (2) ResNet-based semantic consistency... (3) a novel VLM-as-a-Judge"  
- [section II.C] Defines the three tiers and their specific purposes.  
- [section III] The results discussion consistently interprets findings through the lens of trade-offs revealed by comparing tables I, II, and III (e.g., ControlNet's high F1/SSIM but lower VLM damage scores vs. VLM/MoE's lower F1 but higher VLM damage/structural scores).  
**Break condition:** The framework itself could fail if any component is invalid. For instance, if the ResNet classifier's training data is not representative, its F1 scores are meaningless. The VLM-as-a-Judge could be biased against certain realistic damage patterns not well-represented in its training. The framework's value is conditional on the soundness of all three independent assessment axes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Cross-View Synthesis (CVS)**  
  - Why needed here: Understanding general CVS challenges (extreme viewpoint change, geometric distortion, occlusion) is prerequisite to appreciating why disaster scenarios are harder (additional stochastic damage patterns).

- **Concept: Diffusion Models & ControlNet**  
  - Why needed here: The baselines and proposed methods are built on latent diffusion models. ControlNet is the specific mechanism for spatial conditioning. One must understand denoising, U-Net conditioning, and how ControlNet injects spatial constraints to grasp the architecture's behavior and failure modes.  
  - Quick check question: How does adding a ControlNet branch to a frozen diffusion U-Net theoretically help preserve input layout, and why might it still "hallucinate" structural repairs?

- **Concept: Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Semantic Grounding**  
  - Why needed here: The VLM-guided method is a key innovation. Understanding that VLMs can be used as zero/few-shot semantic extractors is crucial. The assumption is that text can steer image generation more precisely for complex, abstract concepts like damage severity.  
  - Quick check question: If a VLM describes a satellite scene as "a building with a collapsed roof," what is the core assumption about how that text string influences the latent diffused image during generation?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
`I_sat` → condition extractor `C(I_sat)` (CNN backbone) → ControlNet-injected Stable Diffusion U-Net (`ϵ_θ`) → iterative denoising on latent `z_t` → decoded `Î_street`.  
For VLM-guided: parallel path `I_sat` → Gemini VLM → text prompt `p` → cross-attention in U-Net (alongside spatial features).  
For Disaster-MoE: `I_sat` → router `R` → gating weights `w_k` → weighted combination of `K` expert noise predictions `ϵ_θ^(k)` (each expert processes `C_k(I_sat)`) → aggregated `ϵ_θ`.

**Critical path:**  
`I_sat` → `C(I_sat)` (+ `p` if VLM) → ControlNet-injected U-Net → noise prediction → latent denoising → decode → `Î_street`. For MoE, routing (`R`) precedes expert aggregation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Spatial Fidelity vs. Perceptual Realism**: Strong spatial constraints (ControlNet) preserve layout but may repair damage; weaker constraints allow varied details but degrade precision.  
- **Determinism vs. Stochastic Detail**: Pix2Pix is deterministic and blurry; diffusion introduces stochasticity; MoE/VLM explicitly encourage stochastic damage details, improving human-perceived realism at the cost of classifier-predicted semantic clarity.  
- **Specialization vs. Generalization**: MoE's expert specialization risks poor routing for unseen damage blends; VLM's external dependency adds latency and API cost.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Hallucinated Repair**: Output shows an intact or "repaired" building facade despite satellite damage; characteristic of strong ControlNet (high F1/SSIM).  
- **Semantic Noise**: Output contains realistic debris texture that feels plausible to humans (high VLM scores) but is spatially incoherent or semantically ambiguous, causing a ResNet classifier to confuse severity levels (low F1); characteristic of VLM/MoE methods.  
- **Mode Collapse**: Output is generically "urban" with no specific damage, or defaults to one severity class; characteristic of Pix2Pix.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline Replication & Sanity Check**: Train `SD1.5 + ControlNet` on the provided 3,821 training pairs. Generate on the 300-pair test set. Confirm replication of Table I & II metrics (F1~0.71, FID~74). If F1 is far lower, spatial conditioning or training pipeline is broken.  
2. **VLM Prompt Ablation**: For the VLM-guided model, run a controlled experiment using (a) no prompt, (b) a generic prompt ("a street view of a building"), and (c) the actual VLM-extracted prompt. Measure change in Table III (VLM-as-a-Judge scores) and Table II (F1). Expect: (c) > (b) > (a) on VLM scores, but **(c)** likely lowers F1 most, isolating the VLM's contribution to the realism-fidelity trade-off.  
3. **MoE Routing Analysis**: Visualize the routing network `R`'s predicted severity distribution for ground-truth severe damage inputs. Compute the F1 score *per expert* on its *specialized* test subset. Expect: Each expert performs best on its own severity slice but poorly on others. A global routing accuracy <70% would explain the overall F1 drop (0.44) due to mis-routing.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What architectural modifications can simultaneously optimize perceptual realism and semantic fidelity in satellite-to-street view synthesis for disasters?** The paper identifies a trade-off but does not propose integrated model designs that could balance both, essential for trustworthy disaster assessment.  
2. **Is the ResNet-based semantic consistency verification adequate for evaluating generated disaster imagery with stochastic damage details?** The classifier shows high confusion between moderate and severe classes for VLM and MoE outputs, raising doubts about its sufficiency for assessing semantic accuracy in complex, variable damage scenes.  
3. **Do the proposed VLM-guided and Disaster-MoE methods generalize to other disaster types and geographic regions beyond Hurricane Ian?** The dataset is exclusively from Hurricane Ian; without broader validation, applicability in unseen contexts remains uncertain.  
4. **How does the choice of Vision-Language Model impact the effectiveness of VLM-guided synthesis?** The paper uses Gemini-2.5-Flash but does not compare alternative VLMs, leaving sensitivity to VLM selection unexplored.

## Limitations
- **Unclear spatial constraint definition**: The exact form of `C(I_sat)` (e.g., Canny edges, depth) for ControlNet is unspecified, hindering replication of geometric fidelity.  
- **Missing MoE implementation details**: Routing network architecture, expert training procedure, and aggregation equation lack sufficient detail for faithful reproduction.  
- **Omitted training hyperparameters**: Epochs, learning rates, and loss weights for all generative models are not provided, making stable reproduction difficult.
