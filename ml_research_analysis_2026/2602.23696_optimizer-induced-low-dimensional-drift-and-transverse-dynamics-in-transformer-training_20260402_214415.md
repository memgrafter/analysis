# Optimizer-Induced Low-Dimensional Drift and Transverse Dynamics in Transformer Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23696
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23696
- **Reference count:** 6
- **Primary result:** AdamW induces a persistent one-dimensional "backbone" direction capturing 60–80% of cumulative parameter drift; this backbone is locally stable but can globally reorient after objective changes, with transverse dynamics linked to task performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates long-horizon parameter drift during transformer training with the AdamW optimizer. Using uncentered PCA on checkpoint trajectories, the authors identify a dominant one-dimensional "backbone" direction that captures the majority (60–80%) of total displacement. This structure emerges not from instantaneous gradient geometry but from the accumulated dynamics of AdamW's momentum and adaptive scaling. The backbone is locally stable yet can reorient sharply (~71°) following an abrupt change in loss weighting. Transverse (orthogonal) dynamics correlate with probe task performance and can be temporarily excited via "reheating" without altering the backbone itself. The findings establish an optimizer-induced slow-fast decomposition in training trajectories, shifting analytical focus from local loss geometry to cumulative update geometry.

## Method Summary
The analysis tracks parameter checkpoints saved every 200 steps over 10k training steps on a 51M-parameter decoder-only transformer trained on TinyStories with a synthetic probe objective. The primary tool is uncentered PCA applied to the drift matrix of trunk parameters (relative to initialization), which isolates a dominant backbone direction. Rolling-window PCA tracks backbone stability, while decomposition into backbone-aligned (slow) and transverse (fast) components reveals their distinct behaviors. Key experiments include: comparing per-batch gradients to 200-step integrated updates, ablating AdamW's β₂ parameter, switching to SGD-family optimizers, introducing an abrupt objective reweighting (λ switch), and applying a "reheating" protocol (resuming from a late checkpoint with a fresh optimizer and modified λ).

## Key Results
- A single backbone direction explains 60–80% of cumulative parameter drift under default AdamW.
- Per-batch gradients are nearly isotropic relative to the backbone (|cos| ~ 0.01), while 200-step optimizer updates align strongly (|cos| ~ 0.15–0.32), indicating the structure arises from accumulated dynamics.
- SGD-family optimizers do not exhibit this low-dimensional concentration; trajectories are nearly collinear (PC1 ≈ 100%) but with far smaller total drift magnitude.
- Reducing AdamW's β₂ parameter weakens backbone dominance monotonically; at β₂=0, PC1 drops from ~68% to ~52% and update alignment halves.
- Following a λ reweighting at step 4000, the backbone reorients globally by ~71°, passing through a transient "dead zone" where it is misaligned with both early and late phase backbones.
- Transverse dynamics (orthogonal residual) show a strong positive correlation with out-of-distribution probe accuracy early in training (~0.85) that flips negative later (~–0.79). Reheating can temporarily improve probe performance by perturbing transverse modes while leaving the backbone coordinate nearly unchanged.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The backbone—a low-dimensional drift direction—emerges from accumulated AdamW dynamics, not from instantaneous gradient geometry.
- **Mechanism:** Momentum (β₁) integrates weak but temporally consistent signed gradient biases across many batches, acting as a low-pass filter. Adaptive second-moment normalization (β₂) suppresses high-variance coordinates while preserving those with persistent bias. Their combination concentrates the effective update into a coherent direction over long horizons.
- **Core assumption:** A persistent, weak gradient bias exists that becomes amplified by temporal integration and adaptive scaling.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Per-batch gradients have near-noise-floor alignment with the backbone (|cos| ~ 0.01), while 200-step updates align strongly (|cos| ~ 0.15–0.32).
  - Section 4.7 states two mechanisms: momentum integrates weak signed bias; adaptive normalization suppresses incoherent variance.
  - β₂ ablation shows PC1 dropping from ~68% to 52% and update alignment halving at β₂=0.
- **Break condition:** Removing momentum (β₁=0) or second-moment normalization (β₂=0) should drastically weaken backbone dominance. SGD-family optimizers without adaptive scaling produce nearly collinear trajectories (PC1 ≈ 100%) but with much smaller total drift, confirming optimizer specificity.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Objective reweighting (doubling λ) causes the backbone to reorient globally by ~71°, a gradual rotation that passes through a transient geometric dead zone and triggers a reversal in update alignment.
- **Mechanism:** Changing λ alters the gradient weighting; the optimizer’s accumulated momentum slowly redirects toward a new dominant direction. Rolling-window backbone tangent remains locally smooth (ρ > 0.7), but phase-level backbones (early vs. late) become misaligned (cos ≈ 0.32). The transition region shows low alignment to both phase directions, accompanied by a spike in Fisher anisotropy and a sign flip in cosine between 200-step updates and the backbone.
- **Core assumption:** The backbone shape is determined by the long-term integrated gradient bias; perturbing the objective modifies that bias, inducing a new optimal drift direction.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Backbone reorients by ~71° following λ switch.
  - Section 4.4 quantifies alignment AE(t)/AL(t) and early–late backbone cosine = 0.323; dead zone near step 3500.
  - Section 4.8 shows signed update-backbone alignment reverses around step 5100.
- **Break condition:** Without an abrupt λ-switch, the dead zone and sign reversal might not appear. If the backbone were purely a landscape property, changing λ should not produce such coordinated reorientation.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Transverse (residual) dynamics oscillate orthogonally to the backbone and correlate with probe performance; these modes can be transiently re-excited by reheating without altering the accumulated backbone drift.
- **Mechanism:** The backbone coordinate a(t) tracks a slow manifold shaped by optimizer integration and exhibits increasing curvature. The residual r(t) contains switching dynamics. Early in training, residual growth correlates positively with improving probe accuracy; after step ~6000, the correlation flips negative. Reheating (fresh optimizer, higher λ) perturbs transverse modes, temporarily boosting probe accuracy, while the backbone component remains nearly unchanged due to stiffness from accumulated drift.
- **Core assumption:** The backbone represents a stiff slow manifold resistant to perturbation, while transverse directions remain flexible and sensitive to gradient changes.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Reheating confirms transverse dynamics can be excited without altering backbone drift.
  - Section 4.6: corr(p_ood, ||r||) ≈ +0.85 (0–4k) and –0.79 (6k–10k).
  - Section 7: Reheating achieves peak p_ood = 0.782 but decays; backbone coordinate unchanged.
- **Break condition:** If backbone curvature is too high, reheating may fail to re-enter the probe basin. If residual dynamics were not orthogonal, reheating would alter the backbone coordinate. The correlation sign flip is also diagnostic.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Uncentered Principal Component Analysis (PCA)  
  **Why needed:** Used to identify the backbone from the drift matrix (θ(t)–θ(0)). Uncentered PCA preserves absolute displacement from initialization, isolating persistent drift; centered PCA would subtract the mean and conflate the dominant monotonic component.  
  **Quick check:** How would using centered PCA change the variance explained by PC1, and why is uncentered PCA more appropriate for cumulative drift analysis?

- **Concept:** AdamW optimizer mechanics (β₁ momentum, β₂ second-moment normalization)  
  **Why needed:** The backbone mechanism stems from AdamW's update: momentum integrates gradient history, while β₂ rescales coordinates by their second moment, introducing temporal coherence and anisotropy absent in SGD.  
  **Quick check:** Why does reducing β₂ weaken backbone dominance and explode drift magnitude?

- **Concept:** Spectral gap and Davis-Kahan stability  
  **Why needed:** The intra-signal gap framework identifies the backbone as the singular mode above the largest gap (σ₁/σ₂) in the rolling-window Gram matrix. Davis-Kahan bounds eigenvector perturbation by the gap size, explaining local backbone stability (high ρ) versus free rotation of transverse modes (low α).  
  **Quick check:** What does a large gap σ₁/σ₂ imply about the persistence of the corresponding singular vector as the training window slides?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Model (51M-parameter decoder-only transformer, 8 layers, d_model=512) -> Data (TinyStories + 10% synthetic probe sequences) -> Composite loss (L_LM + λ L_probe, λ switches 2.0→4.0) -> AdamW optimizer (β₁=0.9, β₂=0.95, LR=1e-3 cosine decay, weight decay=0.5) -> Checkpoints every 200 steps (10k steps) -> Analysis (uncentered SVD, rolling-window PCA, decomposition a(t)/r(t), 200-step updates u(t), Fisher curvature, reheating).

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Train with AdamW, save trunk checkpoints.  
  2. Build drift matrix X (rows = θ(t)–θ(0)).  
  3. Global uncentered PCA → backbone vb, PC1 variance ρ₁.  
  4. Rolling-window PCA (W=10) → v_roll(t), stability ρ(t)=|⟨v_roll(t),v_roll(t+Δ)⟩|.  
  5. Decompose: a(t)=⟨Δθ(t),vb⟩, r(t)=Δθ(t)–a(t)vb.  
  6. Compute 200-step updates u(t); align |cos(u(t),vb)| vs. per-batch gradients.  
  7. β₂ ablation (β₂∈{0.99,0.95,0.90,0.80,0.0}); repeat steps 2–6.  
  8. Locate λ-switch; compute phase backbones v_E (0–4k), v_L (4k–10k); measure rotation |⟨v_E,v_L⟩| and alignments AE(t)/AL(t).  
  9. Reheating from step 10k: fresh optimizer, λ=4.0, LR∈{1e-3,6e-4,3e-4}; track p_ood, a(t), r(t).  
  10. For rolling windows, compute Gram G=XXᵀ; singular ratio R(t)=σ₁/σ₂.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Model size (51M): balances compute and generality; scaling may change backbone dominance.  
  - Synthetic probe (10% replacement): allows controlled λ-switch but may not reflect natural multi-objective training.  
  - Trunk-only analysis: focuses on core weights; misses embedding/LN interactions.  
  - Uncentered PCA: captures absolute drift; centered PCA would obscure monotonic component.  
  - Rolling window W=10 (~2000 steps): trades temporal resolution for stability; finer windows are noisier.  
  - Checkpoint interval 200 steps: coarse; may miss short alignment fluctuations.  
  - Fisher curvature: approximated via minibatch (batch=32); full Fisher intractable, so noisy.  
  - Reheating with fresh optimizer: isolates current gradients; continuing optimizer would mix prior momentum.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - PC₁ (row-normalized) < 60% over full trajectory → no dominant backbone.  
  - Rolling cosine ρ(t) consistently < 0.7 → backbone not locally stable.  
  - β₂ ablation shows no monotonic degradation in PC₁ or update alignment → optimizer not responsible.  
  - SGD-family optimizers produce similarly high PC₁ → not AdamW-specific.  
  - No sign reversal in update-backbone alignment around step 4000 → λ effect absent.  
  - Reheating fails to increase p_ood at any LR → probe basin inaccessible or transverse stiffness.  
  - Residual norm vs. p_ood correlation does not flip sign → link to performance missing.  
  - Gap ratio R(t) remains ~1 → no intra-signal gap structure.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline PCA:** Train default AdamW; compute uncentered PCA on trunk drifts; verify PC₁ > 60%.  
  2. **Gradient vs. update alignment:** For a block, compute per-batch gradient cosine and 200-step update cosine with vb; expect near-zero gradients (|cos|≈0.01) and stronger updates (|cos|≈0.15–0.32).  
  3. **Optimizer control:** Train with SGD+momentum (lr=0.01, momentum=0.9, wd=0.05); PCA on same window (e.g., steps 600–2000); expect PC₁≈100% but much smaller drift magnitude.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does the backbone phenomenon persist in larger transformers and natural multi-objective training settings?
- **Basis in paper:** Explicitly stated: study uses a 51M-parameter transformer and synthetic probe; scalability and natural settings remain future work.
- **Why unresolved:** Evidence limited to one model size and synthetic objective; unknown if PC₁≈60–80% is universal or an artifact.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train larger models (e.g., GPT-2 124M, 355M) on natural corpora (OpenWebText) with/without auxiliary tasks; compute uncentered PCA across seeds; replicate backbone dominance and rotation behaviors.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the empirical slow-fast decomposition be derived from a formal dynamical-systems analysis of the AdamW update rule?
- **Basis in paper:** Explicit: "We do not claim a formal dynamical-systems separation." Introduction notes cumulative geometry is unexplored theoretically.
- **Why unresolved:** Work is purely empirical; no mathematical model explains why AdamW produces one slow manifold and fast modes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop continuous-time or mean-field approximation of AdamW; prove separation into 1D slow variable and fast transverse modes; derive conditions on hyperparameters; validate predictions (rotation angles, spectral gap) against existing data.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Is the backbone phenomenon unique to AdamW? How do other Adam-variant optimizers (RMSprop, AdaGrad, vanilla Adam) affect low-dimensional drift?
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred: comparison only to SGD; β₂ ablation shows second-moment normalization matters, but role of bias-correction or momentum within Adam-style optimizers unknown.
- **Why unresolved:** Since SGD lacks backbone, adaptive scaling is necessary, but sensitivity to exact adaptive scheme not tested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Run identical experiments with RMSprop, AdaGrad, Adam; compute uncentered PCA, alignment, transverse dynamics; compare PC1 and update alignment to AdamW baseline to isolate essential algorithmic features.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What mechanism drives the large (~71°) reorientation of the backbone after λ is increased?
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred: Section 4.4 documents rotation and dead zone but states: "We do not claim that curvature causes rotation." Trigger remains unidentified.
- **Why unresolved:** Rotation coincides with λ-switch and Fisher anisotropy spike, but causal chain (gradient stats, momentum direction, curvature) is speculative.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** High-resolution tracking around switch: measure leading eigenvectors of gradient covariance, momentum buffer direction, Fisher curvature along current/predicted backbone. Correlate with rotation angle. Controlled experiments: vary λ magnitude/timing; simulate two-objective linear model.

## Limitations
- Synthetic probe objective (10% replacement) may not generalize to natural multi-task training.
- Evidence restricted to a single 51M transformer on TinyStories; scaling and domain transfer unknown.
- Checkpoint interval (200 steps) and trunk-only analysis may miss short-term fluctuations and interactions with embedding/LN.
- Optimizer comparison limited to SGD+momentum; other adaptive methods not examined.
- Transverse-performance link is correlational; no causal perturbation isolating residual dynamics.

## Confidence
- **Backbone existence (60–80% variance):** High (robust PCA, β₂ ablation, optimizer contrast).
- **AdamW vs. SGD specificity:** Medium (paper says SGD eliminates backbone, but break condition cites PC₁≈100% for SGD → inconsistency in interpretation).
- **Local stability & reorientation:** Medium (ρ > 0.7, ~71° rotation; window width not varied).
- **Mechanism (momentum + β₂):** Medium (β₂ weakening shown; β₁=0 not directly tested).
- **Transverse-performance link:** Medium (reheating boost, sign-flipped correlation; no perturbation).
- **Generality beyond synthetic probe:** Low (synthetic task only).

## Next Checks
1. **Scale sweep:** Train models from 10M to 1B parameters; verify backbone dominance (PC1) persists across scales and datasets.
2. **Real multi-task fine-tuning:** Introduce a natural secondary task (e.g., sentiment analysis); test backbone emergence, reorientation, and transverse dynamics in a realistic multi-objective setting.
3. **Hyperparameter mapping:** Sweep β₁, β₂, ε, weight decay across AdamW and other optimizers (RMSprop, Adagrad, Lion) to define the regime inducing low-dimensional drift; map phase diagram of backbone strength.
