# Step-Size Decay and Structural Stagnation in Greedy Sparse Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07703
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07703
- Reference count: 5
- Primary result: For the Power-Relaxed Greedy Algorithm with step-size λ_m = m^{-α} and α>1, the residual norm in a realizable two-atom sparse regression is bounded below by b(1-μ)√((1+μ)/2)·P_α > 0, proving structural stagnation even in noiseless settings.

## Executive Summary
The paper studies greedy sparse learning algorithms, focusing on the Power-Relaxed Greedy Algorithm (PRGA) with polynomially decaying step sizes. It shows that when the decay exponent α exceeds 1, the cumulative step size becomes finite, which prevents the iterate from reaching the atomic norm of the target signal. This leads to a non-vanishing residual—termed structural stagnation—in a simple two-atom linear regression problem. The authors derive an explicit lower bound on the residual norm that depends on feature coherence μ, target sparsity b, and the infinite product P_α = ∏_{k=2}^∞ (1 - k^{-α}). Numerical experiments confirm the theoretical dependence on μ and α. The results highlight a subtle pitfall in designing step-size schedules for greedy sparse methods.

## Method Summary
The analysis assumes a realizable setting where the target y is a linear combination of two unit-norm dictionary atoms with coherence μ, and that the first greedy pick is correct. The PRGA proceeds greedily: at each iteration m, it selects the atom most correlated with the current residual and updates the estimate as f_m = (1-λ_m)f_{m-1} + λ_m g_m, with step size λ_m = m^{-α}. For α>1, the sum of step sizes converges, implying that the atomic norm of f_m is bounded away from the target's atomic norm. This gap translates to a Euclidean residual lower bound via geometric properties of incoherent dictionaries. The authors run simulations for various μ and α, measuring the minimal residual norm achieved and comparing to the derived bound.

## Key Results
- Finite cumulative step-size accumulation (∑λ_m < ∞) enforces a uniform upper bound on the atomic norm of iterates: sup_m ‖f_m‖_a ≤ 1 - u_1 P_α, where u_1 = b(1-μ) and P_α = ∏_{k=2}^∞ (1 - k^{-α}) ∈ (0,1).
- The residual norm satisfies inf_m ‖r_m‖_2 ≥ b(1-μ) √((1+μ)/2) P_α > 0, demonstrating structural stagnation.
- Numerical experiments confirm that the residual floor increases with α (since P_α decreases) and decreases with μ (via the geometric factor), matching the theory.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Finite accumulation bounds atomic norm
The PRGA update implies a recurrence for the atomic norm gap u_m := 1 - ‖f_m‖_a. Under the assumption that the first greedy pick is correct and the target lies in the two-atom span, one can show u_m ≥ u_1 ∏_{k=2}^m (1 - λ_k). If ∑λ_m < ∞ (α>1), the infinite product P_α = ∏_{k=2}^∞ (1 - λ_k) is positive, so sup_m ‖f_m‖_a ≤ 1 - u_1 P_α < 1. Thus the iterates can never achieve the full atomic norm of the target.

### Mechanism 2: Atomic norm gap yields Euclidean residual floor
For any u in the span of the selected atoms, Proposition 2.2 gives a lower bound on its Euclidean norm in terms of its atomic norm and the coherence μ. Applying to the residual r_m = y - f_m, and using the dual norm projection onto a fixed direction v, we obtain ‖r_m‖_2 ≥ C(μ)·(‖y‖_a - ‖f_m‖_a). Since the gap ‖y‖_a - ‖f_m‖_a ≥ u_1 P_α > 0 from Mechanism 1, the residual stays bounded away from zero.

### Mechanism 3: Generalization to other greedy methods
The core issue—finite cumulative step-size causing confinement within a proper subset of the convex hull of atoms—applies to any stage-wise greedy algorithm with an update of the form f_m = (1-λ_m)f_{m-1} + λ_m g_m and predetermined step sizes satisfying ∑λ_m < ∞. If the greedy selections are always correct, the algorithm can become trapped in a region that does not contain the target, leading to stagnation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Atomic norm and its dual**  
  The atomic norm ‖u‖_a = inf{ Σ|c_i| : u = Σ c_i g_i, g_i ∈ D } measures representation sparsity. The dual norm maximizes correlation with atoms. Needed because the PRGA iterates are convex combinations of atoms; the dual norm is used to project the residual onto a fixed direction and convert an atomic norm gap into a Euclidean lower bound.  
  *Quick check:* For a symmetric dictionary D = {±x₁, ±x₂} with coherence μ, consider the normalized vector v = (x₁ + x₂)/‖x₁ + x₂‖ (so ‖v‖_2=1). What is the dual norm ‖v‖_a^*? (Answer: max_{g∈D} |⟨v, g⟩| = √((1+μ)/2).)

- **Infinite product convergence for α>1**  
  The constant P_α = ∏_{k=2}^∞ (1 - k^{-α}) appears in the stagnation bound. Its positivity for α>1 follows from the convergence of ∑ log(1 - k^{-α}), which behaves like ∑ -k^{-α}.  
  *Quick check:* Why does ∑_{k=2}^∞ log(1 - k^{-α}) converge for α>1? (Answer: log(1-x) ~ -x for small x, and ∑ k^{-α} converges when α>1.)

- **Gershgorin circle theorem**  
  Used in Proposition 2.2 to bound the smallest eigenvalue of the Gram matrix of s atoms with pairwise coherence ≤ μ_s: λ_min ≥ 1 - (s-1)μ_s. This yields the norm comparison ‖u‖_2 ≥ √(1-(s-1)μ_s)/√s · ‖u‖_a for u in span(S).  
  *Quick check:* For orthonormal atoms (μ_s=0), what does Gershgorin give for λ_min(G)? (Answer: ≥1, so ‖u‖_2 ≥ ‖a‖_2 when u = Σ a_j x_j.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (dictionary D, target y, step-size schedule λ_m) -> State (current iterate f_m, residual r_m, atomic norm gap u_m) -> Core operations (greedy atom selection by max correlation, linear update f_m = (1-λ_m)f_{m-1} + λ_m g_m) -> Output (residual norm sequence, stagnation floor).

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Compute initial correlations ⟨y, x₁⟩, ⟨y, x₂⟩ and select g₁ = argmax (with sign).  
  2. Set f₁ = ⟨y, g₁⟩ g₁, compute u₁ = 1 - ‖f₁‖_a = b(1-μ).  
  3. For m≥2, pick g_m = argmax_{g∈D} ⟨r_{m-1}, g⟩, update f_m with λ_m, maintain product Q_m = ∏_{k=2}^m (1-λ_k).  
  4. Track lower bound u_m ≥ u₁ Q_m; for α>1, Q_m ≥ P_α > 0, so ‖f_m‖_a ≤ 1 - u₁ P_α.  
  5. Choose a fixed dual direction v (e.g., normalized sum of atoms) with known ‖v‖_a^*.  
  6. Bound ⟨r_m, v⟩ ≥ ⟨y, v⟩ u_m and then ‖r_m‖_2 ≥ ⟨r_m, v⟩ (since ‖v‖_2=1).  
  7. Conclude inf_m ‖r_m‖_2 ≥ ⟨y, v⟩ u₁ P_α = b(1-μ) √((1+μ)/2) P_α.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Step-size decay α: α≤1 yields ∑λ_m = ∞, avoiding forced stagnation; α>1 induces finite accumulation and a positive residual floor proportional to P_α (which decreases as α grows).  
  - Feature coherence μ: Lower μ increases the geometric factor √((1+μ)/2) and reduces stagnation.  
  - Target sparsity b: The floor scales linearly with b (the weight of the less-represented atom).

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Residual norm ‖r_m‖_2 plateaus at a positive value that does not decrease with more iterations.  
  - The plateau level rises with α (since P_α shrinks) and falls with μ.  
  - Atomic norm ‖f_m‖_a remains bounded below ‖y‖_a = 1, never reaching the target's atomic norm.

- **First three experiments:**  
  1. Compute P_α for α=1.5 via logarithmic summation and verify it lies in (0,1). Compare empirical inf_m ‖r_m‖_2 to the theoretical bound L for μ=0.1.  
  2. Sweep μ from 0 to 0.9 with α=1.2; plot measured min ‖r_m‖_2 versus μ and overlay the curve b(1-μ)√((1+μ)/2)P_α.  
  3. For fixed μ=0.2, run α from 0.5 to 2.0; verify stagnation appears only for α>1 and that the residual floor grows with α. Check the borderline α=1 (RGA) for slow decay.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How does additive stochastic noise affect the stagnation phenomenon when α > 1?**  
  The paper only provides a heuristic argument; no rigorous analysis or numerical experiments with noise are presented.
- **Does the structural stagnation generalize to other stage-wise greedy algorithms (e.g., boosting, Frank-Wolfe) with rapidly decaying step sizes?**  
  The mechanism via cumulative accumulation A∞ is suggested but not proven for algorithms beyond PRGA.
- **Can the explicit lower bound of Theorem 2.1 be generalized to s-sparse targets (s > 2) under coherence constraints?**  
  The theorem is restricted to two atoms; extending it to higher sparsity would require handling more complex Gram matrix dynamics.

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis assumes a symmetric dictionary, a realizable noiseless model, exactly two atoms, and that the first greedy selection is correct.
- No treatment of sparsity s>2, observation noise, or possible misordering of greedy picks.
- Experimental validation lacks statistical reporting (variance) and precise parameter disclosure (exact μ values used in figures).

## Confidence
- **Theoretical lower bound (existence and formula)**: High – rigorous proof within stated assumptions.
- **Empirical verification of μ and α trends**: Medium – experiments confirm direction but are sparsely reported.
- **Generality to other greedy algorithms**: Low – only PRGA is analyzed; extension remains conjectural.
