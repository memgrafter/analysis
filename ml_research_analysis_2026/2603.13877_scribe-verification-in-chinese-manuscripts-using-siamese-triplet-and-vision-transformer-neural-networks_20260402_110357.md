# Scribe Verification in Chinese manuscripts using Siamese, Triplet, and Vision Transformer Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.13877
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13877
- **Reference count**: 13
- **Primary result**: MobileNetV3+Custom Siamese with contrastive loss achieves 89.19% accuracy and 0.958 AUC on the Tsinghua Bamboo Slips dataset.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles automatic scribe verification for Chinese manuscripts, a task that determines whether two manuscript fragments were written by the same scribe. The core approach uses deep metric learning with Siamese and Triplet neural networks, employing convolutional backbones (MobileNetV3, ResNet) and Vision Transformers (ViTs). Models are trained under a unified PyTorch framework with dynamic sampling and contrastive or triplet loss. The best performance on the Tsinghua Bamboo Slips dataset comes from a MobileNetV3-based Siamese network with contrastive loss, achieving 89.19% accuracy and 0.958 AUC. Results on the MCCD subset further highlight the robustness of lightweight CNNs for both ancient and modern handwriting verification tasks.

## Method Summary
The study implements a unified framework for deep metric learning applied to scribe verification. Input image pairs or triplets are processed through shared backbones to extract feature embeddings. Distances between embeddings are computed and used with either contrastive loss (for Siamese networks) or triplet loss to enforce that same-scribe pairs are closer than different-scribe pairs. A dynamic sampling strategy is integrated into the data loader to actively select challenging pairs or triplets during training. The framework supports multiple backbone architectures—including MobileNetV3, ResNet, and Vision Transformers—allowing comparative analysis of their feature extraction capabilities for the specific visual patterns in Chinese handwriting.

## Key Results
- MobileNetV3+Custom Siamese with contrastive loss achieves 89.19% accuracy and 0.958 AUC on the Tsinghua Bamboo Slips dataset, outperforming all other configurations.
- On the MCCD subset, the same model ranks either first or second in accuracy and AUC across comparisons, demonstrating consistent effectiveness.
- Lightweight CNNs (MobileNetV3) consistently outperform ResNet and Vision Transformer backbones, showing particular robustness for both ancient scripts and modern handwriting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of the MobileNetV3-based Siamese network stems from its efficient architecture, which is well-suited to extract the local stroke and character-level features critical for scribe identification. Contrastive loss directly optimizes the embedding space by minimizing the distance between same-scribe pairs and maximizing it for different-scribe pairs. The dynamic sampling strategy likely ensures that the network is consistently trained on hard-to-classify examples, preventing it from converging to trivial solutions and improving discriminative power. The underperformance of ViTs suggests that the dataset’s limited size and the sparse, structured nature of Chinese characters may not benefit from the global attention mechanisms that require large amounts of data to learn effectively.

## Foundational Learning
- **Siamese Networks**: Learn a similarity metric by processing two inputs through shared weights. *Why needed*: Directly optimizes pairwise comparisons for verification. *Quick check*: Train a simple Siamese network on MNIST and verify that same-digit pairs have lower embedding distance than different-digit pairs.
- **Triplet Networks**: Learn embeddings by comparing an anchor against a positive (same class) and a negative (different class). *Why needed*: Enforces relative distance constraints, potentially more sample-efficient. *Quick check*: Implement triplet loss with a fixed margin and confirm that anchor-positive distance < anchor-negative distance - margin on a small dataset.
- **Contrastive Loss**: Pulls same-class embeddings together and pushes different-class embeddings apart. *Why needed*: Provides a clear objective for distance-based classification. *Quick check*: Monitor that loss decreases while same-class pair distances shrink and different-class pair distances grow during training.
- **Triplet Loss**: Minimizes the distance between anchor and positive while maximizing the distance between anchor and negative by a margin. *Why needed*: Focuses on challenging relative relationships. *Quick check*: Verify that after training, the hardest triplets (where anchor-negative is already close) have reduced loss contribution.
- **Vision Transformers (ViTs)**: Apply self-attention to image patches for global context modeling. *Why needed*: Alternative to CNNs for capturing long-range dependencies. *Quick check*: Train a small ViT on CIFAR-10 to confirm it achieves reasonable accuracy, establishing baseline functionality.
- **Dynamic Sampling**: Adaptively selects training pairs/triplets based on current model difficulty. *Why needed*: Improves training efficiency and final performance by focusing on informative examples. *Quick check*: Compare convergence speed of a triplet network with random vs. semi-hard mining on a synthetic dataset.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Input Image Pair/Triplet -> Dynamic Sampler -> Backbone Network (MobileNetV3/ResNet/ViT) -> Feature Embedding -> Distance Computation -> Loss Function (Contrastive/Triplet) -> Backpropagation

**Critical Path**: Data loading with dynamic sampling selects a batch of pairs/triplets; each image passes through the shared backbone to produce embeddings; pairwise or triplet distances are calculated; loss is computed and backpropagated to update backbone weights.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- CNN (MobileNetV3/ResNet) vs. ViT: CNNs excel at local feature extraction (strokes, radicals) with fewer parameters and less data; ViTs offer global context but are data-hungry and may struggle with small, structured datasets.  
- Siamese vs. Triplet: Siamese is simpler with pairwise loss but may not enforce relative margins; Triplet is more sample-efficient but highly sensitive to mining strategy and training instability.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- ViT models show significantly lower accuracy/AUC, indicating possible overfitting or inability to learn relevant features from limited data.  
- Triplet networks with random sampling converge slowly and underperform Siamese networks, suggesting poor utilization of training samples.  
- Models fail to generalize if dynamic sampling is removed or if backbones are too deep for the small dataset, leading to overfitting.

**First Three Experiments**:  
1. Replicate the best-performing MobileNetV3+Custom Siamese with contrastive loss on the Tsinghua Bamboo Slits dataset, confirming reported metrics (accuracy, AUC).  
2. Diagnose ViT underperformance by training ViT-Base with smaller patch sizes (e.g., 4x4 instead of 16x16) and enhanced data augmentation, measuring changes in accuracy and convergence.  
3. Implement triplet network with online hard example mining (semi-hard and hard negatives) and compare against random sampling in terms of training time, convergence speed, and final AUC.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: What is the performance of hybrid CNN-Transformer models for scribe verification?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Future Work in Section IV proposes "Exploring hybrid CNN–Transformer architectures that combine efficient local feature extraction with global attention mechanisms."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only evaluated pure CNN and ViT models; hybrids were not tested.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Train hybrid architectures on Tsinghua Bamboo Slips and MCCD datasets using the same framework, and compare AUC and accuracy against the best-performing models.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can hard positive and hard negative mining strategies improve the efficiency and effectiveness of triplet-based scribe verification?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Future Work item 2: "Incorporating hard positive and hard negative mining strategies to improve triplet-based training efficiency."
- **Why unresolved**: The triplet network in the study used random sampling and achieved lower performance than Siamese networks; mining was not explored.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Implement triplet networks with hard mining (e.g., online hard example mining), measure training time, convergence speed, and final AUC/accuracy compared to random sampling.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How does expanding the dataset with additional manuscript sources impact the generalization performance of scribe verification models?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Future Work item 3: "Expanding the dataset with additional manuscript sources to enable large-scale pretraining and improve generalization."
- **Why unresolved**: The study used only two datasets with limited samples; broader data could reveal overfitting or robustness issues.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Incorporate new, diverse manuscript datasets, retrain models, and evaluate on out-of-distribution test sets to measure generalization improvements.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Are few-shot or one-shot learning approaches viable for scribe verification when labeled data per scribe is scarce?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Future Work item 4: "Investigating few-shot and one-shot learning approaches to scribe verification with minimal labeled data."
- **Why unresolved**: Current methods require multiple samples per scribe; few-shot scenarios common in historical manuscripts were not addressed.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Adapt models to few-shot learning frameworks (e.g., prototypical or relation networks) and test with limited shots (1-5) per scribe, reporting accuracy and AUC.

### Open Question 5
- **Question**: What is the performance variability of metric learning methods for scribe verification across different random seeds and sampling strategies?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Future Work item 5: "Including multi-seed experiments to quantify performance variability and further assess the robustness of metric learning methods that are sensitive to initialization and sampling strategies."
- **Why unresolved**: The study used a fixed random seed (42) and did not report variance; robustness to initialization is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Perform multiple training runs with varying random seeds for initialization and data sampling, compute statistics (mean, std) of key metrics (AUC, accuracy) to assess stability.

## Limitations
- **Dataset limitations**: The Tsinghua Bamboo Slips dataset is small (~1,200 fragments) and lacks modern Chinese handwriting controls, raising concerns about generalization. Details and balance of the MCCD subset are unspecified.
- **Unexplained architectural results**: Vision Transformers significantly underperformed CNNs, contradicting trends in other vision tasks; reasons such as data scarcity or positional encoding suitability for text strokes are not investigated.
- **Framework component isolation**: The proposed unified framework with dynamic sampling lacks ablation studies against standard fixed sampling or curriculum strategies, making it unclear whether gains are due to sampling or backbone choices.
