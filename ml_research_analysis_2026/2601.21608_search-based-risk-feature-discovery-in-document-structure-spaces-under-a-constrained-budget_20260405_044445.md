# Search-Based Risk Feature Discovery in Document Structure Spaces under a Constrained Budget

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.21608
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21608
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: No single search strategy dominates for discovering diverse IDP failures under a fixed evaluation budget; a portfolio of solvers uncovers more distinct failure modes and enables more accurate risk modeling.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses validation of Intelligent Document Processing (IDP) systems under strict evaluation budgets by framing it as a Search-Based Software Testing (SBST) problem over a combinatorial space of document structural configurations ("risk features"). Fourteen search strategies—spanning evolutionary algorithms, Bayesian optimization, reinforcement learning, quality-diversity, and quantum methods—are benchmarked. Results show persistent complementarity: each solver uncovers failure modes that others miss, and these unique discoveries remain even at extended budgets. Consequently, the union of all solvers recovers the full observed failure space, while relying on any single method systematically delays discovery of important risks. The risk landscape is highly learnable (R² > 0.91) from structural features, and aggregating early trajectories from multiple solvers (a portfolio) yields superior coverage and risk assessment compared to any individual approach. These findings advocate using a diverse set of search algorithms for robust IDP validation under budget constraints.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a synthetic IDP validation framework where document configurations are encoded as binary vectors (“risk features”) capturing structural variants (layout, font, content anomalies). A deterministic oracle (the IDP system under test) processes each generated document; failures are quantified via a risk score that includes a rarity penalty. The search objective is to maximize the number of distinct failure signatures within a fixed number of oracle evaluations. The pipeline consists of a configuration generator, a document generator G(z), the IDP oracle, an error computation module, and a search policy π that proposes new configurations based on an archive of past evaluations. Fourteen algorithms are implemented (e.g., Random, GA, GP-EI, PPO, REINFORCE, MAP-Elites, QAOA) with equal budgets (1000 evaluations) and batch sizes (50) for fair comparison. Experiments measure risk metrics (max, top-10% AUC, unique layouts, diversity), solver exclusivity, cross-temporal overlap, and landscape learnability via Random Forest regression.

## Key Results
- **Complementary discovery**: Different solvers consistently uncover exclusive failure modes; pairwise exclusivity persists across budgets, indicating inherent exploration biases.
- **Portfolio benefit**: Combining early samples from all solvers yields a training set that produces more accurate risk predictors and broader coverage than any single method’s full trajectory.
- **High landscape learnability**: Random Forest models achieve R² > 0.91 on held-out configurations, confirming that risk is predictable from structural features.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

#### Mechanism 1: Persistent Solver Complementarity
**Claim**: Different search algorithms exhibit non-overlapping exploration patterns due to inherent biases, leading to exclusive discovery of failure modes even at high budgets.  
**Mechanism**: Exploitative methods (e.g., GP-EI, REINFORCE) rapidly locate high-risk basins but sample redundantly, while exploratory methods (e.g., Random, QAOA-Corr, MAP-Elites) cover broader structural regions. Their distinct update rules cause divergent trajectories in the configuration space.  
**Evidence**: Cross-temporal analysis shows early discoveries remain exclusive; pairwise exclusivity matrices reveal persistent patterns.

#### Mechanism 2: Learnable Risk Landscape
**Claim**: The risk function is highly predictable from binary structural features, enabling accurate estimation of unseen configurations.  
**Mechanism**: Risk is computed as a deterministic weighted sum of error components that depend on the risk feature vector. Machine learning models trained on evaluated samples capture these interactions, achieving R² > 0.91.  
**Evidence**: Table 3: aggregate RF R²=0.915; single-method models exceed 0.62.

#### Mechanism 3: Instant Portfolio Effect
**Claim**: Aggregating initial samples from diverse solvers immediately improves coverage and risk modeling compared to any single solver’s full budget.  
**Mechanism**: Because solvers are complementary from the first evaluations, the union of their early trajectories (e.g., first 200 per method) provides a more diverse training set than a single method can achieve in the same total samples.  
**Evidence**: Portfolio models outperform the best single-method model even when training size is strictly controlled.

## Foundational Learning

- **Search-Based Software Testing (SBST)**  
  *Why needed*: Provides a formal optimization framework for automated failure discovery under budget constraints.  
  *Quick check*: What is SBST’s primary objective in this paper, and how does it differ from traditional testing?  
  (Answer: Maximize distinct failure types within a fixed evaluation budget, not just a single error metric.)

- **Risk Feature Encoding**  
  *Why needed*: Maps document parameters (categorical, ordinal) to a binary vector so standard search heuristics (bit-flip, QUBO) can operate over the space.  
  *Quick check*: How are categorical and ordinal document parameters transformed into binary features?  
  (Answer: Categorical variables are one-hot encoded; ordinal counts are binned and then represented as binary strings.)

- **Black-box Budgeted Evaluation**  
  *Why needed*: The IDP oracle is expensive and opaque; only scalar risk and a discrete failure signature are returned, forcing sample-efficient search.  
  *Quick check*: What limited feedback does the search policy receive after each evaluation, and why does this make the problem challenging?  
  (Answer: Only a risk score and failure signature; no internal gradients or intermediate outputs, making it a black-box optimization with costly queries.)

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:  
Search policy (π) → configuration z → Document generator G(z) → IDP oracle → error computation → risk r(z) + failure signature → archive A → policy update (π)

**Critical path**: The cyclic process of proposing a configuration, generating the document, evaluating it, storing the result, and updating the search policy.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Exploration vs. exploitation is algorithm-specific (e.g., GP-EI uses acquisition functions, MAP-Elites maintains behavior niches).  
- Batch size fixed at 50 for fairness across methods.  
- Initial random samples (n_init=100) used by BO and RL methods.  
- Caching prevents re-evaluation of identical configurations.

**Failure signatures**: A binary vector where each bit corresponds to an error component; a bit is set to 1 if that error exceeds a predefined threshold. Unique signatures define distinct failure types.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. Benchmark 14 search strategies on risk metrics (max risk, top-10% AUC, unique layouts, diversity) under a budget of 1000 oracle evaluations.  
2. Compute configuration-level exclusivity and cross-temporal overlap to quantify how often solvers discover configurations missed by others.  
3. Train Random Forest regressors on data from each solver and on portfolios; assess predictive performance (R²) and compare estimated versus ground-truth risk distributions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Budget allocation across solvers**: How should a fixed evaluation budget be divided among multiple search algorithms to maximize coverage of distinct failure modes? The paper demonstrates complementarity but does not propose an allocation strategy.  
- **Generalization to real systems**: Do the observed complementarities hold for real-world IDP systems and natural documents, or are they limited to the synthetic benchmark? No validation on authentic data was performed.  
- **Mechanisms of exclusivity**: What algorithmic properties cause solvers to persistently discover exclusive failure modes? A mechanistic understanding could guide the design of more complementary search methods.

## Limitations

- The study relies on a single synthetic document generator and a deterministic IDP oracle, which may not capture the complexity and non-determinism of real systems, limiting external validity.  
- Evaluation budgets consider only the number of IDP queries, ignoring the computational overhead of search algorithms (e.g., surrogate training, RL updates), which could shift practical efficiency.  
- The risk feature encoding (binary mapping, binning) and the rarity weight λ are fixed; their impact on solver complementarity and landscape learnability remains unexplored.

## Confidence

- **Complementarity and persistence of solver discoveries**: Medium  
- **High learnability of the risk landscape**: High  
- **Portfolio superiority**: High

## Next Checks

1. Replicate the benchmark on at least two additional, diverse IDP systems (e.g., invoice and contract processing) with real documents and non-deterministic outputs to test robustness of complementarity and learnability.  
2. Measure total computational cost (time, memory) of each search strategy, including policy updates and surrogate modeling, to evaluate trade-offs under a combined budget of IDP queries and compute resources.  
3. Conduct an ablation study varying the risk feature encoding (e.g., bin sizes, one-hot schemes) and the rarity weight λ to assess sensitivity of solver complementarity and landscape predictability.
