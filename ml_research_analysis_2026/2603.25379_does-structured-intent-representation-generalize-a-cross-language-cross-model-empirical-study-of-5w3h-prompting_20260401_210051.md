# Does Structured Intent Representation Generalize? A Cross-Language, Cross-Model Empirical Study of 5W3H Prompting

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.25379
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25379
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** AI-assisted expansion of a single-sentence prompt into a structured 5W3H specification (Condition D) achieves statistically indistinguishable goal alignment from manually crafted specifications (Condition C) across Chinese, English, and Japanese.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether a structured intent representation framework (PPS, based on the 5W3H schema) generalizes effectively across languages and model architectures to reduce intent transmission loss. By comparing four prompting conditions—unstructured, raw JSON, manual 5W3H, and AI-expanded 5W3H—across three large language models and three languages, the research finds that structuring intent into a natural language rendering of 5W3H (Condition C) dramatically improves consistency and alignment compared to raw JSON or leaving the prompt unstructured. Critically, an AI-powered authoring tool that automatically expands a simple user request into a full 5W3H specification (Condition D) performs on par with expert manual crafting, democratizing access to structured prompting. The study also reveals that traditional unstructured baselines exhibit a dual-inflation bias, artificially inflating quality scores while suppressing apparent cross-model variance, which challenges their validity for assessing structured intent transmission.

## Method Summary
The experimental design evaluated 2,160 outputs generated by three LLMs (DeepSeek-V3, Qwen-max, Kimi) for 60 tasks (20 per domain: Travel, Business, Technical) in Chinese, English, and Japanese. Four conditions were tested: (A) unstructured single-sentence prompt (temperature=0.7); (B) raw JSON specification (temperature=0); (C) manually crafted 5W3H specification rendered as labeled natural language (temperature=0); (D) AI-expanded 5W3H, where DeepSeek-V3 inferred seven dimensions from the user's "What" and rendered them identically to Condition C (temperature=0). Outputs were evaluated by a separate DeepSeek-V3 instance using five sub-metrics (task_completion, structure, specificity, constraint_adherence, overall_quality) to compute a composite score and a goal alignment (GA) score. Statistical analyses (Mann-Whitney U, Kruskal-Wallis) compared conditions, with cross-model standard deviation (σ) measuring protocol reliability.

## Key Results
*   AI-expanded prompting (Condition D) yields goal alignment scores statistically indistinguishable from manually crafted 5W3H prompting (Condition C) in all three languages (p > 0.05).
*   Raw JSON prompting (Condition B) fails significantly compared to both structured natural language conditions (C and D) on goal alignment across all languages (p < 0.001), demonstrating the necessity of a native-language rendering layer.
*   Unstructured prompts (Condition A) exhibit a dual-inflation bias: they receive artificially high composite scores due to a constraint-adherence ceiling and show artificially low cross-model variance, masking divergent goal inferences among models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: The Rendering Layer is Essential for Cross-Language Generalization
*   **Claim:** Structured 5W3H intent generalizes across languages because the framework's rendering layer translates the schema into labeled natural language, which aligns with LLMs' pre-trained expectations for instruction processing, whereas raw JSON is misinterpreted as data.
*   **Mechanism:** The PPS framework decomposes intent into eight dimensions. A distinct rendering layer converts this structured data into natural language sections (e.g., "Task Goal (What): ..."). This format is processed more effectively than raw JSON (Condition B), which models treat as code or data payload rather than an instruction specification. The consistent superiority of Condition C over B across Chinese, English, and Japanese confirms this ordinal effect.
*   **Evidence:** Finding 1.2 shows C > B and D > B on Goal Alignment in all languages (p < 0.001). Section 3.2 explicitly hypothesizes that rendering into the model's "native format" is necessary.

### Mechanism 2: AI-Mediated Expansion Democratizes Structured Prompting
*   **Claim:** An AI authoring interface can match manual expert structuring because a capable expansion model can generate plausible, context-appropriate values for the seven unspecified 5W3H dimensions from a minimal "What" seed.
*   **Mechanism:** Condition D uses DeepSeek-V3 to infer Why, Who, When, etc., from a single-sentence user input. The expanded output is rendered identically to Condition C. The mechanism succeeds if the model's world knowledge allows it to generate generic but functionally adequate defaults that align with the user's unstated intent for the purpose of transmission.
*   **Evidence:** Finding 2.1 and Table 3 show no significant difference between C and D on Goal Alignment (p > 0.05, Cohen's d ≈ 0). Section 6.1 concludes a single-sentence input achieves comparable intent alignment.

### Mechanism 3: The Dual-Inflation Bias Invalidates Unstructured Baselines
*   **Claim:** Unstructured prompts (Condition A) are flawed evaluation baselines because they produce two contradictory artifacts: (1) artificially high composite scores and (2) artificially low apparent cross-model variance.
*   **Mechanism:** **Inflation 1 (Composite Score):** The composite metric includes `constraint_adherence`. Condition A specifies no constraints, so this sub-score defaults to its maximum, creating a ceiling. **Inflation 2 (Cross-Model Variance):** With no constraints, all models generate high-quality, model-default outputs, leading to uniformly high composite scores and thus low variance (σ). This low σ is spurious—it reflects convergence on a default distribution, not convergence on a shared, specified intent. The Goal Alignment metric, which requires judges to infer the "most likely intent," reveals higher variance, proving models interpret vague prompts differently.
*   **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 5.3/ Finding 3.1 describe the dual-inflation. Table 3/4 show A has the lowest composite σ in ZH/JA but higher GA σ. Section 6.2 details the inflation mechanics.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: 5W3H Intent Dimensions (What, Why, Who, When, Where, How-to-do, How-much, How-feel)**
    *   *Why needed:* This is the core schema PPS uses to decompose vague requests into a complete, structured specification. It defines the eight facets an ideal prompt should communicate.
    *   *Quick check:* Which dimension specifies the target audience's technical expertise level? (Who) Which specifies the desired tone? (How-feel).
*   **Concept: Goal Alignment (GA) vs. Composite Score**
    *   *Why needed:* GA measures true *intent transmission* by anchoring evaluation to the user's actual intent (explicit for C/D, inferred for A). The composite metric is vulnerable to the dual-inflation bias.
    *   *Quick check:* Why might an output following a perfect 5W3H spec score lower on composite but higher on GA than an unconstrained output? (Composite penalizes any constraint violation, which structured specs explicitly list and risk; GA rewards matching the specified intent regardless of constraint penalties).
*   **Concept: Cross-Model Standard Deviation (σ) as a Protocol Reliability Metric**
    *   *Why needed:* A valid intent representation protocol should yield consistent interpretations across different receiver models (LLMs). Low σ in structured conditions suggests reliability. However, low σ in Condition A is an artifact.
    *   *Quick check:* What does a low composite σ for Condition A in Japanese actually indicate? (It indicates models all produce high-scoring, default-style outputs due to lack of constraints, not that they share a common interpretation of the vague prompt).

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** User Input -> Condition Selector -> [Path A: Unstructured Prompt] OR [Path B: Raw JSON Wrapper] OR [Path C: Manual PPS Renderer] OR [Path D: Expander -> PPS Renderer] -> Target Generator (LLM) -> Output -> LLM-as-Judge (DeepSeek-V3) -> Scores -> Metric Aggregator.
*   **Critical path (Condition D workflow):** User Single-Sentence -> Condition D Expander (DeepSeek-V3) -> Full 5W3H Spec -> PPS Renderer (Natural Language) -> Target Generator (e.g., Qwen-max) -> Model Output -> LLM-as-Judge -> Scores (Composite, GA).
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Temperature Confound:** Condition A uses 0.7 for ecological validity, while B/C/D use 0 for determinism. This confounds structure with stochasticity in A-vs-structured comparisons.
    *   **Baseline Validity:** Condition A is ecologically realistic but metric-inflated; Condition B tests pure structured data but fails due to rendering gap.
    *   **Expansion Quality:** Condition D's performance is lower-bounded by the expansion model's capability (DeepSeek-V3). A weaker expander would degrade D's scores.
    *   **Judge Asymmetry:** For GA, judges use explicit PPS specs for C/D but must infer a gold intent for A, potentially biasing comparisons.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    1.  **Raw JSON Failure:** Condition B's consistently low scores, especially in English, indicate the rendering layer is indispensable for current LLMs.
    2.  **Composite Inflation Artifact:** Condition A's high composite scores paired with sometimes lower GA than C/D reveal the baseline is misleading.
    3.  **Spurious Low Variance:** Condition A's low composite σ in ZH/JA is not true reliability but an artifact of unconstrained score ceilings.
    4.  **English Format Dissociation:** Condition C's very low composite but high GA in English suggests the structured headers trigger format-focused generation that misses quality dimensions the composite rewards.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Replicate Core Ordinal Patterns:** Run the 3-language, 4-condition study with a new model family (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude) to test if C > B, C ≈ D, and B ≪ A hold beyond Chinese-origin LLMs. *Controls for model-family bias.*
    2.  **Isolate Temperature Confound:** Run all four conditions at temperature=0 to directly test the effect of structure vs. sampling randomness. *Expected: C/D advantage over A diminishes but likely remains.*
    3.  **Test Expansion Model Robustness:** Replace DeepSeek-V3 in Condition D with a significantly weaker model (e.g., Llama-3-8B). Hypothesis: D's GA scores will drop below C's. *Tests the core assumption about expansion quality.*

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** Under what conditions do AI agents spontaneously reuse PPS Instruction IDs across independent sessions, and what mechanisms enable this cross-session persistence?
    *   *Basis:* Appendix A documents a single case; Section 6.7 states systematic investigation is future work.
    *   *Why unresolved:* The observation is anecdotal; generalizability across agents/tasks is unknown.
*   **Open Question 2:** How does temperature sampling variability confound the comparison between unstructured (A) and structured (B/C/D) prompts, and what is the isolated effect of structural completeness on goal alignment?
    *   *Basis:* Section 6.7 notes the confound (A at 0.7 vs. B/C/D at 0) and recommends a robustness experiment.
    *   *Why unresolved:* Current results conflate structure with stochasticity.
*   **Open Question 3:** Does the judge's access to explicit 5W3H specifications inflate goal alignment scores for structured conditions (C/D) relative to an external gold-standard user intent?
    *   *Basis:* Section 6.7 identifies an asymmetry (A judged on inferred intent, C/D on explicit spec) and warns it may advantage C/D.
    *   *Why unresolved:* The evaluation method may overestimate true intent transmission for structured prompts.
*   **Open Question 4:** Does the overlap of DeepSeek-V3 as expansion model (D), generation model, and LLM-as-Judge bias the evaluation, particularly for Condition D?
    *   *Basis:* Section 6.7 describes the "DeepSeek triple role" confound and calls for independent models.
    *   *Why unresolved:* Self-preference or stylistic bias could inflate D's scores.
*   **Open Question 5:** How do novice users interact with the AI-assisted authoring interface (Condition D) in practice, and does user modification of AI-expanded prompts affect outcomes?
    *   *Basis:* Section 6.1 notes Condition D used unmodified expansions (a lower bound); Section 6.7 calls for a user study on usability.
    *   *Why unresolved:* Real-world usability, trust, and iterative refinement patterns are unknown.

## Limitations
*   **Missing core artifacts:** The exact 60-task set and manually crafted PPS specifications in all three languages are undisclosed, preventing direct replication of stimuli.
*   **Evaluation opacity:** The precise LLM-as-judge prompts, especially how Goal Alignment is operationalized differently for Condition A versus C/D, are not documented, risking scorer bias.
*   **Confounded design:** Condition A uses temperature=0.7 while structured conditions use temperature=0, conflating prompt structure with sampling stochasticity in comparative analyses.

## Confidence
*   **AI-expanded PPS ≈ Manual PPS (C ≈ D):** High. Statistically indistinguishable Goal Alignment (p > 0.05) across languages is a robust, directly reported result.
*   **Raw JSON Failure (B ≪ C/D):** High. Consistent underperformance of JSON across metrics and languages is strongly evidenced.
*   **Dual-inflation bias in unstructured prompts:** Medium. The proposed mechanism (constraint ceiling + spurious variance) is plausible and aligned with data patterns, but relies on assumptions about metric validity that could be tested with alternative scoring.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-model-family replication:** Re-run the 4-condition study using non-Chinese-origin LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-3.5) to test generalizability beyond the DeepSeek/Qwen/Kimi cluster.
2.  **Temperature disentanglement:** Execute all four conditions at identical temperature (e.g., 0) to isolate the effect of prompt structure from sampling randomness.
3.  **Expansion-model ablation:** Replace DeepSeek-V3 in Condition D with a weaker model (e.g., Llama-3-8B). Failure to maintain C ≈ D would confirm reliance on high-capacity expansion.
