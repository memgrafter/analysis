# GeoChemAD: Benchmarking Unsupervised Geochemical Anomaly Detection for Mineral Exploration

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.13068
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13068
*   **Reference Count:** 0
*   **Primary Result:** GeoChemFormer achieves state-of-the-art unsupervised geochemical anomaly detection with an average AUC of 0.7712 across eight diverse, open-source subsets, demonstrating superior robustness and generalization over statistical, machine learning, and deep generative baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces GeoChemAD, the first open-source benchmark for unsupervised geochemical anomaly detection (GAD), addressing a critical lack of reproducibility in mineral exploration. The dataset comprises eight diverse subsets from Western Australian government surveys, covering various sampling media, regions, and target elements. To leverage this benchmark, the authors propose GeoChemFormer, a two-stage transformer framework. It first self-supervisedly learns spatial context from local neighborhoods and then models multi-element dependencies conditioned on this context. Anomaly scores are derived from reconstruction error. Experiments show GeoChemFormer significantly outperforms prior methods, establishing a new standard for GAD research and providing a foundation for building more generalizable models.

## Method Summary
GeoChemFormer is a two-stage transformer model for unsupervised GAD. **Stage 1 (Spatial Context Learning):** For each sample, its K-nearest neighbors are retrieved. A transformer encoder processes a sequence containing the query's coordinates and its neighbors' spatial offsets and elemental concentrations, with the objective of predicting the target element's concentration at the query point. This pretrains a spatial encoder (`Esc`) that produces a contextualized representation (`q'`) for every sample. **Stage 2 (Anomaly Detection):** The pretrained `Esc` is frozen. For each sample, its `q'` vector is combined with tokens for all element identities and concentrations. A second transformer encoder models their interdependencies, and a linear decoder reconstructs the concentration vector. The mean squared reconstruction error serves as the anomaly score. The entire pipeline is trained and evaluated on the new GeoChemAD benchmark.

## Key Results
*   Introduced **GeoChemAD**, an open, diverse benchmark with eight subsets, enabling reproducible GAD research.
*   Proposed **GeoChemFormer** achieved the highest average AUC (0.7712) across all subsets, outperforming statistical (e.g., SA, Moran’s I), classic ML (e.g., Isolation Forest), and deep generative (AE, VAE) baselines.
*   Demonstrated superior **robustness** (lower performance variance across runs) and **generalization** (consistent high ranks) compared to all other models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Learning Local Spatial Context
*   **Claim:** Predicting a target element's concentration from its local K-nearest neighbors forces the model to learn spatially coherent geochemical patterns (e.g., dispersion halos, background gradients) that are indicative of mineralization.
*   **Mechanism:** A transformer encoder processes a sequence of `[query_2D_PE, neighbor_offsets, neighbor_concentrations]` to predict the query's target value. The resulting latent vector `q'` encodes the co-variation of elements within the local neighborhood.
*   **Core Assumption:** Anomalous (mineralized) samples exist within a neighborhood context that is statistically distinct from the background patterns learned during pretraining.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [section 4.1] Describes the SCL pretraining objective. [section 6.3, Fig 4(d)] Shows performance peaks after pretraining, indicating effective representation learning. [section 6.1, Table 3] GeoChemFormer (T2) outperforms models without explicit spatial modeling (AE, VAE).

### Mechanism 2: Modelling Multi-Element Dependencies Conditioned on Context
*   **Claim:** Modelling the relationships among all elemental concentrations, given the learned spatial context, generates a reconstruction error sensitive to the multivariate geochemical signatures of mineralization.
*   **Mechanism:** The frozen `q'` is concatenated with element tokens and fed to a second transformer. The model reconstructs all element concentrations. Anomalies are samples where the multivariate pattern deviates from the "normal" relationships learned for that spatial context.
*   **Core Assumption:** Mineralization affects a *suite* of pathfinder and related elements, creating a correlated multi-element signal, not just a univariate spike in the target commodity.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [section 4.2] Details the element dependency modeling and anomaly score formula. [section 6.1, Table 3] The full GeoChemFormer (T2) significantly outperforms the vanilla transformer (T1) that lacks the two-stage separation. [section 6.4, Fig 4(f)] Shows GeoChemFormer's anomaly maps are more spatially coherent with known deposits.

### Mechanism 3: Self-Supervised Pretraining for Generalizable Representations
*   **Claim:** Decoupling spatial context learning (Stage 1) from multivariate reconstruction learning (Stage 2) via self-supervised pretraining yields more robust and transferable features than end-to-end training.
*   **Mechanism:** The spatial encoder (`Esc`) is trained on a well-defined, auxiliary task (spatial prediction) and then frozen. This prevents its representations from collapsing or overfitting to the noise patterns of the final reconstruction loss.
*   **Core Assumption:** A representation useful for predicting a single element from its neighborhood captures fundamental, spatially coherent geochemical structures that are also beneficial for detecting multivariate anomalies.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] Mentions "target-element-aware geochemical representations" via pretraining. [section 6.3, Fig 4(d)] Performance improves with pretraining epochs, supporting its benefit. [section 6.1, Table 3] GeoChemFormer outperforms end-to-end trained AE/VAE variants.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Compositional Data Analysis & Closure.
    *   **Why needed:** Geochemical concentrations are compositional (sum to a constant). Using raw values creates spurious correlations. Models must address this (e.g., via CLR/ILR transforms) to avoid being misled by major elements.
    *   **Quick Check:** Why might a model using raw ppm values flag a sample as anomalous simply because SiO2 is very high, even if all pathfinder element ratios are normal?
*   **Concept:** Spatial Autocorrelation & Local Neighborhoods.
    *   **Why needed:** Geochemical fields are spatially continuous. The value at a point is dependent on its neighbors. GeoChemFormer's core mechanism explicitly models this via K-NN retrieval.
    *   **Quick Check:** If sampling density doubles in a region, what happens to the average distance to the 32nd nearest neighbor? Why is a fixed K potentially problematic across datasets with different densities?
*   **Concept:** Unsupervised Anomaly Detection via Reconstruction Error.
    *   **Why needed:** The task is unsupervised. Models like AE, VAE, and GeoChemFormer are trained to reconstruct "normal" data well; high reconstruction error on new samples indicates anomaly.
    *   **Quick Check:** In a reconstruction-based method, what is the expected anomaly score for (a) an exact copy of a training sample, and (b) a sample with all element concentrations multiplied by 10?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Input CSV `[x, y, conc_vector]` -> Preprocessing (handle NaNs, CLR/ILR, feature selection) -> For each sample: K-NN retrieval (KD-tree) -> Build token sequence `[target_token, query_PE, neighbor_tokens(offset+conc)]` -> Stage 1: Transformer Encoder (`Esc`) (predict target) -> Freeze `Esc` -> For each sample: use frozen `q'` from `Esc` -> Build token sequence `[Wg*q', element_token_1...C]` -> Stage 2: Transformer Encoder -> Linear Decoder -> Reconstruct all concs -> Compute `s_i = mean(MSE(recon, actual))` -> Rank by `s_i` for AUC evaluation.

**Critical path:** Sample -> K-NN Retrieval -> Token Building for SCL -> `Esc` (frozen) -> `q'` -> Token Building for EDM -> Element Encoder -> Reconstruction -> Anomaly Score.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **K (neighbors):** Small K (e.g., 16) focuses on sharp, local contrasts (rock chips/soil). Large K (e.g., 256) captures regional trends (sediments). Larger K increases compute and may dilute local signal.
*   **Preprocessing:** Choice of compositional transform (CLR/ILR/Raw) and feature selection method (manual/PCA/LLM) significantly impacts performance and is dataset/model-dependent.
*   **Pretraining Epochs:** Too few -> `q'` not learned. Too many -> overfit to spatial prediction noise, harming downstream anomaly detection generalization.

**Failure signatures:**
*   **AUC ~ 0.5:** Likely catastrophic preprocessing (ignoring closure), `K=1`, or no detectable anomalies in the region.
*   **High variance across runs:** Unstable K-NN due to extreme sampling sparsity/density, or noisy/irrelevant feature set.
*   **Poor spatial localization (high DTD):** Detected anomalies are far from known deposits. May indicate model capturing non-mineralization signals (contamination, lithology) or wrong spatial scale (K).
*   **VAE/AE beating transformer:** Dataset anomalies are purely univariate/local and don't benefit from modeling long-range spatial dependencies or element interactions.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Ablation: Pretraining vs. No Pretraining.** Train GeoChemFormer with and without freezing `Esc` and the Stage 1 loss. Expect the pretrained/frozen version to show higher average AUC and lower variance.
2.  **Sensitivity to K.** Sweep K (e.g., 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256) on one sediment (e.g., `sed1`) and one rock chip (e.g., `rock2`) subset. Expect `sed1` AUC to increase with K; `rock2` AUC to peak at small K and decline.
3.  **Preprocessing Pipeline Comparison.** On a single subset (e.g., `soil2`), compare: (a) Raw vs. CLR vs. ILR, (b) No selection vs. PCA vs. LLM-selected features. Track AUC. Expect ILR + automated selection (PCA/LLM) to yield best/most stable results.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** How do different geochemical preprocessing strategies interact with model architecture to optimize unsupervised GAD performance across varying dataset characteristics?
*   **Basis:** Section 6.2 shows preprocessing effectiveness varies by dataset and model (e.g., ILR helps transformers more than AE). No unified framework exists.
*   **Why unresolved:** No large-scale factorial experiment exploring all interactions (transform * selection * interpolation) across all baselines and subsets.
*   **Resolution Evidence:** A comprehensive factorial design experiment with statistical analysis of main effects and interactions.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** Can unsupervised GAD models reliably distinguish anomalies linked to target element mineralization from spurious ones (e.g., contamination, lithology)?
*   **Basis:** Introduction states anomalies may not correspond to actual mineralization. Evaluation uses deposit points as positives, not mechanistic process labels.
*   **Why unresolved:** Validation against "mineralization-related" vs. "non-mineralization-related" expert labels (e.g., from drill core/geology) is absent.
*   **Resolution Evidence:** Evaluation on datasets with independently verified, process-characterized anomaly labels. Model confidence/attention should correlate with these labels.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** To what extent does GeoChemFormer's performance generalize beyond the geological/geochemical contexts of Western Australia?
*   **Basis:** GeoChemAD is restricted to WA surveys (Au, Cu, Ni, W; soil, sediment, rock chip). Generalization is inferred, not proven.
*   **Why unresolved:** No testing on external datasets from other continents, terrains, or with different element suites (e.g., REEs).
*   **Resolution Evidence:** Direct application (without fine-tuning) to public surveys from other regions (e.g., USGS, Geoscience Australia) and analysis of performance degradation.

## Limitations
*   GeoChemAD is geographically restricted to Western Australian surveys; generalization to other continents, survey styles (borehole, glacial), and element suites (e.g., REEs) is untested.
*   The K-NN spatial module assumes anomalies are local deviations; it may underperform for regionally extensive geochemical trends.
*   Performance is highly sensitive to preprocessing choices (compositional transform, feature selection, K), and the AUC metric does not penalize spatially dispersed false positives.

## Confidence
*   **GeoChemAD benchmark utility:** **High** – It is open, diverse, and already adopted.
*   **GeoChemFormer’s superior performance on GeoChemAD:** **Medium** – Statistically significant on the provided datasets, but out-of-distribution validation is missing.
*   **Spatial context learning mechanism:** **Medium** – Ablations support its contribution, but direct causal evidence is limited.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-regional evaluation:** Test the published GeoChemFormer model (fixed hyperparameters) on public non-Australian geochemical survey datasets (e.g., USGS) to quantify geographic robustness.
2.  **Hyperparameter & preprocessing sensitivity sweep:** On a single GeoChemAD subset, vary K (8–256) and key preprocessing choices (transform, feature selection). Report AUC stability and spatial coherence (mean distance to known deposits) to identify robust settings.
3.  **Update baselines with modern models:** Re-benchmark GeoChemFormer against recent anomaly detection architectures (e.g., Graph Neural Networks, modern transformer variants) to ensure its lead persists beyond the selected baselines.
