# Learning to Approximate Uniform Facility Location via Graph Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.13155
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13155
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Learnt GNN achieves near-optimal facility location costs (optimality ratios 1.002–1.012) with 4ms inference and provable generalization to 10× larger graphs.

## Executive Summary
The Uniform Facility Location problem seeks to place facilities on a metric space to minimize total opening and connection costs. This paper combines the robustness of approximation algorithms with the adaptability of machine learning by designing a fully differentiable message-passing neural network (MPNN) that embeds a distributed O(log n)-approximation algorithm. The MPNN is trained end-to-end using an unsupervised loss derived from the expected facility location cost, avoiding expensive supervision from integer linear programming solvers.

Empirical results on synthetic and real-world graphs show the approach attains near-optimal solutions with optimality ratios between 1.002 and 1.012, significantly outperforming non-learned baselines and matching ILP solvers while requiring only 4ms inference. Theoretically, the method provably generalizes to instances up to ten times larger than those seen during training while maintaining constant approximation guarantees.

## Method Summary
The method processes a facility location instance as a graph with vertices representing client locations and edges for pairs within distance 1. The core architecture is an MPNN that, for each vertex, aggregates information from neighbors to estimate a critical radius. Discretized thresholds are used: multiple feed-forward networks compute whether the true radius exceeds each threshold, and these are combined into a radius estimate. A probability head then maps the estimated radius and graph size (log n) to an opening probability for each vertex. The final solution is obtained by independently sampling facilities according to these probabilities and assigning clients to the nearest open facility.

Training minimizes a closed-form expression for the expected total cost, which is differentiable with respect to the opening probabilities. This unsupervised loss avoids the need for optimal solutions. The MPNN uses order-normalized aggregation and Lipschitz-continuous components, enabling a theoretical guarantee that parameters learned on small graphs transfer to larger ones with bounded error.

## Key Results
- Near-optimal solutions: optimality ratios 1.002–1.012 on synthetic and real-world graphs, outperforming baselines (SimpleUniformFL, UniformFLRecursionStart) and matching ILP.
- Efficient inference: ~4ms per instance, comparable to ILP solvers while scaling to larger graphs.
- Provable generalization: guarantees constant approximation even on graphs up to 10× larger than training set.
- Robustness: works on real city maps where ILP fails to converge, handling metric irregularities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The MPNN architecture encodes a distributed O(log n)-approximation algorithm by transforming local neighborhood information into radius estimates and probabilistic facility decisions.
- **Mechanism**: For each vertex x, the MPNN aggregates features from neighbors within distance ≤1 (edges in GS) to compute t(i)x values that estimate whether the true radius rx exceeds discretization thresholds a_i. These are combined into a radius estimate r̂x, which then determines the opening probability p_x = min(1, c·log(n)·r̂x) via a small feed-forward network. The final solution is obtained by independently opening facilities according to these probabilities and assigning unassigned clients to nearby open facilities.
- **Core assumption**: Local aggregation over edges with weight ≤1 suffices to estimate radii with bounded error, which holds because the radius rx is defined by a continuous function over the ball B(x, rx) (Equation 2), and the discretized thresholds allow capturing when φ_x(r) crosses 1.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "A fully differentiable MPNN that embeds a distributed O(log n)-approximation algorithm... avoiding the need for solver supervision."
  - [section 3.2]: Details the FNN-parameterized computation of t(i)x from neighbor distances and the construction of r̂x and p_x.
  - [corpus]: Neighbor papers (e.g., "Are Graph Neural Networks Optimal Approximation Algorithms?") show GNNs can mimic combinatorial algorithms, but direct evidence for this specific mechanism comes from the paper’s own analysis.
- **Break condition**: If the metric space has pathological structure where local neighborhoods do not well-approximate the global ball B(x, rx) (e.g., highly disconnected graphs with long-range dependencies), the radius estimate error may exceed Δ, leading to worse than O(log n) approximation. Proposition 4 shows a limit: constant-depth deterministic MPNNs cannot achieve better than Ω(log n) in the worst case.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Unsupervised training is feasible by minimizing a closed-form expression for the expected facility location cost, bypassing optimal solution supervision.
- **Mechanism**: The loss L(p) = E[cost(X, p)] (Equation 5) is derived analytically from the probabilistic opening mechanism. It decomposes into expected opening cost (∑ p_f + ∑ ∏_{x:d(x,f)<1}(1-p_x)) and expected connection cost (∑_x ∑_{f:d(x,f)<1} d(x,f)·p_f·∏_{z:d(x,z)<d(x,f)}(1-p_z)). This expression is differentiable with respect to the p_x outputs of the MPNN, enabling end-to-end gradient-based training.
- **Core assumption**: Minimizing the expected cost over the training distribution correlates with finding low-cost solutions on that distribution; the gradient of L(p) provides a useful signal for adjusting the MPNN parameters that produce p.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "trained end-to-end on an unsupervised loss derived from expected cost, avoiding expensive solver supervision."
  - [section 3.2]: Derives Equation 5 and explains how it is used as a differentiable training objective.
  - [section 4]: Empirical results show the trained MPNN improves over the non-learned SimpleUniformFL, supporting the effectiveness of this loss.
- **Break condition**: If the distribution of training instances has high variance or the expected cost estimator has excessive Monte Carlo noise during training (e.g., due to many small probabilities), gradient estimates may be unstable, leading to poor convergence. The loss assumes pairwise distances are distinct; ties could cause non-differentiable assignments.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Parameters learned on small instances generalize to arbitrarily larger graphs due to order-normalized message passing and Lipschitz continuity.
- **Mechanism**: The MPNN uses order-normalized sum aggregation (dividing summed messages by n) and Lipschitz-continuous FNNs. This makes the function mapping graph instance to opening probabilities Lipschitz continuous with respect to a pseudo-metric d on graph vertices (Proposition 15). Training on a finite ε-cover of the space of small graphs ensures that for any larger graph, there exists a nearby training point whose learned parameters transfer with bounded error.
- **Core assumption**: The class of UniFL instances of size n is "covered" by a finite set of small graphs in a suitable metric space; the MPNN’s Lipschitz constant M_θ is bounded for learned parameters. This relies on the architectural bias (order-normalized aggregation) aligning with the scaling of radii (which are O(1) in typical metrics).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "provably generalizes to instances up to 10× larger than training while maintaining constant approximation guarantees."
  - [section 3.4]: Proves Proposition 6 using covering arguments and Lipschitz continuity of the MPNN.
  - [section 4.2]: Empirical size generalization results (Table 2, Table 7) show near-constant optimality ratios on graphs up to 10× larger.
- **Break condition**: If the training distribution does not cover the test distribution in the Lipschitz metric (e.g., training on low-dimensional Euclidean graphs but testing on highly irregular road networks), generalization error may increase. The guarantee also depends on the Lipschitz constant not exploding during training; if learned parameters lead to unbounded sensitivity, coverage may fail.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Input Graph Encoder -> Radius Estimation Module -> Probability Head -> (Loss Module for training / Post-Processing Sampler for inference)

**Critical path**  
Graph → MPNN (t(i)x, r̂x, p_x) → loss L(p) → gradient → weight updates → improved p_x → better expected cost

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Discretization granularity*: Finer bins (larger k) reduce radius approximation error Δ but increase FNN size and computation.
- *Depth vs. approximation*: Proposition 4 shows constant-depth MPNNs cannot achieve o(log n) approximation; going deeper might help empirically but loses the worst-case guarantee.
- *Order normalization*: Used for size generalization (Section 3.4) but may affect learning dynamics compared to sum aggregation.

**Failure signatures**  
- Radius estimates r̂x systematically deviate from true r_x (measure by comparing to post-hoc radius computation on validation set).
- Training loss plateaus at a high value relative to baseline SimpleUniformFL.
- Generalization fails on larger graphs: optimality ratio increases sharply with size.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Baseline comparison**: Train on small synthetic graphs (e.g., Geo-1000-10) and compare total cost and ratio against non-learned SimpleUniformFL and UniformFLRecursionStart. Expect near-optimal ratios (1.002–1.012) and balanced opening/connection costs.
2. **Size generalization test**: Take the model trained on 1000-vertex graphs and evaluate on 2000, 5000, 10000-vertex graphs from the same distribution. Expect minimal ratio increase (e.g., 1.002 → 1.003).
3. **Real-world robustness**: Test on city map graphs (Paris, London, etc.) where ILP fails to converge. Expect the MPNN to outperform baselines in total cost and show stable performance despite metric irregularities.

## Limitations
- Generalization assumes order-normalized aggregation and bounded Lipschitz constant; violation on irregular metrics could break guarantees.
- Unsupervised loss may suffer high variance with many small probabilities; distance ties could cause non-differentiability, risking unstable training.
- Evaluation limited to ≤10k vertices and Euclidean/city graphs; larger or non-metric graphs untested.

## Confidence
- MPNN embeds O(log n) algorithm: Medium
- Unsupervised loss is effective: High
- Generalization to larger graphs: Medium
