# Multi-Task GRPO: Reliable LLM Reasoning Across Tasks
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.05547
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05547
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MT-GRPO improves worst-task accuracy by 16–28% over GRPO and reaches 50% accuracy 50% faster, while maintaining competitive average performance.

## Executive Summary
Multi-task reinforcement learning (RL) post-training for large language models using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) often suffers from imbalanced task performance because some tasks yield zero gradients, leading to uneven learning across tasks. To address this, the paper introduces Multi‑Task GRPO (MT‑GRPO), which combines improvement‑aware task reweighting with a ratio‑preserving sampler. The reweighting mechanism dynamically prioritizes underperforming or slowly improving tasks using both task-level rewards and per‑step improvement signals, while the sampler ensures that after filtering zero‑gradient prompts, the intended task proportions are maintained in each batch. Experiments on 3‑task and 9‑task reasoning benchmarks show that MT‑GRPO achieves 16–28% absolute improvement in worst‑task accuracy over standard GRPO and 6% over DAPO, keeps average accuracy competitive, and converges 50% faster to 50% worst‑task accuracy in the 3‑task setting.

## Method Summary
MT‑GRPO extends standard GRPO for multi‑task post‑training with two core components. First, **improvement‑aware task reweighting** computes a dynamic weight per task from a combination of its cumulative reward and its per‑step improvement rate, giving higher weight to tasks that lag or progress slowly. Second, a **ratio‑preserving sampler** adjusts the batch after discarding zero‑gradient prompts so that the original target task mixture proportions are retained; this ensures the reweighting actually influences gradient updates. By integrating these, MT‑GRPO balances learning across tasks without sacrificing overall performance, directly tackling the gradient imbalance and zero‑gradient prompt problems that plague naive multi‑task GRPO.

## Key Results
- **16–28% absolute improvement in worst‑task accuracy** over standard GRPO and **6% over DAPO** on multi‑task reasoning benchmarks.
- **50% faster convergence** to 50% worst‑task accuracy in the 3‑task setting.
- **Competitive average accuracy** maintained relative to baseline methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MT‑GRPO’s mechanisms directly counter the causes of imbalance. The **improvement‑aware reweighting** adapts to both absolute task performance and learning dynamics, ensuring that struggling tasks receive stronger gradient signals. The **ratio‑preserving sampler** guarantees that after removing zero‑gradient prompts, the batch still reflects the desired task distribution; without this, the reweighting could be negated by a biased batch that drops underperforming tasks entirely. Together, they create a stable training dynamic where every task contributes appropriately, leading to reliable gains in the worst‑performing tasks while preserving overall accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Prompt -> ZeroGradientFilter -> RatioPreservingSampler -> GRPOTrainer(ImprovementTracker, TaskReweighter) -> PolicyUpdate  

**Critical Path:**  
1. Sample a batch uniformly from all tasks.  
2. Filter out prompts that produce zero gradient.  
3. Apply ratio‑preserving sampling to restore original task proportions.  
4. Compute task weights via improvement‑aware reweighting (using cumulative reward and per‑step improvement).  
5. Scale each task’s GRPO loss contribution by its weight.  
6. Perform a policy gradient update.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- Aggressive reweighting can rescue weak tasks but may destabilize strong ones.  
- Ratio‑preserving sampler adds computational overhead and may reduce batch diversity if constraints are too strict.  
- Tracking per‑step improvement requires extra memory and may be noisy for small batches.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Worst‑task accuracy plateaus while average improves, indicating reweighting or sampler failure.  
- The sampler repeatedly fails to assemble a valid batch (e.g., too many zero‑gradient prompts), causing training slowdown.  
- Task weights explode or vanish, leading to gradient explosions or dead tasks.  
- Over‑suppression of high‑performing tasks harms their accuracy and overall balance.

**First Experiments:**  
1. **Ablation of the ratio‑preserving sampler:** Train without it to quantify its impact on worst‑task accuracy and gradient balance.  
2. **Sensitivity to reweighting hyperparameters:** Vary the strength of improvement‑aware weighting to observe trade‑offs.  
3. **Cross‑benchmark validation:** Apply MT‑GRPO to a different multi‑task suite (e.g., BBH) to test generalizability.
