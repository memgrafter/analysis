# VLM2Rec: Resolving Modality Collapse in Vision-Language Model Embedders for Multimodal Sequential Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17450  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17450  
- **Reference Count:** 40  
- **Primary Result:** VLM2Rec resolves modality collapse in VLM embedders for multimodal sequential recommendation, achieving up to 32.07% relative gain in N@20 over VLMSFT on Amazon datasets.

## Executive Summary
Modality collapse in vision‑language model (VLM) embedders is a critical issue for multimodal sequential recommendation, where standard contrastive fine‑tuning amplifies imbalance between text and visual modalities, leaving the visual modality under‑optimized. VLM2Rec tackles this problem by introducing two complementary objectives: Weak‑modality Penalized Contrastive Learning (LWPCL), which dynamically increases negative penalties for the user‑specific weak modality to restore its discriminative power; and Cross‑Modal Relational Topology Regularization (LCRTR), which preserves cross‑modal geometric consistency. The framework employs external fusion (separate text/image prompts with element‑wise summation) to minimize interference during encoding. Experiments on four Amazon datasets demonstrate consistent state‑of‑the‑art improvements for both direct recommendation (Task 1) and downstream sequential recommender initialization (Task 2). Ablations confirm that both objectives are essential, and the approach robustly scales across VLM architectures and parameter sizes.

## Method Summary
VLM2Rec fine‑tunes a pre‑trained vision‑language model (e.g., Qwen2.5‑VL‑3B) using LoRA. Input sequences are constructed separately for text (item titles) and images; each is processed by the VLM via modality‑specific prompts, yielding sequence embeddings \(z^T_u\) and \(z^V_u\). These are fused by simple element‑wise summation to form the final user representation \(z_u\). Candidate items are encoded similarly to obtain \(e_i^T\) and \(e_i^V\) and summed to \(e_i\). The loss combines: (1) LWPCL, a contrastive loss where the negative term is weighted by a user‑specific penalty \(w_{u,pen}>1\) derived from the discriminative margin gap between the weak and strong modalities; (2) LCRTR, which computes softmax‑normalized similarity distributions \(P^T_i\) and \(P^V_i\) of sequences against in‑batch candidates within each modality and minimizes their bidirectional KL divergence to align relational topologies. Optimization uses AdamW. Two recommendation scenarios are evaluated: Task 1 directly ranks items using mean‑pooled historical item embeddings; Task 2 uses the fine‑tuned item embeddings (optionally adapted by a linear projection) to initialize a downstream sequential model such as SASRec.

## Key Results
- Task 1 (direct recommendation): up to 32.07% relative gain in N@20; e.g., Beauty: 0.4121 vs VLMSFT 0.3434.  
- Task 2 (SR initialization with SASRec): up to 11.82% relative gain in N@20; e.g., Beauty: 0.3932 vs 0.3553.  
- Ablations confirm both LWPCL and LCRTR are essential; the method proves robust across different VLM backbones and scales.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Weak‑modality Penalized Contrastive Learning (LWPCL)
- **Claim:** LWPCL rectifies gradient imbalance during SFT by dynamically amplifying negative penalties for the user‑specific weak modality, restoring its discriminative power.  
- **Mechanism:** For each user, discriminative margins \(M_{u,m}\) (cosine difference between positive and mean negative) are computed for each modality. The modality gap \(\Delta_{u,gap} = \text{sg}(M_{u,strong}) - M_{u,weak}\) uses a stop‑gradient on the strong margin. This gap is transformed via learnable scalars \(\alpha,\beta\) (through Softplus) into a penalty weight \(w_{u,pen} > 1\), which multiplies the negative term in the fused contrastive loss. The weak modality’s negative samples are pushed farther apart while the strong modality’s learning is preserved.  
- **Core assumption:** Modality collapse in VLMs during contrastive SFT for SR stems from optimization imbalance, where gradients predominantly update the strong (easier) modality, leaving the weak one under‑trained.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] penalty concept; [section 3] gradient similarity analysis (cos(g_total, g_strong)→1, cos(g_total, g_weak) declines during standard SFT); [section 4.2.1] margin definitions.  
- **Break condition:** Removing the stop‑gradient on \(M_{u,strong}\) (ablation) causes the model to reduce \(\Delta_{u,gap}\) by degrading the strong modality, harming overall performance.

### Mechanism 2: Cross‑Modal Relational Topology Regularization (LCRTR)
- **Claim:** LCRTR preserves geometric consistency between modalities by aligning their relative similarity structures, preventing distortion from LWPCL’s aggressive negative pushing.  
- **Mechanism:** For each sequence in a batch, similarity distributions \(P^T_i\) and \(P^V_i\) are obtained by applying softmax (with temperature \(\tau_{CRTR}\)) to the dot products between the sequence’s modality‑specific embedding and all candidate item embeddings within that modality. The bidirectional KL divergence \(\text{KL}(P^T_i\|P^V_i) + \text{KL}(P^V_i\|P^T_i)\) is averaged across sequences. This aligns ranking neighborhoods without forcing point‑wise equality, thus retaining modality‑specific geometries while ensuring cross‑modal semantic consistency.  
- **Core assumption:** LWPCL’s strong negative separation can over‑expand or distort the weak modality’s embedding space; preserving relative topology (ranking consistency) is sufficient to maintain cross‑modal alignment.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] geometric consistency claim; [section 4.2.2] design rationale; [section 5.4.1] ablation (removing LCRTR causes sharp drop).  
- **Break condition:** If LWPCL is active but LCRTR is removed, the fused space reverts to mirroring the text modality, and vision‑to‑fusion performance degrades.

### Mechanism 3: External Fusion
- **Claim:** External fusion (separate text/image sequence prompts) with element‑wise summation minimizes cross‑modal interference during encoding, providing a cleaner optimization path for the objectives.  
- **Mechanism:** Text and image sequences are encoded independently via dedicated prompts (\(P^T, P^V\)). The VLM produces separate embeddings \(z^T_u, z^V_u\), which are fused only by summation at the final step. This avoids self‑attention bias toward the dominant modality at the input level and prevents early entanglement of gradients.  
- **Core assumption:** The root cause of modality collapse lies in optimization dynamics; a simple fusion strategy reduces extraneous variables and lets the objectives directly address gradient imbalance.  
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 2.2.1] external fusion superior post‑SFT compared to internal fusion; [section 2.3] design choice; [section 5.1.3] consistent use in baselines.  
- **Break condition:** Using internal fusion with VLM2Rec’s objectives diminishes gains, as interleaved inputs reintroduce attention biases that complicate gradient flow.

## Foundational Learning
#### Contrastive Learning & InfoNCE Loss
- **Why needed:** The base contrastive objective (maximizing similarity of positive pairs against negatives) is used; LWPCL modifies it by adjusting the weight on the negative term.  
- **Quick check:** How does increasing the weight on the negative term in a contrastive loss affect the gradient for representations of negative samples?

#### Modality Collapse & Gradient Imbalance
- **Why needed:** The paper’s core problem is gradient imbalance leading to one modality being under‑trained; LWPCL directly addresses this.  
- **Quick check:** In a multimodal contrastive loss, what would a rapidly decreasing cosine similarity between the total gradient and one modality's gradient indicate about that modality's learning?

#### Representation Geometry Metrics (Alignment, Uniformity, Separability)
- **Why needed:** These metrics (from Wang & Isola 2020) quantify the quality of the embedding space and are used to evidence geometric collapse and recovery.  
- **Quick check:** What does a separability score \(S < 1\) imply about the relationship between positive and negative sample distances in a representation space?

#### Cross‑Modal Consistency vs. Modality Individuality
- **Why needed:** LCRTR aligns relative similarity structures across modalities without forcing identical embeddings, preserving useful modality‑specific information.  
- **Quick check:** Why might minimizing point‑wise L2 distance between text and vision embeddings be harmful for multimodal recommendation, compared to aligning their similarity distributions to a set of items?

## Architecture Onboarding
#### Component Map
Input Constructor → VLM Encoder (Φ) → Modality Gap Estimator → Penalty Calculator → Loss Calculator (LWPCL + LCRTR) → Fusion Module (sum \(z^T_u + z^V_u\) → \(z_u\)).

#### Critical Path
1. For a batch of users, construct separate text and image sequence prompts.  
2. Encode each with the VLM to obtain sequence embeddings \(z^T_u, z^V_u\) and candidate item embeddings \(e_i^T, e^V_i\).  
3. Compute per‑user modality margins \(M_{u,m}\), identify weak and strong modalities, calculate \(\Delta_{u,gap}\), and derive penalty weight \(w_{u,pen}\).  
4. Compute LWPCL: contrastive loss on fused \(z_u\) with negatives weighted by \(w_{u,pen}\).  
5. Compute LCRTR: for each modality, get softmax‑normalized similarity distributions to in‑batch candidates; average bidirectional KL.  
6. Backpropagate total loss \(L = \text{LWPCL} + \lambda\,\text{LCRTR}\) to update LoRA parameters and \(\alpha,\beta\).

#### Design Tradeoffs
- **Simplicity vs. Control:** External fusion + summation adds no trainable parameters but assumes additive compatibility. More complex fusion (e.g., attention) could capture interactions yet introduces hyperparameters and overfitting risk.  
- **Awareness vs. Stability:** LWPCL aggressively targets the weak modality; without LCRTR, this risks distorting its space. The \(\lambda\) tradeoff balances discrimination (high \(\lambda\) preserves structure) versus modality balancing (low \(\lambda\) allows stronger weak‑modality push).  
- **User‑adaptivity vs. Variance:** Per‑user modality gap estimation adds computation but captures individual preferences. A global gap would be simpler but less precise.

#### Failure Signatures
- **Performance:** vision‑to‑fusion (v2f) NDCG lower than text‑to‑fusion (t2f) after SFT, and fused (f2f) worse than t2f.  
- **Gradients:** \(\cos(g_{\text{total}}, g_{\text{weak}})\) rapidly declines to near 0 within early training steps.  
- **Geometry:** Separability \(S^V \le 1.0\) (or \(S^F \approx S^T\)), indicating weak modality collapse; uniformity \(U^V\) significantly lower than \(U^T\).  
- **Ablation:** Removing stop‑gradient causes \(M_{\text{strong}}\) to drop, confirming degradation of the strong modality.

#### First Three Experiments
1. **Modality‑specific evaluation:** For a trained model, report NDCG@20 using only text (t2f), only images (v2f), and fused inputs (f2f). Expect v2f to show >10% relative N@20 gain over VLMSFT baseline.  
2. **Gradient dynamics tracking:** In the first 1 k steps, log cosine similarity between total gradient (w.r.t. LWPCL) and gradients from separate text‑only and image‑only forward passes. VLM2Rec should maintain higher alignment with the image gradient than VLMSFT.  
3. **Ablation – LWPCL components:** Train three models: (a) full VLM2Rec, (b) without stop‑gradient on \(M_{\text{strong}}\), (c) without \(w_{\text{pen}}\) (standard SFT). Compare NDCG@20 on Task 1. Expect (b) to underperform (a) and possibly match (c), confirming the stop‑gradient’s critical role.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
#### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does enlarging the candidate set for cross‑modal relational topology regularization (beyond in‑batch negatives) yield better preservation of weak‑modality geometry and higher recommendation accuracy?  
- **Basis in paper:** The authors use in‑batch target items for LCRTR “for computational efficiency” but do not evaluate larger pools.  
- **Why unresolved:** A larger, more diverse candidate set may capture global item space structure more faithfully, potentially further reducing modality collapse; the trade‑off between gain and cost is unknown.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically vary candidate set size (e.g., via memory bank or nearest‑neighbor search) and measure geometry metrics (separability, uniformity) and N@20; check for performance plateaus.

#### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can VLM2Rec’s weak‑modality detection and penalty mechanism be generalized to three or more modalities, and how should the modality gap be defined in such settings?  
- **Basis in paper:** The framework identifies strong/weak modalities via max/min over two modality margins; no discussion for >2 modalities is provided.  
- **Why unresolved:** Real‑world systems may integrate audio, metadata, etc. The binary strong/weak definition does not scale; a multi‑modal gap concept (e.g., percentile‑based penalties) is needed but unexplored.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend LWPCL to multiple modalities using relative margins; evaluate on a dataset with ≥3 modalities and compare against naive extensions.

#### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Would an adaptive fusion strategy (e.g., learned weighted sum or attention) for combining multimodal representations improve performance over the current fixed element‑wise summation without re‑introducing optimization instability?  
- **Basis in paper:** The authors choose “the simplest external fusion: element‑wise summation” to avoid extra parameters; internal fusion is inferior post‑SFT but learnable fusion is not investigated.  
- **Why unresolved:** Adaptive fusion could dynamically allocate importance per user/sequence, possibly enhancing discrimination; however, it might amplify gradient imbalances or collapse if not regularized.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement a learnable fusion module (gating or attention) within VLM2Rec, keep all other objectives unchanged; compare recommendation metrics and modality gradient alignment against the fixed‑sum baseline and assess whether the regularizers still prevent collapse.

## Limitations
- Negative sampling scope is unspecified (likely only in‑batch negatives), which may limit generalizability and performance.  
- Hyperparameters \(\alpha,\beta\) in the penalty weight and LoRA target modules are not fully detailed, hindering exact replication.  
- Evaluation is limited to four Amazon datasets with a single VLM backbone (Qwen2.5‑VL‑3B); performance on other domains and VLM architectures remains unknown.

## Confidence

- **Claim:** LWPCL rectifies gradient imbalance by amplifying weak‑modality negative penalties. **Confidence:** Medium.
