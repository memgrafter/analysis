# Kolmogorov Complexity Bounds for LLM Steganography and a Perplexity-Based Detection Proxy

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21567
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21567
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** Semantic-preserving LLM steganography necessarily increases Kolmogorov complexity by at least the payload’s complexity, detectable via a perplexity-ratio score (Binoculars), with experimental validation showing a significant paired difference (t=5.11, p<10⁻⁶).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the emerging risk of LLM-generated steganography, where hidden payloads are embedded in text while preserving surface meaning. Theoretically, it proves that any lossless semantic-preserving embedding must increase the Kolmogorov complexity of the stegotext by at least the payload’s complexity (minus logarithmic terms). To render this uncomputable quantity operational, the authors propose the Binoculars score—a ratio of cross-entropies from a base and an instruction-tuned language model—as a practical proxy for complexity increase. Using a color-based steganographic scheme on 300 samples of *Three-Body Problem* text, they demonstrate that stegotexts have significantly higher Binoculars scores than semantically equivalent paraphrases (paired t-test: t=5.11, p<10⁻⁶), aligning with the theoretical prediction.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates a pipeline where a covertext sentence is rewritten by an LLM to encode a hidden payload (a 3-bit color code) while preserving semantic content. Sentences from *Three-Body Problem* are paired with one of eight colors; `claude-sonnet-4-20250514` rewrites each sentence to strongly evoke its assigned color through associated nouns/adjectives without explicit naming. An adversarial LLM paraphrases the resulting stegotext to remove color cues, serving as a baseline that strips the payload. The Binoculars score `B(x) = log PPL_{M₁}(x) / log X-PPL_{M₁,M₂}(x)` is computed using Falcon-7B (base) and Falcon-7B-Instruct. The Quadroculars score `Q = B(stegotext) - B(paraphrase)` is tested via paired t-test across 300 samples, and decoding accuracy measures payload retention before and after paraphrasing.

## Key Results
- **Theoretical bound:** Any lossless semantic-preserving embedding satisfies `K(M₂) ≥ K(M₁) + K(P) - O(log n)`, where `M₂` is stegotext, `M₁` covertext, and `P` payload.
- **Proxy proposal:** Binoculars score serves as a computable upper bound for Kolmogorov complexity increase under assumptions (base LM near-optimal, cross-perplexity stability).
- **Experimental validation:** Stegotexts have significantly higher Quadroculars scores than paraphrases (t=5.11, p<10⁻⁶); direct decoding accuracy is 95.7% versus 54.8% after paraphrasing.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Complexity Increase Bound
**Claim:** Any lossless semantic-preserving embedding must increase the Kolmogorov complexity of the stegotext by at least the payload’s complexity.
**Mechanism:** Using symmetry of information and the chain rule, with fixed `Embed`/`Extract` (constant-size programs) and semantic preservation (`K(Mᵢ|S)=O(1)`), one recovers `S` from `M₂`, then `M₁` and `P`. This forces `K(M₂) ≥ K(M₁, P) + O(log n)`. Assuming `P` independent of `M₁` yields the bound.
**Core assumption:** Both `M₁` and `M₂` are conditionally simple given a shared semantic description `S` (Definition 1).
**Evidence anchors:** Theorem 3 in Section 3 gives the full proof; Step 5 argues payload independence.
**Break condition:** If semantic preservation fails (e.g., embedding alters factual meaning), `K(M₂|S)` may not be constant, breaking the recovery chain.

### Mechanism 2: Perplexity as KC Proxy
**Claim:** Language model cross-entropy (perplexity) is a computable operational proxy for Kolmogorov complexity.
**Mechanism:** For any lossless compressor `C`, `K(x) ≤ |C(x)| + c_C`. A language model’s cross-entropy `H_M(x)` upper-bounds the source entropy rate; for ergodic sources, entropy rate ≤ per-symbol `K(x)`. Thus `H_M(x)` approximates `K(x)/T`.
**Core assumption:** (A1) Base LM `M₁` is `ε`-optimal for the source distribution (its per-token cross-entropy is within `ε` of the true per-symbol Kolmogorov complexity rate).
**Evidence anchors:** Section 4.1 links `K(x)` to compression length; Section 4.2 derives `h(X) ≤ H_M(x) ≤ |C(x)|/T + O(T⁻¹)`.
**Break condition:** If `M₁` is a poor compressor (`ε` large), the proxy becomes noisy; non-ergodic sources may break the entropy-rate-to-KC link.

### Mechanism 3: Quadroculars Detection
**Claim:** The Quadroculars score `Q(M₂, M₁) = B(M₂) - B(M₁)` is proportional to `K(M₂) - K(M₁)` under assumptions A1–A3.
**Mechanism:** `B(x) ≈ H_{M₁}(x) / D` where `D` is stable cross-perplexity. Under A1 (`H_{M₁}(x) ≈ K(x)/T`), A2 (denominator stable), and A3 (equal lengths), `Q` isolates `(K(M₂)-K(M₁))/(T·D)` plus bounded residuals.
**Core assumption:** (A2) Cross-perplexity is stable for semantically equivalent texts: `|log X-PPL(M₂) - log X-PPL(M₁)| ≤ δ`.
**Evidence anchors:** Proposition 6 in Section 4.4 derives proportionality; Section 5.3 shows experimental significance.
**Break condition:** If instruction-tuned `M₂` is semantically misaligned with `M₁`, A2 fails, and the ratio may not isolate the complexity difference.

## Foundational Learning
- **Kolmogorov Complexity (K(x)):** The length of the shortest program (in bits) that generates `x`. *Why needed:* The core theoretical bound (Theorem 3) is expressed in `K`-terms; understanding it explains why embedding a payload increases descriptive complexity. *Quick check:* If `K(M₁|S)=O(1)` and `K(M₂|S)=O(1)`, what does that imply about the relationship between `M₁` and `M₂`?
- **Lossless Compression as KC Proxy:** For any lossless compressor `C`, `K(x) ≤ |C(x)| + c_C`. *Why needed:* Bridges the uncomputable `K` to practical metrics; justifies using compressor output lengths or model perplexity as operational stand-ins. *Quick check:* Why does `K(x) ≤ |C(x)| + c_C` hold for any lossless compressor `C`?
- **Semantic Load / Conditional Complexity:** Formalizing “identical meaning” via constant conditional complexity given a shared semantic description `S`. *Why needed:* The paper’s definition of semantic preservation hinges on this; it distinguishes from distributional similarity. *Quick check:* What is the difference between `K(M₁)` and `K(M₁|S)` for two “meaning-equivalent” texts?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Covertext (M1) -> Payload (P) -> Embedding function (Embed) -> Stegotext (M2) -> Binoculars scorer (B) -> Quadroculars score (Q). Parallel: M1 and M2 (instruct-tuned) feed into B.

Critical path: Input sentences with assigned colors are processed by `Embed` to produce stegotexts. For each text (stegotext and its paraphrase), `B` is computed using base and instruct models. The difference `Q` is compared to a threshold to detect steganography.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Model choice:** Larger base model improves assumption A1 (tighter `K`-proxy) but increases compute; instruct model must be semantically aligned with base for A2.
- **Normalization:** Per-token score and cross-perplexity `D` affect sensitivity; longer texts dilute signal, higher `D` reduces ratio magnitude.
- **Threshold selection:** Balances false positives on clean text vs. false negatives on stegotext; observed mean shift (~0.31) is modest.

Failure signatures:
- Low `Q` for known stegotext: possible semantic violation, poor base LM compression (`ε` large), or semantic misalignment (`δ` large).
- High `Q` for clean paraphrases: confounding effect from paraphrasing itself, violating A2.
- Token length mismatch: If `|M1| ≠ |M2|`, A3 fails, distorting normalization.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Baseline Binoculars distribution:** Compute `B(x)` for a large corpus of natural text to establish the null distribution for detection.
2. **Paraphrasing control:** Generate paraphrases of covertexts and verify `Q` centers near zero, confirming specificity to payload-embedding rewrites.
3. **Payload complexity sweep:** Encode payloads with varying `K(P)` (e.g., random bits vs. low-entropy) and verify linear scaling of `Q` with approximated `K(P)`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The authors do not explicitly pose open questions beyond the limitations discussed. However, the findings naturally suggest further inquiry:
- How robust is the Binoculars detection across different language model families and diverse text domains?
- Can adversaries design embedding methods that preserve semantics while avoiding the Kolmogorov complexity increase, thereby evading detection?
- What are the empirical values and stability of assumptions A1–A3 in practice across various LMs and corpora?

## Limitations
- Theoretical bound assumes perfect semantic preservation (constant conditional complexity), which LLM embeddings likely approximate rather than achieve.
- Perplexity proxy relies on unverified assumptions: base LM `ε`-optimality (A1) and cross-perplexity stability (A2).
- Experimental validation is limited to one text domain (*Three-Body Problem*), one stego scheme (color cues), one model pair (Falcon-7B), and n=300 samples.
- Paraphrasing baseline and token length mismatches may independently affect Binoculars scores, confounding the payload effect.

## Confidence
- **Theorem 3 (complexity bound):** Medium – proof correct under assumptions, but practical semantic preservation uncertain.
- **Perplexity as KC proxy:** Medium – theoretical link solid, but empirical `ε`-optimality untested.
- **Quadroculars detection (Proposition 6):** Medium – depends on A2/A3; experiments supportive but narrow.
- **Reported t-test result:** High – statistically significant on given data; reproducible with exact setup.

## Next Checks
