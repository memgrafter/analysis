# Solar Open Technical Report
## Quick Facts
*   arXiv ID: 2601.07022
*   Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07022
*   Reference count: 20
*   Primary result: State-of-the-art Korean performance (+2.7pp KMMLU, +8.6pp KorMedMCQA) with competitive English results using a fraction of a baseline's token budget, enabled by synthetic data scaling and a progressive bilingual curriculum.

## Executive Summary
Solar Open is a 102B parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language model designed to overcome data scarcity and reasoning limitations for underserved languages, using Korean as a primary case study. It synthesizes 4.5T high-quality, domain-specific tokens and coordinates a progressive, language-aware curriculum across 20T total tokens. Its novel SnapPO reinforcement learning framework decouples generation, reward computation, and training for scalable multi-objective optimization. The model achieves leading performance on Korean academic and medical benchmarks while maintaining competitive English results with significantly reduced token consumption compared to prior strong baselines.

## Method Summary
The methodology comprises three coordinated innovations: First, aggressive synthetic data generation (4.5T tokens) using models like Solar Pro 2 addresses low-resource language scarcity and creates diverse reasoning trajectories for reinforcement learning. Second, a progressive bilingual curriculum dynamically adjusts data quality filters and synthetic data ratios (from 10% to 64%) across pre-training phases to optimize knowledge and reasoning acquisition. Third, the SnapPO framework employs a decoupled, off-policy RL pipeline that caches rollouts and computes rewards in separate stages, enabling linear scaling and flexible composition of heterogeneous objectives.

## Key Results
*   Korean capabilities: State-of-the-art on KMMLU (+2.7pp vs. gpt-oss-high) and KorMedMCQA (+8.6pp), with leading finance, law, and medical benchmarks.
*   English competitiveness: Achieves scores comparable to gpt-oss-medium while using only 48–77% of GLM-4.5-Base's token budget.
*   Efficiency: Custom tokenizer (196.8k vocab, Korean-optimized) achieves low Bytes per Token (4.69), improving inference throughput for Korean text.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Synthetic Data for Scarcity and Reasoning
*   **Claim:** Aggressive synthetic data generation (4.5T tokens) overcomes data scarcity for underserved languages like Korean while simultaneously creating RL-oriented reasoning trajectories.
*   **Mechanism:** Synthetic expansion compensates for low natural data coverage. The pipeline stages synthesis: mid-training generates diverse reasoning paths; SFT curates successful ones; RL uses generated queries for compositional tasks.
*   **Core assumption:** Appropriately filtered and diversified synthetic data can match or supplement real data in linguistic and reasoning complexity.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 1.2.A describe dual roles of synthesis; related work (EuroLLM) shows parallel synthetic approaches.
*   **Break condition:** Systematic biases or factual inconsistencies in synthetic data that survive filtering degrade real-world task performance, especially in culturally specific domains.

### Mechanism 2: Progressive Bilingual Curriculum
*   **Claim:** A progressive curriculum with language-aware quality filtering coordinates data composition across 20T tokens, optimizing knowledge and reasoning.
*   **Mechanism:** The curriculum transitions from broad, low-quality data (Phase 1) to high-quality, filtered content (Phase 2.C) with increasing synthetic ratios. Language-aware educational scoring and embedding-based topic clustering ensure balanced domain coverage in Korean and English.
*   **Core assumption:** A staged data mixture with dynamic quality thresholds and language proportions efficiently teaches general knowledge and complex reasoning within a fixed token budget.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.2 detail the multi-phase progression and three-method filtering framework.
*   **Break condition:** Language-specific quality models misranking Korean content (due to cultural bias) leads to under-representation of key domains or over-filtering, harming Korean performance.

### Mechanism 3: Decoupled RL with SnapPO
*   **Claim:** SnapPO, a decoupled off-policy RL framework, enables scalable, multi-objective optimization without infrastructure redesign.
*   **Mechanism:** SnapPO separates generation (vLLM), reward computation (domain-specific functions), and training (GSPO) into independent, cached steps. This allows linear scaling with compute, flexible mixing of heterogeneous data/rewards, and rapid iteration.
*   **Core assumption:** Decoupling eliminates synchronization bottlenecks and allows independent stage optimization, making multi-domain RL tractable at scale.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 6.1 describe the cyclic decoupling and its advantages (linear scaling, flexible composition).
*   **Break condition:** Cached rewards become stale relative to policy updates (off-policy drift) or conflicting reward functions across domains cause divergence, requiring frequent full regenerations and undermining scalability.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) routing and load balancing**
    *   *Why needed here:* Solar Open uses a 102B-parameter MoE with 128 routed experts and 1 shared expert; understanding expert imbalance, load balancing loss, and activation patterns is critical for training stability and inference efficiency.
    *   *Quick check question:* Why might expert bias alone fail to balance early MoE layers, and how does combining it with load balancing loss help?
*   **Concept: Curriculum learning for bilingual models**
    *   *Why needed here:* The 20T-token curriculum must balance Korean/English proportions, quality thresholds, and domain coverage across phases; this requires grasping how data mixture affects catastrophic forgetting and capability acquisition.
    *   *Quick check question:* How might increasing synthetic data ratio from 10% to 64% while raising quality filters impact both linguistic diversity and reasoning pattern exposure?
*   **Concept: Off-policy reinforcement learning with cached rollouts**
    *   *Why needed here:* SnapPO’s efficiency relies on generating and caching multiple responses per prompt offline; understanding the trade-offs between on-policy vs. off-policy updates, and how cached log-probabilities enable stable gradient computation, is key.
    *   *Quick check question:* What are the risks of using a stale behavior policy to generate responses for training a newer policy, and how does SnapPO mitigate them?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Tokenizer (custom BPE, 196.8k vocab, Korean-optimized) → Pre-training data pipeline (multi-source aggregation, PDF parsing, synthetic generation) → Curriculum controller (quality filters, topic clustering, phase scheduler) → MoE model (48 layers, 128 experts + 1 shared, RoPE) → Mid-training (RL-oriented trajectory synthesis) → SFT pipeline (difficulty-aware curation, agent simulation, safety data) → SnapPO RL (generation cache → reward modules → GSPO trainer) → Evaluation harness (Korean/English benchmarks).
*   **Critical path:** Tokenizer design → Data curriculum implementation → MoE stability during pre-training → Mid-training trajectory integration → SnapPO cycle setup → RL phase data mixing. Any failure in tokenizer efficiency or curriculum phase timing propagates to final performance.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Large vocabulary (196.8k) improves Korean compression but increases embedding memory; for 102B model, cost is negligible vs. MoE parameters.
    *   MoE: 8 experts per token gives performance close to dense but requires careful load balancing; removing dense layers simplifies code but risks early-layer imbalance.
    *   Curriculum: aggressive synthetic ratio (64% in Phase 2.C) boosts efficiency but risks synthetic bias if filtering fails.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   Korean bytes-per-token >5.0 during inference indicates tokenizer underfitting for Korean.
    *   MoE expert utilization variance >20% across layers signals routing instability.
    *   Sudden drop in Korean benchmarks after Phase 2.C suggests over-filtering or curriculum mis-timing.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  Tokenizer efficiency benchmark: Compute Bytes/Token on 10k Korean textbook samples; compare against baseline. Target: ≤4.8 BPT.
    2.  MoE load balancing ablation: Train 10B-A1B prototype with/without load balancing loss; monitor per-layer expert imbalance. Target: early-layer variance <15%.
    3.  Curriculum phase impact: Run mini-pre-training (100B tokens) with/without progressive quality increase; evaluate on KMMLU after 50B tokens. Target: ≥5 pp gain with curriculum.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** Does the Solar Open methodology effectively transfer to languages with even lower resource availability than Korean?
    *   *Basis:* The paper validates only on Korean; applicability to lower-resource languages remains unvalidated.
    *   *Resolution evidence:* Replicate the full pipeline on multiple low-resource languages across families, compare gains, and measure factors like synthetic data quality and curriculum effectiveness.
*   **Open Question 2:** Can curriculum learning for underserved languages be achieved without relying on ML-based quality filtering models?
    *   *Basis:* The current three-method filtering requires training specialized models, which may be infeasible for many communities and introduces bias.
    *   *Resolution evidence:* Develop and implement alternative, assumption-free curriculum strategies (e.g., statistical heuristics), then conduct head-to-head comparisons on performance, stability, and accessibility.
*   **Open Question 3:** What are the optimal RL training decompositions for balancing reasoning enhancement with human preference alignment?
    *   *Basis:* The paper uses one specific decomposition (Phase A: reasoning; Phase B: preference). Other architectures (interleaved, multi-objective from start) are unevaluated.
    *   *Resolution evidence:* Comprehensive ablation experiments varying RL phase order, reward weights, and domain data proportions, measuring outcomes on reasoning, preference metrics, and capability retention.
*   **Open Question 4:** How does incrementally adding a new language to a fixed-capacity model during continued training affect performance, and what are the scaling laws?
    *   *Basis:* Solar Open was trained from scratch; dynamics of expanding coverage in an already-trained model are unknown.
    *   *Resolution evidence:* Controlled experiments starting from a strong multilingual base, sequentially adding language data at varying proportions, and tracking per-language performance and forgetting to derive empirical scaling laws.

## Limitations
*   **Hyperparameter granularity:** Exact learning rate schedules, batch sizes per phase, MoE load balancing coefficients, and context length extension timing are unspecified, critically affecting training stability and capability distribution.
*   **Synthetic data pipeline:** Full details on RL-oriented trajectory generation (prompts, chaining steps, model versions) and the SFT "difficulty estimator" implementation are missing, blocking replication of reasoning enhancement.
*   **RL reward specification:** Precise reward model architectures, verifiable execution environments, and the composite agent simulation reward formula are undisclosed, preventing faithful reproduction of alignment gains.

## Confidence
*   **Mechanism 1 (Synthetic expansion):** Medium. Anchored by claims and parallel work, but no direct validation that Solar’s specific synthesis quality matches real data or that trajectories are effective.
*   **Mechanism 2 (Progressive curriculum):** Medium. Supported by section descriptions, but lacks empirical ablation proving phase sequencing is necessary—simpler mixtures might suffice.
*   **Mechanism 3 (SnapPO scalability):** Medium. Architectural decoupling is clear and analogies exist (PRIME-RL), but no benchmarks measuring linear scaling versus coupled baselines under identical compute.

## Next Checks
1.  **Tokenizer Korean Efficiency Replication:** Train a Korean corpus subset using reported oversampling (22%) and pre-tokenization rules (digit splitting, whitespace preservation). Measure Bytes/Token on held-out samples; target ≤4.8 to match Figure 3.
2.  **MoE Load Balancing Necessity:** On a 10B–1B MoE prototype, train with/without the auxiliary load balancing loss for 100B tokens. Track per-layer expert utilization variance; confirm <15% early-layer imbalance and that removal degrades downstream accuracy by >2pp.
3.  **Curriculum Phase Sequence Ablation:** Run a 100B-token mini-pre-train using the full data mix but randomizing phase order (quality thresholds, synthetic ratio progression). Evaluate KMMLU and a Korean reasoning subset after 50B tokens; expect ≥5pp drop versus the reported progressive schedule.
