# PHAST: Port-Hamiltonian Architecture for Structured Temporal Dynamics Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.17998
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17998
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Enforces port-Hamiltonian structure with explicit conservative-dissipative split to achieve superior long-horizon stability (e.g., Windy Pendulum rollout MSE reduced from 0.435 to 0.092) and accurate damping recovery when sufficient physics is provided.

## Executive Summary
PHAST is a neural architecture for forecasting dissipative dynamical systems from position-only data. It embeds a port-Hamiltonian (pH) system structure, explicitly separating conservative and dissipative terms via a learned Hamiltonian and a positive-semidefinite damping matrix. The model uses a velocity observer (finite differences corrected by a causal TCN) and a Strang-splitting integrator to ensure stable long-horizon rollouts. Evaluated on 13 benchmarks, PHAST significantly outperforms baselines in multi-step prediction, especially in highly dissipative regimes. It also demonstrates accurate physical parameter recovery when partial ground-truth structure (potential or mass) is provided, revealing a trade-off between forecasting accuracy and identifiability in fully unknown settings. The work advocates a two-axis evaluation that decouples prediction performance from parameter recovery.

## Method Summary
PHAST learns dissipative dynamics from sequences of generalized positions \(q_t\). A velocity observer corrects finite-difference estimates using a causal TCN. The phase state \((q, p)\) is obtained by applying a canonicalizer (e.g., \(\hat{p} = M \hat{\dot{q}}\) if mass is known). The core model represents the system as a port-Hamiltonian system: \(\dot{x} = (J - R) \nabla H\), where \(J\) is skew-symmetric and \(R \succeq 0\) is block-diagonal damping. The Hamiltonian \(H = V(q) + \frac{1}{2} p^\top M(q)^{-1} p\) is decomposed into potential \(V\), mass \(M\), and damping \(D(q) = R \nabla H|_{\text{dissipative}}\). Mass and damping are parameterized as low-rank positive (semi)definite matrices to guarantee structural constraints. Integration uses Strang splitting: half-step explicit damping, a symplectic conservative step (leapfrog or implicit midpoint), and another half-step damping. Training uses only next-step MSE; passivity and energy regularizers are optional. Three knowledge regimes are studied: KNOWN (true \(V, M\) given), PARTIAL (one anchor), and UNKNOWN (all learned).

## Key Results
- PHAST reduces long-horizon rollout MSE on the Windy Pendulum benchmark from 0.435 (best baseline D-LinOSS) to 0.092.
- In the KNOWN regime, it recovers damping parameters with \(R^2\) up to 0.996; in the UNKNOWN regime, identifiability is poor despite accurate forecasts.
- Across 13 diverse benchmarks (mechanics, electronics, molecular dynamics, ecology), PHAST achieves the most stable rollouts, particularly for strongly dissipative systems.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Passivity via Port-Hamiltonian Structure
- **Claim:** Enforcing \(\dot{x} = (J - R)\nabla H\) with \(R \succeq 0\) guarantees non-increasing energy \(H\) in continuous time, stabilizing long-horizon rollouts.
- **Mechanism:** The dissipation matrix \(R\) is block-diagonal with zero on the \(q\) block, so only momenta decay. Energy balance yields \(\dot{H} = -\nabla H^\top R \nabla H \le 0\).
- **Core assumption:** \(H\) is a valid storage function (positive definite, bounded below), which holds if \(V\) is bounded below and \(M(q) \succ 0\).
- **Break condition:** Discrete Strang splitting can violate monotonicity if \(\Delta t \cdot \lambda_{\max}(D) / \lambda_{\min}(M) > 2\) or if \(R\) is not strictly PSD during training.

### Mechanism 2: Low-Rank PSD/SPD Parameterization
- **Claim:** Outer-product forms for \(D(q)\) and \(M(q)\) guarantee positive (semi)definiteness by construction and enable \(O(nr)\) matrix-vector operations.
- **Mechanism:** \(D(q) = d_0 I + \sum_{i=1}^r \beta_i(q) k_i k_i^\top\) with \(\beta_i \ge 0\) and \(\|k_i\|=1\) forces \(D \succeq 0\). Inversion uses Woodbury identity.
- **Core assumption:** True damping/inertia have low-dimensional structure plus an isotropic base—valid for many physical systems (e.g., separable friction).
- **Break condition:** If true damping is highly anisotropic and coupled, low-rank approximation (with small \(r\)) may become too restrictive, harming accuracy.

### Mechanism 3: Identifiability via Anchoring
- **Claim:** Anchoring either \(V(q)\) or \(M(q)\) (KNOWN/PARTIAL regimes) breaks gauge freedom and renders remaining parameters identifiable; leaving both free (UNKNOWN) leads to non-unique solutions.
- **Mechanism:** Conservative systems have scaling invariance \(V \to \alpha V\), \(M \to \alpha M\). Dissipative systems have additional trade-offs between \(V\) and \(D\). Providing one component removes these degrees of freedom.
- **Core assumption:** The anchored component is accurate. Misspecification biases recovered parameters.
- **Break condition:** In UNKNOWN, even with perfect forecasts, damping can absorb modeling errors and become physically meaningless.

## Foundational Learning
- **Port-Hamiltonian (pH) Systems:** Mathematical framework that splits dynamics into conservative (\(J\)) and dissipative (\(R\)) parts, with Hamiltonian \(H\) as energy storage. *Why needed:* PHAST’s architecture relies on pH to guarantee passivity explicitly. *Quick check:* Given \(\dot{x} = (J-R)\nabla H\) with \(J=-J^\top\) and \(R \succeq 0\), prove \(\dot{H} \le 0\).
- **Gauge Freedom in System Identification:** Existence of parameter transformations that preserve trajectories (e.g., scaling \(V\) and \(M\) simultaneously). *Why needed:* Explains why physical parameters cannot be uniquely recovered from trajectories alone; anchoring is necessary for identifiability. *Quick check:* For a conservative 1-DOF oscillator \(H = p^2/(2m) + V(q)\), why can \(V\) and \(m\) trade off? How does adding damping affect this?
- **Strang Splitting:** Symmetric operator splitting for ODE integration that preserves structure (e.g., symplecticity, passivity) to second order. *Why needed:* PHAST uses it to discretize the split dynamics while maintaining stability guarantees. *Quick check:* Write the Strang splitting formula for \(\dot{x} = f_H(x) + f_D(x)\) and explain its symmetry.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** input \(q_{0:T}\) → finite-difference \(\dot{q}_{\text{fd}}\) → observer TCN \(\delta_t\) → corrected \(\hat{\dot{q}}_t\) → canonicalizer \(C_\psi\) (e.g., \(\hat{p}_t = \hat{\dot{q}}_t\) for constant mass) → PHAST core \(\Phi_\theta\) (Strang-split integrator using learned \(V, M, D\)) → projected \(\hat{q}_{t+1}\).
- **Critical path:** \(q_{0:T} \xrightarrow{\text{FD}} \dot{q}_{\text{fd}} \xrightarrow{\text{TCN}} \hat{\dot{q}} \xrightarrow{C_\psi} \hat{x} \xrightarrow{\Phi_\theta} \hat{x}' \xrightarrow{\Pi_q} \hat{q}_{t+1}\). Pipeline must be causal and differentiable.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - *Regime vs. flexibility:* KNOWN recovers damping best but requires exact \(V, M\); UNKNOWN is flexible but identifiability not guaranteed.
  - *Damping bounding:* Upper bound \(\sum \beta_i \le \bar{\beta}\) improves identifiability but restricts capacity; unbounded damping may absorb errors.
  - *Nonseparable mass:* Using true \(M(q)\) with implicit midpoint improves some rollouts but costs more than constant-mass leapfrog.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Autoregressive rollouts diverge much faster than takeover rollouts → velocity observer error compounding.
  - Negative damping \(R^2\) in KNOWN/PARTIAL → missing anchoring or damping bounds too loose.
  - Discrete energy increases on rollouts → timestep too large, or \(D\) not PSD due to implementation bug.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Windy Pendulum q-only forecasting:** Compare all regimes and baselines; PHAST PARTIAL achieves 0.092 MSE vs. D-LinOSS 0.435.
  2. **Windy Pendulum identifiability:** PHAST KNOWN recovers damping with \(R^2 = 0.996\); UNKNOWN shows \(R^2 \ll 0\).
  3. **Damping bound ablation (PARTIAL):** Unbounded damping gives accurate rollouts but \(R^2 \ll 0\); bounding \(\sum \beta_i\) recovers identifiability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can damping strength bounds be learned automatically from data rather than manually calibrated, while maintaining both forecasting stability and parameter identifiability?** The paper introduces damping bounds to prevent \(D\) from absorbing errors, but these require physics knowledge. Future work aims to learn bounds from data.
- **How can PHAST be scaled to high-DOF robotic and continuum systems while preserving passivity guarantees and computational efficiency?** Current low-rank approximations and constant-mass assumptions may not scale; multi-body and continuum systems are open.
- **What velocity estimation methods enable robust closed-loop Energy–Casimir control in the q-only regime under sensor noise?** The control study shows performance hinges on velocity estimate quality; noise-trained observers help but degrade under high noise.
- **How can the forecasting-identifiability trade-off in the UNKNOWN regime be mitigated to achieve both accurate rollouts and physically meaningful parameter recovery?** In UNKNOWN, \(D\) may absorb errors; imposing bounds can help identifiability but sometimes harms forecasting. A better balance is needed.
- **Can PHAST be equipped with a test-time adaptation mechanism that uses the initial burn-in window to tune the physics components for the specific instance?** Current models are static after training; adapting \((V, M, D)\) on the burn-in context via meta-learning could improve generalization across system parameters.

## Limitations
- Architectural details (observer TCN kernel/dilation, damping MLP size, training schedule) are underspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Low-rank damping may be insufficient for systems with highly coupled anisotropic dissipation.
- Anchoring assumptions: inaccurate ground-truth \(V\) or \(M\) biases recovered \(D\); identifiability in UNKNOWN regime is fundamentally limited.
- Discrete Strang splitting can violate passivity if timestep is too large or PSD constraints on \(D\) are not strictly enforced.

## Confidence
- **Superior long-horizon forecasting:** High (consistent improvements across 13 benchmarks, clear pipeline).
- **Accurate damping recovery in KNOWN/PARTIAL:** Medium (strong results but hinge on correct anchoring and damping bounds, whose selection details are omitted).
- **Forecasting-identifiability trade-off in UNKNOWN:** High (theoretically expected and empirically observed).
- **Passivity/stability guarantees:** Medium (continuous-time guarantee holds; discrete-time behavior depends on integration choices not exhaustively validated).
- **Velocity observer effectiveness:** Medium (FD+TCN works but architecture underspecified; could be a hidden source of error).

## Next Checks
1. **Observer robustness test:** Train PHAST with alternative causal TCN designs (e.g., kernel size 5, depth 3) and confirm Windy Pendulum rollout MSE varies by less than 5%.
2. **Low-rank adequacy stress test:** Construct a 4-DOF mechanical system with a full-rank damping matrix; evaluate PHAST’s damping recovery \(R^2\) as a function of rank \(r\) and quantify forecasting accuracy loss when \(r\) is too small.
3. **Strang splitting passivity audit:** For Windy Pendulum, sweep timestep \(\Delta t\) and compute passivity violation rate (fraction of rollouts with energy increase). Verify that \(\Delta t=0.05\) yields near-zero violations and that violations rise sharply beyond the theoretical stability threshold \(\Delta t \cdot \lambda_{\max}(D)/\lambda_{\min}(M) \approx 2\).
