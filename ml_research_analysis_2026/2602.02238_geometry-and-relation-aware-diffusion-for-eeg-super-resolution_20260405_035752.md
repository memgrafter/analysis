# Geometry- and Relation-Aware Diffusion for EEG Super-Resolution

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02238
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02238
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** TopoDiff achieves superior signal fidelity in EEG spatial super-resolution, with notable improvements at high upscaling factors (e.g., SNR gains of +10.4% on SEED and +24.0% on MI/MM at 8× SR) and consistent downstream task improvements.

## Executive Summary
This work presents TopoDiff, a diffusion model for EEG spatial super-resolution that explicitly integrates physiological scalp geometry and time-varying inter-electrode dependencies. Unlike prior methods that ignore spatial context, TopoDiff conditions its generative process on (1) global topographic map embeddings to capture scalp-wide patterns and (2) dynamic channel-relation graphs to model non-stationary functional connectivity. Evaluated on multiple datasets under up to 8× upsampling, TopoDiff consistently outperforms baselines in signal fidelity and downstream tasks (emotion recognition, motor imagery, seizure detection), demonstrating the critical role of geometric and relational priors.

## Method Summary
TopoDiff takes a low-resolution EEG recording with sparse channels (C_lr × T) and reconstructs a high-density version (C_hr × T). The time series are first patchified along the temporal dimension. Two parallel branches generate conditioning tokens: the topographic branch creates scalp topoplots from temporally averaged patches, encodes them with a frozen DINOv3 visual encoder, and produces global spatial tokens. The graph branch constructs a time-specific sparse adjacency matrix among visible channels based on spatial proximity and signal similarity, then enriches channel embeddings via a two-layer GNN with residual connections. These tokens, along with noisy latent tokens representing unseen channels, are concatenated and fed into a diffusion Transformer trained with x-prediction loss only on the generated channels. At inference, the reverse diffusion process denoises the unseen channels while respecting both conditioning streams.

## Key Results
- TopoDiff achieves higher signal fidelity (lower NMSE, higher SNR, higher PCC) than prior methods across SEED, SEED-IV, PhysioNet MI/MM, and TUSZ datasets under 2×, 4×, and 8× upsampling.
- At 8× SR, it yields SNR gains of +10.4% on SEED and +24.0% on MI/MM compared to the best baseline.
- Downstream task performance (emotion recognition, motor imagery classification, seizure detection) consistently improves when using TopoDiff-reconstructed channels.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Global scalp geometry prior
TopoDiff embeds topographic maps of the low-resolution EEG via a frozen pre-trained visual encoder, providing a global scalp context that guides the diffusion model toward anatomically plausible activations. This prior captures spatial patterns that are montage-agnostic, ensuring coherence across the entire scalp. Ablation removing this branch causes significant degradation, especially at high upsampling factors, confirming its necessity.

### Time-varying relational prior
The model constructs a dynamic graph per temporal patch to reflect non-stationary functional connectivity. A lightweight GNN propagates information along these edges, producing channel embeddings that respect time-specific dependencies. This is critical because EEG connectivity changes rapidly and task-relevant patterns are often transient. Ablating the graph branch harms reconstruction, and the method shows particular benefits on seizure detection, which relies on dynamic connectivity changes.

### Joint multimodal conditioning
The diffusion Transformer receives both global topological tokens and local relational tokens, which complement each other: global tokens set the overall scalp activation pattern, while dynamic graph tokens refine inter-channel coupling within each time window. Their joint integration yields spatially grounded generation that scales robustly to extreme upsampling where prior methods collapse.

## Foundational Learning

- **EEG Topographic Mapping (Topoplots):* 2D scalp visualizations created by interpolating channel voltages, preserving spatial patterns beyond simple channel coordinates. *Why needed:* The global topology branch uses these images to capture scalp-wide context. *Quick check:* How does a topoplot differ from a simple plot of channel amplitudes, and what spatial information does it preserve that positional embeddings might miss?
- **Graph Neural Networks for Relational Modeling:* Neural networks that operate on graphs, performing message passing along edges to propagate relational information. *Why needed:* The dynamic graph branch uses a GNN to inject time-varying inter-electrode dependencies. *Quick check:* Why use cosine similarity for edge weights and symmetric normalization in this EEG graph, and how does the GNN residual design act as an adapter?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Low-resolution EEG (C_lr×T) -> temporal patchification -> parallel branches: (a) Topographic branch: averaged patches -> topoplot images -> frozen DINOv3 -> feature map -> flatten & project -> topological tokens; (b) Graph branch: same patchified input -> group-wise cosine similarity -> sparse adjacency (with ns spatial neighbors, top-k selection) -> symmetric normalize -> two-layer GNN (residual) -> relation tokens. All tokens + noisy unseen-channel tokens -> concatenate -> linear projection -> diffusion Transformer -> output projection -> predicted unseen channels.

**Critical path:** Input EEG -> [parallel topographic and graph branches] -> fused token sequence -> diffusion Transformer -> reconstructed channels. Conditioning from both branches influences every Transformer layer.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Graph sparsity parameters (ns, k): Too dense adds noise; too sparse loses informative edges. Optimal values vary with SR factor and montage size.
- Frozen visual encoder: Leverages strong pre-trained features but cannot adapt to subject-specific head shapes; fine-tuning may help but risks overfitting.
- Temporal patch size: Must align with neurophysiological timescales; misalignment causes loss of dynamic connectivity or noisy graphs.

**Failure signatures:**
- Poor 8× SR quality: may indicate weak or misaligned topological prior.
- Static adjacency matrices across time: suggests dynamics not captured, possibly from large patch size or degenerate cosine similarities.
- Downstream task gains << signal fidelity gains: reconstructed channels preserve morphology but lose task-discriminative spatial patterns.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Ablate topographic conditioning and compare convergence and SNR to full model.
2. Sweep graph sparsity (ns, k) on SEED 2× SR to locate optimal trade-off.
3. Train on one montage (e.g., 32 channels) and test on a disjoint symmetric subset (e.g., 16 channels) to assess montage invariance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. *Subject-specific spatial cues:* Could individualized electrode coordinates or head models improve anatomical plausibility and cross-subject generalization?
2. *State-dependent connectivity:* Can the dynamic graph be enhanced to model multi-scale or frequency-specific relational graphs aligned with neurophysiological priors?
3. *Uncertainty estimation:* Would incorporating uncertainty enable quality control, active sensing, or downstream-aware training?
4. *Robustness to real-world challenges:* How does TopoDiff handle noise, channel dropouts, and montage shifts? Can it be adapted for unified imputation, denoising, and cross-montage translation?

## Limitations
- **Topographic image construction details** (interpolation method, resolution, scalp boundary) are unreported, risking reproducibility and feature quality.
- **Dynamic graph parameter scaling** lacks generalization rules for varying montage sizes, especially small ones like TUSZ's 19 channels.
- **Montage invariance** of frozen DINO features is unverified; subject-specific head shapes or electrode shifts may misalign global priors.

## Confidence
- Global geometry prior from topographic embeddings: Medium
- Time-varying relational prior from dynamic graphs: High
- Joint integration of both priors: High
