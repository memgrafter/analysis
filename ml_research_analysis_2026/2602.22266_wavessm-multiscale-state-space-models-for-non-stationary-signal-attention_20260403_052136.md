# WaveSSM: Multiscale State-Space Models for Non-stationary Signal Attention

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.22266
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22266
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: WaveSSM outperforms S4 on non-stationary benchmarks (e.g., PTB-XL AUROC 0.942 vs 0.934; Speech Commands 96.55% vs 96.08%; ETTh1 MSE 0.102 vs 0.116) and shows dramatically lower reconstruction error on continuous window copying.

## Executive Summary
WaveSSM introduces a novel family of state-space models that utilize wavelet frames instead of polynomial bases to capture localized, transient signal features. This design addresses a key limitation of prior SSMs (like S4) which rely on global polynomials and struggle with non-stationary data. Across multiple benchmarks—including ECG classification, speech command recognition, long-horizon forecasting, and the LRA suite—WaveSSM variants built from Morlet, Gaussian, Mexican hat, DPSS, and Daubechies wavelets consistently outperform S4. The continuous window copying task demonstrates an order-of-magnitude improvement in multi-window storage, highlighting the model’s ability to retain temporally precise information. These results suggest that wavelet-based state representations are particularly effective for real-world signals rich in transients and local patterns.

## Method Summary
WaveSSM redefines the state matrix in state-space models using frames derived from wavelet bases, providing time-frequency localization absent in global polynomial expansions. The construction follows the SaFARi framework, allowing arbitrary frames (including wavelets) to parameterize the state update. The model incorporates DPLR (diagonal plus low-rank) structure and frame tightening to ensure numerical stability. Input sequences are processed by the wavelet-SSM block, which evolves states with locally supported kernels, enabling the model to focus on specific time windows. The output is obtained via a linear readout. Variants employ different wavelet families (Morlet, Gaussian, Mexican hat, DPSS, Daubechies) to explore the effect of wavelet properties on performance.

## Key Results
- **PTB-XL ECG classification**: Best WaveSSM (db6T) achieves AUROC 0.942 vs S4’s 0.934.
- **Speech Commands (raw)**: WaveSSM reaches 96.55% accuracy vs S4’s 96.08%.
- **Long-horizon forecasting (ETTh1)**: WaveSSM reduces MSE to 0.102 compared to S4’s 0.116.
- **Continuous window copying**: WaveSSM shows an order-of-magnitude lower reconstruction error, demonstrating superior multi-window storage.
- **LRA suite**: Improvements are reported on ListOps, Text, Retrieval, and Image tasks (qualitative).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
WaveSSM’s core innovation is the substitution of global polynomial bases with localized wavelet frames. Polynomial bases, used in S4, are smooth and extend over the entire sequence, which suits stationary, slowly varying signals but averages out transients. Wavelet frames, in contrast, are compact in both time and frequency, providing “temporally addressable” states that can isolate and retain information from specific local windows. This locality allows the model to better capture non-stationary features such as spikes, edges, or short-term patterns common in ECG, speech, and other real-world signals. Additionally, the use of frame tightening and DPLR ensures that the state matrix remains well-conditioned, enabling stable training and effective long-range mixing despite the localized atoms.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input sequence -> Linear projection -> Wavelet-based SSM block (with DPLR and frame tightening) -> Linear output -> Task head  
**Critical path**: The input embedding is fed into the wavelet-SSM, which iteratively updates the hidden state via a locally parameterized matrix. The final state is used to produce predictions through a linear readout.  
**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Wavelet locality vs. global context: Short-wavelets excel at transients but may miss long-range trends; long-support wavelets mitigate this but reduce locality.
- Wavelet family selection: Vanishing moments, smoothness, and frequency response affect performance; no universal best (db6T works best on PTB-XL but others may suit other data).
- Frame tightening improves conditioning but adds computational overhead.

**Failure signatures**: 
- Performance collapse on PathX (extremely long sequences) due to insufficient state dimension or numerical instability from ill-conditioned frames.
- Poor results on stationary tasks where polynomial bases are known to excel (e.g., simple periodic signals).
- Training instability if frame tightening is omitted or DPLR constraints violated.

**Three first experiments**:
1. Replicate PTB-XL classification: Train db6T WaveSSM and S4 with equal hyperparameter budgets; compare AUROC.
2. Continuous window copying: Measure reconstruction error for varying window sizes to verify local memory capacity.
3. Stationary baseline: Test both models on a synthetic sine wave with added noise to assess relative strengths.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Can WaveSSM be scaled to extremely long sequences (e.g., Path-X-128) without losing its performance on transient signals?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note as a limitation that all WaveSSM variants are infeasible on PathX, suggesting a trade-off between locality and long-range dependencies.
- Why unresolved: The infeasibility is not fully understood; it could stem from numerical instability, memory limits, or insufficient state dimension. The hypothesis of a trade-off is untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: A variant that successfully processes >16k tokens on PathX while maintaining or improving performance on PTB-XL and Speech Commands.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is there a theoretical principle to select the best wavelet family for a given dataset's signal characteristics?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper compares several wavelet families and reports varying results (e.g., db6T best on PTB-XL), but provides no guidelines.
- Why unresolved: The link between wavelet properties (e.g., vanishing moments, time-frequency localization) and task performance is not analyzed.
- What evidence would resolve it: A meta-study correlating wavelet features with performance across diverse datasets, or a learnable wavelet selection algorithm.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Would a hybrid SSM combining wavelet and polynomial bases achieve better performance across both transient and long-range tasks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] WaveSSM (wavelet) and S4 (polynomial) each have strengths; the SaFARi framework allows arbitrary frames, so a mixture is possible but unexplored.
- Why unresolved: It is unknown if combining bases can overcome the locality-global trade-off that limits WaveSSM on PathX.
- What evidence would resolve it: A hybrid model that matches WaveSSM on PTB-XL and S4 on PathX, demonstrating complementary benefits.

### Open Question 4
- Question: To what extent do improvements over S4 come from wavelet localization versus other design choices (e.g., frame tightening, DPLR)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] WaveSSM uses frame tightening and DPLR, but the ablation of these factors is missing; S4 might also use DPLR and could be tightened.
- Why unresolved: Without isolating variables, it is unclear whether gains are due to the wavelet atoms themselves or improved conditioning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing S4 with wavelet frames and tightening, S4 with polynomial frames but no tightening, etc.

## Limitations
- Baseline fairness: Hyperparameter tuning budgets for S4 and WaveSSM may differ; unclear if comparison is fair.
- Statistical rigor absent: Results lack confidence intervals or significance tests; some improvements are small.
- Generalization limited: Only a few datasets tested; LRA gains are asserted without numbers.
- Computational trade-offs unknown: No throughput/memory measurements; wavelet transforms may be costly.
- Causality unestablished: The role of localized bases not isolated via ablation.
