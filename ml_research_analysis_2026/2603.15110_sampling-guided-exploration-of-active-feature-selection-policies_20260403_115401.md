# Sampling-guided exploration of active feature selection policies
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.15110
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15110
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: Outperforms state-of-the-art (EDDI, Wang et al.) in accuracy, particularly on high-dimensional data, and achieves lower policy complexity (fewer visited superstates) without sacrificing the accuracy-cost trade-off.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles instance-specific active feature selection, where the optimal set of features to acquire varies per instance and must balance prediction accuracy against acquisition cost. The core method extends a prior reinforcement learning framework formulated as a Markov decision process (MDP) with variable-dimensional "superstates"—each representing a specific subset of acquired features—to avoid bias from data imputation. Two key contributions enable scaling to larger feature sets: a sampling-guided heuristic that incrementally explores only the most promising superstate combinations instead of all, and a post-fit Lq-regularization that reduces the number of distinct superstates visited, yielding compact policies. Evaluated on four binary classification datasets (up to 56 features), the method shows superior accuracy-cost trade-offs compared to baselines, especially on high-dimensional data, while maintaining simpler policies.

## Method Summary
The approach frames active feature selection as a finite-horizon MDP. States are "superstates" \(S_f\), each corresponding to a subset \(f\) of acquired features, avoiding imputation by keeping variable dimensionality. Actions consist of acquiring a new feature or terminating. Rewards combine classification accuracy (evaluated out-of-sample via cross-validation) minus a cost penalty \(\lambda \cdot \text{cost}_f\). The optimal policy is derived by solving the Bellman equation over the superstate DAG. For scalability, a sampling-guided heuristic (Algorithm 2) incrementally builds a restricted set \(T\) of explored superstates: starting from the empty root, it repeatedly selects the neighboring superstate with the highest estimated return \(dRet(S_n)\), computed by randomly sampling deeper superstates and taking the per-instance maximum accuracy-minus-cost. Policy values are updated efficiently (Algorithm 3) by propagating changes only along affected ancestors. To encourage compact policies, an Lq-regularization (\(0<q<1\)) on state occupancy \(\mu(S_f)\) is added iteratively as a virtual reward, derived via the REPS framework, prompting the agent to avoid rarely visited superstates.

## Key Results
- Outperforms state-of-the-art methods (EDDI, Wang et al.) in AUC across all four datasets, with significant gains on the high-dimensional HTA dataset where imputation-based baselines fail.
- Achieves a smoother accuracy-cost trade-off compared to prior instance-specific methods, particularly on the Heart dataset with real monetary costs.
- Visits substantially fewer distinct superstates than the exhaustive baseline while preserving the accuracy-cost frontier, demonstrating the effectiveness of the Lq-regularization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Variable-dimensional "superstates" enable instance-specific active feature selection without data imputation bias.
- **Mechanism**: The state space is defined as a disjoint union of subspaces \(S_f\) for each feature subset \(f \subseteq F\), avoiding a fused connected space with imputed values. Transitions between superstates are deterministic and depend only on the action (acquiring a feature), not on instance values. The value \(V(s)\) for a state in superstate \(S_f\) depends solely on the set \(f\), not acquisition order.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract]: "formulated as an MDP with variable-dimensional states (superstates) to avoid imputation." [section 2.2.1]: Defines superstates as disconnected domains \(S_f\). [corpus]: Neighbors discuss feature selection but not this variable-dimensional MDP formulation; evidence is weak.
- **Break condition**: If features are highly correlated such that imputation from partial views is accurate, the overhead of managing multiple superstates may not be justified.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: A sampling-guided heuristic incrementally explores only the most promising superstate combinations, scaling to larger feature sets.
- **Mechanism**: The algorithm maintains an explored set \(T\). Starting from the empty root, it iteratively selects the neighboring superstate \(S_n\) with the highest estimated return \(dRet(S_n) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_j \max_{k \in \text{sample}(S_n)} \text{acc}_j^k - \lambda \cdot \text{cost}_k\), where samples are drawn logarithmically over superstate depth. This focuses computation on a subgraph of the full DAG.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract]: "sampling-guided heuristic that incrementally explores only the most promising superstate combinations instead of all." [section 2.3]: Details sequential expansion of \(T\) and \(dRet\) computation (Algorithm 2). [corpus]: Neighbors use RL for feature tasks but not this specific graph traversal; evidence is weak.
- **Break condition**: The heuristic is optimistic and may overestimate returns for far nodes, potentially pruning regions containing optimal policies for some instances, especially under high acquisition costs.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Post-fit Lq-regularization reduces the number of distinct superstates visited, yielding compact policies without sacrificing the accuracy-cost trade-off.
- **Mechanism**: An Lq penalty (\(0<q<1\)) on occupancy measure \(\mu(S_f)\) is integrated as a virtual reward \(R_{\text{virtual}}(s,a,s') = \alpha \cdot q \cdot \mu(S'_f)^{1-q}\) into the MDP. The optimization alternates: (1) compute \(\mu\) under current policy, (2) add virtual reward and re-optimize policy. This iteratively prunes superstates with low occupancy.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract]: "post-fit Lq-regularization that reduces the number of distinct superstates visited." [section 2.4]: Derives virtual reward and describes iterative optimization. [corpus]: Neighbors discuss feature selection regularization but not this occupancy-based Lq approach; evidence is weak.
- **Break condition**: If \(\alpha\) is too large or \(q\) too small, regularization may force premature convergence to a suboptimal policy with too few superstates, losing accuracy. Complex feature interactions may require many superstates.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and Bellman optimality.  
  *Why needed here*: The active feature selection process is framed as a finite-horizon MDP where states are superstates, actions are feature acquisitions, and rewards combine accuracy and cost. Solving Bellman's equation yields the optimal policy.  
  *Quick check question*: How does the DAG structure of superstates allow a direct (non-sampling) solution of Bellman's equation in Algorithm 1?
- **Concept**: Instance-specific vs. population-level feature selection.  
  *Why needed here*: The method targets instance-specific selection where the optimal feature set varies per instance, unlike global methods (e.g., lasso) that select one set for all instances, which is suboptimal when features are predictive only for subsets.  
  *Quick check question*: Why might a globally optimal feature set be inadequate for a patient with atypical symptoms?
- **Concept**: Regularization via occupancy measures (REPS).  
  *Why needed here*: The Lq-regularization is derived from the REPS framework, which optimizes a policy by manipulating state occupancy. This converts a policy-level penalty into an instance-level virtual reward.  
  *Quick check question*: In REPS, what is the relationship between the Lagrange multiplier \(\alpha\) and the step-size in gradient methods, as mentioned in Section 2.4.3?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Superstate graph (DAG of feature subsets) -> Q-estimators (kernel ridge regression per superstate/action) -> Heuristic explorer (maintains explored set \(T\), computes \(dRet\)) -> Policy updater (propagates value changes to ancestors) -> Regularizer (adds virtual reward, iterates).
- **Critical path**: Initialize with empty superstate. Heuristic explorer selects next superstate \(S_f\). Policy updater computes optimal \(Q\) for all ancestors of \(S_f\) using current estimates. Repeat until \(T\) reaches size \(N_{\text{max}}\). Apply Lq-regularization iteratively if desired.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  Exhaustive vs. heuristic: Exhaustive search guarantees optimality but is exponential; heuristic sacrifices optimality for scalability.  
  Regularization strength: Controlled by \(\alpha\) and \(q\); higher \(\alpha\)/lower \(q\) yields fewer superstates but risks underfitting.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  Heuristic overexploration of shallow nodes: Figure 5 shows heuristic explores nodes close to root first, potentially missing deep valuable combinations.  
  Regularization trapping: Once a superstate's occupancy reaches zero, it is permanently removed; early misestimation may lose promising superstates.  
  Imputation reliance in baselines: EDDI fails on high-dimensional HTA data because imputation is inaccurate.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Accuracy comparison**: Run on all four datasets with fixed max acquisitions (e.g., 3). Compare AUC against EDDI and Wang et al. (Figure 2). Expect superiority on high-dimensional HTA and Spam.  
  2. **Cost-aware evaluation**: On Heart dataset with real costs, vary \(\lambda\) to generate accuracy-cost trade-off curves. Compare to Wang's method (Figure 4). Expect smoother trade-off from instance-specific policy.  
  3. **Heuristic analysis**: On Spam dataset (56 features), track policy depth, accuracy, and cost as \(T\) grows. Compare heuristic vs. random node selection with two cost settings (Figure 5). Expect heuristic to manage cost better at high acquisition cost.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can the sampling-guided heuristic be improved to avoid overestimating the value of deeper superstates and achieve a better exploration-exploitation trade-off?** The heuristic is optimistic and may overestimate returns, especially near the root, leading to excessive shallow exploration. Adding an uncertainty penalty (e.g., UCB-style) is suggested but not implemented.
- **What theoretical guarantees exist for the convergence and stability of the iterative Lq-regularisation procedure, and how sensitive is it to the hyperparameters \(\alpha\) and \(q\)?** The optimisation is non-concave and only converges to a local maximum; \(\alpha\) acts like a step-size. No convergence proof is provided, and the impact of hyperparameters on sparsity vs. reward is not systematically studied.
- **Can the proposed method be extended to handle continuous or high-cardinality categorical features without relying on pre-processing like PCA?** The framework treats each post-PCA component as a scalar feature. Raw high-dimensional streams (e.g., waveforms) are reduced via PCA, which may lose discriminative information. Extending superstates to functional data or learned embeddings is unresolved.
- **How can meaningful acquisition costs be learned or estimated for datasets where true costs are unavailable?** Only the Heart dataset has real monetary costs; others use uniform costs. Without domain-specific cost labels, the method cannot optimise real-world trade-offs (time, discomfort). A cost-proxy learning framework is needed.
- **Does the sampling-guided exploration strategy scale to datasets with hundreds of features, and what are the computational bottlenecks?** Testing was limited to up to 56 features (Spam). Exponential growth of superstates may make even guided sampling intractable beyond ~50–100 features, especially with limited samples. Computational complexity scaling is not analysed.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter values (\(\lambda\), \(\alpha\), \(q\)) and their tuning procedures (grid search ranges, validation set size) are unspecified.
- Kernel method details (bandwidth, Nyström rank, logistic regularization strength) are vaguely described as "standard" choices.
- The "logarithmic sampling" distribution for heuristic exploration lacks a precise formula, blocking exact replication.

## Confidence
- **High**: MDP/superstate formulation (clear mathematical definition in paper).
- **Medium**: Sampling-guided heuristic (algorithm outline present, but sampling mechanics ambiguous).
- **Low**: Lq-regularization (derivation sketch provided, but no empirical tuning protocol or robustness evidence).
