# A Lyapunov Analysis of Softmax Policy Gradient for Stochastic Bandits

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26547
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26547
- **Reference count:** 2
- **Primary result:** Establishes an expected regret of \(O(k \log n \log k / \eta)\) for softmax policy gradient with learning rate \(\eta = O(\Delta_{\min}^2 / (\Delta_{\max} \log(nk)))\), revealing a quadratic dependence on the minimum gap \(\Delta_{\min}\).

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the softmax policy gradient algorithm for \(k\)-armed stochastic bandits in discrete time. By constructing a Lyapunov function and deriving high-probability bounds on the logit trajectories, the authors prove that with a gap-dependent learning rate \(\eta = O(\Delta_{\min}^2 / (\Delta_{\max} \log(nk)))\), the expected regret scales as \(O(k \log(n) \log(k) / \eta)\). The analysis aligns with continuous-time limits but yields a regret bound with a quadratic inverse dependence on the smallest gap \(\Delta_{\min}\), which is atypical for bandit problems and suggests either a non-tight analysis or non-standard assumptions.

## Method Summary
The analysis centers on a Lyapunov function defined in terms of the policy’s suboptimality gaps. The method uses high-probability bounds to control the evolution of the logit parameters under the softmax policy gradient update. The learning rate \(\eta\) is chosen based on the minimum and maximum gaps \(\Delta_{\min}\) and \(\Delta_{\max}\) to ensure the Lyapunov drift is negative in expectation, leading to a bound on cumulative regret. The derivation carefully tracks second-order terms and leverages concentration inequalities for stochastic rewards.

## Key Results
- The softmax policy gradient algorithm is analyzed via a Lyapunov function for \(k\)-armed stochastic bandits.
- A gap-dependent learning rate \(\eta = O(\Delta_{\min}^2 / (\Delta_{\max} \log(nk)))\) is required for the theoretical guarantee.
- The resulting expected regret is \(O(k \log(n) \log(k) / \eta)\), implying a quadratic dependence on \(1/\Delta_{\min}\).
- The bound matches the continuous-time analysis, supporting the Lyapunov approach but raising questions about tightness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Lyapunov function measures the weighted sum of suboptimality gaps, and its expected decrease per round is bounded using the gradient update structure and reward concentration. The high-probability logit bounds ensure parameters do not diverge, allowing the drift analysis to hold uniformly over time. The specific \(\eta\) choice balances the step size against the gap magnitudes, ensuring sufficient progress while controlling variance.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Initial logits \(\theta\) -> Softmax probabilities -> Action selection -> Reward observation -> Gradient computation -> Parameter update (with \(\eta\)) -> New logits.

**Critical path**: The core loop is: compute softmax over current logits, sample action, observe reward, compute gradient estimate, update logits via \(\theta \gets \theta + \eta \cdot \hat{\nabla}\). The regret accumulates from suboptimal actions during this loop.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Larger \(\eta\) speeds initial learning but risks instability and poor logit bounds.
- Softmax temperature (often absorbed into \(\eta\)) trades off exploration vs. exploitation.
- Using \(\Delta_{\min}\) in \(\eta\) yields theoretical guarantees but is impractical without prior knowledge.

**Failure signatures**: 
- Regret scaling as \(1/\Delta_{\min}^2\) empirically, contradicting standard \(1/\Delta_{\min}\) lower bounds.
- Logit values diverging or oscillating if \(\eta\) is too large relative to gaps.
- Performance collapse when \(\Delta_{\min}\) is very small due to overly conservative \(\eta\).

## Limitations
- The learning rate \(\eta\) depends on the unknown minimum gap \(\Delta_{\min}\), making the algorithm impractical without prior knowledge or adaptation.
- The derived regret bound shows a quadratic dependence on \(1/\Delta_{\min}\), which is significantly worse than the standard \(O(1/\Delta_{\min})\) lower bound and may indicate a non-tight analysis.
- The analysis is restricted to finite-armed stochastic bandits; extensions to infinite-armed or contextual settings are not considered.

## Confidence
- **Lyapunov-based regret bound**: Medium. The method is consistent with recent policy gradient analyses, but constants and interaction with \(\eta\) need careful verification.
- **Gap-dependent step size \(\eta\) & continuous-time match**: Low. The dependence on \(\Delta_{\min}\) is atypical and likely impractical; the continuous-time analogy requires rigorous discretization error control.
- **Quadratic \(\Delta_{\min}\) dependence**: Low. This scaling contradicts known lower bounds, suggesting either a misinterpretation or a non-standard performance metric.
