# Dynamics Reveals Structure: Challenging the Linear Propagation Assumption

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.21601
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21601
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: First-order linear parameter updates cannot preserve logical coherence for compositional reasoning due to a geometric incompatibility between negation and conjunction.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether infinitesimal parameter updates can maintain logical coherence in neural models, formalizing the Linear Propagation Assumption (LPA) through the lens of relation algebra. The authors define Systematic Linear Propagation (SLP), which requires that gradient features of queries respect logical equivariance (negation as sign flip, converse as invariance) and linear independence across query families. Using group representation theory, they prove that satisfying negation and converse forces the feature representation to factorize into a tensor product separating entity‑pair context from relation content. However, incorporating conjunction—necessary for composition—demands bilinearity, which contradicts the sign‑flip constraint and leads to a total collapse of the feature space. Thus, first‑order linear updates are fundamentally incapable of supporting systematic compositional reasoning.

Empirical analysis of large language models reveals that gradient features for facts and their negations are positively aligned (mean cosine similarity ~0.85) rather than anti‑aligned (-1.0). This violates the SLP negation condition and suggests that current models do not admit the required factorization. The authors argue that this geometric mismatch underlies persistent failures in knowledge editing, the reversal curse, and multi‑hop reasoning, pointing to the need for either non‑linear update dynamics or explicit architectural biases that enforce logical structure.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a theoretical framework based on relation algebra, treating logical negation, converse, and composition (conjunction) as operations on binary relations. They model the gradient feature map ϕ(q) = ∇_θ s_θ(q)|_{θ₀} and impose SLP: (i) logical equivariance (ϕ(¬q) = −ϕ(q), ϕ(rev(q)) = ϕ(q)), and (ii) linear independence of representatives from each logical family. The query space is equipped with a product group action **G**<sub>E</sub> × **Z**<sub>2</sub> (entity renaming and negation). By Maschke’s theorem, the representation decomposes into irreducible components; Schur’s lemma then forces ϕ to split as a tensor product of **G**<sub>E</sub>- and **Z**<sub>2</sub>-representations (Theorem 1). Adding converse invariance yields an additional involution, decomposing each tensor block into symmetric and antisymmetric parts aligned in parity (Theorem 2). For conjunction, they assume kernel stability under updates (Assumption 1), implying a bilinear operator on the quotient space. Combining bilinearity with negation’s sign flip forces every feature vector to zero, i.e., feature collapse (Theorem 3).

Empirically, the authors compute ϕ for factual triples and their negations from several LLMs. They measure average cosine similarity and find strong positive alignment (~0.85), far from the required -1.0. No tests for converse or conjunction are reported. The results indicate that real models violate the SLP premises, thus falling outside the regime where the theoretical incompatibility applies, yet still experiencing the practical failures it explains.

## Key Results
- Propagation for negation and converse requires a tensor‑factorized representation that separates entity‑pair context from relation content.
- Conjunction must be bilinear in the feature space, which conflicts with the sign‑flip from negation, causing a structural collapse (all features forced to zero) if both constraints hold.
- Empirical gradients from LLMs show positive alignment (~0.85) between facts and negations, violating the negation equivariance condition and indicating that current models do not satisfy SLP.
- Consequently, first‑order linear updates cannot support coherent compositional reasoning, providing a geometric explanation for editing failures, the reversal curse, and multi‑hop reasoning breakdowns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The theoretical analysis rests on three core mechanisms:

#### Mechanism 1: Negation forces tensor factorization
- **Claim**: If linear features satisfy SLP with respect to negation, the feature map decomposes as a tensor product separating entity‑pair context from relation content.
- **Mechanism**: The query space carries a representation of the product group **G**<sub>E</sub> × **Z**<sub>2</sub> (entity permutations and negation). By Maschke’s theorem, the representation is completely reducible; Schur’s lemma then implies that any equivariant linear map splits into a tensor product of irreducible **G**<sub>E</sub>- and **Z**<sub>2</sub>-components. The **Z**<sub>2</sub> factor carries the sign flip required for negation.
- **Core assumptions**: SLP (negation equivariance and linear independence of query families) and Tarski’s invariance under entity renaming.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract; Section 3.1 (Theorem 1). Related work *Logical Negation Augmenting and Debiasing for Prompt‑based Methods* studies negation robustness but does not directly confirm the factorization.
- **Break condition**: Negation equivariance fails (ϕ(¬q) not anti‑aligned with ϕ(q)) or linear independence is violated; then the factorization need not hold.

#### Mechanism 2: Converse refines factorization to parity‑aligned blocks
- **Claim**: If features are also converse‑invariant (ϕ(rev(q)) = ϕ(q)), each tensor block further decomposes into symmetric and antisymmetric parts that match the parity of the entity‑pair factor with that of the relation factor.
- **Mechanism**: Converse induces involutions **P**<sub>pair</sub> on the context space (swap head/tail) and **P**<sub>rel</sub> on the relation space (conjugate relation). Invariance forces the feature into the +1 eigenspace of **P**<sub>pair</sub> ⊗ **P**<sub>rel</sub>, i.e., a direct sum of blocks where context and relation have identical symmetry.
- **Core assumptions**: SLP plus converse invariance.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract; Section 3.2 (Theorem 2). *ROG: Retrieval‑Augmented LLM Reasoning for Complex First‑Order Queries over Knowledge Graphs* handles reversed queries but does not expose the geometric alignment.
- **Break condition**: Converse invariance is not enforced; then the symmetric/antisymmetric alignment constraint is unnecessary and directionality may be encoded arbitrarily.

#### Mechanism 3: Conjunction leads to feature collapse
- **Claim**: Under kernel stability (Assumption 1), conjunction in the feature space must be bilinear; combining bilinearity with negation equivariance forces all feature vectors to zero, i.e., structural collapse.
- **Mechanism**: Assumption 1 (T<sub>q</sub>(ker Φ) ⊆ ker Φ for all q) ensures conjunction is well‑defined on the quotient space **W**<sub>∧</sub> and inherits a bilinear operator **F̃**. Idempotence of conjunction and negation’s sign flip yield **F̃**(−u,−u) = −u from logic, while bilinearity gives **F̃**(−u,−u) = u, forcing u = 0 for all u.
- **Core assumptions**: Assumption 1 (kernel stability), symmetry of conjunction, and exact negation equivariance.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract; Section 4 (Theorem 3, Fig. 3). No direct corpus anchor; *ComLQ: Benchmarking Complex Logical Queries* evaluates complex queries but does not investigate geometric incompatibility.
- **Break condition**: Either kernel stability fails (conjunction not bilinear) or negation equivariance is only approximate; then collapse is avoided but systematic propagation for composition is not guaranteed.

## Foundational Learning
- **Relation Algebra**  
  *Why needed*: Provides the algebraic operations (negation, converse, composition) that define the logical structure the analysis aims to preserve under updates.  
  *Quick check*: What is the set‑theoretic definition of the converse of a binary relation *r* ⊆ *E*×*E*? (Answer: *r*<sup>∩</sup> = { (t,h) | (h,t) ∈ *r* }.)

- **Group Representation Theory**  
  *Why needed*: Translates symbolic symmetries (entity renaming, negation) into linear constraints on feature spaces; Maschke’s theorem and Schur’s lemma derive the tensor factorization.  
  *Quick check*: What does it mean for a vector space *V* to be a representation of a group *G*? (Answer: A homomorphism ρ : *G* → GL(*V*) assigning a linear operator to each group element, preserving multiplication and identity.)

- **Systematic Linear Propagation (SLP)**  
  *Why needed*: The paper’s central formalization of the Linear Propagation Assumption; defines exactly what “coherent update” means in the linearized regime.  
  *Quick check*: What are the two conditions of SLP? (Answer: (1) Logical equivariance: ϕ¬q = –ϕq and ϕ<sub>rev</sub>(q) = ϕq. (2) Linear independence of representatives from each logical family.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Query encoder → score s_θ(q); Linearized feature extractor → ϕ_q = ∇_θ s_θ(q)|_{θ₀} (typically last block + LM head); Logical operations (¬, rev, ∧) impose constraints on ϕ_q; Feature space W = span{ϕ_q}; Symmetry modules G_E (entity permutations) and Z_2 (negation) act linearly on W.

- **Critical path**:
  1. Choose a reference model state θ₀ and a diverse query set Q (including negations and converses).
  2. Compute gradient features ϕ_q for all q ∈ Q.
  3. Test negation equivariance: average cosine(ϕ_q, ϕ_{¬q}) should be ~ –1.0.
  4. Test converse invariance: compare ϕ(h,r,t) with ϕ(t,r^∩,h).
  5. Verify linear independence across logical families (e.g., via matrix rank).
  6. For composition, identify unique‑witness triples and test bilinear combination of component features (e.g., via regression).
  7. If failures occur, probe for factorization structure (e.g., CP decomposition).

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - *Exact SLP vs. capacity*: Strict equivariance reduces expressive capacity; approximate equivariance via regularization may balance performance and coherence.
  - *Factorization vs. parameter efficiency*: Tensor‑product structures introduce interpretable slots but increase parameters; unstructured risks interference.
  - *Linear conjunction vs. nonlinear dynamics*: The collapse is proven for first‑order updates; allowing nonlinearities or finite step sizes may evade it but sacrifices analytical guarantees.
  - *Global vs. local updates*: Infinitesimal updates are assumed; practical editing may require iterative nonlinear procedures to achieve propagation.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Gradient misalignment: positive mean cosine similarity (> 0.5) between facts and negations.
  - No context‑relation separation: probing queries fail to decode entities and relations independently from features.
  - Editing backfire: updating a fact increases score of its negation or unrelated queries.
  - Compositional collapse: multi‑hop queries show no systematic change after editing component facts; feature norms near zero.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Negation gradient test**: For 100 factual queries, compute ϕ_q and ϕ_{¬q}; report distribution of cosine similarities. Median near –1 supports SLP; ~0.85 indicates failure.
  2. **Factorization probe**: Assemble a 3‑D feature tensor (head, tail, relation). Apply CP decomposition; compare estimated rank against number of logical families. Low rank ≈ factorization; high rank suggests entanglement.
  3. **Bilinear conjunction check**: For many (p, q) pairs with a unique witness b, compute ϕ_{p∧q} directly and compare to bilinear combination ⟨ϕ_p, M⟩ϕ_q learned by regression. High R² indicates bilinear structure; low indicates violation of Assumption 1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Weaker/approx equivariance?** Do approximate notions of logical equivariance (e.g., allowing small deviations from perfect anti‑alignment) still lead to the same geometric constraints and collapse, or can they enable systematic composition under first‑order updates? (Explicitly mentioned; unresolved because the impossibility relies on strict equivariance.)
2. **Enforcing geometry?** Can architectural modifications or training objectives explicitly enforce the tensor‑factorized geometry and compatible bilinear/negation structures, thereby improving negation consistency and compositional propagation? (Explicit; unresolved as no concrete interventions were tested.)
3. **Nonlinear conjunction/updates?** Does the fundamental incompatibility persist if conjunction is allowed to be nonlinear or updates are not strictly first‑order? (Inferred; unresolved because analysis is confined to the linearized regime; higher‑order effects might evade collapse.)
4. **Causal role of gradient misalignment?** To what extent does the observed positive gradient alignment between facts and negations causally contribute to failures in knowledge editing and the reversal curse, versus other factors like data sparsity? (Explicit; unresolved because only correlation is shown.)
5. **Learnability of factorization?** Are the tensor‑factorized structures required by Theorem 1 inherently learnable via standard gradient‑based pretraining on factual data, or do they require explicit architectural biases or special initialization? (Inferred; unexplored in the paper.)

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis relies on strong, exact assumptions (SLP, linear independence, kernel stability) that real models only approximate.
- Empirical evaluation is limited to negation alignment; converse and conjunction remain untested.
- No reproduction artifacts are provided, hindering independent verification.
