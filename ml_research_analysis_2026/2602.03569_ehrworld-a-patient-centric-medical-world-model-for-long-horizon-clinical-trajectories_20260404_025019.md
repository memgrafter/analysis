# EHRWorld: A Patient-Centric Medical World Model for Long-Horizon Clinical Trajectories

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03569
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03569
- **Reference count:** 36
- **Primary result:** EHRWorld-14B achieves S@25=0.716, Label F1=0.913, retention=92.6%, and inference efficiency 23,318 tokens/s, significantly outperforming GPT-5.2 on full-trajectory prediction.

## Executive Summary
Clinical simulation with large language models (LLMs) suffers from error accumulation over long horizons due to inconsistent patient state tracking. This work introduces EHRWorld, a patient-centric medical world model trained on a causally sequential paradigm, and the EHRWorld-110K dataset of 110K hospitalization episodes with 17.5M events derived from MIMIC-IV. EHRWorld uses a set-based conditional generator with a dual-mode mechanism that distinguishes inquiry (predicting values) and intervention (state-altering) actions, updating patient states deterministically via a history buffer. In full-trajectory prediction, EHRWorld-14B attains an S@25 of 0.716 and Label F1 of 0.913, far exceeding GPT-5.2 (0.674, 0.553). It maintains a retention rate of 92.6% versus GPT-5.2’s 86.2%, demonstrating superior stability. The model is also more robust under acute clinical shifts and offers the best inference efficiency (1.29s latency, 23,318 tokens/s).

## Method Summary
EHRWorld addresses the problem of simulating long-horizon clinical trajectories with consistent patient state evolution. The task is to predict a sequence of clinical events (both inquiries and interventions) from admission to discharge, conditioned on the patient’s evolving state. Inputs are derived from the EHRWorld-110K dataset, which combines static patient context (demographics, hierarchical diagnoses) extracted from discharge summaries with a temporally ordered event sequence from structured EHR logs. Events are categorized as *Inquiry* (e.g., lab tests with values) or *Intervention* (e.g., medications with no immediate value). The model uses a set-based conditional generator fine-tuned from a base LLM (Qwen variants). At each step, given the current state S_t (timestamp, demographics, diagnoses, interaction history) and a concurrent set of actions A_t, the generator predicts outcomes V_t: for inquiries, continuous/discrete values; for interventions, an empty outcome. The interaction history is deterministically updated by appending (action, outcome) pairs, and the next state S_{t+1} includes the updated history. Training employs a next-step prediction objective with causal masking and a loss solely on inquiry outcomes. The model is evaluated on full-trajectory autoregressive rollout, measuring numerical precision (S@25, SMAPE), label accuracy (F1), stability (retention rate = full/next-step score), and robustness on high-sensitivity steps.

## Key Results
- EHRWorld-14B achieves S@25=0.716 and Label F1=0.913 on full-trajectory prediction, significantly outperforming GPT-5.2 (S@25=0.674, F1=0.553).
- The model maintains a retention rate of 92.6% (full vs. next-step), compared to GPT-5.2’s 86.2%, indicating much lower error accumulation.
- Under clinical shifts where values change drastically (>50%), EHRWorld-14B shows the smallest performance degradation (S@25 drop -1.9, SMAPE increase +0.030) and is the most inference-efficient (1.29s latency, 23,318 tokens/s).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** EHRWorld mitigates long-horizon error accumulation by enforcing deterministic, history-conditioned state updates.
- **Mechanism:** After each simulation step, the interaction history H_t is strictly updated with the new event set E_t. The next patient state S_{t+1} is constructed from this cumulative history, ensuring all future predictions are conditioned on the complete, unaltered sequence of prior inquiries and interventions. This creates a closed-loop where the state is a pure function of the history.
- **Core assumption:** Physiologically consistent simulation requires that the patient state at any time t be fully determined by the chronological sequence of all preceding clinical events, not by the model's parametric recall.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section 4.4] "The interaction history is then deterministically updated: H_{t+1} = H_t ∪ {E_t}, S_{t+1} = <τ_{t+1}, d, Y, H_{t+1}>. This update loop ensures that all subsequent predictions are conditioned on the complete sequence of prior inquiries and interventions."
  - [Abstract] "...trained under a causal sequential paradigm... updating patient states deterministically."
- **Break condition:** If the deterministic update rule is violated (e.g., by allowing stochastic history pruning or non-causal attention), the model would lose its guaranteed consistency, reverting to error-prone parametric state tracking.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The dual-mode prediction mechanism (inquiry vs. intervention) prevents spurious value generation for state-altering actions, preserving state integrity.
- **Mechanism:** The model is trained to treat intervention actions (e.g., drug administration) differently from inquiry actions (e.g., lab tests). For interventions, the outcome is set to empty (∅), meaning no immediate observable value is predicted. The action's sole purpose is to be recorded in the history H_t to influence future state evolution. This separates the concept of an immediate measurement from a latent state change.
- **Core assumption:** Clinical interventions primarily exert causal influence on future patient states rather than producing immediate, directly observable readouts (unlike inquiries). Modeling them as value-less events prevents the model from hallucinating immediate, often incorrect, physiological effects.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section 4.3] "For intervention actions... the model is not required to generate an immediate observable value. We formalize this by assigning an empty outcome Pθ(v=∅)=1. Instead of yielding direct feedback, these actions are recorded into the patient’s history as state-altering intervention events."
  - [Section 1, Figure 1] Illustrates a naive LLM failing "to infer implicit physiological states or correctly update patient status following medical interventions."
- **Break condition:** If the model were required to predict an immediate value for interventions, it would learn to correlate actions with short-term, often noisy or absent, measurements, corrupting the latent state transition model.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Training on the EHRWorld-110K dataset, constructed with a causally sequential alignment of static context and temporally ordered events, grounds the model in real-world clinical dynamics.
- **Mechanism:** The data pipeline extracts a static patient profile (d, Y) from discharge summaries and integrates it with a strictly chronological event sequence from structured logs. This creates training examples where each event step t is conditioned on the true preceding history, forcing the model to learn the true conditional distribution P(V_t | S_t, A_t) derived from real care trajectories.
- **Core assumption:** Learning a medical world model requires supervision that mirrors the causal, sequential nature of clinical care—where today's state is the result of all prior actions and observations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section 3] Details the pipeline: "extracting episode-level static patient profiles... organizing temporally ordered event sequences... integrating... at the hospitalization-episode level, followed by rigorous quality filtering."
  - [Abstract] "...trained on a causally grounded, temporally evolving clinical data."
- **Break condition:** Using non-sequential data (e.g., shuffled EHR records) or static QA pairs for training would not provide the necessary signal for learning state transitions, leading to poor long-horizon performance akin to naive LLMs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Causal/Multi-step Time Series Forecasting**
  - **Why needed here:** The core task is predicting future values in a sequence where each step depends causally on all previous steps and interventions. This is more complex than single-step prediction.
  - **Quick check question:** Given a sequence of [Medication A, Lab Test X, Medication B], what must a model condition on to predict the Lab Test Y after Medication B? (Answer: The entire prior sequence and the latent effect of Medication A on the patient's state, not just the immediate prior value).
- **Concept: State-Space Modeling**
  - **Why needed here:** EHRWorld explicitly represents the patient state S_t as a latent bundle of timestamp, demographics, diagnoses, and history. Understanding that the world model's job is to update this hidden state based on actions is key.
  - **Quick check question:** In S_{t+1} = <τ_{t+1}, d, Y, H_{t+1}>, which component changes deterministically after a new event, and which remain static? (Answer: H_{t+1} and τ_{t+1} change; d and Y are static).
- **Concept: Set-Based Conditional Generation**
  - **Why needed here:** Real clinical orders are issued in batches (a set of actions at a timestamp). The model must generate a coherent set of outcomes V_t for this batch, conditioning on the shared current state.
  - **Quick check question:** If a clinician issues {Heparin, Acetaminophen} simultaneously, must the prediction for Heparin's effect depend on Acetaminophen's immediate outcome? (Answer: No, they are conditionally independent given S_t per Eq. 3, but both will affect H_{t+1} and thus future states).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** The architecture consists of an Input Encoder that processes the current patient state S_t (static demographics/diagnoses, interaction history H_t, timestamp τ_t); a Set-Based Conditional Generator (core) that uses a transformer with cross-attention to produce outcomes V_t for a set of actions A_t; a Dual-Mode Head that routes each action to either an inquiry branch (predicting a value) or an intervention branch (outputting an empty token); and a Deterministic State Update Module that appends (action, outcome) pairs to H_t and updates τ_t. The loss sums negative log-likelihoods only over inquiry outcomes.
- **Critical path:** Data Preprocessing (extract static profiles and ordered events to create EHRWorld-110K) → Model Training (fine-tune base LLM on next-event prediction with dual-mode loss and causal masking) → Autoregressive Rollout (simulate full trajectories using deterministic state updates).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Set-based vs. Sequential Action Modeling: Sets mirror real-world order batches but sacrifice fine-grained intra-batch temporal dependencies. Tradeoff: clinical realism vs. modeling simplicity.
  - Dual-Mode Simplicity vs. Expressiveness: Forcing interventions to have no immediate outcome simplifies learning but may miss rare, immediate adverse events documented as an inquiry. The model must learn that some effects are latent.
  - Deterministic Updates vs. Stochastic State Evolution: The update is deterministic, but the underlying distribution Pθ is stochastic. Stability comes from the structure; a fully stochastic update would re-introduce error accumulation.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High retention rate crash: A significant drop in Ret (%) (full/next) from ~90%+ to <80% indicates the deterministic history buffer fails to constrain the model, often due to training instability or data sparsity.
  - Sensitivity to clinically abrupt shifts: Large performance delta (S@25 drop, SMAPE spike) on the H_sens subset shows the model hasn't learned consistent response dynamics for acute events.
  - Inquiry mode degradation on post-intervention labs: If lab metrics (F1, S@25) collapse for tests immediately after major interventions during full rollout (but not next-step), it signals latent effects not propagating correctly through history.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Establish Baseline Error Accumulation: Run a naive LLM (e.g., GPT-5.2) on full trajectories and compare next-step vs. full rollout metrics to quantify the retention gap.
  2. Ablate Dual-Mode Mechanism: Train a variant where interventions must also predict a dummy value; expect severe drop in retention rate and high-sensitivity stability.
  3. Test Generalization on UnseenPatients: Use the patient-centric test split; a small performance drop from next-step to full rollout indicates robust learning versus patient-specific memorization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can EHRWorld further mitigate residual error propagation inherent in its autoregressive state updates over extremely long horizons (e.g., months of simulated care)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Limitations section states the model "still relies on autoregressive generation, which can propagate residual inaccuracies over extended rollouts."
- **Why unresolved:** The current deterministic update loop (Eq. 5) uses the entire history H_t, but the paper does not analyze if this design is optimal for preventing drift in sequences beyond the evaluated trajectory length. The mechanism for compressing or summarizing long histories to avoid error compounding is not explored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies comparing different history representation strategies (e.g., learned summary vectors, fixed-length buffers, or attention-based compression) on rollout fidelity for trajectories significantly longer than those in EHRWorld-110K, measuring performance decay curves.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the superior trajectory stability of EHRWorld translate into improved performance on downstream, decision-centric clinical tasks like treatment effect estimation or counterfactual policy evaluation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The Limitations section explicitly notes evaluation "does not directly assess downstream clinical utility such as treatment optimization, policy learning, or real-world decision support."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper benchmarks state prediction fidelity but does not connect this to a clinical outcome of interest. A model can generate plausible lab values yet still fail to correctly simulate the causal effect of a specific medication on a critical outcome like mortality or readmission.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An evaluation where EHRWorld-simulated trajectories are used as a component in an off-policy evaluation framework (e.g., to estimate the effect of a drug regimen). The estimated effects would be compared against outcomes from a validated causal model or real-world clinical trial data.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent does EHRWorld's performance depend on the specific data distribution and preprocessing choices of the MIMIC-IV-derived EHRWorld-110K dataset, and how well does it generalize to other hospital systems or geographies?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The dataset construction (Section 3) involves aggressive filtering (e.g., LOS and event count percentiles, removal of zero-medication episodes) and uses MIMIC-IV, a single US hospital network. The patient-centric split (by subject_id) prevents leakage but doesn't test generalization to entirely new healthcare systems with different documentation practices, coding standards, or treatment protocols.
- **Why unresolved:** The high retention rate (92.6%) is demonstrated on a held-out test set from the same data generation pipeline. It is unclear if the model's causal sequential learning captures universal physiological principles or merely memorizes patterns specific to the source EHR's structure and treatment norms.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** External validation on a completely independent, non-MIMIC EHR dataset (e.g., from a different country or private hospital network). Performance should be reported with and without lighter, more domain-agnostic preprocessing to test robustness to source heterogeneity.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does EHRWorld handle clinically plausible but rare or missing data patterns (e.g., co-occurring lab abnormalities, unmapped intervention codes) that were filtered out or are underrepresented in the training data?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The data pipeline applies "stringent filtering criteria" and removes episodes with "incomplete records" or "incomplete, duplicated, or administratively recorded entries" (Section 3.1). The model is trained on a curated subset, so its response to low-frequency, high-severity clinical scenarios or ambiguous documentation is unknown.
- **Why unresolved:** The evaluation metrics (S@25, F1) are population-averaged. A model that performs well on common cases may still fail catastrophically on edge cases critical for safety, such as simulating a patient with a rare combination of comorbidities or an intervention with a delayed, non-linear effect. The paper's case study (Figure 4) shows a common scenario.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Targeted stress-testing on a curated set of clinically challenging, low-prevalence trajectories (e.g., septic shock progression, complex polypharmacy interactions) extracted from the data's tails or from expert-constructed cases. Metrics should include failure rate and plausibility of the entire simulated sequence, not just point-wise accuracy.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** What specific architectural components of EHRWorld (e.g., the set-based generator, dual-mode mechanism, or deterministic state transition) are most responsible for its improved stability compared to a naive LLM, and can this insight be transferred to improve general LLM-based simulators?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper proposes a unified architecture but does not provide an ablation study isolating the contribution of its key innovations (causal sequential training objective, set-based generation, intervention vs. inquiry modes). The comparison is against end-to-end fine-tuned or prompted LLMs, not against ablations of the EHRWorld design.
- **Why unresolved:** While the results show EHRWorld outperforms baselines, the critical factor—whether it's the training data, the loss function's treatment of interventions, or the structured state representation—removes ambiguity. Without ablation, it's unclear if the gains require the full proposed system or if simpler modifications to LLM training could achieve similar robustness.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study training variants of the base model (e.g., without set-based generation, with a unified mode for all actions, or with a probabilistic instead of deterministic state update) on the same dataset. Performance would be compared on full-trajectory retention rate and error accumulation metrics to identify the minimal necessary components for stability.

## Limitations
- Architecture details (dual-mode heads, history truncation) missing, hindering reproduction.
- No ablations for deterministic update or dual-mode; gains could stem from scale or data size.
- Only MIMIC-IV evaluated; external validity untested.

## Confidence
- Deterministic updates reduce error accumulation: Medium
- Dual-Mode preserves state integrity: Medium
- Causal sequential training essential: Medium
