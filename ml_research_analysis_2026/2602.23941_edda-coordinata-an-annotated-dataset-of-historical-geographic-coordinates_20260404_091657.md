# EDDA-Coordinata: An Annotated Dataset of Historical Geographic Coordinates

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.23941  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23941  
- **Reference count**: 0  
- **Primary result**: A two-step pipeline (BERT classifier + mT5-small seq2seq) for historical coordinate retrieval achieves 86% exact match in cross-validation and generalizes to 18th-century French (61%) and 19th-century English (77%) texts.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces EDDA-Coordinata, a gold standard dataset of 4,798 historical geographic entries from the *Encyclopédie*, annotated with normalized coordinates. It tackles the difficulty of automatically extracting coordinates from historical texts that exhibit diverse, non-standard formats. The solution is a two-step pipeline: first, a BERT-based binary classifier filters articles containing coordinates; second, an mT5-small sequence-to-sequence model retrieves and normalizes them to DMS format. The system reaches 86% exact match in 5‑fold cross‑validation and demonstrates promising cross‑lingual and cross‑domain performance (61% on an 18th‑century French dictionary, 77% on a 19th‑century English encyclopaedia). The dataset and models are publicly released.

## Method Summary
The method consists of two fine‑tuned transformer models. A multilingual BERT classifier processes raw article text (limited to 512 tokens) and predicts whether the article contains any geographic coordinates. Articles flagged as positive are then fed to an mT5‑small sequence‑to‑sequence model, which generates normalized coordinate strings in DMS format. For articles with multiple coordinates (e.g., subentries or multiple sources), the outputs are concatenated into a single target string, though the exact delimiter is not specified. Both models are trained using 5‑fold cross‑validation: BERT for 4 epochs, mT5‑small for 10 epochs, with Adam optimizer and default settings where specifics are omitted. Evaluation employs Exact Match (EM) and Character Error Rate (CER) for retrieval, and standard classification metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1) for the classifier.

## Key Results
- **In‑domain CV EM**: 86%
- **Out‑of‑domain EM**: 61% on the 18th‑century French *Trévoux* dictionary, 77% on the 19th‑century English *Encyclopædia Britannica*
- The gold standard dataset (4,798 positives + 10,480 negatives) and trained models are publicly available.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not explicitly elaborate the underlying mechanisms that drive performance. The two‑step design likely leverages the classifier to isolate relevant articles, thereby simplifying the seq2seq task. The mT5 model, pre‑trained on multilingual data, may generalize to varied historical notations due to its generative formulation and exposure to diverse patterns during pre‑training. However, the authors do not provide theoretical or empirical justification for these choices in the supplied summary.

## Foundational Learning

2. **mT5 for multilingual seq2seq generation** – A multilingual T5 variant that maps raw text to structured DMS strings. *Why needed*: Handles noisy, inconsistent historical notations by learning to generate a normalized target format. *Quick check*: Can mT5 reliably output consistent DMS representations when trained on concatenated multi‑coordinate strings?
3. **DMS normalization** – Standardizes output to degrees‑minutes‑seconds format. *Why needed*: Ensures interoperability and aligns with common historical coordinate representations. *Quick check*: Would decimal degrees be a viable alternative for modern downstream use?
4. **5‑fold cross‑validation** – Splits data into five train/test folds. *Why needed*: Maximizes use of limited annotated data and yields robust performance estimates. *Quick check*: Is the variance across folds sufficiently low to trust the mean EM?
5. **Pre‑filtering pipeline** – Separates detection from extraction. *Why needed*: Reduces false positives and focuses seq2seq training on relevant examples. *Quick check*: Would an end‑to‑end model (detection + extraction) suffer from increased label noise?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Raw article text → BERT classifier → contains_coordinates flag → if true → mT5‑small seq2seq → normalized coordinate string(s)

**Critical path**  
The pipeline’s critical path runs sequentially through both models; end‑to‑end latency is dominated by the mT5 generation step.

**Design tradeoffs**
- Two‑step vs. end‑to‑end: Pre‑filtering simplifies the retrieval task but introduces an extra failure point if the classifier misclassifies.
- Model size: mT5‑small is computationally efficient but may underfit complex, irregular historical notations; larger variants could improve accuracy at higher cost.
- Concatenation of multiple coordinates: Treating all coordinates as a single string avoids structured output complexity but may harm parsing when many coordinates appear; a structured delimiter or JSON‑like format could be more robust.

**Failure signatures** (from reported modes)
1. Poor performance on DMS‑format coordinates and surface/sequence types (bounding boxes, polygonal chains) → model struggles with irregular notations.
2. High Character Error Rate (CER) but near‑Exact Match → minor formatting issues (e.g., missing apostrophes, cardinal direction case) rather than numeric mistakes.
3. Significant out‑of‑domain drop (61% vs. 86% in‑domain) → limited generalization to new linguistic/typographic conventions.

**First three experiments**
1. Replicate BERT classification: train on 4/5 folds, evaluate on held‑out; report accuracy, precision, recall, F1.
2. Replicate mT5‑small retrieval: train on same splits; compute EM and CER; manually inspect a sample of failures to identify error patterns.
3. Out‑of‑domain evaluation: test the trained pipeline on the Trévoux and Britannica samples; verify that EM aligns with the reported 61% and 77%.

## Limitations
- Unspecified concatenation delimiter for multiple coordinates in mT5’s target strings.
- Missing hyperparameter details (learning rate, batch size) and tokenization settings (padding/truncation).
- No provided error‑type breakdown (formatting vs. numeric) or per‑category performance (D/DM/DMS, point/surface/sequence).
