# Is Memorization Helpful or Harmful? Prior Information Sets the Threshold

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09405
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09405
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Memorization necessity or harmfulness in overparameterized linear regression is determined by prior-dependent thresholds \(J_\pi^{-1}\) and \(V_\pi\); Bayes training error bounds and asymptotic regimes are derived, and optimal generalization requires training error near these bounds.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies the role of memorization (perfectly fitting training data) in overparameterized linear regression under a Bayesian prior \(\pi\). The authors focus on the Bayes estimator and derive tight bounds on its training error in terms of two prior-dependent parameters: the Fisher information \(J_\pi\) and the variance \(V_\pi\). The bounds reveal two distinct regimes: when the noise variance \(\sigma^2\) is small (\(\sigma^2 \le J_\pi^{-1}\)), memorization is necessary for near‑optimal generalization; when \(\sigma^2\) is large (\(\sigma^2 \ge V_\pi\)), overfitting is harmful. These thresholds are fully determined by the prior, and the paper illustrates them with isotropic and low‑rank priors, showing how the effective dimension of the prior dictates which regime applies. The results provide a precise, prior‑specific criterion for when interpolating solutions are benign or detrimental.

The analysis combines information‑theoretic inequalities (Stam, Cramér–Rao) with an exact Bayes risk decomposition that ties training error to prediction error. This approach yields asymptotic expansions for extreme noise levels and quantifies the cost of deviating from the Bayes training error. The work clarifies when memorization is an unavoidable feature of optimal learning in high‑dimensional linear models.

## Method Summary
The authors consider a linear model \(y = X\theta + \varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I_n)\) and a Bayesian prior \(\theta \sim \pi\). The Bayes estimator minimizes expected prediction error. The training error of an estimator \(\hat\theta\) is defined as \(\frac{1}{n}\|X\hat\theta - y\|^2\). The key insight is that the training error of the Bayes estimator can be expressed as \(\sigma^4\) times the Fisher information \(J(\sigma^2)\) of the marginal distribution of \(y\) under the prior. By bounding \(J(\sigma^2)\) using the Fisher information of the prior density (\(J_\pi\)) and the variance parameter \(V_\pi = \frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}[\|X\theta\|^2]\), the authors obtain both upper and lower bounds on \(\operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta_B)\). The lower bound uses the Cramér–Rao inequality, while the upper bound uses Stam’s inequality on the convolution of the prior score with Gaussian noise. Asymptotic analysis for \(\sigma^2 \to 0\) and \(\sigma^2 \to \infty\) yields simple approximate formulas. To connect training error to generalization, the authors prove a Bayes risk decomposition showing that any estimator’s excess prediction error is at least \(\frac{1}{\lambda_\Sigma}\) times the squared deviation of its training error from the Bayes training error, where \(\lambda_\Sigma\) depends on the feature covariance. Combining these pieces gives the memorization and overfitting thresholds.

## Key Results
- The Bayes training error satisfies: \(\sigma^4 / V_\pi + \sigma^2 \le \operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta_B) \le \sigma^4 / J_\pi^{-1} + \sigma^2\).
- As \(\sigma^2 \to 0\), \(\operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta_B) \sim J_\pi \sigma^4\) (near‑zero memorization necessary); as \(\sigma^2 \to \infty\), \(\operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta_B) \sim \sigma^2 - V_\pi\) (limited overfitting).
- For any estimator, if \(\sigma^2 \le J_\pi^{-1}\) then \(\operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta) > J_\pi \sigma^4\) implies strictly positive excess risk; if \(\sigma^2 \ge V_\pi\) then \(\operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta) < \sigma^2 - V_\pi\) implies strictly positive excess risk. Thus memorization is necessary in the first regime and overfitting harmful in the second.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Bayes estimator's training error is tightly bounded by two prior-dependent parameters: it satisfies  
  \[
  \sigma^4 / V_\pi + \sigma^2 \;\le\; \operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta_B) \;\le\; \sigma^4 / J_\pi^{-1} + \sigma^2.
  \]
- **Mechanism:** The bound emerges from the representation of training error as \(\sigma^4 J(\sigma^2)\), where \(J(\sigma^2)\) is the Fisher information of the marginal distribution of \(y\). The upper bound follows from the Fisher information inequality (Stam) applied to the convolution \(\pi' * \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I_n)\), yielding \(J(\sigma^2) \le J_\pi^{-1} + \sigma^2\). The lower bound follows from the Cramér–Rao inequality and the variance parameter \(V_\pi = (1/n) \mathbb{E}[\|X\theta\|^2]\).
- **Core assumption:** The prior \(\pi\) possesses a positive and differentiable density on \(\mathbb{R}^d\) so that Fisher information and the variance parameter exist and are finite.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] identifies Fisher information \(J_\pi\) and variance \(V_\pi\) as the determining factors and explicitly states the thresholds.
  - [section 2.3] Theorem 1 contains the exact inequality (2) and the asymptotic expansions (3)–(4).
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers such as “Rethinking Benign Overfitting in Two-Layer Neural Networks” study phase transitions in overfitting but do not derive explicit prior‑based thresholds, highlighting the novelty of the mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If \(\pi\) lacks a smooth density (e.g., exactly low‑rank priors in Section 3.2), \(J_\pi\) may blow up and the upper bound becomes trivial; the analysis would require alternative tools (e.g., direct computation for discrete priors).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** As the noise level \(\sigma^2\) approaches extreme values, the Bayes estimator’s training error simplifies to two distinct regimes:  
  - \(\sigma^2 \to 0\): \(\operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta_B) = J_\pi \sigma^4 + o(\sigma^4)\) (near‑zero, memorization is necessary).  
  - \(\sigma^2 \to \infty\): \(\operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta_B) = \sigma^2 - V_\pi + o(1)\) (limited overfitting, never far from \(\sigma^2\)).
- **Mechanism:** The asymptotic forms are obtained by expanding the Fisher information function \(J(\sigma^2)\) (and consequently \(\operatorname{Train}(\sigma^2) = \sigma^4 J(\sigma^2)\)) using properties of Gaussian convolutions and Tweedie’s formula. The low‑noise limit uses continuity of \(J(\sigma^2)\) at \(0^+\); the high‑noise limit exploits the fact that \(\pi'_\sigma\) becomes indistinguishable from \(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I_n)\), giving \(J(\sigma^2) \to 1/\sigma^2\).
- **Core assumption:** Finite \(J_\pi\) and \(V_\pi\) together with the differentiability of the prior density allow interchange of limits and expectations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] plainly states the asymptotic approximations.
  - [section 2.3] Equations (3) and (4) are the precise statements proved.
  - [corpus] Works such as “How Does Label Noise Gradient Descent Improve Generalization in the Low SNR Regime?” discuss low‑noise behavior but do not connect the scaling constant to a prior‑specific Fisher information, underscoring the specificity of this mechanism.
- **Break condition:** The continuity argument for \(J(\sigma^2)\) at \(0^+\) relies on lower semicontinuity of Fisher information under weak convergence; heavy‑tailed or singular priors could violate this property, making the low‑noise expansion invalid.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Whether an optimal estimator must memorize (i.e., have very low training error) or must avoid overfitting (i.e., have training error near \(\sigma^2\)) is completely determined by comparing \(\sigma^2\) against the thresholds \(J_\pi^{-1}\) and \(V_\pi\).  
  - If \(\sigma^2 \le J_\pi^{-1}\), any estimator with \(\operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta) > J_\pi\sigma^4\) suffers a strictly positive excess risk (Corollary 2.1).  
  - If \(\sigma^2 \ge V_\pi\), any estimator with \(\operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta) < \sigma^2 - V_\pi\) suffers a strictly positive excess risk (Corollary 2.2).
- **Mechanism:** The Bayes estimator is optimal for prediction error (Proposition 1). Moreover, Proposition 1 gives a lower bound on the excess prediction error in terms of the difference between training errors. By plugging the training error bounds of Theorem 1 into this inequality, the two corollaries follow directly: deviating from the Bayes training error incurs a cost that scales quadratically or linearly in the deviation.
- **Core assumption:** The Bayes risk decomposition (variance‑bias style) holds and \(\lambda_\Sigma = \| \Sigma^{-1/2} X^\top X \Sigma^{-1/2}\| / n\) is positive and finite (i.e., \(\Sigma\) is positive definite and \(X\) has full row rank).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] highlights that the thresholds are “determined solely by the prior.”
  - [section 2.4] Corollaries 2.1 and 2.2 present the exact conditions and lower bounds.
  - [corpus] In “Harmful Overfitting in Sobolev Spaces,” overfitting can be detrimental, but the criticism is expressed through Sobolev smoothness, not prior‑dependent thresholds; this contrast clarifies the unique Bayesian‑prior perspective of the present work.
- **Break condition:** In infinite‑dimensional Hilbert space settings (e.g., kernel regression), \(\lambda_\Sigma\) can be infinite, which changes the form of the lower bound entirely (the theory predicts benign overfitting at all noise levels). Hence the threshold mechanism does not directly apply without adjusting the cost decomposition.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Bayesian inference with a prior over parameters  
  - Why needed here: The whole analysis is built on the model \(\theta \sim \pi\) and the Bayes posterior; the Bayes estimator is the reference optimal predictor.  
  - Quick check question: How does the zero‑mean assumption on \(\pi\) simplify the expressions for \(V_\pi\) and the Fisher information?
- **Concept:** Fisher information as a local measure of effective dimension under the prior  
  - Why needed here: \(J_\pi^{-1}\) approximates the local curvature of \(\pi\) around zero and thus governs how much the data must be memorized when the noise is small.  
  - Quick check question: Why does a larger \(J_\pi\) (i.e., a “spikier” prior) lower the memorization threshold?
- **Concept:** Training error vs. prediction error trade‑off via the Bayes risk identity  
  - Why needed here: Proposition 1 shows that any estimator’s excess prediction error is lower bounded by a function of the difference between its training error and that of the Bayes estimator. This is the key step linking memorization thresholds to generalization.  
  - Quick check question: What role does \(\lambda_\Sigma\) play in the quantitative lower bound on \(\operatorname{Cost}_\Sigma\)?
- **Concept:** Overparameterized linear regression (\(d \ge n\)) and interpolation capacity  
  - Why needed here: Overparameterization is required for memorization to be possible; otherwise zero training error cannot be achieved at all.  
  - Quick check question: Under what condition on \(X\) is there always a solution in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) that interpolates any response vector \(y\)?
- **Concept:** Asymptotic analysis of \(\operatorname{Train}(\sigma^2)\) and monotonicity  
  - Why needed here: To classify the low‑noise and high‑noise regimes, we use that \(\operatorname{Train}(\sigma^2)\) is non‑decreasing and \(\operatorname{Train}(\sigma^2)/\sigma^4\) is non‑increasing (Proposition 6).  
  - Quick check question: Why might \(\operatorname{Train}(\sigma^2)/\sigma^2\) be non‑monotonic, and what does that imply about the threshold transition?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  - **Inputs:** Prior distribution \(\pi\) (density function or sampling oracle), design matrix \(X \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\), noise variance \(\sigma^2\), test point covariance \(\Sigma\).  
  - **Core components:** Bayes estimator \(\hat\theta_B = \mathbb{E}[\theta \mid X,y]\), prior‑dependent quantities \(J_\pi = \frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\nabla\log p(X\theta)\|^2\bigr]\) and \(V_\pi = \frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}[\|X\theta\|^2]\), and \(\lambda_\Sigma = \|\Sigma^{-1/2}X^\top X \Sigma^{-1/2}\|/n\).  
  - **Outputs:** Training error bounds \(\sigma^4/V_\pi+\sigma^2 \le \operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta_B) \le \sigma^4/J_\pi^{-1}+\sigma^2\), memorization threshold \(J_\pi^{-1}\), overfitting harm threshold \(V_\pi\), and the lower bounds on excess prediction risk.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Characterize the marginal density \(p(y)\) induced by \(X\theta\) under \(\pi\).  
  2. Compute or approximate \(J_\pi\) and \(V_\pi\) (analytically for simple priors or via Monte Carlo).  
  3. Compare the given \(\sigma^2\) with the thresholds \(J_\pi^{-1}\) and \(V_\pi\) to decide which regime (memorization necessary or overfitting harmful) applies.  
  4. For any estimator \(\hat\theta\) under consideration, evaluate its training error and use the corollaries to verify whether it is near‑optimal.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Computing \(J_\pi\) exactly may be infeasible; one can use score‑function estimation techniques or finite‑difference approximations if sampling from \(\pi\) is easy.  
  - \(\lambda_\Sigma\) involves the extremal eigenvalues of \(X\Sigma^{-1/2}X^\top\); in high dimensions these can be estimated via random matrix theory (e.g., Bai–Yin law).  
  - The theory assumes a smooth density \(\pi\); if the prior has singular support (e.g., exact sparsity), the Fisher information can be infinite and the thresholds must be interpreted with care (see Section 3.2 for the low‑rank example).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Infinite or undefined \(J_\pi\): the upper bound collapses, and memorization may be “free” at all noise levels, contradicting the stated necessity threshold.  
  - Violating the full‑row‑rank assumption on \(X\): leads to a degenerate least‑squares solution and can invalidate the interpolation argument.  
  - Using the thresholds in underparameterized settings (\(d < n\)): the paper’s results assume \(d \ge n\); underparameterization changes the landscape entirely (interpolation impossible).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Isotropic Gaussian prior:** Generate \(X\) with i.i.d. entries as in Assumption 1; estimate \(V_\pi\) and \(J_\pi\) numerically and confirm the limits \(V_\pi \to 1\), \(J_\pi \to \gamma/(\gamma-1)\).  
  2. **High‑noise test:** For a fixed prior (e.g., isotropic), increase \(\sigma^2\) and compute \(\operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta_B)\); verify it approaches \(\sigma^2 - V_\pi\).  
  3. **Low‑noise test:** Decrease \(\sigma^2\) and check that \(\operatorname{Train}(\hat\theta_B) \approx J_\pi\sigma^4\) and that any estimator with training error \(> 2J_\pi\sigma^4\) yields a non‑zero excess risk (Corollary 2.1).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Generalize memorization thresholds (J_π⁻¹, V_π) to non-linear models and non-Bayesian settings?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Sec. 5: "extend to more general model classes F_Λ ... outside the Bayesian framework."
- **Why unresolved:** Paper relies on linear structure and Bayesian conjugacy; nonlinear settings lack tractable tools.
- **Evidence:** Develop theory for neural nets or empirical tests of memorization thresholds.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the memorization theory in infinite-dimensional settings (e.g., kernel regression) where λ_Σ = ∞?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Sec. 5: "In Hilbert spaces ... λ_Σ = ∞ ... benign overfitting occurs for arbitrary noise levels. A future theory ... different analytical approach."
- **Why unresolved:** Paper's thresholds become vacuous when λ_Σ = ∞; Hilbert space results contradict finite-dimensional thresholds.
- **Evidence:** Characterize thresholds via kernel eigenvalue decay; prove necessity/harmfulness for specific kernels.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How do results change for singular priors (e.g., exactly low-rank) without a density?
- **Basis:** [inferred] Paper assumes differentiable density; exact low-rank prior is excluded (Sec. 3.2 handled separately).
- **Why unresolved:** J_π, V_π may be undefined; phenomenon might still hold with different parameters.
- **Evidence:** Extend Fisher information approach to singular measures; compute thresholds for ℓ0-like priors.

## Limitations
- The theoretical framework requires the prior to have a smooth, everywhere‑positive density; singular or discrete priors can lead to infinite Fisher information and invalidate the bounds.
- Results are limited to fixed‑design linear regression with known noise variance; extensions to random designs, heteroscedastic noise, or adaptive estimation are not addressed.
- Computing \(J_\pi\) and \(V_\pi\) may be intractable for complex priors, and the analysis excludes underparameterized (d < n) and infinite‑dimensional settings where different overfitting behaviors arise.

## Confidence
- **High:** Theorem 1 (training error bounds) and the asymptotic expansions (Eqs. 3–4) are rigorously proved under the smooth‑prior assumptions.
- **Medium:** Corollaries 2.1–2.2 (threshold conditions) rely on the finiteness of \(\lambda_\Sigma\), which may not hold in high‑dimensional regimes, potentially weakening practical relevance.
- **Low:** Extensions to arbitrary prior structures beyond the illustrative isotropic and low‑rank cases are speculative and not supported by formal results.

## Next Checks
- Monte Carlo experiments with non‑smooth priors (e.g., Laplace, spike‑and‑slab) to test whether the bounds hold or require modification.
- High‑dimensional empirical study: generate random \(X\) with large \(n, d\), estimate \(J_\pi\) and \(V_\pi\) from samples, and verify that the regime transitions occur at the predicted \(\sigma^2\) thresholds.
- Extension to kernel ridge regression: derive analogous thresholds in terms of kernel effective rank and compare with existing literature on benign overfitting.
