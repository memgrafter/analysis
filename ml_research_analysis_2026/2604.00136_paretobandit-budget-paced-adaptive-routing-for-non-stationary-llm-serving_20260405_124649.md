# ParetoBandit: Budget-Paced Adaptive Routing for Non-Stationary LLM Serving

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.00136
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00136
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A contextual bandit router with a closed-loop budget pacer maintains per-request cost within 0.4% of target across a 530× cost range, adapts to price/quality drift, and safely onboards new models.

## Executive Summary
ParetoBandit solves the problem of adaptively routing LLM inference requests across a portfolio of models under a strict per-request dollar budget, even when model costs and qualities change over time. It combines a cost-aware contextual bandit with an online primal-dual budget pacer to enforce cost ceilings and uses geometric forgetting to rapidly track non-stationarity. Evaluated on 1,824 prompts, it achieves precise budget compliance, recovers from simulated price cuts and quality regressions, and safely integrates new models. The system operates with low latency (~10 ms) and requires only CPU resources.

## Method Summary
ParetoBandit extends LinUCB, a linear contextual bandit. For each request with context *x*ₜ (a PCA-reduced prompt embedding), it scores each model *a* by combining predicted reward (θ̂ₐᵀ*x*ₜ) with an exploration bonus and a cost penalty. The cost penalty is dynamically adjusted by a Budget-Pacer, an online primal-dual controller that increases a dual variable λₜ when the recently smoothed average cost exceeds the budget *B*. This λₜ modifies scores and applies a hard ceiling to filter out overly expensive models. To handle non-stationarity (e.g., price changes, quality drift), the bandit applies geometric forgetting to its sufficient statistics (Aₐ, bₐ), discounting old observations by a factor γ per time step. New models are hot-swapped into a registry with a short forced-exploration phase to gather initial data without violating the budget. The system is initialized with offline warm-start priors derived from historical logs.

## Key Results
- Maintains mean per-request cost within 0.4% of seven different budget targets across a portfolio with ~530× cost spread.
- Under a simulated 10× price cut for the most expensive model, achieves up to +0.071 quality lift (relative) and restores budget compliance when the cut is reversed.
- Detects a silent quality regression in one model and re-allocates traffic away from it within the same budget envelope.
- A cold-started model reaches meaningful adoption (~20% traffic share) in approximately 142 interactions without breaching the cost ceiling.
- End-to-end routing latency is 9.8 ms on a single CPU core, with the routing decision itself taking 22.5 µs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Budget-Pacer Enforcement**: An online primal-dual loop uses an exponential moving average (EMA) of recent costs. If the EMA exceeds budget *B*, a dual variable λₜ ascends, adding a penalty λₜ·c̃ₐ (log-normalized static cost) to the UCB score. A secondary hard ceiling excludes arms whose blended price exceeds cₐ^max/(1+λₜ). This closed-loop control replaces manual penalty tuning.
  - *Core assumption*: The static log-normalized cost c̃ₐ preserves the relative cost ordering across prompts, allowing a single λₜ to scale penalties universally.
  - *Evidence*: Table 2 shows mean cost deviation ≤0.4% across all budgets. The dual variable dynamics are defined in Section 3.2.
  - *Break condition*: If the dynamic ceiling falls below the cheapest arm's cost, no candidates remain. λₜ oscillates if EMA smoothing αₑₘₐ is too low; response is sluggish if too high.

- **Geometric Forgetting for Drift**: Before incorporating a new observation for arm *a*, its sufficient statistics (Aₐ, bₐ) are multiplied by γ^dt, where *dt* is steps since the last update. Staleness-based variance inflation increases the UCB bonus for arms not recently played, encouraging re-exploration of potentially changed arms.
  - *Core assumption*: The environment drifts slowly enough that recent data is more relevant; the forgetting rate γ can be tuned to the drift timescale.
  - *Evidence*: Section 4.4 demonstrates recovery from a silent quality regression; Section 4.3 shows exploitation of a 10× price cut.
  - *Break condition*: If γ is too low (<0.99), variance inflates excessively causing instability; if γ=1 (no forgetting), adaptation fails (as shown by the "Forgetting Bandit" overspend in Table 2).

- **Runtime Portfolio Management**: A hot-swap model registry allows adding/removing models without downtime. New arms start with optional warm-start priors and undergo a brief forced-exploration phase (e.g., 20 pulls) to collect initial data. The Budget-Pacer ensures compliance during this burn-in.
  - *Core assumption*: Forced exploration yields sufficiently accurate initial estimates within a bounded number of steps.
  - *Evidence*: Section 4.5 shows a cold-started model achieves ~20% adoption in ~142 steps without budget breaches.
  - *Break condition*: Forced exploration too short yields poor estimates; too long wastes budget. If a new arm's cost always exceeds the dynamic hard ceiling, it may never be selected.

## Foundational Learning
- **LinUCB (Contextual Bandit with Linear Payoffs)**
  - *Why needed*: Provides the base online learning algorithm that balances exploitation (θ̂ₐᵀ*x*ₜ) and exploration (α√xₜᵀAₐ⁻¹xₜ). The per-arm sufficient statistics Aₐ (design matrix) and bₐ (reward sum) are core to updates.
  - *Quick check*: Aₐ accumulates outer products of context vectors; its inverse gives the covariance estimate used in the exploration bonus.

- **Geometric Discounting for Non-Stationarity**
  - *Why needed*: Allows the bandit to down-weight old observations so it can adapt when reward or cost distributions drift.
  - *Quick check*: For γ=0.997, the effective half-life of observations is ln(2)/(1-γ) ≈ 231 steps.

- **Lagrangian Relaxation with Dual Ascent**
  - *Why needed*: Converts the constrained budget maximization into an unconstrained problem by adding a penalty λₜ·cₐ to the selection score, with λₜ updated based on overspending.
  - *Quick check*: λₜ increases when the EMA of recent costs exceeds *B*; enforcement occurs via a soft penalty in the score and a hard ceiling on candidate arms.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Feature Extractor (embed + PCA) -> BanditRouter (LinUCB engine) -> BudgetPacer (dual controller) <- Model Registry (add/delete arms, forced exploration)
- **Critical path**:
  - Synchronous inference: prompt → feature extraction → router scoring (K arms) → dispatch.
  - Asynchronous feedback: reward/cost → geometric forgetting update → BudgetPacer EMA/dual ascent.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - PCA dimensionality: d=26 vs. 385 gives ~14.8× throughput gain but risks losing context nuance.
  - Exploration vs. budget: Forgetting inflates variance, which can select expensive arms; λₜ penalizes but requires careful tuning of α and γ.
  - Warm-start priors: Reduce early regret and per-seed variance but risk harm if mis-calibrated; *n*ₑff acts as a safety knob.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Budget overshoot: λₜ fails to rise or hard ceiling ineffective → check EMA αₑₘₐ and candidate filtering logic.
  - Poor adaptation: γ too close to 1 → monitor forgetting decay (effective memory) and staleness inflation *V*ₘₐₓ.
  - New model never adopted: forced exploration too short or cost always above dynamic ceiling → check exploration count and *V*ₘₐₓ cap.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Validate stationary budget pacing across 7 targets, confirming cost compliance ≤1.004× and frontier shape (Section 4.2).
  2. Simulate a 10× price cut on the costliest model; verify quality lift and budget recovery after restoration (Section 4.3).
  3. Artificially degrade one model's reward; check detection, reallocation, and budget hold (Section 4.4).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Live deployment with asynchronous human feedback**: How does the system perform with stochastic reward delays and real-world traffic shifts? The paper's evaluation uses offline, deterministic judge scores.
- **Aggregate budget enforcement over billing cycles**: Can the router enforce a cumulative dollar budget (e.g., monthly) while optimizing per-request quality? The current pacer regulates long-run average, not short-term bursts.
- **Joint cost and latency SLAs**: How to extend ParetoBandit to respect tail-latency guarantees alongside cost ceilings? The current formulation ignores inference delays and endpoint congestion.
- **Inverse objective: minimize cost subject to quality floor**: Can the method be re-formulated for quality-constrained routing (minimize cost while meeting a reward threshold)? The current Lagrangian is designed for cost-constrained maximization.

## Limitations
- **Missing PCA projection**: The exact 26-component PCA matrix from 46k LMSYS prompts is unreleased, preventing exact context encoding.
- **Unreleased reward matrices**: Per-prompt judge scores for each model are not provided, blocking precise replication of the reward signal.
- **Unspecified warm-start priors**: The offline sufficient statistics used for initialization (A_off, b_off) are not detailed, affecting cold-start curve replication.
- **Portfolio scale**: Evaluation uses only 3 models; performance on larger, more diverse portfolios is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Tight budget compliance (0.4%)**: Medium. Results are consistent across seeds and budgets, but reproducibility gaps (PCA, rewards) lower confidence.
- **Adaptation to non-stationarity**: Medium. Simulations show recovery and lift, yet real-world drift dynamics may be more complex.
- **Cold-start performance**: Medium. The ~142-step adoption metric depends on forced-exploration length and priors; exact priors are unknown.
- **Low routing latency**: High. Direct timing measurements on CPU are straightforward and credible.
