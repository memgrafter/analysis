# From Debate to Deliberation: Structured Collective Reasoning with Typed Epistemic Acts

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11781
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11781
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** DCI improves non-routine task quality by +0.95 over unstructured debate and sets a hidden-profile SOTA (9.56), but fails on routine tasks (5.39) at ~62× higher token cost than single-agent generation.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Deliberative Collective Intelligence (DCI), a framework for structured multi-agent reasoning where differentiated delegates exchange typed epistemic acts, preserve disagreements as persistent "tensions," and converge via a guaranteed-termination algorithm (DCI-CF). Evaluated on 45 tasks, DCI significantly outperforms unstructured debate on non-routine problems and achieves the highest recorded score on hidden-profile perspective-integration tasks. However, it degrades on routine tasks and incurs substantial token overhead. The core trade-off is rigorous deliberation for tasks that require explicit risk surfacing and minority accountability, at the cost of efficiency.

## Method Summary
DCI operationalizes collective reasoning through a phased session model (Arrival, Independent Thought, Mutual Engagement, Collective Shaping, Closure). Four delegate archetypes (Framer, Explorer, Challenger, Integrator) communicate via a grammar of 14 typed epistemic acts (e.g., `CHALLENGE`, `BRIDGE`, `SYNTHESIZE`), with each move constrained by mode, act, and intent. A shared workspace tracks problem views, key frames, emerging ideas, explicit tensions, and synthesis. The DCI-CF convergence algorithm loops through option development, challenge rounds, and multi-criteria scoring, terminating via a deterministic fallback cascade (outranking → minimax → satisficing → Integrator pick) if needed. Outputs are structured decision packets including minority reports and reopen conditions. Evaluation used Gemini 2.5 Flash delegates and Gemini 3 Flash Preview as an LLM judge across seven task domains, scoring outputs 1–10 on completeness, reasoning, risk ID, tradeoffs, and actionability.

## Key Results
- On non-routine tasks, DCI surpasses unstructured debate by +0.95 points (statistically significant).
- On hidden-profile perspective-integration tasks (n=5), DCI achieves 9.56, exceeding prior SOTA.
- DCI fails on routine tasks, scoring 5.39 vs. debate’s 8.58 (degradation ~3.0).
- Token cost is ~62× higher than single-agent generation, which achieves higher overall quality.
- DCI produces 100% structured decision packets and 98% minority reports; baselines produce none.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Typed Acts Enforce Accountable Progression
Structuring interaction via 14 typed epistemic acts transforms parallel monologue into a traceable protocol where each move has a prescribed purpose and response grammar. This prevents undifferentiated chatter and aligns outputs with cognitive functions (e.g., probing vs. generating). The grammar’s soft/hard move distinction naturally guides progression from exploration to convergence. Evidence: The paper’s abstract and Section 3.3 describe the grammar and its phased appropriateness. Break condition: If delegates ignore type constraints (high invalid-move rates), the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 2: Tension Preservation Counteracts Premature Consensus
Persistent, explicit tracking of disagreements as "tensions" in the shared workspace prevents superficial agreement from burying epistemic diversity. Differentiated archetypes ensure complementary perspectives, and tensions remain visible across rounds, forcing explicit engagement. Evidence: Section 3.4 states tensions are preserved rather than resolved prematurely; Section 7.2 notes delegates contributed distinct partial perspectives integrated during synthesis. Break condition: If delegates ignore listed tensions or the Integrator prematurely resolves them without challenge, the mechanism collapses.

### Mechanism 3: Guaranteed Convergence via Bounded Fallback
The DCI-CF algorithm’s constraints (max rounds, capped options, recursion depth) and deterministic fallback cascade ensure termination even under persistent disagreement, while preserving minority positions in the final packet. Evidence: Section 4.1 and Theorem 1 (Appendix) prove termination; Section 7.5 reports 100% task completion with structured packets. Break condition: If forced fallback rate is high (>80%) with low-quality outcomes, or recursion limits cause incomplete sub-sessions, closure is unreliable.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Phased Session Model  
  **Why needed:** Separates divergence (independent proposals) from engagement (structured challenge) to prevent early social influence from destroying perspective diversity, which is critical for hidden-profile tasks.  
  **Quick check:** What harm occurs if the "Independent First Thought" phase is skipped? Early influence flattens diversity needed for integrating fragmented information.

- **Concept:** Typed Epistemic Acts Grammar  
  **Why needed:** Transforms undifferentiated messages into analyzable reasoning moves. Response rules (e.g., `CHALLENGE` invites `DEFEND`) create predictable interaction structure.  
  **Quick check:** How does `BRIDGE` differ from `EXTEND`? `BRIDGE` connects distinct ideas; `EXTEND` builds on a single idea.

- **Concept:** Tension Preservation in Shared Workspace  
  **Why needed:** Makes disagreements persistent, first-class objects rather than ephemeral chat, ensuring they are addressed and carried into the final decision packet.  
  **Quick check:** Why is a workspace-stored tension better than a transcript disagreement? Workspace tensions are explicitly tracked and revisited; transcript disagreements are easily forgotten.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Delegates (4 archetypes: Framer, Explorer, Challenger, Integrator) -> Interaction Move Schema (14 acts, triple structure) -> Shared Workspace (JSON state with 6 sections) -> DCI-CF Workflow (8-stage algorithm) -> Structured Decision Packet (with minority reports)

### Critical Path
1. Session init -> independent proposals (Stage 1-2).  
2. Loop (Stage 3-6): collect typed acts -> revise/compress options -> score -> test convergence (score dominance/majority/no blocking objection).  
3. If not converged and rounds remain -> loop back.  
4. If rounds exhausted or no convergence -> Stage 7 forced fallback (outranking → minimax → satisficing → Integrator pick).  
5. Finalize packet (Stage 8).

### Design Tradeoffs
- Cost vs. Structure: ~62× token cost vs. structured outputs, minority reports, hidden-profile gains.  
- Flexibility vs. Guarantee: Bounded openness (new evidence allowed pre-cutoff) trades exploration for termination guarantee.  
- Archetype differentiation: Prompt-based role assignment is cheaper than model diversity but may drift; true diversity (multi-model) showed preliminary gains (+0.58) but adds complexity.

### Failure Signatures
- Routine task degradation: DCI scores ~3 points below debate on straightforward tasks (5.39 vs 8.58).  
- Archetype role drift: Delegates abandon cognitive orientation after ~3 rounds (e.g., Challenger softens objections).  
- Sycophantic convergence: Even specialized delegates converge prematurely.  
- Cascade failures: Malformed early-stage output propagates.  
- Forced fallback overuse: >50% fallback rate indicates natural convergence rarely achieved.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Component ablation on hidden-profile tasks:** Run A1 (No Archetypes), A2 (No Typed Grammar), A3 (No DCI-CF) on the 5 hidden-profile tasks. Expect DCI’s advantage (9.56) to drop most without typed grammar or DCI-CF.  
2. **Token efficiency baseline comparison:** Measure quality-per-token on routine vs. non-routine tasks. Confirm ~62× multiplier and that single-agent dominates on efficiency.  
3. **Archetype adherence monitoring:** Log each delegate’s act-type distribution per phase. Test if `Challenger` issues >40% critical acts (`CHALLENGE`, `ASK`) early and declines post-round 2 (validate drift).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does incorporating model-diverse delegates affect DCI’s effectiveness across domains, and does this interact with task type? Preliminary diverse-council experiment (n=5) improved architectural-domain quality but generalizability is unknown.
- Can task characteristics be reliably identified ex-ante to predict whether DCI’s overhead yields net benefits over single-agent or debate? The paper suggests an adaptive system but lacks a formal predictive model.
- Why do DCI component ablations sometimes match or exceed the full framework’s mean quality, and what explains the high variance when the typed grammar is removed? Ablations showed non-significant improvements with "No Typed Grammar" exhibiting high variance (±1.47 vs. ±0.68).
- To what extent do DCI’s structured decision packets improve accountability and decision quality in applied settings, and how should they be designed for maximum practical utility? Evaluation uses only LLM-as-judge scores, not real-world uptake; human evaluation is needed.

## Limitations
- Unknown exact task prompts and judge rubrics, hindering direct replication and risking ecological mismatch.  
- Missing verbatim system prompts for archetypes; described roles may not enforce intended cognitive constraints (role drift observed).  
- High token cost estimate lacks phase breakdown; implementation details could alter efficiency significantly.  
- Evaluation solely on Gemini models; generalizability to other LLM families is unclear.  
- Hidden-profile tasks are few (n=5); statistical power for domain-specific claims is limited.

## Confidence
- **Structured outputs & minority reports (100%/98%):** High (concrete, directly measured).  
- **Non-routine task improvement (+0.95):** Medium (paired test reported, but task heterogeneity and judge subjectivity introduce variance).  
- **Routine task failure (-3.0 vs. debate):** Medium (clear negative, but cause—over-structuring vs. prompt sensitivity—needs more tasks).  
- **Hidden-profile state-of-the-art (9.56):** Low-Medium (narrow benchmark, unknown difficulty ceiling, possible evaluation setup differences).  
- **Guaranteed termination (Theorem 1):** High (proof provided; 100% session completion reported).

## Next Checks
1. **Grammar enforcement stress test:** Run 50 DCI sessions with automated move-type validation logging. Target: <5% invalid moves per round; if >15%, prompts/schema need reinforcement.
2. **Convergence algorithm audit:** Replicate DCI-CF on 20 held-out non-routine tasks with logging of every convergence test. Target: natural convergence (Stage 6) achieved in >40% of rounds; else, adjust scoring function or `convergence_margin`.
3. **Archetype drift quantification:** During 30 sessions, classify each delegate’s move by act family (critical vs. generative). Target: `Challenger` issues >40% critical acts in Engagement phase; if `Explorer` drops below 30% generative acts, role prompts require strengthening.
