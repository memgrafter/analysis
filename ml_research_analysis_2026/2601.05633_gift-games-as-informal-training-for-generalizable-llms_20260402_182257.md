# GIFT: Games as Informal Training for Generalizable LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05633  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05633  
- **Reference count:** 39  
- **Primary result:** Combining formal math training with nested game-based RL significantly improves LLM generalization, raising average scores on MMLU, MMLU-Pro, CommonGen, and SocialIQA from 38.34% to 42.43% (1.5B) and from 42.00% to 55.84% (7B); nested training consistently outperforms naive mixed training.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) lack generalizable "practical wisdom" — such as strategic and social reasoning — largely due to insufficient informal learning from interactive experience. This paper introduces GIFT, which treats games (Matrix Games, TicTacToe, Who’s the Spy) as informal learning environments and proposes a Nested Training Framework. The framework sequentially composes formal and informal tasks into a single trajectory, enforcing an explicit AND objective that prevents the task interference typical of naive mixed (OR) training. Experiments show that formal math training combined with nested game-based RL yields substantial gains in broad ability benchmarks across model sizes, demonstrating that both structured and informal learning are necessary for robust generalization.

## Method Summary
GIFT uses reinforcement learning (via GRPO) to train a base LLM on a combination of formal mathematical problems and three game environments designed to cultivate strategic, planning, and social reasoning. The key innovation is the nested training paradigm, where multiple sub-tasks (e.g., a math problem followed by a Matrix Game) are executed sequentially within a single rollout. The model receives a reward equal to the average success across all sub-tasks only after completing the entire sequence, forcing it to maintain competence across all tasks simultaneously. This contrasts with mixed training, which samples one sub-task per trajectory and suffers from gradient dominance and entropy collapse. For smaller models (1.5B), a warm-up phase with mixed data precedes full nested training to ensure learnability.

## Key Results
- For the 1.5B model, the average score on MMLU, MMLU-Pro, CommonGen, and SocialIQA increased from 38.34% (formal-only training) to 42.43% when formal math was combined with nested game-based RL.
- For the 7B model, the average score rose from 42.00% to 55.84% under the same combination.
- Nested training consistently outperformed mixed training across all tested configurations on general ability benchmarks, while mixed training often showed in-domain overfitting and collapse.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Treating games as informal learning environments provides scalable, interactive feedback signals that cultivate generalizable competencies like strategic and social reasoning, which formal learning alone does not develop.
- **Mechanism:** Games act as abstracted sandboxes where the model learns through interaction and intrinsic rewards (e.g., winning, correct voting) rather than via manually annotated, goal-oriented instruction. This mimics human informal learning, which occurs through unstructured experience and iterative feedback, fostering "practical wisdom."
- **Core assumption:** Intelligence requires more than structured knowledge acquisition; it needs the ability to adapt and reason in open-ended, interactive scenarios, which games can simulate.
- **Evidence anchors:**

  - [section 3.2] Details how games embody informal learning properties: unstructured interaction, no explicit instructions, and diverse scenarios. It explicitly links game categories to targeted abilities.
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors show active research on "LLM for Games" and "Game for LLM", supporting the relevance of games as a training paradigm.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Nested Training Framework prevents task interference and ensures simultaneous acquisition of multiple abilities by enforcing an explicit AND objective, in contrast to naive mixed training's implicit OR objective.
- **Mechanism:** Nested training sequentially composes multiple sub-tasks into a single trajectory. The model receives a reward based on the average success across all sub-tasks only after completing the entire sequence. This forces the policy to maintain competence across all tasks within one rollout, preventing any single task from dominating gradient updates and preserving exploration (higher entropy).
- **Core assumption:** Multi-task degradation in mixed training stems from the OR-style objective allowing sub-tasks to be solved independently; coupling tasks at the trajectory level aligns gradients for joint competence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Explicitly contrasts the framework's "explicit AND objective" with naive mixing's "implicit OR objective".
  - [section 3.4] Formally defines the nested objective and argues it prevents premature convergence, maintains higher entropy, and yields stable gradients, supported by visualizations.
  - [section 4.2] Empirical results show nested training consistently outperforms mixed training on general ability benchmarks, while mixed training often shows in-domain overfitting and collapse.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Combining formal learning (structured math) with informal learning (games) is necessary for achieving superior generalization to broad ability benchmarks; neither paradigm alone suffices.
- **Mechanism:** Formal training builds precise, structured reasoning on symbolic tasks. Informal training via games adds robustness, creativity, and social reasoning by exposing the model to interactive, multi-faceted feedback. Their integration produces complementary representations that transfer better to unseen, heterogeneous benchmarks.
- **Core assumption:** Generalizable intelligence arises from the interplay of structured knowledge (formal) and adaptive, experience-based skills (informal), as suggested in cognitive science.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] States that combining formal math with nested game-based RL "significantly improves generalization".
  - [section 4.2] Single-task results show formal learning helps structured benchmarks, informal helps creative/social ones; the combination yields highest average scores.
  - [section 4.4.2] Ablation study: removing formal learning causes significant drops in MMLU and average general ability, proving informal-only is insufficient.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning with GRPO/PPO**  
  *Why needed here:* The training uses GRPO (a PPO variant) for policy optimization over trajectories. Understanding advantage estimation, clipping, and group-based normalization is key to interpreting training dynamics.  
  *Quick check:* How does Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) normalize advantages within a group of trajectories, and why might this be important when training on heterogeneous tasks?
- **Concept: Multi-Task Learning Pitfalls (Catastrophic Interference, Gradient Dominance)**  
  *Why needed here:* The core problem is naive mixed training's performance degradation. Understanding why tasks interfere—e.g., one task's gradients dominating—is prerequisite to grasping nested training's necessity.  
  *Quick check:* In an OR-style multi-task objective, why might a task with higher reward scale or easier convergence cause gradients for other tasks to vanish?
- **Concept: Game Theory Basics (Nash Equilibrium, Zero-sum, Role of Information)**  
  *Why needed here:* Matrix Games target strategic reasoning toward Nash Equilibrium. TicTacToe and Who’s the Spy involve adversarial/cooperative dynamics. Without understanding game forms, one cannot evaluate if trained policies exhibit desired reasoning.  
  *Quick check:* In a two-player zero-sum Matrix Game, what does it mean for a strategy pair to be a Nash Equilibrium, and why is selecting such a pair considered a measure of "strategic reasoning"?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Base LLM (Qwen2.5-Instruct) initializes policy π_θ. Task Environments (Math generator, Matrix Games engine, TicTacToe simulator, Who’s the Spy multi-agent env) output trajectories τ_k with terminal rewards. Nested Task Composer orchestrates sequential execution of sub-tasks, concatenates τ_k into τ_nested, and computes R_nested = mean(R_1, R_2, ...). GRPO Trainer (StarPO* in RAGEN) samples G nested trajectories, computes advantages via GAE, applies clipping and entropy regularization, and updates π_θ. Opponent Models (Qwen3-14B, Gemini-2.5-Flash) provide moves in multi-player games.
- **Critical path:** Data flow: Task Env → Trajectory → Nested Composer → τ_nested + R_nested → GRPO (loss calculation) → policy update. The key innovation is the composer's sequential composition, which couples gradients across all sub-tasks.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Nested trajectory length vs. credit assignment:* Longer trajectories improve joint learning but complicate assigning credit for early sub-task successes; mitigated by trajectory-level reward averaging.
  - *Task diversity vs. optimization stability:* More diverse informal tasks improve generalization but increase risk of unsolvable nested sequences for smaller models, necessitating warm-up phases.
  - *Fixed opponent vs. self-play:* Fixed strong opponents provide stable signals but may limit exploration of novel strategies compared to self-play.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **High inter-task variance in success rates:** During training, if one sub-task's success plateaus high while others remain low, nested training may still improve weak tasks—but mixed training would collapse to the dominant task.
  2. **Entropy collapse:** Rapid drop in policy entropy across all sub-tasks indicates premature convergence, typical in mixed training; nested should maintain higher entropy.
  3. **Nested reward saturation failure:** For smaller models (1.5B), directly training on full F+I3 from random initialization may yield ~0% success on all sub-tasks, indicating the AND objective is too difficult; a warm-up phase is required.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Single-task baselines:** Train on Math, Matrix Games, TicTacToe, Who’s the Spy separately to establish that each informal task improves specific general abilities.
  2. **Mixed vs. Nested (F+I_k):** For k=1,2,3, compare mixed (OR) and nested (AND) training. Observe that mixed often achieves higher in-domain scores early but degrades on general abilities later; nested shows steady gains on both.
  3. **Ablation: Necessity of Formal Learning:** Train I2 (Matrix+TicTacToe) vs. F+I2 under both mixed and nested. Demonstrate that removing Math consistently harms MMLU and average scores, proving formal learning is irreplaceable.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Does the GIFT framework's nested training approach remain effective when applied to more complex, real-world interactive environments beyond the controlled game settings studied?**  
   *Basis in paper:* The limitations section states the work considers a limited set of formal and informal environments, and game designs represent only a subset of possible interactive settings.  
   *Why unresolved:* Only three specific game types with simple rules and clear success conditions were tested. Real-world interactive environments have greater complexity, ambiguous objectives, and noisier rewards.
2. **How does the nested training framework perform when opponents can adapt and evolve, rather than being fixed as in the current experiments?**  
   *Basis in paper:* The limitations section mentions that experiments use fixed opponent models and extending to self-evolve or adaptive opponents is left for future work.  
   *Why unresolved:* Fixed opponents provide stationary signals, but real-world interactions often involve learning opponents. Self-play or adaptive opponents could introduce non-stationarity affecting stability.
3. **Is the improvement from nested training specific to GRPO-based reinforcement learning, or does it generalize to other RL algorithms for LLM training?**  
   *Basis in paper:* Experiments exclusively use GRPO (via RAGEN with StarPO*). The paper contrasts nested vs. mixed within this algorithm but does not test alternatives.  
   *Why unresolved:* Different RL algorithms handle credit assignment, exploration, and multi-task learning differently. The AND objective might interact uniquely with GRPO's group-relative advantage estimation.
4. **What is the optimal composition strategy for nested training regarding the number, difficulty, and diversity of tasks to maximize generalizability?**  
   *Basis in paper:* The paper tests progressive combinations (F+I1, F+I2, F+I3) but doesn't systematically vary task selection or investigate theoretical limits.  
   *Why unresolved:* Adding more tasks increases trajectory length and may introduce diminishing returns or interference if tasks are too diverse or difficult. Task difficulty balance is unexplored.
5. **What is the theoretical mechanism by which enforcing an AND objective through nested training improves gradient stability and maintains higher entropy, leading to better generalization?**  
   *Basis in paper:* The paper empirically shows nested training leads to more stable gradients and higher entropy, attributing this to the AND objective preventing any single task from dominating, but this is descriptive rather than explanatory.  
   *Why unresolved:* Understanding why AND objectives promote balanced learning could inform future multi-task framework design. It's unclear whether benefits come from forced joint credit assignment, increased trajectory diversity, smoother reward surfaces, or a combination.

## Limitations
- Exact implementation of StarPO* (GRPO variant with clipping, trajectory filtering) is underspecified, risking reproduction errors.  
- Prompt templates and sampling distribution for game environments are not fully enumerated; minor variations may affect results.  
- Evaluation opponents (Qwen3-14B, Gemini-2.5-Flash) are fixed; performance may not generalize to other opponent policies.  
- Warm-up schedule for the 1.5B model lacks details; optimal warm-up hyperparameters remain unknown.

## Confidence
- **Games as informal learning:** Medium. Correlative evidence links specific games to targeted abilities, but causal mechanism (interactive feedback vs. other factors) is not isolated.
- **Nested training prevents interference:** High. Consistent empirical superiority over mixed training across multiple model sizes and task sets, supported by gradient/entropy trends.
- **Formal+informal synergy is necessary:** High. Ablations show formal learning irreplaceable for structured benchmarks; informal alone insufficient for social/creative tasks.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-architecture replication:** Implement nested F+I3 training on a third model family (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B) using identical hyperparameters and tasks. Success would confirm generalizability beyond Qwen.
2. **Opponent robustness test:** Replace fixed opponents with a random policy, a weaker model (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B), and self-play variants. Compare final benchmark scores to isolate opponent-dependent effects.
3. **Gradient and entropy analysis:** Log per-task gradient norms and policy entropy during training for nested vs. mixed runs. Verify that nested maintains higher cross-task gradient alignment and slower entropy decay, directly testing the proposed mechanism.
