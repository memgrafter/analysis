# Airborne Magnetic Anomaly Navigation with Neural-Network-Augmented Online Calibration

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.08265  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08265  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Achieves navigation accuracy comparable to state-of-the-art pre-trained models without any prior calibration, reducing DRMS by 10–90% across sensors via a cold-start EKF that jointly estimates Tolles-Lawson coefficients and a small neural network.

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the logistical barrier of offline calibration in airborne magnetic anomaly navigation (MagNav) by introducing a fully adaptive extended Kalman filter (EKF) with "cold-start" capability. The filter simultaneously estimates navigation states, physics-based Tolles-Lawson calibration coefficients, and a small neural network’s parameters in an augmented state. The neural network acts as a residual learner, modeling only nonlinearities beyond the Tolles-Lawson baseline. Validated on the MagNav Challenge dataset, the system bounds inertial drift using only magnetometer data, achieving up to 60% reduction in DRMS for high-interference sensors compared to a Tolles-Lawson-only filter, with convergence within ~50–100 km.

## Method Summary
The method is an error-state EKF with an augmented state vector that includes: (1) inertial navigation system (INS) position, velocity, and attitude errors; (2) accelerometer and gyroscope biases; (3) stochastic time-varying (STV) states for sensor noise modeling; (4) a constant bias (SCB) state; (5) 18 Tolles-Lawson coefficients; and (6) all weights/biases of a single-hidden-layer neural network (tanh activation, 2–5 neurons). The measurement model fuses a pre-computed magnetic anomaly map, the IGRF core field, the Tolles-Lawson model, the neural network residual, and the SCB. The neural network receives magnetometer-only features (raw vector/scalar readings) and its output is scaled by α=400 nT to constrain it to a residual role. The EKF’s parameter update is mathematically equivalent to online Natural Gradient descent, with the state covariance matrix P serving as an adaptive preconditioner. Process noise is set extremely low for neural network parameters (Q_NN=10⁻²⁰I) to enable slow, steady adaptation, and measurement noise R is fixed at 10 nT². The filter requires no prior calibration data and can be initialized with uninformative priors.

## Key Results
- For Magnetometer 2 (highest interference), DRMS improved from 107 m (Tolles-Lawson only) to 42 m (proposed cold start, Nh=5).
- Similar DRMS reductions (10–90%) were achieved across all tested sensors.
- The neural network output remained bounded (~±200 nT), confirming its residual role, while the Tolles-Lawson model captured the bulk of interference (~±6000 nT).
- Cold-start convergence typically occurred within 50–100 km of flight.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Simultaneous State Augmentation
**Claim:** The EKF can jointly estimate navigation states, Tolles-Lawson coefficients, and neural network parameters in a single augmented state, enabling true online calibration without prior data.  
**Mechanism:** The state vector is expanded to include all parameters. During correction, the magnetic measurement residual updates all states simultaneously; the neural network’s Jacobian is computed via backpropagation and inserted into the observation matrix.  
**Core assumption:** Aircraft magnetic interference evolves slowly (random walk), and EKF linearization remains valid near the operating point.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract and Section II explicitly describe the augmented state.  
- Neighboring works (e.g., "Physics-Informed Calibration") use similar augmented-state frameworks.  
**Break condition:** Abrupt interference changes (e.g., powering large electrical systems) may violate the random-walk model, causing large innovations and potential filter destabilization.

### Mechanism 2: Natural Gradient via EKF Covariance
**Claim:** The EKF parameter update is equivalent to online Natural Gradient descent, providing second-order optimization that accelerates learning and improves robustness over first-order backpropagation.  
**Mechanism:** For a static parameter model, the EKF update Δx = Kν can be rewritten as Δx = P∇L, where P approximates the inverse Fisher Information Matrix (FIM), thus following the natural gradient direction. The Q/R ratio tunably controls the steady-state learning rate.  
**Core assumption:** Observation noise is Gaussian and the FIM adequately captures local loss curvature.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract and Section II state the equivalence.  
- Appendix B provides a step-by-step derivation.  
**Break condition:** In high-noise or non-Gaussian regimes, the FIM approximation degrades; ill-conditioned P can amplify noise.

### Mechanism 3: Residual Learning with Physics-Based Baseline
**Claim:** Constraining the neural network to model only the residual after Tolles-Lawson compensation preserves explainability, prevents fitting large constant biases, and bounds the adaptive element to physically plausible corrections (~400 nT), mitigating instability.  
**Mechanism:** Total platform field: B_pf = f_TL(m, m, m, β_TL) + g_res(φ, Λ_NN). Tolles-Lawson captures permanent, induced, and eddy fields (thousands of nT). The network’s tanh output is scaled by α=400 nT, limiting its range. A separate SCB state handles constant biases.  
**Core assumption:** Tolles-Lawson model structure sufficiently captures bulk dynamic interference, leaving a bounded residual learnable by a small network.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract and Section I describe the residual role.  
- Section V notes the network tracks interference not modeled by TL.  
- Fig. 11 empirically shows TL output ~±6000 nT, NN output ~±200 nT.  
**Break condition:** If TL is severely misspecified (e.g., unusual airframe), residual may exceed NN capacity or saturation limits, degrading compensation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Extended Kalman Filter (EKF):** The entire system is an EKF; understanding state augmentation, prediction-correction, and Jacobian linearization is essential for implementation and tuning.  
  *Quick check:* How does the EKF update change when the state includes parameters (like neural net weights) that are not dynamic but need to be adapted?
- **Natural Gradient descent:** Explains why EKF-based NN training converges faster and more reliably than standard backpropagation, especially with small datasets. The Q/R ratio becomes a principled learning-rate tuner.  
  *Quick check:* Why is the EKF’s covariance matrix P considered an approximation of the inverse Fisher Information Matrix during parameter learning?
- **Tolles-Lawson calibration model:** This physics-based model is the backbone of interference compensation; its parameters and Jacobian must be correctly integrated into the EKF.  
  *Quick check:* What are the three interference components in the Tolles-Lawson model, and how are they represented in the A_TL matrix?
- **Residual learning architecture:** The design choice to bound the NN’s role is critical for safety and interpretability; violating it (e.g., by removing output scaling) risks filter divergence.  
  *Quick check:* Why is the neural network output scaled by 400 nT, and what would happen if it were scaled by 4000 nT instead?
- **Observability in joint navigation-calibration systems:** Simultaneous estimation requires measurements provide information about both navigation states and calibration parameters. The CRLB analysis shows over-parameterization can hurt navigational observability.  
  *Quick check:* Increasing the number of hidden neurons N_h without additional features tends to increase position error—why?
- **Time-derivative computation for magnetometers:** The Tolles-Lawson model uses ∂m/∂t as inputs to capture eddy currents. These are computed as finite differences from vector magnetometer samples.  
  *Quick check:* How are ∂B_x/∂t, ∂B_y/∂t, ∂B_z/∂t computed from sequential measurements, and why are they necessary?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
INS mechanization -> Magnetic map -> Core field model -> TL module -> NN module -> EKF core -> Measurement model -> Jacobian assembler

**Critical path:**  
1. Predict: x̂⁻ = F x̂⁺, P⁻ = F P⁺ Fᵀ + Q.  
2. Compute predicted field: h(x̂⁻).  
3. Innovation: ν = z - h(x̂⁻).  
4. Gain: K = P⁻ Hᵀ (H P⁻ Hᵀ + R)⁻¹.  
5. Update: x̂⁺ = x̂⁻ + K ν, P⁺ = (I - K H) P⁻.  
6. Compose: p = p_ins + δp, m = m (magnetometer state overwritten by measurement via pseudo-control).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **NN size N_h:** 2–5 neurons optimal; larger networks improve calibration RMSE but can degrade position error due to observability dilution.  
- **Feature set φ:** Magnetometer-only (φ_m) works best; adding velocity (φ_mv) gives small gains; using all inputs (φ_all) often hurts due to redundancy.  
- **Q_NN:** Should be tiny (10⁻²⁰) for slow, steady adaptation; too high causes forgetting.  
- **P₀_NN:** Identity with large scale enables fast cold-start; decrease for warm-start.  
- **Output scaling α:** 400 nT matches expected residual magnitude; larger α risks overfitting, smaller α underfits.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Diverging DRMS after 50 km: Check map coverage, TL model validity, or NN saturation.  
- Persistent high innovations (>100 nT): Likely model mismatch; inspect TL output and NN residual; consider outlier rejection.  
- Covariance collapse without error reduction: Overconfident due to incorrect linearization or map artifacts.  
- NN parameters drifting to extremes: Q_NN too high or features lack information; reduce Q or reconsider feature set.  
- SCB growing monotonically: Normal (absorbs constant bias); but if >5000 nT, map quality may be poor.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **TL-only baseline:** Run with Λ_NN disabled. Verify β_TL converges within ~50 km and position DRMS bounds (e.g., ~100 m for Mag 2). Confirm TL output captures most interference.  
2. **Residual scaling check:** With TL+NN (N_h=3, φ_m), plot NN output time series. It should stay within ~±200 nT and correlate with maneuvers not captured by TL.  
3. **Cold vs warm start:** Initialize warm with final P and x from a previous cold run. Measure convergence distance to reach DRMS <50 m. Expect warm start to lock within first 10 km vs 50–100 km cold start.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Architectural modifications:** Can decoupled or federated filters (e.g., Partial-Update Schmidt-Kalman Filter) prevent initial learning transients from destabilizing primary navigation states while maintaining real-time adaptability?
2. **Moving Horizon Estimation (MHE):** Can MHE effectively mitigate errors due to map inaccuracies, and how does it compare to the current EKF approach?
3. **Calibration maturity metric:** How can a metric be designed to indicate when the online-learned model has converged sufficiently for high-integrity operations and to reliably seed warm starts?
4. **Theoretical convergence guarantees:** What are the convergence guarantees for the EKF with neural network augmentation, and under what conditions does it converge to a stable solution?
5. **Non-saturating activations:** Do non-saturating activation functions (e.g., Mish, SiLU, GELU) reduce innovation spikes and improve learning during cold starts compared to tanh?

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Exact discrete-time process noise covariance values (Q_d) for TL/IMU/stochastic states and precise composition of the "magnetometer-only" feature vector are unspecified, blocking exact replication of reported DRMS.
- **Generalization risk:** The method assumes the Tolles-Lawson model captures the bulk of dynamic interference. Unusual airframe modifications or residual exceeding the network’s bounded capacity (~400 nT) may cause failure.
- **Observability-accuracy trade-off:** Increasing neural network size (Nh) beyond 2–5 neurons degrades position error due to parameter "cannibalization" of the anomaly signal, creating a sensitive tuning dependency.
