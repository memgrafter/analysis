# Reverse-engineering NLI: A study of the meta-inferential properties of Natural Language Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.05170  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05170  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Models trained on SNLI align strongly with an existential import reading, achieving up to 99.6% agreement on key meta-inferences where EI diverges from the strict conditional.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the logical notion of inference captured by Natural Language Inference (NLI) datasets, focusing on SNLI. The authors formalize three readings of NLI labels—material conditional, strict conditional, and existential import—within a modal framework. To diagnose which reading models learn, they construct an “inferred” test set by combining existing SNLI items with shared premises and new hypotheses generated by large language models. This test set probes meta‑inferential consistency: whether models’ predictions obey logical constraints derived from each reading. Experiments with BERT and RoBERTa+SE show that models align overwhelmingly with the existential import reading, with up to 99.6% agreement on EI‑specific meta‑inferences. Overall meta‑inference consistency exceeds 96% on most patterns, suggesting that SNLI implicitly encodes a modal entailment relation with existential import. Residual artifacts are indicated by hypothesis‑only baselines and lower scores on some meta‑inferences.

## Method Summary
The authors first define three formal readings (material conditional MC, strict conditional SC, existential import EI) in a modal logic framework, capturing different assumptions about the truth of premises. To probe which reading models acquire, they build an inferred test set: from SNLI training items they select groups sharing the same premise and then use LLMs (Llama3.2/3.3, DeepSeek‑R1) to generate additional hypotheses. For any two NLI judgments (e.g., Eab, Ebc), they derive expected labels for the inferred pair (e.g., Eac) under each reading based on meta‑inferential validities in a formal logic table. Models trained on SNLI (± LLM‑generated data) are evaluated on this inferred set; their predictions are compared to the expectations of each reading. The primary models are BERT and RoBERTa+SE, trained with standard fine‑tuning (Adam/AdamW, frozen layers for BERT, full fine‑tuning for RoBERTa+SE). The analysis reveals that models overwhelmingly conform to EI, suggesting SNLI’s annotation protocol implicitly assumes premises describe possible situations.

## Key Results
- Models (BERT, RoBERTa+SE) trained on SNLI show near‑perfect alignment with existential import, achieving up to 99.6% agreement on EI‑specific meta‑inferences (e.g., Cab→¬Eba).
- Overall meta‑inference consistency exceeds 96% on most patterns, indicating a strong modal entailment with existential import is learned.
- Hypothesis‑only baselines and lower agreement on certain meta‑inferences point to residual dataset artifacts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** SNLI’s data generation protocol implicitly encodes a modal notion of entailment with existential import (EI).
- **Mechanism:** SNLI premises are Flickr image captions describing possible scenes. The existence of the photo implies that the premise describes a logically possible situation (♢a). Annotators were instructed to write hypotheses that are “definitely true” (entailment) or “definitely false” (contradiction) given that possible scene. This aligns with the modal definition Eab ≡ ♢a ∧ □(a→b), where □ quantifies over accessible possible worlds consistent with the premise’s possibility. Models trained on this data learn to approximate this reading because the training signal (human labels) consistently reflects it.
- **Core assumption:** The Flickr caption → possible world link is preserved in annotator judgments.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “SNLI implicitly encodes a modal notion of entailment with existential import.”
  - [section 2] “Every SNLI premise comes along with the implicit assumption that the premise is possible… the existence of the photo as implying that there is a model satisfying the caption.”
  - [corpus] Neighbors discuss modalities in NLI (e.g., Gubelmann et al. 2024), but focus differs; this paper’s EI claim is novel to its author set.
- **Break condition:** If substantial portions of SNLI premises were found to describe impossible or contradictory states (counter to the photo assumption), the EI reading would be undermined.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Meta‑inferential consistency tests using “inferred” NLI items can diagnose which logical reading (MC, SC, EI) a model has internalized.
- **Mechanism:** By combining existing SNLI items with shared premises or LLM‑generated hypotheses, the authors create novel premise‑hypothesis pairs (e.g., from Eab and Ebc, infer ac). The expected label for the new pair under a given reading is derived from formal meta‑inferential validities (Table 2). A model’s prediction on the new item is compared against these derived expectations. High agreement with EI‑specific predictions (e.g., Cab→¬Eba) signals EI encoding.
- **Core assumption:** Model predictions on inferred items reflect the same internal logic learned from training data, and that logic is compositional over the base relations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5] “Using the SNLI and generated test sets as input, we create an inferred test set… certain values of l1 and l2 allow us to infer a restricted set of possible labels.”
  - [section 6.2] Table 4 shows 99.6% agreement on EI‑specific pattern Cab→¬Eba.
  - [corpus] Neighbor paper (Srikanth & Rudinger 2025) uses atomic decomposition for consistency, a different but complementary probing method.
- **Break condition:** If models showed systematic disagreement with *all* formal readings across key patterns, the approach would fail to diagnose a coherent underlying logic.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** LLM‑generated NLI data (from models like DeepSeek‑R1) adequately replicates the distribution of human‑generated SNLI data, enabling safe augmentation.
- **Mechanism:** The LLM is prompted with SNLI‑style instructions and few‑shot examples. The generated hypotheses are evaluated by training NLI models on mixed datasets (SNLI + LLM‑gen). If the augmented model performs similarly on the original SNLI test set and shows no drastic shift in meta‑inferential patterns, the generated data is considered distributionally aligned.
- **Core assumption:** LLM‑generated hypotheses follow the same annotator guidelines and modal assumptions as SNLI.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 6.1] Table 3 shows comparable or improved performance on SNLI test set when training with LLM‑generated data (e.g., RoBERTa+SE with DS‑R1: 90.7% vs. SNLI‑only baseline not shown but implied).
  - [section 6.1] “These results suggest that the generated NLI data… adequately replicates the distribution of the human‑generated SNLI dataset.”
  - [corpus] Neighbors (e.g., Banerjee et al. 2024) also use generation for NLI, but this paper provides specific validation via consistency metrics.
- **Break condition:** If generated data introduced systematic biases (e.g., always including certain lexical cues), model performance on meta‑inferences would shift unpredictably, or hypothesis‑only baselines would show large gaps.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Modal readings of NLI relations (MC, SC, EI)**  
  *Why needed here:* The entire analysis hinges on distinguishing these formal definitions to interpret model behavior. Without this, meta‑inferential expectations are ambiguous.  
  *Quick check question:* What is the key difference between SC (□(a→b)) and EI (♢a ∧ □(a→b)) in how they treat premises that describe impossible situations?

- **Concept: Meta‑inferential properties**  
  *Why needed here:* These are the logical constraints (e.g., symmetry of contradiction, transitivity of entailment) used to probe which relation reading the model learned. They are the diagnostic tool.  
  *Quick check question:* Why is the pattern Eab ∧ Cbc → ¬Eca valid under EI but not under SC? (See Table 2, last row of first block.)

- **Concept: Hypothesis‑only baselines**  
  *Why needed here:* They detect dataset artifacts where the hypothesis alone correlates with the label (e.g., certain words bias toward “entailment”). The paper uses the performance gap between full and hypothesis‑only models to assess data quality.  
  *Quick check question:* If a hypothesis‑only model achieves near‑chance performance, what does that suggest about the dataset?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** SNLI train/test + LLM‑generated datasets (Llama3.2/3.3, DS‑R1). Inferred test set generator (combines items based on shared sentences). Models: BERT (baseline), RoBERTa+SE (SOTA). Training with Adam/AdamW, frozen layers (BERT) or full fine‑tuning (RoBERTa+SE). Evaluation: Standard NLI test (SNLI/LLM‑gen) + meta‑inference probe on inferred items. Metric: label agreement per meta‑inference pattern.
- **Critical path:** Data generation (LLM prompts) → Augmented training → Inferred item creation → Model prediction on inferred items → Agreement analysis vs. EI/SC predictions.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Using LLM‑gen data risks propagating model biases, but retention of SNLI size (SNLI±gen) mitigates this.
  - Inferred items rely on compositional assumptions; non‑compositional model behavior would break the analysis.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low agreement (<90%) on EI‑specific patterns (e.g., Cab→¬Eba).
  - Large performance drop on LLM‑gen test sets vs. SNLI (Table 3) indicating distribution shift.
  - Hypothesis‑only model performance approaching full model, suggesting superficial heuristics.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Data quality check:** Train on SNLI+LLM‑gen, evaluate on standard SNLI/LLM‑gen test sets (Table 3). Goal: confirm minimal distribution shift.
  2. **Meta‑inference baseline:** Test a model trained on vanilla SNLI on the inferred set. Expect: moderate agreement, likely mixed EI/SC patterns.
  3. **Augmented consistency test:** Train on SNLI±DS‑R1, evaluate on inferred set. Expect: high EI agreement (>95%) on key patterns, as found.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can we uniquely identify MC SC EI contributions in SNLI via targeted meta‑inferences?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Discussion: cannot disentangle contributions due to overlapping invalidity (Sec 7).
- **Why unresolved:** Many meta‑inferences have identical predictions across readings, making it hard to tell which reading the model follows.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Create meta‑inferences with diverging predictions per reading; test models; or use adversarial probing.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Which modal reading (MC SC EI) fits other NLI datasets (e.g., FraCaS, RTE, MNLI)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work calls for disentanglement across datasets; RTE‑5 yields new modal notion (Sec 8).
- **Why unresolved:** The inference logic of these datasets is unknown; they may capture different aspects of reasoning.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply meta‑inferential tests to each dataset; compare reading agreements.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How do closed‑world assumption and anaphora bias contradiction labeling and meta‑inferential consistency?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work: examine closed‑world (Sec 8) and discourse effects like anaphora (Sec 8).
- **Why unresolved:** Both may cause artifacts distorting labels and consistency.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Annotate SNLI for these factors; modify premises (open‑world, resolved anaphora); measure label shifts and consistency.

## Limitations
- The claim that all SNLI premises describe metaphysically possible scenes (♢a) relies on the Flickr photo assumption. Systematic invalid premises would undermine the EI reading.
- Meta‑inferential consistency assumes models compose the base NLI relations logically. Non‑compositional heuristics could coincidentally match EI patterns.
- LLM‑generated data quality is validated only by standard test accuracy; subtle distributional shifts (e.g., lexical biases) may persist despite comparable scores.

## Confidence
- Core empirical finding (EI agreement up to 99.6%): **High**
- Mechanism linking SNLI’s photo protocol to a modal EI reading: **Medium**
- Assertion that LLM‑generated hypotheses safely replicate SNLI’s distribution: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Premise possibility audit:** Recruit annotators to label a random SNLI sample for whether the premise describes a logically possible situation. Correlate labels with model meta‑inference patterns.
2. **Non‑compositional model probe:** Train a model with a broken compositional inductive bias (e.g., bag‑of‑words) on SNLI and evaluate on the inferred meta‑inference set. Disentangle heuristic vs. logical alignment.
3. **LLM‑generation bias analysis:** Perform fine‑grained lexical and syntactic analysis of LLM‑generated vs. human‑written hypotheses, then measure meta‑inference consistency after filtering for identified bias markers.
