# Learning Heat-based Equations in Self-similar variables

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.00872  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00872  
- **Reference count:** 22  
- **Primary result:** Training neural PDE solvers in self-similar variables (SSV) significantly improves long-time extrapolation stability for heat-based equations, reducing relative error and capturing correct qualitative dynamics beyond the training window.

## Executive Summary
Neural PDE solvers often fail to extrapolate accurately to long times because they learn time-dependent spreading patterns in physical coordinates. This paper introduces training in self-similar variables (SSV) that exploit inherent scaling symmetries of heat-type equations. The transformation ξ = x/√(t+1), τ = log(t+1) rescales space and time so that solutions localize in a fixed ξ-window and approach a stationary profile asymptotically. On 2D Navier-Stokes and 1D Burgers equations, SSV-trained models (MLP, FCN) achieve much lower relative mean squared error during extrapolation (e.g., t up to 5 vs. training up to 0.3–0.5) and correctly reproduce qualitative features like vorticity merging or shock stability, while physical-coordinate models degrade. The consistent gains across systems and architectures highlight SSV as a mathematically grounded inductive bias for learning parabolic dynamics.

## Method Summary
The core method transforms physical coordinates (x,t) into SSV (ξ,τ) via ξ = x/√(t+1), τ = log(t+1). A paired sampling protocol enables fair comparison: for each (ξ,τ) uniformly sampled from a fixed spatial disk |ξ| ≤ C and τ-interval covering the training time range, the corresponding physical sample is (x = √(t+1)ξ, t = e^τ−1). Models are trained with a windowed mean squared error loss: for SSV, the loss integrates over the fixed ξ-window D_C; for physical coordinates, the spatial window expands as D_{t,C} = {x: |x| ≤ C√(t+1)}. Extrapolation performance is evaluated by predicting on the expanding physical window and computing relative MSE (normalized by solution energy). SSV predictions are mapped back to physical space using scaling factors from the SSV ansatz (e.g., ω = (t+1)^{-1} Ω for NS vorticity). Training uses identical architectures (MLP or FCN) and optimization, differing only in input coordinates.

## Key Results
- SSV models achieve significantly lower relative MSE during long-time extrapolation (beyond t=0.3 for NS, t=0.5 for Burgers) compared to physical-coordinate baselines.
- Qualitatively, SSV models reliably capture asymptotic features (e.g., vorticity merger, shock profile stability) while physical-coordinate models show increasing errors, oscillations, or incorrect steady states.
- Improvements are consistent across both MLP and FCN architectures and for both Navier-Stokes and Burgers equations, demonstrating the broad applicability of SSV as an inductive bias.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** SSV training localizes the solution within a fixed spatial window independent of time, enabling efficient learning of long-time dynamics.
- **Mechanism:** The transformation ξ = x/√(t+1), τ = log(t+1) rescales physical space so that the effective support of the solution in ξ remains bounded for all t. For heat-based equations, solutions converge to a time-independent self-similar profile in SSV (e.g., Gaussian for heat, Oseen vortex for NS). The tails decay exponentially in |ξ|, so a fixed window D_C captures the essential dynamics.
- **Core assumption:** The solution converges to a localized self-similar profile at large t (Proposition 1 for NS, Proposition 2 for Burgers).
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "SSV coordinates ... localizing solutions and stabilizing dynamics."  
  [section 3.1] Derivation of SSV for NS; Ω(ξ,τ)→αG(ξ) as τ→∞.  
  [corpus] Neighbors show limited direct evidence; related work on coordinate transformations for PDEs exists but SSV-specific gains are not corroborated here.
- **Break condition:** If the solution does not converge to a localized self-similar profile (e.g., non-heat-based PDEs, irregular initial data violating L^1 assumptions), SSV localization fails and the fixed window may omit essential dynamics.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** SSV stabilizes the loss landscape by reducing temporal variation in the target function, making the learning problem easier.
- **Mechanism:** In physical coordinates, the solution ω(x,t) spreads diffusively (support ~√t), so the network must learn a time-dependent amplitude and scale. In SSV, Ω(ξ,τ) approaches a steady profile for large τ, so the training target becomes less time-varying within the window D_C. The time-averaged loss L_ssv(C) thus sees a quasi-stationary target during later training epochs.
- **Core assumption:** The training window t∈[0, t_max] is chosen before the solution fully enters the asymptotic regime (e.g., t_max=0.3 for NS merging), so Ω(ξ,τ) still evolves temporally in SSV but with reduced amplitude modulation compared to physical coordinates.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 3.4] Loss definition L_ssv(C) = E_τ[E_ssv(C;τ)] uses a fixed ξ-window; contrast with physical loss L_phy(C) = E_t[E_phy(C;t)] where the window D_{t,C} expands.  
  [section 3.5] "Training window t∈[0,0.3], terminating before merging completes" ensures target dynamics are present but scaled.  
  [corpus] Weak direct evidence; adjacent work on time-aligned representations for PDEs suggests stabilization benefits, but SSV-specific claims remain paper-local.
- **Break condition:** If the self-similar profile itself is time-dependent (e.g., for porous medium equation with m≠1), SSV dynamics may not stabilize; or if t_max is too large, training would fit a near-steady state without learning the transient.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** SSV encodes an inductive bias aligned with the PDE’s scaling symmetry, improving generalization to long times.
- **Mechanism:** The heat-based PDEs (NS, Burgers) are invariant under the scaling ω_λ(x,t) = λ^2 ω(λx, λ^2 t). SSV coordinates (ξ,τ) are precisely the variables that make this symmetry a translation in τ and scaling in ξ. Networks trained in SSV thus see a representation where the governing equation’s symmetry is geometrically simple, which may act as a built-in prior that reduces the complexity needed to approximate the solution operator.
- **Core assumption:** The neural network architecture (MLP/FCN) can effectively leverage the coordinate-induced symmetry without explicit regularization; the symmetry is reflected in the training data distribution via the SSV sampling.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 1] "Exploit the intrinsic scaling structure ... work in variables that better reflect its long-time behavior."  
  [section 3.1] Explicit scaling symmetry used to derive SSV form.  
  [corpus] Neighbors include "Learning vertical coordinates via automatic differentiation" (paper 3087) showing coordinate design impacts learning, but no direct SSV symmetry evidence.
- **Break condition:** For systems without parabolic scaling symmetry (e.g., hyperbolic wave equations), SSV may not provide a symmetry-aligned representation and could hurt performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Data sampler -> SSV transformer (for SSV model) -> Network head (MLP or FCN) -> Loss (windowed MSE) -> Evaluator (for extrapolation, maps SSV predictions back to physical space)

### Critical path
- **Training:** Sample batch of (ξ,τ) from fixed D_C x τ-interval. For SSV model, use (ξ,τ) directly; for physical model, compute (x,t) = (√(t+1)ξ, e^τ−1). Forward pass yields predicted field. Compute windowed MSE over sampled points in respective domain. Backpropagate.
- **Extrapolation evaluation:** Generate reference solution on fine grid over expanding physical domain D_{t,C}. For SSV model, map grid points to (ξ,τ), predict in SSV, then scale back to physical using ansatz (e.g., ω = (t+1)^{-1} Ω). Compute relative MSE vs. reference.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Window size C:** Larger C captures more of the solution but increases memory/computation; smaller C relies on exponential decay in ξ (assumption 1). For SSV, C is fixed; for physical, window expands with t, requiring dynamic remeshing/interpolation.
- **Architecture parity:** Both MLP and FCN are simple fully connected networks; FCN factorizes space and time variables, which may help but the paper shows SSV gains are architecture-independent.
- **Training horizon:** Must be early enough that SSV target is not yet fully steady (to ensure learning transients), but long enough to see scaling effects.

### Failure signatures
- **SSV model:** If the solution is not localized in ξ (e.g., heavy tails or non-convergence), fixed window D_C omits critical dynamics → high error on D_{t,C} after mapping back.
- **Physical model:** As t increases, the expanding window D_{t,C} samples regions where the solution amplitude is very small; network may neglect far-field accuracy → poor capture of merged profiles or shock tails.
- **General:** If SSV transformation is incorrectly implemented (e.g., τ = log t vs log(t+1)), the stationary profile may not emerge, breaking localization.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline replication:** Train both MLP and FCN on NS with t∈[0,0.3] using the fair comparison protocol (paired samples, windowed MSE). Validate training loss decreases similarly for both coordinate systems.
2. **Extrapolation metric:** For t∈[0.3,5], compute relative MSE on D_{t,C}. Expect SSV models to show lower and more stable error curves (fig 1).
3. **Qualitative check:** Visualize vorticity (NS) or u (Burgers) at t=0.5,1,1.5 (figs 2-3, 5-6). SSV predictions should show correct merging/shock stability; physical predictions should show artifacts (e.g., spurious oscillations, incorrect merging).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** For which broader classes of heat-based PDEs (beyond Navier-Stokes and Burgers) does SSV training consistently improve long-time extrapolation?  
  **Basis:** Conclusion lists "extending the framework to broader classes of systems with known scaling laws" as a natural direction, citing porous medium equation.  
  **Why unresolved:** Only validated on two equations; scaling symmetries and asymptotic localization may differ.  
  **What evidence needed:** Systematic empirical evaluation across diverse PDEs (e.g., porous medium with varying exponents, reaction-diffusion with heat-like linear parts) under identical protocols.

- **Question:** How can adaptive sampling window strategies be designed for SSV training when asymptotic localization is weak or non-Gaussian?  
  **Basis:** Conclusion calls for "developing adaptive windowing strategies for settings where asymptotic localization is weaker," noting fixed windows rely on exponential decay in ξ.  
  **Why unresolved:** Fixed windows assume strong self-similar decay; weaker decay could leave dynamics outside the window.  
  **What evidence needed:** Analytical decay rates for broader PDEs; algorithms that adjust window size C during training based on metrics like gradient norms; validation on PDEs with slower-than-exponential tails.

- **Question:** Does enforcing PDE residuals in SSV coordinates (i.e., physics-informed training in SSV) complement data-driven SSV training to further improve robustness?  
  **Basis:** Conclusion suggests "combining SSV-based training with physics-based constraints," implying the current data-only approach may not fully exploit physical structure.  
  **Why unresolved:** Paper uses pure supervised learning in SSV; unknown if PINN-style losses in (ξ,τ) interact positively.  
  **What evidence needed:** Train models with combined data and PDE losses in SSV; compare rollout stability against pure SSV models, especially under limited data or noise.

- **Question:** What theoretical guarantees exist for the generalization and rollout stability of neural operators trained in self-similar versus physical coordinates?  
  **Basis:** Conclusion highlights "establishing theory that connects SSV-based sampling and coordinate choices to generalization and rollout stability," noting current results are empirical.  
  **Why unresolved:** No formal analysis linking coordinate transformation to approximation/generalization bounds; rollout stability observed but unexplained.  
  **What evidence needed:** Generalization bounds (e.g., Rademacher complexity) under SSV; theoretical relations between windowed SSV sampling and error propagation during rollout; empirical validation correlating theory with performance.

## Limitations
- Missing critical hyperparameters (network dimensions, optimizer settings, window size C, reference solver details) prevents exact replication.
- Validation restricted to two heat-type PDEs; transfer to other parabolic or hyperbolic systems is uncertain.
- Theoretical mechanisms rely on assumptions (e.g., convergence to self-similar profile) not fully validated; low citation count (0.28 average) indicates limited independent corroboration.

## Confidence
- **SSV improves long-time extrapolation** (primary result): Medium (strong internal evidence but reproduction blocked by missing details).
- **Localization in ξ** (Mechanism 1): Medium (sound mathematically for heat equations with suitable ICs; break conditions lack empirical stress tests).
- **Loss landscape stabilization** (Mechanism 2): Low (indirect reasoning only; no ablation of temporal variation in training target).
- **Symmetry-aligned inductive bias** (Mechanism 3): Medium (coordinate choice aligns with scaling symmetry; causal contribution unproven).
