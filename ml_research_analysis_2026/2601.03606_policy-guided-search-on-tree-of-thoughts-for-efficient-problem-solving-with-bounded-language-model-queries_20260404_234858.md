# Policy-Guided Search on Tree-of-Thoughts for Efficient Problem Solving with Bounded Language Model Queries

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03606
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03606
- **Reference count:** 8
- **Primary result:** By using LM probabilities as a search policy, LTS matches or exceeds DFS and beam search accuracy under equal query budgets, with up to 21.4% absolute improvement, while avoiding DFS’s initial-commitment problem and using fewer queries than beam search.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Levin Tree Search (LTS) as an inference-only adaptation to the Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT) framework for problem-solving under strict language model query budgets. LTS eliminates separate state-evaluation queries by using the LM’s own token probabilities as a policy to guide search, computing state priority as depth divided by cumulative path probability. Theoretical guarantees for LTS are extended to pruned ToT trees, bounding expansions. Experiments on Blocksworld, PrOntoQA, and Array Sorting with Llama 1B/3B/8B and Qwen 2 7B show LTS achieves higher or equal accuracy compared to DFS and beam search under identical budgets. A temperature analysis reveals that moderating the cost function’s temperature (τ≈0.5–1.5) balances early performance and final accuracy.

## Method Summary
The method adapts Levin Tree Search to the Tree-of-Thoughts paradigm. The LM serves a dual role: it generates candidate next thoughts (via sampling with a fixed generation temperature, e.g., τ=0.8) and implicitly defines a policy π(s) as the product of probabilities along a state’s path. Search maintains a frontier of states prioritized by cost = g(s)/π(s), where g(s) is depth. The algorithm expands the lowest-cost state, adds its unique children (up to a max branching factor b_max=3), and terminates upon finding the first terminal state or exhausting the query budget. Unlike DFS or beam search, LTS does not issue separate evaluation queries; the policy uses probabilities already computed during thought generation, making it query-efficient. Theoretical analysis bounds the number of expansions even when the tree is pruned via sampling, linking effort to the depth and probability of the first-discovered goal in the explored subtree.

## Key Results
- LTS matches or exceeds DFS and beam search accuracy under equal query budgets across Blocksworld, PrOntoQA, and Array Sorting, with up to 21.4% absolute improvement.
- LTS avoids DFS’s initial-commitment problem by exploring high-probability paths more broadly early on.
- LTS uses fewer queries than beam search to reach the same accuracy level.
- A cost-function temperature τ in the range 0.5–1.5 yields the best trade-off between early performance (low τ) and final accuracy (moderate τ).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Using the language model's token probabilities directly as a policy for state expansion eliminates the need for separate, costly state-evaluation queries.
- **Mechanism:** In standard guided DFS or beam search within ToT, each candidate state (sequence of thoughts) is scored by querying the LM (e.g., with a self-evaluation prompt). LTS instead uses the cumulative probability of the path to a state, π(s) = Π p(t_i|history), as an inverse heuristic in its cost function (cost = g(s)/π(s)). Lower cost states are prioritized, meaning states on more probable paths are expanded earlier, all without extra LM calls beyond those needed to generate the thoughts themselves.
- **Core assumption:** The LM's generative probability distribution over thoughts correlates with the likelihood of that thought leading to a valid solution. Higher probability implies a better chance of being on a productive path toward a goal.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "leverages LMs as policies to guide the search" and "eliminating separate state evaluation queries."
  - [section 2.2.1] Formal definition of LTS cost function using π(s).
  - [section 3] Algorithm 1, Line 6: `s ← arg min_{s∈F} g(s)/π(s)`.
  - [corpus] Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers focus on ToT accuracy/completeness, not query efficiency via policy-guided search.
- **Break condition:** The LM's probability distribution is poorly calibrated or misaligned with the solution space (e.g., favors common but unproductive thoughts). This would cause LTS to waste queries on high-probability dead ends.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** LTS provides a theoretical bound on the number of state expansions (and thus LM queries) that holds even when the search tree is pruned via sampling (as in ToT).
- **Mechanism:** Original LTS theory guarantees N ≤ min_{s∈H} g(s)/π(s) for full trees. The paper extends this to pruned subtrees (T) by showing the bound applies to the minimum over terminal states *within the sampled subtree* (H' ⊆ H). This links the search effort directly to the depth and probability of the *first-discovered* goal state in the explored subtree.
- **Core assumption:** The sampling process for generating child thoughts retains at least one optimal or near-optimal child at each expansion step with non-negligible probability. The bound is a worst-case guarantee over the *best-case* discovered goal.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "extend the theoretical results of LTS by showing that, for ToT (a pruned tree), LTS guarantees a bound on the number of states expanded."
  - [section 3] Proposition 1 and Corollary 1 formalize the bound for the pruned case.
- **Break condition:** Aggressive pruning (e.g., very low b_max or high temperature) removes all children on the optimal path at a critical node, forcing the search into a subspace with no solution, making the bound irrelevant as it assumes a solution exists in H'.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The temperature parameter τ used in the LM's final softmax layer critically influences the trade-off between early performance (low τ) and final accuracy under budget (moderate τ), by affecting the probability spread π(s) and thus the LTS cost function's sensitivity.
- **Mechanism:** Temperature scales logits before softmax. Lower τ (e.g., 0.01) makes the distribution more peaked, causing LTS to behave greedily (like DFS) on high-probability paths, leading to quick terminal states but risk of initial-commitment failure. Moderate τ (0.5-1.5) flattens distribution slightly, encouraging more exploration before commitment, improving solution discovery under larger budgets. Higher τ (2.0) makes distribution nearly uniform, mimicking BFS and requiring many more queries for the same accuracy.
- **Core assumption:** The LM is "sufficiently accurate" (Definition 1), meaning tokens on the true solution path have a meaningful expected log-probability advantage (Δ⁺) over incorrect tokens. This allows the theoretical gradient analysis (Theorem 3) to hold.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1] Theorem 3 derives ∂M/∂τ ≤ ... based on Definition 1.
  - [section 4.8] Empirical results showing τ=0.5-1.5 best for trade-off on Blocksworld.
  - [corpus] Not directly addressed in neighbor abstracts; they generally fix τ or tune for accuracy, not query-budget trade-offs.
- **Break condition:** The LM is not "sufficiently accurate" (Δ⁺ ≈ 0). In this case, temperature has minimal effect on π(s) for correct vs. incorrect paths, so LTS's guidance degrades to random or uniformly-biased search, and the temperature tuning rationale collapses.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  1. **Language Model (LM) `p`**: Provides two functions: (a) Generates candidate next thoughts `t_i ~ p(t_i|history)` (Line 10, Alg 1). (b) Implicitly defines the policy π(s) via cumulative probability of the path to state s.
  2. **ToT Tree Generator**: Constructs the implicit search tree T by repeatedly calling the LM's thought generation (with sampling, b_max). Duplicate thoughts are pruned.
  3. **LTS Controller (Alg 1)**: Maintains frontier `F`, computes `cost(s) = g(s)/π(s)`, selects state with min cost for expansion, adds children to frontier, terminates on first terminal state.
  4. **Terminal State Evaluator**: Domain-specific function (e.g., PDDL planner for Blocksworld) that determines if a state's final thought constitutes a valid solution. *Not* called during search for LTS (only at termination).
  5. **Budget Monitor**: Tracks number of thoughts generated (each LM call for a thought) and aborts if budget `b` is exceeded.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialize frontier with root state s0 (`g=0`, `π=1`).
  2. While budget not exceeded and frontier not empty:
     a. Pop state `s` with minimal `g(s)/π(s)` from frontier.
     b. If `s` is terminal (per LM's own termination token), **stop and return** solution path.
     c. Else: Generate `b_max` next thoughts via `p(·|s).sample()`.
     d. For each unique thought, create child state `s_child`, compute its `g = g(s)+1` and `π = π(s) * p(thought)`.
     e. Push `s_child` to frontier.
  3. If budget hit, return failure (no solution found in explored subtree).

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **b_max (branching factor)**: Larger b_max increases per-expansion coverage but multiplies frontier size and total queries. Must balance with budget.
  - **Thought Generation τ**: Fixed for all expansions (typically 0.8 in experiments). Controls diversity of children per state. Higher τ increases chance of including an optimal child but also more noise.
  - **Cost Function τ**: Separate from thought gen τ. Controls sensitivity of `g(s)/π(s)` to probability differences. Tuned for budget-accuracy trade-off (0.5-1.5 optimal in experiments).
  - **Termination condition**: Paper uses "first terminal state found" (Line 8). This is query-efficient but may miss better solutions deeper in tree. A full budget run could explore more but costs more.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **LTS stagnates early**: Monitor if frontier cost (`g/π`) stops decreasing significantly over many expansions. Indicates LM policy π(s) is misleading (high π on dead-end paths).
  - **Expansion rate too slow**: Frontier size grows linearly with b_max but LTS should find shallow solutions fast. If many expansions occur at small g(s), budget is being spent on deep, low-probability paths—likely τ too high or LM weak.
  - **Zero accuracy despite DFS having >0**: Suggests LTS's cost function caused it to avoid a successful path that DFS's greedy evaluation found. Check if π(s) for the successful path is anomalously low at some early node.
  - **High duplicate thought rate**: Indicates LM generation is low-entropy even at thought-gen τ=0.8. Reduce τ or adjust prompt to increase diversity.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate Table 1, Llama 3.2 3B, BW step 4**: Verify LTS > DFS accuracy under identical query budget (set to ensure DFS can reach terminal). Confirms core claim on a mid-difficulty domain.
  2. **Temperature sweep on BW step 4 (Section 4.8)**: Run LTS with cost τ ∈ {0.01, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0} under fixed time budget. Expect τ=0.5-1.5 to outperform extremes. Validates the exploration-exploitation trade-off mechanism.
  3. **Scaling b_max on BW step 4**: Test LTS with b_max = {1, 2, 3, 5} under fixed query budget. Expect b_max=3 (paper's setting) to be near-optimal; b_max=1 degrades to DFS-like, b_max=5 may cause frontier bloat without proportional gain. Tests robustness to branching factor.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the theoretical expansion bound for LTS in ToT be extended to scenarios with multiple terminal states, given that real-world problems often have several goal states rather than a unique solution?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Proposition 1 assumes a unique minimum-cost terminal state, but the authors note that experiments often involved multiple goal nodes, deviating from this assumption. They state the theoretical results should be viewed as general guidance, not strict bounds.
- Why unresolved: The current bound relies on a single optimal terminal state; with multiple goals, the "minimum" cost may not accurately capture search behavior, leading to looser guarantees that don't reflect practical performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Formal analysis deriving a bound that accounts for multiple terminal states, validated through experiments on synthetic trees with controlled numbers of goals to compare predicted vs. actual expansions under varying LM qualities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: For domains where the base LM generates a high rate of syntactically invalid thoughts (e.g., >40% in Game of 24), can LTS's policy-guided search remain effective, or does it require modifications to handle invalid actions without exceeding query budgets?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper excluded Game of 24 and Crosswords due to high invalid action generation, stating such domains prevent meaningful algorithmic comparison because the focus is on search algorithms, not action validity mechanisms.
- Why unresolved: LTS assumes sampled thoughts form a valid subtree; invalid thoughts break this, causing wasted expansions or incorrect state representations. The paper doesn't address incorporating validity checks without additional LM queries, which would violate budget constraints.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on domains with high invalid rates, measuring LTS accuracy and query usage with and without a lightweight validity filter (e.g., a fast LM-based check), assessing trade-offs in success rate within the same budget.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the temperature parameter τ for the LTS cost function be adaptively tuned per instance or domain to better balance exploration and exploitation under varying budget constraints, rather than using a fixed value?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 4.8 shows intermediate τ (0.5–1.5) yields higher final accuracy but poorer early performance, while low τ acts like DFS. Authors note optimal τ may vary by domain and involves a trade-off between early and final performance.
- Why unresolved: Fixed τ across all instances may not optimize for both early and late performance; adaptive strategies could dynamically adjust τ based on search progress or instance difficulty, but this remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement an adaptive τ scheme (e.g., based on expansion history, early terminal state detection, or difficulty estimates) and compare against fixed τ across domains under identical budgets, measuring accuracy convergence and robustness to budget variations.

## Limitations
- Exact prompt templates and self-evaluation prompts for DFS/beam baselines are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Theoretical guarantees depend on strong assumptions: the LM is “sufficiently accurate” (Δ⁺ > 0) and pruning retains at least one near-optimal child at each step.
- Experiments are restricted to three planning/sorting domains and small models (1B–8B); generalization to larger LMs or diverse tasks is unknown.
