# CounterVid: Counterfactual Video Generation for Mitigating Action and Temporal Hallucinations in Video-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04778
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04778
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: MixDPO fine-tuning on Qwen2.5-VL reduces action/temporal hallucinations, improving held-out CounterVid accuracy by 8.4 pp and VidHalluc TSH by 7.8 pp.

## Executive Summary
Video-language models often hallucinate actions and temporal sequences due to over-reliance on language priors. This paper introduces CounterVid, a dataset of ~26k counterfactual video preference pairs generated via a modular pipeline that preserves scene context while altering actions or temporal order. The training method MixDPO jointly optimizes textual preferences (standard DPO) and visual preferences (forcing the model to assign the correct answer only to the matching video). Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL with MixDPO yields large gains on hallucination benchmarks (up to 27.3 pp on temporal ordering) without harming general video understanding.

## Method Summary
CounterVid construction starts from real video-caption pairs (PE Video Dataset). A SigLIP-based keyframe extractor selects a frame matching the caption. A multimodal LLM (Claude-4-Sonnet) proposes distinct, plausible alternative actions, which are filtered for realism. Each action is converted into an editing prompt; Qwen-Image-Edit applies the edit to the keyframe, with an iterative LLM-guided refinement loop to maintain scene identity. Wan2.2-I2V then generates a short video clip from the keyframe and edited end frame. This yields pairs of videos (V⁺, V⁻) that are visually similar but differ in the action or temporal order. Preference pairs are formed as either textual (same video, swapped answers) or visual (same answer, swapped videos). MixDPO combines a standard DPO loss (L_t-pref) with a visual preference loss (L_v-pref) that maximizes the likelihood gap between π_θ(A|V⁺, Q) and π_θ(A|V⁻, Q). The model (Qwen2.5-VL) is fine-tuned with LoRA on the LLM, a frozen vision encoder (except the merger), and a mixing coefficient λ=1.

## Key Results
- Qwen2.5-VL-7B accuracy on held-out CounterVid increased by 8.4 percentage points (57.8% → 66.2%).
- Temporal ordering accuracy improved from 16.5% to 43.8% (27.3 pp gain).
- VidHalluc temporal sequence hallucination (TSH) score rose by 7.8 pp (82.7% → 90.5%).
- General video understanding (VideoMME, NExT-QA, TempCompass) remained unchanged, demonstrating no regression.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of CounterVid and MixDPO stems from three interconnected mechanisms:

**1. Generation of scene-preserving counterfactuals:** The pipeline (Section 3.2) ensures that V⁺ and V⁻ share identical context except for the targeted action or temporal structure. This creates semantically hard negatives that force the model to rely on visual dynamics rather than language priors. Evidence: [abstract] "synthesizes videos differing only in actions or temporal structure while preserving scene context"; [section 3.2] states "By construction... share the same context... and differ in the action trajectory." Break condition: if editing alters background/objects or actions are infeasible, the preference pairs become noisy.

**2. Joint textual and visual preference optimization:** MixDPO (Section 3.4) extends DPO with L_v-pref, which explicitly maximizes the likelihood gap between the same answer under two counterfactual videos. This penalizes the model for assigning high probability to an answer when the video evidence is wrong, thereby increasing input sensitivity. Evidence: [abstract] "MixDPO, a unified Direct Preference Optimization approach that jointly leverages textual and visual preferences"; [section 3.4] defines L_v-pref (Eq. 8) and notes it "forces the policy to attend to discriminative temporal cues." Break condition: low-quality visual pairs (imperfectly matched or ambiguous) degrade the signal; λ must be tuned to balance L_t-pref and L_v-pref.

**3. Modular pipeline enabling scalable high-quality hard negatives:** Separating semantic reasoning (LLM for action proposals) from visual synthesis (image editing + video generation) allows leveraging state-of-the-art models in each domain. The iterative refinement loop (LLM evaluating edited frames) reduces semantic drift. Core assumption: the LLM can propose contextually appropriate, expressible actions and the synthesis chain can realize them. Evidence: [abstract] "Our pipeline combines multimodal LLMs for action proposal and editing guidance with diffusion-based image and video models"; [section 3.2] details the refinement process. Break condition: if LLM proposals are low-diversity/infeasible or video generation introduces temporal inconsistencies, the resulting pairs lack informative contrast.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Counterfactual video generation for machine learning.  
  **Why needed:** Requires synthesizing videos that differ minimally in action/temporal structure while preserving scene context, combining image editing and video generation.  
  **Quick check:** What if counterfactual edits change unintended elements (e.g., lighting)? How does the iterative refinement loop prevent this?

- **Concept:** Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for multimodal alignment.  
  **Why needed:** MixDPO builds on DPO to align VLMs using pairwise preferences; understanding the log-likelihood ratio and reference model is prerequisite.  
  **Quick check:** In standard DPO, what is the role of the reference model π_ref, and how does it translate when the input is a video?

- **Concept:** Temporal grounding in video-language models.  
  **Why needed:** Target failures are temporal (action misidentification, order errors). Engineers must understand typical failure modes (e.g., inferring typical sequences from language) to design counterfactuals.  
  **Quick check:** Why might a model correctly describe objects but fail at "What happened first?" even if the order is visually clear?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
PVD dataset → Keyframe Extractor (SigLIP-SO400M) → Action Proposer (Claude-4-Sonnet) → Action Filter (LLM) → Editing Instructor (LLM) → Image Editor (Qwen-Image-Edit) → Refinement Loop (LLM evaluation) → Video Synthesizer (Wan2.2-I2V) → Preference Pair Construction (textual & visual) → MixDPO Training (Qwen2.5-VL with LoRA).

**Critical path:** Keyframe extraction → Action proposal & filtering → End-frame editing (with iterative refinement) → Video synthesis → Preference construction → MixDPO training. Any failure in editing or synthesis breaks the pipeline for that anchor.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Quality vs. Diversity: More action proposals increase diversity but raise infeasibility risk; filtering balances this.  
- Compute vs. Scale: ~3000 GPU-hours for ~26k pairs; scaling further is costly.  
- Module specialization vs. End-to-End: Separate models allow leveraging SOTA but introduce coordination overhead and error propagation.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Pipeline: Human eval "good" rate <60% (Table 1) indicates poor generation quality; CLIP similarity between anchor and edited frame may drop.  
- Training: L_v-pref gradient near zero suggests degenerate visual pairs; L_t-pref improves while L_v-pref does not suggests lack of visual grounding.  
- Evaluation: Gains on CounterVid but no transfer to VidHalluc/EventHallusion indicate overfitting to synthetic distribution.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Pipeline sanity check:** Run full generation on 50 anchors; conduct human eval (like Table 1) on action correctness & video quality. Target >70% "good". Identify bottleneck stage.  
2. **Preference validity test:** On a small held-out set, verify that the "chosen" video/answer is unambiguously supported; measure human-model agreement on preference labels. Target >85% agreement.  
3. **MixDPO component ablation:** Train three models on CounterVid: (a) L_t-pref only, (b) L_v-pref only, (c) MixDPO. Compare separately on action recognition vs. temporal ordering. Expect L_v-pref to drive temporal gains; MixDPO to balance both.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Question:** How does the varying quality (e.g., ambiguous labels, visual artifacts) of the synthetic CounterVid dataset affect the robustness and generalization of models trained with MixDPO?  
   **Basis in paper:** Human evaluation shows 68% "good" samples, with 7% "bad visual quality" and 17% "ambiguous" (Table 1). Limitations mention evaluation mechanisms "could be further refined."  
   **Why unresolved:** It is unclear whether performance gains are consistent across quality subsets or if noise limits theoretical improvements.  
   **What evidence would resolve:** Ablation training on curated high-quality vs. lower-quality subsets, measuring performance on clean held-out benchmarks and robustness to perturbations.

2. **Question:** Can the counterfactual generation framework be extended to produce longer, more complex temporal sequences (beyond ~2-second clips and simple action swaps) to address hallucinations in longer-form video understanding?  
   **Basis in paper:** Limitations state: "our current counterfactuals focus on short-term actions (<2 s); extending the framework to longer and more complex temporal structures remains an important direction."  
   **Why unresolved:** The pipeline synthesizes short, single-action clips and concatenates for ordering; scalability to longer, intertwined sequences is untested.  
   **What evidence would resolve:** Develop a modified pipeline generating >10-second counterfactuals and evaluate on long-video benchmarks (MovieChat, LongVideoBench).

3. **Question:** What is the impact of unfreezing and fine-tuning the vision encoder (vs. freezing it, as done) on MixDPO's ability to improve fine-grained temporal and action grounding?  
   **Basis in paper:** Limitations note: "we adopt parameter-efficient fine-tuning by freezing the vision encoder; while full end-to-end training may further enhance sensitivity to fine-grained visual cues, it incurs substantially higher computational cost... which we leave for future exploration."  
   **Why unresolved:** Freezing the vision encoder likely limits adaptation to subtle counterfactual differences; reported gains may be constrained.  
   **What evidence would resolve:** Train MixDPO with LoRA or full fine-tuning on the vision encoder, compare performance on fine-grained tasks and general understanding.

4. **Question:** How does the relative weighting (λ) between textual preference loss (L_t-pref) and visual preference loss (L_v-pref) in MixDPO affect the trade-off between improving grounding and maintaining general video-language fluency?  
   **Basis in paper:** MixDPO sets λ=1 without justification or sensitivity analysis. The paper argues both signals are important but the optimal balance is unknown.  
   **Why unresolved:** Fixed λ=1 might over-penalize or under-penalize; impact on generative quality is unexplored.  
   **What evidence would resolve:** Systematic experiments varying λ (e.g., 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0) and evaluating on both hallucination benchmarks and generative metrics (BLEU, METEOR).

5. **Question:** Does the MixDPO framework generalize to mitigating other hallucination types beyond action and temporal ordering, such as object existence, attribute, or spatial reasoning errors?  
   **Basis in paper:** CounterVid is action/temporal-centric; tests on VidHalluc (TSH) and EventHallusion (event-level) do not break down other error types. The pipeline is action-centric.  
   **Why unresolved:** It is unclear if input sensitivity learned from visual preferences transfers to other hallucination modes.  
   **What evidence would resolve:** Analyze per-category results on benchmarks like VideoHallucer; create counterfactual datasets targeting other hallucinations and test cross-task generalization.

## Limitations
- **Proprietary components:** The pipeline relies on PVD dataset (unreleased) and Claude-4-Sonnet (closed), making exact reproduction difficult; substitutes may degrade quality.
- **Compute and scalability:** Generating ~26k pairs required ~3000 GPU-hours; scaling further is costly, and risk of overfitting to synthetic distribution remains.
- **Annotation reliance:** The pipeline uses LLM self-evaluation for refinement; human validation is limited to a small sample (Table 1), leaving potential for undetected systematic errors.

## Confidence
- **Counterfactual generation mechanism:** High (pipeline details are explicit; break conditions well-defined).
- **MixDPO effectiveness:** Medium (unified loss described, but success contingent on preference quality; ablations supportive but not fully isolating components).
- **Generalization preservation:** Medium (reported stable scores on general benchmarks, but based on single model seed/lambda; possible benchmark overfitting).

## Next Checks
1. **Counterfactual fidelity test:** Run pipeline on 100 anchors; compute CLIP similarity between original and edited end frames (target >0.75). Conduct human eval on 50 samples for action correctness and visual coherence (target >70% "good").
2. **MixDPO component ablation:** Train three Qwen2.5-VL-7B models on CounterVid: (a) L_t-pref only, (b) L_v-pref only, (c) MixDPO. Evaluate separately on action recognition and temporal ordering; expect L_v-pref to boost temporal ordering, MixDPO to balance both.
3. **Distribution shift robustness:** After MixDPO training, evaluate on a held-out real temporal hallucination benchmark (e.g., VidHalluc subset). Compare accuracy delta vs. base model; aim for ≤2 pp drop relative to CounterVid gains to avoid catastrophic overfitting.
