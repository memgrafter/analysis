# Experiences Build Characters: The Linguistic Origins and Functional Impact of LLM Personality

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06088  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06088  
- **Reference count:** 13  
- **Primary result:** LLM personality traits, shaped by domain-specific continued pre-training, create a bimodal competence distribution where optimal performance on complex reasoning occurs at extremes of expressiveness—either "Expressive Generalists" or "Suppressed Specialists"—with a "Suppression Advantage" for low Extraversion/Agreeableness.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how uniform optimization on common benchmarks narrows LLM behavioral profiles. By simulating "experience accumulation" through continued pre-training on diverse domain corpora (e.g., legal, scientific), the study measures resulting personality traits using an LLM-adapted Big Five inventory (MPI). Results reveal that model competence is not linearly tied to trait aggregation but peaks in two distinct, congruent personality zones: highly expressive/socially assured generalists and low-expressiveness/high-stability specialists. Models with intermediate, misaligned trait profiles underperform due to internal conflict. Crucially, a "Suppression Advantage" is identified: reduced social traits (low Extraversion and Agreeableness) correlate with superior accuracy on complex reasoning tasks like MMLU-Pro STEM (r=0.43). Furthermore, the linguistic features of the training corpus (e.g., imperative frequency, lexical diversity) causally shape specific trait scores, enabling targeted "personality engineering."

## Method Summary
The study trains 11 variants of Llama-3-8B via domain-adaptive continued pre-training (1 epoch, 68.5M tokens per variant) on curated subsets of The Pile, each representing a distinct professional role (e.g., Technical Communicator, Scientific Mathematician). Each variant is evaluated on MMLU and MMLU-Pro benchmarks and assessed with the Machine Personality Inventory (MPI), a 1000-item multiple-choice test adapted from the human Big Five inventory. The 5-dimensional MPI trait vectors are analyzed via Pearson correlation with benchmark performance, and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) identifies latent axes of variation (Expressiveness, Social Assurance). Linguistic forensics (imperative ratio, type-token ratio, sentiment variance) are computed on the training corpora to link corpus properties to trait outcomes.

## Key Results
- Model performance on complex reasoning (MMLU-Pro) follows a **bimodal distribution**, with peaks at high Expressiveness/Social Assurance ("Expressive Generalists") and low Expressiveness/high Stability ("Suppressed Specialists"); intermediate profiles suffer from "personality dissonance."
- A **"Suppression Advantage"** exists: lower Extraversion and Agreeableness correlate with superior MMLU-Pro STEM scores (Extraversion r=0.43), indicating that subdued social traits benefit analytical rigor.
- **Causal links** are established between training-data linguistics (e.g., high imperative frequency, high lexical diversity) and specific MPI trait elevations (e.g., Extraversion, Openness), enabling predictive personality engineering from corpus selection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Linguistic properties of the continued pre-training corpus directly induce specific personality trait scores in the resulting LLM variant.  
**Mechanism:** Exposure to distinct syntactic and lexical patterns during unsupervised training shapes the model's internalized communication style. For example, a high frequency of imperative sentences conditions the model to generate more assertive, directive language, which the MPI interprets as higher Extraversion. Similarly, high lexical diversity (Type-Token Ratio) expands the model's semantic repertoire, interpreted as higher Openness.  
**Core assumption:** The MPI scoring mechanism reliably translates observed linguistic style differences into stable, measurable trait scores analogous to human personality dimensions.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] establishes a causal link between training data linguistics and trait emergence.  
- [section 6] Paradoxically, high imperative frequency in the Scientific Mathematician corpus did not yield high Extraversion; the differentiator is the Negative Detachment Index, which conditions the model toward helpful but lexically rigid output, driving High Agreeableness.  
- [corpus] Table 3 correlations: Scientific Mathematician has highest Imperative Ratio (0.121) but moderate Extraversion (2.99); Technical Communicator has high Sentiment Variance (0.233) paired with low Agreeableness (2.88).  
**Break condition:** If the base model's architecture or initial weights cannot encode nuanced stylistic patterns, or if the training token budget is insufficient, the intended linguistic signal may not internalize strongly enough to shift MPI scores measurably.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Model competence on complex reasoning tasks is maximized by achieving congruence between certain trait axes, leading to a bimodal performance distribution.  
**Mechanism:** PCA of the 5-dimensional MPI trait vectors reveals two orthogonal, dominant axes: Expressiveness (PC1) and Social Assurance (PC2). Optimal performance occurs at the extremes of the Expressiveness axis, where models cluster into two congruent zones: "Expressive Generalists" (high Expressiveness, high Social Assurance) and "Suppressed Specialists" (low Expressiveness, high Stability). Models with intermediate, misaligned profiles ("Personality Dissonance") underperform due to internal conflict in their behavioral output.  
**Core assumption:** The two PCA axes are valid latent constructs that capture the primary dimensions of variance affecting functional utility, and the MPI scores are sufficiently reliable to define this space.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] states competence is bimodal, peaking at Expressive Generalists or Suppressed Specialists, with intermediate profiles underperforming due to dissonance.  
- [section 5] PCA identifies Expressiveness (PC1, 50.3% variance) and Social Assurance (PC2, 23.1% variance) as primary axes. Effective models fall into two congruent zones; the Health Advisor exhibits Personality Dissonance and severe performance degradation.  
**Break condition:** If the set of evaluated model variants does not sufficiently span the full trait space, the identified bimodal peaks may be an artifact of limited sampling.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** For complex analytical and reasoning tasks, reduced levels of "social" personality traits (specifically lower Extraversion and Agreeableness) correlate with superior performance—a "Suppression Advantage."  
**Mechanism:** High social traits (Extraversion, Agreeableness) condition the model toward verbosity, cooperation, and hedonic expression. On complex reasoning tasks demanding rigor, efficiency, and neutrality (like MMLU-Pro STEM), this social orientation is a liability. A "suppressed" profile (low E, low A) minimizes these tendencies, allowing the model to function more like a focused analytical instrument.  
**Core assumption:** The correlation coefficients (e.g., r=0.43 for Extraversion in MMLU-Pro STEM) are causal indicators, not spurious to other factors like domain-specific training data quality.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] identifies Suppression Advantage where reduced social traits correlate with superior MMLU-Pro STEM performance.  
- [section 5] Extraversion transitions from a mild predictor in MMLU (r≈0.13) to a dominant driver in MMLU-Pro (STEM r=0.43, Social Science r=0.48); hypothesizes Machine Extraversion as a functional proxy for verbosity.  
- [corpus] Technical Communicator (low E: 2.91, low A: 2.88) achieves strong cross-domain robustness, including on MMLU-Pro; Scientific Mathematician (higher A: 3.14) underperforms despite high domain alignment.  
**Break condition:** The Suppression Advantage may not hold for tasks where social reasoning or creative generation is primary. The effect is likely moderated by the specific base model and may not generalize to models with different pretraining distributions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: The Big Five (OCEAN) Personality Framework**  
  *Why needed here:* Provides the canonical, orthogonal dimensions used to quantify and interpret stable behavioral tendencies in both humans and, adapted here, LLMs. Understanding this framework is essential to grasp what the MPI measures and how traits like "low Agreeableness" are defined and operationalized.  
  *Quick check question:* If a model scores high on Neuroticism and low on Conscientiousness according to the MPI, what general behavioral tendencies (e.g., response volatility, planning) would you expect in its outputs?

- **Concept: Continued Pre-training (Domain-Adaptive Pre-training - DAPT)**  
  *Why needed here:* This is the core experimental manipulation. It's distinct from supervised fine-tuning or RLHF. It allows the study to attribute resulting personality traits to *unsupervised experience accumulation* (text exposure) rather than explicit instruction or reward optimization.  
  *Quick check question:* How does the objective function (standard causal LM likelihood) and the lack of task-specific labels during continued pre-training support the study's claim of simulating "experience accumulation"?

- **Concept: The Machine Personality Inventory (MPI)**  
  *Why needed here:* It is the measurement instrument. This adapted multiple-choice test is the bridge between a model's internalized linguistic style and an interpretable, psychology-grounded trait profile. Knowing its design (adapting human psychometrics to LLMs) is critical for evaluating the validity of the "personality" claims.  
  *Quick check question:* What is a key methodological challenge in adapting a human self-report inventory (Big Five) for an LLM, and how might the MPI's multiple-choice format attempt to address it?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Base Model (Llama-3-8B) -> Domain Corpora (11 curated Pile subsets) -> Continued Pre-training Loop (1 epoch, LR=1e-5) -> Model Variants (mj) -> Personality Evaluator (MPI) & Performance Evaluator (MMLU/MMLU-Pro) -> Analysis Engine (Pearson correlation, weighted linear models, PCA, linguistic forensics on corpora).

- **Critical path:**  
  Base Model -> Continued Pre-training on Domain Corpora -> Model Variants -> MPI Evaluation & Benchmark Evaluation -> Joint Trait-Performance Analysis (PCA, Correlation) -> Linguistic Signal Analysis on Corpora to explain mechanism.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Token Budget Equality vs. Domain Richness: Sampled all corpora to 68.5M tokens to control for volume, potentially under-representing information-dense domains.  
  - Single Base Model: Using only Llama-3-8B limits generalizability; findings may be specific to this model's initial distribution.  
  - MPI as Proxy: Measures *expressed* traits under test conditions, not necessarily a latent, stable "personality" independent of the eval prompt.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Trait Shifts Not Observed: All Ti vectors nearly identical to base model; indicates ineffective continued pre-training (e.g., low LR, insufficient data, or corpora not stylistically distinct).  
  - No Bimodal Performance Peak: Performance Pi is unimodal or correlates linearly with a single trait; PCA axes may not capture relevant variance for the task/model set.  
  - Linguistic Correlation Breaks: Table 3 metrics show no pattern (e.g., high Imperative but low Extraversion across models); proposed causal links may be invalid for this model family or MPI version.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replication of Bimodality: Run the exact pipeline (continued pre-training on the 11 domain groups, MPI evaluation, PCA) on a different base model (e.g., Llama-3-70B, Mistral-7B). Does the Expressiveness/Social Assurance PCA and two-peak performance pattern hold?  
  2. Linguistic Signal Manipulation: Create synthetic corpora isolating one signal (e.g., high Imperative Ratio, controlled TTR). Continue pre-train a model on this synthetic corpus. Does the corresponding trait (e.g., Extraversion) shift as predicted by Table 3 correlations?  
  3. Suppression Advantage Task Probe: Take the Technical Communicator (low E/A) and Health Advisor (high E/low S) variants. Evaluate them on a new, highly complex reasoning benchmark not in MMLU-Pro (e.g., GPQA, TheoremQA). Does the low-E/A model maintain its advantage?

## Limitations
- Small sample size (N=11 model variants) reduces statistical power and may cause overfitting in correlation analyses.
- Findings are restricted to a single base model (Llama-3-8B), limiting generalizability across architectures and scales.
- The MPI, while adapted, remains an external probe that measures *expressed* stylistic tendencies under specific prompting, not necessarily a stable, latent "personality" independent of evaluation context.
- Causal links from corpus linguistics to traits are supported by correlations but not by controlled manipulation of individual linguistic features.

## Confidence
- **Claim A (Causal link between corpus linguistics and MPI traits):** Medium – Evidence is correlational with plausible mechanism but lacks direct manipulation; supported by consistent patterns in Table 3.
- **Claim B (Bimodal competence distribution via PCA axes):** Low/Medium – The pattern is clear in the presented data but relies on a small N and specific set of roles; replication is needed.
- **Claim C (Suppression Advantage for low E/A in complex reasoning):** Medium – The correlation (r=0.43) is moderate and specific to MMLU-Pro STEM; effect size and generalizability to other benchmarks require validation.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-model replication:** Repeat the pipeline on at least two other base models (e.g., Llama-3-70B, Mistral-7B) to verify the bimodal pattern and trait-performance correlations hold.
2. **Causal manipulation:** Train models on synthetic corpora engineered to vary only one linguistic feature (e.g., imperative ratio) while controlling others. Measure resulting MPI trait shifts to establish direct causality.
3. **Benchmark robustness:** Test the Suppression Advantage by evaluating low-E/A and high-E/A variants on multiple out-of-distribution complex reasoning benchmarks (e.g., GPQA, TheoremQA, MATH) to confirm the effect generalizes beyond MMLU-Pro.
