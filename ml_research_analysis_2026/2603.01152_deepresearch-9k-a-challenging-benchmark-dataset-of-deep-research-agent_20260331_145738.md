# DeepResearch-9K: A Challenging Benchmark Dataset of Deep-Research Agent

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.01152
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01152
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** A low-cost pipeline produces a challenging 9K-question dataset; RL-trained 3B models achieve SOTA (~22.5%) on it, outperforming larger systems.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DeepResearch-9K, a novel 9,000-question benchmark designed to train and evaluate deep-research agents that perform multi-step, tool-aided web exploration. The dataset features three difficulty levels (L1-L3) constructed via an autonomous, low-cost (~$200) pipeline that transforms existing multi-hop QA data by enforcing minimum search call counts and systematically obfuscating entities. Each instance includes a high-quality search trajectory and verifiable answer generated by the Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B-A3B teacher model. The authors also release the open-source DeepResearch-R1 framework, which supports multi-turn web interactions and various RL algorithms. Key findings show the teacher model's accuracy drops drastically with difficulty (72.47% L1 → 23.73% L3), confirming the benchmark's challenge. Agents trained on this data, exemplified by a 3B Llama model using PPO, achieve up to 22.50% accuracy, surpassing larger baselines like DeepSeek V3 (20.18%) and establishing a new state-of-the-art.

## Method Summary
The core contribution is a two-part system: a dataset and a training framework. The DeepResearch-9K dataset is constructed from seed entities in existing multi-hop QA corpora. A four-stage pipeline builds reasoning chains of constrained length, applies entity obfuscation (replacing named entities with functional descriptions), and validates quality. A powerful teacher agent (Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B-A3B) then generates trajectories—alternating `Think` reasoning steps and `search` tool calls—to produce final answers. The DeepResearch-R1 framework enables training open-source agents (e.g., Llama, Qwen) on this data. It implements a simulated web search environment and supports two RL paradigms: (1) **Zero-RL**, where a base model learns directly from environment interaction, and (2) **SFT+RL**, where a model is first fine-tuned on correct teacher trajectories before RL refinement. Rewards are provided either by a rule-based outcome check (answer correctness) or an LLM-as-judge (DeepSeek-V3).

## Key Results
*   Teacher model accuracy declines sharply with intended difficulty: L1 (72.47%), L2 (~44%), L3 (23.73%).
*   A 3B Llama3.2 model trained via Zero-RL (PPO) achieves 22.50% test accuracy, exceeding the 20.18% of the much larger DeepSeek V3 baseline.
*   Training paradigm efficacy is model-dependent; SFT+RL boosts Qwen2.5-3B, while Zero-RL yields the best result for Llama3.2-3B.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Difficulty Scaling via Search Call Minimums and Entity Obfuscation
*   **Claim:** The dataset’s design forces agents to develop genuine deep-research capabilities (long-horizon planning, synthesis) by eliminating shortcuts.
*   **Mechanism:** Questions are engineered to require a minimum number of independent search queries (e.g., ≥15 for L3) while masking key entities with functional descriptions and complex syntax. This prevents single-source retrieval or memorization, mandating multi-step exploration and iterative verification.
*   **Core assumption:** Deep-research task difficulty is primarily governed by the required number of tool invocations and the degree of entity obfuscation; success on harder levels indicates mastery of sustained, strategic exploration.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "9000 questions spanning three difficulty levels... high-quality search trajectories..."; [section] "We assume that the more difficult the problem, the more times the search tool is called... L3... minimum of 15 search iterations."
*   **Break condition:** If agents achieve high L3 accuracy without meeting the search count threshold (e.g., through internal recall or heuristic shortcuts), the difficulty scaling fails as a measure of deep-research skill.

### Mechanism 2: High-Quality Teacher Trajectories as Behavioral Priors
*   **Claim:** Distilling trajectories from a capable teacher model (Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B-A3B) bootstraps smaller agents with effective tool-use strategies and planning patterns.
*   **Mechanism:** The teacher generates solutions with interleaved `Think` (planning/verification) and `search` actions, creating detailed demonstrations of coherent reasoning chains. These are used for SFT, providing a strong initial policy and guiding the subsequent RL stage.
*   **Core assumption:** A sufficiently capable teacher produces solutions that are both correct and exemplify robust deep-research behavior; these patterns can be transferred to smaller models via distillation and refined with RL.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "high-quality search trajectories with reasoning chains from Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B-A3B"; [section] "We collect the search trajectory with reasoning chains... By alternating between these steps, the agent progressively constructs a coherent reasoning chain."
*   **Break condition:** If teacher trajectories are systematically biased, noisy, or contain incorrect strategies (teacher accuracy is 55.84%), student models may inherit suboptimal behaviors, limiting the benefit of SFT.

### Mechanism 3: Open-Source Framework Enabling Paradigm Analysis and Efficient Training
*   **Claim:** The DeepResearch-R1 framework, with its integrated environment and RL support, facilitates systematic training analysis and allows smaller models to achieve SOTA performance.
*   **Mechanism:** The framework couples a simulated web search environment with implementations of PPO and GRPO, and supports both LLM-as-judge and rule-based rewards. Experiments reveal that SFT mitigates cold-start for some base models (Qwen), while zero-RL elicits complex reasoning in others (Llama), enabling a 3B model to outperform larger proprietary systems.
*   **Core assumption:** RL from environmental interaction, with well-designed rewards, can effectively train deep-research agents; the optimal pre-training stage (SFT vs. zero-RL) interacts with the base model's inherent capabilities.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "open-source training framework DeepResearch-R1... agents trained on DeepResearch-9K achieve state-of-the-art results."; [section] "We systematically compare zero-RL and SFT+RL... Llama-3.2-3B with PPO (Zero-RL) achieves highest accuracy 22.50%."
*   **Break condition:** If reward hacking, training instability, or poor generalization prevents meaningful gains, or if results fail to replicate on other benchmarks, the framework's claimed advantages are undermined.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Environment (Simulated Web Search via Serper API) -> Agent (Base LLM with policy for `Think`/`search` actions) -> Reward Model (DeepSeek-V3 as judge or rule-based) -> Trainer (PPO/GRPO for rollouts and policy updates). Data Synthesizer (Teacher model + obfuscation) feeds into dataset splits for training.
*   **Critical path:** Seed entity extraction -> Reasoning chain construction (with length constraints) -> Entity obfuscation -> Teacher trajectory generation -> Dataset splitting -> SFT/Zero-RL training -> Evaluation (accuracy, search count).
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Cost vs. scalability:** The $200 pipeline is affordable but depends on a proprietary teacher model; scaling up may increase cost and reduce reproducibility.
    *   **Teacher dependence:** High-quality teacher demonstrations are essential, but the teacher’s 55.84% accuracy introduces noise; SFT risks propagating both effective and flawed strategies.
    *   **Reward design:** LLM-as-judge offers flexibility but can be inconsistent and costly; rule-based rewards are sparse and may not guide intermediate steps well.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Performance plateau:** Test accuracy remains near ~20% despite training, suggesting inherent task difficulty or an ineffective reward signal.
    *   **Inefficient exploration:** High search counts but low accuracy indicate poor planning or query formulation.
    *   **Pattern regurgitation:** Model replicates teacher’s specific query patterns but fails on novel questions, showing a lack of generalization beyond distillation.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Teacher replication:** Run Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B-A3B on the DeepResearch-9K test set to verify the reported accuracy gradient across L1-L3.
    2.  **Baseline training replication:** Using DeepResearch-R1, train Llama3.2-3B with both Zero-RL (PPO) and SFT+RL; confirm Zero-RL reaches ~22.5% and surpasses DeepSeek V3.
    3.  **Cross-benchmark generalization:** Evaluate the trained model on BrowseComp-Plus; a significant drop would indicate overfitting to dataset-specific patterns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** Does tool call count reliably correlate with actual difficulty of deep-research tasks? The paper defines levels by call count but does not validate this proxy against human judgment or other difficulty factors.
*   **Open Question 2:** Why does supervised fine-tuning (SFT) before RL improve Qwen2.5-3B but not Llama3.2-3B? The paper shows this interaction but provides no explanatory analysis of model-specific dynamics.
*   **Open Question 3:** How reliable is LLM-as-judge for evaluating complex deep-research answers, and can alternative methods reduce bias? The evaluation uses DeepSeek-V3 without addressing known biases or comparing to human evaluation.

## Limitations
*   Proprietary teacher model (Tongyi-DeepResearch-30B-A3B) limits full reproducibility and may embed hidden biases.
*   Teacher accuracy (55.84%) is modest, meaning many training trajectories contain errors, risking error propagation.
*   LLM-as-judge reward (DeepSeek V3) is costly, potentially inconsistent, and lacks human validation, raising concerns about metric reliability.

## Confidence
*   **Difficulty scaling (search count + obfuscation)** – Medium: Teacher accuracy drop supports a gradient, but no direct evidence that meeting the search minimum is necessary for success; shortcuts may exist.
*   **Teacher trajectory effectiveness** – Medium-Low: Teacher accuracy below 60% suggests significant noise; SFT+RL gains may capture only partial correct strategies and risk inheriting errors.
*   **DeepResearch-R1 SOTA claim** – Medium: Results surpass larger models but are unreplicated; reward design and RL stability could introduce measurement artifacts.
