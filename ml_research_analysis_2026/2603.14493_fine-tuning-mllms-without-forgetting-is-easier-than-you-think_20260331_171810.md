# Fine-tuning MLLMs Without Forgetting Is Easier Than You Think

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.14493
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14493
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Catastrophic forgetting in MLLM fine-tuning can be largely avoided using simple regularization (low learning rates, LoRA) and diverse data mixing, achieving state-of-the-art continual learning without replay buffers.

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the assumption that fine-tuning Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) inevitably causes catastrophic forgetting. Through a rigorous 2x2 evaluation matrix (in-distribution/out-of-distribution for both images and text), the authors find that conservative fine-tuning—using low learning rates or Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)—preserves performance on novel visual domains while matching the accuracy of aggressive full fine-tuning on the target task (e.g., ImageNet-VQA). The sole failure mode, where in-distribution images with out-of-distribution text suffer, is attributed to "task-specific overfitting" (memorizing the prompt template). This is effectively mitigated by mixing in diverse instruction data (e.g., LLaVA-665K) during training. These insights extend to continual learning, where simple strategies like incremental LoRA or sequential low-rate fine-tuning surpass complex SOTA methods on the MLLM-CL benchmark, all without replay buffers.

## Method Summary
The study fine-tunes MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA) using LLaMA-Factory. The core experimental design employs a 2x2 evaluation framework, testing combinations of in-distribution (ID) vs. out-of-distribution (OOD) images and text to isolate forgetting modes. For single-task specialization (e.g., ImageNet-VQA), they compare aggressive full fine-tuning (high LR) against conservative regimes: full fine-tuning at a very low learning rate (1e-6) and LoRA (rank 8). To address the identified OOD text/ID image failure, they experiment with mixing the target task data with general-purpose instruction datasets like LLaVA-665K at varying ratios. For continual learning, they implement two simple strategies on the MLLM-CL benchmark: **IncLoRA** (training and merging a new LoRA adapter per task) and **SeqFull** (sequential full fine-tuning at LR 1e-6), both in no-replay settings.

## Key Results
*   Conservative fine-tuning (low LR or LoRA) reduces the accuracy drop on OOD images from -33.6 pp (aggressive full FT) to within -2.97 pp, while matching target-task performance (differences ≤0.6 pp).
*   Mixing 30-50% diverse instruction data (LLaVA-665K) into the target task data recovers OOD text accuracy with negligible loss (<1 pp) on the target task.
*   In no-replay continual learning, SeqFull achieves 79.10% on the first task (RS, Last), surpassing all prior methods and matching or exceeding complex SOTA approaches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Constraining parameter updates via low learning rates or LoRA prevents catastrophic forgetting on out-of-distribution (OOD) images by preserving pre-trained visual representations.
*   **Mechanism:** Aggressive full fine-tuning at high learning rates overwrites weights encoding general visual features. Conservative updates (low LR or low-rank adaptation) make smaller, targeted adjustments that specialize on the target task without disrupting the invariant feature space for novel visual domains.
*   **Break condition:** Using a high learning rate (>1e-5) or full fine-tuning of all parameters without constraint on OOD image evaluation (ID T–OODI, OOD T–OODI).

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Specialization (in-distribution accuracy) and generalization preservation (OOD accuracy) are not inherently at odds when using regularized fine-tuning.
*   **Mechanism:** Parameter-efficient or low-rate updates achieve comparable ID task performance to aggressive full fine-tuning because the primary knowledge for the narrow target task is already present; fine-tuning merely calibrates decision boundaries. The regularization prevents collateral damage to other capabilities.
*   **Break condition:** Fine-tuning only the multimodal projector, which shows a significant ID accuracy drop (-4.70 pp), indicating over-constraining can harm specialization.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Forgetting in the ID-image/OOD-text quadrant (OOD T–IDI) arises from "task-specific overfitting"—memorizing the fine-tuning prompt template—and is mitigated by mixing diverse instruction data during training.
*   **Mechanism:** When images are in-distribution, the model's output is dominated by its language component. Repeated exposure to a single task format causes the model to shortcut by ignoring novel prompt instructions and defaulting to outputting the class-label token. Mixing datasets with varied instruction styles forces the model to attend to the prompt text.
*   **Break condition:** Mixing with datasets that share the same instruction style (e.g., another classification task) yields only marginal gains.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Catastrophic Forgetting**
    *   *Why needed here:* The paper directly challenges the conventional assumption that fine-tuning MLLMs inevitably causes catastrophic forgetting. Understanding this concept is necessary to grasp the significance of the paper's counter-intuitive findings.
    *   *Quick check question:* What is the difference between parameter overwriting (catastrophic forgetting) and prompt-ignoring (task-specific overfitting) as failure modes?
*   **Concept: Distribution Shift (In-Distribution vs. Out-of-Distribution)**
    *   *Why needed here:* The entire 2x2 evaluation framework is built on isolating shifts in the image and text domains. Correctly defining ID vs. OOD is fundamental to interpreting the results.
    *   *Quick check question:* Why is the combination "OOD Text + ID Images" considered a distinct evaluation quadrant, and what does it isolate?
*   **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT), specifically LoRA**
    *   *Why needed here:* LoRA is the primary advocated technique for preventing forgetting. Understanding its mechanism (low-rank adaptation freezing original weights) explains *why* it acts as a strong regularizer.
    *   *Quick check question:* How does the low-rank constraint in LoRA inherently limit the magnitude of change to the base model's weights compared to full fine-tuning?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Vision Encoder -> Multimodal Projector -> LLM Backbone
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Start with a strong pre-trained MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA).
    2.  For single-task specialization: Apply **low learning rate (~1e-6) full fine-tuning of the LLM backbone** OR **LoRA on the LLM backbone**. Freeze vision encoder and projector.
    3.  If evaluation involves **novel text instructions over familiar images** (OOD T–IDI), supplement step 2 with **data-hybrid training**: mix 30-50% of a diverse, general-purpose instruction dataset (e.g., LLaVA-665K) into the target task data.
    4.  For sequential multi-task learning (continual learning): Apply **incremental LoRA (train new adapter per task, then merge)** or **sequential full fine-tuning at low LR (1e-6)** *without replay buffers*.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **LoRA vs. Low-LR Full Fine-tuning:** LoRA is more parameter-efficient and slightly more robust for OOD images. Low-LR full fine-tuning may match target task accuracy more exactly. Choose LoRA for efficiency/stability; choose low-LR full for absolute best ID performance.
    *   **Data-Hybrid Ratio:** Increasing general data mix (LLaVA-665K) improves OOD T–IDI robustness up to ~50-70%. Beyond 50%, ID task accuracy starts a minimal drop. Cost is longer training and potential dilution if too high.
    *   **Replay Buffer in Continual Learning:** Simple methods (IncLoRA, SeqFull) work *better* without replay buffers in no-replay settings. Replay adds complexity but may offer marginal gains; authors advocate avoiding it due to privacy/storage.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    1.  **Sharp Accuracy Drop on OOD T–IDI:** Performance on ImageWikiQA plummets after fine-tuning on ImageNet-VQA, despite stable OOD image performance.
    2.  **High Accuracy on Class-Label Distractor Test:** In the diagnostic test, accuracy collapses when a class-name distractor is present *after* fine-tuning.
    3.  **Projector-Only Fine-Tuning Underperformance:** Final ID accuracy <90% on ImageNet-VQA suggests under-specialization.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Reproduce Figure 2:** Fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL-3B on ImageNet-VQA with `LLM Backbone, Full, lr=1e-6`. Plot ID accuracy vs. all 4 quadrant accuracies during training. Expect: ID↑, OOD T–OODI ~flat, OOD T–IDI↓.
    2.  **Validate Data-Hybrid Remedy:** Take the failing `Full, 1e-6` model. Mix 50% LLaVA-665K with ImageNet-VQA. Re-evaluate OOD T–IDI (ImageWikiQA). Expect: Significant recovery (~+10pp) with <1pp ID loss.
    3.  **Test on Continual Learning (MLLM-CL):** Implement `IncLoRA` on LLaVA-1.5-7B for MLLM-CL sequence *without replay*. Report `Last` and `Average`. Expect: Competitive/SOTA vs. methods with routers/regularization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** What specific mechanism triggers task-specific overfitting only when evaluating on in-distribution images with out-of-distribution text, and why do common regularizers (low learning rate, LoRA) fail to mitigate it?
    *   *Basis in paper:* The authors isolate this failure mode in §4.1–4.2, linking it to overfitting the classification template via a distractor experiment. They note that forgetting persists despite standard regularization but do not explain the quadrant-specific cause.
    *   *Why unresolved:* The paper provides strong empirical evidence but stops short of a mechanistic analysis (e.g., attention patterns, gradient dynamics) that would clarify why the issue is unique to ID-image/OOD-text.
    *   *What evidence would resolve it:* Ablations that systematically vary prompt consistency during fine-tuning, together with probing studies (e.g., saliency maps, representation similarity) across all four evaluation quadrants.
*   **Open Question 2:** Which properties of mixed instruction data (diversity, image domain, task format) are necessary to prevent task-specific overfitting, and does mixing inadvertently reduce performance on other OOD axes (e.g., OOD images)?
    *   *Basis in paper:* §4.3 shows that mixing LLaVA‑665K (broad instructions) mitigates overfitting while mixing Flowers102 (similar task) does not, hinting at the importance of instruction diversity. The authors do not isolate the exact factor nor evaluate impact on OOD T‑OODI.
    *   *Why unresolved:* The study compares only a few datasets and ratios; the critical dimension(s) of diversity remain unclear, and possible trade‑offs with other general capabilities are unexplored.
    *   *What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled mixtures where datasets are orthogonalized along instruction variety, image distribution, and reasoning type, combined with a full 2×2 evaluation after each mixture.
*   **Open Question 3:** Do the reported findings hold for MLLMs with alternative architectures (e.g., cross‑attention fusion, encoder‑decoder LLMs), different vision encoders (e.g., SigLIP, DINO), and for generative tasks (e.g., captioning, video QA)?
    *   *Basis in paper:* The paper validates only Qwen2.5‑VL and LLaVA (both CLIP‑vision + decoder‑only LLM) on multiple‑choice VQA. The general claim that “forgetting is not inevitable” is made without testing a broader range of models and task formats.
    *   *Why unresolved:* Architectural differences (e.g., fusion strategy, pre‑training objectives) could alter forgetting dynamics; the current evidence base is too narrow to support universal conclusions.
    *   *What evidence would resolve it:* Replication of the 2×2 evaluation across a diverse suite of MLLMs (e.g., Flamingo, InternVL, Cambrian) and tasks (captioning, video QA) using the same fine‑tuning recipes.
*   **Open Question 4:** What theoretical principle explains why low learning rates and LoRA preserve OOD performance in MLLM fine‑tuning?
    *   *Basis in paper:* §3.1 and §3.2 demonstrate empirically that small updates avoid catastrophic forgetting, but the paper offers no theoretical analysis.
    *   *Why unresolved:* Without a formal understanding, it is difficult to predict when these strategies will succeed or to design principled alternatives; the findings remain a useful observation rather than a predictive framework.
    *   *What evidence would resolve it:* Analytical studies linking parameter update magnitude/rank to preservation of pre‑trained feature manifolds (e.g., via Fisher information, loss‑landscape flatness) or generalization bounds tailored to multimodal fine‑tuning.

## Limitations
*   **ImageNet-VQA construction:** Exact CLIP model, distractor selection threshold/process, and fixed validation split are unspecified, risking task distribution shifts and reproducibility issues.
*   **Data-hybrid sampling:** The strategy (per-batch vs. epoch-level mixing) and precise mixing ratios beyond tested points are unclear, affecting implementation fidelity.
*   **Continual learning protocol:** Exact dataset splits, evaluation decoding parameters, and per-task epoch counts for the MLLM-CL benchmark are not detailed, potentially affecting the reported SOTA results.

## Confidence
*   **Cluster 1 (Low LR/LoRA prevents OOD image forgetting):** **High.** Strong internal evidence from controlled 2x2 matrix experiments (Table 1, Fig 2).
*   **Cluster 2 (No inherent ID/OOD performance trade-off):** **High.** Consistent quantitative results showing minimal accuracy differences between aggressive and regularized fine-tuning on the target task.
*   **Cluster 3 (Task-specific overfitting fix via diverse data):** **Medium.** Supported by ablation studies (Fig 4), but the mechanism relies on a specific, unquantified "diversity" threshold in the mixed dataset.
