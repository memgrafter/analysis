# Does Your Optimizer Care How You Normalize? Normalization-Optimizer Coupling in LLM Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.01563
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01563
- **Reference count**: 39
- **Primary result**: Derf normalizer shows a large, silent coupling with Muon optimizer: validation loss gap triples from +0.31 nats (with AdamW) to +0.97 nats (with Muon) due to severe erf saturation.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether normalization layers and optimizers in LLM training can be chosen independently. A controlled 3×2 factorial experiment at 1B parameters reveals a significant interaction: the Derf normalizer (using erf) degrades substantially under the Muon optimizer compared to AdamW, while DyT (using tanh) remains robust. The failure mechanism involves Muon’s faster weight growth pushing Derf’s activations into erf saturation, discarding scale information. Simple mitigations—reducing Derf’s α or adding an EMA-blend—recover most of the performance gap. The coupling is silent (no crashes) and specific to Derf’s squashing nonlinearity, challenging the assumption of orthogonal design choices and highlighting the need for joint normalization-optimizer tuning in large-scale training.

## Method Summary
The authors trained 1B-parameter Llama models (GQA, SwiGLU, RoPE) on the FineWeb-Edu dataset with sequence length 2048. A full 3×2 factorial design was used: three normalizers (RMSNorm, Derf, DyT) × two optimizers (AdamW, Muon). Each configuration ran for 1000 steps with three random seeds, using cosine LR decay (100-step warmup), gradient clipping (max norm 1.0), and bfloat16 autocast (normalization parameters in float32). AdamW used LR 3e-4, betas (0.9, 0.95), weight decay 0.1; Muon used LR 0.02, no weight decay. Primary metric was validation loss (nats) at step 1000, with diagnostics including per-layer activation RMS, erf saturation fraction, and weight norms.

## Key Results
- Derf+Muon validation loss gap to RMSNorm+Muon reached +0.97 nats, triple the Derf+AdamW gap (+0.31 nats).
- Derf+Muon induced severe erf saturation: 83% at layer 15 vs. 10% with AdamW; weight norms were 2.2× larger under Muon.
- DyT showed no negative interaction with Muon (gap ~+0.22 nats) due to tanh’s wider useful regime and adaptive α.
- Two fixes recovered most of the gap: Derf α=0.3 (instead of 0.5) recovered ~80%; EMA-blend (λ=0.9) recovered ~84%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Muon-Driven Weight Growth → erf Saturation
Claim: Muon’s faster weight growth (vs. AdamW) pushes Derf’s pre-activation magnitudes into the steep erf saturation zone (|αx|>3), causing lossy compression.  
Mechanism: Muon’s orthogonalization updates produce larger spectral norm growth than AdamW’s √v_t damping. Without a reduction-based rescaling (like RMSNorm), activation magnitudes inherited from these weights grow unchecked. When |αx| exceeds ~3, erf(αx) collapses to ±1, discarding information.  
Core assumption: Muon’s update rule leads to uncalibrated weight growth in the absence of a normalizer that tracks and rescales activation magnitude.  
Evidence anchors:  
  - [abstract] “erf reaches 99% output at |αx|=1.82… Muon grows weights ∼2× faster… pushing Derf into a regime where distinct inputs collapse to identical±1 outputs”  
  - [section 5.2] “Muon produces weight norms of∼156 vs. AdamW’s∼70 (2.2×)… erf input std from 1.8 to 11.8… saturation from 10% to 83%”  
Break condition: erf saturation fraction at a given layer exceeds ~50% (observed threshold where gap widens dramatically; see Figure 7, L15 ~80% saturation correlates with ~1 nats gap).

### Mechanism 2: Scale Blindness Beyond Saturation
Claim: erf discards activation magnitude information even when unsaturated, a distinct failure mode from saturation alone.  
Mechanism: erf is bounded and non-invertible; it removes absolute scale information. Norms of post-activation vectors are not preserved relative to inputs, unlike RMSNorm’s lossless rescaling. This “scale blindness” harms optimization even if inputs stay in the linear-ish regime.  
Core assumption: Optimizer effectiveness depends on the normalizer preserving relative magnitude information for gradient-based learning.  
Evidence anchors:  
  - [abstract] “asinh pre-compression, which eliminates saturation without restoring scale, recovers∼49%”  
  - [section 5.5] “asinh eliminates saturation but not scale information… 35 pp gap between asinh and EMA-blend… consistent with scale information being a distinct factor”  
Break condition: When using bounded squashing normalizers (erf, tanh) with optimizers that do not naturally regulate activation scale (Muon), performance degrades unless scale information is explicitly preserved (e.g., via EMA running estimates).

### Mechanism 3: DyT’s Implicit Resilience via Wider Saturation Geometry and Adaptive α
Claim: DyT avoids the Derf-Muon interaction because its tanh nonlinearity has a wider useful regime and its α parameter adapts more aggressively under Muon.  
Mechanism: tanh reaches 99% output at |x|=2.65 vs. erf’s 1.82 (46% wider margin), delaying saturation onset. Additionally, DyT’s learnable α per sublayer decreases more under Muon (42% drop vs. 28% for Derf), effectively reducing the nonlinearity’s gain and keeping inputs in a less saturated regime.  
Core assumption: The interaction is Derf-specific due to erf’s specific geometric properties; not all bounded normalizers fail with Muon.  
Evidence anchors:  
  - [abstract] “DyT shows no such penalty… The coupling is Derf-specific”  
  - [section 4.2] “tanh’s 99% output occurs at |x|=2.65 vs. 1.82 for erf (46% wider useful regime), and DyT’s α self-corrects more aggressively… because tanh retains usable gradients at lower saturation”  
Break condition: If a normalizer’s saturation threshold is too narrow (erf) or its scale parameters do not adapt downward sufficiently under fast weight growth, the coupling manifests.

## Foundational Learning
- **Lossless vs. Lossy Normalization**: RMSNorm rescales activations preserving relative magnitudes; Derf’s erf squashes, discarding scale. Why needed: explains robustness differences. Quick check: “If an activation vector has components [1, 2] and another has [10, 20], how would RMSNorm and Derf (with default α) transform these two vectors differently in terms of their pairwise ratios?”
- **Optimizer Update Spectrum and Weight Growth**: Muon’s orthogonal updates cause faster spectral norm growth vs. AdamW’s adaptive damping. Why needed: activation scale in normalization-free layers depends directly on weight norms. Quick check: “Why might an optimizer that applies updates with a fixed spectral norm (Muon) cause faster growth in weight matrix norms compared to AdamW, especially in early training?”
- **Saturation Regimes and Useful Operating Zones**: erf saturates quickly (|αx|>~1) before steep saturation; tanh’s linear region is wider. Why needed: setting α and diagnosing failures requires knowing these thresholds. Quick check: “For erf with α=0.5, what absolute input magnitude corresponds to 90% of the output range? How does that compare to tanh with α=0.5?”

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Normalizer (RMSNorm/Derf/DyT) -> Optimizer (AdamW/Muon) -> Forward/backward pass linking weight updates to activation magnitudes and saturation.  
Critical path: Monitor **per-layer erf saturation fraction** and **activation RMS** relative to normalizer parameters (especially α). A widening loss gap to a robust baseline (RMSNorm+Muon) without crashes is the primary symptom. The saturated fraction in deep layers (L12+) is the leading indicator.  
Design tradeoffs:  
- Derf: Reduction-free, fastest kernels under TP, but α must be tuned for the optimizer; default α=0.5 only safe with AdamW-like damping.  
- DyT: Reduction-free and robust to Muon due to wider saturation and adaptive α, but still incurs a small gap vs RMSNorm.  
- RMSNorm: Robust but expensive under tensor parallelism due to allreduces.  
- EMA-blend: Adds a scalar running variance per layer (negligible memory) to Derf, regains ~84% of Muon performance while retaining most speedup.  
Failure signatures:  
- Progressive loss gap widening from step ~200 onward under Muon, with no NaNs.  
- Deep layer (L12+) erf saturation >70%.  
- Validation loss gap (Derf+Muon vs RMSNorm+Muon) >0.5 nats after 500 steps.  
- Ineffective α gradients (∇_α ≈ 0) indicating saturation zone.  
First 3 experiments:  
1. **Factorial validation**: Run {RMSNorm, Derf, DyT} × {AdamW, Muon} for at least 300–500 steps at target scale (≥1B). Plot loss gap trajectories. A diverging gap under Muon only signals Derf-specific coupling.  
2. **Diagnostic probes**: At mid-training (step 300–400), log per-layer erf saturation and activation std. If Derf+Muon shows L15 saturation >60% and input std >5, the mechanism is active.  
3. **Remediation sweep**: Test α=0.3 for Derf+Muon and EMA-blend (λ=0.9). Both should recover ≥80% of the gap (target: <0.2 nats residual). If not, investigate whether running estimates (ˆσ) are converging properly or if α is still too high.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Does the Derf-Muon coupling persist at larger model scales (7B+) and longer training schedules approaching Chinchilla-optimal token counts (~20K steps)? Basis: Limitation: "We test at 1B parameters... Whether the interaction persists at 7B+ and longer schedules remains open."
- **Open Question 2**: Will a fixed reduced α=0.3 continue protecting Derf from saturation over Chinchilla-optimal training, or will activation magnitudes eventually exceed the near-linear regime, necessitating adaptive mechanisms like EMA-blend? Basis: Limitation: "Fixed α may not scale... at Chinchilla-optimal ∼20K steps, continued weight growth may push residuals beyond the current regime."
- **Open Question 3**: Do other Muon variants (Moonlight, SOAP, MuonClip) exhibit similar interactions with Derf, or do their stability modifications mitigate the coupling? Basis: Limitation: "Other architectures, Muon variants, and cross-node TP are untested."
- **Open Question 4**: Does the coupling generalize beyond Llama-style transformers to other architectures (e.g., GPT-style, state-space models), or is it architecture-specific? Basis: Limitation: "LlamaForCausalLM and standard Muon only. Other architectures... untested."
- **Open Question 5**: Can further tuning of the EMA momentum parameter *m* improve upon the λ=0.9 recovery (~84%), or is current performance near-optimal? Basis: Limitation: "We sweep λ∈{0.5,0.7,0.9,1.0} but fix *m*=0.5; sweeping momentum may further improve results."

## Limitations
- Unknown exact resource requirements and wall-clock time for the 1000-step 1B runs on the specified hardware (H200 SXM); per-run time and memory footprint could vary with software stack versions.
- Precise details of the "EMA-blend" implementation beyond the formula (lambda=0.9, smoothing factor m=0.5) are not explicitly given; update equation and initialization must be inferred or reconstructed.
- Sensitivity to dataset preprocessing, tokenization, or weight initialization beyond described parameters remains untested; the paper uses standard Llama 3.2 tokenizer and FineWeb-Edu but does not specify train/validation split details.

## Confidence
- Coupling claim (large Derf-Muon degradation): **High** (large effect, multi-seed).
- Mechanism (saturation/scale blindness as cause): **Medium** (correlational, not fully causal).
- Fixes (α reduction, EMA-blend recovery): **Medium** (partial recovery, EMA-blend underspecified).

## Next Checks
1. Scale/architecture extension: Test 3B/7B models and vanilla Transformer to see if coupling persists at larger scales and different architectures.
2. EMA-blend audit: Implement exact version with full hyperparameter sweep (λ, m) and measure recovery percentage; verify if >84% gap closure is possible.
3. Optimizer isolation: Substitute Muon with variants (Moonlight, SOAP, MuonClip) or other optimizers (Lion, Sophia) in the factorial to determine Muon-specificity.
