# Efficient Dialect-Aware Modeling and Conditioning for Low-Resource Taiwanese Hakka Speech Processing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.22522
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22522
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Proposed RNN-T framework achieves 57.00% relative CER reduction on Hanzi and 40.41% relative SER reduction on Pinyin over single-task baselines.

## Executive Summary
Taiwanese Hakka ASR faces dual challenges: high dialectal variability (Sixian, Hailu, NanSixian) and two writing systems (Hanzi, Pinyin), which cause models to conflate dialectal style with linguistic content. This paper introduces an RNN-T framework with dialect-aware modeling—an Auxiliary Dialect Classifier (ADC) and Dialect Information Integration (DII)—to disentangle style from content, and joint Hanzi/Pinyin modeling where cross-script tasks mutually regularize the shared encoder. Token-Interleaved Conditioning (TIC) further stabilizes dialect-conditional generation. The best model reduces character error rate (CER) by 57.00% and syllable error rate (SER) by 40.41% relative to single-task baselines, with only a modest parameter increase (71.73M to 78.71M) and maintained inference speed.

## Method Summary
The method uses a shared Zipformer encoder feeding two separate RNN-T decoders—one for Hanzi characters, one for Pinyin syllables. Dialect-aware components include ADC, which adds an auxiliary classification loss to encourage the encoder to extract dialect-discriminative features, and DII, which fuses a learned dialect embedding into the encoder output. Training combines the two ASR losses with the ADC loss. For dialect conditioning, TIC interleaves a dialect ID token after every linguistic token in the target sequence, providing dense temporal context to the decoder. This design aims to separate dialectal style from core content while leveraging orthographic synergy, all within a parameter-efficient setup using stateless RNN-T-SLP prediction networks.

## Key Results
- 57.00% relative CER reduction on Hanzi over single-task baseline.
- 40.41% relative SER reduction on Pinyin over single-task baseline.
- Modest parameter increase from 71.73M to 78.71M, with maintained inference speed.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Dialect-Aware Disentanglement
Dialect-aware modeling prevents the shared encoder from learning entangled representations where dialect-specific features interfere with invariant content features. The Auxiliary Dialect Classifier (ADC) provides a supervision signal via an auxiliary classification loss, encouraging the encoder to extract dialect-discriminative features. Dialect Information Integration (DII) then explicitly fuses a learned dialect embedding into the encoder output, making the acoustic representation dialect-aware. Evidence: Models with ADC/DII show consistent CER/SER reductions over a multi-task baseline (e.g., DII: 5.21% CER vs. MTL's 5.57% in Table 5). Break condition: If ADC's dialect classification accuracy degrades to near random chance (~33% for three dialects), the regularization signal is lost and disentanglement fails.

### Mechanism 2: Multi-Orthography Mutual Regularization
Jointly modeling Hanzi and Pinyin tasks via a shared encoder creates a mutual regularization effect. The phonetically precise Pinyin objective forces the encoder to maintain acoustically accurate representations, while the semantically rich Hanzi objective requires capturing higher-level linguistic context. These complementary gradients update the shared encoder toward a more robust representation than either task alone. Evidence: The joint MTLHP model reduces Hanzi CER by 8.23% relative to the single-task Hanzi baseline (5.57% vs. 6.07% in Table 5). Break condition: If the two tasks have conflicting gradient signals (e.g., due to very low symbol set overlap), joint training could degrade performance on one or both tasks.

### Mechanism 3: Token-Interleaved Conditioning (TIC)
TIC interleaves a dialect ID token with every linguistic output token, providing dense, temporal dialect context to the RNN-T decoder. This forces the autoregressive prediction network to condition each linguistic token on the most recent dialect cue, mitigating the "forgetting" problem that plagues sparse conditioning (prefix/suffix). Evidence: TIC dramatically outperforms other strategies (2.71% CER vs. 5.41% for prefix and 10.54% for suffix in Table 5) and degrades more gracefully under incorrect dialect cues (Figure 2). Break condition: If the decoder learns to ignore the dialect tokens (e.g., due to weak training signal or embedding collapse), TIC offers no benefit and may increase sequence length detrimentally.

## Architecture Onboarding
Input (80-dim log-Mel filterbank) -> Shared Encoder (6-block Zipformer, D=768) -> Dialect-Aware Module [ADC: attention pooling -> linear -> dialect logits; DII: dialect embedding -> concatenated with encoder output] -> Dual Decoder Branches (per task: Prediction Network RNN-T-SLP -> Joint Network -> task-specific token logits) -> Output (Hanzi/Pinyin sequences).

Critical path: Acoustic Features -> Shared Encoder -> Dialect Fusion (via DII + ADC prediction) -> Task-specific Joint Network (conditioned on interleaved dialect token from TIC) -> Token Prediction. The critical integrity point is dialect information flow: ADC must extract a reliable signal for DII's embedding at inference, and TIC-interleaved dialect tokens must be correctly placed in the target sequence for the decoder to consume.

Design tradeoffs: ADC vs. ground-truth dialect—ADC enables zero-dialect-label inference but risks error propagation; using ground-truth dialect at test time is cheating but shows upper-bound. TIC increases target sequence length by ~50%, slowing convergence marginally but improving stability; the benefit must outweigh cost. Parameter efficiency hinges on RNN-T-SLP prediction networks; full LSTMs would blow up parameters.

Failure signatures: 1) ADC accuracy near random (~33%)—dialect-aware benefits lost. 2) MTL performance worse than single-task—negative transfer. 3) TIC misalignment—model fails to predict interleaved dialect tokens or shows no improvement over sparse conditioning. 4) Dialect overfitting—good on in-dialect test but catastrophic on sparse dialects.

First 3 experiments: 1) Establish mixed-dialect baseline: Train single-task Hanzi and Pinyin on mixed training data; confirm mixed vastly outperforms any single-dialect model. 2) Validate MTL synergy: Train MTLHP (shared encoder, separate decoders) without dialect modules; verify improvement over both single-task baselines. 3) Ablate dialect components: Starting from MTLHP, add ADC, then DII (with GT dialect), then TIC; monitor dev CER/SER to replicate additive gains. Also run TIC stress test (vary dialect cue correctness) to verify robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How effectively can large-scale pretrained speech or language models (e.g., wav2vec 2.0, Whisper, Parakeet-TDT) be integrated into the proposed multi-task RNN-T framework to mitigate data scarcity in low-resource Taiwanese Hakka ASR?
2. To what extent do the proposed dialect-aware and dialect-conditioning strategies (ADC, DII, TIC) remain effective when scaling beyond the three evaluated dialects (Sixian, NanSixian, Hailu) to other Taiwanese Hakka variants (e.g., Dapu, Raoping, Zhaoan) that may exhibit greater phonological and lexical divergence?
3. What is the precise mechanistic role of the multi-orthography (Hanzi and Pinyin) synergy in improving shared encoder representations—does the phonetic precision of Pinyin primarily regularize acoustic embeddings, while the semantic richness of Hanzi primarily regularizes contextual linguistic representations, or is the benefit more interactive?
4. Why does Token-Interleaved Conditioning (TIC) substantially outperform other dialect conditioning strategies (PSC, PRSC), and what specific properties of the RNN-T decoder's alignment and prediction dynamics make dense, token-level dialect context more effective than sequence-level prefixes or suffixes?

## Limitations
- No reporting of ADC classification accuracy, a critical signal for dialect-aware modeling.
- Mutual regularization hypothesis lacks direct evidence (e.g., representation analysis, gradient conflict).
- Evaluation restricted to three dialects; generalizability to other Hakka variants unknown.
- Key implementation details unspecified (acoustic front-end, joint network structure, decoding algorithm).

## Confidence
- Dialect-aware disentanglement: Medium (supported by Table 5 gains but no ADC accuracy or representation probes).
- Joint modeling regularization: Medium (MTL improvement shown but could stem from increased capacity; no gradient alignment analysis).
- TIC effectiveness: High (dramatic gains over other methods and robust in stress tests).

## Next Checks
