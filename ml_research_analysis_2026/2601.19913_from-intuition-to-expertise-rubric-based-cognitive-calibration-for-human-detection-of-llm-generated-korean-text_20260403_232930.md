# From Intuition to Expertise: Rubric-Based Cognitive Calibration for Human Detection of LLM-Generated Korean Text

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.19913  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19913  
- **Reference count:** 27  
- **Primary result:** Rubric-based cognitive calibration boosted human detection accuracy from 60% (intuition) to 100% on a held-out elementary Korean essay subset, with inter-annotator agreement rising from -0.09 to 0.82.

## Executive Summary
Distinguishing human-written Korean text from LLM-generated content is challenging even for linguistically trained readers, who tend to over-trust surface fluency. This study introduces LREAD, a rubric grounded in national Korean writing standards and tailored to micro-level linguistic artifacts (e.g., punctuation optionality, spacing, register shifts). In a three-phase blind longitudinal study with three Korean linguistics majors, intuition-only detection (Phase 1) achieved 60% majority-vote accuracy. After calibrating annotators with criterion-scoring and explicit justifications (Phase 2), accuracy rose to 90% and Fleiss’ κ agreement from -0.09 to 0.82. On a limited held-out subset (Phase 3), internalized application yielded 100% accuracy. Calibration primarily reduced false negatives on AI essays (12→2) without increasing false positives. Compared to zero-shot LLM judges (96.67% accuracy), calibrated humans relied on language-specific micro-diagnostics (spacing regularity, translationese) rather than coarse discourse priors. The rubric scaffolding thus made human attribution reasoning explicit and auditable, achieving expert-level mastery in a controlled Korean argumentative-essay setting.

## Method Summary
The study recruited three advanced Korean Language & Literature majors with minimal prior LLM exposure. Data comprised 90 essays from the KatFish benchmark: human student writings and outputs from four LLMs (GPT-4o, Solar-10.7B, Qwen2-72B, Llama3.1-70B), stratified by school level (elementary/middle/high). Disjoint essay sets were used across three phases: Phase 1 (intuition-only binary attribution), Phase 2 (criterion-anchored scoring with the LREAD rubric and justifications), and Phase 3 (internalized binary attribution on a held-out elementary subset). The LREAD rubric (100 points across Content, Organization, Expression) was constructed collaboratively after Phase 1, mapping observed heuristics to national Korean writing standards and micro-artifacts. Primary metrics included majority-vote accuracy, inclusive Fleiss’ κ, false-negative rates, and unanimity counts.

## Key Results
- Majority-vote accuracy: Phase 1 = 60% → Phase 2 = 90% → Phase 3 = 100% (10 essays, Clopper–Pearson CI [0.692, 1.000]).
- Fleiss’ κ agreement: -0.09 → 0.82, resolving directional disagreement.
- False negatives on AI essays dropped from 12 to 2; false positives nearly unchanged (0→1).
- Zero-shot LLM judges achieved 96.67% accuracy, but calibrated humans relied on Korean-specific micro-patterns (spacing regularity, translationese) rather than broad fluency cues.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Explicit rubric scaffolding converts diffuse linguistic intuition into stable, auditable diagnostic criteria.
The LREAD rubric externalizes previously implicit heuristics (e.g., “overly regular spacing”) into concrete, measurable criteria derived from national Korean writing standards and observed failure modes. Annotators evaluate discrete linguistic strata (lexical, orthographic, register) rather than holistic “fluency” impressions, which are misleading for LLM outputs.  
*Core assumption:* Human readers possess relevant linguistic knowledge but lack a structured application procedure.  
*Evidence anchors:* Rubric grounded in standards; post-Phase 1 calibration; justifications show micro-level focus.  
*Break condition:* If micro-diagnostics fail to correlate with actual generator artifacts in new samples (e.g., newer models, different genres), the mechanism collapses.

### Mechanism 2: Criterion-anchored scoring primarily corrects the “fluency trap” by suppressing false negatives on AI text.
The rubric redirects attention from surface-level correctness (LLM strength) to systematic deviations from human-like variation (e.g., unnatural register stability, translationese). This targeted reorientation reduces the tendency to mistake fluent AI output for human writing while maintaining low false-positive rates on human essays.  
*Core assumption:* The primary intuition-only failure is over-trusting surface well-formedness, not an inability to recognize human-like errors.  
*Evidence anchors:* False negatives on AI essays dropped from 12 to 2 with negligible change in false positives; confusion matrices (Figure 2).  
*Break condition:* If rubric-guided scoring increases false positives significantly, it indicates over-correction and failure of targeted reorientation.

### Mechanism 3: Shared rubric criteria align annotators’ interpretive boundaries, moving from directional disagreement to consensus.
The rubric provides a common, explicit decision framework, reducing “directional ambiguity” by standardizing how cues are weighted and translated into binary decisions. Agreement (Fleiss’ κ) rises as annotators converge on a shared mapping from linguistic features to attribution.  
*Core assumption:* Phase 1 disagreement stems from inconsistent cue interpretation, not completely different cue sets.  
*Evidence anchors:* κ improvement from -0.09 to 0.82; qualitative shift from directional opposition to alignment; Table 8 shows pair-specific κ increases.  
*Break condition:* If agreement does not improve (or worsens) after rubric introduction, the criteria are likely ambiguous or annotator mental models irreconcilable.

## Foundational Learning
### Korean Orthographic and Spacing Conventions (어문 규범, 띄어쓰기)
**Why needed here:** LREAD’s diagnostic cues (e.g., spacing regularity, optional comma habits) require recognizing deviations from standard Korean orthography that LLMs mishandle (over-regularization in optional contexts).  
**Quick check:** Given “그는 빨리 달렸다.”, would you insert a space after “그는”? Is “나는그책을읽었다” correct? What makes spacing irregular to a native speaker?

### Translationese and Cross-Linguistic Transfer Patterns
**Why needed:** Calibrated humans detect “translationese” micro-patterns where LLM outputs are influenced by English syntax/lexicon (e.g., heavy nominalization, calqued phrases).  
**Quick check:** Between “문제를 해결하다” and “문제해결을 하다”, which sounds more natural in Korean? Why might an LLM produce the less natural option?

### Rubric-Based Scoring and Anchored Judgments
**Why needed:** The mechanism depends on mapping qualitative observations to quantitative, anchored criteria (e.g., error thresholds).  
**Quick check:** If an essay has 100 eojeols and 2 instances of inaccurate lexical use, what score does the “Lexical Accuracy” criterion (1% threshold) yield? What if the 2 errors are of the same type (e.g., repeated particle misuse)?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Data Pipeline → Calibration Engine → Scoring & Audit Module → Analysis Module

### Critical Path
1. Recruit linguistically trained annotators with minimal LLM exposure.  
2. Run Phase 1 blind task, collect initial heuristics.  
3. Disclose labels, facilitate collaborative rubric design mapping heuristics to national standards.  
4. Run Phase 2 with rubric-mandated justifications.  
5. Disclose labels again, run Phase 3 on held-out elementary subset without rubric.  
6. Elicit post-hoc cue inventories and compare to zero-shot LLM judge signatures.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Internal vs. External Validity:** Small homogeneous pool isolates calibration effects but limits generalizability.  
- **Rubric Specificity:** LREAD tuned to Korean argumentative essays; other genres require re-validation.  
- **Phase 3 Power:** 10-item subset yields perfect majority vote but wide CI; proof-of-concept only.  
- **Post-hoc Rubric Design:** Rubric built after Phase 1 risks overfitting; mitigated by disjoint sets but needs external validation.

### Failure Signatures
- Persistent Fluency Trap: No reduction in false negatives from Phase 1 to Phase 2.  
- Rubric Misalignment: Low/negative Fleiss’ κ in Phase 2 despite rubric use.  
- Domain Collapse: High accuracy on argumentative essays but failure on other genres.  
- Temporal Decay: Accuracy drops on essays from newer LLM versions.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Replication of Fluency Trap:** Run Phase 1 protocol on a new set of 30 Korean argumentative essays; expect ~60% majority-vote accuracy and negative Fleiss’ κ.  
2. **Rubric-Only Intervention:** Provide the finalized LREAD rubric to annotators without Phase 1 gold-label disclosure and collaborative design; compare accuracy/agreement to full calibrated protocol.  
3. **Cross-Domain Stress Test:** Apply internalized annotators to a held-out set of 15 Korean narrative essays (5 human, 10 AI from same models); expect accuracy drop if mechanisms are genre-bound.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **To what extent does rubric-based cognitive calibration improve detection accuracy for non-linguistically trained annotators and across languages with different orthographic systems?**  
   *Basis:* Study used only three Korean linguistics majors; limitations state future work should expand to diverse annotator pools and cross-linguistic populations.  
   *Why unresolved:* Gains may depend on homogeneous expertise; non-experts might not internalize micro-diagnostics; languages without spacing conventions may require different cue families.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Replicate the three-phase protocol with diverse annotator backgrounds and typologically varied languages, measuring phase-wise accuracy and agreement trajectories.

2. **Can the LREAD rubric and calibration framework generalize to non-argumentative genres without substantial re-engineering?**  
   *Basis:* Evaluation focused exclusively on argumentative essays; limitations note unknown generalization to genres with distinct stylistic conventions.  
   *Why unresolved:* Other genres rely on different micro-artifacts (e.g., dialogue punctuation in narratives, jargon in technical docs).  
   *Resolving evidence:* Adapt LREAD’s micro-diagnostics for target genres, run the calibration protocol on those corpora, and compare accuracy gains against the argumentative-essay baseline.

3. **Does the post-hoc construction of LREAD cause overfitting to the specific benchmark and models used?**  
   *Basis:* Rubric derived after Phase 1; limitations raise potential overfitting concerns.  
   *Why unresolved:* Rubric may capture transient artifacts of KatFish or specific models; newer models might smooth out these cues.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Apply the original rubric (unmodified) to a new corpus of Korean essays from different LLMs or human sources, and evaluate whether calibrated annotators maintain high accuracy and agreement.

4. **Which components of the three-phase calibration protocol are necessary for the observed improvement?**  
   *Basis:* The protocol bundles multiple interventions; limitations call for ablation studies.  
   *Why unresolved:* The bundled design cannot isolate the causal contribution of each element; simpler interventions might yield comparable results.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Conduct an ablation study with separate groups: (a) feedback only, (b) rubric only, (c) full protocol, (d) control (no intervention); compare phase-wise accuracy and agreement to identify the minimal effective combination.

## Limitations
- **Small Phase 3 sample:** Only 10 essays (95% CI [0.692, 1.000]); result statistically fragile and preliminary.  
- **Annotator homogeneity:** All three annotators were Korean linguistics majors; limits generalizability to non-experts or other languages.  
- **Genre specificity & overfitting risk:** Rubric tuned to argumentative essays and derived post-hoc from Phase 1; may not generalize to other genres or newer LLM artifacts.

## Confidence
- Rubric scaffolding stabilizes intuition: **Medium**  
- Fluency trap correction: **High**  
- Annotator alignment via shared criteria: **High**  
- Overall “expert-level mastery” claim: **Low-Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Large-scale replication:** Run the three-phase protocol on ≥100 new balanced essays to obtain robust confidence intervals for final accuracy and Fleiss’ κ.  
2. **Rubric-only vs. full process ablation:** Provide the finalized LREAD rubric to new annotators without Phase 1 feedback/collaborative design; compare calibration gains to the full protocol to isolate the rubric’s standalone effect.  
3. **Cross-domain stress test:** Apply calibrated annotators to a held-out set of 15 Korean narrative essays (same generator mix) to test generalization beyond the trained argumentative genre.
