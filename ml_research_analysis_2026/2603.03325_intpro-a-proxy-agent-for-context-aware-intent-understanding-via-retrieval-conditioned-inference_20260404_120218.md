# IntPro: A Proxy Agent for Context-Aware Intent Understanding via Retrieval-conditioned Inference

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03325
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03325
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** IntPro achieves state-of-the‑art performance on three datasets; with Qwen3‑4B on MIntRec2.0 it reaches 67.37% accuracy, 56.68 Macro‑F1, and 66.59 Weighted‑F1.

## Executive Summary
IntPro is a proxy agent designed for context-aware intent understanding that adaptively retrieves relevant historical intent patterns from a per-user library. It represents intents via natural language explanations that abstract how contextual signals connect to user intentions. The agent learns when to retrieve and how to reason over retrieved evidence through supervised fine‑tuning on retrieval‑conditioned trajectories provided by a teacher model, followed by multi‑turn Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with tool‑aware rewards that encourage retrieval on ambiguous contexts and direct inference on clear ones. Evaluated on three datasets (Highlight‑Intent, MIntRec2.0, Weibo Post‑Sync), IntPro consistently outperforms prior work, including large cloud LLMs and discriminative models. Notably, it improves as user history accumulates (+5.5 percentage points) and generalizes across domains, demonstrating the effectiveness of personalized, retrieval‑augmented intent understanding.

## Method Summary
IntPro constructs a per-user intent history library containing personalized explanations for past intents, generated by a teacher model. During inference, a base policy model (e.g., Qwen3‑4B) decides whether to call a retrieval tool that fetches top‑k relevant explanations from the library based on embedding similarity with the current context and candidate intents. The retrieved examples are incorporated into a multi‑turn dialogue, and the model produces a final intent label with an explanation. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) Supervised fine‑tuning on trajectories produced by the teacher, where the model learns to interleave tool calls and reasoning; (2) Reinforcement learning via GRPO, using a reward that combines format correctness, intent accuracy, and a tool‑aware component that adaptively rewards retrieval on hard contexts and direct answers on easy contexts. Group‑relative advantages computed from multiple rollouts stabilize learning.

## Key Results
- State‑of‑the‑art intent understanding accuracy on MIntRec2.0 (67.37% acc, 56.68 Macro‑F1, 66.59 Weighted‑F1 with Qwen3‑4B), surpassing both cloud LLMs and discriminative baselines.
- Consistent gains across three datasets, with +5.5 percentage points improvement as user‑specific history accumulates.
- Strong cross‑domain generalization, maintaining high performance when applying the model to new domains with minimal adaptation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

#### Mechanism 1: Intent explanations as retrieval representations
- **Claim:** Representing intents via natural language explanations improves personalized pattern matching compared to raw context or generic explanations.
- **Mechanism:** The teacher generates explanations that abstract how contextual signals connect to the expressed intent. These are stored in a per‑user library and retrieved via semantic similarity, providing a compact, discriminative key for matching past patterns.
- **Evidence:** Personalized explanations achieve higher intra‑class similarity (0.8128) and lower inter‑class similarity (0.7790) than raw context (0.6274/0.5893) and yield better retrieval metrics (User LOO Acc 0.3631, Global R@1 0.5316) (Table 2).
- **Break condition:** If explanations lack discriminability (high inter‑class similarity), retrieval will recall low‑quality patterns and fail to improve intent understanding.

#### Mechanism 2: Supervised fine‑tuning on retrieval‑conditioned trajectories
- **Claim:** SFT on trajectories that demonstrate both direct inference and retrieval teaches the model when and how to use retrieval.
- **Mechanism:** A teacher model generates multi‑turn dialogues that include tool calls with candidate intents and final answers, following Algorithm 1. The student model is fine‑tuned on these trajectories, initializing it with retrieval‑conditioned behavior.
- **Evidence:** Retrieval‑conditioned SFT yields lower single‑shot accuracy but higher Pass@4 and a better generalization gap than answer‑only SFT (Figure 5), indicating learning of robust reasoning patterns rather than memorization.
- **Break condition:** If teacher trajectories are biased or noisy, the initial policy may inherit poor adaptive behavior, hindering subsequent RL.

#### Mechanism 3: Multi‑turn GRPO with tool‑aware reward functions
- **Claim:** Tool‑aware rewards based on context difficulty enable the model to learn an adaptive retrieval strategy.
- **Mechanism:** During GRPO, each rollout group produces trajectories. Group accuracy α_G indicates difficulty: for easy contexts (α_G≥0.5) the reward incentivizes correct direct answers (r_d) and penalizes failed retrieval (r_f); for hard contexts (α_G<0.5) it rewards successful retrieval (r_s) and penalizes incorrect direct answers (r_e). Advantages are computed group‑relatively.
- **Evidence:** Ablations show that removing the successful retrieval reward on hard contexts harms performance the most (Sec 6.4.1). Figure 8 demonstrates that tool retrieval miss count declines while direct answer accuracy improves during GRPO, confirming adaptive learning.
- **Break condition:** If group accuracy does not correlate with true difficulty (e.g., due to noise), the reward may misguide; improper reward magnitudes may cause extreme policies (always/never retrieve).

## Foundational Learning

### Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG)
- **Why needed:** IntPro extends RAG by using retrieval to inform intent inference rather than only augmenting response generation. Understanding embedding‑based retrieval and query formulation is essential.
- **Quick check:** "How does IntPro’s retrieval query differ from standard RAG queries, and why is the retrieved content used for decision‑making instead of direct concatenation?"

### Policy Gradient Methods (PPO/GRPO)
- **Why needed:** The GRPO training phase optimizes behavior via rewards. Engineers must grasp how advantages are estimated, why group‑relative baselines reduce variance, and how clipping/KL penalties stabilize updates.
- **Quick check:** "What is the main advantage of GRPO over PPO for training tool‑augmented agents, and how does the group‑relative advantage reduce variance without a learned value function?"

### Intent Representation and Explanation Generation
- **Why needed:** The core of IntPro is generating high‑quality intent explanations that serve both as retrieval keys and interpretable outputs. The structure (label + explanation) and the distinction between generic and personalized explanations are crucial.
- **Quick check:** "What distinguishes a personalized intent explanation from a generic one, and why do personalized explanations yield better retrieval discriminability?"

## Architecture Onboarding

#### Component Map
Intent History Library -> Retrieval Module -> Policy Model  
(Trajectory Generator and GRPO Trainer are training‑time components.)

#### Critical Path
**Inference:**
1. Given context C and user u, the policy generates output.
2. If it emits a tool call `retrieve_intent_context(user, intents)`, the retrieval module fetches top‑k entries (u, ℓ, exp) from the library where ℓ is among the candidate intents.
3. The tool response is appended; the policy continues and eventually outputs `<answer>` with `<intent_explanation>` and the final intent label.
4. The label may be passed to a downstream LLM for further actions.

**Training:**
- **SFT:** Teacher generates retrieval‑conditioned trajectories (Algorithm 1). Filter correct ones; fine‑tune the base model on multi‑turn dialogues with assistant‑only loss masking.
- **GRPO:** For each prompt, sample G rollouts from the current policy. Compute rewards (format + accuracy + tool‑aware). Derive group‑relative advantages, update policy with clipped surrogate loss and KL penalty (β).

#### Design Tradeoffs
- **Retrieval latency vs. accuracy:** Larger k improves coverage but adds latency; k=3 was optimal.
- **Explanation specificity:** Personalized explanations boost discriminability but risk overfitting to limited history; generic ones generalize but lose user adaptation.
- **Reward shaping:** Tool‑aware rewards must be secondary to accuracy; magnitudes too large may incentivize gaming, too small may not guide behavior.
- **SFT initialization:** Retrieval‑conditioned SFT yields lower single‑shot accuracy but better reasoning patterns for RL to refine; answer‑only SFT overfits and generalizes poorly.

#### Failure Signatures
- **Tool call rate extremes:** Near 0% (never retrieves) or 100% (always retrieves) indicates reward mis‑specification or insufficient SFT initialization.
- **Low retrieval precision:** Top‑k matches often irrelevant; suggests poor explanation quality or embedding mismatch.
- **High rollout variance:** Unstable policy updates; may require smaller KL penalty or larger group size.
- **Cold‑start for new users:** No history in library; model may default to direct inference, which could be error‑prone without personalization.

#### First 3 Experiments
1. **Discriminability validation:** Replicate the analysis of Table 2 on your data to confirm that personalized intent explanations yield higher intra‑class similarity and lower inter‑class similarity than raw context.
2. **Tool‑aware reward ablation:** Train with Naive GRPO (no R_tool) and compare performance; expect a drop in accuracy and adaptive retrieval behavior.
3. **Retrieval strategy test:** Evaluate three settings—No Retrieval, Forced Retrieval, Self‑decided (IntPro)—to ensure the model learns to use retrieval only when beneficial (self‑decided should outperform both extremes).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Open‑vocabulary intent generation:** The current approach assumes a closed set of intent labels. Extending to open‑vocabulary generation (where intents can be novel or user‑defined) remains future work.
2. **Cold‑start adaptation:** For users with no interaction history, the agent lacks personalized patterns to retrieve. Few‑shot adaptation mechanisms (e.g., using population priors or meta‑learning) are needed to bootstrap performance.
3. **Robustness to noisy user identities:** Real‑world deployments may have shared devices or account sharing, leading to mixed histories. The robustness of IntPro to such identification noise is unexplored.
4. **Human interpretability of explanations and downstream impact:** It is unclear whether the generated intent explanations align with human judgments of quality or actually improve the final response from an LLM. Human evaluation and A/B testing are required.

## Limitations
- **Teacher model dependency:** The quality of SFT trajectories depends on access to Qwen3‑30B‑A3B; substitutions may degrade explanation discriminability and trajectory diversity.
- **GRPO hyperparameter ambiguity:** Exact RL training settings (e.g., rollout length, group size, optimizer) are not fully disclosed, making stable reproduction challenging.
- **Cross‑domain evaluation protocol:** The method for constructing per‑user libraries when testing on new domains is unspecified, threatening the validity of claimed generalization.

## Confidence

- Mechanism 2 (SFT on retrieval‑conditioned trajectories): **Medium**
- Mechanism 3 (tool‑aware GRPO): **Medium**
