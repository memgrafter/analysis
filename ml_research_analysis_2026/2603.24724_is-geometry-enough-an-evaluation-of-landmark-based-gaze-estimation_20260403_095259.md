# Is Geometry Enough? An Evaluation of Landmark-Based Gaze Estimation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.24724
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24724
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Landmark-based gaze estimation achieves competitive cross-domain performance compared to appearance-based CNNs, demonstrating that sparse geometric features are sufficient for robust gaze estimation with better generalization.

## Executive Summary
The paper evaluates whether sparse geometric features from facial landmarks can serve as a lightweight, interpretable alternative to appearance-based convolutional neural networks for gaze estimation. Using three datasets (Gaze360, ETH-XGaze, GazeGene), they implement a standardized pipeline: detect 20 facial landmarks with MediaPipe, estimate head pose via SolvePnP, and normalize coordinates and gaze vectors into a virtual camera space. Lightweight models (XGBoost, holistic MLP, siamese MLP) are trained on these geometric features. Within-domain, landmark models underperform a ResNet18 baseline (e.g., siamese MLP MAE 8.5–14.7° vs. ResNet18 3.76–12.23°) due to noise from landmark detection errors. However, in cross-domain evaluation the siamese MLP shows a smaller performance drop than ResNet18, indicating superior generalization. Permutation feature importance analysis reveals that different landmark groups are prioritized depending on the training dataset, enhancing interpretability. The main limitation is the upstream landmark detector’s accuracy, particularly at extreme head poses. The results suggest that purely geometric features can support robust gaze estimation, especially for resource-constrained edge applications.

## Method Summary
The method constructs a fully geometric gaze estimation pipeline. Input images are processed by MediaPipe’s face mesh (478 points) which is filtered to 20 semantic landmarks (pupil centers, iris extrema, eye corners, nose tip, glabella). Head pose is estimated using SolvePnP with a canonical 3D face model; the resulting rotation matrix and a warping matrix transform 2D landmarks and 3D gaze vectors into a normalized virtual camera coordinate system. Two feature representations are used: a global 40-dimensional vector (holistic/XGBoost) and a binocular representation with separate per-eye vectors plus inter-eye displacement and head anchors (siamese MLP). Models are trained with angular error loss. Evaluation comprises within-domain MAE on held-out test splits and cross-domain generalization by training on one dataset and testing on the others. The pipeline is fully specified but lacks exact canonical 3D landmark coordinates and has ambiguous descriptions of the siamese fusion layer, hindering exact replication.

## Key Results
- Within-domain, landmark-based models underperform ResNet18: siamese MLP MAE ranges 8.5–14.7° while ResNet18 achieves 3.76–12.23°.
- Cross-domain, siamese MLP generalizes better than ResNet18, e.g., training on Gaze360 and testing on GazeGene yields 15.99° (siamese) vs. 18.80° (ResNet18).
- Permutation feature importance shows dataset-dependent reliance on different landmark groups (e.g., eye contours vs. head anchors), confirming interpretability advantages.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Sparse geometric sufficiency** – The claim is that 20 facial landmarks encode enough information to regress gaze direction. Normalization via SolvePnP maps landmarks and gaze to a stable virtual camera frame, removing head pose, distance, and in-plane rotation variability. Evidence: cross-domain results demonstrate that a model trained on one dataset generalizes to others, suggesting the geometric relationship is consistently captured. Break condition: landmark detector failures at extreme poses corrupt the input representation.

**Invariance to appearance shifts** – Landmark models generalize better across datasets because they operate on normalized (x,y) coordinates rather than RGB pixels, making them inherently invariant to illumination, skin tone, texture, and other photometric variations that differ between datasets. Evidence: the performance drop from within- to cross-domain is smaller for the siamese MLP than for ResNet18. Break condition: misalignment of coordinate conventions (e.g., different definitions of eye midpoint) across datasets would violate the invariance assumption.

**Siamese architecture captures binocular geometry** – The siamese MLP processes each eye separately and fuses their embeddings with inter-eye displacement and head anchors, explicitly modeling binocular disparities. This inductive bias yields better performance than holistic MLP and XGBoost. Evidence: the siamese MLP achieves the best within-domain and cross-domain results among landmark models; PFI analysis shows flexible reliance on different feature groups. Break condition: asymmetric detection errors (one eye poorly detected) could propagate through the separate branches.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Conformal Geometric Normalization (Homogeneous Coordinates & PnP)**
  Why needed: The entire methodology depends on transforming raw 2D pixel coordinates and 3D gaze vectors into a common, normalized camera space. This requires solving the Perspective-n-Point (PnP) problem to estimate head pose and constructing a warping matrix that performs a perspective projection.
  Quick check: Given a 3D-2D landmark correspondence and a camera intrinsic matrix `K_c`, what is the purpose of solving PnP, and how does the rotation matrix `R_n` from Eq. 1 define the "virtual camera" coordinate system?

- **Concept: Feature Representation for Geometric Data (Centering & Scaling)**
  Why needed: The MLP inputs are flattened coordinate vectors. Centering (e.g., relative to eye corners or per-eye centroids) and scaling (by normalized image width) define the scale and reference frame of the features, directly affecting learnability.
  Quick check: What is the semantic difference between the "global features" `f_G` used by the holistic MLP and the "local features" `f_L, f_R` used by the siamese MLP, and why might the latter be more suitable for capturing binocular disparities?

- **Concept: Model Generalization vs. Within-Domain Performance**
  Why needed: The paper’s key finding is a trade-off: landmark models have lower within-domain accuracy but better cross-domain generalization. This requires understanding that a held-out test set from the same distribution measures fitting to noise, while a different distribution measures learning of invariant principles.
  Quick check: Why would a model that is less accurate on its own test set potentially outperform a more accurate model when tested on a completely different dataset?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Raw image -> MediaPipe face mesh (478 pts) -> filter to 20 key landmarks -> SolvePnP (head pose) -> Normalization (compute M, transform landmarks/gaze) -> Feature formatting (global or siamese) -> Regressor (MLP/XGBoost) -> Angular error loss

Critical path: The detection and pose estimation stages (MediaPipe + SolvePnP) are the first and most critical path; any error in landmark coordinates propagates irreversibly through normalization and degrades downstream performance.

Design tradeoffs:
- Accuracy vs. Robustness: Holistic MLP (higher capacity) may overfit detector noise; siamese MLP (lower per-branch capacity) introduces inductive bias for binocular geometry, potentially more robust.
- Interpretability vs. Performance: XGBoost offers native feature importance but is outperformed by MLPs; PFI provides post-hoc interpretability for neural models at computational cost.
- Lightweight vs. State-of-the-Art: Landmark models sacrifice within-domain accuracy for computational efficiency and cross-domain robustness.

Failure signatures:
- Upstream: MediaPipe confidence <0.8 or extreme head pose leads to complete extraction failure; 18–27% data loss observed.
- Midstream: Incorrect SolvePnP solution yields a distorted warping matrix, placing normalized landmarks in unnatural positions.
- Downstream: Poor performance on samples with extreme gaze angles; high variance in PFI across datasets indicates shifting feature reliance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can facial landmark detection be improved to maintain accuracy at extreme gaze and head pose angles? The paper identifies the detector as the primary bottleneck but does not explore alternatives.
2. What specific factors (illumination, skin tone, etc.) account for the superior cross-domain generalization of landmark-based models? The hypothesis of invariance is suggested but not isolated experimentally.
3. To what extent can purely synthetic datasets (like GazeGene) replace real-world data for training robust landmark-based estimators? The claim is based on cross-dataset performance but lacks a controlled synthetic-vs-real comparison.
4. Why do Permutation Feature Importance analyses reveal different critical feature groups across datasets? The paper observes this variation but does not investigate causes such as dataset biases.
5. Is the binocular siamese architecture strictly necessary, or could a well-designed holistic model with appropriate feature engineering achieve comparable generalization? The paper shows siamese performs best but does not ablate architecture vs. feature representation.

## Limitations
- Canonical 3D coordinates for the 20 selected landmarks are unreported, blocking exact replication of the SolvePnP step.
- Siamese MLP fusion layer dimensions are ambiguously described (“2×66” vs. calculated 134D), risking architectural mismatch.
- Reliance on MediaPipe detector with high failure rates (18–27%) on extreme poses introduces selection bias and limits real-world applicability.

## Confidence
- Sparse geometry sufficiency: Medium
- Invariance-driven generalization: Medium-High
- Siamese binocular modeling: Medium
