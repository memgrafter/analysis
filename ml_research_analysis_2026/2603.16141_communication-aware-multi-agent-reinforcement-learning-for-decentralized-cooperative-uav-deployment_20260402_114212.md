# Communication-Aware Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Decentralized Cooperative UAV Deployment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.16141
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16141
- Reference count: 23
- Primary result: Achieved 74% coverage with 5 UAVs and 10 ground nodes under communication and partial observation constraints, competitive with MILP upper bound (80%); zero-shot generalization to varying team sizes; 62% win rate in adversarial DroneCombat, improving over non-communicating baseline by 13 points.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of decentralized multi-UAV deployment for coverage in environments with partial observability and limited communication. The authors propose a centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) framework that uses a graph-based multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) approach. Each UAV processes its local observations and aggregates messages from neighbors via dual attention modules: one for encoding the local environment and another for iterative neighbor communication. The method is evaluated on two tasks: cooperative coverage (DroneConnect) and adversarial combat (DroneCombat). Results show that the approach achieves near-optimal coverage (74% vs. 80% MILP upper bound) and generalizes zero-shot to different team sizes, while significantly outperforming non-communicating baselines.

## Method Summary
The approach employs a CTDE paradigm with a shared policy across UAVs. During execution, each agent observes only entities within its sensing radius and can communicate with neighbors within a communication radius. The policy network first encodes the agent’s state and observed entities using MLPs, then applies agent–entity attention to produce an initial embedding that captures local environment context. This embedding is iteratively refined over K message-passing rounds using neighbor self-attention on the communication graph, allowing information to propagate through the network of UAVs. Actions are sampled from a Gaussian policy conditioned on the final embedding. For training, a centralized critic takes the global state to estimate the value function, enabling PPO-style updates. The framework is trained for 2 million environment steps and evaluated over 50 episodes.

## Key Results
- Achieved 74% average coverage ratio in DroneConnect with 5 UAVs and 10 ground nodes under partial observability and communication constraints, approaching the MILP upper bound of 80%.
- In DroneCombat, attained 62% win rate, a 13 percentage point improvement over the non-communicating baseline.
- Demonstrated zero-shot generalization to varying numbers of UAVs without any fine-tuning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The dual attention mechanism effectively combines local perception with neighbor communication, enabling agents to build situational awareness beyond their individual observation range. The CTDE setup allows the critic to learn from global information while the actor remains fully decentralized at execution, respecting communication constraints. The graph-based structure naturally handles varying numbers of agents and dynamic connectivity. Message-passing rounds (K) allow information to propagate across the network, facilitating coordinated decision-making even when direct communication is limited. The use of shared policies promotes scalability and generalization.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Agent state & local entities -> f_a, f_e encoding -> Agent-entity attention -> h_i(0) -> Neighbor self-attention (K rounds) -> h_i(K) -> Action head (Gaussian policy). Critic: Global state s -> V_phi.

Critical path: During training, experiences are collected with decentralized execution; then advantages are computed using the centralized critic; actor and critic are updated via PPO loss. The attention modules are key for information integration.

Design tradeoffs: Dual attention provides flexibility but increases computational cost; message-passing rounds (K) balance communication overhead and coordination; shared policy reduces parameters but may limit specialization.

Failure signatures: (1) Coverage drops sharply when communication radius is reduced; (2) Training instability (high value loss, low entropy); (3) Poor zero-shot generalization to different team sizes.

First three experiments:
1. Train with default parameters and compare coverage against a random policy and a non-communicating independent PPO baseline.
2. Ablate the neighbor attention module (set K=0) to quantify the impact of communication.
3. Vary communication radius r_c and observe coverage to confirm sensitivity.

## Limitations
- Exact environment dynamics (UAV/node motion models, sensing/communication radii, reward coefficients) are not fully specified, hindering precise replication.
- Critical hyperparameters (embedding dimensions, number of message-passing rounds, PPO coefficients) are unreported, requiring manual tuning.
- The method’s zero-shot generalization claim is based on limited evaluation; it is unclear whether training on a single team size suffices for robust scaling.
