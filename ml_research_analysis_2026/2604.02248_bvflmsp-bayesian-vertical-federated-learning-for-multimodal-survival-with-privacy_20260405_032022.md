# BVFLMSP : Bayesian Vertical Federated Learning for Multimodal Survival with Privacy

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.02248  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02248  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **One-line primary result:** Bayesian vertical federated learning framework for multimodal survival prediction that provides (ϵ,δ)-DP and uncertainty, with centralized variant achieving up to 0.02 higher C-index than MultiSurv on TCGA.

## Executive Summary
BVFLMSP addresses privacy and uncertainty in multimodal survival prediction through a Bayesian vertical federated learning (VFL) framework. Clients (each owning a distinct modality) use Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) to extract embeddings, which are perturbed with calibrated Gaussian noise to ensure (ϵ,δ)-differential privacy before transmission to a server. The server fuses these noisy embeddings via an attention mechanism and predicts discrete-time hazard probabilities using a Bayesian prediction head. Evaluated on TCGA cancer data, the method demonstrates a measurable privacy-utility tradeoff: with ϵ=10, C-index drops by 0.031 for two modalities and 0.09 for three modalities relative to non-private VFL, while maintaining formal privacy guarantees and providing uncertainty quantification.

## Method Summary
The framework vertically partitions multimodal data (clinical, miRNA, DNA methylation) across clients. Each client trains a Bayesian neural network with spike-and-slab priors to produce a 512-dimensional embedding. Before sending to the server, the embedding is L2-clipped (norm ≤1) and Gaussian noise is added, with noise scale σ set by the target privacy budget ϵ via the Gaussian mechanism. The server applies an attention-based fusion over the noisy embeddings, then passes the result through a Bayesian fully connected head to output hazards for 30 discretized time intervals. The loss combines: (1) the discrete-time survival negative log-likelihood, (2) a KL divergence term regularizing the Bayesian posteriors, and (3) an auxiliary cosine similarity loss (weight 0.05) to align embeddings from the same patient. Training proceeds in a VFL loop: server computes loss, backpropagates gradients to the embedding layer, sends ∂L/∂E to each client, and clients update their local BNN parameters via the chain rule.

## Key Results
- Centralized BayesianMultisurv variant achieves up to 0.02 higher C-index than MultiSurv on TCGA.
- In VFL with ϵ=10, C-index decreases from 0.743 to 0.712 for Clinical+miRNA (Δ=0.031) and from 0.752 to 0.662 for three modalities (Δ=0.09).
- DP noise effectively obfuscates patient-level structure: t-SNE plots show embeddings merging into a single cluster under privacy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BVFLMSP succeeds by integrating three synergistic mechanisms:  
1. **Bayesian neural networks** provide posterior distributions over embeddings, enabling uncertainty propagation to predictions.  
2. **Local Gaussian noise injection** with clipping ensures each client’s embedding release satisfies (ϵ,δ)-DP, preventing the server from reconstructing individual patient data.  
3. **Attention-based fusion** dynamically weights noisy modality embeddings, allowing the server to emphasize more reliable modalities despite DP-induced distortion. The auxiliary cosine similarity loss further encourages embeddings of the same patient to remain close, partially counteracting noise. Together, these elements enable collaborative survival modeling without raw data sharing, while delivering quantifiable privacy and uncertainty.

## Foundational Learning
- **Bayesian Neural Networks**: Approximate posterior inference (variational, spike-and-slab) for uncertainty. *Why needed*: Captures epistemic uncertainty in hazard predictions. *Quick check*: Presence of KL divergence in loss and stochastic forward passes for inference.
- **Differential Privacy**: Gaussian mechanism with clipping for privacy budgeting. *Why needed*: Guarantees that embedding release does not leak individual patient data. *Quick check*: Noise scale σ derived from ϵ, δ, sampling probability p, and iteration count τ.
- **Vertical Federated Learning**: Data partitioned by modality across clients; only embeddings shared. *Why needed*: Enables cross-institutional collaboration without centralizing raw data. *Quick check*: Clients retain raw data; server only receives embeddings.
- **Discrete-Time Survival Analysis**: Time is split into intervals; model outputs hazard probabilities. *Why needed*: Handles censoring and yields a full survival function. *Quick check*: Output layer size equals number of intervals (30) and loss is negative log-likelihood for discrete events.
- **Attention Mechanism**: Learns to weight client embeddings. *Why needed*: Adapts to varying noise levels across modalities under DP. *Quick check*: Module (e.g., linear layer + tanh + softmax) that processes embeddings before fusion.
- **Spike-and-Slab Priors**: Mixture of narrow and broad Gaussians. *Why needed*: Encourages sparsity in Bayesian CNN weights. *Quick check*: Prior specification with spike variance, slab variance, and mixing proportion.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Raw Data (Client 1: Clinical) → BayesianClientNet → (Embedding + DP Noise) →  
Raw Data (Client 2: miRNA) → BayesianClientNet → (Embedding + DP Noise) → Server: Attention Fusion → BayesianFC → Hazard Output  
Raw Data (Client 3: DNA methylation) → BayesianClientNet → (Embedding + DP Noise) →  

**Critical Path**  
1. Each client computes embedding, clips to norm C, adds Gaussian noise.  
2. Server collects noisy embeddings, applies attention to obtain fused representation.  
3. Server forward passes through BayesianFC to get hazards, computes total loss.  
4. Server backpropagates to embedding inputs, sends ∂L/∂E to each client.  
5. Clients backpropagate through local BNN using received gradients to update parameters.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Privacy‑utility: Lower ϵ reduces noise but weakens DP; optimal ϵ depends on clinical tolerance.  
- Bayesian complexity: Full BNNs yield uncertainty but increase compute (multiple MC samples, KL term).  
- VFL communication: Embedding size (512) trades off between information retention and privacy noise impact.  
- Auxiliary loss: Weight 0.05 aligns embeddings but may over‑regularize if mis‑tuned.

**Failure Signatures**  
- VFL converges slower/worse than centralized: Check client embedding norms and gradient magnitudes; verify auxiliary loss is active.  
- DP noise too high: C-index plummets below 0.6 or t‑SNE shows a single indiscriminate cluster; recalc σ from p and τ.  
- Uncertainty miscalibrated: Posterior samples produce too narrow/broad intervals; inspect KL weight and number of MC passes.  
- Attention collapse: One modality’s attention weight dominates across batches; may indicate mis‑scaled embeddings or missing auxiliary loss.

**First 3 Experiments**  
1. Centralized baseline: Train a single Bayesian model on all modalities (no DP) and confirm C-index exceeds MultiSurv by ~0.02.  
2. Non‑private VFL: Train 2‑modality (Clinical+miRNA) VFL without DP; expect C-index within 0.01 of centralized.  
3. Private VFL: Train same 2‑modality VFL with ϵ=10 (δ=1e‑5); expect C-index ~0.712 and t‑SNE of noisy embeddings to show merged clusters.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the BVFLMSP framework be extended to incorporate imaging modalities (e.g., histopathology, radiology) and what architectural modifications are required to effectively process high-dimensional image data within the Bayesian VFL paradigm?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Future work will extend the proposed framework to incorporate imaging modalities (e.g., histopathology and radiology), which were not considered in this study but are central to many real world survival analysis applications."  
- **Why unresolved:** The current client models handle tabular/omics data; imaging demands spatially-aware feature extractors (e.g., Bayesian CNNs) and may require adjustments to the DP noise injection and fusion mechanisms.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of Bayesian CNN-based clients for image data, integration into VFL, experimental validation on TCGA imaging datasets, and demonstration of improved C-index while maintaining (ϵ,δ)-DP guarantees.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How robust is the embedding perturbation-based differential privacy mechanism against stronger adversarial threat models, such as malicious servers employing generative reconstruction attacks or gradient manipulation?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "We also aim to investigate robustness under stronger adversarial threat models..." and earlier "We consider a feature reconstruction attack under an honest but curious server assumption."  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only addresses an honest-but-curious server with a specific shadow model attack; more powerful adversaries (e.g., using GANs or inference attacks) may still extract sensitive information from noisy embeddings or gradients.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct comprehensive security evaluations with a suite of advanced attacks, quantify privacy leakage (e.g., via reconstruction error, membership inference AUC), and potentially refine the noise mechanism or introduce additional defenses.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can adaptive noise allocation strategies improve the privacy‑utility trade‑off in Bayesian VFL for survival analysis across different privacy budgets and modality combinations?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "and study adaptive privacy utility trade offs for different privacy budgets."  
- **Why unresolved:** The current approach adds Gaussian noise with a uniform scale derived from the global ϵ; different modalities may have varying sensitivities, and per‑client or per‑layer adaptive scaling could preserve more utility under the same privacy budget.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Propose and compare adaptive mechanisms (e.g., noise scaling based on gradient norms or modality importance) against the fixed baseline, measuring differences in C‑index, IBS, and convergence speed while respecting the same (ϵ,δ)-DP guarantee.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does the specific Bayesian formulation (spike‑and‑slab priors with KL regularization) compare to alternative uncertainty quantification methods (e.g., Monte‑Carlo dropout, deep ensembles) in terms of calibration and decision‑making within the VFL survival setting?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper employs Bayesian neural networks for uncertainty but does not benchmark against other uncertainty methods; the advantages of this particular Bayesian approach in the VFL context remain unclear.  
- **Why unresolved:** Without comparative studies, it is unknown whether the added computational cost and complexity of full Bayesian inference yield significantly better calibrated uncertainties or improved clinical utility over simpler approximations.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement equivalent non‑Bayesian variants (MC dropout, ensembles) with identical architectures, evaluate calibration metrics (e.g., Brier score, prediction interval coverage), and assess decision quality on survival outcomes.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Do the convergence theoretical results extend to non‑convex deep Bayesian networks, and how does the embedding noise from DP affect convergence speed and stability in practice?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The convergence proof relies on α‑strong convexity and β‑smoothness (Assumptions 4 and 5), which are unrealistic for deep models; the practical convergence behavior under DP noise may differ from the bound.  
- **Why unresolved:** The theoretical guarantee might be loose or inapplicable, leaving a gap in understanding how quickly (or whether) the VFL algorithm converges under realistic conditions and privacy constraints.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical measurement of convergence rates (e.g., loss gradient norms, epochs to target loss) across architectures and privacy budgets; possibly develop new theory for non‑convex settings (e.g., using PL condition or Lyapunov arguments) that matches empirical observations.

## Limitations
- Missing critical hyperparameters (batch size, epochs, early stopping) and implementation details (Bayesian layer posterior form, attention mechanism) hinder exact replication and performance benchmarking.
- DP privacy budget computation is highly sensitive to undisclosed training schedule (batch size, total iterations), risking deviations from the claimed ϵ in practice.
- Absence of public code and fixed random seeds undermines result reproducibility and may mask variability across implementations.
