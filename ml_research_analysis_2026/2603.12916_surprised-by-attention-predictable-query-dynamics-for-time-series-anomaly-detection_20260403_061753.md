# Surprised by Attention: Predictable Query Dynamics for Time Series Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12916
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12916
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** AxonAD improves AUC-PR by 2.2× (0.285 vs 0.128) and Event-F1 by 1.6× (0.420 vs 0.255) on proprietary in-vehicle telemetry, and leads the TSB-AD multivariate suite with mean AUC-PR 0.437, VUS-PR 0.493, and Range-F1 0.471.

## Executive Summary
AxonAD addresses a key limitation of residual-based detectors for multivariate time series anomalies: many anomalies manifest as shifts in cross-channel dependencies rather than amplitude excursions. The method treats the evolution of multi-head attention query vectors as a predictable short-horizon process under nominal operation. It couples a bidirectional reconstruction pathway with a history-only predictor that forecasts future query vectors. The predictor is trained via masked cosine loss against exponentially moving average (EMA) target queries, ensuring stable learning. At inference, a unified anomaly score combines robustly standardized reconstruction error and tail-aggregated query mismatch. Extensive evaluation on in-vehicle telemetry and 17 TSB-AD datasets shows substantial gains over state-of-the-art baselines, with ablations confirming the contribution of query prediction and combined scoring.

## Method Summary
AxonAD processes overlapping windows of multivariate time series through a shared embedding layer that bifurcates into two pathways. The **reconstruction pathway** uses bidirectional self-attention to reconstruct the input window, yielding per-window ℓ₂ error \(d_{\text{rec}}\). The **predictive pathway** uses a causal temporal convolutional network (TCN) to forecast the next-step multi-head query vectors from a time-shifted embedding stream. An EMA target encoder provides stable supervision: the predictor is trained to match the target’s queries via a masked cosine loss (JEPA-style). During inference, tail-aggregated cosine deviation between predicted and target queries gives \(d_q\). Both scores are robustly standardized (median/IQR) on nominal training data and summed to produce the final anomaly score \(S\). Training uses a combined loss of reconstruction MSE and query cosine loss, optimized with AdamW.

## Key Results
- On proprietary in-vehicle telemetry (19 channels, 80k steps), AxonAD outperforms baselines by large margins: AUC-PR 0.285 vs 0.128 (2.2×), Event-F1 0.420 vs 0.255 (1.6×).
- On the TSB-AD multivariate benchmark (17 datasets, 180 series), AxonAD achieves state-of-the-art threshold-free ranking: mean AUC-PR 0.437, VUS-PR 0.493, and Range-F1 0.471.
- Ablations confirm that predicting queries (not keys/values/attention maps) and combining both scores are primary performance drivers; removing either degrades metrics significantly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Under nominal operation, attention query vectors evolve predictably over short horizons; anomalies disrupt this temporal predictability.
- **Mechanism:** A history-only predictor maps past embeddings to future multi-head query vectors. During training, it is trained via a masked cosine loss to match queries from an EMA target encoder. At inference, the cosine deviation between predicted and target queries on the tail of the window yields the query mismatch score \(d_q\).
- **Core assumption:** Query vectors encode cross-channel relational structure; their short-horizon dynamics are stable in the nominal regime.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "treats multi-head attention query evolution as a predictable short-horizon process."
  - [section 3.2] "The predictive pathway forecasts query vector evolution using only past context."
  - [corpus] Neighbors like Anomaly Transformer and MIXAD also focus on relational discrepancies, but AxonAD specifically targets query predictability; corpus evidence is supportive but not direct.
- **Break condition:** If nominal query dynamics are inherently unpredictable (e.g., highly stochastic relationships) or if coordination anomalies do not affect query predictability, \(d_q\) may not signal anomalies.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Reconstruction error and query mismatch capture complementary anomaly types (amplitude vs. coordination shifts); their robustly standardized additive combination yields a unified score sensitive to both.
- **Mechanism:** The reconstruction pathway computes ℓ₂ error per window (\(d_{\text{rec}}\)). The query pathway computes tail-aggregated cosine deviation (\(d_q\)). Each is standardized using median and IQR computed on nominal training data, then summed: \(S = rz(d_{\text{rec}}) + rz(d_q)\).
- **Core assumption:** On nominal windows, \(d_{\text{rec}}\) and \(d_q\) are approximately uncorrelated and both low; anomalies elevate at least one.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "combines robustly standardized reconstruction error and tail-aggregated query mismatch."
  - [section 3.4] Equations (4)-(6) and Figure 3 illustrate complementarity; ablations (Table 3) show combined scoring outperforms single components.
  - [corpus] Several neighbors (e.g., MIXAD, Nominality Score Conditioned) also combine multiple signals, but the specific robust standardization and tail-aggregation are novel; corpus provides indirect validation of multi‑signal approaches.
- **Break condition:** If the two scores become highly correlated even under anomalies, or if robust standardization fails due to heavy‑tailed nominal distributions, the combination may lose discriminative power.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** An EMA target encoder provides stable, non‑collapsing supervision for the query predictor, enabling effective learning of predictable query dynamics.
- **Mechanism:** The predictor is trained to match queries from a target network whose parameters are updated via exponential moving average (\(\theta_{\text{tgt}} \leftarrow m \theta_{\text{tgt}} + (1-m)\theta_{\text{on}}\)). This BYOL/JEPA‑style distillation avoids trivial solutions and stabilizes training.
- **Core assumption:** The EMA target represents a slowly converging, consistent model of nominal query evolution.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "trained via masked cosine loss against EMA target queries."
  - [section 3.3] Details of EMA update and masked JEPA loss.
  - [section 6, ablations] Removing EMA (\(m=0\)) or using extreme \(m\) (0.99, 0.999) degrades performance (Table 3), confirming the need for balanced momentum.
  - [corpus] BYOL/JEPA papers are cited; the mechanism is well‑established in self‑supervised learning.
- **Break condition:** If nominal dynamics drift over time, a fixed EMA momentum may cause the target to lag, reducing predictor accuracy; similarly, inappropriate \(m\) can make targets too volatile or too static.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input window \(X \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times F}\) → shared linear embedding + positional bias → \(\text{H}_{\text{on}}\) → bifurcates into Reconstruction encoder (bidirectional self-attention → FFN → \(\hat{X}\) → \(d_{\text{rec}}\)) and Predictive branch (time-shifted \(\text{eH}\) → causal TCN predictor → \(\hat{Q}_{\text{pred}}\)). EMA target encoder (parallel to online) → \(\text{H}_{\text{tgt}}\) → \(Q_{\text{tgt}}\). Loss: masked cosine(\(\hat{Q}_{\text{pred}}\), stop‑grad(\(Q_{\text{tgt}}\))). Inference: compute \(d_q\) (tail‑aggregated cosine) → robust standardization → sum → \(S\).

**Critical path:**  
1. Compute \(\text{H}_{\text{on}}\).  
2. Reconstruction branch: compute \(\hat{X}\) and \(d_{\text{rec}}\).  
3. Predictive branch: shift \(\text{H}_{\text{on}}\) to \(\text{eH}\), run causal TCN to get \(\hat{Q}_{\text{pred}}\).  
4. EMA branch: compute \(\text{H}_{\text{tgt}}\) and \(Q_{\text{tgt}}\).  
5. Compute \(d_q\) via tail‑aggregated cosine on last valid timesteps.  
6. Standardize both scores and sum for final \(S\).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Bidirectional vs. causal: Reconstruction uses full context; predictor uses only past for temporal causality.  
- Predict queries vs. keys/values: Queries control attention routing; ablations show predicting queries yields best performance.  
- Cosine vs. MSE distance: Cosine focuses on directional mismatch; ablations show cosine is superior.  
- EMA momentum: \(m=0.9\) balances stability and responsiveness; extremes harm performance.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Reconstruction collapse: \(d_{\text{rec}}\) near zero on all windows → check reconstruction MSE on nominal validation.  
- Predictor degeneracy: \(d_q\) high on nominal → predictor under‑fitting or EMA mis‑aligned; inspect training loss.  
- Leakage: Predictor uses future context → verify causal masking.  
- Non‑stationary drift: EMA target lags → \(d_q\) rises on nominal over time; monitor online \(d_q\) distribution.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Reconstruction sanity:** Train on nominal telemetry; compute \(d_{\text{rec}}\) distribution. Inject amplitude anomalies (spikes) → \(d_{\text{rec}}\) should increase.  
2. **Coordination break test:** Inject correlation shift (e.g., permute one channel) → \(d_q\) should increase while \(d_{\text{rec}}\) may stay low.  
3. **Scoring combination ablation:** Compare AUC‑PR using \(d_{\text{rec}}\) alone, \(d_q\) alone, and combined on validation set containing both anomaly types to verify complementarity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Why does predicting query vectors outperform predicting other attention components?**  
   *Basis:* Ablations (Table 3) show query prediction yields significantly higher AUC-PR and Event-F1 than keys, values, attention maps, or hidden states.  
   *Unresolved:* No theoretical explanation for why queries are more sensitive to dependency shifts.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Synthetic anomalies selectively affecting query generation; probing head-wise information content.

2. **Is forecast horizon s=1 universally optimal?**  
   *Basis:* Ablations (Figure 6) show s=25 reduces AUC-PR by 0.056 vs. s=1.  
   *Unresolved:* Only two horizons evaluated; intermediate or multi-horizon prediction may benefit some anomaly types.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Systematic evaluation across finer s grid (e.g., 1-25) on datasets with varied anomaly tempos.

3. **What dataset characteristics cause failure?**  
   *Basis:* Authors use a single configuration across all datasets, justified by a label-free transfer check.  
   *Unresolved:* The coarse transfer check does not guarantee similar attention dynamics; performance on datasets with different dependency structures, channel counts, or nonstationarity is unknown.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Failure analysis on poorly-performing TSB-AD series; correlating performance with metadata (channels, sampling rate, nonstationarity).

4. **How robust to imperfect nominal training data?**  
   *Basis:* Method assumes clean nominal training and uses robust standardization; contamination impact is untested.  
   *Unresolved:* Sensitivity to undetected anomalies or label noise in training set is unknown.  
   *Resolution evidence:* Controlled experiments injecting anomalies into training at varying percentages; measuring degradation vs. noisy-training-robust baselines.

## Limitations
- Exact TCN predictor architecture (layers, residuals) unspecified; replication may vary.
- EMA target update schedule (per batch vs. per epoch) unclear; could affect stability.
- Robust standardization assumes clean nominal training; contamination or non-stationarity may bias score combination.
- Evaluation limited to in-vehicle telemetry and TSB-AD; domain generalizability untested.

## Confidence
- Query predictability mechanism: Medium confidence (ablations support contribution but direct evidence of predictability is limited).
- Complementary scoring: High confidence (combined score consistently outperforms single components).
- EMA stabilization: High confidence (momentum ablation shows clear optimal range).
- Overall performance gains: High confidence (consistent improvements across 17 datasets).

## Next Checks
1. **TCN architecture sensitivity:** Systematically vary TCN depth (2–6 layers) and residual connections; measure impact on TSB-AD AUC-PR.
2. **EMA update frequency ablation:** Compare per-batch vs. per-epoch EMA updates across momentum (0.8–0.99); identify most stable configuration.
3. **Direct predictability validation:** On held-out nominal set, compute one-step query prediction error and correlate with \(d_q\); repeat on injected coordination anomalies to confirm alignment.
