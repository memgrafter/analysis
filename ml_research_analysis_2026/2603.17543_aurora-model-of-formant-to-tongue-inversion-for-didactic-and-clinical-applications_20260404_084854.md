# AURORA Model of Formant-to-Tongue Inversion for Didactic and Clinical Applications

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17543
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17543
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** A formant-to-tongue inversion model trained on ultrasound and acoustic data produces empirically accurate tongue predictions for most vowels via linear regression and PCA/Procrustes, with systematic failure for rounded vowels due to unmodelled lip rounding.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the AURORA model, a lightweight system that maps vowel formants (F1, F2) to tongue articulation for educational and therapeutic use. It addresses the oversimplification of traditional F1/F2 metaphors by generating spatial tongue contours from acoustic input, using a linear regression model trained on ultrasound and acoustic data from 40 Northern English speakers. The authors deliver two tools: an interactive Shiny app for exploration and a real-time biofeedback prototype that visualizes predicted tongue shapes alongside live formant tracking. The model achieves qualitative accuracy for non-rounded vowels but fails for rounded vowels due to excluded lip and jaw parameters.

## Method Summary
The model is trained on 3,856 vowel tokens from 40 Northern English speakers, combining ultrasound tongue imaging and acoustic recordings. It employs multivariate linear regression with inputs F1, F2, and their interaction term (F1×F2) to predict six articulatory parameters: coordinates for two tongue landmarks (tip and vallecula) and two principal components capturing shape variance. Full tongue contours are reconstructed by inverting the PCA to obtain a normalized shape, then applying a Procrustes alignment using the predicted landmark coordinates to determine absolute position, rotation, and scale. The resulting pipeline is implemented in a static Shiny app (slider-based) and a real-time prototype using LPC for online formant extraction.

## Key Results
- The predicted tongue contours systematically vary with F1/F2 as predicted by speech theory (e.g., high F2 yields anterior tongue, interactions affect dorsum convexity) and closely match mean empirical contours for most vowels, with the notable exception of rounded vowels like /u/.
- Two accessible tools are released: a Shiny app for interactive formant-to-tongue exploration and a real-time biofeedback prototype that overlays predicted contours on formant tracking, enabling intuitive visual guidance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The core mapping from formants to a low-dimensional articulatory space is captured by a linear model.
- **Mechanism:** Multivariate linear regression learns direct coefficients relating [F1, F2, F1×F2] to six parameters (two point coordinates, two shape PCs) from the training corpus. This encodes empirical acoustics-articulation correlations.
- **Evidence anchor:** The model is trained on 3,856 tokens from 40 speakers; coefficients are derived from this specific dataset (Section 3).
- **Break condition:** Non-linear relations in untrained speakers/dialects will cause systematic prediction errors.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Full tongue contours are accurately reconstructed from low-dimensional predictions.
- **Mechanism:** Predicted PC scores invert PCA to a normalized shape. A Procrustes alignment uses the predicted tip/vallecula coordinates as a reference vector to place this shape in absolute space (rotation, translation, scale), assuming shape is separable from pose.
- **Evidence anchor:** The first two PCs capture 67.54% of shape variance (Section 3); visual comparisons show reconstructed contours matching mean data for most vowels (Figure 3).
- **Break condition:** If important shape details lie in higher PCs or the Procrustes assumption fails, reconstruction misrepresents true geometry.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The system provides intuitive biofeedback by visualizing tongue shape directly controlled by formants.
- **Mechanism:** Real-time formant tracking (LPC) feeds the regression and reconstruction, outputting a spatial contour that bridges acoustic goals and articulatory instructions for non-specialists.
- **Evidence anchor:** The Shiny app and real-time prototype implement this loop (Section 5); external clinical work on biofeedback provides paradigm context.
- **Break condition:** Inaccurate or jittery formant tracking (especially for rounded vowels) or latency >~100ms would degrade utility.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vowel Formants (F1, F2) as Acoustic Correlates**  
  *Why needed:* Inputs are formant frequencies, which are determined by vocal tract shape, not direct tongue positions. Users must grasp this indirect relationship.  
  *Quick check:* Lowering the tongue body increases pharyngeal volume. How does this affect F1 and F2, and why might the relationship be non-linear?
  
- **Dimensionality Reduction (PCA & Procrustes)**  
  *Why needed:* The model compresses many contour points into two shape PCs and two landmark coordinates, separating shape from overall position/scale.  
  *Quick check:* Why must Procrustes alignment be performed before PCA on the tongue contour data? What would happen if PCA used raw, unaligned coordinates?
  
- **Model Limitations and Biases**  
  *Why needed:* The model excludes lips/jaw and is trained on one dialect/gender-imbalanced data. Users must know where predictions fail (e.g., rounded vowels).  
  *Quick check:* For /u/ as in "booed", the model mismatches mean data. What omitted articulatory factor explains this, and how does it affect formants?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Formant Input (F1,F2 or audio) -> Regression Engine (multivariate linear model) -> Reconstruction Pipeline (PCA inversion + Procrustes alignment) -> Visualization (Shiny app / real-time display)
- **Critical path:**  
  *Didactic:* User adjusts F1/F2 sliders -> regression -> reconstruction -> immediate visualization (negligible latency).  
  *Real-time biofeedback:* Audio -> LPC formant tracking -> lookup/regression -> reconstruction -> display. LPC tracking is the bottleneck and source of instability; parameters must be calibrated per user.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Simplicity vs. Accuracy:* Linear regression + 2 PCs is interpretable and fast but cannot model non-linearities or high-frequency shape details, causing errors for rounded vowels.  
  *Generality vs. Specificity:* Trained on one dialect (Northern English) with gender imbalance; may not generalize without retraining.  
  *Tongue-only vs. Full VT:* Excluding lips/jaw/velum simplifies but limits physiological fidelity.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  *Qualitative:* Unnatural tongue dorsum concavity/convexity for expected F1/F2; complete failure for rounded vowels.  
  *Quantitative:* High residual error on held-out speakers outside training phonological space.  
  *Real-time:* Erratic contour jitter due to poor LPC formant tracking (noisy input, threshold issues).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Sanity check:** Input canonical F1/F2 for cardinal vowels (/i/, /a/, /u/) and verify predicted shapes match textbook expectations (high front: high & anterior; low back: low & retracted).  
  2. **Edge case test:** Input formants for rounded vowels (e.g., F1≈300, F2≈900 for /u/) and observe mismatch vs. empirical mean (Figure 3); attribute to lip omission.  
  3. **Real-time latency & jitter benchmark:** Run prototype with a sustained vowel (e.g., /a/). Measure frame-to-frame jitter and end-to-end latency. Systematically vary LPC settings to find stable operating range per voice.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
None explicitly stated by the paper. However, based on its limitations and future work implications, the following questions arise:
- How does the model generalize to dialects and speaker populations outside the Northern English training set?
- Can incorporating lip and jaw parameters improve prediction accuracy for rounded vowels?
- What is the real-world efficacy and user experience of the biofeedback tools in clinical or didactic settings?

## Limitations
- Geographic and demographic bias: trained only on 40 Northern English speakers with noted gender imbalance; unknown generalization to other dialects or populations.
- Omitted articulators: lips, jaw, and velum are excluded, causing systematic failure for rounded vowels (e.g., /u/).
- Linearity assumption: multivariate linear regression may not capture non-linear acoustics-articulation relations outside the training distribution.

## Confidence
- **High:** Internal consistency of the inversion mechanism (linear regression + PCA/Procrustes) on the training corpus; qualitative match to mean empirical contours for non-rounded vowels is well-supported by visuals.
- **Medium:** Claims of accuracy generalize only to phonological/demographic space similar to the training set; limited by corpus size and lack of speaker cross-validation.
- **Low:** Clinical/didactic utility claims; no user studies, learning outcome data, or comparative trials are presented.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-dialect holdout test:** Evaluate predictions on a held-out corpus from a different English dialect (e.g., Southern US, Australian). Quantify RMSE of tongue coordinates vs. in-dialect performance.
2. **Lip-rounding extension experiment:** Augment model with an explicit lip parameter (e.g., from video annotation). Remeasure accuracy for rounded vowels to isolate impact.
3. **Real-time latency & jitter benchmark:** Measure contour stability and end-to-end latency (<~100ms target) across diverse voices (pitch, formant ranges) under varying LPC settings; correlate jitter with formant tracking error.
