# Satellite-Based Detection of Looted Archaeological Sites Using Machine Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.19608  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19608  
- **Reference count**: 30  
- **Primary result**: A ResNet‑50 CNN with ImageNet pretraining and spatial masking achieves an F1 score of 0.926 on 2023 PlanetScope imagery for detecting looted sites, substantially outperforming traditional ML methods (best F1=0.710).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of scalable detection of looted archaeological sites from satellite imagery. Using a dataset of 1,943 sites in Afghanistan with multi‑year PlanetScope imagery (4.7 m/pixel) and manually annotated footprint masks, the authors compare end‑to‑end CNN classifiers against traditional machine learning approaches that use handcrafted spectral/texture features and embeddings from geospatial foundation models. The top‑performing model is a ResNet‑50 CNN initialized with ImageNet weights and augmented with spatial masking, which attains an F1 score of 0.926 on held‑outказ
the best traditional method (SatCLIP‑V + Random Forest + Mean aggregation, F1=0.710). Extensive ablations demonstrate that spatial masking alone contributes a 30–45% improvement, ImageNet pretraining adds a further 6–14% gain, and training on a single temporally consistent year reduces label noise. The results highlight the effectiveness of deep learning with spatial priors for automated monitoring of cultural heritage sites.

## Method Summary
The study leverages PlanetScope monthly mosaics (RGB+NIR, 4.7 m/pixel) covering 1 km×1 km patches centered on 1,943 archaeological sites across Afghanistan, complemented by binary raster masks of site footprints. For CNN pipelines, patches from a selected year (2023) are resized to 224×224 pixels, and the mask is applied via element‑wise multiplication to suppress background context. Models are trained with class‑weighted cross‑entropy, Adam optimizer (lr=3e‑4, weight decay=0.01), step learning rate decay, and standard augmentations. Traditional ML follows two paths: (1) handcrafted spectral and texture features (42 dimensions) extracted from masked pixels; (2) embeddings from frozen geospatial foundation models (SatCLIP‑V, DINOv3, etc.), also derived from masked or unmasked patches. Monthly feature vectors are aggregated (e.g., mean, median, PCA) to yield a single site‑level descriptor, which is fed to a classifier (Random Forest, XGBoost, Logistic Regression). Evaluation uses stratified 5‑fold site‑level cross‑validation, with early stopping on validation F1.

## Key Results
- A ResNet‑50 CNN with ImageNet pretraining and spatial masking achieves an F1 score of **0.926** on held‑out 2023 imagery, far surpassing the best traditional ML method (SatCLIP‑V + Random Forest + Mean aggregation, F1=0.710).
- Spatial masking provides a **30–45% relative improvement** in F1 by suppressing irrelevant background context; ImageNet pretraining yields an additional **6–14% F1 gain** despite domain shift.
- Training on imagery from a single, temporally consistent year (2023) reduces label noise and improves accuracy; performance declines for earlier years due to mismatch with the 2023 label.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Mechanism 1: Spatial Masking Improves Focus**  
  Claim: Applying a binary footprint mask to input patches before feeding them to a CNN significantly boosts detection performance.  
  Mechanism: The mask is applied via element‑wise multiplication, zeroing out background pixels. This forces the CNN to concentrate on the probable site area, reducing distraction from modern context (roads, settlements).  
  Core assumption: The looting signal is localized within the footprint; background contains confounding patterns.  
  Evidence: The abstract reports a 30–45% F1 improvement; Section 4.3 and Table 4 quantify gains of +0.301 to +0.455 for CNNs.

- **Mechanism 2: ImageNet Pretraining Transfers Useful Low‑Level Features**  
  Claim: Initializing CNNs with ImageNet weights yields a 6–14% F1 gain even though the target domain (satellite imagery) differs from natural images.  
  Mechanism: Early layers of ImageNet‑trained networks learn generic edge and texture detectors reusable across domains; fine‑tuning adapts higher layers to looting patterns.  
  Core assumption: Low‑level visual features are broadly applicable; fine‑tuning can overcome spectral shifts.  
  Evidence: The abstract and Section 6.1 (Table 4) show consistent improvements for ResNet and EfficientNet backbones; Section 7 discusses that transfer learning helps despite domain shift.

- **Mechanism 3: Single‑Year Training Mitigates Temporal Label Noise**  
  Claim: Training on imagery from a single year (2023) improves accuracy relative to multi‑year training.  
  Mechanism: The static 2023 label (“looted” or “preserved”) may not match earlier years if looting occurred after those images. Using only 2023 aligns the training data with the label, reducing noise from temporally inconsistent examples.  
  Core assumption: The binary label reflects the site state as of 2023; the goal is to detect degraded condition, not timing.  
  Evidence: The abstract notes label noise reduction; Section 3 discusses the issue; Figure 2 shows performance peaks for 2020 (most looting year) and declines for older years, indicating increasing temporal mismatch.

## Foundational Learning
- **Transfer Learning & Domain Adaptation**  
  Why needed: The top CNN relies on ImageNet‑pretrained features, which must generalize from natural images to satellite imagery. Understanding why low‑level features transfer despite spectral differences is key.  
  Quick check: What property of early neural network layers makes them transferable across domains like ImageNet (photos of objects) and satellite imagery (patterns of soil and disturbance)?

- **Spatial Attention via Hard Masking**  
  Why needed: The spatial mask is a hard‑coded attention mechanism that drastically changes the model’s inductive bias.  
  Quick check: How does applying a binary mask to an input patch before the first convolutional layer differ from a model that learns to ignore background through training on unmasked data?

- **Temporal Aggregation for Irregular Time Series**  
  Why needed: Traditional ML must condense 96 monthly mosaics into a fixed‑length vector; the choice of aggregation (Mean, Median, PCA) affects noise vs. dynamics.  
  Quick check: Why might a simple “Mean” aggregation across all months work well for this task, as suggested by the best traditional ML result (SatCLIP‑V+RF+Mean)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Data ingestion (PlanetScope monthly mosaics + manual footprints) -> Preprocessing & Masking (rasterize mask, resize, apply mask) -> [CNN branch] -> CNN backbone (ResNet/EfficientNet) -> FC classifier -> Prediction; [Traditional ML branch] -> Feature extraction (handcrafted 42‑D or foundation model embeddings on masked pixels) -> Temporal aggregation (Mean/Median/PCA) -> Classifier (Random Forest/XGBoost) -> Prediction.
- **Critical path** (top model): Single‑year, site‑centered 1 km patch -> Rasterize & apply footprint mask -> Resize to 224×224 -> ImageNet‑pretrained ResNet‑50 -> FC classifier -> Looted/Preserved.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Masking helps CNNs but can hurt some foundation model embeddings (SatMAE/Prithvi) because they expect full‑scene context; compatibility must be validated.
  - Single‑year training reduces label noise but discards temporal change information; tasks requiring change detection would need multi‑year modeling.
  - CNN capacity: ResNet‑50 outperforms smaller ResNets, but EfficientNet‑B1 offers similar performance with fewer parameters, enabling efficiency trade‑offs.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor mask quality (misaligned or wrong size) degrades performance; diagnose by visualizing masked patches.
  - Temporal mismatch: deploying a model trained on 2023 imagery on older imagery for a site looted later causes failures; ensure temporal alignment or retrain.
  - Foundation model degradation with masking: using masked patches with SatMAE/Prithvi yields poor results; for traditional ML, extract embeddings from unmasked full patches unless validated otherwise.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Replicate Table 4 (Masking Impact): Train ResNet‑50 with and without spatial masking on 2023 imagery; expect ~0.36 F1 point gain with masking.
  2. Single‑Year vs. Multi‑Year CNN: Train EfficientNet‑B1 (masked) on only 2023 vs. all years mixed; expect single‑year to outperform; inspect validation loss curves for divergence.
  3. Foundation Model + Masking Sanity Check: For each foundation model, extract embeddings from masked and unmasked 2023 patches, train a Random Forest, and compare F1. Expect DINOv3/SatCLIP‑V to be robust or improved, while SatMAE/Prithvi to degrade.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Cross‑region generalization**: How well do models trained on Afghan archaeological sites generalize to other countries with different geology, land use, and imaging conditions? The authors note scaling to Syria, Sudan, Egypt as future work but current performance may vary.
2. **Reducing annotation reliance**: Can active learning or semi‑supervised methods lessen dependence on expert‑annotated site footprints and labels without sacrificing accuracy? This remains unexplored.
3. **Robustness to mask inaccuracies**: How robust are CNN‑based detectors to noise or errors in the manually annotated spatial masks? The paper notes that mask definitions affect inductive bias but does not stress‑test with imperfect masks.
4. **Leveraging multi‑temporal data**: How can multi‑year imagery be used to improve detection while mitigating label noise from inconsistent site states across years? Single‑year training discards potentially valuable change information; a temporal fusion approach is needed.

## Limitations
- Replication is hindered by unspecified details such as exact CV splits, mask resizing interpolation, and the specific month used for CNN inputs.
- Generalizability is limited to Afghanistan PlanetScope imagery; performance may degrade for other regions, sensors, or environmental conditions.
- Spatial masking requires accurate footprint polygons, which may be unavailable at deployment; some foundation models also degrade when masks are applied.

## Confidence
- CNN superiority (F1=0.926 vs. 0.710): **High**  
- Spatial masking benefit (30–45% improvement): **Medium** (effect size depends on mask details)  
- ImageNet pretraining benefit (6–14% gain): **High**  
- Single‑year training reduces label noise: **Medium** (magnitude not explicitly quantified)

## Next Checks
1. **Mask robustness test**: Systematically dilate and erode footprint masks to simulate annotation errors; measure F1 degradation to establish tolerance bounds.
2. **Cross‑region generalization**: Train on the full Afghanistan dataset and evaluate on a held‑out region (e.g., Egypt or Syria) with distinct environmental conditions; assess zero‑shot transfer.
3. **Multi‑temporal change modeling**: Extend the top CNN to accept paired images (e.g., 2023 vs. 2017) via channel concatenation or a siamese architecture; compare F1 against the single‑year model to quantify the value of explicit change information.
