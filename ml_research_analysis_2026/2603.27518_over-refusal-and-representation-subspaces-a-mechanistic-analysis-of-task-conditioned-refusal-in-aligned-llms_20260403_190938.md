# Over-Refusal and Representation Subspaces: A Mechanistic Analysis of Task-Conditioned Refusal in Aligned LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27518
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27518
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** Harmful-refusal is mediated by a single, task-agnostic direction enabling effective global suppression, whereas over-refusal is task-dependent, higher-dimensional, and requires task-conditioned steering for selective reduction.

## Executive Summary
This paper mechanistically analyzes two refusal behaviors in aligned LLMs: correct harmful-refusal (declining unsafe prompts) and erroneous over-refusal (declining safe prompts). Using LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, the authors find that harmful-refusal is driven by a unified direction converging in mid-layers, making it susceptible to simple global ablation. In contrast, over-refusal directions are task-specific, embedded within benign task clusters, and span higher-dimensional subspaces, rendering global interventions ineffective. Linear probes reveal both refusal types are distinguishable from early layers, enabling targeted, task-conditioned steering that reduces over-refusal with minimal impact on safety compliance.

## Method Summary
The study uses residual-stream activations from LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct on 270 samples across five NLP tasks. Responses are classified by a GPT-4o judge into over-refusal (safe prompts refused) and harmful-refusal (harmful prompts refused). For each layer, Difference-in-Means (DIM) vectors are computed for both refusal types. Geometric analyses (PCA, silhouette scores, centroid distances, explained variance) characterize the representational structure. Global ablation removes the harmful-refusal direction; task-conditioned steering uses the SafeConstellations method (Maskey et al., 2025) to target over-refusal within task subspaces. Linear probes test separability of task identity and refusal type per layer. Causal patching swaps attention head outputs between prompts to identify causal components.

## Key Results
- Harmful-refusal is mediated by a single, task-agnostic direction converging around mid-layers; global ablation reduces harmful-refusal from 68% to 20% while leaving harmless answers unaffected.
- Over-refusal directions are task-dependent, reside inside benign task clusters, and require more principal components (higher-dimensional), making them poorly targeted by global ablation (suppression ratio 0.67).
- Task-conditioned steering achieves a higher suppression ratio (1.57) for over-refusal with less safety degradation.
- Linear probes achieve near-perfect accuracy (>99%) in distinguishing refusal types from layer 1 onward.
- Causal patching shows refusal mechanisms are distributed across late, task-specific heads with no single bottleneck (flip rates mostly <50%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Harmful-refusal is mediated by a single, task-agnostic direction that converges in mid-layers, enabling effective global ablation.
- **Mechanism:** The refusal direction extracted via difference-in-means (DIM) from harmful-refusal vs. harmless-answered samples aligns across tasks. Steering (ablating) this direction reduces refusal rates for harmful prompts without affecting harmless answers because the signal is shared universally.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states convergence mid-layers and effective global ablation. Section 4.1 shows projection score gap peaks at layers 11 and 17; global ablation reduces harmful-refusal from 68% to 20% while harmless refusal remains 0%. Figure 1 (top) visualizes convergence of per-task harmful-refusal directions. Corpus: Arditi et al. (2024) identified a single refusal direction, providing external corroboration.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Over-refusal is task-dependent, resides within benign task clusters, and spans a higher-dimensional subspace, making global directions insufficient.
- **Mechanism:** Over-refusal samples do not form a separate cluster but overlap with their respective benign task clusters. Per-task over-refusal directions are inconsistent and require many principal components to capture variance, so a single global vector cannot selectively target them.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes task-dependence and higher-dimensional subspace. Section 4.2: centroid distances smaller inter-task vs. per-task (Figure 4); inter-task silhouette peaks earlier/stronger than per-task (Figure 5); cosine similarity between global harmful-refusal direction and over-refusal direction is only ~0.47 (Figure 6); n80 (components for 80% variance) is higher for over-refusal (Figure 8). Figure 3 (right) shows over-refusal samples (triangles) inside task clusters. Corpus evidence is weak; neighbors focus on benchmarks/mitigation, not geometry.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Harmful-refusal and over-refusal are linearly separable from early transformer layers, enabling early detection and task-conditioned interventions.
- **Mechanism:** A linear probe trained on residual-stream activations can distinguish the two refusal types with near-perfect accuracy from layer 1 onward, meaning the representational basis for targeted steering exists before task geometry consolidates.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states linear probing shows separability from early layers. Section 4.3 shows probe accuracy for refusal type reaches ~100% from layer 1 (Figure 9c), while task identity also saturates early (Figure 9a). Early separability implies intervention could be applied at early layers to selectively modulate over-refusal without disrupting harmful-refusal.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Difference-in-means (DIM) steering
  - Why needed here: Used to extract refusal directions by contrasting activations of refused vs. answered samples; enables ablation/steering experiments.
  - Quick check: Given two sets of residual activations (refused and answered), how do you compute the DIM vector and apply it to modify a new activation?
- **Concept:** Linear probing
  - Why needed here: Tests whether a target property (e.g., refusal type) is linearly decodable from activations at each layer, revealing when information becomes available.
  - Quick check: What does a high probe accuracy indicate about the representation of the target property?
- **Concept:** Causal patching
  - Why needed here: Identifies which components (e.g., attention heads) causally influence the refusal decision by swapping activations between prompts.
  - Quick check: What does a low flip rate (<50%) in causal patching imply about the distribution of the refusal mechanism?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Token embedding -> Transformer layers (attention heads + MLPs, residual stream) -> Output logits. Analysis uses final token residual activations across all layers.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Prepare dataset spanning tasks with benign, sensitive, harmful content; classify responses into over-refusal, harmful-refusal, harmless-answered using GPT-4o judge.
  2. Extract residual-stream activations at final token for every layer.
  3. Compute DIM directions per layer: harmful-refusal direction (µ_harmful_refused – µ_harmless_answered); over-refusal direction (µ_benign_refused – µ_benign_answered).
  4. Visualize with PCA; compute silhouette scores, centroid distances, explained variance to assess cluster structure.
  5. Train linear probes per layer to predict task identity and refusal types.
  6. Apply global ablation using harmful-refusal direction; measure refusal rates.
  7. Apply task-conditioned steering (SafeConstellations); compute suppression ratios.
  8. Perform causal patching on top-attributed heads under global and per-task conditions.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Global ablation: simple, requires one direction, but imprecise for over-refusal; may damage safety mechanism.
  - Task-conditioned steering: requires per-task data and more compute, but selectively suppresses over-refusal without harming harmful-refusal.
  - Steering layer choice: Mid-layers (e.g., layer 12) where task clusters form vs. late layers where final decision is made.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Global ablation reduces harmful-refusal but leaves over-refusal nearly unchanged (suppression ratio <1).
  - Linear probe accuracy for refusal type remains at chance, indicating no early separability.
  - Causal patching reveals a head with >50% flip rate, suggesting a bottleneck that contradicts distributed mechanism.
  - PCA shows over-refusal and harmless-answered samples well-separated, contradicting task-dependent embedding.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate global ablation: Extract harmful-refusal direction at layer with max projection gap (e.g., L11), apply ablation, and confirm reduced harmful-refusal rate and unchanged harmless refusal.
  2. Test task-dependence of over-refusal: For each task with over-refusal samples, compute per-task over-refusal direction and measure cosine similarity with global harmful-refusal direction; expect low similarity.
  3. Validate early separability: Train a linear probe on layer 1 activations to classify over-refusal vs. harmful-refusal; expect high accuracy (>90%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can early-layer linear probes that distinguish refusal types be used to dynamically trigger task-specific steering interventions at inference time without modifying model weights?
- Does the directional geometry of over-refusal—task-dependent directions spanning a higher-dimensional subspace—generalize to other aligned LLMs beyond LLaMA-3.1-8B?
- What is the causal contribution of mid-layer MLP neurons (as opposed to attention heads) to the formation of task-dependent over-refusal subspaces, and can editing MLP activations more precisely suppress over-refusal?
- Which specific lexical, semantic, or syntactic features of benign task prompts cause over-refusal in some tasks (e.g., sentiment analysis, translation) but not others (e.g., cryptanalysis, RAG), and can these features predict over-refusal likelihood across models?

## Limitations
- Generalizability limited to one model (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct) and five tasks; over-refusal geometry may vary elsewhere.
- Over-refusal task-dependence claim rests on internal metrics (silhouette, n80) without external corroboration.
- Linear probing depends on unreported hyperparameters; results could be unstable.
- SafeConstellations implementation not detailed, hindering exact replication.
- Causal patching uses few prompt pairs with unclear selection, risking biased flip rates.

## Confidence
- Harmful-refusal mediated by single task-agnostic direction: **High**
- Over-refusal is task-dependent, higher-dimensional: **Medium**
- Refusal types linearly separable from early layers: **High**
- Task-conditioned steering selectively reduces over-refusal: **Medium** (relies on unreplicated method)

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-model test:** Replicate DIM extraction and global ablation on at least two other aligned LLMs (e.g., Mistral-7B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B). Expect consistent task-agnostic harmful-refusal direction.
2. **Task-conditioned steering:** Implement SafeConstellations (Maskey et al., 2025) from description; apply to same dataset; verify suppression ratio >1 for over-refusal vs global ablation.
3. **Probing robustness:** Perform linear probing with multiple regularization strengths (e.g., 1e-4 to 1) and random seeds; report mean ± std accuracy for early layers to confirm early separability.
