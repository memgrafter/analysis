# findsylls: A Language-Agnostic Toolkit for Syllable-Level Speech Tokenization and Embedding

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.26292
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26292
*   **Reference Count:** 0
*   **Primary Result:** Modular recombination of neural envelopes with classical peak detection yields state-of-the-art syllable boundary F1 (69.9), demonstrating a clear accuracy-throughput trade-off against purely classical or neural pipelines.

## Executive Summary
The `findsylls` toolkit addresses fragmentation in syllable-level speech processing by unifying classical envelope-based and modern neural syllabification methods into a single, modular framework. It decouples representation extraction from segmentation logic, enabling systematic recombination of components. Benchmarking across English, Spanish, and Kono corpora reveals that applying the `peakdetect` algorithm to Sylber's neural cosine-similarity envelope significantly improves boundary fidelity over default neural configurations. The results confirm a pronounced trade-off: classical amplitude-envelope methods (e.g., SBS) offer the highest computational throughput (684x real-time) and strong nucleus detection, while neural representations incur higher cost but yield more precise boundaries. This work provides a standardized, reproducible platform for cross-lingual syllable research and tokenization.

## Method Summary
The toolkit implements a pipeline where an audio input is processed into either a 1-dimensional envelope (e.g., from energy or neural similarity traces) or a frame-level feature matrix (e.g., HuBERT embeddings). A segmentation module then consumes this cue to predict syllable intervals. Crucially, the architecture is modular: any envelope can be paired with a peak detector, and any feature matrix can be paired with a graph-based or thresholding segmenter. Performance is evaluated using a multi-granular protocol (nuclei, boundaries, full spans) with a 50ms tolerance against gold-standard TextGrids across seven diverse corpora. This design allows direct comparison of historical methods and novel recombinations on equal footing.

## Key Results
*   Recombining Sylber's cosine-similarity envelope with `peakdetect` achieved the best boundary F1 (69.9) and span F1 (47.0), outperforming Sylber's default neural segmentation.
*   The classical SBS envelope baseline attained the highest nuclei F1 (91.2) and computational throughput (684x real-time).
*   Results demonstrate a clear accuracy-throughput trade-off: neural representations improve boundary placement but reduce processing speed by two orders of magnitude compared to classical envelopes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Modular recombination of representations and segmentation algorithms yields measurable segmentation accuracy gains beyond packaged pipelines.
*   **Mechanism:** The toolkit decouples representation extraction (envelopes or neural features) from segmentation logic (e.g., `peakdetect` vs. cosine threshold). This allows applying a high-fidelity segmentation algorithm (like `peakdetect`) to a refined representation cue (like Sylber’s cosine-similarity trace), which may be more discriminative than the default segmenter used with that representation.
*   **Core assumption:** The dominant bottleneck for boundary fidelity lies in the segmentation criterion applied to a given representation, not solely in the representation itself.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   Abstract: "recombining components yields measurable gains: applying peak detection to Sylber's cosine-similarity envelope achieves the best boundary F1 (69.9)... outperforming default neural configurations."
    *   Section 4.1: Describes exporting SSL-derived traces (cosine similarity, featSSM means) as pseudo-envelopes for `peakdetect`.
    *   Corpus: Evidence is strong across seven diverse corpora (Table 2), showing consistent improvements in boundary F1 for recombined configurations.
*   **Break condition:** If the representation cue lacks clear, separable peaks corresponding to syllable nuclei, `peakdetect` may fail despite its algorithmic superiority. The gain depends on the representation having a peak-like structure amenable to the detector.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Classical amplitude-envelope methods provide a computationally efficient, language-agnostic baseline with strong nucleus detection, while neural representations trade throughput for improved boundary precision.
*   **Mechanism:** Envelope methods (e.g., SBS) compute a smoothed energy contour directly from audio, requiring minimal computation and no pretrained models. Neural feature extraction incurs significant overhead (model forward passes) but yields representations where phonetic and syllabic structure is more explicitly disentangled, leading to cleaner segmentation cues that better resolve syllable boundaries.
*   **Core assumption:** The computational cost of neural feature extraction is the primary factor reducing throughput compared to classical signal processing.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   Abstract: "clear accuracy-throughput trade-off... neural representations improving boundary fidelity at higher cost... SBS envelope baseline attains the highest nuclei F1 (91.2) and computational throughput (684x real-time)."
    *   Section 5.2: "Envelope-based baselines are consistently the most computationally efficient... neural representations incur higher cost but can yield improved boundary placement... configurations that improve boundaries and spans generally reduce throughput."
    *   Corpus: Throughput differences are stark (Table 2: SBS RTFx 684x vs. VG-HuBERT featSSM RTFx 6x). Nuclei F1 gap is small (SBS 91.2 vs. Sylber default 93.3), but boundary F1 gap is large (SBS 60.6 vs. Sylber default 63.1; recombined Sylber+`peakdetect` 69.9).
*   **Break condition:** The throughput advantage of envelopes assumes real-time audio I/O and simple CPU-bound processing. On GPU-accelerated setups or with batched neural inference, the relative gap may narrow. The boundary fidelity advantage of neural methods assumes the representation was trained on data phonetically similar to the test language; cross-lingual transfer may degrade this advantage.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** A unified, standardized evaluation framework with multi-granular metrics (nuclei, boundaries, spans) enables reproducible, cross-lingual comparison and reveals that errors compound when moving from nucleus to full span recovery.
*   **Mechanism:** By applying identical matching tolerances (50 ms) and interval evaluation logic across corpora and methods, the toolkit isolates the effect of the tokenization algorithm. The multi-granular scores show that high nucleus F1 does not guarantee high boundary or span F1, as boundary errors compound to ruin full interval recovery.
*   **Core assumption:** Syllable segmentation quality is not monolithic; performance on detecting syllable peaks (nuclei) is a different problem than accurately placing both onsets and offsets (spans).
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   Abstract: "multi-granular evaluation... Nuclei = syllable peak detection; Boundaries = inter-syllabic boundary detection; Spans = full syllable interval recovery."
    *   Section 4.1: Details the evaluation protocol with 50 ms tolerance and syllable-weighted F1.
    *   Section 5.1: "nuclei detection is substantially easier than recovering full spans... For example, the SBS envelope... attains 91.2 nuclei F1, but drops to 60.6 boundary F1 and 37.1 span F1."
    *   Corpus: Patterns hold across all seven corpora in Table 2.
*   **Break condition:** The 50 ms tolerance is arbitrary and may be too lenient or strict for specific linguistic theories or applications. Some "errors" may reflect genuine ambiguity in syllable boundary definition rather than algorithmic failure. The compounding effect presumes errors are independent; systematic biases might not compound as severely.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Modular System Design**
    *   **Why needed:** The core innovation of `findsylls` is its decoupled architecture (envelopes, features, segmentation). A new contributor must understand why this decomposition is powerful: it turns a monolithic set of "syllabifiers" into a combinatorial design space where components can be mixed to test specific hypotheses (e.g., "Is this neural segmenter good, or is its representation good?").
    *   **Quick Check:** If you wanted to test whether the `peakdetect` algorithm performs better on Sylber features or HuBERT features, what modules would you configure in `findsylls`?

*   **Concept: Amplitude Envelope & Peak Detection**
    *   **Why needed:** This is the foundation of classical, signal-driven syllabification. Understanding how an envelope (e.g., SBS) is derived from the raw waveform and how `peakdetect` identifies local maxima as syllable nuclei is crucial for using and interpreting the fastest, most language-agnostic baselines.
    *   **Quick Check:** Why might a simple RMS energy envelope fail as a syllable cue for a language with very quiet, unstressed syllables (e.g., some varieties of Spanish)?

*   **Concept: Self-Supervised Speech Representations & Cosine Similarity**
    *   **Why needed:** Neural methods like Sylber and VG-HuBERT rely on representations where adjacent frames within a syllable are more similar than frames across syllable boundaries. The greedy cosine-merging strategy explicitly uses this property. Understanding this "segment coherence" hypothesis is key to using neural feature extractors.
    *   **Quick Check:** What does a low cosine similarity between adjacent feature vectors suggest about the likelihood of a syllable boundary between them?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Input (16kHz mono WAV) -> Envelope Module (optional; SBS, Theta, neural pseudo-envelope) -> Feature Module (required for neural segmentation; MFCC, log-mel, HuBERT, Sylber, VG-HuBERT) -> Segmentation Module (`peakdetect`, MinCut, cosine_thresh, CLS-threshold) -> Syllable intervals -> Embedding Module (pooling) -> Output tokens. Evaluation Module compares intervals against gold TextGrids.
*   **Critical path for a new experiment:** `audio.wav` -> [Feature Module] -> `features.npy` -> [Segmentation Module] -> `hypothesis.TextGrid` -> [Evaluation Module] vs. `gold.TextGrid` -> metrics report. For envelope-only pipelines, skip Feature Module. For recombining, ensure the segmentation module's required input type matches the output of the previous stage (e.g., `peakdetect` needs a 1D envelope).
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Accuracy vs. Throughput:** Envelope + `peakdetect` (e.g., SBS) is fastest but has lower boundary/span F1. Neural feature + sophisticated segmenter (e.g., VG-HuBERT featSSM + MinCut) is slowest but can improve boundaries. The recombined sweet spot (Sylber cos-sim + `peakdetect`) offers a practical balance.
    *   **Generality vs. Optimality:** Classical envelopes (SBS, Theta) are language-agnostic and fast but may underfit phonetic detail. Neural representations are data-driven and can capture language-specific patterns but require relevant pretraining data and incur cost.
    *   **Granularity vs. Cost:** Token rate (tok/s) is primarily determined by the segmentation algorithm, not the feature extractor. `peakdetect` on any envelope tends to yield ~3-4 tok/s; CLS-attention thresholding yields ~5-6 tok/s (finer segmentation).
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   Sporadic, noisy nuclei from envelope: Suggests envelope smoothing parameters are inadequate for the recording's signal-to-noise ratio or prosody.
    *   Systematically late/early boundaries from neural segmenter: Indicates the segmentation criterion (e.g., cosine threshold) is misaligned with the representation's internal scale or the language's phonotactics.
    *   Catastrophically low span F1 despite decent nuclei F1: Classic sign of boundary placement errors—nuclei are found, but onsets/offsets are consistently offset by >50ms.
    *   Runtime orders of magnitude slower than expected: Likely due to loading large neural models repeatedly in a loop instead of caching features or using batched processing.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Reproduce a published baseline:** Run the Sylber default (cosine-similarity envelope + cosine-threshold segmentation) on LibriSpeech-100h. Verify nuclei F1 ≈93 and span F1 ≈46 match Table 2. This validates the end-to-end pipeline and evaluation against gold alignments.
    2.  **Perform a controlled component swap:** Using Sylber's cosine-similarity cue *only*, run segmentation with both `cosine_thresh` (default) and `peakdetect`. Isolate the effect of the segmentation algorithm. Expect boundary F1 increase from ~63 to ~70.
    3.  **Cross-lingual portability test:** Take a model pretrained on English (e.g., VG-HuBERT) and run its `featSSM+MinCut` pipeline on the Kono corpus and WikiSpanish. Compare the drop in boundary F1 from English TIMIT to these languages. This tests the toolkit's utility for under-resourced settings and quantifies representation transfer limitations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question:** How stable are segmentation F1 scores to variations in hyperparameters and implementation details across different syllabification configurations?
    *   **Basis:** The authors note that "some configurations are sensitive to implementation and tuning choices" and that they "do not claim exact reproduction for every prior system."
    *   **Why unresolved:** The paper acknowledges this sensitivity but does not measure or quantify its impact on reported scores, leaving unclear how much of the performance variation is due to arbitrary choices versus inherent algorithmic differences.
    *   **Resolving evidence:** A systematic ablation study varying key hyperparameters (e.g., peak detection thresholds, expected syllable duration) and implementation details (e.g., frame rate, smoothing) across multiple runs with fixed random seeds, reporting confidence intervals or variance in F1 scores.

*   **Question:** Which additional syllabification algorithms from the literature (e.g., sonority-based, rhythm-guided oscillators) should be prioritized for integration into findsylls to maximize cross-lingual coverage?
    *   **Basis:** The authors state that "the current release does not yet include all methods surveyed in related work" and that "expanding coverage... is a primary goal of the next iteration."
    *   **Why unresolved:** While the toolkit aims to unify existing methods, the paper does not specify which omitted algorithms are most critical for comprehensive benchmarking or how to prioritize them.
    *   **Resolving evidence:** A systematic review of syllabification methods published in the last two decades, evaluating each for (a) cross-lingual robustness claims in prior literature, (b) availability of reference implementations, and (c) potential for modular recombination within findsylls.

*   **Question:** What proportion of segmentation errors within the 50ms evaluation window reflect linguistically plausible boundary ambiguity versus true systematic mistakes across different languages?
    *   **Basis:** The authors note that "syllable boundaries are sometimes genuinely ambiguous" and that they "will manually audit a sample of errors and develop refined evaluation criteria."
    *   **Why unresolved:** The current evaluation treats all 50ms mismatches as errors, but it is unknown how many represent valid annotation variation (e.g., due to gradience, dialect, or annotation guidelines) versus clear algorithmic failures.
    *   **Resolving evidence:** A human annotation study where linguists judge a stratified sample of 50ms-mismatch errors (across languages and methods) as "plausible alternative" or "clear mistake," then report the distribution and correlate with language-specific features or algorithmic types.

*   **Question:** Why do certain representation-segmentation recombinations (e.g., Sylber cosine-similarity with peakdetect) improve boundary F1 beyond default configurations?
    *   **Basis:** Results show that applying peakdetect to Sylber's cosine-similarity envelope yields the best boundary F1 (69.9), exceeding Sylber's default cosine-threshold segmentation (63.1). The paper demonstrates recombination benefits but does not analyze the underlying reasons.
    *   **Why unresolved:** The paper presents empirical gains from recombination but offers no investigation into the representational properties (e.g., envelope shape, peak prominence, similarity matrix coherence) that make peakdetect more effective for certain cues.
    *   **Resolving evidence:** An analysis correlating segmentation performance with quantitative properties of the time-series cues (e.g., peak-to-valley ratios, spectral flatness, dynamic range) across multiple languages, coupled with visual inspection of failure cases to identify patterns where peakdetect outperforms thresholding.

## Limitations
*   **Cross-lingual Generalization:** The neural representation advantage is demonstrated on English, Spanish, and a very small Kono corpus (0.07h). Claims about robust cross-lingual transfer, especially to truly under-resourced languages, remain uncertain.
*   **Evaluation Metric Arbitrariness:** The 50ms boundary matching tolerance is a standard but arbitrary choice. Performance rankings could shift with stricter/looser tolerances, and some "errors" may reflect genuine linguistic boundary ambiguity rather than algorithmic failure.
*   **Throughput Context:** The stark accuracy-throughput trade-off (e.g., 684x vs. 6x real-time) is measured on CPU. The gap would likely narrow significantly with GPU-accelerated batched neural inference, altering the practical trade-off landscape.

## Confidence
*   **Modular Recombination Gains:** High. Consistently observed improvement across multiple corpora when applying `peakdetect` to Sylber's cosine-similarity envelope, with clear mechanistic explanation.
*   **Accuracy-Throughput Trade-off:** High. Stark, reproducible differences in Table 2 between classical envelope (fast, decent nuclei) and neural (slow, better boundaries) pipelines, supported by computational analysis.
*   **Multi-Granular Error Compounding:** Medium. Pattern holds strongly across presented results, but the underlying assumption of independent errors requires deeper error analysis to confirm.
*   **Cross-lingual Portability of Neural Models:** Medium-Low. Evidence is limited to two related languages and one tiny corpus; transfer to phonetically distant languages is an open question.

## Next Checks
1.  **Stress-Test Recombination on Diverse Languages:** Systematically apply the winning configuration (Sylber cos-sim + `peakdetect`) to at least three new, typologically diverse low-resource languages with small, manually annotated test sets. This directly tests the portability of the "modular gain" finding beyond the initial Indo-European focus.
2.  **Tolerance Sensitivity Analysis:** For the top 3 performing configurations per corpus, re-run evaluation with matching tolerances of 25ms, 50ms (original), and 100ms. Quantify how system rankings change. This assesses the robustness of conclusions to the arbitrary evaluation parameter.
3.  **GPU Throughput Re-measurement:** Replicate the throughput experiment (Table 2) using a standard GPU with batched feature extraction for all neural pipelines. Report the new real-time factors (RTFx) and recalculate the accuracy-throughput Pareto frontier. This validates whether the trade-off is as severe in common modern hardware setups.
