# Block-Sample MAC-Bayes Generalization Bounds
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12605
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12605
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Block-sample MAC-Bayes bounds can be finite and achieve O(n^{-1/2}) decay where standard PAC-Bayes bounds diverge; optimal block size depends on growth exponent γ; a high-probability O(n^{-1/2}) PAC-Bayes bound is impossible.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the problem of vacuous PAC-Bayes generalization bounds by introducing block-sample MAC-Bayes bounds. These bounds partition the training data into disjoint blocks and replace the single KL divergence term in the standard PAC-Bayes bound with a sum of KL divergences between the posterior averaged over other blocks and a fixed prior, computed separately for each block. This modification yields bounds that remain finite and decay as O(n^{-1/2}) even for deterministic algorithms where standard PAC-Bayes bounds become infinite, as shown in a Gaussian mean estimation example with truncated loss. The optimal block size is governed by the growth rate γ of the summed divergences: constant block size is optimal when γ<1, while a block size linear in n is best when γ>1. However, the paper also establishes a negative result: if the MAC-Bayes bound attains an O(n^{-1/2}) rate, then no PAC-Bayes bound with logarithmic dependence on the confidence parameter δ can achieve a faster-than-O(1/log n) decay, highlighting a fundamental trade-off between rate and high-probability guarantees.

## Method Summary
The proposed block-sample MAC-Bayes bound operates by dividing the training sample S of size n into J = n/m disjoint blocks of equal size m (assuming n divisible by m). For each block S_j, the algorithm's posterior P_W|S is averaged over all other blocks to define P_W|S_j = E_{S_{-j}}[P_W|S]. The bound then uses the sum of expected KL divergences ∑_j E_{S_j}[D(P_W|S_j || Q_W)] in place of the full-data divergence. By optimizing a free parameter λ, the bound upperbounds the expected difference d(EP_{W|S}ℓ, EP_{W|S}L) for any convex comparator d, with a specific moment function Φ_m derived from the loss properties. The resulting bound depends only on the block-wise divergences and the moment term, which can be controlled by the choice of m. This approach can yield finite, non-vacuous bounds even for deterministic learners, because the block-wise posterior averaging reduces the KL growth with n.

The block-size m controls the trade-off between the two terms: the moment term shrinks as m grows, while the sum of divergences typically increases with m but sublinearly. The overall decay rate is O(√(m^{γ-1}/n)) if the sum of divergences scales as O(m^γ / n). This leads to the dichotomy described in the key results. The method applies for any convex comparator d; common choices (Catoni’s C_β, binary KL, difference) specialize the bound to various forms, including direct bounds on the generalization gap or in-probability bounds.

## Key Results
- The block-sample MAC-Bayes bound can be finite and decay as O(1/√n) for a Gaussian mean estimation task with a truncated quadratic loss, whereas the standard PAC-Bayes bound is infinite, demonstrating the method's ability to avoid vacuity.
- The optimal block size m is determined by the growth exponent γ of the summed KL divergences: constant m (e.g., m=1) is optimal when γ < 1; linear scaling m = Θ(n) is optimal when γ > 1; for γ = 1 the rate is independent of m.
- It is impossible to construct a PAC-Bayes bound that simultaneously has logarithmic dependence on δ and converges at rate O(1/√n); any such bound would be violated by a carefully designed overfitting algorithm that produces a large gap with probability 1/(n log n).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper provides three complementary perspectives on why the block-sample approach yields non-vacuous bounds and how the optimal block size emerges.

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Partitioning the training data into blocks and evaluating posterior/prior KL divergence on each block separately can yield finite, non-vacuous bounds where standard PAC-Bayes bounds are infinite.

**Mechanism:** The bound (Equation 6) replaces the single divergence term `D(P_W|S || Q_W)` with a sum `∑_j E_{S_j}[D(P_W|S_j || Q_W)]`, where `S_j` are disjoint blocks. This changes the dependence from the full dataset's KL structure to block-wise structure, which can be smaller for certain algorithms (e.g., deterministic mean estimation).

**Core assumption:** The algorithm's posterior `P_W|S` is such that its marginalization over other blocks `P_W|S_j` has a KL divergence to `Q_W` that does not grow with `n` for fixed block size `m`.

**Evidence:** Theorem 1 formally derives the bound; the abstract emphasizes the use of "divergence terms that only depend on subsets (or blocks) of the training data".

**Break condition:** If `n` is not an integer multiple of `m`, the exact block partition must be defined carefully; some analyses assume divisibility and may need adjustment.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The optimal block size `m` depends on the growth rate `γ` of the summed divergences `∑_j E_{S_j}[D(P_W|S_j || Q_W)] = O(m^γ / n)`.

**Mechanism:** The bound's rate is `O(√(m^{γ-1} / n))`. Minimizing this over `m = Θ(n^α)` yields `α* = 0` (constant `m`) if `γ < 1`, and `α* = 1` (`m` linear in `n`) if `γ > 1`.

**Core assumption:** The divergence sum's growth behavior satisfies Equation (15) with some `γ ≥ 0`. This must be verified per problem.

**Evidence:** Section 5 discusses the dichotomy and presents Table 1; Section 4 shows that for the Gaussian example, `γ = 1` and constant `m` is optimal.

**Break condition:** If `γ` is unknown or depends on `n` in a way not captured by `O(m^γ / n)`, the tradeoff analysis may fail or require numerical search.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** A high-probability (PAC-Bayes) version with logarithmic `δ`-dependence and `O(n^{-1/2})` convergence is impossible whenever the MAC-Bayes bound achieves `O(n^{-1/2})`.

**Mechanism:** Construction of a learning algorithm (Section D) that with probability `ϕ = 1/(n log n)` overfits drastically, causing the instant divergence sum `∑_j D(P_W|S_j || Q_W)` to be `Θ(log n)` on that event. A PAC-Bayes bound of the form `gen ≤ B_n + f(1/δ)` with `B_n = o(1/f(n log n))` would be violated by setting `δ = ϕ`.

**Core assumption:** The overfitting event has sufficiently small probability (`ϕ`) but large enough gap to break any such PAC-Bayes bound. The divergence calculation on that event is correct (Equations 26-28).

**Evidence:** Theorem 2 states and proves the impossibility; the abstract summarizes: "it is not possible to establish a PAC-Bayes bound which ... vanishes with a rate faster than `O(1/log n)` whenever the proposed MAC-Bayes bound vanishes with rate `O(n^{-1/2})`."

**Break condition:** The impossibility holds for any nondecreasing `f` and `B_n = o(1/f(n log n))`. If one allows `f` to grow faster than logarithmic, or `B_n` to decay slower, the result may not apply.

## Foundational Learning
### PAC-Bayes vs. MAC-Bayes
- **Why needed:** The paper contrasts high-probability PAC-Bayes bounds (which hold uniformly over the posterior) with expectation MAC-Bayes bounds (which control the expected generalization gap). The block-sample bound is a MAC-Bayes generalization, and the impossibility result highlights a trade-off between convergence rate and confidence dependence.
- **Quick check:** What is the key difference between bounding `E[generalization gap]` (MAC) and bounding the gap with high probability (PAC)? How does this affect the `δ` term's role in the bound?

### Block-wise posterior averaging `P_W|S_j`
- **Why needed:** `P_W|S_j` is defined as `E_{S_{-j}}[P_W|S]`, the posterior averaged over all other blocks. This construction makes the KL divergence in the bound depend only on block `S_j` and is crucial for the derivation of Theorem 1.
- **Quick check:** Why is `P_W|S_j` not simply the posterior conditioned on the smaller dataset `S_j`? How does averaging over other blocks change the KL divergence calculation?

### Comparator functions `d(r,s)`
- **Why needed:** The bound holds for any convex comparator `d`. Specific choices (Catoni `C_β`, binary KL `kl`, difference `s-r`) yield different forms for the generalization error, e.g., directly bounding `gen` with difference, or an in-probability bound with `C_β`.
- **Quick check:** For bounded loss in [0,1], which comparator yields the tightest bound on `gen` according to Corollary 1 and why? What assumption does `kl` lose compared to `C_β`?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
`S, Q_W, d → Partition S into J blocks S_1,...,S_J → Compute P_W|S_j = E_{S_{-j}}[P_W|S] for each j → Compute block divergences D_j = E_{S_j}[D(P_W|S_j||Q_W)] → Compute moment Φ_m(λm/n) → Assemble: (n/m) log Φ_m + (1/λ)∑_j D_j → Optimize λ → Bound on E[d(EP_{W|S}ℓ, EP_{W|S}L)]`

### Critical path
1. **Block size selection.** Must analyze growth `γ` of divergence terms (Equation 15). Requires understanding of algorithm's sensitivity to data subsets.
2. **Validator choice `d` and `Φ_m`.** For bounded loss, `d=C_β` gives clean bound (Corollary 1). For subgaussian, `d=s-r` yields variance-like bound (Corollary 2).
3. **Prior `Q_W`.** Need choice that makes `D(P_W|S_j || Q_W)` small and computable. Gaussian priors common for Gaussian posteriors.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Finite vs. infinite bounds:** Original PAC-Bayes may be infinite (vacuous) for deterministic algorithms; block-sample can be finite if `m` avoids full data dependence.
- **Rate vs. `δ`:** MAC-Bayes gives `O(n^{-1/2})` possible, but cannot achieve PAC-Bayes style `O(1/log n)` with log `δ` (Theorem 2). Choose MAC-Bayes for faster rates when high-probability not essential.
- **Data dependence:** The bound depends on `P_Z` via the divergences. It is tighter if `P_Z` is known, but may be uncomputable if `P_Z` is unknown. Must upper-bound divergences using algorithm properties.

### Failure signatures
- **Divergence blowup:** If `E_{S_j}[D(P_W|S_j || Q_W)]` grows faster than `O(m)` for any `m < n`, the bound may not beat PAC-Bayes. Check via Equation 15.
- **Impossible PAC-Bayes:** If you need a high-probability bound with `log(1/δ)` and fast `n` rate, Theorem 2 says it's generally impossible when MAC rate is `Θ(n^{-1/2})`. You must accept slower rate (`O(1/log n)`) or weaker `δ` dependence.
- **Non-divisible `n`:** The theorem assumes `n` divisible by `m`. If not, adjust block sizes (last block larger) and check if analysis holds.

## Limitations
- The block-sample bound’s tightness heavily depends on the problem-specific exponent γ, which can be difficult to compute for complex learners such as deep neural networks.
- The theoretical analysis assumes an exact partition of the data into equal‑sized blocks; when `n` is not divisible by `m`, the stated rates may no longer hold without adjustments.
- The bound provides only an expectation (MAC‑Bayes) guarantee and does not yield high‑probability (uniform) statements, which may be required in safety‑critical applications.
