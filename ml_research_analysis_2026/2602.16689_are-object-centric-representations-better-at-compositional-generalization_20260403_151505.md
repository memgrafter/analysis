# Are Object-Centric Representations Better At Compositional Generalization?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16689
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16689
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Object-centric representations improve compositional generalization on harder tasks under constrained compute or data, while dense models match performance on easier tasks only with substantially more downstream resources.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates whether object-centric (OC) visual representations enhance compositional generalization compared to dense representations. Using a controlled visual question answering (VQA) benchmark spanning three synthetic datasets (CLEVRTex, Super-CLEVR, MOVi-C), the authors compare dense pretrained encoders (DINOv2, SigLIP2) with their OC counterparts (DINOSAURv2, SigLIPSAUR2). Training diversity is systematically varied (easy, medium, hard) to test generalization to unseen object property combinations. Results indicate OC models consistently outperform dense models on more challenging generalization scenarios, particularly when downstream compute is limited or training data is scarce. Dense models can achieve comparable performance on easier tasks but require significantly more computational resources during fine-tuning. The study concludes that OC representations offer superior sample efficiency and compositional generalization under practical constraints.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a VQA benchmark tailored for compositional generalization. They finetune pretrained vision encoders (dense and OC variants) on subsets of each dataset that control the diversity of observed object property combinations. After fine-tuning, models are evaluated on held-out combinations requiring generalization. The core comparison is between representations derived from standard contrastive or self-supervised vision models versus those from models that additionally produce object-centric slot representations via unsupervised segmentation. Performance is measured across varying training set sizes and downstream model scales to assess compute and data efficiency.

## Key Results
- OC models (DINOSAURv2, SigLIPSAUR2) significantly outperform dense baselines (DINOv2, SigLIP2) on harder generalization settings where training data has lower diversity.
- Dense models can match OC performance on easier generalization tasks but require 5-10x more downstream compute (larger VQA heads, more training steps).
- OC representations are more sample-efficient, achieving stronger generalization with fewer training images across all datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not provide a dedicated mechanistic analysis but implies that OC representations inherently disentangle object properties (shape, color, material) into separate slots. This structure aligns with the compositional nature of the test questions, where unseen property combinations must be recombined. Dense representations, while rich, mix features globally, making it harder to isolate and recombine attributes without interference, especially under data-scarce conditions. The segmentation process in OC models may act as a built-in inductive bias for part-whole decomposition, facilitating zero-shot recombination of learned attributes.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept 1: Object-Centric Representations**  
*Why needed:* Provide discrete, interpretable slots for objects, enabling explicit recombination of attributes.  
*Quick check:* Can the representation be segmented into per-object feature vectors without supervision?

**Concept 2: Compositional Generalization**  
*Why needed:* Tests ability to recombine learned concepts (e.g., colors, shapes) in novel ways, a hallmark of human-like reasoning.  
*Quick check:* Does the evaluation require generalizing to unseen combinations of training attributes?

**Concept 3: Unsupervised Segmentation**  
*Why needed:* OC models rely on segmenting scenes into objects without labels, forming the basis for slot-based representations.  
*Quick check:* Does the model produce a mask or bounding box for each object slot in an unsupervised manner?

**Concept 4: Vision-Language Pretraining**  
*Why needed:* Dense baselines (SigLIP2) and some OC models (SigLIPSAUR2) build on contrastive image-text pretraining, which may affect feature compositionality.  
*Quick check:* Is the encoder initialized from a model trained on image-text pairs?

**Concept 5: Slot Attention**  
*Why needed:* A common mechanism in OC models to iteratively refine object slots via attention, enabling binding of features to entities.  
*Quick check:* Does the architecture include iterative cross-attention between a set of slots and image features?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**  
Input Image -> Dense Encoder (ResNet/ViT backbone) -> Global Feature Map -> VQA Head  
Input Image -> OC Encoder (Backbone + Segmentation Module) -> Object Slots (set of vectors) -> pooled slot features -> VQA Head

**Critical Path:**  
For OC models, the critical addition is the segmentation module (e.g., slot attention with mask decoder) between backbone and slot extraction. This path introduces object-level discretization absent in dense models. The downstream VQA head then processes either global features or aggregated slot features.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *OC vs. Dense:* OC adds computational overhead for segmentation but reduces downstream compute needs by providing structured features. Dense is simpler but may require larger downstream models to capture compositional structure.
- *Segmentation Quality:* OC performance hinges on segmentation fidelity; errors propagate to VQA. Dense avoids this but may suffer from feature entanglement.
- *Representation Granularity:* OC slots naturally handle a variable number of objects; dense representations must implicitly detect and reason about objects.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- OC models failing on complex scenes with occlusions or similar-looking objects (segmentation collapse).
- Dense models overfitting to spurious correlations in training combinations, failing on novel attribute conjunctions.
- Both models degrading when test scenes have more objects than seen during training (OC may have fixed slot counts).

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. Evaluate on real-world VQA datasets (e.g., GQA, VQA v2) to test if advantages transfer beyond synthetic domains.
2. Scale the downstream VQA head dramatically (e.g., 10x parameters) to see if dense models can overcome their representational gap given enough capacity.
3. Introduce relational reasoning questions (e.g., "What is to the left of the red cube?") to test if OC benefits extend beyond attribute combination to spatial relations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do OC representations perform on real-world, noisy images with complex backgrounds and occlusions?
- Does the advantage of OC representations hold for other downstream tasks beyond VQA, such as embodied reasoning or robotic manipulation?
- Can the benefits be achieved with more efficient OC architectures that reduce segmentation overhead?
- How do different unsupervised segmentation objectives affect downstream compositional generalization?

## Limitations
- Experiments are limited to synthetic datasets (CLEVRTex, Super-CLEVR, MOVi-C), which may not capture the diversity and noise of real-world visual scenes.
- Compositional generalization is tested only on attribute conjunction tasks; other forms like relational or hierarchical reasoning remain unexplored.
- OC results are tied to specific segmentation-based architectures (DINOSAUR, SigLIPSAUR); performance may vary with alternative OC approaches.
- Downstream VQA models are relatively small; conclusions might not hold for very large or transformer-based downstream architectures.

## Confidence
- *OC representations improve compositional generalization on hard tasks under constrained compute*: **Medium** (validated on synthetic benchmarks; real-world transfer uncertain).
- *Dense models can match OC on easy tasks but require more downstream compute*: **High** (consistent across three datasets and multiple encoder pairs).
- *OC representations are more sample-efficient*: **High** (empirically demonstrated with varying training set sizes).

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the study on real-world VQA benchmarks (e.g., GQA, VQA v2) with natural images to assess domain transfer.
2. Test the impact of downstream model scale by training much larger VQA heads (e.g., >100M parameters) for both representation types.
3. Extend the benchmark to include relational and comparative questions (e.g., CLEVR-style) to evaluate broader compositional generalization.
