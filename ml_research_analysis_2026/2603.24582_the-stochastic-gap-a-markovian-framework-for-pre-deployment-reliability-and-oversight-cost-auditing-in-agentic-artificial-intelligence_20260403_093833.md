# The Stochastic Gap: A Markovian Framework for Pre-Deployment Reliability and Oversight-Cost Auditing in Agentic Artificial Intelligence

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.24582
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24582
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: A Markovian framework using event logs quantifies unreliability via state-action blind mass and couples reliability with oversight cost through an entropy-based gate, enabling pre-deployment auditing.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces a Markovian framework to audit the reliability and oversight costs of agentic AI systems before deployment. By analyzing historical event logs, the method identifies statistically unsupported state-action pairs (blind mass) and escalates ambiguous or risky states to human review via an entropy- and risk-weighted gate. Evaluation on a purchase-to-pay process log shows that refining the state abstraction significantly increases detected blind mass, and a surrogate based on maximum policy confidence predicts step accuracy within 3.4 percentage points. The approach yields a quantitative trade-off envelope between safe case completion and human oversight burden, providing a measurable autonomy envelope from historical data.

## Method Summary
The framework treats a business process as a Markov decision process inferred from an event log. It first abstracts raw events into states and estimates a policy \(\hat{\pi}(a|s)\) from state-action frequencies. For a given support threshold \(\tau\), it computes the state-action blind mass \(B^{\text{SA}}_{\pi,n}(\tau)\), representing the probability mass of state-action pairs with fewer than \(\tau\) historical occurrences—these are deemed high-risk for autonomous execution. An escalation gate combines three criteria: low support (\(N(s) < \tau\)), high next-step entropy \(H(\hat{\pi}(\cdot|s)) > h_0\), and high risk weight \(w(s) > w_0\). Event-level autonomy \(A_{\text{event}}\) and case-level autonomy \(A_{\text{case}}\) are derived from the gate, with the latter accounting for multiple escalations along a trajectory. A theoretical surrogate \(m(s) = \max_a \hat{\pi}(a|s)\) is used to approximate step-level correctness. Applied to the BPI 2019 log, the method demonstrates how varying thresholds produces a reliability-cost envelope.

## Key Results
- Refining the state space from 42 to 668 states increased the state-action blind mass from 0.0165 at \(\tau=50\) to 0.1253 at \(\tau=1000\), revealing substantially more unsupported, high-risk decision points.
- The surrogate \(m(s)\) tracked realized autonomous step accuracy on a held-out chronological split with a maximum deviation of 3.4 percentage points, validating its predictive power.
- The framework quantifies the trade-off: stricter escalation gates (higher \(\tau\), \(h_0\), or \(w_0\)) improve safe case completion but raise expected human oversight burden, yielding a measurable autonomy envelope from historical data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Statistical Support for Next-Step Decisions
The state-action blind mass \(B^{\text{SA}}_{\pi,n}(\tau)\) quantifies the deployment probability mass over state-action pairs \((s,a)\) that fall below a historical support threshold \(\tau\). This mass represents trajectories that are statistically unsupported and thus high-risk for autonomous execution. The core assumption is that the historical event log provides a sufficiently representative sample of the true state-action distribution under normal operation. Evidence: refining the state space increases blind mass, indicating hidden risk; Eq. (8) defines \(B\) and Eq. (11) shows it as a coverage ceiling for step-level correctness. Break condition: fails if the log is non-stationary or if state abstraction misses critical decision variables, leading to underestimation.

### Mechanism 2: Compounding Ambiguity and Consequence
A human-in-the-loop gate \(G_{\tau,h_0,w_0}(s)\) escalates states with (\(N(s) < \tau\)), high next-step entropy \(H(\hat{\pi}(\cdot|s)) > h_0\), or high risk weight \(w(s) > w_0\). Autonomy differs at event vs case level because multiple escalations compound along a trajectory. Core assumptions: Shannon entropy and the proxy risk score \(w(s)\) adequately capture local ambiguity and decision consequence. Evidence: the entropy gate produces a gap between event-level (72.2%) and case-level (49.6%) autonomy; the surrogate \(m(s)\) correlates with accuracy. Break condition: fails if entropy is miscalibrated due to small \(N(s)\) or if \(w(s)\) is unrelated to actual business criticality.

## Foundational Learning
- **Markov Decision Process (MDP) Abstraction**: Represents the business process as states and actions with transition probabilities. *Why needed*: Enables statistical analysis of sequential decisions. *Quick check*: Verify transition probabilities sum to 1 for each state.
- **State-Action Blind Mass**: The aggregate probability of state-action pairs with insufficient historical support (\(\tau\)). *Why needed*: Identifies regions of the state space where autonomous execution is statistically unjustified. *Quick check*: Compute \(B^{\text{SA}}_{\pi,n}(\tau)\) for a small \(\tau\) and ensure it increases with \(\tau\).
- **Shannon Entropy as Ambiguity Metric**: Measures uncertainty in the policy’s next-step distribution. *Why needed*: Flags states where the model is indecisive, warranting human review. *Quick check*: For a state with uniform action distribution, entropy should be \(\log |A|\).
- **Risk Weighting**: A scalar \(w(s)\) attached to states to reflect consequence severity. *Why needed*: Prioritizes escalation for high-impact decisions beyond mere ambiguity. *Quick check*: Ensure risk weights are assigned consistently (e.g., based on domain rules).
- **Human-in-the-Loop Gate**: A logical OR of conditions (support, entropy, risk) to trigger escalation. *Why needed*: Balances autonomy with safety by combining multiple risk signals. *Quick check*: With \(\tau\) large, nearly all states should be escalated; with \(h_0\) low, high-entropy states escalate.
- **Theoretical Surrogate \(m(s)\)**: The maximum policy probability for a state, used to approximate step-level correctness. *Why needed*: Provides a cheap, pre-deployment estimate of reliability without running the agent. *Quick check*: \(m(s)\) should correlate with observed accuracy in held-out data.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Event Log -> State Abstraction -> Frequency Counting -> Policy Estimation -> Blind Mass Calculator -> Entropy & Risk Evaluator -> Gate -> Autonomy Metrics

Critical path: The computation flows from raw logs to state-action frequencies; those feed into blind mass (which depends on \(\tau\)), entropy, and risk; the gate then combines these to produce event-level and case-level autonomy metrics. The surrogate \(m(s)\) is derived directly from the policy.

Design tradeoffs:
- State abstraction granularity: finer states increase blind mass detection but may cause data sparsity; coarser states may miss critical distinctions.
- Threshold selection (\(\tau\), \(h_0\), \(w_0\)): higher values increase safety but reduce autonomy and increase oversight cost; requires business-driven calibration.
- Risk weight assignment: heuristic vs data-driven; subjective weights may not align with actual business impact.

Failure signatures:
- Blind mass near 0 at all \(\tau\): likely overfitted or unrealistic state abstraction; model may be too confident.
- Entropy consistently high: policy may be poorly estimated due to insufficient data or inherent stochasticity.
- Discrepancy between event-level and case-level autonomy small (<5%): suggests escalations are concentrated early in cases, not compounding; may indicate gating too lenient.
- Surrogate \(m(s)\) poorly correlates with held-out accuracy: model miscalibration or distribution shift.

First experiments:
1. **State abstraction sensitivity**: Vary the state definition (e.g., coarse 42-state vs refined 668-state) and plot blind mass vs \(\tau\) to quantify impact on perceived risk.
2. **Threshold sweep**: Systematically vary \(\tau\), \(h_0\), and \(w_0\) to generate a 3D autonomy-oversight trade-off surface; identify Pareto frontier.
3. **Surrogate validation**: Split the log chronologically, train on early segment, compute \(m(s)\) on later segment, and compare to actual autonomous step accuracy; measure deviation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalization across domains**: The framework is validated only on a single purchase-to-pay log. Its effectiveness on other agentic domains remains to be demonstrated.
- **Risk weight specification**: The current heuristic for \(w(s)\) lacks data-driven grounding. Can risk weights be learned from historical outcomes or expert feedback?
- **Non-stationary environments**: The stationarity assumption may be violated in real-world processes. How does the blind mass and autonomy envelope evolve under process drift?
- **State abstraction impact**: The paper shows that refining states increases blind mass, but what is the optimal abstraction? Could automated state merging or splitting improve the trade-off?
- **Surrogate accuracy**: The surrogate \(m(s)\) deviates up to 3.4 percentage points. Can we develop a more accurate pre-deployment reliability estimator?

## Limitations
- Single-domain validation: Results are based solely on the BPI 2019 purchase-to-pay log; generalizability to other agentic domains remains unproven.
- Stationarity assumption: The framework assumes the historical log represents a stationary process; process drift or rare catastrophic failures could invalidate blind mass estimates.
- Subjective risk weighting: The risk weight \(w(s)\) is a domain-specific heuristic without ground-truth business impact labels, leaving its calibration unvalidated.

## Confidence
- Blind mass as risk metric: Medium (shown via state-space refinement but no external replication)
- Entropy/risk gate compounding: Medium (empirically demonstrated but relies on unverified assumptions about entropy and risk surrogates)
- Overall reliability-cost coupling: Low (theoretical envelope demonstrated on one dataset; requires cross-industry validation)

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-dataset replication**: Apply the framework to at least two additional event logs from distinct domains (e.g., IT service management, manufacturing) to verify blind mass trends and autonomy envelopes.
2. **Temporal robustness test**: Recompute blind mass on chronological sliding windows of the BPI log to quantify sensitivity to process drift; a steep increase over time would challenge the stationarity assumption.
3. **Human-judgment alignment study**: For a stratified sample of escalated states, collect domain expert ratings on perceived ambiguity and criticality; correlate with entropy and \(w(s)\) to validate the gate's operational surrogates.
