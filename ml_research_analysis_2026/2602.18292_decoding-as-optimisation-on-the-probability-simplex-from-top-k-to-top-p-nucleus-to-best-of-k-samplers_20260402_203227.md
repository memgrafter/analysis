# Decoding as Optimisation on the Probability Simplex: From Top-K to Top-P (Nucleus) to Best-of-K Samplers

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.18292
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18292
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** A unified optimization framework for decoding leads to a new "Best-of-K" sampler that substantially improves accuracy (e.g., +18.6% on MATH500 at high temperature) with modest compute overhead.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a principled foundation for language model decoding by unifying existing strategies (greedy, softmax, top-K, top-P, sparsemax) as solutions to a regularized convex optimization problem over the probability simplex. The framework casts decoding as maximizing a linear score (the logits) minus a convex regularizer, subject to support constraints. For decoders without closed-form solutions, the authors advocate using mirror ascent with entropic geometry (KL divergence) as a solver. The key novel contribution is the Best-of-K (BoK) sampler, which optimizes a "coverage utility" to maximize the probability of including at least one high-quality token within a fixed K-sample budget, while anchoring to the model distribution via a KL penalty. Empirically, BoK yields large accuracy gains on math and coding benchmarks, especially at high sampling temperatures, with minimal additional computation.

## Method Summary
The core method is a convex optimization perspective on per-token decoding: select a distribution \( q \) over the vocabulary by solving \( \max_{q \in C_t} \langle q, s_t \rangle - \lambda \Omega(q) \), where \( s_t \) are the logits, \( \Omega \) a convex regularizer, \( \lambda \) a strength parameter, and \( C_t \) a constraint set (e.g., top-K support). This recovers known decoders via specific choices of \( \Omega \) and \( C_t \). For objectives without closed-form solutions (like the proposed Best-of-K), the authors employ mirror ascent with the entropic potential (negative Shannon entropy). This yields iterative, multiplicative updates \( q^{j+1} \propto q^j \odot \exp(\eta \nabla f(q^j)) \) that naturally respect simplex constraints. The BoK regularizer explicitly maximizes a coverage term \( \sum_v w_t(v) [1 - (1-q(v))^K] \), where \( w_t(v) \) weights token importance, encouraging the distribution to allocate probability to valuable but individually unlikely tokens to improve multi-sample outcomes.

## Key Results
*   On MATH500 with Qwen2.5-Math-7B at temperature \( \tau=0.9 \), Best-of-K improves accuracy from 53.0% to 71.6% (+18.6%).
*   Similar strong gains are observed on GPQA (+6.06%) and HumanEval (+14.64%) at high temperatures.
*   The improvement is robust across a range of hyperparameters (\( \lambda, \beta, K \)) and incurs only modest compute overhead (approximately 1 second for 5 mirror ascent steps per token).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Unification via Simplex Optimization
*   **Claim:** Standard decoding strategies are implicit solutions to a convex optimization problem on the probability simplex.
*   **Mechanism:** Decoding selects \( q \) by maximizing \( \langle q, s_t \rangle - \lambda\Omega(q) \) subject to \( q \in C_t \). The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions derived from this problem dictate which tokens receive positive probability, unifying the behavior of greedy, softmax, top-K, top-P, and sparsemax.
*   **Core Assumption:** Per-token distribution selection can be decoupled from sequential context; standard heuristics correspond to explicit objectives with specific \( \Omega \) and \( C_t \).
*   **Evidence Anchors:** The paper formally derives the "MASTER PROBLEM" and KKT conditions (Equation 5), showing how each decoder satisfies them. Independent work framing softmax as a variational principle provides external corroboration.
*   **Break Condition:** A mismatch between intended behavior and the chosen \( \Omega/C_t \) yields incorrect recovery (e.g., using \( \ell_2 \) regularization without sparsity constraints does not produce sparsemax).

### Mechanism 2: Mirror Ascent for Constrained Geometry
*   **Claim:** Mirror ascent with an entropic potential (KL divergence) is the correct iterative solver for the simplex-constrained problem when no closed form exists.
*   **Mechanism:** Unlike projected gradient ascent (Euclidean geometry), mirror ascent uses a Bregman divergence \( D_\psi \) induced by a convex potential \( \psi \). Choosing \( \psi(q)=\sum q \log q \) yields KL divergence and a multiplicative update \( q^{j+1} \propto q^j \odot \exp(\eta \nabla f(q^j)) \), which preserves non-negativity and normalization automatically.
*   **Core Assumption:** The natural geometry for navigating probability distributions is given by KL divergence, not Euclidean distance.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** The paper derives the entropic mirror ascent update (Equation 13) and contrasts it with projected gradient ascent's \( \ell_2 \)-subproblem, highlighting the geometric mismatch.
*   **Break Condition:** Using an unsuitable Bregman divergence (e.g., non-Legendre or domain-mismatched \( \psi \)) can violate simplex constraints or cause slow convergence.

### Mechanism 3: Coverage Utility in Best-of-K
*   **Claim:** BoK improves multi-sample pipelines (e.g., self-consistency) by directly optimizing the probability of covering at least one high-quality token within K draws, via a coverage regularizer.
*   **Mechanism:** The probability token \( v \) appears at least once in K samples is \( 1-(1-q(v))^K \). BoK maximizes \( \langle q, s_t \rangle - \lambda \text{KL}(q||p_t) + \beta \sum_v w_t(v)[1-(1-q(v))^K] \). This anchors to the model (\( \text{KL} \)) while spreading mass to valuable tokens (coverage term), whose marginal benefit \( K(1-q(v))^{K-1} \) naturally declines as \( q(v) \) grows, promoting diversity.
*   **Core Assumption:** The relevant objective for multi-sample decoding is set-level coverage, not per-token likelihood; the decoder can be optimized upfront to serve this downstream goal.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** The paper derives the BoK objective (Equation 17) and gradient (Equation 21). Empirical results (Tables 1-3) show large gains at high temperatures with ~1s overhead. Neighboring work on multi-sample objectives (e.g., MBR) indirectly supports this design space.
*   **Break Condition:** Poor choice of coverage weights \( w_t(v) \) or excessive \( \beta \) relative to \( \lambda \degrades quality; gains are minimal at low temperatures where the model is already peaky.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Convex Optimization on the Simplex**
    *   **Why needed:** The entire framework solves \( \max_{q \in \Delta} \langle q, s_t \rangle - \lambda\Omega(q) \). Understanding convexity, constraints, and KKT conditions is essential to derive existing decoders and design new ones.
    *   **Quick check:** Why does the KKT condition for an *inactive* token (\( q(v)=0 \)) take the form \( s_t(v) - \lambda \partial\Omega/\partial q(v) \leq \eta \) (inequality) rather than equality? What does this imply about the token's score relative to active tokens?

*   **Concept: Bregman Divergences & Mirror Ascent**
    *   **Why needed:** Provides the algorithmic machinery to solve the master problem when closed forms are unavailable (e.g., BoK). It explains why multiplicative updates (\( q \propto q \odot \exp(g) \)) naturally preserve probability constraints.
    *   **Quick check:** If you used \( \ell_2 \) projection (projected gradient ascent) instead of mirror ascent with KL, what specific numerical issue might arise near the simplex boundary when \( q(v) \) is very small?

*   **Concept: Multi-Sample Decoding Objectives**
    *   **Why needed:** BoK explicitly targets a pipeline-level goal (coverage in K samples) rather than per-step likelihood. This shifts design from single-token to set-level utilities.
    *   **Quick check:** What is the mathematical form of the marginal benefit of increasing \( q(v) \) on the total weighted coverage \( \sum_v w(v)[1-(1-q(v))^K] \), and why does it naturally promote diversity across K samples?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Master Problem Formulator -> KKT Condition Deriver / Solver Selector -> (Closed-form Implementer OR Mirror Ascent Engine) -> Pipeline Integrator (for BoK)
    Regularizer Builder feeds into Master Problem Formulator and Mirror Ascent Engine.

*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Define master problem \( \max_{q \in C_t} \langle q, s_t \rangle - \lambda\Omega(q) \).
    2.  Choose \( \Omega \) and \( C_t \) based on desired decoding behavior.
    3.  Check for closed-form solution via KKT. If exists, implement directly.
    4.  If no closed-form, initialize \( q^0 = p_t \) (model distribution) and run mirror ascent: \( q^{j+1} = q^j \odot \exp(\eta \nabla f(q^j)) / \|\cdot\|_1 \).
    5.  For BoK, compute gradient \( \partial f/\partial q(v) = s_t(v) - \lambda(\log(q(v)/p_t(v)) + 1) + \beta w_t(v) K (1-q(v))^{K-1} \) at each iteration.
    6.  Sample tokens from final \( q^J \) or take the mode.

*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Closed-form vs. iterative:** Closed-form (e.g., softmax) is O(|V|) and stable. Iterative mirror ascent adds O(J|V|) overhead but enables richer objectives (BoK). Paper shows 2-5 steps suffice for gains.
    *   **KL anchoring strength \( \lambda \):** Higher \( \lambda \) keeps \( q \) closer to model \( p_t \), reducing diversity/coverage. Lower \( \lambda \) allows more deviation but risks low-quality tokens. Requires tuning.
    *   **Coverage strength \( \beta \) (BoK):** Controls weight of coverage utility. Too low → negligible effect; too high → over-dispersion to low-scoring tokens. Empirically robust in a moderate range (e.g., 0.01-0.05).
    *   **Weight design \( w_t(v) \):** In BoK, \( w_t(v) \) defines "good" tokens. Paper uses logit-scaled weights implicitly. Alternatives: top-M indicator, learned verifier scores. Choice impacts performance.

*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Degenerate distribution:** \( q \) collapses to a single token despite non-zero \( \lambda \). **Cause:** \( \lambda \) too small or \( \Omega \) not encouraging diversity. **Check:** Entropy of \( q \) near 0.
    *   **Over-dispersion / nonsense tokens:** \( q \) spreads mass to very low-scoring tokens. **Cause:** \( \beta \) too high in BoK without sufficient \( \lambda \) anchoring, or noisy \( w_t(v) \). **Check:** \( q(v) \) non-negligible for tokens with \( s_t(v) \ll \max \).
    *   **Numerical instability in mirror ascent:** \( q^j \odot \exp(\eta g) \) yields `NaN` or extreme values. **Cause:** large gradients \( g \) (\( \eta \) too large) or \( q^j(v)=0 \) causing `log(0)` in BoK gradient. **Fix:** use log-sum-exp shift; ensure \( q^0 > 0 \) (e.g., \( q^0 = p_t + \epsilon \)).
    *   **No improvement over baseline:** BoK gains primarily at high temperatures (\( \tau \ge 0.7 \)). At low \( \tau \), model is already peaky; coverage utility may be unnecessary/harmful. **Check:** evaluate across temperature regime.

*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Closed-form recovery test:** For random \( s_t \), implement greedy (\( \lambda=0 \), no \( C_t \)), softmax (\( \Omega=-\text{entropy} \), \( \lambda>0 \)), and top-K (\( C_t \) = top-K support). Verify \( q \) matches formulas and KKT conditions hold (active tokens have equal adjusted score \( s_t(v) - \lambda(1+\log q(v)) \)).
    2.  **Mirror ascent convergence:** Implement entropic mirror ascent for \( f(q)=\langle q,s_t \rangle - \lambda(-\text{entropy}(q)) \). Initialize \( q^0 = \text{softmax}(s_t) \). Run for J steps and verify \( q^j \) converges to softmax and remains on the simplex. Compare with projected gradient ascent (\( \ell_2 \) projection) to observe stability differences.
    3.  **BoK micro-validation:** On synthetic |V|=10, set \( s_t, p_t \). Choose \( K=3 \), \( w_t(v)=1 \), \( \lambda=0.1 \), \( \beta=0.1 \). Run mirror ascent for BoK. Analyze final \( q \): does it assign higher probability to moderately scoring tokens vs. softmax? Compute coverage \( \sum_v w_t(v)[1-(1-q(v))^K] \) and verify it exceeds softmax/top-K. Sweep \( \beta \) to see trade-off curve between \( \text{KL}(q||p_t) \) and coverage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The theoretical unification assumes per-token decoupling; extending to sequential dependencies (e.g., repetition penalties) is unclear. The robustness of BoK gains across broader model families (encoder-decoders, small models), languages, and long-form generation remains an open question. The optimal design of coverage weights \( w_t(v) \) (beyond simple logit scaling) and the theoretical characterization of the \( \lambda \)-\( \beta \) trade-off for a given temperature are not settled.

## Limitations
*   The decoupling assumption (per-token optimization independent of context) is a simplification; real-world decoding involves sequential constraints that the framework does not naturally capture.
*   Empirical validation is limited to two model families on three benchmarks; generalizability to other architectures, tasks, and generation lengths requires further testing.
*   The coverage utility's weight design (\( w_t(v) \)) is ad-hoc (logit-based); it is unclear whether more sophisticated quality estimators would improve or destabilize performance.

## Confidence
*   **Unification via Simplex Optimization:** High. Derivations are explicit, recover known decoders correctly, and align with independent variational views of softmax.
*   **Mirror Ascent as Solver:** Medium. Geometric justification is standard, but empirical comparison to other Bregman divergences or solvers is limited.
*   **BoK's Empirical Gains:** Medium. Results are strong on curated benchmarks, but scope is narrow (2 models, 3 tasks) and potential evaluation overfitting (hyperparameter tuning on test sets?) is a concern.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-model/task generalization:** Replicate BoK on at least two additional architectures (e.g., Llama-3, Mistral) and two non-code/math tasks (e.g., summarization, dialogue). Measure gain vs. temperature and compute overhead.
2.  **Sequential dependency stress test:** Construct a synthetic task where token quality depends on prior tokens (e.g., repeating a pattern). Compare BoK vs. baselines to see if decoupling causes failures (e.g., coverage picks incoherent tokens).
3.  **Ablation of \( w_t(v) \) and \( \beta \):** On MATH500, systematically vary coverage weight design: (a) uniform \( w_t(v)=1 \), (b) logit-scaled \( \text{sigmoid}(s_t(v)/\tau) \), (c) scores from a small trained reward model. Plot accuracy vs. \( \beta \) for each to identify robust regimes and whether learned weights help.
