# Agentic Personas for Adaptive Scientific Explanations with Knowledge Graphs

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.21846
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21846
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Introduces agentic personas to guide RL for adaptive KG explanations in drug discovery, achieving state-of-the-art predictive performance (Hits@1 0.358) with 187× faster training and 2 orders of magnitude less expert feedback, while being strongly preferred by experts.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the static nature of user models in explainable AI by introducing "agentic personas"—data-driven representations of expert epistemic stances—to personalize scientific explanations. By clustering small amounts of expert textual feedback into coherent personas and using them to condition a reinforcement learning reward function, the system generates knowledge graph explanations tailored to different reasoning styles (e.g., preference for mechanistic detail vs. conciseness). In the drug discovery domain, this approach matches state-of-the-art predictive accuracy while drastically reducing the need for expert-in-the-loop training and yielding explanations that experts strongly prefer over non-adaptive baselines.

## Method Summary
The method operates in two phases. First, a small set of expert-provided textual feedback on example explanations is normalized, embedded, and clustered. An LLM then synthesizes each cluster into a narrative "persona" capturing a distinct epistemic stance. Second, an RL agent is trained to navigate a biomedical knowledge graph to generate explanation paths. The reward function combines base graph fidelity with a "persona score" from an LLM prompted with the persona narrative, scoring candidate paths on dimensions like validity and relevance. A curriculum schedule increases the relevance threshold over time, focusing persona alignment on higher-quality paths. At inference, the appropriate persona is selected (e.g., via user profiling) to guide explanation generation.

## Key Results
*   **Performance:** Matches state-of-the-art (Hits@1 0.358 vs. REx 0.338) on drug discovery KG reasoning tasks.
*   **Expert Preference:** Persona-driven explanations are strongly preferred by experts over non-adaptive baselines (63.3–76.0%).
*   **Efficiency:** Reduces required expert feedback by ~2 orders of magnitude; training completes 187× faster (1.34 vs. 250 expert hours).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Clustering expert feedback semantically extracts coherent, interpretable subgroups (epistemic stances) from a small, diverse expert cohort.
*   **Mechanism:** Expert textual feedback is normalized into first-person preference statements, embedded (Sentence-BERT), and clustered. An LLM synthesizes a narrative "persona" per cluster by identifying frequently expressed thematic traits, creating a stable proxy for a subset of experts' reasoning strategies.
*   **Break condition:** If expert feedback is too sparse or homogenous, clustering produces unstable clusters (e.g., k=1), failing to derive distinct personas.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Conditioning the RL reward on a persona's LLM-based evaluation steers the policy toward paths aligning with that stance without sacrificing path fidelity.
*   **Mechanism:** Paths passing fidelity and relevance thresholds are scored by an LLM prompted with the persona narrative on validity, completeness, and relevance. This `R_persona` is combined with base KG fidelity in the reward. A curriculum increases the relevance threshold over time.
*   **Break condition:** If the LLM fails to differentially score paths across personas (low variance), the reward signal becomes uninformative and the policy does not diverge from the baseline.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Using persona-conditioned rewards instead of direct expert feedback reduces the human annotation burden by ~2 orders of magnitude.
*   **Mechanism:** The persona LLM replaces a human expert for providing dense feedback during RL training. A single persona evaluation is ~86x faster than an expert's, leading to massive total training time savings.
*   **Break condition:** If persona-LLM ratings have low correlation (<0.5) with the target expert cluster's judgments, the trained policy optimizes a distorted signal.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Knowledge Graph Path-Based Reasoning**
    *   *Why needed here:* The explanation is a set of paths in a biomedical KG connecting a drug and disease. Fidelity measures valid connections; relevance uses node surprisal to prioritize informative entities.
    *   *Quick check question:* How does "relevance" (surprisal) encourage paths with less generic, more specific biomedical entities?
*   **Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Preference Feedback**
    *   *Why needed here:* The core optimization is an RL agent learning a policy. The "preference" signal is approximated by the persona's multi-dimensional score, adapting RLHF to a KG explanation task.
    *   *Quick check question:* In the reward equation `R(pi, t) = α(pi) * [conditional R_persona or fixed small reward]`, what is the purpose of the fixed small rewards (0.25, 0.10)?
*   **Concept: Epistemic Stances & Explanation Virtues**
    *   *Why needed here:* This is the target for adaptation. The paper defines stances via three virtues: validity (scientific consistency), relevance (causal mechanism informativeness), and completeness (balance of detail/parsimony). Personas encode differing weightings/interpretations.
    *   *Quick check question:* Based on example narratives, how does "Leo's" stance interpret "completeness" differently than "Elena's"?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Expert Feedback -> Persona Generator (Embed, Cluster, LLM-Narrative) -> Persona Repository -> RL Training (Agent <-> KG Environment, Persona Reward from Persona Evaluator LLM, Curriculum Manager) -> Trained Policy -> Inference (with Profiler -> Persona Selector -> Verbalization Module)

**Critical path:** Expert Feedback Collection -> Persona Generation (Offline) -> RL Training with Persona Reward -> Inference with Assigned Persona. Persona must be generated and validated before RL training.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Persona Fidelity vs. Generalization:** More personas capture nuance but risk overfitting to small expert subsets. Fewer are robust but may average away distinct stances.
*   **LLM Judge Consistency vs. Cost:** More powerful LLMs improve narrative quality and scoring reliability but increase cost/latency. The curriculum partially mitigates cost by avoiding scoring on low-relevance paths.
*   **Adaptivity vs. Predictive Performance:** The persona reward is an additional objective. If weighted too heavily, it could steer paths away from highly predictive but persona-mismatched connections. The paper uses equal weights and a fidelity gate to balance.

**Failure signatures:**
*   **Persona Validation Failure:** Low correlation (r < 0.5) between persona-LLM ratings and held-out expert cluster ratings.
*   **Training Instability:** RL agent's rolling average reward does not improve or fluctuates wildly, indicating a noisy or conflicting persona reward signal.
*   **No Preference in User Study:** Adaptive explanations are not statistically preferred over the non-adaptive baseline by users profiled for that persona.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Persona Cluster Validation:** Compute Silhouette, Calinski-Harabasz, Davies-Bouldin indices for k=2-5. Confirm k=2 is optimal and stable across clustering algorithms.
2.  **Persona LLM Scoring Reliability:** For a held-out set, compute Pearson correlation between persona-LLM scores and mean expert scores from the originating cluster. Pass if r >= 0.7 for at least one persona.
3.  **Policy Convergence Check:** Train RL agent with persona reward for a fixed budget (e.g., 10k steps). Plot moving average of combined reward. Pass if it shows a clear upward trend and plateaus.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** How reliable and generalizable are agentic personas derived from very small expert clusters (e.g., n=2 for Leo)?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors explicitly caution that the Leo persona, formed from only two users, requires careful interpretation due to its minimal sample size.
*   **Why unresolved:** Such small clusters risk representing idiosyncratic preferences rather than a stable epistemic stance, and may not scale to broader expert populations.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Replication studies with larger, more diverse expert cohorts across multiple tasks to measure persona stability, predictive validity, and consistency in guiding explanation generation.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** Do persona-driven adaptations maintain consistent effectiveness across varied biomedical reasoning tasks, or are they task-specific?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Elena’s persona showed strong correlation with expert ratings in drug repurposing (r=0.74–0.80) but weaker alignment in drug–target interaction (validity r=0.56), indicating task-dependent variation.
*   **Why unresolved:** This variation suggests persona criteria may overfit to task-specific patterns, limiting their applicability to new or different scientific contexts without retraining or adjustment.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic cross-task evaluation in multiple drug discovery and biomedical reasoning scenarios, tracking persona-expert agreement and user preference stability.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** How faithfully do LLM-synthesized persona narratives capture the original expert epistemic stances compared to purely human-derived clustering?
*   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The methodology uses GPT-4o-mini to synthesize persona narratives from clustered expert feedback, while acknowledging risks of persona essentialization and the lack of direct comparison to human-only construction.
*   **Why unresolved:** LLMs may introduce synthetic biases, oversimplify nuances, or generate plausible but unfaithful representations, potentially compromising the reward model’s alignment with true expert preferences.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled studies where human experts rate the authenticity of LLM-generated persona narratives against the original feedback, and ablation experiments comparing RL performance when using LLM-synthesized vs. human-curated personas.

## Limitations
*   **Limited Expert Cohort:** Persona clustering relies on a small, potentially non-representative sample (18 users); generalizability to broader populations or domains is uncertain.
*   **Moderate LLM Judge Fidelity:** Correlation between persona-LLM ratings and expert cluster ratings ranges from r=0.56–0.91; lower-bound performance risks distorting RL rewards.
*   **Domain-Specific Validation:** Efficacy is demonstrated primarily on drug discovery KGs; transferability to other KG structures or scientific explanation tasks is not established.

## Confidence
*   **High:** Empirical speedup (187×) and reduced annotation burden are well-supported by timing data.
*   **Medium:** Persona-driven RL improves preference in drug discovery KG; robustness to other KG structures or explanation tasks is uncertain.
*   **Medium:** Clustering yields interpretable personas, but validation is limited to one domain and a small expert sample.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cluster stability with expanded cohorts:** Recruit 30–50 experts across multiple biomedical subfields; re-cluster feedback and compute silhouette/CH indices for k=2–5. Check if k=2 remains optimal and personas remain separable.
2.  **Persona LLM scoring audit:** For each persona, compute Pearson correlation between LLM (GPT-4o-mini, Claude 3, Llama 3) scores and held-out expert ratings across 3 prompt variants. Report mean r and variance; fail if any persona r < 0.5 consistently.
3.  **Cross-KG generalization:** Train persona-conditioned policies on a held-out biomedical KG (e.g., PharmKG). Evaluate Hits@1 and expert preference (n=12 new experts); compare to non-adaptive baseline. A significant drop (>10% Hits@1) signals limited transferability.
