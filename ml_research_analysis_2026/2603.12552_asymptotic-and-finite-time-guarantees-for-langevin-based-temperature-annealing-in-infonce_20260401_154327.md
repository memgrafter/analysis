# Asymptotic and Finite-Time Guarantees for Langevin-Based Temperature Annealing in InfoNCE

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12552  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12552  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: Logarithmic temperature annealing β(t)=c ln(t+K) with rate c not exceeding a problem‑dependent threshold c* guarantees convergence in probability to globally optimal representations; faster annealing can cause permanent trapping; the InfoNCE Hessian sharpens linearly with β.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies the temperature parameter in InfoNCE contrastive learning, where a fixed temperature often underperforms and heuristic annealing schedules lack theoretical justification. The authors model the evolution of embeddings under stochastic gradient descent as an overdamped Langevin stochastic differential equation on a compact Riemannian manifold (e.g., the hypersphere induced by ℓ2‑normalization). The inverse temperature β is annealed according to a logarithmic schedule β(t)=c ln(t+K). Under smoothness and finite energy‑barrier assumptions, they prove that if c ≤ c* (with c* = 1/ΔE_max, ΔE_max being the maximum energy barrier of the limiting potential), the process converges in probability to a global optimum. Faster schedules (c > c*) risk trapping in suboptimal minima with positive probability. Finite‑time analysis yields error bounds that decay polynomially at the critical rate (c=c*) and stretched‑exponentially for slower cooling (c<c*). Moreover, a geometric analysis reveals that the Hessian of the InfoNCE loss scales at least linearly with β away from global minima, effectively sharpening the loss landscape and stabilizing convergence in later stages. These results provide a principled foundation for temperature scheduling in contrastive learning.

## Method Summary
The training dynamics are formalized as a continuous-time overdamped Langevin process on a compact product manifold ℳ:  
dZ_t = -grad L(Z_t, β(t)) dt + √(2/β(t)) dW_t^ℳ,  
where L is the InfoNCE loss and β(t)=c ln(t+K). A key step is showing that the scaled loss L(Z,β)/β converges uniformly to a limiting potential U₀(Z) as β→∞ (Γ‑convergence). Using Freidlin‑Wentzell large‑deviation theory, the authors establish that for c ≤ c* (c* = 1/ΔE_max) the resulting Gibbs measure concentrates on the global minimizers of U₀, implying convergence in probability to optimal representations. For c > c*, trapping occurs with positive probability. Finite‑time bounds are derived by integrating the exponential escape rate from suboptimal basins, yielding polynomial decay at the critical schedule and stretched‑exponential decay otherwise. Finally, a Hessian decomposition (Eq. 6) shows that, away from global minima, the operator norm of the InfoNCE Hessian grows at least linearly with β, sharpening the landscape as temperature drops and thus accelerating late‑stage convergence.

## Key Results
- Logarithmic cooling β(t)=c ln(t+K) with c ≤ c* (c* = 1/ΔE_max) ensures convergence in probability to globally optimal representations; faster cooling (c>c*) may permanently trap the process.
- Finite‑time error bounds: for c<c* the failure probability decays stretched‑exponentially; at the critical c=c* it decays polynomially with a rate determined by the dimension of the global optimum set.
- The InfoNCE Hessian exhibits linear growth with the inverse temperature β away from global minima, sharpening the loss landscape and stabilizing convergence once near a good region.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The theoretical guarantees rest on three interrelated mechanisms:

1. **Langevin dynamics as a continuous model for SGD**  
   Discrete SGD (or SGLD) updates for embeddings are approximated by an overdamped Langevin SDE on a compact Riemannian manifold (assumptions A1–A2). The SDE is dZ_t = -grad L(Z_t, β(t)) dt + √(2/β(t)) dW_t^ℳ, where the diffusion coefficient √(2/β) governs exploration noise. This correspondence allows the use of stochastic differential geometry and simulated annealing theory. Evidence appears in Section 2.2. The mechanism breaks if the embedding manifold is non‑compact (e.g., unnormalized embeddings) or if SGD noise is strongly anisotropic, requiring extensions to state‑dependent covariances.

2. **Simulated annealing with a logarithmic cooling schedule**  
   Classical results (Hajek; Geman–Geman) state that logarithmic cooling yields convergence to the global minimum of a potential with finite energy barriers. Here the potential is the limiting function U₀(Z) = lim_{β→∞} L(Z,β)/β (Lemma B.1). The authors prove that if the cooling rate satisfies c ≤ c* where c* = 1/ΔE_max (ΔE_max is the maximum energy barrier of U₀), then the Gibbs measure concentrates on the global minimizers (Theorem 3). Conversely, for c > c* trapping occurs with positive probability (Proposition 4). The proof combines Γ‑convergence of the scaled potentials with Freidlin‑Wentzell large‑deviation estimates of exit times. Break condition: liminf β(t)/ln t > c* or ΔE_max = ∞.

3. **Hessian sharpening under increasing inverse temperature**  
   The InfoNCE loss Hessian H(Z,β) (Eq. 6) splits into β² Cov[∇sim] and β(E[H_sim] - H_sim). As β grows, the softmax distribution concentrates, making the covariance term decay like O(1/β), while the second term remains O(β). Thus, away from global minima (where the positive sample is not the most similar), the dominant eigenvalues of H scale at least linearly with β (Lemma 6). This “sharpening” means the deterministic gradient pull becomes stronger as temperature drops, speeding convergence once near a good region. Near exact global minima the scaling may weaken, but the reduced noise still aids stability. The mechanism relies on smoothness and boundedness of the similarity function (assumption A3); non‑smooth similarities break the linear scaling.

## Foundational Learning
- **Langevin Dynamics**: A stochastic process combining gradient flow and Brownian motion, used to model noisy optimization. *Why needed*: It provides the mathematical bridge between SGD and statistical physics, enabling analysis of exploration (noise) and exploitation (drift) as temperature varies. *Quick check*: What does the noise coefficient √(2/β) in the Langevin SDE physically represent, and how does it change as annealing proceeds?
- **Simulated Annealing**: An optimization metaheuristic that gradually reduces a temperature parameter to escape local minima and converge to a global optimum. *Why needed*: The paper’s convergence guarantees are a direct application of classical simulated annealing theory to the InfoNCE setting, with the critical cooling rate derived from energy barriers. *Quick check*: Why is a logarithmic cooling schedule necessary, and what does the constant c* = 1/ΔE_max encode?
- **Compact Riemannian Manifold**: A manifold that is closed and bounded (e.g., a hypersphere). *Why needed*: Compactness ensures the existence of a finite energy landscape and a normalizable stationary distribution, which are essential for Γ‑convergence and escape‑time arguments. *Quick check*: How would the theory change if embeddings lived on an open manifold (e.g., ℝᵈ without normalization)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Langevin SDE (Eq. 3) -> InfoNCE loss L(Z,β) -> Limiting potential U₀(Z) (Eq. 4) -> Annealing schedule β(t)=c ln(t+K) -> Compact manifold ℳ = (ℳ⁰)ᴺ (e.g., hypersphere) -> Energy barrier ΔE_max -> Critical rate c* = 1/ΔE_max -> Hessian sharpening analysis (Eq. 6, Lemma 6).

**Critical path**:  
1. Establish uniform convergence L(Z,β)/β → U₀(Z) (Lemma B.1).  
2. Apply Γ‑convergence to show Gibbs measures π_{β(t)} weakly converge to the uniform distribution over global minimizers of U₀.  
3. Use Freidlin‑Wentzell large‑deviation theory to prove that a logarithmic schedule with c ≤ c* guarantees escape from all suboptimal basins (Theorem 3).  
4. For finite‑time bounds, integrate the escape rate r(t) ≈ A·exp(−β(t)ΔE_max) and employ spectral gap refinements to obtain polynomial/stretched‑exponential bounds.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Compact manifold*: ℓ²‑normalization enforces compactness but may limit representational capacity compared to unconstrained embeddings.
- *Isotropic noise*: Assuming a scalar diffusion coefficient simplifies analysis; real SGD noise is anisotropic and data‑dependent, which could alter the critical cooling rate.
- *Logarithmic schedule*: Theoretically optimal for asymptotic convergence but extremely slow; practical schedules (cosine, square‑root) are faster but lack global convergence guarantees.
- *Unknown c*: The critical rate depends on ΔE_max, which is problem‑specific and generally unknown, hampering direct application of the theory.

**Failure signatures**:
- Embeddings drift off the compact manifold (e.g., norm explosion), violating compactness assumptions.
- Temperature schedule decays faster than logarithmic (e.g., linear β(t)=t), which can cause permanent trapping with positive probability.
- Extremely rugged loss landscape (large ΔE_max) forces c* to be tiny, making even logarithmic schedules effectively too fast.
- Insufficient Hessian sharpening (e.g., flat regions) yields weak gradient signals even at low temperature.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Synthetic annealing‑rate test**: On a controlled contrastive task with known energy barrier (e.g., two‑anchor cosine similarity on S¹), run annealed Langevin dynamics for various c values. Verify that for c ≤ c* the success probability (reaching global optimum) tends to 1, while for c > c* it plateaus below 1.
2. **Finite‑time rate validation**: For fixed c < c* and c = c*, plot the failure probability against (t+K)^{1-c/c*} and (t+K)^{−d} respectively to confirm the predicted stretched‑exponential and polynomial decays. Fit constants to assess the theoretical rates.
3. **Hessian sharpening measurement**: Train a shallow contrastive model (e.g., two‑layer network) on a small dataset like CIFAR‑10. At different inverse temperatures β, compute the Hessian of the InfoNCE loss (via finite differences) for a set of embedding configurations not at global minima. Verify that the top eigenvalue grows approximately linearly with β, and that the slope correlates with the curvature predicted by Lemma 6.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Relaxing compactness**: The current theory relies on compactness to obtain finite energy barriers and a normalizable stationary measure. Extending the results to non‑compact manifolds (e.g., ℝᵈ) remains open.
- **Anisotropic noise**: The analysis assumes isotropic Brownian motion; anisotropic covariance may affect escape times and the critical cooling rate. A generalized theory is needed.
- **Momentum‑based optimizers**: Adam and similar methods introduce momentum, leading to underdamped Langevin dynamics. Proving analogous annealing guarantees for such systems is an open challenge.
- **Practical faster schedules**: Empirically, cosine or square‑root annealing often works despite the theory requiring logarithmic cooling for asymptotic global optimality. Understanding the finite‑time behavior of faster schedules is an important open direction.

## Limitations
- The critical cooling rate c* depends on the maximum energy barrier ΔE_max of the limiting potential, which is generally unknown and intractable to compute for deep models.
- Core assumptions (compact embedding manifold, isotropic noise, smooth bounded similarity) may not hold in realistic training: embeddings can drift off the manifold, SGD noise is anisotropic, and networks may use non‑smooth activations.
- Finite‑time constants are not explicitly quantified and could be large, meaning the predicted asymptotic rates might only become visible at impractically large iteration counts.

## Confidence
- Langevin–SGD correspondence: **Medium** (theoretical link, limited empirical validation).  
- Asymptotic convergence for c ≤ c*: **High** (rigorous under assumptions), but practical confidence **Medium** due to unknown c* and possible assumption violations.  
- Hessian sharpening: **Medium** (proof provided, sparse experimental verification).  
- Finite‑time rates: **Medium** (standard methodology, constants not bounded).
