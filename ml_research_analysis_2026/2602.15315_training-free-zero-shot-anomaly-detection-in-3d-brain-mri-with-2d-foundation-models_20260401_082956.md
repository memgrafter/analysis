# Training-Free Zero-Shot Anomaly Detection in 3D Brain MRI with 2D Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15315
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15315
- **Reference Count:** 26
- **Primary Result:** A training-free framework uses 2D foundation models to construct 3D tokens for volumetric anomaly detection, achieving patient-level AUROCs of 96.9% (T2w) and 97.5% (T1w) and substantially outperforming zero-shot CLIP baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CoDeGraph3D, a training-free zero-shot anomaly detection (ZSAD) method for 3D brain MRI. It overcomes the lack of 3D foundation models by processing slices along three orthogonal axes with a frozen 2D encoder (e.g., DINOv2), aggregating patch-aligned features into localized 3D cubic tokens, reducing dimensionality with random projection, and fusing multi-axis views. These tokens are then used in a batch-based outlier detection pipeline without fine-tuning or prompts. Evaluated on T2- and T1-weighted brain MRI from IXI and BraTS, the approach achieves strong patient-level AUROCs (~97%) and voxel-level Dice scores (~41% T2w, ~34% T1w), significantly beating zero-shot CLIP-based baselines and approaching supervised model performance. The method is efficient (~4 sec/volume, <10GB VRAM) and generalizes to glioma and stroke lesions.

## Method Summary
The method processes a 3D volume by extracting 2D slices along axial, coronal, and sagittal planes. Each slice is encoded by a frozen 2D foundation model to produce patch tokens. For each axis, features are aggregated via patch-aligned average pooling over non-overlapping blocks of depth *p*, constructing cubic tokens of size *p*×*p*×*p*. Tokens from all axes are concatenated after random projection to a fixed dimension *k*. For a batch of volumes, these tokens are pooled into a single collection. Anomaly scores are computed per token using the Mutual Similarity Vector (MSV) paradigm: each token's distance to its nearest neighbors in other volumes forms a vector, and the average of the smallest *K* distances (MuSc) is the anomaly score. CoDeGraph mitigates "consistent anomalies" that repeat across volumes by detecting collections of suspicious tokens and excluding them from neighbor calculations. Volumetric score maps are generated by reshaping token scores and trilinearly resampling to the original resolution.

## Key Results
- **Patient-level AUROC:** 96.9% (T2-weighted) and 97.5% (T1-weighted) on IXI/BraTS.
- **Voxel-level Dice:** 41.3% (T2w) and 33.8% (T1w), substantially higher than zero-shot CLIP baselines (WinCLIP Dice 8.1%, AnomalyCLIP 11.8%).
- **Efficiency & Generalization:** Processes a volume in ~4 seconds using <10GB VRAM; validated on glioma (BraTS) and stroke lesions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Multi-Axis Volumetric Tokenization
Constructs localized 3D patch tokens from 2D slices to restore cubic spatial context while reducing token count. For each anatomical axis, the 3D volume is sliced into 2D images processed by a frozen 2D encoder. Patch-aligned average pooling aggregates features over non-overlapping blocks of depth *p*, creating cubic tokens of size *p*×*p*×*p*. Coordinates are permuted to align to a unified grid. The core assumption is that averaging slice features within a cubic block preserves semantic coherence, making normal and anomalous tissues distinguishable. Evidence: The abstract describes constructing localized volumetric tokens via multi-axis slice aggregation; Section 3.2.1 details the patch-aligned pooling. Break condition: Anomalies smaller than the patch size or with low slice-feature contrast may be diluted, reducing sensitivity to small lesions.

### Mechanism 2: Random Projection for Dimensionality Reduction
Applies a fixed Gaussian random matrix to project tokens from high dimension *D* (e.g., 1024) to low dimension *k* (e.g., 128), making quadratic batch similarity computation tractable while approximately preserving pairwise distances via the Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma. The core assumption is that distance preservation is sufficient for outlier detection. Evidence: Section 3.2.2 explicitly invokes the JL lemma; Table 3 shows performance stabilizes for *k*≥50, with negligible Dice change between *k*=128 and 200. Break condition: If *k* is too small (e.g., *k*≤10), distance distortion harms segmentation.

### Mechanism 3: Batch-Based Outlier Detection with Consistent Anomaly Handling
Identifies anomalies as statistical outliers via Mutual Similarity Vectors (MSV) across a batch. For each token, distances to nearest tokens in other volumes are sorted to form an MSV; the average of the smallest *K* distances (MuSc) is the anomaly score. CoDeGraph detects volumes sharing anomalous patterns and excludes their tokens from MSV calculation to avoid masking normal outliers. The core Doppelgänger assumption: normal tokens find many close matches across the batch, anomalous tokens find few. CoDeGraph corrects the vulnerability when anomalies are consistent (repeat across volumes). Evidence: Section 3.1 defines MSV and MuSc scoring and describes CoDeGraph's exclusion logic; results show high AUROCs and large margins over slice-wise CLIP baselines, validating batch-based efficacy in 3D. Break conditions: Very small batch sizes (e.g., *B*=15) degrade performance (Table 5); if anomalies are ubiquitous, CoDeGraph may over-exclude normal tokens.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision Transformers (ViT):** Needed to extract dense patch-based feature tokens from 2D slices. DINOv2 provides self-supervised representations without medical training. Quick check: How does patch granularity in ViT affect anomaly detection sensitivity?
- **Batch-Based Zero-Shot Anomaly Detection (ZSAD):** The core paradigm scoring anomalies based on rarity across a batch of tokens, eliminating training or prompts. Quick check: What is the Doppelgänger assumption, and why does it fail with consistent anomalies?
- **Johnson-Lindenstrauss Lemma:** Justifies random projection for dimensionality reduction while preserving pairwise distances critical for MSV computation. Quick check: How does the target dimension *k* trade off efficiency vs. distance preservation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input 3D MRI → Preprocessing (registration, skull-strip, crop, resize) → Multi-axis slice extraction → 2D encoder (frozen) → Patch-aligned pooling (per axis) → Random projection (per axis) → Multi-view fusion (concatenation) → Token collection per volume → Batch-based scoring (CoDeGraph) → Volumetric score map → Trilinear resize to original resolution.
- **Critical path:** The tokenization pipeline (slice extraction → pooling → projection → fusion) must produce compact, spatially coherent tokens; batch scoring depends entirely on this representation. Any failure in token semantic richness propagates to the final scores.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Patch size *p*: Larger *p* reduces token count and memory but blurs small anomalies; smaller *p* increases tokens and computation.
  - Projection dimension *k*: Lower *k* saves memory/speed but risks distance distortion; *k*=128 balances stability (Table 3).
  - Number of axes: More views improve context but linearly increase extraction cost; axial+coronal nearly matches tri-axial (Table 4).
  - Batch size *B*: Larger *B* improves outlier detection but increases O(*B*²) similarity cost; chunking allows scalability with mild performance drop (Table 5).
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High patient-level AUROC but low voxel Dice: Coarse tokenization may detect abnormal volumes but poorly localize small lesions.
  - Sharp performance drop when *k*<50 (Table 3).
  - Inconsistent multi-view benefit: If one axis yields poor features, fusion may not help; consider axis selection.
  - Memory spikes with large *B* or high resolution despite token reduction; monitor VRAM (Table 8 shows <10GB for *B*=180).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline comparison (Tables 1–2):** Verify CoDeGraph3D outperforms zero-shot CLIP baselines on patient-level and voxel-level metrics across T1w/T2w.
  2. **Random projection ablation (Table 3):** Test *k*={1,10,50,100,128,200} to confirm stability above *k*=50 and computational feasibility.
  3. **Multi-view aggregation ablation (Table 4):** Compare single vs. dual vs. tri-view to show axial+coronal nearly matches full tri-axial performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Small/ sparse lesion sensitivity:** How can cubic tokenization be modified to improve sensitivity to small, sparse, or low-contrast lesions without sacrificing efficiency? The fixed patch size (*p*=14) may attenuate such anomalies via averaging.
- **Scalability of quadratic computation:** To what extent does the O(*B*²) similarity calculation limit scalability to larger cohorts or higher resolutions, and can approximate nearest-neighbor search (e.g., FAISS) preserve accuracy?
- **Influence of 2D encoder choice:** What properties of the frozen 2D foundation model most strongly influence 3D token quality and downstream ZSAD performance? DINOv2 outperforms CLIP, but the reasons are unclear.
- **3D context capture:** Does multi-axis aggregation fully capture 3D context for all anomaly morphologies, or are non-axis-aligned shapes (e.g., tubular lesions) systematically missed?
- **Batch anomaly prevalence robustness:** How does batch anomaly prevalence affect the Doppelgänger assumption, and does CoDeGraph's consistent-anomaly detection suffice under high anomaly loads? Experiments used a fixed batch with ~36% anomalies; varying prevalence is untested.

## Limitations
- **Localization granularity:** Voxel-level Dice scores (~41% T2w, ~34% T1w) indicate that fixed cubic tokenization may blur boundaries of small or thin lesions.
- **Batch dependency:** Performance relies on sufficient batch diversity (*B*≥~100); small daily batches or homogeneous cohorts may degrade sensitivity.
- **Pathology generalization:** Validation is restricted to IXI/BraTS (glioma) and a small stroke subset; generalization to other anomalies (e.g., microbleeds) or MRI contrasts is unverified.

## Confidence
- **High:** Patient-level AUROC superiority over zero-shot CLIP baselines and computational efficiency (<10GB VRAM, 4 sec/vol). Supported by robust ablations (Tables 1–5).
- **Medium:** Voxel-level segmentation capability (Dice ~41%/34%) and stability of random projection (*k*≥50). Results show clear trends but leave room for improvement; Dice is substantially lower than AUROC.
- **Low:** Broad generalization across diverse 3D pathologies and scanner vendors. Evidence is limited to two datasets and a small stroke cohort; no external multi-vendor validation.

## Next Checks
1. **Small-lesion sensitivity test:** Quantify detection performance for lesions with volume <*p*³ (e.g., <8mm³ if *p*=2) using synthetic or curated small-anomaly subsets. Expect Dice drop while AUROC remains high.
2. **Clinical batch-size stress test:** Evaluate with batches of *B*=20–50 (simulating daily radiology workflow) on BraTS test set. Monitor AUROC/Dice degradation; if >5% drop occurs, investigate adaptive batch composition.
3. **Cross-modality & vendor robustness audit:** Run inference on external 3D MRI datasets (e.g., UCSF stroke, ADNI Alzheimer’s) with different field strengths (1.5T/3T) and vendors (Siemens/GE/Philips). Measure variance in Dice/AUROC to expose dataset bias.
