# Small LLMs for Medical NLP: a Systematic Analysis of Few-Shot, Constraint Decoding, Fine-Tuning and Continual Pre-Training in Italian

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17475
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17475
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Fine-tuning ~1B parameter models (e.g., Qwen3-1.7B) on Italian medical tasks outperforms larger baselines (e.g., Qwen3-32B) by +9.2 points on average, while few-shot prompting with constraint decoding offers a robust low-resource alternative.

## Executive Summary
This study systematically evaluates whether small language models (~1 billion parameters) can effectively handle Italian medical NLP tasks. Testing models from the Llama-3, Gemma-3, and Qwen3 families across 20 clinical tasks, the authors find that supervised fine-tuning is the most effective adaptation strategy. For resource-constrained settings, combining few-shot prompting with constraint decoding yields strong results. The work contributes a new Italian medical corpus (126M words), a collection of datasets, and top-performing adapted models, demonstrating that size-optimized models can achieve superior in-distribution performance compared to larger counterparts when properly specialized.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates three small model families (Llama-3.2-1B, Gemma-3-1B, Qwen3-1.7B) on 20 Italian medical NLP tasks spanning NER, relation extraction, QA, and more. Adaptation strategies tested include: (1) few-shot prompting (0/4-shot), (2) grammar-constrained decoding to enforce valid JSON output, (3) supervised fine-tuning with LoRA, and (4) continual pre-training on a 405M-word Italian medical corpus before fine-tuning. Models are compared against larger baselines like Qwen3-32B. Performance is measured via F1 (for structured tasks) or accuracy (for QA), with statistical significance testing. The best results come from fine-tuning, while the combination of 4-shot prompting and constraint decoding provides the most effective inference-time approach without training.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuning (FT) with LoRA is the most effective single strategy, significantly outperforming other methods across all models and tasks.
- Combining 4-shot prompting with constraint decoding (CD) yields a strong low-resource alternative, achieving an average gain of +13.2 points over zero-shot.
- The best model (Qwen3-1.7B fine-tuned) beats the larger Qwen3-32B baseline by an average of 9.2 points on in-distribution tasks (p>0.95), though gaps remain on out-of-distribution tests.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Supervised fine-tuning (FT) is the most effective adaptation strategy because it directly aligns model parameters with task-specific input-output mappings.
- **Mechanism:** FT updates model weights via gradient descent on instruction-JSON pairs, teaching both semantic content (e.g., entity types) and required output format. This overcomes the base model's lack of structured medical instruction tuning.
- **Core assumption:** The pre-trained parameters provide a transferable language foundation; targeted updates on modest task datasets (thousands of examples) suffice for specialization without catastrophic forgetting.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "Fine-tuning emerges as the most effective approach"; Section 5 concludes FT "consistently produces the best results"; Section 4 describes LoRA fine-tuning on each dataset's training split.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Constraint decoding (CD) improves structured output generation by enforcing syntactic validity at inference time, reducing parse failures without additional training.
- **Mechanism:** CD uses a formal grammar (e.g., JSON schema) to dynamically mask invalid tokens during auto-regressive generation, ensuring valid partial structures (e.g., bracket nesting) and preventing format errors.
- **Core assumption:** The base model possesses sufficient semantic knowledge; the primary failure mode is syntactic invalidity, not semantic error.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes CD with few-shot provides a "strong low-resource alternative"; Section 2 describes CD as restricting generation to expected format; Section 5 reports CD has "no significant impact on runtime" while improving compliance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Few-shot prompting (4-shot) combined with CD yields robust, low-resource performance by leveraging in-context learning for semantics and CD for structure.
- **Mechanism:** 4-shot demonstrations teach task-specific patterns and format; CD then locks in structural rules, ensuring syntactically valid outputs. The synergy occurs because shots establish the pattern and CD enforces the grammar.
- **Core assumption:** Provided examples are representative and correctly formatted; the model has sufficient capacity to learn from few demonstrations.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract highlights the combination as a "strong low-resource alternative"; Section 5 reports the combination yields "the most reliable results (+13.2 on average)"; Section 2 notes examples improve task understanding and format adherence.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)**
  - *Why needed here:* Enables fine-tuning ~1B parameter models on multiple medical datasets with limited compute (single H200 GPU) by updating only low-rank matrices instead of all weights.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the core trade-off when choosing the LoRA rank `r`?
- **Concept: Domain-Specific Continual Pre-Training (CPT)**
  - *Why needed here:* Exposes the model to 405M words of Italian medical text (clinical + scientific) to internalize domain terminology before task-specific fine-tuning, theoretically building a stronger foundation.
  - *Quick check question:* According to the paper, what was the observed impact of CPT when followed by FT compared to FT alone for most models?
- **Concept: Grammar-Constrained Decoding (Output Schema)**
  - *Why needed here:* Medical NLP systems require machine-readable outputs (JSON). CD via libraries like `outlines` ensures syntactically valid generations, critical for integration into clinical pipelines.
  - *Quick check question:* What specific mechanism does `outlines` use to enforce constraints during generation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Curation (20 Italian datasets + 405M-word CPT corpus) -> Preprocessing (tokenization, sequence packing via Flash Attention 2) -> Model Zoo (Llama-3.2-1B, Gemma-3-1B, Qwen3-1.7B base/instruct) -> Adaptation Pipeline (CPT optional -> FT via LoRA -> Inference with 0/4-shot ± CD) -> Evaluation (F1/Accuracy with t-tests).
- **Critical path:** 1) Select Qwen3-1.7B for best results; 2) Apply supervised FT on all 14 in-distribution medical sub-tasks using LoRA; 3) Deploy with zero-shot inference and constraint decoding to ensure valid JSON.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - FT vs CPT: CPT (405M tokens, 8–12 hrs) adds minimal benefit over FT alone for most models (except Gemma-3-1B-it), representing high compute cost for small gain.
  - Few-shot vs CD: 4-shot (+9.4 avg) outperforms CD (+3.8 avg), but CD adds negligible latency. Combining them yields highest inference-time performance (+13.2).
  - Base vs Instruct: Instruct models often need less adaptation (e.g., Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct FT +7.2 vs base +4.3), but some (Gemma-3-1B-it) benefit more from CPT.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - Relation Extraction F1 remains low (~11–24) even after FT, indicating a fundamental limitation of 1B models for complex relational reasoning.
  - Out-of-distribution evaluation shows smaller models lag behind Qwen3-32B baseline, indicating overfitting to training tasks/datasets.
  - Zero-shot base models often output 0.0 F1, showing no inherent capability for structured medical tasks without adaptation.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline sanity check:** Run 4-shot + CD on Qwen3-1.7B-Base for all 14 in-distribution sub-tasks. Expect ~33.8 avg (Table 3). Validates prompt format and constraint decoding setup.
  2. **FT validation:** Fine-tune Qwen3-1.7B-Base on the combined training set of all 14 sub-tasks using LoRA (r=64, lr=5e-4). Expect ~63.9 avg, +9.2 over baseline. Confirms primary mechanism.
  3. **CD utility test:** Run the fine-tuned model from exp2 with and without constraint decoding on a high-precision task (e.g., NER CardioCCC). Expect negligible difference if model already outputs valid JSON, but CD ensures no catastrophic format failures.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Question:** Can significantly smaller LLMs (e.g., <500M parameters) or different architectural families achieve competitive performance on Italian medical NLP tasks when optimally adapted?
   - **Basis in paper:** The study focuses on "small LLMs (around one billion parameters)" and does not evaluate models substantially below this range.
   - **Why unresolved:** The research establishes a performance ceiling for ~1B parameter models but does not explore the lower bound of the "small" regime.

2. **Question:** To what extent do the findings on adaptation strategies generalize beyond the Italian language and the specific clinical domains studied?
   - **Basis in paper:** The "Limitations" section explicitly states the focus on Italian medical NLP datasets may limit generalizability to other languages.
   - **Why unresolved:** All datasets, pre-training corpora, and evaluation are Italian-specific; linguistic and clinical conventions vary across languages and healthcare systems.

3. **Question:** Why does Continual Pre-Training (CPT) provide a clear benefit for only one model family (Gemma-3-1b-it) while being neutral or detrimental for others?
   - **Basis in paper:** Table 3 shows CPT+FT improves over FT alone for Gemma-3-1b-it (+4.7 delta vs baseline) but yields smaller or negative gains for Llama and Qwen models.
   - **Why unresolved:** The paper does not investigate the interaction between each model's pre-training data composition and the domain shift introduced by CPT.

4. **Question:** For the most challenging task (Relation Extraction), what specific knowledge or reasoning capabilities are still lacking in small fine-tuned models compared to larger baselines, and can targeted data augmentation or architectural changes close this gap?
   - **Basis in paper:** The "Task-level analysis" states RE emerges as the most challenging, with small models showing significantly lower F1 (~11–24) than the larger Qwen3-32B baseline.
   - **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies RE as a bottleneck but does not diagnose the underlying cause or test targeted interventions.

5. **Question:** Can the superior few-shot + constraint decoding performance observed in some models (e.g., Qwen3-1.7B-Base) be systematically leveraged to reduce the need for supervised fine-tuning, perhaps through improved prompting templates or synthetic demonstration generation?
   - **Basis in paper:** Figure 1 and Table 3 show for Qwen3-1.7B-Base, 4-shot+CD achieves 36.8 avg, only ~27 points below its FT result (63.9), but the study uses random few-shot selection.
   - **Why unresolved:** It is unknown if curated, task-specific examples or automatically generated demonstrations could further narrow the gap to fine-tuning.

## Limitations
- **Data/Generalizability:** CPT sources have restrictive licenses; public approximations may not match original distribution. Findings are limited to Italian medical domains and may not generalize to other languages or clinical settings.
- **Methodology:** Key reproduction details unspecified (e.g., per-task vs. multi-task FT, exact LoRA hyperparameters, CPT epochs). Out-of-distribution evaluation covers only 6 test sets; small models still lag behind larger baselines.
- **Task-specific:** Relation Extraction remains challenging for 1B models even after fine-tuning (F1 ~11–24), indicating capacity limits for complex relational reasoning.

## Confidence
- **Fine-tuning is best:** High (consistent across tasks, statistically significant p>0.95).
- **Constraint decoding helps:** Medium (average gain +3.8, grammar-sensitive, runtime negligible).
- **4-shot + CD works:** Medium (average gain +13.2, dependent on shot quality).
- **Small > large (in-dist):** Medium (in-distribution gain confirmed, but OOD gap persists).
