# LORA-CRAFT: Cross-layer Rank Adaptation via Frozen Tucker Decomposition of Pre-trained Attention Weights

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.17510  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17510  
- **Reference count:** 22  
- **Primary result:** CRAFT achieves competitive GLUE performance (e.g., 88.0 average on RoBERTa-large) with only 41,152 trainable parameters—a 7–75× reduction compared to LoRA.

## Executive Summary
CRAFT introduces a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) method that captures cross-layer correlations in transformer attention weights. By stacking the query (Q) and value (V) projection matrices across all layers into 3D tensors and applying a frozen Tucker-3 decomposition via Higher-Order SVD (HOSVD), CRAFT learns only small square adaptation matrices that modulate the frozen factors. This design yields a trainable parameter count independent of model depth and hidden size, while a residual-preserving initialization guarantees exact recovery of pre-trained weights. On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa-base and RoBERTa-large, CRAFT achieves accuracy competitive with LoRA and other PEFT methods but with far fewer parameters.

## Method Summary
CRAFT first extracts Q and V weight matrices $W^{(\ell)}_{\alpha} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$ from each of the $N_L$ layers and stacks them into two 3D tensors $W_{\alpha} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_L \times d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$. For each tensor, it computes a full Tucker-3 decomposition via HOSVD, yielding a core tensor $G \in \mathbb{R}^{r_1 \times r_2 \times r_3}$ and three factor matrices $U^{(1)} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_L \times r_1}$, $U^{(2)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times r_2}$, $U^{(3)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{in}} \times r_3}$. All factors and the initial reconstruction $R_{\alpha} = G \times_1 U^{(1)} \times_2 U^{(2)} \times_3 U^{(3)}$ are frozen. Adaptation matrices $J^{(1)}, J^{(2)}, J^{(3)} \in \mathbb{R}^{r_n \times r_n}$ (initialized near identity) are learned for each projection. During training, the adapted tensor is reconstructed as $T_{\alpha} = G \times_1 (U^{(1)}J^{(1)}) \times_2 (U^{(2)}J^{(2)}) \times_3 (U^{(3)}J^{(3)})$. The final weight matrix for layer $\ell$ is given by $\widetilde{W}^{(\ell)}_{\alpha} = W^{(\ell)}_{\alpha} + (T^{(\ell)}_{\alpha} - R^{(\ell)}_{\alpha})$, ensuring that when $J^{(n)} = I$, the pre-trained weights are exactly recovered. The total trainable parameters are $2(r_1^2 + r_2^2 + r_3^2)$ (for both Q and V), independent of $N_L$ and $d$.

## Key Results
- CRAFT attains an average GLUE score of 88.0 on RoBERTa-large using only 41,152 trainable parameters.
- Compared to LoRA and other PEFT methods, CRAFT reduces the number of trainable parameters by a factor of 7 to 75 while maintaining competitive accuracy.
- The parameter count remains constant regardless of model depth or hidden dimension for fixed Tucker ranks, demonstrating superior efficiency scaling.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Stacking pre-trained Q and V weight matrices across layers into a 3D tensor captures cross-layer, output-dimension, and input-dimension correlations that per-layer methods miss.
- **Mechanism:** The weight matrices $W^{(\ell)}_\alpha \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$ for $\ell=1..N_L$ are stacked into a tensor $W_\alpha \in \mathbb{R}^{N_L \times d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$. This structure allows a single multilinear decomposition to model interactions between all three modes simultaneously.
- **Core assumption:** The pre-trained attention weights exhibit low multilinear rank across these three modes, meaning significant correlations exist that can be compressed via Tucker decomposition.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "stacks pre-trained Q and V attention weights across layers into 3D tensors"
  - [section 3.2]: "Given a transformer with $N_L$ layers, CRAFT proceeds... Stack: $W_\alpha \leftarrow \text{stack}(W^{(1)}_\alpha, ..., W^{(N_L)}_\alpha) \in \mathbb{R}^{N_L \times d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$"
  - [corpus]: Evidence is indirect; neighboring papers (e.g., LoTR, SuperLoRA) also explore cross-layer tensors but for $\Delta W$, supporting the premise that such structure exists. No direct citation of cross-layer weight correlations in the provided corpus.
- **Break condition:** If the true multilinear rank $(r_1, r_2, r_3)$ required to approximate $W_\alpha$ is close to $(N_L, d_{\text{out}}, d_{\text{in}})$, the decomposition provides no compression and loses parameter efficiency benefits.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Freezing all Tucker factors (core tensor $G$ and factor matrices $U^{(n)}$) from HOSVD provides a stable, compact basis for adaptation, decoupling trainable parameters from model dimension and depth.
- **Mechanism:** HOSVD computes $W_\alpha \approx G \times_1 U^{(1)} \times_2 U^{(2)} \times_3 U^{(3)}$. All factors are frozen. Adaptation is performed only by learning small square matrices $J^{(n)} \in \mathbb{R}^{r_n \times r_n}$ that transform the factor matrices before tensor reconstruction.
- **Core assumption:** The frozen Tucker factors constitute a sufficient basis to represent task-specific variations when modulated by the learned $J^{(n)}$ matrices. The HOSVD initialization captures the dominant subspace of the pre-trained weights.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "applies full Tucker-3 decomposition via Higher-Order SVD (HOSVD), freezes all factors, and trains only small square adaptation matrices"
  - [section 3.2]: "Compute the mode-$n$ unfolding... truncated SVD: $U^{(n)}_\alpha \leftarrow$ first $r_n$ left singular vectors... Core tensor... Freeze: $W_\alpha, G_\alpha, R_\alpha, U^{(1)}_\alpha, ...$"
  - [section 4.4]: Trainable parameter count $n_p(r_1^2 + r_2^2 + r_3^2)$ is independent of $d$ and $N_L$.
- **Break condition:** If the frozen factors do not span the relevant subspace for the downstream task (i.e., the required update lies outside the column spaces of $U^{(n)}$), the adaptation matrices $J^{(n)}$ cannot compensate, leading to poor performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The residual-preserving formula $\widetilde{W}_\alpha = W_\alpha + (T_\alpha - R_\alpha)$ guarantees exact recovery of pre-trained weights at initialization ($J^{(n)}=I$) regardless of Tucker approximation error, ensuring stable fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism:** $R_\alpha = G \times_1 U^{(1)} \times_2 U^{(2)} \times_3 U^{(3)}$ is the initial reconstruction (which equals $W_\alpha$ only if ranks are full). $T_\alpha = G \times_1 (U^{(1)}J^{(1)}) \times_2 (U^{(2)}J^{(2)}) \times_3 (U^{(3)}J^{(3)})$ is the adapted reconstruction. The difference $(T_\alpha - R_\alpha)$ represents the *change* from the pre-trained solution, which is added to the original $W_\alpha$. When $J^{(n)}=I$, $T_\alpha = R_\alpha$, so $\widetilde{W}_\alpha = W_\alpha$ exactly.
- **Core assumption:** Starting fine-tuning from the exact pre-trained solution is critical for stable convergence and avoids catastrophic forgetting or divergence early in training.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2]: "To preserve the pre-trained weights exactly at initialization, we store both $W_\alpha$ and $R_\alpha$... Remark 1: When $J^{(n)}_\alpha = I_{r_n}$ for all $n$, we have $T_\alpha = R_\alpha$, so $\widetilde{W}_\alpha = W_\alpha$."
  - [section 4.2]: Contrasts with PiSSA, which also uses a residual design but operates per-layer.
- **Break condition:** If the Tucker ranks are too low, $R_\alpha \neq W_\alpha$, but the formula still preserves $W_\alpha$ exactly at init. The break condition is not about preservation but about whether the low-rank approximation $R_\alpha$ (and thus the adaptation manifold) is too crude to model the required task-specific shift, leading to a fundamental capacity limit.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Tucker/HOSVD Decomposition**  
  - *Why needed here:* CRAFT's core operation is applying HOSVD to a 3D tensor. Understanding mode-$n$ unfolding, core tensor, and factor matrices is essential to grasp how cross-layer structure is compressed.  
  - *Quick check question:* What is the relationship between the multilinear rank $(r_1, r_2, r_3)$ and the sizes of the core tensor $G$ and factor matrices $U^{(n)}$ in a Tucker-3 decomposition?
- **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)**  
  - *Why needed here:* CRAFT is positioned as a PEFT method. Understanding the problem (cost of full fine-tuning), the LoRA baseline (per-layer low-rank updates), and the efficiency-accuracy tradeoff is necessary to evaluate CRAFT's contribution.  
  - *Quick check question:* Why does LoRA's parameter count scale as $O(N_L \cdot r \cdot d)$ while CRAFT's is $O(r_1^2 + r_2^2 + r_3^2)$ at fixed ranks?
- **Concept: Transformer Attention Projections**  
  - *Why needed here:* CRAFT specifically adapts the Query ($Q$) and Value ($V$) projection matrices. Knowing their role (Q computes attention scores, V provides content) explains why adapting them is a common PEFT practice.  
  - *Quick check question:* In the standard transformer attention mechanism $\text{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \text{softmax}(QK^T/\sqrt{d_k})V$, how does adapting $Q$ versus $K$ versus $V$ affect the model's behavior differently?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Tensor Constructor → HOSVD Engine → Freeze Manager → Adaptation Head → Residual Combiner  
- **Critical path:** Tensor construction → HOSVD (one-time, pre) → Initialize $J^{(n)}$ → Training loop: compute $T_\alpha$ from $J^{(n)}$ → compute $\widetilde{W}_\alpha$ via residual → forward pass with $\widetilde{W}^{(\ell)}_\alpha$ → backward pass updates only $J^{(n)}$.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Ranks $(r_1, r_2, r_3)$:* Smaller ranks drastically reduce parameters ($\sim r_n^2$) but risk underfitting. $r_1$ compresses layers; setting $r_1 = N_L$ (as in RoBERTa-large experiments) means no layer compression. Tradeoff between parameter savings and capacity.  
  - *Projection choice ($Q$ vs $V$ vs both):* Paper uses $Q+V$ based on PEFT convention. Adapting more projections increases parameters linearly ($n_p$ factor) but may improve performance.  
  - *Residual design:* Guarantees pre-trained recovery but adds memory overhead (storing $W_\alpha$ and $R_\alpha$). Without it, training could start from a poor initialization if $R_\alpha \neq W_\alpha$.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *Poor convergence/performance:* Check if $r_1, r_2, r_3$ are too small for the task/model size. Monitor if $R_\alpha$ is a poor approximation of $W_\alpha$ (high reconstruction error).  
  - *Exploding gradients:* Verify $J^{(n)}$ are initialized near identity ($\epsilon=0.01$). Larger $\epsilon$ or poor initialization destabilizes the residual connection.  
  - *No parameter savings:* If $r_n \approx \text{dim}(n)$, counts approach full fine-tuning. Confirm ranks are set correctly (e.g., $r_2=100 \ll d_{\text{out}}=768$ for RoBERTa-base).  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. *Reconstruction Sanity Check:* Compute $\| W_\alpha - R_\alpha \|_F / \| W_\alpha \|_F$ for various ranks. Should decrease with higher ranks. This validates the HOSVD implementation.  
  2. *Parameter Count Verification:* For given $(r_1,r_2,r_3)$ and $n_p=2$, compute $2(r_1^2+r_2^2+r_3^2)$. Confirm it's independent of $N_L$ and $d$ by testing with a different model (e.g., change $d_{\text{in}}$).  
  3. *Baseline Comparison (LoRA):* Fine-tune a single GLUE task (e.g., SST-2) with CRAFT and LoRA (same rank $r$ for LoRA). Plot accuracy vs. trainable parameters. Expect CRAFT to use far fewer parameters for comparable accuracy (as in Table 2).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. *Rank Scaling:* How should Tucker ranks scale with model depth and dimension to maintain performance? Whether the same ranks suffice as model dimension or depth grows substantially—or whether ranks must increase to maintain accuracy—remains an open empirical question.  
2. *Generalization to LLMs and Generation:* Does CRAFT generalize to large decoder-based LLMs and generative tasks? Extension to larger LLMs and generation tasks would strengthen the empirical contribution.  
3. *Statistical Variance:* What is the variance in CRAFT's performance across different random seeds? Results are reported for a single seed; future work should include variance analysis across multiple seeds.  
4. *Projection Choice:* Why does adapting both Q and V projections together enable effective cross-layer correlation capture? PCA analysis shows Q weights are more dispersed across layers than K/V; CRAFT adapts Q+V, but the paper does not ablate this choice or explain how Tucker decomposition exploits dispersion.  
5. *Tucker Error Impact:* How does the error from Tucker-3 approximation of pre-trained weights interact with the residual-preserving adaptation? CRAFT uses HOSVD (lossy) but preserves original weights via residual; the impact of initial reconstruction error on convergence and final accuracy is not analyzed.

## Limitations
- Ranks $(r_1, r_2, r_3)$ used in experiments are not explicitly reported; only total parameter counts are given, making exact reproduction difficult.  
- Training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, epochs, scheduler, warmup, weight decay) are omitted, hindering reproducibility and fair comparison.  
- The method's scalability to very deep or wider models, as well as to decoder-only architectures for generative tasks, is not established.

## Confidence
- **Cross-layer tensor captures inter-layer correlations:** Medium  
- **Frozen Tucker factors suffice for adaptation:** Low  
- **Residual-preserving initialization:** High  
- **Parameter efficiency & accuracy:** Medium
