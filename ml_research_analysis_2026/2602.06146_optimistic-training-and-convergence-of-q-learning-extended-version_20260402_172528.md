# Optimistic Training and Convergence of Q-Learning -- Extended Version

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.06146
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06146
- Reference count: 37
- Primary result: Oblivious Q-learning with linear function approximation may be unstable (no solution or multiple solutions to the projected Bellman equation). In contrast, optimistic (ε,κ)-tamed Gibbs policies guarantee almost sure ultimate boundedness (stability) but not convergence when the parameter dimension d≥2. An ideal basis (true Q-function in the span) can still yield two distinct solutions to the PBE under greedy and tamed Gibbs policies, proving non-convergence. The analysis extends to MSBE-SGD with similar findings.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the convergence behavior of Q-learning with linear function approximation. It shows that using a fixed (oblivious) training policy can lead to instability because the projected Bellman equation (PBE) may have no solution or multiple solutions. To address this, the authors consider optimistic, parameter-dependent policies—specifically (ε,κ)-tamed Gibbs—which ensure almost sure ultimate boundedness of the iterates for any dimension. However, they prove that convergence is not guaranteed when the parameter dimension is at least two: they construct a two-dimensional example where the linear class is ideal (the true Q-function lies in the span), yet there are two distinct solutions to the PBE, and Q-learning trajectories converge to one or the other depending on initialization. The same non-convergence phenomenon is observed for the MSBE-SGD variant. The paper thus clarifies the limits of linear Q-learning and highlights that stability does not imply convergence in higher dimensions.

## Method Summary
The paper studies Q-learning with linear function approximation \(Q_\theta(x,u)=\theta^\top\psi(x,u)\). The training policy \(\breve{\phi}^\theta\) determines the state-action distribution \(\varpi^\theta\) that appears in the mean-field ODE \(\dot{\theta} = \bar{f}(\theta)\), where \(\bar{f}(\theta)\) is the expected TD error under \(\varpi^\theta\). Two classes of policies are analyzed: oblivious (fixed, independent of \(\theta\)) and parameter-dependent (tamed Gibbs). The analysis consists of:
- One-dimensional analysis (Section 2.2) showing that an oblivious policy can yield zero, one, or multiple roots of \(\bar{f}\), and instability when roots are absent or unstable.
- Stability guarantee for tamed Gibbs policies (Section 2.3): For any \(\varepsilon>0\) and sufficiently large \(\kappa\), the mean flow is coercive and forces iterates into a bounded set almost surely.
- Non-convergence in higher dimensions (Section 3.1): Construction of a two-dimensional MDP with an ideal basis where the PBE has two distinct solutions under greedy and tamed Gibbs policies. The corresponding mean flow exhibits two asymptotically stable equilibria, leading to sample-path convergence to one of them depending on initialization.
- Extension to MSBE-SGD (Section 2.5 and 3.3): Similar stability and non-convergence patterns hold when using the MSBE eligibility vector.

The theoretical results are supported by simulations on a finite MDP with two states and two actions.

## Key Results
- Oblivious Q-learning may diverge because the projected Bellman equation can have no solution or multiple unstable solutions (Proposition 2.2).
- (ε,κ)-tamed Gibbs policies ensure almost sure ultimate boundedness (stability) of Q-learning iterates for any dimension (Proposition 2.3).
- For one-dimensional linear approximation, Q-learning with tamed Gibbs converges almost surely to the unique solution of the PBE (Proposition 2.4).
- In dimensions \(d \ge 2\), even with an ideal basis (true \(Q^\star\) in the span), the PBE can have multiple solutions under greedy and tamed Gibbs policies (Proposition 3.1). Consequently, Q-learning sample paths converge to different equilibria depending on initialization, and global convergence is not guaranteed.
- Similar non-convergence phenomena occur for MSBE-SGD (Section 3.3, Figure 6).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Oblivious policy instability
An oblivious (fixed) training policy yields a parameter-independent invariant distribution \(\varpi\). The mean flow \(\bar{f}(\theta)\) becomes a fixed vector field whose Jacobian \(A\) depends on \(\varpi\). In one dimension, the sign of \(A(\theta)\) relative to the bias term determines root existence. Proposition 2.2 identifies three cases: (1) unique stable root if \(\gamma R_{1,\ominus} < R\); (2) two roots with the larger one stable and trajectories diverging to \(-\infty\) if \(\gamma R_{1,\ominus} > R\) and other conditions; (3) no root if \(\gamma R_{1,\ominus} > R\) and sign mismatch, leading to divergence to \(+\infty\). Thus, oblivious training can easily produce instability.

### Mechanism 2: Stability via optimistic tamed Gibbs
The \((\varepsilon,\kappa)\)-tamed Gibbs policy smoothly approximates the greedy policy while ensuring exploration (\(\varepsilon>0\)). This makes the mean flow \(\bar{f}_{\kappa,\varepsilon}(\theta)\) satisfy a coercivity condition: for large \(\|\theta\|\), \(\theta^\top \bar{f}_{\kappa,\varepsilon}(\theta) \le -\varepsilon_0 \|\theta\|^2\) (Eq. (27)). This forces all trajectories into a bounded set almost surely (Proposition 2.3). The key is the policy's dependence on \(\theta\) and the small positive \(\varepsilon\) that prevents the flow from pointing outward at infinity. However, this policy does not guarantee a unique root when \(d\ge2\).

### Mechanism 3: Multiple equilibria in higher dimensions
Even with an ideal basis where the true \(Q^\star\) lies in the span, the PBE under a parameter-dependent policy can have multiple solutions. The paper constructs a two-dimensional basis using two distinct Q-functions \(Q^\star\) and \(Q^\diamond\) (Eq. (30)). Under assumptions (full rank autocorrelation matrices, unique greedy policies), the Implicit Function Theorem yields two distinct roots \(\theta^\star\) and \(\theta^\diamond\), each locally asymptotically stable (Proposition 3.1). For the tamed Gibbs policy with small \(\varepsilon\), these persist as stable equilibria. Hence, Q-learning trajectories converge to one of them based on initialization, and the algorithm does not converge to a single fixed point globally. This phenomenon is independent of the eligibility vector choice (PBE vs MSBE).

## Foundational Learning
- **Projected Bellman Equation (PBE)**: The PBE \(\bar{f}(\theta)=0\) characterizes fixed points of the mean-field TD update. It is the target condition for the learned parameters. The eligibility vector (choice of \(\zeta^\theta_k\)) determines whether the algorithm targets the PBE (Eq. 6a) or the gradient of MSBE (Eq. 6b). Quick check: What role does \(\zeta^\theta_k\) play? It defines the direction in parameter space used to project the Bellman error.
- **Eligibility Vectors (TD(0) vs. MSBE)**: The two common choices are \(\zeta^\theta_k = \psi(Z_k)\) (PBE) and \(\zeta^\theta_k = \psi(Z_k) - \gamma \mathbb{E}[\psi^\theta(Z_{k+1}|k)]\) (MSBE gradient). The latter introduces a model-dependent expectation, altering the Jacobian \(A(\theta)\). Both can exhibit similar stability and non-convergence issues. Quick check: How does the presence of \(\psi^\theta(Z_{k+1}|k)\) change analysis? It makes the mean flow a gradient field of the MSBE objective, but the structure of equilibria remains similar.
- **Parameter-Dependent vs. Oblivious Policies**: The training policy \(\breve{\phi}^\theta\) influences the invariant distribution \(\varpi^\theta\) and thus \(\bar{f}(\theta)\). An oblivious policy fixes \(\varpi\) independent of \(\theta\), simplifying analysis but risking non-existence or non-uniqueness of roots. A parameter-dependent policy (like tamed Gibbs) introduces coupling that can ensure stability (coercivity) but may still permit multiple equilibria in \(d\ge2\). Quick check: Why does tamed Gibbs guarantee stability while an oblivious policy may not? Because the policy’s dependence on \(\theta\) and exploration (\(\varepsilon>0\)) make \(\bar{f}(\theta)\) point inward for large \(\|\theta\|\).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: 
  Linear Q-approximator \(Q_\theta(x,u)=\theta^\top\psi(x,u)\) -> Training policy \(\breve{\phi}^\theta(u|x)\) (oblivious or tamed Gibbs) -> Invariant distribution \(\varpi^\theta\) -> Mean flow \(\bar{f}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\varpi^\theta}[\zeta^\theta_k (c + \gamma Q_\theta(X_{k+1}) - Q_\theta(Z_k))]\) -> ODE \(\dot{\theta} = \bar{f}(\theta)\) -> Stochastic process \(\{\theta_n\}\) (Q-learning iterates)
- **Critical path**: The policy choice determines \(\varpi^\theta\), which shapes \(\bar{f}(\theta)\). The properties of \(\bar{f}\) (existence, uniqueness, stability of zeros) dictate whether the stochastic algorithm is stable and/or convergent.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Oblivious policy: Simple analysis but may cause instability (no PBE solution or multiple unstable roots). Works only in special cases where \(\bar{f}\) has a unique stable zero.
  - Tamed Gibbs policy: Guarantees stability (ultimate boundedness) for any dimension by ensuring coercivity. However, it does not guarantee convergence in \(d\ge2\) because multiple stable equilibria can exist. Requires tuning \(\varepsilon\) (small) and \(\kappa\) (large) to satisfy stability conditions while remaining near-greedy.
  - Eligibility vector: PBE (Eq. 6a) is simpler; MSBE-SGD (Eq. 6b) yields a gradient-like flow but suffers similar non-uniqueness issues.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Divergence: \(\|\theta_n\| \to \infty\) a.s. when using oblivious policy and \(\bar{f}\) lacks a stable root (e.g., Cases 2,3 in Prop 2.2).
  - Non-convergent behavior: In \(d=2\), trajectories converge to different equilibria depending on initialization (e.g., Fig 1, Fig 5). The final policy performance (discounted cost) can vary widely across runs.
  - Sensitivity to hyperparameters: Too large \(\varepsilon\) or too small \(\kappa\) may destroy stability; too small step-sizes may cause slow mixing.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Oblivious policy stress test**: Implement the one-dimensional Gaussian example from Section 2.2. Vary basis parameters \(r, m, w\) to produce each of the three cases (unique stable root, two roots with divergence to \(-\infty\), no root). Monitor \(\|\theta_n\|\) to verify divergence in Cases 2 and 3.
  2. **Optimism gradient test**: For the two-dimensional ideal basis (Prop 3.1), run Q-learning with tamed Gibbs (\(\kappa=20, \varepsilon=10^{-3}\)) from diverse initial \(\theta_0\). Count the fraction of trajectories converging to \(\theta^\star \approx (1,0)\) vs \(\theta^\diamond \approx (0,\beta^\diamond)\). Vary \(\varepsilon\) to observe the path of the second equilibrium \(\theta^t(\varepsilon)\) (Fig 4).
  3. **Best-of-M policy selection**: Given non-convergence, run \(M=500\) independent Q-learning trials with tamed Gibbs from the same \(\theta_0\). Evaluate the discounted cost of each resulting greedy policy via Monte Carlo. Histogram the costs (Fig 5) to illustrate performance spread and the need for best-of-M selection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Uniqueness of PBE solutions under tamed Gibbs**: The paper leaves open conditions for uniqueness of the projected Bellman equation solution when using (ε,κ)-tamed Gibbs policies. The two-dimensional counterexample shows that even with an ideal basis, multiple solutions exist. Resolving this would require a general characterization (e.g., geometric conditions on the basis or MDP) that ensures a single root.
- **Convergence criteria beyond d=1**: Almost sure convergence is only proven for one-dimensional linear approximation. For \(d\ge2\), the paper demonstrates non-convergence due to multiple equilibria. Determining necessary and sufficient conditions for convergence (e.g., additional structural assumptions on the basis or MDP) remains open.
- **MSBE-SGD convergence**: While the paper extends stability to MSBE-SGD and provides numerical evidence of multiple equilibria, an analytic counterpart to Proposition 3.1 for MSBE-SGD is missing. It is unclear under what precise conditions MSBE-SGD suffers from non-unique stationary points and non-convergent sample paths.

## Limitations
- The analysis is restricted to finite MDPs and linear function approximation with a fixed basis; extensions to deep neural networks or continuous state/action spaces are not addressed.
- The constructed two-dimensional example proves existence of multiple equilibria but does not quantify how generic such phenomena are; the required assumptions (full rank, unique greedy policies) may be non-trivial to verify in practice.
- The stability bound \(B_{\varepsilon,\kappa}\) is implicit, offering little guidance for practical selection of \(\varepsilon\) and \(\kappa\).
- No algorithmic modifications are proposed to guarantee convergence; the paper primarily identifies shortcomings of existing approaches.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Propositions 2.2 (oblivious instability), 2.3 (stability of tamed Gibbs), 2.4 (convergence for d=1), and 3.1 (multiple equilibria in d≥2) are rigorously proven and cornerstone results.
- **Medium confidence**: Extension to MSBE-SGD (Section 3.3) is supported by analysis and a single numerical experiment but lacks a general theorem analogous to Proposition 3.1.
- **Low confidence**: Any claims extrapolating the findings to nonlinear function approximation or infinite state/action spaces are unsupported; the paper explicitly leaves these as open.
