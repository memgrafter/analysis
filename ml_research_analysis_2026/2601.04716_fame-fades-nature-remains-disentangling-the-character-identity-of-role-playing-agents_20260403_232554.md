# Fame Fades, Nature Remains: Disentangling the Character Identity of Role-Playing Agents

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.04716
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04716
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Moral Valence is the primary bottleneck for character fidelity; alignment priors suppress immoral traits (especially Goals/Morality), and Field-Aware Contrastive Decoding (FACD) can mitigate this gap by 25–62%.

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically investigates the factors that determine a role-playing agent's (RPA) ability to faithfully embody a character. It decomposes character identity into two components: **Parametric Identity** (pre-trained knowledge about famous characters) and **Attributive Identity** (behavioral traits from a structured profile). Through controlled experiments across axes of Familiarity, Structure, and Disposition, the study identifies **Moral Valence** as the dominant bottleneck: portraying immoral characters leads to severe, consistent performance drops. This degradation is attributed to safety alignment priors that suppress the generation of value-laden attributes like Goals and Morality. To address this, the authors propose **Field-Aware Contrastive Decoding (FACD)**, a training-free inference-time method that amplifies suppressed signals for immoral-field tokens, successfully narrowing the fidelity gap.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a controlled dataset of 211 character profiles (both famous and synthetic) with structured (28-field schema) and unstructured variants, labeled moral/immoral via an LLM judge. RPAs are built by embedding these profiles into prompts for backbone LLMs. Performance is evaluated on two benchmarks: **PersonaGym** (single-turn persona consistency) and **CoSER** (multi-turn character fidelity, anthropomorphism, storyline quality). The core diagnostic isolates the effect of the **Disposition** axis (Moral vs. Immoral), revealing a stark performance drop for immoral characters. The proposed mitigation, **FACD**, operates at inference without training. For each generation step, it performs two forward passes: one with the full character profile (positive prompt) and one with a negative prompt that includes all moral fields plus disposition-neutral Personality Attributes and certain fallback fields. The logits are then modulated via `z = z_pos + α * (z_pos - z_neg)` with α=1 to amplify tokens associated with the target (immoral) character specification.

## Key Results
*   **Moral Valence Bottleneck:** Immoral characters suffer a severe and consistent performance degradation (Δ -5.89 to -9.22 on CoSER) compared to moral ones, with the drop concentrated in the **Goals** and **Morality** fields.
*   **Fame Fades:** Known (famous) characters have a significant initial advantage due to Parametric Identity, but this gap closes as multi-turn dialogue history accumulates and dominates the context.
*   **FACD Mitigation:** Field-Aware Contrastive Decoding narrows the Moral–Immoral performance gap by 25–62% across evaluated models, improving Character Fidelity for immoral characters without harming performance on moral ones.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1.  **Alignment Suppression:** Safety fine-tuning (e.g., RLHF) shapes the model's output distribution to avoid harmful content. When prompted with an immoral character profile, the model's internal decision boundary conflicts with the profile's specifications, actively suppressing the token likelihoods needed to portray immoral **Goals** and **Morality** during decoding.
2.  **Context Dilution:** In multi-turn role-play, the transformer's attention mechanism increasingly weights recent tokens. As the dialogue history grows, attention shifts away from the initial character profile (containing Parametric Identity) toward accumulated context and self-generated tokens, causing the "Fame Fades" effect.
3.  **Selective Targeting:** Alignment priors focus on preventing harmful actions and endorsements, which are directly expressed through motivational attributes (Goals, Morality). This leads to stronger suppression in these fields compared to neutral personality traits (e.g., Extraversion), which remain relatively stable.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Character Profile Schema -> Dataset Construction (Known/Unknown, Moral/Immoral) -> Evaluation Benchmarks (PersonaGym, CoSER) -> Diagnostic Axes Analysis -> Mitigation Module (FACD) -> Judge LLM (GPT-4o)

Critical path:
1.  Define a character using the 5-dimension, 28-field schema.
2.  Construct dataset variants (familiar, structured, disposition).
3.  Run RPA on benchmarks with backbone LLMs.
4.  Analyze performance to identify Disposition (Moral) as the bottleneck.
5.  Implement FACD: generate positive/negative prompts based on field moral classification, apply contrastive logit modulation during decoding.
6.  Re-evaluate to measure gap reduction.

Design tradeoffs:
*   Schema granularity: 28 fields balance expressiveness and noise.
*   Dataset: Famous characters use parametric knowledge but risk canonical inconsistency; synthetic ones ensure coherence but lack prior knowledge.
*   FACD amplitude (α): Higher values amplify immoral signals more but risk incoherence/safety violations; paper uses α=1 unsensitized.
*   Evaluation: LLM-as-judge is scalable but may have biases vs. human judgment.

Failure signatures:
*   Low PersonaScore / CoSER scores, especially for immoral characters.
*   No "Fame Fades" effect (Known-Unknown gap persists across turns).
*   FACD reduces moral character performance or increases toxicity.
*   Attention analysis shows minimal lift from history in early turns for Known characters.

First 3 experiments:
1.  **Baseline:** Establish performance scores for all five backbone models on PersonaGym and CoSER using structured, moral character profiles.
2.  **Axis Isolation:** For each of the three axes (Familiarity, Structure, Disposition), run paired evaluations (e.g., Known vs. Unknown, Structured vs. Unstructured, Moral vs. Immoral) to quantify individual effect sizes.
3.  **Turn Ablation:** On CoSER, run multi-turn dialogues at varying lengths (6, 12, 18, 24 turns) to observe the decay of the Known-Unknown performance gap and record corresponding attention lifts from profile vs. history.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Continuous Morality:** How can the binary Disposition axis be extended to model continuous or multi-faceted moral spectra for anti-heroes and ambiguous characters?
*   **Generalization of FACD:** Does Field-Aware Contrastive Decoding generalize to larger architectures and diverse training paradigms beyond the evaluated frontier LLMs?
*   **Differential Suppression Mechanism:** Why are motivation-related attributes (Goals, Morality) more severely suppressed by alignment than personality traits (Extraversion)?
*   **Unexplored Identity Dimensions:** Are there unexplored dimensions (e.g., cultural context, relational dynamics) beyond the 28-field schema that significantly impact RPA performance?

## Limitations
*   Dataset and scenario generation rely on proprietary LLMs (Claude, Gemini), hindering open replication.
*   FACD hyperparameter choice (α=1, fallback field rules) lacks systematic justification and sensitivity analysis.
*   Evaluation uses GPT-4o as judge, which may carry biases not present in human assessment.

## Confidence
*   **Identity Decomposition (Parametric vs. Attributive):** High — robust axis-based experiments isolate effects.
*   **Moral Valence Bottleneck:** High — steep, consistent degradation in Goals/Morality across models; supported by attention shifts.
*   **FACD Mitigation:** Medium — gap reduction demonstrated but hyperparameter robustness and side-effects need deeper analysis.
