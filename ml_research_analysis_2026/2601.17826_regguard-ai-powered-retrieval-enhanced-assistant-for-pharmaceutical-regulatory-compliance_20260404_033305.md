# RegGuard: AI-Powered Retrieval-Enhanced Assistant for Pharmaceutical Regulatory Compliance

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.17826
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17826
*   **Reference Count:** 36
*   **Primary Result:** A RAG system with novel chunking (HiSACC) and reranking (ReLACE) automates compliance analysis, achieving high groundedness (0.8453) and faithfulness (0.9252) at low operational cost (~$200/month).

## Executive Summary
Pharmaceutical regulatory compliance involves interpreting vast, evolving, and heterogeneous documents. RegGuard is an AI-powered assistant designed to automate this analysis, reducing manual effort and LLM hallucination. It combines a hierarchical semantic chunking method (HiSACC) that intelligently links related non-contiguous content with a domain-adapted cross-encoder reranker (ReLACE) trained on in-house QA data. Evaluations demonstrate that the integrated system significantly outperforms baseline retrieval methods on several key metrics. The system is deployed cost-effectively on cloud infrastructure with a reported monthly cost of approximately $180–200.

## Method Summary
The system is built on a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline. First, documents (PDFs, DOCX, etc.) are processed, with OCR applied to scanned files. The core innovation lies in the retrieval stage. **HiSACC** chunks documents hierarchically: text is split into minimal units (e.g., sentences), groups are formed based on local semantic similarity, and then groups are merged across a skip-window if inter-group similarity is high, all while respecting a token budget. This produces coherent, context-rich chunks even when relevant information is distributed. Retrieved chunks are then **reranked by ReLACE**, a cross-encoder fine-tuned with a listwise loss on a proprietary set of ~4.3k query-passage pairs. Finally, a generative LLM (GPT-4) synthesizes an answer grounded in the top reranked passages. Critical hyperparameters for HiSACC and ReLACE's training configuration are not publicly disclosed, posing a challenge for exact replication.

## Key Results
*   **High Faithfulness & Groundedness:** Achieved a Faithfulness Test score of 0.9252 and a Groundedness Rate of 0.8453 at top-15 retrieval.
*   **Low Over-Retrieval:** The Over-Retrieval Penalty was reduced to 0.0054, indicating precise retrieval.
*   **Cost-Effective Operation:** The system runs at an approximate monthly cost of $180–200 on AWS infrastructure.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RegGuard's performance gain stems from addressing two critical weaknesses in standard RAG for regulatory text. First, **HiSACC** mitigates the problem of rigid, contiguous chunking (e.g., sliding windows) by creating semantically continuous chunks that can bridge sections, capturing context that spans document structures like tables, lists, and paragraphs. Second, **ReLACE** overcomes the limitations of off-the-shelf retrievers or simple bi-encoders by undergoing domain adaptation via listwise fine-tuning. This teaches the model to prioritize passages that are truly relevant to a specific regulatory query over superficially similar ones, directly improving the quality of context provided to the generator and reducing hallucination. The combination ensures the retrieved evidence is both comprehensive and precise.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Hierarchical Semantic Chunking:** Why needed: Regulatory meaning often crosses traditional document boundaries (e.g., a definition in section 1 clarifies a requirement in section 5). Rigid chunking breaks this. Quick check: Visualize chunk boundaries on a sample regulation and verify that a single regulatory concept does not fracture across chunks.
*   **Cross-Encoder Reranking:** Why needed: Bi-encoders (e.g., SBERT) sacrifice ranking accuracy for speed. For high-stakes compliance, a more nuanced relevance assessment is required. Quick check: On a held-out set, compare NDCG@10 of a bi-encoder vs. a cross-encoder fine-tuned on domain data.
*   **Listwise Ranking Loss:** Why needed: Pairwise or pointwise losses don't optimize the full ranking order. Listwise loss (e.g., ListNet) directly optimizes metrics like NDCG by considering the entire candidate list. Quick check: During training, monitor validation NDCG@K to confirm it improves with listwise training versus a pairwise baseline.
*   **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** Why needed: Prevents LLM hallucination by grounding generation in verifiable, up-to-date source documents. Quick check: Compare generator outputs with and without retrieved context on factual questions; context should increase citation accuracy.
*   **Domain Adaptation:** Why needed: General-purpose embeddings/retrievers perform poorly on specialized jargon and structure of regulatory texts. Quick check: Evaluate zero-shot retrieval (e.g., using `text-embedding-ada-002`) on a regulatory QA set vs. a domain-tuned model; domain model should show significant MRR improvement.
*   **Evaluation Metrics (GR, FT, ORP):** Why needed: Traditional retrieval metrics (Recall@K) don't measure whether the *retrieved content actually supports a correct answer*. GR/FT measure answer grounding and factuality; ORP penalizes retrieving irrelevant passages. Quick check: Manually inspect a sample of retrieved passages labeled as "grounded" or "faithful" to correlate with the automatic metric scores.

## Architecture Onboarding
Data Ingestion (PDF/DOCX/OCR) -> HiSACC Chunking -> Vector Index (Milvus/FAISS) -> ANN Retrieval (Top-K) -> ReLACE Reranking (Cross-Encoder) -> Context Assembly -> LLM Generation (GPT-4/Llama-3) -> Answer Output

*   **Critical Path:** The end-to-end latency is dominated by the cross-encoder reranking step (ReLACE), which must score ~K candidates. HiSACC's chunking quality is the upstream determinant of retrieval ceiling.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   *HiSACC Token Budget (L)*: Larger L yields richer context but may include noise and increase generator input cost/latency. Smaller L may omit crucial details.
    *   *Reranking List Size (K)*: Larger K increases chance of including the perfect passage but linearly increases reranking latency and cost.
    *   *Model Selection:* Using a proprietary model (GPT-4) ensures quality but locks in vendor cost and API dependency. Open-source alternatives (Llama-3) reduce cost but may require more prompt engineering for reliability.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   *HiSACC Failure:* Chunk boundaries split a single regulatory clause or merge unrelated sections from different documents. Symptom: Low intra-chunk semantic cohesion scores, generator answers cite contradictory passages.
    *   *ReLACE Failure:* Reranking does not improve (or worsens) over the initial ANN order. Symptom: Similar MRR@K before and after rerank; validation loss fails to converge during fine-tuning.
    *   *Systemic Failure:* High faithfulness score coupled with low groundedness suggests the generator is producing plausible but unsupported answers. Symptom: Disconnect between retrieval metrics and human evaluation of answer correctness.
*   **First Three Experiments:**
    1.  **Validate HiSACC chunk coherence:** Apply the algorithm (with a sweep of θ, γ, w) to a public FDA drug label corpus. Compute average intra-chunk cosine similarity and have a regulatory expert rate 100 random chunks for semantic integrity on a 1-5 scale.
    2.  **Benchmark ReLACE ranking efficacy:** Using the synthetic QA pairs generated for training, evaluate the fine-tuned reranker against the base `bge-reranker-base` on a held-out set using ranking metrics (MRR@10, NDCG@10). Goal: >10% relative improvement on base model.
    3.  **Measure end-to-end GR/FT:** Run the full pipeline (HiSACC+ReLACE+LLM) on 50 manually verified QA pairs from the public corpus. Compute GR and FT using the same embedding model as claimed (e.g., `text-embedding-ada-002`). Establish a baseline performance target before attempting to match proprietary numbers.

## Limitations
*   **Critical Hyperparameters Omitted:** Exact values for HiSACC's similarity thresholds (θ, γ), skip-window size (w), and token budget (L) are not reported, preventing exact replication.
*   **Proprietary Evaluation Data:** The RC-QA dataset (967 pairs) is in-house and not public. Metric computation details (e.g., embedding model for GR/FT, ORP threshold τ) are undisclosed.
*   **Infrastructure & Training Details:** Google Drive sync frequency, OCR configuration, Milvus index parameters (IVF nlist, nprobe), and ReLACE's full training setup (batch size, negative sampling ratio, listwise candidate size K) are not specified.
