# UNet-Based Keypoint Regression for 3D Cone Localization in Autonomous Racing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21904  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21904  
- **Reference count:** 35  
- **Primary result:** UNet keypoint regression achieves MSE 3.4172 and mAP 0.83, significantly surpassing ResNet (MSE 6.3165, mAP 0.42) and SIFT for 3D cone localization.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a UNet-based neural network for precise keypoint regression to enable accurate 3D cone localization in autonomous racing. Trained on a newly assembled 25k-image dataset capturing diverse cone appearances and conditions, the method regresses six keypoints per cone. The approach outperforms a replicated ResNet baseline and traditional SIFT features in keypoint accuracy (MSE, mAP). Integrated into a stereo-vision pipeline, it maintains real-time performance with minimal GPU overhead (3% increase) and demonstrates high accuracy in simulation and recorded real-world data. The work highlights the value of spatial-preserving architectures and large-scale custom datasets for robust perception in dynamic racing environments.

## Method Summary
The core method uses a UNet encoder-decoder architecture to perform keypoint regression. Inputs are 80×80 RGB images cropped around cone detections from a YOLOv8 model. The encoder downsamples through four blocks, doubling channels each time (64→512), while the decoder upsamples via transpose convolutions, concatenating skip connections from corresponding encoder layers to preserve fine spatial details. The output layer produces six heatmaps (one per keypoint). Training uses a combined loss: mean squared error on Gaussian-blurred target heatmaps and L1/smooth L1 loss on extracted coordinates (via soft-argmax or peak finding). The model is trained on 20k filtered samples from a 25k custom dataset, with rotation-based augmentation (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) and random boundary cropping applied to both images and keypoint coordinates. AdamW with an exponential learning rate scheduler (×0.99 per epoch) optimizes the network.

## Key Results
- UNet achieves MSE 3.4172 and mAP 0.83 for keypoint detection, substantially better than ResNet (MSE 6.3165, mAP 0.42) and SIFT.
- The integrated pipeline shows feasible real-time performance, with only a 3% GPU usage increase on an in-car PC.
- The method demonstrates high accuracy in simulation and recorded real-world driving data, with robust cone localization under variable lighting, mud, and partial occlusions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The UNet encoder-decoder architecture with skip connections preserves spatial resolution, enabling precise keypoint localization.
- **Mechanism:** The encoder captures semantic context through downsampling; the decoder upsamples while concatenating skip connections from encoder layers to recover fine-grained spatial details lost during pooling. This is critical for regressing exact pixel coordinates of small cone keypoints.
- **Break condition:** If keypoints are severely occluded or the cone occupies fewer than ~20 pixels, spatial details may be irretrievably lost in early encoder layers, causing peak divergence.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A large-scale, diverse custom dataset (25k images) provides sufficient variance in cone appearance, lighting, and partial occlusions to train a robust model.
- **Mechanism:** The dataset’s diversity—captured from various perspectives and conditions—exposes the model to a wide distribution of cone manifestations (mud, damage, lighting). This reduces overfitting to specific scenarios and improves generalization to unseen real-world tracks.
- **Break condition:** If deployment environments introduce novel cone variants (e.g., extreme mud coverage, completely new shapes) not represented in the 25k set, performance may degrade sharply.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Accurate keypoint regression improves 3D cone localization and downstream pipeline stability by providing precise correspondence for stereo disparity.
- **Mechanism:** The six predicted keypoints per cone allow a robust estimate of the cone’s image-plane center (mean x). This mean, used in disparity computation (Z = f*T/D), yields more consistent depth than bounding-box centroids or sparse feature matches. Stable depth feeds the EKF, reducing state estimation error and covariance growth.
- **Break condition:** If the stereo pair has significant vertical disparity mismatch or keypoints are systematically biased, the mean x computation propagates error into depth, potentially causing error snowballing in path planning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Keypoint Regression (KPR) vs. Bounding Box Detection  
  **Why needed:** The task requires precise geometric points (six per cone) for 3D triangulation and color segmentation, not just a coarse region. Understanding KPR’s heatmap-based output (dense per-pixel probability) versus discrete coordinate regression is key.  
  **Quick check:** Why might predicting a heatmap be more robust to small localization shifts than directly regressing (x,y) coordinates?

- **Concept:** Stereo Disparity and Depth Computation  
  **Why needed:** The pipeline converts 2D keypoint matches into 3D world coordinates. The engineer must understand how disparity (D) from corresponding points in left/right images, along with camera intrinsics (f) and baseline (T), yields depth (Z = f*T/D).  
  **Quick check:** Given f=1400px, T=0.12m, and disparity D=15px, what is the depth Z in meters?

- **Concept:** Data Augmentation for Geometric Consistency  
  **Why needed:** Rotations (90°, 180°, 270°) are applied to both image and keypoint coordinates to expand dataset size and teach rotational invariance. The engineer must implement coordinate transformation matrices correctly during augmentation.  
  **Quick check:** If an image is rotated 180°, how do the (x,y) coordinates of a keypoint at (100,200) transform in the new image space (assuming same origin at top-left)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 80x80x3 input -> UNet Encoder (4 downsampling blocks, channels 64→128→256→512) -> Bottleneck (5x5 resolution, 512 channels) -> UNet Decoder (4 upsampling blocks with skip connections from encoder, channels halved each step) -> Output Layer (1x1 conv to 6 channels) -> Coordinate decoding (peak finding or soft-argmax).
- **Critical path:** Image -> Encoder (downsample, capture context) -> Bottleneck -> Decoder (upsample + skip connections to restore details) -> 6-channel heatmap -> Extract (x,y) per channel -> Loss computed against ground-truth keypoints.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Heatmap vs. Direct Coordinate Regression: Heatmaps are more stable but require post-processing and are sensitive to resolution.  
  - Model capacity: Fixed channel progression; increasing parameters may improve accuracy but risks overfitting and higher latency.  
  - Loss function: Combined heatmap (MSE on Gaussians) and position (L1/smooth L1) losses; weighting affects convergence and error distribution.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Systematic failure on cones with extreme specular highlights or heavy mud obscuring base/stripe keypoints.  
  - Failure on cones partially outside the YOLOv8 bounding box—keypoints predicted outside valid image region.  
  - High variance in predicted keypoints across slightly perturbed inputs (lack of predictive confidence).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate the ResNet baseline on the same 20k dataset with identical splits; compare validation MSE/mAP to UNet’s reported 3.4172 MSE and 0.83 mAP.  
  2. Ablate rotation augmentation: train UNet without rotations, evaluate on a held-out set containing rotated cones to quantify robustness gain.  
  3. Integrate UNet into the ROS2 pipeline using a recorded bag; log 3D cone positions, compare detection count and position error against YOLOv8-only baseline, and monitor GPU/CPU usage to verify <5% overhead.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** How can occlusion scenarios be mitigated for cone keypoint detection while maintaining real-time performance?  
  **Basis:** Future work may also explore mitigating occlusion scenarios in a computationally efficient manner. (Discussion and Conclusion)  
  **Why unresolved:** The model exhibits a ~3% failure rate on partially occluded or densely clustered cones; no occlusion-specific solutions are implemented.  
  **What evidence would resolve it:** Train on a dataset enhanced with synthetically occluded cones and targeted augmentations; evaluate failure-rate reduction and inference time on the in-car PC.

- **Question:** Can the UNet-KPR framework be generalized to full 3D scene understanding (e.g., depth-aware object maps, semantic voxel grids)?  
  **Basis:** While our method focuses on cone keypoints, the framework can be extended to full 3D scene understanding by regressing depth-aware object maps or semantic voxel grids. (Discussion and Conclusion)  
  **Why unresolved:** Validation is limited to cone keypoint localization; extending to dense 3D tasks requires architectural modifications and broader datasets, which are not addressed.  
  **What evidence would resolve it:** Modify the network to output dense 3D representations, train on KITTI or Cityscapes, and benchmark against state-of-the-art 3D detection methods in accuracy and speed.

- **Question:** Does image-text supervision improve keypoint detection accuracy or enable zero-shot generalization without sacrificing real-time performance?  
  **Basis:** Future work may also explore image-text supervision... (Discussion and Conclusion)  
  **Why unresolved:** The potential benefits of multi-modal supervision (e.g., leveraging language descriptions for rare conditions) and its computational trade-offs are not investigated.  
  **What evidence would resolve it:** Integrate CLIP-based text embeddings as auxiliary inputs; test on out-of-distribution scenes (e.g., unseen weather/lighting) for accuracy and generalization, while measuring inference latency on target hardware.

## Limitations
- **Architecture details unspecified:** Decoder upsampling method (transpose vs. interpolation), exact channel halving point, and post-upsample convolutions are unclear, risking reproduction errors.
- **Dataset coverage gaps:** The 25k-image set may lack extreme mud, heavy occlusion, or very small keypoints (<20 px), leading to failure in those cases.
- **Evaluation limitations:** ResNet baseline lacks ablation of skip connections and statistical variance; 3% GPU overhead claim is hardware-dependent and may not generalize.
