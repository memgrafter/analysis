# Progressive Ideation using an Agentic AI Framework for Human-AI Co-Creation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.00475
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00475
- Reference count: 36
- Primary result: The MIDAS framework shifts ideation from semantically clustered, variant-heavy outputs to "noise" clusters, reducing 70+ raw AI ideas to 11–24 globally novel, diverse concepts per session while maintaining human designer engagement within 20 minutes.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses a key limitation of current AI ideation tools: single-spurt large language models (LLMs) produce semantically clustered ideas with low diversity, hampering true innovation for novice designers. To solve this, the authors introduce MIDAS, a distributed agentic AI framework implementing a Continuous Generation/Continuous Assessment (CG/CA) pipeline. The system uses 13 specialized agents to progressively generate, evaluate, and refine design ideas, checking both local novelty (within the session) and global novelty (against real-world prior art via web search). Human designers participate as equal partners from the start through the Participatory, Active, Collaborative (PAC) model. Empirical results across six design domains show a measurable shift from dense clusters to a single "noise" cluster in DBSCAN analysis, demonstrating extreme semantic diversity in final curated sets. User studies indicate the PAC model sustains engagement and ownership during short sessions.

## Method Summary
MIDAS is a distributed, multi-agent system that decomposes ideation into discrete meta-cognitive roles. It operates on structured data models: AI3C (problem representation), AOC (action-object idea format), and PFIC (final concept schema). The core CG/CA pipeline treats ideation as a temporal, iterative process rather than a single batch output. Each generated idea is immediately assessed for local and global novelty; these insights directly inform subsequent generation steps. The PAC model reserves the first generative step for human ideas, which are commingled with AI outputs and evaluated blindly, ensuring designer co-ownership. Performance is measured via the NDFR framework (Novelty, Diversity, Feasibility, Relevance), with local diversity empirically validated through UMAP dimensionality reduction and DBSCAN clustering—aiming for a "noise" cluster pattern indicating extreme semantic spread.

## Key Results
- Achieves extreme semantic diversity: final curated sets form a single "noise" cluster in DBSCAN analysis, contrasting with dense clusters from single-spurt LLM generation.
- Efficient novelty filtering: reduces 70+ raw AI-generated ideas to 11–24 globally novel, diverse concepts per 20-minute session.
- Maintains human engagement: the PAC model elevates designers to active co-creators, with participants reporting high ownership and sustained involvement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Continuous Generation/Continuous Assessment (CG/CA) Pipeline**
- Claim: The CG/CA pipeline progressively enhances novelty and diversity by treating ideation as an iterative process.
- Mechanism: Each idea is immediately evaluated for local novelty (semantic distance from prior session ideas via vector clustering) and global novelty (distance from existing solutions via web search); assessment insights directly shape the next generation step (e.g., Mint deconstructs ideas into Action-Object pairs, Navigator synthesizes new concepts).
- Core assumption: Human ideation is path-dependent; violating this temporal logic (as in single-spurt generation) causes semantic fixation and variant-heavy outputs.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] describes progressive refinement with local/global novelty checks; [section 2.3] emphasizes the intertwined cycle; [section 4.1] questions CG/CA's impact; corpus neighbors support iterative ideation models.
- Break condition: If semantic distance computation fails or feedback loop disrupts (e.g., Mint's deconstruction yields indistinct seeds), progression stalls, reverting to variant generation.

**Mechanism 2: Participatory, Active, Collaborative (PAC) Model**
- Claim: PAC maintains engagement by making the human an equal contributor from the start, not a passive filter.
- Mechanism: The Muse agent reserves the first generative step for designer ideas, converted to AOC format and commingled in the Idea Vault; downstream evaluators (Gatekeeper, Challenger) are blind to origin; designer retains pruning/steering via Sentinel and Director.
- Core assumption: Disengagement stems from tools that sideline human creativity; co-ownership emerges when human and machine contributions compete and evolve equally.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] elevates human from passive filterer; [section 3.2] details Muse's human seeding; [section 6.3] reports high ownership; corpus neighbors emphasize partnership.
- Break condition: If human ideas are structurally incompatible with AOC or downstream agents override inputs without transparency, partnership perception collapses.

**Mechanism 3: Distributed Agentic Specialization**
- Claim: 13 specialized agents overcome monolithic LLM "novelty illusion" by decomposing ideation into discrete meta-cognitive roles.
- Mechanism: Agents perform targeted operations (Forge for exploration, Mint for deconstruction, Scout for feasibility, Navigator for synthesis) with tailored LLMs/temperatures; mimics human team division of labor.
- Core assumption: A single generalist LLM cannot simultaneously manage divergent, convergent, evaluative, and synthetic demands; specialization reduces cognitive interference.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.3] lists all 13 agents with specific purposes; [section 3.4.1] describes ensemble model selection; [section 6.1] credits specialization with deconstructing the novelty illusion; corpus neighbors discuss multi-agent benefits.
- Break condition: If agent roles overlap (e.g., Navigator and Forge produce redundant ideas) or orchestrator misroutes data, the pipeline loses its specialization advantage, reverting to clustering.

- Why needed: These schemas are the shared language enabling specialized agents to pass high-fidelity data; incompatibility breaks the pipeline.

- Why needed: The system intentionally reduces raw idea volume through successive novelty/feasibility/relevance checks; understanding this funnel explains the final "noise cluster" outcome.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map (plain text flow):
User -> Scribe (to Problem Vault); User -> Muse (to Idea Vault); Forge (reads Problem Vault, adds to Idea Vault); Gatekeeper (reads Idea Vault, filters local novelty); Librarian (reads Problem Vault, populates Literature Vault); Challenger (compares Gatekeeper output vs Literature Vault, filters global novelty); Mint (reads Challenger output, extracts Action-Object); Scout (scores Action-Object pairs); Navigator (synthesizes new AOCs with local novelty check); Sentinel (validates against Problem Vault, designer intervenes); Director (AOCs -> PFIC to Concept Vault); Leo (PFIC -> images).

Critical path:
1. User provides unstructured problem.
2. Scribe structures problem into AI3C in Problem Vault.
3. Muse converts human ideas to AOC in Idea Vault.
4. Forge (Formulator+Explorer) generates AOCs from AI3C.
5. Gatekeeper computes local novelty/diversity via embeddings and DBSCAN, shortlisting.
6. Librarian searches web for existing solutions, populates Literature Vault.
7. Challenger compares Gatekeeper shortlist to Literature Vault, filters for global novelty.
8. Mint deconstructs Challenger output into Action-Object pairs.
9. Scout scores Action-Object pairs for feasibility.
10. Navigator synthesizes new AOCs from top pairs (with local diversity check).
11. Sentinel validates against AI3C; designer can prune/steer.
12. Director converts AOCs to PFIC concepts; Leo generates photorealistic images.

Design tradeoffs:
- Specialization vs. Overhead: 13 agents increase clarity/control but add orchestration complexity and latency versus a single fine-tuned LLM.
- Filtering Aggressiveness: High thresholds ensure extreme diversity but risk discarding viable adjacent ideas; tuned via DBSCAN parameters.
- Human-in-the-Loop Timing: PAC engages designer early (Muse) and late (Sentinel/Director), but mid-pipeline lacks explicit intervention points, potentially missing intuition during combinatorial explosion.

Failure signatures:
- Clustering Failure: Final ideas form dense clusters (like single-spurt) → embedding model mismatch or DBSCAN parameters (eps, min_samples) incorrect.
- Global Novelty Miss: Final ideas locally diverse but close to prior art → Challenger's similarity threshold too high or Librarian's search too narrow.
- Designer Disengagement: Designer never adds ideas to Muse or overrides Sentinel → UX hinders intervention or designer perceives AI ideas as superior.

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline Comparison: Run single-spurt (100 ideas from Forge) and MIDAS on same problem; compare DBSCAN clusters and NDFR metrics. Expected: MIDAS shows "noise" cluster; single-spurt shows dense clusters.
2. PAC Validation: Run two MIDAS sessions per problem: with Muse enabled (human seeds) vs. disabled (AI-only start); post-session survey on ownership/engagement. Expected: Muse-enabled shows higher perceived ownership.
3. Global Novelty Sanity Check: After Challenger, manually verify top 5 ideas against Google Patents/Google Scholar; record false positives. Expected: Low false positive rate (<10%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can MIDAS produce ideas demonstrably more novel, diverse, and feasible than single-stage, non-agentic AI ideation? Unresolved due to limited study with six novice designers across six predefined problems in short sessions; generalizability to experts and complex problems is unknown.
2. Does the CG/CA pipeline independently contribute to enhanced local novelty and diversity? Unresolved because the paper shows MIDAS's overall success but does not isolate CG/CA's causal impact from other components (e.g., agent specialization, global checks).
3. Does the global novelty check reliably filter for truly innovative ideas, not just semantic variants? Unresolved because Librarian's web-search grounding may have incomplete prior-art coverage (e.g., limited patents, paywalled literature), risking false positives.
4. How does the PAC model concretely impact designer engagement, ownership, and co-creation fluency over time? Unresolved because findings are based on immediate feedback from isolated 20-minute sessions with novices; long-term effects on creative habits and expert-novice differences are unknown.

## Limitations
- Exact agent prompts, temperature settings, and LLM assignments are undisclosed, hindering exact behavioral replication.
- Specific embedding model and DBSCAN parameters (eps, min_samples) that reliably produce the "noise" cluster outcome are not specified.
- Precise UI/API for designer intervention at Sentinel/Director stages and how inputs are integrated remain ambiguous.

## Next Checks

2. PAC timing intervention study: Conduct a within-subjects study comparing designer engagement (via NASA-TLX and ownership surveys) between sessions with early human seeding (Muse enabled) versus late intervention only (Sentinel/Director), quantifying impact of intervention timing.
3. Global novelty false positive audit: Manually verify the top 20 Challenger-filtered ideas against Google Patents/Google Scholar using exact phrase matching and semantic review; calculate false positive rate to validate Librarian/Challenger grounding accuracy.
