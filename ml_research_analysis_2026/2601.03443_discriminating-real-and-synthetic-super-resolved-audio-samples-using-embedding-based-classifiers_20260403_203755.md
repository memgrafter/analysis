# Discriminating real and synthetic super-resolved audio samples using embedding-based classifiers
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03443
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03443
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Linear classifiers on embeddings achieve near-perfect accuracy (100% with external embeddings) in separating real and synthetic super-resolved audio, revealing a gap between perceptual quality and distributional fidelity.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the distributional fidelity of audio super-resolution (ADSR) models by training linear classifiers on various embeddings (frozen discriminator features, OpenL3, log-Mel) to discriminate real wideband audio from synthetic upsampled audio. Despite high perceptual quality scores (MUSHRA) and state-of-the-art signal metrics (SNR, LSD), classifiers achieve near-perfect accuracy (up to 100%) across GAN and diffusion models, indicating a persistent gap between perceptual realism and true distribution matching.

The study reveals that even when a GAN's discriminator reaches equilibrium (~50% classification accuracy), its intermediate features retain discriminative power. External embeddings particularly expose the representational gap, suggesting that current ADSR models fail to capture the high-frequency manifold of natural audio. The findings call for new evaluation paradigms that better measure distributional fidelity.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate five audio super-resolution models (AudioUNet, MU-GAN, HiFi-GAN, FlowHigh, FlashSR) on VCTK and FMA-small datasets for 4→16 kHz and 16→48 kHz upsampling. For each model, they generate synthetic wideband audio from narrowband inputs. They extract three types of embeddings: (1) frozen discriminator pre-FC features from MU-GAN, (2) OpenL3 embeddings (512-D, for ≤16 kHz audio), and (3) log-Mel spectrograms (256 bins, adaptively pooled). Using these embeddings, they train Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) classifiers to separate real vs. synthetic samples, reporting test accuracy and visualizing projections. Additionally, they compute traditional metrics (SNR, LSD) and conduct MUSHRA subjective tests.

## Key Results
- Linear classifiers on discriminator embeddings achieve 56–93% accuracy, varying by model.
- Using external embeddings (OpenL3, log-Mel), classifiers attain 100% accuracy across all ADSR models and upsampling tasks.
- High perceptual scores (MUSHRA) and low SNR/LSD do not correlate with classifier accuracy, indicating a disconnect between perceptual quality and distributional fidelity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Frozen discriminator intermediate features retain enough signal to linearly separate real from synthetic audio, even when the discriminator's final classification accuracy has converged to chance (~50%).
- **Mechanism:** During GAN training, the discriminator learns rich hierarchical features to distinguish real from generated samples. Even after the generator becomes strong enough to fool the discriminator's final layer (achieving ~50% accuracy), the intermediate feature representations (pre-FC layer) remain sensitive to subtle statistical differences between real and synthetic distributions. A new linear classifier trained on these frozen features can exploit this latent discriminative structure.
- **Core assumption:** The discriminator's internal representations capture characteristics of the real data distribution that are not erased by adversarial training.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Classifiers trained on [...] frozen discriminator representations [...] achieve near-perfect separation."
  - [section 4.3] Table 2 shows MU-GAN discriminator embeddings yield 83–93% accuracy for other models, while for its own synthetic outputs accuracy is lower (56–70%) but still well above chance. LDA projections in Fig. 3 show separation despite discriminator equilibrium.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., "Unraveling Hidden Representations") explore similar concepts for image/video forensics, providing indirect support that discriminator features retain forensic traces, but direct evidence for ADSR is weak or missing in the provided corpus.
- **Break condition:** If the generator perfectly matches the target distribution, the discriminator's intermediate features would become indistinguishable for real and synthetic inputs, leading to random LDA projections and ~50% linear classifier accuracy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Embeddings from models trained on natural audio (OpenL3, log-Mel) expose a fundamental representational gap between real wideband audio and any current ADSR output, indicating failure to match the true high-frequency manifold.
- **Mechanism:** Pre-trained models like OpenL3 learn embeddings that capture perceptually and semantically relevant structure from natural audio corpora. Log-Mel spectrograms represent standard time-frequency representations used in audio processing. Linear classifiers operating in these spaces achieve 100% accuracy, suggesting that ADSR outputs occupy a distinct, non-overlapping region of the feature manifold defined by natural audio statistics—even when they sound subjectively similar.
- **Core assumption:** These external embeddings define a feature space where "real" audio forms a cohesive, well-separated cluster from synthetic audio if distributional fidelity is low.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "external embeddings (OpenL3, log-Mel) achieve near-perfect separation (100%) across GAN and diffusion models."
  - [section 4.3] Figure 3 (b,d,f,h) and text: "log-Mel and OpenL3 embeddings achieve perfect classification accuracy (100%) across all tasks and models."
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Improving Perceptual Audio Aesthetic Assessment via Triplet Loss and Self-Supervised Embeddings" confirm that embeddings like OpenL3 are sensitive to audio quality/realism, but direct evidence for the specific 100% separation finding is absent in the provided corpus.
- **Break condition:** If future ADSR models achieve true distributional matching, external embeddings would show overlapping distributions and linear classifiers would fail to separate with high accuracy.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Traditional signal-level metrics (SNR, LSD) and even subjective perceptual tests (MUSHRA) are insensitive to the high-dimensional distributional discrepancies captured by embeddings because they measure aggregate reconstruction error or coarse perceptual quality, not fine-grained manifold alignment.
- **Mechanism:** SNR/LSD compute sample-wise or spectral errors, which can be low even if global statistical properties (e.g., higher-order spectral correlations, temporal coherence) deviate from real audio. MUSHRA tests focus on local perceptual quality (e.g., absence of artifacts) but may not probe the subtle, global statistical regularities that embedding spaces encode. Embedding-based classifiers aggregate information over larger contexts (e.g., 1-second segments) and in spaces optimized for audio realism, making them more sensitive to distributional shifts.
- **Core assumption:** Perceptual quality and distributional fidelity are distinct objectives; a sample can be perceptually plausible yet statistically inconsistent with the target distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "even when the generated audio attains high perceptual quality and state-of-the-art metric scores" classifiers separate perfectly; "highlighting a persistent gap between perceptual quality and true distributional fidelity."
  - [section 4.2] MUSHRA scores for MU-GAN are close to the wideband reference, yet [section 4.3] shows perfect separation in embedding spaces.
  - [section 4.1] Notes FlowHigh has negative SNR but low LSD, showing metrics can be decoupled from perceptual realism; Table 1 shows models with similar SNR/LSD have vastly different classifier accuracies (e.g., MU-GAN vs. AudioUNet in Table 2).
  - [corpus] Evidence is indirect; neighbors discuss evaluating generative models with embeddings (e.g., Frechet distances), but the specific dissociation between perceptual metrics and embedding-based separation for ADSR is not covered.
- **Break condition:** If perceptual metrics were redesigned to directly optimize for distributional matching (e.g., using adversarial losses in embedding spaces), the gap would close.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** GAN training dynamics and discriminator feature utility.
  - **Why needed here:** Understanding why the discriminator's frozen features remain discriminative after equilibrium (~50% accuracy) is key to Mechanism 1. Requires knowledge that GANs drive the generator to match the data distribution, but the discriminator's internal representations may still encode real-data characteristics.
  - **Quick check question:** If a GAN's discriminator achieves 50% accuracy on real/fake classification, does that mean its intermediate features are useless for distinguishing real vs. fake? Explain why or why not based on the paper's findings.
- **Concept:** Audio feature extraction and embedding spaces (log-Mel, OpenL3).
  - **Why needed here:** Mechanism 2 relies on external embeddings as ground-truth representations of natural audio. Understanding what these embeddings capture (spectral-temporal patterns, semantic content) explains their sensitivity.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might OpenL3 fail for 16→48 kHz upsampling, and what is the alternative used in the paper?
- **Concept:** Linear separability as a proxy for distributional similarity.
  - **Why needed here:** The paper uses linear classifier accuracy on embeddings to quantify distributional gap. This assumes that if two distributions are identical, any linear classifier should perform at chance in their shared space.
  - **Quick check question:** If two distributions are identical, what would you expect the accuracy of a linear classifier trained to separate them to be? What does high accuracy imply?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **ADSR generators:** AudioUNet (supervised), MU-GAN (GAN), HiFi-GAN, FlowHigh, FlashSR (diffusion). Take narrowband input → wideband output.
  2. **Embedding extractors:** (a) Discriminator pre-FC layer (from MU-GAN), (b) OpenL3 (1-sec audio → 512-D), (c) Log-Mel spectrogram (adaptive pooled → fixed-D).
  3. **Classifier:** Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) trained on embeddings to predict real (wideband original) vs. fake (super-resolved).
  4. **Evaluation metrics:** SNR, LSD, MUSHRA (subjective), classifier accuracy.
- **Critical path:** For a given model and dataset: (i) Generate synthetic wideband audio from narrowband inputs using the ADSR model. (ii) Extract embeddings from *both* real wideband audio and synthetic outputs using chosen extractor. (iii) Standardize embeddings, split into train/test. (iv) Train LDA on training set, report accuracy on held-out test set. (v) Visualize with LDA projection.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Embedding choice:** Discriminator features are model-specific (only available for GANs) but directly tied to the training objective. External embeddings (OpenL3/log-Mel) are general but may be insensitive to certain artifacts (OpenL3 bandwidth-limited).
  - **Classifier simplicity:** Linear classifier tests linear separability; nonlinear classifiers (e.g., MLP) might achieve even higher accuracy but would conflate model capacity with distributional gap.
  - **Embedding duration:** OpenL3 uses fixed 1-second segments; log-Mel uses adaptive pooling—these choices affect temporal context sensitivity.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Low classifier accuracy (<55%)** across embeddings: Potential success—the ADSR output distribution closely matches real wideband distribution *in the embedding space*. Must verify embeddings are sensitive (e.g., check that real vs. *clearly distorted* synthetic gives high accuracy).
  - **High discriminator accuracy on its own real/fake task** (>70%): Indicates generator is weak; GAN not converged. Should be ~50% for trained MU-GAN.
  - **Inconsistent results across embeddings:** If discriminator features show separation but external embeddings do not, the generator may be overfitting to the discriminator's specific biases rather than the true data distribution.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate baseline:** Train MU-GAN on VCTK 4→16 kHz. Confirm discriminator final accuracy ~50% and MUSHRA scores comparable to prior work. Extract discriminator pre-FC features for real/synthetic, train LDA, reproduce ~56% accuracy (Table 2) for MU-GAN's own outputs.
  2. **External embedding validation:** Using same MU-GAN outputs, extract OpenL3 (4→16 kHz) and log-Mel (16→48 kHz) embeddings. Train LDA—expect 100% accuracy. This confirms the core finding with independent features.
  3. **Cross-model generalization:** Apply the *trained* LDA classifier from Experiment 2 (on MU-GAN outputs) to embeddings from *other* models (AudioUNet, HiFi-GAN). Expect still high accuracy, demonstrating the separation is consistent across architectures.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why do ADSR models produce audio that is perceptually high quality yet remains easily separable from real audio in embedding spaces?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "These results suggest that high perceptual quality does not necessarily imply accurate distribution modeling, revealing a persistent gap between perceptual realism and representational fidelity—an open challenge for future ADSR research."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates the gap but does not investigate the specific acoustic or statistical factors that cause it.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative analysis of discriminative embedding features (e.g., saliency maps, feature ablation) correlated with human perceptual attributes.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why do external embeddings (OpenL3, log-Mel) achieve perfect separability while discriminator embeddings show varying accuracy depending on the model?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Figure 3 and Table 2 show external embeddings yield 100% accuracy across all models, whereas discriminator embeddings give lower accuracy for MU-GAN (56%) compared to other models, suggesting model-specific effects.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not analyze why the discriminator's own representations are less separable for its generated audio, possibly due to training dynamics or representation bias.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Similarity metrics between real and synthetic distributions in the discriminator's feature space, or probing what information the discriminator retains.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the observed distributional gap generalize to other audio domains (e.g., environmental sounds) and higher upsampling ratios?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The study is limited to VCTK (speech) and FMA-small (music) and upsampling ratios 4→16 kHz and 16→48 kHz. The authors do not test broader conditions.
- **Why unresolved:** Unknown if the findings hold for other audio types or more extreme bandwidth expansions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate the embedding classifier experiments on datasets like ESC-50 (environmental sounds) and for ratios like 8→48 kHz or 22→48 kHz.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can ADSR training objectives be modified to reduce embedding-space separability while maintaining perceptual quality?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper highlights the gap as an open challenge but does not propose or evaluate any mitigation strategies.
- **Why unresolved:** Current models are not optimized for distribution matching in embedding spaces; it's unclear if it's feasible.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train ADSR models with additional losses (e.g., minimizing Fréchet distance in OpenL3 or log-Mel embeddings) and evaluate changes in both classifier accuracy and MUSHRA scores.

## Limitations
- Limited scope: only five ADSR models on two datasets (VCTK, FMA-small), may not generalize to other domains or newer architectures.
- Incomplete reporting: MUSHRA test parameters (listener count, clip selection) missing, hindering exact replication.
- Linear separability assumption: using LDA accuracy as a proxy for distributional similarity is an approximation; more sophisticated distribution similarity measures could yield different insights.

## Confidence
- Core finding (external embeddings achieve ~100% separation): High
- Discriminator intermediate features remain discriminative after equilibrium: Medium
- Traditional perceptual metrics are insensitive to distributional gap: Medium-High
