# Breeze Taigi: Benchmarks and Models for Taiwanese Hokkien Speech Recognition and Synthesis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19259
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19259
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** Introduces the first standardized benchmarks and strong baseline models for Taiwanese Hokkien (Taigi) ASR and TTS, demonstrating that models trained on ~10,000 hours of synthetic speech achieve state-of-the-art performance on a reproducible evaluation framework.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical absence of standardized benchmarks for Taiwanese Hokkien (Taigi) speech technology. It introduces a reproducible evaluation framework that leverages parallel Mandarin-Taigi public service announcement audio, using normalized Character Error Rate (CER) against Mandarin transcriptions as a fair cross-system comparison metric. The authors generate approximately 10,000 hours of synthetic Taigi speech to train models. Their ASR model, BreezeASR-Taigi, achieves a 30.13% average CER on the benchmark, surpassing existing commercial and research systems. Their TTS model, BreezyVoice-Taigi, achieves the best automatic evaluation (19.09% CER) and a perfect 5.0 Mean Opinion Score for naturalness in human evaluation. However, it exhibits lower strict Taigi pronunciation authenticity (59.2%), a trade-off attributed to its learned naturalistic code-switching behavior.

## Method Summary
The methodology centers on two pillars: a novel benchmark and large-scale synthetic training data. The benchmark is built from 30 curated parallel Mandarin-Taigi audio pairs from Taiwan's Executive Yuan. Reference Mandarin transcriptions are created by applying a high-quality Mandarin ASR to the parallel Mandarin audio, followed by LLM refinement and human expert verification against official scripts, ensuring reproducibility. For training, a proprietary pipeline generates ~10,000 hours of diverse synthetic Taigi speech. The ASR model fine-tunes a Whisper base on this dataset, while the TTS model fine-tunes only the LLM component of CosyVoice 2 on the same data. Evaluation uses normalized CER for both tasks, with TTS additionally assessed via human evaluation on transcription accuracy, "Percentage of Taiwanese Pronunciation" (authenticity), and naturalness (MOS).

## Key Results
-   The BreezeASR-Taigi ASR model achieves a 30.13% average Character Error Rate (CER) on the proposed benchmark, outperforming all tested commercial and research baselines.
-   The BreezyVoice-Taigi TTS model achieves the best automatic evaluation (19.09% CER) and a perfect 5.0 Mean Opinion Score (MOS) for naturalness in human evaluation.
-   Despite high naturalness, BreezyVoice-Taigi scores lowest on "Percentage of Taiwanese Pronunciation" (59.2%), revealing a trade-off between naturalistic code-switching and prescriptive linguistic authenticity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Standardized Benchmark via Parallel Resources
A standardized ASR benchmark for Taigi is created by mapping audio to Mandarin transcriptions using parallel public service announcement (PSA) resources. The availability of official Mandarin-Taigi PSA pairs allows the construction of high-quality ground truth by combining state-of-the-art Mandarin ASR, LLM text refinement, and human expert verification. The core assumption is that evaluating output against Mandarin text provides a meaningful relative performance ranking despite the non-one-to-one mapping between Taigi phonetics and Mandarin characters. The key risk is that systems capturing authentic Taigi phonology might produce outputs that are penalized as "non-Mandarin" errors.

### Mechanism 2: Effective Training via Large-Scale Synthetic Data
Robust models can be trained on approximately 10,000 hours of synthetic Taigi speech when real labeled data is scarce. A proprietary large-scale speech synthesis pipeline generates a dataset with diverse speakers, acoustic environments, and conversational contexts. This scale and diversity are posited to cover Taigi's phonological variations sufficiently for transfer to real speech. The core assumption is that the synthetic data distribution closely resembles real-world Taigi speech. The primary risk is that the generator may fail to capture key low-frequency or region-specific phonetic patterns (e.g., certain tone sandhi variations), leading to performance degradation on authentic speech.

### Mechanism 3: Multi-Dimensional Human Evaluation for Code-Switching Contexts
Multi-dimensional human evaluation (transcription accuracy, pronunciation authenticity, naturalness) is necessary for TTS in low-resource, code-switching contexts because automatic metrics alone are insufficient. While automatic TTS evaluation uses an ASR-based CER (measuring intelligibility), human evaluation adds metrics for phonetic authenticity ("Percentage of Taiwanese Pronunciation") and naturalness. This triad reveals trade-offs: natural-sounding code-switching speech scores high on naturalness and intelligibility but low on prescriptive authenticity, while stilted "pure" Taigi scores high on authenticity but lower on naturalness. The core assumption is that native listeners can reliably distinguish these quality dimensions.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Parallel PSA Resource Crawler → Mandarin ASR + LLM Refinement + Human Verification → Normalized Mandarin Ground Truth; Synthetic Speech Generator → ~10k-hour Taigi Synthetic Corpus → (Whisper Fine-tune → BreezeASR-Taigi) & (CosyVoice 2 LLM Fine-tune → BreezyVoice-Taigi) → Evaluation Harness [Benchmark Test Set (CER) & TTS Output → BreezeASR-Taigi (CER) + Human Eval Interface (Taiwanese Pronunciation %, MOS)]

**Critical path:**
1.  Secure/curate parallel PSA data and establish reproducible transcription normalization rules.
2.  Generate/obtain sufficient diverse synthetic Taigi speech data.
3.  Fine-tune Whisper for ASR, validate on held-out PSA set.
4.  Fine-tune CosyVoice 2 (LLM only) for TTS.
5.  Run full evaluation harness (automatic + human) on both models and competitors.

**Design tradeoffs:**
-   **Benchmark Design:** Using Mandarin text as reference maximizes reproducibility and leverages existing resources but cannot measure absolute Taigi transcription accuracy or penalize code-switching as "error".
-   **Training Data:** Synthetic data provides scale and diversity at low cost but risks distribution shift from real spontaneous speech and may embed the generator's own biases (e.g., promoting code-switching).
-   **TTS Objective:** Optimizing for naturalness (MOS) and intelligibility (ASR-CER) conflicts with optimizing for prescriptive phonological purity (Taiwanese Pronunciation score).

**Failure signatures:**
-   **ASR Benchmark:** A system outputs grammatically correct Mandarin but completely misrepresents Taigi phonetics could still score well (e.g., transcribing Taigi audio as a plausible but unrelated Mandarin sentence).
-   **TTS Model:** High ASR-CER + low MOS indicates unintelligible/robotic speech. Low ASR-CER + low Taiwanese Pronunciation score (like BreezyVoice) indicates natural-sounding but phonetically impure (code-switched) output. High Taiwanese Pronunciation + moderate CER/MOS (like Aten AI) may indicate stilted, prescriptive speech.
-   **Synthetic Data Reliance:** Catastrophic failure on real-world, non-PSA data with unseen dialectal variations or spontaneous disfluencies not captured in the controlled synthetic corpus.

**First 3 experiments:**
1.  **Benchmark Reproducibility & Baseline:** Implement the exact normalization pipeline described in Section 3.3. Run it on the published 30 test samples with a off-the-shelf Whisper model (no fine-tuning). Does it reproduce the reported CER ranges? This validates the evaluation harness.
2.  **Synthetic Data Ablation:** Train two ASR models: one on the full 10k hour synthetic set, one on a smaller subset (e.g., 100 hours). Compare CER on the benchmark to quantify the marginal utility of scale and establish a performance curve vs. data volume.
3.  **Human Evaluation Consistency:** Recruit 2-3 new evaluators (blind to system identities) to repeat the TTS human evaluation on a subset of 5-10 samples. Compute inter-rater reliability (e.g., Krippendorff's alpha) for the "Taiwanese Pronunciation" and "MOS" scores to assess metric stability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1.  **How to build a controllable Taigi TTS that enables users to select between naturalistic code-switching and prescriptive monolingual pronunciation?** The paper's systems occupy extremes: BreezyVoice-Taigi produces naturalistic speech with only 59.2% Taigi pronunciation, while Aten AI Voice achieves 89.8% prescriptive Taigi but with lower naturalness. No system offers adjustable control. Future work should investigate controllable TTS systems with a parameter to modulate code-switching frequency and pronunciation authenticity.
2.  **How can we establish a standardized ASR benchmark for Taigi that uses proper Taigi orthography as ground truth instead of Mandarin mappings?** The current Mandarin-based ground truth may unfairly penalize systems that correctly output Taigi orthography; there is no gold-standard Taigi orthography reference. This requires creation of a reference transcription in a standardized Taigi orthography for the 30 test audios.
3.  **How can the Breeze Taigi benchmark be enlarged and diversified to better cover Taigi dialects, speaking styles, and domains?** The current benchmark contains only 30 PSA audio pairs from a single, limited domain. This may not reflect real-world variation, leading to overfitting. Resolution requires collection of hundreds of audio samples from varied sources (conversations, media, different regions) and demonstration that expanded-set scores correlate with practical application performance.
4.  **Can we develop an automatic metric to quantify code-switching and pronunciation authenticity in Taigi TTS output?** Human evaluation of "Percentage of Taiwanese Pronunciation" is used because automatic CER "cannot distinguish between naturalistic conversational Taigi (with code-switching) and prescriptive monolingual Taigi." An objective automatic metric would enable systematic, large-scale benchmarking. Evidence would be an algorithm that detects code-switched segments and measures phonetic authenticity against a Taigi lexicon, validated by high correlation with human annotations.

## Limitations
-   **Synthetic Data Black Box:** The core 10,000-hour training dataset's generation pipeline is proprietary and undescribed, preventing assessment of its linguistic fidelity (e.g., coverage of tone sandhi, regional accents) and risking unknown biases.
-   **Benchmark Access & Scope:** The 30-sample benchmark's public availability and licensing are unspecified. Furthermore, the benchmark's design inherently evaluates mapping to Mandarin text, not pure Taigi transcription, limiting claims about absolute linguistic accuracy.
-   **Human Evaluation Rigor:** The multi-dimensional human evaluation lacks reporting of inter-rater reliability (e.g., Krippendorff's alpha) for the critical "Taiwanese Pronunciation" metric, introducing potential noise in the key authenticity finding.

## Confidence
-   **Benchmark Mechanism (reproducible mapping via parallel PSA):** Medium. The described process is plausible and anchored in the paper's methodology, but confidence is reduced by weak external validation and unaddressed risks of systematic ranking bias from the non-one-to-one language mapping.
-   **Synthetic Data Utility (10k hours enabling strong real-world performance):** Low. This claim rests on a major, unverified assumption of distributional fidelity between synthetic and real speech. No direct evidence is provided to confirm the synthetic data captures key phonetic/prosodic patterns of authentic Taigi.
-   **TTS Evaluation Trade-off (naturalness vs. pronunciation authenticity):** High. The dissociation between high naturalness/MOS and low prescriptive pronunciation authenticity is strongly evidenced by the reported results and explicitly analyzed, making this finding robust within the evaluated systems.

2.  **Synthetic Data Scale Ablation:** Train two ASR models: one on the full 10k-hour synthetic set, one on a significantly smaller subset (e.g., 100 hours). Compare their CERs on the real benchmark to quantify the marginal utility of scale and detect potential overfitting to synthetic artifacts.
