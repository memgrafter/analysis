# Enhancing Predictability of Multi-Tenant DNN Inference for Autonomous Vehicles' Perception
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11004  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11004  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** PP-DNN dynamically selects environment-critical frames and ROIs, predicts non-critical detections, and coordinates multi-tenant inference to significantly improve fusion rate, reduce delay and its variation, and boost cost-effectiveness.

## Executive Summary
PP-DNN addresses unpredictable delays in multi-tenant deep neural network (DNN) inference for autonomous vehicle perception. It introduces an environment-aware framework that dynamically identifies critical video frames and regions of interest (ROIs) to reduce processed data volume. A detection predictor generates outputs for non-critical frames by tracking object motion, decoupling result publication from DNN inference. A FLOPs-aware scheduler coordinates the remaining inference tasks. Evaluated on BDD100K and nuScenes, PP-DNN increases fused frames by up to 7.3×, reduces fusion delay by >2.6× and its variation by >2.3×, improves detection completeness by 75.4%, and achieves up to 98% better cost-effectiveness versus a baseline processing all frames.

## Method Summary
PP-DNN is a ROS-based pipeline with four main modules: (1) an ROI Generator that marks frames as critical based on SSIM, object tracking (IoU), and traffic scenario, then outputs one or multiple ROIs; (2) a FLOPs Predictor that estimates compute cost (GMACs) from ROI dimensions for each DNN; (3) a Task Coordinator that dispatches critical frames to perception nodes (object detection, lane detection, segmentation) according to scheduling rules and frame deadlines; and (4) a Detection Predictor that, for non-critical frames, updates bounding boxes via velocity and warps segmentation masks. This design reduces the computational load while maintaining predictable, synchronized multi-task outputs.

## Key Results
- Increased number of fused frames by up to 7.3×.
- Reduced fusion delay by more than 2.6× and its variation by more than 2.3×.
- Improved detection completeness by 75.4% and achieved up to 98% better cost-effectiveness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Environment-Aware Critical Frame and ROI Selection
- **Claim:** Dynamically selecting environment-aware critical frames and ROIs reduces processed data volume without sacrificing functional predictability.
- **Mechanism:** ROI Generator computes per-frame SSIM and bounding box IoU against prior frames, incorporates traffic scenario context (e.g., pedestrian ratio). A frame is critical if SSIM < τ (e.g., 0.95), IoU drops significantly, objects are missed, a safety-critical scenario is detected, or a maximum frame interval elapses. ROIs are generated as a single bounding box covering all tracked objects or multiple ROIs per object class.
- **Core assumption:** The AV's safety-critical information changes infrequently relative to camera FPS, and this change is detectable via pixel/box similarity and scenario understanding.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "dynamically selecting 'critical' frames and regions of interest (ROIs)...critical frames and ROIs for AVs vary with their surrounding environment." [section III-A] "Environment-aware critical frames...the number of critical frames should vary with the underlying traffic scenario." [section IV-C] Algorithm 1 details selection.
- **Break condition:** If the lightweight tracker (YOLOv4-tiny + DeepSort) fails to maintain object IDs (e.g., heavy occlusion, extreme motion), ROI generation may miss critical objects, reducing detection completeness.

### Mechanism 2: Detection Predictor Decouples Publication from Inference
- **Claim:** Decoupling detection result publication from DNN inference via a detection predictor eliminates the "wooden barrel effect" of multi-tenant fusion, increasing fused frame rate.
- **Mechanism:** Detection Predictor maintains a queue of recent critical-frame detections. For non-critical frames, it predicts updates by (a) updating tracked object bboxes using calculated velocity (Δx, Δy, Δh, Δw) and (b) warping cached segmentation masks via velocity-based pixel updates. Predictions are published at the camera's native FPS, independent of the slower inference pipeline.
- **Core assumption:** Object motion is approximately linear between critical frames, allowing accurate short-term prediction.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Finally, we design a detection predictor for the perception of non-critical frames." [section IV-D] Describes velocity-based update (Eq. 4) and segmentation mask warping. [section VI-B] "The introduction of the detection predictor (DP) greatly increases the number of fusion messages because the DP publishes prediction detection messages...This decoupling allows the prediction to run in parallel with the detection."
- **Break condition:** If an object's motion is highly non-linear (e.g., sudden braking, sharp turn), constant-velocity prediction diverges from true state until the next critical frame.

### Mechanism 3: FLOPs-Aware Frame Scheduler Coordinates Multi-Tenant Inference
- **Claim:** A FLOPs-aware frame scheduler coordinates multi-tenant DNN inference to minimize fusion delay and its variation.
- **Mechanism:** Task Coordinator includes a FLOPs Predictor modeling GMACs as a function of ROI height/width, accounting for model-specific preprocessing (e.g., resize ranges for Faster R-CNN). ROI Scheduler selects one-ROI or multi-ROI format per task based on predicted FLOPs. Frame Dispatcher uses shared state (sequence numbers, deadlines) to route frames: (1) if a task lags (missed deadline), route exclusively to it; (2) if frame is safety-critical, broadcast to all; (3) else, skip frames for tasks whose delay exceeds a threshold.
- **Core assumption:** Predicted FLOPs is an accurate, monotonic proxy for actual execution time on target GPU; skipping outdated frames does not harm functional completeness because the predictor covers the gap.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "leverages a FLOPs predictor to predict multiply-accumulate operations (MACs)...The ROI scheduler coordinates the processing..." [section IV-E] Formulates FLOPs model; ROI scheduler and dispatcher rules described. [section VI-B] "FD helps reduce the fusion delay by coordinating multi-tenant DNN inference." Tables V/VI show speedups.
- **Break condition:** If FLOPs model is inaccurate for a specific DNN/hardware (e.g., due to memory bandwidth contention), scheduler decisions are suboptimal. Skipping frames could create gaps if the detection predictor fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Temporal Locality in Video Streams**  
  **Why needed:** PP-DNN's premise is that consecutive video frames are highly similar. Quantifying this via SSIM/IoU is foundational for identifying skippable frames.  
  **Quick check:** If you increase the SSIM threshold τ, what is the expected trade-off between the number of critical frames selected and the potential drop in detection completeness?

- **Multi-Task Scheduling & Fusion in ROS**  
  **Why needed:** The system coordinates multiple DNN nodes publishing at different rates. Understanding ROS message filters, callback queues, and timing slop is necessary to diagnose fusion-delay issues and implement the dispatcher.  
  **Quick check:** Why does the "wooden barrel effect" occur in the baseline ROS pipeline, and how does PP-DNN's dispatcher specifically counteract it?

- **ROI-Based Input Reduction for DNNs**  
  **Why needed:** PP-DNN reduces computation by cropping inputs to dynamic ROIs. This requires understanding how DNN preprocessing (e.g., resizing) interacts with variable input sizes to affect FLOPs non-uniformly.  
  **Quick check:** Why does Faster R-CNN show non-monotonic GMACs for small image sizes, while SSD does not? How does this inform the ROI scheduler's choice?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input (/image_raw) -> ROI Generator -> Task Coordinator -> Perception Task Nodes (Object, Lane, Segmentation) -> Fusion Node  
Tracker -> ROI Generator, Detection Predictor  
Perception Task Nodes -> Detection Predictor  
Detection Predictor -> Fusion Node  

**Critical path:** Frame capture → ROI Gen → Dispatcher → DNN Inference → Detection Predictor (for non-critical) → Fusion. The predictor runs in parallel with inference, decoupling publication from computation.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- One-ROI vs. Multi-ROI: Single ROI simplifies batching but may be larger; multi-ROIs reduce total pixels but require padding, potentially increasing FLOPs. Scheduler chooses based on FLOPs prediction.  
- Dispatcher aggressiveness: Skipping more frames reduces fusion delay but increases reliance on predictor accuracy.  
- Tracker complexity: A heavier tracker improves ROI accuracy but adds latency, potentially negating benefits.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Detection completeness drop: Check tracker misses, SSIM threshold suitability, or predictor drift.  
- Fusion delay spike/variance: Monitor per-task delays; if one task lags, dispatcher should isolate it; if variance high, FLOPs predictor may be inaccurate or GPU contention high.  
- Stuck IDs / lost objects: Tracker failure mode; monitor `obj_miss` in ROI Gen.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **SSIM Threshold Sweep:** Run pipeline with τ_SSIM = {0.90, 0.95, 0.98} on BDD100K. Plot fusion frames % and detection completeness vs. τ. Identify point of diminishing returns.  
2. **Predictor-only Ablation:** Disable all DNN inference, feed only tracker + predictor. Compare predicted bboxes against ground truth for non-critical frames to quantify velocity model error over time since last critical frame.  
3. **FLOPs Predictor Validation:** For each task's DNN, collect inference time for hundreds of crops of varying (h,w). Fit the model from Eqs. 5-7. Compute R² and max error. Test if scheduler's one/multi-ROI choice aligns with the *actual* faster option (measured GMACs vs. runtime).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How should the SSIM threshold for critical frame selection be dynamically tuned for different driving scenarios?  
  **Basis:** Algorithm 1 uses a fixed τ_SSIM=0.95 without justification or adaptation.  
  **Why unresolved:** A static threshold may either miss critical frames in high-dynamic scenes or select too many in static scenes, affecting efficiency.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Systematic evaluation of detection completeness and fusion delay across varying τ_SSIM on diverse scenarios; design of an adaptive mechanism based on scene change metrics.

- **Open Question 2:** Can the FLOPs predictor be generalized to arbitrary DNN architectures without re-derivation?  
  **Basis:** The predictor uses piecewise functions (Eqs 5-7) tailored to Faster R-CNN, Deeplabv3+, and LaneNet.  
  **Why unresolved:** These analytic models may not apply to new architectures, limiting PP-DNN's extendability.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Compare prediction accuracy across a wide range of DNNs; develop a learnable predictor (e.g., neural network) from ROI dimensions to FLOPs.

- **Open Question 3:** What are the failure modes of PP-DNN in safety-critical edge cases not well-represented in standard datasets?  
  **Basis:** Evaluation on BDD100K and nuScenes shows average improvements but lacks stress-testing on rare, dangerous scenarios.  
  **Why unresolved:** Autonomous driving requires guarantees even in worst cases; PP-DNN's detection predictor might fail for sudden, small, or partially occluded objects.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Adversarial testing with synthetic edge cases; simulation-based safety metrics (e.g., collision rates); formal analysis of detection completeness bounds under predictor errors.

- **Open Question 4:** How does PP-DNN scale to a larger number of heterogeneous perception tasks with conflicting deadlines?  
  **Basis:** The task coordinator uses simple heuristics (lowest FLOPs first) and only 3 tasks are evaluated.  
  **Why unresolved:** With more tasks, scheduling becomes NP-hard; heuristics may not meet deadlines or optimize resource usage.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Scale experiments with 5-10 tasks of varying compute demands; compare heuristic scheduler to optimal or approximate optimization (e.g., MILP); measure deadline miss rates.

## Limitations
- Thresholds for SSIM, traffic scenario classification, and frame skipping are not fully specified; they may require dataset-specific tuning, hurting reproducibility.
- FLOPs predictor coefficients and resizing parameters (e.g., smin, smax, stride) are absent; model may deviate from actual GPU runtime, causing suboptimal scheduling.
- The linear velocity predictor can drift during abrupt maneuvers; the lightweight tracker may lose objects under heavy occlusion, reducing detection completeness.
- Evaluation is restricted to BDD100K and nuScenes mini; performance under varied weather, lighting, or camera geometries remains unknown.
- Hardware dependence: results measured on specific GPUs; memory bandwidth and kernel launch overheads are not captured by the FLOPs model.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (ROI selection):** Medium – Algorithm described but relies on undisclosed thresholds; break condition plausible.
- **Mechanism 2 (Detection predictor):** Medium – Sound design but linear motion assumption may fail; error bounds unquantified.
- **Mechanism 3 (FLOPs-aware scheduling):** Medium – Depends on FLOPs runtime correlation; no validation of model accuracy provided.
- **Overall performance claims (e.g., 7.3× fused frames):** High on reported datasets, but Medium for broader AV deployment due to limitations.
