# Strategic Navigation or Stochastic Search? How Agents and Humans Reason Over Document Collections
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12180  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12180  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Top multimodal agents match human accuracy on a novel document QA benchmark but exhibit severe inefficiency, poor effort calibration, and rely on brute-force search rather than strategic navigation.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MADQA, a benchmark of 2,250 questions over 800 diverse PDFs designed to differentiate strategic reasoning from brute-force stochastic search in document agents. Using a novel accuracy-effort calibration metric (Kuiper statistic) grounded in Classical Test Theory, the authors find that state-of-the-art agents (e.g., Gemini 3 Pro BM25 Agent, 82.2% accuracy) achieve parity with humans using the same retrieval tool. However, agents and humans succeed on largely disjoint question subsets, and agents demonstrate dramatic inefficiency: humans reach ~50% accuracy on the first query while agents start near 12%. Agents also show poor effort calibration (Kuiper scores 25–73 vs. human 14.6). A nearly 20% oracle accuracy gap persists, primarily due to retrieval bottlenecks (35.7% of errors) and unproductive loops. The findings suggest current agents compensate for weak strategic planning with verbose, stochastic search.

## Method Summary
The study constructs MADQA from human-authored questions requiring navigation across heterogeneous PDFs (e.g., legal, academic, financial). Agents interact via a search engine interface (BM25), producing query sequences and final answers. Performance is evaluated not just by accuracy but also by effort (number of queries), using the Kuiper statistic to measure calibration between the two—higher scores indicate less efficient, more brute-force search. Human baselines are collected via a replicated interface. Error analysis categorizes failures into retrieval, comprehension, looping, etc., and an oracle upper bound simulates perfect retrieval.

## Key Results
- Top agents match human accuracy (82.2%) but on different question subsets, indicating divergent reasoning strategies.
- Agents suffer a severe cold-start inefficiency (~12% first-query accuracy vs. ~50% for humans) and poor effort calibration (Kuiper 25–73 vs. human 14.6).
- A ~20% oracle gap remains, with retrieval failures (35.7% of errors) and unproductive loops as primary bottlenecks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Kuiper statistic, derived from Classical Test Theory, effectively separates strategic navigation (high accuracy with low effort) from stochastic search (high accuracy only after excessive queries). By calibrating accuracy against query count, itreveals that agents achieve parity with humans not through superior planning but through compensatory brute-force search. The human-agent tool parity ensures differences stem from reasoning, not tool access. The benchmark's heterogeneity and human-authored questions force genuine multi-document reasoning rather than single-pass retrieval.

## Foundational Learning
- **MADQA benchmark**: A dataset of 2,250 questions over 800 PDFs requiring cross-document reasoning; needed to evaluate strategic navigation; quick check: verify question diversity and document types.
- **Kuiper statistic**: A calibration metric measuring the trade-off between accuracy and effort; needed to quantify strategic vs. stochastic behavior; quick check: compute on sample agent trajectories.
- **Classical Test Theory**: The psychometric framework grounding the accuracy-effort relationship; needed to justify the calibration approach; quick check: review item response theory basics.
- **Brute-force vs. strategic search**: The core behavioral distinction—repeated, unguided queries vs. goal-directed planning; needed to interpret agent trajectories; quick check: analyze query sequence patterns.
- **Retrieval bottleneck**: The dominant failure mode where relevant passages are not found; needed to target improvements; quick check: categorize errors by retrieval vs. comprehension.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: PDF Corpus -> BM25 Retriever -> LLM Agent (query generator + reasoner) -> Answer  
**Critical path**: Initial query -> retrieve top-k passages -> LLM reasons/decides next action -> loop until answer or limit.  
**Design tradeoffs**: Recall vs. precision in retrieval (more passages increase cost but risk noise); query cost vs. accuracy (more queries improve odds but increase latency); exploration (broad search) vs. exploitation (deep reasoning on a subset).  
**Failure signatures**: Unproductive loops (repeating similar queries), retrieval misses (low Page F1 on relevant docs), cold-start poor queries (irrelevant first attempts), lack of query refinement based on intermediate results.  
**First experiments**: 1) Replace BM25 with a dense retriever to isolate retrieval's impact on the oracle gap. 2) Impose a strict step limit (e.g., 3 queries) to measure the accuracy-efficiency trade-off and force planning. 3) Compare agent first-query quality against human seed queries to diagnose cold-start causes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Do multimodal document agents perform genuine strategic planning, or do they rely on stochastic search?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The core question posed in the abstract and introduction.
- **Why unresolved:** Top agents match human accuracy but exhibit different error patterns and poor effort calibration, suggesting brute-force compensation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments isolating reasoning depth; trajectory analyses contrasting strategic vs. verbose search; interventions that enforce step limits and measure accuracy trade-offs.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can agent performance generalize to non-English and culturally diverse document collections?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Limitations note English-only and US-centric corpus bias.
- **Why unresolved:** The benchmark’s scope limits external validity; unknown if findings hold across languages/cultures.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Build multilingual, multicultural document benchmarks; evaluate identical agent architectures; compare performance gaps.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What architectural changes can close the nearly 20% oracle gap due to retrieval bottlenecks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The oracle gap is largely due to retrieval failures and unproductive loops.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies retrieval as the bottleneck but does not pinpoint root causes (e.g., query formulation, indexing, semantic representation).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Fine-grained error analysis separating retrieval vs. comprehension failures; ablation studies of retrieval components; development of retrieval-specific metrics beyond Page F1.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Why do agents suffer a severe "cold start" inefficiency, achieving only ~12% first-query accuracy versus ~50% for humans?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Figure 9 shows the cold-start gap; human vs. agent trajectories differ.
- **Why unresolved:** The gap is documented but unexplained—whether it stems from poor initial queries, misinterpretation, or lack of prior corpus knowledge.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze first-query quality; compare agent vs. human search logs; train agents on human seed queries; measure improvement curves.

## Limitations
- Corpus limited to 800 English PDFs, reducing generalizability to other document types (e.g., web pages, scanned images) and languages.
- Human baseline sample size and demographics are narrow, potentially biasing comparisons.
- The Kuiper metric's threshold choices and sensitivity to calibration curves require further justification and robustness checks.

## Confidence
- **Top agents match human accuracy with identical tools:** High  
- **Agents and humans succeed on largely different question subsets:** Medium  
- **Agents are severely inefficient (first query ~12% vs human ~50%):** High  
- **Agents show poor effort calibration (Kuiper 25-73 vs human 14.6):** Medium  
- **~20% oracle gap persists, mainly due to retrieval bottlenecks and loops:** Medium  
- **Agents compensate weak strategic planning with brute-force search:** Low  

## Next Checks
1. **Oracle retrieval test:** Replace the retriever with perfect lookup (all relevant passages returned) to measure the upper bound on agent accuracy without retrieval errors.
2. **Planning ablation:** Disable or simplify the agent's planning/query refinement module to isolate the contribution of brute-force search to overall performance.
3. **Cross-domain replication:** Run the same experiment on a different document collection (e.g., HTML pages or scientific articles) with a fresh set of human participants to test robustness of findings.
