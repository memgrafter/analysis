# PREBA: Surgical Duration Prediction via PCA-Weighted Retrieval-Augmented LLMs and Bayesian Averaging Aggregation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.13275
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13275
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Reduces mean absolute error by up to 40% over zero-shot LLMs and matches best supervised baselines without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Executive Summary
PREBA introduces a training-free retrieval-augmented framework for accurate surgical duration prediction that leverages institution-specific historical cases without costly model fine-tuning. By combining PCA-weighted similarity retrieval, hierarchical clinical filtering, and Bayesian aggregation of multi-temperature LLM outputs with population priors, PREBA achieves substantial error reductions and competitive performance against fully supervised machine learning models on two large real-world datasets.

## Method Summary
PREBA begins by encoding heterogeneous clinical features (numerical, categorical, ordinal, boolean, text) using type-specific encoders. Each encoded sub-vector is scaled by $1/\sqrt{\text{dim}}$ to prevent high-dimensional modalities (e.g., BERT embeddings) from dominating the unified representation. For a query case, PCA—fit on the training embeddings—derives a weight vector from aggregated absolute loadings of the top 42 principal components. Query and candidate embeddings are weighted by this vector before cosine similarity search with FAISS. Retrieved candidates (80) undergo hierarchical filtering: same department, exact match on department/surgery/level, then IQR outlier removal, yielding 8 few-shot exemplars. The statistical prior (median duration, strength $w_{\text{prior}}{=}0.9$) is computed from the most specific stratum (e.g., dept/surgery/level) with enough cases. The LLM generates 5 predictions conditioned on the query, exemplars, and prior; the first uses temperature 0, others from $\mathcal{U}(0.05,0.4)$. The final estimate fuses the LLM average $\bar{y}$ and prior mean $\mu_{\text{prior}}$ via $y_q = (w_{\text{prior}}\mu_{\text{prior}} + n\bar{y})/(w_{\text{prior}}+n)$. No training is required.

## Key Results
- MAE reduced from 58.62 to 35.19 minutes (40% reduction) with Qwen3-8B on MMSDP dataset.
- R² improved from -0.13 to 0.62.
- Achieves performance comparable to supervised models (e.g., Qwen3-32B MAE 33.43 vs best ML baseline 33.80).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** PCA-weighted retrieval aligns the similarity search with clinically salient feature dimensions, improving the relevance of retrieved historical cases.
- **Mechanism:** PCA is applied to the normalized embeddings of historical cases to derive a data-driven weight vector $w_{\text{PCA}}$ based on loadings from top principal components. This weight vector emphasizes feature dimensions that contribute most to variance in the training data, presumed to correlate with surgical duration. Retrieval then uses cosine similarity on PCA-weighted embeddings.
- **Core assumption:** Features with high variance across the institution's historical surgical data are more predictive of duration and should dominate the similarity metric.
- **Evidence anchors:** abstract and Section III-C1; weak direct evidence; neighboring papers like EHR-RAG use retrieval but not PCA weighting.
- **Break condition:** If the principal components capture noise, systemic biases, or non-clinical variance (e.g., data entry patterns), the weighting misguides retrieval, degrading prediction accuracy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Bayesian averaging aggregates multi-round LLM predictions with institution-specific statistical priors, yielding calibrated estimates that resist clinically implausible outputs.
- **Mechanism:** For a query, a prior mean $\mu_{\text{prior}}$ and strength $w_{\text{prior}}$ are computed from historical cases matching the query's clinical stratum. Multiple LLM predictions (via varied temperatures) are averaged to $\bar{y}$. The final estimate combines these: $y_q = (w_{\text{prior}}\cdot\mu_{\text{prior}} + n\cdot\bar{y})/(w_{\text{prior}} + n)$.
- **Core assumption:** The distribution of surgical durations within a clinically coherent stratum is stable enough to serve as a meaningful prior, and the LLM's output distribution contains useful signal around the true value.
- **Evidence anchors:** abstract and Section III-D; neighboring papers like Reasoning-Enhanced Healthcare Predictions lack this specific Bayesian fusion.
- **Break condition:** If the prior stratum is underpopulated or its distribution is multi-modal/non-Gaussian, the Bayesian update may inadequately constrain LLM outputs; conversely, if LLM predictions are consistently biased due to prompt mis-specification, aggregation may remain miscalibrated.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Heterogeneous feature encoding with dimensionality normalization prevents high-dimensional modalities (e.g., BERT text embeddings) from dominating the unified representation space, enabling balanced similarity computation.
- **Mechanism:** Clinical features are type-specifically encoded (StandardScaler, OrdinalEncoder, OneHotEncoder, BERT). Each encoded vector is scaled by $\alpha_c = 1/\sqrt{\text{dim}(v_c)}$ before concatenation, normalizing for vector magnitude differences arising from disparate output dimensions.
- **Core assumption:** Without normalization, Euclidean/cosine similarity will be disproportionately influenced by high-dimensional feature vectors regardless of clinical relevance.
- **Evidence anchors:** abstract and Section III-B; Table IV ablation shows pure BERT encoding performs worse.
- **Break condition:** If normalization coefficients are miscalculated or certain feature types (like text) are genuinely highly predictive and should dominate, the normalization will suppress their signal, harming retrieval quality.

## Foundational Learning

### Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
- **Why needed:** PREBA's core innovation is retrieval-augmentation to provide institution-specific context, bypassing the need for fine-tuning.
- **Quick check:** How does PREBA's retrieval mechanism (PCA-weighted, post-processed) differ from a naive k-NN search on raw features?

### Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for Feature Weighting
- **Why needed:** PCA is used not for dimensionality reduction but to derive a data-driven importance weight for each feature dimension, which directly shapes the retrieval space.
- **Quick check:** Why use PCA loadings from all components (weighted by explained variance) instead of just the first few principal components?

### Bayesian Model Averaging for Aggregation
- **Why needed:** The final prediction fuses uncertain LLM outputs with a grounded population prior.
- **Quick check:** What does the prior strength $w_{\text{prior}}$ implicitly represent about the reliability of the institutional historical data for the query's stratum?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
1. Heterogeneous Encoder: Type-specific encoders (StandardScaler, OrdinalEncoder, OneHotEncoder, BERT) with per-type scaling ($\alpha_c = 1/\sqrt{\text{dim}}$) produce a unified normalized embedding $V_{\text{norm}}$.  
2. PCA-Weighted Retriever: Fit PCA on training $V_{\text{norm}}$ to compute weight vector $w_{\text{PCA}}$ from top $M{=}42$ PCs (aggregated absolute loadings). Weight query and candidate embeddings, then use FAISS Flat (cosine) to retrieve $M_{\text{candidate}}{=}80$ candidates. Apply hierarchical filtering (same department → exact match on dept/surgery/level → IQR outlier removal) to yield top $K{=}8$ cases.  
3. Multi-round LLM Generator: Construct prompt with retrieved cases, statistical prior $S_q$, and query details. Run LLM $n{=}5$ times: first with $\tau{=}0$, subsequent with $\tau \sim \mathcal{U}(0.05,0.4)$. Collect predictions $\hat{y}_q^{(r)}$.  
4. Bayesian Aggregator: Compute prior mean $\mu_{\text{prior}}$ (median of retrieved cases in most specific stratum) and prior strength $w_{\text{prior}}{=}0.9$. Final prediction: $y_q = (w_{\text{prior}}\cdot\mu_{\text{prior}} + n\cdot\bar{y})/(w_{\text{prior}} + n)$.

**Critical path:** Query case → Heterogeneous encoding → PCA weighting → Retrieval & filtering → Prompt construction → Parallel LLM inference (n rounds) → Prior computation → Bayesian fusion → Final prediction.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Retrieval size $K$: Larger $K$ improves context but increases prompt length and latency; paper finds diminishing returns beyond 8-shot.  
- Candidate expansion $n{\times}K$: Larger $n$ improves chance of finding coherent cases post-filtering but increases FAISS search time; paper uses $n{=}10$.  
- Prior strength $w_{\text{prior}}$: Tuned via CV; too high overrides LLM, too low fails to correct implausible outputs; paper uses fixed $w_{\text{prior}}{=}0.9$.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High MAE + low R²: Likely poor retrieval (check retrieved case clinical consistency, PCA weighting).  
- High variance across LLM temperatures: Indicates unstable LLM outputs; may need more rounds or different prompting.  
- Predictions systematically outside historical range: Prior too weak or stratum mismatch.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Zero-shot vs. RAG comparison: Establish baseline lift from adding retrieval.  
2. PCA weighting ablation: Compare with and without PCA weighting to validate contribution.  
3. Aggregation strategy ablation: Compare Bayesian averaging against median, majority voting, etc.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Prior strength selection:** How should $w_{\text{prior}}$ be optimally set for diverse clinical settings or data regimes? The paper uses a fixed value via CV but lacks a principled, context-aware method.  
2. **Temperature scheduling:** What is the justification for the specific multi-temperature schedule ($\tau_1{=}0$, subsequent $\tau \sim \mathcal{U}(0.05,0.4)$)? The choice appears heuristic without analysis of its effect on output diversity or calibration.  
3. **Hierarchical filtering:** Are the rule-based filtering criteria (department → surgery name → level → IQR) universally optimal, or could they be learned?  
4. **Alternative weighting:** How does PCA-weighted retrieval perform relative to other data-driven weighting or metric learning (e.g., supervised contrastive learning)? PCA captures variance, not necessarily predictive signal.  
5. **Component disentanglement:** To what extent does the gain stem from retrieved exemplars versus statistical priors, and how do they interact? The paper lacks a clean isolation of each component's contribution.

## Limitations
- Incomplete reproduction details (filter relaxation logic, full Chinese prompt, IQR multiplier) hinder faithful implementation.
- Core assumptions (PCA captures clinical variance, prior distributions are stable Gaussian, normalization does not suppress predictive signals) may not hold universally.
- Evaluation limited to two Chinese hospital datasets; generalizability to other institutions or specialties is uncertain.

## Confidence
- PCA-weighted retrieval aligns similarity with clinical salience: Medium  
- Bayesian averaging fuses LLM outputs with priors for robustness: Medium  
- Heterogeneous encoding prevents modality dominance: High  
- Overall reported performance (MAE reduction, R² gains): Medium

## Next Checks
1. **PCA weight plausibility check:** Compute PCA-derived feature weights and correlate with domain-expert rankings of duration predictors. Low correlation would challenge the core assumption.  
2. **Prior strength sensitivity analysis:** Vary $w_{\text{prior}}$ (0.1–2.0) across strata with different sample sizes (<10, 10–50, >50). If optimal $w_{\text{prior}}$ decreases with smaller cohorts, it validates the need for adaptive prior weighting.  
3. **Normalization ablation:** Remove the $1/\sqrt{\text{dim}}$ scaling and re-run retrieval/prediction. A significant MAE increase would confirm that normalization critically balances heterogeneous feature contributions.
