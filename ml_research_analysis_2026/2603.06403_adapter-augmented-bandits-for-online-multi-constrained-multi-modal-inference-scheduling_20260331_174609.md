# Adapter-Augmented Bandits for Online Multi-Constrained Multi-Modal Inference Scheduling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.06403
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06403
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: M²-CMAB achieves up to 14.18% higher average inference reward than state-of-the-art baselines across three budget regimes and closely tracks an oracle upper bound, with a regret guarantee under multi-dimensional knapsack constraints.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of online scheduling for multi-modal large language model (MLLM) inference across heterogeneous backends under strict multi-dimensional budget constraints (latency and monetary cost). The authors propose M²-CMAB, which integrates three key components: (1) a predictor that uses a frozen MLLM backbone with lightweight adapters to estimate reward and costs from multimodal inputs; (2) a primal-dual constrainer that maintains online Lagrange multipliers to enforce long-term budgets; and (3) a two-phase contextual bandit scheduler that balances exploration and exploitation.

Evaluated on a composite benchmark comprising five backends and six datasets, M²-CMAB achieves up to 14.18% higher average reward compared to existing baselines across Restricted, Normal, and Generous budget regimes, and its performance nearly matches an oracle policy that knows true rewards and costs. The method is supported by a theoretical regret guarantee under multi-dimensional knapsack constraints.

## Method Summary
M²-CMAB consists of an initial probing phase and a main scheduling phase. During initialization, it collects a small amount of data for each backend to estimate a safe dual radius Λ. In the main loop, each arriving task is processed by a frozen MLLM backbone (e.g., Qwen3-VL-2B). A special [CLS] token is prepended to the input; attention weights from this token to all tokens are used to pool the last-layer hidden states into a task context vector z_t_x. An action embedding z_a (from a separate embedding model) is concatenated with z_t_x and fed into lightweight MLP adapters, which predict the task's reward r̂_t_a and cost vector φ̂_t_a for each backend a. The scheduler then forms a Lagrangian score S_t(a) = r̂_t_a − ⟨φ̂_t_a/Φ − 1/T, λ_t⟩, where λ_t are current dual variables and Φ is the total budget vector. The action with highest score is selected with high probability, while other actions are sampled with probability decreasing in their score gap, controlled by hyperparameter ρ. After execution, true reward r_t and costs φ_t are observed; the adapters are updated via online ridge regression, and λ_t is updated using Online Mirror Descent constrained to an ℓ₁ ball of radius Λ. This primal-dual loop ensures that cumulative costs stay within budget with high probability while learning to maximize reward.

## Key Results
- M²-CMAB outperforms state-of-the-art baselines (e.g., BGT-planner, threshold-based methods) by up to 14.18% in average reward across three budget regimes.
- Its performance consistently approaches the oracle upper bound (which knows true rewards and costs), achieving over 90% of the oracle reward in the Normal regime.
- The method provides a theoretical regret guarantee under multi-dimensional knapsack constraints, with empirical results confirming low constraint violations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of M²-CMAB stems from three synergistic mechanisms.

### Mechanism 1: Frozen-Backbone CLS-Attentive Predictor
The predictor freezes a pretrained MLLM to extract stable, semantically rich representations without incurring online fine-tuning overhead. A [CLS] token is prepended to each multimodal input; attention weights from this token to all tokens enable attention-based pooling of the last-layer hidden states, yielding a compact context embedding z_t_x. This design leverages the backbone's knowledge while avoiding reliance on the final token output. Lightweight adapter MLPs then predict reward and costs from the concatenated context and action embeddings. The core assumption is that the frozen backbone's representations remain meaningful over time and that the [CLS]-attentive pooling adequately summarizes global semantics. If the task distribution shifts drastically, the frozen representations may become misaligned, degrading prediction accuracy.

### Mechanism 2: Primal-Dual Constrainer for Long-Horizon Budgets
The constrainer enforces long-term, multi-dimensional budget constraints by maintaining a dual variable λ_t per constraint dimension. It formulates a Lagrangian per round: L_t(a, λ_t) = −r_t_a + ⟨φ_t_a/Φ − 1/T, λ_t⟩, penalizing resource consumption relative to the budget. The λ_t are updated via Online Mirror Descent (OMD) on the dual problem, projected onto an ℓ₁ ball of radius Λ estimated in the initial phase. This decouples constraint management from per-round decisions, allowing the scheduler to consider long-term feasibility. The approach assumes the Lagrangian relaxation adequately characterizes the constrained optimum and that OMD with a bounded feasible set yields sublinear constraint violation. If Λ is misestimated or cost predictions are biased, constraint enforcement may become too restrictive or too lenient.

### Mechanism 3: Two-Phase Contextual Bandit Scheduler
The scheduler balances exploration and exploitation under irreversible budget constraints. After initialization, for each context x_t it scores all actions by the predicted Lagrangian S_t(a). It then chooses the highest-scoring action with high probability, while other actions are sampled with probability inversely proportional to their score gap, controlled by ρ. This ensures frequent selection of promising actions while probing alternatives sufficiently to improve predictions. After each round, dual variables λ_t are updated via OMD, gradually increasing penalties for resource-intensive actions. The core assumption is that scores S_t(a) correctly rank actions by long-term value when penalties are included. The exploration parameter ρ must be tuned carefully: too high leads to excessive exploration and budget waste; too low causes greedy behavior and poor convergence. The two-phase structure—initial data collection followed by adaptive scheduling—strikes a balance between safe exploration and effective exploitation.

## Foundational Learning
Understanding M²-CMAB relies on three foundational concepts:

### Contextual Multi-Armed Bandits with Knapsack Constraints (CBwK)
- **Why needed**: This framework models online decision-making where each round's reward and costs depend on context (the multimodal task), and long-term multi-dimensional resource limits must be respected. M²-CMAB is an instantiation of CBwK.
- **Quick check**: How does the Lagrangian transform the constrained maximization problem into an unconstrained per-round optimization, and what is the role of the dual variable?

### Primal-Dual Methods for Online Convex Optimization
- **Why needed**: To enforce irreversible, multi-dimensional budgets in an online setting, the primal (action selection) and dual (budget penalties) are updated simultaneously. The OMD update on λ_t penalizes actions that over-consume resources.
- **Quick check**: Why is an ℓ₁-bounded feasible set for λ_t critical for achieving sublinear regret in CBwK?

### Representation Learning via Frozen Foundation Models with Lightweight Adapters
- **Why needed**: The scheduler must operate online with minimal overhead. Fine-tuning a large MLLM is too costly; freezing the backbone preserves a stable, generalizable representation. Adapters then specialize it for the specific regression tasks (reward, costs) with cheap updates.
- **Quick check**: What specific architectural design extracts the task-level representation from the frozen MLLM (hint: involves a specific token and attention pooling), and why is it preferred over using the final output token?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: 
  Predictor: frozen MLLM backbone → [CLS]-attentive pooling → task context z_t_x → concat with action embedding z_a → adapter MLPs → predictions (r̂_t_a, φ̂_t_a). Constrainer: maintains λ_t vector, updates via OMD using observed costs. Scheduler: orchestrates the main loop: receives context x_t, predictions (r̂, φ̂), and λ_t; computes scores S_t(a); samples action a_t; triggers execution; observes true (r_t, φ_t); updates predictor adapters (online ridge) and λ_t. Initial phase module: runs (A+1)·T₀ rounds to gather data, trains an oracle predictor, solves LP to estimate OPT(T₀), sets Λ = T/Φ_min · (OPT + M).
- **Critical path**: Task arrival (x_t) → Predictor generates predictions for all actions → Constrainer supplies current λ_t → Scheduler selects a_t based on scores → Backend executes task → Observations (r_t, φ_t) fed back to update Predictor and Constrainer.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
   - *Frozen backbone vs. adapters*: Freezing saves computation and preserves general semantics but may limit prediction capacity if the backbone's representations are not sufficiently discriminative for the scheduling task.
   - *Dual radius Λ*: Larger Λ allows more exploration before penalties increase, but risks larger budget overruns. Smaller Λ enforces budgets strictly but may prematurely restrict promising actions.
   - *Exploration parameter ρ*: High ρ encourages uniform-like exploration, risking budget waste on suboptimal actions. Low ρ leads to greedy behavior, potentially missing better actions. Tuning ρ is crucial for balancing reward maximization and constraint satisfaction.
- **Failure signatures**:
   - *High cumulative regret*: Predictor errors remain high (inspect MAE/MSE) or ρ is misconfigured causing poor exploration/exploitation balance.
   - *Budget violation*: Λ too large or cost predictions φ̂ systematically underestimated, leading to insufficient λ_t penalties.
   - *Low average reward*: Overly conservative λ_t (e.g., from early budget overruns) or inaccurate reward predictions.
   - *High variance across seeds*: Initial phase too short (poor Λ estimate) or adapter learning rate unstable.
- **First 3 experiments**:
   1. **Validate Predictor Accuracy**: On held-out data from initial phase, compute MAE/MSE for reward and each cost predictor. Verify errors decrease with more historical data.
   2. **Test Constraint Satisfaction**: Run M²-CMAB with a fixed budget Φ. Track cumulative costs per dimension; final usage should be within ~5% of Φ. Sweep Λ to gauge sensitivity.
   3. **Compare against Oracle and Random**: On a small trace (e.g., 500 rounds) with a deterministic oracle (using true rewards and costs), compute average reward and regret. Expect M²-CMAB to achieve >90% of oracle reward under the Normal budget regime.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extending regret guarantee to general MLLM-based predictors**: The current analysis assumes predictor regret sublinear, but no concrete bound exists for frozen-backbone MLLMs with adapters under the decoupled scheduling scheme. Deriving such a bound is an open theoretical challenge.
2. **Asymmetric importance of reward vs. cost adapters**: Ablation results show removing the reward adapter hurts performance most, while cost adapters have less impact. The underlying reason—whether due to higher reward variance, greater sensitivity to reward errors, or learnability differences—remains unexplained.
3. **Frozen backbone robustness vs. fine-tuned alternatives**: The design choice to freeze the backbone and use CLS-attentive pooling is motivated by stability and computational efficiency, but it is unclear how it compares to fine-tuning or other representation methods under distribution shifts (e.g., new modality combinations).
4. **Adaptive initial phase duration**: The number of probing rounds T₀ is fixed a priori. An adaptive criterion based on observed context heterogeneity or cost variance could improve the trade-off between Λ estimation accuracy and exploitation time.
5. **Handling correlated multi-dimensional costs**: The model assumes cost dimensions are independent. Real-world correlations (e.g., simultaneous latency and monetary spikes) may destabilize the primal-dual updates, which treat constraints separately. Extending the method to handle correlated costs is an open problem.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (T₀, ρ, ridge λ, OMD step size) are not fully specified in the paper, requiring guesswork for reproduction.
- The exact budget vectors Φ per dataset and regime are not provided; recomputing them may yield different results.
- Details of backend cost formulas depend on hardware-specific metrics (FLOPS, power) that are insufficiently described, hindering precise replication.
