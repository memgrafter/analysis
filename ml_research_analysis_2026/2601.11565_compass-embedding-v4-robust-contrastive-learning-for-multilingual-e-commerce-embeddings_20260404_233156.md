# Compass-Embedding v4: Robust Contrastive Learning for Multilingual E-commerce Embeddings

- **arXiv ID**: 2601.11565

## Executive Summary
Compass-Embedding v4 addresses the scarcity of high-quality multilingual embeddings for low-resource Southeast Asian languages in e-commerce. It tackles false negatives in contrastive learning via Class-Aware Masking (CAM), mitigates data scarcity through a diversified pipeline including synthetic generation and translation, and introduces production optimizations (vLLM, FP8 quantization). The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on SEA languages and significant efficiency gains, while maintaining competitive English MTEB scores.

## Method Summary
No reproduction notes were provided. The method, inferred from the key outcome, comprises three core components: (1) Class-Aware Masking (CAM) to suppress invalid in-batch negatives using metadata (e.g., categories, personas); (2) a diversified data pipeline blending retrieval, classification, and synthetic data (∼50% synthetic for low-resource languages); and (3) production optimizations integrating vLLM and FP8 quantization. The training excludes multilingual NLI due to distinct requirements that negatively impacted global performance. Model merging via SLERP recovers English performance after domain adaptation.

## Key Results
- SOTA on SEA languages: 84.26 weighted average, outperforming OpenAI models by 10.5–26.6%.
- 16.2% improvement on e-commerce tasks over Compass-Embedding v3.
- Competitive English MTEB scores; inference efficiency gains of 8.3× throughput, 88% cost reduction versus v3, and only 15% of OpenAI's cost with minimal accuracy loss.

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can Compass-Embedding v4 be extended to multimodal inputs (e.g., product images) while preserving its low-resource language and e-commerce strengths?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The conclusion explicitly lists "multimodal embeddings that integrate textual and visual signals" as a promising future direction.
- **Why unresolved**: The current model is purely text-based, yet e-commerce data is inherently multimodal (item images, diagrams). Integrating vision-language modeling without degrading performance on SEA languages or e-commerce tasks remains unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Train and evaluate a multimodal variant (e.g., with a vision encoder) on e-commerce benchmarks that include image-text retrieval, measuring trade-offs against the current text-only model's SEA and domain performance.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Does Class-Aware Masking (CAM) generalize beyond the specific e-commerce and multilingual tasks studied, or does it risk over-masking in domains with coarser or noisier class metadata?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] CAM leverages class identifiers from metadata (e.g., categories, personas) to mask false negatives. The paper notes success in curated e-commerce and synthetic data but excludes multilingual NLI due to "distinct training requirements [that] negatively impacted global performance."
- **Why unresolved**: The reliance on clean, discrete class identifiers may not transfer to domains where such metadata is ambiguous, hierarchical, or unavailable. Over-masking could suppress valid negatives and harm representation learning.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Ablation studies applying CAM to benchmarks with varying class granularity (e.g., general MTEB tasks without explicit class labels) and measuring performance changes; analyze masking decisions to identify potential over- or under-masking.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What is the optimal balance between general-purpose and domain-specific data in the training mixture for low-resource languages, and does the current 50% synthetic data ratio for low-resource languages induce distributional artifacts?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper states that ∼50% of low-resource language data is synthetic, and that NLI was excluded for multilingual tasks due to performance degradation. This implies a delicate trade-off in data composition.
- **Why unresolved**: The data mixture (retrieval 68%, etc.) is chosen heuristically. Synthetic data, while addressing scarcity, may not capture real-world linguistic diversity or e-commerce nuances, potentially leading to overfitting to generated patterns or poor cross-domain generalization.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic experiments varying the proportion of synthetic vs. real data for individual low-resource languages, evaluating on both in-domain (e-commerce) and out-of-domain (MTEB) tasks, with qualitative analysis of generated samples versus authentic text.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Can the spherical model merging (SLERP) approach be automated or learned to avoid manual intervention, and what are the theoretical limits of its ability to reconcile catastrophic forgetting between merged checkpoints?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper uses SLERP to merge a domain-adapted checkpoint with an earlier one to recover English performance. This is presented as a successful heuristic.
- **Why unresolved**: The interpolation factor is chosen ad hoc. The geometry-preserving properties of SLERP are assumed beneficial, but there is no analysis of when merging fails (e.g., if checkpoints are too dissimilar) or whether adaptive merging (e.g., layer-wise) could improve the balance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Study the angular divergence between checkpoint embedding spaces along training trajectory; test automated merging strategies (e.g., based on validation performance) and compare against SLERP across diverse domain shifts; analyze performance bounds analytically.
