# Spatially Robust Inference with Predicted and Missing at Random Labels

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11368
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11368
- **Reference count:** 34
- **Primary result:** Proposed Spatial DR-JK-HAC method achieves ~90% coverage under MAR and spatial dependence, while competitors drop to 33-81% and intervals are 1.3-1.6× wider than unjackknifed spatial HAC.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses inference for population means when outcome labels are missing at random (MAR) and only predictions from an auxiliary model are available for unlabeled units, with additional spatial dependence violating i.i.d. assumptions. The authors propose a doubly robust (DR) estimator that cross-fits nuisance models for outcome regression and label propensity. They identify a novel issue: cross-fitting introduces fold-level correlations that bias spatial variance estimation. To resolve this, they develop a jackknife spatial HAC variance correction that removes the artificial fold-shared noise while preserving genuine spatial dependence. Theoretically, the resulting intervals are asymptotically valid under standard assumptions. Empirical evaluation on synthetic and real datasets, varying MAR mechanisms and spatial dependence strength, shows the Spatial DR-JK-HAC method maintains coverage near the nominal 90% level (typically 0.87–0.91), while competing methods suffer coverage as low as 0.33–0.81. The calibrated intervals are only modestly wider (about 1.3–1.6×) than those from unjackknifed spatial HAC, illustrating a favorable robustness‑efficiency trade‑off.

## Method Summary
The proposed method combines doubly robust (DR) estimation with cross-fitted nuisance models to handle missing at random (MAR) labels, then applies a jackknife-corrected spatial heteroscedasticity and autocorrelation consistent (HAC) variance estimator to account for spatial dependence. Cross-fitting avoids overfitting but introduces fold-level correlation; the jackknife correction removes this artifact by centering scores within folds and combining a within-fold HAC estimate with a between-fold ANOVA term. The final variance estimator yields asymptotically valid confidence intervals under spatial dependence, and empirically maintains near‑nominal coverage even under strong dependence and MAR missingness.

## Key Results
- Spatial DR-JK-HAC maintains coverage close to 90% (0.87–0.91) across synthetic and real datasets under MAR and strong spatial dependence, while competing methods fall to 0.33–0.81.
- The calibrated intervals are only 1.3–1.6 times wider than unjackknifed spatial HAC, demonstrating a modest robustness‑efficiency trade‑off.
- The jackknife correction successfully isolates genuine spatial dependence from cross-fitting noise, and the approach is modular enough to be paired with other dependence‑matched covariance structures (e.g., two‑way clustering).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Doubly Robust Identification under MAR
- **Claim:** The DR estimator corrects selection bias under MAR without requiring both nuisance models to be correctly specified.
- **Mechanism:** The DR score combines an outcome regression estimate \(\hat{m}(W,s)\) and an inverse-probability weighting term using the estimated label propensity \(\hat{\pi}(W,s)\). Under MAR (\(Y_i \perp R_i \mid (W_i,s_i)\)), if either \(\hat{m}\) or \(\hat{\pi}\) is consistent, the score has mean zero, eliminating bias.
- **Evidence:** Proposition 1 formalizes identification; Equation (1) gives the DR estimating function. Related work such as Cross-Prediction-Powered Inference and Dual-Label Learning address MAR-like settings but assume i.i.d. sampling.
- **Break condition:** Failure of the overlap assumption (e.g., some covariate-location strata never labeled) prevents point identification of \(\theta_0 = E[Y]\) and invalidates intervals.

### Mechanism 2: Cross-Fitting-Induced Fold-Level Noise
- **Claim:** Cross‑fitting nuisance models to avoid overfitting introduces artificial correlation among units in the same fold, distorting spatial variance estimates.
- **Mechanism:** All units within fold \(k\) share the same fitted nuisance estimates \((\hat{m}_{-k},\hat{\pi}_{-k})\). Consequently, the DR score \(\hat{\psi}^\circ_i\) contains a fold‑specific random effect \(a_k\) (Equation 5). Since spatially close units often reside in the same fold, a Conley HAC estimator interprets this shared noise as short‑range spatial dependence, inflating standard errors.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 3; Equation (5) decomposes the score; buffered cross‑fitting is introduced to mitigate leakage.
- **Break condition:** With extremely strong autocorrelation, even buffered folds may contain many within‑fold spatial pairs, leaving residual bias in the within‑fold covariance.

### Mechanism 3: Jackknife-HAC Variance Correction
- **Claim:** Centering scores within folds before applying spatial HAC removes fold‑shared noise; the between‑fold term restores legitimate macro‑scale variation, yielding asymptotically valid intervals under spatial dependence.
- **Mechanism:** Define within‑fold centered scores \(\tilde{\psi}_i = \hat{\psi}^\circ_i - \bar{\psi}_{k(i)}\). Proposition 2 shows that fold‑constant terms cancel, so the within‑fold HAC applied to \(\tilde{\psi}\) isolates only genuine residual spatial correlation. The jackknife variance \(\hat{V}_{JK}\) combines this with a fold‑level ANOVA term to reintroduce between‑fold variability (Equation 6).
- **Evidence:** Abstract, Section 3, Equation (6), Proposition 2.
- **Break condition:** The correction relies on the fold‑shared component being asymptotically negligible after centering relative to the oracle score’s spatial covariance accumulation (Assumption 6), and on the number of folds \(K_n \to \infty\). Non‑local dependence or misspecified bandwidth \(h_n\) can cause inconsistency; the Moran gate provides a heuristic fallback but is not covered by Theorem 1.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Doubly Robust Estimation
- **Why needed here:** Corrects bias from covariate‑dependent MAR labeling without requiring both nuisance models to be correctly specified; integrates with the base prediction.
- **Quick check:** Under MAR, what is the bias of a simple mean of predictions if the prediction error correlates with the labeling propensity?

### Concept: Cross-Fitting
- **Why needed here:** Prevents overfitting nuisance models on the small labeled subset, crucial when the outcome model is flexible and labels scarce.
- **Quick check:** Why would fitting nuisance models on the same data used to compute the DR score lead to under‑coverage even under MCAR?

### Concept: Spatial HAC Covariance Estimation
- **Why needed here:** Under spatial dependence, the iid variance estimator is invalid; a HAC estimator weights covariances by distance to account for local dependence.
- **Quick check:** What assumption about the spatial dependence structure allows a Conley‑style HAC estimator to be consistent (e.g., long‑range covariance accumulation)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data ingestion → base predictor (external or within‑sample, out‑of‑fold if within) → fold partition (with optional buffering based on spatial distance quantile \(r_n\)) → cross‑fit nuisance models (outcome regression \(\hat{m}\) and label propensity \(\hat{\pi}\)) → DR scores \(\hat{\psi}^\circ_i\) → fold centering (\(\tilde{\psi}_i\)) → within‑fold spatial HAC + between‑fold ANOVA combination → jackknife variance \(\hat{V}_{JK}\) → confidence interval.

**Critical path:** The essential pipeline consists of: (1) partitioning the data into \(K\) (possibly buffered) folds; (2) training \(\hat{m}\) and \(\hat{\pi}\) out‑of‑fold; (3) computing the DR scores; (4) centering scores within folds; (5) estimating the within‑fold spatial HAC covariance and the between‑fold ANOVA term; (6) combining them via the jackknife formula to form \(\hat{V}_{JK}\); (7) constructing the final CI. Breaks occur if buffering fails to separate spatially close units, if propensity clipping \(\pi_{\text{min}}\) is too aggressive relative to true overlap, or if the bandwidth \(h_n\) is not a valid spatial quantile.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Number of folds \(K\): larger \(K\) reduces nuisance estimation error but stabilizes the between‑fold term less (asymptotics require \(K_n \to \infty\)); typical choices are \(K=5\) vs. \(10\), trading width inflation for robustness (Appendix C).  
- Buffering radius \(r_n\): larger buffers reduce within‑fold spatial pairs but may yield folds too small for stable nuisance estimation; chosen as a distance quantile for reproducibility.  
- Bandwidth \(h_n\): must grow with \(n\) but slower than domain size; selected as a distance quantile to avoid tuning.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Under‑coverage under MAR even with DR suggests propensity misspecification or severe overlap violation.  
- Over‑conservative intervals often arise from strong spatial dependence or an excessively large \(h_n\); the jackknife‑HAC may still be conservative relative to truth if between‑fold variation is high.  
- Frequent triggering of the Moran gate indicates that residual spatial signal in the labeled residuals is weak, making the HAC adjustment potentially unnecessary but not harmful.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Synthetic spatial fields:** Vary smoothing radius \(\sigma\) (dependence), sampling scheme (i.i.d. vs. soft‑block), and missingness (MCAR vs. MAR). Confirms Spatial DR‑JK‑HAC maintains ~90% coverage while baselines collapse under MAR or strong dependence.  
2. **Observed benchmarks:** Five datasets (Forest, Galaxies, Census, Malaria, Health+) with auxiliary‑split predictions. Evaluate coverage under MCAR/MAR and i.i.d./soft‑block sampling. Shows consistent MAR gains over dependence‑aware baselines.  
3. **Mechanism ablation:** Fix point estimator (DR), compare variance choices (iid, spatial HAC, jackknife‑HAC) in the hardest synthetic cell. Demonstrates jackknife‑HAC specifically calibrates under MAR + strong dependence by removing fold‑shared noise.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What is a complete finite‑sample theory for the jackknife‑HAC variance correction, including its bias, variance, and coverage properties under various spatial dependence structures and label budgets?  
- **Open Question 2:** Under what conditions is the jackknife‑HAC variance estimator asymptotically equivalent to the conventional spatial HAC estimator, and when does it actually improve finite‑sample robustness compared to the naive HAC?  
- **Open Question 3:** How can the proposed inference framework be adapted to settings where the overlap assumption is severely violated, such as when certain covariate‑location strata have extremely low labeling probability?  
- **Open Question 4:** How can the jackknife‑HAC variance correction be generalized to other dependence structures beyond spatial, such as time series, multiway clustering, or network dependence, while maintaining asymptotic validity?

## Limitations
- MAR assumption is critical but untestable; reliance on observed covariates may leave residual bias if missingness depends on unobserved spatial factors.  
- Overlap requirement: some covariate‑space strata may be completely unlabeled, making point identification impossible without additional structure.  
- Cross‑fitting artifact: even with buffered folds, spatially close units can share folds under strong autocorrelation, and the jackknife correction’s between‑fold term can be noisy for small \(K\).

## Confidence
- Doubly robust identification under MAR: **High**  
- Cross‑fitting induces fold‑level correlation: **High**  
- Jackknife‑HAC asymptotic validity: **Medium**  
- Empirical coverage near nominal (≈90%) under MAR + strong dependence: **Medium**
