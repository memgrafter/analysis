# Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation of Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07079
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07079
- Reference count: 35
- Primary result: EOPD mitigates diversity collapse by augmenting reverse KL with forward KL on high-entropy tokens, achieving Pass@8 gains of up to +5.05 on math reasoning benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Standard on-policy distillation (OPD) uses reverse KL divergence to train a student model to mimic a teacher's token distributions. However, reverse KL is mode-seeking and can cause diversity collapse and unstable learning when the teacher distribution is high-entropy (i.e., flat or ambiguous). This paper introduces Entropy-Aware On-Policy Distillation (EOPD), which dynamically adds a forward KL term only on tokens where the teacher's conditional entropy exceeds a threshold τ. Forward KL is mode-covering and preserves teacher uncertainty. Across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks, EOPD improves Pass@8 accuracy relative to OPD for Qwen3 students of varying sizes (0.6B: +1.37, 1.7B: +2.39, 4B: +5.05). It also shows competitive performance on out-of-domain tasks like GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro, and AlpacaEval. Analyses confirm that EOPD maintains higher token-level entropy and lower forward KL on high-entropy tokens compared to OPD, demonstrating its ability to transfer teacher uncertainty while retaining training efficiency.

## Method Summary
EOPD augments the standard on-policy distillation objective with a forward KL component that is activated per token based on the teacher's conditional entropy. Specifically, given a student rollout, the teacher is queried at each token position to compute the teacher's log-probability, the top-k token set (k=16), and the conditional entropy H_te. The loss per token is L_EOPD = L_OPD + I[H_te > τ] * L_FKL, where L_OPD is a clipped reverse KL surrogate (following PPO) and L_FKL is the forward KL over the top-k tokens. The threshold τ (default 0.8) determines when to apply forward KL. Training proceeds by generating rollouts with the current student, computing the mixed loss, and performing gradient updates. The approach retains the efficiency of reverse KL in confident regions (low entropy) while covering ambiguous (high-entropy) regions to prevent mode collapse. Hyperparameters include learning rate 3e-6, batch size 128, and mini-batch size 32, with training for 3 epochs on MATH or 2 on DAPO-Math-14k.

## Key Results
- Pass@8 gains over OPD: +1.37 (Qwen3-0.6B-Base), +2.39 (1.7B-Base), and +5.05 (4B-Base) on six math reasoning benchmarks.
- EOPD retains significantly higher token-level entropy in high-entropy positions (H_te ≥ 1.0) compared to OPD, and achieves lower forward KL divergence specifically on those tokens.
- Competitive or improved Avg@8/Pass@8 on out-of-domain benchmarks: GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro, AlpacaEval 2.0.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper identifies three key mechanisms:

**Mechanism 1: Reverse KL causes diversity collapse under high-entropy teachers.** Reverse KL (student||teacher) is mode-seeking; it encourages the student to match the teacher's high-probability modes but ignores teacher modes the student does not visit. When the teacher distribution is flat (high entropy), the student's limited capacity cannot cover all modes, leading to aggressive pruning of plausible tokens and volatile gradients. Evidence: OPD retains only 6.8% of high-entropy tokens vs. teacher’s 18.5%; toy experiment shows top-10 set fails to converge under high-entropy teacher (top-1 changes 84% of the time). This collapse occurs when the teacher's entropy exceeds the student's representational capacity and the loss is pure reverse KL.

**Mechanism 2: Forward KL on high-entropy tokens preserves uncertainty and stabilizes learning.** Forward KL (teacher||student) penalizes the student for assigning low probability to any teacher-likely token, thus covering all teacher modes. When applied selectively to high-entropy tokens, it fills the student's distribution in ambiguous regions without forcing it to mimic low-probability tails in confident regions. Evidence: EOPD retains more probability mass in high-entropy tokens and shows lower forward KL divergence at those positions compared to OPD. The mode-covering property transfers global structure and uncertainty.

**Mechanism 3: Dynamic per-token switching balances efficiency and diversity.** The switch is triggered by teacher entropy H_te. Reverse KL (efficient, low-variance) is used where the teacher is confident (low H_te), while forward KL (corrective, distribution-matching) is used where the teacher is uncertain (high H_te). This adaptively matches the objective to local teacher structure. Evidence: Performance peaks around τ=0.8; random forward KL placement underperforms EOPD, confirming that selective placement on high-entropy tokens is key.

## Foundational Learning
**KL Divergence Asymmetry (Forward vs. Reverse)**
- Why needed: EOPD mixes two complementary KL divergences per token. Understanding that KL(teacher||student) covers all teacher modes while KL(student||teacher) ignores unseen modes explains why mixing is necessary.
- Quick check: If a teacher has two equally likely tokens (entropy=1.0) but a student only ever samples one, which KL penalizes the student for missing the second? What does that imply about the student's final distribution shape? (Answer: Forward KL penalizes, forcing the student to cover both modes.)

**On-Policy vs. Off-Policy Distillation**
- Why needed: EOPD is on-policy; the student generates its own rollouts, which interacts with reverse KL differently than off-policy KD that uses a fixed dataset. The efficiency claim relies on avoiding full forward KL everywhere.
- Quick check: What is exposure bias in language model training? Why does generating rollouts with the student (on-policy) better align the training distribution with inference? (Answer: Exposure bias arises when training on a fixed dataset that differs from the model's own predictions; on-policy rollouts match inference distribution.)

**Token-Level Entropy as Uncertainty Signal**
- Why needed: EOPD's switch is triggered by per-token entropy. Entropy in a conditional distribution measures the teacher's uncertainty about the next token given context. High entropy indicates multiple plausible continuations (e.g., reasoning steps), not noise; preserving this is key for transferring reasoning diversity.
- Quick check: In a math reasoning task, would you expect higher token entropy at the beginning (setting up variables) or at a deterministic arithmetic step? Why does preserving the teacher's entropy profile matter? (Answer: Higher at the beginning where multiple approaches are plausible; preserving entropy maintains diversity in solution strategies.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** 1. Rollout Generator → 2. Teacher Query Module → 3. Loss Computer → 4. Optimizer

**Critical path:** Sample rollout → Per-token teacher query → Conditional forward KL activation → Loss aggregation → Parameter update.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Memory vs. Fidelity (Top-k):** Forward KL over full vocabulary is infeasible; top-k (k=16) reduces memory linearly but risks missing significant probability mass in very flat distributions. Must ensure k captures sufficient cumulative probability (>97% typically).
- **Computation vs. Selectivity:** Calculating entropy and top-k adds overhead vs pure OPD, but forward KL is only applied to ~15-20% of tokens (high-entropy), so net cost is manageable.
- **Threshold Sensitivity:** τ controls bias-variance: low τ applies forward KL more often (robust but less efficient); high τ is more efficient but may miss ambiguous regions. Performance peaks around τ=0.8.

**Failure signatures:**
1. **No Diversity Retention:** High-entropy token ratio in student rollouts remains low (close to OPD) instead of approaching teacher's ~18.5%. Check histogram.
2. **Unstable High-Entropy Forward KL:** Average forward KL on tokens where H_te > τ remains high and fluctuating during training, not consistently decreasing.
3. **Threshold Mismatch:** Ablating τ shows Pass@8 degrades monotonically (or non-monotonically) without a clear peak, indicating the teacher's entropy distribution doesn't align with the heuristic threshold.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Reproduce Toy Instability:** Implement the categorical toy setup (low vs. high entropy teacher). Verify reverse KL reward fails to converge on top-10 token set under high-entropy teacher, while mixed KL stabilizes.
2. **Token Entropy Histogram Validation:** Train OPD and EOPD on a small subset (e.g., 200 math prompts). Generate rollouts, compute per-token teacher entropy, and plot histograms. Confirm EOPD retains more mass in H_te ≥ 1.0 bin.
3. **Forward KL at High Entropy:** During training, log average L_FKL only on positions where H_te > τ. Compare OPD (effectively 0), OPD+Entropy-Bonus, and EOPD. EOPD should show lower and more stable forward KL in these regions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** What theoretical principles underlie the choice of a fixed entropy threshold τ for applying forward KL, and how does τ relate to the teacher's distributional characteristics beyond empirical tuning? Basis: Paper ablates τ but provides no theoretical justification. Why unresolved: τ is empirical on math tasks; may not generalize. What evidence would resolve: Derive analytical bounds linking token entropy to teacher-student KL; test τ across diverse tasks and architectures.
- **Question 2:** How does the top-k approximation for forward KL affect fidelity of teacher uncertainty transfer, and what are the trade-offs in choosing k for varying student capacities and teacher distributions? Basis: Appendix F analyzes memory vs. cumulative mass for k=16 in one setup. Why unresolved: Optimal k likely depends on teacher sparsity, vocabulary, and task. What evidence would resolve: Systematically vary k across teacher-student pairs and tasks; measure performance, probability mass coverage, gradient variance.
- **Question 3:** Is the observed performance gain from EOPD primarily due to preserved token-level entropy in high-uncertainty regions, or does it also arise from improved gradient stability or reduced variance in learning signals? Basis: Section 5.4 shows entropy preservation correlates with better Pass@k, but causation not established. Why unresolved: Alternative factors like smoother gradients could contribute equally. What evidence would resolve: Controlled ablations decoupling entropy from KL objectives (e.g., add noise to mimic entropy without forward KL, measure gradient variances).
- **Question 4:** How effective is EOPD for non-reasoning language tasks (e.g., summarization, dialogue), where teacher uncertainty patterns and the importance of diversity may differ? Basis: Experiments focus on math benchmarks and limited out-of-domain tests; non-reasoning tasks are excluded. Why unresolved: Method targets high-entropy decision points common in reasoning; may be unnecessary or detrimental where low-entropy authoritative outputs are preferred. What evidence would resolve: Evaluate EOPD on diverse tasks including summarization, translation, conversational AI, comparing entropy distributions and performance.

## Limitations
- **Implementation ambiguities:** Exact details of top-k token selection for forward KL approximation and the sign convention in the clipped reverse KL surrogate are not fully specified, requiring source code inspection.
- **Generalizability:** The optimal entropy threshold (τ=0.8) and performance gains are demonstrated only for Qwen3 models on specific math datasets; it is unclear if findings transfer to other architectures or domains.
- **Causal attribution:** While token-level entropy correlates with improvement, it is not proven that entropy alone is the causal signal for when to apply forward KL. Other correlated factors could be driving the effect.

## Confidence
- **Standard OPD with pure reverse KL causes measurable diversity collapse and instability on high-entropy teacher distributions:** High confidence.
- **EOPD's selective forward KL activation based on teacher token entropy is the direct cause of improved stability and retained diversity:** Medium confidence.
- **The reported Pass@8 gains will robustly replicate on new setups and generalize to out-of-domain benchmarks:** Low confidence.
