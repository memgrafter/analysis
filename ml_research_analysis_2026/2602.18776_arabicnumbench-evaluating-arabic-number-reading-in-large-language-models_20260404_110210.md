# ArabicNumBench: Evaluating Arabic Number Reading in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18776
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18776
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** Few-shot CoT boosts accuracy 2.8× and structured output 4.8× over zero-shot, revealing a dissociation where elite-accuracy models (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro at 99.05%) often produce predominantly unstructured responses (92.86% fallback).

## Executive Summary
ArabicNumBench is a specialized benchmark addressing the gap in evaluating Arabic number reading across Eastern (Arabic-Indic) and Western numerals. It assesses 71 models using 59,010 test cases spanning six contextual categories and four prompting strategies, tracking both numerical accuracy and structured output generation via a hierarchical extraction pipeline. Key findings demonstrate that few-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting dramatically improves performance, while a critical dissociation emerges: models with near-perfect numerical accuracy frequently fail to adhere to required output formats, implying that correctness alone is insufficient for reliable production deployment.

## Method Summary
The evaluation pipeline generates synthetic test cases covering numeral-only text, addresses, monetary amounts, dates, scientific notation, and mixed contexts. Each case is formatted with one of four prompting strategies (zero-shot, few-shot, zero-shot CoT, few-shot CoT). Model responses are harvested via the OpenRouter API with deterministic sampling (temperature=0). A six-tier hierarchical extraction parser then attempts to identify the final numeric answer, prioritizing explicit markers (e.g., "final answer") and falling back to broader heuristics if needed. Metrics include exact-match accuracy against ground truth and the percentage of responses classified as "structured" (those yielding an answer via the first three extraction methods). This design separates numerical reasoning capability from instruction-following discipline.

## Key Results
- Accuracy across all models and strategies ranges from 14.29% to 99.05%.
- Few-shot CoT achieves 2.8× higher accuracy (80.06% vs. 28.76%) and 4.8× more structured output (34.12% vs. 7.14%) than zero-shot.
- A dissociation is evident: elite-accuracy models (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro at 99.05%) produce >92% fallback (unstructured) responses; only 6 models (8.5%) consistently achieve ≥90% structured output.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Few-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting substantially improves both numerical accuracy and structured output generation compared to zero-shot or few-shot alone.
- **Mechanism:** Demonstrations with explicit reasoning chains scaffold the model's internal process and establish a format template, increasing the likelihood of outputs with recognizable conclusion markers.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] "Few-shot Chain-of-Thought prompting achieves 2.8× higher accuracy (80.06% vs 28.76%) and 4.8× more structured output (34.12% vs 7.14%) than zero-shot." [section IV.B] Table II confirms these averages. [corpus] Direct support from the paper's own results; neighboring CoT studies (e.g., arXiv:2512.09679) are domain-adjacent but not specific to Arabic numerals.
- **Break condition:** Fails if demonstrations are unrepresentative, in a different language, or the model is too small (<7B parameters) to benefit from exemplars.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Numerical accuracy and structured output generation are dissociated capabilities; high accuracy does not imply reliable instruction-following.
- **Mechanism:** Numerical reasoning relies on internal knowledge of numeral systems, while structured output requires suppression of default language generation and adherence to format constraints—skills developed through different training objectives (e.g., instruction-tuning, RLHF).
- **Evidence:** [abstract] "A key finding reveals a dissociation: elite-accuracy models (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro at 99.05%) often produce predominantly unstructured responses (92.86% fallback). Only 6 models (8.5%) consistently achieve ≥90% structured output." [section IV.C] "Models in the 'High Accuracy, Low Structure' group achieve elite-level accuracy (98-99%) while producing predominantly unstructured output (92% fallback)."
- **Break condition:** May shrink if models are explicitly trained with format constraints (e.g., RLHF rewarding compliance).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The ability to generate structured output is bounded by model architecture and training; prompting strategies cannot fully compensate for weak instruction-following capabilities.
- **Mechanism:** Instruction-following emerges from specific training regimes (supervised fine-tuning on instructions, RLHF). Prompting can activate existing capabilities but cannot create novel ones; thus, a ceiling exists determined by the model's inherent design.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] "No prompting strategy can transform a weak instruction-follower into a strong one. Model selection is more important than prompt engineering for production systems requiring structured output." [section V.B] "This capability divide suggests a fundamental limitation: prompting strategies have a ceiling determined by model architecture and training."
- **Break condition:** If future architectures incorporate explicit format constraints during training or decoding, the ceiling may rise.

## Architecture Onboarding
Test case generator -> Prompt formatter -> Model client (OpenRouter API) -> Response parser (6-tier extraction) -> Metrics module -> Storage

Critical path: generate test case -> format prompt -> call model -> parse response -> extract answer -> compare to ground truth -> record extraction method & correctness -> aggregate metrics.

Design tradeoffs:
- Test case diversity vs. evaluation cost: 210 cases per model-strategy balances coverage and API expenses.
- Extraction simplicity vs. robustness: Hierarchical approach prioritizes simple delimiters first; more complex methods might improve recall but increase false positives.
- Deterministic sampling (temperature=0) vs. stochasticity: Chosen for reproducibility but may underrepresent model variance.
- Focus on Arabic numeral reading vs. broader numerical reasoning: Narrow scope allows deep analysis but limits generalizability.

Failure signatures:
- High fallback extraction rate (>50%) indicates poor format adherence.
- Low accuracy despite structured output suggests reasoning failures.
- Inconsistent performance across similar contexts (e.g., pure numerals vs. addresses) may reveal contextual brittleness.
- API errors or timeouts require retry logic.
- Extraction method misclassifications (e.g., missing answer in CoT) detectable by manual sample review.

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline sanity check: Run a small subset (e.g., 10 test cases) across all four prompting strategies with a known model (e.g., GPT-4o-mini) to verify pipeline extraction correctness and metric alignment.
2. CoT validation: Compare few-shot vs. few-shot CoT on the same model to confirm expected boost in structured output and accuracy; if absent, debug.
3. Elite model confirmation: Evaluate the six models identified as elite (≥90% structured output) on a held-out set of 50 cases to verify consistency; if performance drops, investigate overfitting or data leakage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How would expanding ArabicNumBench to more Arabic domains and cases change the accuracy–structured output dissociation? (Basis: V.D notes pilot dataset only 210 cases; future work needs scientific, legal, dialectal Arabic.)
- Which model factors (architecture, training, RLHF) explain the dissociation between high accuracy and low structured output? (Basis: Elite models like Qwen3 Max, Llama405B achieve ≥90% structure; high-accuracy models like Gemini2.5 Pro have >92% fallback.)
- Is the hierarchical extraction pipeline valid, or does it misclassify structured outputs, creating an artificial dissociation? (Basis: Six extraction methods used but no validation of classification into structured vs fallback.)

## Limitations
- Benchmark scope is narrow (numeral reading only), limiting generalizability to broader Arabic NLP tasks.
- Synthetic test cases may not capture real-world noise, dialects, or contextual variations.
- Hierarchical extraction heuristics risk misclassifying responses, especially for CoT where answers may be embedded.
- Model selection via OpenRouter may miss specialized or open-weight Arabic-optimized models.

## Confidence
- **Few-shot CoT superiority:** High (robust across 71 models with large effect sizes; direct evidence from Tables II and IV).
- **Accuracy–structure dissociation:** Medium (clear pattern e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro but depends on extraction criteria; possible overestimation if misparsing).
- **Prompting ceiling:** Medium (consistent underperformance suggests training limitations, but not all techniques tested).

## Next Checks
1. Cross-validate extraction: Use an LLM-based parser (e.g., GPT-4) to re-analyze a sample of responses, measuring agreement with the original hierarchical method.
2. Real-world stress test: Deploy ArabicNumBench on a fresh corpus of Arabic texts (e.g., social media, news) containing numerals to evaluate robustness beyond synthetic data.
3. Format-imitation ablation: Run few-shot CoT with demonstrations that omit explicit "final answer" markers, isolating the effect of reasoning scaffolding versus format templating.
