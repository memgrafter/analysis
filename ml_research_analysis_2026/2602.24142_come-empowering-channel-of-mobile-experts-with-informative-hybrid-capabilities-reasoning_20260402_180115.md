# CoME: Empowering Channel-of-Mobile-Experts with Informative Hybrid-Capabilities Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.24142
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24142
- **Reference count**: 35
- **Primary result**: CoME (5B activated parameters) achieves 66.98% action match on AITZ and 72.61% on AMEX, outperforming dense and MoE baselines by significant margins.

## Executive Summary
Mobile agents require multi-stage reasoning (screen summary → subtask plan → action decision → function execution), but existing methods struggle with both decoupling specialized capabilities and balancing their integration. CoME introduces a Channel-of-Mobile-Experts (CoME) architecture that extends each layer’s FFN into four capability-specific experts and uses an output-oriented channel router to select the appropriate expert per reasoning stage. A progressive training pipeline—Expert-FT for capability-specific enhancement, Router-FT for stage-aligned activation, and CoT-FT for balanced collaboration—enables effective multi-stage reasoning. To mitigate error propagation, InfoGain-Driven DPO (Info-DPO) evaluates intermediate step contributions via information gain, reinforcing informative reasoning trajectories. CoME sets new state-of-the-art on AITZ and AMEX benchmarks with a compact activated parameter budget.

## Method Summary
CoME builds on a base MLLM (e.g., Qwen2VL-2B) by replacing each FFN with four parallel experts (screen summary, subtask plan, action decision, action function). During autoregressive generation, a channel router predicts per-token expert logits based on the current reasoning stage, fusing the selected expert’s hidden state to produce output. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) Expert-FT fine-tunes each expert’s FFN on stage-specific data to establish specialized capabilities; (2) Router-FT trains the router using token-level stage labels with a cross-entropy and router norm loss to align expert activation with reasoning stages; (3) CoT-FT fine-tunes the full model on hybrid-capabilities trajectories with SFT loss and router norm regularization to enable seamless expert collaboration. Finally, Info-DPO samples multiple reasoning trajectories per task, computes an InfoGain reward that measures each stage’s reduction in entropy of the final action, and performs DPO using pairs of high-InfoGain correct vs. low-InfoGain incorrect trajectories.

## Key Results
- CoME (5B activated) achieves 66.98% overall action match on AITZ, surpassing dense agents (+1.73%) and MoE methods (+5.72%).
- On AMEX, CoME reaches 72.61%, beating dense models (+1.90%) and sparse MoE (+8.05%).
- Ablation studies confirm the critical contributions of Info-DPO (+4.68% overall) and the necessity of both Router-FT and Expert-FT (each removal causes ~4% drop).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
CoME activates experts per reasoning stage via output-oriented routing. Unlike conventional MoE’s input-oriented routing, the channel router selects the expert aligned with the current stage (screen summary, subtask plan, etc.) to generate each output token. This ensures stage-specific capability utilization and mitigates interference. Evidence includes the abstract’s explicit claim, Section 3.2’s router design, and ablation showing performance collapse if activation distributions remain uniform across stages.

### Mechanism 2
The progressive training curriculum (Expert-FT → Router-FT → CoT-FT) sequentially achieves decoupled enhancement, stage-aligned activation, and balanced integration. Expert-FT initializes specialized FFNs; Router-FT aligns the router using stage labels; CoT-FT fine-tunes the integrated model with router norm regularization. Ablation in Section 4.3 demonstrates that removing any stage degrades performance significantly (e.g., -4.08% without Router-FT), confirming each stage’s necessity.

### Mechanism 3
Info-DPO mitigates error propagation by rewarding reasoning steps that increase the information gain (reduction in entropy) toward the correct final action. A reward model estimates the entropy of the final action given cumulative reasoning up to each stage; DPO pairs are formed from high-InfoGain correct and low-InfoGain incorrect trajectories. The correlation between positive InfoGain and correct trajectories (Figure 6) and the +4.68% ablation gain validate its effectiveness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)**: Understands the standard input-oriented routing paradigm. Why needed: Contrasts with CoME’s output-oriented activation to highlight the novelty for multi-stage reasoning. Quick check: Why does input-oriented MoE fail to align expert usage with autoregressive generation stages?
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning**: Grasps structured multi-step decomposition. Why needed: Hybrid-capabilities reasoning is a specialized CoT; understanding CoT’s benefits and error propagation challenges is prerequisite. Quick check: How does decomposing an action into stages introduce new optimization challenges compared to end-to-end prediction?
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**: Knows the standard framework of pairing chosen/rejected completions. Why needed: Info-DPO modifies DPO by using a step-wise InfoGain reward instead of a final-outcome reward. Quick check: In standard DPO, what defines chosen vs. rejected, and why is that inadequate for evaluating intermediate reasoning steps?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Capability-specific datasets → Expert-FT (specialized FFNs) → Initialized Experts + Random Router → Router-FT (stage-aligned router) → Full CoME → CoT-FT (balanced collaboration) → Info-DPO (InfoGain-aligned DPO)
- **Critical path**:
  1. Prepare stage-specific datasets from AITZ/AMEX (screen summary, subtask plan, etc.).
  2. Perform Expert-FT on each expert’s FFN using capability-specific data.
  3. Assemble CoME with initialized experts and a new channel router.
  4. Annotate hybrid-capabilities trajectories with per-token expert labels based on reasoning stage.
  5. Train the router with Router-FT using cross-entropy and router norm losses.
  6. Fine-tune the entire CoME on hybrid trajectories with CoT-FT (SFT + router norm).
  7. Train a reward model to predict final action from partial reasoning.
  8. Sample trajectories, compute InfoGain, construct DPO pairs, and run Info-DPO.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Number of experts**: More experts (e.g., 8-expert) yield marginal gains (+0.66% on AITZ) at higher activation cost.
  - **Reward model scale**: A 7B RM outperforms a 72B RM due to better distribution fit and larger InfoGain separation.
  - **Reward function**: Continuous (distance/F1-based) rewards outperform discrete (0/1) for precise actions like clicks.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Router activation entropy high and uniform across stages (misalignment).
  - InfoGain values negative or near-zero for chosen trajectories (poor RM signal).
  - Later-stage performance (action decision/function) lags behind early stages (unbalanced optimization).
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Expert activation analysis**: On held-out data, compute per-stage expert selection accuracy; expect >90% peak alignment, not uniform distribution.
  2. **Info-DPO vs. Rule-DPO**: Replace InfoGain with rule-based reward (e.g., final action accuracy only); expect ~1-2% drop, especially on click actions.
  3. **Progressive training ablation**: Train variants skipping one stage each (Expert-FT, Router-FT, or CoT-FT); expect largest drop from skipping Router-FT (~-4%) and Info-DPO (~-4.7%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Is the three-stage progressive training strictly necessary, or could joint training achieve similar decoupling and integration?
- Does InfoGain reliably correlate with reasoning quality, or can it be gamed by superficial reasoning that introduces spurious correlations?
- Is four experts (one per stage) optimal, or could different granularity (e.g., 2 or 8 experts) or shared experts yield better efficiency?
- How robust is output-oriented activation to router errors when reasoning stage boundaries are ambiguous in deployment?
- Can Info-DPO’s reward model, trained on the same distribution, over-optimize spurious correlations and fail to generalize to OOD instructions or apps?

## Limitations
- Data splits and preprocessing for AITZ/AMEX are not fully specified, hindering exact reproduction.
- Reward model details (architecture, entropy computation, calibration) and channel router dimensions remain ambiguous.
- Training hyperparameters (batch size, epochs, optimizer) and LoRA configuration specifics are missing.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (output-oriented activation)**: Medium. Supported by ablation and design, but break condition not fully ruled out.
- **Mechanism 2 (progressive training)**: High. Strong ablation evidence for each stage’s necessity.
- **Mechanism 3 (Info-DPO)**: Medium. Ablation shows benefit; correlation between InfoGain and correctness not explicitly validated.
- **Overall performance claims**: Medium. Results are dataset-specific and reproduction is obstructed by missing details.
