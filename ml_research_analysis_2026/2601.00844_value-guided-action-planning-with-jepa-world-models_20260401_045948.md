# Value-guided action planning with JEPA world models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.00844
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00844
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: Value-guided JEPA with quasi-distance significantly improves planning accuracy, achieving up to 0.96 success on wall medium task.

## Executive Summary
Joint-Embedded Predictive Architectures (JEPA) learn latent dynamics for planning but often lack alignment between representation geometry and task-specific costs. This paper introduces a method to shape JEPA’s state embedding space so that the (quasi-)distance between embeddings approximates the negative goal‑conditioned value function for a reaching cost. This is enforced via an Implicit Q‑Learning (IQL) loss during training, turning the embedding distance into an implicit cost function. Planning then uses Model Predictive Control (MPC) with the MPPI optimizer. Experiments on wall and maze environments show that the value‑guided approach, especially with a quasi‑metric distance, substantially outperforms standard prediction‑only JEPA baselines (e.g., 0.96 vs. 0.46 planning accuracy in the wall medium task). The results demonstrate that aligning representation with value functions enhances planning without requiring an explicit reward model.

## Method Summary
The method augments a standard JEPA architecture—state encoder, identity action encoder, and predictor—with an auxiliary IQL loss. The IQL loss enforces \(V_\theta(s,g) \approx -\text{dist}(E_\theta(s), E_\theta(g))\), where \(\text{dist}\) is either Euclidean or a quasi‑metric. Two training regimes are used: **Sep** (train encoder with IQL loss first, then predictor with standard prediction loss) and **joint** (sum both losses). The quasi‑distance variant replaces the symmetric Euclidean metric with an asymmetric quasimetric from Wang & Isola (2022). For planning, MPC with MPPI generates action sequences; the cost of a trajectory is the embedding distance to the goal over a prediction horizon. Performance is evaluated via planning accuracy—the proportion of successful reaches from random start‑goal pairs—on wall (small/medium action norms) and maze datasets.

## Key Results
- VF quasi attains the highest planning accuracy: 0.71 (wall small), 0.96 (wall medium), and 0.63 (maze), surpassing Euclidean VF and prediction‑only baselines.
- Joint training of IQL and prediction losses (VF pred, VF quasi pred) degrades performance, highlighting the importance of separate training (Sep) to avoid interference.
- The quasi‑distance variant consistently outperforms the symmetric Euclidean version, confirming the benefit of modeling asymmetry in value functions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by directly optimizing the latent space to mirror the goal‑conditioned value structure. The IQL loss ensures the embedding distance (or quasi‑distance) becomes a surrogate for the negative value: closer embeddings imply lower reaching cost. This shaped representation turns the world model into an implicit cost function that aligns with optimal costs, enabling MPC to plan by simply minimizing that distance. The quasi‑metric further captures directional effects (e.g., reaching a goal may differ from returning), which symmetric distances cannot, leading to better accuracy. Separate training of the encoder with IQL before learning the predictor prevents conflicting gradients between value‑shaping and dynamics prediction objectives.

## Foundational Learning
**JEPA (Joint‑Embedding Predictive Architecture)**  
- *Why needed*: Learns latent dynamics without reconstructing observations, suitable for planning.  
- *Quick check*: Does the predictor accurately forecast future embeddings given current embedding and action?

**Implicit Q‑Learning (IQL)**  
- *Why needed*: Learns value functions via expectile regression, allowing representation shaping without explicit Bellman backups.  
- *Quick check*: Is the IQL loss minimizing expectiles of TD errors (e.g., with τ=0.8)?

**Quasimetric**  
- *Why needed*: Models asymmetric distances between states, matching the directional nature of goal‑conditioned values.  
- *Quick check*: Is \(d(s,g) \neq d(g,s)\) for valid transitions in a deterministic environment?

**Model Predictive Control (MPC) with MPPI**  
- *Why needed*: Uses the learned world model to plan actions by optimizing a cost over a horizon.  
- *Quick check*: Does the planner sample trajectories and re‑weight them according to the cost?

**Representation Shaping**  
- *Why needed*: Aligns embedding geometry with the optimal cost structure for planning.  
- *Quick check*: Does the embedding distance correlate with the negative value function on a validation set?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
State, Goal → StateEncoder → \(E(s), E(g)\)  
Action → ActionEncoder (identity) → \(E(a)\)  
Predictor: \([E(s), E(a)]\) → \(\hat{E}(s')\)  
IQL: \(\text{dist}(E(s), E(g))\) → \(V(s,g) \approx -\text{dist}\)  
Training: separate (IQL on encoder first, then predictor) or joint (sum losses)  
Planning: MPC/MPPI uses \(\text{dist}(E(s_t), E(g))\) as cost over horizon

**Critical path**  
1. Train state encoder with IQL loss to align embeddings with value (using target network, stop‑gradient).  
2. Train predictor with standard prediction loss (for Sep mode) or add it jointly.  
3. For evaluation, run MPPI: at each step, embed current state and goal, simulate trajectories using predictor, compute cost as distance to goal embedding, select action.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Euclidean vs. quasi‑distance: Euclidean is symmetric and simpler; quasi‑distance adds asymmetry to better capture directed values.  
- Separate vs. joint training: Sep avoids interference but requires two phases; joint is simpler but reported to degrade performance.  
- IQL hyperparameters (τ, γ): τ controls expectile (bias‑variance); γ affects effective planning horizon.

**Failure signatures**  
- Joint training leads to unstable or poor accuracy.  
- Poor dataset coverage (e.g., rarely crossing door) causes inaccurate value estimates.  
- Incorrect quasi‑distance implementation yields no improvement over Euclidean.  
- Over‑regularization (e.g., VCReg mis‑configured) harms representation quality.

**First experiments**  
1. Replicate baseline JEPA with VCReg/EMA and report prediction‑only planning accuracy.  
2. Implement VF (Euclidean) with separate training and measure improvement.  
3. Implement VF quasi with separate training and compare against VF and baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Hierarchical representations**: Could a hierarchy of representation spaces better capture long‑range dependencies and improve planning for distant state‑goal relationships?  
- **Stochastic environments**: How does the value‑guided approach (especially VF quasi) perform in non‑deterministic settings compared to prediction‑based baselines?  
- **Trajectory optimality**: What is the trade‑off between using expert trajectories versus diverse, suboptimal trajectories for training IQL‑based JEPA models?

## Limitations
- Exact state encoder architecture (layer counts, residuals, activations) is not fully specified, requiring inference from external code.  
- The quasimetric distance formulation is cited but not defined; it must be imported from Wang & Isola (2022).  
- Evaluation is limited to deterministic grid‑world visual tasks; scalability to complex, high‑dimensional or stochastic environments remains unproven.
