# Reasoning Model Is Superior LLM-Judge, Yet Suffers from Biases

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.03630
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03630
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) achieve higher judgment accuracy than standard LLMs, especially on reasoning tasks, but exhibit strong biases toward superficial response qualities; the proposed PlanJudge strategy significantly reduces these biases while preserving accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically compares Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) with standard LLMs in the role of automated judges for evaluating model responses. Experiments across multiple benchmarks show that LRMs, leveraging extended chain-of-thought reasoning, deliver superior judgment accuracy—particularly on reasoning-intensive tasks like code and mathematics—and demonstrate better instruction-following and robustness against adversarial attacks. However, LRMs inherit significant biases, preferentially scoring responses that are longer or more concrete. To mitigate this, the authors introduce PlanJudge, a two-step prompting strategy that first generates an explicit evaluation plan before execution. PlanJudge substantially reduces bias metrics (e.g., +32.5 points on BiasBench) while maintaining or improving judgment accuracy for both reasoning and non-reasoning models, all without additional training.

## Method Summary
The study conducts controlled experiments using established benchmarks (RewardBench, JudgeBench, Helpsteer2-trivial, RobustJudge, BiasBench, LLMBar) to compare paired LRMs and their non-reasoning counterparts (e.g., DeepSeek-R1 vs. DeepSeek-V3). Performance is measured across judgment accuracy, instruction-following (via Reversal Rate), robustness to prompt injection (iSDR), and susceptibility to superficial biases. The core intervention, PlanJudge, modifies the judging prompt in two phases: first, the model generates a structured evaluation plan (using heuristic, self-synthesized, or combined strategies); second, it executes the evaluation strictly adhering to that plan. All experiments are inference-only, requiring no model retraining.

## Key Results
- LRMs outperform non-reasoning LLMs on judgment accuracy, with the largest gains on reasoning-intensive domains (code/math).
- LRMs show stronger resilience to instruction misalignment attacks but remain highly vulnerable to biases favoring longer, more concrete responses.
- PlanJudge, especially the "Combined" plan variant, reduces bias scores dramatically (up to +32.5 on BiasBench) while preserving or enhancing overall accuracy across model types.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LRMs achieve superior judgment accuracy on reasoning tasks due to extended chain-of-thought (CoT) generation.
- **Mechanism:** The deliberate CoT process allows LRMs to systematically analyze logical correctness, consistency, and instructional alignment before finalizing a judgment, moving beyond superficial heuristics.
- **Core assumption:** Additional reasoning steps enable deeper evaluation of complex criteria.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states LRMs outperform on reasoning-intensive tasks; Section 2.2 Finding 1 confirms greater improvement in code/math domains.
- **Break condition:** Potential "overthinking" on simpler tasks may degrade accuracy; footnote notes DeepSeek-R1's exception on Knowledge tasks.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Despite reasoning prowess, LRMs exhibit strong biases toward superficial qualities like length and concreteness.
- **Mechanism:** The reasoning objective lacks explicit constraints to decouple from training data correlations where longer/concrete responses often equate to higher quality, allowing heuristics to persist.
- **Core assumption:** Biases stem from dataset patterns; reasoning alone does not automatically correct for them.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 2.2 Finding 4 document susceptibility; Section 3 Table 6 shows low BiasBench scores for LRMs.
- **Break condition:** Perfectly debiased training data or reasoning objectives that penalize superficial correlations could prevent this.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** PlanJudge mitigates superficial biases by enforcing explicit criteria-focused planning prior to evaluation.
- **Mechanism:** Generating a plan first externalizes and commits to primary evaluation dimensions, structuring subsequent reasoning to adhere to those criteria and avoid drift toward surface heuristics.
- **Core assumption:** The planning step makes criteria salient and leverages the model's instruction-following strength.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3 attribute bias reduction to plan execution; Table 6 shows significant BiasBench improvements with Combined PlanJudge.
- **Break condition:** Poorly formulated plans or ignored execution steps would fail; Combined variant's superiority implies plan quality matters.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Parser -> Plan Generator (Heuristic/Self-synthesized/Combined) -> Plan Execution Judge -> Output Formatter
- **Critical path:** The plan must be generated before responses A/B are seen to avoid contamination; execution phase strictly adheres to the plan.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  Heuristic plans are reliable but inflexible; self-synthesized plans adapt but risk irrelevance.  
  Detailed plans improve bias mitigation but increase token cost and latency.  
  The execution prompt must rigidly enforce plan adherence; otherwise the model may ignore it.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  Plan omits key criteria (e.g., safety in relevant domain).  
  Judge's explanation shows no reference to the plan steps.  
  No improvement on BiasBench/LLMBar after PlanJudge application.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate bias reduction: Run baseline vs. PlanJudge (Combined) on BiasBench; expect ≥+10 point gain.  
  2. Plan adherence stress test: Remove "only stick to the given plan" from Prompt B.6; measure drop in mitigation.  
  3. Domain transfer test: Apply Heuristic PlanJudge to a new domain (e.g., creative writing); evaluate if handcrafted heuristics still reduce bias.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do proprietary large reasoning models (e.g., GPT-4, Claude) compare to open-source ones in judgment accuracy, bias, and robustness?
- What are the impacts of large reasoning models on judgment consistency (across multiple evaluations) and interpretability (clarity of explanations)?
- Why does DeepSeek-R1’s "zero" training approach lead to higher hallucination rates on knowledge-centric tasks compared to other reasoning models?
- Is PlanJudge effective in mitigating other forms of evaluation biases beyond superficial qualities (e.g., positional bias, stylistic preferences)?
- How does the length of the reasoning process in LRMs affect judgment consistency and susceptibility to biases?

## Limitations
- Generalizability is limited to specific model pairs and benchmarks; future models or architectures may behave differently.
- PlanJudge's effectiveness is prompt-sensitive; real-world deployment requires careful validation.
- Bias measurement focuses on limited superficial dimensions (length, concreteness); other bias types remain unexplored.
- Potential "overthinking" on non-reasoning tasks may harm efficiency and accuracy for simpler queries.
- Exact model checkpoints and full prompt templates are not released, hindering faithful replication.

## Confidence
- Claim: LRMs achieve superior judgment accuracy on reasoning tasks.  
  Label: High. Supported by consistent improvements across multiple benchmarks and model families.
- Claim: LRMs suffer from strong superficial biases.  
  Label: High. Robust evidence from dedicated bias benchmarks (BiasBench, LLMBar).
- Claim: PlanJudge mitigates bias without compromising accuracy.  
  Label: Medium. Evidence shows improvement on bias metrics and stable accuracy, but gains vary and long-term robustness is unproven.

## Next Checks
1. Cross-architecture replication: Test PlanJudge on LRMs from different families (e.g., o1, Claude-3.5-Sonnet) and non-reasoning LLMs to confirm generalizability.
2. Plan adherence analysis: Log and correlate whether the execution phase references the generated plan with bias reduction scores to validate the mechanism.
3. Expanded bias suite: Evaluate PlanJudge on additional bias benchmarks (e.g., testing for position effects, demographic cues) to assess generalization beyond superficial qualities.
