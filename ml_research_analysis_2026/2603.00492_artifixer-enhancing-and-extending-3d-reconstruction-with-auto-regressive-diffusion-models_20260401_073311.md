# ArtiFixer: Enhancing and Extending 3D Reconstruction with Auto-Regressive Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.00492
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00492
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Primary result:** Outperforms all prior baselines by 1–3 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 sparse-view splits and nearly 3 dB on a challenging novel content generation task with large unobserved regions.

## Executive Summary
ArtiFixer addresses a key limitation of per-scene 3D reconstructions such as 3D Gaussian Splatting: poor extrapolation to under-observed areas. The method uses a two-stage pipeline. First, a bidirectional generative model is trained with an opacity-aware noise mixing strategy to maintain consistency with existing observations while enabling plausible generation in unseen regions. Second, this model is distilled into a causal auto-regressive model that can generate hundreds of temporally consistent novel views in a single pass. These views can be rendered directly or used as pseudo-supervision to improve the 3D representation. On standard benchmarks, ArtiFixer achieves significant PSNR improvements over prior work, especially in scenarios with large unobserved regions where existing methods fail.

## Method Summary
ArtiFixer begins with an initial 3D reconstruction from sparse input views (e.g., via 3D Gaussian Splatting). In stage one, a pretrained video diffusion model (Wan 2.1 T2V-14B) is fine-tuned as a bidirectional teacher. The teacher conditions on degraded renderings of the scene, camera rays (Plücker coordinates), and opacity maps. Critically, an opacity-aware mixing strategy combines the degraded rendering latent with Gaussian noise only in low-opacity regions, preserving observed content while enabling generation elsewhere. Training uses conditional flow matching. In stage two, the teacher is distilled into a causal auto-regressive student via Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD). The student uses block-causal attention and a rolling KV cache to generate arbitrarily long sequences in one pass. The generated videos either serve as direct renderings or provide pseudo-supervision to iteratively refine the 3D reconstruction.

## Key Results
- On Mip-NeRF 360, ArtiFixer outperforms all prior baselines by 1–3 dB PSNR across all sparse-view splits (3/6/9 views).
- On a challenging novel content generation task with large unobserved regions (DL3DV “holes” split), it surpasses the next-best method by nearly 3 dB PSNR.
- The approach successfully generates plausible, view-consistent content in scenarios where existing methods fail completely.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Opacity-Aware Noise Mixing
This mechanism prevents mode collapse in unobserved areas while preserving consistency with observed content. The degraded rendering latent \( z_{deg} \) is mixed with Gaussian noise via \( z_{mix} = O_z z_{deg} + (1 - O_z) \epsilon \), where \( O_z \) is a downscaled opacity map. Pixels with zero opacity start from pure noise, enabling generative capacity; observed regions retain the original rendering. The core assumption is that rendered opacity maps accurately distinguish observed from unobserved regions. Evidence: the abstract describes this strategy for consistency and generation; section 4.1 confirms it preserves consistency while retaining generative capabilities. Break condition: inaccurate opacity maps (e.g., from poor reconstruction) could inject noise into observed areas or fail to inject noise in unobserved areas, causing mode collapse or inconsistency.

### Causal Distillation
The bidirectional teacher is transformed into a causal auto-regressive model that generates long, temporally consistent videos in a single pass. Distillation uses Self-Forcing-style Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) with block-causal attention and a rolling KV cache. The core assumption is that the teacher’s multi-frame outputs contain sufficient temporal structure, and the strong conditioning (degraded rendering, reference views) mitigates error accumulation. Evidence: the abstract states the model generates hundreds of consistent views; section 4.2 notes the conditioning signals are sufficient to prevent drift. Break condition: extremely sparse reference views could weaken conditioning, leading to exposure bias or drift over long sequences.

### Strong 3D Conditioning
Even a degraded 3D reconstruction provides a dense, view-consistent signal that grounds the generative process. This explicit prior simplifies distillation, reduces the training-inference gap, and suppresses hallucinations. The core assumption is that the degraded 3D contains enough valid scene information (geometry, appearance) to guide generation. Evidence: the introduction highlights that the explicit 3D representation grounds generation and mitigates drift; section 4.2 notes that conditioning allows fewer denoising steps. Break condition: catastrophically incorrect initial 3D (e.g., wrong camera poses, gross geometry errors) could mislead the model and propagate errors.

## Foundational Learning
- **Flow Matching for Generative Modeling:** Needed because the bidirectional teacher is trained via conditional flow matching to learn a deterministic vector field from degraded renderings to clean outputs. This continuous-time transport framework differs from score-based diffusion by directly regressing the flow between distributions. *Quick check:* How does flow matching differ from score-based diffusion in terms of the training objective and generative path?
- **3D Gaussian Splatting and Opacity Rendering:** Central because the opacity map \( O \) is derived from Gaussian opacities and determines which pixels are considered observed. This map drives the opacity-aware noise mixing. *Quick check:* In 3DGS rendering, what does a pixel’s final alpha value represent, and how is it computed from Gaussian primitives?
- **Auto-regressive Video Generation with KV Caching:** Essential for the causal student to efficiently generate long sequences. Block-causal attention ensures each frame only sees past frames, while KV caching avoids recomputing key-value states for previous frames, scaling to hundreds of steps. *Quick check:* In a transformer-based auto-regressive video model, what is the purpose of the block-causal mask, and how does KV caching reduce computational cost during rollout?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
1. Pretrained Text-to-Video Teacher (Wan 2.1 T2V-14B): Frozen VAE and text encoder; finetuned transformer blocks.
2. Modality Injection Module: Linear layers \( f_r, f_o \) for camera rays \( R \) and opacity \( O \) added after self-attention in each block.
3. Reference View Encoder: Patchified reference views injected via cross-attention with PRoPe relative camera conditioning and zero-initialized \( K_n, V_n \).
4. Opacity Mixer: Downscales and max-pools opacity to \( O_z \) to mix \( z_{deg} \) and noise \( \epsilon \).
5. Causal Student: Same architecture as teacher but with block-causal mask; trained via DMD for 4-step generation.
6. 3D Distillation Module: Uses student outputs as pseudo-supervision to update the 3DGS representation (post-processing step).

**Critical path:**
1. Train bidirectional teacher with opacity mixing on paired reconstruction–ground-truth data (Algorithm 1 for sparse camera sampling).
2. Initialize causal student from teacher weights; apply Diffusion Forcing-style noise perturbation per frame during rollout.
3. Perform autoregressive rollout with KV caching; apply DMD to train student for 4-step generation.
4. For ArtiFixer3D, generate a long video and reconstruct 3DGS from all frames. For ArtiFixer3D+, apply the student again to the new 3DGS renderings to further refine.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Consistency vs. Generative Capacity:** Direct input (\( z_{mix} = z_{deg} \)) maximizes consistency but fails in unobserved areas; channel concatenation protects generation but introduces inconsistencies. Opacity mixing balances both.
- **Bidirectional vs. Causal Efficiency:** Bidirectional models yield higher quality but are limited to short chunks; causal models scale to arbitrary length but risk drift. The strong 3D conditioning reduces this drift.
- **Few-step vs. Many-step Generation:** DMD reduces steps from ~50 to 4, speeding up inference. Fewer steps may degrade quality in fully novel regions.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Mode collapse in unobserved areas:** Occurs if opacity mixing is omitted or fails; manifests as blurry or repeating patterns in zero-opacity regions.
- **Temporal drift in long rollouts:** Manifests as drifting geometry or flickering textures beyond the training horizon, indicating insufficient conditioning or KV cache issues.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Opacity Mixing Ablation:** Train teacher with (a) channel concatenation, (b) direct \( z_{deg} \) input, (c) opacity mixing. Evaluate PSNR/LPIPS on Mip-NeRF 360 sparse splits. Expect (c) to outperform, especially in unobserved regions.
2. **Causal Initialization Test:** Compare random initialization vs. teacher-weight initialization for the causal model. Measure convergence speed and final PSNR on a validation split; initialization should yield faster convergence and higher quality.
3. **Conditioning Dropout Analysis:** Ablate the random dropping of last \( K \) frames and reference views during training. Without dropout, the model may overfit to observed areas and fail to generate plausible content in fully unobserved regions. Validate on the DL3DV “holes” split.

## Limitations
- **Opacity map dependency:** Opacity mixing assumes accurate opacity maps from the initial 3D reconstruction; errors can inject noise into observed areas or fail to generate in unobserved ones.
- **Temporal drift risk:** Auto-regressive generation may experience drift over very long sequences despite strong conditioning.
- **Proprietary model barrier:** Reliance on a large video diffusion model (Wan 2.1 T2V-14B) limits accessibility and reproducibility.
- **Distillation quality loss:** The causal student may degrade in quality compared to the teacher, especially in fully novel regions.

## Confidence
- **Overall benchmark SOTA (1–3dB PSNR gains):** High – consistent improvements across Mip-NeRF 360 and DL3DV splits.
- **Opacity mixing prevents mode collapse while preserving consistency:** Medium – supported by ablations but break condition (opacity map inaccuracy) remains plausible.
- **Causal model generates hundreds of temporally consistent frames in one pass:** Medium – validated up to a few hundred frames; longer-term scaling not fully explored.

## Next Checks
