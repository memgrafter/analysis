# RECAP: Local Hebbian Prototype Learning as a Self-Organizing Readout for Reservoir Dynamics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.06639
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06639
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: RECAP achieves a relative mean corruption error (mCE) of 34.1% on MNIST-C, substantially lower than MLP (52.1%) and ESN with ridge regression (55.0%).

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the lack of robustness in modern machine learning systems to common data corruptions when trained solely on clean data. It introduces RECAP, a bio-inspired method that couples an untrained reservoir computing dynamics with a self-organizing Hebbian prototype readout. The reservoir transforms inputs into stable population codes, which are discretized into co-activation masks. Class-specific prototypes are incrementally updated via a local potentiation-decay rule, and classification is performed by template matching. Evaluated on MNIST-C, RECAP achieves a relative mean corruption error (mCE) of 34.1%, substantially lower than MLP (52.1%) and ESN with ridge regression (55.0%), demonstrating strong zero-shot robustness without exposure to corrupted samples. This robustness comes at the cost of higher clean error, highlighting a trade-off.

## Method Summary
RECAP processes an input image by feeding it into a fixed, randomly initialized leaky echo-state network (ESN) for T=60 timesteps and averaging the states to obtain a stable high-dimensional feature vector. This vector is discretized into K=8 uniform activation levels (bins) over the expected range [-1,1]. A binary co-activation mask M is then constructed where M_ij=1 if two distinct reservoir units fall into the same bin, and zero on the diagonal. For each class, a continuous prototype matrix S^(c) is incrementally updated during training using a local Hebbian rule: entries corresponding to co-activated pairs are incremented (η+=0.6) and clipped to [0,1]; all other entries are multiplicatively decayed (η−=0.96). After training, S^(c) is binarized by thresholding at the (1-p) quantile of its off-diagonal values (p=0.3 sparsity) to obtain a sparse binary template P^(c). During inference, the test mask M(u) is compared to each P^(c) via Frobenius inner product, and the class with the highest score is predicted.

## Key Results
- RECAP achieves a relative mean corruption error (mCE) of 34.1% on MNIST-C.
- This substantially outperforms MLP (52.1%) and ESN with ridge regression (55.0%) in zero-shot robustness.
- The method exhibits a trade-off with higher clean error compared to standard models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RECAP's robustness emerges from three interconnected mechanisms:

1. **Reservoir transformation into stable population codes**: The untrained leaky ESN processes each static input over multiple timesteps. Its recurrent dynamics (with spectral radius near 1) nonlinearly expand the input into a high-dimensional trajectory; time-averaging yields a stable feature vector that preserves class-discriminative structure while being robust to perturbations. Evidence: abstract, Section 3.2 (equations), and related work on RC dynamics. Break condition: spectral radius too low (<1) loses expressive power; too high (>~1.2) induces chaos and unstable averages.

2. **Discretization and co-activation masks**: The continuous reservoir activations are quantized into K uniform bins. A binary mask indicates pairs of units sharing the same bin. This relational representation is insensitive to small corruptions because perturbations may shift activations but often preserve bin assignments. Evidence: abstract, Section 3.3, Section 6 (explicit statement). Break condition: bin edges misaligned with activation distribution; K too small loses granularity, K too large regains sensitivity.

3. **Local Hebbian prototype learning**: For each class, a continuous prototype matrix is updated via a differential potentiation-decay rule: co-activated pairs in incoming masks are incremented and clipped, while others are multiplicatively decayed. This amplifies consistent in-class relations and suppresses noise/spurious patterns, yielding sparse binary templates after thresholding. Evidence: abstract, Section 3.4, Section 6. Break condition: learning rates unbalanced (η+ too low/η- too high leads to decay; η+ too high/η- too low causes saturation); overlapping class distributions may limit discriminativity.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input → Flatten → Reservoir (leaky ESN, fixed) → Time-average (¯x) → Discretizer (K bins) → Mask Builder (M) → Training: Prototype Learner (per-class S^(c) with Hebbian update) → Post-training Prototype Binarizer → Binary prototypes P^(c); Inference: same M → Matcher (Frobenius overlap with all P^(c)) → Prediction.

**Critical path**: During inference, an image is flattened, fed into the reservoir for T steps, its states averaged to get ¯x, discretized to get bin assignments, used to construct M, and then M is compared to each P^(c) to select the class with highest overlap.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Reservoir size N_r: Larger sizes increase discriminative capacity but linearly increase mask size (O(N_r²)) and memory for prototypes (C·N_r²).
- Discretization levels K: Small K promotes robustness but loses granularity; large K approaches continuous sensitivity.
- Prototype sparsity p: Lower p yields sparser templates, higher precision but risk missing patterns; higher p increases recall but may include noise.
- Potentiation/decay rates (η+, η_-): Control speed of template formation vs. stability; must balance to avoid saturation or extinction.

**Failure signatures**:
- All prototypes nearly identical: indicates non-discriminative reservoir outputs or faulty Hebbian dynamics.
- Very high clean error with low corruption error: expected trade-off, but extreme (>50% on MNIST) suggests over-sparsification (p too low) or excessive discretization (K too small).
- Prototype matrices saturated (all ~1) or vanished (all ~0): points to inappropriate η+/η- values or incorrect update implementation.
- Persistent noise sensitivity: likely misaligned discretization bins relative to reservoir activation distribution.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Clean baseline validation: Train RECAP on clean MNIST, report clean test error. Should be > classical MLP but not catastrophic.
2. Discretization sensitivity ablation: Vary K ∈ {2,4,8,16,32}, plot clean error vs. relative mCE to find sweet spot.
3. Hebbian rule necessity test: Replace Eq. (7) with a simple median-threshold prototype (e.g., P^(c)_ij = 1 if median over class masks > τ). Compare robustness; a significant drop confirms the incremental potentiation-decay dynamics' contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does RECAP maintain robustness under adversarial perturbations or domain shifts? The authors state they leave adversarial robustness and domain shift to future work.
2. Can the online prototype update mechanism support effective continual learning without catastrophic forgetting? The authors note they did not test sequential task learning or catastrophic forgetting protocols.
3. How does RECAP's robustness and accuracy scale to more complex image datasets beyond MNIST? The study is restricted to MNIST-C, and the authors acknowledge that robustness behavior may differ on complex natural images.
4. To what extent can the robustness-accuracy trade-off be mitigated by adjusting the discretization or prototype binarization? The authors attribute the trade-off to discretization and binarization discarding information but did not explore alternative configurations.
5. What is the impact of reservoir hyperparameters (size, spectral radius, sparsity) on RECAP's robustness-accuracy trade-off and computational efficiency? The reservoir settings followed standard ESN practices, and exhaustive ablations remain unexplored.

## Limitations
- Incomplete specification of reservoir initialization (distributions for W and W_in, sparsity pattern) and input normalization scaling hinders exact reproducibility.
- Missing details on the MNIST-C corruption pipeline and AlexNet baseline computations prevent faithful calculation of relative mCE.
- Unspecified training schedule (number of epochs) may affect prototype convergence and performance.

## Confidence
- Reservoir transformation robustness: Medium (sensitive to initialization and spectral radius scaling).
- Discretization robustness: Medium (depends on bin alignment with activation distribution).
- Hebbian prototype learning: Medium (hyperparameter sensitivity untested).
