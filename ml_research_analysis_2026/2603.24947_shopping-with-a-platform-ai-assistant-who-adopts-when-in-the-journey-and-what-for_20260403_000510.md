# Shopping with a Platform AI Assistant: Who Adopts, When in the Journey, and What For
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24947
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24947
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Platform-embedded AI shopping assistants see highest adoption among older and female users, are used mid-journey well before purchase, and serve exploratory “attraction” queries that align with downstream purchase categories.

## Executive Summary
This study analyzes 31 million users of Ctrip’s LLM-based AI assistant “Wendao” to understand who adopts it, when in the shopping journey it is used, and for what purpose. Adoption is highest among older consumers (10.7% for ages 50+ vs. 4.6% for under 24), female users, and highly engaged existing users—reversing the typical younger, male-dominated profile of general-purpose AI tools. AI chat appears in the same mid-journey phase as traditional keyword search and well before purchase; when both are used, they are interleaved in 53% of journeys. Attraction-type queries dominate chat intent (42% of requests), and chat intent systematically aligns with subsequent purchase categories, suggesting the assistant is primarily used for exploratory, hard-to-keyword discovery rather than as a direct search substitute.

## Method Summary
The analysis uses linked log data from 31 million Ctrip users over a 35-day period in 2025, combining demographics, timestamped session activity (logins, searches, clicks), full chat transcripts with platform-classified intent, and transaction records. The core unit of analysis is the “journey,” constructed by stitching adjacent login sessions with <48-hour gaps and splitting journeys between consecutive order-containing sessions with >4-hour gaps. No model training is performed; instead, the study provides descriptive statistics on adoption rates, event timing within normalized journeys, sequence patterns of chat and search, and alignment between chat intent and purchase categories. Marginal effects are estimated via linear/logit/probit models, and variable importance is assessed with random forests.

## Key Results
- **Demographics reversal:** Adoption increases with age (peaking at 50+ at 10.7%) and is higher among females (7.1% vs. 6.0% for males), contrasting with general-purpose AI trends.
- **Mid-journey, interleaved usage:** AI chat and search both cluster around the 40–60% journey progress mark; in journeys with both, 53% show interleaved (chat-before/-after/-between) patterns rather than sequential use.
- **Exploratory discovery focus:** “Attraction” queries dominate chat intent (42%), and chat intent strongly predicts the category of subsequent orders (e.g., attraction chat → vacation orders), indicating use for vague, hard-to-express needs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper proposes that the assistant primarily reduces search costs for users less comfortable with structured keyword search—helping explain higher adoption among older and female users. However, this mechanism is not tested causally; the observational design can only show correlation between demographics, engagement, and adoption. The observed alignment between chat intent and purchase categories may reflect pre-existing user intent rather than AI-driven persuasion, and interleaving patterns could stem from journey complexity or user traits rather than a direct effect of AI availability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What causal mechanism explains the higher adoption of platform-embedded AI among older consumers and female users? The paper proposes reduced search costs but cannot test causality; randomized experiments varying feature visibility or measuring search comfort are needed.
- **Open Question 2:** Does AI access causally alter keyword search behavior, and how does interleaving impact conversion? The descriptive interleaving pattern could reflect selection or journey complexity; field experiments with randomized AI access and pre-post within-user comparisons are required.
- **Open Question 3:** Does the alignment between chat intent and purchase categories imply causal influence on purchase composition? Temporal correlation may reflect pre-existing intent; experiments steering chat intent or instrumental variable approaches are necessary to establish causality.

## Limitations
- Chat intent classification relies on an opaque internal algorithm with no labeling details, risking misclassification bias.
- The definition of “hotel search” events is unspecified (e.g., search box vs. clicks vs. navigation), creating ambiguity in key metrics.
- Handling of multi-device or concurrent sessions is unclear, potentially distorting user-level adoption and journey stitching.
