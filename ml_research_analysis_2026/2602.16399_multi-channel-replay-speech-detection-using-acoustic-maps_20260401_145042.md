# Multi-Channel Replay Speech Detection using Acoustic Maps
**arXiv ID:** 2602.16399  
**Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16399  
**Reference count:** 19  
**Primary result:** A lightweight CNN (~6k parameters) classifies beamformed acoustic maps, achieving competitive EERs (e.g., 10.1% on D3) but shows limited robustness to unseen acoustic environments (EER ~40-50%).

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles replay attack detection for voice assistants using multi-channel recordings. It introduces *acoustic maps*—3-D tensors encoding directional energy distributions derived from beamforming over a spherical grid—as a compact, physically interpretable spatial representation. A lightweight convolutional neural network classifies these maps. Evaluated on the ReMASC dataset, the method yields competitive error rates for matched conditions (in-device testing), with performance improving with larger microphone arrays. However, environment-independent testing reveals a substantial drop in accuracy, highlighting limited generalization to novel acoustic scenes despite the model's efficiency.

## Method Summary
The method transforms multi-channel audio into an *acoustic map*: a (frequency-band × azimuth × elevation) tensor. For each time-frequency bin, a beamformer (e.g., delay-and-sum) computes pseudo-power across a discrete spatial grid. These responses are averaged over time and grouped into four fixed frequency bands. The resulting 3-D map captures the spatial distribution of sound energy. A lightweight CNN with depthwise separable convolutions processes this map. The architecture minimizes parameters (~6k) to reduce overfitting on the modest ReMASC dataset, using MixUp augmentation and categorical cross-entropy loss. Separate models are trained per microphone array device.

## Key Results
*   Achieves competitive Equal Error Rates (EERs) in environment-dependent, per-device testing (e.g., 10.1% on the 7-mic D4 array).
*   Performance strongly correlates with array size; smaller arrays (2, 4 mics) yield significantly higher EERs (19–22%).
*   Generalization to unseen acoustic environments is poor; environment-independent testing results in EERs of ~40–50%, indicating limited robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Acoustic maps provide a spatial feature representation that distinguishes genuine human speech from loudspeaker replay by encoding directional energy distributions.
*   **Mechanism:** Multi-channel signals are beamformed (e.g., delay-and-sum) over a discrete azimuth/elevation grid. The pseudo-power response per grid point is averaged over time and frequency bands, yielding a tensor that captures how sound energy distributes across space. The hypothesis is that human speech radiation patterns differ from loudspeaker directivity, and these differences persist after room propagation.
*   **Core assumption:** The directional signature of a human talker versus a loudspeaker is measurably distinct at the array and robust enough for a simple beamformer to capture.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "Acoustic maps encode directional energy distributions that reflect physical differences between human speech radiation and loudspeaker-based replay."
    *   [section III.A] Details beamforming equations (Eq. 6, 8) and states maps "capture array-manifold structure, direct-path cues, and early reflections."
*   **Break condition:** Environment-independent EERs jump to ~40-50% (Table III), indicating fixed beamforming and frequency band grouping fail to generalize where room acoustics alter directivity cues.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** A lightweight CNN with depthwise separable convolutions can effectively classify acoustic maps with high efficiency, suitable for data-limited scenarios.
*   **Mechanism:** The CNN processes the 3-D map through blocks of (depthwise separable conv → batch norm → ELU → max pooling), reducing spatial resolution while increasing channel depth. A shallow MLP classifier follows. This design minimizes parameters (~6k) to mitigate overfitting on the limited dataset.
*   **Core assumption:** Local spatial patterns in the map (e.g., energy lobes) are salient for detection and can be captured by small kernels without large receptive fields.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract] "A lightweight convolutional neural network... with approximately 6k trainable parameters."
    *   [section III.B] Describes architecture and parameter count rationale ("Early spatial downsampling... limiting sensitivity to noise").
*   **Break condition:** Does not consistently outperform larger baselines (e.g., M-ALRAD on D1, D2, D4 in Table I), suggesting capacity limits accuracy when spatial cues are weak.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Acoustic map effectiveness is contingent on microphone array geometry (more mics improve performance) and beamformer choice (delay-and-sum offers stable results).
*   **Mechanism:** A larger array provides denser spatial sampling, yielding finer beamformer resolution and more reliable directional cues. The beamformer determines which spatial information is emphasized; delay-and-sum is robust but simplistic, while MVDR/SRP-PHAT may better suppress interference but are sensitive to noise/covariance estimation.
*   **Core assumption:** Spatial resolution of the map directly impacts separability of genuine/replay patterns, and simple beamformers can suffice if their biases do not discard critical cues.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [section IV.B] "Detection accuracy is clearly influenced by the number of microphones... arrays with more microphones (D3 and D4) consistently yield lower EERs."
    *   [section IV.C] Table II shows delay-and-sum is competitive; MVDR/SRP-PHAT are device-dependent and sometimes worse.
*   **Break condition:** Performance degrades on small arrays (D1, D2: EER 19–22%); SRP-PHAT on D1 yields 31.4% EER, indicating insufficient resolution or corrupted cues.

    *   **Why needed here:** Drastically reduce CNN parameter count (vs. standard conv) while processing spatial patterns in the acoustic map, enabling the ~6k-parameter target.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Multi-channel audio → Per-channel STFT → Beamforming loop over (azimuth, elevation) grid → Average over time & frequency bands → 3-D acoustic map tensor (bands, azimuth, elevation) → CNN (depthwise separable conv blocks → 1x1 conv → flatten → MLP) → Softmax output (genuine/replay)
*   **Critical path:** STFT → beamforming computation over grid → tensor formation → CNN → classification loss. Beamforming is the computational bottleneck (O(F·A·E·N) per frame).
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Grid resolution: Coarser grids reduce computation but risk losing fine directivity details.
    *   Frequency bands: Fixed bands (e.g., 100-500Hz) are simple but may not align with optimal discriminative regions; learning band selection is future work.
    *   Beamformer: Delay-and-sum is fast/stable but not adaptive; MVDR/SRP-PHAT may capture different cues but are noisier/more complex.
    *   Model size: ~6k parameters limit capacity but risk underfitting; larger baselines (~300k) sometimes perform better.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   High EER (>40%) in environment-independent test, especially for D1/D2.
    *   Inconsistent beamformer results: SRP-PHAT significantly worse than delay-and-sum on same device.
    *   Parameter starvation: Persistent underperformance vs. larger models on arrays with good spatial sampling (D3).
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  Replicate acoustic map computation on a ReMASC multi-channel file using delay-and-sum. Visualize maps; verify distinct directional patterns (e.g., front-focused for genuine).
    2.  Train the proposed CNN on ReMASC D3 with official splits. Confirm reported EER ~10% (±2.8) over 5 runs. Ablate MixUp to measure variance impact.
    3.  Perform environment-independent test for D3: train on environments A/B/C, test on D. Expect EER ~43%. Repeat for other hold-out environments; compare to Table III.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question 1:** How can a learning-based frequency band selector be designed and integrated into the acoustic map pipeline to better capture directivity cues?
    *   **Basis:** [explicit] Conclusion proposes "learning-based frequency band selector" as future work.
    *   **Why unresolved:** Fixed bands may not optimally represent directional energy differences, especially across varying acoustics.
    *   **Evidence to resolve:** Implement a differentiable band selection module within beamforming; evaluate EER impact on ReMASC (dependent/independent).
*   **Question 2:** Which specific environmental factors (reverberation, noise, geometry) cause acoustic maps to lose discriminative power in unseen conditions, and how can the representation be made more invariant?
    *   **Basis:** [inferred] Section IV.D attributes poor generalization to "fixed frequency bands and static beamforming."
    *   **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the limitation but does not isolate disruptive factors or propose adaptive spatial features.
    *   **Evidence to resolve:** Controlled synthetic experiments varying environmental parameters; measure acoustic map statistics; develop adaptive beamforming/normalization.
*   **Question 3:** Can acoustic maps be extended to incorporate spectral features (e.g., CQCCs) in a unified model to leverage both spatial and spectral cues without significant complexity increase?
    *   **Basis:** [inferred] Discussion notes maps "do not fully replace carefully designed spectral features" and prior work uses spectral cues.
    *   **Why unresolved:** The work focuses solely on spatial features; potential synergy with spectral features is unexplored.
    *   **Evidence to resolve:** Design a multimodal fusing acoustic maps and spectral features (e.g., as extra channels); compare against single-modality baselines on ReMASC.

## Limitations
*   Unknown STFT parameters (window, hop size) and exact frequency band bin mapping—critical for beamformer computation—are unreported, blocking faithful replication.
*   Training protocol details (optimizer settings, learning rate schedule, MixUp implementation) are omitted, introducing variance in reproduction.
*   The cause of poor environment-independent generalization is not isolated; it could stem from fixed beamforming, band grouping, or model capacity.

## Confidence
*   **Claim:** Acoustic maps from fixed beamforming enable lightweight replay detection.
    *   **Confidence:** Medium. Mechanically plausible and competitive in-device, but severe generalization collapse undermines robustness claim.
*   **Claim:** The 6k-parameter CNN architecture is efficient and as-specified.
    *   **Confidence:** High. Fully described, parameter count verified, and compared to larger baselines.
*   **Claim:** Performance depends on array geometry (more mics → better results).
    *   **Confidence:** High. Clear performance gradient across D1–D4 in experiments.
