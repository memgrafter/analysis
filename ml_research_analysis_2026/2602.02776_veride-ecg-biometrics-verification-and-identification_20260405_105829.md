# VerIde ECG Biometrics: Verification and Identification
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02776
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02776
- **Reference Count:** 27
- **Primary Result:** Large-scale ECG biometric verification achieves TAR=0.908 at FAR=1e-3 and EER=2.53% using ArcFace on raw waveforms with consistent normalization.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the absence of large-scale, operationally relevant benchmarks for ECG biometrics. It presents a comprehensive evaluation of verification (1:1) and identification (1:N) tasks using ArcFace deep learning models. The study leverages a derived test set of approximately 23,000 identities from a clinical ECG database, comparing performance on two input representations: tabular fiducial features and raw 12-lead waveforms. Key findings demonstrate a substantial performance gain when moving from tabular features to raw waveforms, particularly when employing dataset-wide (`train_global`) normalization instead of per-sample normalization. For the large-scale test, verification reaches a True Accept Rate of 90.8% at a False Accept Rate of 0.1%, with an Equal Error Rate of 2.53%. Closed-set identification yields a Rank-1 accuracy of 81.2%. A proposed two-stage open-set identification pipeline (shortlist retrieval followed by score re-ranking) achieves a Detection and Identification Rate up to 97.6% at FAR=0.1%. The work establishes a robust benchmark, confirming a strong and persistent individually distinctive signal in ECGs suitable for privacy and operational security assessments.

## Method Summary
The research addresses ECG biometric verification and identification. Inputs are two representations from a filtered clinical database: (1) a 13-dimensional vector of fiducial features (e.g., ventricular rate, PR interval), and (2) raw 12-lead, 10-second waveforms sampled at 1 kHz. The dataset is derived from ~1M ECGs, filtered to 164,440 exams from 53,079 patients, with strict patient-disjoint splits for training, validation, and a large-scale test set of ~23k identities and ~56k exams. For tabular data, a Siamese MLP with contrastive/triplet loss is used. For waveforms, a custom 1D Convolutional Neural Network backbone is trained with the ArcFace loss (additive angular margin) in a multi-class classification framework; post-training, only the backbone serves as an embedding extractor, producing L2-normalized 512- or 1024-dimensional vectors. Normalization is a critical factor: `train_global` (using mean/std from the entire training set) consistently outperforms `per_sample` normalization. Evaluation uses standard operational metrics: verification (TAR@FAR, EER), closed-set identification (Rank@K), and open-set identification via a two-stage pipeline (top-K shortlist by cosine similarity, then re-ranking with score aggregation like best-of-K or s-norm, with thresholds calibrated on held-out impostors to report DIR@FAR).

## Key Results
- **Verification:** TAR=0.908 at FAR=1e-3; EER=2.53% (waveform, `train_global` normalization).
- **Closed-set Identification:** Rank@1=0.812 on the ~23k identity test set.
- **Open-set Identification:** Two-stage pipeline (shortlist + re-ranking) achieves DIR@FAR up to 0.976 at FAR=1e-3.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The persistent high accuracy across large-scale verification and identification tasks confirms that ECGs contain a strong, individually distinctive pattern robustly captured by deep learning. The significant performance leap from tabular to raw waveform inputs indicates that the full temporal and spectral information in the signal—beyond conventional clinical measurements—is crucial for biometric discrimination. The effectiveness of the `train_global` normalization suggests that consistent, population-scale feature scaling stabilizes learning and exposes inter-subject variations more effectively than signal-level normalization. The two-stage open-set pipeline successfully mitigates the computational cost of 1:N search in massive galleries while maintaining high accuracy through re-ranking with aggregated similarity scores.

## Foundational Learning
- **ECG Biometric Uniqueness:** The premise that ECG waveforms have sufficient inter-subject variation and intra-subject stability to serve as a biometric trait. *Why needed:* Establishes the fundamental validity of the research problem. *Quick check:* Review prior literature on ECG variability due to anatomy, physiology, and acquisition.
- **ArcFace Loss:** An angular margin-based loss for deep face recognition, adapted here to enforce discriminative embedding space structure via additive angular penalty. *Why needed:* Provides state-of-the-art performance on metric learning tasks by maximizing inter-class angular separation. *Quick check:* Understand the loss formula: `L = -cos(θ + m) / s` where `θ` is the angle between weight and embedding, `m` is margin, `s` is scale.
- **Patient-Disjoint Data Splitting:** A rigorous protocol ensuring no patient's ECG exams appear in more than one set (train/val/test). *Why needed:* Prevents data leakage and ensures performance metrics reflect true generalization to unseen identities. *Quick check:* Audit patient IDs across all dataset splits; ensure mutual exclusivity.
- **Normalization Strategy Impact:** The choice between `per_sample` (z-score per waveform) and `train_global` (z-score using training set statistics) normalization. *Why needed:* Affects model convergence and final performance by altering the distribution and relative scaling of features. *Quick check:* Compare performance results across normalization conditions in the paper's tables.
- **Open-set Identification Pipeline:** A search strategy for 1:N matching where the gallery contains unknown (impostor) identities. *Why needed:* Real-world biometric systems must reject enrolled subjects not present in the gallery, necessitating different evaluation metrics (DIR@FAR) than closed-set (Rank@K). *Quick check:* Understand the shortlist (top-K by initial score) and re-ranking (aggregated score) stages.
- **Large-Scale Benchmarking:** Evaluation on a test set of ~23k identities. *Why needed:* Moves beyond small, academic datasets to assess scalability and operational relevance, providing a meaningful benchmark for privacy and performance trade-offs. *Quick check:* Compare test set size and identity count against prior ECG biometric studies.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Raw Waveform -> Preprocessing (filtering, resampling) -> `train_global` Normalization -> 1D CNN Backbone (16 Conv1d blocks, pooling) -> Linear Projection -> L2 Normalization -> Embedding Vector -> Cosine Similarity -> Verification/Identification Decision.

**Critical Path:** Patient-disjoint dataset split -> Compute `train_global` mean/std on training set -> Apply normalization to all splits -> Train 1D CNN backbone with ArcFace loss -> Extract normalized embeddings -> Evaluate all-vs-all similarities for verification/identification.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- *Tabular vs. Waveform:* Tabular features (clinical parameters) are compact and efficient but discard temporal dynamics, leading to significantly lower accuracy. Waveforms capture full signal information but require heavier CNN models and more computation.
- *Normalization:* `train_global` preserves relative amplitude differences across the population but requires stable training set statistics. `per_sample` is simpler but removes absolute amplitude information, harming performance.
- *Open-set Pipeline:* A two-stage approach (shortlist + re-ranking) balances accuracy and computational cost for massive galleries. A larger shortlist `K` improves recall but increases re-ranking computation; aggregation method (best-of-K vs. top-K mean vs. s-norm) trades off robustness to noise.

**Failure Signatures:**
- **Data Leakage:** Overly optimistic metrics if any patient appears in both training and test sets. Diagnose by verifying complete patient ID disjointness across splits and checking exam-per-patient counts match reported tables.
- **Normalization Mismatch:** Performance drop if test set normalization does not use training set statistics (e.g., accidentally using per-test-sample stats). Diagnose by auditing normalization code to ensure `train_global` pipeline.
- **Open-set Pipeline Misconfiguration:** Incorrect DIR@FAR if shortlist size `K`, re-ranking aggregation, or threshold calibration (on a separate impostor set) deviates from the described procedure. Diagnose by replicating the exact two-stage flow and validating calibration.

**First Three Experiments to Run:**
1. **Normalization Ablation Study:** Train and evaluate waveform models using both `per_sample` and `train_global` normalization to confirm the reported performance delta (~5-10% TAR gain).
2. **Tabular vs. Waveform Comparison:** Train the specified tabular ArcFace (MLP) and waveform ArcFace (1D CNN) on identical train/val splits and compare verification EER and Rank@1 on the same test set.
3. **Open-set Pipeline Sanity Check:** On a held-out subset of test embeddings, implement the two-stage pipeline (e.g., K=20 shortlist, top-K mean re-ranking). Calibrate the decision threshold on a separate set of impostor scores to achieve FAR=1e-3, then compute DIR on known probes to match the paper's reported value.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not specify several critical implementation details, hindering exact replication:
- **ArcFace Hyperparameters:** Exact values for scale `s`, margin `m`, learning rate schedule, batch size, optimizer, and number of training epochs.
- **Waveform Preprocessing Pipeline:** Specific steps for baseline wander removal, powerline interference filtering, handling of missing leads, and segment extraction beyond the stated 10-second, 12-lead format.
- **Open-set Re-ranking Details:** Precise formulation of score aggregation methods (best-of-K, top-K mean, s-norm) and the threshold calibration protocol for DIR@FAR reporting.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter Opacity:** The unreported ArcFace training regimen (LR, batch size, margin, epochs) introduces uncertainty in reproducing the exact model convergence and final performance.
- **Preprocessing Ambiguity:** Vague description of waveform signal processing steps prevents isolating the contribution of the model architecture versus signal cleaning.
- **Open-set Pipeline Gaps:** Insufficient detail on re-ranking aggregation and threshold calibration impedes faithful replication of the reported DIR@FAR results.
