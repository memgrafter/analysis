# Adaptive Moments are Surprisingly Effective for Plug-and-Play Diffusion Sampling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.16797
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16797
- **Reference count**: 36
- **Primary result**: AdamDPS and AdamCG stabilize guidance gradients in plug-and-play diffusion sampling, achieving state-of-the-art improvements in image restoration (e.g., 9.1% LPIPS reduction on ImageNet 16× super-resolution) and class-conditional generation (+9.86 percentage points accuracy on CIFAR-10) with negligible computational overhead.

## Executive Summary
Plug-and-play diffusion sampling enables solving inverse problems by guiding a pre-trained diffusion model with task-specific gradients. However, the computed guidance gradients are inherently noisy, which degrades performance, especially under challenging conditions. This paper introduces a simple yet effective stabilization: applying the adaptive moment estimation (Adam) optimizer to the guidance gradients across diffusion timesteps. By maintaining exponential moving averages of the first and second moments, AdamDPS and AdamCG produce smoother updates, leading to more stable convergence and higher-quality samples. The methods achieve state-of-the-art results on a range of tasks including super-resolution, deblurring, inpainting, and class-conditional generation, with consistent gains over more complex baselines and minimal additional compute.

## Method Summary
The approach builds on plug-and-play diffusion sampling (e.g., Diffusion Posterior Sampling, DPS, and Classifier Guidance, CG). At each diffusion timestep \(t\), a guidance loss \(L\) is evaluated using a differentiable guidance model (e.g., a classifier or a measurement operator) on an estimate of the clean image \(x_0|t\) (for DPS) or directly on the noisy latent \(x_t\) with a time‑aware classifier (for CG). The raw gradient \(g_t = -\nabla L\) is then used to update the sample with a strength \(\rho\). AdamDPS and AdamCG modify this step by maintaining two buffers across timesteps: the first moment \(m\) and the second moment \(v\). After each \(g_t\) is computed, the buffers are updated:  
\(m \gets \beta_1 m + (1-\beta_1) g_t\),  
\(v \gets \beta_2 v + (1-\beta_2) g_t^2\),  
with a step counter \(k\) and bias‑correction \( \hat{m}=m/(1-\beta_1^k)\), \( \hat{v}=v/(1-\beta_2^k)\). The stabilized gradient is \(\hat{g} = \hat{m} / (\sqrt{\hat{v}} + \epsilon)\).  
- For **AdamDPS**: \(x_{t-1}\) is obtained by the standard diffusion reverse step conditioned on \(x_t + \rho \hat{g}\).  
- For **AdamCG**: the diffusion reverse step is adjusted by injecting \(\rho \hat{g} \sigma_t^2\) into the predicted \(x_0|t\).  
All other hyperparameters (noise schedule, stochasticity, etc.) remain identical to the baseline DPS/CG implementations. The Adam buffers span the entire sampling process, effectively smoothing the guidance signal.

## Key Results
- **ImageNet 16× super-resolution**: AdamDPS reduces LPIPS by 0.03 (9.1%) compared to DPS.
- **CIFAR-10 class-conditional generation**: AdamDPS improves held‑out classifier accuracy by 9.86 percentage points over DPS.
- Adam variants consistently outperform more complex methods (e.g., TFG) as task difficulty increases, while adding negligible computational overhead.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Adam optimizer’s exponential moving averages act as a low‑pass filter on the guidance gradients, reducing high‑frequency noise that arises from approximating \(x_0\) from a noisy latent and from the stochastic nature of diffusion. The first moment (\(m\)) tracks the direction of the gradient, smoothing out fluctuations; the second moment (\(v\)) normalizes the step size per parameter, preventing large updates when gradients are momentarily high. Bias correction ensures unbiased estimates early in the process. Together, these mechanisms yield a Stabilized gradient that is less sensitive to step‑to‑step variance, enabling more reliable convergence and higher sample quality, particularly in hard inverse problems where the raw guidance is most erratic.

## Foundational Learning
Key concepts required to understand and implement the paper:

- **Plug‑and‑play diffusion sampling**: Integrates arbitrary differentiable guidance into a pre‑trained diffusion model without retraining. *Why needed*: Forms the baseline that suffers from noisy gradients. *Quick check*: Derive the DPS update equation for \(x_{t}\) given a reconstruction loss \( \|A x_0 - y\|^2 \).
- **Diffusion reverse process (DDPM/VE/EDM)**: The stochastic or deterministic update that transforms \(x_t\) to \(x_{t-1}\). *Why needed*: Provides the generative prior within which guidance is applied. *Quick check*: Write the formula for \(x_{t-1}\) using the predicted \(x_0\), noise schedule, and added noise.
- **Guidance gradient (DPS/CG)**: The gradient of a task‑specific loss with respect to either \(x_0|t\) or \(x_t\). *Why needed*: The signal that steers sampling toward the conditioning. *Quick check*: Compute \(\nabla_{x_0} \|A x_0 - y\|^2\) for a linear operator \(A\).
- **Adam optimizer**: Maintains exponential moving averages of gradient (\(m\)) and squared gradient (\(v\)) with bias correction. *Why needed*: Core mechanism to stabilize noisy guidance. *Quick check*: Implement a single‑step Adam update given \(g_t\), \(m\), \(v\), \(\beta_1\), \(\beta_2\), \(\epsilon\).
- **Bayesian optimization for hyperparameter tuning**: Automates selection of guidance strength \(\rho\). *Why needed*: Ensures fair comparison across methods by finding near‑optimal \(\rho\). *Quick check*: Outline a BO loop that proposes \(\rho\) values to minimize LPIPS on a validation set.

## Architecture Onboarding
The overall system combines a pre‑trained diffusion sampler, a task‑specific guidance model, and Adam buffers that persist across diffusion timesteps.

**Component map (plain text)**  
Pre‑trained Diffusion Model → Guidance Model (classifier/operator) → Compute raw gradient \(g_t\) → Adam buffers (\(m,v\)) → Stabilized gradient \(\hat{g}\) → Sample update (AdamDPS: \(x_t + \rho \hat{g}\); AdamCG: \(\rho \hat{g} \sigma_t^2\) into \(x_0\)) → Diffusion reverse step → \(x_{t-1}\)

**Critical path**:
1. Initialize \(x_T\) (e.g., Gaussian), \(m=0\), \(v=0\), step counter \(k=0\).
2. For \(t = T, T-1, \dots, 1\):  
   a. Obtain \(x_0|t\) (or directly use \(x_t\) for CG).  
   b. Evaluate guidance loss \(L\) and compute \(g_t = -\nabla L\).  
   c. Update Adam: \(m \gets \beta_1 m + (1-\beta_1) g_t\), \(v \gets \beta_2 v + (1-\beta_2) g_t^2\), \(k \gets k+1\).  
      Compute bias‑corrected \(\hat{m}=m/(1-\beta_1^k)\), \(\hat{v}=v/(1-\beta_2^k)\), \(\hat{g}=\hat{m}/(\sqrt{\hat{v}}+\epsilon)\).  
   d. Apply Adam‑stabilized update:  
      - AdamDPS: \(x_t' = x_t + \rho \hat{g}\); then compute \(x_{t-1} = \text{DiffStep}(x_t, \dots)\) using \(x_t'\) to guide.  
      - AdamCG: modify predicted \(x_0|t\) as \(x_0|t + \rho \hat{g} \sigma_t^2\) before computing \(x_{t-1}\).  
   e. Continue with next timestep.
3. Output final sample \(x_0\) after \(T\) steps.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- **\(\beta_1,\beta_2\)**: Defaults (0.9, 0.999) work well; tuning could further adapt to specific noise characteristics but adds complexity.
- **Bias correction**: Important in early steps; omitting it leads to underestimation of moments.
- **Guidance strength \(\rho\)**: Adam changes the effective scale of \(\hat{g}\); optimal \(\rho\) differs from DPS/CG and must be retuned.
- **Memory overhead**: Storing \(m\) and \(v\) doubles the per‑sample memory (still modest for typical image sizes).
- **Time‑aware classifier (CG)**: Requires a classifier trained on noisy latents across timesteps; increases training effort but is necessary for stable CG.

**Failure signatures**:
- **No improvement over DPS**: Likely due to using DPS’s \(\rho\) without retuning, resetting Adam state each step, or forgetting bias correction.
- **Divergence or NaNs**: \(\rho\) too high for the normalized \(\hat{g}\); missing \(\epsilon\) inside \(\sqrt{v̂}\); numerical overflow in \(v\) accumulation.
- **Blurry or artifacts**: Incorrect diffusion schedule (e.g., mixing DDIM with DDPM assumptions) or mismatched guidance model (e.g., using a standard classifier for CG).
- **Inconsistent metrics**: LPIPS computed on different value ranges (0‑1 vs \(-1\)‑1) or using a different Inception model for FID.

**3 First experiments**:
1. **Baseline replication**: Reproduce DPS (or CG) on a canonical task (e.g., ImageNet 16× SR) with the same diffusion model and guidance; validate LPIPS/accuracy numbers match the paper’s reported baseline.
2. **Adam integration**: Add Adam buffers as described, keep all other settings identical, and compare sample quality and loss stability against DPS/CG. Expect smoother loss curves and improved metrics with the same \(\rho\).
3. **\(\rho\) retuning**: Run Bayesian optimization (≈150 trials) over a validation set (e.g., 32 images) to find optimal \(\rho\) for AdamDPS and AdamCG on at least one task; verify that the optimal \(\rho\) differs from the baseline and yields the reported gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Evaluation standards for plug‑and‑play methods**: Current evaluations often focus on easy tasks, potentially misrepresenting robustness. A benchmark suite with varying difficulty levels (e.g., super‑resolution factors, blur kernels, mask ratios) is needed for systematic comparisons.
- **Theoretical understanding of Adam’s stabilization**: The method is motivated by analogy to stochastic optimization, but diffusion sampling differs from SGD. A formal convergence analysis of Adam under diffusion‑specific noise assumptions could explain why it works so well.
- **Generality to recurrent guidance methods**: The paper shows gains for DPS, CG, MPGD, PiGDM, but not for TFG/UGD. It is unknown whether Adam can improve these more complex methods, which may already incorporate stabilization. Testing Adam‑augmented TFG/UGD on hard tasks would clarify its scope.

## Limitations
- **Missing precise checkpoints and architectures**: The paper references HuggingFace links for classifiers but does not specify the exact diffusion model (e.g., ADM, LDM) used for each dataset, risking replication mismatches.
- **Undisclosed tuned \(\rho\) values**: Optimal guidance strengths for AdamDPS and AdamCG across tasks are not listed; re‑tuning is required for faithful comparison.
- **Time‑aware classifier details unclear**: Architecture, training data (noisy latents), and checkpoint for ImageNet CG are not provided, hindering exact reproduction.
- **Evaluation metric inconsistencies**: Pre‑processing (e.g., LPIPS network version, image range normalization, FID Inception model) is not fully specified, which can lead to incomparable numbers.

## Confidence
- **Adaptive moments stabilize noisy gradients**: Medium (method described, but effect size depends on hyperparameters and baseline)
- **Performance gains over DPS/CG**: Medium (improvements reported, but replication details missing)
- **Negligible computational overhead**: High (Adam adds constant‑time operations; overhead is predictably small)
