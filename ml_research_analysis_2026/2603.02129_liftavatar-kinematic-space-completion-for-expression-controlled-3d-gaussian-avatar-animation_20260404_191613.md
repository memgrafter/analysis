# LiftAvatar: Kinematic-Space Completion for Expression-Controlled 3D Gaussian Avatar Animation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.02129  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02129  
- Reference count: 18  
- Primary result: LiftAvatar synthesizes diverse high‑fidelity expression sequences by completing sparse pose/expression cues, significantly improving 3D Gaussian avatar quality on unseen expressions (PSNR 28.69, LPIPS 0.1008, SSIM 0.8478, FID 38.65).

## Executive Summary
Monocular video provides limited kinematic coverage for 3D avatar creation, leading to artifacts when avatars are driven by unseen expressions (kinematic‑space incompleteness). LiftAvatar tackles this by formulating a video‑diffusion Transformer that completes missing pose and expression sequences from sparse cues. The method employs multi‑granularity control via NPHM shading maps and expression coefficients, together with multi‑reference conditioning to ground identity. When plugged into state‑of‑the‑art Gaussian‑splatting backbones (MonoGaussianAvatar, SplattingAvatar), LiftAvatar yields substantial quantitative gains and is preferred by users in a study. This represents a robust step toward expressive, high‑fidelity avatars from everyday monocular video.

## Method Summary
LiftAvatar treats kinematic‑space completion as a video generation problem. Given a few reference frames and sparse pose/expression parameters, a diffusion‑based Transformer predicts a temporally coherent, complete sequence of kinematic controls (e.g., expression coefficients, shading maps). These controls then drive a pre‑trained 3D Gaussian avatar model to render the final video. Multi‑granularity conditioning separates coarse pose (handled by NPHM shading maps) from fine expression (handled by coefficients), providing precise control over facial nuances. Multi‑reference conditioning aggregates features from several reference images, ensuring identity consistency throughout the synthesized expression sequence. The pipeline is trained end‑to‑end on monocular video data, learning to fill gaps where the original capture was limited.

## Key Results
- PSNR reaches 28.69 (+1.7 over the best baseline), LPIPS 0.1008 (−0.0129), SSIM 0.8478, and FID 38.65, consistently outperforming MonoGaussianAvatar and SplattingAvatar on unseen expressions.
- A user study with 50 participants rates LiftAvatar’s output at 8.5/10, significantly higher than alternatives.
- Qualitative improvements show more natural, diverse expressions without identity drift or temporal flickering.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
LiftAvatar succeeds because it directly targets the root cause of artifacts—sparse kinematic data—by using a powerful video diffusion model to probabilistically complete missing controls. The model learns the joint distribution of pose, expression, and appearance from abundant training video, enabling it to generate plausible, diverse sequences that respect the subject’s identity. Multi‑granularity control decouples coarse geometry (pose, lighting via NPHM) from fine‑scale expression details, allowing the system to preserve identity while achieving subtle expression nuances. Multi‑reference conditioning further stabilizes identity by fusing information from several reference views, preventing drift across generated frames. The completed kinematic sequence then precisely drives the Gaussian avatar, producing high‑fidelity, temporally coherent results.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Input sparse kinematics → NPHM shading map generator → Expression coefficient encoder → Multi‑reference conditioning module → Video diffusion Transformer → Completed kinematic sequence → 3D Gaussian avatar renderer → Output frames

**Critical Path**  
Sparse pose/expression cues are first processed by the NPHM shading and expression modules to produce conditioning features. These are fused with features extracted from multiple reference images (identity grounding). The fused conditioning guides the diffusion Transformer, which iteratively denoises a random latent into a temporal kinematic sequence. The completed sequence controls a pre‑trained Gaussian avatar, yielding the final video frames.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Diffusion ensures diversity but requires multiple denoising steps, increasing inference latency.  
- Multi‑granularity control improves expression precision but complicates the conditioning architecture.  
- Multi‑reference conditioning strengthens identity preservation at the cost of additional memory for storing and processing reference images.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Identity drift: facial features change across generated expressions.  
- Expression flattening: only extreme expressions appear, subtle ones are lost.  
- Temporal instability: flickering or jitter between frames due to poor video coherence.

**First Experiments**  
1. Train the diffusion Transformer on a synthetic dataset where the complete kinematic sequence is known; verify it reconstructs the full sequence from sparse inputs.  
2. Ablate the multi‑reference conditioning (disable it) and measure identity preservation using feature distance (e.g., CLIP) on a held‑out subject.  
3. Sweep the number of denoising steps to quantify the quality‑speed tradeoff and choose an operating point for real‑time use.

## Limitations

- Omitted comparisons with several recent Gaussian avatar methods (CAG‑Avatar, GGAvatar, GAF, FreeAvatar), potentially overstating superiority.  
- No ablation study isolating the contributions of multi‑granularity control and multi‑reference conditioning.
