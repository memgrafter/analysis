# Reinforced Reasoning for End-to-End Retrosynthetic Planning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.29723
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29723
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ReTriP achieves 45.5% Top-1 accuracy on RetroBench, a 3.4-point improvement over prior SOTA.

## Executive Summary
Retrosynthetic planning traditionally separates single-step prediction from search, leading to fragmented chemical reasoning across steps. This paper introduces ReTriP, an end-to-end framework that reformulates retrosynthesis as a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation process. ReTriP employs a path-coherent molecular representation to maintain fragment traceability across steps and a progressive curriculum: supervised fine-tuning with answer-conditioned CoT distillation, followed by reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards optimizing for precursor availability and route depth. On the RetroBench benchmark, ReTriP attains 45.5% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art by 3.4 points. It shows particular robustness on long-horizon routes (depth≥5), maintaining ~40% accuracy while search hybrids degrade. Test-time augmentation with 16 variants boosts Top-5 accuracy to 57.6%, highlighting the benefit of representational diversity.

## Method Summary
ReTriP addresses the fragmentation problem by modeling retrosynthesis as an end-to-end CoT generation task. The system first generates multiple root-aligned SMILES representations of the target molecule to mitigate notational bias. A pre-ranking module selects the most promising k variants (default 16). For each variant, a generative planner (an LLM fine-tuned through a three-stage supervised curriculum and then reinforcement learning) produces a reasoning chain and a sequence of reaction SMILES that constitute a complete synthetic route. The outputs from all variants are aggregated by a test-time augmentation orchestrator, which applies consensus voting to select the most frequently predicted set of precursors. The training curriculum starts with supervised fine-tuning that distills structured reasoning from a teacher model, then refines the policy via reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards that directly measure route quality (precursor availability, depth). This combination allows the model to internalize chemical logic while being guided toward globally optimal routes.

## Key Results
- ReTriP achieves 45.5% Top-1 accuracy on RetroBench, surpassing the previous SOTA by 3.4 points.
- For routes of depth ≥5, ReTriP maintains ~40% accuracy, whereas search hybrids drop sharply, demonstrating superior long-horizon planning.
- With 16-fold test-time augmentation, Top-5 accuracy improves to 57.6%, indicating significant gains from exploring diverse representations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Path-coherent molecular representation preserves fragment traceability across retrosynthetic steps, reducing notational noise and enabling the model to focus on chemical logic rather than syntactic variations.
- **Mechanism:** By anchoring each intermediate’s SMILES to the root atom inherited from its product, the model maintains consistent atom indexing throughout the synthetic route. This alignment ensures that topological transformations remain traceable and minimizes the divergence (e.g., Levenshtein distance) between successive representations.
- **Core assumption:** Without structural consistency, the model is forced to learn to map arbitrary SMILES changes instead of genuine reaction patterns, impairing multi-step reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "It employs a path-coherent molecular representation that anchors SMILES strings to product atoms to preserve fragment traceability"
  - [section 4.2]: Detailed algorithm for global topological synchronization via root propagation.
  - [corpus]: The corpus lists BioMedGPT-Mol (2512.04629) as a base model; the path-coherent extension is a novel contribution not directly evidenced in neighbors.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The progressive curriculum (answer-conditioned CoT distillation → RL with verifiable rewards) aligns stepwise generation with long-term synthetic utility, overcoming the short-sightedness of pure imitation learning.
- **Mechanism:** Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) internalizes structured reasoning patterns from a teacher LLM, providing a stable policy that generates chemically plausible steps. Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) then fine-tunes this policy using objective metrics (precursor availability, route depth) that are not captured by SFT, thereby optimizing for practical route quality.
- **Core assumption:** Imitation learning alone cannot guarantee globally optimal routes because it replicates possibly biased or non-parsimonious examples from the dataset; explicit reward signals are necessary to steer toward deeper planning criteria.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "employs a progressive curriculum: initial supervised fine-tuning with answer-conditioned distillation of reasoning chains, followed by reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards that optimize for precursor availability and minimal route depth"
  - [section 4.3 and 4.4]: Formal description of the three-stage SFT and the hierarchical RL reward function.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors include works on RL for retrosynthesis (e.g., DeepRetro, GLM-5), but RLVR with verifiable rewards as described is a novel combination with limited direct corpus evidence.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Notational pre-ranking and test-time augmentation (TTA) mitigate sensitivity to initial SMILES representation and increase trajectory diversity, leading to higher success rates, especially for long-horizon routes.
- **Mechanism:** A lightweight ranker selects the most informative root-aligned SMILES variant for the target, reducing initial notational bias. TTA then runs parallel generations across the top-k notations and aggregates their precursor sets by majority vote, effectively scaling inference to uncover a broader distribution of valid routes.
- **Core assumption:** A single fixed representation may bias the model toward a narrow subspace of possible routes; exploring multiple equivalent representations uncovers diverse reasoning paths, some of which yield better outcomes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "With 16-fold test-time augmentation, Top-5 accuracy improves to 57.6%, showing gains from representational diversity."
  - [section 4.5]: Pre-ranking and TTA pipeline, including consensus voting.
  - [corpus]: Self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023) is cited; the specific augmentation scheme is an adaptation not directly evidenced in neighboring papers.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Target Molecule → Notational Pre-ranking Module (selects k SMILES variants) → Generative Planner (generates CoT and reaction SMILES for each variant) → Test-Time Augmentation Orchestrator (aggregates and votes) → Final Retrosynthetic Route

- **Critical path:**
  1. Receive target molecule and generate 20 root-aligned SMILES via augmentation.
  2. Pre-ranking scores each variant; select top-k (default k=16 for final evaluation).
  3. For each variant, run the generative planner to obtain a complete retrosynthetic route (CoT + reaction SMILES sequence).
  4. Parse each route into a set of starting materials; validate chemical feasibility implicitly via format parsing.
  5. Aggregate precursor sets across k branches via frequency-based majority voting.
  6. Output the highest-consensus route or, if none adequate, the best-scoring single route.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **LoRA vs. full fine-tuning:** SFT uses LoRA (rank 16) for parameter efficiency, while RLVR uses full-parameter updates to allow broader policy adaptation; this balances stability and flexibility.
  - **TTA size vs. latency:** Larger TTA improves accuracy (Fig. 4) but increases inference cost linearly; the chosen k=16 offers a practical tradeoff on a single GPU.
  - **Reward shaping complexity:** The hierarchical reward (Equation 2–4) includes bonuses, penalties, and gating; simpler rewards may train faster but risk misalignment with synthetic utility.
  - **Representation diversity vs. consistency:** Path-coherent alignment reduces notational noise but may restrict diversity; TTA reintroduces diversity by exploring different root anchors upfront.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Unparsable outputs:** Generated text does not conform to the expected reaction SMILES format → reward 0; indicates CoT or formatting issues.
  - **Low consensus among TTA branches:** Precursor sets disagree widely; suggests the policy is unstable or representation-sensitive.
  - **High depth penalties in RL:** Routes consistently exceed reference depth, triggering clipping; indicates the RL has not learned to prefer parsimonious paths.
  - **Elevated NLD in alignment:** Large Normalized Levenshtein Distance between target and RHS fractions (Fig. 7) signals breakdown in traceability.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablate path alignment:** Train a baseline with random inter-step root selection (no alignment) and compare NLD and Top-1 accuracy to the full model.
  2. **Ablate RLVR:** Evaluate the SFT-only model (after Stage 3) against the full pipeline to isolate the contribution of RL in improving long-horizon robustness.
  3. **Scale TTA:** Measure Top-1/Top-5 accuracy across TTA sizes {1,4,8,12,16} to empirically determine the point of diminishing returns and validate the consensus mechanism.

## Limitations
- Convergent branch handling: Path-coherent root alignment may cause representational drift when shared fragments need distinct roots, potentially impairing complex route synthesis.
- Reward specification: Hierarchical RL rewards risk misalignment (e.g., over-penalizing depth) without extensive tuning; break conditions not fully stress-tested.
- Scalability: Test-time augmentation’s 16-fold inference is costly; viability for larger k or real-time use is unclear.

## Confidence
