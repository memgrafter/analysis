# Thinking with Gaze: Sequential Eye-Tracking as Visual Reasoning Supervision for Medical VLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06697
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06697
- **Reference count:** 32
- **Primary result:** Inserting four temporally-ordered gaze tokens into a VLM, supervised by radiologist eye-tracking, achieves state-of-the-art in-domain performance (AUROC 90.17) and consistent zero-shot gains on external chest X-ray benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method to enhance vision-language models (VLMs) for radiology by leveraging eye-gaze trajectories as a sequential supervision signal. The authors modify Qwen2.5-VL by adding four special tokens that are trained to predict gaze-selected image patch indices in chronological order. A two-stage training pipeline first aligns these tokens with gaze data and then fine-tunes for multi-label classification with a rigid output format. The approach attains superior results on the MIMIC-EYE dataset and demonstrates robust zero-shot transfer to external benchmarks. Ablations confirm that preserving the original temporal order of gaze is essential, highlighting the value of human-like visual search patterns for VLM reasoning.

## Method Summary
The method adapts a pretrained VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) for chest X-ray multi-label classification by inserting four placeholder tokens (`<st1>`–`<st4>`) at the start of the text response. During training, the hidden states of these tokens are projected to distributions over image patch IDs and supervised with cross-entropy against temporally aggregated gaze targets derived from radiologists' eye movements. Stage 1 trains only these projection heads and LoRA adapters to align the tokens with ordered patch sequences. Stage 2 adds a 14-label classifier head and trains with a combined loss (classification, gaze, optional language modeling). At inference, the model is constrained to produce exactly the four tokens followed by a fixed-format "Answer: [yes/no...]" string. The gaze supervision teaches a step-wise evidence acquisition routine, grounding the VLM's internal reasoning in human-like visual search.

## Key Results
- **In-domain (MIMIC-EYE test):** AUROC 90.17, Accuracy 89.02, F1 87.61; outperforms SFT baseline and other methods.
- **Zero-shot external benchmarks:** CheXpert (Acc 62.45, F1 61.73), RSNA (Acc 76.61, F1 53.73), SIIM-ACR (Acc 64.07, F1 61.89) – consistent gains over baselines.
- **Ablation:** Original gaze order > Shuffled (AUROC 88.51) > Random (86.45), proving temporal structure is critical.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Preserving the original temporal order of radiologist gaze trajectories provides a structured, sequential supervision signal that improves a VLM's visual reasoning and calibration.
- **Mechanism:** The four gaze tokens are trained to predict gaze-derived patch indices in the exact chronological sequence observed by radiologists. This forces the VLM to learn a step-by-step evidence acquisition routine rather than a static spatial prior. The autoregressive token-by-token computation aligns with this sequential supervision.
- **Core assumption:** The temporal structure of human gaze encodes a causal, expert evidence-gathering process transferable to the VLM.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.1 show Original-Gaze outperforms Shuffled and Random; ablation confirms the temporal structure's role.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Injecting gaze supervision at the feature level via latent gaze tokens creates a "latent visual chain-of-thought" that keeps the VLM's intermediate reasoning visually grounded, avoiding text-only bottlenecks.
- **Mechanism:** The gaze tokens exist within the autoregressive text generation stream but are anchored to visual evidence via a projection head. This encourages the model to process and accumulate visual evidence in a latent, non-verbalizable space before producing the final diagnosis.
- **Core assumption:** The VLM's intermediate layers can effectively ground textual generation in visually supervised latent tokens, and this latent visual reasoning is superior to purely textual CoT for medical tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:** Sections 1 and 3.3 describe the design; results show gains over pure SFT.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Learning human-like, temporally-ordered evidence acquisition patterns via gaze supervision yields a VLM that is more robust to distribution shift (zero-shot generalization) than one trained only on text report labels.
- **Mechanism:** The gaze signal teaches a general, expert strategy for scanning and integrating evidence from chest X-rays, which is less tied to dataset-specific lexical patterns or artifacts. This strategy transfers better to external datasets with different populations, equipment, or prevalence.
- **Core assumption:** Gaze trajectories represent a stable, fundamental cognitive process invariant across protocols, whereas textual reports are highly dataset-specific.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.2 show consistent zero-shot gains; relative improvements on external F1 are larger than in-domain, suggesting robustness.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input Processor -> Qwen2.5-VL Backbone (with 4 inserted gaze tokens) -> Gaze Projection Head (linear) -> Gaze CE loss; Last token -> Classifier Head (linear + sigmoid) -> Classification loss; Backbone also outputs text with LM loss.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Data preprocessing: Convert raw gaze to temporally ordered patch indices (top-k per step).  
  2. Forward pass: Insert gaze tokens; extract hidden states; apply projection heads.  
  3. Stage 1 training: Update LoRA adapters + gaze head only with gaze CE loss.  
  4. Stage 2 training: Add classifier head; train with combined classification + gaze CE (+ LM) loss.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Fixed 4 tokens vs. variable length: Simplifies architecture but may not match natural scanpath lengths.  
  - Patch-index classification vs. heatmap regression: Classification aligns with sequential search but discards intra-patch precision.  
  - Two-stage vs. joint training: Stabilizes by separating gaze alignment from classification objective.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Shuffled/Random gaze ablation: AUROC drops significantly (90.17 -> 88.51/86.45).  
  - Stage 1 failure: Gaze token predictions random or identical across tokens; check per-token CE loss.  
  - Format violation: Model deviates from fixed output string; monitor exact match rate.  
  - Zero-shot failure: Gains inconsistent or negative on external datasets.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Reproduce Table 1: Train full pipeline on MIMIC-EYE; report in-domain metrics for Vanilla, SFT, Original-Gaze.  
  2. Gaze order ablation: Train Original, Shuffled, Random models; compare MIMIC-EYE test performance.  
  3. Visualize per-token predictions: After Stage 1, show top-5 patches for each gaze token; Original should show spatial progression resembling radiologist scanpath; controls should not.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does the level of gaze aggregation (e.g., sentence-level vs. fixation-level) influence the quality of gaze supervision for medical VLMs?  
  *Basis:* Authors aggregate gaze at the sentence level; finer/coarser strategies not explored.  
  *Resolution:* Compare aggregation strategies on in-domain and zero-shot tasks; measure alignment with human scan paths.

- **Open Question 2:** What is the optimal number of dedicated gaze tokens, and does fixing it to four limit representation of variable-length scan paths?  
  *Basis:* Exactly four tokens used arbitrarily; scanpaths vary in length.  
  *Resolution:* Systematically vary token count (e.g., 2,4,6,8) and evaluate classification and gaze prediction accuracy.

- **Open Question 3:** Do the internal representations of gaze tokens correlate with actual radiologist eye-tracking data, and does this correlation predict out-of-domain robustness?  
  *Basis:* Model trained to predict gaze-derived indices but not validated against held-out human gaze; zero-shot gains attributed to gaze without direct evidence.  
  *Resolution:* Compare gaze token outputs/attention with independent radiologist gaze on external datasets; correlate alignment with performance.

- **Open Question 4:** Can gaze supervision improve free-text radiology report generation, and does it yield more clinically accurate or interpretable reports?  
  *Basis:* Keywords mention report generation; all experiments on structured classification.  
  *Resolution:* Adapt method to report generation (e.g., MIMIC-CXR with gaze); evaluate with metrics and clinician review.

## Limitations
- Unspecified preprocessing parameters (patch grid size, top-k per time step, temporal aggregation window) could affect reproducibility.
- Missing training details (LoRA rank/dropout, learning rates, epoch counts) hinder exact replication.
- Fixed four gaze tokens may not capture natural variability in radiologist scanpaths.
