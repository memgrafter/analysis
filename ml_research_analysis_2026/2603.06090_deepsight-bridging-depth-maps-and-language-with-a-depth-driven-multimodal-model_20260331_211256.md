# DeepSight: Bridging Depth Maps and Language with a Depth-Driven Multimodal Model

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06090
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06090
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** DeepSight-7B achieves 53.85% average accuracy on the Depth Template Benchmark, outperforming fine-tuned baselines by significant margins (e.g., ImageBindLLM 39.28%, LanguageBind 48.54%) and sets a new zero-shot scene classification state-of-the-art (67.0% on NYU-D).

## Executive Summary
Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with depth perception, often misinterpreting stereoscopic and distance information in images. DeepSight addresses this by introducing a depth-specific MLLM. It modifies a CLIP visual encoder with a bounding box convolution layer to explicitly capture local object-level depth relationships. The model is trained on a newly constructed dataset of 118K synthetic depth-text pairs (generated via GLPN) for alignment, followed by fine-tuning on 22K depth instructions (curated using GPT-4). On the proposed Depth Template Benchmark—a suite of 13,473 QA pairs across four depth reasoning tasks—DeepSight-7B fine-tuned substantially outperforms other fine-tuned MLLMs. The work demonstrates that targeted architectural changes and depth-focused data can significantly improve an MLLM's 3D scene understanding.

## Method Summary
DeepSight’s architecture centers on a modified CLIP ViT encoder. It processes a depth image through a standard path while simultaneously processing an auxiliary binary mask channel indicating object bounding boxes via a parallel "Bbox Conv" branch. The features from both branches are summed before entering the transformer blocks, forcing the model to integrate local object-level depth cues. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: first, only a trainable projection layer is aligned between the fused depth encoder and the LLM using a large-scale synthetic depth-text dataset (118K pairs); second, both the projection layer and the LLM are fine-tuned on a smaller set of depth-specific instructions (22K samples). A key data sampling strategy during alignment randomly replaces 10% of the depth-text-bbox triplets with pure depth-text pairs to preserve the encoder’s global depth understanding.

## Key Results
- **Benchmark Dominance:** Fine-tuned DeepSight-7B achieves 53.85% average accuracy on the Depth Template Benchmark, leading fine-tuned ImageBindLLM (39.28%) and LanguageBind (48.54%) by over 4-14 percentage points.
- **Zero-shot Generalization:** After alignment, DeepSight sets a new state-of-the-art in zero-shot scene classification on NYU-D (67.0%).
- **Ablation Validation:** Incorporating the Bbox Convolution at both training and inference yields a 4.71% absolute gain on the "Distance Judge" task compared to training it only, confirming its critical role.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Bbox Convolution for Local Depth Cues**
A bounding box convolution layer is added to the CLIP ViT encoder. It processes a binary mask of object regions in parallel with the depth image patches. This design explicitly guides the attention mechanism to weigh local object boundaries and their relative depth contrasts, which is essential for tasks like distance judgment between specific objects. Ablations show a significant 4.71% absolute gain on the Distance Judge task when the Bbox Conv is used consistently at both training and inference versus being trained away. The mechanism's effectiveness depends on the core assumption that depth perception for 3D reasoning relies heavily on localized object relationships, not just global layout.

**Mechanism 2: Two-Stage Training on Synthetic Data**
The training uses a two-stage paradigm. Stage one (Alignment) trains only a projection layer on 118K synthetic depth-text pairs (from GLPN-projected COCO images), establishing a coarse cross-modal mapping while freezing the depth encoder and LLM. Stage two (SFT) fine-tunes both the projection layer and the LLM on 22K depth instructions. This approach leverages the structural depth cues in synthetic data for alignment and then adapts the LLM’s reasoning to depth-specific contexts. The significant performance gap over fine-tuned baselines validates that the synthetic data and staged training effectively specialize the model for depth, assuming the synthetic depth (GLPN) is sufficiently accurate and the LLM can be specialized without catastrophic forgetting.

**Mechanism 3: Data Sampling for Global-Local Balance**
During alignment, 10% of the depth-text-bbox triplets are randomly replaced with depth-text pairs (no bbox mask). This exposes the encoder to both global-only and local-augmented samples, preventing overfitting to the Bbox Conv branch and preserving the pre-trained CLIP encoder’s holistic depth understanding. Zero-shot scene classification results show an inverted-U curve: performance peaks at a 10% replacement rate and declines with more aggressive sampling, confirming the need for this balance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision Transformer (ViT) Patch Encoding:** ViT splits an image into fixed-size patches, linearly embeds them, and processes them with a transformer. In DeepSight, this initial embedding must handle a single-channel depth map (typically replicated to 3 channels for CLIP compatibility) and a separate binary bbox mask channel. *Quick Check:* How does a ViT's patch embedding layer differ in parameter count when processing a single-channel vs. a three-channel input image?
- **Multimodal Alignment via Contrastive Learning:** Aligning two modalities (vision and language) is typically done by maximizing similarity for matching pairs and minimizing it for non-matching pairs, using a cross-entropy loss. DeepSight’s alignment stage uses this exact mechanism to map depth features to the LLM’s textual space. *Quick Check:* Why is it standard practice to freeze the vision and text encoders during this initial alignment phase when introducing a new projection layer?
- **Instruction Tuning for MLLMs:** This technique fine-tunes a pre-trained LLM on a dataset of (instruction, response) pairs to improve its ability to follow user commands. In DeepSight’s SFT stage, the LLM learns to generate responses grounded in depth image features. *Quick Check:* What is the primary distinction between the loss functions used in the alignment stage (contrastive) and the SFT stage (language modeling) of a typical MLLM?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Depth Image + Bbox Mask -> Modified CLIP Depth Encoder (Dual-path: Depth Conv + Bbox Conv) -> Fused Features -> Projection Layer (MLP) -> LLM (Vicuna-1.5-7B) -> Text Output
**Critical Path:** The data flow is linear from modified encoder through projection to the LLM. Training critically flows from the Alignment stage (training only the Projection Layer) to the SFT stage (training Projection Layer + LLM), with the depth encoder frozen throughout.
**Design Tradeoffs:** 
- *Bbox Conv addition:* Adds minor compute but provides explicit spatial depth guidance; depends on availability/quality of bounding box masks. 
- *Synthetic depth data:* Solves data scarcity but introduces a domain gap from real sensor depth, potentially capping performance. 
- *Two-stage training (freeze-then-fine-tune):* Stabilizes training and preserves pre-trained knowledge versus simpler end-to-end training, but requires careful sampling strategy design.
**Failure Signatures:** 
- Poor "Distance Judge" scores (<50%) suggest ineffective BboxConv integration or incorrect sampling ratio. 
- Degraded zero-shot scene classification after alignment indicates overfitting to local features (sampling ratio `r` too low or too high). 
- Irrelevant LLM outputs point to insufficient SFT or low-quality instruction data.

3. Sweep the alignment-stage sampling ratio `r` across {0.0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2}; evaluate zero-shot scene classification on NYU-D to replicate the inverted-U curve and confirm `r=0.1` optimum.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Real Depth Generalization:** The model is trained on GLPN-generated synthetic depth from COCO images. Its performance on real sensor depth (e.g., LiDAR, structured light from NYU-D) may degrade due to domain gaps in noise, holes, and absolute scale. The paper does not test this.
- **BboxConv Necessity Without Bboxes:** While the BboxConv branch uses an all-ones mask during inference, it is trained with real object masks. The ablation shows a gain when used at both train and inference versus train-only, but it is unclear if the branch is essential when *no* bbox annotations are available at *any* stage.
- **Benchmark Scope:** The Depth Template Benchmark uses fixed multiple-choice templates based on object annotations and average depths. This may not capture holistic, open-ended 3D scene understanding (e.g., "describe the layout") or complex multi-object spatial reasoning, limiting its validity as a general depth reasoning test.

## Limitations
- **Unreported Training Details:** Critical hyperparameters (learning rates, batch sizes, optimizer settings, total epochs) and the exact Bbox Conv architecture (kernel sizes, fusion point) are missing, hindering faithful reproduction.
- **Dataset Construction Ambiguity:** The specifics of the GPT-4 prompt for instruction generation, the caption scoring threshold with LanguageBind, and the GLPN model version are not provided.
- **Evaluation Nuances:** Exact train/val/test splits for the Depth Template Benchmark and the method for parsing multiple-choice answers are undefined. The "Human (75%)" baseline is unexplained.
- **Synthetic Data Domain Gap:** Reliance on GLPN-generated depth for training introduces a potential performance ceiling when applied to real-world sensor data.

## Confidence
- **Bbox Conv Improvement:** Medium. The ablation is clear, but implementation specifics (mask generation, fusion) could significantly alter the magnitude of the effect.
- **Two-Stage Training Efficacy:** Medium. Large gains over baselines support the claim, but the exclusive use of synthetic depth and unreported training details introduce uncertainty about generalizability and optimal hyperparameters.
- **Sampling Strategy (r=0.1):** Medium. The inverted-U curve in zero-shot evaluation is strong evidence, but the optimal ratio is likely dataset and architecture-dependent.
