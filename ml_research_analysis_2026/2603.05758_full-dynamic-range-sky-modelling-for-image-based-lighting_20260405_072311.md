# Full Dynamic Range Sky-Modelling For Image Based Lighting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.05758
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05758
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Icarus reduces FID from AllSky’s 24.7 to 15.1 and improves HDR-VDP3 from 8.15 to 8.41 on the Laval HDR Sky database, achieving results closer to ground truth in exposure value and integrated illumination.

## Executive Summary
Icarus is a deep learning sky model for image-based lighting that specifically addresses the failure of existing DNN sky-models to accurately reproduce the high dynamic range solar region. Instead of relying on tone mapping, it decomposes full dynamic range HDRI into low dynamic rate exposure brackets and uses a style-aware generator with synchronous per-exposure decoders, accompanied by both LDR and HDR discriminators. This architecture enables user control over solar position and cloud formations. Evaluated on the Laval HDR Sky database at 512² resolution, Icarus significantly improves fidelity metrics (FID, HDR-VDP3, exposure value, integrated illumination) compared to the prior state-of-the-art AllSky, and demonstrates stable performance and superior photorealism in rendered scenes.

## Method Summary
The method decomposes a full dynamic range HDRI into a set of low dynamic range exposure brackets, thereby avoiding the information loss associated with tone mapping. A style-aware generator produces a latent representation conditioned on user-specified sky parameters (e.g., solar position, cloud coverage). This latent code is then processed by multiple synchronous per-exposure decoders, each dedicated to generating a consistent LDR image for a specific exposure level. The per-exposure outputs are fused using the Robertson HDR merging algorithm to produce the final HDR image. Two discriminators – one operating on LDR images and another on the HDR result – provide adversarial feedback to ensure realism across the dynamic range. This design allows precise modeling of the solar region and offers user control over sky attributes.

## Key Results
- With Robertson fusion, Icarus reduces FID from AllSky’s 24.7 to 15.1 and improves HDR-VDP3 from 8.15 to 8.41 on the Laval HDR Sky database at 512² resolution.
- Exposure value (EV) improved to 9.38 (vs AllSky 9.99) and integrated illumination to 1.23 (vs AllSky 1.58), both closer to ground truth (EV 9.44, I 1.22).
- The model shows stable performance across sequential samples (low standard deviation in EV) and yields superior photorealism in renderings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Decomposing HDRI into exposure brackets eliminates the need for tone mapping, which typically discards detail in extreme luminance regions like the solar disc. The synchronous per-exposure decoders enforce cross-exposure consistency from a shared latent representation, ensuring that features such as cloud edges align perfectly across all brackets – a prerequisite for accurate HDR fusion. The dual discriminators (LDR and HDR) supply multi-scale adversarial signals that preserve both fine textures in individual exposures and the overall high dynamic range in the final fused image. The style-aware generator encodes global sky characteristics, allowing users to manipulate solar and cloud attributes while maintaining photorealistic outputs.

## Foundational Learning
- **High Dynamic Range Imaging (HDRI)**: Why needed? To capture the vast luminance range of natural skies, especially the intense solar irradiance that would be clipped in standard LDR images. Quick check: Can the model reconstruct a solar disc with smooth gradients without overexposure artifacts?
- **Exposure Bracketing**: Why needed? To avoid tone mapping by providing multiple LDR exposures that collectively encode the full dynamic range. Quick check: Are all exposures used during training/inference and does their fusion yield a seamless HDR image?
- **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)**: Why needed? To learn the complex distribution of sky appearances and generate realistic HDR images conditioned on input parameters. Quick check: Does the generator produce diverse sky conditions (e.g., clear, partly cloudy, overcast) when sampling latent codes?
- **Style-Aware Generator**: Why needed? To encode the overall visual style (e.g., cloud formations, solar position) into a latent space for controllable generation. Quick check: Can users edit solar azimuth/elevation by interpolating in the latent space?
- **Synchronous Per-Exposure Decoders**: Why needed? To decode the shared latent representation into consistent LDR images across different exposures, ensuring accurate HDR reconstruction. Quick check: Check for consistency of features (e.g., cloud edges) across the generated exposures; mismatches indicate decoder misalignment.
- **LDR/HDR Discriminators**: Why needed? To enforce realism at both the LDR level (each exposure) and the final HDR level, capturing both local texture and global dynamic range. Quick check: Evaluate whether the discriminators effectively distinguish real vs. generated images in both domains via adversarial loss curves.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Input parameters (solar position, cloud coverage) → Style-Aware Generator (produces latent code) → Synchronous Per-Exposure Decoders (each outputs an LDR image for a specific exposure) → HDR Fusion (e.g., Robertson) → Final HDR output. LDR images feed LDR Discriminators; HDR output feeds HDR Discriminator; discriminators provide adversarial gradients to generator and decoders.

**Critical Path**: The main generation flow is from input parameters through the generator, then in parallel through all per-exposure decoders, followed by HDR fusion to produce the final image. During training, adversarial feedback from both discriminators to the generator and decoders is also essential.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- Separate decoders per exposure increase model capacity and allow exposure-specific feature learning but may risk inconsistency across exposures if not properly regularized.
- Employing both LDR and HDR discriminators improves multi-range realism but doubles adversarial training complexity and may require careful loss weighting.
- Avoiding tone mapping preserves solar region detail but necessitates accurate HDR fusion; the choice of fusion algorithm (Robertson) is thus crucial and may not generalize to other fusion methods.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Overexposed solar disc with loss of detail or bleeding into surrounding sky indicates insufficient modeling of high luminance.
- Inconsistent cloud patterns or outlines across different exposures suggest decoder misalignment or lack of cross-exposure consistency.
- Unnatural color shifts or lack of contrast in shadowed areas may point to inadequate HDR fusion or discriminator guidance.
- High variance in exposure value across sequential samples could indicate unstable training or insufficient style conditioning.

**First Experiments**:
1. Ablate the synchronous decoders by replacing them with a single shared decoder for all exposures; measure impact on HDR fusion quality (e.g., FID, HDR-VDP3).
2. Remove the LDR discriminators and train only with the HDR discriminator; assess whether LDR image quality degrades and how it affects final HDR realism.
3. Substitute Robertson fusion with alternative HDR merging algorithms (e.g., Debevec, Mantiuk) on fixed decoder outputs; quantify sensitivity of final image quality to the fusion choice.

## Limitations
- Database scope: Evaluation is exclusively on the Laval HDR Sky database; performance on geographically/seasonally diverse skies and cloud types is unknown.
- Fusion dependency: Reported gains are specific to Robertson fusion; robustness to other HDR merging algorithms is not evaluated.
- User control evaluation: Claims of superior control lack qualitative user studies or objective metrics for precision and artifact rates.

## Confidence
- Quantitative superiority on Laval Database: High
- Efficacy of exposure decomposition & synchronous decoders: Medium
- Superior photorealism & stability: Low-to-Medium

## Next Checks
1. Cross-Dataset Generalization Test: Re-train and evaluate Icarus on two other full-sky HDR databases (e.g., Westbed, CPC) and report metric deltas (FID, HDR-VDP3) compared to AllSky.
2. Ablation of HDR Fusion Strategy: Keep all components fixed, vary the HDR fusion method (Robertson vs. Debevec vs. Mantiuk) applied to per-exposure decoder outputs, and measure impact on image quality metrics.
3. Perceptual User Study for Control & Realism: Conduct a double-blind study with expert artists/lighting designers performing specific tasks with Icarus and AllSky, recording task success rate, time, and subjective ratings.
