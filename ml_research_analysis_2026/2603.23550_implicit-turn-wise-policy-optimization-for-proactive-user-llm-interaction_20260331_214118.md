# Implicit Turn-Wise Policy Optimization for Proactive User-LLM Interaction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.23550
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23550
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Norm-ITPO improves trajectory-level baselines by up to 34.4% on math tutoring and achieves 73–75% agreement with human judgments on turn-wise reward alignment.

## Executive Summary
Multi-turn human-AI collaboration suffers from sparse outcome rewards and high stochasticity in user responses, making reinforcement learning (RL) for language agents challenging. This paper proposes Implicit Turn-Wise Policy Optimization (ITPO), which introduces an implicit process reward model (PRM) to derive token-level rewards directly from outcome signals without human-labeled process data. Token rewards are summed within each conversational turn to obtain robust turn-wise rewards; a normalized variant, Norm-ITPO, further redistributes the global outcome reward via a Softmax-weighted mechanism to stabilize training. Evaluated on math tutoring, document writing, and medical recommendation tasks, ITPO and Norm-ITPO consistently outperform baselines (including value models, PRIME, and LLM-as-a-Judge) when combined with PPO, GRPO, or RLOO. Norm-ITPO achieves up to 34.4% improvement over trajectory-level baselines on math tutoring. Moreover, the learned turn-wise rewards converge quickly and align closely with human judgments (73–75% agreement), demonstrating both training stability and semantic interpretability.

## Method Summary
ITPO operates in an online RL loop. At each iteration, the current policy π_θ interacts with a user simulator to generate multi-turn trajectories τ and outcome rewards R(τ) (e.g., accuracy, BLEU, or hierarchical score). An implicit PRM π_ϕ (initialized from π_θ and distinct from a fixed reference policy π_ref) is trained via binary cross-entropy to align its cumulative token-level log-likelihood ratio R_ϕ(τ)=∑ r_ϕ with the outcome R(τ). The token-level process reward is defined as r_ϕ(y_t)=β log(π_ϕ(y_t)/π_ref(y_t)). These token rewards are aggregated per turn to produce turn-wise implicit rewards R_k^ϕ = ∑_{t in turn k} r_ϕ. Optionally, Norm-ITPO normalizes by computing weights w_k = Softmax(R_k^ϕ/η) and setting ˜R_k = w_k·R, ensuring the total outcome budget R is distributed according to the model’s inferred turn contributions. A length penalty γ·|y_k| is subtracted from each turn’s reward. Advantages are then computed using RLOO, GRPO, or GAE (with a learned value model) and the policy is updated via PPO’s clipped surrogate objective with a KL penalty towards π_ref. The entire process repeats every iteration, with π_ϕ updated before policy optimization.

## Key Results
- Norm-ITPO yields up to 34.4% improvement over trajectory-level baselines on the math tutoring task.
- ITPO and Norm-ITPO consistently surpass value models, PRIME, and LLM-as-a-Judge across PPO, GRPO, and RLOO on all three tasks.
- Learned turn-wise rewards achieve 73–75% agreement with human judgments, indicating strong semantic interpretability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Implicit Token-Level Reward Generation
**Claim:** An implicit process reward model (implicit PRM) generates dense, token-level supervision signals from sparse outcome rewards without human-labeled process data.  
**Mechanism:** The implicit PRM π_ϕ is trained to align its cumulative log-likelihood ratio R_ϕ(τ)=∑_{t} r_ϕ(y_t) with the ground-truth outcome reward R(τ) via binary cross-entropy loss, where r_ϕ(y_t)=β log(π_ϕ(y_t)/π_ref(y_t)). This allows gradient signals from the outcome to implicitly propagate credit to individual tokens.  
**Core assumption:** The token-level log-likelihood ratio parameterized by π_ϕ can be optimized to correlate with trajectory-level outcome quality, i.e., the policy’s generative probabilities contain sufficient signal to infer each token’s contribution.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states ITPO leverages an implicit PRM to derive fine-grained signals; Section 3.2 formalizes r_ϕ and training loss; Figure 5 (not shown here) demonstrates positive correlation between R_ϕ(τ) and R(τ) (Kendall’s τ ~0.5–0.75).

### Mechanism 2: Turn-Wise Aggregation
**Claim:** Summing token-level implicit rewards within a conversational turn yields robust, semantically interpretable supervision and mitigates token-level noise.  
**Mechanism:** For each turn k, the turn-wise implicit reward R_k^ϕ = ∑_{t∈turn k} r_ϕ(y_t). This aggregation acts as a low-pass filter, reducing variance from volatile token scores while preserving the semantic unit of a turn.  
**Core assumption:** A conversational turn is a meaningful atomic unit for credit assignment; intra-turn noise is approximately i.i.d. and thus cancels out upon summation.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions turn-level signals exhibit superior robustness; Section 3.3 describes R_k^ϕ as the cumulative implicit advantage; Figure 6 shows turn-wise rewards converge quickly and stably across training steps, unlike token-level rewards.

### Mechanism 3: Normalization via Softmax Redistribution
**Claim:** Norm-ITPO stabilizes training by redistributing the global outcome reward across turns via Softmax weights, aligning the scale of implicit rewards with the fixed outcome budget.  
**Mechanism:** Compute w_k = Softmax(R_k^ϕ/η) and set normalized turn reward ˜R_k = w_k·R. This enforces that the sum of ˜R_k equals R while preserving the relative ordering of R_k^ϕ.  
**Core assumption:** The relative ranking of turn-wise implicit rewards is more reliable than their absolute magnitudes; using Outcome R as a fixed total budget prevents reward scale drift.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes the Softmax-weighted normalization; Section 3.3 gives a Bayesian interpretation (posterior probability that a turn is pivotal); Figure 7 shows that the slope of R_ϕ(τ) vs. R fluctuates for ITPO but stabilizes for Norm-ITPO, indicating improved scale consistency.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP)**  
- *Why needed:* The multi-turn User-LLM interaction is formalized as a POMDP where the agent does not observe the user’s latent goal g, only the conversation history and query. This captures inherent uncertainty in user intent and response stochasticity.  
- *Quick check:* What constitutes the observation at turn k and what is the unobservable state component?  

**Concept: Implicit Process Reward Model (Implicit PRM)**  
- *Why needed:* It is the core mechanism to derive dense token-level rewards from sparse outcome signals without human annotations or expensive Monte Carlo sampling, enabling online RL in multi-turn settings.  
- *Quick check:* How is the token-level process reward r_ϕ mathematically defined, and what is the training objective for the implicit PRM π_ϕ?  

**Concept: Advantage Estimation (RLOO, GRPO, GAE)**  
- *Why needed:* The turn-wise rewards are used to compute advantages for policy optimization. Different estimators (RLOO, GRPO, PPO with GAE) offer distinct variance-bias trade-offs and stability properties, which the paper evaluates.  
- *Quick check:* What is the key difference between how RLOO and GRPO compute the advantage from a group of roll-out trajectories, and why might GRPO be more stable?  

**Concept: Reference Policy and KL Regularization**  
- *Why needed:* A fixed reference policy π_ref serves as a baseline for defining token-level rewards in the implicit PRM (via log ratios) and for constraining the policy update via a KL penalty, which prevents excessive deviation and maintains stability in multi-turn RL.  
- *Quick check:* What is the role of π_ref in the implicit PRM training loss and in the PPO update, and how does it prevent policy collapse?  

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (data flow):  
User Simulator -> Policy (π_θ) generates trajectory (τ) -> Outcome Verifier computes R(τ) -> Implicit PRM (π_ϕ) produces token-level r_ϕ -> Reward Aggregator sums to turn-wise R_k^ϕ -> (optional Normalizer) computes ˜R_k^ϕ via Softmax -> Advantage Calculator (RLOO/GRPO/GAE) computes advantages Â_i,k -> Policy Optimizer updates π_θ.

Critical path:
1. Online roll-out with current policy π_θ and user simulator U to collect trajectories τ and outcome rewards R(τ).
2. Update implicit PRM π_ϕ by minimizing binary cross-entropy to align R_ϕ(τ) with R(τ).
3. Compute token-level rewards r_ϕ using updated π_ϕ.
4. Aggregate per turn to obtain R_k^ϕ.
5. For Norm-ITPO, normalize via Softmax to get ˜R_k^ϕ; then subtract length penalty γ·|y_k| from each turn reward. For ITPO, directly subtract length penalty.
6. Compute advantages using chosen estimator (RLOO, GRPO, or GAE).
7. Update π_θ via PPO’s clipped surrogate loss with KL penalty to reference policy.

Design tradeoffs:
- ITPO vs. Norm-ITPO: ITPO uses raw aggregated turn rewards, which may have unstable scale relative to R. Norm-ITPO enforces scale consistency via Softmax redistribution, improving stability (especially with learned value models) but introduces temperature η and may lose absolute magnitude information.
- Token-level vs. Turn-level: Token-level provides finer granularity but suffers high variance and poor interpretability. Turn-level is more robust and interpretable but may miss critical intra-turn decisions.
- Value Model vs. Group-Based Estimators: A learned critic (GAE) reduces variance but can overfit and be unstable in stochastic multi-turn settings. Group-based methods (RLOO, GRPO) are simpler and more stable but often have higher variance.

Failure signatures:
1. Reward oscillation: Turn-wise rewards R_k^ϕ do not stabilize during training.
2. Degenerate normalization: Norm-ITPO weights w_k become nearly uniform (high η) or one-hot (low η) across all trajectories.
3. Policy collapse: π_θ generates overly short or repetitive responses.
4. Poor human alignment: Turn-wise rewards disagree with human judgments on a probe set.

First three experiments:
1. **Sanity check - Implicit PRM trajectory correlation:** On a fixed validation set, monitor Kendall’s τ between R_ϕ(τ) and R(τ); should exceed 0.5 and stabilize within ~100 steps (replicates Fig. 5).
2. **Granularity ablation - Token vs. Turn:** Compare ITPO with token-level rewards (no turn grouping) against turn-level rewards on a simple 2-turn QA task; expect turn-level to exhibit lower variance in advantage estimates and more stable policy loss.
3. **Normalization sensitivity - Temperature η sweep:** For Norm-ITPO, evaluate performance across η ∈ {0.1, 0.4, 1.0, 2.0}; anticipate a sweet spot (η≈0.4) where gains peak, with degradation at extremes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does turn-wise aggregation of token-level implicit rewards affect the variance and bias of advantage estimates in multi-turn RL, and what is the theoretically optimal granularity?
- What is the sensitivity of Norm-ITPO’s performance to the Softmax temperature η, and could an adaptive η improve results?
- To what extent do the learned turn-wise rewards generalize across different user dynamics (simulators) or policy architectures? Are they task-specific or transferable?
- Can ITPO be extended to handle partial observability in user goals more explicitly (e.g., by inferring latent goals) rather than relying solely on the implicit reward to capture them?

## Limitations
- Core assumptions (token-level log-likelihood as outcome proxy, turn as atomic credit unit) lack theoretical grounding; causal attribution of token contributions is not validated.
- Dependence on a fixed reference policy π_ref and LLM-based outcome verifier may introduce hidden biases and limit applicability to other domains.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (e.g., temperature η, scaling factor β, length penalty γ) is not thoroughly characterized; performance may degrade with suboptimal settings.
- Evaluation is restricted to Qwen2.5 models on three specific tasks; broader generalization across model families and domains remains unproven.

## Confidence
- Implicit PRM Mechanism: Medium (token–outcome correlation shown but causal link not proven).
- Turn-wise Aggregation: Medium–High (stable convergence and 73–75% human alignment).
- Normalization Stabilization: Medium (improves stability but hinges on η).
- Overall Superiority: High (consistent gains across tasks and RL algorithms).

## Next Checks
1. **Perturbation Test:** Mask the top-contributing tokens (according to implicit PRM scores) in each turn, regenerate trajectories, and measure the resulting change in outcome reward. A steep drop would confirm that the assigned credits are meaningful.
2. **Cross-Domain Transfer:** Train ITPO/Norm-ITPO on the math tutoring task and evaluate zero-shot on document writing. Significant performance loss would indicate that the learned rewards (or policies) are highly task-specific.
3. **Turn Granularity Ablation:** Split each conversational turn into two half-turns and apply ITPO. If performance degrades, it validates the turn-level as the optimal granularity; if it improves, it suggests the current turn unit may be too coarse.
