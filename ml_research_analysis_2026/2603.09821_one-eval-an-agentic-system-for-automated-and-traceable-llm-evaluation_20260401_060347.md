# One-Eval: An Agentic System for Automated and Traceable LLM Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09821  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09821  
- **Reference count:** 38  
- **Primary result:** Automates LLM evaluation with 84% full plan success and traceable workflows.

## Executive Summary
One-Eval tackles the manual effort in evaluating large language models by converting natural-language evaluation requests into end-to-end, traceable workflows. Its agentic system comprises three stages: (1) NL2Bench structures user intent and selects personalized benchmarks, (2) BenchResolve automatically resolves datasets and normalizes schemas for execution, and (3) Metrics & Reporting provides task-aware metric selection and decision-oriented reporting. Human-in-the-loop checkpoints allow refinement. In tests with 100 diverse requests, it achieved 99% plan executability, 85% auto-completion (correct schema/config), and 84% full plan success without human edits, with a median runtime of ~11.4 minutes. This enables reproducible, audit-ready evaluation in industrial settings.

## Method Summary
The system begins with NL2Bench, which interprets natural-language evaluation intents and matches them to appropriate benchmarks, including custom user-provided ones. BenchResolve then automatically acquires datasets, resolves schema mismatches, and normalizes configurations to ensure executability across heterogeneous benchmark formats. Finally, Metrics & Reporting selects relevant metrics beyond scalar scores and generates reports tailored to decision-making contexts. Human oversight is embedded at checkpoints to correct ambiguities or failures, maintaining traceability and auditability throughout the workflow.

## Key Results
- **High executability:** 99% of generated evaluation plans were executable.
- **Strong automation:** 85% auto-completion rate for correct schema and configuration.
- **Robust end-to-end success:** 84% of full plans completed without human edits, median runtime ~11.4 minutes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The agentic design offloads tedious, error-prone manual steps—benchmark selection, schema resolution, metric configuration—to specialized modules that handle variability and normalization. Human-in-the-loop checkpoints intervene only when automated reasoning encounters ambiguity, drastically reducing effort while preserving accuracy and auditability. The traceable workflow logs all decisions, enabling reproducibility and accountability.

## Foundational Learning
- **NL2Bench (natural-language to benchmark):** *Why needed:* Manual benchmark selection is time-consuming and expertise-intensive. *Quick check:* Test with ambiguous or multi-intent requests to verify correct disambiguation and benchmark matching.
- **Schema resolution:** *Why needed:* Dataset schemas vary widely; mismatches cause execution failures. *Quick check:* Introduce intentionally misaligned schemas to stress-test normalization logic.
- **Task-aware metric selection:** *Why needed:* Generic metrics (e.g., accuracy) often miss domain-specific success criteria. *Quick check:* Compare recommended metrics against expert selections on niche tasks (e.g., safety, creativity).
- **Traceable workflow logging:** *Why needed:* Industrial deployments require audit trails for compliance and debugging. *Quick check:* Verify that all steps, including human edits, are logged and reproducible.
- **Personalized benchmark integration:** *Why needed:* Standard benchmarks may not reflect user-specific needs. *Quick check:* Validate custom benchmark ingestion with user-provided datasets and evaluation scripts.
- **Human-in-the-loop checkpoint design:** *Why needed:* Full automation risks silent failures on edge cases. *Quick check:* Measure false-positive/negative rates of automated decisions to calibrate checkpoint sensitivity.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
NL2Bench → BenchResolve → Metrics & Reporting (with human-in-the-loop checkpoints after each stage)  

**Critical path:** Natural-language request → intent structuring → benchmark selection → dataset resolution/schema normalization → metric configuration → report generation.  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Automation vs. flexibility: High automation may limit support for highly custom benchmarks, but One-Eval balances this by accepting user-defined benchmarks.  
- Speed vs. thoroughness: Median runtime is modest (~11.4 min), but complex benchmarks may increase time; tradeoff is managed by heuristic-driven resolution.  
- Scalability vs. resource use: Claims unvalidated on large-scale or multi-modal benchmarks; resolution algorithms may not scale linearly.  

**Failure signatures:**  
- BenchResolve fails to normalize schemas (dataset incompatibility).  
- Metrics & Reporting selects irrelevant metrics for the task.  
- Human checkpoint interventions exceed acceptable thresholds (indicating automation gaps).  

**First experiments:**  
1. Replicate the paper’s 100-request test set to verify reported success rates.  
2. Introduce adversarial schema variations (e.g., nested JSON, missing fields) to probe BenchResolve limits.  
3. Measure human intervention frequency across different domains (e.g., NLP vs. vision) to identify scope of automation.

## Limitations
- Dataset/request diversity unclear: "100 diverse requests" lacks domain/complexity breakdown; may not cover industrial edge cases.
- Human-in-the-loop scope unspecified: Unclear which stages most often require edits; success metrics may underestimate real-world effort.
- Scalability unvalidated: Runtime measured on unspecified hardware; performance on large/complex benchmarks unknown.
- Comparative analysis limited: "Unique support" claims based on limited sampling; broader tool landscape not examined.

## Confidence
- **High:** System design and agentic workflow logic (clearly described).
- **Medium:** Automation success rates (84–99%) – dependent on reported data; potential selection bias.
- **Low:** Generalizability/sustainability claims – no stress-testing on scale, custom schema volatility, or long-term maintenance.
