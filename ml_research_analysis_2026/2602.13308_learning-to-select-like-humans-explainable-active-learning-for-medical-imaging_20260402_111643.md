# Learning to Select Like Humans: Explainable Active Learning for Medical Imaging
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.13308
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13308
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Using only 570 strategically selected samples, EG-AL achieves classification accuracies of 77.22% on BraTS, 52.37% on VinDr-CXR, and 52.66% on SIIM-COVID, consistently outperforming random sampling.

## Executive Summary
Active learning (AL) in medical imaging often selects samples based on uncertainty alone, which may not ensure that the model learns clinically relevant features. This paper introduces EG-AL, a dual-criterion acquisition function that combines classification entropy with Grad-CAM attention misalignment measured by Dice similarity to expert regions of interest (ROIs). By jointly considering uncertainty and spatial explanation quality, EG-AL prioritizes samples that are both uncertain and where the model’s attention is clinically irrelevant. On three datasets (BraTS, VinDr-CXR, SIIM-COVID), EG-AL reaches high accuracy with only 570 labeled images and demonstrates improved Grad-CAM alignment, indicating that the model learns more interpretable and relevant features.

## Method Summary
EG-AL uses an acquisition score Score(x) = λ·H(x) + (1-λ)·D_exp(x), where H(x) is the classification entropy of model predictions, and D_exp(x) = 1 - Dice(Grad-CAM(x), ROI_mask). The hyperparameter λ balances the two terms. Starting from 150 balanced seed samples, the method performs 7 rounds of selection, each adding 60 samples. The model (DenseNet-121 with ImageNet pretraining) is retrained from scratch on the expanded labeled set each round, using a combined training loss: cross-entropy + α·Dice(Grad-CAM, ROI) (α=0.10). The ROI masks are available for all unlabeled images to compute misalignment during acquisition. The approach is evaluated on three medical imaging tasks, showing consistent gains over random sampling and improved visual alignment of Grad-CAM heatmaps with expert annotations.

## Key Results
- EG-AL achieves 77.22% accuracy on BraTS, 52.37% on VinDr-CXR, and 52.66% on SIIM-COVID using only 570 labeled samples.
- The method consistently outperforms random sampling baselines across all datasets.
- Grad-CAM visualizations confirm that models trained with EG-AL-selected samples focus more on diagnostically relevant regions, indicating improved spatial interpretability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The dual-criterion acquisition addresses a key limitation of uncertainty-only AL: uncertain samples may not necessarily teach the model to attend to clinically relevant features. By explicitly penalizing attention misalignment (low Dice between Grad-CAM and expert ROI), EG-AL selects samples that are likely to correct the model’s focus. The entropy term ensures we query difficult examples, while the misalignment term guides learning toward human-like spatial reasoning. The combined score thus steers training toward samples that both challenge the classifier and align model explanations with expert knowledge, leading to better generalization and interpretability.

- **Classification Entropy**: Measures prediction uncertainty; high entropy indicates the model is unsure. *Why needed*: Primary criterion in many AL methods. *Quick check*: Compute entropy on a batch of predictions; confirm it correlates with difficulty.
- **Grad-CAM**: Gradient-based class activation mapping that produces coarse localization maps highlighting relevant image regions. *Why needed*: Provides model attention maps that can be compared to expert ROIs. *Quick check*: Apply Grad-CAM to a pretrained model on a sample; visualize heatmap.
- **Dice Similarity**: Overlap metric between two binary masks (range 0-1). *Why needed*: Quantifies spatial agreement between Grad-CAM and ROI. *Quick check*: Compute Dice between two simple binary masks (e.g., overlapping squares).
- **Dual-criterion Acquisition**: Combines uncertainty and explanation quality into a single score for sample selection. *Why needed*: Encourages learning of relevant features beyond pure uncertainty. *Quick check*: Implement the score formula; verify that high-entropy, low-Dice samples get high scores.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (data flow):
Raw Images + Labels + ROI Masks -> Preprocessing (resize, normalize) -> Labeled Pool & Unlabeled Pool -> DenseNet-121 (with 512-d embedding) -> Grad-CAM on last conv layer -> Compute Entropy & D_exp -> Combine Score -> Top-K Selection -> Add to Labeled Pool -> Retrain Model from Scratch -> Repeat until labeling budget exhausted -> Final Model Evaluation on Test Set.

Critical path:
1. Initialization: Sample 150 balanced seed images.
2. Active learning loop (7 rounds):
   - Train model on current labeled set with loss L = CE + α·Dice(Grad-CAM, ROI).
   - For each unlabeled image, compute H(x) and D_exp(x) using the trained model.
   - Compute Score(x) = λ·H(x) + (1-λ)·D_exp(x).
   - Select top 60 images by score; add to labeled pool.
3. After final round, evaluate on fixed test set (accuracy, macro AUC) and visualize Grad-CAM alignment.

Design tradeoffs:
- λ balancing uncertainty vs misalignment: Too low λ overemphasizes attention, potentially ignoring uncertainty; too high λ reverts to uncertainty-only. Requires tuning per dataset.
- Grad-CAM Dice computation: Raw heatmaps are continuous; thresholding may be needed to create binary masks for Dice. The choice of threshold (e.g., top-50% activations) affects the misalignment score stability.
- Model retraining from scratch each round ensures fairness but is computationally expensive; incremental learning could be faster but may introduce bias.
- α in the training loss controls the strength of attention supervision; too high may dominate cross-entropy.

Failure signatures:
- If score distributions of H and D_exp are imbalanced (e.g., H spans 0-2.5 while D_exp 0-0.1), one term may dominate regardless of λ. Check histograms; consider normalizing each to [0,1] before combination.
- Grad-CAM produces noisy or diffuse heatmaps for small lesions, leading to high D_exp even for correct models. Inspect CAMs on a random subset; if many have low max activation, consider sharpening or using a different XAI method.
- Model fails to improve over random: Could be due to poor λ choice, or that attention supervision (α) hinders classification. Try varying α and λ; monitor training loss components to ensure both terms contribute.

First experiments (validate implementation):
1. **Grad-CAM sanity check**: Run Grad-CAM on a known image (e.g., with a clear tumor) and verify the heatmap highlights the tumor region qualitatively. Compare with the provided ROI mask.
2. **Score distribution check**: On a fixed random unlabeled subset (e.g., 1000 images), compute raw H and D_exp scores, and plot histograms. Confirm they are roughly comparable ranges after any intended normalization; adjust λ if one term is extreme.
3. **Baseline comparison**: Run one active learning round with EG-AL (using chosen λ) and with random sampling. After the first round, train on the selected 60+150=210 samples and evaluate on a small validation set. EG-AL should already show a noticeable improvement over random (or at least not degrade), indicating the acquisition score is meaningful.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Can EG-AL function without pre-existing ROI masks for the entire unlabeled pool?** The paper requires ROI masks for all unlabeled images to compute misalignment, which is often impractical. Evidence to resolve: develop an approximation method for ROIs (e.g., using ensemble saliency) and evaluate against random and uncertainty-only baselines on a dataset without full masks.
- **How does the selection of the XAI technique (beyond Grad-CAM) influence EG-AL's acquisition effectiveness?** Grad-CAM is coarse and architecture-sensitive. Evidence: an ablation study replacing Grad-CAM with Integrated Gradients, SHAP, or attention maps across datasets, analyzing correlation between misalignment scores and actual errors, and reporting final accuracy.
- **Is a fixed λ hyperparameter optimal throughout the active learning cycle?** The relative importance of uncertainty and misalignment likely shifts as the model improves. Evidence: implement an adaptive λ that adjusts based on score distributions each round and compare performance to the best static λ.
- **Can the EG-AL framework be extended to segmentation or detection tasks?** The method is designed for classification; segmentation/detection have different output structures. Evidence: redesign acquisition for segmentation (e.g., pixel-wise entropy and Dice misalignment against an atlas) and validate on a segmentation dataset like BraTS.

## Limitations
- Classification task definitions (number of classes, label mapping) for the three datasets are unclear, making exact replication difficult.
- Model architecture details (512-d embedding) and training procedure (use of prototypical networks) are ambiguous, potentially affecting reproducibility.
- Grad-CAM misalignment scores may be noisy, especially for small or low-contrast lesions; the paper does not specify how heatmaps are thresholded for Dice computation.
