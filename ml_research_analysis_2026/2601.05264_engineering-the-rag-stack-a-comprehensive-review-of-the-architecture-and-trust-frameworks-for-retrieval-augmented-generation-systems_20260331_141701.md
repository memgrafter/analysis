# Engineering the RAG Stack: A Comprehensive Review of the Architecture and Trust Frameworks for Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the architectural fragmentation in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems, which hinders systematic progress and practical deployment. Through a systematic literature review of academic and industrial sources (2018-2025), it consolidates RAG techniques into a unified taxonomy across five dimensions: retrieval strategy, fusion mechanism, modality, adaptivity, and trust calibration. The analysis provides empirical benchmarks showing enterprise deployments achieve up to 3.7x ROI and reduced knowledge updating costs, along with engineering best practices that cut deployment cycles by 45%. The paper also provides quantitative trust frameworks (e.g., RAGAS metrics) and identifies critical anti-patterns, offering a pragmatic reference for building resilient, domain-adaptable RAG systems.

## Method Summary
The paper conducts a systematic literature review of RAG systems from 2018-2025, analyzing academic and industrial sources to identify architectural patterns and consolidation opportunities. It establishes a unified taxonomy across five dimensions: retrieval strategy, fusion mechanism, modality, adaptivity, and trust calibration. The methodology includes empirical benchmarking of enterprise deployments and development of quantitative trust frameworks. Key technical implementations involve canonical RAG architectures (DPR + BART/T5), multi-hop retrieval patterns, fusion mechanisms (early/late/marginal), and agentic orchestration patterns. Evaluation employs both traditional IR metrics and LLM-as-judge frameworks for comprehensive assessment of system performance and trustworthiness.

## Key Results
- Enterprise RAG deployments achieve up to 3.7x ROI and significantly reduced knowledge updating costs
- Engineering best practices can cut deployment cycles by 45% compared to ad-hoc implementations
- Trust calibration frameworks using metrics like RAGAS demonstrate measurable improvements in system reliability and user confidence

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RAG systems work by combining information retrieval with text generation to produce more accurate, contextually relevant responses. The architecture leverages dense vector embeddings for semantic document retrieval, followed by fusion with the original query and generation using sequence-to-sequence models. The mechanism succeeds because it grounds generation in retrieved evidence rather than relying solely on parametric knowledge, reducing hallucination and improving factual accuracy. Multi-hop and iterative retrieval strategies enable complex reasoning over multiple documents, while fusion mechanisms (early, late, marginal) optimize how retrieved information integrates with the generation process. Trust calibration through confidence scoring and abstention mechanisms further enhances reliability by allowing systems to decline answering uncertain queries.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Query Encoder → Document Retriever → Fusion Module → Sequence Generator → Trust Evaluator → Output
- **Critical Path**: Retrieval (bi-encoder + ANN search) → Fusion (context concatenation) → Generation (BART/T5) → Evaluation (LLM-as-judge)
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Single-pass vs. iterative retrieval (simplicity vs. accuracy), fixed vs. dynamic chunking (efficiency vs. semantic integrity), confidence thresholds (coverage vs. reliability)
- **Failure Signatures**: Low context precision (poor retrieval or chunking), high hallucination (generator ignoring context), system hesitation (inadequate trust calibration)
- **First Experiments**: 1) Implement baseline RAG with Sentence-BERT + ANN + BART; 2) Add RAG-Fusion with query rewriting and reciprocal rank fusion (k=60); 3) Deploy RAGAS evaluation on HotpotQA dataset to measure faithfulness and context precision

## Limitations
- The systematic literature review lacks explicit methodological details for study selection, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and quality assessment procedures
- Empirical benchmarks lack clear baseline comparisons and detailed experimental conditions, making generalizability uncertain
- Optimal chunking strategy and trust calibration thresholds require domain-specific tuning not provided in the paper

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Identification of RAG architectural fragmentation as a real problem affecting the field's progress
- **Medium Confidence**: Consolidated taxonomy and engineering best practices, though some categorizations may be arbitrary
- **Low Confidence**: Quantitative trust frameworks and specific ROI figures due to insufficient methodological detail and baseline comparisons
