# Hyperparameter Transfer Laws for Non-Recurrent Multi-Path Neural Networks
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.07494
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07494
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: The optimal learning rate scales as \(L^{-3/2}\) with effective depth for CNNs, ResNets, and Transformers, enabling zero-shot depth transfer via \(\eta(L) = \eta(L_0) (L/L_0)^{-3/2}\).

## Executive Summary
Modern deep networks (CNNs, ResNets, Transformers) are expensive to tune because hyperparameters interact with depth in complex ways. This work introduces a unified notion of *effective depth*—the length of the shortest computational path from input to output, counting layers and residual additions—and proposes *Arithmetic-Mean Maximal Update Parametrization* (AM-µP), a network-wide criterion that keeps the average one-step pre-activation change at initialization approximately constant. Theoretical analysis shows that under stabilizing initializations, the optimal learning rate must scale as \(L^{-3/2}\) with effective depth. Empirically, depth sweeps across nine architecture–dataset combinations yield fitted exponents between –1.18 and –1.57 (mean –1.38), confirming the law and enabling simple zero-shot transfer. Ablations demonstrate robustness to optimizers, activations, normalization, and regularization, with the exponent remaining stable near –3/2.

## Method Summary
The authors combine a graph-theoretic definition of depth with a mean-field analysis of neural network initialization. Effective depth \(L\) is computed by identifying the minimal path length from input to output, where each fundamental transformation unit (e.g., a convolution followed by nonlinearity, a residual block, or a Transformer residual addition) contributes one unit. Under AM-µP, the arithmetic mean of the layerwise second-moment updates \(\bar{S}(\eta) = \frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^L S_\ell(\eta)\) is fixed to 1, controlling the average magnitude of representation change. Linearizing the network at initialization and assuming stabilizing initializations (fan-in scaling, unit-affine LayerNorm), the overlap propagators \(T_\ell\) grow linearly in depth, leading to a cumulative budget \(\bar{S} = \Theta(\eta^2 L^3)\). Setting \(\bar{S}=1\) yields \(\eta \propto L^{-3/2}\). The theory is validated by a minimal viable reproduction: for each architecture (CNN, ResNet, ViT) with various effective depths, perform a logarithmic grid search to find the learning rate that minimizes one-epoch training loss; fit a power law \(\log \eta^* = \beta_0 + \alpha \log L\); the exponent \(\alpha\) should be \(-1.5\). The implementation must respect precise initialization (e.g., residual branch weights scaled by \(1/\sqrt{K}\) for ResNets, LayerNorm parameters at (1,0) for ViT) and count depth units as defined.

## Key Results
- Optimal learning rate scales universally as \(\eta^* \propto L^{-3/2}\) for CNNs, ResNets, and Transformers under AM-µP.
- Empirical exponents range from –1.18 to –1.57 (mean –1.38) across nine architecture–dataset pairs, confirming the law.
- Zero-shot transfer \(\eta(L) = \eta(L_0) (L/L_0)^{-3/2}\) works within ~20% error, turning depth scaling into a predictable hyperparameter-transfer problem.
- Ablations show stability across SGD/Adam, ReLU/GELU, normalization choices, and regularization, though the exponent remains near –1.5 while the prefactor may vary.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The depth scaling law emerges from three interconnected mechanisms.

1. **Effective depth as a unified depth index** (Mechanism 1).  
   The learning rate’s dependence on depth is not merely the raw layer count but on the *minimal computation path* from input to output. Each fundamental unit—a convolution plus nonlinearity, a residual block, or a Transformer residual addition—counts as one depth unit. This graph‑based definition yields a single index \(L\) for heterogeneous architectures. Evidence: the paper defines effective depth in Section 3.1 and applies it uniformly. The approach fails for recurrent or irregular graphs where minimal path length is ill‑defined.

2. **Arithmetic‑Mean µP controls average update magnitude** (Mechanism 2).  
   Classical µP forces every layer’s update energy \(S_\ell\) to be 1; this is too restrictive for multi‑path networks. AM‑ µP instead fixes \(\bar{S} = \frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^L S_\ell = 1\). This allows heterogeneous per‑layer updates while keeping the *typical* unit’s change \(O(1)\). Linearizing gives \(S_\ell = \Theta(\eta^2 \sum_{h_1,h_2\le \ell} \min(h_1,h_2))\). The inner sum equals \(\Theta(\ell^3)\), hence \(\bar{S} = \Theta(\eta^2 L^3)\). Solving \(\bar{S}=1\) yields \(\eta \propto L^{-3/2}\). Evidence: Section B.3 derives the \(T_\ell\) recursion and \(\Theta(\eta^2 \ell^3)\) growth. The break condition requires stabilizing initializations and that the weak‑dependence approximation (product-to-sum conversion for overlaps) holds.

3. **Universality of the –3/2 exponent** (Mechanism 3).  
   For ResNets, the residual‑branch scaling \(W_{\text{res}} \propto K^{-1/2}\) makes the conditional expectation \(E[T_\ell|\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}] = (1 + cq/K) T_{\ell-1} + \dots\), where the factor is \(\Theta(1)\). For Transformers, LayerNorm (with unit‑affine init) and the isotropy of residual branches give \(E[T_\ell|\mathcal{F}_{\ell-1}] = \Theta(1)\cdot T_{\ell-1}\). Thus the per‑unit Jacobian gain is constant, preserving the \(\Theta(\eta^2 L^3)\) budget and the \(-3/2\) exponent. Evidence: Propositions in Sections 3.4 and 3.5; appendix proofs. Break conditions: ResNets without depth‑scaled branch weights; Transformers with non‑unit‑affine LayerNorm or pre‑LN order changes.

## Foundational Learning
Three core concepts underpin the paper:

- **Effective depth as minimal path length**  
  *Why needed*: Provides a single depth index that unifies sequential (CNNs) and multi‑path (ResNets, Transformers) architectures, making depth‑wise hyperparameter transfer possible.  
  *Quick check*: For a ResNet with 3 plain layers and 5 residual blocks, what is its effective depth under the paper’s convention? (Answer: 3 + 5 = 8.)

- **Arithmetic‑Mean µP vs. classical µP**  
  *Why needed*: Classical µP requires every layer’s update energy to be exactly 1, which is incompatible with residual additions that naturally accumulate larger updates. AM‑µP relaxes this by fixing the *average* update, accommodating heterogeneity while still controlling instability.  
  *Quick check*: If layerwise update energies are \(S_1=1\), \(S_2=4\), \(S_3=9\), what is \(\bar{S}\)? Would classical µP be satisfied? What would AM‑µP (\(\bar{S}=1\)) require of the learning‑rate scale? (Answer: \(\bar{S}=14/3\approx4.67\); classical µP not satisfied; to achieve \(\bar{S}=1\), the learning rate must be scaled down by roughly \(\sqrt{4.67}\approx2.16\).)

- **One‑step linearized analysis at initialization**  
  *Why needed*: The maximal‑update principle ties the optimal learning rate to the magnitude of the first gradient step’s pre‑activation change. This reduces the problem to analyzing Jacobian products and their second moments under random initialization, bypassing long‑term dynamics.  
  *Quick check*: Why does the analysis use an MSE loss surrogate even though experiments use cross‑entropy? What \(O(1)\) property must the output‑layer gradient statistics have at initialization? (Answer: MSE gives a simple gradient scalar; the gradient norm at the output must be \(O(1)\) after scaling, which holds for standard head initializations.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
- **CNN**: A chain of \(L\) depth units, each unit = 2‑D convolution (padding circular) + bias‑free linear map + ReLU, with constant channel width \(C\) and He fan‑in initialization. Plain sequential feedforward.
- **ResNet**: First two units (stem conv+ReLU and final linear head) count as plain depth; \(K\) residual blocks each contribute one depth unit. Each block: input → branch (conv+ReLU) → output added to identity skip. Residual‑branch weights scaled by \(1/\sqrt{K}\) (depth‑dependent). No BatchNorm.
- **Transformer (Post‑LN)**: Patch embedding (linear) counts as 1; each Transformer block contributes 2 units (two residual additions); final linear head counts as 1. LayerNorm parameters fixed to \((\gamma=1,\beta=0)\) at initialization; positional embeddings small variance.

### Critical path
1. Enumerate all directed paths from network input to the final pre‑logit representation.
2. For each path, count how many depth units it traverses using the architecture‑specific unit definition.
3. The **effective depth** \(L\) is the *minimum* such count over all paths.
4. To transfer hyperparameters across depths, use \(\eta(L) = \eta(L_0) (L/L_0)^{-3/2}\).

### Design tradeoffs
- **Depth unit granularity** must preserve the minimal‑path count when regrouping layers; coarse vs. fine definitions should not alter the computed \(L\).
- **Residual scaling** in ResNets: the \(1/\sqrt{K}\) factor on branch weights is essential to keep the per‑block update contribution \(\Theta(1)\). Without it, the depth exponent changes.
- **Normalization**: BatchNorm/LayerNorm parameters at init must be close to unit‑affine (\(\mu=0,\sigma=1\)) to bound the LayerNorm Jacobian; learned parameters drift later but the initial scaling law governs early training.

### Failure signatures
- **Depth exponent drift**: Fitted exponent significantly > –1.5 (e.g., –1.0) suggests missing residual‑branch scaling or miscounted depth.
- **Non‑monotonic transfer**: Optimal learning rate does not decrease monotonically with increasing depth, likely due to violation of stabilizing initialization or incorrect effective‑depth assignment.
- **Optimizer sensitivity**: Using Adam shifts the prefactor; if exponent remains stable only under SGD but changes under Adam, this may indicate a breakdown of the mean‑field assumptions for adaptive methods.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Depth sweep validation**: For each architecture, train with varying effective depths \(L\). For each \(L\), perform a log‑grid search over learning rate and select the one minimizing the average one‑epoch training loss. Fit \(\log\eta^*\) vs. \(\log L\); the slope should be \(\approx -1.5\).
2. **Padding ablation (CNN)**: Repeat the depth sweep with circular padding (theory‑consistent) and zero padding. Expect the same slope but different intercepts, confirming that boundary effects are \(O(1)\) corrections.
3. **Cross‑architecture transfer**: Tune \(\eta^*\) at a reference depth \(L_0\) for one architecture (e.g., ResNet‑20). Use \(\eta(L) = \eta(L_0)(L/L_0)^{-3/2}\) to predict optimal rates for deeper variants of the same architecture and for a different architecture (e.g., ViT). Verify that the predictions are within ~20% error (as in Table 1).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Universality beyond the three families**  
   Does the unified \(-3/2\) scaling law hold for all non‑recurrent multi‑path architectures (e.g., GNNs, MLP‑Mixers, NAS cells)? The paper’s theory defines effective depth via minimal path length and AM‑µP, but other designs may violate the assumptions (homogeneous depth units, stabilizing initialization). Systematic experiments and theoretical extensions are needed.

2. **Deviation under out‑of‑distribution conditions**  
   Under what circumstances does the exponent stray from \(-3/2\) when training extends beyond the one‑step linearized regime, when initializations are not perfectly stabilizing, or for extremely deep networks? The current analysis is a first‑order approximation; deeper architectures, non‑Gaussian initializations, or adaptive optimizers could induce corrections.

3. **Multi‑hyperparameter transfer**  
   Can the depth scaling rule be extended to jointly adjust other hyperparameters (batch size, weight decay, warmup steps) in a depth‑dependent manner? The paper focuses on learning‑rate transfer but notes that other knobs mainly affect the prefactor. Whether a composite scaling law exists remains an open question.

## Limitations
- **Effective depth specification** is underspecified for some architectures (e.g., positional embeddings in ViT, exact ResNet block internals), leading to potential mis‑counts.
- **Empirical exponent spread**: Fitted exponents range from –1.18 to –1.57, indicating sensitivity to implementation details, finite‑width effects, or optimizer choices, not always hitting the theoretical \(-3/2\).
- **Theoretical scope**: The analysis assumes a one‑step linearized dynamics at initialization with stabilizing initializations and weak‑dependence; it does not cover recurrent connections, highly irregular graphs, or long‑term nonlinear dynamics.

## Confidence
- **Effective depth & AM‑µP formulation**: High – well‑motivated and consistently applied.
- **η ∝ L⁻³ᐟ² scaling law**: Medium – empirical support strong but exponent variability implies conditional validity.
- **Universality across CNNs, ResNets, Transformers**: Medium – ablations confirm robustness but depend on strict adherence to initialization and depth‑counting rules.
