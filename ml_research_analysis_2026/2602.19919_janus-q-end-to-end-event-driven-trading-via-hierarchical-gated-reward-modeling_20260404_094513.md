# Janus-Q: End-to-End Event-Driven Trading via Hierarchical-Gated Reward Modeling
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.19919
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19919
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: A two-stage LLM trading system with a Hierarchical Gated Reward Model improved Sharpe Ratio by up to 102% and directional accuracy by over 17.5% over baselines on a curated event-centric dataset.

## Executive Summary
Janus-Q addresses the fundamental misalignment between news-driven semantic reasoning and profitable trading by constructing a large-scale, fine-grained event dataset with market-grounded impact labels (Cumulative Abnormal Returns). It employs a two-stage training process: first, supervised fine-tuning to teach the model the basic mapping from news to event type, direction, strength, and predicted impact; second, reinforcement learning with a novel Hierarchical Gated Reward Model (HGRM) that decomposes reward into interpretable, prioritized components (direction, event-type consistency, magnitude, process). This design prevents reward hacking and aligns the model's reasoning with executable trading decisions, leading to significant improvements in risk-adjusted returns and prediction accuracy.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is a two-stage pipeline. Stage I constructs a dataset of 62,400 news articles linked to Chinese A-share stocks. Each article is manually labeled with one of 10 event types and assigned a target label comprising event type, trading direction (Buy/Sell/Hold), strength, and the event-driven Cumulative Abnormal Return (CAR). Stage II first performs Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with LoRA on a base LLM using a structured prompt to predict this composite label. Then, Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with the custom HGRM. The HGRM is a deterministic function that applies a "hard gate" on direction correctness and a "soft gate" on event-type consistency to shape the final reward from profit and magnitude components, enforcing a hierarchy of financial primitives during optimization.

## Key Results
- Sharpe Ratio (SR) improved by up to 102% over baselines like GPT-4 and specialized models.
- Directional Accuracy (DA) increased by over 17.5%.
- Event-Type Accuracy (ETA) saw gains of up to 18.2%.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical synergy of SFT and the hierarchical reward structure; removing SFT causes DA to drop ~14% and SR to become negative.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: A large-scale, fine-grained event-centric dataset with market-grounded impact labels (CAR) provides the necessary supervision to learn heterogeneous event-to-market mappings.
- **Mechanism**: Dataset construction uses a classical event study methodology. An estimation window models normal returns via a market model and CNE5 risk model; an event window computes CAR to isolate price movement attributable to the news. Manual labels for 10 fine-grained event types create a joint semantic-impact supervision signal.
- **Core assumption**: Computed CAR accurately represents the economically meaningful, event-driven impact, and manual event-type labels are consistent.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract cites 62,400-article dataset with CAR; Section 3.1.1 details MR, RM neutralization, and CAR aggregation; corpus shows unique joint modeling of event type, sentiment, and CAR.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: A Hierarchical Gated Reward Model (HGRM) resolves misalignment between an LLM's semantic reasoning and executable, profitable trading by decomposing reward into interpretable, gated components.
- **Mechanism**: HGRM reward is hierarchical. A "hard gate" `g_dir` blocks all subsequent reward if predicted direction is opposite to ground truth. If direction is correct/neutral, a "soft gate" (`m_evt`) discounts profit-based rewards (`r_pnl`) if predicted event type is incorrect. Magnitude shaping (`r_mag`) and process rewards (`r_proc`) provide finer signals. This forces prioritization: correct direction → event-type consistency → accurate magnitude.
- **Core assumption**: The defined gates and parameterizations (e.g., `λ_dir > 1`) effectively capture the financial primacy of direction and that event-type consistency is necessary for trustworthy reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions HGRM capturing trade-offs; Section 3.2.2 defines `s_dir`, `s_evt`, `m_evt` and Eq. 10; neighbors use simpler reward designs.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: A two-stage training paradigm (SFT then RL with GRPO) is more effective than either alone for building a stable, decision-oriented policy from event-centric data.
- **Mechanism**: SFT establishes a robust initial policy by teaching the basic mapping from news context to `{event type, direction, strength, CAR}` via structured prompts. RFT with GRPO then optimizes this policy directly for the final trading objective using HGRM. The SFT stage provides a stable prior that prevents RL from diverging into nonsensical but temporarily rewarding strategies.
- **Core assumption**: SFT instills sufficient understanding of event semantics and market impact structure that GRPO can refine for decision quality without forgetting core concepts.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract cites combination of SL and RL; Section 4.6.1 ablation shows "w/o SFT" causes severe drop (DA ↓14%, SR negative); "Trading-R1" uses RL but without explicit preceding SFT.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Event Study Methodology (CAR Calculation)
  - **Why needed**: The entire data foundation rests on correctly computing event-driven abnormal return (CAR). Misunderstanding estimation window, market/risk model, or aggregation period invalidates target labels.
  - **Quick check**: Given an event date, explain the purpose of the estimation window `Test` vs. event window `Tevt` and why a risk model (CNE5) is applied after the market model.
- **Concept**: Reinforcement Learning from Policy Feedback (RLHF/RLAIF) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)
  - **Why needed**: Stage II uses GRPO to optimize the LLM policy against the composite HGRM reward. Understanding on-policy nature, KL penalty, and group sampling is crucial for implementing the fine-tuning loop.
  - **Quick check**: What is the primary role of the "reference policy" (`ref`) in GRPO, and how does it differ from standard PPO?
- **Concept**: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (e.g., LoRA)
  - **Why needed**: The paper uses LoRA for SFT. An engineer must understand rank (`r`), alpha (`α`), and target modules to configure adaptation without full retraining.
  - **Quick check**: How does LoRA's low-rank decomposition (`W = W₀ + ΔW = W₀ + BA`) reduce trainable parameters, and what is the typical relationship between `r` and `α`?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Data Pipeline (raw news + prices, CAR computation, manual annotation) -> SFT Module (prompt + dataset -> LLM with LoRA) -> HGRM (deterministic function: model output + ground truth -> scalar reward) -> RL Trainer (GRPO: SFT model as initial policy, sample responses, HGRM reward, policy gradient updates with KL penalty)
- **Critical path**: Data Construction (CAR & Labels) -> SFT Training -> Design/Implement HGRM -> GRPO Fine-Tuning -> Backtesting Evaluation. SFT model quality directly limits RL optimization ceiling.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Annotation Cost vs. Data Quality**: Manual event typing is expensive but ensures financial relevance; could weak supervision/semi-automation be used?
  - **Reward Complexity vs. Stability**: HGRM's hierarchical gates improve alignment but introduce hyperparameters (`λ_dir`, `λ_evt`, `α`, `τ`); tuning is critical.
  - **SFT vs. RL**: SFT provides stability but may cap performance; RL optimizes true objective but can destabilize; two-stage approach balances this but requires coordinated hyperparameters.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Poor CAR Alignment**: Model's predicted `ĉ` uncorrelated with true `c`. Check SFT MAE/RMSE and `r_mag` effectiveness.
  - **Reward Hacking Despite Gates**: Model learns correct `d̂` but incorrect `ê` to get reward (if `m_evt` discount `α` too high). Monitor `ETA` vs. `DA` gap.
  - **Mode Collapse**: Policy predicts only `Hold` or always `Strong`. Check strength prediction distribution.
  - **Backtest-Performance Decay**: High training `SR` but low test `SR`. Indicates overfitting or non-stationarity.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Dataset Integrity Check**: Recompute CAR for 5% random sample using MR+RM; confirm event-type inter-annotator agreement (Kappa).
  2. **SFT Sanity Check**: After SFT, evaluate zero-shot `DA`, `ETA`, and `MAE` on validation set. Low `ETA` indicates flawed SFT.
  3. **HGRM Gate Isolation Test**: Run GRPO with modified rewards (e.g., remove `g_dir`, set `α=1`). Compare resulting `DA`/`ETA`/`SR` to full HGRM.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1**: How can Janus-Q’s event taxonomy be extended to support hierarchical or multi-label event categorization to capture nuanced or overlapping event characteristics?
- **Question 2**: What multimodal data sources (e.g., earnings call audio, visual charts, social media images) can be integrated, and how should the architecture fuse these with textual news?
- **Question 3**: Does the strategy generalize to other financial markets (e.g., U.S. equities, cryptocurrencies) with different regulations and microstructures?
- **Question 4**: How sensitive is performance to the event window definition in CAR calculation, and would adaptive/event-type-specific windows improve robustness?
- **Question 5**: To what extent does the HGRM's manual design limit adaptability to evolving regimes, and can these components be learned dynamically?

## Limitations
- **CAR correctness**: Event study methodology may fail to isolate true event impact, poisoning supervision labels.
- **Annotation agreement**: Manual event typing lacks reported inter-annotator reliability metrics.
- **HGRM tuning**: Gate thresholds and penalties are hand-tuned and hyperparameter-sensitive.
- **Out-of-sample risk**: Backtest gains may not generalize due to non-stationary impacts or overfitting.
