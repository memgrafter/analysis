# Towards Explainable Deep Learning for Ship Trajectory Prediction in Inland Waterways

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04472
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04472
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** Final displacement error ~40 m for a 5‑minute prediction horizon; the best model’s learned ship domain parameters do not consistently increase for closing opponents, revealing a gap between accuracy and plausible interaction logic.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the lack of explainability in deep learning models for ship trajectory prediction in inland waterways, where understanding the reasoning behind predictions is essential for safety and trust. It introduces a trainable ship domain parameter tensor within an LSTM encoder‑decoder architecture to modulate attention weights over neighboring vessels’ hidden states, making the fusion of interaction information interpretable. Three model variants explore separating interaction‑aware and interaction‑agnostic prediction paths. Although the top model (E‑DA) achieves a competitive final displacement error of approximately 40 meters for a 5‑minute horizon, analysis of the learned parameters shows it assigns low weights to opposing vessels even on collision courses. This disconnect between performance and expected maritime interaction logic underscores the need for evaluation beyond standard error metrics and highlights a performance‑interpretability gap.

## Method Summary
The method uses an LSTM‑based encoder‑decoder with spatial attention. Each vessel’s trajectory is encoded into hidden states. During decoding, a trainable 3×4×4 ship domain tensor **S**(Γ,Θ,Φ) computes a weight for every neighbor (including the vessel itself in some variants) based on discretized relative lateral distance (Γ), relative heading (Θ), and distance change rate (Φ). The weight is *w* = max(**S**(Γ,Θ,Φ) – Δ, 0), where Δ is the longitudinal distance. Neighbor hidden states are weighted and summed to form an interactive state, which is combined with the target’s previous prediction to generate the next step. Three variants differ: EA‑DA (attention in encoder and decoder), E‑DA (attention only in decoder), and E‑DDA (dual decoders: one interaction‑agnostic, one interaction‑aware). Training runs for 750 epochs with a hidden size of 300; **S** is initialized to 0.1 wkm.

## Key Results
- Final displacement error (FDE) for a 5‑minute horizon is approximately 40 m, competitive with existing approaches.
- Analysis of the learned ship domain tensor reveals that the best‑performing model (E‑DA) assigns low weights to opposing vessels even when they are closing, contrary to maritime expectations.
- The model with more plausible **S** values (E‑DDA) exhibits higher error, exposing a performance‑interpretability gap: high accuracy does not guarantee the model uses reasonable interaction reasoning.

## Why This Works
The model’s explainability stems from a trainable ship domain tensor **S**(Γ,Θ,Φ) that modulates attention weights over neighboring vessel hidden states. For each target vessel *i* and neighbor *j* at time *t*, discretized encounter features (Γ, Θ, Φ) index into **S**; the weight is *w*ₜᵢⱼ = max(**S**(Γₜᵢⱼ,Θₜᵢⱼ,Φₜᵢⱼ) – Δₜᵢⱼ, 0), where Δₜᵢⱼ is longitudinal distance. The weighted sum of neighbor hidden states forms an interactive state fed to the decoder. This mechanism makes the selection of “relevant” ships interpretable: inspecting **S** reveals whether the model assigns higher thresholds (and thus higher weights) to encounter types that maritime rules deem critical (e.g., opposing, closing ships). The paper finds that for the best model (E‑DA), **S** values for closing opponents are low and even decrease with negative distance change, indicating the model’s high accuracy does not reliably arise from correctly modeling interactions.

Separating the prediction path into interaction‑agnostic (target‑only) and interaction‑aware (attended neighbors) components (E‑DDA) isolates the effect of ship‑to‑ship attention. The dual‑decoder structure allows assessing how much the interaction pathway contributes, but this variant shows less accurate and sometimes counterintuitive **S** values (e.g., higher weights for laterally distant ships), suggesting the separation may discard useful self‑context.

Together, these design choices expose a potential “performance‑interpretability gap”: a model can achieve low displacement error while its learned interaction logic (as encoded in **S**) deviates from human‑expected maritime behavior. This gap challenges the trustworthiness of interaction‑aware predictors and calls for evaluation that goes beyond aggregate error metrics.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Input Preprocessor → Encoder LSTM (EncLSTM) → Ship Domain Tensor **S** → Weighting Function ωᵢ(ℎₜ) → Attention Function α → Decoder LSTM(s) (single for EA‑DA/E‑DA, dual for E‑DDA) → Output Layer f_out

**Critical path**  
For each target vessel *i* at prediction step *t*: Encoder produces hidden states ℎₜ for all vessels → compute Γ, Θ, Φ for every pair (i,j) → index **S** to get domain values → compute weights *w*ₜᵢⱼ = max(**S**(Γ,Θ,Φ) – Δₜᵢⱼ, 0) → weight and sum neighbor hidden states to form interactive state → decoder LSTM updates with previous prediction *y*ₜ₋₁ and interactive state → f_out projects to Δ*k*, Δ*f* prediction.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Separating weighting from attention improves modularity for XAI but may hinder joint optimization of the two stages.  
- Excluding the vessel itself from the neighbor set in E‑DDA clarifies neighbor relevance but discards self‑context, making **S** purely external and possibly harming performance.  
- Fixed discretization of Γ, Θ, Φ reduces **S**’s complexity but may group dissimilar encounters, masking nuanced interaction rules.

**Failure signatures**  
- **S** values for critical encounters (e.g., Θ = –1, Φ < 0) near initialization (0.1 wkm) or decreasing during training.  
- E‑DDA showing **S** increasing with lateral distance, counterintuitive if closer ships should matter more.  
- Large performance drop in E‑DDA versus E‑DA, suggesting the separation discarded necessary signals.

**First three experiments**  
1. **Baseline replication:** Train E‑D (no attention) and E‑DA; confirm E‑DA improves validation FDE₅.  
2. **Ship domain audit:** Extract final **S** for test encounters; plot distributions by (Θ, Φ) bins; quantify % of closing opponents (Θ = –1, Φ < 0) with **S** < 0.15 wkm.  
3. **Interaction isolation test:** For situations with ≥1 neighbor within 0.5 wkm, compare FDE₅ of a purely interaction‑agnostic path (BlindLSTM alone) versus the full model.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What evaluation can isolate the causal effect of ship‑to‑ship interaction?** Displacement error conflates interaction and non‑interaction effects; learned weights may not reflect true interactions. Evidence needed: metrics that compare attention patterns with maritime rules or counterfactual perturbations varying only interaction inputs.
2. **Why does the best model (E‑DA) assign low weights to opposing vessels even on collision courses yet achieve low error?** The model may rely on non‑interaction cues or the attention mechanism may be mis‑specified, raising safety risks. Evidence: ablation studies removing attention and feature importance analysis on collision cases.
3. **Why does E‑DDD weight laterally distant ships more heavily, contrary to maritime expectations?** Could be an artifact of the ship domain definition or reflect hidden navigation patterns. Evidence: expert validation of case studies where high weight is given to large‑distance neighbors.
4. **How can the trainable ship domain parameters be used for counterfactual explanations?** Their potential to generate “what‑if” scenarios for explainability and safety remains untested. Evidence: a framework that perturbs neighbor positions and observes resulting weight and prediction changes, validated against human intuition and rules.

## Limitations
- Data preprocessing details are missing: computation of Δ*k*, Δ*f*, waterway curvature features, and situation generation rely on proprietary trip‑splitting and kilometerization algorithms.
- Training hyperparameters are undisclosed (learning rate, weight decay, gradient clipping, batch handling for variable vessel counts, early stopping).
- The ship domain tensor **S** may suffer from sparse gradients due to the max(S–Δ, 0) operation zeroing many weights; there is no quantitative ground truth for plausibility of learned **S** values.
- Evaluation is confined to a single inland waterway segment; generalization to other rivers or sea areas is uncertain.
- E‑DDA’s performance drop suggests that separating the interaction‑aware pathway may discard useful self‑context or that attention alone is insufficient.
