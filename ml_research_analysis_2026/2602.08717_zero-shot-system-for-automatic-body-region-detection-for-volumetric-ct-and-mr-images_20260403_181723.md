# Zero-shot System for Automatic Body Region Detection for Volumetric CT and MR Images
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08717
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08717
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: A zero-shot body region detection system for CT/MR scans using a segmentation-driven rule-based pipeline achieves weighted F1-scores of 0.947 (CT) and 0.914 (MR), outperforming MLLM-based and supervised baselines.

## Executive Summary
The paper proposes a zero-shot system to automatically detect body regions in volumetric CT and MR images, addressing the unreliability of DICOM metadata. The core method leverages a pre-trained multi-organ segmentation model (TotalSegmentator) and radiologist-defined anatomical rules to infer regions from the presence and spatial arrangement of segmented structures. Evaluated on 887 scans, the rule-based pipeline attains the strongest performance with weighted F1-scores of 0.947 (CT) and 0.914 (MR), demonstrating robustness across modalities and atypical scan coverage. Alternative approaches using a multimodal large language model (MLLM) and a segmentation-aware hybrid are also explored, with the MLLM showing competitive performance in visually distinctive regions but failing on ambiguous cases.

## Method Summary
The system operates without task-specific training. For the primary segmentation-driven pipeline, each volume is processed by TotalSegmentator (using modality-specific models) to obtain binary masks for a fixed set of anatomical structures. A downstream rule engine maps the presence, absence, and spinal levels of specific organs and vertebrae to predefined region boundaries. Three independent analyses—Vertebrae Presence, Region Extent, and Edge Slices—are computed per region and must satisfy quantitative thresholds (e.g., at least 60% of expected vertebrae present). The final label is an ordered concatenation of all qualifying regions (e.g., "head+neck+chest+abdomen+pelvis"). Two MLLM-based baselines use composite 2D projections and a system prompt encoding the same anatomical rules; one variant also injects segmentation outputs as textual evidence. The best results come from the deterministic rule-based approach, while the MLLM and hybrid methods exhibit limitations in specificity and multimodal reasoning.

## Key Results
- Weighted F1-score: 0.947 (CT), 0.914 (MR) on 887 scans.
- Outperforms MLLM-based and supervised baselines across most regions.
- Robust to varying scan coverage and works across both CT and MR modalities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A deterministic rule-based system operating on high-quality multi-organ segmentation outputs can reliably infer body regions without any training.
- **Mechanism:** Pre-trained segmentation models (TotalSegmentator) produce binary masks for a fixed set of anatomical structures. A downstream rule engine maps the presence, absence, and spinal levels of specific organs/vertebrae to predefined region boundaries (e.g., liver → abdomen; T1–T12 → chest). The final region label is aggregated from three independent analytical criteria (Spinal Column, Region Extent, Edge Slices) that must each meet quantitative thresholds (e.g., ≥60% expected vertebrae).
- **Core assumption:** The upstream segmentation model is sufficiently accurate and consistent across CT and MR to serve as a proxy for ground-truth anatomy; the radiologist-defined rules accurately map segmented structures to body regions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports weighted F1-scores of 0.947 (CT) and 0.914 (MR); Section II.B details the three-criteria analysis and thresholds; neighboring work (e.g., CTARR) supports the segmentation-atlas paradigm.
- **Break condition:** Segmentation failure for a key organ/vertebra directly causes a rule failure; noted as a key limitation in Section V.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A general-purpose Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM), guided only by textual rules and orthogonal 2D projections, can perform zero-shot body region detection via visual reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** The 3D volume is preprocessed into composite axial/sagittal/coronal center slices. This image and a system prompt containing radiologist-defined anatomical rules are fed to the MLLM (Gemini 2.5). The model is instructed to synthesize a single label by visually assessing whether ≥≈60% of each region is visible across the views.
- **Core assumption:** The MLLM possesses inherent visual-spatial understanding of human anatomy and can apply abstract percentage-based completeness rules to 2D projections.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes competitive performance in visually distinctive regions (e.g., head F1=0.931 in MR); Section II.C describes prompt engineering; the paper’s own evaluation provides primary evidence.
- **Break condition:** The MLLM lacks specificity for "other" scans (CT F1=0.108) and performs poorly in regions with subtle boundaries (e.g., CT head F1=0.681; MR neck F1=0.400).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Providing the MLLM with explicit, structured anatomical facts (segmentation outputs) alongside images would ground its reasoning and improve accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** A hybrid "segmentation-aware" MLLM receives the composite image plus a text list of segmented organs and vertebral levels. The hypothesis is that textual evidence would constrain the model to verify visual cues, reducing hallucination.
- **Core assumption:** The MLLM can perform robust sequential multimodal reasoning: use textual facts as a hypothesis, then visually verify them to assess completeness within the correct region.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes the hybrid; Section II.D notes it remains training-free; Section IV reports that performance did not improve—recall spikes (e.g., chest 1.00) but precision collapses (CT 0.619, MR 0.483).
- **Break condition:** The MLLM fails to integrate auxiliary evidence, falling back on over-prediction whenever characteristic organs are mentioned, ignoring the completeness requirement. This reveals a limitation in zero-shot sequential reasoning.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Multi-Organ Medical Image Segmentation
- **Why needed:** The entire segmentation-driven pipeline relies on TotalSegmentator's outputs. Its class ontology (117 CT/75 MR classes), modality-specific models, and known failure modes must be understood to diagnose errors.

### Concept: Rule-Based Systems for Medical Inference
- **Why needed:** The top-performing method is a deterministic engine applying IF-THEN logic on discrete anatomical findings. Engineers must understand how to modify radiologist-defined boundaries, organ-to-region mappings, and thresholds for clinical variations.
- **Quick check:** How would you adjust the "Region Extent" threshold for the abdomen (currently 15–25 cm, 70–130% win band) if scans frequently cover only the diaphragm to the kidneys?

### Concept: Prompt Engineering for Multimodal LLMs
- **Why needed:** The MLLM pipelines' success hinges on a system prompt that encodes anatomical rules. Engineers must craft unambiguous, structured instructions to coax the model into a specific classification schema while adhering to logical constraints.
- **Quick check:** What two specific prompt design strategies are used to force the MLLM to output a normalized, concatenated label (e.g., "chest+abdomen") instead of free-form text?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map (as plain text)
- Segmentation pipeline: Input Handler → Segmentation Backend → Analytical Engine → Decision Aggregator → Output Normalizer
- MLLM pipeline: Input Handler → Composite Image Generator → MLLM Client → Output Normalizer
- Hybrid pipeline: Segmentation pipeline (to Structured Facts) → MLLM Client (with facts) → Output Normalizer

Additional components: Prompt Manager (feeds system prompts to MLLM Client) and Output Normalizer (parses responses into canonical labels).

### Critical path
- For the rule-based method: Input → Segmentation → compute three criteria (vertebrae presence, organ count, region extent) → apply per-region win logic → assemble final label.
- For the MLLM method: Input → generate composite image → call MLLM with prompt → parse response → output label.
- The hybrid combines segmentation output as textual facts before the MLLM step.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Rule-based vs. MLLM:** Rule-based offers deterministic, auditable, high-accuracy results but is rigid and depends on segmentation quality. MLLM offers flexibility but is non-deterministic and less specific.
- **Threshold selection:** Heuristic thresholds (e.g., 60% vertebrae, 30% organs, extent bands) balance sensitivity/specificity; tighter thresholds risk under-detection, looser risk over-prediction.
- **Modality handling:** Separate TotalSegmentator models for CT and MR; organ/vertebrae class lists differ, requiring modality-aware rule application.

### Failure signatures
- **Segmentation pipeline:** Missing critical organs/vertebrae; inconsistent vertebral labeling; discrepancy between organ-based extent and edge-slice extent.
- **MLLM pipeline:** Non-canonical output (e.g., "lower chest"); high recall/low precision in hybrid mode; consistent failure on "other" scans.
- **Shared:** Extremely truncated scans may be misclassified if edge-slice analysis finds no disqualifying organs but insufficient region organs.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Segmentation sanity check:** Run a known whole-body CT scan through the full pipeline. Verify that all expected organs (brain, lungs, liver, spleen, kidneys, bladder) and a continuous vertebra chain (C1–L5) are segmented, and that the output label is "head+neck+chest+abdomen+pelvis".
2. **Threshold stress test:** On a validation set of partial abdomen scans (e.g., liver present, kidneys cut), vary the abdomen organ count "O" threshold (20%–50%) per Table III and plot F1-score vs. threshold to identify the point where recall drops sharply.
3. **MLLM prompt robustness:** For a challenging case (e.g., CT abdomen-only), test five prompt variants: (a) original rules, (b) rules with examples, (c) minimal rules, (d) explicit "if unsure, output 'other'", (e) chain-of-thought. Measure output consistency and accuracy to quantify prompt sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can foundation models be enabled to coherently integrate multimodal anatomical evidence and perform robust sequential reasoning for zero-shot medical tasks?**  
   The Discussion highlights this as an open question, noting the failure of the segmentation-aware MLLM to improve upon the visual-only baseline.

2. **Can the robustness of the segmentation-driven rule-based system be improved by ensembling outputs from multiple diverse segmentation models to mitigate upstream segmentation failures?**  
   The Limitations section points out the direct dependency on upstream segmentation performance; ensembling could address errors on atypical anatomy.

3. **What specific modifications to MLLM prompting or architecture are necessary to prevent over-reliance on provided textual anatomical evidence and maintain decision specificity?**  
   The hybrid approach’s catastrophic precision drop suggests current MLLMs cannot properly fuse textual and visual cues without tailored interventions.

4. **Does improving the coverage of cervical vertebrae in MR segmentation models enhance neck region detection in the rule-based system?**  
   The uniformly low performance on MR neck is attributed to insufficient cervical vertebrae segmentation in the pre-trained MR model; better coverage may improve detection.

## Limitations
- **Segmentation dependency:** The rule-based pipeline’s accuracy is directly tied to TotalSegmentator’s performance; missed organs or vertebrae cause incorrect region labels.
- **MLLM brittleness:** The pure visual reasoning MLLM fails on "other" scans (CT F1=0.108) and regions with subtle visual boundaries (e.g., MR neck F1=0.400).
- **Hybrid over-prediction:** Adding segmentation text as evidence leads to high recall but low precision (~0.5) due to the MLLM’s tendency to over-predict.
- **Reproduction ambiguities:** The mapping from Table II to TotalSegmentator class names is unspecified, and the integration strategy for the two MR models is unclear.

## Confidence
- **Rule-based segmentation pipeline:** High confidence, supported by strong cross-modality results (F1>0.91) and deterministic logic.
- **Zero-shot MLLM visual reasoning:** Medium confidence; competitive in some regions but inconsistent and fails on edge cases.
- **Segmentation-aware MLLM hybrid:** Low confidence; the paper’s own evaluation shows no improvement and highlights fundamental reasoning limitations.

## Next Checks

2. **Threshold sensitivity sweep:** On partial abdomen scans, vary the organ count threshold (20–50%) and record F1-score to identify the threshold where recall drops.
