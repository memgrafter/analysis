# The performances of the Chinese and U.S. Large Language Models on the Topic of Chinese Culture
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.02830
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02830
- **Reference Count:** 0
- **Primary Result:** Chinese LLMs significantly outperformed U.S. LLMs on a 629-question benchmark of traditional Chinese culture, with near-perfect scores on objective formats and a >16% gap on short-answer questions.

## Executive Summary
This study benchmarks ten large language models from China and the United States on their knowledge of traditional Chinese culture using a 629-question dataset. In a direct-questioning setup without search capabilities, Chinese models achieved near-perfect accuracy on multiple-choice and fill-in-the-blank questions, while U.S. models failed to reach 100%. The performance gap widened for short-answer questions, where Chinese models averaged over 80% accuracy compared to under 64% for U.S. models. The most pronounced differences appeared in poetry and literary classics, with some question error rates exceeding 50% for U.S. models.

## Method Summary
The evaluation used a fixed dataset of 629 questions spanning multiple domains of traditional Chinese culture, formatted as multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, and short-answer items. All models were queried with a direct-questioning paradigm; search functionalities were disabled, and default inference parameters were used. Model responses were scored against a reference answer key. The study did not report details on the answer key's construction, scoring automation for open-ended responses, or statistical validation of results.

## Key Results
- Chinese LLMs achieved approximately 100% accuracy on multiple-choice and fill-in-the-blank questions; no U.S. model reached 100%.
- For short-answer questions, Chinese models averaged >80% accuracy versus <64% for U.S. models, a gap of 16.38 percentage points.
- The largest performance discrepancies occurred in the poetry and literary classics categories, with some individual questions having error rates above 50% for U.S. models.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Pretraining Data Collection -> Tokenizer Training -> Base Model Training -> Supervised Fine-Tuning -> Inference Pipeline

**Critical Path:**  
The critical path for performance on cultural knowledge likely originates in the **Pretraining Data Collection** phase, as the volume and diversity of Chinese cultural texts in the training corpus directly shape the model's knowledge base. This is followed by **Tokenizer Training**, which must adequately represent classical Chinese characters and compounds. **Base Model Training** then internalizes patterns, while **Supervised Fine-Tuning** may align model outputs to the question-answer format. The **Inference Pipeline** (including decoding strategies) finalizes response generation.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Data Scale vs. Depth:* Broad pretraining on general web text may dilute deep cultural knowledge versus curated, high-quality cultural corpora.
- *Sequence Length:* Classical texts and poetry often require long context windows; tradeoffs exist between model efficiency and preserving full passages.
- *Objective Function:* Standard language modeling may not optimize for precise factual recall in niche cultural domains.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Hallucination of plausible but incorrect historical or literary details, especially in open-ended answers.
- Inconsistent performance across sub-domains (e.g., strong on historical facts but weak on poetic analysis).
- Lower confidence scores or higher entropy in outputs for culturally specific queries.

**First Experiments:**  
1. **Data Ablation:** Fine-tune a base model on a controlled subset of Chinese cultural text (e.g., only classical poetry) and measure performance change on poetry vs. non-poetry questions.
2. **Search Augmentation:** Re-run the short-answer evaluation with a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) setup using a Chinese cultural corpus to isolate the effect of parametric vs. external knowledge.
3. **Cross-Lingual Probe:** Translate the question set into English and re-evaluate the U.S. models to determine if performance gaps persist when cultural context is linguistically neutralized.

## Limitations
- The single benchmark dataset of 629 questions lacks reported validation for cultural bias, difficulty calibration, or expert review from both cultural contexts.
- The model selection is small and non-exhaustive (10 total models), potentially not representing the current landscape of LLM capabilities, especially newer releases.
- The "disabled search" and default parameter conditions limit ecological validity, as real-world use often involves retrieval augmentation or prompt engineering.
- Short-answer scoring methodology (automated vs. human) and its reliability are unspecified, introducing potential subjectivity.
- No statistical significance testing is reported for the observed performance differences.

## Confidence
- **High:** Chinese LLMs' near-perfect accuracy on multiple-choice/fill-in-the-blank questions (objective formats reduce scoring ambiguity).
- **Medium:** The measured short-answer gap (>80% vs. <64%) given dependence on unverified scoring and potential dataset quality issues.
- **Medium:** The assertion that poetry/literary classics show the largest gaps, based on subset analysis with unspecified error distribution.

## Next Checks
1. **Audit the question set:** Engage domain experts from both cultural contexts to validate question neutrality, domain coverage (e.g., regional traditions, modern vs. classical), and difficulty distribution.
2. **Replicate with retrieval augmentation:** Repeat the evaluation with search/retrieval enabled to determine if the performance gap persists when external knowledge is accessible.
3. **Expand model pool:** Include at least five newer LLMs from each region (e.g., 2023–2024 releases) to assess whether the gap narrows or widens with state-of-the-art models.
