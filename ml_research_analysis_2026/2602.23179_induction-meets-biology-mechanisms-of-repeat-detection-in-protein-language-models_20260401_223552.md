# Induction Meets Biology: Mechanisms of Repeat Detection in Protein Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23179
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23179
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** A unified circuit, shared between exact and approximate repeat detection, uses early positional/biochemical alignment, middle-layer induction heads for token copying, and late MLP refinement, achieving >99% accuracy on exact repeats and ~79% on approximate repeats.

## Executive Summary
This study reverse-engineers how protein language models (PLMs) complete repeating sequence segments. Using circuit discovery across synthetic, identical, and approximate repeat prediction tasks, the authors find that a single circuit for approximate repeats functionally subsumes the circuit for exact repeats. The mechanism operates in three stages: early layers create a position-aware, biochemically informed alignment between repeat instances using relative-position attention heads and MLP neurons that encode amino-acid similarity (aligning with BLOSUM62 scores); middle-layer induction heads then attend to the aligned token in the other repeat instance to retrieve the correct amino acid; and late MLP neurons refine the final output distribution. Neuron analysis reveals interpretable groups selective for specific amino acids, biochemical properties, and repeat positions. Both ESM-3 and ESM-C share this core mechanism, with ESM-3 additionally employing secondary-structure neurons due to its multi-modal training.

## Method Summary
The authors identify minimal circuits sufficient for repeat detection using attribution patching (AP-IG/EAP-IG). They generate three datasets: synthetic repeats (random sequences with two identical repeats), natural identical repeats (UniRef50 sequences filtered for two exact repeats), and natural approximate repeats (same but with up to 50% substitutions). Counterfactuals for circuit discovery are created by replacing every amino acid in the non-masked repeat with its highest-scoring BLOSUM62 substitution. For each task, components (attention heads and MLP blocks) are ranked by importance and added incrementally until a circuit achieves ≥85% faithfulness relative to the full model. Cross-task comparison (structural IoU/recall and functional faithfulness) tests whether the approximate-repeat circuit subsumes others. Neuron-level importance is matched to curated biological concepts (amino acids, BLOSUM62 groups, secondary structure) via AUROC. Edge-level circuits reveal interactions, and logit lens tracks the evolution of correct token probability across component groups.

## Key Results
- A single circuit for approximate repeats (∼17% of components in ESM-3, ∼25% in ESM-C) achieves >85% faithfulness and functionally subsumes circuits for exact and synthetic repeats, as shown by cross-task faithfulness >1.0.
- The circuit operates in two main stages: early layers build alignment via relative-position heads and biochemical similarity neurons (BLOSUM62-aligned); middle layers use induction heads to copy aligned tokens; late MLPs refine the output.
- Model accuracy exceeds 99% on exact repeats and ∼79% on approximate repeats, but performance collapses to near-random (42–48%) on repeats with insertions/deletions, indicating a hard boundary of the mechanism.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper reverse-engineers how protein language models complete repeating sequences, finding a shared circuit that generalizes from exact to approximate repeats. Three mechanisms are central.

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Early layers build aligned token representations using positional attention and biochemical similarity neurons.
- **Mechanism:** Relative-position heads attend to fixed offsets (±n tokens), while MLP neurons encode amino-acid similarity matching BLOSUM62 scores. This establishes a soft alignment between repeat instances even when residues differ.
- **Core assumption:** PLMs first create a position-aware, biochemically informed context before pattern matching.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "early layers use positional attention heads and MLP neurons encoding amino-acid biochemical similarity (aligning with BLOSUM62 scores)"
  - [section 4.1] Clustering reveals relative-position heads across all layers and biochemical similarity neurons in MLPs.
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors focus on repeat curation/design; weak direct evidence for this mechanistic stage.
- **Break condition:** If positional or biochemical features are ablated (via circuit patching), model accuracy on approximate repeats drops significantly, as the alignment signal is disrupted.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Middle-layer induction heads attend to aligned tokens across repeats to retrieve the correct amino acid.
- **Mechanism:** Induction-like attention heads detect the repeated pattern and copy the token from the aligned position in the other repeat instance, directly promoting the correct answer in the masked position.
- **Core assumption:** Standard induction mechanisms from LLMs are repurposed for biological repeat detection, relying on the alignment built in Mechanism 1.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "middle layers employ induction heads that attend to aligned tokens across the repeat instances to retrieve the correct amino acid."
  - [section 5] Logit lens shows induction heads are the first component group to increase probability of the correct token.
  - [section 4.1] Cluster C1 (induction heads) shows high repeat-focus and context sensitivity.
- **Break condition:** Ablating induction heads (or their incoming connections from biochemical neurons) cripples repeat completion, especially for approximate repeats where exact token copying is insufficient.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Final MLP neurons refine the output distribution, with amino-acid–biased heads providing supplementary modulation.
- **Mechanism:** Late MLPs, populated with amino-acid–selective and repeat-sensitive neurons, shape the final probability distribution. Amino-acid–biased heads contribute minor adjustments.
- **Core assumption:** Final-layer computation is primarily a refinement step, not the core pattern-matching engine.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Later MLP neurons refine the output."
  - [section 5] Logit lens shows MLPs contribute to correct prediction in later layers; final-layer MLPs sometimes inhibit (ESM-3) or boost (ESM-C) confidence.
  - [section 4.2] Amino-acid neurons are more important in later layers; repeat neurons often inhibitory.
- **Break condition:** Ablating late MLP neurons reduces confidence/accuracy even if induction succeeded, indicating refinement is necessary for robust output.

## Architecture Onboarding
A new engineer wanting to replicate or extend this work should follow this concrete guide.

### Component Map
Early layers (0–15) contain relative-position heads and biochemical similarity neurons, which feed into middle layers (15–30) with induction heads and repeat-sensitive neurons, ultimately processed by late layers (30–final) with amino-acid–selective neurons and refinement MLPs. ESM-3 uniquely integrates secondary-structure neurons from its multi-modal training.

### Critical Path
1. Input: Masked protein sequence with two repeat instances (exact or approximate).
2. Alignment Phase: Relative-position heads + biochemical MLP neurons create position- and property-aware representations that loosely align the two repeats.
3. Copy Phase: Induction heads attend from masked position to the aligned token in the other repeat, copying its representation to promote the correct amino acid.
4. Refinement Phase: Late MLP neurons (amino-acid/repeat selective) and AA-biased heads finalize the output distribution.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Counterfactual choice: Using 100% BLOSUM substitution yields smallest, most compact circuits but may over-prune; permutation or mask counterfactuals include more components but risk capturing general sequence processing.
- Circuit size vs. faithfulness: 85% threshold is standard but arbitrary; higher thresholds require exponentially more components.
- Neuron concept definition: Manually curated biochemical/structural groupings (IMGT, BLOSUM62, secondary structure) introduce bias; automated concept discovery (e.g., SAEs) could reveal additional features.
- Task definition: Restricting to two repeats, no indels, and substitution-adjacent masking simplifies analysis but limits generalization to natural repeat diversity.

### Failure Signatures
- Low accuracy on indel repeats: Both models perform near random (~42–48%) when repeats contain insertions/deletions, indicating the circuit does not handle gap alignment.
- Circuit failure on complex natural repeats: If a protein has >2 repeat instances or overlapping repeats, the discovered circuit may not capture the full logic.
- Model-specific differences: ESM-3 uses secondary-structure neurons; ESM-C does not. A circuit discovered in one may not transfer perfectly to the other without adaptation.
- High variance in neuron selection: Neuron-level components are noisier than heads/MLPs; consistent neurons require strict thresholds (e.g., AUROC>0.75).

### First 3 Experiments
1. Reproduce circuit discovery for approximate repeats: Use the provided code/data to identify the ~17% (ESM-3) or ~25% (ESM-C) component circuit achieving 85% faithfulness on the approximate-repeat task. Verify the three-phase mechanism via logit lens.
2. Cross-task generalization test: Take the approximate-repeat circuit and evaluate its faithfulness on synthetic and identical repeat datasets. Expect >100% normalized faithfulness, confirming functional subsumption.
3. Neuron concept validation: For a high-importance neuron in the late MLP layers, compute AUROC against curated concept sets (e.g., BLOSUM62 cliques, helix-breakers). Validate that the top-matched concept aligns with manual inspection of activation patterns. If using ESM-3, check for secondary-structure–propensity matches; ESM-C should not show these.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do the identified repeat-detection mechanisms generalize to proteins with more than two repeat occurrences, or to hierarchical/overlapping repeat patterns common in natural proteins?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The study restricts analysis to sequences with exactly two repeat occurrences (Section 2, Conclusion & Limitations). The authors note this as a key scope limitation.
- **Why unresolved:** The circuit analysis was performed on a curated dataset with only two repeats. Natural proteins often contain tandem arrays of multiple repeats or nested repeat structures, which may require additional computational steps or different circuit configurations.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend the circuit discovery methodology to datasets with three or more repeats, including overlapping or hierarchical patterns. Compare component importance and interaction graphs to see if the same core mechanism scales or if new components (e.g., higher-order positional heads) emerge.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent are the manually defined biochemical concepts (e.g., BLOSUM62 groups, secondary structure propensities) exhaustive, and could automated concept discovery reveal additional, unforeseen interpretable features used by PLMs for repeat detection?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The neuron analysis relies on hand-crafted concept groups based on prior biological knowledge (Section 4.2, Appendix I). The authors acknowledge this introduces inductive bias and suggest automated methods could address it (Conclusion & Limitations).
- **Why unresolved:** The pre-defined concepts may miss novel patterns the model has learned. For instance, neurons might combine multiple biochemical properties in ways not captured by standard classifications, or encode context-dependent features specific to repeat contexts.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply automated concept discovery techniques (e.g., sparse autoencoders, clustering on neuron activation datasets) without a priori biochemical groupings. Compare the resulting feature dictionary to the manual set, and test whether automatically discovered features improve mechanistic explanations or prediction of model behavior on out-of-distribution repeat tasks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the functional role of the inhibitory effects observed from repeat-related neurons, and how do they interact with induction heads to modulate output confidence?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Attribution analysis (Fig. 9) shows repeat-focused neurons often have negative contributions across layers. The paper notes they play an inhibitory role (Section 5) but does not explain its purpose.
- **Why unresolved:** Inhibition could serve various functions: sharpening the induction head signal, preventing over-activation on false positives, or calibrating final output probabilities. The exact computational role and its necessity for robust repeat detection remain unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Perform targeted ablation experiments where repeat-focused neurons are silenced or artificially enhanced. Measure changes in accuracy, confidence calibration, and error patterns (e.g., false positives on non-repeat sequences). Analyze attention patterns in induction heads under these perturbations to see if inhibition shapes their alignment.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Why do PLMs struggle with approximate repeats containing insertions/deletions (indels), and does the failure indicate a fundamentally different or absent mechanism for handling gapped alignments?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors report very low accuracy on indel-containing repeats (Appendix C) and exclude them from circuit analysis due to unstable performance.
- **Why unresolved:** The identified mechanism relies on aligned token copying via induction heads, which assumes positional correspondence. Indels break this alignment, potentially requiring mechanisms for gap detection, frameshift handling, or flexible pattern matching not captured by the current circuit.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Curate a controlled dataset with only indel variations (varying gap length and position). Use circuit discovery on successful instances to see if any components specialize for gap handling. Test whether training models with augmented data containing indels induces new circuit elements (e.g., attention heads that skip positions or MLPs that model insertion likelihoods).

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How does multi-modal training (as in ESM-3) specifically lead to the incorporation of secondary structure–sensitive neurons into the repeat-detection circuit, and does this confer any measurable advantage in identifying biologically meaningful repeats?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] ESM-3 (multi-modal) uses secondary structure–related neurons, while ESM-C (sequence-only) does not (Section 4.2, Fig. 6c). The paper speculates this relates to training differences but does not test causality or functional impact.
- **Why unresolved:** It is unclear whether secondary structure knowledge is actively used by the circuit (e.g., to filter out implausible repeats) or is an epiphenomenon of multi-task learning. The advantage in terms of biological accuracy or robustness to natural variation is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare model performance on repeat detection in proteins where secondary structure constraints are critical (e.g., repeats that must adopt specific folds). Ablate secondary structure–selective neurons in ESM-3 and measure accuracy drops relative to ablating other neuron types. Alternatively, train a sequence-only model with auxiliary supervision predicting secondary structure from sequences and test if it develops similar neurons.

## Limitations
- **Task Scope:** Analysis restricted to two repeat instances with no indels; unknown if circuit scales to natural proteins with >2 repeats, overlapping repeats, or insertions/deletions.
- **Counterfactual Construction:** The "highest-matching BLOSUM62 substitution" rule is specific and its exact implementation (e.g., tie-breaking) is not fully detailed, creating a reproducibility dependency.
- **Neuron Interpretability:** Matching neurons to curated concepts (BLOSUM62 groups, IMGT classes) relies on manually defined groupings and arbitrary AUROC thresholds (≥0.75), introducing selection bias.

## Confidence
- **Existence of a Shared Circuit (Exact → Approximate):** High. Supported by robust cross-task faithfulness metrics (>1.0) and structural overlap.
- **Two-Stage Alignment + Copy Mechanism:** High. Logit lens and ablation evidence strongly align with known LLM induction phenomena.
- **Late MLP Refinement & Amino-Acid Selectivity:** Medium. Logit lens shows contributions, but neuron selectivity is noisier and less directly probed.
- **Neuron Concept Groupings (BLOSUM62, etc.):** Medium. AUROC matches provide statistical association, but causality and functional necessity are not established.
- **ESM-3 vs. ESM-C Differences (Secondary Structure):** High. Clear, reproducible differences in neuron concept distributions are reported.
