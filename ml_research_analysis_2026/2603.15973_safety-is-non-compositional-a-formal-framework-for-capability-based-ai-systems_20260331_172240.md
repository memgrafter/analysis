# Safety is Non-Compositional: A Formal Framework for Capability-Based AI Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.15973  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15973  
- **Reference count**: 8  
- **Primary result**: Safety in modular AI systems is formally proven non-compositional due to conjunctive capability dependencies; a polynomial-time Safe Audit Surface certifiably maps safe acquisitions.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical safety risk in modular AI systems: individually safe components can combine to achieve forbidden goals via conjunctive capability dependencies. The authors model capabilities as directed hypergraphs with AND-semantics hyperedges, define a closure operator for reachability, and prove that safety is non-compositional. Key contributions include a polynomial-time Safe Audit Surface for certifying safe acquisitions, complexity characterizations of emergent capability detection (P-complete) and minimal unsafe set membership (coNP-complete), and empirical validation showing 42.6% of real multi-tool trajectories contain conjunctive dependencies, with the hypergraph planner eliminating AND-violations that plague baseline planners.

## Method Summary
The core method represents a capability system as a directed hypergraph \( H = (V, F) \), where vertices \( V \) are capabilities and hyperedges \( F \) are rules \( S \to \{v\} \) requiring all \( S \subseteq V \) to derive \( v \). A closure operator \( \operatorname{cl}(A) \) computes all capabilities reachable from an initial set \( A \) via repeated hyperedge firing. Safety is defined relative to a forbidden set \( F \): a set \( A \) is safe if \( F \not\subseteq \operatorname{cl}(A) \). The Safe Audit Surface \( \operatorname{GF}(A) \) for a safe \( A \) comprises (1) already emergent capabilities, (2) the near‑miss frontier of single‑step safe expansions, and (3) top‑k marginal gain capabilities, providing a complete map of safely acquirable capabilities. Empirically, trajectories from ToolBench G3 and TaskBench DAG are parsed to mine conjunctive hyperedges, and three planners (hypergraph, workflow, graph) are simulated to measure AND‑violation rates.

## Key Results
- **Theoretical**: Safety is non‑compositional; a minimal counterexample with two safe singletons combining via a conjunctive hyperedge to reach a forbidden goal (Theorem 9.2).
- **Computational**: The Safe Audit Surface is polynomial‑time computable and complete for safe acquisitions (Theorem 10.2); emergent capability detection is P‑complete; minimal unsafe set membership is coNP‑complete.
- **Empirical**: 42.6% of real multi‑tool trajectories contain conjunctive dependencies; the hypergraph planner yields zero AND‑violations versus workflow (38.2%) and graph (21.4%) planners on asynchronous traces.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Safety is non‑compositional: individually safe components can combine to reach forbidden goals via conjunctive hyperedges.
- **Mechanism**: A minimal counterexample with vertices \( u_1, u_2, f \) and hyperedge \( \{u_1, u_2\} \to \{f\} \). Singletons \( \{u_1\} \) and \( \{u_2\} \) are safe (closure excludes \( f \)), but their union fires the hyperedge, reaching \( f \).
- **Core assumption**: Capability composition requires simultaneous satisfaction of all preconditions (AND‑semantics), modeled by hyperedges with \( |S| \ge 2 \).
- **Evidence anchors**: Theorem 9.2 and its proof in Section 9.2; minimality is tight.
- **Break condition**: If all hyperedges have singleton tails (i.e., the system is a capability graph), then \( \operatorname{cl}(A \cup B) = \operatorname{cl}(A) \cup \operatorname{cl}(B) \) when \( \operatorname{cl}(A) \cap \operatorname{cl}(B) \neq \emptyset \), so the failure cannot arise.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The Safe Audit Surface provides a polynomial‑time computable, certifiable map of all safely acquirable capabilities from a given safe state.
- **Mechanism**: For a safe set \( A \in \mathcal{R}(F) \), the surface combines (1) emergent capabilities already reachable without acquisition \( (\operatorname{Emg}(A) \setminus F) \), (2) the near‑miss frontier \( \operatorname{NMF}_F(A) \) of single‑step safe acquisitions, and (3) top‑k marginal gain capabilities. Completeness follows from the First‑Step Lemma: any safe acquisition path must start with a frontier step.
- **Core assumption**: The closure operator correctly captures capability composition; the near‑miss frontier exactly characterizes one‑step safe expansions.
- **Evidence anchors**: Theorem 10.2 and its \( O(|V| \cdot (n + mk)) \) complexity; Section 8.1 defines \( \operatorname{NMF}_F(A) \) and \( \gamma(v, A) \).
- **Break condition**: If the near‑miss frontier is empty or marginal gain computation becomes super‑polynomial (e.g., in probabilistic hypergraphs with correlated firing), the guarantee may fail.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The closure gain function \( f(B) = |\operatorname{cl}(A \cup B)| - |\operatorname{cl}(A)| \) is submodular, enabling a \( (1-1/e) \) greedy approximation for optimal acquisition.
- **Mechanism**: The closure system \( (V, \operatorname{cl}) \) forms a Moore family; its rank function is submodular via the polymatroid rank theorem. Greedy selection thus achieves the \( (1-1/e) \) guarantee.
- **Core assumption**: The closure operator defines a matroid‑like structure with diminishing returns; holds for deterministic, monotone hypergraphs.
- **Evidence anchors**: Theorem 8.4 (submodularity) and Corollary 8.5 (greedy guarantee); proof in Section 8.4.
- **Break condition**: If the gain function loses submodularity—e.g., under stochastic hyperarc firing or non‑monotone effects—the greedy guarantee no longer holds.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Directed Hypergraphs**  
  *Why needed*: They naturally model conjunctive dependencies (AND‑semantics) without artificial conjunction nodes, unlike pairwise graphs.  
  *Quick check*: Can you represent a task requiring two tools simultaneously using a directed graph without adding extra nodes? What does that imply about the graph’s fidelity?
- **Concept: Closure Operator**  
  *Why needed*: Computes all capabilities derivable from an initial set via hyperedge firing; planning and safety reduce to closure inclusion tests.  
  *Quick check*: What is the difference between \( \operatorname{cl}(A) \) and the transitive closure of a directed graph? How does AND‑semantics affect fixed‑point computation?
- **Concept: Lower Set Lattice of Safe Region**  
  *Why needed*: The safe region \( \mathcal{R}(F) \) is downward‑closed; its boundary is the antichain \( \mathcal{B}(F) \) of minimal unsafe sets. This structure enables offline preprocessing and online coalition safety checks as set‑cover queries.  
  *Quick check*: Why is \( \mathcal{R}(F) \) closed under intersection but not union? How does Theorem 9.3 help partition safe vs. unsafe acquisitions?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Hypergraph store -> Closure engine -> Safety auditor <-> Acquisition planner
- **Critical path**: Ingest capabilities -> build/update hypergraph -> compute closure -> derive audit surface (Emg, NMF, gains) -> against \( \mathcal{B}(F) \) check acquisitions/coalitions.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Exact \( \mathcal{B}(F) \) vs. approximation: coNP‑complete; practical for small \( |V| \) or bounded \( k \), but may need heuristics for large systems.  
  - Static vs. dynamic hypergraph: Theorem 11.4 supports incremental closure; choose based on tool volatility.  
  - Deterministic vs. probabilistic: Stochastic extensions lose polynomial closure; use expected closure or sampling.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - AND‑violations: Workflow or graph planners fire conjunctive hyperedges from partial preconditions; hypergraph planner prevents this.  
  - Coalition breach: Joint capability set covers some \( B \in \mathcal{B}(F) \); monitor via linear‑time set‑cover check.  
  - Audit surface mismatch: Discovered goal not in \( \operatorname{GF}(A) \); indicates bug in closure or boundary computation.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Reproduce minimal non‑compositionality counterexample with \( \{u_1\}, \{u_2\} \) safe, union reaches \( f \).  
  2. Compute Safe Audit Surface for the Paris booking hypergraph starting from \( A = \{c_1, c_2\} \); verify NMF includes \( c_5 \) as first safe step.  
  3. On ToolBench G3 subset, measure conjunctive dependency prevalence; validate that pairwise graph planner AND‑violation rate exceeds hypergraph planner’s zero.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: What are the PAC sample complexity and algorithmic foundations for learning capability hyperedge structure from trajectory logs under realistic noise models? The paper uses a heuristic conjunctive‑witness rule but provides no formal learning guarantees; VC‑dimension for sparse low‑fan‑in hypergraphs remains unanalyzed.
- **Open Question 2**: How does stochastic hyperarc firing impact the capability closure operator and safety certification, and can expected closure be computed efficiently under correlated dependencies? The deterministic framework does not account for stochasticity; expected reachability under correlated firing is computationally harder and uncharacterized.
- **Open Question 3**: What are the computational barriers and practical defense strategies against adversarial hyperedge injection, and can submodularity yield efficient approximation guarantees? While single‑edge injection is polynomial and multi‑edge is NP‑hard, no concrete defense algorithms with provable guarantees are provided despite the submodular robustness function.
- **Open Question 4**: Are conjunctive dependencies as prevalent across diverse benchmarks as suggested, and does the hypergraph planner universally prevent AND‑violations? Current prevalence (42.6%) relies on automated annotation validated only by intra‑team agreement; external validity is threatened by lack of independent ground truth.

## Limitations
- **Workflow baseline ambiguity**: Paper does not specify firing logic; the “any tail” assumption may misrepresent the baseline, affecting violation comparisons.
- **Hyperedge mining vagueness**: Conjunctive‑witness rule details are unclear; using full invocation prefixes as tails likely overestimates conjunctive hyperedge sizes versus minimal tails.
- **Initial state uncertainty**: Unclear if trajectories start with pre‑acquired tools; empty‑start assumption could inflate conjunctive dependency detection.
- **Instance counting discrepancy**: Reported 383 conjunctive instances may differ due to dataset filtering or hyperedge frequency thresholds.

## Confidence
- **Non‑compositionality proof**: High (Theorem 9.2 with minimal counterexample and formal proof).
- **Safe Audit Surface**: Medium (Theorem 10.2 depends on correct near‑miss frontier definition and closure assumptions).
- **Submodularity/greedy guarantee**: High (Theorem 8.4 rooted in established polymatroid theory for deterministic monotone hypergraphs).
- **Empirical H1 (prevalence)**: Low (sensitive to mining method, baseline assumptions, initial state).
- **Empirical H2 (violation rates)**: Low (planner implementations and hypergraph construction introduce ambiguity).
