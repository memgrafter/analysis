# Search-P1: Path-Centric Reward Shaping for Stable and Efficient Agentic RAG Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.22576
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22576
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Search-P1 achieves an average accuracy gain of 7.7 points over baselines on multiple QA benchmarks, with a 20.6-point gain on the complex AD-QA benchmark, converges faster during training, and uses fewer interaction turns.

## Executive Summary
Agentic retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) trained with reinforcement learning suffers from sparse outcome rewards that discard intermediate reasoning signals and low sample efficiency where failed trajectories receive no reward. Search-P1 introduces a path-centric reward shaping framework that provides dense supervision for reasoning trajectories. It features dual-track path scoring: self-consistency (execution of the model’s own plan) and reference-alignment (coverage of an offline-generated expert plan). Additionally, soft outcome scoring assigns partial credit to incorrect answers based on reasoning quality, and a buffered format reward stabilizes early training. Experiments on public QA benchmarks and an internal advertising dataset show Search-P1 significantly outperforms Search-R1 and other baselines, achieving an average accuracy gain of 7.7 points and a 20.6-point gain on AD-QA with the Qwen2.5-7B model. The method also converges faster and uses fewer interaction turns, with ablations confirming the complementary value of both scoring tracks.

## Method Summary
Search-P1 operates in an agentic RAG setting where a model generates trajectories composed of a plan, reasoning steps, retrieval actions, observations, and a final answer. The reward structure consists of three parts:
1. **Path reward (R_path)**: Computed via dual-track scoring. An offline reference planner (using a high-capability LLM with rejection sampling and voting) produces a reference plan and reasoning path for each training question. During online rollouts, a dual-track evaluator LLM scores the generated trajectory on two axes: self-consistency (S_self) measures how faithfully the model executes its own declared plan; reference-alignment (S_ref) measures step coverage of the expert path regardless of order. R_path = max(S_self, S_ref) to allow either track to dominate when the other is unreliable.
2. **Soft outcome reward (R_outcome)**: For trajectories with a correct final answer, reward is 1.0. For incorrect answers, a blended score: R_outcome = α·r_acc + (1-α)·r_reason (α=0.8), where r_acc is a partial correctness score and r_reason is an LLM-evaluated reasoning quality score. This ensures failed trajectories still yield non-zero gradients.
3. **Format reward (R_format)**: A tiered reward (0.1 for valid format with tool calls and answer; 0.05 for having an answer but malformed tool calls; 0 for completely invalid outputs) prevents early training collapse due to sparse formatting violations.

These rewards are combined as R_total = λ_p·R_path + λ_a·R_outcome + λ_f·R_format (paper uses λ_p=0.3, λ_a=0.6, λ_f=0.1). The policy (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B) is updated using the GRPO algorithm with a KL penalty. Training begins by generating and caching all reference plans; then online rollouts are performed with SGLang, and rewards are computed by the evaluator. The approach densifies the training signal, improves sample efficiency, and stabilizes learning.

## Key Results
- Average accuracy improvement of 7.7 points across multiple QA benchmarks compared to strong baselines like Search-R1.
- 20.6-point accuracy gain on the challenging AD-QA benchmark.
- Faster convergence during training and fewer average interaction turns per trajectory.
- Ablations confirm that both self-consistency and reference-alignment tracks contribute complementary signals to performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dual-track path scoring for dense intermediate supervision
- **Claim:** Dual-track path scoring provides dense, order-agnostic intermediate supervision that alleviates reward sparsity in agentic RAG training.
- **Mechanism:** The framework evaluates a generated trajectory T through (a) Self-Consistency (S_self), which checks if the model executes its own declared plan p (extracted from r_planner), and (b) Reference-Alignment (S_ref), which measures coverage of an offline-generated expert reasoning path R_ref regardless of execution order. The final path reward R_path = max(S_self, S_ref) takes the better of the two, allowing the model to be rewarded for either faithful self-execution or expert-like coverage.
- **Core assumption:** The structural quality of a reasoning trajectory (plan fidelity, step coverage, efficiency) is a leading indicator of eventual answer accuracy, even for intermediate or failed trajectories.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Dual-Track Path Scoring ... assesses paths from both self-consistency and reference-alignment perspectives."
  - [section 3.2.2] Formal definitions of S_self (Eq 6) and S_ref (Eq 7) with order-agnostic matching and efficiency penalties.
  - [corpus] Weak/indirect. Neighbors (e.g., "Planner-R1", "DecEx-RAG") explore reward shaping for agentic RL but do not explicitly implement a *dual-track max* composition for path evaluation.
- **Break condition:** If the offline-generated reference plan R_ref is systematically suboptimal or misaligned with valid reasoning strategies, the S_ref track may penalize creative but correct alternatives. The max composition only helps if at least one track is reliable.

### Mechanism 2: Soft outcome scoring improves sample efficiency
- **Claim:** Soft outcome scoring converts previously zero-reward failed trajectories into useful training signals by assigning partial credit based on reasoning quality, improving sample efficiency.
- **Mechanism:** For trajectories with incorrect final answers, R_outcome = α·r_acc + (1-α)·r_reason (with α=0.8). This blends a partial answer correctness score (r_acc) with an LLM-evaluated reasoning quality score (r_reason), ensuring failed samples still provide a non-zero gradient.
- **Core assumption:** A trajectory with high reasoning quality but a slightly incorrect final answer is more valuable for training than a completely incoherent failure; preserving this signal improves learning from limited data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Soft outcome scoring that assigns partial credit to incorrect answers based on reasoning quality."
  - [section 3.2.3] Explicit formula (Eq 8) and decay factor details.
  - [corpus] Weak. Neighbors discuss "process vs. outcome" (paper 7689) but do not implement a *blended soft scoring* specific to failed outcomes.
- **Break condition:** If r_reason is poorly calibrated (e.g., correlates weakly with true reasoning soundness), this could reward superficially plausible but logically flawed trajectories, potentially degrading final accuracy.

### Mechanism 3: Soft format reward stabilizes early training
- **Claim:** A buffered "soft" format reward (vs. strict binary) provides stable, continuous gradient feedback during early training, preventing reward collapse from formatting violations and accelerating convergence.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of assigning R_format = 0 for any format violation, the paper uses a tiered reward: 0.1 for valid format with tool calls/answer, 0.05 for having an answer but malformed tool calls, and 0 (trajectory discard) only for completely invalid outputs. This creates a smoother learning signal.
- **Core assumption:** Early in training, models frequently violate strict output schemas; providing partial reward for partial compliance maintains gradient flow while the policy learns correctness.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3.1 & Figure 3] Direct comparison showing "Soft Format" achieves faster accuracy improvement and higher stable rewards vs. "Strict Format" (zero reward) and "Without Format".
  - [section B.2] Details of the λ_f weighting and default λ_f=0.1.
  - [corpus] Absent. No neighbor explicitly studies format reward buffering for agentic RAG.
- **Break condition:** If the buffer is too generous (e.g., 0.05 for severely malformed outputs), models may learn to "game" the format reward without improving reasoning or retrieval, leading to brittle policies.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning with Sparse/Shaped Rewards**
  - *Why needed here:* Search-P1 builds on GRPO (a variant of PPO), which typically uses binary outcome rewards. Understanding reward shaping (Ng et al., 1999) is key to seeing how R_path and soft R_outcome densify the signal.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the risk of using only binary accuracy rewards in a multi-step RL task like agentic RAG?

- **Agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Agentic RAG)**
  - *Why needed here:* The entire problem formulation assumes an agent that interleaves reasoning (r), search actions (a), and observations (o). You must grasp the (p, r1, a1, o1, ..., r_final, â) trajectory structure.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the explicit planner p in Search-P1 differ from the implicit planning in standard CoT or Search-R1?

- **Process Reward Modeling vs. Outcome Reward Modeling**
  - *Why needed here:* Search-P1's R_path is a form of process reward. Understanding the trade-off (denser but noisier signals vs. sparse but reliable signals) is central to the design rationale.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might a process reward based on step coverage (n_covered) be order-agnostic, and why is that important for multi-hop QA?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
1. Offline Reference Planner Generator (Algo 1): Uses a high-capability LLM (M_ref) with rejection sampling & voting to produce (p_ref, r_ref) for all training (q, a*) pairs. Cached before RL.
2. Agentic RAG Policy (π_θ) (Algo 2): Generates trajectories T = (p, r1, a1, o1, ..., â) during rollouts. Must emit structured tags (<reasoning>, <search>, <answer>).
3. Dual-Track Evaluator (Algo 3/Prompt D.3): An external LLM (e.g., HY 2.0-Instruct) that scores a trajectory T against (p_ref, r_ref), outputting: planner_score, eff_steps_self, eff_steps_ref, acc_score, reason_score.
4. Reward Aggregator (Eq 4): Combines R_format, R_path = max(S_self, S_ref), and soft R_outcome with weights λ_p, λ_a, λ_f.
5. RL Optimizer (GRPO/PPO): Updates π_θ using R_total as the trajectory-level reward.

**Critical path:** Offline (p_ref, r_ref) generation → Online rollout with π_θ → Dual-track LLM evaluation → Reward computation → Policy gradient update.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- R_path = max(S_self, S_ref) vs weighted sum: Max protects against a single poor reference but may destabilize gradients if scores are highly disparate.
- Soft R_outcome blending factor α: Higher α emphasizes answer correctness; lower emphasizes reasoning. Set to 0.8 per paper.
- Format reward buffering: Prevents early training collapse but requires careful tuning of λ_f to avoid format hacking.

**Failure signatures:**
- High S_self but low accuracy: Model executes a flawed self-plan competently; indicates p extraction or planning capability issue.
- High S_ref but low accuracy: Model copies reference steps without true understanding; possible overfitting to reference.
- Low format compliance rate (<80%): Indicates λ_f is too low or prompt/template is unclear.
- Decreasing turns but stagnant accuracy: Path reward overfitting to efficiency (n_effective/n_actions) without semantic progress.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Validate evaluator reliability: Sample 200 trajectories, have human annotators score path steps, compare with LLM evaluator agreement (aim >85% on step coverage).
2. Ablate dual-track composition: Replace max(S_self, S_ref) with average; expect drop on datasets where reference is high-quality but model discovers superior strategies.
3. Test soft outcome scaling: Vary α ∈ {0.0, 0.5, 0.8, 1.0} on a validation set; measure correlation between r_reason and final accuracy on held-out failures.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Open Question 1**: How does the offline reference planner generation scale with dataset size and domain diversity, and can it be replaced with learned or distilled planners to reduce reliance on high-capability LLMs?
   - *Basis in paper*: [inferred] The paper caches reference planners generated via rejection sampling and LLM voting (§3.2.1, Appendix B.3), noting a one-time cost but not addressing scalability or domain transfer of this offline process.
   - *Why unresolved*: The method assumes reference plans are high-quality and generalizable, but generating them for large or out-of-domain datasets may be costly or infeasible; no analysis of planner quality decay across domains.
   - *What evidence would resolve it*: Scaling experiments measuring accuracy and cost when increasing training samples or using out-of-domain data; comparisons with online or lightweight planner generation methods.

2. **Open Question 2**: Is the max(S_self, S_ref) operation in dual-track scoring optimal, or would alternative combinations (e.g., min, weighted sum) better balance self-consistency and reference alignment in different task contexts?
   - *Basis in paper*: [explicit] The paper uses R_path = max(S_self, S_ref) to avoid dilution when one track is suboptimal (§3.2.2), but does not justify this choice over other functions.
   - *Why unresolved*: The design choice lacks empirical validation against other aggregation strategies; the trade-off between flexibility (allowing one track to dominate) and stability (ensuring both tracks contribute) is unexplored.
   - *What evidence would resolve it*: Ablations comparing max, min, average, and learned combinations across datasets with varying complexity; analysis of per-example failure modes.

3. **Open Question 3**: To what extent does order-agnostic step coverage (n_covered) reliably capture reasoning quality, and how sensitive is the path reward to the LLM-based semantic matching thresholds used?
   - *Basis in paper*: [inferred] The paper uses order-agnostic matching with an LLM evaluator for step coverage (§3.2.2, Appendix D.3), but details on matching criteria and robustness are limited.
   - *Why unresolved*: Semantic matching thresholds are not quantified; the assumption that covered steps equate to high-quality reasoning may not hold for all reasoning patterns (e.g., creative or non-linear approaches).
   - *What evidence would resolve it*: Human evaluations of step coverage agreement; sensitivity analyses varying matching thresholds; testing on trajectories with correct answers but unconventional reasoning structures.

4. **Open Question 4**: How well does Search-P1 generalize to non-QA complex reasoning tasks (e.g., mathematical proof, code generation) where final answer correctness is less binary and reasoning path diversity is higher?
   - *Basis in paper*: [inferred] The paper evaluates only on QA benchmarks and one internal advertising QA (§4.1), implicitly assuming QA settings; no discussion of task transfer.
   - *Why unresolved*: QA tasks often have clear answer matching, but other domains have nuanced correctness and multiple valid reasoning paths; the soft outcome scoring and path-centric rewards may need adaptation.
   - *What evidence would resolve it*: Experiments on math, code, or strategic games; analysis of reward correlations with final accuracy in these domains; adjustments to path scoring for continuous or multi-faceted outcomes.

## Limitations
- Offline reference planner generation details (K, voting algorithm) are not fully specified, leading to uncertainty in reproduction.
- Dependence on the proprietary HY 2.0-Instruct evaluator and the internal AD-QA dataset hinders reproducibility and external validation.
- Exact format validation rules are approximated via regex; break conditions and potential mismatches with the original implementation remain untested.

- **Mechanism 2 (soft outcome scoring)**: Medium
- **Mechanism 3 (soft format reward)**: High

## Next Checks
1. **Evaluator reliability**: Sample 200 rollouts, obtain human annotations for plan adherence and step coverage; compute step-level agreement with the LLM evaluator. Target >85% concordance.
2. **Dual-track composition**: Replace max(S_self, S_ref) with weighted sum (0.5 each) and test single-track variants (S_self-only, S_ref-only). Additionally, corrupt references by removing 20% of steps. Expect the max composition to maintain performance while others degrade, confirming robustness to imperfect references.
3. **Soft outcome calibration**: Sweep α ∈ {0.0, 0.5, 0.8, 1.0} on a validation set; measure correlation between r_reason and final accuracy on held-out failures. Choose α that maximizes sample efficiency (reward per turn) without sacrificing final accuracy.
