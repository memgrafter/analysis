# Rethinking Time Series Domain Generalization via Structure-Stratified Calibration

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02756
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02756
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Proposed Structure-Stratified Calibration Framework (SSCF) significantly outperforms strong baselines (e.g., CORAL, MMD, IRM) across sleep staging, arrhythmia detection, and human activity recognition, achieving 75.12% average Macro-F1 on external sleep staging targets vs. 71.16% for the best baseline.

## Executive Summary
Existing time series domain generalization methods assume all samples can be aligned in a shared feature space, but datasets from different dynamical systems often have structurally incompatible spectral patterns. This leads to spurious alignments and negative transfer. This paper introduces the Structure-Stratified Calibration Framework (SSCF), which first stratifies samples into structurally compatible clusters using power spectral features, then performs amplitude calibration only within each cluster using a reference anchor. Evaluated on 19 public datasets (100.3k samples) under zero-shot protocols, SSCF shows robust improvements, demonstrating that establishing structural comparability is a more reliable pathway for cross-dataset generalization than uniform alignment.

## Method Summary
SSCF operates in two stages. First, an offline stratification phase uses a shallow encoder trained with ERM to extract power spectral density (PSD) features from all source samples. These are clustered via K-Means to obtain K structurally compatible clusters. For each cluster, a mean-amplitude-squared (MAS) template is computed as a calibration reference. Second, during online training, for each input batch, its PSD is matched to the nearest cluster anchor. The sample's spectral amplitude is then scaled to match the cluster's MAS template while preserving phase, via a closed-form operation. The discriminative classifier is trained on these calibrated features. This decouples structure estimation from task learning and prevents cross-domain alignment between incompatible spectral geometries.

## Key Results
- SSCF achieves an average Macro-F1 of 75.12% on external target domains for sleep staging, surpassing the best baseline (71.16%).
- Significant gains are observed across 19 public datasets spanning diverse tasks, confirming the broad applicability of structure-stratified calibration.
- Ablation studies confirm that stratification before calibration is critical; global or dataset-wise calibration degrades performance, validating the core premise of structural heterogeneity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Claim:** Stratifying samples by structural compatibility *before* calibration prevents spurious cross-domain correspondences and negative transfer.
**Mechanism:** When datasets originate from heterogeneous dynamical systems, their spectral patterns (e.g., peak locations, energy bands) are fundamentally incompatible. Enforcing global alignment uniformly forces the model to match samples that lack stable one-to-one relationships, distorting structure-related representations. By first clustering samples with similar power spectral densities (PSD), SSCF restricts calibration to subsets where cross-domain correspondences are well-defined and interpretable.
**Core assumption:** Structural heterogeneity is a primary cause of negative transfer in cross-dataset time series generalization, and coarse spectral patterns are a practical proxy for assessing comparability.
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "...perform global alignment while neglecting structural differences is highly prone to establishing spurious correspondences and inducing negative transfer." [section 4.2] "We use P(x) as the structural (spectral-pattern) representation of the sample... we perform hard clustering... to obtain K structurally compatible clusters."
**Break condition:** If all source domains share identical spectral structures (K=1 suffices), stratification adds no benefit and may introduce noise.

**Claim:** Power spectral density (PSD) computed via Welch method from shallow encoder features serves as an effective, low-complexity descriptor for coarse structural stratification.
**Mechanism:** The PSD captures stable, channel-wise energy distributions across frequencies. Clustering in this space separates samples with distinct spectral morphologies (e.g., dominant low-frequency vs. broad mid-frequency energy). This stratification is done offline after an ERM warm-up to obtain stable features, and the resulting clusters are fixed.
**Core assumption:** Shallow feature spectra reflect the "structure" of the latent dynamical system, and this structure is relatively stable within a cluster across domains.
**Evidence anchors:** [section 4.2] "We use P(x) as the structural representation... perform hard clustering... to obtain K structurally compatible clusters." [appendix C] Spectral visualizations show clear inter-stratum differences in PSD shape when K≥2, and high intra-stratum consistency.
**Break condition:** If spectral patterns are highly non-stationary within a sample window or the encoder's shallow features are already corrupted by domain-specific artifacts, PSD may not reflect true dynamical structure.

**Claim:** Within-stratum amplitude calibration using a Mean-Amplitude-Squared (MAS) template aligns scale discrepancies while preserving phase, avoiding the mixing of incompatible spectral subspaces.
**Mechanism:** For a matched stratum, the MAS template (square of mean amplitude across samples) acts as a reference. Each sample's spectral amplitude is scaled channel-wise and frequency-wise to match the template's amplitude, while its phase is preserved. This is a closed-form, non-parametric operation that normalizes amplitude variations *only* among structurally comparable samples.
**Core assumption:** Within a structurally consistent cluster, the key cross-domain discrepancy is amplitude/scale rather than spectral shape; thus, amplitude alignment suffices and is safe.
**Evidence anchors:** [section 4.4] "Amplitude calibration is performed exclusively within that stratum... preserved the phase... scaling factor Mk(c,f) = sqrt(¯Pk(c,f)) / (P(c,f)+ϵ)." [table 3] "Global Anchor Calibration" and "Dataset-based Anchor" underperform vs. SSCF, indicating the necessity of stratum-wise calibration.
**Break condition:** If within-stratum samples also exhibit incompatible spectral shapes (e.g., different peak frequencies), simple amplitude scaling would be insufficient or harmful.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Spectral Representations (PSD)**
  - *Why needed here:* SSCF's entire stratification and calibration logic depends on the power spectral density as a proxy for dynamical system structure. Understanding PSD estimation (Welch method) and its interpretation (energy distribution vs. frequency) is fundamental.
  - *Quick check question:* If two time series have identical PSD shapes but different phases, are they considered structurally similar by SSCF? (Answer: Yes, structure is defined by PSD shape, phase is preserved.)

- **Concept: Cross-Domain Structural Heterogeneity**
  - *Why needed here:* This is the core problem SSCF addresses. The engineer must recognize that domain shift isn't just statistical (mean/covariance) but can be *structural*—incompatible spectral geometries that make global alignment ill-posed.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might CORAL fail even after perfect feature alignment? (Answer: Because it assumes all samples lie in a single comparable geometry; structurally incompatible samples will still be misaligned at the amplitude/scale level.)

- **Concept: Calibration as Post-hoc Adjustment**
  - *Why needed here:* SSCF separates structure estimation (offline) from discriminative learning (online). The calibration step is a deterministic, non-learnt transformation applied to encoder outputs. This decoupling is key to its efficiency and stability.
  - *Quick check question:* Are the MAS reference anchors updated during training? (Answer: No, they are fixed after offline construction; gradients do not flow to them.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Encoder (f_enc) -> Offline Stratifier (PSD Estimator + K-Means) -> Anchor Builder (MAS templates) -> Online Matcher (nearest anchor) -> Calibrator (amplitude scaling) -> Classifier (g)

- **Critical path:**
  - **Stage 1 (Offline):** Train encoder with ERM -> Freeze encoder -> Extract PSDs from all source samples -> Run K-Means -> Compute {¯P_k}.
  - **Stage 2 (Online):** For each batch: x -> f_enc(x) -> calibration (using fixed anchors) -> g(·) -> loss -> update f_enc and g.
  - *Note:* Anchors and clusters are never updated after Stage 1.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **K (number of strata):** Too small (K=1) fails to separate structures; too large risks overfitting to noise in PSD estimation. Paper finds stable gains for K≥3 in sleep staging (Fig 3a). K should be chosen via source-domain validation only.
  - **Matching heuristic:** Nearest-anchor in PSD space is simple but approximate. Performance correlates roughly with matching rank (R) but not strictly (Fig 3b). More sophisticated matching (e.g., learned) may help but adds complexity.
  - **Encoder depth:** Stratification uses *shallow* features. Too deep features might blend structural information with task-specific patterns, harming stratification quality.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Stratification collapse:** All samples assigned to one or two clusters despite K>2. Indicates PSD features lack discriminative power—check encoder training or Welch parameters.
  - **Calibration degradation:** Performance worse than baseline after applying calibration. Likely due to poor matching (samples assigned to wrong stratum) or within-stratum structural inconsistency. Validate by visualizing PSDs within each C_k.
  - **Anchor instability:** Significant performance drop when re-running clustering with different seeds. Suggests clusters are not robust; consider increasing K or using spectral clustering.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation: Calibration without stratification** – Apply global MAS calibration (K=1). Expect performance drop per Table 3, confirming need for stratification.
  2. **Ablation: Stratification without calibration** – Use clusters to guide data sampling or weighting but no amplitude adjustment. Should show partial benefit, indicating calibration adds value.
  3. **Anchor sensitivity** – Vary K ∈ {1,2,3,5,8,16} on a single LODO split. Plot Macro-F1 vs. K. Expect improvement from 1→3, then plateau or slight decline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can the structure‑stratified calibration framework be extended to time series with non‑stationary spectral patterns that evolve over time?**
  - *Basis:* The authors conclude: “Future work will further investigate stratification and calibration strategies under non‑stationary dynamics, where structural patterns evolve over time.”
  - *Why unresolved:* SSCF assumes each sample has a static spectral structure; it does not model temporal changes within a long recording or across sliding windows, which limits applicability to drifting regimes.

- **What is a principled, automated way to choose the number of structural clusters K without relying on source‑domain validation?**
  - *Basis:* The paper varies K in sensitivity analysis and notes that performance stabilizes for K≥3, yet K is treated as a hyperparameter selected by source validation. No automatic selection method is proposed.
  - *Why unresolved:* Manual tuning of K for each new dataset hinders deployment; an adaptive criterion would improve robustness and usability.

- **Would alternative spectral representations beyond shallow power spectral density (e.g., wavelets, learned frequency embeddings) yield more discriminative structural stratification?**
  - *Basis:* SSCF uses Welch PSD of shallow features for clustering. The Related Work section references spectral methods like wavelets but does not explore them for structural stratification.
  - *Why unresolved:* Shallow PSD may fail to capture multi‑scale or non‑stationary nuances that differentiate latent dynamical systems; richer representations could improve cluster purity and downstream generalization.

- **How can SSCF detect and handle target‑domain samples whose spectral structure does not correspond to any source‑derived cluster (i.e., out‑of‑distribution structures)?**
  - *Basis:* The method assigns every input to the nearest anchor, implicitly assuming target structures lie within the source cluster space. The paper does not address scenarios with novel structures.
  - *Why unresolved:* Forcing a nearest‑neighbor match may lead to incorrect calibration and negative transfer when the target introduces entirely new dynamical regimes, limiting reliability in open‑world settings.

## Limitations
- SSCF presumes spectral structure drives generalization; this may not hold when label distribution shift or sensor noise dominate.
- PSD-based stratification depends on shallow encoder quality; unstable training or depth blur may yield inconsistent clusters.
- Fixed offline clusters cannot adapt to target domains with novel spectral forms, risking mis-matches.
- Choice of cluster count K and Welch parameters lacks theoretical guidance, requiring dataset-specific tuning.

## Confidence
- **Stratification before calibration prevents negative transfer:** High (robust gains across 19 datasets, ablation confirms necessity).
- **PSD from shallow features as structural proxy:** Medium (empirical but sensitivity to encoder architecture and non-stationarity not fully explored).
- **Within-stratum amplitude calibration suffices:** Medium (ablations support, but break condition warns of within-stratum shape mismatches).

## Next Checks
