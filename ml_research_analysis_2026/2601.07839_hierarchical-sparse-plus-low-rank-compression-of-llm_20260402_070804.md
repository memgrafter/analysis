# Hierarchical Sparse Plus Low Rank Compression of LLM
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.07839
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07839
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: sHSS-RCM with 30% sparsity and rank 512 achieved a 1.7× storage reduction and perplexity 1.64 on WikiText-103 for LLaMA-7B self-attention projections.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) suffer from excessive memory and computational demands, hindering deployment on resource-constrained devices. This paper introduces Hierarchical Sparse Plus Low Rank (HSS) compression, a novel approach that first extracts the largest-magnitude weights into a sparse matrix and then recursively applies a hierarchical low-rank factorization to the residual, enhanced by Reverse Cuthill-McKee (RCM) reordering to align weights toward the diagonal for improved compressibility. 

On LLaMA-7B’s self-attention projection matrices (1.6B parameters), the sHSS-RCM variant with 30% sparsity and rank 512 achieved a perplexity of 1.64 on WikiText-103, outperforming sparse-SVD baselines while reducing storage by up to 1.7×. The results demonstrate that HSS can effectively balance compression and accuracy for large-scale LLMs.

## Method Summary
The method consists of three steps. First, a sparse matrix S is formed by retaining the top-p% weights (by magnitude) from the original weight matrix W. The residual R = W - S captures the remaining entries. Second, the Reverse Cuthill-McKee algorithm is applied to R to reorder rows and columns, moving larger-magnitude entries toward the diagonal and producing a permuted residual R̃. Third, R̃ is recursively factorized using a two-level Hierarchically Sparse Separable (HSS) structure. At each recursion level, the matrix is split into diagonal blocks D and off-diagonal blocks; off-diagonals are approximated via low-rank SVD (factors U and R'), while diagonal blocks are further partitioned in deeper levels. Target ranks are halved at each level, with a fixed recursion depth (e.g., three levels). The compressed representation stores S, the permutation matrices at each level, the diagonal blocks, and the low-rank factors. At inference, the product Wx is computed as Sx + (P⁻¹ HSS P x), where the HSS product traverses the hierarchy efficiently by exploiting the low-rank factorizations and stored permutations.

## Key Results
- Achieved WikiText-103 perplexity of 1.64 on LLaMA-7B self-attention projections with sHSS-RCM (30% sparsity, rank 512).
- Outperformed sparse-SVD baseline methods in accuracy at comparable compression levels.
- Reduced storage requirements by up to 1.7× relative to the original dense matrix.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Preserving the largest-magnitude weights as exact sparse entries (𝑆) retains critical outlier information that would otherwise be poorly approximated by low-rank factors.
- **Mechanism:** A top-𝑝% magnitude mask is applied to |𝑊|, and those entries are stored in a sparse matrix 𝑆. The residual 𝑅 = 𝑊 − 𝑆 is then compressed hierarchically.
- **Core assumption:** The most significant weights in LLM projection matrices represent irreducible, high-impact terms that exact preservation is necessary for maintaining model accuracy.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...first extracts the largest-magnitude weights into a sparse matrix..."
  - [section 4.5] "As a first step (1) we take out a sparse significant entries... in matrix 𝑆¹₀..."
  - [corpus] Corpus shows related S+LR frameworks (e.g., SLTrain, 3BASiL) also separate sparse outliers first, indicating a community assumption, but no direct evidence for *why* outliers must be kept exact is provided in the cited text.
- **Break condition:** If the weight distribution lacks prominent outliers (e.g., very heavy-tailed), the sparse mask may capture too few entries to justify the storage overhead, or the residual may not become sufficiently low-rank.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Recursive hierarchical low-rank factorization (HSS) efficiently captures the multi-scale low-rank structure of the residual matrix 𝑅 after outlier removal.
- **Mechanism:** The residual 𝑅 is split into diagonal blocks (𝐷) and off-diagonal blocks. Off-diagonals are approximated via low-rank SVD (𝑈𝑅ᵀ). Diagonal blocks are recursively compressed in deeper levels, with ranks halved at each step.
- **Core assumption:** After removing top-magnitude entries, the remaining matrix exhibits a hierarchically block-diagonal structure where off-diagonals are numerically low-rank at multiple scales.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2] "...off-diagonal blocks in matrices... contain a lot of long-range interactions, and this property can be leveraged because these long-range interactions tend to be numerically low-rank..."
  - [section 4.3] "We further partition 𝐷₀ and 𝐱 into 2×2 contiguous blocks... We subsequently perform two-level compression at each node..."
  - [abstract] "...applies a recursive Hierarchically Sparse Separable (HSS) low-rank factorisation to the dense residual matrix."
- **Break condition:** If the residual matrix does not show diminishing rank requirements in smaller blocks (i.e., off-diagonals of sub-blocks are not low-rank), recursion yields no compression benefit and adds permutation overhead.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Reverse Cuthill-McKee (RCM) reordering of the residual matrix concentrates high-magnitude entries near the diagonal, reducing the numerical rank of off-diagonal blocks within the HSS hierarchy.
- **Mechanism:** RCM is applied to the residual matrix before HSS factorization. This graph-based reordering reduces matrix bandwidth, aligning larger values into the diagonal blocks of the HSS tree, thereby making off-diagonal blocks more compressible (lower rank).
- **Core assumption:** The raw ordering of weight matrices is suboptimal for hierarchical factorization; a bandwidth-reducing permutation improves off-diagonal low-rank approximability.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...a reverse Cuthill–Mckee (RCM) permutation are introduced to align high weights towards the diagonal with the block-diagonal hierarchy, maximising off-diagonal compressibility..."
  - [section 4.5] "Then we do RCM reordering to bring the largest entries along the diagonal blocks..."
  - [section 5.4] "From Figure 2, we find that when RCM is applied after 10% of the matrix entries are taken away, then RCM has better PPL scores..."
- **Break condition:** If the weight matrix's non-zero pattern or value distribution is not graph-like (e.g., completely random), RCM may not reduce bandwidth meaningfully, and its permutation cost could outweigh gains.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Hierarchical Matrix Formats (HSS/H-matrices)**
  - **Why needed here:** The core compression algorithm (sHSS) is a recursive application of two-level HSS. Understanding block partitioning, off-diagonal low-rank storage (𝑈𝑅ᵀ), and recursive contraction is essential to grasp how matrix-vector products are computed efficiently.
  - **Quick check question:** How does a three-level HSS factorization reduce a dense 𝑁×𝑁 matrix-vector product from 𝑂(𝑁²) to approximately 𝑂(𝑁𝑟) operations, where 𝑟 is the typical off-diagonal rank?
- **Concept: Truncated SVD and Randomized SVD**
  - **Why needed here:** HSS uses low-rank approximations (via SVD) for off-diagonal blocks. Knowing the trade-off between exact SVD (optimal Frobenius norm) and randomized SVD (lower compute, slightly higher error) explains the paper's choice of randomized SVD in implementation.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the computational complexity difference between exact SVD and randomized SVD for an 𝑚×𝑛 matrix when target rank 𝑘 ≪ min(𝑚, 𝑛)?
- **Concept: Reverse Cuthill-McKee (RCM) Algorithm**
  - **Why needed here:** RCM is the key preprocessing step. It's a graph-theoretic algorithm for reducing matrix bandwidth. Understanding its root-finding and level-traversal strategy clarifies why it might cluster large weights near the diagonal.
  - **Quick check question:** Briefly describe how RCM reorders a symmetric matrix to reduce its bandwidth, starting from a node with minimal degree.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** The compression pipeline comprises five key components: (1) Sparse mask (𝑆) storing top-𝑝% magnitude weights as explicit nonzeros; (2) Permutation matrices (𝑃) representing RCM reorderings applied at each recursion level; (3) Diagonal blocks (𝐷) which are small dense matrices from block-diagonals at each HSS level; (4) HSS factors (𝑈, 𝑅) – low-rank factor pairs for off-diagonal blocks that multiply as 𝑈(𝑅𝑣) during matrix-vector operations; (5) Recursion controller that manages hierarchy depth, rank halving per level, and assembles the final approximation 𝑊 ≈ Σ𝑆 + 𝑃(𝐻𝑆𝑆)𝑃ᵀ. The data flow proceeds from the original matrix 𝑊 to sparse extraction, residual computation, RCM permutation, recursive HSS factorization, and storage of all components.
- **Critical path:** The compression pipeline runs as follows: (1) Extract top-𝑝% sparse mask 𝑆¹₀ from 𝑊. (2) Compute residual 𝑅 = 𝑊 − 𝑆¹₀. (3) Apply RCM to 𝑅 to obtain 𝑅̃. (4) Recursively apply the two-level HSS algorithm to 𝑅̃ (Algorithm 1), saving permutation matrices 𝑃, diagonal blocks 𝐷, and factor pairs 𝑈,𝑅 at each node. (5) Store all components. During inference: (a) Compute the product of 𝑆¹₀ with the input vector. (b) Apply all stored permutation matrices in top-down order to the input. (c) Recursively compute the HSS product on the permuted input. (d) Inverse-permute the HSS result. (e) Sum the sparse and HSS outputs to obtain the final output.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - **Sparsity % vs. Rank:** Higher sparsity leaves a smaller residual, allowing lower rank for a given accuracy, but sparse storage grows; lower sparsity increases residual size, requiring higher rank.
  - **HSS Depth:** Deeper recursion increases permutation overhead and storage for diagonal blocks but can better capture multi-scale structure. The paper uses fixed depth=3 for billion-parameter matrices.
  - **RCM cost:** RCM runs in 𝑂(𝑁+𝐸) time for a matrix with 𝑁 rows/columns and 𝐸 nonzeros. For billion-parameter matrices, this cost is non-trivial but amortized over many inference calls; it is incurred only once during compression.
  - **Precision:** All experiments use half-precision (fp16). Lower precisions (e.g., int8) may degrade low-rank SVD quality and are not explored.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Perplexity spike >5%** compared to the uncompressed model immediately after compression, indicating rank reduction or sparsity too aggressive.
  - **Storage not reducing** as expected: Check if ranks are not halving recursively (e.g., sub-blocks remain full rank) or if the sparse mask captures too few entries.
  - **Inference slowdown** despite compression: Likely due to deep recursion overhead or poor RCM bandwidth reduction leading to unbalanced block sizes.
  - **Numerical instability** in matrix-vector products: Caused by absorbing the sparse matrix into the factors incorrectly or using insufficient precision for SVD on fp16 weights.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replication baseline:** Compress LLaMA-7B Q, K, V projection matrices (each ~1.3B parameters) with sHSS-RCM at sp=30%, rank=512, depth=3. Verify storage reduction ~1.7× and WikiText-103 PPL ~1.64 as in paper.
  2. **Ablation - RCM effect:** For the same (sp, rank), run sHSS *without* RCM. Compare PPL and storage; expect slightly worse PPL (paper shows ~0.01-0.03 difference). If no difference, RCM may not help for that matrix.
  3. **Sparsity-Rank sensitivity sweep:** Fix depth=3. Vary sparsity {10%, 20%, 30%} and rank {128, 256, 512}. Plot PPL vs. effective compression ratio. Expect higher sparsity allows lower rank for same PPL; identify knee point for target hardware budget.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to LLaMA-7B’s self-attention projections; generalizability to larger models, other architectures, and non-attention weight matrices is unknown.
- The compression-accuracy trade-off is explored only for a single sparsity/rank setting; sensitivity across the full Pareto frontier and layer-specific hyperparameters remain unexamined.
- The computational overhead of RCM ordering and recursive HSS factorization during compression is not reported and may be prohibitive for billion-parameter matrices.

## Confidence
- Overall claim (HSS achieves higher accuracy at comparable compression vs. sparse-SVD): Medium
- Mechanism 1 (critical outlier preservation): Low
- Mechanism 2 (hierarchical low-rank factorization): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (RCM alignment improves compressibility): Medium
- Reported numbers (1.7× storage reduction, WikiText-103 PPL 1.64): High for the exact experiment; Low for broader applicability.
