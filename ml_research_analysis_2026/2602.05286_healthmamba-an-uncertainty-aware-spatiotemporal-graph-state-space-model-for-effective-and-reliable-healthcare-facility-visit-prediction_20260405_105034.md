# HealthMamba: An Uncertainty-aware Spatiotemporal Graph State Space Model for Effective and Reliable Healthcare Facility Visit Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05286
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05286
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: HealthMamba reduces MAE by 6.0% and improves Interval Score by 3.5% over SOTA, achieving robust 90% coverage under extreme events.

## Executive Summary
Healthcare facility visit prediction often ignores spatial relationships between facilities and struggles during public emergencies. HealthMamba introduces an uncertainty-aware spatiotemporal framework that integrates heterogeneous data through a Unified Spatiotemporal Context Encoder, captures dynamic spatial dependencies via adaptive graph learning, models hierarchical temporal patterns with a UNet-style Mamba backbone, and provides calibrated uncertainty via a multi-mechanism quantification module. Evaluated on four large-scale U.S. state datasets, HealthMamba outperforms state-of-the-art baselines (6.0% lower MAE, 3.5% higher Interval Score), consistently reaches 90% coverage, and remains robust during events like COVID-19 lockdowns and hurricanes while maintaining computational efficiency (~892K parameters, 65 seconds per epoch).

## Method Summary
The method comprises three core components: (1) a Unified Spatiotemporal Context Encoder that fuses heterogeneous spatiotemporal inputs (e.g., visit counts, demographic indicators, policy signals) into unified node and temporal embeddings; (2) a GraphMamba backbone that combines an adaptive graph learner to dynamically infer spatial adjacency from data with a UNet-structured Mamba encoder-decoder for multi-scale temporal modeling; (3) a comprehensive uncertainty quantification module that merges node-level, distribution-level, and parameter-level uncertainties to produce probabilistic forecasts. The model is trained end-to-end with a combined loss of prediction error (e.g., MAE) and calibration objectives (e.g., negative log-likelihood, interval score).

## Key Results
- 6.0% reduction in MAE and 3.5% improvement in Interval Score compared to state-of-the-art.
- Consistently achieves 90% coverage in predictive intervals across test periods.
- Maintains robustness during extreme events (COVID-19 lockdowns, hurricanes) with only 892K parameters and 65 seconds per training epoch.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
HealthMamba succeeds through synergistic design choices. The Unified Spatiotemporal Context Encoder effectively aligns heterogeneous data streams, enabling the model to leverage auxiliary information that improves prediction under normal and stressed conditions. The GraphMamba’s adaptive graph learning captures evolving spatial relationships among healthcare facilities, which are often neglected by fixed-graph methods, while the Mamba backbone provides efficient linear-complexity sequence modeling over long horizons. The multi-module uncertainty quantification integrates aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties, leading to well-calibrated intervals that remain reliable even during distribution shifts such as pandemics or natural disasters.

## Foundational Learning
- **State Space Models (SSMs)**: Provide efficient O(L) temporal modeling for long sequences; quick check: Mamba uses structured SSMs with selective scanning.
- **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)**: Enable propagation of information over spatial graphs; quick check: Graph convolution layers aggregate neighbor features.
- **Adaptive Graph Learning**: Allows the graph structure to be inferred from data rather than relying on static adjacency; quick check: self-attention or similarity-based edge generation.
- **Uncertainty Quantification (UQ)**: Combines multiple uncertainty sources (e.g., heteroscedastic noise, model ensemble) for reliable confidence intervals; quick check: output both mean and variance, or use Monte Carlo dropout.
- **Heterogeneous Data Fusion**: Integrates diverse input modalities (visits, demographics, policies) through embeddings; quick check: separate embedding layers concatenated or summed.
- **UNet-style Hierarchies**: Capture multi-scale patterns via downsampling and upsampling with skip connections; quick check: encoder-decoder blocks with pooling and transposed convolutions.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input Data → Unified Spatiotemporal Context Encoder → GraphMamba Backbone (Adaptive Graph Learner → Mamba Blocks) → Uncertainty Quantification Module → Output (visit predictions + intervals)

**Critical Path:**  
Raw spatiotemporal features are first transformed into node features and temporal embeddings by the Encoder. These are fed into the GraphMamba, where the Adaptive Graph Learner produces a dynamic adjacency matrix that guides message passing across Mamba blocks. The final node representations are processed by the UQ module to produce means and variances for each facility and forecast horizon.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- Adaptive graph vs. fixed graph: flexibility to learn relationships but risk of overfitting and instability; a hybrid may balance both.  
- Multi-source uncertainty fusion improves calibration but adds complexity and hyperparameter tuning.  
- UNet-style Mamba enhances multi-scale capture but increases depth and memory usage.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- Poor coverage (<90% on test sets) or overly wide intervals indicate mis-specified uncertainty.  
- Degenerate adjacency (e.g., fully connected or empty graph) suggests adaptive learner collapse.  
- Training divergence or NaN losses often stem from unstable uncertainty weighting or aggressive graph updates.

**First Experiments:**  
1. **Shape Validation**: Run a dummy batch (random tensors) through the full pipeline; confirm output shapes (num_nodes × horizon for predictions, same for uncertainty parameters).  
2. **Overfit Sanity Check**: Train on a single minibatch for several steps; loss should approach near-zero, verifying gradient flow and component integration.  
3. **Baseline Comparison**: Compare against a persistence model (e.g., yesterday’s visits) on a small validation set to ensure the model learns meaningful patterns beyond trivial baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How does HealthMamba's performance generalize across diverse abnormal scenarios beyond the two events (COVID-19 lockdowns, hurricanes) evaluated in the paper?  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper addresses RQ4 on abnormal situations but only tests two specific events (Section 4.5).  
- **Why unresolved**: Limited to two case studies; other public emergencies (e.g., wildfires, economic recessions, pandemics with different characteristics) may exhibit different spatiotemporal dynamics that could challenge the model's adaptability and uncertainty estimates.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Evaluation on multiple abnormal datasets from varied contexts (e.g., different disaster types, geographic regions, policy interventions) with consistent metrics to assess robustness and calibration under stress.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Does the adaptive graph learning in GraphMamba capture meaningful healthcare facility dependencies, or does it overfit to spurious correlations in the training data?  
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The adaptive graph is learned from visit patterns (Section 3.2), but no validation against real-world healthcare network structures (e.g., patient referral networks, facility partnerships) is provided.  
- **Why unresolved**: Without ground-truth spatial dependencies, it's unclear whether the learned adjacency reflects true functional relationships or merely statistical coincidences, which could limit interpretability and transferability.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Comparing the learned adjacency matrices with external data on healthcare facility interactions (e.g., patient transfer records, shared electronic health systems) or ablation studies with perturbed graphs to test sensitivity.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How does HealthMamba's integrated uncertainty quantification compare to specialized conformal prediction or Bayesian methods in terms of calibration and coverage guarantees?  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper proposes a comprehensive UQ module (Section 3.3) and evaluates against baselines with UQ, but does not include recent conformal prediction techniques like CF-GNN mentioned in Related Work (Section 5.2).  
- **Why unresolved**: The UQ module combines multiple mechanisms, but its theoretical guarantees (e.g., coverage validity) are not formally proven, and empirical comparison to state-of-the-art distribution-free methods is lacking.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Rigorous comparison with conformalized graph neural networks or Bayesian neural networks on the same datasets, including metrics like conditional coverage and efficiency, and perhaps theoretical analysis of the calibration loss.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Can HealthMamba scale to national-level or multi-country healthcare systems with heterogeneous facility types and vastly larger spatial graphs?  
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The model is evaluated on four US states (Section 4.1), and complexity analysis shows low parameters, but scalability to graphs with thousands of nodes (e.g., entire US counties or international regions) is not tested.  
- **Why unresolved**: Larger graphs may introduce computational bottlenecks in the adaptive graph learning and Mamba blocks, and spatial dependencies might become noisier or more non-stationary, affecting performance.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments on larger-scale datasets (e.g., nationwide US data, or European countries) with reported training time, memory usage, and performance metrics to assess scalability and potential need for architectural modifications.

## Limitations
- Generalizability restricted to four U.S. state datasets; performance in other regions or healthcare systems remains unknown.
- Uncertainty fusion mechanism lacks detailed justification; heuristic merging may affect calibration.
- Robustness validation relies on limited extreme-event windows without controlled causal analysis; risk of data leakage.
