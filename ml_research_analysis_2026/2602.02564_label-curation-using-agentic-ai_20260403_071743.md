# Label Curation Using Agentic AI
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.02564
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02564
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: AURA coordinates multiple off-the-shelf AI models to infer true labels and annotator reliability without ground truth, improving accuracy over majority voting by up to 5.8% (and up to 50% in low-quality annotator settings).

## Executive Summary
Label curation is a critical but costly bottleneck in machine learning pipelines, typically relying on slow and variable human annotation. This paper introduces AURA, an agentic AI framework that coordinates multiple off-the-shelf AI models to generate and aggregate labels without requiring any ground truth. AURA adapts the classical Dawid–Skene probabilistic model using an Expectation–Maximization algorithm to jointly infer the latent true label for each instance and each annotator’s class-conditional confusion matrix.

Evaluated on four image/video benchmarks (Kinetics-400, ImageNet-ReaL, Food-101, CUB-200), AURA improves accuracy over majority voting by up to 5.8% on average, with gains reaching 50% in settings that include poor-quality annotators. The learned reliability scores strongly correlate with true annotator accuracies, enabling automatic quality assessment. Because AURA treats AI models as black boxes and requires no fine-tuning or in-context learning, it generalizes across modalities and can be deployed with any pre-trained model.

## Method Summary
AURA treats each AI model as a noisy annotator producing a single label per instance. The core algorithm, AEML, is an EM adaptation of the Dawid–Skene model. Input is a dataset and a label matrix from M annotators across N instances. The algorithm initializes uniform class priors and confusion matrices with a diagonal value λ/100 (λ unspecified) and off-diagonals uniform. In the E-step, it computes posterior label probabilities for each instance by weighting annotators’ observed labels with their current confusion matrices. In the M-step, it updates each annotator’s confusion matrix via expectation-weighted counts and updates class priors accordingly. The loop repeats until the change in log-likelihood falls below a threshold γ (value not reported). After convergence, the aggregated label per instance is the argmax of its posterior, and each annotator’s reliability is captured by its final confusion matrix. The approach makes no assumptions about the internal workings of the AI agents and thus works with any off-the-shelf model across modalities.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy gains:** AURA outperforms majority voting by up to 5.8% on average across Kinetics-400, ImageNet-ReaL, Food-101, and CUB-200; with poor-quality annotators, the improvement reaches 50%.
- **Reliability estimation:** The inferred reliability scores (from confusion matrices) show strong correlation with true annotator accuracies, confirming that AURA can assess annotator quality without validation data.
- **Generality:** The framework seamlessly integrates diverse pre-trained models (e.g., GPT, Gemini, Swin, CLIP) without fine-tuning and generalizes across image and video tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Joint Inference of Latent Labels and Annotator Reliability
- **Claim:** AURA infers latent true labels and annotator reliability simultaneously, breaking the circular dependency where label correctness depends on reliability and vice versa.
- **Mechanism:** It adapts the Dawid–Skene model via EM. In the E-step, posterior label probabilities are computed by multiplying class priors and the product of annotator-specific likelihoods, each derived from the current confusion matrix. In the M-step, confusion matrices are updated by normalizing soft counts weighted by these posteriors, and class priors are updated from the averaged posteriors. Iteration maximizes the joint likelihood of observed annotations.
- **Assumptions:** True labels exist as latent variables; annotator predictions are conditionally independent given the true label; error patterns are class-conditional and stationary.
- **Evidence:** The abstract describes this joint inference approach. Section 3.1 provides the E-step (Eq. 4) and M-step (Eqs. 5,6) equations. While the corpus does not directly validate AURA’s specific EM, neighboring work on annotator behavior modeling supports the broader probabilistic approach.
- **Break condition:** Violations of conditional independence (e.g., correlated annotator errors) or non-class-conditional error patterns can degrade estimates; poor initialization or insufficient data may lead to local optima.

### Mechanism 2: Reliability-Weighted Aggregation
- **Claim:** By modeling each annotator’s reliability via a full confusion matrix, AURA weights their contributions more effectively than equal-weight schemes, especially when annotator quality varies.
- **Mechanism:** The posterior probability for a candidate true label is proportional to the product of class prior and the likelihood of each observed annotation given that label. The likelihood term is the corresponding entry from the annotator’s confusion matrix, thus higher reliability for the relevant transition increases influence. This probabilistic weighting optimally combines evidence under the model.
- **Assumptions:** The estimated confusion matrix accurately reflects class-dependent error rates.
- **Evidence:** Section 3.1 explicitly shows that `Pr(ℓ(a)_i | y⋆_i = y⋆, Θ_a) = Θ_a[y⋆, ℓ(a)_i]`, directly modulating influence. Section 4.2 attributes performance gains to this weighting mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If all annotators are homogeneous, majority voting may perform similarly; inaccurate confusion matrix estimation (e.g., due to few samples per class) can lead to mis-weighting.

### Mechanism 3: Off-the-Shelf Agent Coordination Without Fine-Tuning
- **Claim:** AURA provides a generic framework that can coordinate diverse, pre-trained AI models as annotators without requiring any model-specific fine-tuning, in-context learning, or architectural changes.
- **Mechanism:** The framework treats each model as a black box that outputs a label. AURA’s statistical algorithm operates only on the label matrix; it never accesses model internals, logits, or prompts. The annotation generation process is completely decoupled from the aggregation model.
- **Assumptions:** AI agents produce stable label distributions across the dataset; their errors, though complex, are stationary enough to be captured by a static confusion matrix; all agents address the same classification task with identical label spaces.
- **Evidence:** The abstract and section 3 emphasize no fine-tuning or in-context learning is needed. Section 4.1 demonstrates integration of many models (Gemini, GPT, Swin, CLIP, Pegasus) across modalities.
- **Break condition:** If an agent’s output distribution is unstable (e.g., prompt-sensitive) or misunderstands the task, the assumptions break down.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Expectation-Maximization (EM) Algorithm
- **Why needed here:** AEML uses EM to solve the intractable joint estimation of latent true labels and annotator confusion matrices by iteratively maximizing a lower bound on the likelihood.
- **Quick check question:** In the EM framework for this problem, what is the "complete-data" log-likelihood that the M-step maximizes, and why is it intractable to compute directly?

### Concept: Probabilistic Graphical Models (Latent Variable Models)
- **Why needed here:** The Dawid–Skene model is a latent variable model where the true label is a hidden node influencing observed annotator labels. Understanding the conditional independence structure (`y⋆ -> ℓ(a)`) is crucial for deriving the inference equations.
- **Quick check question:** Draw the simple graphical model for one instance and two annotators in AURA, indicating observed and latent nodes and their conditional dependencies.

### Concept: Confusion Matrix / Error Matrix
- **Why needed here:** The confusion matrix `Θ_a` parameterizes an annotator’s reliability and bias, capturing class-dependent error rates, which is more expressive than a single accuracy number.
- **Quick check question:** Why is a full confusion matrix necessary instead of just reporting an overall accuracy for an annotator? Give an example of two annotators with the same overall accuracy but different confusion matrices.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  1. **Annotation Ingestion Module:** Accepts a dataset `X` and a dictionary `{annotator_id: {instance_id: label}}`. Handles parallel calls to diverse AI agents (API or local).  
  2. **AEML Core Engine:** Implements the EM loop. Contains sub-modules for: (a) **E-step Calculator:** Computes posterior `w_iy⋆` using current `p_y⋆` and `Θ_1..M`. (b) **M-step Updater:** Recomputes `Θ_a` (Equation 5) and `p_y⋆` (Equation 6) using posterior weights. (c) **Convergence Checker:** Computes log-likelihood (Equation 7) and compares to threshold `γ`.  
  3. **Output Generator:** After convergence, outputs (a) **Aggregated Labels:** `argmax_y⋆(w_iy⋆)` per instance, and (b) **Reliability Reports:** final confusion matrices `Θ_1..M` and derived scalar reliability scores.

- **Critical path:** The EM loop (E-step → M-step → convergence check) is the heart. Correct matrix operations in the E-step (normalization over classes) and M-step (weighted counting with posterior `w_iy⋆`) are essential. The convergence check must use the same log-likelihood formula as the M-step objective.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Initialization Sensitivity:* Hyperparameter `λ` (diagonal initialization of `Θ_a`) and uniform `p_y⋆` may affect convergence speed and local optimum. More informed initialization (e.g., using a small validation set) could help but violates the "no pre-validation" premise.  
  - *Computational Cost:* Complexity is `O(e * n * M * |Y|^2)`. For large `n`, `M`, or `|Y|`, memory for storing `w_iy⋆` and `Θ_a` becomes a bottleneck; approximations or sparse matrices may be needed.  
  - *Label Space Assumption:* Assumes a fixed, closed label space `Y`. Open-set or hierarchical classification requires modification.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. **Non-monotonic likelihood:** If `LL_curr` decreases in an iteration, there is a bug in the E-step or M-step equations.  
  2. **Naive reliability estimates:** If all final `Θ_a` are nearly identical (e.g., all near uniform), the model failed to distinguish annotators, likely due to insufficient data or poor initialization.  
  3. **Degenerate posteriors:** For many instances, `w_iy⋆` is uniform across classes, indicating the model is uncertain—often happens with very few annotators or extremely noisy ones.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Synthetic Data Recovery Test:** Generate a small dataset (e.g., 100 instances, 5 classes, 3 annotators) with known ground truth and known confusion matrices. Verify AEML recovers the true `Θ_a` and `p_y⋆` closely.  
  2. **Random Annotator Injection Test:** On a real benchmark (e.g., CUB-200), add a "random" annotator (uniform random labels). Confirm AURA down-weights its reliability near 1/|Y| and that its inclusion does not degrade accuracy vs. using only the real models.  
  3. **Convergence Diagnostics:** Plot log-likelihood and label changes per iteration. Ensure monotonic increase in LL and rapid stabilization of label assignments within 10–20 iterations for a moderate-sized dataset. If convergence is slow, investigate threshold `γ` and consider variance in `w_iy⋆`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can heterogeneous computational and financial costs of different AI annotators be systematically incorporated into AURA's framework to enable explicit cost-performance trade-offs?
- **Basis:** The conclusion states: "As an ongoing work, we are investigating how to incorporate heterogeneous costs in the AI models and design a framework that exhibits cost-performance trade-off."
- **Why unresolved:** The current AURA implementation treats all annotators equally in terms of operational cost, focusing solely on label accuracy and reliability without considering inference time, API pricing, or computational resources.
- **Evidence needed:** An extended version of AURA that integrates cost parameters (e.g., monetary cost per inference, latency) into the EM objective or as a regularization term, evaluated through experiments showing trade-offs across budget constraints.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does AURA's underlying assumption of conditional independence among annotators (given the latent true label) remain valid when using modern AI models that may share similar training data, architectures, or failure modes leading to correlated errors?
- **Basis:** The probabilistic model assumes independence conditioned on the true label; the paper does not discuss violations for heterogeneous AI models.
- **Why unresolved:** If AI models exhibit correlated mistakes (e.g., all vision transformers failing on similar patterns), the independence assumption is violated, potentially leading to overconfident reliability estimates and suboptimal aggregation.
- **Evidence needed:** Controlled experiments where annotators are deliberately made to have correlated error patterns (e.g., using model families with shared pretraining), measuring degradation in AURA's accuracy and reliability estimation.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent can AURA generalize to non-visual modalities (e.g., text, audio, structured data) and domains where the label space or annotator error structures differ significantly from the image/video benchmarks evaluated?
- **Basis:** The abstract and introduction claim multi-modal annotation but experiments only cover image/video; no pure text, audio, or tabular tests.
- **Why unresolved:** The EM framework and confusion matrix modeling are modality-agnostic in theory, but performance may degrade in settings with different label distributions, sequential dependencies, or error types like hallucination in LLMs.
- **Evidence needed:** Replication on diverse modality datasets (e.g., GLUE for text, AudioSet for audio), comparing AURA's gains over majority voting and reliability estimation accuracy.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does AURA's performance and convergence behavior scale when the number of AI annotators (M) increases substantially (e.g., to tens or hundreds of models)?
- **Basis:** Experiments use 3–7 annotators; time complexity analysis notes O(e × n × M × |Y|²) but no empirical scalability study.
- **Why unresolved:** Real-world agentic systems might ensemble many specialized models; EM updates and matrix operations may become inefficient or numerically unstable with many annotators.
- **Evidence needed:** Empirical scaling experiments varying M (e.g., 5, 10, 50, 100) on a fixed dataset, tracking convergence epochs, log-likelihood improvement, and accuracy.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Can AURA's reliability estimates accurately capture class-specific expertise of AI annotators in a data-efficient manner, especially for long-tail classes with few annotations?
- **Basis:** The paper claims AURA identifies which agents are better for specific classes and uses per-class confusion matrices, but evaluation only reports overall reliability vs. accuracy, not class-wise calibration.
- **Why unresolved:** For rare classes, posterior label distributions may be too uncertain to reliably estimate per-class confusions, leading to poor identification of class-specific strengths/weaknesses.
- **Evidence needed:** Analysis of class-wise reliability estimates (e.g., per-class F1 of each annotator as inferred by AURA vs. true per-class accuracy) on datasets with controlled class imbalance, showing correlation and discussing failure modes for low-support classes.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter values (λ, γ) and prompt templates are not reported, hindering exact reproducibility and introducing ambiguity in label generation.
- Core assumptions—class-conditional error patterns, conditional independence among annotators, and stationary AI agent behavior—may be violated when using heterogeneous models with correlated failures or prompt-sensitive outputs.
- The paper's evidence for generalization across modalities is weak; evaluation is limited to image/video datasets and lacks external validation on text, audio, or tabular tasks.

## Confidence
- **Joint Inference Mechanism:** High (mathematical derivation aligns with classical Dawid–Skene, explicitly provided).
- **Reliability-Weighted Aggregation Benefit:** Medium (empirical gains shown but disentanglement from other factors unclear).
- **Off-the-Shelf Generalization Across Modalities:** Low (evidence limited to authors' own multi-dataset evaluation; no external replication or unseen modalities).
