# Budget-Sensitive Discovery Scoring: A Formally Verified Framework for Evaluating AI-Guided Scientific Selection
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12349
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12349
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: A simple RF-based greedy proposer achieves the best Discovery Quality Score (DQS = -0.046), outperforming all LLM configurations (best LLM reranker DQS = -0.141) on MoleculeNet HIV.

## Executive Summary
Scientific discovery often involves selecting promising candidates from a large pool under tight budget constraints. Existing evaluation metrics, such as AUROC, fail to account for budget limits and asymmetric costs of false discoveries versus missed opportunities. This paper introduces the Budget-Sensitive Discovery Score (BSDS) and its averaged form, the Discovery Quality Score (DQS), to fill this gap. BSDS is a formally verified metric—with 20 theorems checked in Lean 4—that jointly penalizes false discoveries and excessive abstention at each budget level. The authors evaluate 39 proposers on the MoleculeNet HIV dataset, demonstrating that a simple random forest (RF) based greedy selector outperforms all large language model (LLM) configurations, indicating no marginal value of LLMs over a trained classifier under realistic deployment. The metric generalizes robustly across datasets and penalty weights.

## Method Summary
The framework evaluates AI-guided candidate selection by defining BSDS(B) = HR@B − λ·FDR@B − γ·(1−Cov@B) for any budget B, where HR is hit rate, FDR false discovery rate, and Cov coverage. Averaging BSDS over a set of budgets yields DQS, preventing cherry-picking. The study compares 39 proposers, including a random forest baseline (Greedy-ML), various MLP variants, generative models, retrieval systems, and 28 LLM configurations in zero-shot, few-shot, and reranking modes. The RF baseline uses ECFP4 fingerprints and six molecular descriptors, trained with 500 trees under 5-fold cross-validation to produce out-of-fold probabilities. Evaluation uses bootstrap resampling (1,000 replicates) to compute confidence intervals, and experiments extend to additional datasets (Tox21, ClinTox, etc.). The formal verification of BSDS properties (boundedness, incentive compatibility, oracle dominance) is completed in Lean 4.

## Key Results
- RF-based Greedy-ML proposer achieves the best DQS (−0.046) on MoleculeNet HIV.
- All 28 LLM configurations underperform, with the best LLM reranker DQS = −0.141; no LLM surpasses RF in zero-shot, few-shot, or reranking modes.
- BSDS/DQS distinguish proposers invisible to standard metrics like AUROC and generalize robustly (Kendall τ ≥ 0.636) across datasets and penalty parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** BSDS evaluates candidate selection under realistic budget constraints by jointly penalizing false discoveries (FDR) and excessive abstention (coverage gap) with tunable, asymmetric cost weights.
- **Mechanism:** For a selected subset *S* (|*S*| ≤ *B*) and abstention set *A*, BSDS(*B*) = HR@*B* − λ·FDR@*B* − γ·(1−Cov@*B*). HR@*B* measures recall over true hits; FDR@*B* is the false positive fraction; Cov@*B* is the fraction of candidates receiving a definitive decision (selected or rejected). λ weights the cost of a false positive relative to a true hit; γ penalizes leaving candidates unevaluated.
- **Core assumption:** Scientific discovery problems inherently involve budget limits and asymmetric error costs (false positives waste expensive validation slots; false negatives represent missed opportunities; abstention has an opportunity cost).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "jointly penalizes false discoveries (λ-weighted FDR) and excessive abstention (γ-weighted coverage gap) at each budget level."
  - [section 2.1] Formal Definition 1 and equations (1)-(4).
  - [corpus] Weak: Corpus neighbors discuss LLM evaluation and formal frameworks but do not anchor this specific mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If the problem has no hard budget constraint (*B* = *N*), or if error costs are perfectly symmetric (λ = 0) and abstention is costless (γ = 0), BSDS collapses to simple recall.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Formal verification via Lean 4 ensures BSDS's mathematical properties (boundedness, monotonicity, oracle dominance) are correct by construction, preventing metric manipulation or hidden edge cases when evaluating opaque models like LLMs.
- **Mechanism:** The authors prove 20 theorems (e.g., P1. Boundedness: −(λ+γ) ≤ BSDS ≤ 1; P2. Incentive compatibility; P4. Oracle dominance) using the Lean 4 proof assistant. These theorems guarantee the metric behaves as intended across all valid inputs.
- **Core assumption:** Evaluation metrics for high-stakes, opaque AI systems must be immune to logical flaws, especially when the evaluated system (an LLM proposer) may exploit any metric imperfections.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "formally verified metric—20 theorems machine-checked by the Lean 4 proof assistant."
  - [section 2.2] "We state four core properties... Each has been mechanically verified in Lean 4; proofs are provided in Basu and Chakraborty (2025)."
  - [corpus] Weak: Neighbor "SENTINEL" uses formal frameworks for safety but does not anchor BSDS's verification.
- **Break condition:** If the Lean 4 proofs contain errors (undetected by the checker), or if the implementation deviates from the verified specification.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The budget-averaged Discovery Quality Score (DQS) prevents proposers from cherry-picking a single favorable budget level, providing a single, robust summary statistic for cross-proposer comparison.
- **Mechanism:** DQS = (1/|*B*|) Σ<sub>*B*∈*B*</sub> BSDS(*B*), averaging BSDS across a pre-specified set of budgets *B* = {*B*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *B*<sub>*M*</sub>}. This ensures a proposer cannot achieve a high score by optimizing for a narrow, arbitrarily chosen budget.
- **Core assumption:** A proposer's true quality is its average performance across the spectrum of plausible resource allocations, not its peak performance at one budget.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Its budget-averaged form, the Discovery Quality Score (DQS), provides a single summary statistic that no proposer can inflate by performing well at a cherry-picked budget."
  - [section 2.2] Definition 2 and associated discussion.
  - [section 4.9] "BSDS/DQS distinguish proposers invisible to standard VS metrics... Standard VS metrics... produce identical values for 7 of 8 proposers... only BSDS/DQS distinguish them."
- **Break condition:** If the budget set *B* is not representative of real-world deployment scenarios (e.g., only includes tiny or huge budgets), DQS may misrepresent practical utility.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Constrained Utility Optimization**
  - *Why needed here:* BSDS is a linear utility function subject to a cardinality constraint (|*S*| ≤ *B*). Understanding this trade-off (recall vs. FDR vs. coverage) is fundamental to interpreting scores and setting λ, γ.
  - *Quick check question:* If λ is increased while holding a proposer's (HR, FDR, Cov) constant, what happens to its BSDS at a fixed budget? What does this imply about the relative cost of false positives?
- **Concept: Metric Design Against Gaming**
  - *Why needed here:* The framework must resist "gaming" by proposers that might learn to optimize the metric without improving real discovery outcomes (e.g., by abstaining excessively). The formal properties (incentive compatibility, boundedness) are guardrails against this.
  - *Quick check question:* What would a proposer do if it only cared about maximizing BSDS at a *single* budget *B* without the DQS average? Could it exploit the metric? How does DQS mitigate this?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  **Requirement Spec:** Natural language goal (e.g., "Find HIV antivirals") → Technical spec (target, budget *B*, λ, γ).
  2.  **Proposer:** Any strategy that takes the candidate pool *X* (with or without labels/ML scores) and outputs a ranked list or a selected set *S* (|*S*| ≤ *B*) and optional abstention set *A*.
  3.  **BSDS Evaluator:** Computes HR@*B*, FDR@*B*, Cov@*B* from ground truth *g(x)* for all candidates. Applies λ, γ to compute BSDS(*B*) for each budget in *B*. Aggregates to DQS.
  4.  **Bootstrap Engine:** Resamples dataset (with replacement), retrains base ML models (RF), reruns proposer & evaluator, computes confidence intervals (BCa).
- **Critical path:** Technical spec → Proposer ranking → BSDS evaluation per budget → DQS aggregation → Bootstrap replication. The base ML model (RF) training is a shared upstream dependency for all ML-based proposers.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Simplicity vs. Expressiveness:* Greedy-ML (top-*B* by RF score) is simple and effective. More complex proposers (BSDS-Recursive, LLM rerankers) add parameters and failure modes but aim for marginal gains.
  - *Formal Guarantees vs. Practical Performance:* BSDS is formally verified but relies on correctly set λ, γ. The RF baseline is a simple, high-performing model; adding MLP layers or LLM reasoning degrades performance, showing that complexity isn't free.
  - *Evaluation Rigor vs. Cost:* 1,000 bootstrap replicates and 39 proposers are computationally expensive but necessary for reliable confidence intervals.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Random or worse-than-random DQS:* Proposer lacks discriminative signal (e.g., Generative with high τ, many Direct-mode LLMs).
  - *High variance in DQS across bootstrap seeds:* Proposer is unstable or sensitive to training set fluctuations (e.g., Retrieval's knowledge base diversity).
  - *Strong degradation under scaffold split:* Proposer overfits to molecular scaffolds seen during training (observed for MLP variants).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Baseline sanity check:** Run Greedy-ML and Random on a small subset. Verify Greedy-ML's BSDS is positive at some budgets and its DQS >> Random's (~-0.8). Check that increasing *B* increases HR but may increase FDR.
  2.  **LLM marginal value test:** Run one zero-shot LLM (e.g., ChatGPT-5.2 Direct) and its Rerank variant. Confirm neither exceeds Greedy-ML's DQS and that Rerank > Direct (partial recovery).
  3.  **Parameter sensitivity probe:** Compute DQS for Greedy-ML, Retrieval, and BSDS-Recursive across a small grid (λ ∈ {0.1, 1.0}, γ ∈ {0.1, 0.5}). Verify ranking stability (Kendall τ > 0.7) and that absolute DQS scales with λ (more negative at high λ).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can advanced LLM techniques (CoT, RAG, tools) match or exceed the Greedy-ML baseline in DQS?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Sec 5.7 states "the open question is whether structured molecular reasoning can close the gap with Greedy-ML." Sec 5.6 notes LLM protocol limitations.
- **Why unresolved:** Current tests only include zero/few-shot SMILES prompting; no CoT/RAG/tool use.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate LLMs on HIV/Tox21 using CoT, RAG (ChEMBL), and tool-augmented docking; compare DQS to Greedy-ML.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Is BSDS applicable to regression and multi-objective discovery tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Sec 5.7 calls for "multi-objective extension" and Sec 5.6 lists "regression targets" as a gap.
- **Why unresolved:** BSDS currently only defined for binary outcomes; no validation on continuous or multi-endpoint data.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Adapt BSDS for thresholds on continuous outcomes or conjunctive binary objectives; evaluate on MoleculeNet regression or multi-task datasets.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Do graph neural networks (GNNs) outperform the ECFP4+RF baseline on DQS?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Sec 5.6 says "GNNs remain unexplored." Sec 4.7 shows frozen text embeddings underperform ECFP4+RF.
- **Why unresolved:** The study did not include GNNs; only RF with ECFP4 and text embeddings were tested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train GNNs (e.g., D-MPNN) on HIV using same CV; use greedy selection on GNN scores; compute DQS and compare against RF baseline.

## Limitations
- LLM evaluation stability depends on proprietary APIs and prompt sensitivity; results may not generalize across model versions or query times.
- Penalty parameter generality: Robustness tested on a limited grid (λ∈{0.1,1}, γ∈{0.1,0.5}); behavior outside this range or for other scientific domains is unknown.
- External validity: All experiments use molecular screening datasets; BSDS/DQS performance in other scientific selection contexts (e.g., literature review, materials design) is unvalidated.

## Confidence
- BSDS/DQS mathematical properties (via Lean 4 verification): High
- Comparative outperformance of RF over LLMs on MoleculeNet: Medium
- Generalization claim across datasets and penalties: Low
