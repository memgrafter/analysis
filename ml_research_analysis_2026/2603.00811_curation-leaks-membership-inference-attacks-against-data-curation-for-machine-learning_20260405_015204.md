# Curation Leaks: Membership Inference Attacks against Data Curation for Machine Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00811
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00811
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Data curation pipelines leak membership information across all stages (scores, selection, model), with image-based curation showing >98% TPR at 1% FPR; differential privacy (ε=10) reduces leakage to baseline.

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies and measures membership inference vulnerabilities in data curation pipelines, which are used to select training data from a public pool using a private target dataset. The authors design systematic attacks targeting each stage of curation—score computation, subset selection, and final model training—for two representative methods: image-based nearest-neighbor similarity and TRAK gradient-based scoring. Results show severe leakage, particularly for image-based curation, and demonstrate that even models trained only on curated data can be attacked via poisoned fingerprints. Differential privacy is shown to be an effective mitigation, reducing attack success to random chance at ε=10.

## Method Summary
The attack framework adapts membership inference techniques to the curation setting. For observed curation scores, the attacker uses likelihood ratio attacks (LiRA) or custom methods exploiting structural properties (e.g., voting for deterministic nearest neighbors, least squares for TRAK's linear system). For the binary selection mask, a binary LiRA models selection likelihoods via shadow curation runs, while deterministic methods enable iterative reconstruction attacks. The end-to-end attack actively poisons the public pool with fingerprinted samples that imprint a detectable signal in the final model if the corresponding target sample was used in curation. Defenses are evaluated using differentially private mechanisms applied to score computation.

## Key Results
- Image-based curation scores leak catastrophically: custom voting achieves >98% TPR at 1% FPR, matching an oracle.
- TRAK's gradient averaging provides partial protection but still yields 100% TPR at 1% FPR for small target sets (|T|=100); leakage diminishes for larger |T|.
- Selection mask alone leaks significantly; binary LiRA and iterative voting recover membership effectively.
- End-to-end fingerprinting attacks succeed by poisoning the pool, imprinting detectable signals in the final model.
- Differentially private curation (ε=10) reduces all attacks to near-baseline (~1% TPR).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Score Leakage
- **Claim:** Continuous curation scores leak membership information about the private target dataset.
- **Mechanism:** Attackers observe scores assigned to public pool samples. For image-based curation (nearest-neighbor similarity), the deterministic mapping allows reverse-engineering which target samples influenced scores via voting. For TRAK (gradient averaging), signals are diffused, but sparse recovery techniques attempt to solve for the membership mask from the linear system of scores.
- **Core assumption:** Scores are observable; scoring function has invertible or exploitable structure.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] attacks target "computation of curation scores"; [section 3.3] details "LiRA for Scores", "Custom Voting (Image-based)", and "Least Squares (TRAK)".

### Mechanism 2: Selection Leakage
- **Claim:** The binary selection of the curated subset leaks membership, even without score magnitudes.
- **Mechanism:** Attacker observes only which public samples were selected (binary mask). Using shadow curation runs on random target subsets, they model selection probabilities (Bernoulli) for each target and compute likelihood ratios (Binary LiRA). For deterministic methods like image-based, iterative voting based on reconstructed nearest-neighbor relationships recovers the target set.
- **Core assumption:** Selection rule is known and consistent (e.g., top-k); adversary can perform shadow curation with access to target datasets.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] attacks target "selection of the curated subset"; [section 3.4] describes "LiRA for Subsets" and "Voting-based Membership Inference (Image-based)".

### Mechanism 3: End-to-End Model Leakage via Poisoning
- **Claim:** Models trained solely on curated public data leak membership about the target set used for curation.
- **Mechanism:** Attacker inserts a few "fingerprinted" samples into the public pool *before* curation. These are crafted so their curation score is highly sensitive to the presence of a specific target sample. If the target was used, the fingerprint is selected and imprints a detectable signal (e.g., elevated probability of an unrelated concept) in the final model, detectable via black-box queries.
- **Core assumption:** Public pool can be poisoned or contains manipulable samples; model training preserves the fingerprint signal.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes inserting "fingerprinted samples" that "imprint detectable signals"; [section 3.5] details fingerprint construction for image-based and TRAK.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Membership Inference Attack (MIA)
  - **Why needed:** The paper quantifies leakage via MIAs; understanding the standard MIA framework (shadow models, likelihood ratios) is prerequisite to adapting it for curation stages.
  - **Quick check:** How does a standard MIA on a model differ from attacking a curation score or selection mask?
- **Concept:** Data Curation Pipeline
  - **Why needed:** The paper attacks specific stages (score, selection, model) of a curation pipeline; one must understand how curation uses a private target set T to select from a public pool D.
  - **Quick check:** What is the attacker's knowledge about D and T in the threat model?
- **Concept:** Differential Privacy (DP)
  - **Why needed:** DP is the primary mitigation evaluated (Section 5); designing DP for curation requires understanding the Gaussian mechanism and sensitivity of curation operations.
  - **Quick check:** How does adding noise to the *mean* of target embeddings (DP mean) provide stronger privacy for image-based curation than noisy-max on individual similarities?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  - Private target dataset T -> Score computation (image-based or TRAK) -> Continuous scores -> Subset selection (top-k) -> Binary mask -> Model training on selected pool -> Final model
  - Attack points: scores, mask, final model; plus optional poisoning of public pool D.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Identify curation method and threat model capability (passive scores/subset vs. active poisoning).
  2. For scores/subset: Generate shadow curation outputs using random subsets of T. Filter for most informative public samples per target (Eq. 1). Fit in/out distributions. Compute membership score (LiRA or custom).
  3. For end-to-end: Craft fingerprints (image-based: mislabeled captions; TRAK: orthogonal suffixes). Identify candidate fingerprints via correspondence or SNR. Insert into D. Run victim curation/training. Query model for fingerprint signal. Compute membership surprise.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **LiRA vs. Custom:** LiRA is method-agnostic but needs many shadow runs and good distribution fit. Custom attacks exploit specific structure (e.g., nearest-neighbor determinism) but may not generalize (e.g., to TRAK).
  - **Poisoning necessity:** End-to-end attack requires poisoning pool; score/subset attacks are passive. Feasibility depends on pool trustworthiness.
  - **Efficiency vs. Efficacy:** Custom attacks (voting) can be more sample-efficient but fail if influence is sparse (many zero-influence targets). LiRA requires more resources.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Image-based: Many targets have zero nearest-neighbor influence (Figure 2); attack AUC ~0.5 for such targets.
  - TRAK: Large target set size (>~10k) dilutes signal; attack AUC drops. Low projection dimension (<1024) may cause failure due to information loss.
  - End-to-end: Fingerprint signal weak or undetectable in trained model (Figures 10,11); may require many fingerprints or large training sets.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Score leakage baseline: Run image-based curation on small dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10 targets on CommonPool). Apply LiRA and custom voting attacks on released scores. Verify AUC > 0.9 and custom matches oracle.
  2. Subset leakage without scores: Given only top-k selected public samples, run iterative voting attack for image-based. Verify reconstructed target set matches actual for samples with non-zero influence.
  3. End-to-end fingerprinting: Insert 5 fingerprints into pool, run TRAK curation and model training. Query model for fingerprint concept probability. Verify statistically significant difference between models trained with/without corresponding target in T.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What is the full privacy-utility trade-off of differentially private data curation?** Evaluation is limited to ε={10,100} on six datasets; many ε values, utility metrics, and composition effects across stages remain unknown.
- **Do other popular data curation methods (e.g., uncertainty sampling, diversity selection) suffer similar membership leakage?** The study explicitly focuses on two methods; different mechanisms may leak in distinct ways.
- **Are fingerprinting attacks practical in real-world curation services where public data pools are reused?** Feasibility in operational settings is not demonstrated; required fingerprints must be selective and benign; insertion may be constrained by provenance tracking or quality controls.

## Limitations
- Access to scores/selection or pool poisoning may be unrealistic in practice.
- Only image-based and TRAK curation evaluated; other methods and modalities untested.
- TRAK attack effectiveness for large target sets (>10k) or low-dimensional projections is unknown.
- DP evaluated only at ε=10; tighter privacy budgets unstudied.

## Confidence
- **Image-based leakage (scores & subset): High** (≥98% TPR@1%FPR).
- **TRAK leakage for small |T|: High** (100% TPR@1%FPR); **scalability: Medium**.
- **Poisoning end-to-end: Medium** (validated on two models only).
- **DP mitigation: High** (TPR≈1% at ε=10).

## Next Checks
