# From Prompt to Production:Automating Brand-Safe Marketing Imagery with Text-to-Image Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.13349
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13349
- **Reference count**: 31
- **Primary result**: Fully automated multi-stage pipeline increases product fidelity by 30.77% (DINOv2 similarity) and human preference by 52.00% over baseline models.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of generating scalable, brand-consistent marketing imagery using text-to-image models. It proposes a fully automated, multi-stage pipeline that structures prompts, retrieves assets, plans compositions with systematic variations, and applies a multi-modal quality control guardrail system. Evaluated on 1,030 product-caption pairs, the system yields a 30.77% improvement in product fidelity (measured by DINOv2 similarity) and a 52.00% boost in human preference compared to existing baselines such as ICLight, Nova Canvas, and Replace Anything.

## Method Summary
The solution pipelines the generation process into four key stages: (1) **prompt structuring** to encode brand guidelines and product details into model-friendly text; (2) **asset retrieval** to fetch reference product images and brand assets; (3) **composition planning** that introduces controlled variations while preserving key product elements; and (4) a **multi-modal quality control guardrail** that filters outputs based on fidelity and safety criteria. The pipeline operates end-to-end without human intervention, enabling high-throughput production of brand-safe marketing images.

## Key Results
- Product fidelity (DINOv2 similarity) increased by **30.77%** over baseline methods.  
- Human preference improved by **52.00%** compared to competing systems.  
- Demonstrated end-to-end automation on a dataset of **1,030 product-caption pairs**.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism relies on decomposing open-ended text-to-image generation into constrained, sequential stages. Prompt structuring steers the model toward brand-compliant descriptions; asset retrieval grounds the generation in real product visuals; composition planning balances diversity with consistency; and the quality guardrail enforces multimodal safety and fidelity thresholds. This structured approach mitigates the variability of free-form diffusion models while preserving creative flexibility, resulting in reliable brand-safe outputs at scale.

## Foundational Learning
- **Text-to-image diffusion models**: Generative foundation; must support high-quality synthesis from detailed prompts. *Quick check*: Generate a baseline image from a simple prompt and inspect realism.
- **Multimodal similarity metrics (DINOv2)**: Quantifies product fidelity by comparing generated and reference embeddings. *Quick check*: Compute similarity between two known similar images to verify metric behavior.
- **Asset retrieval**: Provides visual grounding; ensures product appearance matches catalog. *Quick check*: Perform a nearest‑neighbor search in the asset database for a given product ID.
- **Prompt engineering/structuring**: Translates brand rules into effective model inputs. *Quick check*: Compare outputs of unstructured vs. structured prompts for adherence to constraints.
- **Quality control guardrails**: Filters unsafe or low‑fidelity images using multimodal signals. *Quick check*: Run a mixed set of compliant/non‑compliant images through the guardrail and check recall/precision.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Product Caption → Prompt Structurer → Asset Retriever → Composition Planner → Image Generator (text‑to‑image model) → Quality Guardrail → Output Image.

**Critical path**: Prompt Structurer → Asset Retriever (if needed) → Composition Planner → Image Generator → Quality Guardrail.

**Design tradeoffs**: Automation trades off flexibility for consistency; systematic variations limit diversity but protect brand elements; guardrail thresholds balance false positives against brand safety; asset retrieval improves fidelity at the cost of latency.

**Failure signatures**: Low DINOv2 similarity scores; guardrail rejections; visual artifacts (e.g., product distortion); missing brand elements; excessive generation time.

**First experiments**:
1. Smoke test: Run the pipeline on a single product‑caption pair and validate intermediate outputs at each stage.
2. Variation impact study: Adjust the composition planner’s randomness parameter and measure changes in product fidelity and human preference.
3. Guardrail audit: Evaluate detection accuracy using a balanced test set of known compliant and non‑compliant generated images.

## Limitations
- Dataset diversity and realism are unclear; the 1,030 product‑caption pairs may not represent broad retail categories.
- Implementation details (e.g., asset retrieval mechanisms, prompt‑structuring rules) are opaque, hindering replication.
- Long‑term operational costs and failure modes (e.g., handling unseen product angles or ambiguous brand guidelines) are unaddressed.
