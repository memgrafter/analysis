# Locate-then-Sparsify: Attribution Guided Sparse Strategy for Visual Hallucination Mitigation

## Quick Facts
* **arXiv ID:** 2603.16284
* **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16284
* **Reference count:** 40
* **Primary result:** Reduces hallucinations (CHAIR S: 50.2→46.8 with Nullu on LLaVA-7B; POPE accuracy: 79.11%→79.92%), preserves generalization (LLaVA-Bench detailedness: 5.51→6.23), and improves MME perception scores while maintaining inference speed.

## Executive Summary
Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) are prone to generating hallucinated content. Existing feature steering methods apply uniform adjustments across all layers, which can degrade performance on non-hallucination tasks. This paper proposes Locate-then-Sparsify (LTS-FS), a two-stage framework: first, identify layers most responsible for hallucinations using causal intervention attribution on a synthetic bi-granularity (token/sentence) dataset; second, apply sparse layer-wise steering with intensities proportional to each layer's relevance. Experiments on LLaVA and Qwen-VL models show LTS-FS effectively reduces object hallucinations and improves factuality while preserving or enhancing general vision-language capabilities and maintaining inference efficiency.

## Method Summary
LTS-FS comprises three steps: (1) **Dataset construction:** Generate responses from a base LVLM on existing hallucination benchmarks (CHAIR, POPE, Antidote) and annotate hallucinated tokens (token-level) or sentences (sentence-level). (2) **Attribution computation:** For each layer, mask individual attention heads and measure the change in logit probability for hallucinatory tokens. Sum across heads to get token-level attribution scores; for sentence-level, aggregate token scores using weights based on cue tokens, position, and hallucination indicator. Average over samples to obtain a layer relevance score. (3) **Sparse steering:** Threshold the scores to create a binary mask; normalize the masked scores to get per-layer scaling factors. Combine with a base steering intensity (from Nullu or VTI) to derive layer-specific intensities. During inference, apply the steering function (e.g., projection) to each layer's features using its intensity.

## Key Results
- **Hallucination reduction:** CHAIR_S decreases from 50.2 to 46.8 (with Nullu on LLaVA-7B); POPE accuracy improves from 79.11% to 79.92%.
- **Generalization preserved:** LLaVA-Bench detailedness score increases from 5.51 to 6.23; MME perception scores improve.
- **Efficiency maintained:** Inference speed comparable to regular (non-steered) models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Causal Attribution Quantifies Layer Relevance
- **Claim:** The method identifies which transformer layers contribute most to hallucination outputs by measuring the causal effect of each layer’s attention heads on the logits of hallucinatory tokens.
- **Mechanism:** For each layer \(l\), the output of its multi-head attention \(a^l\) is masked head-by-head; the change in logit probability for hallucination tokens (Eq. 5) is summed across heads to form an attribution score \(s_l^{tok}\). For sentence-level hallucinations, these token scores are weighted and aggregated (Eq. 7) using cues like position and hallucination indicators.
- **Core assumption:** Intervening on specific attention heads isolates a layer’s causal influence on hallucination generation; higher attribution scores indicate greater relevance.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [Abstract] explicitly states they “introduce an attribution method based on causal interventions to quantify the hallucination relevance of each layer.”  
  - [Section 3.2] details the intervention procedure (masking MHA outputs) and sentence-level weighting scheme.  
  - [Corpus] Neighbors (e.g., “Reducing Hallucinations... via Latent Space Steering”) also explore causal analysis, but LTS-FS uniquely applies head-level interventions to assign fine-grained layer scores; corpus supports the general viability of causal approaches but not the exact scoring procedure.
- **Break condition:** If attention heads are highly redundant or distributed representations dominate, masking may not cleanly isolate a layer’s contribution, leading to noisy or flat attribution scores that fail to distinguish relevant layers.

### Mechanism 2: Sparse Steering Preserves Generalization
- **Claim:** Steering features only in high-relevance layers (via a mask threshold \(r_s\)) reduces hallucinations while minimizing disruption to the model’s original feature distribution, thereby preserving generalization.
- **Mechanism:** Attribution scores \(s_l\) are thresholded (\(τ = r_s \cdot \frac{1}{L}\sum s_l\)) to create a binary mask \(m_l\). Normalized scores \(\tilde{s}_l\) scale the steering intensity \(\lambda_l = \lambda \cdot m_l + \lambda \cdot \tilde{s}_l\) (Algorithm 1). Feature steering (e.g., Nullu’s projection) is then applied with layer-specific \(\lambda_l\).
- **Core assumption:** Hallucination-causing computations are concentrated in a subset of layers; applying uniform steering to all layers unnecessarily perturbs layers that handle general language or visual reasoning, harming performance on non-hallucination tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [Abstract] notes they “propose a layerwise strategy that converts these scores into feature steering intensities for individual layers, enabling more precise adjustments specifically on hallucination-relevant layers.”  
  - [Section 3.3] presents the masking and scaling equations.  
  - [Corpus] Multiple neighbors adopt sparsity (e.g., “Steering LVLMs via Sparse Autoencoder” uses SAE latents; “Dynamic Multimodal Activation Steering” finds sparse attention subsets), indicating sparse steering is a recognized direction for targeted editing.
- **Break condition:** If hallucinations arise from many layers with similarly high relevance, the mask may either include too many (still harming generalization) or exclude critical ones (insufficient mitigation). Also, if attribution scores are miscalibrated, the intensity scaling could over- or under-correct.

### Mechanism 3: Bi-granularity Dataset Aligns Layer Scores to Hallucination Forms
- **Claim:** Constructing a dataset with both token-level and sentence-level hallucinations allows attribution to capture layer relevance for both short, precise outputs (e.g., QA) and longer, free-form descriptions (e.g., captions).
- **Mechanism:** Token-level samples (from POPE/Antidote) are labeled for specific hallucinated tokens; sentence-level samples (from CHAIR) are labeled for entire hallucinatory clauses. During attribution, task-specific granularity guides whether to use \(s_l^{tok}\) directly or aggregate via weighted sentence scoring.
- **Core assumption:** Different hallucination manifestations (single incorrect entity vs. extended fabrication) engage different computational pathways; using a single granularity may miss relevant layers for other output types.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [Section 3.1] describes the dataset construction from CHAIR, POPE, Antidote with granularity split.  
  - [Section 4.5] Table 4 ablation shows combining both granularities yields the best CHAIR_S and POPE accuracy, confirming complementary benefits.  
  - [Corpus] Evidence is weak; most neighbors focus on one granularity (e.g., object-level in “Causally-Grounded Dual-Path Attention”). The corpus does not strongly support the bi-granularity concept itself, only the broader goal of granular analysis.
- **Break condition:** If the granularity split does not correlate with actual layer behavior (e.g., sentence-level attribution also works well for short answers), the extra dataset construction cost may not be justified. Additionally, if the weighting scheme in Eq. 6 misrepresents token importance, aggregated scores could be biased.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Causal Interventional Analysis**  
  *Why needed:* LTS-FS relies on do-calculus style interventions (masking attention heads) to estimate a layer’s causal effect on hallucination logits. Without grasping counterfactual reasoning, one cannot interpret attribution scores correctly.  
  *Quick check question:* How does masking the output of one attention head while keeping others fixed help isolate its contribution to a specific output token?
- **Concept: Transformer Layer Mechanics**  
  *Why needed:* The method manipulates features after Multi-Head Attention and MLP sublayers (Eqs. 1–3). Understanding residual connections, LayerNorm, and attention flow is essential to predict where steering will propagate.  
  *Quick check question:* In the forward pass, how does the residual connection around the MLP (Eq. 3) affect where feature steering should be applied to change the final hidden state?
- **Concept: Feature Steering in LVLMs**  
  *Why needed:* LTS-FS is a layer-wise controller on top of existing steering methods (Nullu, VTI). Knowing how those baseline methods define the steering function \(f\) (e.g., projection onto anti-hallucination direction) is necessary to implement the intensity scaling.  
  *Quick check question:* What would happen if you applied a steering vector with magnitude \(\lambda\) uniformly across all layers versus applying varying \(\lambda_l\) per layer?
- **Concept: Hallucination Benchmarks (CHAIR, POPE)**  
  *Why needed:* These metrics quantify object hallucinations and factuality. Interpreting results (CHAIR_S, POPE accuracy) guides hyperparameter choices (e.g., \(r_s\)).  
  *Quick check question:* Why does a lower CHAIR_S indicate better performance, and how does it differ from POPE’s yes/no accuracy?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  1. *Data Construction Module:* Takes existing hallucination benchmarks (CHAIR, POPE, Antidote) and filters/generates token- or sentence-level labeled samples.  
  2. *Attribution Module:* For a calibrated subset (e.g., 100 samples per granularity), runs forward passes with masked attention heads to compute \(s_l^{tok}\) and aggregates to \(s_l^{sent}\).  
  3. *Steering Control Module:* Computes threshold \(τ\) from \(s_l\), creates mask \(m_l\), normalizes to \(\tilde{s}_l\), derives per-layer intensity \(\lambda_l\), and applies the steering function \(f\) (from chosen baseline) to each layer’s feature during inference.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Build bi-granularity dataset (ensuring no overlap with evaluation sets).  
  2. Run attribution on calibration samples → obtain \(s_l\).  
  3. Choose \(r_s\) (default 0.5) and compute \(m_l, \tilde{s}_l\).  
  4. During inference of target LVLM, apply steering with \(\lambda_l\) to selected layers.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - \(r_s\): Lower values (e.g., 0.3) steer more layers → stronger hallucination reduction but risk over-altering feature distribution; higher values (e.g., 0.7) preserve generalization better but may miss some relevant layers. Tab. 5 shows stable gains for \(r_s \in [0.5, 0.7]\).  
  - Weighting parameters (\(\lambda_{cue}, \lambda_{pos}, \lambda_{hall}\)): Ablation (Tab. 11) indicates hallucination token weight is most critical; cue and position provide auxiliary gains. Simpler setups can omit them.  
  - Granularity choice: Use token-level for short QA (POPE); sentence-level for longer captions (CHAIR); both for mixed tasks.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Attribution scores nearly uniform across layers → dataset or intervention design flawed; reconsider the masking strategy or calibration sample diversity.  
  - Steering drastically reduces Recall or output length → over-sparsification or incorrect \(\lambda_l\); adjust \(r_s\) upward or reduce \(\lambda\).  
  - No improvement on out-of-distribution benchmarks (MME, LLaVA-Bench) → possible overfitting to training hallucination distribution; try calibrating on more diverse samples or reducing reliance on sentence-level weights.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. *Attribution sanity check:* Run attribution on a small set; plot \(s_l\) vs. layer index. Expect a non-uniform pattern with peaks at later layers for sentence hallucinations (per Fig. 1 intuition).  
  2. *Steering isolation test:* Apply LTS-FS to a single input; extract features from a high-score and a low-score layer; compare t-SNE distributions to the regular model (as in Fig. 1a). High-score layers should show more shift; low-score layers should remain similar.  
  3. *End-to-end replication:* Evaluate on CHAIR and POPE using default \(r_s=0.5\) and Nullu as steering base. Verify that LTS-FS beats both regular and plain Nullu (e.g., CHAIR_S reduction from ~50.2 to ~46.8 on LLaVA-7B). If numbers diverge, check attribution score stability and mask computation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper highlights several open questions for future work:
1. **What are the intrinsic characteristics and functional roles of the layers identified as hallucination-relevant by LTS-FS?** (Explicitly noted in the Conclusion; need to investigate whether these layers are consistently early or late, specific to certain processing types, or vary across architectures and tasks.)
2. **How does LTS-FS scale to larger LVLMs (e.g., 70B parameters)?** (Limitations mention that existing feature steering techniques have not been evaluated on larger models, and extending to 70B remains a challenge due to potential differences in layer-wise dynamics and computational costs.)
3. **Can LTS-FS be integrated into the pre-training process of LVLMs to more fundamentally reduce hallucinations?** (Conclusion suggests exploring pre-training integration to address root causes rather than post-hoc steering.)
4. **How robust is the causal attribution method to variations in hallucination types and dataset domains beyond the synthetic bi-granularity dataset?** (Inferred from the limited scope of the calibration dataset; real-world hallucinations are diverse, and the current dataset may not capture all patterns.)

## Limitations
- Calibration dataset specifics (exact samples, annotation rules) are not disclosed, risking noisy attribution and reproducibility issues.
- Attribution computation protocol (intervention on attention heads, handling of KV cache) is underspecified.
- Base steering parameters (λ, f for Nullu/VTI) and generalizability of \(r_s=0.5\) across models are unknown.
- Sentence-level weighting scheme (cue/position/hallucination weights) lacks detailed ablation beyond Table 11.

## Next Checks
1. **Attribution score stability:** Compute layer attribution scores on 5 random subsets (n=200) of calibration data. Report coefficient of variation (std/mean) per layer; expect most layers to have CV < 0.1.
2. **Cross-model transferability:** Apply LTS-FS (with \(r_s=0.5\) and same base λ from LLaVA-7B) to LLaVA-13B and Qwen-VL2.5-7B without retuning. Expect similar relative reductions in CHAIR_S and preservation of LLaVA-Bench detailedness.
3. **Misattribution stress test:** Deliberately invert the layer ranking (assign high \(r_s\) to low-score layers and vice versa) and run steering. Should cause significant drop in LLaVA-Bench detailedness and/or MME perception, confirming that correct layer identification is crucial.
