# HLE-Verified: A Systematic Verification and Structured Revision of Humanity's Last Exam

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.13964
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13964
*   **Reference Count:** 20
*   **Primary Result:** Systematically verifies and revises 2,520 Humanity's Last Exam items, yielding 668 gold and 1,143 revised items, with 7-10 percentage point accuracy gains on state-of-the-art models.

## Executive Summary
Humanity's Last Exam (HLE) is a large-scale, multi-domain benchmark for evaluating advanced reasoning in AI systems. However, the original dataset contains significant noise from erroneous problem statements or answers, which biases model evaluations. The HLE-Verified project addresses this through a rigorous two-stage protocol. Stage I uses expert review and model cross-checks to isolate a high-confidence gold subset of 668 items. Stage II systematically repairs 1,143 flawed but fixable items while preserving their original evaluation intent. The remaining 689 items are retained in an "Uncertain" subset with explicit documentation of their error sources. Evaluating eight state-of-the-art models on HLE-Verified shows substantial improvements in both accuracy (7–10 percentage points absolute gain) and calibration compared to the original benchmark, demonstrating that structured verification is essential for faithful capability measurement.

## Method Summary
The core method is a two-stage verification and revision pipeline applied to the full HLE corpus. **Stage I (Binary Validation)** decomposes each item into `Problem`, `Answer`, and `Rationale` components. Domain experts provide binary judgments on the validity of the `Problem` and `Answer`. Concurrently, multiple frontier AI models attempt each item; systematic failure to reproduce the reference answer (`pass@8`) flags potential errors. Items pass only if both expert reviews confirm validity. **Stage II (Systematic Revision)** targets items failed in Stage I but deemed repairable (restricted to domains with decidable correctness, like math and science). Two independent expert teams propose edits following a `Problem Fix → Solution Fix → Answer Fix` sequence, augmented by model suggestions. A final adjudicator synthesizes a canonical revision under strict constraints: preserve the original evaluation objective, ensure correctness, and minimize edits. Items not passing Stage I and not repairable are placed in an `Uncertain` subset with structured metadata about the source of indeterminacy and required expertise.

## Key Results
*   A verified gold subset of 668 items with validated problem statements and answers.
*   A revised subset of 1,143 items where original errors were corrected while maintaining evaluation intent.
*   Average absolute accuracy gains of 7–10 percentage points for eight state-of-the-art models on HLE-Verified versus original HLE; gains reach 30–40 percentage points on items with originally erroneous problems or answers.
*   Significant reduction in model calibration error, indicating more reliable confidence estimates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Component-wise Binary Validation
*   **Claim:** Decomposing validity into independent `Problem` and `Answer` judgments, combined with expert review and model replication signals, effectively isolates a high-confidence gold subset by filtering out flawed items.
*   **Mechanism:** Each item is assessed on binary validity for its `Problem` and `Answer` components. Expert judgment provides human-domain knowledge. Model `pass@8` (replication rate across multiple solvers) acts as an auxiliary consistency check: systematic solver failure suggests a specification or answer-key error. Only items with both components adjudicated as valid enter the gold set.
*   **Core Assumption:** Flaws in `Problem` or `Answer` are the primary drivers of measurement bias and are detectable via this hybrid human-model workflow; `Rationale` defects alone do not invalidate an answer-based evaluation.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] yield of 668 gold items; [section 3.2] details the serial-hybrid expert-model-adjudication workflow.

### Mechanism 2: Constraint-bound Systematic Revision
*   **Claim:** A structured repair process with explicit "preserve evaluation intent" constraints can correct common error types (e.g., incorrect constants, ambiguous phrasing) without altering the core reasoning skill being tested.
*   **Mechanism:** For repairable items (domains with decidable correctness), two expert teams independently propose fixes in a `Problem → Solution → Answer` sequence, with model outputs as auxiliary candidates. A final adjudicator synthesizes revisions under three principles: (1) objective preservation, (2) correctness, and (3) minimal necessary edits. Revised items undergo re-verification.
*   **Core Assumption:** Many HLE flaws are local and corrective rather than fundamental, allowing repairs that maintain comparability with the original benchmark.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] revision of 1,143 items; [section 3.3] defines repairability scope and the dual-track repair workflow; [section 4.3] shows dominant defect types (e.g., `Incorrect Answer`) are often locally correctable.

### Mechanism 3: Transparent Uncertainty Documentation
*   **Claim:** Explicitly documenting items that remain indeterminate after verification, with structured metadata on error source and required expertise, separates epistemic limits from benchmark indeterminacy and enables principled community refinement.
*   **Mechanism:** Items not passing Stage I and deemed unrepairable are not discarded. They are tagged with an `uncertainty_source` (e.g., "underspecified assumptions") and a `required_expertise` (e.g., "specialist in quantum chemistry"), making the boundaries of current verification capability transparent.
*   **Core Assumption:** Transparency about unverifiable items is preferable to silent exclusion; metadata guides future targeted refinement efforts.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] 689 uncertain items with explicit sources and expertise tags; [section 3.4] defines the uncertainty criteria and metadata schema; [section 4.1] shows high uncertainty rates across components, especially in Humanities.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Component-wise Validity Decomposition**
    *   **Why Needed Here:** Treats `Problem`, `Answer`, and `Rationale` as having independent validity. An item can have a valid problem but an invalid answer, or vice versa. Aggregate item-level scoring obscures this, leading to misattribution of model failures.
    *   **Quick Check:** A math problem correctly states "Solve x²=4" (valid `Problem`) but the `Answer` is "x=2" (invalid, missing x=-2). This means a model failing on this item is not necessarily bad at solving quadratics; it may have been penalized for a correct answer conflicting with a flawed key.
*   **Concept: Conservative Decision Theory in Benchmark Construction**
    *   **Why Needed Here:** The protocol prioritizes minimizing false inclusions (keeping flawed items) over maximizing coverage. This asymmetric risk calculus explains the small gold subset (668 items) and the decision to preserve uncertain items rather than guess.
    *   **Quick Check:** Including a flawed item that incorrectly penalizes all models adds uniform noise but can distort rankings if it favors models with specific biases. Excluding a potentially valid item merely reduces statistical power. In high-stakes evaluation, reliability is prioritized over coverage.
*   **Concept: Error Taxonomy as a Structured Audit Trail**
    *   **Why Needed Here:** A 19-category taxonomy (e.g., `Q1 Semantic Error`, `A1 Incorrect Answer`) allows precise defect attribution. This enables statistical reporting of defect prevalence and guides targeted repair actions.
    *   **Quick Check:** The dominant defect in the `Answer` component across all subjects is `Incorrect Answer` (factual error in the reference solution). This implies the primary noise source in HLE is incorrect answer keys, not ambiguous questions or flawed reasoning in rationales.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Raw HLE Dataset (2,520 items) -> Stage I Module (Component Validation) -> Gold Subset (668 items)
                                                              -> Remainder -> Stage II Module (Repair) -> Revised Subset (1,143 items)
                                                                                              -> Uncertain Subset (689 items)
    *   **Input:** Raw HLE items (`Problem`, `Rationale`, `Answer`).
    *   **Stage I Module:** Takes an item, outputs component validity flags (`Problem_valid`, `Answer_valid`) via expert review and model `pass@8` check.
    *   **Stage II Module:** For items with at least one invalid component but deemed repairable, outputs a revised item and revision metadata (`problem_fix`, `solution_fix`, `answer_fix`).
    *   **Uncertain Module:** For non-repairable items, outputs uncertainty metadata (`uncertainty_source`, `required_expertise`).
    *   **Final Output:** Three disjoint subsets (Gold, Revised, Uncertain) with full annotations.

*   **Critical Path:** Item → Stage I validation → if `Problem_valid` AND `Answer_valid` → **Gold**. Else, if item domain is in repairable set {math, physics, chemistry, biomed, CS} → Stage II repair → re-verification → **Revised**. Else → **Uncertain**.

*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   **Coverage vs. Reliability:** Gold subset is maximally reliable but small. Revised subset expands coverage but introduces correction subjectivity. Uncertain subset maintains transparency at the cost of usability.
    *   **Expert Effort vs. Scalability:** Heavy domain expert reliance limits throughput. Model-assisted checks (`pass@8`, repair proposals) reduce but do not eliminate the burden.
    *   **Preservation vs. Correction:** The "preserve intent" constraint avoids creating a new benchmark but may restrict repair scope, especially in domains with subjective conventions (e.g., Humanities).

*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   High `Uncertain` fractions in specific domains (e.g., >75% for Humanities `Problem` component) indicating verification boundaries exceeded.
    *   Model confidence does not increase on repaired items with `Problem` errors, suggesting repairs failed to resolve core ambiguity.
    *   Revised subset for a domain dominated by a single defect type (e.g., `Format Semantic Error` in CS) indicating systemic fragility in that domain's representation.

*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  Replicate Stage I on a held-out HLE slice: Recruit 2–3 domain experts to independently judge `Problem`/`Answer` validity on 100 random items. Compute inter-rater agreement (Cohen’s κ). Low κ (<0.6) indicates ambiguous validity criteria.
    2.  Measure revision impact on model calibration: Select 50 items with `Problem` errors (e.g., `Q1 Semantic Error`). Compare model confidence on raw vs. repaired versions. Hypothesis: confidence should increase if repairs reduce underspecification.
    3.  Stratify defect distribution by domain: Using the released taxonomy, compute the proportion of `Incorrect Answer` vs. `Format Semantic Error` in `Answer` and `Problem` components across subjects. Validate against Table 1 and Figure S1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** To what extent do specific defect types (e.g., answer-key errors vs. rationale inconsistencies) systematically distort cross-model rankings on HLE, and can we quantify ranking instability attributable to each defect class? *Basis:* Section 2.2 discusses benchmark flaws favoring certain models; Section 4.3 shows defect patterns vary by domain. *Why Unresolved:* The paper shows overall accuracy gains but does not decompose ranking distortion by defect type. *Resolving Evidence:* Stratified analysis of model performance on HLE-Verified subsets corrected for single defect types, comparing rank correlations (e.g., Kendall’s τ) between raw and revised subsets per type.
*   **Open Question 2:** What is the causal mechanism by which problem-statement errors reduce model confidence, and does this effect hold uniformly across reasoning-intensive versus knowledge-intensive domains? *Basis:* Section 5.4 shows confidence increases on repaired items with statement-level errors but does not dissect the mechanism. *Why Unresolved:* The correlation is established but not causally explained; domain differences are noted but not explained. *Resolving Evidence:* Controlled studies with perturbed statements (adding/removing ambiguity) measuring confidence, reasoning length, and internal consistency metrics; cross-domain comparison.
*   **Open Question 3:** Can the two-stage verification protocol be scaled to domains with high epistemic uncertainty (e.g., Humanities/Social Science) without sacrificing reliability, and what supplementary evidence is needed to convert uncertain items into revised ones? *Basis:* Section 3.3 limits Stage II to domains with decidable correctness; Section 4.2 shows Humanities has >75% uncertainty. *Why Unresolved:* The protocol intentionally avoids high-uncertainty domains; it is unknown if their indeterminacy is intrinsic. *Resolving Evidence:* Pilot studies applying multi-round expert consensus or grounding in external canonical sources to uncertain Humanities items; success measured by reduced disagreement and stable revisions.
*   **Open Question 4:** How does training on the revised HLE-Verified items affect model reasoning generalization, and do revisions inadvertently alter the construct being measured? *Basis:* The paper evaluates on HLE-Verified but does not test training impact; "preserving intent" is assumed. *Why Unresolved:* Revisions may change difficulty or the specific skill tested (e.g., fixing a format error might reduce a linguistic parsing challenge). *Resolving Evidence:* Fine-tune models on original vs. revised subsets and compare performance on held-out original HLE, out-of-distribution benchmarks, and qualitative analysis of reasoning style shifts.
*   **Open Question 5:** Is model confidence a reliable automatic filter for detecting noisy items in large-scale benchmarks, and can confidence-based triage replace or augment expert review? *Basis:* Section 5.4 suggests confidence is a useful diagnostic but does not test it as a standalone detector. *Why Unresolved:* It is unknown if confidence thresholds can achieve high precision/recall for flagging noisy items across diverse domains. *Resolving Evidence:* Develop a confidence-based scoring function (e.g., mean confidence, variance) and evaluate its ability to predict defect labels via ROC analysis; test generalizability to other benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, GPQA).

## Limitations
*   **Expert Subjectivity:** No measurement of inter-rater reliability for Stage I/II judgments, risking inconsistent gold/revision quality.
*   **Intent Preservation Unquantified:** Stage II revisions claim to preserve evaluation objectives but lack empirical validation that the underlying construct remains unchanged.
*   **Uncertain Metadata Utility Unproven:** The 689 uncertain items (~28% of total) have explicit tags, but their effectiveness for guiding community refinement is untested.
*   **Small Gold Subset:** The verified gold set (668 items) may not fully capture the diversity of the original HLE, potentially limiting the generalizability of findings based solely on it.

## Confidence
*   **Gold subset (668 items) correctness:** Medium (protocol is rigorous, but reliability is not measured).
*   **Revised subset (1,143 items) preserves intent:** Medium (repairs are defect-driven and constrained, but no construct equivalence validation).
*   **Uncertain documentation enables refinement:** Medium (metadata is transparent, but its utility for community correction is unknown).
*   **HLE-Verified yields higher accuracy & calibration:** High (consistent multi-model gains with large effect sizes, supported by calibration error reduction).

## Next Checks
1.  **Inter-rater reliability audit:** Sample 100 items; have three experts independently judge `Problem`/`Answer` validity. Compute Cohen’s κ; target κ≥0.7.
2.  **Intent preservation audit:** On a sample of 50 revised items, have experts rate concept preservation (1–5 scale) and post-revision correctness; target ≥80% agreement on preservation.
3.  **Subset performance split:** Evaluate models separately on the Gold-only subset and the Revised-only subset. Confirm that both subsets show accuracy gains over original HLE; if gains diverge, analyze item-level error patterns to understand the difference.
