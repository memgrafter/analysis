# Estimation of instrument and noise parameters for inverse problem based on prior diffusion model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.11711
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11711
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: Hyper-G-DPS, a Gibbs sampler combining diffusion posterior sampling with conjugate updates, accurately estimates instrument and noise parameters in Bayesian inverse problems on MNIST with a Lorentz PSF and Gaussian noise.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Hyper-G-DPS, a Bayesian method for joint estimation of instrument parameters (e.g., point spread function width), noise parameters (mean and variance), and the underlying image in linear inverse problems, using a diffusion model as a prior. The algorithm employs a Gibbs sampler that alternates between updating the extended diffusion state via a G-DPS scheme and directly sampling the noise parameters from conjugate posteriors, while the instrument parameter is updated via random-walk Metropolis-Hastings. Experiments on MNIST with a Lorentz PSF and Gaussian noise demonstrate accurate recovery of all parameters, with true values falling within ±2 posterior standard deviations, and achieve high-fidelity image reconstruction. The method requires minimal tuning and runs efficiently on a standard PC with GPU acceleration.

## Method Summary
Hyper-G-DPS treats the inverse problem in a Bayesian framework: given observed data \( y = H_\iota x_0 + e \), with unknown instrument parameter \(\iota\) (defining linear operator \(H_\iota\)), noise parameters \(m_e\) and \(v_e\), and image \(x_0\), the posterior is explored via Gibbs sampling. The image prior is defined by a pre-trained diffusion model, which specifies a Markov chain over latent variables \(x_{0:T}\). Each Gibbs iteration updates: (1) the noise variance \(v_e\) from a Gamma conditional (conjugate to Gaussian likelihood), (2) the noise mean \(m_e\) from a Gaussian conditional, (3) the instrument parameter \(\iota\) using a random-walk Metropolis-Hastings step targeting its conditional posterior, and (4) the extended diffusion state \(x_{0:T}\) using G-DPS—a block-Gibbs that leverages the diffusion model’s Gaussian forward and backward transitions. For \(x_0\), the update combines the backward conditional from the diffusion prior with the likelihood, resulting in a linear-Gaussian problem solved via FFT; intermediate latents \(x_t\) (\(0 < t < T\)) are updated via analytical Gaussian conditionals from the product of forward and backward transitions. The algorithm stops when the running average of \(x_0\) samples stabilizes.

## Key Results
- Parameter recovery: \(\iota = 0.82\) (8.9% error), \(m_e = 0.103\) (3.3% error), \(v_e = 0.00254\) (1.4% error) with posterior standard deviations \(0.063\), \(0.0082\), \(0.000116\); true values lie within \(\pm2\) PSD intervals.
- Uncertainty quantification: Per-pixel credible intervals for the reconstructed image cover the true values, and the posterior mean image closely matches the ground truth.
- Computational efficiency: 1012 iterations in 62 seconds on a standard PC with GPU, with 80% of time spent on neural network inference; no fine-tuning required beyond the iteration count.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A Gibbs sampler mixing a diffusion-prior-specific image update (G-DPS) with direct conjugate updates for noise parameters and a random-walk Metropolis-Hastings (MH) for the instrument parameter enables joint posterior exploration.
- **Mechanism:** The algorithm iteratively samples conditional posteriors: (1) the extended image state \(x_{0:T}\) via G-DPS (a block-Gibbs scheme that leverages the diffusion model's Markov structure), (2) the noise variance \(v_e\) from a Gamma conditional (conjugate to Gaussian likelihood), (3) the noise mean \(m_e\) from a Gaussian conditional (conjugate), and (4) the instrument parameter \(\iota\) via a random-walk MH step using the Gaussian likelihood residual.
- **Core assumption:** The diffusion prior's forward and backward processes are well-matched after training (\(p^+_{0:T} \approx p^-,\theta_{0:T}\)), making the G-DPS proposal valid. The Gaussian noise model is correct. The priors for \(\iota\), \(m_e\), and \(v_e\) are as specified.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "proposed Hyper-G-DPS method employs a Gibbs sampler that alternately updates the extended diffusion state \(x_{0:T}\) using the G-DPS scheme and the parameters."; [section III] Details the four conditional updates: G-DPS for images (A), Gamma for \(\gamma_e\) (B), Gaussian for \(m_e\) (C), MH for \(\iota\) (D).; [corpus] Corpus neighbors discuss diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) and physics-informed parameter estimation, but this paper's specific Gibbs integration for *multiple* parameters with a diffusion prior appears novel.
- **Break condition:** If the diffusion model's backward kernel \(\mu_\theta\) poorly approximates the score of the data-conditional distribution, the G-DPS image updates will not represent the true posterior. MH for \(\iota\) will also fail if the proposal covariance is poorly tuned or if the posterior is highly non-Gaussian.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The diffusion prior's Markov chain structure across latent variables \(x_{1:T}\) enables computationally efficient and analytically tractable conditional updates for each \(x_t\) during Gibbs sampling.
- **Mechanism:** The prior is defined as a Markov chain: \(p^+_{0:T}\) (forward) and \(p^-,\theta_{0:T}\) (backward). Conditional posteriors for latent \(x_t\) (\(t \neq 0,T\)) become products of two Gaussians (from forward and backward transitions), yielding a Gaussian conditional with analytically computable mean and precision. For \(x_0\), it combines the backward conditional \(p^-_{0|1}\) with the likelihood, yielding a linear-Gaussian problem solvable via FFT.
- **Core assumption:** The transitions \(p^+_{t|t-1}\) and \(p^-_{t|t+1}\) are Gaussian with diagonal covariance. The learning stage ensures the marginals \(p^-_0 \approx \pi_0\) and \(p^+_T \approx N(0,I)\).
- **Evidence anchors:** [section II.B] Defines the forward/backward joint priors as Markov chains with Gaussian transitions (Eqs 6–9).; [section III.A] States: "the conditional posterior... reveals a linear-Gauss problem" for \(x_0\), and for \(x_t\) (\(t \neq T\)) it yields a Gauss pdf from product of two Gaussians.; [appendix] Provides explicit precision/mean updates for each \(x_t\).
- **Break condition:** If the learned backward transitions \(\mu_\theta\) are not well-calibrated (e.g., due to insufficient training data), the conditional posteriors for \(x_t\) will be misspecified, leading to biased image samples.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Conjugate priors for the noise parameters (\(m_e\) Gaussian, \(v_e\) via \(\gamma_e = 1/v_e\) Gamma) allow direct, no-tuning sampling from their conditional posteriors, avoiding costly optimization or approximate inference.
- **Mechanism:** Given the Gaussian likelihood \(N(y; m_e + H_\iota x_0, v_e I)\) and conjugate priors, the conditional posteriors are analytically derived: \(p(\gamma_e | \dots) \propto \gamma_e^{a_0 + P/2 -1} \exp(-\gamma_e (b_0 + \frac{1}{2}\|y - m_e - H_\iota x_0\|^2))\) (Gamma), and \(p(m_e | \dots) \propto \exp(-\frac{1}{2}(p_0 + P\gamma_e)(m_e - m)^2)\) (Gaussian). These are sampled directly.
- **Core assumption:** The noise is i.i.d. Gaussian with constant mean \(m_e\) and variance \(v_e\). The priors (Eqs 4,5) are correctly specified.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section II.A] Assumes stationary, white Gaussian noise: \(N(e; m_e, C_e)\) with \(C_e = v_e I\).; [section III.B & C] Explicitly derives the Gamma and Gaussian conditional posteriors and states sampling is "direct and efficient."; [section IV.B] Table I shows accurate recovery of \(m_e\) and \(v_e\), supporting the efficacy of direct sampling.
- **Break condition:** If the true noise is non-Gaussian (e.g., heavy-tailed) or correlated, the conjugacy advantage is lost and the conditionals will no longer match the true posterior, leading to biased parameter estimates.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Bayesian inference for inverse problems**  
  Why needed here: The entire framework is Bayesian: unknowns (\(x_0\), \(\theta\)) are treated as random variables with prior distributions, and inference is based on the posterior \(\pi(x_0, \theta | y)\). Understanding prior→likelihood→posterior is essential.  
  Quick check question: Given a Gaussian prior \(N(x_0; \mu, \Sigma)\) and Gaussian likelihood \(N(y; Hx_0, \sigma^2 I)\), what is the form of the conditional posterior \(p(x_0 | y, \theta)\)?

- **Concept: Diffusion models as Markov priors**  
  Why needed here: The prior for \(x_0\) is defined via a diffusion process (forward noising + backward denoising network). The G-DPS sampler critically relies on the Markov chain structure across \(x_{0:T}\).  
  Quick check question: Why does the diffusion prior's Markov property simplify the Gibbs update for an intermediate latent \(x_t\) (\(0 < t < T\))?

- **Concept: Gibbs sampling and conditional conjugacy**  
  Why needed here: The Hyper-G-DPS algorithm *is* a Gibbs sampler. Its efficiency stems from having conditionals that are either Gaussian (from diffusion) or conjugate (for noise parameters), allowing direct sampling without Metropolis-Hastings accept/reject steps for most blocks.  
  Quick check question: In a Gibbs sampler, what is the role of the "burn-in period"? How does one assess convergence of the chains for \(\iota\), \(m_e\), \(v_e\)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Gibbs controller orchestrates updates: sample noise parameters via conjugate samplers, instrument parameter via MH module, and diffusion state via G-DPS image updater; G-DPS relies on diffusion prior network (\(\mu_\theta\)) for backward transitions.

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Initialize \(x_0 = y\), \(x_{1:T}\) by forward diffusion, \(\iota\) and \(m_e\) from priors, \(v_e\) arbitrary.  
  2. Gibbs iteration:  
     a. Sample \(v_e\) conjugate (uses \(\|y - m_e - H_\iota x_0\|^2\)).  
     b. Sample \(m_e\) conjugate (uses same residual and prior \(m_0, p_0\)).  
     c. Sample \(\iota\) via MH (proposes \(\iota' \sim N(\iota, \sigma_\iota^2 I)\), computes acceptance ratio using likelihood residual).  
     d. Update \(x_T\) via forward transition.  
     e. For \(t = T-1, \dots, 1\): update \(x_t\) using forward/backward Gaussians.  
     f. Update \(x_0\) via likelihood + backward conditional (FFT).  
  3. Update empirical mean of \(x_0\) samples, check convergence (change below \(10^{-2}\)).

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Accuracy vs. speed: Using diagonal covariances in transitions is computationally cheap but may limit modeling of complex correlations; FFT for \(x_0\) is fast but assumes periodic boundary conditions.  
  - Robustness vs. tuning: G-DPS requires no tuning for image updates, but MH for \(\iota\) needs proposal variance \(\sigma_\iota^2\).  
  - Memory vs. time: Storing all \(x_{0:T}\) is memory-intensive (\(O(T \times \text{image size})\)); 80% of runtime is neural network inference for \(\mu_\theta\).

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Diverging MH acceptance rate for \(\iota\): indicates poorly tuned proposal variance.  
  - Deteriorating \(x_0\) samples: indicates G-DPS failure due to poor \(\mu_\theta\) calibration or forward/backward mismatch.  
  - Noise parameter chains stuck: suggests incorrect prior specification or non-Gaussian noise.  
  - No reduction in residual \(\|y - m_e - H_\iota x_0\|^2\): sampler not exploring posterior correctly.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Unit test conjugate samplers:** With fixed \(x_0\), \(\iota\), generate synthetic residual. Verify \(v_e\) samples match Gamma(\(a_0+P/2, b_0 + \text{residual}^2/2\)) and \(m_e\) samples match Gaussian(\(m, (p_0 + P\gamma_e)^{-1}\)) over many draws.  
  2. **G-DPS validation on image-only problem:** Fix \(\theta = \theta^*\). Run G-DPS updates for \(x_{0:T}\) only. Check that posterior mean of \(x_0\) recovers ground truth from blurred/noisy \(y\). Validate chain mixing (autocorrelation decays).  
  3. **Full Gibbs on simple linear case:** Use small image (e.g., 8×8), known Gaussian blur (\(\iota\) scalar width), Gaussian noise. Run full Hyper-G-DPS. Check: (a) parameter chains stationarity (trace plots), (b) true \(\iota, m_e, v_e\) within ±2 PSD of posterior mean, (c) \(x_0\) credible intervals cover truth. Tune MH proposal for \(\iota\) to ~25% acceptance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Can the Hyper-G-DPS method accurately estimate instrument and noise parameters under non-Gaussian noise or correlated error models? The paper states it can be generalized but provides no validation.
2. How can model selection be performed for choosing among different instrument PSF models (e.g., Gaussian vs. Lorentz) and noise distributions within the Hyper-G-DPS framework? The conclusion identifies this as a future direction.
3. Does the Hyper-G-DPS method maintain parameter estimation accuracy and uncertainty quantification when the diffusion prior is learned from image datasets significantly different from MNIST (e.g., natural images, medical scans)? No domain-shift experiments are reported.
4. How does the computational complexity of Hyper-G-DPS scale with increasing image resolution (e.g., 128×128, 256×256) and dimensionality (e.g., 3D volumes), given that 80% of time is spent on neural network inference? The paper claims scaling is not an obstacle but provides no empirical scaling results.

## Limitations
- The paper does not specify the diffusion model’s architecture, training data, or noise schedule, preventing exact replication.
- The random‑walk Metropolis‑Hastings step for the instrument parameter \(\iota\) lacks a reported proposal covariance; efficient mixing likely requires ad‑hoc tuning.
- The method assumes a known linear forward operator, i.i.d. Gaussian noise, and a well‑calibrated diffusion prior; no analysis of robustness to model mismatch or scalability to higher‑resolution images is provided.

## Confidence
- **High:** Gibbs sampler structure and conditional posterior derivations are mathematically sound.
- **Medium:** Reported parameter recovery on a single MNIST experiment is plausible but needs independent verification.
- **Low:** Claims of generalizability to other domains, datasets, or noise models are unsupported beyond the specific test case.
