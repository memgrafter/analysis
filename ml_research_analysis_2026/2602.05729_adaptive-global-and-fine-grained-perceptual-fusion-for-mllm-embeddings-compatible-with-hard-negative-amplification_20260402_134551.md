# Adaptive Global and Fine-Grained Perceptual Fusion for MLLM Embeddings Compatible with Hard Negative Amplification

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.05729
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05729
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: AGFF-Embed achieves SOTA on MMEB (74.9%) and MMVP-VLM (61.5%) by adaptively fusing global and fine-grained perceptual patterns with EGA compatibility.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) typically produce embeddings that capture only global semantics, limiting performance on tasks requiring fine-grained perceptual reasoning. This paper introduces AGFF-Embed, a method that leverages a frozen MLLM to generate both global and multiple fine-grained embeddings via learnable prompt tokens. It computes four similarity patterns between these embedding types and adaptively aggregates them using a smooth logsumexp function. This design enables compatibility with Explicit Gradient Amplification (EGA), which enhances the training signal from in-batch hard negatives without modifying the dataset. AGFF-Embed sets new state-of-the-art results on the MMEB and MMVP-VLM benchmarks, demonstrating significant gains on fine-grained subtasks. Extensive ablations validate the necessity of each perceptual pattern, the logsumexp aggregation, and the EGA technique.

## Method Summary
AGFF-Embed extends an MLLM-based embedding model by introducing parallel fine-grained embedding modules alongside a global embedding module. The global embedding is extracted from a standard `<EMBED>` token, while each fine-grained module prepends a sequence of learnable `<PROMPT>` tokens before its own `<EMBED>` token, steering the frozen MLLM to capture specific fine-grained aspects. For a query-target pair, the model computes four similarity patterns: global↔global, fine↔global, global↔fine, and fine↔fine (for each of N fine-grained modules). These (3N+1) scores are aggregated via `logsumexp` with a temperature parameter, producing a final similarity score that softly weights the most relevant patterns. The model is trained with an InfoNCE contrastive loss. To improve hard negative discrimination without dataset alteration, AGFF-Embed employs Explicit Gradient Amplification (EGA): during training, the gradients from negative samples are scaled by a factor derived from their hardness (based on similarity scores) before backpropagation. This amplification is stable due to the smooth logsumexp aggregation. Training uses GradCache for memory efficiency, LoRA for parameter-efficient LLM fine-tuning, and optimized learning rates for the learnable prompt tokens.

## Key Results
- AGFF-Embed achieves 74.9% overall accuracy on MMEB, surpassing the previous SOTA (QQMM) by 2.4%.
- On the fine-grained MMVP-VLM benchmark, it reaches 61.5% average accuracy, with notable improvements on six attribute subtasks.
- Ablation studies confirm that removing any perceptual pattern, switching from logsumexp to a sharp aggregation (max), or disabling EGA all cause significant performance drops.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Adaptive Integration of Perceptual Patterns
The core hypothesis is that different query-target pairs require different combinations of global and fine-grained alignment. AGFF-Embed generates one global embedding and N fine-grained embeddings via learnable prompt tokens. It computes four similarity types (global↔global, fine↔global, global↔fine, fine↔fine) for each negative. The logsumexp aggregation acts as a smooth maximum, allowing the model to softly weight the most relevant perceptual patterns during training. The weights are derived from the similarity scores themselves and are learned end-to-end. This adaptivity matches the hybrid demands of complex retrieval tasks better than fixed global-only or fine-grained-only embeddings. Evidence: The abstract states that complex scenarios require a hybrid perceptual pattern. Section 3.1 defines the four similarity types and logsumexp, noting it "aims to enable the MLLM to softly favor more suitable patterns." Ablations in Section 4.3 show performance drops when any pattern is removed, confirming their complementary roles. Break condition: If a single pattern dominates almost all pairs (e.g., >95% weight), the adaptive overhead is unnecessary and may cause instability.

### Mechanism 2: Smooth Aggregation Enables EGA Compatibility
Explicit Gradient Amplification (EGA) enhances training by scaling gradients from harder negative samples. For stable amplification, the loss must be smooth and well-behaved. The logsumexp function is continuously differentiable, unlike sharp functions like `max`. Its gradient decomposition naturally incorporates the classification probability of each negative (hardness) and the normalized pattern weight. This creates a smooth amplification: harder negatives and more relevant patterns contribute larger gradients. EGA would be incompatible with non-differentiable or piecewise-smooth aggregators. Evidence: Section 3.2 derives the explicit gradient forms (Eq 7-9), showing the amplification factors. It notes that sharp contrastive losses like MetaEmbed are incompatible. Section 4.3 ablates EGA and shows performance drops when removed; also shows that using `max` aggregation severely degrades performance. Break condition: If the hardness metric `h⁻` is miscalibrated early in training (when embeddings are noisy), amplifying incorrect "hard" negatives could destabilize training. The paper acknowledges this risk and the MLLM's pre-trained knowledge helps.

### Mechanism 3: Learnable Prompt Tokens for Scalable Fine-Grainedness
Instead of hand-crafting prompts for each fine-grained attribute (e.g., "Describe the color"), AGFF-Embed uses M learnable tokens per fine-grained module. These tokens are optimized to steer the frozen MLLM toward generating embeddings that capture discriminative fine-grained dimensions relevant to the training distribution, without requiring explicit attribute labels. This is more scalable than fixed templates and allows the model to discover task-specific fine-grained concepts. Core assumption: a pre-trained MLLM contains disentangled fine-grained representations that can be selectively activated via soft prompt tuning. Evidence: Section 3.1 describes the fine-grained module with learnable tokens "aiming to allow the MLLM to autonomously determine the types of fine-grained information." Ablations removing fine-grained patterns (e.g., fine→global) cause performance drops, confirming their contribution. Break condition: If the MLLM's pre-training lacks fine-grained diversity or the training data doesn't require fine-grained discrimination, the learned tokens may capture noise rather than meaningful patterns.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE Loss)**
  - **Why needed here**: AGFF-Embed uses InfoNCE loss to train the embeddings. Understanding how in-batch negatives work, temperature scaling, and gradient flow is essential to grasp the EGA mechanism and ablation results.
  - **Quick check**: In InfoNCE, decreasing temperature τ makes the distribution sharper, increasing gradient magnitudes for hard negatives relative to easy ones.

- **Concept: Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) Architecture & Tokenization**
  - **Why needed here**: The method treats an MLLM as a frozen feature extractor. It relies on how images/text are processed (vision encoder, LLM) and how special tokens (`<EMBED>`) extract hidden states. Learnable tokens are inserted into the LLM sequence.
  - **Quick check**: The global embedding `x0` is extracted from the hidden state at the `<EMBED>` token position, representing the MLLM's integrated multimodal understanding.

- **Concept: Gradient Caching (GradCache)**
  - **Why needed here**: Training with large batch contrastive loss requires memory-efficient gradient caching. The EGA technique is applied within this cached gradient framework, making it crucial to understand the two-stage backpropagation.
  - **Quick check**: GradCache detaches cached embeddings before the second backpropagation to avoid recomputing the forward pass and to enable gradient scaling/amplification on the cached gradients.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**:
Input Processor -> MLLM (Frozen) -> Similarity Calculator -> Aggregation Module -> Loss & EGA Module -> (GradCache) -> Update LoRA & Prompt Tokens

More detailed: Image-Text Pair -> Prompt Templates with Global and Fine-grained Tokens -> MLLM Forward (extract global embedding x0 and N fine-grained embeddings {xi}) -> Compute all 4 similarity patterns for each query-positive and query-negative pair -> Apply logsumexp over (3N+1) scores to get sfinal -> Compute InfoNCE loss -> Calculate hardness h⁻ for negatives -> Amplify classification probabilities p⁻ to ˜p⁻ -> Scale cached gradients ∂L/∂X by ˜p⁻ (and pattern weights w) -> Backprop through scaled grads to update LoRA (LLM) and learnable prompt tokens.

**Critical path**: The essential data flow during training: (1) Forward pass through frozen MLLM to obtain embeddings (cache them). (2) Compute final similarities sfinal for all query-positive and query-negative pairs in the batch. (3) Compute InfoNCE loss, derive per-negative hardness h⁻ and pattern weights w. (4) Compute amplified classification probabilities ˜p⁻ based on h⁻. (5) Scale the cached gradients by ˜p⁻ (and implicitly by w through the loss derivative). (6) Backpropagate the scaled gradients to update only the LoRA parameters and the learnable prompt tokens (vision encoder frozen).

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Performance vs. Cost: More fine-grained modules (N) and prompt tokens (M) increase expressivity but linearly increase MLLM sequence length and computation.
- Smoothness vs. Discretization: logsumexp is smooth and EGA-compatible but is a soft maximum; a true max might be more precise for single-pattern tasks but is non-differentiable and breaks EGA. mean-max (MetaEmbed) is a compromise but performs worse.
- Hard Negative Reliance: EGA boosts performance but introduces hyperparameter α. Setting α too high can destabilize training by over-amplifying noisy gradients from early-stage "hard" negatives.

**Failure signatures**:
- Pattern Collapse: The weights w for one perceptual pattern (e.g., sg2g) dominate >95% across all batches, indicating the model isn't learning to use fine-grained patterns. Check: Monitor the mean/variance of w distributions during training.
- EGA Instability: Loss becomes NaN or oscillates wildly after a few steps. Likely cause: α is too high for current embedding quality, amplifying spurious hard negatives. Remedy: lower α or warm up α from 0.
- Poor Fine-Grained Transfer: MMVP-VLM score doesn't improve despite MMEB gain. Indicates the learned fine-grained embeddings may be task-specific to MMEB's distribution and not generalizable to out-of-distribution fine-grained challenges.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Perceptual Pattern Ablation**: Train with one similarity pattern zeroed out (set to -∞). Validate on MMEB and MMVP-VLM to quantify each pattern's unique contribution. Expected: MMVP-VLM suffers most when fine-grained→* patterns are removed.
2. **EGA Ablation & Sensitivity**: Train with α=0 (no EGA) and α∈{5,10,20,50}. Plot performance vs. α. Expected: A peak exists; too low (α=0) sees a drop, too high (α=50) causes degradation or instability.
3. **Aggregation Function Swap**: Replace logsumexp with max and mean-max (Eq 12-13). Keep all else identical. Expected: max and mean-max will underperform significantly, especially on fine-grained subsets, and will be incompatible with any EGA implementation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can Explicit Gradient Amplification (EGA) be extended to amplify gradients based on the alignment of specific perceptual patterns (e.g., fine-to-fine vs. global-to-fine), and would this further improve performance on tasks requiring hybrid perception?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors state they reserve applying EGA to perceptual patterns for future work, as the MLLM's understanding of patterns is learned from scratch and unreliable early in training (Section 3.2).
- **Why unresolved**: The current EGA only amplifies based on negative sample hardness, not on which similarity pattern (sg2g, sf2g, etc.) is most relevant for a given pair. This may leave performance gains untapped for tasks where certain perceptual patterns dominate.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An ablation comparing the full AGFF-Embed with a variant that incorporates pattern-specific gradient amplification (e.g., weighting gradients by normalized pattern probabilities wg2g, wf2g, etc.), measured on MMEB and MMVP-VLM.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Is the logsumexp aggregation function optimal for combining multiple perceptual patterns, or could a learned, context-aware weighting mechanism (e.g., attention over patterns) yield better adaptive fusion?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The authors choose logsumexp as a smooth max approximation for its gradient properties and compatibility with EGA, but they do not compare against other adaptive aggregation methods beyond max and mean-max (Section 4.3).
- **Why unresolved**: Logsumexp assumes all patterns contribute smoothly, but the optimal weighting may be task-dependent or input-specific. A fixed function might not fully capture nuanced interactions between patterns.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Replace logsumexp with a small neural network that takes the four similarity scores and outputs learned weights (summing to 1), trained end-to-end, and compare performance on benchmarks while ensuring EGA compatibility.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Do the learnable prompt tokens in the fine-grained modules induce semantically distinct and interpretable embeddings, or do they capture overlapping aspects of fine-grained information?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper introduces N parallel fine-grained modules with learnable <PROMPT> tokens but does not analyze what semantic aspects each module learns (Section 3.1). Ablation shows all patterns are necessary but not their specialization.
- **Why unresolved**: Without understanding what each fine-grained embedding represents, it is unclear whether the modules are learning complementary features or redundant ones, which could inform better module design or token initialization.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conduct a probing study: cluster the fine-grained embeddings from each module on a fine-grained dataset (e.g., MMVP-VLM subtasks) and analyze which attributes (color, shape, etc.) each cluster corresponds to via human evaluation or automated attribute labeling.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: How does the performance of AGFF-Embed scale with the number of fine-grained embedding modules (N) beyond N=10, and is there a point of diminishing returns or degradation due to interference between modules?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The authors test N=3,5,10 (Appendix C) and observe improvements over smaller N, but do not explore larger N. The logsumexp aggregation over 3N+1 similarities may become noisy or computationally inefficient as N grows.
- **Why unresolved**: While more modules could capture more fine-grained aspects, they might also increase redundancy or training difficulty. The optimal N likely depends on task complexity and dataset diversity.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Train AGFF-Embed with N ranging from 10 to 50 on MMEB, tracking overall score and per-module usage (via normalized weights w). Analyze gradient norms or weight distributions to detect interference or saturation.

## Limitations
- Base model opacity: QQMM architecture details are unspecified, hindering exact replication and limiting confidence in generalizability.
- Black-box fine-grained learning: Learnable prompts lack interpretability; no comparison to hand-crafted attribute prompts weakens the scalability claim.
- EGA stability risk: Hyperparameter α requires careful tuning; absence of warm-up strategy may cause early training instability.

## Confidence
- **Adaptive fusion via logsumexp**: High (SOTA on MMEB, strong ablations confirm necessity of each pattern)
- **EGA compatibility and benefit**: Medium-High (ablations show performance drop without EGA and degradation with non-smooth aggregation, but α sensitivity not fully mapped)
- **Learned prompts for fine-grained perception**: Medium (fine-grained patterns contribute, but no direct evidence they outperform explicit attribute-based prompting)

## Next Checks
1. Monitor the distribution of pattern weights w during training; report mean and variance to detect collapse (one pattern >95% weight).
2. Conduct EGA sensitivity analysis: vary α ∈ {0,5,10,20,30,50} on MMEB and MMVP-VLM; plot performance curves and note any loss instability (NaNs, spikes).
3. Probe the fine-grained embeddings with a linear classifier trained on MMVP-VLM attribute labels to evaluate semantic alignment and interpretability.
