# AGFT: Alignment-Guided Fine-Tuning for Zero-Shot Adversarial Robustness of Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.29410  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29410  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** AGFT achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot adversarial robustness (e.g., 46.57% robust accuracy vs. 43.46% for GLADIATOR) and clean accuracy (61.35%) on ViT‑B/32 under ε=1/255, with similar gains for ViT‑B/16.

## Executive Summary
AGFT (Alignment‑Guided Fine‑Tuning) improves the adversarial robustness of vision‑language models like CLIP while preserving their zero‑shot generalization. It replaces hard label supervision with soft probabilistic targets derived from the original model’s predictions and uses temperature‑scaled distribution calibration to maintain semantic structure. On 15 zero‑shot benchmarks, AGFT outperforms prior methods in both clean and robust accuracy, and shows better generalization across stronger attacks and out‑of‑distribution data, all with modest computational overhead.

## Method Summary
AGFT fine‑tunes only the image encoder of a frozen CLIP model. For each clean image, it computes a soft target distribution over class prompts using the original model’s cosine similarities between image features and frozen text embeddings; the distribution is scaled by temperature τ/γ to calibrate confidence. Adversarial examples are generated via PGD using the current model. The training loss is the cross‑entropy between the model’s predictions on adversarial examples (with temperature τ) and the calibrated soft targets. By steering the trainable image encoder toward these alignment‑preserving targets, AGFT simultaneously boosts robustness and retains zero‑shot performance.

## Key Results
- Average robust accuracy of **46.57%** on ViT‑B/32 (ε=1/255) surpasses GLADIATOR (43.46%) and other baselines.
- Clean accuracy reaches **61.35%**, also exceeding comparators.
- Consistent improvements for ViT‑B/16 (robust 49.84% vs. 47.08%) and across stronger attacks, unseen perturbations, and out‑of‑distribution datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Soft Supervision via Original Model Predictions
**Claim:** Using the original model’s soft predictive distribution instead of hard labels preserves cross‑modal semantic structure and thereby maintains zero‑shot generalization while improving robustness.  
**How:** For a clean image, AGFT computes a probability distribution over all class prompts by applying a temperature‑scaled softmax to the cosine similarities between the original image encoder’s features and the frozen text encoder’s embeddings. This distribution encapsulates the pre‑trained model’s holistic view of image‑text relationships and guides learning on adversarial examples.  
**Assumption:** The original model’s predictions encode the intended relative similarities; preserving this distribution prevents catastrophic forgetting of the shared embedding space.  
**Evidence:** Abstract and Section 3.2 explicitly state the use of soft supervision; related work (e.g., *Navigating the Trade‑off*, arXiv:2508.05237) shows alignment‑based methods outperform label‑based ones for zero‑shot robustness.  
**Break condition:** If the original model’s predictions are unstable or the distribution is too flat/peaked, guidance may be ineffective or reintroduce alignment disruption.

### Mechanism 2: Temperature‑Scaled Distribution Consistency Calibration
**Claim:** Adjusting the temperature of the original model’s logits before forming the target distribution resolves confidence‑scale mismatches between the original and robust feature spaces, preserving global similarity structure without being misled by overconfident logits.  
**How:** The calibration scale τ/γ (with γ∈(0,1]) softens the distribution: lower γ increases effective temperature, reducing reliance on absolute confidence magnitudes and focusing on relative ordering.  
**Assumption:** The original model’s confidence scale may be incompatible with the robust feature space; temperature scaling decouples semantic structure from confidence.  
**Evidence:** Abstract mentions “temperature‑scaled distribution calibration”; Section 3.2 details Eq. (8) for calibration; *Revisiting the Robust Generalization of Adversarial Prompt Tuning* (arXiv:2405.11154) highlights the need for output alignment during fine‑tuning.  
**Break condition:** γ too small yields an overly flat distribution (loss of discriminative signal); γ too large (≈1) eliminates calibration benefits.

### Mechanism 3: Text‑Guided Adversarial Training Framework
**Claim:** Freezing the text encoder and generating adversarial examples supervised by the original model’s soft distribution aligns adversarial visual features with textual embeddings in a manner directly compatible with CLIP’s zero‑shot mechanism, leading to superior transfer robustness.  
**How:** The training loop follows a min‑max objective: for each clean image, craft an adversarial example that maximizes the cross‑entropy loss between the current model’s predictions and the calibrated targets; then update only the image encoder.  
**Assumption:** Zero‑shot performance relies on preserving alignment between visual features and a fixed textual embedding space; updating only the image encoder while matching soft, text‑based targets prevents drift.  
**Evidence:** Abstract and Section 3.2 describe the text‑guided approach; *RLBind* (arXiv:2509.14383) also emphasizes adversarial‑invariant cross‑modal alignment.  
**Break condition:** If adversarial perturbations exceed model capacity, clean‑image targets may become misaligned; if the text encoder is not truly frozen or prompts are unrepresentative, generalization degrades.

## Foundational Learning

#### Concept: CLIP pre‑training and zero‑shot inference
- **Why needed:** AGFT builds directly on CLIP; understanding its contrastive pre‑training (image‑text pairs, InfoNCE loss) and zero‑shot classification (matching image features to text embeddings) is essential to grasp cross‑modal alignment and its preservation.
- **Quick check:** How does CLIP perform zero‑shot classification without fine‑tuning, and what role does the shared embedding space play?

#### Concept: Adversarial training (min‑max formulation)
- **Why needed:** AGFT is an adversarial fine‑tuning method; the inner maximization (generating adversarial examples, typically via PGD) and outer minimization (updating the model) define its training dynamics.
- **Quick check:** What is the purpose of the inner maximization in adversarial training, and why is Projected Gradient Descent commonly used?

#### Concept: Cross‑modal alignment in vision‑language models
- **Why needed:** The paper’s premise is that label‑based fine‑tuning disrupts cross‑modal alignment; zero‑shot generalization depends on a continuous shared space where semantic similarity (not classification boundaries) is preserved.
- **Quick check:** What does ‘cross‑modal alignment’ refer to in CLIP, and why is it important for recognizing unseen categories?

#### Concept: Softmax temperature and distribution sharpness
- **Why needed:** The calibration mechanism scales the temperature of the softmax over cosine similarities, controlling the entropy of the target distribution and its alignment with the robust model.
- **Quick check:** How does increasing the temperature affect the softmax output? What would happen if we set the temperature extremely high or low?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Frozen pre‑trained backbone (original image encoder f_orig, text encoder f_text) → Target distribution calculator → Cross‑entropy loss; Trainable robust image encoder f_rob (updated) → Adversarial example generator (PGD) → f_rob predictions.

### Critical path (data flow per training iteration)
1. Input clean image x.  
2. Compute soft target distribution p_rob using f_orig (no grad) and f_text with temperature τ/γ.  
3. Generate adversarial example x_adv via PGD using current f_rob.  
4. Compute f_rob(x_adv) features, normalize, and obtain logits via dot product with text embeddings scaled by τ.  
5. Apply softmax to logits, compute cross‑entropy loss against p_rob.  
6. Backpropagate loss, update parameters of f_rob only.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Speed vs. alignment purity:** Extra forward pass through f_orig roughly doubles computation compared to standard adversarial training using hard labels; omitting it harms alignment.  
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** γ (calibration strength) and τ (temperature) govern the robustness‑accuracy trade‑off; inappropriate values cause under‑regularization or over‑softening.  
- **Memory footprint:** Storing activations for PGD and two forward passes (f_rob and f_orig) doubles memory usage.

### Failure signatures
- **Clean accuracy collapse:** Significant drop (e.g., >10% absolute) suggests too low γ or excessively high adversarial budget.  
- **Poor robustness transfer:** Low robust accuracy on unseen attacks (e.g., C&W) indicates calibration distorted semantics (bad γ) or noisy targets.  
- **Training instability:** Loss oscillations or divergence may be due to high learning rate, insufficient PGD steps, or gradient mismatch from frozen text encoder.  
- **Distribution mismatch:** Persistently high KL divergence between p_rob and model predictions on x_adv suggests ineffective calibration.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Sanity check:** Train AGFT on 10% of ImageNet for a few epochs. Expect: loss decreases, robust accuracy (PGD‑20, ε=1/255) improves over the pre‑trained model, and clean accuracy stays within 5% of pre‑trained.  
2. **Ablation test:** Compare three variants: (i) hard label supervision, (ii) no calibration (γ=1), (iii) full AGFT. Full AGFT should outperform in both robust and clean accuracy, confirming contributions of soft supervision and temperature scaling.  
3. **Distribution consistency verification:** On a held‑out set, compute Top‑5 IoU between the original model’s clean predictions and the fine‑tuned model’s predictions on adversarial images. AGFT should exhibit significantly higher IoU than label‑based methods, indicating preserved semantic structure.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Other threat models:** How does AGFT perform under ℓ2‑bounded perturbations, text‑level attacks, or joint text‑image attacks? (Currently only ℓ∞ image‑only attacks evaluated.)  
- **Generalization to other VLMs:** Does the approach extend to non‑CLIP vision‑language models and diverse transformer backbones? (Experiments limited to CLIP variants.)  
- **Beyond classification:** Can AGFT improve zero‑shot robustness for multimodal tasks such as VQA or image captioning? (Evaluation only on image classification.)

## Limitations
- Prompt templates and training augmentations are unspecified, potentially causing performance variance.  
- Temperature τ=1/180 and scaling factor γ=0.4 are given without full sensitivity analysis; their optimality is uncertain.  
- Fine‑tuning solely on ImageNet risks overfitting, possibly limiting generalization to some zero‑shot benchmarks.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (soft supervision):** High – well‑grounded in alignment theory and supported by related empirical findings.  
- **Mechanism 2 (temperature calibration):** Medium – benefit shown empirically, but break conditions require deeper analysis.  
- **Overall superiority over SOTA:** Medium – reported gains are solid, yet reproducibility uncertainties and limited ablations moderate confidence.
