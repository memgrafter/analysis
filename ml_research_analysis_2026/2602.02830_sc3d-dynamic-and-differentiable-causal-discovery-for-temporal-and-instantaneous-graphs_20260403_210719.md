# SC3D: Dynamic and Differentiable Causal Discovery for Temporal and Instantaneous Graphs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.02830  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02830  
- Reference count: 13  
- **Key result**: SC3D achieves lower total structural Hamming distance (SHD) and higher AUROC/AUPRC for both lagged and instantaneous structures compared to baselines, demonstrating improved accuracy, stability, and scalability to higher dimensions.

## Executive Summary
SC3D tackles the challenging problem of joint causal discovery in multivariate time series, where both lagged (temporal) and instantaneous (contemporaneous) dependencies must be recovered. Existing methods either ignore instantaneous effects or suffer from scalability and stability issues. SC3D proposes a two-stage differentiable framework: Stage 1 performs node‑wise temporal preselection to generate sparse masks for both edge types, guaranteeing (under assumptions) that all true dynamic parents are retained. Stage 2 then refines these masks by optimizing a Gaussian likelihood with sparsity penalties while enforcing acyclicity on the instantaneous block via a stable spectral penalty and a 2‑cycle penalty. Extensive experiments on synthetic systems (including chaotic Lorenz96 and nonstationary TVSEM) show that SC3D consistently outperforms strong baselines like DYNOTEARS and PCMCI+ in terms of SHD, AUROC, and AUPRC, with particularly robust recovery of instantaneous graphs as dimensionality grows. Ablation studies confirm the necessity of both stages and the 2‑cycle penalty for accurate instantaneous structure learning.

## Method Summary
SC3D assumes data follow a structural vector autoregression (SVAR) model: each variable is a linear combination of its own and other variables’ past values (lagged matrices \(A_\ell\)) and contemporaneous values (instantaneous matrix \(B\)), with i.i.d. Gaussian noise. The two‑stage approach first reduces the combinatorial search space and then refines the structure under acyclicity constraints.

**Stage 1 (Preselection):** For each target node \(j\), a neural predictor is trained on a window containing all other variables’ current and lagged values (up to \(L\) lags). The first‑layer weights are grouped by input variable (including each lag separately). An \(\ell_1\) penalty on the group norms induces sparsity; after training, the \(\ell_2\) norm of each group determines an edge score, and thresholding yields binary masks \(M_{A}^\ell\) (lagged) and \(M_B\) (instantaneous). Stage 1 thus produces candidate edge sets that (under assumptions) contain all true parents.

**Stage 2 (Refinement):** The lagged and instantaneous adjacency matrices (\(\{A_\ell\}, B\)) are initialized within the masked entries (masked positions fixed to zero). The objective minimizes the negative log‑likelihood (Gaussian MSE) plus sparsity penalties: \(\alpha\sum_\ell\|A_\ell\|_1 + \beta\|B\|_1\). Acyclicity of \(B\) is enforced by a spectral radius penalty \(\gamma \rho(B)\), where \(\rho(B)\) is approximated via differentiable power iteration. An additional 2‑cycle penalty \(\lambda_{2c}\|B \odot B^\top\|_1\) explicitly suppresses reciprocal instantaneous edges. The coefficient \(\gamma\) is increased gradually until a greedy extraction (selecting strong edges while preserving acyclicity) yields an acyclic graph with a minimum number of edges, after which \(\gamma\) is frozen. Optimization proceeds with Adam. Final binary graphs are obtained by thresholding the learned weights (and using the greedy extraction for \(B\)).

## Key Results
- SC3D attains lower total SHD and higher AUROC/AUPRC than state‑of‑the‑art baselines (DYNOTEARS, PCMCI+) across synthetic and benchmark dynamical systems.
- The method maintains low instantaneous error as dimensionality increases, while competing methods either fail to estimate instantaneous effects or degrade sharply.
- Ablation studies demonstrate that removing Stage 1 or the 2‑cycle penalty significantly harms instantaneous recovery, confirming their critical roles.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Stage 1 node‑wise temporal preselection narrows the combinatorial search space by generating sparse masks that preserve all true dynamic parents within a temporal window.
- **Mechanism:** For each target variable, a penalized conditional log‑likelihood is maximized over subsets of candidate parents from a fixed lagged and contemporaneous window. Group \(\ell_1\) regularization on input‑layer weights yields edge scores; thresholding produces binary masks.
- **Core assumption:** The true conditional distribution belongs to the model class, the joint density is positive, and strict faithfulness holds.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  • Abstract: “Stage 1 performs edge preselection through node‑wise prediction to obtain masks for lagged and instantaneous edges.”  
  • Section 3.1 and Theorem 3.1 guarantee that for sufficiently small regularization, the selected set contains all true parents.  
  • Neighboring methods (e.g., UnCLe) focus on lagged discovery only, highlighting SC3D’s two‑stage masking as a novel differentiator.
- **Break condition:** If the temporal window is too small or the regularization strength is mis‑tuned, true edges may be excluded, causing irreversible downstream errors.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Stage 2 refines masks by optimizing a regularized likelihood while enforcing acyclicity on the instantaneous block via a spectral radius penalty, ensuring stable scalability.
- **Mechanism:** The optimization (Eq. 6) minimizes negative log‑likelihood with \(\ell_1\) sparsity on lagged and instantaneous matrices, subject to \(\rho(B)=0\). The spectral penalty replaces smooth trace constraints, avoiding numerical instability in high dimensions. A gradual penalty schedule prevents oscillatory behavior.
- **Core assumption:** The instantaneous ground‑truth graph is acyclic, and the spectral radius constraint effectively promotes acyclicity despite non‑convexity.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  • Abstract: “Stage 2 refines these masks by optimizing a likelihood with sparsity along with enforcing acyclicity on the instantaneous block.”  
  • Section 3.2 defines the optimization and spectral penalty; Proposition 3.2 proves that enforcing acyclicity on \(B\) alone suffices for the time‑unrolled graph.  
  • Section 4.6 ablation shows that removing the spectral penalty degrades instantaneous F1, especially at \(d=40\).
- **Break condition:** Too aggressive \(\gamma\) early on may trap optimization in poor local minima; too lenient \(\gamma\) may allow cycles to form during refinement.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A 2‑cycle penalty \(\|B \odot B^\top\|_1\) is added to the spectral constraint to explicitly penalize reciprocal instantaneous edges, improving identifiability and stability in high dimensions.
- **Mechanism:** Even with small spectral radius, pairs of opposing edges (2‑cycles) may remain, causing ambiguous causal assignments. The element‑wise product term directly penalizes such reciprocal pairs, reinforcing sparsity and acyclicity without compromising smooth optimization.
- **Core assumption:** Reciprocal instantaneous edges are unlikely in the true data‑generating process and their presence degrades recovery.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  • Section 3.2: “This additional term reinforces identifiability and stabilizes optimization in high dimensional regimes without negating the smoothness and scalability properties.”  
  • Section 4.6 ablation: “No 2‑cycle penalty” leads to lower F1\(_B\) and higher SHD\(_B\), particularly at \(d=40\).  
  • The corpus lacks direct neighboring work on a 2‑cycle penalty, making this contribution distinctive.
- **Break condition:** If the ground truth contains genuine reciprocal instantaneous edges (violating the DAG assumption), this penalty will erroneously remove them; however, the SVAR model explicitly assumes an acyclic instantaneous graph.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Structural Vector Autoregression (SVAR)**  
  *Why needed:* SC3D assumes data is generated by an SVAR: \(X_{t+1} = \sum_{\ell=1}^L A_\ell X_{t+1-\ell} + B X_{t+1} + \varepsilon_t\). Understanding this generative model is essential to interpret the output matrices \(A_\ell\) (lagged effects) and \(B\) (instantaneous DAG).  
  *Quick check:* Given an SVAR with \(L=2\) and \(d=3\), how many total lagged parameters and instantaneous parameters must be recovered, and why must \(B\) be acyclic?

- **Concept: Differentiable Causal Discovery & Acyclicity Constraints**  
  *Why needed:* SC3D belongs to the differentiable causal discovery family, reformulating structure learning as continuous optimization over weighted adjacency matrices. The key challenge is enforcing acyclicity smoothly; SC3D uses the spectral radius \(\rho(B)=0\), a stable alternative to trace‑based penalties like in NOTEARS.  
  *Quick check:* Why does the trace‑exp(·) constraint in NOTEARS become numerically unstable as dimension grows, and how does the spectral penalty address this?

- **Concept: Two‑Stage Pruning in High‑Dimensional Optimization**  
  *Why needed:* The joint search space over lagged and instantaneous edges is combinatorial. Stage 1’s preselection reduces problem size by discarding unlikely edges while guaranteeing (under assumptions) no true parents are lost. Stage 2 then optimizes only over the remaining candidates with acyclicity enforcement.  
  *Quick check:* What would happen if Stage 1 were skipped and the full unconstrained optimization of Eq. (6) were attempted directly on a \(d=50\) system?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Temporal predictor window → Node-wise neural predictors → Mask generators → Constrained optimizer → Spectral acyclicity module → 2‑cycle penalty module → Penalty scheduler

- **Critical path:** Input time series → Stage 1 per‑node training → mask generation → initialize \(\{A_\ell\}, B\) with masked values → Stage 2 loop: forward pass (compute NLL), compute spectral radius & 2‑cycle penalty, backpropagate total loss, update parameters, periodically check acyclicity → output final \(\{A_\ell\}, B\).

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Two‑stage vs. end‑to‑end:* Stage 1 decouples screening from acyclicity enforcement, preventing the spectral penalty from overwhelming early learning; but it introduces a thresholding hyperparameter and may discard edges if too aggressive.  
  *Spectral vs. trace penalty:* Spectral radius is more stable for large \(d\) but only approximates acyclicity; trace‑exp(·) is exact but prone to vanishing/exploding gradients. SC3D augments with a 2‑cycle penalty to compensate.  
  *Gradual penalty schedule:* Prevents early dominance of the acyclicity term, allowing likelihood to first align with masked structure; however, it requires careful scheduling and freeze criteria.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High total SHD with particularly high instantaneous SHD\(_B\) (e.g., when Stage 1 is disabled or 2‑cycle penalty removed).  
  - High variance across random seeds in AUROC/AUPR for instantaneous graph (indicating instability).  
  - Oscillatory loss curves in Stage 2 when \(\gamma\) increases too rapidly.  
  - Extracted instantaneous graph containing cycles even after many epochs (penalty freezing never triggers).

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Scalability sweep (Figure 2):** Vary dimension \(d\) (10→50) under constant sparsity. SC3D shows lowest or competitive total SHD and lowest lagged error, demonstrating robustness to high dimensionality.  
  2. **Decomposition of error (Figure 3):** Break total SHD into lagged vs. instantaneous components. SC3D maintains low instantaneous error as \(d\) grows, while baselines either do not estimate \(B\) (infinite error) or degrade sharply.  
  3. **Ablation study (Table 5):** Disable Stage 1, linear predictor, penalty freezing, or 2‑cycle penalty. “No Stage 1” catastrophically increases SHD\(_{total}\) and destroys F1\(_B\), proving Stage 1’s role in controlling search space. Removing the 2‑cycle penalty hurts instantaneous recovery significantly at \(d=40\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can SC3D be extended to handle nonstationary and online time series settings where causal structures change over time?  
  *Basis:* The conclusion states future work will focus on extensions to nonstationary and online settings.  
  *Why unresolved:* The current framework assumes a stationary SVAR; two‑stage optimization and fixed windows are not designed for streaming or regime‑switching data.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Implement an online variant (e.g., sliding windows or adaptive regularization) and evaluate on synthetic nonstationary datasets (like TVSEM) with continuous regime changes.

- **Open Question 2:** Under what conditions (e.g., graph density, dimension) does the 2‑cycle penalty become necessary for stable instantaneous DAG recovery?  
  *Basis:* Ablation results show that removing the penalty worsens F1 at \(d=40\) but less at \(d=20\); the paper notes it “reinforces identifiability and stabilizes optimization in high dimensional regimes.”  
  *Why unresolved:* The exact threshold where the 2‑cycle penalty is needed is not characterized.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Systematically vary graph sparsity and dimension, measuring prevalence of 2‑cycles without the penalty and impact on SHD.

- **Open Question 3:** How robust is SC3D to violations of the population‑level assumptions in Theorem 3.1 (e.g., non‑strict faithfulness, model misspecification, non‑positive densities) in finite‑sample settings?  
  *Basis:* Theorem 3.1 guarantees Stage 1 preserves all true parents under strict assumptions (Appendix A.1). The paper validates only on data adhering to these assumptions.  
  *Why unresolved:* Real‑world time series often have weak signals, hidden confounders, or non‑Gaussian noise; the paper does not test robustness.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Generate synthetic data with controlled violations (e.g., added weak conditional dependencies, misspecified predictor class, heavy‑tailed noise) and measure degradation in Stage 1 recall and overall recovery.

- **Open Question 4:** What is the empirical trade‑off between the computational cost of Stage 1’s node‑wise screening and the overall accuracy/stability of SC3D, especially regarding the choice of the sparsity parameter \(\lambda\)?  
  *Basis:* The complexity analysis (Appendix C) shows Stage 1 scales as \(O(E_1 n d H(dL+d_{\text{inst}}))\). The paper uses a fixed \(\lambda=0.15\) without sensitivity analysis.  
  *Why unresolved:* The two‑stage design trades screening thoroughness for optimization stability; the optimal \(\lambda\) likely depends on dimension, sparsity, and signal strength.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Perform a sensitivity analysis over a range of \(\lambda\) values on different dimensional datasets, tracking mask recall/precision, Stage 2 runtime, and final SHD.

- **Open Question 5:** Can the spectral acyclicity penalty and gradual coefficient schedule in Stage 2 be theoretically linked to avoiding local minima or oscillation issues that plague smooth trace‑based constraints in high dimensions?  
  *Basis:* SC3D advocates the spectral radius penalty as “numerically stable” compared to trace‑based methods (e.g., NOTEARS) and uses a gradual schedule to “impede oscillatory behavior.” However, no formal optimization guarantees are provided.  
  *Why unresolved:* The superiority of spectral constraints is attributed to stability, but the paper lacks an ablation on the schedule and does not analyze loss landscapes or gradient properties.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Compare optimization trajectories (loss curvature, gradient norms, cycle violations during training) of SC3D’s spectral penalty versus a trace‑based penalty on high‑dimensional synthetic graphs, and possibly derive conditions under which the spectral penalty ensures monotonic decrease in an acyclicity measure.

## Limitations
- Stage 1 specifics (network width, depth, binarization threshold) are not reported, hindering exact reproducibility.
- The spectral acyclicity penalty (power‑iteration steps, \(\gamma\) schedule, freeze criteria) is underspecified, risking instability or inaccurate acyclicity enforcement.
- Evaluation is restricted to synthetic and benchmark datasets; real‑world applications with potential violations of SVAR assumptions remain unvalidated.
