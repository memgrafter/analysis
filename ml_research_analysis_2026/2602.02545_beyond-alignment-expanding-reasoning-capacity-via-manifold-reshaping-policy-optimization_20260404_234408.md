# Beyond Alignment: Expanding Reasoning Capacity via Manifold-Reshaping Policy Optimization
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02545  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02545  
- **Reference count:** 23  
- **Primary result:** A 4B‑parameter model achieves state‑of‑the‑art results on multiple math benchmarks (AIME 2024 56.7%, AIME 2025 43.3%, MATH‑500 88.8%, OlympiadBench 43.0%, Omni‑Hard 17.4%), with a mean of 49.8% – significantly outperforming larger Qwen3‑32B (mean 37.8%) and standard GRPO (46.0%).

## Executive Summary
Standard reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) aligns model capabilities but often confines the policy to a low‑rank “bias manifold,” limiting reasoning expansion due to entropy reduction and spectral collapse. This paper introduces Manifold‑Reshaping Policy Optimization (MRPO), which combines two innovations: (1) Spectral Orthogonal Exploration (SOE) cold‑start data synthesis, ejecting the policy into the orthogonal null space via weak‑to‑strong latent stitching, and (2) Rank‑Aware Group Relative Policy Optimization that adds an effective‑rank regularizer to sustain high‑dimensional reasoning trajectories. A 4B model using MRPO surpasses much larger baselines on math reasoning benchmarks and shows expanded coverage in Pass@k analyses. Ablations confirm that both SOE and the rank reward are necessary, and training maintains higher effective rank, preventing collapse.

## Method Summary
MRPO operates in two stages. **Stage I: SOE Cold‑Start Data Synthesis.** For each problem where the Teacher model (e.g., Qwen3‑4B‑Instruct) fails, a weaker Student model (e.g., Gemma‑3‑4B‑IT) generates multiple “probe” completions. Using a Micro‑SVD on the Teacher’s incorrect traces, the top‑k bias subspace \(U_\parallel\) is identified. Each student probe is mapped into the Teacher’s latent space and its orthogonality to \(U_\parallel\) is scored. The most orthogonal probe is stitched into the Teacher’s context, forcing a generation step in the null space. The resulting trace is verified; correct and high‑orthogonality traces are collected into a dataset (~10k examples) for one epoch of supervised fine‑tuning of the Teacher. **Stage II: Rank‑Aware GRPO.** RL fine‑tuning starts from the SFT checkpoint. For each prompt, a group of outputs is sampled. The total reward combines binary correctness and a normalized effective‑rank bonus: \(R_{\text{total}} = \mathbb{I}(\text{correct}) \times (1 + \alpha \cdot \text{NormRank})\). NormRank is the minimum effective rank (over a sliding window) of the final‑layer hidden‑state covariance, scaled to \([0,1]\). Group‑relative advantages (\(\hat{A}_i = (R_i - \mu_{\text{group}})/\sigma_{\text{group}}\)) drive policy updates with GRPO. Training uses OpenRLHF, vLLM rollout (8192 context), DeepSpeed ZeRO‑3, LR = 1e‑6, KL = 0, for 4 epochs.

## Key Results
- MRPO (4B) dramatically outperforms larger Qwen3‑32B and standard GRPO on five challenging math benchmarks, achieving a mean accuracy of 49.8% vs. 37.8% and 46.0% respectively.
- Pass@32 analysis reveals much broader accessibility: 89.1% on AIME 2024, confirming that MRPO expands the set of solvable problems beyond greedy decoding.
- Ablations show both components are essential: removing the SOE cold‑start or the rank reward each causes a drop in performance (~2–3% absolute).
- MRPO maintains a significantly higher effective rank of correct trajectories during RL, preventing the spectral collapse observed under pure GRPO.
- Results are stable across random seeds, indicating robust manifold reshaping.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Bias Manifold Entrapment & Spectral Collapse
- **Claim:** Standard RLVR reduces policy entropy and contracts the latent reasoning space onto a low‑rank “Bias Manifold,” limiting discovery of novel, high‑complexity reasoning paths.
- **Mechanism:** Reward maximization increases logit scales (effective temperature decrease), acting as a rank‑reducing operator on the covariance of hidden states \(H\). The effective rank \(\text{erank}(H)\) collapses toward a small \(k\), trapping trajectories in a dominant low‑dimensional subspace \(M_{\text{bias}}\) of common heuristics.
- **Core assumption:** Latent reasoning capacity is distributed high‑dimensionally, but optimization preferentially exploits low‑rank shortcuts from pre‑training.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.4 formalize “Reasoning Collapse” (Proposition 3.3, Hypothesis 1). Indirect support from related work on diversity collapse in RL.

### Mechanism 2: Geometric Ejection via Spectral Orthogonal Exploration (SOE)
- **Claim:** SOE cold‑start data synthesis mechanically ejects the policy from \(M_{\text{bias}}\) into the orthogonal null space \(\mathcal{N}\), making high‑rank, novel reasoning trajectories accessible for RL.
- **Mechanism:** A Student model generates probe fragments mapped into the Teacher’s latent space. Orthogonality to the Teacher’s current bias subspace (computed via Micro‑SVD) is measured by \(\Omega(s)\). The most orthogonal probe is stitched into the Teacher’s context, forcing a generation step in \(\mathcal{N}\). Repeating builds a dataset of correct, high‑rank traces for initial SFT.
- **Core assumption:** The weaker model’s latent biases are non‑overlapping enough with the stronger model’s \(M_{\text{bias}}\) to serve as an effective orthogonal probe.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.1 details OLS; ablation shows cold‑start improves mean accuracy (46.1% vs. Base 44.5%); citation of Wang et al. (2026) for the SOE framework.

### Mechanism 3: Rank‑Aware Regularization via Effective Rank Reward
- **Claim:** Integrating an effective‑rank reward into GRPO explicitly incentivizes and sustains high‑dimensional reasoning trajectories, counteracting RL’s intrinsic entropy reduction.
- **Mechanism:** Total reward \(R_{\text{total}}(y) = \mathbb{I}(\text{correct}) \cdot (1 + \alpha \cdot \text{NormRank}(y))\). Among correct solves, those with higher \(\text{erank}\) (over a sliding window) receive a bonus, so correctness is necessary but not sufficient. The group‑relative advantage thus incorporates geometric richness.
- **Core assumption:** High \(\text{erank}\) in a verified‑correct trajectory is a proxy for complex, multi‑faceted reasoning and correlates with robust problem‑solving.
- **Evidence anchors:** Logistic regression (Section 3.2) shows \(\text{EffRank}\) is a significant positive predictor of correctness; MRPO maintains higher mean \(\text{EffRank}\) vs. Pure GRPO (Figure 4); ablation removing rank reward drops accuracy (49.0% → 49.8%).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Effective Rank (Stable Rank)**  
  *Why needed:* Quantifies the geometric dimensionality of a reasoning trajectory; core metric for MRPO’s rank regularization.  
  *Quick check:* Given trajectory matrix \(H\) (\(T \times d\)) and centered covariance \(\Sigma\), derive \(\text{erank}(H)\) from eigenvalues \(\lambda_i\) of \(\Sigma\).  
  *Answer:* Compute normalized probabilities \(p_i = \lambda_i / \sum_j \lambda_j\); then \(\text{erank}(H) = \exp\bigl(-\sum_i p_i \ln p_i\bigr)\).

- **Concept: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**  
  *Why needed:* MRPO builds upon GRPO’s group‑normalized advantage estimation; understanding \(\hat{A}_i = (R_i - \mu_{\text{group}})/\sigma_{\text{group}}\) is key to seeing how the rank‑augmented reward modifies gradients.  
  *Quick check:* How does GRPO’s group‑relative advantage formulation remove the need for a separate value network?  
  *Answer:* Within each sampled group, the mean reward serves as a baseline; advantage is computed as the centered and scaled reward, avoiding explicit value function learning.

- **Concept: Latent Space Geometry & Orthogonal Projections**  
  *Why needed:* The entire framework (Bias Manifold, Null Space, OLS) is defined via subspaces and projections (e.g., \(\text{Proj}_{\mathcal{N}}(H) = (I - U_\parallel U_\parallel^T)H\)).  
  *Quick check:* Explain in linear algebra terms what the matrix \(I - U_\parallel U_\parallel^T\) does to a vector \(z_j\) in the context of Equation 6.  
  *Answer:* It projects \(z_j\) onto the orthogonal complement of the subspace spanned by \(U_\parallel\), i.e., removes the component along the bias manifold, leaving only the component in the null space.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Student Model → Latent Probe Generation → Teacher Model (initial traces) → Micro‑SVD (bias subspace \(U_\parallel\)) → Orthogonality Scoring (\(\Omega(s)\)) → Probe Selection (max \(\Omega\)) → Context Stitching → Symbolic Verifier → Filtered Correct & High‑Orthogonal Traces → Stage I SFT (Teacher) → RL Checkpoint → GRPO with Effective Rank Reward → Final MRPO Policy

**Critical Path:**  
1. Prepare base Teacher (Qwen3‑4B‑Instruct) and Student (Gemma‑3‑4B‑IT).  
2. For each failing problem: generate multiple Teacher completions, extract final‑layer hidden states, compute Micro‑SVD to obtain \(U_\parallel\). Generate student probes, map to Teacher latent, compute \(\Omega(s)\), select most orthogonal, stitch into context, regenerate trace. Symbolically verify; keep correct traces with high \(\Omega\).  
3. Collect ~10k such traces; fine‑tune Teacher for one epoch.  
4. Initialize RL from SFT checkpoint. For each group of sampled outputs, compute Effective Rank (minimum over sliding window), form \(R_{\text{total}} = \mathbb{I}(\text{correct}) \times (1 + 0.5 \times \text{NormRank})\), compute group‑relative advantage, and update policy via GRPO for 4 epochs (OpenRLHF, vLLM rollout, DeepSpeed ZeRO‑3, LR = 1e‑6, KL = 0).

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- **SOE Overhead:** Dual‑model inference plus SVD is computationally expensive initially, but amortized over the benefits of improved geometric initialization.  
- **Rank Reward Sensitivity:** The coefficient \(\alpha\) balances correctness vs. complexity; improper tuning may cause unnatural verbosity or insufficient collapse prevention.  
- **Context Length:** Training uses 8192 tokens to accommodate deep reasoning, but this can disadvantage benchmarks with stricter limits (e.g., Omni‑Hard).

**Failure Signatures:**  
- **Geometric Failure:** \(\text{EffRank}\) of correct trajectories plummets to near 1 during RL, matching pure GRPO; indicates rank reward ineffective.  
- **Stability Failure:** High variance in Pass@1 or Pass@32 across random seeds; unstable manifold reshaping.  
- **Quality Failure:** High \(\text{EffRank}\) paired with low correctness; suggests optimization of irrelevant null‑space directions (rank reward detached from truth).

**First 3 Experiments:**  
1. **Ablation Smoke Test:** Train three variants on a small subset (500 MATH samples): Base + SOE, Base + Rank Reward, and Base + SOE + Rank Reward. Verify that both components are needed for synergistic gains in mean accuracy and \(\text{EffRank}\).  
2. **Rank Trajectory Tracking:** During the first 100 RL steps, monitor the minimum sliding‑window \(\text{EffRank}\) of correct samples per batch. Expect pure GRPO to trend downward, while MRPO stabilizes or increases.  
3. **Pass@k Scaling Curve:** On a fixed set of 100 hard problems, compute unbiased Pass@k for \(k = 1,4,8,16,32\) after one RL epoch. A gap > 15% absolute between MRPO and GRPO at \(k=32\) would confirm expanded accessibility.

## Limitations
- **Micro‑SVD specifics:** Exact algorithm (top‑k selection, approximations, per‑trace adaptivity) is not detailed; missing hyperparameters could substantially affect orthogonality scoring.  
- **Hidden state extraction:** Unclear whether all tokens, only final‑layer representations, or a specific sliding window are used to compute effective rank; stride and window size are unspecified.  
- **Student‑Teacher manifold assumption:** SOE relies on the Student’s latent biases being sufficiently non‑overlapping; if models are already aligned, probes may not eject meaningfully.

## Confidence
- Spectral Collapse Mechanism: Medium  
- Spectral Orthogonal Exploration (SOE): Medium  
- Effective Rank Regularization: High  

## Next Checks
1. **SOE orthogonality audit:** Reimplement Micro‑SVD with likely hyperparameters (e.g., fixed k = 64) and report the distribution of \(\Omega(s)\). A median near 0 would indicate failed ejection.  
2. **EffRank‑correctness correlation:** Compute \(\text{EffRank}\) exactly as described (layer, sliding window, normalization) on a held set; the correlation with correctness must remain statistically significant, otherwise the rank reward may optimize a spurious signal.  
3. **Cross‑model transfer:** Replace Gemma‑3‑4B‑IT with a structurally different small model (e.g., Phi‑3‑mini). If MRPO’s gains persist, the findings generalize beyond a specific Student‑Teacher pair.
