# BanglaLorica: Design and Evaluation of a Robust Watermarking Algorithm for Large Language Models in Bangla Text Generation

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.04534
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04534
*   **Reference count:** 15
*   **Primary result:** A layered watermarking strategy combining token-level embedding (KGW/EXP) with post-generation perturbation (Waterfall) achieves 40-50% detection accuracy after a Bangla→English→Bangla round-trip translation attack, a 3×–4× relative improvement over single-layer methods that collapse to 9-13%.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical vulnerability in text watermarking for Bangla, a low-resource language. State-of-the-art token-level watermarking methods (KGW, EXP) are catastrophically fragile under cross-lingual round-trip translation (RTT) attacks, where text is translated to another language and back, causing detection accuracy to plummet to near-random levels (9-13%). The authors propose BanglaLorica, a practical, training-free solution that layers a post-generation watermark (Waterfall) on top of a standard token-level watermark. This creates an orthogonal statistical signal that survives RTT, restoring detection accuracy to 40-50%. However, this robustness gain comes with a cost: the post-generation layer introduces moderate semantic degradation as measured by automatic similarity metrics, establishing a clear trade-off between robustness and quality for forensic attribution in Bangla LLM-generated content.

## Method Summary
The proposed method builds upon two existing watermarking paradigms. The first layer uses standard token-level watermarking (KGW or EXP) during the LLM's generation process, biasing sampling to embed a statistical signal. The second layer applies a post-generation "Waterfall" technique, which perturbs the already-generated text through controlled paraphrasing to encode a separate, independent watermark signal. During inference, for each watermarked text, the system generates multiple paraphrase candidates with the Waterfall signal and selects the one optimizing a weighted combination of watermark strength and semantic similarity to the original text. Detection requires applying the appropriate statistical test for each layer (Z-test for KGW, Gamma p-value for EXP, and a custom score for Waterfall). The system is evaluated on a Bangla LLaMA-3-8B model using a dataset of 500 Bangla prompts, with robustness tested via a Bangla→English→Bangla round-trip translation attack using a dedicated NMT model.

## Key Results
*   Single-layer token-level watermarking (KGW/EXP) achieves high benign detection accuracy (>88%) but suffers a near-total collapse under RTT, with accuracy dropping to 9-13%.
*   The layered watermarking approach (KGW/EXP + Waterfall) restores post-RTT detection accuracy to a robust 40-50%, representing a 3× to 4× relative improvement over single-layer baselines.
*   This robustness gain is accompanied by moderate semantic degradation, as measured by lower sentence similarity scores for double-watermarked text compared to single-layer watermarked text.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Token-level watermarking (KGW/EXP) fails under RTT because it relies on the stability of specific token identities and their frequencies or scores in the generated sequence. RTT acts as an adversarial transformation that performs lexical substitution and syntactic reordering while preserving semantics. This alters the surface form enough to erase the original token-level statistical signature, making watermarked text indistinguishable from unwatermarked text after translation. Evidence: Abstract and Section 4.2 report detection accuracy collapsing to 9-13% with overlapping score distributions (Figures 4, 5). The break condition is when the transformation induces sufficient lexical/syntactic variation to disrupt the token-based signal.

### Mechanism 2
Layered watermarking improves RTT robustness by introducing a secondary, orthogonal signal from the post-generation Waterfall layer. While the primary token-level signal is disrupted by RTT, the Waterfall signal—encoded via different statistical properties (vocabulary permutation, distributional skew) derived from paraphrasing—has a higher chance of surviving the same attack. The combined detection leverages the surviving secondary signal. Evidence: Abstract reports 3×–4× improvement; Section 4.3/Figure 6 shows layered configurations significantly outperform single-layer post-RTT; Section A.4 describes the joint optimization for combining layers. The break condition is an attack strong enough to disrupt both independent signals simultaneously.

### Mechanism 3
The robustness-quality trade-off is inherent because the Waterfall post-generation layer must perturb the text to embed its signal. To mitigate damage, a selection algorithm chooses the paraphrase candidate that balances watermark strength with semantic similarity to the original text (weighted by λ). This controlled perturbation is necessary to create a signal resilient to semantic-preserving attacks like RTT but inevitably causes some meaning drift, measured as "moderate semantic degradation." Evidence: Abstract notes the cost; Section 4.3/Figure 7 shows lower similarity scores for double-watermarked text; Section 5 discusses the trade-off. The break condition is when required perturbations push semantic similarity below the usability threshold for the target application.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Token-Level Watermarking (KGW/EXP)**
    *   **Why needed here:** These are the baseline single-layer methods whose failure under RTT motivates the study. Understanding their mechanism (green-list biasing, exponential sampling) is key to grasping why detection collapses after translation.
    *   **Quick check:** Why does changing a token to a synonym (as in RTT) break a KGW watermark that relies on counting specific "green list" tokens?

*   **Concept: Cross-Lingual Round-Trip Translation (RTT) as an Attack**
    *   **Why needed here:** RTT is the primary adversarial evaluation framework. It's not just paraphrasing; it's a cross-lingual transformation that drastically alters surface form while preserving meaning, exposing a specific vulnerability in token-based methods for Bangla.
    *   **Quick check:** How does a Bangla→English→Bangla round-trip differ from a monolingual paraphrase attack in its effect on a Bangla text, and why is it particularly challenging for watermarking?

*   **Concept: Post-Generation Watermarking (Waterfall)**
    *   **Why needed here:** This provides the second, orthogonal layer. It operates on already-generated text via perturbation, making its statistical signal theoretically more resilient to the lexical changes of RTT compared to token-level methods tied to the original generation.
    *   **Quick check:** In what way is the statistical signal from Waterfall (e.g., based on vocabulary permutation and distributional skew) structurally different from the token-count signal of KGW, and why might that difference confer RTT robustness?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Input Prompt -> Bangla LLaMA-3-8B Generator -> [Primary Watermark (KGW/EXP)] -> Text T0 -> [Secondary Watermark (Waterfall Paraphrase Selection)] -> Layered Output Tw -> [Adversarial RTT: Bangla->English->Bangla] -> Attacked Text -> [Detection: KGW Z-test / EXP Gamma / Waterfall Score]

*   **Critical path:** The core experimental loop is: Prompt -> Primary WM Generation -> (Optional) Secondary WM Selection -> RTT Attack -> Detection. For layered evaluation, the secondary WM selection step is the critical addition that trades quality for robustness.

*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Robustness vs. Quality:** Adding Waterfall increases post-RTT detection but reduces semantic similarity. The λ parameter in the selection algorithm controls this trade-off.
    *   **Generality vs. Performance:** Token-level methods are efficient and preserve quality under benign conditions but are linguistically fragile. Waterfall is more robust but computationally heavier and degrades quality more.
    *   **Training-free vs. Optimal:** The solution is training-free and theoretically language-agnostic, but performance (especially quality impact) likely requires per-language calibration of λ.

*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Single-layer RTT failure:** Detection accuracy plunges to 9-13% after RTT, with score distributions overlapping completely with unwatermarked baselines.
    *   **Layered quality collapse:** If λ is set too high (favoring watermark strength), semantic similarity scores drop excessively, making text unusable.
    *   **Detector mismatch:** Applying the wrong detector (e.g., KGW detector to EXP text) yields meaningless scores.

*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline Single-Layer Benign Evaluation:** Generate 500 prompts with KGW and EXP at lengths {100,150,200}. Measure detection accuracy, perplexity, ROUGE. *Purpose: Confirm methods work in Bangla under no attack.*
    2.  **Single-Layer RTT Robustness Test:** Take outputs from Exp. 1, apply Bangla→English→Bangla RTT. Run detection on translated text. *Purpose: Quantify catastrophic failure under cross-lingual attack.*
    3.  **Layered Watermarking Efficacy Test:** For 100-token generations, apply KGW/EXP then Waterfall. Compare detection accuracy before and after RTT against single-layer baselines. Measure semantic similarity of layered output vs. original. *Purpose: Validate robustness recovery and characterize quality cost.*

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question 1:** How does layered watermarking perform under multi-hop round-trip translation attacks involving multiple intermediate languages or longer translation chains?
    *   **Basis:** Explicitly stated in Future Work; current study restricted to a single Bangla→English→Bangla pathway.
*   **Question 2:** Can integrating semantic-aware watermarking methods into the layered framework reduce semantic degradation while maintaining robustness against RTT?
    *   **Basis:** Explicitly proposed in Future Work but unexplored due to architectural complexity and calibration needs.
*   **Question 3:** Can adaptive layer selection—applying secondary watermarking only when threat models indicate high-risk scenarios—optimize the robustness-quality trade-off without compromising detectability?
    *   **Basis:** Mentioned as future work, requiring explicit modeling of attacker capabilities and deployment assumptions.
*   **Question 4:** Do human evaluations reveal more severe semantic degradation from layered watermarking in Bangla than automatic metrics suggest, given the language's rich morphology and flexibility?
    *   **Basis:** Inferred from Limitations; authors acknowledge automatic metrics may not capture subtle meaning drift in morphologically rich languages and suggest human evaluation.

## Limitations
*   **Bangla-specificity of results:** The reported failure (9-13%) and recovery (40-50%) are from a single Bangla model; no comparison to other low-resource languages isolates language morphology from model-specific factors.
*   **Waterfall implementation opacity:** Critical hyperparameters for the Waterfall layer (paraphrase model, vocabulary partition, perturbation budget) are not disclosed, hindering reproducibility.
*   **Semantic degradation measurement:** The "Semantic Score" metric is undefined; without specifying the embedding model, the reported moderate degradation may reflect metric artifacts.
