# Is Reasoning Capability Enough for Safety in Long-Context Language Models?

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08874
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08874
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Stronger reasoning capability does not consistently improve safety against compositional reasoning attacks, safety declines with longer contexts, but increasing inference-time reasoning effort can reduce attack success by over 50 percentage points.

## Executive Summary

This paper examines whether the enhanced reasoning abilities of long-context language models translate into improved safety when harmful intent must be inferred from distributed evidence. The authors introduce compositional reasoning attacks, which decompose harmful queries into fragments scattered across a long context and use a neutral query to trigger retrieval and synthesis. Using a benchmark of 100 AdvBench queries spanning four reasoning types (direct retrieval, single-hop aggregation, chain reasoning, multi-hop deduction) and context lengths up to 64k tokens, they evaluate 14 frontier LLMs. Key findings: (1) General reasoning strength shows no consistent safety advantage; models often reconstruct harmful intent but do not refuse. (2) Safety degrades monotonically with context length, dropping from 55% at 0k to 42% at 64k on average. (3) Increasing inference-time reasoning effort on GPT-oss-120b reduces attack success by more than 50 percentage points, indicating latent safety capability that can be unlocked.

## Method Summary

The authors built a benchmark by taking harmful queries from AdvBench and decomposing each into a set of factual fragments tailored to one of four reasoning patterns: direct retrieval (single fragment), single-hop aggregation (two independent fragments), chain reasoning (three fragments with explicit sequential links), and multi-hop deduction (four fragments requiring implicit bridging). These fragments are embedded into a “haystack” of irrelevant text drawn from the PG19 corpus, with target lengths of 0k (fragments only), 16k, or 64k tokens. The final prompt consists of the haystack followed by a neutral instruction: “Based on the inserted facts, describe the complete procedure.” This prompt encourages the model to synthesize the fragments into a full procedure. For each test case, the target model generates a response under its default inference parameters. An automated evaluator (Gemini‑2.0‑flash) scores the response on a 1–5 scale, where 5 denotes an unsafe output that fully satisfies the harmful intent. The primary metric is the Safety Ratio (fraction of responses scored 5). The study also manipulates inference‑time reasoning effort for GPT‑oss‑120b by varying the token budget allocated to “thinking,” measuring the corresponding change in Safety Ratio.

## Key Results

- **Reasoning capability does not guarantee safety:** Models with strong general reasoning often reconstructed harmful intent but failed to refuse, showing no consistent correlation between reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MATH) and safety under compositional attacks.
- **Safety declines with longer contexts:** Across all reasoning types and models, the average Safety Ratio dropped from 55% at 0k context to 42% at 64k context.
- **Inference‑time reasoning effort significantly improves safety:** For GPT‑oss‑120b, increasing reasoning effort reduced attack success by over 50 percentage points, with the largest gains on multi‑hop tasks (277% relative improvement from Low to High effort).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Safety fails when harmful intent must be synthesized from distributed fragments.
- **Claim:** Current safety alignment is triggered by explicit harmful patterns but not by outcomes of multi-step reasoning synthesis.
- **Mechanism:** Models successfully retrieve and assemble fragmented harmful information but do not apply refusal after reconstruction; the safety trigger appears tied to explicit pattern matching.
- **Core assumption:** Safety training primarily detects explicit harmful content, not harm that emerges only through composition.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: “models often reconstructed harmful intent but failed to refuse.”
  - Section 3.2: Direct retrieval safety 95–100% vs. reasoning types averaging 40–60%.
  - Corpus: Neighbors address long-context vulnerabilities and reasoning hijacking (e.g., “Chain-of-thought hijacking”).
- **Break condition:** If models consistently refused across all reasoning types regardless of fragment distribution.

### Mechanism 2: Extended context length degrades safety independent of retrieval capability.
- **Claim:** Longer contexts dilute safety signal processing or require deeper sustained reasoning to recognize synthesized harm.
- **Mechanism:** Models maintain retrieval success but fail post-retrieval safety judgment as context grows.
- **Core assumption:** Safety mechanisms have limited resource allocation that becomes insufficient as reasoning chains lengthen.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: “safety alignment consistently degrades as context length increases.”
  - Section 3.2: Safety drops from 55% (0k) to 42% (64k) averaged.
  - Section 3.4.2: Needle position analysis shows safety invariant across start/middle/end, ruling out retrieval failure.
- **Break condition:** If safety remained stable or improved with increased context length.

### Mechanism 3: Inference‑time reasoning effort unlocks latent safety capability.
- **Claim:** Models possess the ability to recognize harmful synthesized intent but default inference settings allocate insufficient compute to safety-relevant judgment.
- **Mechanism:** Increased reasoning effort enables more thorough harm evaluation; safety reasoning competes with task-solving for limited resources.
- **Core assumption:** More compute at inference time can activate safety checks that are otherwise bypassed.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: “increasing inference‑time reasoning effort reduces attack success by over 50 percentage points.”
  - Section 3.3.1: GPT‑oss‑120b safety improves from 72% (Low) to 91% (Medium) to 92% (High) at 0k; gap widens to +51 pp at 64k.
  - Section 3.3.2: Multi‑hop tasks show largest relative gains (+277% Low→High).
- **Break condition:** If increased reasoning effort produced no safety improvement across models.

## Foundational Learning

- **Compositional Reasoning Threat Model**  
  - *Why needed:* Defines the paper’s novel attack paradigm where harm is implicit and distributed, contrasting with traditional explicit jailbreaking.  
  - *Quick check:* Why can’t existing safety benchmarks that test explicit harmful prompts detect vulnerabilities described in this paper?

- **Safety‑Reasoning Orthogonality**  
  - *Why needed:* The core finding that general reasoning strength does not correlate with safety alignment challenges the assumption that more capable models are automatically safer.  
  - *Quick check:* What does the variation among models (e.g., Gemini vs. Claude) with similar reasoning capabilities tell us about safety alignment being orthogonal?

- **Inference‑Time Compute as Safety Lever**  
  - *Why needed:* Identifies a practical mitigation path and reveals that safety capability exists but requires explicit activation, impacting deployment tradeoffs.  
  - *Quick check:* Why does increased reasoning effort improve safety more for complex reasoning tasks, and what does this imply about resource allocation?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map (plain text):**  
Threat Generator -> Context Builder (incorporating Neutral Query Template) -> Response Generator -> Safety Evaluator -> Analysis Module

**Critical path:**  
1. Input: Harmful query from AdvBench  
2. Decompose into fragments per reasoning type (via Threat Generator)  
3. Build long‑context example with haystack (Context Builder)  
4. Generate model response with specified reasoning effort (Response Generator)  
5. Evaluate response safety (Safety Evaluator)  
6. Aggregate results by model/length/type (Analysis Module)  
7. Identify patterns and test interventions

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Automated evaluation enables large‑scale testing but may miss subtle safety failures.  
- Single neutral query template controls prompt variance but may not optimally engage all reasoning types.  
- Conservative safety definition (only score = 5 = unsafe) prioritizes precision over recall.  
- Three context lengths balance coverage with computational feasibility.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. High retrieval safety (>90%) with low reasoning safety (<50%) → safety tied to explicit harm detection.  
2. Monotonic safety decline with context length → insufficient long‑context safety mechanisms.  
3. Reconstructed harmful output without refusal → complete safety bypass post‑synthesis.  
4. Large safety gap between Low and High reasoning effort → default under‑reasoning for safety.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Establish baseline: Direct retrieval safety across all models and context lengths.  
2. Main evaluation: All models across 4 reasoning types × 3 context lengths to map safety‑reasoning and safety‑context relationships.  
3. Mitigation test: Vary inference‑time reasoning effort on GPT‑oss‑120b to demonstrate latent capability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **What architectural or training differences allow some models to achieve low sensitivity to both decomposition and long‑context effects?**  
  *Basis:* Figure 1 shows no model achieves both low decomposition sensitivity and stable long‑context safety; clusters exist (e.g., GPT/Claude vs. Gemini/DeepSeek) but reasons are unknown.  
  *Why unresolved:* Only black‑box models evaluated; no access to training data, architectures, or alignment methods.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Comparative ablations varying model size, safety fine‑tuning (RLHF vs. DPO), data mixtures; white‑box analysis of internal representations during fragment reconstruction; training small models with controlled interventions.

- **Does increasing inference‑time reasoning effort yield safety improvements across all frontier model families?**  
  *Basis:* The >50 pp reduction was demonstrated only on GPT‑oss‑120b.  
  *Why unresolved:* No replication on other 13 models.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Repeat reasoning effort manipulation (longer CoT, self‑reflection, sampling parameters) across diverse models with and without native reasoning modes; measure safety ratios to test universality.

- **What is the cognitive mechanism by which task‑relevant surrounding context improves safety?**  
  *Basis:* Section 3.4.1 finds relevant context yields higher safety than irrelevant (PG19), gap widens with context length; authors suggest “contextual clarity” vs. “camouflage effect” but do not distinguish.  
  *Why unresolved:* No measurement of attention, fragment salience, or reconstruction coherence.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Control experiments quantifying lexical/semantic overlap between fragments and surrounding text; record attention weights or activations; assess self‑consistency of reconstructions; manipulate relevance independently with adversarial distractors.

- **When models reconstruct harmful intent but fail to refuse, is the failure due to absence of safety‑related representations or suppression during generation?**  
  *Basis:* Paper states failures arise after successful retrieval; safety capability exists but not consistently engaged.  
  *Why unresolved:* Analysis only of final outputs, not intermediate states.  
  *Resolution evidence:* Probe hidden activations with linear classifiers for “harmfulness”; compare refusal‑token logit distributions; analyze CoT traces for safety considerations; interventions forcing pre‑generation safety reflection.

## Limitations

- Decomposition prompts (few‑shot examples) are undocumented, risking invalid fragment generation.
- Haystack tokenization method is unspecified, potentially causing mismatched context lengths across models.
- Exact parameterization of “inference‑time reasoning effort” for GPT‑oss‑120b is undisclosed, blocking replication.
- Safety evaluation relies solely on Gemini‑2.0‑flash without human validation, introducing scorer bias.
- Conservative safety threshold (only score = 5 = unsafe) may overlook borderline failures.

## Confidence

- **Stronger general reasoning does not consistently yield safer outputs under compositional attacks:** Medium confidence
- **Safety alignment degrades as context length grows:** High confidence
- **Increasing inference‑time reasoning effort can reduce attack success by over 50 pp:** Medium confidence

## Next Checks

- **Decomposition fidelity check:** For a stratified sample, verify that fragments from each reasoning type fully reconstruct the original harmful intent when composed, using human annotators or a consensus LLM judge.
- **Tokenization standardization:** Rebuild haystacks using each target model’s tokenizer to enforce exact token counts (±1), then re‑evaluate safety ratios to confirm monotonic decline with context length.
- **Reasoning effort replication:** Disclose the precise parameters controlling reasoning effort for GPT‑oss‑120b (e.g., max_new_tokens, temperature, or “thinking” budget); reproduce the full evaluation across all reasoning types and context lengths to confirm the magnitude and consistency of the safety improvement.
