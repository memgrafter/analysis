# From Shallow to Deep: Pinning Semantic Intent via Causal GRPO

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.02675
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02675
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: Two-Stage Causal-GRPO (TSC-GRPO) reduces Attack Success Rate (ASR) on AdvBench to near 0% across multiple jailbreak methods while preserving utility on reasoning and coding benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) are vulnerable to adversarial prefix attacks (e.g., “Sure, here is”) because internal malicious intent representations decay during generation—a phenomenon termed Semantic Representation Decay. This work introduces TSC-GRPO, a two-stage framework to pin semantic intent. Stage 1 trains a causal intent probe using hard-negative augmentation to disentangle invariant malicious intent from stylistic variations. Stage 2 employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a cumulative causal penalty; by forcing harmful prefixes, the model learns that accumulating harmful tokens monotonically decreases reward, enabling robust late-stage refusals. Experiments on AdvBench show drastic ASR reduction (often to 0%) versus strong baselines, with maintained performance on GSM8K, HumanEval, MBPP, and TruthfulQA, and resistance to fine‑tuning attacks.

## Method Summary
TSC‑GRPO comprises two stages. **Stage 1 (Probe Training)**: For a frozen LLM, extract final‑layer hidden states for multiple stylistic views of each query (e.g., raw, with compliance prefix “Sure”, with adversarial suffix, partial harmful response). Train a lightweight probe `g_ϕ` with a contrastive loss (`L_align`) to pull together embeddings of the same intent across styles, and a uniformity loss (`L_un`) to spread different intents on the hypersphere. This provably recovers a style‑invariant malicious intent vector `c` under independence (`c ⊥ s`) and connectivity assumptions. **Stage 2 (Causal‑GRPO)**: Freeze `g_ϕ`. For each harmful query, compute a static malicious intent anchor `z_anchor` from its raw views. During reinforcement learning, force a prefix from the adversarial views (“fork‑in‑the‑road”), sample a group of continuations, and compute reward as `R_total = R_general + α * R_causal`. `R_causal` cumulatively penalizes token‑wise cosine similarity between `g_ϕ`’s representation and `z_anchor` above a threshold `τ`. GRPO’s group‑relative advantages encourage the policy to avoid harmful token accumulation. Safe queries are optimized with standard GRPO without `R_causal`.

## Key Results
- TSC‑GRPO reduces ASR on AdvBench to 0% for GCG, AutoDAN, and other jailbreak attacks, significantly outperforming baselines like PSR and Constrained SFT.
- Utility benchmarks (GSM8K, HumanEval, MBPP, TruthfulQA) show minimal to no degradation.
- The approach remains effective under fine‑tuning attacks that attempt to remove safety alignment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Semantic Representation Decay
- **Claim**: Shallow Safety Alignment fails because internal malicious intent representations decay during auto‑regressive generation when faced with adversarial prefixes.
- **Mechanism**: The model’s hidden state evolves as `h_t = f(c, s_t)`, where `c` is invariant malicious intent and `s_t` is the time‑varying style/prefix. A compliant prefix (e.g., “Sure”) causes `s_t` to dominate, collapsing `h_t` into a representation indistinguishable from safe intents. PCA and linear probe experiments show clear separation at `t=0` that collapses after prefix injection, with probe accuracy dropping to chance.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract identifies “Semantic Representation Decay”; Section 4.1 presents PCA/probe results; related work (e.g., “Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep”) discusses shallow alignment.
- **Break condition**: If intent representation does not decay (probe accuracy remains high throughout), the diagnosis is invalid.

### Mechanism 2: Causal Intent Probe
- **Claim**: A probe trained with contrastive learning and uniformity disentangles invariant intent `c` from style `s`.
- **Mechanism**: Train `g_ϕ` on frozen LLM hidden states with loss `L = L_align + λL_un`. `L_align` aligns representations of the same intent under different styles; `L_un` (KoLeo estimator) spreads different intents uniformly on the hypersphere. Under independence (`c ⊥ s`) and connectivity, this provably recovers `c` up to an invertible transform.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions “grounding in causal identifiability” and “hard‑negative augmentation”; Section 5.1 details the 2×2 view matrix for harmful/safe queries.
- **Break condition**: If `L_align` cannot align same‑intent representations (e.g., due to brittle frozen representations), the probe fails.

### Mechanism 3: Causal‑GRPO
- **Claim**: A cumulative causal penalty within “fork‑in‑the‑road” GRPO forces robust late‑stage refusals.
- **Mechanism**: For harmful queries, force a prefix from Stage 1’s adversarial views, sample group continuations, and compute `R_causal(y) = -Σ_t max(0, cos(g_ϕ(s_t), z_anchor) - τ)`. This penalizes token‑wise semantic similarity to the malicious anchor. Combined with `R_general` and GRPO’s group‑relative advantages, the policy learns that harmful continuations yield lower relative reward than refusals.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract notes “cumulative causal penalty within fork‑in‑the‑road training”; Section 5.2 defines `R_causal` and group construction.
- **Break condition**: If `g_ϕ` is inaccurate (false positives/negatives) or groups lack clear harmful/refusal splits, the gradient signal becomes ineffective.

## Foundational Learning
- **Causal Representation Learning / Identifiability**  
  *Why needed*: The framework hinges on provably extracting an invariant latent `c` from mixed observations. The assumptions (independence `c ⊥ s` and connectivity) are central to the probe’s theoretical guarantee.  
  *Quick check*: How is independence enforced in data construction? (Answer: Hard‑negative augmentation pairs each harmful query with both raw and compliance‑prefix views, artificially breaking any natural `c‑s` correlation.)
- **Contrastive Learning with Invariance**  
  *Why needed*: Stage 1 uses a contrastive loss to achieve style invariance by treating different augmentations of the same intent as positives.  
  *Quick check*: If the augmentation set for harmful queries omitted the “Sure” prefix view, what would happen? (Answer: The probe might not learn to ignore compliance prefixes, leaving a vulnerability to such prefixes.)
- **Reinforcement Learning with Group‑Relative Advantages (GRPO)**  
  *Why needed*: Stage 2 updates the policy using group‑relative advantages within continuations from the same forced prefix, enabling a direct comparison between harmful and refusal paths.  
  *Quick check*: Why compute advantages relative to the group instead of a global baseline? (Answer: The forced prefix creates a common context; group‑relative advantages isolate the trade‑off between continuing harm and refusing, independent of absolute reward scaling.)

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (plain text):
Data Augmentation Pipeline → Causal Intent Probe (`g_ϕ`) training → Anchor Computation → Main Loop: For harmful queries: Sample Forced Prefix → Generate Group of continuations → Compute `R_causal` (using frozen `g_ϕ` and `z_anchor`) and `R_general` → `R_total` → GRPO Update. Safe queries: standard GRPO without forced prefix.

Critical path:
1. Create augmented dataset (harmful: raw, +compliance prefix, +adversarial suffix, +partial harmful response; safe: raw, +partial safe response).
2. Train probe `g_ϕ` on frozen LLM hidden states with contrastive + uniformity losses.
3. For each harmful query, compute `z_anchor` as mean `g_ϕ` over raw query views.
4. Initialize policy `π_θ` from base model.
5. Alternate between harmful queries (with forced prefix and `R_causal`) and safe queries (standard GRPO) to update policy.

Design tradeoffs:
- **Probe complexity**: Deeper probe may better disentangle nonlinear mixtures but risks overfitting to training styles; linear probe may fail if `f(c,s)` is highly nonlinear.
- **Cumulative penalty parameters**: `α` balances `R_causal` vs. `R_general`; too high causes over‑refusal (utility loss), too low fails to suppress harm. `τ` sets similarity threshold sensitivity.
- **Group size `G`**: Larger groups stabilize advantage estimates but increase compute; too small yields noisy gradients.

Failure signatures:
- Probe fails: Linear classifier accuracy on validation set (different styles of same intent) remains low (<90%).
- Training instability: GRPO loss diverges or KL penalty spikes.
- Evaluation failure: ASR on AdvBench does not drop significantly; utility metrics drop >5 points.
- Inadequate group diversity: Forced‑prefix groups contain only harmful continuations, providing no refusal contrast.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Probe Invariance Test**: Train `g_ϕ` on frozen LLM. For held‑out queries, compute `||g_ϕ(h_raw) - g_ϕ(h_+Sure)||₂` for same intent (should be small) and compare to inter‑intent distances (should be large).
2. **Causal Reward Sanity Check**: For a fixed forced prefix (`q + "Sure"`), generate one harmful continuation and one refusal. Compute accumulated `R_causal`; harmful continuation should have a significantly more negative penalty (e.g., ΔR_causal < -2.0).
3. **Toy GRPO Efficacy**: Apply full TSC‑GRPO on 50 harmful queries for 10 steps. Measure ASR on held‑out 10 queries with “Sure” prefix; expect drop from >80% to ~0% with acceptable refusal coherence.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
Open Question 1:
- **Question**: Does the enforced independence between latent intent (c) and style (s) in Stage 1’s data construction create a distributional shift that limits the model’s ability to handle novel attack styles that naturally correlate with harmful intent in deployment?
- **Basis**: [explicit] The independence assumption (`c ⊥ s`) is a core theoretical requirement, enforced via a hard‑coded 2×2 contrastive matrix (each harmful query paired with raw and compliance‑prefix views).
- **Why unresolved**: The paper evaluates robustness on known attack families (GCG, AutoDAN) but not on entirely novel jailbreak patterns that may re‑introduce natural `c‑s` correlations.
- **Resolving evidence**: Test TSC‑GRPO on a hold‑out set of novel jailbreak attacks and measure whether the probe’s invariance property degrades for unseen style variations.

Open Question 2:
- **Question**: How do errors or limitations in the frozen causal intent probe affect the stability and final performance of the GRPO policy, given that `R_causal` directly derives from it?
- **Basis**: [inferred] Stage 2 uses a frozen pre‑trained probe as a “semantic compass”; the paper provides no analysis of probe error sensitivity.
- **Why unresolved**: Probe misclassifications (false negatives on emerging harm or false positives on safe transitions) could yield a noisy reward signal, leading to suboptimal policy updates, reward hacking, or unnecessary refusals.
- **Resolving evidence**: Ablations where controlled noise/bias is injected into the probe’s output during Stage 2, measuring resultant changes in ASR and utility. A formal robustness analysis of the GRPO objective to perturbations in `R_causal` would clarify sensitivity.

Open Question 3:
- **Question**: To what extent does the “fork‑in‑the‑road” training paradigm generalize to attacks that induce gradual semantic drift without a single, clear decision point?
- **Basis**: [explicit] Stage 2 constructs “fork‑in‑the‑road” scenarios by initializing with forced harmful prefixes; the cumulative penalty is designed for monotonic token‑by‑token harm accumulation.
- **Why unresolved**: Real‑world jailbreaks (e.g., multi‑turn persuasion, narrative‑based) may shift semantics gradually over many tokens. Evaluation focuses on prefix/suffix benchmarks; performance on continuous‑trajectory attacks is undiscussed.
- **Resolving evidence**: Evaluate TSC‑GRPO against multi‑turn or narrative‑based jailbreaks and analyze probe trajectories to see if intent remains pinned across continuous stylistic shifts.

Open Question 4:
- **Question**: What are the computational and data efficiency trade‑offs of the two‑stage pipeline versus end‑to‑end RL, and does the disentanglement in Stage 1 limit the policy’s adaptability to new domains?
- **Basis**: [inferred] TSC‑GRPO requires pre‑training a separate probe and running GRPO with a composite reward; the paper reports final ASR/utility but not training time, memory overhead, or sample efficiency.
- **Why unresolved**: Two‑stage separation may improve interpretability but could be less efficient than unified approaches. The probe’s uniformity objective might lack granularity for specialized domains (e.g., medical, legal), possibly requiring costly retraining.
- **Resolving evidence**: Compare end‑to‑end GRPO with an integrated causal regularizer against TSC‑GRPO in convergence speed, final performance, and transfer to out‑of‑distribution safety benchmarks. Analyze probe representation quality (e.g., linear evaluation on new intent categories) to see if uniformity harms selectivity.

## Limitations
- The specific hidden layer(s) used for `h` in Stage 1 are unspecified (assumed final layer before LM head).
- Probe architecture details (depth, projection dimension) and hyperparameters (`λ`, training epochs) are not provided.
- The scaling and normalization of `R_general` relative to `R_causal` are unclear.
- The method for computing `z_anchor` (e.g., stable averaging over raw query views) is not detailed.
- Reliance on hard‑negative augmentation quality may limit adaptability to unseen attack styles.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Semantic Representation Decay): Medium (internal evidence strong but external validation sparse).
- Mechanism 2 (Causal Intent Probe): High (grounded in identifiability theory; augmentation design explicit and testable).
- Mechanism 3 (Causal‑GRPO): Medium (coherent but hinges entirely on probe accuracy and group construction fidelity).
