# A Unified Revisit of Temperature in Classification-Based Knowledge Distillation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.02430
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02430
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Systematic study of temperature in KD reveals optimizer-dependent robustness and dataset-specific optimal ranges, reducing need for grid search.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the temperature hyperparameter in knowledge distillation (KD), which scales teacher logits before softmax but lacks clear selection guidelines. Through controlled experiments across optimizers, training schedules, teacher finetuning, student initialization, and dataset characteristics, it finds that AdamW is more robust to temperature than SGD, large temperatures (τ≥10) excel with long training and well-pretrained teachers, fine-grained datasets with pretraining overlap benefit from higher temperatures, and excessive teacher finetuning harms distillation. These findings provide actionable recommendations to minimize costly temperature tuning in KD practice.

## Method Summary
The study uses standard KD with a fixed temperature τ in the KL-divergence loss between teacher and student softmax outputs. Experiments vary τ extensively (1–40) while manipulating key factors: optimizer (AdamW vs. SGD), training duration (e.g., 50 vs. 1024 epochs), teacher finetuning epochs on target datasets, student architectures (ResNet18, MobileNetV4), and datasets (Pets, CIFAR100, Cars). Teachers are ImageNet-1K pretrained (ResNet50, ViT-S) and finetuned with label smoothing. Students are trained with strong augmentation (RandAugment, MixUp/CutMix) and per-layer gradient clipping, evaluated by top-1 test accuracy. Teacher output entropy metrics probe relational knowledge retention.

## Key Results
- AdamW demonstrates significantly greater temperature robustness compared to SGD.
- Large temperatures (τ≥10) achieve strong performance when combined with long training schedules and teachers with robust general pretraining.
- Fine-grained datasets with class overlap in the teacher's pretraining corpus favor larger temperatures.
- Excessive finetuning of the teacher on the target dataset diminishes distillation benefits and shifts optimal temperature lower.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Optimizer-Specific Temperature Sensitivity
The choice of optimizer fundamentally alters the effective temperature range for optimal KD due to differences in how optimization dynamics interact with softened logits. AdamW's decoupled weight decay and adaptive per-parameter learning rates stabilize training in the flatter loss valleys induced by high-temperature softening, making performance less sensitive to exact τ values. SGD, with uniform learning rates and stronger implicit regularization from momentum, requires a more precise temperature match to the training stage: early epochs benefit from smaller τ to maintain learning signal strength, while later epochs can exploit larger τ as the student matures. This stems from the implicit bias of optimizers interacting with the entropy of the teacher's distribution. Evidence shows AdamW's accuracy remains stable across τ, while SGD exhibits a clear crossover where small τ wins with short training but large τ outperforms with long training. The break condition is that with SGD and a short fixed budget (e.g., <100 epochs on CIFAR100), τ≥10 consistently underperforms relative to τ∈[1,5].

### Mechanism 2: Teacher's Relational Knowledge Retention
The optimal temperature correlates with the amount of general, relational knowledge retained by the teacher relative to its target-specific expertise. High τ exaggerates small differences between class probabilities, which only yields a useful signal if those differences encode semantic relationships (e.g., "cat" closer to "dog" than to "car") present in robustly pretrained models. Excessive target finetuning erodes this general structure, making teacher outputs converge toward one-hot vectors; then, high τ amplifies noise and lower τ (closer to standard cross-entropy) is superior. The "dark knowledge" transferred by KD is the relative probability structure across non-target classes, and its utility depends on informational content. Evidence includes entropy measures decreasing (sharper) for the top class and increasing (more uniform) for non-max classes as finetuning progresses, correlating with τ=1 becoming competitive. The break condition is that if the teacher is trained from random initialization or finetuned excessively (>200 epochs on studied datasets), the benefit of τ≥10 disappears, and τ≤4 is often optimal.

### Mechanism 3: Dataset Granularity & Class Hierarchy Alignment
The optimal temperature is inversely related to dataset granularity only if the dataset's class hierarchy aligns with the taxonomy seen during teacher pretraining. Fine-grained datasets with many sub-classes under common superordinates (e.g., dog breeds) benefit from larger τ to expose subtle relationships, but this fails if fine-grained classes are novel relative to pretraining. Larger τ flattens the distribution, forcing the student to learn from a broader set of low-probability classes, which is beneficial when the teacher's pretraining captured a rich, multi-level hierarchy. If classes are absent from pretraining (e.g., specific car models), the teacher lacks coherent relational structure, and high τ amplifies noise. Evidence contrasts Pets (ImageNet-1K overlap) showing upward τ trend with Cars (no overlap) showing negative or flat trend. The break condition is that for fine-grained datasets without pretraining overlap, τ≥10 harms performance relative to τ∈[1,5].

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Knowledge Distillation (KD) Output Matching
**Why needed here**: This paper directly studies the standard KL-divergence loss between teacher and student softmax outputs, scaled by a shared temperature τ. Understanding this base formulation is prerequisite to grasping how τ modulates the "softness" of the target and what "dark knowledge" is being transferred.
**Quick check**: What happens to the teacher's softmax distribution (e.g., on a correct but not top-1 class) when τ is increased from 1 to 10? How does this change the gradient signal for the student?

### Concept: Optimizer Dynamics (AdamW vs. SGD)
**Why needed here**: The central finding depends on the starkly different temperature robustness between AdamW and SGD, requiring understanding how adaptive vs. non-adaptive optimizers navigate loss curvature and how their implicit biases interact with the softened loss landscape.
**Quick check**: Why might AdamW's per-parameter learning rates make it less sensitive to the exact scale of the logits (controlled by τ) than SGD?

### Concept: Dataset Granularity & Semantic Hierarchy
**Why needed here**: The paper's dataset-based recommendation hinges on distinguishing coarse-grained (few, broad classes) from fine-grained (many, specific sub-classes) datasets and assessing their overlap with the teacher's pretraining set, requiring a mental model of how class taxonomies affect inter-class similarity.
**Quick check**: For a fine-grained dataset like "bird species," why would a larger temperature help the student learn subtle differences between similar species compared to a coarse-grained dataset like "vehicle types"?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Teacher Network -> Student Network -> Distillation Core -> Training Pipeline -> Evaluation

### Critical Path
1. **Teacher Preparation**: Select a teacher with appropriate general pretraining (ImageNet-1K preferred) and minimal target finetuning (≤50 epochs on experimental datasets).
2. **Student Baseline**: Initialize student (random or general pretrained if available).
3. **Temperature Sweep**: Run a small-scale, short-duration KD training (e.g., 50-100 epochs) across τ ∈ {1, 3, 5, 10, 20} to get a preliminary signal.
4. **Full Training with Informed τ**: Based on sweep and teacher/dataset factors, select a τ range. For fine-grained datasets with pretraining overlap and long training (>500 epochs), prioritize τ≥10. For coarse datasets or over-finetuned teachers, prioritize τ≤5.
5. **Optimizer Consideration**: If using SGD, plan for a longer training schedule to allow larger τ to converge; if using AdamW, τ choice is more forgiving.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **τ vs. Training Duration**: High τ requires significantly more epochs to realize benefits (patient distillation). A short budget forces low τ.
- **τ vs. Teacher "Freshness"**: More general pretraining → higher τ viable. More target-specific finetuning → lower τ required. This is a direct tradeoff.
- **Simplicity vs. Performance**: Shared fixed τ is simple and effective. Dynamic/adaptive τ schemes add complexity with unclear benefit over simply choosing a large fixed τ with long training + good teacher.

### Failure Signatures
- Student accuracy plateaus or degrades when using τ≥10 on a coarse-grained dataset (e.g., CIFAR100, Tiny ImageNet) or with a teacher finetuned >200 epochs.
- Huge variance in student accuracy across seeds for a given τ when using SGD with short training—indicates instability from too-soft targets.
- Student's final softmax distribution becomes nearly uniform at τ≥10, but accuracy is low; this suggests the teacher's relational knowledge is poor (no overlap, excessive finetuning) and large τ is amplifying noise.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Optimizer-Temperature Interaction**: Train a ResNet18 student from a ResNet50 teacher on CIFAR100 for 50 and 400 epochs using AdamW and SGD. Plot accuracy vs. τ for both durations. Expect: SGD shows clear crossover (low τ wins at 50 epochs, high τ at 400), AdamW curve is flat.
2. **Teacher Finetuning Duration**: Take an ImageNet-1K pretrained ResNet50. Finetune it on Pets for {20, 50, 200, 400} epochs. For each, distill to a ResNet18 with τ∈[1,40] for 1024 epochs using AdamW. Measure student accuracy and teacher's output entropy (ˆH(¯p)). Expect: longer finetuning → lower student best accuracy and best τ shifts down.
3. **Dataset Granularity & Pretraining Overlap**: Compare two fine-grained datasets: (a) Pets (classes exist in ImageNet-1K) and (b) Cars (classes not in ImageNet-1K). Use a standard ImageNet-1K pretrained ViT-S teacher. Distill to ResNet18 with long training (1024 epochs, AdamW). Plot accuracy vs. τ. Expect: Pets shows upward trend, Cars shows flat or downward trend.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why do CLIP-pretrained teachers differ in temperature behavior compared to ImageNet-pretrained ones? The paper notes this exception but does not explore the cause.
2. What explains AdamW's greater robustness to temperature compared to SGD? The paper shows empirical patterns but provides no mechanistic explanation.
3. Can a continuous metric of teacher-target dataset similarity predict the optimal temperature, reducing the need for grid search? Current evidence is anecdotal and based on binary overlap.
4. Does a dynamic temperature schedule that evolves during training combine the early benefits of small τ (stability) and late benefits of large τ (rich relationships), leading to improved student performance? Fixed τ forces a compromise; schedules might harvest both regimes.

## Limitations
- Core assumptions (optimizer bias, teacher relational knowledge, dataset hierarchy alignment) rely on correlational rather than causal evidence.
- Experiments use a narrow set of architectures (ResNet, ViT) and datasets (Pets, CIFAR100, Cars); applicability to other domains or KD variants is unclear.
- Critical implementation details are underspecified (e.g., half-period cosine LR schedule, teacher finetuning LR scheduler), and extreme training durations (1024 epochs) may be impractical, potentially altering temperature trends.

## Confidence
- Optimizer–temperature sensitivity: Medium
- Teacher relational knowledge retention: Medium-Low
- Dataset granularity & pretraining overlap: Medium
