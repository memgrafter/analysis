# Improving Medical Visual Reinforcement Fine-Tuning via Perception and Reasoning Augmentation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.10619  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10619  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: VRFT-Aug achieves 60.93% average accuracy on MedMNIST (256‑shot), 3.77% above standard V‑RFT and 14.83% above supervised fine‑tuning.

## Executive Summary
Medical visual reinforcement fine‑tuning (V‑RFT) is hampered by sparse rewards and insufficient domain‑specific perception and reasoning. This paper introduces VRFT‑Aug, a set of four augmentations applied to the GRPO algorithm: (1) injecting visually descriptive attributes into prompts, (2) pre‑training the policy on a localization task to embed spatial priors, (3) penalizing recitation of prompt knowledge in reasoning traces, and (4) using a multi‑grade fuzzy reward for ordinal classification. Experiments on MedMNIST, HAM10000, Heel, and disease‑grading datasets show substantial improvements, demonstrating that enhancing perception and reward design stabilizes and boosts medical V‑RFT performance.

## Method Summary
VRFT‑Aug builds on GRPO applied to the Qwen2.5‑VL‑3B‑Instruct model. The four augmentations are: Prompt Augmentation (PAP), where prompts are enriched with visual attributes generated by GPT‑4o and manually validated; Perception Augmentation (PAπ), which pre‑trains the policy on bounding‑box prediction before zero‑shot classification; Recitation Reward (R_recite), adding a BLEU‑based penalty (δ < 0) to discourage output that merely echoes the prompt; and Multi‑Grade Fuzzy Reward (R_MFRS), which awards fractional rewards based on grade proximity for ordinal tasks (e.g., retina disease severity). Training uses GRPO with a KL penalty (β = 0.04), temperature 0.9, and learning rate 1e‑6. Evaluation reports classification accuracy and IoU for detection.

## Key Results
- On MedMNIST (256‑shot), VRFT‑Aug reaches 60.93% average accuracy, improving by 3.77% over standard V‑RFT and 14.83% over supervised fine‑tuning.  
- Adding localization pre‑training (PAπ) yields an absolute gain of 35.30% on HAM10000.  
- For ordinal disease grading (RetinaMNIST, COVID‑19), the fuzzy reward raises average performance to 45.16% versus 33.84% with exact‑match reward, an 11.32% improvement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Perception Augmentation via Knowledge‑Enhanced Prompts and Localization Pre‑training
- **Claim**: Enriching the model’s visual perception through explicit prompt‑based knowledge injection and implicit spatial localization pre‑training reduces exploration burden and improves sample efficiency.  
- **Mechanism**: A richer prompt `P̂` (e.g., “irregular shape, spiculated margins”) brings the initial policy closer to the optimal policy, lowering KL divergence. Localization pre‑training on bounding boxes (`π_loc_θ`) injects anatomical spatial knowledge, focusing attention on clinically relevant regions and filtering out irrelevant visual noise.  
- **Core assumption**: The bottleneck in medical image recognition is insufficient domain‑specific visual perception rather than reasoning over already‑perceived features; enhancing perception directly eases the reasoning load.  
- **Evidence**: The paper reports that V‑RFT+PAP and PAπ drive the largest gains, especially a +35.30% improvement on HAM10000 with PAπ (Fig. 3c).  

### Mechanism 2: Recitation Reward to Discourage Superficial Pattern Matching
- **Claim**: Penalizing the recitation of prompt‑provided knowledge during reasoning (negative `R_recite`) mitigates over‑reliance on superficial copying and promotes deeper visual reasoning.  
- **Mechanism**: `R_recite = δ × BLEU(O_i, P̂)` with δ < 0 assigns a negative reward when the model’s reasoning trace highly overlaps the prompt. This forces the policy to generate distinct traces that rely on actual visual processing instead of parroting the prompt.  
- **Core assumption**: V‑RFT models can “game” the reward by mimicking prompt text; explicit penalization prevents this shortcut.  
- **Evidence**: Table 2 and Fig. 4 show that negative `R_recite` (δ = −2.0) yields higher average accuracy (62.44%) than positive δ (57.86%).  

### Mechanism 3: Multi‑Grade Fuzzy Reward for Ordinal Classification
- **Claim**: A fuzzy reward scheme (`R_MFRS`) alleviates the sparse reward problem by providing graded feedback for near‑correct predictions, stabilizing early‑stage RL exploration.  
- **Mechanism**: For ordinal labels (0‑6), exact‑match reward is binary; `R_MFRS` assigns 1.0 for exact, 0.25 for grade difference = 1, 0.0625 for difference = 2, and 0 otherwise. This denser signal encourages the model to learn subtle relationships between adjacent grades.  
- **Core assumption**: Fine‑grained grading requires learning a continuous spectrum; binary rewards fail to guide the policy toward partially correct but promising directions.  
- **Evidence**: On RetinaMNIST and COVID‑19, `R_MFRS` lifts average accuracy to 45.16% from 33.84% with exact‑match reward (Table 3).

## Foundational Learning
- **GRPO Algorithm & Rule‑Based Rewards**  
  *Why needed*: VRFT‑Aug uses GRPO as its base V‑RFT algorithm; understanding the KL penalty and group‑based advantage estimation is essential for interpreting the optimization objective (Eq. 1).  
  *Quick check*: How does the KL divergence term prevent the policy from forgetting pre‑trained knowledge during RL fine‑tuning?  
- **Large Vision‑Language Model (LVLM) Architecture & Conditioning**  
  *Why needed*: The paper modifies the prompt and policy; grasping that an LVLM’s output distribution `π_θ(O|P, I)` is conditioned on both text and image embeddings is crucial.  
  *Quick check*: In Qwen2.5‑VL, what are the two primary input streams to the language model head, and how does augmenting `P` affect computation?  
- **Reward Shaping in Sparse Reward Settings**  
  *Why needed*: `R_recite` and `R_MFRS` are reward‑shaping techniques; understanding their purpose and risks (e.g., reward hacking) is key.  
  *Quick check*: What is the risk of using a denser reward like `R_MFRS` that gives partial credit for incorrect but “close” ordinal predictions?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Input Pipeline (medical image + prompt `P`) → Augmentation Layer (PAP: `P`→`P̂`; PAπ: `π_θ`→`π_loc_θ`) → Reasoning & Generation (LVLM produces `<think>` and `<answer>`) → Reward Engine (computes `R_accuracy`/`R_IoU`, `R_format`, plus `R_recite` or `R_MFRS`) → Optimizer (GRPO update with KL penalty).  
- **Critical path**:  
  1. Prepare knowledge‑enhanced prompts (`P̂`) via LLM generation and manual validation.  
  2. Implement localization pre‑training (PAπ) on bounding‑box data; train `π_loc_θ`.  
  3. Integrate and freeze `π_loc_θ` weights for the main classification task.  
  4. Configure reward: use `R_MFRS` for ordinal tasks, `R_accuracy` otherwise; set `R_recite` with δ = −2.0.  
  5. Run GRPO training with combined reward.  
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - *Cost vs. gain*: PAπ requires extra bounding‑box data and compute but yields large improvements (e.g., +35.30% on HAM10000); PAP is low‑cost but gives moderate gains.  
  - *Generality vs. specificity*: `R_MFRS` is effective only for ordinal tasks; `R_recite` with negative δ may harm models with limited reasoning capacity.  
  - *Knowledge source*: LLM‑generated attributes need manual clinical validation, adding human effort and potential bias.  
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - Reward hacking with `R_recite` if δ>0: model recites prompt in `<think>` but fails on `<answer>`.  
  - Collapse with `R_MFRS` if fuzzy weights are too generous: policy converges to predicting a central grade.  
  - Negative transfer from PAπ: if localization prior is misaligned (e.g., organ focus for soft‑tissue pathology), classification worsens.  
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Baseline Replication**: Reproduce standard V‑RFT on a small MedMNIST subset (e.g., 20‑shot PneumoniaMNIST) to establish a baseline.  
  2. **Isolate PAP**: Train with knowledge‑enhanced prompts only (no PAπ, no `R_MFRS`). Expect moderate gains (e.g., PneumoniaMNIST 10‑shot: 51.28% → 64.42%).  
  3. **Isolate `R_MFRS`**: On RetinaMNIST, compare V‑RFT+PAP with exact‑match vs. `R_MFRS`. Expect significant drop without fuzzy reward (paper: 60.25% vs. 43.50%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How to effectively perform RL post‑training on pretrained LVLMs to improve clinical utility and generalization?**  
   - *Basis*: Abstract explicitly states it is unclear.  
   - *Why unresolved*: VRFT‑Aug shows gains on selected datasets but lacks multi‑center robustness, sample‑efficiency studies, and real‑world clinical metrics (e.g., sensitivity/specificity).  
   - *Resolving evidence*: Multi‑institution evaluations across diverse devices, comprehensive clinical metrics, and comparisons with supervised fine‑tuning and other RL methods.  

2. **Can reinforcement learning effectively address tasks requiring a hybrid of perception and reasoning, such as medical image understanding?**  
   - *Basis*: Introduction poses this question.  
   - *Why unresolved*: VRFT‑Aug focuses on classification; more complex hybrid tasks (e.g., multi‑step VQA) remain untested.  
   - *Resolving evidence*: Apply VRFT‑Aug to medical VQA with explicit reasoning chains, evaluate chain‑of‑thought quality and final accuracy against non‑RL baselines.  

3. **How can VRFT‑Aug be extended to fine‑grained segmentation tasks in medical imaging?**  
   - *Basis*: Conclusion notes the limitation to classification.  
   - *Why unresolved*: Current framework outputs labels or bounding boxes; segmentation requires dense pixel‑level predictions and suitable rewards.  
   - *Resolving evidence*: Modify policy to generate segmentation masks, define rewards based on Dice/IoU, train on segmentation datasets (e.g., BRATS, ISIC), and compare to supervised segmentation models.  

4. **How can knowledge injection for medical V‑RFT incorporate clinically grounded experiential knowledge?**  
   - *Basis*: Conclusion highlights lack of experiential knowledge.  
   - *Why unresolved*: Injection uses only visual attributes and cross‑task priors, missing nuanced heuristics from clinicians.  
   - *Resolving evidence*: Build a knowledge base from expert interviews, integrate it as prompts or auxiliary rewards, and measure improvement on ambiguous cases via clinician validation.

## Limitations
- Exact visual attribute prompts for MedMNIST are undisclosed, hindering precise PAP replication.  
- Bounding‑box extraction from ROI masks lacks methodological details (thresholds, normalization), affecting PAπ reproducibility.  
- Evaluation is limited to a few datasets and a single base LVLM; broader applicability is unknown.

## Next Checks

2. **δ Sensitivity**: Train BloodMNIST with δ ∈ {−3, −2, −1, 0, +1} and plot accuracy vs. δ to confirm the optimal negative range and robustness.
