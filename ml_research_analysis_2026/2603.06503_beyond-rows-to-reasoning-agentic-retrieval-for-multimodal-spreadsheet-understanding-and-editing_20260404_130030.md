# Beyond Rows to Reasoning: Agentic Retrieval for Multimodal Spreadsheet Understanding and Editing

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.06503
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06503
*   **Reference Count:** 22
*   **Primary Result:** Introduces an iterative agentic framework that achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on spreadsheet benchmarks, with gains of +7 to +32 percentage points over prior work.

## Executive Summary
Standard multimodal spreadsheet understanding systems fail on complex enterprise workbooks due to reliance on single-pass retrieval, compression-induced data loss, and context window constraints. This paper presents "Beyond Rows to Reasoning" (BRTR), an agentic framework that replaces single-pass retrieval with iterative tool-calling loops and employs a planner-executor architecture for decomposing composite workflows. Evaluated across three benchmarks (FRTR-Bench, SpreadsheetLLM, FINCH), BRTR sets new accuracy records, validated by over 200 hours of expert human evaluation. The work identifies NVIDIA NeMo Retriever 1B as the top multimodal embedding model and demonstrates that full auditability is maintained through explicit tool-call traces.

## Method Summary
BRTR processes large, multimodal workbooks (text, tables, embedded images) by first chunking content into row, column, rectangular window, and image extracts. All chunks are embedded using NVIDIA NeMo Retriever 1B and indexed via hybrid dense (cosine) and lexical (BM25) retrieval with Reciprocal Rank Fusion. For a given query, an LLM with function-calling capability enters an iterative loop: it issues search tool calls (e.g., `search_rows`, `search_windows`), receives retrieved chunk metadata, and decides whether to refine the query, change search scope, or declare sufficient evidence (up to T=50 iterations). For complex workflows, a dedicated planner LLM analyzes the query and intermediate results to decompose the task into a dependency graph of 1-6 subtasks. These are dispatched in parallel to specialized executors (Search, Excel, I/O, Web, OCR, Validation). The system always prunes base64 image data from context to manage token limits, retaining only metadata.

## Key Results
*   Achieves state-of-the-art accuracy: +25 pp on FRTR-Bench (99% with GPT-5), +7 pp on SpreadsheetLLM (98%), and +32 pp on FINCH (95% with Claude Opus 4.6).
*   Ablation studies confirm substantial contributions from the iterative retrieval loop, the planner-executor decomposition, and the multimodal retrieval setup.
*   GPT-5.2 provides the best efficiency-accuracy trade-off among evaluated models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Iterative Tool-Calling Loop
**Claim:** Iterative tool-calling loops enable evidence gathering beyond a single retrieval pass, recovering from initial misses.
**Mechanism:** The LLM invokes structured search tools (e.g., `search_rows`, `search_columns`), inspects returned chunk metadata, and decides whether to refine the query, change content types, or apply coordinate filters. This repeats (up to T=50) until evidence is sufficient.
**Core Assumption:** Frontier LLMs can plan multi-step search strategies and interpret intermediate results to guide subsequent retrievals.
**Evidence Anchors:** The core innovation replaces single-pass retrieval with an iterative tool-calling loop. Weak direct corpus evidence; neighboring work (MC-Search, SheetAgent) explores agentic search but BRTR's specific tool-based iteration is novel.
**Break Condition:** Loop terminates if the model declares evidence sufficient; hard break at T=50 iterations.

### Mechanism 2: Planner-Executor Decomposition
**Claim:** A planner-executor architecture decomposes composite workflows into parallelizable subtask DAGs, mitigating error accumulation in long-horizon tasks.
**Mechanism:** A planner LLM call analyzes the user query and exploration results to produce a dependency graph of subtasks (1-6), each assigned to a specialized executor (Excel, IO, Web, Search, OCR, Validation). Independent subtasks execute concurrently.
**Core Assumption:** Complex spreadsheet workflows naturally decompose into semi-independent subtasks that can be solved by specialized tools/LLM calls.
**Evidence Anchors:** Ablation shows a ~20-point accuracy gap between "No Planner" and "Full" configurations, confirming the planner's substantial contribution. Performance varies by task category (e.g., larger gains for cross-sheet retrieval).
**Failure Mode:** Over-decomposition can harm performance; two tasks failed with BRTR that passed without the planner.

## Foundational Learning
1.  **Hybrid Retrieval:** Combines dense vector search with lexical BM25, fused via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF). *Why needed:* Captures both semantic and exact keyword matches over diverse chunk types. *Quick check:* Verify RRF implementation (k=60) yields higher nDCG@K than either method alone on a sample query.
2.  **Multimodal Chunking:** Segmenting spreadsheets into semantically coherent units (rows, columns, windows, images). *Why needed:* Enables fine-grained retrieval without overwhelming context; preserves structural relationships. *Quick check:* Count chunks per sheet; ensure window size/overlap handles merged cells without duplication.
3.  **Tool-Calling Loop:** Using LLM-generated function calls to iteratively query a knowledge base. *Why needed:* Moves beyond a single shot to refine search based on intermediate evidence. *Quick check:* Log tool call sequences; confirm the LLM sometimes issues a follow-up call *based on* a previous result.
4.  **Planner-Executor Pattern:** Separating high-level task decomposition from low-level execution. *Why needed:* Manages complexity of multi-step, cross-modal workflows; enables parallelization. *Quick check:* For a multi-sheet query, verify the planner outputs a DAG with at least two parallelizable subtasks.
5.  **Context Pruning Heuristic:** Always removing base64 image data from the context window. *Why needed:* Images are token-intensive; metadata often suffices for retrieval reasoning. *Quick check:* Inspect context tokens per turn; confirm no base64 strings are present after pruning.
6.  **Auditability via Traces:** Recording every tool call and its result. *Why needed:* Enables debugging, validation, and human review of agent decisions. *Quick check:* Reconstruct the workflow from the trace log; it should be a complete, step-by-step recreation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** User Query -> Planner (creates DAG) -> Dispatcher -> Parallel Executors (Search, Excel, I/O, Web, OCR, Validation) -> Search Tools -> Vector/BM25 Index (Chunks) -> Iterative Loop LLM (gathers evidence per subtask) -> Synthesis LLM (final answer). Core loop: Search Tools <- Iterative LLM <- Retrieved Chunks.

**Critical Path:** For a pure retrieval query (e.g., "Find revenue in Q1"): 1) Query -> Search Executor -> 2) Iterative LLM issues `search_*` tool calls -> 3) Retrieve chunks -> 4) LLM decides evidence sufficient -> 5) Answer. For a composite workflow (e.g., "Reconcile figure from PDF with sheet data"): 1) Planner decomposes into (a) extract PDF data, (b) search sheet, (c) compare -> 2) Dispatcher runs (a) and (b) in parallel -> 3) Each may use iterative search loops -> 4) Results feed into (c) -> 5) Final answer.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   *Iteration Limit (T=50):* Prevents infinite loops at the cost of potentially stopping productive long searches. Tradeoff between completeness and efficiency.
*   *Fixed Subtask Limit (≤6):* Enforces manageable decomposition but may force coarse splits for very complex tasks, leading to over-decomposition failure.
*   *Image Pruning:* Saves tokens but risks losing critical visual context (e.g., chart trends). Relies on image metadata being sufficient.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   **Stuck Loop:** Tool call sequence repeats similar queries (>5 times) without new relevant chunks returned.
*   **Planner Cyclic DAG:** Executor dependencies form a cycle; dispatcher deadlocks.
*   **Context Overflow:** Token count exceeds model limit before T=50, even after image pruning; likely due to accumulating non-image result summaries.
*   **Tool Misuse (small models):** LLM attempts to "write code" or answer directly instead of calling available tools, indicating poor function-calling adherence.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Chunking Sanity Check:** Implement the row/column/window extractor. Run it on 5 random FRTR-Bench workbooks. Record total chunks, average chunk size, and check that windowing respects sheet boundaries and maskes merged cells appropriately.
2.  **Planner Output Validation:** Feed the planner prompt (Appendix D template) with 10 diverse FINCH workflow descriptions. Validate that 100% of outputs are valid DAGs (acyclic, ≤6 nodes, correct executor types) and that parallelizable subgraphs exist.
3.  **Baseline Retrieval Comparison:** Using the BRTR chunking and embedding setup, run single-pass retrieval (top-10) on a 20-query FRTR-Bench sample. Compare nDCG@10 and Recall@10 against the iterative loop's *first* retrieval pass. This isolates the value of iteration from the base retriever quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** How can BRTR’s multimodal retrieval be extended to other structured document types (e.g., PDFs with complex layouts, databases, or slide decks) while maintaining high accuracy and auditability?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Future work will explore extending BRTR to additional structured document types beyond spreadsheets..."
*   **Why unresolved:** The paper focuses exclusively on spreadsheet workbooks; multimodal embedding evaluation (NVIDIA NeMo Retriever 1B) was conducted only on spreadsheet content. Generalization is unevaluated.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Replication of the BRTR framework and embedding model evaluation on new, diverse document-type benchmarks with cross-document dependencies and multimodal content (text, tables, charts).

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** What is the optimal balance between task decomposition and monolithic execution in the planner-executor architecture, and can this be learned or predicted from task characteristics to avoid over-decomposition?
*   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Ablation shows a 20-point accuracy gap, but two tasks failed with BRTR that passed without the planner, suggesting over-decomposition harm. Planner contribution varies by task category.
*   **Why unresolved:** The planner uses a fixed heuristic (1-6 subtasks) without adaptive criteria. Conditions where decomposition introduces error/inefficiency are not modeled.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic study correlating task features (workflow length, sheets, artifact types, dependency density) with performance delta between decomposed and monolithic execution, yielding a decision model.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** Can the iterative search loop’s efficiency be improved without sacrificing accuracy by incorporating learned retrieval strategies that adapt query refinement based on intermediate result quality?
*   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Credits iterative refinement for gains; frontier models average 3–6 tool calls, some more exploratory. Search strategy is solely LLM-driven; "always-prune" is a simple heuristic.
*   **Why unresolved:** No feedback mechanism optimizes query formulation based on past success/failure. Pruning may discard useful visual context.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiment comparing current LLM-driven refinement against a learned policy (e.g., RL) optimizing evidence per token on a held-out set of complex queries with annotated optimal paths.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** Why do smaller models (e.g., LLaMA 4 Maverick 17B) exhibit a capability threshold where BRTR performance degrades compared to simpler compression baselines, and can this be mitigated through targeted fine-tuning or constrained tool-call spaces?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "Smaller models such as LLaMA 4 Maverick 17B show limited gains... perform worse with BRTR than with SpreadsheetLLM compression. LLaMA 4 Maverick 17B tried to write code instead of calling tools..."
*   **Why unresolved:** Attributed to insufficient reasoning capacity; does not disentangle failure in planning tool calls, interpreting results, or general instruction-following. No fine-tuning experiments.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablations on smaller models comparing BRTR with/without supervised fine-tuning on tool-calling trajectories, or with constrained tool sets, to isolate bottleneck (planner, executor, or instruction-following).

### Open Question 5
*   **Question:** How does the auditability provided by explicit tool-call traces scale in terms of comprehension and utility for human reviewers as workflow complexity (number of tool calls, sub-tasks) increases, and what trace summarization techniques preserve diagnostic value?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "BRTR maintains full auditability through explicit tool-call traces." FINCH median tool calls 25–37. Paper mentions 200+ hours human evaluation but no analysis of reviewer burden or error-detection efficacy vs. trace length.
*   **Why unresolved:** Auditability is claimed but practical limits (cognitive load for long traces, automated summary utility) are unexplored. Long traces may become opaque.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** User study measuring auditor accuracy and time-to-diagnosis on synthetic faults in workflows of varying trace lengths, comparing raw traces vs. automated summaries (e.g., hierarchical clustering, confidence tagging).

## Limitations
*   Exact multimodal chunking (window size, overlap, merged cell handling) and image extraction logic are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
*   Planner’s decomposition prompt, DAG validation rules, and executor routing logic are partially redacted in Appendix D.
*   Reported SOTA gains depend on frontier LLMs (GPT-5, Claude Opus 4.6); performance with open-weight models is less clear.

## Confidence
*   **Performance claims:** **High** – Backed by three benchmark suites, 200+ human eval hours, and ablation studies.
*   **Mechanism claims** (iterative retrieval, planner-executor): **Medium** – Supported by architecture diagrams and ablations, but mechanism isolation relies on variable LLM behavior.
*   **Implementation specifics** (chunking, planner prompts): **Low** – Critical details omitted; would require inference or author disclosure.
