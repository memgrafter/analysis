# All That Glisters Is Not Gold: A Benchmark for Reference-Free Counterfactual Financial Misinformation Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.04160
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04160
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Reference-free detection by 14 LLMs peaks near chance (53.6%), while comparative diagnosis achieves 85–97%, highlighting accommodation failure.

## Executive Summary
RFC-Bench introduces a benchmark for reference-free financial misinformation detection using subtle counterfactual perturbations that preserve surface plausibility but shift warranted meaning. Evaluation of 14 large language models reveals a stark asymmetry: without an original reference, models struggle (accuracy ~53–57%), yet when given an explicit original–perturbed pair, they achieve high accuracy (85–97%). This indicates that current LLMs default to accommodation—accepting surface-plausible input without internal consistency checks—and require external grounding to reliably detect misinformation.

## Method Summary
The benchmark constructs 1,826 paragraph-level original–perturbed pairs from real Yahoo Finance news across four manipulation types: directional flipping, numerical perturbation, sentiment amplification, and causal distortion. Perturbations are generated via GPT-4.1 using category-specific prompts that enforce minimal edits (e.g., preserving entities and dates). A human QA pipeline validates category correctness and minimality. Two tasks are evaluated: (1) **Reference-free detection** (binary classification of a single paragraph); (2) **Comparative diagnosis** (4-way classification of the manipulation type given a pair). Zero-shot prompting is primary, with few-shot ablations. Metrics include accuracy, macro-F1, MCC, and invalid output rate.

## Key Results
- Reference-free detection accuracy peaks at 53.6% (zero-shot, DeepSeek-reasoner), improving only to 56.7% with few-shot.
- Comparative diagnosis accuracy ranges from 85% to 97% across models.
- Invalid output rates are significantly higher in the reference-free setting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Plausibility-preserving perturbations shift warranted meaning
Minimal counterfactual changes alter the inferences a reader is licensed to draw (common ground commitments) while keeping surface fluency and factual skeleton intact. This isolates detection of internal discourse inconsistency from external fact-checking. Evidence: rewriting prompts enforce exact entity/number preservation; human validation shows category correctness near 1.0; break condition would be non-minimal edits that conflate external verification.

### Mechanism 2: LLMs default to accommodation without a stable belief state
When presented a single, plausible paragraph, models lack a stable internal belief state to flag subtle commitment shifts. As stateless next-token predictors, they accommodate the input rather than question it. The comparative task succeeds by providing an explicit contrast, triggering sensitivity to lexical and semantic differences. Evidence: near-chance reference-free vs. high comparative accuracy; error analysis shows reliance on surface cues and over-rejection of forward-looking statements.

### Mechanism 3: Comparative diagnosis leverages explicit contrast
The paired design transforms the problem into a constrained difference attribution task. Models need only identify the edit category, not evaluate a single text’s warrant. High accuracy proves LLMs possess the discriminative capacity; however, this capacity is not activated in the reference-free setting. Evidence: dual-annotator agreement 0.988; Task2 accuracy up to 0.969; surface-feature baseline underperforms median LLM.

## Foundational Learning
- **Pragmatic Theory of Meaning (Common Ground / Warranted Assertions)**  
  *Why needed*: The benchmark treats misinformation as shifts in what a text warrants, not merely false facts. This distinction explains why reference-free detection is hard: models must assess a paragraph’s commitments in isolation.  
  *Quick check*: Does “Company X may double earnings” warrant a stronger commitment than “Company X will double earnings”? How can you tell without external data?

- **Financial Misinformation Taxonomies**  
  *Why needed*: The four manipulation types (directional, numerical, sentiment, causal) are adapted from prior surveys; knowing the taxonomy explains the benchmark’s coverage of deceptive patterns.  
  *Quick check*: Provide a “causal distortion” headline that keeps all entities/numbers but changes the inferred reason for a price move.

- **Reference-Free vs. Evidence-Based Evaluation**  
  *Why needed*: RFC-Bench isolates reference-free detection to study internal consistency, contrasting with benchmarks like FEVER that require external evidence. This split is central to interpreting the results.  
  *Quick check*: A model that always outputs “true” scores 0% on FEVER but what percentage on RFC-Bench’s Task1?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Data Source (Yahoo Finance RSS/API) → Pre-filter (rule-based keywords) → Perturbation Engine (GPT-4.1 with category-specific prompts) → Human QA Pipeline (expert review, dual annotation, adjudication) → Evaluation Harness (unified prompting, metric computation) → Analysis Suite (confusion matrices, baselines, error tracking).

**Critical path**: Data acquisition → rule-based pre-filter → GPT rewriting → expert review → dual annotation → adjudication → final dataset → model evaluation → analysis.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Paragraph-level vs. claim-level: more context but introduces noise; claims are simpler but miss narrative distortions.
- Minimal perturbations: harder to generate reliably but better test subtle awareness; larger edits become obvious.
- English-only, US stocks: controls domain variance but limits generalizability.
- LLM-as-generator: risks inheriting LLM biases; mitigated by human audit.

**Failure signatures**:
- High invalid output rate in Task1.
- Confusion between Flipping and Numerical when polarity reversal coincides with symmetric numeric changes.
- Sentiment amplification over-shooting into factual change or extreme language.
- Causal distortion failing to preserve the outcome while only changing cause.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Zero-shot Task1: establish baseline accommodation failure (expect ~53% accuracy).
2. Zero-shot Task2: establish comparative diagnosis ceiling (expect >85% accuracy).
3. Surface-feature baseline: train logistic regression on length ratio, Jaccard similarity, numeric token difference to confirm Task2 success isn’t due to lexical artifacts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can LLMs be explicitly trained or architected to maintain coherent belief states and trigger doubt in a purely reference-free setting?** The paper diagnoses accommodation failure but offers no architectural changes. Resolution would require systematic ablation studies of reasoning mechanisms on RFC-Bench.

2. **To what extent do the controlled, LLM-generated perturbations capture the diversity and strategic adversarial nature of real-world financial misinformation?** The benchmark may not fully capture longer-range inconsistencies, cross-document contradictions, or coordinated narratives. Evidence needed: comparative studies with authentic misinformation corpora.

3. **How can models effectively integrate internal discourse cues with external verification in a hybrid system?** RFC-Bench isolates reference-free detection, but real-world use likely combines both. Future work should develop hybrid pipelines that fuse reference-free signals with retrieval-based fact-checking.

4. **Do observed failure modes reflect fundamental limitations in pre-training data and objectives, and can they be mitigated by targeted data curation or training modifications?** Error analysis shows models reject plausible forward-looking statements, accept fabricated narratives, and anchor to training cutoffs. Investigating root causes in data distribution is required.

5. **Can the four manipulation types be generalized to a finer-grained or continuous taxonomy, and can models detect a broader spectrum of subtle distortions?** Real misinformation may blend categories; the discrete simplification may be insufficient. Evidence needed: expanded annotation studies and multi-label detection training.

## Limitations
- Original article URLs may be inaccessible or changed, compromising faithful reproduction.
- Model versions and inference settings are ambiguous; API defaults may have drifted.
- The benchmark’s narrow scope (English, US equities) limits generalizability.
- High invalid output rates indicate prompt adherence issues affecting metrics.

## Next Checks
1. **URL retrieval audit**: Randomly sample 100 URLs, attempt to fetch original paragraphs, and report success rate and semantic alignment with the perturbed versions.
2. **Exact version replication**: Identify precise model checkpoints and decoding parameters used in the paper; re-run zero-shot Task1 and Task2 to quantify deviation from published scores.
3. **Independent minimality validation**: Engage domain annotators to evaluate a stratified sample for minimal edit distance, factual skeleton preservation, and correct category; compare inter-annotator agreement against the reported 0.988.
