# Task-Specific Knowledge Distillation via Intermediate Probes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12270
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12270
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** PROBE-KD uses lightweight probes on teacher hidden states to generate cleaner supervision signals, outperforming standard logit distillation by up to +5.6% absolute accuracy on AQuA-RAT and improving student calibration.

## Executive Summary
Standard knowledge distillation from large language models (LLMs) for reasoning tasks is hampered by noisy output logits, which distort task-relevant information through the vocabulary projection layer. PROBE-KD addresses this by training a lightweight probe on the frozen teacher's intermediate hidden states to produce soft labels that better capture the teacher's latent knowledge. This probe acts as a task-specific decoder, bypassing the general-purpose unembedding layer. The resulting supervision consistently yields more accurate and better-calibrated student models, with particularly strong gains in low-data regimes. The method requires no changes to model architecture and adds minimal computational overhead.

## Method Summary
PROBE-KD is a two-stage distillation framework. First, a frozen teacher LLM processes the training data to extract and cache concatenated hidden states from all layers for each example. Second, a lightweight probe (e.g., a two-layer MLP) is trained on these cached representations, using task labels to learn a mapping directly to the output label space. Once trained, the frozen probe generates soft probability distributions for each training example. Finally, a student model is trained from scratch using a combined loss that matches the student's output distribution to the probe's distribution (via KL divergence) while also fitting the ground-truth labels (via cross-entropy). This replaces the standard practice of distilling from the teacher's final logits.

## Key Results
- PROBE-KD (MLP probe) student accuracy exceeds Logit-KD by +2.2% and Feature-KD by +5.0% on AQuA-RAT with DeBERTa-v3-base.
- The probe itself can outperform the teacher's 5-shot accuracy by up to +5.6% absolute on the same task.
- Student calibration improves dramatically; the mean prediction confidence of a PROBE-KD student aligns much more closely with its actual accuracy than a Logit-KD student.
- Gains are largest in low-data regimes (e.g., +3.5% absolute improvement at 1% data).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Cleaner Supervision from Latent States
PROBE-KD improves student accuracy because the teacher's intermediate hidden states encode a cleaner, task-relevant signal than the final output logits. A trainable probe inserted between the frozen teacher representations and the student loss learns a task-specific projection. This bypasses the teacher's original, general-purpose unembedding layer, which is optimized for next-token prediction, not the specific reasoning task. The probe's soft predictions thus provide denoised supervision. **Evidence:** Probe accuracy (50.3%) exceeds teacher 5-shot accuracy (44.7%) on AQuA-RAT (Table 2). The abstract and section 3.3 explicitly frame the probe as providing better task alignment.

### Mechanism 2: Preserving "Dark Knowledge" without Output Noise
Standard Logit-KD forces the student to mimic a probability distribution over fixed answer tokens, which is corrupted by the teacher's unembedding matrix and prompt formatting. The probe, trained directly on the label space, produces soft labels that reflect the teacher's internal confidence among meaningful classes, discarding spurious token probabilities. **Evidence:** PROBE-KD student (29.4%) surpasses Logit-KD student (26.6%) despite the teacher itself being less accurate (44.7%) than its probe (50.3%) (section 5.1). The introduction argues the output layer is suboptimal for expressing task-specific knowledge.

### Mechanism 3: Improved Calibration Transfer
The teacher LLM is systematically overconfident. A probe trained on ground-truth labels learns to map hidden states to probabilities that better align with actual correctness. Distilling from this probe transfers better calibration to the student. **Evidence:** The teacher has a 29.8 pp calibration gap (74.5% confidence vs. 44.7% accuracy). The MLP probe gap is only 2.3 pp (52% vs. 50.3%). The PROBE-KD student's gap is 6.1 pp (35.5% vs. 29.4%), far better than the Logit-KD student's 21.4 pp gap (48% vs. 26.6%) (section 5.4, Figure 3).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Knowledge Distillation (KD)**
  - *Why needed here:* PROBE-KD is a variant of KD. Understanding the standard framework (student minimizing KL divergence to teacher logits) clarifies what PROBE-KD changes: the source of the teacher distribution.
  - *Quick check:* In standard Logit-KD, what does the student's loss function minimize? (Answer: KL divergence between student and teacher output probability distributions).

- **Concept: Linear and Non-linear Probes**
  - *Why needed here:* The probe is the core component. It is a lightweight classifier trained on frozen representations to decode a specific task. Comparing linear (Logistic) and non-linear (MLP) probes is central to the method's design.
  - *Quick check:* What is the fundamental difference between a "probe" (in this paper) and the teacher's "unembedding layer"? (Answer: The probe maps to the *task label space* using task labels; the unembedding maps to the *vocabulary space* using next-token prediction).

- **Concept: Calibration in Classification**
  - *Why needed here:* A major claimed benefit is improved calibration, where predicted confidence reflects true accuracy.
  - *Quick check:* If a model is perfectly calibrated on a binary task with 60% accuracy, what should its average predicted probability for the chosen class be? (Answer: ~60%).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Teacher (frozen LLM) -> [extract & cache hidden states] -> Probe (trainable, then frozen) -> [generate soft labels] -> Student (trainable)
- **Critical path:**
  1.  **Extract & Cache:** Run all training data through frozen teacher to extract concatenated hidden states from all layers and save to disk.
  2.  **Train Probe:** Using cached hidden states and task labels, train a lightweight probe (e.g., MLP) to high accuracy.
  3.  **Generate Soft Labels:** Freeze the trained probe. For each training example, compute the probe's softmax output with a temperature (τ=2.0).
  4.  **Train Student:** Train the student model from scratch using combined loss: `L = α·KL(p_probe∥p_S) + (1−α)·CE(y, S(x))` with α=0.7.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Probe Capacity:** MLP probes outperform linear ones but risk overfitting on small probe training sets.
  - **Data Regime:** Relative gains over Logit-KD are largest in low-data settings (e.g., 1% of training data).
  - **Supervision Type:** Supervised probes (using `y`) generally outperform unsupervised (CCS) probes, trading label requirement for accuracy.
  - **Storage vs. Compute:** Caching all hidden states is memory-intensive but makes subsequent probe training and student distillation fast; streaming is possible but increases compute.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Probe accuracy ≤ teacher output accuracy (latent signal not decodable).
  - Student performance after PROBE-KD ≤ Logit-KD (probe labels not higher quality).
  - Poor student calibration persists despite probe being well-calibrated (student architecture/inability to inherit calibration).
  - No benefit or regression when scaling student size (e.g., DeBERTa-large underperforms base in some results).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Reproduce Probe vs. Teacher Accuracy:** Train Logistic and MLP probes on teacher hidden states. Verify probe accuracy strictly exceeds teacher's output accuracy on validation.
  2.  **Ablate Probe Architecture & Supervision:** Compare student performance distilled from (a) Linear probe, (b) MLP probe, (c) CCS probe, and (d) a probe trained to mimic teacher logits.
  3.  **Data Scaling Curve:** Train PROBE-KD (MLP) and Logit-KD students on increasing fractions of training data (1%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 100%). Plot accuracy vs. data points; verify PROBE-KD curve is consistently above Logit-KD with largest margin at smallest data.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Extending to generation tasks:** Can PROBE-KD be adapted for sequence generation (e.g., summarization), requiring probes that decode sequences?
- **Memory overhead reduction:** How can the storage cost of caching full hidden states be reduced (e.g., via layer selection or dimensionality reduction) without compromising probe accuracy?
- **Black-box teachers:** Can PROBE-KD work with teachers that do not expose internal representations (API-only)?
- **Optimal probe architecture:** What probe design (depth, width, layer selection) optimally extracts latent knowledge across tasks and teachers while avoiding overfitting?
- **Mechanism for low-data gains:** What is the precise reason PROBE-KD shines in low-data regimes (e.g., variance reduction, better uncertainty), and is this generalizable?

## Limitations
- Requires access to teacher's intermediate hidden states, excluding black-box API models.
- Memory overhead from caching hidden states can be substantial for large datasets.
- Probe validation protocol and few-shot prompt details are underspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Evaluation is limited to one teacher (Qwen2.5-7B) and one student (DeBERTa-base), so generalizability across architectures is uncertain.
- Calibration analysis uses a single metric (mean confidence vs. accuracy) without standard measures like ECE.

## Confidence
**High confidence:** Accuracy gains over Logit-KD/Feature-KD baselines; Probe denoising (probe accuracy > teacher).
**Medium confidence:** Calibration improvement; Superiority in low-data regimes.
**Low confidence:** Architecture-agnostic claim (insufficient model diversity); Universality of low-data effect (not tested across many tasks).

## Next Checks
1.  **Replication with proper validation:** Train probes using a held-out validation set for early stopping; repeat across multiple random seeds to confirm the probe accuracy > teacher gap and persistent student gains.
2.  **Hyperparameter robustness sweep:** Systematically vary probe architecture (depth, width, layer subset), distillation temperature (τ), and KL weight (α) to identify minimal settings that retain the performance margin over Logit-KD.
3.  **Generalizability test:** Evaluate PROBE-KD on at least three different teachers (e.g., Llama-2, Mistral) and three different students (e.g., RoBERTa, TinyBERT) across multiple reasoning benchmarks (e.g., BBQ, HellaSwag) to validate broader applicability.
