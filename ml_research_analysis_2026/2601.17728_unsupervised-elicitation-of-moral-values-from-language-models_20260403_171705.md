# Unsupervised Elicitation of Moral Values from Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17728
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17728
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The Internal Coherence Maximization (ICM) algorithm elicits latent moral reasoning from pretrained language models without human supervision, achieving ≥81% accuracy on Norm Bank, matching human-supervised fine-tuning on ETHICS, and reducing social bias failure rates by >50% on UDHR-Demographics.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Internal Coherence Maximization (ICM), an unsupervised method for extracting consistent moral judgments from pretrained language models. ICM optimizes for label assignments that are mutually predictive and logically coherent across a dataset, leveraging the model’s own output distribution. Evaluated on three moral benchmarks—Norm Bank, ETHICS, and UDHR-Demographics—ICM substantially outperforms zero-shot and many-shot prompting baselines, narrows the gap to human-supervised fine-tuning, and mitigates demographic biases. The approach offers a scalable path to moral alignment without expensive human annotation, though it depends on the salience of moral concepts in the base model and requires careful hyperparameterization.

## Method Summary
The core method is the Internal Coherence Maximization (ICM) algorithm. Starting from a small random seed of labeled examples, ICM iteratively updates labels for remaining items by proposing new assignments that maximize a scoring function. This function combines (1) mutual predictability—the log-probability of a label given the input and all other provisional labels—and (2) logical consistency penalties for contradictory label pairs (e.g., assigning opposite moral valence to near-identical statements). Updates are accepted via a simulated annealing schedule to escape local optima. The process yields a fully labeled dataset that reflects a coherent set of moral judgments extracted from the model’s own distribution. These pseudo-labels can then be used to fine-tune the model (FT-Unsupervised) or to train reward models for reinforcement learning. The method is applied to base (non-chat) pretrained LMs; no human supervision is used at any point.

## Key Results
- ICM achieves at least 81% accuracy on Norm Bank, outperforming zero-shot and many-shot chatbot baselines by at least 8 percentage points and matching the performance of models fine-tuned on human labels.
- On the ETHICS benchmark, ICM shows its largest relative gains in the Commonsense and Justice moral domains, while Utilitarianism remains the most challenging.
- On the UDHR-Demographics benchmark, ICM reduces social bias failure rates by more than half compared to pretrained and chatbot models, with the strongest improvements for race, socioeconomic status, and political identity categories.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Internal Coherence Maximization (ICM) elicits latent moral reasoning by optimizing for internally consistent and mutually predictive label assignments across a dataset.
- **Mechanism:** ICM iteratively proposes label updates via simulated annealing, maximizing a score that combines mutual predictability (the log-probability of each candidate label given the input and all other provisional labels) and logical consistency (penalizing contradictory label pairs). This search surfaces a coherent set of moral judgments from the model's own output distribution.
- **Core assumption:** Pretrained language models encode sufficiently coherent latent representations of moral concepts that can be accessed without external supervision.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "ICM significantly outperforms zero-shot and many-shot chatbot baselines on the Norm Bank and ETHICS benchmarks."; [section 2.2] Describes the ICM algorithm's scoring function and annealing process in detail.; [corpus] Neighboring paper "Unsupervised Elicitation of Language Models" (arXiv:2506.10139) provides independent validation of the approach on related tasks.
- **Break condition:** Fails to improve performance if the targeted moral concept is not salient in the pretrained model's representations, as coherence optimization cannot invent missing knowledge.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** ICM mitigates social bias by aligning model outputs with aspirational human rights norms through coherence-based filtering of inconsistent responses.
- **Mechanism:** When applied to demographic statements (e.g., UDHR dataset), coherence optimization penalizes label assignments that conflict across similar identity groups or between current-world and ideal-world formulations, thereby suppressing outputs that reflect real-world biases and promoting those consistent with normative human rights principles.
- **Core assumption:** Pretrained models contain both biased and unbiased reasoning paths; coherence maximization selectively amplifies the latter by rejecting contradictory judgments.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "ICM reduces social bias failure rates by more than half... with the largest improvements in race, socioeconomic status, and politics."; [section 3.3] Presents Figure 4 showing error rate reductions across 12 social identity categories.; [corpus] Indirect support from neighboring papers on moral preferences and bias, but no direct evidence for ICM's coherence-based bias mitigation mechanism.
- **Break condition:** Less effective for demographic groups with extremely sparse representation in pretraining data, where coherent unbiased judgments may not be learnable.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** ICM enables scalable moral alignment by generating high-quality pseudo-labels that rival human annotations for fine-tuning reward models and policies.
- **Mechanism:** Labels produced by ICM are used to train reward models that score response morality; these models then guide reinforcement learning, producing policies that win head-to-head against those trained on human-supervised reward models.
- **Core assumption:** A model's own coherence-optimized self-judgments approximate human moral preferences well enough to serve as supervision for downstream alignment.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Fine-tuning on ICM-generated labels performs on par with or surpasses those of human labels."; [section 3.4] Shows reward model accuracies (71.3–75.4%) and RL win-rates (71.7%) against human-supervised baselines.; [corpus] Neighboring papers on reward modeling (e.g., arXiv:2504.13837) support the feasibility of self-supervised reward learning, but lack direct ICM comparisons.
- **Break condition:** For moral norms requiring deep cultural or contextual nuance absent from pretraining, ICM-generated labels may not capture necessary pluralism, leading to fine-tuned models that inherit this narrowness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Mutual Predictability in ICM  
  **Why needed here:** This term defines the core scoring component where each provisional label's likelihood is conditioned on all other labels, enforcing that the entire label set explains itself cohesively—essential for understanding how ICM surfaces latent knowledge.  
  **Quick check:** Why does conditioning a label on all other labels help avoid spurious correlations compared to independent labeling?

- **Concept:** Social Bias in LM Outputs  
  **Why needed here:** The paper's bias mitigation claims rely on knowing how biases manifest (e.g., differential failure rates across demographic groups in UDHR); this context explains why coherence matters.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Pretrained base LM -> ICM engine (scoring function, simulated annealing search) -> Pseudo-labeled dataset (ICM assignments) -> Fine-tuned model (on pseudo-labels) -> Evaluation suite (Norm Bank, ETHICS, UDHR)
- **Critical path:** Implement ICM scoring → run on dataset sample → generate pseudo-labels → fine-tune base LM → evaluate on benchmarks → compare to zero-shot/chat/fine-tuned-on-human baselines.
- **Design tradeoffs:** ICM's computational cost (search over label assignments) vs. bias reduction benefits; dataset size (larger improves coherence signal) vs. tractability; using base vs. chat models as starting point (base shows larger gains).
- **Failure signatures:** (a) Low coherence scores during ICM search; (b) Minimal improvement over zero-shot on Utilitarian tasks; (c) No bias reduction on specific demographic categories (e.g., religion, sexual orientation); (d) Fine-tuned model regresses on out-of-distribution moral frameworks.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Reproduce ICM on Norm Bank subset: Run ICM on 2024 examples for one LM (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B), verify accuracy >81% and >8pp gain over zero-shot chat.  
  2. Cross-framework generalization: Apply same ICM model to all ETHICS tasks; expect largest relative gains in Commonsense and Justice, smallest in Utilitarianism.  
  3. Bias audit on UDHR: Compute failure rates per demographic category; confirm >50% overall reduction vs. zero-shot base, with strongest gains in race/socioeconomic status.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What causes ICM's dependence on pretrained model concept salience: algorithmic limits or LM structure?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "it is still unclear whether this limitation arises from ICM itself or the LMs’ structure." (p.10)
- **Why unresolved:** Determining the source is necessary to extend unsupervised elicitation to more concepts.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablate ICM components across model architectures; analyze the objective function's properties.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why does ICM excel on Commonsense/Justice but falter on Utilitarianism and on identities (religion, sexual orientation, gender identity)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] ICM yields largest gains on Justice/Commonsense; Utilitarianism most challenging; smallest gains for those identities (pp.6-7).
- **Why unresolved:** These patterns indicate mismatches between coherence objective and certain moral reasoning or deep biases; insight could improve elicitation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze ICM label errors per framework/identity; probe model representations to see what coherence captures.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does ICM compare to other unsupervised methods (e.g., CCS) on moral benchmarks?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] No comparison in paper despite Related Work mentioning CCS.
- **Why unresolved:** Need to identify the most effective unsupervised approach for moral alignment.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmark ICM, CCS, entropy minimization on Norm Bank/ETHICS with same models; compare accuracy, bias reduction, compute cost.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can ICM's computational cost be reduced for scalability?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Paper notes ICM is computationally intensive, uses subsets, and has context-window limits.
- **Why unresolved:** Scalability is crucial for real-world deployment; current limits hinder broad application.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop/test approximate scoring (e.g., sampling, distillation) on full datasets; measure performance trade-offs.

## Limitations
- **Hyperparameter opacity:** Exact ICM parameters (temperature schedule, consistency penalty weights, seed size) are unreported, risking reproducibility gaps.
- **Baseline prompt variance:** Zero-shot and many-shot prompt templates are described as "optimized" but not specified, making baseline comparisons potentially non-standardized.
- **Mechanistic evidence:** While ICM reduces bias, the paper does not prove *why*—e.g., whether coherence filtering truly suppresses biased reasoning paths or merely shortcuts spurious correlations.

## Confidence
- **Performance on Norm Bank/ETHICS:** High  
- **Bias reduction on UDHR:** Medium  
- **Reward model/RL parity with human labels:** Medium-Low
