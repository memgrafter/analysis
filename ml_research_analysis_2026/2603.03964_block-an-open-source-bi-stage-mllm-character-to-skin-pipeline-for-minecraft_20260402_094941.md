# BLOCK: An Open-Source Bi-Stage MLLM Character-to-Skin Pipeline for Minecraft
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.03964
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03964
*   **Reference count:** 15
*   **Primary result:** A bi-stage pipeline that robustly generates pixel-perfect, structurally valid 64×64 Minecraft skins from arbitrary character concepts by decomposing the task into semantic preview synthesis (Stage-1) and constrained UV-atlas generation (Stage-2).

## Executive Summary
BLOCK solves the problem of generating exact, playable Minecraft skins from arbitrary character images, a task where single-end-to-end models fail due to the strict UV-structure requirements and a large domain gap from natural images. The system uses a two-stage design: first, a large multimodal model (Gemini Nano Banana Pro) interprets the input and synthesizes a canonical, dual-panel (front/back) oblique-view preview in the Minecraft pixel-art style. Second, a fine-tuned FLUX.2 diffusion model, trained via a progressive curriculum called EvolveLoRA, translates this normalized preview into a valid 512×512 skin atlas. A deterministic decoder then converts the atlas into the final 64×64 RGBA texture with correct UV mapping and layer separation. This decomposition allows each stage to specialize, overcoming the brittleness of previous approaches and enabling the generation of high-fidelity, identity-preserving skins.

## Method Summary
The pipeline consists of two main stages. **Stage-1** uses the proprietary Gemini Nano Banana Pro MLLM with a precise, multi-part prompt template to convert an input character reference (single or multiple images) into a consistent dual-panel preview showing front and back views at an oblique angle. This preview normalizes the input distribution and enforces a canonical pose and style. **Stage-2** employs a FLUX.2 model fine-tuned on the `Minecraft-Skins-20M` dataset (~200K skins) using the proposed **EvolveLoRA** curriculum: three sequential LoRA phases (text-to-image → image-to-image with front/back renders → preview-to-atlas), where each phase initializes from the previous adapter's weights for stability. The model is trained to generate a 512×512 atlas conditioned on the Stage-1 preview. Finally, a deterministic **decoder** performs an 8× nearest-neighbor downsample on the atlas and enforces the standard Minecraft 64×64 UV layout (separate base and overlay layers) to produce the final, game-ready skin.

## Key Results
*   Overcomes the failure of single-stage methods by achieving robust generation of **structurally valid** Minecraft skins that adhere to strict UV layout and layer rules.
*   Preserves **character identity** from diverse input concepts (photos, illustrations) through the bi-stage decomposition and precise preview synthesis.
*   The **EvolveLoRA** curriculum stabilizes and accelerates fine-tuning for the challenging conditional mapping from preview to atlas, especially compared to training the final phase from scratch.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Bi-stage Decomposition
*   **Claim:** Separating semantic interpretation (Stage-1) from UV-atlas generation (Stage-2) avoids the brittleness of an end-to-end model.
*   **Mechanism:** The MLLM handles open-world understanding and layout adherence to produce a normalized, canonical preview. The fine-tuned diffusion model then focuses on the well-defined, constrained mapping from this preview to a valid atlas under strict UV rules.
*   **Core assumption:** MLLMs excel at high-level understanding but fail at pixel-perfect UV mapping; diffusion models can learn this structured mapping reliably when conditioned on a standardized preview.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "BLOCK decomposes the problem into (i) a 3D preview synthesis stage driven by a large multimodal model … and (ii) a skin decoding stage based on a fine-tuned FLUX.2 model." [section 3.3] Discusses the pragmatic choice of decomposition over synthesizing training data by reversing the pipeline.
*   **Break condition:** Stage-1 produces a malformed preview (e.g., front/back swap, incorrect camera), which directly causes Stage-2 to fail.

### Mechanism 2: EvolveLoRA Progressive Curriculum
*   **Claim:** The three-phase LoRA curriculum stabilizes and speeds up fine-tuning for the preview-to-atlas translation.
*   **Mechanism:** Phases sequentially warm-start the adapter: I) adapts to Minecraft skin priors (text-to-image), II) learns front/back conditional mapping (image-to-image), III) masters the final preview-to-atlas task. Reusing adapters mitigates catastrophic forgetting and optimization instability.
*   **Core assumption:** The task difficulty is cumulative; decomposing it and reusing features accelerates convergence under limited compute.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "We further propose EvolveLoRA, a progressive LoRA curriculum (text-to-image → image-to-image → preview-to-skin) that initializes each phase from the previous adapter." [section 4] Details the three-phase decomposition for stability and efficiency.
*   **Break condition:** Early phases overfit to coarse patterns or misalign layouts, poisoning the initialization for Phase III; the curriculum assumes a gradual domain shift.

### Mechanism 3: Deterministic Nearest-Neighbor Decoding
*   **Claim:** Using a fixed nearest-neighbor downsampling enforces pixel-perfect UV layout and layer validity by removing stochasticity from the final step.
*   **Mechanism:** The Stage-2 model generates a conceptual 8× upscaled (512×512) version of the target 64×64 skin. The decoder downsamples via nearest-neighbor, preserving exact pixel values and the grid structure. This guarantees a valid UV map if the atlas conforms to the grid.
*   **Core assumption:** A faithful upscaled representation can be losslessly mapped back to the original grid without interpolation artifacts.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.2] "After generation, a deterministic decoder performs nearest-neighbor downsampling and converts the output into a 64×64 RGBA skin with a valid structure." [section 4.2] Describes nearest-neighbor upscaling of training skins to 512×512.
*   **Break condition:** Stage-2 produces an atlas with sub-pixel features, anti-aliased edges, or non-grid-aligned elements, which aliases or misplaces pixels upon downsampling.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA).**
    *   **Why needed:** EvolveLoRA relies on LoRA to efficiently fine-tune the large FLUX.2 model across multiple phases without full parameter updates, making the curriculum feasible under resource constraints.
    *   **Quick check question:** What is the mathematical formulation of LoRA and how does freezing the base weights preserve pretrained knowledge while adapting?

*   **Concept: Minecraft UV Skin Mapping.**
    *   **Why needed:** The entire pipeline must output a texture that precisely matches the game's rigid 64×64 RGBA atlas format, with specific pixel regions for head, torso, limbs, and a separate overlay layer.
    *   **Quick check question:** Describe the standard UV layout of a Minecraft skin, including the pixel coordinates/regions for the head, body, arms, legs, and the optional second-layer overlay.

*   **Concept: Conditional Diffusion/Flow Models.**
    *   **Why needed:** Stage-2 uses FLUX.2, a flow-based model; understanding how it incorporates image (preview) conditioning during both training and inference is key to grasping the core translation step.
    *   **Quick check question:** How does an image-conditioned diffusion or flow model incorporate the conditioning input (e.g., a preview image) into its denoising process?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Input Character Image(s) -> Stage-1 (Gemini Nano Banana Pro + Prompt Templates) -> Dual-Panel Preview (512×512) -> Stage-2 (FLUX.2 + EvolveLoRA Adapters) -> Skin Atlas (512×512) -> Deterministic Decoder (Nearest-Neighbor Downsample + UV Enforcement) -> Output Minecraft Skin (64×64 RGBA)
*   **Critical path:** The correctness of the final skin depends on a flawless progression: MLLM must produce a structurally correct preview; FLUX.2 must generate a grid-aligned atlas; the decoder's fixed transformation must preserve that grid. Error at any stage propagates forward.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Proprietary MLLM (Gemini) vs. open-source alternative: reliability and capability of Gemini vs. reproducibility and control of an open model.
    *   FLUX.2 9B parameter size: high-quality generation vs. significant computational cost for training/inference; suggests future distillation.
    *   Three-phase EvolveLoRA curriculum: improved stability and convergence vs. increased training complexity and need for intermediate datasets.
    *   512×512 intermediate atlas: provides consistent resolution across phases vs. higher memory footprint; nearest-neighbor decoding is lossless only if the atlas is perfectly grid-aligned.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Stage-1:** Preview has front/back panels swapped, wrong camera tilt, excessive detail (anti-aliasing, gradients), missing accessories, or non-pixel-art style.
    *   **Stage-2:** Atlas contains soft edges, color bleeding, misplaced body parts (e.g., arms on head), missing overlay layer, or non-grid-aligned features.
    *   **Decoder:** Output skin has misaligned UV regions, pixel leakage between base/overlay, or incorrect dimensions due to a malformed atlas.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Stage-1 Constraint Validation:** Feed a set of known reference images (e.g., official Minecraft skins) into the MLLM using the provided prompt templates. Automatically check if each generated preview satisfies hard constraints: correct dual-panel order (front left, back right), specific oblique camera angles, white background, and enforced pixel-art style (no anti-aliasing).
    2.  **EvolveLoRA Warm-Start Ablation:** Train the Phase-III (preview-to-atlas) model twice on a fixed subset of data: (a) initialized from the Phase-II LoRA weights (warm-start), (b) initialized randomly. Compare convergence speed (training loss curves) and the percentage of generated atlases that pass a basic UV-structure check.
    3.  **End-to-End Structural Audit:** Run the complete BLOCK pipeline on a diverse set of 50 character concept images. For each output 64×64 skin, run an automated validator that checks: (a) all standard body part regions are present and pixel-coordinate correct, (b) no alpha channel data exists in the base layer (overlay only), (c) image dimensions are exactly 64×64.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** How can Stage-2 be fine-tuned to produce atlases that survive the deterministic 8× downsampling without losing critical details or introducing aliasing artifacts?
*   **Open Question 2:** Can an open-source multimodal model be fine-tuned to reliably replace the proprietary Gemini Nano Banana Pro in Stage-1 while maintaining the required consistency in dual-panel preview synthesis?
*   **Open Question 3:** What standardized metrics and benchmark datasets are necessary to objectively measure "pixel-perfect" Minecraft skin generation, balancing UV-structure validity, overlay layer correctness, and cross-view identity preservation?
*   **Open Question 4:** How reliably does Mode-III's meta-prompt injection perform for single-image character references compared to multi-view references, and what are the primary failure modes in attribute extraction and prompt completion?

## Limitations
*   **Proprietary dependency:** The pipeline's core Stage-1 relies on the closed-source Gemini Nano Banana Pro model, hindering full open reproducibility and introducing potential service variability.
*   **Missing implementation details:** Critical components are underspecified: the source of the `Minecraft-Skins-20M` training dataset, the `mc-skin-to-sketch` preview rendering tool's implementation, and the exact hyperparameters (LoRA rank, learning rates) for FLUX.2 fine-tuning.
*   **Decoder specification gap:** The deterministic decoding step is described conceptually; the precise UV mapping rules (exact pixel coordinates for every body part and overlay region) and alpha-channel handling logic are not explicitly provided.

## Confidence
*   **Bi-stage decomposition rationale:** High – The strategic separation of concerns is clearly motivated, logically sound, and central to the paper's contribution.
*   **EvolveLoRA curriculum efficacy:** Medium – The phased approach is well-described and plausible, but lacks ablative experimental results and full hyperparameters to independently verify the claimed stability and efficiency gains.
*   **Pixel-perfect structural validity guarantee:** Medium – The mechanism (nearest-neighbor downsampling) is sound in theory, but the guarantee depends entirely on the unverified `mc-skin-to-sketch` tool's consistency and the unspecified decoder's exact logic.
