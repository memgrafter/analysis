# CBR-to-SQL: Rethinking Retrieval-based Text-to-SQL using Case-Based Reasoning in the Healthcare Domain

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05569
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05569
- **Reference Count:** 39
- **Primary Result:** State-of-the-art logical form accuracy (0.828) and strong execution accuracy (0.882) on MIMICSQL Complete Database; robust performance in sparse data settings (e.g., execution accuracy 0.842 vs 0.777 on Incomplete Database).

## Executive Summary
Standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for medical text-to-SQL struggles with the noisy, variable nature of healthcare jargon, often requiring large and noisy demonstration pools. This paper introduces CBR-to-SQL, a framework inspired by Case-Based Reasoning that abstracts query-SQL pairs into masked templates, separating retrieval into logical structure and entity resolution. The system first retrieves structurally similar templates with masked entities, then resolves placeholders using a curated lookup table. On the MIMICSQL benchmark, CBR-to-SQL achieves state-of-the-art results and demonstrates greater robustness to retrieval perturbations and improved sample efficiency, especially under data scarcity.

## Method Summary
CBR-to-SQL operates in two phases. Offline, it processes 8,000 training question-SQL pairs: an LLM tags domain-specific entities (e.g., drugs, diagnoses) and replaces them with semantic tags (e.g., `[DRUG]`), creating abstract templates that are embedded and indexed in a vector database. Online, for a test question the system (1) masks the input using the same LLM, (2) retrieves top-5 masked templates via dense search, and (3) prompts the LLM to generate a draft SQL containing `[ELEMENT]@[TAG]` placeholders. Next, it builds a lookup table from MIMIC-III values for frequent entity categories, uses a medical embedding model for semantic search and Levenshtein re-ranking, and finally employs an LLM to select the correct schema value for each placeholder. The final SQL is executed, and execution/logical form accuracy are measured.

## Key Results
- **Complete Database:** Logical form accuracy 0.828, execution accuracy 0.882, outperforming RAG baselines.
- **Incomplete Database (sparse data):** Execution accuracy 0.842 vs 0.777, logical form accuracy 0.780 vs 0.747, showing larger gains under scarcity.
- **Robustness:** Lower brittleness (Δ_brittle) under retrieval perturbations, indicating stable performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Masking domain-specific entities during case retention reduces retrieval noise by focusing on structural similarity rather than exact term matching.
- **Mechanism:** An LLM tags entities in natural language questions and SQL, replacing them with general semantic categories (e.g., `[DRUG]`, `[DIAGNOSIS]`). This creates abstract templates that strip away variable medical jargon and typos, allowing the retrieval system to match based on logical query structure (e.g., join patterns, aggregation) instead of surface-level lexical overlap.
- **Core Assumption:** Medical queries often share underlying structural patterns (e.g., "count patients with diagnosis X and drug Y") even when specific entities differ. Abstracting entities exposes these patterns.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract states templates "expose the underlying problem pattern"; Section 3.1 describes masking to "remove noisy information pre-retrieval... allowing the reuse of SQL templates across different problems."
- **Break Condition:** If medical queries have highly unique structures that never repeat even after masking, template reuse fails. The IDB experiment suggests patterns are reusable with minimal data.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Decomposing retrieval into logical structure and entity resolution stages improves accuracy by allowing each stage to optimize for a distinct subproblem, reducing conflicting retrieval signals.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 (Template Construction) retrieves masked cases to generate a draft SQL skeleton with placeholders. Stage 2 (Source Discovery) uses a dedicated lookup table and LLM disambiguation to resolve placeholders to correct schema entities. This separation prevents the retriever from being simultaneously biased toward structural and lexical matches.
- **Core Assumption:** Logical structure and entity resolution are separable subproblems; optimizing them independently yields better overall performance than a single, conflated retrieval step.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract mentions "two-stage retrieval process that first captures logical structure and then resolves relevant entities." Sections 3.2–3.3 detail the two stages and their distinct data sources.
- **Break Condition:** If the logical skeleton from Stage 1 is malformed or the lookup table lacks candidates, decomposition harms performance. Ablation in Table 5 shows dropping Source Discovery causes a sharp drop.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Template-based case representation increases sample efficiency and robustness to retrieval perturbations, especially under data scarcity.
- **Mechanism:** Masked templates act as generalized "cases" that can be reused across many specific queries. A smaller knowledge base of diverse templates can cover more variations than a large pool of specific examples. When top-ranked retrieved cases are perturbed or missing (brittleness test), the system can still find structurally similar templates, leading to smaller performance drops.
- **Core Assumption:** Abstract patterns are more transferable and less brittle than specific instances. A few well-chosen templates can represent many concrete queries.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract states "higher sample efficiency and robustness... particularly under data scarcity and retrieval perturbations." Section 4.2 describes IDB construction yielding ~774 unique structural patterns; Tables 3, 4 show larger gains and lower Δ_brittleness.
- **Break Condition:** Over-abstraction may lose critical distinctions (e.g., conflating `COUNT` and `AVG`), leading to systematic errors. Error analysis (Appendix G.4) shows tagging errors can propagate.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Case Retain (Offline) → [Vector DB with masked cases] → Template Construction (Online Stage 1) → Source Discovery (Online Stage 2) → Final SQL

**Critical path:** Input NL Question → LLM masking → Masked query sent to vector DB → Retrieve top-5 masked cases → LLM generates SQL skeleton with `[ELEMENT]@[TAG]` placeholders → Build lookup table from MIMIC-III → For each placeholder: semantic search + Levenshtein re-rank → LLM selector picks correct entity → Placeholders filled → Final SQL.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Cost vs. Accuracy: Multi-stage design increases latency/tokens but improves logical form accuracy.
- Generality vs. Precision: Masking improves generalization but can lose nuance (e.g., splitting multi-part entities).
- Lookup Table Scope: Too many values increase noise; too few cause misses. Semi-manual curation based on frequent entity categories is a compromise.

**Failure signatures:**
- *Table misreference:* Wrong table chosen for an entity (e.g., `SEPSIS` mapped to `DIAGNOSES` instead of `DEMOGRAPHIC`). Indicates Source Discovery lacks context.
- *Incorrect source retrieval:* Lookup returns irrelevant candidates (e.g., "Glucose, CSF" for "CSF"). Points to limitations in Levenshtein re-ranking for medical aliases.
- *Unrecognized/incorrect entity tagging:* Entity not masked or split incorrectly, so Source Discovery never processes it. Root cause for many failures.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Masking Validation:** On 50 gold pairs, manually verify that masked templates preserve core logical structure (joins, aggregates, WHERE logic) while obscuring specifics. Check that two queries with different drugs but same structure yield similar masks.
2. **Retrieval Relevance Check:** For a held-out query, retrieve top-5 masked cases in CDB. Inspect if the retrieved SQL skeletons (before placeholder injection) are logically similar to the gold query's structure. Compare against a standard RAG retriever; expect more structural alignment, fewer trivial lexical matches.
3. **Entity Resolution End-to-End:** Pipeline: masked query → Template Construction → placeholders → Source Discovery lookup & disambiguation. Measure: (a) Does lookup table contain gold entity? (b) Does LLM selector pick it? Isolate failure in lookup (recall) vs. LLM selection (precision).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to open-domain benchmarks** – Can CBR-to-SQL generalize to domains like Spider/WikiSQL? (Future Work explicitly mentions this.) Unresolved because evaluation only on single-domain MIMICSQL; scalable design required for hundreds of tables and diverse entity vocabularies.
2. **Lookup table dependency** – To what extent does performance depend on the quality/coverage of the semi-manually curated lookup table? Can a more robust, domain-agnostic system (e.g., UMLS) reduce brittleness from "Incorrect Source Retrieval"? Unresolved because no ablation on alternative lookup constructions; limitation noted as future work.
3. **Why does abstraction help?** – Does the masked template abstraction primarily improve separation of logical pattern learning from entity resolution, or does it simply reduce memorization in sparse data? Unresolved; ablations show benefit but not the precise mechanism (embedding space effects, template diversity).
4. **Robustness to tagging errors** – How robust is the pipeline to upstream LLM tagging failures? Can Source Discovery compensate for untagged entities? Unresolved; current system linear; error analysis shows propagation of tagging mistakes.
5. **Computational vs. maintenance trade-offs** – What is the cost-accuracy frontier at scale, and does modularity truly reduce long-term maintenance effort vs. monolithic RAG/fine-tuning? Unresolved; discussion claims long-term efficiency but lacks longitudinal simulation.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Exact LLM prompts, function-calling schemas, and specific embedding models (generalist/medical) are unreported, hindering faithful replication.
- **Evaluation context:** Low neighbor citations (average 0.0) make it hard to benchmark novelty against prior retrieval-based text-to-SQL work without external citation analysis.
- **Scalability trade-off:** Multi-stage design increases latency (Table 9); cost-accuracy trade-offs in real-world deployment are not quantified.

## Confidence
- **Primary accuracy claims (Acc_EX/Acc_LF):** High (backed by ablations and consistent SOTA across CDB/IDB splits).
- **Mechanism 1 (masking for structural generalization):** Medium (supported by IDB clustering rationale and error analysis; direct structural vs. lexical retrieval gain is indirect).
- **Mechanism 2 (two-stage decomposition):** High (clear ablation evidence; two-stage outperforms single-step by design).
- **Mechanism 3 (sample efficiency/robustness):** High (perturbation tests show lower Δ_brittle; IDB results demonstrate large gains in sparse regimes).
- **Robustness (lower Δ_brittle):** High (perturbation experiments statistically significant).
- **Limitations:** High (reproducibility gaps clearly stated; evaluation narrow).

## Next Checks
1. **Entity tagging fidelity:** Sample 100 training questions; manually verify LLM-generated masks preserve logical structure while replacing entities. Target: >95% structural preservation.
2. **Retrieval relevance:** For 50 test queries, compare top-5 retrieved masked SQL skeletons (CBR-to-SQL) vs. unmasked baselines. Quantify structural similarity (e.g., edit distance on JOIN/WHERE patterns). Expect >30% improvement in structural alignment.
3. **Lookup recall:** For each placeholder in development set, check if gold entity exists in curated lookup table. Identify failure rate due to missing values; if >10%, expansion with medical synonym lexicons is needed.
