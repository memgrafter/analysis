# From Images to Words: Efficient Cross-Modal Knowledge Distillation to Language Models from Black-box Teachers
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.10877
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10877
- **Reference count**: 30
- **Primary result**: ARMADA adds only 0.8% trainable parameters yet yields up to 3.4% gain on NLU tasks and 2.6% on zero-shot generative reasoning, with consistent improvements across 25+ experiments.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ARMADA, a cross-modal knowledge distillation framework that transfers knowledge from frozen vision-language teachers (including black-box models) to language-only students. The core innovation is a lightweight TS Aligner (0.8% additional parameters) that aligns student and teacher representations through three losses: output alignment, manifold alignment, and auxiliary output alignment. This approach avoids modifying the teacher and requires negligible teacher compute compared to prior multimodal distillation methods.

Evaluations on 12 NLU tasks (GLUE/SuperGLUE), 8 generative reasoning tasks, and 5 instruction-tuning tasks with diverse student models (BERT-6L, DeBERTa-v2-1.4B, OPT-1.3B, LLaMA-3B/7B/8B) show consistent gains of up to 3.4% on NLU and 2.6% on zero-shot reasoning. ARMADA outperforms unimodal KD baselines and matches multimodal KD methods while using less than 0.8% of the teacher compute, demonstrating an efficient and generalizable solution for cross-modal knowledge transfer.

## Method Summary
ARMADA consists of a trainable TS Aligner that operates between a frozen vision-language teacher and a language student. For each text input, the teacher generates a cross-modal representation (e.g., an image latent from Stable Diffusion). The student processes the text to produce its hidden state. The TS Aligner contains two branches: one processes the teacher's representation via a non-linear projection and a manifold projection; the other processes the student's hidden state via a bottleneck and a manifold projection. Both projected vectors are aligned in a shared subspace of dimension 768. Three losses are computed: output alignment (KL divergence between softmax of aligner logits and student logits), manifold alignment (distance such as Euclidean between projected vectors), and auxiliary output alignment (same as output alignment but on an extra prediction head attached to the projected vectors). Only TS Aligner parameters and optionally student task heads are trained; the teacher is frozen.

Training uses separate AdamW optimizers for student (learning rate 2e‑5 for BERT, 3e‑6 for larger models) and aligner (3e‑3 for GLUE, 1e‑3 for SuperGLUE). Default hyperparameters are α=0.5 (logit weight), β=1 (manifold weight), γ=1 (auxiliary weight), and temperature τ=5. After training, the TS Aligner is discarded and the student is deployed standalone. The method is evaluated across a wide range of teachers (Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, ModelScope, SpeechT5) and student architectures, demonstrating broad applicability.

## Key Results
- Up to 3.4% absolute gain on NLU tasks (e.g., BERT-6L with Stable Diffusion teacher) and 2.6% on zero-shot generative reasoning (LLaMA-7B).
- Average improvements of 1.4% for DeBERTa-v2-1.4B and 1.5% for OPT-1.3B across 12 NLU, 8 reasoning, and 5 instruction-tuning tasks.
- Outperforms unimodal KD baselines (MetaDistil) and matches multimodal KD methods (X-adapter) while consuming less than 0.8% of the teacher compute required by prior approaches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Output Alignment (Predictive Mimicry)
- **Claim:** Matching student and teacher *task predictions* (logits) transfers coarse-grained, task-specific supervision even when modalities differ.
- **Mechanism:** For a given input, the student's output layer is trained to mimic the softened probability distribution (via temperature τ) produced by the TS Aligner's output layer, using a weighted KL divergence loss (α). This aligns the final decision boundaries without requiring identical intermediate features.
- **Core assumption:** Task-relevant knowledge is preserved in the teacher's final output distribution, and this signal can be consumed by a student of a different modality if mapped through the TS Aligner.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...output alignment (matching task predictions)..."
  - [section 3.1]: Details the loss `L_s^(1) = (1-α)·L_T(Y_T, O_T^s) + α·||Φ(O_T^{ts}/τ) - Φ(O_T^s/τ)||²`.
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like "Knowledge Distillation of Black-Box Large Language Models" and "MST-Distill" confirm output/response matching as a core black-box KD strategy. ARMADA extends this to cross-modal via the aligner.
- **Break condition:** If the teacher's final prediction is semantically uninformative (e.g., a random classifier) or the student's capacity is too low to mimic the distribution, this loss provides a weak or misleading signal.

### Mechanism 2: Manifold Alignment (Representational Regularization)
- **Claim:** Projecting student and aligner features into a *shared subspace* and minimizing their distance (cosine, Euclidean, element-wise) transfers fine-grained, abstract semantic structure from the teacher's multimodal latent space to the student's unimodal space.
- **Mechanism:** A linear projection (P_ts, P_s) maps the hidden states `h_X^{ts}` (from TS Aligner) and `h_X^s` (from student) to a common dimensionality (e.g., 768). A distance loss (`L_manifold`) on these projected vectors (`p_X^{ts}, p_X^s`) forces the student's internal representations to topologically resemble the teacher's abstract concept space.
- **Core assumption:** The teacher's latent space (even from a vision-language model) encodes rich, modality-agnostic semantic relationships. A simple linear projection can align a student's unimodal space to this structure.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...manifold alignment (projecting features to a shared subspace using cosine, Euclidean, or element-wise distance)..."
  - [section 3.2]: Defines the three loss functions and Proposition 1 argues `L_elementwise` enforces the strongest regularization.
  - [section 6, Figure 7 & 8]: t-SNE plots and error analysis show ARMADA-distilled student embeddings are more semantically cohesive (higher Silhouette scores) and better separate classes, especially on syntactically complex (CoLA) and socially-grounded (COPA) examples.
- **Break condition:** If the teacher's latent space is not semantically structured (e.g., random features), or the linear projection is insufficient to bridge a large modality gap (e.g., text to chaotic noise), this alignment forces the student toward a meaningless manifold.

### Mechanism 3: Auxiliary Output Alignment (Deep Supervision)
- **Claim:** Adding a secondary prediction head on *both* the student's and TS Aligner's projected features (`p_X^s`, `p_X^{ts}`) and aligning their outputs provides consistent, deep-supervision regularization that stabilizes training and improves the quality of the manifold projection.
- **Mechanism:** An auxiliary linear layer (`O_{aux}`) produces a second set of task predictions from the projected features. The loss `L_s^(2)` aligns the student's auxiliary logits with the TS Aligner's auxiliary logits (similar to output alignment), in addition to the primary output alignment. This creates a cycle: better manifold projection → better auxiliary prediction → stronger auxiliary alignment → better manifold projection.
- **Core assumption:** The projected feature space (`p_X`) is not just an intermediate step but a *meaningful, task-relevant representation*. Supervising directly on this space (via an auxiliary head) is more effective than only supervising the final output.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...auxiliary output alignment (regularizing via an additional prediction head)..."
  - [section 3.3]: Introduces the auxiliary loss `L_t^(2), L_s^(2)` and uses topological arguments (Proposition 2) to show that homeomorphism between student and teacher manifolds ensures homeomorphism between their output spaces, justifying the regularization.
  - [Table 10]: Shows student auxiliary head performance correlates strongly (Spearman 0.99) with its main output head, and TS Aligner gains ~6% on average from its auxiliary head, demonstrating the head's utility.
- **Break condition:** If the auxiliary task is mis-specified or the projected feature space lacks discriminative power for the task, the auxiliary head introduces noise and conflicts with the primary objective.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Knowledge Distillation (KD)**
  - *Why needed here:* ARMADA is fundamentally a KD framework. Understanding classic KD (Hinton et al., 2015)—distilling "dark knowledge" from a teacher's softened logits—is prerequisite to seeing how ARMADA modifies it for cross-modal settings (using an aligner as an intermediary).
  - *Quick check question:* In standard KD, what two loss components typically guide the student, and what does the temperature parameter do?
- **Concept: Representation Learning & Manifolds**
  - *Why needed here:* The core innovation of ARMADA is aligning representations in a *shared manifold* (Section 3.2). This requires intuitions about high-dimensional spaces, projection, and the idea that semantic meaning resides in the geometry (distances/angles) of feature vectors, not just their final classification layer.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is minimizing Euclidean distance between raw teacher and student hidden states problematic in cross-modal KD, and how does projecting to a shared subspace address this?
- **Concept: Black-Box Model Interaction**
  - *Why needed here:* ARMADA explicitly handles black-box teachers (e.g., Midjourney). This requires understanding the constraints: only input/output (or, in this case, teacher-generated representations for given text) are accessible, not gradients or internal architecture. The TS Aligner must learn to map from the teacher's output modality (image latent) back to a space the student (text) can use.
  - *Quick check question:* If you can only get a vector representation from a teacher for a given text input (but can't backpropagate through the teacher), what architectural component must you introduce to enable knowledge transfer?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  **Teacher (Ft):** Frozen, black/white-box vision-language model (e.g., Stable Diffusion). Input: text. Output: multimodal representation (e.g., image latent vector `Xt`).
  2.  **TS Aligner (Fts):** Lightweight trainable module (0.8% params). Contains: `~Fts` (non-linear projection of teacher hidden state), `Pts` (manifold projection), `O_ts` (task output head), `O_aux^ts` (auxiliary output head).
  3.  **Student (Fs):** Frozen or fine-tunable language model (e.g., BERT, LLaMA). Input: text tokens `Xs`. Contains: `Ps` (student manifold projection), `O_s` (task output head), `O_aux^s` (auxiliary output head). `~F_s` is sometimes used.
  4.  **Data Flow:** Text `Xs` → Student → `h_X^s` → `~F_s` → `h'_X^s` → `P_s` → `p_X^s`. Same text `Xs` → Text-to-X Teacher → `X_t` → Teacher → `h_X^t` → `~F_ts` → `h'_X^{ts}` → `P_ts` → `p_X^{ts}`. Parallel predictions from `O_s(p_X^s)` and `O_ts(p_X^{ts})`.
- **Critical path:**
  1.  **Pre-processing:** For each training text `Xs`, query the frozen teacher to obtain its corresponding cross-modal representation `X_t` (e.g., SD latent).
  2.  **Forward Pass (Trainable):** Compute student hidden `h_X^s` and aligner hidden `h_X^t` (from teacher). Project both to manifolds `p_X^s`, `p_X^{ts}`. Get outputs `o_s`, `o_ts` and auxiliaries `o_aux^s`, `o_aux^{ts}`.
  3.  **Loss Calculation:** `L_total = L_s^(1)(ground truth, o_s) + α·logit_loss(o_ts, o_s) + β·L_manifold(p_X^{ts}, p_X^s) + γ·[L_aux^{ts} + L_aux^s]`.
  4.  **Backward:** Update *only* TS Aligner parameters and (if fine-tuning) Student `~F_s`, `P_s`, `O_s`, `O_aux^s` heads. Teacher remains frozen.
  5.  **Inference:** Student operates alone on text `Xs`; TS Aligner is discarded.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Parameter Efficiency vs. Capacity:** TS Aligner is intentionally shallow (0.8% params). More capacity (Table 11 ablation) *hurts* performance, suggesting over-parameterization on the aligner disrupts the delicate regularization signal.
  - **Manifold Loss Choice:** `L_elementwise` (most regularizing, Proposition 1) can be overly strict and degrade performance (Figure 4). `L_euclid` (centroid distance) offers a robust balance. `L_cosine` focuses on direction, not magnitude.
  - **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** β (manifold weight) is generally beneficial (γ=1, β=1 best in Figure 3). α=0 (no logit matching) often outperforms α=0.5, suggesting output alignment via the aligner's *own* predictions (`L_s^(1)`) is more critical than mimicking soft logits.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Aligner Underperforms:** If `TS Aligner` accuracy is very low (Table 6), it still can distill (BERT-6L gains 3.4%), but gains are unstable. A *trained* aligner is essential; a frozen one harms student performance (Figure 10).
  - **Teacher Input Noise:** Gaussian noise degrades student performance, showing reliance on teacher signal quality (Figure 11, 13). *But*, performance does not catastrophically collapse, indicating some regularization effect persists.
  - **Semantic Misalignment:** Shuffling teacher inputs (destroying sample correspondence) causes significant student degradation, proving improvements require structured, semantically aligned cross-modal knowledge (Table 17).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Baseline Sanity Check:** Train student *without* ARMADA (no TS Aligner) on a simple NLU task (e.g., CoLA). Record baseline. Then, train with frozen TS Aligner (random init). Expect significant drop vs. baseline, confirming the need for a trained aligner.
  2.  **Manifold Loss Ablation:** With α=0, γ=1 fixed, run ARMADA with `L_cosine`, `L_euclid`, `L_elementwise` on CoLA and COPA. Validate `L_euclid`'s robustness and `L_elementwise`'s potential over-regularization (Figure 4).
  3.  **Teacher Modality Swap:** Use two starkly different teachers (e.g., Stable Diffusion vs. SpeechT5 text-to-audio) on the same student/task. Verify ARMADA's consistent gains (Table 12, 13) and check for any modality-specific performance patterns via ANOVA (paper finds none significant).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why does cross-modal knowledge distillation from vision-language models improve language-only model performance despite the teacher's lack of explicit textual understanding?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion states: "Our findings challenge conventional knowledge distillation paradigms by demonstrating that even vision-language models, despite lacking direct textual understanding, can significantly enhance language models." The limitations section adds: "understanding how cross-modal distillation further refines their abilities... presents a fertile ground for future investigations." [inferred] While the paper provides theoretical justification via manifold alignment (Proposition 2), the exact nature of transferred knowledge—whether it is visual commonsense, spatial relationships, or grounded semantics—remains unclear.
- **Why unresolved:** The empirical results show consistent improvements across tasks, and the t-SNE visualizations indicate changes in representation space. However, the paper does not dissect *what specific semantic properties* are being transferred or *why* vision-based abstractions benefit pure language tasks. The proposed manifold alignment explains *how* knowledge is aligned but not *what* knowledge is useful.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled ablations using teachers with systematically varied representational content (e.g., low-level visual features vs. high-level semantic concepts), probing studies to identify which linguistic phenomena (syntax, semantics, pragmatics) improve post-distillation, and representation similarity analyses comparing teacher and student latent spaces before/after alignment.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What are the theoretical principles governing the optimal weighting of the three alignment losses (output, manifold, auxiliary) in ARMADA, and why does β=1 with α=0 yield the best results for NLU tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The ablation study (Section 5.1) reports that setting β=1 (full manifold alignment weight) improves 7 of 8 NLU tasks, while setting α=0 (removing logit-matching) gives a consistent 0.6% gain over α=0.5. The paper notes these empirical findings but offers no theoretical explanation. [inferred] The hyperparameters control the trade-off between task-specific supervision, geometric regularization, and auxiliary consistency, yet their interactions are not modeled.
- **Why unresolved:** Without theoretical guidance, practitioners must rely on expensive grid searches. The optimal configuration likely depends on task structure, model capacity, and teacher quality, but the paper does not establish any generalizable principles or bounds.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analytical study of the composite loss landscape and its effect on generalization bounds, sensitivity analysis across a diverse set of architectures (RNNs, Transformers) and modalities, and a formal treatment of how each loss component affects the student's representation invariance properties.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why do different teacher modalities (text-to-image, text-to-video, text-to-audio) yield statistically similar improvements in student performance despite representing distinct semantic spaces?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Tables 12 and 13 compare text-to-image (Stable Diffusion), text-to-video (ModelScope), and text-to-audio (SpeechT5) teachers. ANOVA tests show no significant difference in effectiveness for LLaMA-3B and LLaMA-8B students. The paper states: "ANOVA tests suggest that the teacher modalities might be equally effective." [inferred] This implies the transferred knowledge is highly abstract and modality-agnostic, but the underlying reason is not explored.
- **Why unresolved:** Intuitively, different generative models capture different aspects—spatial layout (images), temporal dynamics (video), acoustic properties (audio). Yet their distillation benefits are comparable. Is there a common abstraction space? Does the TS Aligner project all teachers into a similar manifold regardless of output modality?
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Correlation studies between teacher representation quality on multimodal benchmarks and student improvement, analysis of which semantic dimensions (e.g., objects, actions, events) from each teacher are most utilized by the student, and experiments with modality-corrupted teachers (e.g., shuffling video frames) to test the importance of temporal coherence.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does cross-modal distillation from vision-language teachers introduce unintended social biases into language models, and how can such biases be detected and mitigated?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The limitations section explicitly warns: "care must be taken to ensure that cross-modal knowledge transfer does not introduce unintended biases from vision models into language models, emphasizing the need for fairness and interpretability in future research." [inferred] This acknowledges a critical risk but provides no empirical investigation or mitigation strategies.
- **Why unresolved:** Vision-language models inherit biases from their training data (e.g., stereotypical gender-occupation associations in image generators). If these biases are encoded in the teacher's latent space and distilled into the student, they could exacerbate fairness issues in NLP models. The paper does not evaluate this.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Bias auditing of distilled models on established benchmarks (e.g., StereoSet, CrowS-Pairs) compared to undistilled baselines, probing for associations between demographic attributes and task performance, and development of bias-aware distillation objectives that selectively filter unwanted correlations.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How does the capacity of the TS Aligner relative to the student model affect the efficiency and effectiveness of cross-modal distillation, and is there a theoretical minimum capacity required?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The TS Aligner is deliberately lightweight (0.8% added parameters). Table 11 tests a higher-capacity variant (more hidden layers) without alignment mechanisms and finds it underperforms significantly, suggesting alignment structure matters more than raw capacity. However, the paper does not systematically vary TS Aligner size or analyze the capacity threshold needed for effective alignment.
- **Why unresolved:** Knowing the minimal aligner capacity would inform practical deployments. Is the current 0.8% sufficient for all teacher-student pairs? Could an even smaller aligner work? The trade-off between aligner complexity and distillation quality is not characterized.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic scaling experiments with TS Aligner width/depth, measuring student performance and training time, and theoretical analysis of the alignment problem's dimensionality to establish lower bounds on the aligner's expressive power.

## Limitations
- Teacher vector specification (dimensionality, preprocessing, reduction step) is unclear.
- Student's non-linear mapping and sharing of manifold projections/auxiliary heads are unspecified.
- The default manifold loss used in main results is not explicitly stated, potentially affecting replication.
