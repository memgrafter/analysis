# Agentic retrieval-augmented reasoning reshapes collective reliability under model variability in radiology question answering

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.06271
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06271
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Agentic retrieval-augmented reasoning reduced inter-model decision dispersion (median entropy 0.48→0.13) and increased robustness of correctness (mean 0.74→0.81) across 34 LLMs on radiology QA, with 72% of incorrect outputs carrying moderate or high severity.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines how agentic retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) influences the collective reliability of diverse large language models (LLMs) in radiology question answering. By comparing zero-shot inference with a standardized multi-step retrieval-augmented pipeline—where all 34 models receive identical evidence reports from curated knowledge—the study finds that agentic reasoning significantly lowers inter-model decision dispersion and boosts the fraction of models answering correctly. Majority consensus also strengthens and correlates highly with robustness. However, a substantial portion of incorrect answers retain moderate or high clinical severity, indicating that improved collective metrics do not eliminate safety-relevant errors.

The work demonstrates that shared, structured external knowledge can align heterogeneous models, but also warns that consensus can sometimes synchronize around incorrect answers. These findings highlight both the potential and the risks of agentic RAG for high-stakes medical applications.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 34 LLMs (open- and closed-source) on two expert-curated radiology QA datasets: Benchmark-RadQA (104 questions, 4 options) and Board-RadQA (65 questions, 5 options). In the zero-shot condition, models see only the question and options. In the agentic condition, a fixed orchestration pipeline generates a structured evidence report per question: keywords are extracted using Mistral Large, retrieval is performed via a locally hosted SearXNG instance restricted to Radiopaedia.org, and a neutral report is synthesized with GPT-4o-mini. All models then receive the identical report alongside the original question and options. Primary metrics are inter-model decision entropy (dispersion) and robustness of correctness (fraction of models selecting the reference answer). Paired statistical tests compare conditions across questions.

## Key Results
- Agentic inference reduced inter-model decision dispersion: median entropy dropped from 0.48 (zero-shot) to 0.13.
- Robustness of correctness improved: mean fraction of correct models increased from 0.74 to 0.81.
- 72% of incorrect outputs carried moderate or high clinical severity, despite stronger consensus (ρ=0.87).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Shared structured evidence aligns model outputs.**  
Claim: Agentic retrieval-augmented reasoning reduces inter-model decision dispersion by providing identical structured evidence to all models.  
Mechanism: A standardized pipeline extracts diagnostic concepts, retrieves from Radiopaedia, and synthesizes a neutral report, which is broadcast to every model, overriding individual retrieval variations.  
Core assumption: Diverse LLMs will converge toward similar answer modes when exposed to the same high-quality, structured external knowledge.  
Evidence: Entropy decreased significantly (median ΔH = -0.13, P=5.6e-9), with 68% of questions showing reduced dispersion.  
Break condition: If retrieved evidence is incomplete or misleading, shared context may fail to align models or synchronize errors (18% of questions showed increased entropy).

**Mechanism 2: Process standardization constrains reasoning variability.**  
Claim: Standardized multi-step retrieval and synthesis create a common intermediate representation that reduces model-specific reasoning variability.  
Mechanism: The fixed sequence (concept abstraction → retrieval → synthesis) is experienced identically by all models, guiding attention toward the synthesized evidence and limiting idiosyncratic pathways.  
Core assumption: Process standardization, not just content sharing, is necessary for cross-model consistency.  
Evidence: The orchestration was held fixed; all models received identical retrieved context. Verbosity (a proxy for reasoning depth) showed no association with correctness under agentic inference (Cliff’s δ ≈ 0), suggesting output structure decouples from validity.  
Break condition: If synthesis introduces bias, the common representation may propagate a single error mode, reducing robustness despite lower dispersion.

**Mechanism 3: Evidence-driven consensus amplifies correct majorities.**  
Claim: Agentic reasoning increases robustness of correctness by amplifying correct majorities more frequently than incorrect ones, through evidence-driven consensus formation.  
Mechanism: Shared structured evidence increases salience of diagnostically relevant facts, making correct answers more compelling to a larger fraction of models, shifting response distributions toward correctness.  
Core assumption: Consensus strength and robustness are positively coupled.  
Evidence: Robustness improved in 27% of questions, high-robustness bin increased from 50% to 72%. Correct-majority questions had much higher median majority fractions than incorrect-majority ones (e.g., 0.97 vs. 0.59 under agentic).  
Break condition: In rare cases (1–2% of questions), high consensus coexists with low robustness (M≥0.8 & R<0.4), indicating agreement can synchronize around incorrect answers due to ambiguous evidence or base-rate heuristics.

## Foundational Learning
- **Inter-model decision entropy**: Measures dispersion of model predictions; lower entropy means higher agreement. *Why needed*: Primary metric for decision dispersion. *Quick check*: If 30/34 models choose option A and 4 choose option B in a 4-option question, Shannon entropy ≈ 0.50 (moderate dispersion despite strong majority).
- **Robustness of correctness**: Fraction of models selecting the reference answer; indicates cross-model reproducibility of correct answers. *Why needed*: Captures collective reliability beyond single-model accuracy. *Quick check*: If 20/34 models are correct, robustness score ≈ 0.59 (medium robustness).
- **Majority fraction and coupling**: Majority fraction (M) quantifies consensus strength; coupling (ρ) with robustness indicates that stronger consensus tends to correlate with higher fractions of correct models. *Why needed*: Relates agreement to correctness. *Quick check*: Spearman ρ=0.88 implies questions with stronger consensus tend to have higher robustness.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Orchestrator (planner/coordinator) -> Concept abstractor (keyword extraction from question) -> Retriever (SearXNG + Radiopaedia filter) -> Synthesizer (report writer, GPT-4o-mini) -> Target LLM (any of 34 models) -> Answer selection

**Critical path**: Question → abstracted keywords (via Mistral Large) → per-option retrieval from Radiopaedia → evidence synthesis into structured report (by GPT-4o-mini) → report + question + options → target model generates final answer

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Shared context vs. diversity loss*: Identical reports ensure controlled comparison but eliminate retrieval diversity, potentially reducing complementary error correction.
- *Structured vs. flexible evidence*: Standardized report format improves reproducibility but may omit nuanced context needed by some models.
- *Centralized orchestration vs. distributed agents*: Single orchestrator simplifies evaluation but creates a single point of failure; misleading evidence synchronizes all models.

**Failure signatures**:
- Increased entropy: Disagreement rises under agentic inference; check for ambiguous or conflicting retrieved content.
- High-consensus low-robustness: M≥0.8 & R<0.4; indicates coordinated incorrect convergence.
- Robustness collapse: Large negative ΔR (e.g., ΔR < -0.5); signals evidence-induced model coordination on wrong answer.
- Severity heterogeneity: High clinical severity in incorrect outputs despite high consensus; implies consensus is not a safety proxy.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Baseline stability**: Compute zero-shot entropy and robustness for all questions; identify naturally stable vs. fragile questions.
2. **Agentic effect measurement**: Apply agentic pipeline, compute new entropy, robustness, and majority fraction; perform paired Wilcoxon tests to confirm overall shifts.
3. **Failure mode audit**: Filter questions with ΔH>0 (increased dispersion) or ΔR<-0.3 (robustness collapse); manually inspect retrieved reports and model outputs to identify evidence quality issues or prompt-induced biases.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Which pipeline components drive entropy reduction?** Ablation studies are needed to disentangle which parts (e.g., retrieval depth, report format) contribute to robustness gains vs. synchronized failures. The fixed design prevents component-level attribution.
- **Does robustness improvement generalize to other retrieval sources?** The study used only Radiopaedia; different sources (e.g., PubMed, guidelines) may yield different stability patterns. Replication with multiple sources is required.
- **How do multimodal inputs affect collective reliability?** The work excluded imaging data; extending the framework to vision-language models on radiology QA (text + images) is necessary to assess generalizability.

## Limitations
- Reproduction depends on unreleased supplementary details (exact prompts, retrieval parameters) and evolving model versions, risking drift in evidence generation and outputs.
- The clinical severity analysis is correlational and lacks mechanistic safety validation; high-severity errors persist despite improved collective metrics.
- Corpus support is weak (average citations near zero), limiting external generalizability of findings.

## Confidence
- Dispersion reduction via identical evidence: **High** (direct entropy metrics, controlled design).
- Consensus-robustness coupling: **Medium** (strong ρ=0.87 but 1–2% of questions show high consensus with low robustness).
- Persistent high-severity errors: **Low** (descriptive; no analysis of why consensus fails to prevent dangerous errors).

## Next Checks
1. **Evidence fidelity audit**: Hash-compare synthesized reports against authors' cached outputs to confirm identical context across models; investigate mismatches.
2. **Failure mode deep dive**: Manually inspect the 1–2% of questions with M≥0.8 & R<0.4 to identify evidence ambiguities or biases driving coordinated errors.
3. **Generalization probe**: Re-run the pipeline on an external radiology QA dataset (e.g., MIMIC-CXR-JPG) to test whether entropy reduction and robustness gains replicate.
