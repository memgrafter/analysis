# Rel-MOSS: Towards Imbalanced Relational Deep Learning on Relational Databases

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07916
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07916
- **Reference Count:** 27
- **Primary Result:** Rel-MOSS improves Balanced Accuracy by 2.46% and G-Mean by 4.00% over state-of-the-art methods across 12 RelBench imbalanced entity classification datasets.

## Executive Summary
Rel-MOSS is a novel framework for handling severe class imbalance in relational deep learning (RDL) tasks, where target entities reside in relational databases and are classified using graph neural networks. It introduces two key components: a relation-wise gating controller (Rel-Gate) that modulates messages from each relation type to amplify minority-discriminative signals during neighborhood aggregation, and a relation-guided minority synthesizer (Rel-Syn) that generates synthetic minority entities while preserving their local relational structure. Evaluated on the RelBench suite, Rel-MOSS achieves consistent and substantial gains in balanced accuracy and G-Mean, demonstrating the efficacy of addressing imbalance with relational awareness.

## Method Summary
Rel-MOSS augments a standard heterogeneous GNN backbone (e.g., GraphSAGE) with two modules. **Rel-Gate** sits between the backbone's relation-wise message computation and aggregation. For each relation type *r*, it uses a cross-attention mechanism over the target entity's representation and its relation-specific neighborhood to compute a gating factor Ψ<sub>e,r</sub>. This factor modulates the raw neighborhood message H<sub>e,r</sub>, enhancing signals predictive of the minority class before aggregation. **Rel-Syn** combats data scarcity for minority entities. It maintains a memory bank storing representations (X<sub>e</sub>) and relational signatures (S<sub>e</sub>)—handcrafted descriptors of local structure like neighbor type histograms—for seen minority nodes. During training, for a minority entity *e*, Rel-Syn finds its nearest neighbor *e*' in the bank using a distance metric that combines feature and signature dissimilarity. It then interpolates both the feature and signature to create a synthetic minority sample (X<sub>syn</sub>, S<sub>syn</sub>) that is added to the training batch. The model is trained with a composite loss: binary cross-entropy for classification and a mean squared error loss that reconstructs the relational signature from a fused entity representation, encouraging the latent space to align with structural roles.

## Key Results
- Rel-MOSS achieves an average **2.46% improvement in Balanced Accuracy** and **4.00% improvement in G-Mean** over the strongest RDL and classic imbalance-handling baselines on 12 RelBench datasets.
- Ablation studies show **removing Rel-Syn causes catastrophic performance collapse** (near-random G-Mean), while removing Rel-Gate causes a moderate drop, indicating Rel-Syn as the primary driver.
- Rel-Syn's synthetic minority samples exhibit **stronger structural fidelity** (better manifold overlap with real minorities) compared to non-relational oversamplers like SMOTE and GraphSMOTE.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Rel-Gate Prevents Minority Signal Collapse
- **Claim:** The relation-wise gating controller (Rel-Gate) prevents minority information collapse during message passing.
- **Mechanism:** For each relation type *r*, Rel-Gate estimates a minority-leaning likelihood score Ψ<sub>e,r</sub> using cross-attention over the target entity’s representation and its relation-specific neighborhood. This gating factor modulates the raw neighborhood message H<sub>e,r</sub>, enhancing minority-relevant signals while suppressing majority-dominant ones before aggregation.
- **Core assumption:** Minority entities receive proportionally fewer minority neighbors across relations (π<sub>e,r</sub> ≪ 1), leading to a contraction in their discriminative signal across GNN layers under standard uniform aggregation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "modulate neighborhood messages from each individual relation type to amplify minority-discriminative signals"; [section 4.2, Proposition 4.1] Formal proof shows exponential decay of minority signal norm.
- **Break condition:** If minority neighbors are not statistically rare (π<sub>e,r</sub> ≈ 0.5) for a critical relation, gating provides no contraction benefit and may introduce noise.

### Mechanism 2: Rel-Syn Preserves Relational Consistency
- **Claim:** The relation-guided minority synthesizer (Rel-Syn) generates structurally faithful synthetic minority entities.
- **Mechanism:** For a minority entity *e*, Rel-Syn computes a distance metric D(e, e') = ∥X<sub>e</sub> - X<sub>e'</sub>∥² + ω·∥S<sub>e</sub> - S<sub>e'</sub>∥², where S<sub>e</sub> is a relational signature (e.g., neighbor type histograms). It finds the nearest minority neighbor e* in a memory bank and interpolates both representations and signatures.
- **Core assumption:** Entity labels in RDBs are primarily determined by local relational structure (signature S<sub>e</sub>); interpolating without S risks generating entities with implausible structural roles.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "over-samples minority entities while preserving relational consistency through relational signatures"; [section 4.3, Proposition 4.2] Synthesis without structural constraints induces a distribution shift in relational roles.
- **Break condition:** If relational signatures S<sub>e</sub> are poorly chosen or the memory bank lacks diverse minority prototypes, synthetic samples may violate relational consistency.

### Mechanism 3: Joint Optimization with Signature Reconstruction
- **Claim:** A relational signature reconstruction loss enhances the utility of relational signatures for classification.
- **Mechanism:** Alongside classification loss (BCE), Rel-MOSS minimizes MSE between a fused representation (ϕ(W<sub>x</sub>X<sub>e</sub> + W<sub>s</sub>S<sub>e</sub>)) and the original signature S<sub>e</sub>. This encourages the gated representation X<sub>e</sub> to encode information predictive of the relational signature.
- **Core assumption:** The entity’s gated representation can be trained to be predictive of its relational signature without compromising classification, and this alignment benefits minority class learning where structural cues are critical.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "introduces a reconstruction objective for the relational signatures"; [section 5.4, hyperparameter γ analysis] Shows stable improvements for γ ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1, 2}.
- **Break condition:** If γ is too large (e.g., 5, 10), classification performance degrades due to overfitting to signature reconstruction.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Class imbalance in relational deep learning (RDL).
  - **Why needed:** Standard RDL methods fail on imbalanced RDB entity classification; the problem was previously unstudied.
  - **Quick check:** Given a heterogeneous graph from an RDB where 95% of target entities are class 0, what naive metric might a standard GNN classifier accidentally optimize to, and why? *Answer: It may optimize for majority-class accuracy, ignoring minority recall, due to loss being dominated by majority samples.*

- **Concept:** Relational signatures as structural role descriptors.
  - **Why needed:** Rel-Syn relies on S<sub>e</sub> to constrain generation; the paper argues entity labels in RDBs are more determined by local relational structure than by entity-attached features.
  - **Quick check:** If you only have an entity’s feature vector and its immediate neighbor type counts (e.g., 3 Reviews, 1 Product), why might this "signature" be more reliable than the feature vector for classification in an RDB? *Answer: Because feature vectors may be sparse or noisy, while the neighbor type count directly encodes the entity's functional role in the database schema (e.g., a "Product" typically has many "Review" neighbors), which is often a more consistent predictor of its label.*

- **Concept:** Relation-wise modulation vs. uniform aggregation.
  - **Why needed:** Rel-Gate’s core innovation; standard heterogeneous GNNs assume equal importance per relation, which submerges minority signals.
  - **Quick check:** In a fraud detection RDB, how might the "wrote_review" relation provide different predictive signal strength for a minority (fraud) user versus a majority (legit) user? *Answer: Fraudulent users might write fewer or unusually patterned reviews; Rel-Gate could up-weight this relation for suspicious entities to amplify the weak fraud signal, while down-weighting it for normal users where the relation is less informative.*

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Input RDB -> Heterogeneous Entity Graph G -> Modality-specific Encoders (E<sub>k</sub>) -> Unified Node Features X<sub>e</sub> -> GNN Backbone (per-relation messages H<sub>e,r</sub>) -> Rel-Gate (gated messages Ψ<sub>e,r</sub>⊙H<sub>e,r</sub>) -> Aggregated Representation -> Rel-Syn (synthesis for minority entities) -> Concatenated Batch (real + synthetic) -> Classification Head (f<sub>CLS</sub>) & Signature Reconstruction Head (f<sub>Syn</sub>) -> Composite Loss (BCE + γ·MSE)

**Critical path:**
1. Encode raw RDB rows → X<sub>e</sub>.
2. Run GNN propagation to get H<sub>e,r</sub> for each relation.
3. Apply Rel-Gate: compute Ψ<sub>e,r</sub> for each *r*, gate H<sub>e,r</sub>, aggregate → updated X<sub>e</sub>.
4. For minority entities *e*, extract relational signature S<sub>e</sub>.
5. Rel-Syn: query memory bank with (X<sub>e</sub>, S<sub>e</sub>) → find nearest minority e* → generate (X<sub>syn</sub>, S<sub>syn</sub>).
6. Concatenate real and synthetic entities into batch.
7. Compute classification BCE on all entities using X<sub>e</sub>.
8. Compute signature reconstruction MSE on (original + synthetic) minorities using fused ˜X<sub>e</sub>.
9. Backpropagate total loss; update memory bank (FIFO) with current minority (X<sub>e</sub>, S<sub>e</sub>).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Rel-Gate parameters:** Adds |R| relation embeddings and Q/K/V projections. Cost is O(|R|·B²d) per layer. Benefit: Focused message passing for minority. Risk: Over-gating may drown useful majority patterns.
- **Rel-Syn memory bank:** Size U controls diversity vs. noise. Larger U increases finding a good e* but risks stale prototypes. FIFO update ensures bank reflects recent state.
- **Loss weighting γ:** Balances classification vs. signature reconstruction. γ=0 ablates L<sub>Syn</sub>; too high harms classification (Fig 6). Optimal γ ∈ [0.1,2].
- **Signature design:** S<sub>e</sub> must be cheap and discriminative. Paper uses 1-hop/2-hop type histograms and fan-in/out; complex signatures increase compute and risk overfitting.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Rel-Gate failure:** No improvement in representation distance between minority/majority centroids. Check Ψ<sub>e,r</sub> distribution—if near-uniform or saturated, gating is ineffective.
- **Rel-Syn failure:** Synthetic samples (t-SNE) deviate from true minority manifold. Check distance D(e, e*); if ω=0, samples scatter. Verify memory bank diversity.
- **Training instability:** If γ or ω too large, L<sub>Syn</sub> dominates, classification loss plateaus. Monitor BCE vs. MSE loss scales.
- **Backbone incompatibility:** Rel-Gate assumes per-relation messages H<sub>e,r</sub>. Integration with a backbone without explicit relation-type separation requires adaptation.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline comparison (RQ1):** Run Rel-MOSS vs. RDL, RDL-Focal, SMOTE, GraphSMOTE, GraphSHA, ReVar on 12 RelBench datasets. Measure B-Acc and G-Mean.
2. **Ablation studies (RQ3):** Run *w/o Rel-Gate* and *w/o Rel-Syn*. Expected: Removing Rel-Syn causes catastrophic failure; removing Rel-Gate causes modest drop.
3. **Relational consistency check (RQ4):** Visualize t-SNE of synthetic vs. real minority entities for Rel-MOSS vs. SMOTE/GraphSMOTE. Also compute centroid Euclidean/Manhattan distances.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can the principles of Rel-MOSS be extended to address numerical distribution imbalance in relational entity regression tasks, where target values are continuous rather than categorical?
- *Basis:* [explicit] Limitation section states future direction for entity regression tasks.

**Open Question 2:** To what extent are the performance gains of Rel-MOSS dependent on the specific relational signatures used, and could alternative or learned structural representations further improve relational consistency?
- *Basis:* [inferred] Relational signatures are handcrafted heuristics; their optimality and potential for learned replacements are unexplored.

**Open Question 3:** Does the relation-wise gating mechanism implicitly learn to up-weight relations that are more predictive of minority classes, and can this be validated through relation importance analysis?
- *Basis:* [explicit] Rel-Gate computes Ψ<sub>e,r</sub> to estimate minority-leaning likelihood, but no analysis shows which relations are typically gated.

**Open Question 4:** Can Rel-Syn be decoupled from the memory bank mechanism, and what is the precise role of the memory bank's scale (U) and update strategy (FIFO) in preventing mode collapse?
- *Basis:* [inferred] Memory bank design (size U, FIFO) is empirical; dynamics and impact on diversity are unstudied.

**Open Question 5:** Is the observed superiority of Rel-MOSS primarily driven by Rel-Syn, Rel-Gate, or their combination, and how does this vary with relational complexity?
- *Basis:* [explicit] Ablations suggest Rel-Syn is fundamental, but this may depend on dataset/backbone; a systematic study across varying relational complexity is missing.

## Limitations
- **Unspecified architectural details:** The exact architecture (e.g., MLP depth, activations) of the likelihood estimator ψ in Rel-Gate is not specified.
- **Ambiguous signature definition:** The precise computation, normalization, and dimensionality of the relational signature S<sub>e</sub> remain unclear, risking inconsistent reproduction.
- **Conditional assumption:** The theoretical guarantee for Rel-Gate assumes minority entities have few minority neighbors (π<sub>e,r</sub> ≪ 1) across relations, which may not hold in all relational settings.
