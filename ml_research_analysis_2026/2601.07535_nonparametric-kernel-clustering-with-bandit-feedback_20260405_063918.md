# Nonparametric Kernel Clustering with Bandit Feedback

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.07535
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07535
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** KABC is δ-PAC with total sampling budget τ ≤ O(N · (1/s²_*) · log(N/δ)), where s²_* is an instance-dependent signal-to-noise ratio based on maximum mean discrepancy gaps and RKHS variances.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses active clustering of N arms in a stochastic bandit without parametric assumptions. Existing methods require sub-Gaussian distributions and linearly separable clusters, limiting applicability. The proposed KABC algorithm uses kernel mean embeddings in an RKHS to represent arm distributions. Pairwise two-sample tests based on maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) with variance-aware thresholds determine if two arms share the same distribution. The algorithm adaptively increases per-arm sampling budgets until a graph-based clustering yields exactly K known clusters. KABC is δ-PAC and achieves instance-dependent sample complexity without requiring prior knowledge of the signal-to-noise ratio.

## Method Summary
KABC operates in iterations with geometrically increasing per-arm sample sizes n_k = ⌈2^k log(8(N^2−N)/δ_k)⌉ and confidence δ_k = δ/(4k^2). In each iteration, it collects n_k samples from every arm, computes empirical kernel mean embeddings and RKHS variances, and evaluates pairwise MMD against a variance-adjusted threshold. An undirected graph is built where edges connect arms whose MMD is within the threshold; connected components define the clustering. If the number of components equals K, the algorithm stops. The doubling trick ensures that total error probability sums to at most δ, and the sample size eventually exceeds the instance-dependent requirement, yielding PAC guarantees.

## Key Results
- KABC outputs the correct partition with probability at least 1−δ.
- Total sampling budget τ satisfies τ ≤ O(N · (1/s²_*) · log(N/δ)) with high probability, where s²_* = min_{i≠j, µ_i≠µ_j} ( ||µ_i−µ_j||² / (V_i* ∨ V_j*) ∧ 2||µ_i−µ_j||√ḡ ).
- The algorithm adapts to unknown s²_* and does not require it as input.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Lossless representation via characteristic kernel
- **Claim:** The kernel mean embedding (KME) provides a lossless Hilbert-space representation of each arm's distribution when the kernel is characteristic.
- **Mechanism:** Each arm’s distribution ν_i is mapped to its mean embedding µ_i = E_{X∼ν_i}[φ(X)] in the RKHS H. If kernel g is characteristic, this map is injective, so µ_i = µ_j iff ν_i = ν_j. Thus, grouping arms with identical distributions is equivalent to grouping by equality of embeddings.
- **Core assumption:** Kernel g is characteristic, bounded, and translation-invariant.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] “adopt a kernel‑based approach…” ; [Section 2] “The characteristic property of g implies…” ; corpus context shows no prior use of KME in bandit clustering.

### Mechanism 2: Variance-aware MMD threshold
- **Claim:** A variance-aware threshold derived from empirical RKHS variances yields a high-probability bound for MMD deviation, enabling reliable pairwise two-sample tests.
- **Mechanism:** For per-arm budget n, compute empirical KMEs µ̂_i and empirical variances V̂_i. The threshold B_{i,j}(n,δ') = (√V̂_i+√V̂_j)√(2 log(8(N^2−N)/δ')/n) + (32/3)√(g̃ log(8(N^2−N)/δ')/n) satisfies P( |‖µ̂_i−µ̂_j‖ - ‖µ_i−µ_j‖| ≤ B_{i,j} ) ≥ 1−δ'/(N^2−N). Reject H_0: µ_i=µ_j when ‖µ̂_i−µ̂_j‖ > B_{i,j}.
- **Core assumption:** Finite RKHS variance V_i* and bounded characteristic translation-invariant kernel.
- **Evidence anchors:** [Section 3] equation (6); [Appendix A.3] Lemmas A.2, A.3, A.5; reference to Wolfer & Alquier (2025).

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive doubling schedule
- **Claim:** A doubling-trick schedule with per-iteration confidence δ_k = δ/(4k^2) adapts to unknown s_*^2 and yields a δ-PAC algorithm with near-optimal total sample complexity.
- **Mechanism:** KABC loops for k≥1 with n_k and δ_k as defined, calls CLUSTER(n_k,δ_k). If the graph has exactly K connected components, it stops. Union bound over δ_k gives total error ≤ δ. For any instance with s_*^2>0, eventually n_k ≥ 128 s_*^{-2} log(8(N^2−N)/δ_k), after which error at that iteration ≤ δ_k, ensuring correctness. No prior knowledge of s_*^2 required.
- **Core assumption:** s_*^2 > 0 (positive MMD separation between any distinct clusters).
- **Evidence anchors:** [Algorithm 2] doubling logic; [Theorem 3.1] δ-PAC and sample complexity bound; [Section 3] adaptivity statement.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS):**  
  Why needed: KMEs live in an RKHS; its geometry determines MMD and concentration bounds.  
  Quick check: What property of a kernel ensures that its mean embedding uniquely identifies any probability distribution, and why?  
  *Answer: Characteristicness (injectivity).*

- **Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD):**  
  Why needed: MMC is the metric for pairwise hypothesis testing; it equals the RKHS norm difference of mean embeddings.  
  Quick check: For two arms with mean embeddings µ_i and µ_j, how is the MMD expressed?  
  *Answer: MMD(ν_i,ν_j) = ‖µ_i - µ_j‖_H.*

- **Doubling Trick for Confidence Allocation:**  
  Why needed: KABC uses geometrically increasing sample sizes with per-iteration confidence summing to δ, enabling adaptation to unknown instance difficulty.  
  Quick check: Why does δ_k = δ/(4k^2) guarantee ∑ δ_k ≤ δ?  
  *Answer: Because ∑_{k=1}^∞ 1/k^2 = π^2/6 < ∞, so δ ∑ 1/(4k^2) ≤ δ/4 * (π^2/6) < δ (more precisely, δ/(4) * (π^2/6) ≈ 0.411δ < δ).*

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
KABC main loop -> CLUSTER subroutine -> (Sampler, Statistics, Thresholder, GraphBuilder, ConnectedComponents) -> StoppingEvaluator.

**Critical path:**  
1. Sampling: N * n_k pulls.  
2. Per-arm statistics: O(N n_k) kernel evaluations for µ̂_i and V̂_i.  
3. Pairwise tests: O(N^2) MMD and threshold calculations, O(N^2) edge decisions.  
4. Connected components: nearly O(N) (e.g., union-find).  
Because n_k grows geometrically, total cost dominated by final iteration.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Uniform vs. targeted sampling: All arms equally sampled per iteration for analysis simplicity; could improve efficiency but breaks uniform concentration.
- Threshold choice: Variance-aware bound tighter than sub-Gaussian but requires computing V̂_i; simpler looser threshold increases sample complexity.
- Graph connectivity: Using connected components provides robustness to missing edges but too lenient thresholds may merge clusters.
- Batched vs. online: Current batch approach allows clean concentration; online reuse could reduce pulls but requires adapted analysis.

**Failure signatures:**
1. No convergence to K clusters after many iterations: likely s_*^2 too small, non-characteristic kernel, or bug in threshold logic.
2. Incorrect final partition: check union bound over δ_k, correct δ_k = δ/(4k^2), and that n_k eventually satisfies Lemma A.1 condition.
3. Memory overflow for large N: O(N^2) adjacency matrix; switch to on-the-fly edge generation and sparse union-find.
4. Negative empirical variance due to floating-point: clamp V̂_i to small positive.
5. Excessive runtime: pairwise loop bottleneck; consider early dismissal using cheap lower bounds or exploit expected sparsity.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Gaussian sanity check: N=6, K=3, two arms per mean in ℝ² with Gaussians, linear kernel truncated. Verify success rate ≥ 1−δ and scaling of average total pulls.
2. Non-Gaussian Beta test: N=4, K=2, Beta(2,5) vs Beta(5,2), RBF kernel bandwidth 0.3. Confirm correct clustering.
3. Scalability stress: N=500, K=5, mixed distributions (Gaussian, Beta, mixture). Measure wall-clock time, memory, and stopping iteration; assess O(N^2) behavior.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Automatic determination of K:** The paper assumes K known. Without K, the algorithm never terminates. Under additional separability assumptions, K might be learnable via sequential hypothesis testing on graph connectivity.
2. **Minimax optimality:** The paper provides an upper bound O(N/s_*^2 log(N/δ)) but no lower bound. A matching lower bound via reduction to two-sample testing or bandit pure exploration is needed to confirm optimality.
3. **Reducing computational complexity:** Current complexity O(N^2) per iteration. A sequential evaluation approach is suggested but not developed; it is unclear if it maintains PAC guarantees and sample efficiency.
4. **Relaxing kernel assumptions:** The algorithm requires bounded, characteristic, translation-invariant kernels. For unbounded kernels (e.g., linear), new concentration inequalities or modifications (e.g., truncation) are needed for PAC guarantees.

## Limitations
- Kernel choice and hyperparameters (bandwidth) significantly affect MMD and variance estimates, with no practical selection guidance provided.
- O(N^2) pairwise tests and graph construction may be infeasible for large N (e.g., >10^4).
- Empirical RKHS variance estimates can be noisy for small per-arm budgets, potentially causing early errors.
- Guarantees require strictly positive separation s_*^2; extremely small gaps lead to prohibitively large sample needs.
- Theoretical assumptions (characteristic, bounded, translation-invariant kernels) are critical and may not hold in practice.

## Confidence
- δ-PAC correctness: High (solid union-bound argument under assumptions).
- Instance-dependent sample complexity: Medium (upper bound proven, but tightness of constants unclear).
- Adaptivity without s_*^2: High (doubling trick well-understood and analyzed).
- Variance-aware threshold validity: Medium (depends on finite RKHS variance and kernel bounds; empirical noise possible).
- Lossless representation via characteristic kernel: High (standard property).
