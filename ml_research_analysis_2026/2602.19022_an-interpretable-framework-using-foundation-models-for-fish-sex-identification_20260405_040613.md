# An interpretable framework using foundation models for fish sex identification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.19022
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19022
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: FishProtoNet achieves stage-dependent accuracy: 74.40% (F1 74.27%) for early spawning, 81.16% (F1 79.43%) for post-spawning, and 54.76% for subadults.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FishProtoNet, an interpretable framework for non-invasive sex identification of endangered delta smelt across life stages. It leverages zero-shot fish region extraction using visual foundation models (Grounding DINO and SAM2) to remove background bias, followed by a prototype-based neural network for classification. The approach achieves competitive accuracy (up to 81.16% in post-spawning) while providing biological interpretability through learned prototypes. However, performance drops significantly for subadults (54.76%), highlighting challenges due to minimal morphological dimorphism at that stage.

## Method Summary
The framework first extracts fish regions from images without species-specific training: Grounding DINO generates bounding boxes via a "fish" text prompt, and SAM2 produces segmentation masks. These masks are then augmented (rotation, flipping, cropping, random erasing) to improve robustness. A ResNet-50 backbone extracts features, which are compared to learned class prototypes in a neural decision tree (ProtoTree) to produce sex predictions and explanations.

## Key Results
- Early spawning stage: 74.40% accuracy (F1 74.27%)
- Post-spawning stage: 81.16% accuracy (F1 79.43%)
- Subadult stage: 54.76% accuracy, indicating limited morphological distinctiveness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Zero-shot fish ROI extraction using Grounding DINO and SAM2 minimizes background bias, leading to more discriminative features for sex classification.
- **Mechanism**: Grounding DINO generates fish bounding boxes via text prompt "fish" without species-specific training, then SAM2 segments the fish mask from these boxes. This removes background elements (e.g., tank bottom, VIA tags) that could bias the classifier, ensuring that feature extraction focuses solely on fish morphology.
- **Core assumption**: Background features are non-predictive of sex and can be safely removed; foundation models generalize well to unseen fish images without fine-tuning.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "using visual foundation models (Grounding DINO + SAM2) for zero-shot fish region extraction to minimize background bias"
  - [section]: Section 2.1.1 details the ROI extraction pipeline; Section 4.1 shows that without ROI extraction, class activation mapping highlights background regions.
  - [corpus]: Neighbor papers (e.g., "Detecting Endangered Marine Species...") use few-shot learning for detection but not zero-shot ROI extraction; evidence for this specific mechanism is weak in the corpus.
- **Break condition**: If Grounding DINO fails to detect the fish (e.g., due to poor contrast) or SAM2 produces inaccurate masks (e.g., missing fins), critical morphological cues may be lost, degrading performance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Prototype-based classification (FishProtoNet) provides interpretable decision-making and maintains competitive accuracy by matching features to learned prototypes.
- **Mechanism**: The model learns prototype vectors representing typical male/female features in a metric space. A neural decision tree routes the input based on similarity to prototypes at each node, and leaf nodes output class probabilities. This allows visualization of which prototype patterns are activated, explaining the prediction.
- **Core assumption**: Sex differences in delta smelt correspond to consistent visual patterns that can be captured by prototypes; a metric-based comparison is sufficient for discrimination.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "coupled with a prototype-based neural network for interpretable classification"
  - [section]: Section 2.1.4 describes the prototype learning and neural tree; Section 4.2 visualizes learned prototypes and decision paths for test images.
  - [corpus]: The ProtoTree method (Nauta et al., 2021) is cited; corpus neighbors do not directly investigate prototype learning for fish sex, so evidence is indirect.
- **Break condition**: If the training data lacks diversity, prototypes may not cover the full variation of sexes, leading to poor generalization. Also, the tree depth may limit model capacity for complex distinctions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Systematic data augmentation (including random erasing) improves robustness to imperfect ROI masks and environmental variations, boosting classification accuracy.
- **Mechanism**: Augmentations like rotation, flipping, cropping, and random erasing are applied to fish ROIs. Random erasing simulates incomplete masks or obscures VIA tags, forcing the model to rely on distributed features rather than specific artifacts.
- **Core assumption**: Discriminative morphological features are distributed across the fish body, so partial occlusion does not eliminate all cues; augmentation does not destroy essential patterns.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Systematic data augmentation strategies were systematically selected to improve model robustness"
  - [section]: Section 2.1.2 lists augmentation operations; Table 1 shows that combining rotation, flip, crop, and erasing yields highest accuracy (81.82% in Session 3) compared to base (71.01%).
  - [corpus]: Random erasing is established (Zhong et al., 2020), but its application to fish ROI masks is novel; corpus provides no direct evidence for this mechanism.
- **Break condition**: If augmentation is too aggressive (e.g., erasing large regions covering key features), it can remove discriminative information. Also, if sex cues are highly localized (e.g., only on genital region), random erasing may hurt performance.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:
  - ROI extraction: Grounding DINO (text-to-bounding-box) + SAM2 (mask generation)
  - Data augmentation: resizing, normalization, padding, plus rotation, flip, crop, random erasing
  - Feature extraction: ResNet-50 backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet)
  - Classification: Neural prototype tree (soft decision tree with learned prototypes)
- **Critical path**: Input image → Grounding DINO (text "fish") → bounding boxes → SAM2 → fish mask (ROI) → augmentation → ResNet feature extraction → prototype similarity computation → neural tree routing → sex probability
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Foundation models vs. trained detector: Zero-shot reduces labeling cost but may be less accurate on domain-specific nuances.
  - Prototype tree vs. fully connected layer: Interpretability vs. potential accuracy (ViT outperforms FishProtoNet in some sessions).
  - ResNet backbone vs. ViT: Simpler and fewer parameters vs. higher representational capacity.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor ROI masks (e.g., missing fins, including background) → check Grounding DINO detections and SAM2 masks.
  - Low accuracy on subadults (Session 1) → likely due to insufficient morphological differences; consider multimodal imaging.
  - Overfitting on training data → monitor validation loss; if training loss decreases but validation plateaus, increase augmentation or reduce model complexity.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Visual inspection of ROI extraction: Manually review a sample of fish masks to ensure they capture the fish without excessive background.
  2. Data augmentation ablation: Train with different augmentation combinations (as in Table 1) to identify the most beneficial operations.
  3. Baseline comparison: Fine-tune ResNet-50 and ViT on the same data to benchmark performance and justify the prototype approach.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Can multimodal imaging (e.g., infrared, ultrasound) overcome the morphological indistinctiveness in subadult delta smelt for accurate sex identification?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "Incorporating multi-modal imaging data, such as infrared or ultrasound images, may help capture additional physiological cues and further enhance fish sex identification accuracy for individuals at this stage."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only suggests this as future work without empirical validation on subadult specimens.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A controlled study comparing FishProtoNet's performance using visual-only vs. multimodal inputs (e.g., adding thermal/ultrasound channels) on the subadult dataset, with statistical testing of accuracy gains.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What specific factors cause the zero-shot foundation models (Grounding DINO + SAM2) to generate incomplete or unsatisfactory fish ROIs, and how do these failures propagate to sex classification errors?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper notes "some uncomplete margin shape segmented of fish" and uses random erasing to mitigate, but lacks systematic error analysis of ROI extraction failure modes.
- **Why unresolved**: The framework treats ROI extraction as a black box; failure modes (e.g., truncation, background leakage) and their quantitative impact on downstream classification are not measured.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An error analysis correlating ROI quality metrics (e.g., mask IoU with ground-truth fish outlines, completeness scores) with classification accuracy, identifying which failure patterns most degrade performance.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Would integrating a Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone into FishProtoNet's feature extraction module improve sex identification accuracy across all life stages, and does it maintain interpretability?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "In future work, integrating a ViT backbone into FishProtoNet as the feature extraction module may further improve performance."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only compares ViT as a separate baseline; a hybrid prototype-ViT architecture is untested, and the trade-off between accuracy gains and prototype interpretability is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Train and evaluate a FishProtoNet variant with a ViT feature extractor (keeping the prototype tree head), comparing accuracy/F1 against the ResNet-50 version, and assess interpretability via prototype visualization quality and biologist evaluation.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Do the learned prototypes in FishProtoNet provide meaningful, domain-relevant explanations to biologists, or are they merely post-hoc approximations unrelated to known sexual dimorphism?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper emphasizes interpretability for biologists but does not involve them in evaluating whether prototypes correspond to biologically meaningful features (e.g., gonad size, body shape differences).
- **Why unresolved**: Interpretability claims are based on prototype visualization without user studies; faithfulness to actual biological reasoning is unverified.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conduct a user study where biologists (1) match prototypes to known dimorphic traits and (2) use prototype explanations to predict sex on new images, measuring agreement with model predictions and ground truth.

## Limitations
- **Subadult performance gap**: Accuracy drops to 54.76% for subadults due to insufficient morphological sexual dimorphism.
- **Generalization beyond delta smelt**: Zero-shot ROI extraction untested on other species or environmental conditions.
- **Prototype interpretability validation**: Lack of user study with biologists to confirm explanations are actionable.

## Confidence
- **Zero-shot ROI extraction minimizes background bias**: Medium confidence. Internal evidence (CAM comparisons) supports it, but no external validation on diverse datasets or failure-case analysis.
- **Prototype-based learning provides interpretable, competitive classification**: Medium confidence. Ablations and visualizations exist, but no human evaluation of explanations and potential accuracy ceiling vs. black-box models is unclear.
- **Systematic augmentation (especially random erasing) improves robustness**: High confidence. Direct ablation study (Table 1) shows quantitative gains, and the mechanism is grounded in established augmentation principles.
