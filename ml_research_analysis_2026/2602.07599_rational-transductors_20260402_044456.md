# Rational Transductors

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.07599  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07599  
- Reference count: 16  
- Primary result: Augments Transformers with a dual-stream architecture using a linear matrix-valued recurrence derived from Weighted Finite Automata to achieve perfect length generalization on sequential algorithmic tasks while preserving parallel efficiency.

## Executive Summary
Standard Transformers exhibit a “Regular Gap”: they fail to robustly perform sequential logic and state tracking over long sequences. Rational Transductors close this gap by introducing a sidecar rational head that computes exact state via a linear, matrix-valued recurrence (a Weighted Finite Automaton) in parallel using an associative scan. The computed state vectors are injected into every Transformer layer via learned projections, creating a dual‑stream computation where the recurrence handles precise state tracking and the Transformer handles semantic processing. This design theoretically extends expressivity to all regular languages and NC¹‑complete problems while maintaining O(L + log T) time. Empirically, the approach achieves 100% accuracy on modulo‑k counting and long‑integer addition at lengths 25–1000× beyond training, where standard Transformers collapse to random chance.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is the rational head, which processes the input token sequence through a recurrence: *h*ₜ = *M*ₓₜ *h*ₜ₋₁. The token‑specific transition matrices *M*ₓₜ are produced by structured parameterizations (e.g., scaled Cayley transforms for orthogonality, or column‑stochastic softmax for stochasticity). The entire sequence of states *h*₁…*h*ₜ is computed in parallel via an associative scan over matrix multiplication, yielding O(log T) depth. These rational state vectors are then linearly projected and added to the hidden state of each Transformer layer (deep rational injection), ensuring that precise state information is available at all abstraction levels. The Transformer layers (self‑attention + FFN) operate on this augmented stream, and a final output head produces predictions. This preserves the Transformer’s parallel efficiency while extending its expressive power to regular languages and NC¹.

## Key Results
- Perfect length generalization on modulo‑5 counting and parity: 100% test accuracy on sequences up to 1000× training length; standard Transformers degrade to chance.  
- Same perfect generalization on long‑integer addition (carry propagation) up to 1000 digits, trained on ≤40‑digit numbers.  
- Maintains parallel efficiency: training depth O(L + log T); no sequential bottleneck.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The architecture succeeds by combining three synergistic mechanisms. First, the linear recurrence from Weighted Finite Automata provides exact, time‑invariant state tracking; its associativity enables parallel scan, avoiding RNN‑like bottlenecks while mathematically capturing all regular languages. Second, deep rational injection broadcasts the computed state into every Transformer layer, preventing signal attenuation and allowing the model to use precise state information at all depths. Third, unified scaled Cayley parameterization (or stochastic variants) enforces spectral stability: orthogonal matrices conserve state norm, while contractive ones ensure fading memory, preventing gradient explosion/vanishing during training. Together, these elements give the model both theoretical completeness and practical stability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Weighted Finite Automata (WFA) & Rational Power Series**: The rational head is a WFA; its linear recurrence computes a rational series, which by Schützenberger’s theorem captures all regular languages. *Why needed*: explains why the architecture can solve Parity and Mod‑k exactly. *Quick check*: A 2‑state WFA with *M*₀ = *I*, *M*₁ = [[0,1],[1,0]] recognizes the language of binary strings with even parity.  
- **Krohn‑Rhodes Decomposition**: Any DFA decomposes into a cascade of simple groups (orthogonal dynamics) and an aperiodic monoid (stochastic dynamics). *Why needed*: justifies the Universal Transductor’s mixture of orthogonal and stochastic heads for full regular language coverage. *Quick check*: The two algebraic components are finite simple groups and aperiodic monoids.  
- **Parallel Associative Scan (Prefix Sum)**: A linear recurrence *h*ₜ = *M*ₓₜ *h*ₜ₋₁ is associative (matrix multiplication), allowing all states to be computed in O(log T) parallel steps. *Why needed*: enables the rational head to avoid sequential bottlenecks. *Quick check*: The binary operator must be associative.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input embeddings → Rational head (parallel associative scan over transition matrices) → Rational state *h*ₜ → Deep injection (layer‑specific linear projections added to each Transformer layer’s input) → Transformer layers (self‑attention + FFN) → Output head.  
- **Critical path**: Token sequence → Rational head → *h*ₜ → per‑layer projections → Transformer stream → prediction. Total depth = O(L + log T) (L Transformer layers + log T for scan).  
- **Design tradeoffs**: Single wide head gives O(log T) depth; stacked non‑linear heads increase expressivity but add O(L log T) depth. Orthogonal Cayley parameterization suits exact counting/parity; stochastic (column‑stochastic) suits discrete FSM tasks like addition. Positional encodings are typically disabled for algorithmic tasks.  
- **Failure signatures**: State dimension *d* too small → plateaued training accuracy. Poor spectral control (‖*M*ₓ‖ > 1 or ≪ 1) → gradient explosion/vanishing. Injection failure (projections near zero) → rational state ignored. Unitary regime instability for parity (need *d* ≥ 3 or sign flip).  
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Parity: train on lengths ≤50, test up to 1000; expect 100% accuracy.  
  2. Modulo‑5 counting: same regime; perfect accuracy validates cyclic group dynamics.  
  3. Long‑integer addition: train on ≤40‑digit numbers, test up to 1000 digits; 100% sequence accuracy confirms stochastic head learning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does large‑scale pre‑training on massive datasets validate the theoretical advantages of Rational Transductors, such as solving the Regular Gap and achieving robust length generalization across diverse domains?  
- Under what conditions does the single‑head “sidecar” design outperform or underperform stacked non‑linear recurrence, and what is the precise trade‑off between parameter efficiency, parallel depth, and task‑specific expressivity?  
- For the Universal Transductor’s mixture of orthogonal and stochastic heads, does the architecture autonomously learn to route signals appropriately, and are there regimes where one head type dominates or the mixture harms performance?  
- To what extent do finite‑precision numerical constraints (e.g., 16‑bit or 32‑bit floating point) break the algebraic exactness of the unitary regime, leading to error accumulation and failure of perfect length generalization on extremely long sequences (e.g., *T* > 10⁶)?

## Limitations
- Implementation details of the matrix‑valued associative scan and the exact parameterization of transition matrices (e.g., linear vs. MLP, computation of gain *g*ₓ) are not fully specified.  
- Spectr al clipping (Eq. 51) timing and application to structured forms (Cayley, DPLR) remain unclear.  
- The allocation between orthogonal and stochastic heads in the Universal Transductor is unspecified; no empirical analysis of head utilization is provided.
