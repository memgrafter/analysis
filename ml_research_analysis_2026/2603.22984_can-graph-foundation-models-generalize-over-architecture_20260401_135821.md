# Can Graph Foundation Models Generalize Over Architecture?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22984  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22984  
- **Reference count:** 34  
- **Primary result:** GOBLIN achieves 68.03% accuracy on 25 standard benchmarks (~2% above best zero-shot GFM) and 23.50% on long-range CityNetwork tasks (3–8% ahead), demonstrating architecture adaptivity is essential for zero-shot graph generalization.

## Executive Summary
Graph Foundation Models (GFMs) aim to provide general-purpose graph neural networks that can be applied to a wide range of tasks without retraining. Existing domain‑agnostic GFMs (e.g., GraphAny, TS‑GNN) employ fixed, shallow message‑passing backbones, limiting their effective receptive field. This work shows that such models fail on tasks requiring long‑range interactions because their architecture cannot adapt to the task’s required range. To overcome this, the authors propose GOBLIN, an inference‑time framework that dynamically discovers a task‑specific basis of linear graph operators (e.g., localized Gaussian, diffusion kernels) using Bayesian optimization. These operators are mixed via a permutation‑invariant mixture‑of‑experts (DeepSet) to produce node predictions. Across 25 standard benchmarks, GOBLIN achieves 68.03% accuracy, about 2% absolute improvement over the best competing zero‑shot GFM. On four long‑range CityNetwork tasks (requiring ~16‑hop interactions), it reaches 23.50%, outperforming others by 3–8%. Moreover, on synthetic kHopSign tasks (k=1–8), GOBLIN maintains over 90% accuracy while fixed baselines collapse for k>2. The results demonstrate that architecture adaptivity is crucial for zero‑shot generalization in graph learning.

## Method Summary
GOBLIN operates in a zero‑shot node classification setting: given a graph with adjacency A, node features X, and a small set of labeled nodes Y_L, the model predicts labels for the remaining nodes without any gradient‑based training on the target graph. The core of GOBLIN is a two‑stage process: (1) at inference time, it searches over two parameterized families of linear graph operators—LinGauss (distance‑based Gaussian) and LinHeat (heat kernel based on Laplacian)—using Bayesian optimization. The search evaluates each operator by fitting a linear GNN via closed‑form least‑squares on a subset of Y_L and measuring trimmed accuracy on a held‑out portion. After 25 evaluations, a greedy selection with a diversity penalty picks K=4 operators to form a basis. (2) The operators are fitted on all labeled nodes and their predictions are combined by a permutation‑invariant mixture of experts. This mixer is a DeepSet that ingests per‑node disagreement statistics across the operators (mean, variance, min, max of pairwise L2 differences) and outputs normalized weights. The DeepSet itself is pretrained on Cora using a pool‑based objective: in each iteration, random operators are sampled, experts fitted, and the mixer trained to minimize cross‑entropy on the held‑out labels. At test time, the selected operators are quickly evaluated and weighted, yielding final predictions.

## Key Results
- **Standard benchmarks:** On 25 node classification datasets, GOBLIN achieves an average accuracy of 68.03%, approximately 2% above the best competing zero‑shot GFM (GraphAny/TS‑GNN).
- **Long‑range tasks:** On 4 CityNetwork tasks requiring ~16‑hop interactions, GOBLIN attains 23.50% accuracy, outperforming other GFMs by 3–8% absolute.
- **Synthetic range stress‑test:** On kHopSign with k=1–8, GOBLIN maintains over 90% accuracy, while fixed‑baseline methods fail for k>2, confirming its ability to adapt to varying range requirements.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Fixed‑backbone Graph Foundation Models (GFMs) under‑reach on tasks requiring architectural properties (e.g., range) beyond their static design.
- **Mechanism:** The architecture is frozen during training; the receptive field and aggregation behavior are bounded by the chosen backbone (e.g., 2‑layer MPNN). The model cannot expand its effective range during inference to match task demands.
- **Core assumption:** Task performance depends on matching the model’s effective receptive field to the task’s required range. A fixed receptive field is insufficient for heterogeneous tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: “existing approaches are non‑robust to task‑dependent architectural attributes… use range as a minimal and measurable axis along which this limitation becomes explicit.”
  - [section 3]: Derives that for GraphAny, operator ranges are bounded (e.g., ≤2 for standard basis), proving under‑reaching for tasks with range >2.
  - [corpus]: Weak; related GFMs (e.g., surveys) discuss generalization across graphs/tasks but not architectural rigidity as a failure mode.
- **Break condition:** When a task’s required range (or other architectural attribute) exceeds the maximum capacity of the fixed backbone’s operator basis or depth.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** GOBLIN achieves architecture adaptivity by discovering a task‑specific basis of linear graph operators at inference time, without retraining.
- **Mechanism:** Bayesian optimization searches a parameterized operator family (e.g., distance‑localized Gaussian, diffusion‑based Heat kernel) on a held‑out label subset. Selected operators span short‑, medium‑, and long‑range behaviors, defining an adaptive effective architecture.
- **Core assumption:** A diverse, parameterized operator family can express the range of architectures needed across tasks, and a sample‑efficient black‑box optimizer can identify a suitable subset from few labeled nodes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: “introduce a framework that adapts effective GNN architecture at inference time by discovering and mixing task‑specific linear graph operators.”
  - [section 5.2]: Details BO search over operator parameters using a trimmed accuracy objective on a validation label split.
  - [corpus]: Weak; neighbor papers focus on GFM applications or surveys, not inference‑time architecture adaptation.
- **Break condition:** If the operator family lacks expressive operators for the task’s required range/behavior, or the BO budget is too small to find a suitable basis.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A permutation‑invariant mixture‑of‑experts (DeepSet) over the discovered operators enables robust weighting without dependence on basis order or size.
- **Mechanism:** For each node, computes pairwise disagreement statistics (e.g., variance of expert predictions) across all operators, embeds these into operator‑wise features, and uses a learned function to output normalized weights. The DeepSet aggregates over operators, ensuring invariance.
- **Core assumption:** Summary statistics of expert disagreements capture sufficient signal about which operators are task‑relevant, without overfitting to a specific basis size.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: “mixing them with a permutation‑invariant mixture of experts.”
  - [section 5.3]: Describes the DeepSet architecture using operator disagreement features (mean, variance, min, max).
  - [section A.3.2]: Notes that “pool” training with randomly sampled operators improves robustness over fixed‑basis training.
- **Break condition:** If the chosen summary statistics are uninformative for distinguishing operator utility on the task, or if the DeepSet overfits to the small operator basis seen during training.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Message‑passing GNN receptive field (range)**
  - Why needed here: The paper diagnoses model failure via range—the distance over which node interactions must be aggregated. Understanding how depth, operators, and topology affect range is key to seeing why fixed backbones fail.
  - Quick check question: For a 2‑layer GNN using degree‑normalized adjacency, what is the maximum possible node‑level range on any graph?
- **Concept: Linear GNN analytic solution**
  - Why needed here: GOBLIN’s experts are linear GNNs solved via least‑squares. This avoids gradient‑based training and allows rapid evaluation of many operator candidates during BO.
  - Quick check question: Given operator S and label matrix Y_L, what is the closed‑form weight solution W for a linear GNN ˆY = S X W?
- **Concept: Bayesian optimization for black‑box function evaluation**
  - Why needed here: The BO stage must efficiently select operator parameters from a continuous space using few evaluations (each involves fitting a linear GNN and validating on a held‑out label split). Understanding acquisition functions (UCB) and GP modeling is crucial.
  - Quick check question: In cross‑family UCB competition, how does the algorithm decide which operator family to sample from next?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Operator family** (Sec 5.1): Parameterized linear operators S(θ) (e.g., LinGauss, LinHeat). Requires graph distances (geodesic/resistance) and feature matrix X.
  2. **BO search** (Sec A.1): Maintains separate GPs per operator family, proposes parameters via UCB, evaluates via trimmed accuracy on Y_Lfit/Y_Leval split.
  3. **Basis selection** (Eq 7): Greedy selection of K operators from BO samples, maximizing score minus diversity penalty (cosine similarity of predictions).
  4. **DeepSet MoE** (Sec 5.3): Computes disagreement features H_u^(i) between operator predictions, embeds via ϕ, pools across operators, and weights via ψ.
  5. **Linear GNN solve** (Sec 2.1): For each selected θ, compute W_θ = (S(θ) X)^+ Y_L and predictions ˆY_θ = S(θ) X W_θ.
- **Critical path:** Input graph (A, X) + labeled nodes Y_L → BO search yields operator set Θ → refit linear GNNs on all Y_L → compute node‑wise DeepSet features from Θ → output weighted predictions for unlabeled nodes.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Inference cost vs. adaptability:** Larger BO budget and larger operator family increase adaptability but raise inference time (APSPD computation, GP updates, more linear solves).
  - **Operator family expressivity:** Gaussian operators target precise ranges via geodesic distance; Heat operators provide diffusive smoothing via resistance distance. Tradeoff: Gaussian is range‑specific but requires APSPD; Heat is cheaper but range is less controllable.
  - **DeepSet feature richness:** Simple summary stats (mean, variance) are cheap but may not fully distinguish operator utility; adding score features (Sec A.3.2) can help but risk overfitting.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Poor BO objective:** Trimmed accuracy should select operators that work on substantial subsets, not just a few nodes. If top‑K operators have nearly identical predictions, diversity penalty λ may be too low.
  - **Noise in basis:** Including operators with negligible weight (¯α ≈ 0) dilutes effective range and hurts performance on standard benchmarks (Table 1).
  - **Scalability:** APSPD computation for Gaussian operators becomes prohibitive on very large graphs (e.g., CityNetworks). Mitigation: truncate hops beyond a threshold.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **kHopSign synthetic range stress‑test** (Sec 4): Show GOBLIN maintains >90% accuracy across k=1..8, while fixed‑basis GraphAny collapses for k>2. Validates architecture adaptivity on a known‑range diagnostic.
  2. **25 benchmark comparison** (Table 2): Evaluate zero‑shot against GraphAny and TS‑GNN. GOBLIN’s 2% average gain, especially on larger‑∆% datasets, indicates improved robustness to varying task ranges.
  3. **CityNetworks long‑range** (Table 3): On tasks requiring ~16‑hop range, GOBLIN outperforms other GFMs by 3–8%, demonstrating adaptation beyond shallow receptive fields. Also inspect individual operator ranges (Fig 4) to confirm high‑range selections.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Question:** How can more expressive, permutation‑invariant features improve the DeepSet’s ability to mix operators for unseen tasks?  
   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] “Our current instantiation of GOBLIN uses … a small set of summary features for MoE weighting… More expressive operator features would likely improve performance.”  
   **Why unresolved:** Current features are simple statistics (mean, variance, min, max of operator disagreement) which may not capture nuanced operator interactions required for complex tasks.  
   **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic ablation studies comparing feature sets, or designing theoretically grounded features based on operator properties, then evaluating zero‑shot accuracy across diverse benchmarks.

2. **Question:** Does training GOBLIN’s DeepSet on multiple graphs enhance zero‑shot generalization and reduce inference‑time search costs?  
   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] “An investigation into training across multiple graphs would likely lead to improvements in both efficiency and generalization.”  
   **Why unresolved:** The current GOBLIN trains its DeepSet on a single graph (Cora), which may overfit to that graph’s characteristics; multi‑graph training could expose the mixer to broader operator‑task relationships.  
   **What evidence would resolve it:** Training the DeepSet component on a diverse set of graphs and measuring zero‑shot performance on new tasks, with particular attention to whether Bayesian optimization search budgets can be reduced.

3. **Question:** Can GOBLIN’s operator family be extended to achieve effective ranges beyond 8 hops while maintaining computational efficiency, to match deep MPNNs on very long‑range tasks?  
   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The use of Gauss and Heat operators caps practical range (~8 hops), and CityNetworks tasks show further gains with deeper MPNNs (16+ layers), indicating GOBLIN’s range is insufficient.  
   **Why unresolved:** Current parameterization (Gauss centered on µ with σ, Heat with τ) offers limited long‑range capacity; extending to higher µ or τ may become numerically unstable or computationally expensive.  
   **What evidence would resolve it:** Proposing new operator families (e.g., spectral wavelets with higher‑order filters, learned propagation matrices) and evaluating on tasks requiring 10+ hop interactions, comparing against deep baselines.

4. **Question:** To what extent can label re‑fitting in linear GNN solutions extend the effective receptive field beyond the message‑passing operator’s range?  
   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] In the range analysis (Section E), the authors ignore the weight‑fitting term in the Jacobian, assuming it doesn’t introduce new graph‑dependent paths. This assumption simplifies analysis but may not hold when labels are highly structured or sparse.  
   **Why unresolved:** If label correlations are long‑range, re‑fitting W could propagate information indirectly, effectively increasing range; this is not captured by the fixed‑W range measure.  
   **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare fixed‑W range vs. black‑box range on synthetic tasks with known label propagation properties, or analyze the contribution of the second Jacobian term in controlled settings.

## Limitations
- Missing hyperparameters (e.g., σ, label split ratios), ambiguous batch count, and unspecified Heat kernel implementation hinder exact replication.
- The assumption that range is the sole architectural axis may overlook other factors (e.g., skip connections, attention) that could affect generalization.
- Bayesian optimization relies on all‑pairs shortest‑path distances, which are expensive for large graphs; the paper lacks scalability analysis, limiting applicability to very large datasets.
