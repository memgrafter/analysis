# CHIMERA: Compact Synthetic Data for Generalizable LLM Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00889
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00889
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** 4B Qwen3 model trained on CHIMERA achieves 70.1% GPQA-Diamond, 86.9% AIME24, 67.0% HMMT Nov 25 accuracy

## Executive Summary
CHIMERA addresses critical challenges in LLM reasoning development through a compact synthetic dataset approach. The system tackles cold-start data scarcity, limited domain coverage, and annotation bottlenecks by generating 9K samples with long Chain-of-Thought trajectories across 8 scientific disciplines and 1K+ topics. The methodology employs automated taxonomy construction and cross-model validation to create high-quality reasoning data. Post-training on CHIMERA yields substantial performance gains across multiple reasoning benchmarks, with a 4B Qwen3 model achieving competitive results against 235B-parameter models while using only a fraction of the training data.

## Method Summary
CHIMERA constructs synthetic reasoning data through automated taxonomy generation covering eight scientific disciplines with over 1,000 topics. The system generates long Chain-of-Thought trajectories to create complex reasoning paths, then validates samples across multiple models to ensure quality. Post-training involves fine-tuning a 4B Qwen3 model on this 9K-sample dataset, focusing on improving reasoning capabilities across scientific domains. The approach emphasizes dataset compactness while maintaining high difficulty levels that challenge base model capabilities.

## Key Results
- 4B Qwen3 model achieves 70.1% accuracy on GPQA-Diamond (up 4.3% from baseline)
- AIME24 performance improves to 86.9% (up 5.3%)
- HMMT Nov 25 score reaches 67.0% (up 9.7%)
- Matches or exceeds performance of 235B-parameter models despite 60x smaller size

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of CHIMERA stems from its strategic combination of dataset compactness, controlled difficulty, and broad scientific coverage. By generating synthetic data with long Chain-of-Thought trajectories, the system creates reasoning patterns that require genuine multi-step problem-solving rather than simple pattern matching. The automated taxonomy ensures comprehensive topic coverage across scientific disciplines, exposing models to diverse reasoning contexts. Cross-model validation filters for high-quality samples that genuinely test reasoning capabilities. The extreme difficulty (37.5% base accuracy) forces models to develop robust reasoning strategies rather than memorizing surface patterns.

## Foundational Learning
- **Chain-of-Thought reasoning:** Multi-step problem decomposition needed to solve complex scientific problems that cannot be answered through single-step pattern matching
- **Scientific taxonomy construction:** Structured knowledge organization enables systematic coverage of reasoning domains and prevents catastrophic forgetting
- **Cross-model validation:** Multi-model verification ensures sample quality and prevents dataset contamination from single-model biases
- **Synthetic data generation:** Automated creation of reasoning examples overcomes data scarcity while maintaining control over difficulty and coverage
- **Difficulty calibration:** Base model accuracy metrics guide dataset construction to target optimal learning zones

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Taxonomy Generator -> CoT Trajectory Builder -> Cross-Model Validator -> Dataset Aggregator

**Critical Path:** Taxonomy generation provides topic coverage framework → CoT builder creates reasoning trajectories within topics → Validator filters low-quality samples → Aggregator compiles final dataset

**Design Tradeoffs:** Dataset compactness vs. coverage breadth (9K samples vs. 1K+ topics), synthetic generation vs. human annotation costs, controlled difficulty vs. model accessibility

**Failure Signatures:** Low cross-model agreement indicates poor sample quality, taxonomy gaps reveal coverage holes, insufficient difficulty shows as high base accuracy (>50%)

**First Experiments:** 1) Validate taxonomy coverage completeness across all eight disciplines, 2) Test cross-model validation agreement rates, 3) Measure base model accuracy distribution across generated samples

## Limitations
- Extreme dataset difficulty (37.5% base accuracy) may indicate narrow solution spaces rather than general reasoning capability
- Proprietary evaluation benchmarks limit independent replication and verification
- Taxonomy-based generation may constrain emergence of novel reasoning patterns outside predefined categories
