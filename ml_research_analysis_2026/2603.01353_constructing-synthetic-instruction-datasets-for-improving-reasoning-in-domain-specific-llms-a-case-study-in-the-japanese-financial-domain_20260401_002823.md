# Constructing Synthetic Instruction Datasets for Improving Reasoning in Domain-Specific LLMs: A Case Study in the Japanese Financial Domain

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.01353
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01353
- Reference count: 15
- Primary result: Models fine-tuned on the synthetic dataset (e.g., gpt-oss-20b + CPT + SFT) outperformed official instruction-tuned baselines on Japanese financial benchmarks (72.50 vs. 66.93 on japanese-lm-fin-harness).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of building domain-specific LLMs that combine deep expertise with robust reasoning, focusing on Japanese finance where large-scale Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data was previously unavailable. The authors propose a general pipeline for synthesizing high-quality instruction data with reasoning traces, starting from domain-specific topic words and using an LLM-as-a-judge for quality filtering. This yielded a 9.5B-token dataset. Models trained with Continued Pre-Training (CPT) on domain corpora followed by Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on this dataset significantly surpassed instruction-tuned baselines on Japanese financial benchmarks. The study also identifies an optimal reasoning trace length (~1024 tokens) beyond which performance plateaus or declines due to degenerative behaviors.

## Method Summary
The core method is a multi-stage pipeline for generating synthetic instruction data with CoT reasoning traces. It begins with a seed set of domain-specific topic words (e.g., financial products) which are expanded into sub-topics. An LLM then generates diverse instruction prompts and multi-turn conversations, embedding step-by-step reasoning. Basic filtering (n-gram deduplication, length checks) is applied, followed by a rigorous LLM-as-a-judge stage that scores each sample on five criteria (accuracy, reasoning depth, safety, etc.) with a strict threshold (all criteria = 5) to exclude low-quality or hallucinated content. Public datasets (e.g., NuminaMath) are integrated after identical processing. For model training, a two-stage approach is used: first, Continued Pre-Training (CPT) on a filtered Japanese financial web corpus to embed domain knowledge; second, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on the final synthetic dataset to teach structured reasoning output.

## Key Results
- Models fine-tuned with CPT + SFT on the synthetic dataset (e.g., gpt-oss-20b + CPT + SFT) achieved state-of-the-art results on Japanese financial benchmarks, notably 72.50 average on japanese-lm-fin-harness vs. 66.93 for the strongest official instruction-tuned baseline with CoT.
- Performance on reasoning tasks improved with longer reasoning traces up to approximately 1024 tokens; beyond this, gains plateaued and forced termination led to significant degradation.
- Forcing extended reasoning traces triggered degenerative behaviors such as "early termination" (superficial reasoning) and "output loops" (repetitive confirmations), harming accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Starting from domain-specific topic words and using an LLM-as-a-judge filtering pipeline generates high-quality instruction data with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces for any domain.
- **Mechanism:** Seed topic words are expanded into sub-topics. An LLM generates diverse instruction prompts and multi-turn conversations with embedded CoT reasoning. N-gram/deduplication filtering ensures basic quality. An LLM-as-a-judge then scores each sample on accuracy, reasoning depth, safety, etc., with strict thresholds (all criteria = 5) to exclude low-quality or hallucinated data.
- **Core assumption:** A capable LLM (here `gpt-oss-120b`) can both generate and reliably judge domain-specific reasoning traces, and topic words can sufficiently cover the domain's conceptual space.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "proposes a general method for constructing high-quality synthetic instruction data... starting from domain-specific vocabulary"; [section 3.1–3.6] details topic-word-based generation, expansion, filtering, and LLM-as-a-judge quality control.
- **Break condition:** If the initial topic word set misses critical sub-domains or the LLM-as-a-judge is biased toward superficial coherence over factual accuracy (Section 3.6 notes ~18% exclusion due to accuracy failures), the dataset will contain gaps or subtle errors.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Sequential Continued Pre-Training (CPT) on a domain corpus followed by Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on the synthetic CoT dataset effectively combines domain expertise with reasoning capability in a domain-specific LLM.
- **Mechanism:** CPT adapts the base model's parameters to the domain's linguistic and factual patterns (e.g., Japanese financial texts). SFT then teaches the model to output structured reasoning traces (CoT) for complex tasks without overwriting CPT-acquired knowledge, assuming the SFT data is high-quality and diverse.
- **Core assumption:** The CPT and SFT stages are complementary and not destructive; the SFT data's reasoning traces are sufficiently aligned with the domain knowledge encoded during CPT.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.1–4.3] CPT on filtered Japanese financial web corpora, then SFT on the 9.5B-token synthetic dataset; [Table 1] Models with CPT + SFT surpass official instruction-tuned baselines.
- **Break condition:** If CPT corpora are noisy or too narrow, catastrophic forgetting of general reasoning may occur. If SFT data includes reasoning traces that conflict with CPT knowledge, performance degrades. The paper notes Qwen3-14B-Base + SFT alone underperformed, indicating CPT is often necessary.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** In domain-specific tasks, there is an optimal reasoning trace length (~1024 tokens) beyond which performance plateaus or declines due to degenerative reasoning behaviors.
- **Mechanism:** Forcing longer traces can lead to "early termination" (model quickly concludes with superficial reasoning) or "output loops" (repetitive confirmations). These behaviors indicate the model's inability to produce meaningful, extended reasoning beyond its effective depth for the task complexity.
- **Core assumption:** Model-generated reasoning traces have a natural useful length distribution per task; artificially extending them does not guarantee added value and may trigger degenerate generation patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.4] experiments with fixed reasoning lengths show gains up to 1024 tokens, then diminishing returns; forced termination beyond causes several-point drops; [Figure 2] visualizes the plateau and degradation across subtasks.
- **Break condition:** If the base model's architecture or training does not support coherent long-horizon reasoning (e.g., limited context window), forcing length will reliably produce loops or early termination, harming performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Tokenization & Vocabulary Adaptation**
  - *Why needed here:* Japanese financial text includes specialized terms (e.g., 株式/bond, 決算/earnings) and mixed English/Japanese. The base model's tokenizer must represent these; CPT on financial corpora helps embed domain terms.
  - *Quick check question:* What problems arise if a tokenizer splits "securities" (証券) into subwords during CPT?
- **Concept: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
  - *Why needed here:* SFT teaches the model to follow the `<reasoning>...<answer>` format and produce CoT traces. Without SFT, CPT models lack the instructed output structure for reasoning tasks.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might a model that knows financial facts (via CPT) still fail to output a step-by-step analysis for a complex question?
- **Concept: LLM-as-a-Judge & Quality Filtering**
  - *Why needed here:* Synthetic data can contain hallucinations or incoherent reasoning. LLM-as-a-judge (with strict 5-criteria scoring) is the proposed quality gate to ensure only high-fidelity CoT samples enter SFT.
  - *Quick check question:* If the judge model is not specialized in finance, what types of errors might slip through the filter?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Topic word selector -> Instruction generator -> Instruction expander -> Filter & deduplicator -> Multi-turn dialogue generator -> LLM-as-a-judge quality filter -> Public dataset integrator -> Training pipeline (CPT -> SFT)
- **Critical path:** Topic selection → Instruction generation → Expansion → Filtering → Multi-turn generation → Quality filtering → Dataset assembly → CPT → SFT. Each step feeds the next; failures in quality filtering propagate to SFT.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Quality vs. Scale:* Strict LLM-as-a-judge (5/5 all criteria) yields 75.4% retention. Looser thresholds increase scale but risk low-quality CoT.
  - *Domain vs. General capability:* Mixing general-domain topic words mitigates catastrophic forgetting but may dilute financial depth.
  - *Multi-turn vs. computational cost:* Multi-turn data improves dialogue capability but requires ~3× more tokens per sample.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Data-level:* High repetition in reasoning traces or factual errors (judge accuracy 81.7% top score).
  - *Training-level:* Qwen3-14B-Base + SFT underperforms baseline, suggesting CPT is necessary or SFT data misaligned.
  - *Inference-level:* Forced termination at >1024 tokens causes performance drop.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Topic coverage validation: Sample 100 sub-topics from each seed category; have a domain expert rate if they cover core financial concepts (e.g., 投資/investment, 規制/regulation). If <80% coverage, add seed words.
  2. Judge threshold sweep: Run LLM-as-a-judge on 1k samples with varying score cutoffs (e.g., ≥4 on all criteria vs. ≥5). Manually audit 200 samples per cutoff for factual errors; choose threshold that minimizes hallucinations while retaining >70% data.
  3. CPT-SFT seq order test: Ablate order: (a) SFT only on synthetic data, (b) CPT only, (c) CPT then SFT. Evaluate on japanese-lm-fin-harness. If (a) underperforms (c), confirm CPT is necessary for domain knowledge anchoring.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What are effective methods for naturally terminating reasoning traces in domain-specific LLMs to avoid performance degradation from forced stopping?**
  - *Basis in paper:* The authors state that "examination of various countermeasures including other termination methods is needed in the future" after observing degradation when forcibly terminating traces and noting behaviors like early termination and output loops.
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper only identifies the problem and hypothesizes about natural termination phrases but provides no comparative analysis or proposed solutions.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic experiments comparing different termination strategies (e.g., natural phrase cues, learned halting mechanisms) on domain-specific benchmarks, measuring trade-offs between trace length, coherence, and task accuracy.
- **What is the optimal threshold for LLM-as-a-judge quality filtering when constructing domain-specific reasoning datasets, and how does it impact data quantity versus quality?**
  - *Basis in paper:* The authors used a strict 5-point criteria across all five perspectives, filtering out 24.6% of samples, but note accuracy was the lowest-scoring perspective (81.7% achieving 5 points) and do not explore whether this threshold is optimal.
  - *Why unresolved:* The filtering threshold was set arbitrarily to ensure maximum quality; its impact on downstream model performance and potential loss of useful training signal was not investigated.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Ablation studies varying the quality score threshold (e.g., requiring 4+ points on all criteria vs. 5) and correlating with SFT model performance, while also analyzing the nature of excluded samples (e.g., minor factual errors vs. fundamental reasoning breakdowns).
- **How should domain-specific topic words be systematically selected and expanded to ensure comprehensive coverage and avoid bias in synthetic data generation pipelines?**
  - *Basis in paper:* The pipeline starts with manually chosen seed words (135 financial, 20 general) and generates sub-topics, but the paper does not describe a principled method for selecting these seeds or evaluating coverage, relying on authors' domain knowledge.
  - *Why unresolved:* The generalizability of the method depends on robust topic selection, but the process is ad-hoc and may not transfer to other domains or even comprehensively cover finance without significant manual effort.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Studies comparing different topic seed selection strategies (e.g., corpus-derived vs. expert-curated) using metrics like dataset diversity, benchmark coverage, and final model performance across a wider range of domain tasks.
- **What is the optimal depth (number of turns) for multi-turn conversation generation in domain-specific SFT data, and how does it trade off against reasoning coherence and computational cost?**
  - *Basis in paper:* The pipeline generated conversations with a fixed maximum of 3 turns without justification or analysis of whether this depth is sufficient for complex domain dialogues or if longer/shorter conversations yield better reasoning.
  - *Why unresolved:* The choice appears arbitrary; longer conversations might improve interactive reasoning skills but could introduce incoherence or redundancy, while shorter ones might not capture sufficient depth. The paper reports no experiments varying this parameter.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled experiments varying the maximum turn count (e.g., 1, 3, 5) in the synthetic data generation phase, evaluating trained models on multi-turn benchmarks like pfmt-bench-fin-ja for consistency, relevance, and depth across dialogue history.

## Limitations
- Pipeline generalizability is unproven beyond Japanese finance; reliance on a single judge model (`gpt-oss-120b`) introduces potential bias and may not transfer to other domains or languages.
- The necessity and precise role of CPT (factual anchoring vs. format adaptation) are not fully disentangled; risks of catastrophic forgetting during CPT on narrow corpora are unquantified.
- The optimal reasoning trace length (~1024 tokens) is empirically observed but not theoretically explained; its generalizability to other model scales or task distributions is unknown.

## Confidence
- Claim 1 (Pipeline Generates High-Quality Data): **Medium**. Evidence is strong within the single case study but lacks cross-domain validation; judge model reliability on unseen financial sub-domains is not proven.
- Claim 2 (CPT+SFT Synergy is Effective): **High**. Results are consistent across multiple model architectures and benchmarks; ablation (SFT-only underperformance) supports CPT necessity, though the precise mechanism remains partially unclear.
- Claim 3 (Optimal CoT Length ~1024 Tokens): **Medium-High**. Empirical finding is robust across subtasks in the harness but confined to the specific model-scale (20B) and task distribution used; generalizability is an open question.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain pipeline test:** Apply the exact synthesis pipeline to a new, distinct domain (e.g., Japanese medical QA) using domain-specific seed words. Evaluate dataset quality via human expert audit (factuality, reasoning depth) and benchmark performance of a fine-tuned model.
2. **Judge model sensitivity analysis:** Re-run the LLM-as-a-judge filtering using three different judge models: (a) a generalist LLM (e.g., GPT-4), (b) a smaller open model, (c) a finance-specialized LLM if available. Compare retention rates, score correlation, and final SFT model performance to isolate judge bias impact.
3. **Zero-shot generalization probe:** After training `gpt-oss-20b + CPT + SFT`, evaluate performance on out-of-distribution Japanese financial tasks not represented in the harness (e.g., novel regulatory scenarios) in a zero-shot setting. This tests whether gains reflect true reasoning adaptation or overfitting to the synthetic distribution.
