# Boundary-Constrained Diffusion Models for Floorplan Generation: Balancing Realism and Diversity

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.01949
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01949
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** Introduces Boundary Cross-Attention to enforce user-defined building boundaries, achieving state-of-the-art boundary adherence (BC 0.04), but reveals a critical trade-off where prolonged training collapses layout diversity (DS drops from 52.69 to 33.59) despite improving perceptual realism (FID), and fails to generalize to out-of-distribution architectural geometries.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses fundamental limitations in diffusion-based floorplan generation: the lack of hard spatial constraints, limited output diversity, and poor generalization. The core contribution is a Boundary Cross-Attention (BCA) module that conditions the denoising process on user-specified building boundaries. While BCA dramatically improves boundary adherence, experiments uncover a trade-off between perceptual realism (FID) and layout diversity (DS). Crucially, the model fails on out-of-distribution geometries due to a reliance on dataset-specific heuristics for room complexity, highlighting a gap between learning meaningful spatial rules and memorizing training priors.

## Method Summary
The method extends HouseDiffusion by inserting a Boundary Cross-Attention (BCA) module into each transformer block of the denoiser. BCA first applies self-attention to the boundary polygon embeddings to capture global shape context, then performs cross-attention where room corner tokens query these enriched boundary embeddings. The BCA output is summed with outputs from other attention modules (CSA, GSA, RCA). During training, the boundary input is randomly dropped (p=0.1) to enable classifier-free guidance at inference, where a scale λ blends conditional and unconditional predictions. The model is trained on the RPLAN dataset (80k residential floorplans) for 400k steps, optimizing a noise prediction loss. A new Diversity Score (DS) metric is proposed, computing the trace of the covariance matrix of InceptionV3 features from multiple samples generated under identical constraints.

## Key Results
- BCA achieves state-of-the-art boundary compatibility (BC score 0.04), significantly outperforming the baseline (no BCA).
- Prolonged training (400k vs 10k steps) improves perceptual realism (FID) but causes a severe collapse in diversity (DS drops from 52.69 to 33.59).
- The model fails to generalize to out-of-distribution synthetic layouts with pentagonal rooms, even after 20-shot fine-tuning, due to a heuristic that samples room corner counts from a training histogram rather than inferring complexity from context.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
The Boundary Cross-Attention (BCA) module improves geometric consistency by explicitly conditioning room corner generation on user-defined building boundaries. BCA processes boundary embeddings through self-attention to establish global shape context, then allows room corner tokens to attend to these embeddings via cross-attention, directly integrating boundary positional information into the denoising prediction. This information is fused additively with other attention streams (CSA, GSA, RCA). The core assumption is that this explicit conditioning teaches the model to respect spatial constraints. Evidence includes the abstract’s claim and section 2’s mechanistic description. A break condition would be degraded BC scores at higher guidance scales λ or no improvement over ablations without BCA.

### Mechanism 2
The proposed Diversity Score (DS) successfully quantifies layout diversity under fixed constraints, revealing an inverse relationship with perceptual realism (FID) that FID alone cannot detect. DS computes the trace of the covariance matrix of InceptionV3 features (pool3) extracted from multiple samples generated from identical inputs; a higher trace indicates greater feature dispersion. The inverse FID-DS trend across training checkpoints (Table 2) demonstrates its diagnostic power for mode collapse. The core assumption is that InceptionV3’s perceptual space correlates with meaningful architectural variation. A break condition would be a lack of correlation between DS and human assessments of diversity or failure to detect collapse in other generative tasks.

### Mechanism 3
The model’s poor out-of-distribution (OOD) performance stems from a hard-coded reliance on a room corner count histogram derived from the training set (RPLAN), not learned geometric reasoning. This heuristic fails when the required polygon complexity (vertices) deviates from the training distribution, as seen with synthetic pentagonal rooms. The core assumption is that room complexity can be inferred from global dataset statistics alone. Evidence includes the section 3 analysis attributing failure to the histogram and visual results in Figure 2. A break condition would be no improvement after replacing the histogram with a context-predicted corner count.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Encoder (processes concatenated room corners X₀, graph G, boundary B) -> Diffusion Scheduler (noise addition/removal) -> Transformer Denoiser ϵθ (U-Net-like with CSA, GSA, RCA, BCA blocks) -> Classifier-Free Guidance Controller (blends ϵθ(Xₜ,t,G,B) and ϵθ(Xₜ,t,G,∅) via λ) -> Loss/Sampling (MSE noise loss, DDIM/DDPM sampler)
- **Critical path:** Preprocess floorplans to extract G and B → For each training step: corrupt X₀ to Xₜ → pass Xₜ, t, G, B through transformer with attention masks → predict noise ϵ → compute loss. At inference: iterative denoising with guidance blending.
- **Design tradeoffs:** λ scale (higher λ improves boundary fidelity but reduces diversity); boundary dropout probability pᴮ (regularizes unconditional learning but too high hurts boundary conditioning); fixed corner histogram (simple but causes OOD failure).
- **Failure signatures:** Diversity collapse (DS drops while FID improves); boundary violations (high BC score despite BCA, check λ and BCA integration); OOD failure (FID/GC collapse on novel geometries, inspect room corner counts vs. training histogram).
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Replicate Table 1: train baseline (no BCA) and proposed model on RPLAN, evaluate FID/GC/BC on 512 samples. 2) Monitor diversity collapse: save checkpoints every 50k steps, generate 100 samples for 4 fixed constraints, compute DS, plot DS vs. FID/BC. 3) OOD synthetic test: pre-train on RPLAN, fine-tune 1/5/20 steps on synthetic pentagonal dataset (20 samples), generate layouts, evaluate FID/GC and visually check pentagon room fidelity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can diffusion models for floorplan generation mitigate the trade-off between perceptual realism (FID) and layout diversity (DS) during training? The paper identifies the trade-off but does not investigate mitigation techniques. Resolution requires experiments with modified objectives or regularization that maintain DS while improving FID.
- **Open Question 2:** Can floorplan generation models generalize to out-of-distribution architectural geometries without relying on dataset-specific priors like room corner histograms? The current heuristic fails on novel geometries; a solution would involve predicting room complexity from graph/boundary context, validated on diverse OOD tests.
- **Open Question 3:** What is the optimal guidance scale (λ) for the Boundary Cross-Attention module to balance boundary adherence (BC) and output diversity (DS)? The paper uses λ for steering but does not study its impact on the realism-diversity trade-off; resolution requires systematic λ sweeps and Pareto analysis.

## Limitations
- Implementation details (BCA fusion point, head count, DS feature extraction) are unspecified, risking replication drift.
- The DS metric lacks human validation; it may not align with designer-perceived diversity or generalize beyond RPLAN.
- OOD synthetic datasets are unreleased, preventing independent stress-testing of the histogram-failure claim.
- The mechanism behind the diversity collapse (overfitting vs. loss dynamics) is not analytically dissected.

## Confidence
- **BCA boundary adherence:** Medium. Strong BC score but no ablation on alternative conditioning or attention visualization.
- **DS trade-off revelation:** Low-Medium. Clear inverse trend but DS validity without human studies is uncertain.
- **Histogram-induced OOD failure:** High. Compelling visual evidence and explicit heuristic attribution; replacing histogram is a direct falsification test.
