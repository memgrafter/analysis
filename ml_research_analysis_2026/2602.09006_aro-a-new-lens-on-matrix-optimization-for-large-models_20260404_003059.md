# ARO: A New Lens On Matrix Optimization For Large Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.09006
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09006
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: ARO achieves 1.3–1.35× speedup over AdamW and 1.1–1.15× over Muon on large-scale pretraining benchmarks with minimal computational overhead.

## Executive Summary
ARO (Adaptively Rotated Optimization) introduces a new paradigm for matrix-based optimization in large language models. Instead of directly updating parameters, ARO first adaptively rotates the coordinate system using a rotation matrix derived from the momentum and a base optimizer’s projected gradient. This rotation, computed via a QR decomposition, is informed by the geometric properties of the base optimizer (e.g., SinkGD, SignGD, Adam). The resulting unified update rule applies to all matrix-valued parameters—including embeddings and language modeling heads—and enables efficient exploitation of cross-layer rotational symmetries via rotation sharing. Large-scale pretraining experiments on models up to 8B parameters demonstrate consistent training speedups with negligible throughput cost.

## Method Summary
ARO wraps any base optimizer \( f \) within a rotation layer. For a parameter matrix \( \mathbf{W} \), the update uses momentum \( \mathbf{M}_t \) and a previous rotation \( \mathbf{R}_{t-1} \). The core steps are: (1) compute \( \mathbf{Q}_t = f(\mathbf{R}_{t-1}^\top \mathbf{M}_t) \); (2) form \( \mathbf{A}_t = \mathbf{M}_t \mathbf{Q}_t^\top \) and obtain \( \mathbf{R}_t = \text{QR}(\mathbf{A}_t) \); (3) compute rotated gradient \( \mathbf{G}_{\text{rot}} = \mathbf{R}_t^\top \mathbf{M}_t \); (4) compute update direction \( \Delta = f(\mathbf{G}_{\text{rot}}) \); (5) apply \( \mathbf{W} \leftarrow \mathbf{W} - \eta \mathbf{R}_t \Delta \). For stateful optimizers like Adam, internal states must be updated consistently with the rotation. The method can be applied in “full-model” mode (all ≤2D tensors) or as a hybrid (only hidden layers). Rotation sharing strategies (global, chain-coupled) reduce communication and exploit symmetry.

## Key Results
- ARO-SinkGD (with Sinkhorn base) yields 1.3–1.35× speedup over AdamW and 1.1–1.15× over Muon on GPT2-XL (1.5B), Sigma-MoE (2B), and Qwen3 (8B) under rigorous pretraining benchmarks.
- Speedups hold under up to 8× overtraining with no evidence of diminishing returns.
- Computational overhead is minimal: throughput comparable to AdamW (<1.3% slowdown).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Unified Rotation Interface
ARO provides a single update rule for all matrix parameters, including embeddings and LM heads. The rotation \( \mathbf{R}_t \) is adaptively computed from momentum \( \mathbf{M}_t \) and the base optimizer’s projection \( f(\mathbf{R}_{t-1}^\top \mathbf{M}_t) \) via QR. This non-eigen rotation is conditioned on the base optimizer’s geometry, making the rule universally applicable. *Break condition*: If the base optimizer is rotation-equivariant (e.g., spectral descent), ARO has no effect; full-model application may diverge with unstable bases (e.g., Muon on all parameters).

### Mechanism 2: Symmetry Teleportation
Transformers with RMSNorm and residual connections possess one-sided rotational symmetries. ARO’s rotation selection maximizes the dual-norm gradient magnitude in teleported coordinates, effectively moving along symmetry orbits to points with steeper effective descent. The QR-based policy is a numerically stable approximation of this orbit maximization. *Break condition*: Symmetry weakens with LayerNorm or negligible residuals; overly aggressive sharing (e.g., global) underfits layer-specific features.

### Mechanism 3: Stability–Speed Tradeoff under Noise
Under mini-batch noise, maximizing instantaneous loss decrease (Polar scheme) inflates alignment variance, causing oscillations. Eigen-rotation minimizes variance but suppresses alignment magnitude. ARO’s partial maximization via QR moderately increases alignment while keeping variance low, striking a practical balance. *Break condition*: In noiseless full-batch settings, polar projection may dominate; if noise structure contradicts assumptions (heavy-tailed, dependent columns), stability benefits may erode.

## Foundational Learning
- **Rotated Steepest Descent**: ARO is an instance of performing steepest descent in a rotated coordinate system. *Why needed*: This abstraction explains how any base optimizer \( f \) can be wrapped with a rotation \( \mathbf{R}_t \). *Quick check*: If baseline optimizer corresponds to steepest descent under norm \( \| \cdot \| \), what is the dual norm \( \| \cdot \|^* \)? How does rotating the gradient change the search direction?
- **Normed Steepest Descent & Dual Norms**: The base optimizer \( f(\mathbf{G}) = \nabla_{\mathbf{G}} \| \mathbf{G} \|^* \) links geometry to update. For \( f = \text{Sign} \), the norm is \( \ell_\infty \); for row-norm, it breaks rotational invariance. *Why needed*: Clarifies why different \( f \) behave differently under rotations. *Quick check*: Given \( f(\mathbf{X}) = \sqrt{n} \, Q(\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X} \) (RowNorm), what norm is being steepest-descent under? What invariance does that norm break?
- **Parameter Symmetries in Deep Learning**: Residual connections induce rotational symmetries; RMSNorm preserves them. *Why needed*: Guides rotation sharing designs and justifies symmetry teleportation. *Quick check*: Why do residual connections induce one-sided rotational invariance? In a transformer with RMSNorm, list matrices that must be rotated as \( \mathbf{W} \mapsto \mathbf{R} \mathbf{W} \) vs \( \mathbf{W} \mapsto \mathbf{W} \mathbf{R}^\top \) to preserve function.

## Architecture Onboarding
Momentum buffer -> Rotation estimator -> Base optimizer engine -> Distributed coordinator

**Critical path** (per step, one parameter group):
1. Receive gradient \( \mathbf{G}_t \).
2. Update momentum \( \mathbf{M}_t \leftarrow \beta \mathbf{M}_{t-1} + (1-\beta)\mathbf{G}_t \).
3. Compute projected \( \mathbf{Q}_t = f(\mathbf{R}_{t-1}^\top \mathbf{M}_t) \) (may involve internal state lookahead for stateful \( f \)).
4. Form \( \mathbf{A}_t = \mathbf{M}_t \mathbf{Q}_t^\top \), then \( \mathbf{R}_t = \text{QR}(\mathbf{A}_t) \) (using shifted Cholesky QR with fallback).
5. Compute rotated gradient \( \mathbf{G}_{\text{rot}} = \mathbf{R}_t^\top \mathbf{M}_t \), then update direction \( \Delta = f(\mathbf{G}_{\text{rot}}) \) (updating internal states with \( \mathbf{G}_{\text{rot}} \)).
6. Apply update \( \mathbf{W} \leftarrow \mathbf{W} - \eta \, \mathbf{R}_t \Delta \).

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *QR choice*: Shifted Cholesky QR is faster but may fail on ill-conditioned matrices; fallback to standard QR increases cost.
- *Base optimizer*: SinkGD (stateless) speeds up but lacks adaptivity; Adam brings variance adaptation but increases state and may diverge in full-model mode.
- *Rotation sharing*: Global sharing reduces compute but may underfit; chain-coupled (per layer block) is a compromise; avoid per-parameter independent rotations to exploit symmetry.
- *Full-model vs hybrid*: Full-model simplifies but requires handling 1D params (identity rotation); hybrid uses AdamW elsewhere. Validate which matches target regime (overtrain may favor full-model).

**Failure signatures**:
- *Divergence*: High QR fallback rate indicates instability; monitor loss spikes, especially in embeddings/LM-heads with Adam base.
- *Slow convergence*: Low alignment \( \langle \mathbf{G}, \mathbf{R} f(\mathbf{R}^\top \mathbf{G}) \rangle \) suggests overly conservative rotation.
- *High communication overhead*: Throughput drop >3% suggests all-gather pattern; switch to round-robin ownership.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. GPT-2 124M baseline sweep: Compare ARO (various base \( f \), QR types) vs eigen-rotations and baselines on training loss and speedup; validate rotation benefit dominates base choice.
2. GPT-XL 1.5B scaling: Train to 1× Chinchilla tokens; check stability and speedup vs AdamW/Muon; test hybrid vs full-model modes.
3. Sigma-MoE 2B overtrain: Run up to 8× overtrain; validate full-model ARO-SinkGD’s consistent lead over hybrid; check for divergence with Adam-based bases.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can new matrix optimization paradigms beyond orthogonalization push the efficiency frontier for LLMs? Orthogonalization (e.g., Muon) remains dominant; it is unclear whether alternative geometric constructions—such as general gradient rotations—can consistently outperform orthogonalization across scales, architectures, and overtraining regimes. Resolution requires large-scale pretraining (8B+ models, up to 8× overtrain) showing irreducibly faster loss reduction, plus convergence guarantees under approximate symmetries.
- For a given base optimizer, can we design rotation policies beyond eigen-rotations that are informed by the base optimizer and deliver improved robustness and efficiency? Eigen-rotation is standard; ARO’s QR-based rotation shows empirical gains but theoretical justification for why conditioning on \( f_t \) improves stability and speed is incomplete, and the full policy space is unexplored. Resolution requires systematic comparisons across base optimizers showing ARO’s rotation consistently yields lower loss than eigen-rotation, theoretical links between rotation objective and stepwise progress, and possibly automated search discovering better policies.
- Can rotations enable a unified update rule for all LLM parameters (including embeddings and LM heads) and act as an economical interface for exploiting cross-layer and cross-module couplings? Matrix optimizers typically exclude non-hidden layers; full-model rotation training is rarely evaluated, and efficient cross-layer rotation sharing remains an open design challenge. Resolution requires full-model ARO results at large scale (8B dense, 2B MoE) matching or exceeding hybrid baselines; ablation studies demonstrating consistent gains from rotation sharing with communication–algorithm co-design; parallel analysis showing sharing reduces computation while preserving quality.
- What rotation properties ensure stable and fast convergence under stochastic mini-batch noise, and can we automatically find rotations that meet these criteria? Polar (maximizing instantaneous decrease) rotations cause instability; eigen-rotations stabilize but are conservative; ARO appears to trade off but lacks a principled mechanism to adjust aggressiveness. Resolution requires a theoretical metric (e.g., bound relating rotation to alignment score variance) that predicts stability; empirical identification of a spectrum between eigen- and polar-like rotations; automated tuning or schedule for rotation “intensity” that transfers across tasks and batch sizes.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter transfer details are vague: which models were swept at 1× vs over-train budget? What LR multipliers applied for full-model vs hybrid?
- Distributed FSDP2 implementation (sharding boundaries, round-robin ownership) is not precisely specified, hindering faithful replication.
- Real-world QR fallback frequency and throughput impact at scale are unreported; instability may increase with model size.

## Confidence
- **Core speedup claims** (1.3–1.35× AdamW, 1.1–1.15× Muon): Medium. Empirical results are rigorous but replication hurdles (hyperparams, distribution) prevent definitive verification.
- **Unified update rule across all matrices**: High. Theory is clear and hybrid vs full-model ablation strongly supports applicability.
- **Symmetry teleportation mechanism**: Medium. Derivations are sound, but reliance on RMSNorm/residual invariance may not generalize to all architectures.
