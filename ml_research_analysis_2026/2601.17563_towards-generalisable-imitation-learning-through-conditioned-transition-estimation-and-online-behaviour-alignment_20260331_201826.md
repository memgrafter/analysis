# Towards Generalisable Imitation Learning Through Conditioned Transition Estimation and Online Behaviour Alignment
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.17563
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17563
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: UfO eliminates action supervision and achieves superior generalisation, outperforming the teacher and all ILfO baselines on MuJoCo with highest average normalised performance and lowest coefficient of variation.

## Executive Summary
Existing imitation learning from observation (ILfO) relies on action supervision, assumes a single optimal action per state, and exhibits behaviour-seeking. This paper introduces Unsupervised Imitation Learning from Observation (UfO), a two-stage framework that first jointly trains a policy and generative model on teacher transitions and agent interactions without supervised losses to approximate dynamics, then refines the policy via an adversarial stage with a frozen generator and recurrent discriminator. Evaluated on five MuJoCo environments with 10,000 unseen seeds, UfO surpasses the teacher and all ILfO baselines, demonstrating robust and superior generalisation.

## Method Summary
UfO employs a reconstruction stage where a policy and generative model are jointly trained using both teacher state trajectories and agent-environment interactions. Thisstage approximates environment dynamics without any action-based supervision. In the subsequent adversarial stage, the generator is frozen while a recurrent discriminator and the policy are updated through online interaction. The discriminator provides feedback to align the agent’s behaviour with the teacher’s state distribution, refining the policy while preventing catastrophic forgetting by decoupling dynamics estimation from policy optimisation.

## Key Results
- UfO outperforms the teacher demonstration and all compared ILfO baselines across five MuJoCo tasks.
- Achieves the highest average normalised performance (P=1.0197) and the lowest coefficient of variation.
- Demonstrates robust generalisation when evaluated on 10,000 previously unseen seeds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method succeeds by separating dynamics approximation from policy refinement. The reconstruction stage builds a robust model of state transitions conditioned on actions using only state data, avoiding biases from imperfect action labels. The adversarial stage then aligns the agent’s behaviour with the teacher’s state distribution online, using a recurrent discriminator to capture temporal dependencies. This two-phase approach prevents the policy from overfitting to specific teacher trajectories and reduces mode collapse, leading to better generalisation across diverse initial conditions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Imitation Learning from Observation (ILfO)**: Learning policies from state-only demonstrations without actions. *Why needed*: Overcomes scarcity of action-labelled expert data. *Quick check*: Can the policy match expert performance using only state trajectories?
- **Adversarial Imitation Learning (AIL)**: Uses a discriminator to distinguish agent from expert, training policy to fool it. *Why needed*: Enables imitation without explicit reward. *Quick check*: Does the discriminator’s accuracy stabilise during training?
- **Recurrent Discriminators**: Incorporate temporal context in adversarial training. *Why needed*: Capture sequential patterns in behaviour. *Quick check*: Does adding recurrence improve performance on tasks with long-horizon dependencies?
- **Dynamics Modeling**: Learning state transition functions. *Why needed*: Predict future states for planning. *Quick check*: Can the model accurately predict next states given current state and action?
- **Catastrophic Forgetting**: Performance drop on previously learned tasks when learning new ones. *Why needed*: Maintains competence across diverse conditions. *Quick check*: Does the policy retain performance on earlier tasks after training on new ones?
- **Online Behaviour Alignment**: Adjusting policy during deployment to match expert distribution. *Why needed*: Adapts to distribution shifts. *Quick check*: Does performance improve with more online iterations?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Teacher state trajectories -> Reconstruction stage (Policy, Generative model) -> Frozen generator -> Adversarial stage (Policy, Recurrent discriminator) -> Agent-environment interactions.

Critical path: Collect teacher transitions -> Jointly train policy and generative model (reconstruction) -> Freeze generator -> Interact online, update policy and discriminator (adversarial) -> Evaluate on unseen seeds.

Design tradeoffs: Frozen generator prevents overwriting learned dynamics but may become stale; recurrent discriminator captures temporal patterns but increases complexity. Avoiding supervised losses reduces dependency on action labels but requires sufficient interaction data.

Failure signatures: Mode collapse in policy output, discriminator converging too quickly (policy collapse), unstable training dynamics, performance degradation on unseen seeds.

First experiments: 1) Train on AntMuJoCo with default settings; 2) Replace recurrent discriminator with MLP and compare performance; 3) Add Gaussian noise to teacher states and measure robustness.

## Limitations
- Evaluated only on five MuJoCo tasks; applicability to visual or real-world domains untested.
- Assumes availability of teacher state trajectories; performance with suboptimal or noisy teacher data unknown.
- Lack of ablations for individual components (recurrent discriminator, joint reconstruction, frozen generator).
- Evaluation seeds may share distribution with training, not testing vulnerability to distribution shift.

## Confidence
- UfO eliminates action supervision: High
- Two-stage training prevents catastrophic forgetting and yields robust generalisation: Medium
- UfO outperforms teacher and all ILfO baselines: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate UfO on visual control benchmarks (e.g., DeepMind Control Suite) to test scalability to high-dimensional observations.

3. Ablate the adversarial stage by removing the frozen generator and/or recurrent discriminator to quantify their individual contributions.
