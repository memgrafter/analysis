# The Representational Geometry of Number

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06843
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06843
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** Number concept embeddings maintain a stable relational geometry across tasks and models, with task-specific subspaces that are largely linearly transformable.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses a fundamental question in cognitive science: do conceptual representations converge to a shared manifold for generalization or diverge into orthogonal subspaces to avoid interference? Using number concepts (1–9) in large language models (BERT, GPT-2, Qwen2.5, Qwen2.5-Math), the authors extract contextual embeddings across five numerical tasks. Geometric analyses reveal that the relative positions of numbers remain highly stable across tasks (Procrustes disparity ~0.010 vs ~0.1 random), while tasks occupy distinct subspaces with low overlap (<0.2) and encode low-level features nearly orthogonally. Despite this separation, task subspaces are highly linearly transformable (SVCCA correlations 0.80–0.90). These findings indicate that a shared relational scaffold supports generalization, while subspace disentanglement mitigates interference.

## Method Summary
Researchers used handcrafted sentence templates for five number-related tasks (quantity, comparison, arithmetic, properties, ordinal) to extract contextual embeddings of digits 1–9 from four LLMs. Embeddings were taken from a 75% layer depth (e.g., layer 9 of BERT-base), L2-normalized, and averaged per number within each task. Three analyses were applied: (1) Procrustes alignment comparing geometric configurations of task embeddings, (2) asymmetric subspace overlap based on principal components (95% variance retained) to quantify separation, and (3) SVCCA to measure linear transformability between top PCs. Baselines included random permutation of number labels.

## Key Results
- **Stable relational geometry:** Procrustes disparity between tasks is extremely low (~0.010), far below random (~0.077–0.273), showing preserved relative number positions across tasks.
- **Task subspace disentanglement:** Task subspaces overlap minimally (<0.2); low-level features like parity and magnitude are nearly orthogonal (88.8°), indicating segregation to avoid interference.
- **Linear transformability:** SVCCA correlations are high (0.80–0.90), meaning subspace mappings are largely linear, suggesting a shared underlying structure.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Stable Relational Geometry
- **Claim:** A stable relational geometric structure among number concepts (1–9) is shared across tasks and models, enabling generalization.
- **Mechanism:** The relative positions (pairwise distances/angles) of number embeddings are preserved under rigid transformations (rotation, scaling, translation) across different task contexts. This invariant "scaffold" allows the same underlying numerical relationships to support diverse operations.
- **Core assumption:** Generalization does not require identical coordinates but preserved relational geometry (e.g., the MNL-like ordering).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "the relative geometric relations among numbers remain highly stable across tasks and models—Procrustes disparity ~0.010 vs random permutation 0.077–0.273"
  - [section 4.1] t-SNE projections show consistent sequential ordering of numbers within task clusters; Procrustes disparity confirms non-random alignment.
- **Break condition:** If Procrustes disparity between task embeddings approaches the random permutation baseline (~0.1+), the shared relational structure collapses.

### Mechanism 2: Task Subspace Disentanglement
- **Claim:** Task-specific subspaces are segregated (low overlap) to minimize interference between distinct numerical operations (e.g., parity vs. magnitude).
- **Mechanism:** Different tasks utilize largely non-overlapping principal subspaces (top-k PCs), physically separating their representational "activity." Low-level features (parity, magnitude) are encoded along nearly orthogonal linear directions within these subspaces.
- **Core assumption:** Interference is mitigated by allocating distinct directional resources (subspace occupancy) rather than sharing all dimensions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "task subspaces occupy distinct regions (overlap <0.2)" and "low-level features like magnitude and parity are encoded along nearly orthogonal linear directions (e.g., parity 88.8° to magnitude)"
  - [section 4.2] Asymmetric subspace overlap is low (<0.2) except for functionally linked tasks (Successor/Predecessor: 0.36–0.42); parity vs. magnitude angle is 88.8°.
- **Break condition:** Subspace overlap exceeds a functional threshold (e.g., >0.5) for dissimilar tasks, indicating failure to segregate and high interference risk.

### Mechanism 3: Linear Equivariance Across Subspaces
- **Claim:** Despite physical separation, task subspaces are largely transformable into one another via linear mappings (high SVCCA), preserving functional equivalence and enabling efficient task-switching.
- **Mechanism:** Canonical correlations between top principal components of different task subspaces remain high (0.80–0.90), meaning linear projections can align them. This "linear equivariance" means the shared relational structure is preserved across subspace rotations.
- **Core assumption:** The brain/LLM does not need to reuse identical neurons/dimensions; linear transformations suffice to map between task-specific codes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "these subspaces are largely transformable into one another via linear mappings, indicating that representations share relational structure despite being located in distinct subspaces"
  - [section 4.2] SVCCA correlations are typically 0.80–0.90 across task pairs; parity/primality show lower alignment, suggesting increasing non-linear structure with task complexity.
- **Break condition:** Mean SVCCA correlation drops below ~0.6 for core numerical tasks, indicating that linear mappings are insufficient and significant non-linear re-coding is required.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Representational Geometry**
  - *Why needed here:* It is the analytical framework used to quantify "shape" of concept embeddings (distances, angles, subspace structure) rather than raw vectors.
  - *Quick check question:* What does a low Procrustes disparity between two task embedding sets tell us about their geometric relationship?
- **Concept: Subspace Disentanglement**
  - *Why needed here:* Central to the paper's claim that tasks occupy distinct regions to avoid interference; measured by asymmetric subspace overlap.
  - *Quick check question:* If Task A's top-3 PCs explain 80% of Task B's variance, what does that imply about their subspace relationship?
- **Concept: Linear Equivariance / Transformability**
  - *Why needed here:* Explains how shared relational structure is maintained despite subspace separation; operationalized by SVCCA.
  - *Quick check question:* High SVCCA correlation between two task representations means what about the existence of a linear mapping between them?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input sentence → tokenizer → embedding lookup → transformer stack (multiple layers) → extract activation at [NUM] token position → task-specific representation matrix (numbers × features) → analysis (Procrustes, PCA for subspace, SVCCA)
- **Critical path:** 
  1. Design minimal sentence templates per numerical task to elicit target representation.
  2. Extract mid-to-late layer embeddings (e.g., 75% depth) for numbers 1–9 in digit/word formats.
  3. For each task, compute mean representation per number.
  4. Run Procrustes between all task mean matrices.
  5. For each task, perform PCA to get top-k PCs; compute asymmetric overlap matrix.
  6. Apply SVCCA to all task pairs using top PCs (variance explained threshold ~95%).
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Layer depth: earlier layers mix syntax/lexical info; later layers more abstract but may overfit task.
  - Choosing k for PCA: too low loses variance, too high reduces interpretability.
  - Sentence templates: must unambiguously cue the target number concept without trivial lexical cues.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. Procrustes disparity near random → no stable relational geometry.
  2. Subspace overlap high (>0.5) for dissimilar tasks (e.g., parity vs. successor) → failure to disentangle.
  3. SVCCA low (<0.6) for tasks expected to be related → linear transformability fails, suggesting non-linear transformations or fundamentally different codes.
  4. t-SNE shows no clear number ordering → collapse of MNL-like structure.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replication check:** Run the full pipeline on BERT and GPT-2 (as in paper) to verify the three core results (stable Procrustes, low overlap, high SVCCA) hold in your implementation environment.
  2. **Layer sensitivity test:** Repeat analyses for early (25%), middle (50%), and late (90%) layers to confirm that the reported geometry is stable and not an artifact of a specific depth.
  3. **Task boundary probe:** Introduce a novel "non-numerical" control task (e.g., "The color of the number three is...") to see if it occupies a truly orthogonal subspace (overlap ~0) or contaminates the numerical subspaces, testing the specificity of the disentanglement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalization to larger number spaces:** The findings are limited to single-digit numbers; it is unknown whether the relational geometry remains stable for multi-digit numbers or continuous quantities.
- **Cross-concept disentanglement:** Do other concept groups (e.g., colors, animals) also form task-specific subspaces with minimal overlap, or is numerical cognition special?
- **Impact of model scaling:** How do model size (parameters) and training data diversity affect the degree of subspace disentanglement and linear transformability?
- **Non-linear alignability:** For tasks showing lower SVCCA (e.g., parity vs. primality), what non-linear transformations are needed to achieve full alignment, and does this indicate fundamentally different coding schemes?

## Limitations
- **Replication parameters unclear:** Layer depth (75% of network) and PCA dimensionality (95% variance threshold) are not explicitly reported per task/model, hindering exact reproduction.
- **Scope constraints:** Analysis restricted to digits 1–9 and five predefined tasks; generalizability to larger number ranges, continuous quantities, or other conceptual domains remains unknown.
- **Linearity assumption:** Procrustes and SVCCA rely on linear transformations; non-linear representational relationships could be overlooked.

## Confidence
- **Stable relational geometry:** High confidence. Procrustes disparity effect is large and consistent across models and tasks, supported by t-SNE visualizations.
- **Task subspace disentanglement:** Medium confidence. Overlap and orthogonality support segregation, but some task pairs show moderate overlap (~0.4) and thresholds depend on variance explained.
- **Linear transformability:** Medium confidence. SVCCA correlations are high but lower for some tasks, and results are sensitive to PCA dimensionality.

## Next Checks
1. **Parameter sensitivity sweep:** Re-run analyses across a grid of layer depths (25%, 50%, 75%, 90%) and PCA variance thresholds (90%, 95%, 99%) to quantify robustness and identify optimal defaults.
2. **Non-numerical control task:** Embed a semantically unrelated task (e.g., color association) to measure subspace overlap with numerical tasks; near-zero overlap would confirm specificity of disentanglement.
3. **Non-linear alignment test:** Replace SVCCA with kernel CCA or estimate mutual information between task representations to verify that high correspondence persists under non-linear mappings, strengthening the claim of shared relational structure.
