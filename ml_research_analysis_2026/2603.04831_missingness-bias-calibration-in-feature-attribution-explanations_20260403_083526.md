# Missingness Bias Calibration in Feature Attribution Explanations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04831
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04831
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MCal reduces missingness bias by up to an order of magnitude across medical benchmarks, matching or outperforming heavyweight approaches like full retraining or architectural modifications.

## Executive Summary
Missingness bias in feature attribution explanations arises when models are evaluated on ablated, out-of-distribution inputs, leading to unreliable feature importance scores. Existing solutions require costly retraining or architectural changes. This work introduces MCal, a lightweight post-hoc method that corrects bias by fine-tuning a simple linear head (an affine transformation) on the logits of a frozen base model, using cross-entropy loss between predictions on ablated inputs and clean inputs. MCal is model-agnostic and requires only output logits. Across diverse medical benchmarks (vision, language, tabular), MCal consistently reduces missingness bias—measured via KL divergence of class distributions—often matching or outperforming heavyweight approaches, while improving explanation quality and robustness without harming classifier accuracy. Theoretical guarantees ensure convex optimization to a global optimum.

## Method Summary
MCal applies an affine transformation \( R_\theta(z) = Wz + b \) to the logits of a frozen base model \( f \). The calibrator parameters \( \theta = (W, b) \) are trained to align the model's predictions on ablated inputs \( x' \) with those on the corresponding clean inputs \( x \). Specifically, given a pair \( (x, x') \), we minimize the cross-entropy between \( R_\theta(f(x')) \) and the one-hot label of \( f(x) \) (the clean prediction). This encourages the calibrated model's class distribution on ablated inputs to match the clean distribution, thereby correcting the bias. Optionally, an ensemble of calibrators can be trained for different ablation rates, allowing the model to select the appropriate calibrator at inference based on the ablation fraction. MCal only requires logit outputs from the base model and a dataset of clean-ablated pairs, making it a practical post-hoc correction that avoids retraining the full model.

## Key Results
- MCal reduces missingness bias (KL divergence) dramatically: on CheXpert, it achieves \( 8.82 \times 10^{-3} \) versus Retrain's \( 2.67 \times 10^{-2} \); on MedQA, \( 9.44 \times 10^{-4} \) versus Retrain's \( 1.70 \times 10^{-1} \).
- It improves explanation faithfulness (lower sufficiency and sensitivity) for LIME and SHAP without degrading classifier accuracy on clean or ablated inputs.
- The affine calibration objective is convex, guaranteeing convergence to a global optimum under standard gradient-based optimization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Missingness bias manifests as a distortion in the model’s output distribution rather than a fundamental defect in the model’s internal representations, making it correctable via a post-hoc output-space transformation.
- **Mechanism:** Ablated inputs are out-of-distribution, causing a systematic shift in the predicted class distribution (e.g., toward a "healthy" class). This shift is a consistent, predictable offset in the logit space that can be reversed with an affine transformation (matrix scaling + bias) learned from aligned clean-ablated prediction pairs.
- **Core assumption:** The base model’s feature representations are sufficiently robust that only the final decision boundary needs adjustment for ablated inputs; the distortion is separable from the core representation.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] and [section 3.1] (Equation 3 objective). Weak support from surrounding literature.
- **Break condition:** If the base model’s internal representations collapse or become completely uninformative under ablation (e.g., due to over-reliance on spurious features that are ablated), an affine transformation in output space would be insufficient.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The affine parametrization of the calibrator guarantees that optimization converges to a globally optimal solution, providing stable and reproducible correction.
- **Mechanism:** The objective function \( L(\theta) \) is a cross-entropy loss composed with an affine transformation \( R_\theta(z) = Wz + b \). Since cross-entropy is convex in the logits and an affine transformation preserves convexity, \( L(\theta) \) is convex in the parameters \( \theta = (W, b) \). Therefore, gradient-based optimization is guaranteed to find the global optimum.
- **Core assumption:** The relationship between the uncalibrated (ablated) and target (clean) distributions is effectively linear/affine in the logit space for the given dataset and model.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.2] Theorem 3.1 and proof; [abstract]. Moderate support from calibration literature.
- **Break condition:** If a more complex, non-linear relationship exists between ablated and clean logit distributions, the affine form is misspecified. While optimal under the chosen parametrization, it may not achieve the minimal possible missingness bias.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Conditioning the calibrator on the specific ablation rate (fraction of features ablated) improves mitigation because missingness bias is a function of the ablation intensity.
- **Mechanism:** The severity and nature of the distribution shift vary with the percentage of features ablated (e.g., 10% vs. 90%). An ensemble of specialized affine calibrators, each trained for a narrow range of ablation rates, can learn more precise corrections than a single universal calibrator.
- **Core assumption:** The bias induced by ablating a random 50% of features (used to train the unconditioned calibrator) is not representative of the bias at all other rates; the relationship between ablation fraction and bias is non-uniform.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.3] and [section 4, Question 3] (Figure 6). Weak support from related work.
- **Break condition:** If the bias function with respect to ablation rate is perfectly linear or if the ensemble introduces significant overhead without tangible gain for a given application's ablation range.

## Foundational Learning
### Perturbation-based Feature Attribution (LIME, SHAP)
- **Why needed:** MCal is designed to correct a specific failure mode of these methods—their reliance on perturbed/ablated inputs to estimate feature importance. Understanding the baseline process (replace features, feed to model, measure output change) is essential to grasp the "missingness" problem.
- **Quick check:** Why does replacing an image patch with a black pixel (an out-of-distribution input) not simulate the effect of "removing" the information in that patch for a trained classifier?

### Missingness Bias Quantification (Eq 1, KL Divergence)
- **Why needed:** This is the core metric MCal optimizes against (indirectly). It defines the problem formally: the divergence between the class distribution on clean data vs. the distribution on ablated data. All experiments report this.
- **Quick check:** If a model perfectly preserves its class distribution regardless of which features are ablated, what would the missingness bias KL divergence be?

### Post-hoc Calibration (Temperature Scaling, Platt Scaling)
- **Why needed:** MCal is framed as a *calibration* method applied in output space. It extends classic calibration (which aligns confidence with accuracy on *clean* data) to align predictions between *clean and ablated* data. Understanding temperature (a scalar) and vector scaling (diagonal matrix) provides context for why MCal uses a full affine matrix and why it outperforms them.
- **Quick check:** How does temperature scaling work, and why might it be insufficient for correcting missingness bias, which shifts entire *class distributions*?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Base Model (f) -> Calibrator Head (Rθ) -> Calibrated Output. Training pipeline: clean input x and ablated x', get logits z = f(x), z' = f(x'), train Rθ to minimize cross-entropy(Rθ(z'), onehot(argmax(z))).

### Critical path
1. **Collect Data:** Sample N clean inputs. For each, generate an ablated version with chosen ablation strategy (e.g., random patches). Store pairs (f(x), f(x')).
2. **Initialize Calibrator:** e.g., W ← I (identity), b ← 0.
3. **Optimize:** Minimize L(θ) using Adam (lr=1e-3) for ~5k steps. Early stopping on validation set advisable.
4. **Deploy:** Wrap base model f with Rθ: ˜f(x) = Rθ(f(x)). Use ˜f for downstream perturbation-based explanations (LIME, SHAP, etc.).

### Design tradeoffs
- **Affine vs. Non-linear Head:** Affine guarantees convexity and global optimum but is simpler. A 1-layer neural net might fit more complex shifts but loses guarantees and risks overfitting.
- **Conditioned vs. Unconditioned:** Conditioned (ensemble) typically performs better but requires training multiple heads and logic to select one. Unconditioned is simpler and cheaper, suitable if ablation rate is fixed/predictable.
- **Parameterization:** Full matrix W (m²+m params) vs. diagonal/vector-scaling (2m params). Diagonal reduces overfitting risk for many classes but may be less expressive.

### Failure signatures
- **Training loss zero, test bias high:** Classic overfitting. Symptoms: very small training KL but high test missingness bias. Mitigate: add L2 regularization on W, b; use diagonal W; increase training samples N.
- **Calibrator fails to improve bias:** Check ablation strategy matches intended use-case. Verify base model actually has missingness bias (run baseline metric first). May indicate bias is not affine-correctable.
- **Accuracy drop on clean inputs:** If calibrated model ˜f's accuracy on *clean* data drops significantly, the calibration overcorrected. Monitor clean accuracy during training. Adjust loss weight or training data mix.
- **Mismatch in conditioning:** Using unconditioned calibrator across all ablation fractions when conditioned would perform better. Compare unconditioned vs. conditioned ensembles on a range of ablation fractions.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Replicate Core Result (Table 1):** On a small vision dataset (e.g., subset of CheXpert or Brain MRI), implement MCal (unconditioned). Compute missingness bias (Eq 1) for Base and MCal at 50% ablation. Expect ~10x reduction comparable to paper.
2. **Test Conditioning (Fig 6):** For same model, train a *conditioned* ensemble (one calibrator for each ablation rate p={0.2, 0.5, 0.8}). Compare its bias curve to the unconditioned calibrator. Expect conditioned to dominate, especially at non-training rates.
3. **Validate Convexity & Stability (Section 3.2):** For a simple synthetic 3-class problem, plot training loss curves from different random initializations of (W, b). All should converge to the same minimum. Also, try a non-convex head (1-layer ReLU net); expect non-convergence/variability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does missingness bias magnitude vary with dataset variability (e.g., diversity, domain) and model architecture (e.g., CNN vs. Transformer)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 6: "However, it is not known how missingness bias varies as a function of both dataset variability and model architecture."
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding these dependencies would guide model design for robustness and determine the applicability of MCal.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments across datasets with controlled variability (size, class diversity) and multiple architectures, measuring bias via KL divergence.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What are the trade-offs between affine calibrators and more expressive parametrizations (e.g., diagonal, shallow MLP) regarding theoretical guarantees (convexity) and empirical bias reduction?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work: "One direction is to investigate the theoretical guarantees and empirical performance of different calibrator parametrizations, such as a one-layer feedforward network instead of an affine transform."
- **Why unresolved:** Affine calibrators provide convexity but may lack expressiveness; deeper calibrators could reduce bias further but risk non-convexity.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis of convexity for alternative parametrizations and empirical comparisons on benchmarks, including regularization effects.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does MCal improve explanation quality across a wider set of XAI methods (e.g., Integrated Gradients, DeepLIFT) and faithfulness metrics (e.g., max-sensitivity, infidelity) beyond LIME/SHAP?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work: "Another extension is to broaden our study on the performance of calibrated classifiers in explainability, such as with respect to the explanation methods and metrics surveyed in Section 5."
- **Why unresolved:** Current evaluation is limited to two methods and two metrics; other methods may interact differently with calibration.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comprehensive evaluation on XAI benchmarks using multiple attribution methods and standardized faithfulness metrics, comparing calibrated vs. uncalibrated models.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How can missingness bias be mitigated when prediction logits are unavailable, such as with black-box API models?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Future work: "It would be interesting to explore methods for mitigating missingness bias when prediction logits are not available, a common restriction for API-based large language models." Limitations also note this requirement.
- **Why unresolved:** API models often only return probabilities or text, making the affine transform in MCal impossible to apply directly.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop calibration methods operating on probabilities or synthetic logits, and validate on API LLMs (e.g., GPT-4) by measuring missingness bias reduction via alternative metrics.

## Limitations
- Ablation implementation details (e.g., patch/word selection, replacement values) are not fully specified per modality, leading to ambiguity in reproduction.
- Pre-trained base models are not released; reproduction requires costly retraining from scratch on each dataset.
- Ensemble calibration logic for selecting calibrators at inference is ambiguous, especially for unseen ablation rates.
- The method assumes missingness bias is correctable via an affine transformation; if internal representations collapse under ablation, MCal may fail.

## Confidence
- **Bias reduction:** Medium. Strong empirical results across datasets, but relies on the affine-correctability assumption.
- **Explanation quality:** Medium. Reported improvements depend on bias reduction and attribution setup.
- **Convexity guarantee:** High. Formal proof is sound and follows standard convex optimization.
- **Conditioning benefit:** Medium. Positive experimental evidence (Fig. 6) but ensemble details are unclear.
