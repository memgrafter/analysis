# GraphWalker: Agentic Knowledge Graph Question Answering via Synthetic Trajectory Curriculum

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.28533
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28533
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GraphWalker-7B-SFT-RL achieves state-of-the-art 79.6% exact-match on Complex Web Questions (CWQ) and 91.5% on WebQSP.

## Executive Summary
GraphWalker addresses training data scarcity and poor reasoning generalization in agentic knowledge graph question answering (KGQA). It introduces an automated trajectory synthesis pipeline (GraphSynth) using Constrained Random Walks to generate 15k diverse training trajectories, and a two-stage fine-tuning process: Stage 1 builds broad exploration priors on GraphSynth; Stage 2 imbues reflection using 6k expert trajectories (GraphRoll). A lightweight GRPO reinforcement learning step further pushes performance beyond the SFT ceiling. The resulting GraphWalker-7B-SFT-RL model achieves SOTA exact-match scores of 79.6% on CWQ and 91.5% on WebQSP, and shows strong zero-shot generalization to unseen reasoning structures on GrailQA and GraphWalkerBench. Ablations confirm the importance of the two-stage order and the complementary roles of synthetic and expert data.

## Method Summary
The method comprises three main stages. First, synthetic trajectories are generated via Constrained Random Walks on the Freebase KG, producing 15k diverse reasoning paths (GraphSynth) covering composition chains and conjunction graphs. Natural questions are synthesized and filtered for semantic quality, and environment feedback (KG observations) is simulated using BM25 retrieval with an LLM reranker. Second, a two-stage supervised fine-tuning (SFT) pipeline is applied: Stage 1 trains the agent policy on GraphSynth to acquire broad KG navigation skills; Stage 2 fine-tunes on 6k expert trajectories (GraphRoll) that demonstrate reflection and error recovery, using outcome-based rejection sampling to ensure correctness. Third, a lightweight GRPO reinforcement learning step with sparse exact-match reward is applied to a CWQ subset, using the Stage 2 model as reference, to further optimize long-horizon decisions. The agent uses two tool primitives (`get_relations`, `get_triples`) and an LLM-based reranker (top-20 relations) to manage retrieval noise.

## Key Results
- GraphWalker-7B-SFT-RL achieves SOTA exact-match: 79.6% on CWQ, 91.5% on WebQSP, surpassing prompting methods (ToG, GoG) and training baselines (KG-R1).
- Strong zero-shot generalization: 86.3% EM on GrailQA zero-shot split and 63.5% on the novel GraphWalkerBench (unseen reasoning structures).
- Ablations reveal: removing Stage 1 (GraphSynth) reduces Pass@k, indicating narrowed search space; removing Stage 2 (GraphRoll) causes a 9.00% average EM drop; mixing both datasets in one stage harms performance (70.2% vs 79.6% EM on CWQ), underscoring the critical sequential order.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
GraphWalker’s effectiveness relies on three synergistic mechanisms:

1. **Constrained Random Walk synthesis** generates structurally diverse synthetic trajectories (GraphSynth-15k) that establish a broad exploration prior over the KG. This addresses training data scarcity by exposing the agent to a wide range of reasoning patterns (composition chains, conjunction graphs). Evidence: Removing GraphSynth reduces Pass@k across all k, confirming expanded search space.

2. **Expert trajectories with reflection** (GraphRoll-6k) teach error recovery through explicit backtracking and self-correction. The agent learns to recognize and correct erroneous retrieval actions. Evidence: Ablation removing GraphRoll causes a 9.00% average EM drop, larger than removing Stage1, indicating its critical role in handling KG noise.

3. **Sequential two-stage SFT order** is crucial: exploration first (GraphSynth) then reflection (GraphRoll) creates a competent prior that enables lightweight GRPO RL to surpass the SFT ceiling. Mixed training disrupts this synergy. Evidence: Mixed-SFT+RL underperforms full pipeline (70.2% vs 79.6% EM on CWQ), demonstrating order importance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge Graph as Interactive Environment**: The agent must ground reasoning in a structured KG via tool calls (`get_relations`, `get_triples`), requiring understanding of entities, relations, and query semantics, and managing context across turns.  
  *Why needed*: Enables step-by-step interaction and observation-driven decisions.  
  *Quick check*: Given a KG and a natural language question, design the agent’s action space and observation format so that the agent can efficiently locate the answer without exhaustive search.

- **Trajectory-based Supervised Fine-Tuning**: Unlike static QA, agentic KGQA requires learning a policy over multi-step interactions (state = question + history, action = tool call or answer, observation = KG results). SFT teaches the model to generate appropriate `` and action tokens conditioned on history.  
  *Why needed*: Models must produce structured outputs that drive KG interactions.  
  *Quick check*: What are the key tokens in an agentic trajectory (``, `<kg-query>`, `<information>`, `<answer>`) and how does next-token prediction on such sequences differ from training on chain-of-thought rationales?

- **Curriculum for Exploration vs. Reflection**: Exploration and reflection are distinct competencies; exploration requires diverse path coverage, reflection requires examples of mistakes and recovery. Combining them in one stage leads to interference.  
  *Why needed*: Sequential training yields a competent prior that allows RL to further optimize.  
  *Quick check*: Why might training simultaneously on diverse synthetic paths and expert error-recovery trajectories yield worse performance than sequential training, and how would you design an experiment to test this hypothesis?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
KG Environment -> GraphSynth/GraphRoll Data Generation -> SFT Stage1 -> SFT Stage2 -> GRPO RL -> Agent Policy -> Inference (with Reranker) -> Evaluation

**Critical path**:
1. Synthetic data generation: Constrained random walks produce reasoning paths; natural questions are synthesized and filtered; KG queries are executed to simulate environment feedback; thoughts are generated to form complete trajectories.
2. SFT Stage1: Train policy π on GraphSynth to learn general KG navigation.
3. SFT Stage2: Fine-tune π on GraphRoll to learn reflection and backtracking.
4. RL: Apply GRPO with sparse EM reward on a CWQ subset, using the Stage2 model as reference policy.
5. Inference: Deploy trained agent; at each turn: think → (optional) query → observe; repeat until answer.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Synthetic vs. expert data*: GraphSynth provides scale and structural diversity but risks semantic noise; GraphRoll provides high-quality, focused examples but is limited in size. Both are necessary for complementary skills.
- *SFT order*: Sequential training is crucial; mixing datasets dilutes the exploration foundation and harms performance.
- *RL reward*: Sparse EM is sufficient given a strong SFT prior; dense rewards may be noisy and costly to design.
- *Reranking*: Top-20 balances precision/recall; Top-10 loses critical relations, Top-30 injects noise.
- *Global KG*: Exposes agent to full KG complexity but increases branching; requires robust exploration training.

**Failure signatures**:
- **Low retrieval rate**: Agent fails to have gold answer appear in observations → likely insufficient Stage1 training (GraphSynth ablated).
- **Low recovery rate**: Agent does not correct erroneous queries → missing Stage2 (GraphRoll) or RL.
- **Poor zero-shot generalization**: Large drop on GrailQA/GraphWalkerBench → inadequate structural diversity in GraphSynth or missing RL.
- **Mixed SFT underperformance**: Performance drop when combining both datasets in one stage → order disruption.
- **Reranker ablation**: Significant EM drop without reranking, especially on complex queries, indicating sensitivity to retrieval noise.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Pass@k analysis** (Section 3.3): Sample 500 CWQ questions, run 32 rollouts, compute Pass@k. GraphWalker-SFT-RL achieves highest Pass@k across all k; removing GraphSynth reduces Pass@k, proving it expands the search space.
2. **Stage ablation** (Section 3.4): Remove SFT Stage1, Stage2, or RL individually. Both SFT stages contribute significantly; RL adds additional gain. Mixed-SFT+RL underperforms, demonstrating order importance.
3. **Zero-shot evaluation** (Section 3.3): Test on GrailQA (zero-shot split) and GraphWalkerBench (unseen structures). Full model achieves 86.3% EM on GrailQA and 63.5% on GraphWalkerBench; ablations show Stage1 drives generalization to unseen structures, Stage2 and RL provide further improvements.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can an agent build a robust exploration foundation on KGs to push reasoning capacity?** The paper’s approach relies on synthetic trajectories and a specific curriculum; broader robustness across diverse KGs remains unproven.
2. **Can the GraphWalker pipeline be implemented with open‑source LLMs without quality loss?** The dependency on proprietary models (GPT‑4o‑mini, Gemini‑2.5‑Pro) impedes reproducibility and cost‑effectiveness.
3. **Why does mixing GraphSynth and GraphRoll harm performance relative to the two‑stage approach?** The underlying learning interference mechanism is unclear, which is crucial for curriculum design.
4. **How does GraphWalker generalize to KGs with entirely unseen relation types or domains?** The method has not been evaluated on zero‑relation‑overlap benchmarks (e.g., biomedical QA).

## Limitations
- Synthetic trajectory validity: CRW-generated paths may be KG-invalid or semantically incoherent, potentially misleading the exploration prior.
- Expert trajectory coverage: GraphRoll’s 6k examples may not generalize to the full distribution of test-time errors.
- Implementation fidelity: Underspecified KG deployment, RL framework, and GraphRoll stratification may cause reproduction gaps.

## Confidence
- Two-stage SFT order criticality: High (ablations show mixed training significantly underperforms).
- CRW diversity → generalization: Medium (supported by Pass@k and zero-shot ablations but depends on synthetic data quality).
- RL gains beyond SFT ceiling: Medium (GRPO improves results, but sensitivity to hyperparameters not fully probed).

## Next Checks
1. **Synthetic data audit**: Run CRW independently and sample 100 GraphSynth trajectories; verify predicate chain validity via SPARQL checks and semantic coherence with a small LLM judge.
2. **KG robustness test**: Evaluate final model on a held-out Freebase snapshot (e.g., different release) to measure sensitivity to KG version/drift.
3. **RL ablation with alternative implementation**: Replicate GRPO using standard RL libraries (e.g., RLHF) with identical hyperparameters to confirm performance lift is not `lime`-specific.
