# Can LLMs Discern the Traits Influencing Your Preferences? Evaluating Personality-Driven Preference Alignment in LLMs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.07181
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07181
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Selecting personality-aligned preferences boosts multiple-choice accuracy from 29.25% (random) to 76.00% (with explicit trait labels); fine-tuned retrieval achieves 43.00% without annotations.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses noisy preference signals in LLM personalization by leveraging stable Big Five (OCEAN) personality traits as a latent guide. It proposes retrieving or conditioning on preferences aligned with inferred user traits during question answering. On the PACIFIC benchmark, this approach yields substantial accuracy gains, demonstrating that trait-driven filtering yields more coherent and predictive context than naive preference use. A fine-tuned retrieval-augmented method shows promising results without requiring ground-truth trait annotations.

## Method Summary
The task is personalized multiple-choice QA: given a question, four choices, and five context preference statements, select the option aligned with a user’s trait-driven preference. The PACIFIC dataset (1,200 instances) provides preference statements, questions, choices, and OCEAN scores (with confidence). Evaluation uses exact-match accuracy, stratified by Big Five traits (high/low). Methods include: few-shot with trait-aligned preferences; augmentation with persona hints (trait labels for preferences/choices); adding a reminder to infer personality; and RAG using DPR—either pretrained or fine-tuned with contrastive loss (same-trait positive, different-trait negative). The fine-tuned retriever is trained on filtered data (confidence ≥0.7) to fetch top-5 preferences per test question.

## Key Results
- Random/mixed preferences yield 29.25% accuracy.
- Explicit trait labels for aligned preferences boost accuracy to 76.00%.
- Fine-tuned RAG without trait annotations reaches 43.00%, outperforming pretrained retrieval.
- Accuracy drops significantly for low O/C/E/A when choice traits are provided, indicating social desirability bias.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Stable personality traits (Big Five/OCEAN) act as a consistent latent signal to filter noisy preferences. Aligning context preferences with the user’s inferred traits provides more coherent and relevant guidance, reducing ambiguity. This leverages the psychological finding that preferences rooted in enduring traits are more predictive of true choices than isolated statements. The retrieval component explicitly surfaces trait-relevant preferences, while the LLM leverages this curated context to select the personality-consistent answer. This mechanism assumes that Big Five traits are stable and generalizable across users. It remains unknown whether this approach extends to other personality models or preference domains.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: User Preference -> Trait Inference (implicit/explicit) -> Preference Retriever (DPR) -> Context Assembly (5 trait-aligned preferences) -> LLM (Gemma-3-4B-IT) -> Answer Choice. Critical path: infer user traits, retrieve aligned preferences, generate answer. Design tradeoffs: explicit trait labels vs. inferred traits (accuracy vs. annotation cost); retrieval cost vs. context relevance; few-shot simplicity vs. RAG scalability. Failure signatures: (1) low accuracy for low O/C/E/A due to social desirability bias; (2) poor retrieval trait alignment (near random) without fine-tuning; (3) model ignoring instructions (non-integer outputs). First experiments: (1) replicate with different LLMs (e.g., Llama) to test generalizability; (2) vary context size (3, 5, 10 preferences) to measure saturation; (3) compute per-trait alignment rate of retrieved sets to validate retrieval objective.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do personality-driven preference alignment methods generalize across different LLM architectures and to open-ended generation tasks beyond multiple-choice QA?
- What retrieval-augmented approaches can effectively retrieve personality-aligned preferences without ground-truth trait annotations, closing the performance gap with explicit label methods?
- How can RLHF-induced social desirability bias causing misinterpretation of low-trait expressions be mitigated to improve preference alignment for users with low O/C/E/A scores (except Neuroticism)?

## Limitations
- Test sampling ambiguity: “two questions per trait category” lacks details on replacement, domain stratification, or exact split.
- Context preference provenance unclear: whether preferences must come from different users/domains than test question’s preference.
- Missing RAG fine-tuning specifics: negative sampling strategy, batch composition, and handling of test preferences in retrieval pool.
- Filtered dataset balance: unreported per-trait counts in final test set may hinder sampling of two per category at confidence threshold.

## Confidence
- Personality-aligned selection boosts accuracy (76% vs ~29%): **Medium-High** (trend clear, but exact numbers sensitive to sampling details).
- Fine-tuned RAG outperforms pretrained retrieval (43% vs lower): **Medium** (gap depends on unspecified fine-tuning parameters).
- PACIFIC filtering yields balanced test set: **Low-Medium** (balance claims rely on unreported test split composition).
