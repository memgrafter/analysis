# Weisfeiler and Lehman Go Categorical

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.06787
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06787
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces a categorical Weisfeiler-Lehman framework for hypergraph neural networks via functorial lifting to graded posets; I-HIN and S-HIN achieve state-of-the-art results on hypergraph classification benchmarks, with I-HIN excelling on dense datasets and S-HIN on sparse structures with large hyperedges.

## Executive Summary
Hypergraph neural networks lack a unified, theoretically sound foundation for lifting-based expressivity, leaving a fragmented landscape of specialized architectures. This paper resolves this by formalizing lifting as a functor from the category of hypergraphs to the category of graded posets. Two concrete functors are proposed: the incidence poset functor \(I(H)\) and the symmetric simplicial complex functor \(S(H)\). The resulting Hypergraph Isomorphism Networks (HINs) inherit the expressive power of the generalized Weisfeiler-Lehman (GWL) test. Theoretically, both variants are proven at least as expressive as the standard Hypergraph WL test. Empirically, extensive evaluation on six hypergraph classification benchmarks shows that incidence‑based HIN (I-HIN) attains state‑of‑the‑art accuracy on dense datasets (e.g., 81.41% on IMDB_dir_genre), while the symmetric simplicial HIN (S-HIN) outperforms on sparse structures with large hyperedges (e.g., 61.02% on IMDB_wri_genre). The categorical framework not only unifies existing lifting constructions but also guides the design of topology‑aware GNNs for higher‑order data.

## Method Summary
The method lifts any hypergraph \(H\) into a graded poset \(F(H)\) via a functor \(F\). The poset encodes incidence or simplicial relations and carries a dimension function. A generalized Weisfeiler‑Lehman (GWL) update then iteratively refines features by aggregating messages along four adjacency types of each poset element: boundary, coboundary, lower, and upper neighbors. This discrete process is approximated by a neural architecture called the Categorical Message Passing Network (CatMPN), which implements the GWL dynamics with MLPs and injective aggregators. The choice of functor directly determines the message‑passing topology: the incidence functor \(I(H)\) yields a bipartite poset of nodes and hyperedges, while the symmetric simplicial functor \(S(H)\) builds the face lattice of the simplicial complex generated by hyperedges, capturing subset hierarchies up to a truncation threshold \(\tau\). Both resulting Hypergraph Isomorphism Networks (I‑HIN and S‑HIN) are theoretically guaranteed to be at least as expressive as the Hypergraph WL test. The categorical perspective subsumes prior lifting schemes and offers a systematic path to new architectures.

## Key Results
- **Theoretical guarantee:** Both I‑HIN and S‑HIN are at least as expressive as the Hypergraph Weisfeiler‑Lehman test (Theorems 9 and 11).
- **Empirical performance:** On six hypergraph classification benchmarks, I‑HIN reaches state‑of‑the‑art accuracy on dense datasets (81.41% on IMDB_dir_genre) and S‑HIN outperforms on sparse structures with large hyperedges (61.02% on IMDB_wri_genre), surpassing prior hypergraph neural networks and WL‑based models.
- **Unification:** The functorial lifting framework integrates incidence posets, simplicial complexes, and regular cell complexes into a single graded‑poset paradigm.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Functorial lifting guarantees isomorphism preservation when mapping hypergraphs to a computational domain.
- **Mechanism:** A functor \(F: \mathbf{Hyp} \to \mathbf{Poset}\) maps hypergraph objects and morphisms to graded posets while preserving composition and identity. This ensures that isomorphic hypergraphs \(H \cong H'\) yield isomorphic posets \(F(H) \cong F(H')\), making the lift valid for isomorphism testing.
- **Core assumption:** The source category (hypergraphs) and target category (graded posets) are well‑defined with appropriate morphisms.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "formalizes lifting as a functorial mapping from an arbitrary data category to the unifying category of graded posets."
  - [section 3.2] Formal definition of functors and their preservation properties.
  - [section 3.3] "By defining lifting maps as functors… we provide a rigorous guarantee that the lifting process preserves the isomorphism class."
- **Break condition:** If the functor fails to preserve morphism composition or identity (e.g., due to implementation error), the isomorphism guarantee collapses; distinct hypergraphs could map to indistinguishable posets.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Graded posets serve as a universal domain that subsumes disparate lifting constructions (incidence posets, simplicial complexes) under one algebraic framework.
- **Mechanism:** A graded poset \((P, \leq, \text{dim})\) equipped with a dimension function can encode incidence relations (bipartite graphs), face lattices (simplicial complexes), and cell attachments (CW complexes). This unification allows the same GWL update rule (Eq. 1) to be applied regardless of input type.
- **Core assumption:** All relevant higher‑order structures (hypergraphs, simplicial complexes) admit a faithful representation as graded posets with appropriate dimension assignments.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1] "Incidence posets, simplicial complexes, and regular cell complexes naturally induce graded poset structures."
  - [section 2] "We establish the space of graded posets as a universal target capable of encoding the incidence structure of hypergraphs as well as the face relations of simplicial and regular cell complexes."
- **Break condition:** If the input data contains relations that cannot be captured by a partial order with a consistent dimension function (e.g., cyclic containment not expressible as a poset), the graded poset representation loses information.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The choice of functor directly determines the message‑passing topology of the resulting neural network, enabling topology‑aware architecture design.
- **Mechanism:** Different functors produce graded posets with distinct covering relations \(\prec\). The 4‑adjacency sets (boundary \(B\), coboundary \(C\), lower \(N^\downarrow\), upper \(N^\uparrow\)) in the GWL update (Eq. 1) are computed from this poset structure. Consequently, a functor that creates richer poset hierarchies (e.g., \(S(H)\) with multiple dimensions per hyperedge) yields more message types than a flat bipartite lift (e.g., \(I(H)\)).
- **Core assumption:** The neural relaxation (CatMPN) faithfully approximates the discrete GWL dynamics; sufficient model capacity and injective aggregators are available.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2] Explicit construction of \(I\)-HIN and \(S\)-HIN from their respective functors, showing how \(S(H)\) introduces multi‑level interactions.
  - [section 4.1] "the message passing topology is strictly determined by the choice of functor."
  - [Theorem 9 & 11] Both functors yield models at least as expressive as HWL, but with different structural biases.
- **Break condition:** If the functor’s poset construction is too coarse (e.g., \(I(H)\) flattens hyperedge internals) or too sparse (truncating \(S(H)\) too aggressively), the resulting message‑passing topology may omit task‑critical intersections or connectivity patterns.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. **Functor selector** (chooses \(I\) or \(S\) based on data topology).
  2. **Poset constructor** (builds \(I(H)\) or \(S(H)\) from hypergraph \(H\); for \(S\), includes truncation threshold \(\tau\)).
  3. **Adjacency preprocessor** (computes \(B, C, N^\downarrow, N^\uparrow\) sets for each poset element).
  4. **CatMPN layers** (apply Eq. 9 with MLPs and aggregators for each adjacency type).
  5. **Readout** (global pooling, followed by classification MLP).
- **Critical path:** *Functor selection → Poset construction → Message passing.* The functor choice is determined by hypergraph density: use \(I\) for dense graphs (many hyperedges), \(S\) for sparse graphs with large hyperedges. This dictates all subsequent steps.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Incidence (\(I\)):** Low computational cost (\(O(\sum c_e^2 + \sum d_v^2)\)), efficient on dense data, but flattens hyperedge internals.

## Limitations
- Generalization of the functor selection heuristic beyond the six benchmarks is uncertain.
- Implementation details (e.g., node/hyperedge ID encoding, aggregation injectivity) may affect model robustness, but are not analyzed.
- Truncation in S‑HIN (\(\tau=20\)) introduces a trade‑off between computational cost and information loss, especially for very large hyperedges, which is not systematically studied.

## Confidence
- **High:** Functorial lifting preserves isomorphism (Theorem 6); graded poset framework unifies lifting constructions (Section 3.1).
- **Medium:** Both HIN variants are at least as expressive as HWL (Theorems 9, 11); empirical SOTA claims on six benchmarks.
- **Low:** Universality of the dense/sparse functor selection rule; practical impact of truncation threshold \(\tau\) on expressiveness.

## Next Checks
1. **Dataset diversity test:** Evaluate I‑HIN and S‑HIN on at least 10 additional hypergraph benchmarks spanning diverse densities, hyperedge size distributions, and tasks (classification, regression, link prediction). Validate whether the observed performance dichotomy generalizes.
2. **Truncation ablation:** For S‑HIN, systematically vary \(\tau\) (e.g., 5, 10, 20, ∞) on datasets with large hyperedges. Measure accuracy vs. computational cost to identify the point of diminishing returns and quantify information loss.
3. **Perturbation robustness:** Introduce controlled noise (random node/hyperedge removals, edge rewiring) into benchmark hypergraphs. Compare performance degradation of I‑HIN vs. S‑HIN to test sensitivity to structural perturbations and validate functor stability claims.
