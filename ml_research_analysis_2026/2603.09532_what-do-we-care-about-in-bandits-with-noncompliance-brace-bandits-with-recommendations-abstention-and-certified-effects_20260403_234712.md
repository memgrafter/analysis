# What Do We Care About in Bandits with Noncompliance? BRACE: Bandits with Recommendations, Abstention, and Certified Effects

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09532
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09532
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** BRACE is a parameter-free phase-doubling algorithm for finite-context square-IV bandits that guarantees simultaneous policy-value validity, fixed-gap identification of REC and TRT optima under homogeneity and invertibility, and empirically abstains safely under weak identification while preferring REC under homogeneity failure.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles bandit problems with noncompliance, where the learner's recommendations may not match delivered treatments. It identifies three distinct objectives: Recommendation Welfare (REC), which maximizes outcomes based on recommendations; Structural Treatment Welfare (TRT), which maximizes outcomes from fixed treatment policies; and Inference (INF), which provides valid confidence intervals. These objectives can conflict when downstream actors use private information, giving REC a strict advantage. The proposed BRACE algorithm addresses these with uniform exploration, certification of compliance matrix invertibility, and honest fallback intervals. Theoretically, it ensures simultaneous validity and identification under strong assumptions. Empirically, it demonstrates safety via abstention in weak-IV settings, conservative coverage, and a shift toward REC when structural homogeneity fails.

## Method Summary
BRACE operates in doubling phases with uniform random exploration over recommendations. At each phase end, it aggregates per-context statistics: context frequencies, empirical compliance matrices (mapping recommendations to treatments), and operational mean estimates. For Structural Treatment Welfare (TRT), it certifies each context's compliance matrix by checking invertibility and a norm condition; if certified, it inverts the matrix to estimate structural means with error bounds, otherwise defaults to full-range [0,1] intervals. Recommendation Welfare (REC) intervals are built directly from operational means. For any finite policy class, policy values are computed by aggregating local intervals with context-frequency corrections. For REC and TRT, the algorithm stops and commits to a policy when its lower confidence bound strictly exceeds all others' upper bounds; for INF, it continuously outputs intervals without stopping. This design ensures parameter-free operation and safety through certification and conservative fallbacks.

## Key Results
- Simultaneous policy-value validity holds for all policies and stopping times under uniform exploration.
- Fixed-gap identification of the optimal REC policy and optimal TRT policy is achieved under contextual homogeneity and compliance matrix invertibility.
- Empirical safety manifests as abstention and wide honest intervals under weak identification, REC preference under homogeneity failure, and tighter structural uncertainty with extra instruments; BRACE avoids unsafe deployments where baselines err.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Recommendation welfare (REC) and structural treatment welfare (TRT) are distinct objectives that can be strictly ordered when downstream actors use private information during compliance.  
**Mechanism:** The compliance function \( C(z) \) may depend on private signals \( U \) unknown to the learner. A recommendation \( z \) can exploit these signals indirectly (e.g., by triggering individualized discretion), while any direct treatment policy \( \pi_{str}(w) \) must be fixed given only observable \( w \). This creates a welfare gap: \( \sup_{\pi_{rec}} V^{rec}(\pi_{rec}) > \sup_{\pi_{str}} V^{str}(\pi_{str}) \).  
**Core assumption:** Compliance types \( C_t \) conditionally on \( W_t \) allow a mapping from recommendations to treatments influenced by hidden variables affecting reward potential.  
**Evidence anchors:** Proposition 3.2 constructs an explicit example with private signal \( U \) showing \( V^{rec}(\pi_{rec}^\dagger) = 1 > 1/2 = \sup_{\pi_{str}} V^{str}(\pi_{str}) \); abstract notes REC can strictly exceed every learner-measurable treatment policy.  
**Break condition:** When \( Z_t = X_t \) almost surely (direct control), REC and TRT coincide (Proposition 3.1).

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** BRACE avoids unstable IV inversion under weak identification by certifying compliance matrix invertibility before performing structural (TRT) estimation, and otherwise falls back to full-range honest intervals.  
**Mechanism:** For each context \( w \), after phase \( r \), BRACE checks if the empirical compliance matrix \( \hat{P}^{(r)}(w) \) is invertible and satisfies \( \|\hat{P}^{(r)}(w)^{-1}\|_\infty a_r(w) \leq 1/2 \). If certified, it plugs \( \hat{\mu}^{(r)}(w) = \hat{P}^{(r)}(w)^{-1} \hat{g}^{(r)}(w) \) with error bound \( c_r(w) \). If not, TRT intervals default to \([0,1]\) for that context, propagating wide policy intervals.  
**Core assumption:** The certification condition \( \|\hat{P}^{-1}\|_\infty a \leq 1/2 \) ensures the true matrix \( P(w) \) is invertible and bounds inflation from estimation error (Proposition 6.2).  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 5 details the certification rule and fallback; Proposition 6.2 shows certification implies \( \|P(w)^{-1}\|_\infty \leq 2 \|\hat{P}(w)^{-1}\|_\infty \); Section 7 empirical shows abstention in weak-IV cases and unsafe baseline failures.  
**Break condition:** When the compliance matrix is intrinsically near-singular even with large samples, certification may never trigger, leading to persistent abstention.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Simultaneous policy-value validity for all \( \pi \in \Pi \) across all stopping times is achieved by aggregating phase-end local intervals with uniform exploration and concentration corrections.  
**Mechanism:** Uniform exploration ensures \( N_r(w,z) \gtrsim t_r \nu(w)/K \). Local intervals for REC (ITT means) and TRT (certified IV) are built using Hoeffding and multinomial \( \ell_1 \) concentration. Policy intervals combine these with empirical context frequencies \( \hat{\nu}_r(w) \) plus a correction \( \eta_r \) accounting for context-frequency error. Since corrections are predictable and intervals only update at phase ends, a union bound over phases and policies yields simultaneous validity.  
**Core assumption:** Hoeffding-type concentration holds uniformly over all contexts, actions, and phases; context frequencies concentrate enough to bound aggregation error.  
**Evidence anchors:** Lemma 6.1 provides simultaneous concentration for statistics; Theorem 6.3 Item 1 proves coverage holds simultaneously for all \( t \); Section 7 empirical shows conservative coverage in benchmarks.  
**Break condition:** If exploration is not uniform (e.g., greedy), concentration may not hold uniformly across all \( (w,z) \) pairs, breaking the simultaneous guarantee.

## Foundational Learning

### Instrumental Variables (IV) identification in finite contexts
**Why needed:** The compliance matrix \( P^{(w)} \) maps structural means \( \mu(w) \) to operational means \( g(w) \). Understanding invertibility and the homogeneity assumption is essential to see why TRT is identifiable but REC is not.  
**Quick check:** In the private-signal example (Section 3), why does the IV equation \( g(w) = P(w)\mu(w) \) fail to identify \( \mu(w) \) even though \( P(w) \) is a valid transition matrix?  
*Because \( \mu(w) \) depends on private signals \( U \) that affect rewards but are not captured by \( w \); the mapping from recommendations to treatments via \( P(w) \) does not disentangle these hidden factors, so \( \mu(w) \) is not point-identified from observed operational means.*

### Confidence sequences and anytime-valid inference
**Why needed:** The INF objective requires intervals valid at arbitrary stopping times. BRACE’s phase-doubling and fixed-interval updates provide a simple construction; the rich-context score aims for a semiparametric version.  
**Quick check:** Why does updating intervals only at deterministic phase endpoints (rather than continuously) help maintain simultaneous validity?  
*Because predictable updates allow a union bound over phases; continuous updates would require martingale or self-normalized techniques to handle adaptivity, which is more complex.*

### Uniform exploration in bandits
**Why needed:** BRACE’s sample-complexity guarantees and simultaneous concentration rely on each \( (w,z) \) pair being visited frequently. Uniform exploration ensures \( N_r(w,z) = \Theta(t_r) \) for all \( w,z \), making error terms shrink uniformly.  
**Quick check:** What would happen to the certification rate if exploration were \( \epsilon \)-greedy instead of uniform, in a context with a very rare \( w \)?  
*Rare contexts would be sampled infrequently, leading to high error \( a_r(w,z) \) and failure to certify even if the compliance matrix is well-conditioned, causing unnecessary abstention.*

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Phase controller (doubling schedule) -> Uniform explorer (samples Z_t uniformly) -> Local statistics accumulator (computes per-context ν̂, P̂, ĝ) -> Certification module (checks invertibility and norm condition) -> Interval constructor (builds REC, TRT, INF intervals) -> Policy aggregator (computes LCB/UCB for all π ∈ Π via sum over contexts with frequency correction) -> Stopping/monitor (for REC/TRT, checks strict separation; for INF, no stop).

### Critical path
1. Start phase r with target t_r = 2^r; run uniform exploration until time t_r.
2. At t_r, compute empirical counts N^r(w,z), operational means ĝ_z(w), compliance rows P̂(w).
3. For each context w, run certification: if invertible and ‖P̂(w)⁻¹‖_∞ a_r(w) ≤ 1/2, mark certified.
4. Build local intervals: REC uses ĝ_z(w) ± b_r(w,z); TRT uses certified μ̂(w) ± c_r(w) or [0,1] if not certified; INF uses TRT-style intervals continuously.
5. Aggregate into policy intervals for all π ∈ Π: LCB(π) = ∑_w ν̂_r(w) ℓ_r,w(π(w)) - η_r; UCB(π) = ∑_w ν̂_r(w) u_r,w(π(w)) + η_r.
6. If objective is REC/TRT and there exists π̂ with LCB(π̂) > max_{π≠π̂} UCB(π), commit π̂ and stop; else increment r and repeat.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Parameter-free exploration vs. sample efficiency:** Uniform exploration is simple and yields clean concentration but can be sample-inefficient in heterogeneous or sparse contexts, leading to prolonged exploration and regret.
- **Conservative certification vs. deployment speed:** The 1/2-norm condition ensures stable inversion but may cause delayed or missing deployment in weak-IV settings (abstention). Faster variants trade conservatism for speed but risk incorrect structural claims.
- **Simultaneous intervals vs. interval width:** Aggregating over all policies and contexts with a union bound ensures validity but can produce wide intervals when |Π| or context space size is large, reducing informativeness.

### Failure signatures
- **TRT abstention:** TRT intervals remain [0,1] for many/all contexts across phases → certification failing due to near-singular P(w) or insufficient samples.
- **REC regret accumulation:** REC method not yet separated → operational welfare loss during exploration; persistent high regret suggests exploration inefficiency or non-identifiability.
- **INF interval width inflation:** Wide intervals across many contexts → weak first-stage strength or heterogeneity preventing certification; may indicate over-conservatism.
- **Wrong deployment in TRT:** If a TRT method deploys a policy with suboptimal true value, likely due to using μ̂ without certification (e.g., naive 2SLS with adaptive data) or violation of homogeneity.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Direct-control equivalence:** Implement env where Z_t = X_t (perfect compliance). Verify BRACE-REC and BRACE-TRT produce identical intervals and commit to the same policy at similar times, testing Proposition 3.1 collapse.
2. **Weak IV, small structural gap:** Build env with near-singular P(w) (e.g., small κ = ‖P⁻¹‖_∞). Track certification rate; BRACE-TRT should abstain (intervals = [0,1]) while unsafe baselines (e.g., Actual-UCB, naive 2SLS) deploy incorrect policies.
3. **Homogeneity failure:** Confirm REC methods (BRACE-REC, RECERT) still find a good operational recommendation policy, while TRT methods either abstain or deploy a policy with poor structural value, testing the conceptual boundary.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question:** Can the orthogonal score for rich-context IV bandits be equipped with anytime-valid confidence sequences that guarantee simultaneous coverage over policy classes under cross-fitted nuisance estimation and stabilized matrix inversion?  
**Basis:** Conjecture 8.3 proposes this but leaves it unproven, noting the need for o(t^{-1/2}) uniform remainder and stabilization of the inverse compliance map.  
**Why unresolved:** The product-form bias (Lemma 8.1) is promising but its amplification by P̂^{-1} under weak identification is not controlled; uniform bounds over infinite policy classes require additional arguments.  
**What evidence would resolve:** A rigorous proof using self-normalized martingale techniques and regularized inversion (e.g., Tikhonov) verifying conditions for anytime-valid inference; empirical validation via simulations showing coverage at various horizons.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How can BRACE's certification and safe fallback be generalized beyond square compliance matrices (K = Z) to rectangular IV designs (K ≠ Z) while preserving parameter-free exploration and weak-ID safety?  
**Basis:** Paper theory assumes square Z = X; Section 7 and Appendix A test rectangular overidentification empirically but lack theoretical guarantees.  
**Why unresolved:** Certification uses ℓ_∞ norm condition on inverse; rectangular matrices require different conditions (e.g., minimum singular value), and phase-doubling may need adjustment for non-uniform arm counts.  
**What evidence would resolve:** Derivation of generalized certification (e.g., based on singular value threshold), proof of analogous fixed-gap identification rates, and empirical demonstration of coverage and abstention in rectangular weak-ID regimes.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** When contextual homogeneity (Assumption 4.1) fails, can partial identification or sensitivity analysis recover a meaningful Structural Treatment Welfare (TRT) target for future deployment, or must practitioners default to Recommendation Welfare (REC)?  
**Basis:** Assumption 4.1 is necessary for TRT point identification; Section 7 shows under homogeneity violation REC remains strong but TRT unreliable, suggesting REC may be the only viable target.  
**Why unresolved:** Paper does not develop partial-ID methods for TRT under heterogeneity; it merely notes non-certification should push analysis toward weaker estimands.  
**What evidence would resolve:** Formal derivation of identified sets for V_str(π) under heterogeneity, simulation studies evaluating size and usefulness of those sets, and decision-theoretic analysis comparing policies based on bounds vs. REC optima.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** What is the operational trade-off between REC and TRT in terms of anytime-valid inference costs (e.g., interval width, abstention probability) when the IV is weakly identified?  
**Basis:** Section 7 empirical shows REC maintains narrower intervals and lower abstention under weak IV compared to TRT, but this is not systematically studied or explained theoretically.  
**Why unresolved:** Theory provides separate guarantees for REC and TRT (Theorem 6.3) but does not compare their sample complexities or uncertainty behavior under weak identification; role of homogeneity assumption in TRT uncertainty is not quantified.  
**What evidence would resolve:** Theoretical analysis of gap-dependent rates for REC and TRT intervals under varying first-stage strength (e.g., condition number of P(w)); simulation experiments varying compliance matrix condition number and measuring interval widths and stopping times for both objectives.

## Limitations
- **Assumption sensitivity:** Guarantees rely on finite contexts, square-IV structure, and uniform exploration; continuous/high-dimensional contexts, non-square IV, or adaptive exploration may break validity or certification.
- **Sample efficiency:** Phase-doubling and uniform exploration are theoretically clean but sample-inefficient in sparse/heterogeneous environments, causing prolonged abstention or regret.
- **Stopping for INF:** INF objective never stops; practical deployment requires ad hoc truncation, risking invalid intervals if stopped prematurely.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (REC > TRT under private discretion):** High. Formal propositions and explicit construction support strict ordering; break condition clearly identified.
- **Mechanism 2 (Certification prevents unstable IV inversion):** Medium-High. Theoretical condition is sound, but empirical behavior under near-singularity depends on finite-sample stability; fallback to [0,1] is conservative but may abstain excessively.
- **Mechanism 3 (Simultaneous validity via phase-doubling):** Medium. Relies on uniform exploration and union-bound corrections; plausible but may be loose in practice; coverage guarantees are asymptotic and could be conservative finitely.
