# Analyzing Reasoning Consistency in Large Multimodal Models under Cross-Modal Conflicts
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04073  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04073  
- Reference count: 9  
- Primary result: AVCR reduces textual inertia, improving task accuracy on Keye-Preview-8B from 37% to 47% (first step) and explicit reflection (R2) from 6% to 29%.

## Executive Summary
Large multimodal models (LMMs) often exhibit "textual inertia" — a tendency to cling to erroneous textual reasoning even when faced with contradictory visual evidence. This paper systematically investigates this failure mode by introducing the LogicGraph Perturbation Protocol, which injects counterfactual errors into reasoning chains derived from the STAR video dataset. Across several open-source LMMs, less than 10% of cases show explicit self-correction. To mitigate textual inertia, the authors propose Active Visual-Context Refinement (AVCR), a training-free inference framework that combines active visual re-grounding via a special `<check>` token with an adaptive context folding strategy. Experiments demonstrate that AVCR significantly reduces error propagation, boosts task accuracy, and increases reflection rates. Ablation studies confirm the necessity of both re-grounding and folding components.

## Method Summary
The authors first create a perturbed benchmark by parsing ground-truth reasoning chains into entity-attribute-relation graphs using GPT-4o, then injecting high-likelihood counterfactual perturbations at the first reasoning step. They evaluate multiple LMMs (Keye-preview-8B, Keye-1.5-8B, LongVILA-7B, InternVL3-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) by sampling three continuations per perturbation and classifying the resulting behavior (contamination, passive reflection, explicit reflection, collapse) with a judge model. AVCR operates during inference: when the model detects internal uncertainty, it emits a `<check>` token, prompting the system to predict a temporal window and re-extract corresponding video frames. After a correction is identified, the reasoning history is folded into a concise factual summary, discarding potentially harmful tokens. A lightweight self-evaluation may trigger a global retry if inconsistencies persist. This loop is entirely training-free and applied on top of existing LMMs.

## Key Results
- Baseline LMMs self-correct explicitly in less than 10% of perturbed instances.  
- AVCR improves single-step accuracy on Keye-Preview-8B from 37% to 47% and explicit reflection (R2) from 6% to 29%; similar gains observed for Qwen2.5-VL-7B.  
- Ablating either visual re-grounding or context folding markedly degrades performance, demonstrating both are essential.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Active visual re-grounding breaks textual inertia by forcing the model to retrieve and reason over specific video frames upon detecting uncertainty.  
**Mechanism:** The model generates a special `<check>` token when internal uncertainty (e.g., low confidence) exceeds a threshold. This triggers a branch that predicts a temporal window (start and end time) relevant to the current reasoning step. Visual features from that window are extracted and fed back into the model, overriding the global video representation. This ensures subsequent tokens are conditioned on concrete visual evidence rather than corrupted textual history.  
**Core assumption:** The model can accurately estimate its own uncertainty and predict relevant timestamps; re-extracting visual features from specific frames can override textual bias.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "orchestrates an active visual re-grounding mechanism to enforce fine-grained verification"  
- Section 4.2 details the `Acheck` action, timestamp prediction, and feedback fallback to global video if formatting fails.  
- Corpus includes *Thinking Before Looking: Improving Multimodal LLM Reasoning via Mitigating Visual Hallucination*, which also emphasizes re-examining visual content to reduce hallucination.  
**Break condition:** If uncertainty estimation is poor, `<check>` may not trigger when needed or trigger excessively. Inaccurate timestamp predictions retrieve irrelevant frames, failing to correct errors.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Context folding denoises the reasoning history by compressing erroneous tokens into a factual summary, preventing them from biasing future steps.  
**Mechanism:** After a correction (e.g., following a `<check>` and re-grounding), the raw history containing the error, the check, and the correction is replaced by a concise summary produced by the model itself. This summary captures only verified facts, discarding hallucinated tokens that act as attention sinks. The folded context then serves as the new history for subsequent reasoning.  
**Core assumption:** The model can generate an accurate summary of corrected information; removing raw erroneous tokens reduces interference more than keeping them.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "adaptive context refinement strategy to summarize and denoise the reasoning history."  
- Section 4.3 describes the folding operation, conditional triggering (upon correction or high redundancy), and synthesis of `S_fact`.  
- Corpus includes *ReSum: Unlocking long-horizon search intelligence via context summarization* which explores context compression for long tasks.  
**Break condition:** If summary generation omits necessary details or introduces new errors, critical information may be lost. Incorrect folding timing may not effectively remove interference.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Self-evaluation with global retry ensures final answer correctness by detecting epistemic uncertainty and mandating additional verification.  
**Mechanism:** A lightweight evaluator (the model itself) scans the generated reasoning chain for signs of uncertainty (e.g., hedging language) or logical contradictions between intermediate steps and the final answer. If detected, the entire process restarts from the beginning with an implicit instruction to be more thorough, giving the model another chance to correct itself.  
**Core assumption:** The model can reliably recognize its own uncertainty or inconsistencies; a retry with the same mechanism will yield a better outcome.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Section 4.3: "We further employ a lightweight self-evaluation mechanism to audit the quality of the generated chain... Upon detecting such inconsistencies, the framework triggers a global retry."  
- Corpus includes *Recot: Reflective self-correction training for mitigating confirmation bias in large vision-language models* which uses self-reflection to correct biases.  
**Break condition:** The self-evaluation may miss errors or falsely flag correct chains. Retries may not improve if the model’s capabilities are intrinsically limited.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Uncertainty Estimator -> <check> Token -> Timestamp Predictor -> Visual Re-extractor -> Context Folder -> Self-Evaluator -> Retry Controller

Critical path:  
1. Generate reasoning tokens until `<check>` appears or chain ends.  
2. If `<check>` is triggered, predict timestamps; extract local frames; continue generation with updated visual features.  
3. Upon correction detection (explicit refutation) or high redundancy, fold context: summarize correct facts, discard error-prone tokens.  
4. After final answer, self-evaluate chain; if inconsistencies found, trigger global retry from step 1.

Design tradeoffs:  
- Training-free vs. performance: AVCR adds no training but incurs inference overhead (frame re-extraction, multiple passes).  
- Timestamp prediction accuracy: Poor predictions may retrieve irrelevant visuals, harming performance.  
- Summary quality: Over-aggressive folding may lose necessary details; under-aggressive may leave toxic tokens.  
- Uncertainty heuristic: The trigger for `<check>` is not explicitly defined; likely based on output probabilities or heuristics.

Failure signatures:  
- Low explicit reflection rate (R2) persists despite AVCR.  
- Context folding removes necessary intermediate steps, causing accuracy drop.  
- Retry loop exhausts context window or computational budget without improvement.  
- Timestamp predictions consistently off-target, yielding irrelevant visuals.

First 3 experiments:  
1. LogicGraph Perturbation Protocol evaluation: Inject perturbations into reasoning chains and measure task accuracy and reasoning behavior categories (R0–R3) across models. Shows <10% explicit reflection without AVCR.  
2. Baseline comparison: Compare AVCR against Visual Focus (instruction to prioritize details) and Textual Check (text-based verification) on entity perturbations. AVCR improves accuracy and R2 significantly.  
3. Ablation study: Remove Check or Fold components from AVCR. Both degrade performance, demonstrating their necessity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can the LogicGraph Perturbation Protocol be extended to generate more complex perturbations, such as causal or counterfactual errors, to probe higher-order reasoning failures in LMMs?  
*Basis:* The authors state in Limitations: "Expanding to more complex causal or counterfactual reasoning scenarios remains a challenge for future research."  
*Why unresolved:* Current protocol focuses on entity/attribute/relation perturbations; deeper logical structures like cause-effect are not addressed. Extending would allow comprehensive evaluation of textual inertia across broader tasks.

**Open Question 2:** Can the Active Visual-Context Refinement (AVCR) framework be incorporated into the training process of LMMs to permanently reduce textual inertia, rather than being used only at inference time?  
*Basis:* Limitations: "our proposed AVCR is an inference time strategy. While efficient, it does not fundamentally alter the internal parameters of the model to permanently fix the attention misalignment."  
*Why unresolved:* Training-time interventions (e.g., RL reward for visual grounding) might produce models inherently resistant to textual priors, reducing need for extra inference cost.

**Open Question 3:** Does AVCR scale effectively to large proprietary LMMs and to diverse, uncurated video datasets that more closely resemble real-world deployment scenarios?  
*Basis:* Limitations: "our experiments are primarily conducted on open source models due to computational constraints. Validating the scalability of our approach on larger proprietary models requires further exploration."  
*Why unresolved:* Experiments are limited to open-source models on the curated STAR benchmark. Generalizability to closed-source models (GPT-4V, Claude 3.5) and realistic videos (longer, noisy, multiple actors) is unknown.

**Open Question 4:** What intrinsic model architectures or training modifications could reduce textual inertia at the source, minimizing the need for external interventions like AVCR?  
*Basis:* Inferred: The paper identifies textual inertia as systematic across LMMs, suggesting a common cause (language decoder overriding visual signals), but does not explore architectural or training solutions.  
*Why unresolved:* Understanding whether textual inertia is inevitable or can be mitigated by design (e.g., different fusion strategies, contrastive learning) could lead to more robust models without external overhead.

## Limitations
- Evaluation uses a small, curated subset (100 samples) of the STAR dataset, limiting generalizability to other video reasoning tasks.  
- Reasoning behavior classification relies on a single judge model (Qwen2.5-72B), which may introduce bias.  
- Inference overhead of AVCR (multiple passes, frame re-extraction) is not quantified in terms of speed-accuracy trade-offs.
