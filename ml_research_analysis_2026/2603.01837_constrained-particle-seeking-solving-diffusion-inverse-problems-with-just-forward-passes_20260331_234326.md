# Constrained Particle Seeking: Solving Diffusion Inverse Problems with Just Forward Passes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.01837
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01837
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: Gradient-free method achieving PSNR up to 27.70 on FFHQ tasks and matching gradient-based approaches.

## Executive Summary
Constrained Particle Seeking (CPS) is a novel gradient-free algorithm for solving diffusion inverse problems. It samples multiple particles at each diffusion step, fits a local linear surrogate of the forward operator using all particles, and then finds an optimal particle on a norm-constrained hypersphere that respects the diffusion prior. A Restart strategy mitigates error accumulation by iteratively re-noising and re-denosing intermediate samples. CPS attains performance comparable to gradient-based methods on diverse tasks: image restoration (4× super-resolution PSNR 27.70), black hole imaging (PSNR 25.74), and fluid data assimilation (relative L2 as low as 0.295), while significantly outperforming other gradient-free baselines.

## Method Summary
Given a pre-trained diffusion model and a black-box forward operator H, CPS iteratively denoises from timestep T down to 0. At each timestep t, it draws n particles from the diffusion transition kernel p(x_t|x_{t+1}) = N(μ_t, σ_t^2 I). Each particle is one-step denoised via Tweedie’s formula to obtain a predicted clean image. The forward model is evaluated on all predictions, producing a set of observations. CPS then fits a linear surrogate H( x_0|t ) ≈ A x_t + b in closed form using these pairs. The optimal particle x_t* is obtained by solving min ||y - (A x_t + b)||^2 subject to ||x_t - μ_t||^2 = σ_t^2 d, which yields an analytical solution moving along the direction A^T(y - \bar{H}) on the hypersphere. For the first 20% of timesteps, a Restart loop may re-noise x_t* back to x_{t+1} and repeat up to N_r=5 times, helping to correct trajectory errors. The final sample x_0 after the last timestep is the reconstruction.

## Key Results
- On FFHQ image restoration (inpainting, super-resolution, deblurring, JPEG), CPS achieves PSNR up to 27.70 (4× SR) and LPIPS as low as 0.115, matching or exceeding gradient‑based methods.
- On black hole imaging, CPS achieves PSNR 25.74 vs 25.45 (SCG) and 23.03 (EnKG).
- For fluid data assimilation, CPS yields relative L2 errors 0.295, 0.469, 0.684 for 2×, 4×, 8× downscaling, consistently best among gradient‑free approaches.
- CPS requires comparable forward calls but shows higher particle efficiency, achieving strong performance with as few as 64 particles.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** CPS systematically utilizes information from all sampled particles at each diffusion step, rather than discarding suboptimal candidates.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of passive selection (e.g., SCG's top-1 particle retention), CPS treats the ensemble of particles as data to fit a local surrogate of the forward observation model. This allows the synthesis of an optimal particle that integrates directional information from the entire set.
- **Core assumption:** The observation process H(·) is locally linear (or well-approximated as linear) within the high-probability region defined by the diffusion transition kernel p(x_t | x_{t+1}).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: “CPS ... leverages all candidate particle information to actively search for the optimal particle.”
  - [section 3.1]: Empirical study on black hole imaging shows even bottom-ranked particles can guide denoising when noise signs are reversed, indicating distributed useful information.
  - [corpus]: Weak direct support; neighboring papers (e.g., "Mode-Seeking for Inverse Problems") also explore non-selection-based particle use, but CPS's specific local linearization approach is distinct.
- **Break condition:** If the forward operator H(·) exhibits strong non-linearities within the kernel region, the local linear surrogate A x_t + b becomes a poor approximation, leading to misguided optimal particle selection.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** CPS formulates the inverse problem as a constrained optimization that respects the diffusion prior's high-density region via a norm constraint.
- **Mechanism:** The optimization min ||y - (A x_t + b)||^2 is solved under the constraint ||x_t - μ_t||^2 = σ_t^2 d, which forces the solution onto a hypersphere of radius σ_t √d around the kernel mean μ_t. This aligns the solution with the natural norm statistics of Gaussian-distributed noise in diffusion steps.
- **Core assumption:** The reverse diffusion process p(x_t | x_{t+1}) is an isotropic Gaussian, and its high-density region corresponds to a spherical shell (hypersphere) due to concentration of measure in high dimensions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2]: Derivation of Eq. (14) and the asymptotic closed-form solution x_t^* ≈ μ_t + σ_t/√d · A^T(y- H)/||A^T(y- H)||.
  - [section 3.2]: Cites Menon et al. (2020) and Samuel et al. (2023) for the norm-based density concentration in high-dimensional Gaussians.
  - [corpus]: "Linearly Constrained Diffusion Implicit Models" (CDIM) also uses linear constraints but differs in implementation and scope; CPS's constraint is specifically tied to the diffusion kernel's norm.
- **Break condition:** For very early timesteps (large σ_t), the constraint becomes loose; for very late timesteps (tiny σ_t), the constraint dominates and the solution collapses toward μ_t, potentially ignoring observation guidance if A^T(y- H) is poorly estimated.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The Restart strategy mitigates error accumulation by iteratively re-noising and re-denosing intermediate samples, allowing gradual correction of trajectory deviations.
- **Mechanism:** At selected timesteps (first 20% in the paper), a current sample x_t is noised back to x_{t+1} via the forward diffusion process (Eq. 2) and then re-processed by CPS. This creates a local resampling loop that can escape poor local optima introduced by one-step Tweedie approximations or bad initial noise.
- **Core assumption:** The forward noising process is invertible in distribution, and re-sampling from p(x_{t+1} | x_t) provides a meaningful "reset" that retains some information from x_t while introducing diversity to correct accumulated errors.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: “We incorporate a Restart strategy to iteratively correct for the cumulative errors.”
  - [section 3.3]: Describes the two-step iteration (re-noise, then guided denoise) and its application in early stages.
  - [appendix C]: Ablation (Figure 9) shows Restart improves PSNR/LPIPS stability across random seeds.
  - [corpus]: Restart sampling is also used in Xu et al. (2023) for diffusion models generally; CPS adapts it specifically for gradient-free inverse problems.
- **Break condition:** If restarts are applied too late (when σ_t is small), the noising step adds negligible noise, providing little corrective effect. If applied too frequently, it may cause oscillatory behavior or increase computational cost without benefit.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Diffusion Reverse Process (DDIM/VP Schedulers)**
  - Why needed here: CPS operates within the iterative denoising framework (Algorithm 1). Understanding p(x_t|x_{t+1}) as a Gaussian with mean μ_t and variance σ_t^2 I is essential for sampling particles and defining the norm constraint.
  - Quick check question: What is the dimensional radius of the high-probability hypersphere for a d-dimensional Gaussian with variance σ_t^2 at a given timestep?

- **Concept: Stochastic Optimal Control Framing**
  - Why needed here: The paper situates inverse problems as stochastic control (Eq. 7-8). CPS's active seeking is a more tractable approximation to the optimal policy u_t^* that would require full trajectory rollouts.
  - Quick check question: Why does the exact optimal policy in Eq. (8) require simulating many full trajectories from x_t to x_0?

- **Concept: Statistical Linearization**
  - Why needed here: CPS approximates H(·) locally with A x_t + b using sampled particles (Eq. 11-13). This is a core computational trick to avoid gradients.
  - Quick check question: Given n samples x_i^t and their corresponding H(ˆx_i^0|t), how are the matrix A and vector b computed in closed form?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. Particle Sampler: Draws n candidate x_t^i ∼ N(μ_t, σ_t^2 I).
  2. Local Surrogate Fitter: Computes A and b via Eq. (13) using all particles' denoised predictions through H.
  3. Constrained Optimizer: Solves the sphere-constrained least squares analytically (Eq. 16) to produce x_t*.
  4. Restart Controller: Decides when to re-noise x_t back to x_{t+1} and re-enter the loop (Algorithm 1 lines 6-8).
  5. Diffusion Scheduler: Manages the timestep loop (t = T-1 ... 0) and the μ_t, σ_t parameters based on the chosen schedule (e.g., VP).

- **Critical path:** For each timestep t (from T-1 down to 0): Sample particles → Fit (A,b) → Compute x_t* → (if restart condition) re-noise and loop back to t+1. The final output is x_0.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Particle count n vs. computational cost: More particles yield a more accurate linear surrogate but increase forward model calls linearly. CPS is effective even with small n (e.g., 64) due to efficient use of all particles.
  - Restart frequency vs. overhead: More restarts improve robustness to bad initial noise but multiply forward calls; paper uses N_r=5 only in early 20% of steps as a compromise.
  - Local linearity assumption vs. problem non-linearity: For highly non-linear H, the surrogate A x_t + b may be inaccurate; the norm constraint helps but cannot fully compensate.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor surrogate fit: If per-particle H(ˆx_0|t) values show high variance around the linear fit (large residual sum of squares), the subsequent x_t* will be misdirected. Likely in early timesteps with large σ_t.
  - Constraint domination: When σ_t is very small, x_t* ≈ μ_t regardless of A^T(y- H), leading to failure to match observations if μ_t is far from the data-manifold consistency point.
  - Restart instability: If restarts are triggered too late (small σ_t), the added noise is insufficient to meaningfully alter the trajectory, wasting computation.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Particle efficiency ablation: Run CPS, SCG, EnKG, DPG with varying particle counts (8, 16, 32, 64, 128) on a linear problem (e.g., 4× super-resolution). Expect CPS to achieve near-best performance with the fewest particles, demonstrating information integration efficiency.
  2. Restart impact test: Compare CPS with and without Restart across multiple random seeds for a noisy inverse problem (e.g., JPEG restoration QF=5). Expect Restart to increase mean PSNR and reduce variance (LPIPS) across seeds, confirming robustness to initialization.
  3. Local linearity validation: For a given inverse problem (e.g., deblurring), compute the Jensen gap (Figure 4) for the function H(E[x_0|·]) using the particle ensemble at different timesteps. Expect CPS's error to remain small across timesteps, while EnKG's error grows early on, supporting the local linearization rationale.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can faster diffusion sampling (e.g., shortcut sampling) be integrated into CPS to reduce computational overhead?**  
   - Basis: Appendix D states combining faster solvers or shortcut sampling is a potential direction.  
   - Why unresolved: No experimental or theoretical exploration of this integration in the work.  
   - What evidence would resolve it: Benchmark CPS with integrated accelerators on inverse problems, measuring PSNR/LPIPS vs forward calls.

2. **What are the error bounds of the local linear surrogate for the forward operator, and how do they impact reconstruction quality?**  
   - Basis: The method uses a linear surrogate but gives only a Jensen gap plot for one problem, no general analysis.  
   - Why unresolved: The surrogate's error characteristics and their effect on final results are unknown.  
   - What evidence would resolve it: Derive theoretical bounds (e.g., based on smoothness) and conduct experiments varying operator nonlinearity.

3. **Is the norm-constrained hypersphere (radius σ_t√d) optimal for aligning with the diffusion prior?**  
   - Basis: The constraint is motivated by Gaussian norm concentration but its alignment with the actual prior is untested.  
   - Why unresolved: No theoretical or empirical validation of this constraint versus alternatives.  
   - What evidence would resolve it: Test alternative constraints (e.g., learned) and evaluate prior density/posterior quality.

4. **How accurate is the asymptotic closed-form solution (Eq. 16) for early diffusion steps with large σ_t, and can iterative methods yield improvements?**  
   - Basis: The derivation assumes small σ_t and large λ; the paper applies it to all timesteps without justification.  
   - Why unresolved: The error of this approximation at early steps is unquantified; iterative refinements are not explored.  
   - What evidence would resolve it: Exact Lagrange solutions or root-finding for early steps, comparing quality and cost.

## Limitations
- Core local linearity assumption may break for highly nonlinear forward operators or large noise levels, degrading surrogate quality.
- Restart scheduling (early 20% steps, N_r=5) is heuristic; suboptimal choices reduce gains or increase cost.
- Missing implementation details (exact Tweedie denoising formula, forward operator specifications) hamper precise reproduction and fair baseline comparison.
