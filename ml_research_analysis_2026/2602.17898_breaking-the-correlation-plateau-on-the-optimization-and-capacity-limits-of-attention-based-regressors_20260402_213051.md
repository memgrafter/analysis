# Breaking the Correlation Plateau: On the Optimization and Capacity Limits of Attention-Based Regressors

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17898
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17898
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Extrapolative Correlation Attention (ECA) consistently breaks the PCC plateau in attention-based regression models by addressing optimization conflicts and capacity limits, yielding significant PCC and MSE improvements across synthetic, tabular, spatial transcriptomics, and multimodal benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Attention-based regression models often suffer from an early "PCC plateau" where Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) improvement stalls while mean squared error (MSE) continues to decrease, particularly under high in-sample homogeneity. This work identifies two fundamental causes: an optimization conflict where MSE minimization suppresses PCC gradients, and a model capacity bound from convex aggregation limiting PCC gains relative to the convex hull of input embeddings. To overcome these, the authors propose Extrapolative Correlation Attention (ECA), a plug-in module comprising three novel mechanisms: Dispersion-Normalized PCC Loss (DNPL) to rescale PCC gradients, Dispersion-Aware Temperature Softmax (DATS) to maintain attention selectivity in homogeneous data, and Scaled Residual Aggregation (SRA) to extrapolate beyond the convex hull. Evaluations on four diverse datasets demonstrate that ECA reliably breaks the PCC plateau without sacrificing MSE performance, with ablations confirming the individual contribution of each component.

## Method Summary
ECA replaces the standard softmax-based attention pooling module in a host architecture (e.g., the final layer of a Transformer) with three integrated modifications. First, the loss function substitutes the standard PCC loss term with Dispersion-Normalized PCC Loss (DNPL), scaling it by the stopped gradient of the prediction standard deviation to prevent gradient attenuation. Second, the softmax temperature is dynamically adapted per sample based on the in-sample dispersion of embeddings (DATS), sharpening attention when embeddings are homogeneous. Third, the aggregated embedding is computed via Scaled Residual Aggregation (SRA), which introduces a learnable scaling factor γ≥1 applied to the weighted residual from the mean, enabling extrapolation beyond the convex hull of input embeddings. Training uses a joint objective of MSE, DNPL, and a optional regularization on γ. The components are designed as drop-in replacements, requiring only minor parameter overhead (~5%) and negligible compute cost.

## Key Results
- On three UCI regression tasks (Appliance Energy, Online News Popularity, Superconductivity), ECA increased PCC by up to 0.042 and reduced MSE by up to 0.318×10³ compared to baselines.
- On the 10x Proteomic spatial transcriptomics dataset, ECA improved PCC by ~14.64% and reduced MSE by 9.83% without any MSE performance drop.
- On the MOSI multimodal sentiment analysis dataset, ECA consistently surpassed baseline PCC across epochs while maintaining competitive MSE.
- Ablations confirmed that DNPL is critical for gradient rescue, DATS is essential under high homogeneity, and SRA provides the capacity lift beyond the convex hull bound.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Dispersion-Normalized PCC Loss (DNPL)**  
The PCC gradient magnitude decays as O(1/σ̂y^{3/2}) during joint MSE+PCC training because MSE-driven standard deviation matching increases σ̂y. DNPL multiplies the PCC loss term (1−ρ) by StopGrad(σ̂y), effectively re-normalizing the gradient scale relative to the MSE gradient. This counteracts the attenuation, preventing the PCC gradient from vanishing prematurely. The approach assumes σ̂y grows early in training to match the target standard deviation σy, which is empirically observed. If σ̂y were to decrease during training, the normalization could over-amplify gradients and destabilize optimization, though this was not seen in experiments.

**Mechanism 2: Dispersion-Aware Temperature Softmax (DATS)**  
Under high in-sample homogeneity, the PCC gradient magnitude is proportional to the within-sample dispersion σs. With fixed softmax temperature, low dispersion causes attention weights to become near-uniform (αsi≈1/ns), flattening gradients. DATS sets the temperature τs as a function of σs (τs = Tmin + β·σs⁻²), lowering τs when σs is small. This sharpens the softmax distribution, restoring the Jacobian magnitude needed for correlation learning. The mechanism assumes σs is a reliable proxy for embedding informativeness. If σs is miscalculated (e.g., due to noise), τs may be mis-set, causing over-sharpening (loss divergence) or under-sharpening (ineffectiveness), with potential numerical instability if τs approaches zero.

**Mechanism 3: Scaled Residual Aggregation (SRA)**  
Theorem 2.2 proves that any convex aggregator’s PCC gain is bounded by the radius of the convex hull of input embeddings, a severe limitation under homogeneity (small hull radius). SRA modifies the aggregated embedding to vs = µs + γs·Δvs, where Δvs = Σ_i αsi(hsi−µs) and γs≥1 is a learnable scaling factor. For γs>1, vs extrapolates beyond the convex hull, lifting the theoretical bound. The mechanism assumes γs can be learned stably (e.g., via a Softplus-activated MLP) and that the optimal vs lies outside the hull. If γs grows excessively (even with clipping/regularization), vs may extrapolate to poor generalization regions, causing overfitting or output explosion; poor initialization can also trap γs≈1, nullifying benefits.

  Why needed: The PCC gradient w.r.t. attention logits contains a factor proportional to within-sample dispersion. Understanding this shows how homogeneity flattens gradients and motivates DATS.

- **Concept: Convex Hull Capacity Limit (Theorem 2.2)**

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Standard Attention Pooling (softmax over embeddings) → Replace with: (1) Dispersion-Aware Temperature Softmax (DATS) computes αsi = softmax(zsi/τs) with τs = Tmin + β·σs⁻²; (2) Scaled Residual Aggregation (SRA) outputs vs = µs + γs·Σ_i αsi(hsi−µs), with γs = 1 + Softplus(MLPθγ(µs)). Loss uses DNPL: L_DNPL = StopGrad(σ̂y)·(1−ρ) instead of standard PCC loss.

**Critical path:** Implement in order: (a) DNPL (loss-only change, simplest), (b) DATS (add temperature scaling to softmax), (c) SRA (modify aggregation with γ scaling). Ablate one component at a time to isolate effects.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- SRA adds a small MLP and scaling factor; may cause extrapolation artifacts (use γmax clipping and λγ regularization).  
- DATS requires per-sample dispersion σs computation; minor compute overhead.  
- DNPL is parameter-free but requires correct StopGradient application.  
Overall parameter overhead ~5%, negligible FLOPs increase.

**Failure signatures:**  
- PCC still plateaus: Check if σ̂y fails to increase (log σ̂y vs. σy); if so, λPCC may be too low or DNPL not applied.  
- Attention collapses (αsi≈1/ns): Compute attention entropy; if high, DATS β may be too low.  
- Unstable loss: Monitor γs; if exploding, reduce LR or increase λγ.  
- Overfitting on PCC but test MSE rises: Likely SRA extrapolation; lower γmax or strengthen regularization.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Synthetic data ablation:** Train baseline vs. ECA on synthetic data with controlled homogeneity η. Verify ECA lifts PCC bound and delays plateau (replicates Figure 2).  
2. **UCI regression with FT-Transformer:** Integrate ECA into FT-Transformer’s final attention pooling on three UCI tasks. Confirm PCC gains without MSE drop (replicates Table 1).  
3. **Component isolation:** Ablate SRA, DATS, DNPL individually on a homogeneous dataset (e.g., MOSI). Expect DNPL most critical for gradient rescue, DATS for homogeneity, SRA for capacity (replicates Table 1 ablations).

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (Tmin, β, λγ, γmax) are dataset-specific and not fully tabulated, hindering exact replication.
- Theoretical assumptions (e.g., stable σ̂y growth, reliable γs>1) may fail under strong regularization or noisy embeddings.
- Evaluation covers only four datasets; broader testing on more diverse regression tasks is needed to confirm generalizability.

## Confidence
- **Fundamental limitations (optimization conflict & convex hull bound):** High (rigorous theoretical analysis and gradient evidence).
- **ECA efficacy:** Medium (results consistent across datasets but hyperparameter sensitivity and extrapolation overfitting remain concerns).
- **Generalization to unseen domains:** Medium (promising cross-domain results but limited dataset variety).

## Next Checks
1. **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Systematically vary Tmin, β, λγ, γmax on all benchmarks; report performance distributions to quantify robustness.
2. **Extrapolation test:** Compare ECA with ablations fixing γs=1 (no extrapolation) and γs clipped; confirm extrapolation effect isolates SRA’s contribution.
3. **Gradient verification:** Log DNPL scaling factor (StopGrad(σ̂y)) and DATS temperature τs per sample; verify scaling tracks 1/σ̂y and temperature inversely tracks σs as theory predicts.
