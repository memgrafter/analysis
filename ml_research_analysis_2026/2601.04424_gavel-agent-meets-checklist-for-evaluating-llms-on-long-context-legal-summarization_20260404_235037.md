# Gavel: Agent Meets Checklist for Evaluating LLMs on Long-Context Legal Summarization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.04424
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04424
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Introduced GAVEL-REF for evaluation and GAVEL-AGENT for efficient extraction; top model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) scores 50/100 on SGAVEL-REF; agent reduces token usage by 36% with only 7% score drop vs. end-to-end GPT-4.1 extraction.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of evaluating large language models (LLMs) on long-context (32K–512K tokens) legal case summarization. The authors propose GAVEL-REF, a comprehensive evaluation framework that combines checklist matching (with multi-value support and applicable-item scoring), residual fact analysis, and writing-style similarity. They assess 12 LLMs on 100 civil rights cases and find that even the best model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) attains only 50/100 on the integrated SGAVEL-REF score, with particular struggles on multi-value and rarely occurring checklist items. To reduce reliance on human reference summaries, they develop GAVEL-AGENT, an autonomous agent equipped with six tools for selectively navigating and extracting checklists directly from source documents. GAVEL-AGENT achieves a 36% reduction in token consumption compared to end-to-end extraction with GPT-4.1, while suffering only a 7% drop in checklist extraction accuracy.

## Method Summary
GAVEL-REF operates in three stages: (1) checklist extraction from a summary (using an LLM to output structured items, each with a value and supporting text), (2) comparison between model and reference checklists using multi-value F1 and applicable-item scoring, (3) residual fact extraction (identifying atomic facts not covered by the checklist) and writing-style rating. The three component scores are combined into SGAVEL-REF with weighting. GAVEL-AGENT is an LLM scaffolded with six tools—list_documents, read_document, search_document_regex, get_checklist, append_checklist, update_checklist—that interact with a context snapshot containing a document catalog, interim checklist, and recent action history. After each tool call, the context is refreshed to stay within token limits. The agent iterates until it judges the checklist complete, enabling efficient extraction from very long cases without processing all documents end-to-end.

## Key Results
- **Top model performance**: Gemini 2.5 Pro achieved the highest SGAVEL-REF score of 50/100, indicating substantial room for improvement in long-context legal summarization.
- **Model weaknesses**: All models scored notably lower on multi-value checklist items (e.g., settlements, related cases) and rare items, often below 0.2, while performing better on single-valued items.
- **Agent efficiency**: GAVEL-AGENT (with Qwen3 30B-A3B) lowered token usage by 36% versus end-to-end GPT-4.1 extraction, with only a 7% decrease in checklist extraction score.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Improved Checklist Evaluation
GAVEL-REF refines checklist-based evaluation by supporting multi-value items (e.g., several remedies sought) and scoring only applicable items. It structures extraction as lists of values with supporting text, applies element-wise F1 for multi-value matching, and excludes checklist items that are absent from both model and reference summaries from the score calculation. This avoids inflation from irrelevant items and better reflects legal summaries where many fields are naturally multi-valued and not every case contains all items. The design is grounded in the observation that legal checklists often contain fields that can have multiple valid entries and that not all items apply to every case. The framework’s effectiveness is supported by meta-evaluation showing improved agreement with human judgments (Section 2.1).

### Mechanism 2: Residual Fact Evaluation
Beyond the checklist, GAVEL-REF captures factual content that appears in both summaries but is not covered by any checklist item. After initial matching, unmatched text spans are extracted from both summaries and broken into atomic facts; these facts are then compared via list-wise F1. This ensures that important information omitted from the expert checklist still influences the evaluation score. The underlying assumption is that expert checklists, while comprehensive, may not enumerate all salient facts in a legal summary; thus, ignoring residual content could unduly penalize summaries that include valuable non-checklist information. The mechanism is described in Section 2.1 and validated by meta-evaluation that demonstrated correlation with human assessments of residual content.

### Mechanism 3: Efficient Extraction via GAVEL-AGENT
GAVEL-AGENT reduces token usage for checklist extraction by mimicking human selective navigation. The agent is equipped with six tools that allow it to list documents, read specific documents, search by regex, and maintain an evolving checklist. After each action, the context is refreshed to contain only a catalog, the current checklist summary, and the most recent actions, keeping the working memory small. This selective approach—reading only when needed and skipping irrelevant portions—avoids the high token cost of end-to-end or fixed-chunk processing of all documents. The core assumption is that many checklist items can be located by targeted searching rather than exhaustive reading, enabling near-complete extraction with far fewer tokens. Empirical results confirm a 36% token reduction with only a 7% score drop compared to GPT-4.1 end-to-end extraction (Section 4).

## Foundational Learning
- **Legal case document structure and terminology**: The 26-item checklist (e.g., distinguishing a decree from a settlement, identifying monitor reports) requires familiarity with legal processes and document types such as complaints, orders, and rulings. Misinterpretation of these terms leads to extraction errors. Quick check: What is the difference between a "decree" (court order) and a "settlement" (party agreement) in this framework's definitions?
- **Long-context LLM limitations**: The evaluation reveals that even models with 1M-token contexts suffer performance declines as input length grows from 32K to 512K tokens. Understanding the causes—e.g., lost-in-the-middle effect, token truncation during preprocessing, or attention distribution—is essential to interpret results and design robust agents. Quick check: Why do open-source models degrade more on 256K/512K cases than proprietary ones?
- **Evaluation design for generative tasks**: GAVEL-REF integrates multiple evaluation signals (checklist, residual facts, style) and improves upon prior methods like ExpertLongBench. Recognizing its design choices (LLM-as-judge, reference-based) and limitations is crucial for applying it appropriately. Quick check: What are the two key improvements GAVEL-REF makes to the checklist evaluation component from ExpertLongBench? (Answer: multi-value extraction with supporting text; applicable-item scoring.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
- GAVEL-REF evaluator: Checklist Extractor → Checklist Comparator (handles multi-value logic) → Residual Fact Extractor → Style Rater.  
- GAVEL-AGENT scaffold: Agent loop with tools (list_documents, read_document, search_document_regex, get_checklist, append_checklist, update_checklist) and context snapshot (document catalog, checklist summary, recent action history).

**Critical Path**  
1. Define checklist items for the target domain (adapted from the paper’s 26-item set).  
2. For evaluation: generate model summaries → run GAVEL-REF (extract checklists, compare, evaluate residual/style) → compute SGAVEL-REF.  
3. For direct extraction: select extraction method (end-to-end, chunk-by-chunk, GAVEL-AGENT) → configure agent/tools → extract from raw documents → evaluate extracted checklist against human references or use as test-time feedback.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *GAVEL-REF accuracy vs. cost*: High-capability LLMs (e.g., GPT-5) yield strong meta-eval agreement but are expensive; open-source backbones are cheaper but less reliable.  
- *GAVEL-AGENT efficiency vs. completeness*: Selective navigation cuts token usage but may miss sparsely mentioned items; multi-agent variants (e.g., 26-agent) outperform single-agent at the cost of more tokens.  
- *Reference-based vs. reference-free*: Current evaluation depends on human summaries, which may become a ceiling as LLMs surpass humans; GAVEL-AGENT offers a reference-free future path but currently lags in extraction quality.

**Failure Signatures**  
- GAVEL-REF: Low scores on multi-value/rare items (settlements, related cases); high over/under-specification rates; style mismatch on long cases where models produce shorter, less narrative summaries.  
- GAVEL-AGENT: Error propagation in chunk-by-chunk baselines (e.g., high "Ref Empty, Model Not" mismatches); agent looping or failing to mark "Not Applicable"; token savings diminishing if agent re-reads visited ranges.  
- General: Sharp performance drop beyond 256K tokens, especially for open-source models; GPT-5 exhibiting overly verbose, non-narrative style despite prompting.

**First 3 Experiments**  
1. **Replicate meta-evaluation**: Run GAVEL-REF with chosen backbone models (e.g., GPT-oss 20B for extraction, Gemma3 27B for comparison) on 40 long summaries; measure S_checklist agreement and style rating Cohen’s κ. Expect open-source models to achieve acceptable reliability (~65 S_checklist, κ > 0 for style).  
2. **Benchmark a new LLM across lengths**: Use GAVEL-REF to evaluate Qwen3 32B on cases from all five bins (32K–512K). Confirm the expected degradation trend and identify which checklist groups (e.g., settlements, related cases) are weakest.  
3. **Test GAVEL-AGENT configuration**: Compare 26-agent, 9-agent, and 1-agent setups (using Qwen3 30B-A3B) on S_checklist and total token usage. Anticipate 26-agent to perform best but consume more tokens than 9-agent; both should beat chunk-by-chunk baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**1. Agent architectures for long-horizon extraction**  
Why unresolved: GAVEL-AGENT’s simple tool-call loop underperforms end-to-end extraction; planning agents or auto-spawned sub-agents could improve handling of long tasks.  
Needed evidence: Systematic comparison of alternative agent architectures (planning, sub-agent generation) on the 100-case test set, measuring S_checklist, token usage, and success rates across lengths.

**2. Two-stage checklist-conditioned summarization**  
Why unresolved: The paper separates evaluation and extraction but does not test whether extracting a structured checklist first and then generating a summary conditioned on it improves content coverage.  
Needed evidence: Compare summaries from end-to-end prompting vs. checklist-conditioned prompting using GAVEL-REF.

**3. Performance of strongest proprietary LLMs in GAVEL-AGENT**  
Why unresolved: Cost prevented testing GPT-5.2 or Claude 4.5 Pro; it is unclear whether these models would achieve higher extraction quality, maintain efficiency gains, or shift trade-offs.  
Needed evidence: Run GAVEL-AGENT with GPT-5.2 and Claude 4.5 Pro on the dataset and compare S_checklist and token usage to Qwen3 30B-A3B and GPT-4.1.

**4. Causes of degradation in 1M-token models on long cases**  
Why unresolved: The paper observes performance drops from 32K to 512K tokens even for models with 1M context, but does not diagnose whether this is due to positional encoding, attention sparsity, training data distribution, or inherent integration difficulty.  
Needed evidence: Controlled experiments isolating factors (e.g., positional interpolation, attention patterns) on synthetic long-context tasks; longer-sequence fine-tuning; retrieval-augmented approaches.

**5. Specific limitations behind poor multi-value/rare item performance**  
Why unresolved: Models struggle especially with settlements and related cases, but it is unclear whether the failure lies in locating relevant spans, aggregating multiple values, lacking legal-domain knowledge, or flaws in the evaluation’s matching logic.  
Needed evidence: Fine-grained error analysis of model outputs; ablations adding few-shot examples, specialized prompting, or retrieval augmentation targeted at settlement/related-case patterns.

## Limitations
- **Data and model access**: Proprietary model outputs and the exact 100-case dataset with human reference checklists are not public, hindering direct replication.
- **Evaluation reliability**: Automatic components of GAVEL-REF rely on moderate-performing open-source backbones; style evaluation exhibits very low human agreement, increasing score variance.
- **Generalization**: Findings are limited to U.S. civil rights litigation; the checklist and agent tool design may not transfer to other legal domains without adaptation.
