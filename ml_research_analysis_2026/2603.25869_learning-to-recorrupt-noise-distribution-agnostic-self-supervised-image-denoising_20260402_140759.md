# Learning to Recorrupt: Noise Distribution Agnostic Self-Supervised Image Denoising

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25869
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25869
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** L2R achieves state-of-the-art performance among distribution-agnostic self-supervised denoising methods, approaching supervised results on synthetic noise benchmarks including heavy-tailed, correlated, and Poisson-Gaussian noise.

## Executive Summary
Self-supervised image denoising typically requires known noise statistics to avoid trivial solutions like identity mapping. Learning to Recorrupt (L2R) eliminates this requirement by learning a monotonic neural network to generate synthetic noise pairs via a min-max saddle-point objective. This adversarial framework forces the denoiser to remain orthogonal to all admissible recorruption directions, implicitly aligning with the true noise without explicit distribution modeling. Consequently, L2R adapts to unknown noise types and matches or exceeds prior distribution-agnostic approaches across diverse noise models.

## Method Summary
L2R formulates denoising as a constrained saddle-point problem between a denoiser *f* and a learned recorruptor *h*. Given a noisy image *y*, a Gaussian sample *w'* is drawn, and *h(w')* produces an additive noise pattern. The corrupted pair *y₁ = y + τ h(w')* is fed to *f*, and the loss *L* = ‖*f(y₁) – y*‖² + (2/τ)*f(y₁)<sup>⊤</sup>*h(w')* is minimized over *f* while maximized over *h*. The maximization enforces that *f* must be uncorrelated with any direction produced by *h*; if *h*'s function class *ℋ* contains the true noise map, this also decorrelates *f* from the real noise. The recorruptor *h* is implemented as a monotonic neural network (e.g., MLP with non-decreasing activations) to guarantee it can represent any continuous noise distribution via a transport from a standard Gaussian. For noise with specific structure (e.g., spatial correlation or signal dependence), *h* is augmented with a spatial convolution kernel or multiplicative scaling (e.g., √*y* for Poisson-Gaussian). The method is trained with alternating or joint gradient updates, often using a stop-gradient on *y₁* during the *h* update to stabilize learning.

## Key Results
- On BSDS500 with log-gamma noise (ℓ=1.0), L2R obtains 30.77 dB PSNR / 0.871 SSIM, significantly outperforming UNSURE (27.83 dB / 0.756) and NBR2NBR (29.45 dB / 0.830) and approaching supervised (31.44 dB / 0.898).
- For spatially correlated Gaussian noise, L2R reaches 29.72 dB / 0.854, narrowing the gap to SURE (30.37/0.891).
- Visual comparisons confirm superior artifact suppression and detail preservation across heavy-tailed, correlated, and Poisson-Gaussian noise settings.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
L2R avoids trivial solutions without needing known noise statistics by casting denoising as a min-max saddle-point optimization between a denoiser *f* and a learned recorruptor *h*. The loss *L*<sub>L2R</sub> = 𝔼[ ‖*f(y₁) – y*‖² + (2/τ)*f(y₁)<sup>⊤</sup>*h(w')* ] is minimized over *f* and maximized over *h*. The inner maximization forces *f* to be orthogonal to any direction generated by *h* at equilibrium. If the function class *ℋ* for *h* is expressive enough to contain the true noise transformation *g*, this orthogonality also eliminates correlation between *f* and the real noise. The monotonic neural network parameterization of *h* ensures it can represent any continuous noise distribution because any absolutely continuous noise ε can be written as *ε = g(w)* with *w* ~ *N*(0,*I*) and *g* strictly monotone; the monotonic network approximates such *g*. The adversarial training dynamics implicitly align *h* with true noise statistics (moments, spatial correlations) because the competition drives *h* to produce perturbations that are as adversarial as possible while *f* adapts to remove their effect. This alignment is observed in the convergence of correlation metrics *C*<sub>*ε*</sub> and *C*<sub>*h*</sub> to similar values, with their difference *C*<sub>*Δ*</sub> approaching zero.

  *Why needed:* The saddle-point formulation automatically enforces the orthogonality condition via the max step on *h*, replacing manual constraint design.  
  *Quick check:* In the L2R objective, what does maximizing with respect to *h* encourage, and why does that help?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Noisy image *y* → Gaussian sample *w'* → monotonic MLP *h* (with per-channel normalization and optional spatial kernel) → recorruption *τ h(w')* → *y₁ = y + τ h(w')* → denoiser *f* → output *x̂*. Loss combines MSE(*x̂*, *y*) and correlation term (2/τ)*x̂<sup>⊤</sup>*h(w')*.
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Sample minibatch of noisy images *y*.  
  2. For each image, sample *w'* ~ *N*(0,*I*).  
  3. Compute *h(w')* via mMLP, normalize, convolve with spatial kernel.  
  4. Form *y₁ = y + τ · h(w')*.  
  5. Compute denoised *x̂ = f(y₁)*.  
  6. Compute reconstruction loss = MSE(*x̂*, *y*).  
  7. Compute correlation term = (2/τ) · mean(*x̂* · *h(w')*).  
  8. Total loss = reconstruction + correlation.  
  9. Update *f* parameters by descending total loss (optionally with stop‑gradient on *y₁*).  
  10. Update *h* parameters by ascending total loss (or descending its negative).
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *h* expressivity: deeper/wider networks approximate more complex noise but may destabilize min‑max; 3‑layer MLP with 4–8 neurons works well.  
  - Recorruption strength τ: too large yields unrealistic *y₁*; too small weakens correlation term; typically small (e.g., 0.1).  
  - Stop‑gradient on *y₁*: reduces memory and stabilizes *h* updates; recommended.  
  - Joint vs alternating updates: joint is simpler; alternating may offer more stability but requires double forward passes.  
  - Spatial kernel size: must match noise correlation; 1×1 for i.i.d. noise, 3×3 for correlated noise; mismatch severely degrades performance.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High residual correlation *C*<sub>*Δ*</sub> (difference between *C*<sub>*ε*</sub> and *C*<sub>*h*</sub>) indicates *h* did not align with noise.  
  - Reconstruction loss plateaus near identity level, suggesting *f* failed to learn.  
  - *h* outputs collapse to zero or constant values.  
  - Training instability: loss spikes, parameter explosion, cyclic behavior.  
  - Performance drops drastically when *h* architecture mismatches noise structure (e.g., 1×1 kernel for correlated noise, missing √*y* scaling for Poisson‑Gaussian).
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Gaussian sanity check:** Train L2R with known Gaussian variance σ. Verify that the learned *h* approximates a linear scaling √σ·*w* and that PSNR approaches the supervised baseline.  
  2. **Monotonicity ablation:** Replace the monotonic MLP with a standard MLP of identical size for log‑gamma noise. Expect significant PSNR/SSIM drop and instability, confirming the necessity of monotonicity.  
  3. **Spatial correlation match:** For correlated Gaussian noise, train L2R with *h* using a 1×1 versus a 3×3 spatial kernel. Expect >3 dB PSNR improvement for the 3×3 variant, demonstrating the need for spatial modeling in *h*.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What recorruptor architectures beyond the simple monotonic MLP can enhance L2R's performance across diverse noise models?  
- Does L2R theoretically converge to the supervised MMSE estimator under mild conditions on the recorruptor class *ℋ*?  
- Can L2R automatically adapt the recorruption strength τ and the architecture of *h* to unknown noise characteristics without extensive hyperparameter tuning?  
- How well does L2R perform on real-world noisy images from actual cameras or sensors, where noise may be signal-dependent, spatially varying, and non-additive?

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter details (τ, learning rates, *h* width) are unspecified, hindering exact replication.  
- Theoretical assumption that noise has a continuous CDF; failure modes for discrete or mixed noise are not explored.  
- Min‑max training may be unstable without careful tuning and is sensitive to initialization and learning-rate schedules.  
- Evaluation is limited to synthetic additive noise models; real-world signal‑dependent or spatially varying noise remains untested.  
- Computational cost is roughly doubled due to training both *f* and *h*.

## Confidence
- Saddle‑point formulation avoids trivial solution: **High** (theoretical derivation matches empirical convergence).  
- Monotonic network can represent any continuous noise distribution: **Medium** (foundations solid but finite capacity may restrict heavy‑tailed distributions).  
- Adversarial dynamics implicitly learn noise statistics: **Medium** (correlation metrics decline, but convergence requires balanced updates and may occasionally be unstable).

## Next Checks
1. **Gaussian sanity:** Train L2R on images with known additive Gaussian noise of variance σ. Plot the learned *h* mapping (e.g., quantiles of *h(w')* vs. *w*) and verify approximate linearity √σ·*w*. PSNR should approach the supervised baseline.  
2. **Monotonicity ablation:** For log‑gamma noise, replace the monotonic MLP with a standard MLP (same size) under identical training. Quantify monotonicity violations (e.g., proportion of non‑increasing outputs after sorting). Expect a significant PSNR/SSIM drop.  
3. **Spatial correlation match:** For correlated Gaussian noise, train L2R with *h* configured with a 1×1 kernel versus a 3×3 kernel. Measure PSNR/SSIM difference; expect >3 dB improvement for the 3×3 configuration, confirming the necessity of matching spatial statistics in *h*.
