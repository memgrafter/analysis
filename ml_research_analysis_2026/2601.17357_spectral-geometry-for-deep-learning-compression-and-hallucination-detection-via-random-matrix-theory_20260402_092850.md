# Spectral Geometry for Deep Learning: Compression and Hallucination Detection via Random Matrix Theory

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.17357
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17357
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: EigenTrack detects hallucinations and OOD inputs with AUROC 0.82–0.96; RMT‑KD compresses models up to 80.9% with accuracy gains.

## Executive Summary
This thesis introduces a unified spectral framework based on Random Matrix Theory (RMT) to tackle two critical challenges in deep learning: hallucination detection and model compression. The first contribution, EigenTrack, monitors the temporal evolution of eigenvalue statistics from hidden activations to detect hallucinations and out‑of‑distribution inputs in real time, achieving AUROC scores between 0.82 and 0.96 across multiple LLMs. The second contribution, RMT‑KD, uses the Marchenko–Pastur law to identify causal eigenvectors and project networks onto low‑dimensional subspaces via iterative knowledge distillation, reducing parameters by up to 80.9% while improving accuracy and yielding significant speedup and power savings. Together, these methods demonstrate that spectral geometry offers a principled approach to enhancing both reliability and efficiency.

## Method Summary
EigenTrack constructs covariance matrices from sliding windows of activations, distills their eigenvalue spectra into interpretable descriptors (e.g., top‑eigenvalue sum, entropy, divergence from the Marchenko–Pastur bulk), and feeds these time series into a lightweight recurrent network to predict anomalies. RMT‑KD first fits the Marchenko–Pastur law to the activation covariance on a calibration set to estimate the noise bulk; eigenvectors with eigenvalues above the bulk edge are selected as causal directions. The network is then projected onto this subspace, and self‑distillation recovers any lost accuracy. Both methods rely on the principle that in‑distribution, well‑trained models exhibit a spiked spectrum (few outliers on top of a Marchenko–Pastur bulk), while failures or redundancy manifest as deviations from this pattern.

## Key Results
- EigenTrack achieves AUROC 0.82–0.94 for hallucination detection and 0.85–0.96 for OOD detection, outperforming existing methods.
- RMT‑KD reduces model parameters by up to 80.9% and improves accuracy by up to +1.8% on BERT and ResNet.
- RMT‑KD yields up to 2.88× speedup and near‑half power reduction after compression.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Spectral Statistics as a Proxy for Representation Quality
The eigenvalue spectrum of hidden activations provides a compact indicator of whether internal representations are structured (signal‑dominated) or noise‑like. Covariance matrices built from sliding windows yield spectra where a few outlier eigenvalues capture task‑relevant directions, while the bulk follows the Marchenko–Pastur noise law. Hallucinations or OOD inputs erode this separation. Descriptors (e.g., top‑eigenvalue sum, entropy, KL divergence from MP) quantify the deviation.  
*Break condition*: If the activation distribution is highly non‑Gaussian, strongly correlated across features, or the aspect ratio (d/n) is extreme, the MP bulk may not approximate the spectrum well, making thresholding unreliable.

### Mechanism 2: Temporal Dynamics of Spectral Features for Early Detection
Hallucinations and OOD events are often preceded by gradual drifts in the spectral geometry of activations; modeling these dynamics with a recurrent network allows early warning before errors surface. A lightweight RNN/GRU/LSTM ingests a time series of spectral descriptors and learns to recognize patterns that precede representational collapse.  
*Break condition*: If hallucinations emerge abruptly without progressive spectral degradation (e.g., due to a single catastrophic layer failure), the recurrent model may only detect the anomaly after it has already occurred.  
*Evidence anchors*: [abstract] “EigenTrack monitors temporal evolution…”; [section 4.1] “Temporal Modeling and Early Warning: A lightweight recurrent network observes the evolution…”.

### Mechanism 3: Causal Subspace Identification via Outlier Eigenvalues for Compression
Eigenvalues that detach from the Marchenko–Pastur bulk correspond to directions carrying task‑relevant (“causal”) information. RMT‑KD fits the MP law to the activation covariance spectrum, estimates the bulk edge λ₊, selects eigenvectors with eigenvalues > λ₊, and inserts a linear projection onto that subspace. Self‑distillation then recovers accuracy lost during projection.  
*Break condition*: If the model’s task relies on distributed representations without clear spectral outliers (e.g., many weak signals below the BBP threshold), projection onto the outlier subspace may discard critical information, causing irrecoverable accuracy drops.  
*Evidence anchors*: [abstract] “RMT‑KD employs iterative knowledge distillation guided by the Marchenko–Pastur law to identify causal eigenvectors…”; [section 5.1] “Estimating the Noise Bulk via the MP Law” and “Selecting Causal Directions”.

## Foundational Learning
- **Random Matrix Theory (Marchenko‑Pastur law, BBP transition)**  
  *Why needed*: Provides the statistical baseline for distinguishing noise (bulk) from signal (outliers); defines thresholds like λ₊ based on aspect ratio and noise variance.  
  *Quick check*: “Given an activation covariance matrix with aspect ratio q and estimated noise variance σ², how do you compute the Marchenko‑Pastur bulk edges λ₋ and λ₊?”

- **Spectral decomposition of activation covariances**  
  *Why needed*: Eigenvalues and eigenvectors are the raw material for all spectral descriptors; efficient computation (e.g., using truncated SVD on thin matrices) is essential for real‑time operation.  
  *Quick check*: “How would you compute the top‑k eigenvalues of a large but very thin activation matrix X ∈ ℝ^{d×n} (with n << d) without forming the full d×d covariance?”

- **Knowledge distillation (self‑distillation)**  
  *Why needed*: After projection, the smaller student model must recover the original performance; distillation aligns its outputs (and optionally internal states) with the pre‑reduction teacher.  
  *Quick check*: “What is the typical loss combination for knowledge distillation and what role does the temperature parameter play in softening the teacher’s distribution?”

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
EigenTrack: Activation collector -> Sliding window buffer -> Covariance estimator -> Spectral descriptor calculator -> Recurrent classifier  
RMT‑KD: Calibration data loader -> Activation matrix builder -> MP fitting module -> Outlier detector -> Projection layer inserter -> Self‑distillation trainer

### Critical path
EigenTrack: per generated token -> collect activations -> update window -> (re)compute SVD -> extract spectral features -> recurrent forward pass -> output anomaly score.  
RMT‑KD: train baseline -> at validation plateau -> for each target layer: collect activations on calibration set -> compute Σ -> fit MP -> find outlier eigenvectors -> insert projection -> resize downstream -> self‑distill fine‑tune -> re‑evaluate.

### Design tradeoffs
EigenTrack: shorter window -> finer temporal resolution but higher compute; fewer monitored layers -> faster but potentially less sensitive; GRU generally best accuracy/latency trade‑off.  
RMT‑KD: quantile for σ² initialization: lower (e.g., 40%) -> conservative compression; higher (e.g., 60%) -> aggressive but riskier. Calibration set size: larger -> more accurate MP fit but more overhead.

### Failure signatures
EigenTrack: AUROC near 0.5 on validation; recurrent model overfits to training spectral sequences; detection latency exceeds generation step budget.  
RMT‑KD: Post‑projection validation accuracy drop beyond tolerance (e.g., >1%); MP fit fails (high ℓ₂ error between empirical and theoretical densities); outlier ratio in deep layers falls below minimum threshold.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Spectral baseline check**: Compute eigenvalue histograms of activations from a randomly initialized model and a fully trained model; verify the MP bulk appears in the former and outliers emerge in the latter.
2. **EigenTrack sanity test**: On a small synthetic QA dataset where hallucinations are induced (e.g., feeding nonsense contexts), train the recurrent detector and confirm AUROC > 0.9 with a short window.
3. **RMT‑KD knob sweep**: Apply RMT‑KD to a toy 2‑layer MLP on MNIST, vary the initial quantile (e.g., 0.1–0.9), and plot accuracy vs. parameter reduction to observe the optimal 40–50% region.

## Limitations
- EigenTrack: Break conditions for MP law (extreme aspect ratios, non‑Gaussian activations) may degrade spectral descriptors; abrupt hallucination onset limits early warning.
- RMT‑KD: Effectiveness depends on clear spectral outliers; models with distributed, sub‑threshold signals may lose irrecoverable accuracy.
- Unified spectral geometry premise: Assumption that a single spectral framework governs both detection and compression is nascent; interactions not theoretically linked.
