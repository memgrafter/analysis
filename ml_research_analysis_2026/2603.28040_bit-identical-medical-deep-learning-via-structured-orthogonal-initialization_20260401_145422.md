# Bit-Identical Medical Deep Learning via Structured Orthogonal Initialization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28040
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28040
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MD5-verified bit-identical training is achieved by eliminating weight initialization, batch ordering, and GPU operation randomness, reducing per-class AUC variance on rare clinical rhythms by up to 7.5×.

## Executive Summary
Non-determinism in deep learning training leads to significant performance instability, particularly for rare clinical classes, with per-class AUC swings exceeding 20 percentage points. This work eliminates three key sources of randomness—weight initialization via structured orthogonal bases, batch ordering via golden ratio scheduling, and non-deterministic GPU operations via a custom autograd—to enable fully reproducible, bit-identical training. On the PTB-XL ECG dataset, the approach reduces aggregate variance by 2–3× and per-class variability on rare rhythms by up to 7.5×. Cross-domain validation on seven MedMNIST benchmarks shows no performance penalty, and external ECG databases confirm robust AFIB detection with AUC >0.93.

## Method Summary
The proposed system removes randomness from three sources: (1) Weight initialization uses structured orthogonal matrices (DCT, Hadamard, Hartley, sinusoidal) instead of stochastic methods like Kaiming; (2) Batch ordering is fixed across epochs using a golden ratio-based scheduler to eliminate permutation effects; (3) A custom autograd implementation overrides non-deterministic GPU operations (e.g., certain reductions) with deterministic equivalents. These components are integrated into the training loop, requiring only a single code change and no hyperparameter tuning. The approach is evaluated on medical classification tasks, with bit-identity verified via MD5 hashes of model checkpoints across independent runs.

## Key Results
- Structured orthogonal initialization reduces aggregate AUC variance by 2–3× and per-class variability on rare rhythms up to 7.5× (TRIGU range: 4.1 pp vs 30.9 pp under Kaiming) on PTB-XL.
- No performance penalty observed across seven MedMNIST imaging benchmarks compared to standard initialization.
- External validation on separate ECG databases confirms AFIB detection AUC >0.93, matching or exceeding prior work.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Bit-identical training is achieved by systematically removing all sources of non-determinism. Structured orthogonal initialization ensures consistent starting weights with controlled spectral properties, preventing gradient explosion/vanishing while eliminating random seed dependence. Golden ratio scheduling produces a fixed, aperiodic batch order that is invariant to data loader shuffling, removing order-based variance. Custom autograd replaces GPU operations with undefined numerical behavior (e.g., atomic adds in reduction) with deterministic alternatives, ensuring identical forward/backward passes across hardware. Together, these guarantee that given identical inputs, hyperparameters, and hardware, training trajectories are byte-for-byte reproducible.

## Foundational Learning
- **Orthogonal Initialization:** Weight matrices with orthonormal rows/columns preserve gradient norms. *Why needed:* Prevents signal decay/explosion in deep networks and ensures deterministic starting points. *Quick check:* Verify `W.T @ W ≈ I` for linear layers.
- **Deterministic Scheduling:** Fixed data ordering independent of sampler randomness. *Why needed:* Batch order affects gradient directions; randomness causes run-to-run variance. *Quick check:* Log first 10 batch indices across epochs—must be identical.
- **Custom Autograd:** Overrides non-deterministic CUDA kernels. *Why needed:* Operations like `TORCH.ARGMIN` or certain reductions have unspecified numerical behavior. *Quick check:* Compare loss values across runs with same seed—should match to machine epsilon.
- **Non-determinism in DL:** Sources include GPU atomics, parallel reduction order, and data loader shuffling. *Why needed:* Understanding these is critical to reproducibility. *Quick check:* Enable `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)` and observe failures.
- **Medical Class Imbalance:** Rare classes (e.g., rare ECG rhythms) have high variance in performance metrics. *Why needed:* Non-determinism disproportionately hurts low-support classes. *Quick check:* Compute per-class AUC standard deviation across random seeds.
- **MD5 Verification:** Cryptographic hash for byte-identical comparison. *Why needed:* Gold standard for bit-identical reproducibility. *Quick check:* `md5` of checkpoint files should match exactly across runs.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Structured Orthogonal Initialization -> Custom Autograd Context -> Golden Ratio Batch Scheduler -> Deterministic Training Loop

**Critical Path:**  
1. Initialize all linear/convolutional weights from a deterministic orthogonal basis.  
2. Wrap training step in custom autograd context that forces deterministic kernels.  
3. At each epoch, generate batch indices via golden ratio increment (no shuffling).  
4. Compute loss, backpropagate through custom autograd, update parameters.  
5. Save checkpoint; MD5 must match across runs with same seed and hardware.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- *Structured init vs. expressivity:* Orthogonal bases may limit representational capacity compared to unstructured random init, but mean performance is preserved.  
- *Custom autograd vs. compatibility:* May break with future PyTorch ops or mixed precision; requires maintenance.  
- *Fixed ordering vs. generalization:* Golden ratio scheduling removes benefits of shuffling; could harm convergence on some datasets.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- MD5 hashes differ between runs with identical seeds.  
- Per-class AUC variance remains high (>10 pp) on rare classes.  
- Training time increases due to slower deterministic kernels.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Bit-identity verification:** Train PTB-XL model with same seed on same GPU; compute MD5 of final weights—must match exactly.  
2. **Variance ablation:** Compare per-class AUC standard deviation across 10 seeds for Kaiming vs. DCT vs. Hadamard init.  
3. **Cross-domain parity:** Train on MedMNIST (e.g., PathMNIST) with structured init; compare mean accuracy to Kaiming baseline—should be within 0.5%.

## Limitations
- Generalization limited to ECG (PTB-XL) and MedMNIST imaging; other modalities (pathology, genomics) remain untested.
- Orthogonal basis comparison excludes Stiefel manifold initialization, challenging the claim of universal equivalence.
- External validation focuses solely on AFIB, omitting other rare rhythms' performance.

## Confidence
- **Bit-identical training via MD5:** High  
- **Aggregate variance reduction (2–3×):** Medium (empirical on single ECG dataset)  
- **Per-class variability reduction (up to 7.5×):** Medium (empirical on single ECG dataset)  
- **No performance penalty on MedMNIST:** Medium (7 small-scale tasks)  
- **Equivalence across orthogonal bases:** Medium-Low (incomplete basis sampling)  
- **External AFIB AUC >0.93:** High (replicated on external databases)
