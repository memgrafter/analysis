# From Conflict to Consensus: Boosting Medical Reasoning via Multi-Round Agentic RAG

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03292
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03292
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MA-RAG improves average accuracy by +6.8 points over the backbone model across seven medical benchmarks, with a 37% improvement on MedXpertQA and 62.2% average accuracy for MA-RAG-ext, surpassing the strongest RAG baseline by 5.3 points.

## Executive Summary
MA-RAG is a multi-round agentic retrieval-augmented generation framework designed for complex medical question answering. It combats hallucinations and outdated knowledge in LLMs by transforming semantic conflicts among multiple candidate responses into targeted retrieval queries, while strategically reorganizing historical reasoning traces to mitigate long-context degradation. The system iterates through a Solver, Retrieval, and Ranking Agent until consensus emerges, mimicking a boosting process that progressively refines answers. Across seven medical benchmarks, MA-RAG achieves a +6.8-point average accuracy gain over its Qwen3-8B backbone, with particularly strong improvements on MedXpertQA (+37%) and when using an extrinsic evaluator (MA-RAG-ext, 62.2% average, +5.3 over prior RAG).

## Method Summary
MA-RAG operates in iterative rounds. The **Solver Agent** (Qwen3-8B) generates *N* candidate responses given the current context (documents *D<sub>t</sub>* and ranked history *H<sub>t</sub>*). The **Retrieval Agent** detects semantic conflicts (e.g., contradictory diagnoses) among these candidates and abstracts them into *K* precise queries. An **External Executor** then retrieves documents from MedCorp using BM25 (across four sub-corpora) and MedCPT reranking, returning a fixed budget (e.g., 8 docs total). The **Ranking Agent** scores all previous candidates using either intrinsic entropy or an extrinsic evaluator (fine-tuned ModernBERT) and sorts them to construct *H<sub>t+1</sub>*, placing high-quality traces in salient prompt positions. The loop repeats—updating *D<sub>t+1</sub>* and *H<sub>t+1</sub>*—until all candidates agree, after which majority voting produces the final answer. This design replaces noisy token-level signals with conflict-driven retrieval and uses history ranking to sustain in-context learning across rounds.

## Key Results
- Average accuracy gain of +6.8 points over the backbone model (Qwen3-8B) across seven medical QA benchmarks.
- 37% relative improvement on the challenging MedXpertQA dataset.
- MA-RAG-ext (with extrinsic evaluator) achieves 62.2% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest prior RAG method by 5.3 points.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Conflict as a Retrieval Signal
The Retrieval Agent uses semantic conflicts among multiple candidate responses as a high-level, reliable signal to identify knowledge gaps and formulate targeted queries. Divergent, factually incorrect hallucinations tend to cluster around specific wrong assertions, while correct paths converge; thus, the absence of consensus reliably indicates insufficient external knowledge. By transforming disagreement into precise queries (e.g., “What is the definitive diagnostic test for condition X?”), the system bypasses noisy token-level uncertainty metrics. Evidence from the paper shows that adding the Retrieval Agent alone yields ~+1.9 accuracy points.

### Mechanism 2: History Ranking for Long-Context Mitigation
The Ranking Agent scores all previous candidate responses (intrinsically via entropy or extrinsically via a fine-tuned evaluator) and sorts them in descending quality to construct the next round’s history context. This prioritized placement ensures the most promising reasoning traces occupy the most salient positions in the prompt, counteracting the “lost-in-the-middle” degradation that plagues long in-context learning. Ablations indicate this component contributes ~+1.6 points, and the extrinsic evaluator (MA-RAG-ext) further improves by ~1.2 points over intrinsic ranking.

### Mechanism 3: Boosting-Like Iterative Refinement
MA-RAG mirrors classical boosting by treating semantic conflict in round *t* as a “residual error”—a knowledge gap unsolvable given the current state. The Retrieval Agent provides new evidence to address this gap, and the Ranking Agent supplies optimized history, allowing the Solver to generate a candidate set with reduced conflict in round *t+1*. This iterative error minimization progressively drives the system toward a stable, high-fidelity consensus. The loop terminates when candidates converge, analogous to a weak learner focusing on previously mis-reasoned instances.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Solver Agent → Retrieval Agent → External Executor → Ranking Agent → (back to Solver for next round). After convergence, the loop exits to Aggregation (majority vote). The Convergence Check is embedded after Solver generation.

**Critical path:**  
1. Solver generates *N* candidates *A<sub>t</sub>*.  
2. If all *A<sub>t</sub>* agree → terminate.  
3. Else: Retrieval Agent analyzes conflicts in *A<sub>t</sub>* → produces *K* queries *R<sub>t</sub>*.  
4. External Executor retrieves documents *D<sub>t+1</sub>*.  
5. Ranking Agent scores *A<sub>t</sub>* → sorts to form *H<sub>t+1</sub>*.  
6. Update state *S<sub>t+1</sub>* = {*I, q, D<sub>t+1</sub>, H<sub>t+1</sub>*} → repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Compute vs. gain:** *T* (rounds) and *N* (candidates) scale cost linearly; saturation occurs (e.g., *T*=4, *N*=8).  
- **Retrieval budget:** Fixed total documents (8) split into *K*=4 queries (2 docs each) outperforms *K*=1 (8 docs), favoring query diversity.  
- **Ranking signal:** Extrinsic evaluator (fine-tuned ModernBERT) beats intrinsic entropy by ~1.2 points but requires training data.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Stuck in local consensus:** All candidates agree on a wrong answer early; conflict detection fails to trigger retrieval.  
- **Retrieval mismatch:** Conflict queries are too vague/off-topic → irrelevant documents.  
- **Long-context collapse:** Even with ranking, Solver ignores middle history if *N* is excessive.  
- **Evaluator brittleness:** Extrinsic evaluator may overfit to in-distribution benchmarks.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablation:** Cumulatively add Multi-Refine (+1.9 pts), Retrieval Agent (+1.9 pts), Ranking Agent (+1.6 pts) versus pure multi-round.  
2. **Scaling analysis:** Sweep *T* (1→8) and *N* (2→8); expect rapid gains at *T*=2, saturation by *T*=4; monotonic *N* gain with diminishing returns.  
3. **Ranking signal comparison:** Compare Recall@k for intrinsic vs. extrinsic scorers; expect higher Recall@1 and steeper curve for extrinsic. Also compare MA-RAG-ext vs. static Best-of-N using Recall@1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can MA-RAG reduce its inference-time overhead without sacrificing accuracy for latency-sensitive clinical use? The authors acknowledge the high cost of multi-round refinement but offer no cost-reduction strategies.
- **Open Question 2:** How does the coverage and quality of the static medical corpus (MedCorp) bound MA-RAG’s performance, and can it be dynamically enhanced? The paper does not quantify corpus limitations or test live knowledge updates.
- **Open Question 3:** How can the ranking agent’s quality estimation be made more reliable to better prioritize correct reasoning traces? Current signals (entropy, fine-tuned evaluator) are imperfect, especially on nuanced medical topics.
- **Open Question 4:** Is the core assumption that semantic conflict among candidates reliably signals knowledge gaps valid across diverse medical scenarios, or can conflict arise from non-knowledge factors (e.g., ambiguous questions, model bias)? No empirical validation of this assumption’s universality is provided.

## Limitations
- Inference-time overhead from multiple rounds and candidates hinders real-time deployment.
- Performance is upper-bounded by the static MedCorp; outdated or incomplete evidence cannot be addressed.
- The extrinsic evaluator’s reliability is contingent on training distribution and may degrade on out-of-distribution medical topics.
- The boosting analogy remains conceptual without direct experimental validation (e.g., residual error reduction measurement).

## Confidence
- **Overall accuracy gain (+6.8 avg):** High (consistent across 7 benchmarks).
- **Conflict-driven retrieval mechanism:** Medium (ablation +1.9, but direct evidence weak).
- **History ranking mechanism:** Medium (ablation +1.6, no counterfactuals).
- **Extrinsic evaluator advantage:** High (clear gap in Table 1 & Fig 7).
- **Boosting analogy:** Low (conceptual only, no dedicated experiment).
