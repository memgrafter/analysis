# Modernising Reinforcement Learning-Based Navigation for Embodied Semantic Scene Graph Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.25415
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25415
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: PPO improves Node Recall by 21% relative to the REINFORCE baseline.

## Executive Summary
This paper modernizes reinforcement learning (RL) for embodied navigation tasked with generating semantic scene graphs (SSGs). It replaces the classic REINFORCE algorithm with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and redesigns the discrete action space to enable finer-grained motion. Under identical reward shaping, the authors evaluate single-head versus factorized multi-head policies, the impact of finer actions, curriculum learning, and depth-based collision supervision. Key findings show PPO alone boosts SSG completeness (Node Recall) by 21%, factorized multi-head policies with high-resolution actions offer the best completeness-efficiency trade-off, and depth input significantly improves safety (Move Success Rate) with little effect on completeness. The work sets a stronger foundation for integrating navigation into full embodied SSG pipelines.

## Method Summary
The navigation policy is trained with PPO instead of REINFORCE to reduce variance and stabilize learning. The discrete action space is refined to allow smaller rotational increments and larger per-step translations, enabling more precise positioning. Policies are compared in single-head (joint action output) versus factorized multi-head (separate translation and rotation heads) configurations. Curriculum learning gradually increases scene complexity or step budgets during training. Depth imagery is incorporated as an auxiliary input to provide a dense collision penalty, supplementing sparse task rewards. All variants share the same reward function to isolate the effect of each design choice.

## Key Results
- PPO increases Node Recall by 21% over the REINFORCE baseline.
- Factorized multi-head policies with high-resolution actions achieve the strongest overall trade-off between graph completeness and navigation efficiency.
- Depth-based collision supervision raises Move Success Rate (safety) with minimal negative impact on Node Recall.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PPO's clipped objective prevents destructive policy updates, crucial for sparse-reward navigation where exploration is noisy. Finer-grained actions, especially with increased maximum translation, allow the agent to reach target objects more accurately, directly improving node coverage. Factorizing the policy into translation and rotation heads lets each learn specialized, context-appropriate motions, better handling the diversity of required maneuvers. Depth input provides immediate tactile-like feedback for collision avoidance, reducing wasted steps on invalid movements without discouraging necessary close approaches for object detection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the navigation performance change when equipped with realistic perception (e.g., noisy object detection and data association) instead of perfect metadata?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The conclusion states that future work will "integrate the navigation module into a full ESSG pipeline with realistic perception and data association".
- Why unresolved: All experiments used ground-truth SSG from simulator metadata, so the impact of perception noise on navigation is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Train and evaluate the navigation policy with a perception module that introduces detection and association errors, and measure degradation in Node Recall and safety metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the factorised multi-head policy maintain its superiority in larger and more complex indoor environments with longer horizons?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The conclusion mentions evaluating in "larger and more complex environments to assess end-to-end scalability under noise".
- Why unresolved: Current experiments are limited to small AI2-THOR scenes (FloorPlans 1–30) with a 40-step budget; scalability to larger spaces is untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Train the best configurations on larger floor plans (e.g., more rooms, multiple floors) and compare Node Recall, path length, and safety metrics against baselines.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the navigation component be embedded into a self-configuring and self-healing OC control loop that adapts to dynamic changes in sensing or utility?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Future work aims to "embed the navigation component into higher-level OC control loops, enabling explicit self-configuration and self-healing mechanisms".
- Why unresolved: Navigation was studied as an isolated module; integration with system-level adaptation mechanisms was not demonstrated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement a meta-controller that monitors environmental changes (e.g., sensor degradation) and dynamically adjusts navigation policy parameters or selects alternative policies, then evaluate robustness in simulated dynamic scenarios.

### Open Question 4
- Question: To what extent are the performance gains from high-resolution action spaces due to increased maximum translation per step versus finer angular discretisation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The discussion notes that "our high-resolution action spaces jointly increase discretisation granularity and the maximum translation per step; consequently, the observed gains cannot be attributed to discretisation alone."
- Why unresolved: The design co-varied these factors; no experiment isolated their individual contributions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct an ablation with controlled action spaces: (a) keep max translation fixed (e.g., 0.3m) while increasing angular resolution, (b) keep angular resolution fixed while increasing max translation, and measure Node Recall under fixed step budget.

## Limitations
- Baseline comparison lacks methodological details (e.g., hyperparameters, environment variants), hindering reproducibility.
- Statistical robustness is unreported; improvements lack confidence intervals or significance tests.
- Efficiency trade-offs (completeness vs. path length) are not concretely quantified through explicit analyses.
- Evaluation restricted to small AI2-THOR scenes; generalizability to larger, more complex, or dynamic environments is unverified.
- Corpus shows zero citations, indicating limited peer validation; findings may not yet be community-vetted.

## Confidence
- PPO improves Node Recall: Medium (single-experiment claim; no statistical tests reported).
- Factorized multi-head + high-res actions best trade-off: Medium (trade-off metrics not concretely defined).
- Depth input increases safety (Move Success Rate): High (direct metric reported).
- Curriculum learning improves early safety but slows convergence: Medium-Low (context-dependent; no ablation on curriculum scheduling).
