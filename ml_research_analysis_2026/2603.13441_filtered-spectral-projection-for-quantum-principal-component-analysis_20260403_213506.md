# Filtered Spectral Projection for Quantum Principal Component Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13441
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13441
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** The Filtered Spectral Projection Algorithm (FSPA) enables robust dominant spectral subspace recovery on quantum hardware, with query complexity O(log(1/ε)/log(λ₁/λ₂)), invariance to uniform eigenvalue scaling, and graceful degradation under narrow spectral gaps.

## Executive Summary
Quantum principal component analysis (qPCA) holds promise for extracting principal components from large datasets, but standard approaches via quantum phase estimation are fragile: they suffer from magnitude collapse when eigenvalues are uniformly downscaled and exhibit abrupt failure as the spectral gap narrows. This paper introduces the Filtered Spectral Projection Algorithm (FSPA), an iterative method that amplifies overlap with the leading invariant subspace of a Hermitian operator (e.g., a covariance matrix) by repeatedly applying its block-encoding followed by explicit renormalization. The adaptive doubling schedule (β ← 2β) builds a spectral filter that suppresses subdominant components at a rate governed by the spectral gap log(λ₁/λ₂). The algorithm is theoretically shown to converge to the dominant subspace with gap-limited query complexity and to remain invariant under uniform rescaling of the spectrum. Numerical experiments on the Breast Cancer Wisconsin and Digits datasets demonstrate stability under eigenvector perturbations, immunity to magnitude collapse, and smooth performance degradation as the gap shrinks, while downstream classification accuracy on Digits remains stable when projection quality is preserved. FSPA thus provides a more practical and robust route to qPCA when the concrete goal is subspace projection rather than precise eigenvalue estimation.

## Method Summary
FSPA operates on a Hermitian operator ρ (e.g., a covariance matrix) accessible via a block-encoding unitary U such that ⟨0|U|0⟩ ≈ ρ/α. Given an initial state |ϕ₀⟩ with nonzero overlap with the dominant eigenspace, the algorithm proceeds: (1) normalize |ϕ₀⟩; (2) for T outer rounds, apply the following inner loop β times (starting at β=1 and doubling after each outer round): apply U to the state and then renormalize (e.g., via amplitude amplification or post-selection); (3) output the final state. The normalization step after each U application removes dependence on the global eigenvalue magnitude, yielding invariance to uniform scaling. The composite effect of the increasing β applications builds a polynomial spectral filter that attenuates eigenvalues outside the leading subspace, while the normalization converts the amplified magnitude difference into a stable projection. In classical simulations, the block-encoding is approximated by direct matrix multiplication, and fidelity to the true dominant subspace is measured.

## Key Results
- FSPA maintains high dominant-subspace fidelity under random eigenvector perturbations that degrade individual eigenvector overlap.
- It is immune to the magnitude collapse that afflicts phase-estimation-based qPCA when eigenvalues are uniformly downscaled.
- Performance degrades smoothly as the spectral gap narrows (observed linear scaling of oracle calls with 1/log(λ₁/λ₂)), contrasting the abrupt threshold failure of estimation-first methods.
- Downstream classification accuracy on the Digits dataset remains stable whenever the projection quality is preserved.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** FSPA achieves invariance to uniform spectral rescaling of the covariance operator.
- **Mechanism:** Each application of the Hermitian operator (via block-encoding) is followed by explicit renormalization of the state vector. This normalization step removes dependence on the global magnitude of eigenvalues, as scaling ρ by c>0 produces identical normalized iterates.
- **Core assumption:** The normalization operation (e.g., via heralding or amplitude amplification) is implemented perfectly.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "...remains robust in small-gap and near-degenerate regimes without inducing artificial symmetry breaking" and "invariant to uniform rescaling of the spectrum."
  - [section, p.2, Algo 1 & Prop 1]: Explicitly states the algorithm's invariance under cρ and ties it to the normalization step (line 1,6).
  - [corpus]: Neighbors discuss eigenvalue magnitude issues but direct evidence for this specific normalization property is weak; the mechanism is a novel contribution of this paper.
- **Break condition:** If normalization introduces non-unitary errors or if the initial state's norm cannot be accurately estimated.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Amplification of overlap with the dominant invariant subspace proceeds at a rate governed by the spectral gap (λ₁/λ₂), not absolute eigenvalue magnitudes.
- **Mechanism:** The adaptive doubling schedule (β ← 2β) applies an increasing number of ρ-multiplications per round, building a composite spectral filter that suppresses subdominant eigenvalues geometrically as (λ₂/λ₁)^k. Renormalization then converts this amplified magnitude difference into a stable projection.
- **Core assumption:** The spectral ordering is λ₁ ≥ λ₂ ≥ ... with a strict gap λ₁ > λ₂ for unique eigenvector recovery; for subspace recovery, degeneracy λ₁=...=λᵣ > λᵣ₊₁ is allowed.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "gap-limited convergence to the dominant subspace" and "smooth, gap-dependent degradation."
  - [section, p.3, Theorem 1]: Query complexity bound O(log(1/ε) + log(1/|a₁|²)) / log(λ₁/λ₂) explicitly shows gap dependence.
  - [section, p.4, Fig 2]: Empirical validation shows linear scaling of oracle calls vs. 1/log(λ₁/λ₂).
- **Break condition:** If the spectral gap vanishes (λ₁ → λ₂), convergence to a unique eigenvector fails; only subspace recovery remains possible.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** In near-degenerate dominant subspaces (λ₁ ≈ ... ≈ λᵣ), FSPA converges to the invariant subspace without artificially selecting a specific basis direction, unless a positive semidefinite bias is present.
- **Mechanism:** The normalized power iteration (with adaptive schedule) is a subspace iteration: in the absence of a distinguishing eigenvalue, the iterates converge to the span of the top R eigenvectors, not a single one. A small symmetry-breaking perturbation inside the subspace (e.g., from data or initialization) guides selection.
- **Core assumption:** The operator ρ is Hermitian and the initial state has nonzero overlap with the dominant subspace (∑_{j≤R} |aⱼ|² > 0).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "remains robust in small-gap and near-degenerate regimes without inducing artificial symmetry breaking."
  - [section, p.2, Prop 3]: "Convergence to a unique eigenvector is not guaranteed unless degeneracy is lifted."
  - [supplement, Fig S1 & S2]: Shows amplification only with nonzero warm-start bias and selection after perturbative lifting.
- **Break condition:** If the initial state has zero overlap with the dominant subspace (a₁=...=aᵣ=0), amplification cannot occur; output remains orthogonal.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Block-Encoding & Polynomial Transformation**
  - *Why needed here:* FSPA's abstract operation ρ|ϕ⟩ must be realized unitarily via a block-encoding of ρ. The adaptive schedule corresponds to applying a product of polynomial transforms (filter functions) built from these primitives. Normalization is then a non-unitary post-processing.
  - *Quick check:* If a block-encoding of ρ has error δ, how does this propagate through k sequential applications in the FSPA loop?

- **Concept: Power Iteration & Subspace Convergence**
  - *Why needed here:* FSPA is mathematically equivalent to normalized power iteration with an adaptive step count. Understanding its gap-limited convergence and subspace behavior in degeneracy is essential to interpreting its guarantees.
  - *Quick check:* For a symmetric matrix with eigenvalues [1, 0.99, 0.1], what is the expected limiting behavior of power iteration starting from a random vector?

- **Concept: Density Matrix as Covariance Operator**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper establishes that for amplitude-encoded centered data, the ensemble density matrix ρ = Σ pᵢ|ψᵢ⟩⟨ψᵢ| equals the covariance matrix. For uncentered data, it corresponds to uncentered PCA. This connects quantum state preparation to classical data encoding.
  - *Quick check:* Given a classical dataset {xᵢ}, how would you construct a quantum state |ψᵢ⟩ such that ρ matches the (centered) covariance matrix?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Encoder -> FSPA Controller -> Block-Encoding Module -> Normalization Unit -> Output Handler
- **Critical path:** Prepare initial state |ϕ₀⟩ (Data Encoder) → Normalize → FOR T rounds: (FOR β times: [Apply block-encoded U; Normalize]; β ← 2β) → Output final |ϕ⟩ (Output Handler)
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Normalization cost vs. robustness: Perfect renormalization ensures magnitude invariance but may require amplitude amplification, adding overhead.
  - Warm-start sensitivity: Requires nonzero overlap with dominant subspace; data encoding must ensure this.
  - Adaptive schedule overhead: Doubling β causes geometric growth in inner-loop length; total queries still gap-limited but constant factors matter.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Output remains sensitive to uniform eigenvalue scaling → normalization faulty.
  - Rapid fidelity loss with small gap → insufficient total applications or block-encoding errors.
  - No convergence despite large gap → zero initial overlap or non-Hermitian ρ implementation.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Magnitude Invariance Test:** Construct ρ from a dataset, scale eigenvalues by c=0.01, 0.1, 1, 10. Run FSPA with fixed random seed and compare final dominant-subspace fidelity across scales; should be identical within statistical error.
  2. **Gap-Dependent Scaling:** For synthetic ρ with fixed λ₁=1, vary λ₂ to set gaps log(λ₁/λ₂) ∈ [0.1, 1.0]. Measure oracle calls to reach fidelity 1-10⁻⁴. Verify linear trend of calls vs. 1/log(λ₁/λ₂).
  3. **Degenerate Subspace Handling:** Build ρ with λ₁=λ₂=1, λ₃=0.5. Initialize |ϕ₀⟩ with equal overlap on top two eigenvectors. Run FSPA; final state should have high fidelity with span of top two but not a specific eigenvector. Add tiny PSD perturbation to ρ inside that subspace and verify direction selection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can FSPA's gap-dependent convergence be improved beyond classical power iteration via alternative polynomial filters (e.g., higher-degree QSVT transformations) while retaining magnitude invariance?** The paper does not explore optimized spectral filters beyond the adaptive doubling schedule.
2. **What is the concrete query overhead of normalization (post-selection/amplitude amplification) in a block-encoding model, and how does it affect FSPA's overall resource scaling?** Theoretical analysis ignores normalization cost; practical success probabilities and amplification overheads may dominate in small-gap regimes.
3. **How does FSPA scale to high-dimensional, real-world datasets with complex spectral structures (e.g., heavy-tailed eigenvalues), and does its projection quality translate to stable performance in end-to-end quantum ML tasks?** Demonstrations are limited to Breast Cancer Wisconsin and Digits datasets; scalability to thousands of dimensions and integration into full learning pipelines remain open.
4. **What is FSPA's robustness under realistic noise models (e.g., depolarizing, amplitude damping) and finite-precision block-encodings?** Analysis and experiments assume ideal operations; no discussion of noise resilience despite NISQ relevance.

## Limitations
- Block-encoding error propagation and normalization precision not analyzed.
- Warm-start overlap requirement lacks practical preparation cost.
- Downstream experiments limited to small tabular datasets; high-dimensional scalability open.

## Confidence
- Magnitude invariance (uniform scaling immunity): **Medium** – Theoretical guarantee assumes perfect renormalization; real implementations may suffer from norm-estimation errors.
- Gap-limited convergence rate: **High** – Strong theoretical bound O(log(1/ε)/log(λ₁/λ₂)) confirmed by numerical gap-scaling experiments.
- Subspace convergence in near-degeneracy: **Medium** – Consistent with power iteration theory, but claim of “no artificial symmetry breaking” needs more empirical validation beyond presented perturbations.

## Next Checks
1. **Noisy block-encoding:** Introduce a tunable error δ in the unitary that block-encodes ρ; measure final subspace fidelity as a function of δ and gap size to bound robustness.
2. **Extreme-gap stress test:** Construct synthetic ρ with gaps log(λ₁/λ₂) ∈ [1e‑3, 1e‑2]; verify observed query complexity scales as 1/log(λ₁/λ₂) over this range.
3. **High-dimensional trial:** Apply FSPA to Fashion-MNIST (784-pixel vectors) after random projection to 100 dimensions; compare subspace quality and downstream classification accuracy against classical PCA.
