# Context Selection for Hypothesis and Statistical Evidence Extraction from Full-Text Scientific Articles
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.21193
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21193
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Targeted context selection improves hypothesis extraction (best non-oracle F1 ~0.50 vs full-text ~0.39) and reveals an extractor bottleneck for evidence (oracle F1 0.47-0.55).

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of automatically extracting hypotheses and supporting statistical evidence from full scientific articles, where arguments are often distributed across lengthy documents. The authors propose a two-stage retrieve-and-extract pipeline: first, given the abstract's finding, retrieve candidate paragraphs to extract the hypothesis; then use the hypothesis (plus abstract) to retrieve paragraphs for evidence extraction. Through experiments with four LLMs, they find that targeted context selection significantly improves hypothesis extraction (up to ~0.50 F1 vs. ~0.39 for full-text), especially when retrieval quality is enhanced via reranking or fine-tuning. However, evidence extraction shows only modest gains and plateaus at ~0.47-0.55 F1 even with oracle contexts, indicating that extractors struggle with hybrid numeric-textual statements. The study highlights both the promise of retrieval augmentation for hypothesis extraction and the need for specialized methods to handle structured statistical content.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate their pipeline on a dataset of 2,257 full-text articles from the social and behavioral sciences, where each article is preprocessed into prose-only paragraphs (excluding tables, figures, equations, abstract, bibliography). Given an abstract finding, Stage 1 uses a SciBERT-based retriever (or a fine-tuned variant) to fetch top-k paragraphs; an LLM then extracts the hypothesis. Stage 2 forms a query from the abstract finding and the extracted hypothesis, retrieves another set of paragraphs, and an LLM extracts evidence components (variables, relationships, confidence intervals, test family, sample size). They compare context quantities (k=5,10,20), retrieval quality enhancements (RankGPT reranker, fine-tuned retriever), and oracle settings where gold paragraphs are provided. Evaluation uses semantic-match F1 (cosine similarity ≥0.89) for hypotheses and a custom component-level metric for evidence, across four LLMs (llama-3.2, gpt-oss, gpt-4o-mini, gemini-2.5-flash).

## Key Results
- Hypothesis extraction: best non-oracle F1 ~0.50 with retrieval-based context, outperforming full-text F1 ~0.39; oracle F1 reaches 0.73-0.81.
- Evidence extraction: improvements from better retrieval are modest; even with oracle paragraphs, F1 only reaches 0.47-0.55 across models.
- Retrieval quality enhancements (reranking, fine-tuned retriever) yield consistent gains for hypothesis extraction, but evidence extraction remains limited by extractor handling of hybrid numeric-textual statements.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Targeted context selection reduces signal dilution for hypothesis extraction by filtering out topically related but rhetorically irrelevant paragraphs.
- **Mechanism**: Full-text inputs contain many paragraphs semantically similar to the abstract finding but not containing the hypothesis, confusing the LLM. Retrieved contexts increase the density of sentences relevant to the hypothesis.
- **Core assumption**: Hypothesis statements are paraphrased and distributed across sections, so semantic retrieval can identify candidate paragraphs with higher precision than using all text.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "full-text prompting underperform[s] retrieval-based contexts" and "signal-dilution effect in full papers".
  - [section]: Section 6.1: "full-text inputs underperform even the largest retrieved contexts" and Section 6.2 on context cleanliness.
  - [corpus]: Corpus evidence is weak; the provided corpus papers are not cited in the study, so no direct support.
- **Break condition**: If hypotheses are verbatim and highly localized, full-text might suffice, but this is atypical in scientific articles where arguments are distributed.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Improving retrieval quality via reranking and fine-tuning enhances context cleanliness, leading to consistent gains in hypothesis extraction.
- **Mechanism**: Standard dense retrieval may retrieve paragraphs with high topical similarity but wrong rhetorical role. Reranking (e.g., with RankGPT) and fine-tuned retrievers better distinguish rhetorical functions, selecting paragraphs that actually contain hypothesis statements.
- **Core assumption**: Rhetorical roles can be encoded in embeddings or learned with task-specific training on hard negatives.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "gains concentrated in configurations that optimize retrieval quality and context cleanliness."
  - [section]: Section 6.2 shows improved F1 with reranker and fine-tuned retriever; Section 4.2 describes hard-negative training.
  - [corpus]: Corpus includes papers on reranking for scientific retrieval (e.g., CoRank), but not directly referenced; evidence is indirect.
- **Break condition**: If fine-tuning data is insufficient or reranker introduces bias, gains may not materialize or could regress for some papers.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Evidence extraction is bottlenecked by extractor limitations in handling hybrid numeric-textual statements, not solely by retrieval failures.
- **Mechanism**: Statistical evidence combines text with numerical values (e.g., p-values, coefficients), and current LLMs struggle to extract these precisely even when the correct paragraph is provided (oracle setting).
- **Core assumption**: LLMs require specialized architectures or training to preserve numeric fidelity and variable binding in structured outputs.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Even with oracle paragraphs, performance remains moderate, indicating persistent extractor limitations."
  - [section]: Section 6.3, oracle Evidence F1 0.47-0.55, and discussion on extractor fidelity for structured statistical content.
  - [corpus]: Corpus has papers on schema-constrained extraction (e.g., From Chaos to Clarity), but not cited; evidence is not from the paper.
- **Break condition**: If extractors are adapted for structured output (e.g., via JSON schemas or hybrid parsing), oracle performance might increase, reducing the gap.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Abstract Finding → Stage 1 Retriever (SciBERT/fine-tuned bi-encoder with optional RankGPT) → Hypothesis Extractor (LLM) → Composite Query (abstract finding + hypothesis) → Stage 2 Retriever → Evidence Extractor (LLM)

**Critical path**: Abstract Finding → Stage 1 retrieval → Hypothesis extraction → Composite query construction → Stage 2 retrieval → Evidence extraction. Errors propagate from Stage 1 hypothesis errors to Stage 2 evidence extraction.

**Design tradeoffs**: Larger k increases recall but adds noise; reranking improves precision but adds computational cost; fine-tuned retriever requires labeled data. Sequential pipeline simplifies debugging but risks error propagation; joint extraction might reduce propagation but increase complexity.

**Failure signatures**:
- Hypothesis F1 low despite high retrieval recall indicates extractor issues.
- Evidence oracle F1 moderate (0.47-0.55) shows intrinsic extractor limits for numeric-text.
- Per-paper transition analysis (Appendix Figure 8) reveals when gold paragraphs are lost or gained due to retrieval changes.

**First 3 experiments**:

3. Isolate retrieval quality: Compare Standard RAG k=5 → RAG+Reranker k=5 → Fine-tuned+Reranker k=5 to measure gains from improved context cleanliness, using the same extractor.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: To what extent do the observed benefits of targeted context selection for hypothesis extraction and the persistent challenges in evidence extraction generalize to scientific domains beyond social and behavioral sciences, such as biomedicine or physics, where rhetorical structures and statistical reporting conventions differ significantly?  
  *Basis in paper*: [explicit] "Future work may extend this analysis beyond SBS articles..." (Conclusion) and "our experiments are limited to claim traces extracted from empirical research in the SBS disciplines" (Limitations).  
  *Why unresolved*: The study only evaluates on SBS articles; disciplinary variations in hypothesis phrasing, evidence presentation (e.g., more tables in biomedicine), and argument structure could alter retrieval and extraction dynamics.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Replicate the controlled experiments on diverse full-text datasets from other domains (e.g., PubMed Central for biomedicine, arXiv for physics), comparing performance trends and bottleneck patterns to those reported in the paper.

- **Open Question 2**: Can extraction approaches that enforce structured output schemas or combine neural retrieval with deterministic parsing of numerical fields significantly improve statistical evidence extraction accuracy beyond general LLM-based methods?  
  *Basis in paper*: [explicit] "improving this stage may require extraction methods that more strongly enforce structure (e.g., schema-constrained outputs) or hybrid approaches that combine retrieval with deterministic parsing for quantities..." (Discussion).  
  *Why unresolved*: The paper only tests vanilla LLM extractors; structured or hybrid methods were proposed but not implemented or evaluated, leaving a gap in understanding whether they can overcome the moderate oracle ceiling.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Implement schema-constrained prompting (e.g., JSON output with required fields) or hybrid systems that use regex/template parsers for numeric values within the same two-stage pipeline, then compare evidence F1 against the baseline LLM extractors in both standard and oracle settings.

- **Open Question 3**: How can the sequential two-stage pipeline be extended to extract multiple, distinct hypothesis-evidence pairs from a single paper, given that most articles contain several testable claims and supporting results?  
  *Basis in paper*: [inferred] From Limitations: "Our dataset contains a single annotated claim trace per paper and is not exhaustive, as papers often contain multiple hypotheses and supporting results." The pipeline is designed for one primary claim trace.  
  *Why unresolved*: The current pipeline assumes one hypothesis per paper and processes it sequentially; handling multiple claims would require iterative retrieval, potential cross-reference resolution, and a strategy to avoid duplication or conflict.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Annotate or acquire a dataset with exhaustive claim traces per paper, modify the pipeline to either (a) iteratively extract and mask extracted spans, or (b) jointly retrieve and extract multiple hypotheses/evidence, then evaluate exhaustiveness and precision-recall trade-offs.

- **Open Question 4**: What is the impact of excluding tables, figures, and equations on evidence extraction performance, and how can these non-prose elements be effectively integrated into the retrieval and extraction pipeline?  
  *Basis in paper*: [inferred] From Preprocessing: "Our preprocessing uses prose-only paragraphs and excludes tables, figures (and captions), and equations."  
  *Why unresolved*: Tables, figures, and equations often contain critical statistical results; their exclusion may underrepresent evidence and limit retrieval recall.  
  *What evidence would resolve it*: Incorporate structured representations (e.g., CSV extracts from tables, parsed equations) into the document index, adapt the retriever to handle multimodal embeddings, and re-evaluate evidence extraction with oracle access to all content modalities.

## Limitations
- The study uses a proprietary dataset (2,257 SBS articles) that is not public, preventing full end-to-end replication.
- Critical implementation details—such as the fine-tuned retriever’s training regimen (hard-negative selection, multi-task loss) and the exact RankGPT prompt—are omitted, blocking exact recreation of the best-performing configurations.
- The evidence extraction metric is custom and may not align with human judgments.

## Confidence
- **Signal dilution mitigation via context selection**: Medium
- **Retrieval quality improvements (reranking, fine‑tuning)**: Medium
- **Extractor bottleneck in hybrid numeric‑textual evidence**: High
