# Adaptive Conditional Forest Sampling for Spectral Risk Optimisation under Decision-Dependent Uncertainty

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.12507
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12507
*   **Reference Count:** 19
*   **Primary Result:** ACFS achieves the lowest median oracle spectral risk on one benchmark (6.0–20.0% gap vs. GP-BO) and matches GP-BO medians on another while reducing cross-replication dispersion by 1.7–2.0×.

## Executive Summary
Optimising spectral risk—a combination of expected cost and Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR)—is challenging when the uncertain parameter distribution depends on the decision itself. The authors introduce Adaptive Conditional Forest Sampling (ACFS), a four-phase framework combining Generalised Random Forests for conditional sampling, Cross-Entropy warm-starts, Differential Evolution for global search, and a two-stage surrogate-to-oracle reranking followed by variance-reduced gradient refinement. Evaluated on two synthetic decision-dependent benchmarks across 100 replications, ACFS consistently delivers lower median spectral risk and reduced solution dispersion compared to state-of-the-art baselines like GP-BO, CEM-SO, SGD-CVaR, and KDE-SO.

## Method Summary
ACFS is a four-phase framework for decision-dependent spectral risk optimisation. It begins with **Generalised Random Forests** to model and sample from the decision-dependent conditional distribution. These samples seed a **Cross-Entropy method** to generate a diverse initial population. A **Differential Evolution** algorithm then explores the decision space globally, using rank-weighted augmentation to improve tail estimates. The top candidates undergo a **two-stage reranking**: first via a surrogate model trained on the acquired samples, then by evaluating a subset with the true (expensive) oracle. Finally, a **multi-start gradient refinement** with variance reduction polishes the best solutions. This pipeline integrates conditional nonparametric modelling, stochastic optimisation, and careful oracle budgeting to balance exploration, tail risk estimation, and computational cost.

## Key Results
*   On the Gaussian copula benchmark, ACFS achieves the lowest median oracle spectral risk in all penalty weight settings, outperforming GP-BO by 6.0–20.0%.
*   On the Student-t copula benchmark, ACFS matches GP-BO’s median spectral risk but reduces cross-replication dispersion by 1.8–1.9× (DGP1) and 1.7–2.0× (DGP2).
*   ACFS outperforms CEM-SO, SGD-CVaR, and KDE-SO almost universally across both benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ACFS addresses the core challenge of decision-dependent uncertainty by explicitly modelling the conditional distribution of uncertainties given decisions via Generalised Random Forests. This allows for targeted sampling in regions of the decision space where tail risk (CVaR) is higher. The Cross-Entropy warm-start provides a high-quality initial population, avoiding random starts that waste oracle evaluations. Differential Evolution offers robust global exploration without gradient assumptions, crucial for non-convex, black-box spectral risk landscapes. The rank-weighted augmentation specifically enriches the population with samples relevant to tail-risk estimation. The two-stage surrogate-to-oracle reranking efficiently filters candidates before expensive oracle calls, and the final variance-reduced gradient refinement exploits local curvature for precise tuning. This staged approach balances global search, tail estimation, and computational economy.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Spectral Risk Measure:** Combines expectation and CVaR to quantify risk; needed to model both average and tail losses. *Quick check:* Compute spectral risk for a discrete distribution given a risk averse parameter.
*   **Decision-Dependent Uncertainty:** The distribution of uncertain parameters shifts as decisions change; requires conditional modelling. *Quick check:* Simulate a simple case where variance changes with decision value.
*   **Generalised Random Forests (GRF):** Nonparametric method for conditional mean/quantile estimation; enables sampling from complex conditional distributions. *Quick check:* Fit a GRF to predict a continuous target given features and generate conditional samples.
*   **Cross-Entropy (CE) Method:** Rare-event simulation technique for generating good candidate solutions; used here for warm-start. *Quick check:* Apply CE to sample low-quantile outcomes of a known distribution.
*   **Differential Evolution (DE):** Population-based, gradient-free optimiser for continuous, non-convex problems; provides global exploration. *Quick check:* Optimise the Rastrigin function with DE.
*   **Surrogate Modelling & Oracle Budgeting:** Uses cheap approximations to screen candidates before costly true evaluations; critical for expensive risk metrics. *Quick check:* Train a random forest surrogate on sampled points and predict on new points.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Conditional Distribution Modelling (Generalised Random Forests) -> Initial Population (Cross-Entropy Warm-start) -> Global Search (Differential Evolution) -> Candidate Refinement (Surrogate-to-oracle Reranking) -> Final Polish (Variance-reduced Gradient Refinement)

**Critical Path:**  
1. Fit GRF on initial decision-uncertainty data.  
2. Sample conditionally from GRF to seed CE method.  
3. Run DE to evolve population, using rank-weighted augmentation.  
4. Train surrogate on all evaluated points, rerank population.  
5. Select top-k for oracle reevaluation based on surrogate ranking.  
6. Multi-start gradient descent from best oracle-evaluated points with variance reduction.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
*   **Complexity vs. Robustness:** Integrating four sophisticated phases increases implementation and tuning burden but yields robustness across penalty weights and problem types.  
*   **Oracle Calls vs. Convergence:** The surrogate-oracle reranking trades off additional model error for fewer expensive evaluations; the MC budget hyperparameter critically controls this balance.  
*   **Generality vs. Efficiency:** GRFs handle arbitrary conditional distributions but are computationally heavier than parametric assumptions; acceptable given offline optimisation context.

**Failure Signatures:**  
*   High variance in spectral risk estimates across replications indicates unstable conditional modelling or insufficient sampling.  
*   Poor performance on tail risk (high CVaR) suggests rank-weighted augmentation or surrogate reranking is failing to capture extreme outcomes.  
*   Sensitivity to MC budget (as found) implies the method relies heavily on sufficient oracle evaluations at the reranking stage.

**First Three Experiments:**  
1. **Validate GRF conditional sampling accuracy:** On a synthetic decision-dependent distribution, compare samples from fitted GRF against true conditional quantiles using a probability integral transform test.  
2. **Ablate warm-start:** Run ACFS with and without Cross-Entropy seeding, measuring convergence speed and final spectral risk over 20 replications on a medium-dimensional benchmark.  
3. **Test surrogate reranking efficacy:** Track how often the surrogate’s top-k candidates improve after oracle reevaluation; a low improvement rate indicates surrogate miscalibration.

## Limitations
*   **Validation Scope:** Evaluation is limited to two synthetic copula-based benchmarks; performance on real-world problems with different noise structures, non-stationarity, or higher dimensions is unproven.
*   **Computational Overhead:** The integrated framework’s per-iteration cost and scalability to larger problem sizes are not detailed, despite ablation showing component contributions.
*   **Baseline Coverage:** Key comparators exclude newer decision-dependent optimisation algorithms (e.g., recent zeroth-order methods) that may offer different trade-offs.

## Confidence
*   **Performance on Provided Benchmarks:** Medium – Results are consistent across 100 replications but confined to specific synthetic settings that may be favourable.
*   **Component Contribution:** Medium-High – Ablation validates each phase adds value, but phase interactions in diverse problem settings remain unexplored.
*   **Robustness to Hyperparameters:** Medium – Sensitivity analysis shows robustness except for the direct-oracle MC budget, which is critical but singular.

## Next Checks
1. **Real-World Application Test:** Apply ACFS to a real decision-dependent optimisation problem (e.g., infrastructure planning under policy-dependent demand) and compare spectral risk and computational cost against the same baseline suite.
2. **Component Interaction Analysis:** On a more complex benchmark (e.g., >20 dimensions, heavy-tailed noise), conduct a full factorial ablation to identify synergistic or antagonistic interactions between the four ACFS phases.
3. **Updated Comparative Benchmark:** Replicate the study including recent decision-dependent optimisation algorithms from the literature (e.g., the cited zeroth-order method) as additional baselines, adhering to the same 100-replication protocol.
