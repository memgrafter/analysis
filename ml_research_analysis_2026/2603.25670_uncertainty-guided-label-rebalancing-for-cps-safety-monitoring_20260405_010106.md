# Uncertainty-Guided Label Rebalancing for CPS Safety Monitoring

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25670  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25670  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** U-Balance achieves an F1 score of 0.806 on a UAV safety monitoring benchmark, outperforming the strongest baseline by 14.3 percentage points under extreme class imbalance (46:1).

## Executive Summary
U-Balance addresses severe class imbalance in cyber-physical system (CPS) safety monitoring by introducing uncertainty-guided label rebalancing (uLNR). It first predicts a behavioral uncertainty score for each telemetry window using a GatedMLP on distributional kinematic features. High-uncertainty samples from the safe majority class are then probabilistically relabeled as unsafe, enriching the minority class without synthetic data generation. On a large UAV dataset, this approach yields a substantial gain in F1 score over standard rebalancing and other baselines, while maintaining efficient inference.

## Method Summary
The method operates in two main stages. First, an uncertainty predictor maps preprocessed distributional features (mean, std, min, max per kinematic channel over a window) to a scalar uncertainty score via a GatedMLP. Second, the uLNR module normalizes these scores relative to the safe class distribution, computes a flip probability using a shifted tanh function, and stochastically relabels safe windows with unusually high uncertainty as unsafe. The resulting rebalanced dataset trains a standard safety predictor (a BiLSTM) for final classification.

## Key Results
- Achieves **F1 = 0.806** on a UAV safety benchmark with a 46:1 safe-to-unsafe ratio.
- Outperforms the strongest baseline (standard rebalancing) by **14.3 percentage points**.
- Both the GatedMLP uncertainty predictor and the probabilistic uLNR mechanism contribute significantly to the improvement; ablations show large performance drops when either component is removed.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Uncertainty as a Proxy for Decision Boundary Proximion
- **Claim:** High behavioral uncertainty in safe-labeled windows indicates proximity to the safe/unsafe decision boundary, making these samples informative for the minority class.
- **Mechanism:** The GatedMLP produces an uncertainty score from distributional kinematic features. Safe samples with high z-scores (significantly above the safe distribution mean) are likely ambiguous boundary cases. uLNR flips these to unsafe, enriching the minority class with informative samples without synthetic generation.
- **Core assumption:** Behavioral uncertainty (e.g., erratic motion) correlates with safety outcomes and distinguishes "typical safe" from "uncertain safe" behavior.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes uLNR's probabilistic relabeling of high-uncertainty safe windows; [Section 2.1] links uncertainty to unsafe states and provides an illustrative figure.
- **Break condition:** If uncertainty scores show no significant correlation with true safety labels, the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 2: Probabilistic Relabeling to Mitigate Label Noise Introduction
- **Claim:** Stochastic label flipping via uLRN, controlled by a z-score-derived probability, adds minority samples without overwhelming the model with noisy labels.
- **Mechanism:** uLNR converts a window's uncertainty z-score into a flip probability using a shifted tanh function. Only safe windows above a threshold τ are eligible. This ensures: (1) deep safe samples remain unchanged, (2) highly uncertain safe samples flip with high probability, (3) the amount of relabeling is tunable via τ. Stochasticity avoids deterministic mislabeling of entire high-variance regions.
- **Core assumption:** The uncertainty predictor is accurate enough to produce meaningful z-scores, and the safe class contains a distinct subpopulation of "certainly safe" samples.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions enriching minority class without synthetic data; [Section 3.2] provides the flip probability equation; [Table 4] shows τ-tuning trade-offs (recall vs. precision).
- **Break condition:** If the uncertainty predictor is poorly calibrated, relabeling could introduce harmful label noise.

### Mechanism 3: Distributional Feature Preprocessing + GatedMLP for Robust Uncertainty Estimation
- **Claim:** Summarizing raw telemetry into distributional features and processing with a GatedMLP yields a more discriminative uncertainty score than raw sequence modeling or a plain MLP.
- **Mechanism:** Distributional features (mean, std, min, max) capture temporal variability in a fixed-size vector. The GatedMLP's element-wise gate modulates these features based on relevance to uncertainty. Residual fusion (`g ⊙ h + (1-g)⊙d`) retains original information while allowing non-linear interactions.
- **Core assumption:** Uncertainty manifests in statistical properties of kinematic signals over a window, and these properties are more efficiently captured by summary statistics than by temporal dynamics.
- **Evidence anchors:** [Section 3.1] defines distributional features and shows the GatedMLP architecture; [Table 3] ablation shows ~10 pp F1 drop when removing preprocessing or the GatedMLP.
- **Break condition:** If uncertainty is primarily driven by fine temporal sequences (e.g., sudden spikes), distributional features would lose critical information.

## Foundational Learning
- **Extreme Class Imbalance in Safety-Critical CPS Data**  
  Why needed: A 46:1 safe-to-unsafe ratio biases standard models toward the majority class, yielding high precision but low recall—unacceptable for safety monitoring where missing unsafe states is critical.  
  Quick check: Why does class weighting often fail under extreme imbalance, and how does uLNR differ fundamentally?

- **Behavioral Uncertainty Quantification**  
  Why needed: U-Balance relies on an uncertainty predictor that estimates "doubt" from kinematic signals, requiring understanding of aleatoric/epistemic uncertainty and its manifestation in control signals (e.g., erratic heading changes).  
  Quick check: What specific telemetry patterns indicate high behavioral uncertainty in a UAV, and how does the GatedMLP aggregate them?

- **Label Noise Rebalancing (LNR) Adaptation**  
  Why needed: uLNR adapts LNR (originally for images) to time-series CPS data by flipping majority-class labels near the decision boundary without synthetic data. Understanding safe label flipping is crucial.  
  Quick check: How does uLNR's stochastic, uncertainty-guided flipping differ from deterministic relabeling or synthetic oversampling like SMOTE?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Raw Telemetry Window → Distributional Feature Extraction → GatedMLP → Uncertainty Score (û_t)  
  (û_t, Safe Label) → uLNR (z-score, tanh, Bernoulli) → Flipped Label → Rebalanced Dataset  
  Rebalanced Dataset → BiLSTM Safety Predictor → Unsafe Probability

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Train uncertainty predictor on (window, true unsafe label) to get û_t.  
  2. Apply uLNR to original (window, safe label) using û_t to produce rebalanced dataset with flipped labels.  
  3. Train safety predictor (BiLSTM) on rebalanced dataset to predict unsafe states.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Flip threshold τ:** Low τ → more flips → higher recall, lower precision (noisy labels). High τ → fewer flips → higher precision, lower recall (under-rebalancing). Requires validation tuning.
  - **GatedMLP vs. sequential raw modeling:** GatedMLP + distributional features is simpler/faster but may miss fine temporal patterns. Raw BiLSTM captures sequences but may overfit on imbalanced data.
  - **Modular vs. end-to-end:** Modular pipeline allows precomputed uncertainty scores but may propagate predictor errors; joint training is not explored.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Poor uncertainty-safety correlation:** Low point-biserial r_pb (<0.2) → uLNR becomes arbitrary; F1 gains vanish.
  - **Over-flipping (τ too low):** Many safe samples mislabeled unsafe → safety predictor learns high false positive rate; precision crashes.
  - **Under-flipping (τ too high):** Almost no relabeling → dataset remains imbalanced; recall stays low.
  - **GatedMLP overfitting:** Large train-validation uncertainty accuracy gap → unreliable z-scores → uLNR misidentifies boundary samples.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Correlation validation:** Compute point-biserial correlation between predicted û_t and true safety labels; expect moderate positive correlation (~0.44) significant at p<0.001.
  2. **uLNR strategy comparison:** Implement Early Fusion (concat û_t to input) and Late Fusion (concat to latent), train identical safety predictors, compare F1 to uLNR; hypothesis: uLNR >> fusion strategies.
  3. **Ablation of uncertainty predictor:** Replace GatedMLP with plain MLP and remove preprocessing (use raw sequence with BiLSTM). Measure drop in safety predictor F1 vs. full U-Balance; expect large drops confirming both components' contributions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Generalisability across CPS domains:** Does U-Balance work on other systems like autonomous submarines or industrial robots?
- **Alternative uncertainty estimators:** How do MC Dropout or Deep Ensembles compare to the GatedMLP predictor?
- **Automatic τ selection:** Can the flip threshold be set automatically rather than by manual grid search?
- **Hybrid rebalancing:** Does combining uLNR with other techniques (e.g., SMOTE, class weighting) yield additional gains?

## Limitations
- Evaluation on a single UAV dataset limits generalisability to other CPS domains.
- Lack of reported uncertainty calibration metrics (e.g., ECE) raises concerns about whether high uncertainty truly marks decision-boundary samples.
- No statistical significance tests for the reported 14.3 pp F1 gain; sensitivity analyses beyond τ are limited.

## Confidence
- **Overall performance (F1 0.806, +14.3 pp):** *Medium* (single dataset, no variance/significance)
- **Uncertainty as decision-boundary proxy:** *Medium* (point-biserial correlation not provided)
- **Probabilistic relabeling mitigates label noise:** *Medium* (stochastic vs. deterministic not directly compared)
- **Distributional + GatedMLP uncertainty estimator:** *High* (ablation shows ~10 pp drop when removed)

## Next Checks
