# RE-SAC: Disentangling aleatoric and epistemic risks in bus fleet control: A stable and robust ensemble DRL approach

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.18396  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18396  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** RE-SAC achieves the highest and most stable cumulative reward in bus holding control by disentangling aleatoric (irreducible) and epistemic (data insufficiency) uncertainties, reducing worst-case Q-value estimation error by up to 62% in rare states.

## Executive Summary
Bus holding control via deep reinforcement learning suffers from Q-value instability due to conflated aleatoric (traffic/passenger noise) and epistemic (data scarcity) uncertainties, leading to catastrophic value collapse. RE-SAC resolves this by disentangling the two risks: an IPM-based weight regularization on the critic hedges against aleatoric noise, providing a smooth analytical lower bound for the robust Bellman operator, while a diversified Q-ensemble penalizes overconfident estimates in sparsely visited states. In a high-fidelity simulation of a stochastic bus corridor, RE-SAC outperforms strong baselines (SAC, DSAC, BAC) and ablations in cumulative reward and worst-case robustness under high traffic variability.

## Method Summary
RE-SAC is an ensemble extension of Soft Actor-Critic designed for robust control under two distinct uncertainty types. For aleatoric risk (irreducible noise), it adds an ℓ₁-norm penalty (λₐₗₑ) to the critic loss on weights frozen during the Bellman backup, implementing an IPM-based regularization that yields a smooth lower bound for the robust Bellman operator without expensive perturbations. For epistemic risk (data insufficiency), it employs a diversified Q-ensemble (K=10) and penalizes ensemble variance (λₑₚᵢ) in the Bellman target. The actor adopts a lower-confidence-bound objective using the ensemble mean minus a scaled standard deviation term (βₗc𝑏·σ_ens). Additional out-of-distribution regularization (βₒₒ𝑑·σ_ens) is added to the critic loss. Target networks maintain stable epistemic variance estimates, and the overall design preserves γ-contraction.

## Key Results
- Cumulative reward stabilized near ~ -0.4×10⁶, substantially higher than vanilla SAC (-0.55×10⁶) and the epistemic-only ablation (-1.2×10⁶).
- Oracle Q-value estimation error (MAE) reduced by up to 62% in rare out-of-distribution states (average 1647 vs. SAC’s 4343), demonstrating superior worst-case robustness.
- Q-value estimates remained stable (tight mean ± 2σ bands) across training, while baselines exhibited catastrophic collapse in high-variance scenarios.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Value collapse occurs when epistemic uncertainty (from sparse data) is mistaken for aleatoric noise, causing over-pessimism, or when aleatoric noise corrupts Q-estimates without regularization. RE-SAC’s disentanglement prevents both: the IPM-based ℓ₁ penalty on frozen critic weights smooths the robust Bellman operator analytically, hedging against irreducible noise without breaking contraction; the ensemble variance penalty targets epistemic overconfidence specifically by discouraging high-variance estimates in sparsely visited states. The actor’s LCB objective then leverages the ensemble’s epistemic uncertainty estimate for cautious exploration. This separation ensures that aleatoric and epistemic risks are mitigated by appropriate mechanisms, maintaining stability and improving robustness, especially in rare high-traffic states where estimation errors are most severe.

## Foundational Learning
- **Aleatoric vs. epistemic uncertainty:** Needed to distinguish irreducible environment noise from model insufficiency; quick check: simulate a simple MDP with known stochastic transitions and verify that variance from inherent randomness persists despite more data (aleatoric) vs. variance that diminishes with data (epistemic).
- **Integrated Prediction Uncertainty (IPM):** Provides a smooth lower bound for robust Bellman operators via weight regularization; quick check: implement a critic with ℓ₁ penalty on frozen weights and verify monotonic improvement in worst-case return estimates under noisy rewards.
- **Q-ensemble for epistemic uncertainty:** Diversifies estimates to capture model uncertainty; quick check: train a standard ensemble (without variance penalty) and observe that variance correlates with state visitation frequency.
- **Soft Actor-Critic (SAC):** Off-policy actor-critic with entropy regularization; quick check: re-implement vanilla SAC on a standard control task (e.g., HalfCheetah) to match published performance.
- **Mahalanobis distance for state rareness:** Quantifies how far a state deviates from the training distribution; quick check: fit a Gaussian on a subset of states, compute distances, and verify that distant states have higher prediction error.
- **Contraction in robust RL:** Guarantees convergence of the Bellman operator; quick check: prove that with frozen critic weights and target networks, the modified Bellman backup remains a γ-contraction.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** State (headways, speed, categorical IDs) -> Categorical embeddings (Stop, Bus, Direction, Time) -> Concatenated state vector -> Actor network (policy) and Critic ensemble (K=10, each a 4-layer MLP, 64 units) -> Action (holding time). Critic ensemble outputs feed into target networks (slow updates, τ=0.01). Actor loss: α log π - (ensemble mean - βₗc𝑏·ensemble std). Critic loss: MSE + λₐₗₑ·∑∥W∥₁ (from frozen weights) + βₒₒ𝑑·σ_ens.

**Critical path:** State encoding → Critic ensemble forward pass → Compute target y = r + γ(Mean(Q_target) - λₑₚᵢ·Var(Q_target)) → Update each critic with ℓ₁ reg + OOD penalty → Actor update using LCB → Soft update target networks.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Ensemble size (K=10):** Larger K better captures epistemic uncertainty but increases compute; K=10 balances diversity and cost.  
- **Frozen critic weights for aleatoric penalty:** Ensures IPM smoothness and contraction but may slow adaptation to aleatoric shifts; vs. online weights which break theoretical guarantees.  
- **λₐₗₑ/λₑₚᵢ coefficients:** Higher values increase robustness but risk over-pessimism; fixed values may not adapt to changing traffic regimes.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Epistemic-only ablation:** Q-values collapse sharply in high-noise states (as in Fig. 2), reward plummets to -1.2e6.  
- **Aleatoric penalty misapplied:** Using online critic weights for ℓ₁ penalty breaks contraction; symptoms: unstable training, diverging Q-values.  
- **Excessive OOD penalty:** Policy becomes overly conservative, holding buses too long even in low-traffic states, reducing throughput.

**First experiments:**  
1. Rebuild simulation from description and [20]; train vanilla SAC and confirm Q-value instability (wide mean±2σ bands).  
2. Implement RE-SAC exactly: K=10 ensemble, frozen critic weights during backup, λₐₗₑ=0.01, λₑₚᵢ=0.005, βₗc𝑏=-2. Train 500 episodes; plot cumulative reward and Q mean±2σ; verify stability gap vs. SAC.  
3. Replicate Mahalanobis rareness analysis: fit 2D Gaussian per Stop×Direction on headways, bin test states, compute Oracle MAE per bin; confirm RE-SAC’s flatter error curve and lowest error in rarest bin.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

3. Does integrating distributional reinforcement learning with RE-SAC’s disentangled robustness framework enhance tail-risk management and overall policy performance in stochastic bus control?

## Limitations
- Simulation specifics (passenger arrival rates, traffic speed distributions, dwell time model, ridge reward coefficients) are not fully detailed; must be assumed from [20] or reconstructed.
- Categorical embedding dimensions depend on unknown cardinalities of StopIDs, BusIDs, etc.; only the formula d=min(50, ⌊Nᵢ/2⌋) is given.
- Critical implementation details missing: exact timing of the OOD penalty βₒₒ𝑑·σ_ens (e.g., whether applied per critic or ensemble-wide) and precise enforcement of frozen weights during the Bellman sweep.
- No open-source code; replication depends on accurate interpretation of algorithmic descriptions.

## Confidence
- **Disentanglement & Q-value stability:** Medium – theoretical guarantee relies on frozen critic weights and target networks; any deviation invalidates contraction.
- **Cumulative reward improvement:** Medium – single high-fidelity environment; performance may not transfer to other transit topologies or simulation parameters.
- **Oracle Q error reduction in rare states:** Medium – analysis is clear but depends on correct Mahalanobis binning and unbiased Oracle estimation.
