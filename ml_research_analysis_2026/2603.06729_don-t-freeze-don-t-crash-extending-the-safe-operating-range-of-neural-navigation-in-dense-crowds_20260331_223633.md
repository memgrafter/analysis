# Don't Freeze, Don't Crash: Extending the Safe Operating Range of Neural Navigation in Dense Crowds

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.06729
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06729
- **Reference count:** 24
- **Primary result:** Extends safe navigation to 31% denser crowds (N=21) with >99% goal-reaching and 86% collision-free success, maintaining a >60 percentage-point safety margin over learning-based benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Learning-based robot navigation in crowds fails at higher densities due to observation distribution shift (from running normalizers) and conservative freezing (from unchecked social penalties). This paper introduces PSS-Social, which combines a density-invariant observation encoder—using distance-sorted K-nearest neighbors with fixed-cap truncation and bounded crowd-summary features—with density-randomized training and a potential-based proxemic reward featuring density-adaptive scaling. Trained on 11–16 pedestrians in a 3m×3m arena and tested up to 21 pedestrians, it achieves robust goal completion and safety, markedly outperforming baselines.

## Method Summary
The method stabilizes the robot’s observation distribution across crowd densities by encoding neighbors in a fixed-slot format: the `K_cap` nearest pedestrians are placed in distance-sorted slots, and all further pedestrians are represented by bounded summary scalars (e.g., crowd pressure, zone occupancy). This ensures that the active input dimensions and their normalized statistics remain constant during training (with crowd sizes sampled uniformly from [11,16]) and testing, even when test-time density exceeds `K_cap`. The reward combines standard extrinsic terms with an intrinsic potential-based shaping term that penalizes invasive motions via proxemic zones; this social penalty is scaled down by local neighbor count to prevent it from overwhelming the goal reward in dense crowds. A PPO agent with a simple MLP policy is trained for 12M timesteps with this encoding and shaping.

## Key Results
- >99% goal-reaching success at up to 21 pedestrians (N=21).
- 86% collision-free success at N=21, a >60 percentage-point margin over learning-based benchmarks.
- Significantly less freezing than analytical solvers, with graceful degradation as density increases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Density-Invariant Observation Encoding
**Claim:** Stabilizing the observation distribution across varying crowd densities prevents the policy from encountering out-of-distribution inputs that cause collisions.  
**Mechanism:** The encoder uses a fixed-slot budget (`K_max`). Only the `K_cap` nearest pedestrians occupy distance-sorted slots; further pedestrians are represented by bounded crowd-summary scalars (e.g., pressure, alignment, zone occupancy). This keeps per-slot semantics and overall feature statistics constant as crowd size `N` changes, even when `N > K_cap`.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "encoding represents the distance-sorted K nearest pedestrians plus bounded crowd-summary features, keeping input statistics stable as crowd size grows." [section III.B] details `o_knn` and `o_sum` and the goal of preventing "previously padded dimensions from activating."  
**Break condition:** If many more than `K_cap` pedestrians are within the interaction radius, the local crowd structure may change qualitatively (new critical neighbors emerge between sorted slots), and summaries may fail to capture necessary constraints, leading to collision.

### Mechanism 2: Density-Adaptive Proxemic Reward Shaping
**Claim:** A potential-based shaping reward, scaled down by local crowd density, provides a smooth social gradient that discourages invasive motions without overwhelming the goal-directed reward in dense crowds, preventing both collisions and freezing.  
**Mechanism:** Shaping potential `Φ(s_t)` combines Euclidean goal progress with scaled proxemic costs (`C_I`, `C_P`). The density-adaptive factor `η_t = η(n_t(r_s))` is a non-increasing function of local neighbor count `n_t(r_s)`, down-weighting proxemic penalties as more pedestrians occupy social zones.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "potential-based proxemic reward shaping featuring density-adaptive scaling." [section III.C] defines `η_t` and states it "prevents proxemic penalties from growing simply because more neighbors exist." Ablation (Table III) shows "PSS only" underperforms "PSS-Social" at N=21.  
**Break condition:** If `η(·)` decays too fast, shaping becomes negligible in dense scenes (risk collisions); if too weak, shaping remains too strong (risk freezing).

### Mechanism 3: Joint Action-Space Stabilization via Density-Randomized Training
**Claim:** Training the policy with uniformly sampled crowd sizes `N ∈ [11,16]` exposes the policy and normalizer to varying active-slot counts, making the observation statistics and learned policy robust to the boundary where `N` exceeds `K_cap`.  
**Mechanism:** During PPO training, each environment reset samples a random `N`. This ensures the running normalizer (`VecNormalize`) observes episodes with different numbers of active neighbor slots (`K_t = min(N, K_cap)`). The policy learns to map the same normalized slot pattern to safe actions regardless of whether padding was due to low `N` (true padding) or high `N` (truncation).  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "combined with density-randomized training..." [section III.B] explains the sampling and its role. Ablation in Table IV ("No K-cap + Sort") fails catastrophically at N=17, showing randomization only works when combined with K-cap truncation.  
**Break condition:** If the training density interval does not include values where `N > K_cap`, the normalizer and policy will not experience the truncation regime, and OOD density generalization will fail.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Proxemics (Hall's Interpersonal Distance Theory)**  
  *Why needed here:* The reward shaping is grounded in proxemic zones (intimate, personal) to define socially invasive behavior. These zones define `C_I` and `C_P` and motivate the adaptive scaling rationale.  
  *Quick check:* Why might a fixed proxemic penalty be problematic in a crowd where 10 people stand within 0.5m of the robot?

- **Concept: Policy Invariance under Reward Transformations (Potential-Based Reward Shaping)**  
  *Why needed here:* The paper uses `r_shaping = γΦ(s_{t+1}) - Φ(s_t)` to add a dense social gradient without altering the optimal policy in a fully observed MDP. The paper acknowledges this guarantee does not hold in their POMDP but uses it as a training aid.  
  *Quick check:* In a POMDP, why does potential-based shaping not guarantee policy invariance, and what does that mean for this method's training?

- **Concept: Distribution Shift in Normalization Layers**  
  *Why needed here:* The core failure mode for learning-based methods is that `VecNormalize` running statistics become invalid when the number of active input features changes (e.g., more active neighbor slots at test time). This corrupts the policy's inputs.  
  *Quick check:* If you train an agent with observations of shape `(K_max,)` where only the first 5 slots are ever active, what happens if you test with 8 active slots and the normalizer was never updated for that pattern?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  `Simulator State (s_t)` → `Encoder (ψ)` → `Normalized Observation (o_t)` → `MLP Policy (π_θ)` → `Action (a_t)`.  
  Training adds: `Next State (s_{t+1})` → `Reward Calc (Φ)` → `r_t` → `PPO Update`.  
  1. **State-to-Observation Encoder (`ψ(s_t)`):** Takes full state (ego pos/vel, all pedestrian pos/vel, goal). Outputs fixed-dim `o_t = [o_ego; o_knn; o_sum]`.  
     - `o_ego`: Ego velocity, position, goal vector/distance.  
     - `o_knn`: `K_max` slots. For `k <= K_t = min(N, K_cap)`, slot `k` contains clipped relative pos/vel of *k-th closest* pedestrian. For `k > K_t`, padding sentinel.  
     - `o_sum`: Bounded scalars: crowd pressure `P_t`, alignment `A_t`, smallest-inverse distances, zone occupancies `n_t(r)/K_max`, active fraction `N/K_max`, mean relative velocity.  
  2. **Observation Normalizer:** `VecNormalize` (running mean/variance) applied to `o_t`. Designed stable because `o_knn` slot semantics and `o_sum` magnitudes are density-invariant.  
  3. **Policy Network (`π_θ`):** Simple MLP (no recurrence/attention). Takes normalized `o_t`, outputs continuous velocity command.  
  4. **Reward Calculator:** Computes `r_ext` (goal/collision/time rewards) + `β * r_pss`. `r_pss` uses privileged full-state info to compute `Φ(s_t)` (goal distance + density-scaled proxemic costs) and takes difference `γΦ(s_{t+1}) - Φ(s_t)`.  
  5. **Trainer (PPO):** Standard PPO with density-randomized episode generation (`N ~ U[11,16]`).

- **Critical path:**  
  `Simulator State (s_t)` → `Encoder (ψ)` → `Normalized Observation (o_t)` → `MLP Policy (π_θ)` → `Action (a_t)`.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - `K_cap` vs. `K_max`: Smaller `K_cap` reduces computation and stabilizes normalization but discards distant context. Larger `K_max` allows richer summaries but increases fixed input dimension.  
  - Density-adaptive scaling function `η(·)`: Must balance decay rate; too aggressive → shaping negligible in dense scenes (collisions), too weak → shaping overwhelms goal reward (freezing).  
  - Fixed `K_max` vs. Dynamic Attention: Fixed slots avoid attention dilution but cannot weigh neighbors beyond distance; attention might be more expressive but risks distribution shift as `N` varies.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **High collision rate at `N > K_cap`:** Indicates `K_cap` too small or truncation+sorting failed to preserve critical neighbors.  
  - **High freezing rate (especially at high `N`):** Suggests `η(·)` scaling too weak (proxemic penalty still grows) or `β` too high; also possible if summaries poorly bounded.  
  - **Collisions even at `N <= K_cap`:** Fundamental policy failure—possibly shaping off (`β=0`) or insufficient training randomization.  
  - **Catastrophic failure at `N` just above training max (e.g., `N=17`):** Classic distribution shift at the `K_cap` boundary.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Ablation: No K-cap truncation.** Train with full `K_max` slots populated (no fixed `K_cap`). Expect sharp drop at `N` where more neighbors than slots exist. Result: Safe success plummets at N=17 (25.4% vs 94.0% full method).  
  2. **Ablation: Remove distance sorting.** Populate slots with any `K_t` neighbors (random order). Expect very poor learning even at low `N`. Result: 39.0% safe success at N=11 vs 96.2% with sorting.  
  3. **Ablation: Remove density-adaptive scaling (`η_t = 1`).** Compare "PSS only" vs "PSS-Social". Expect widening gap at high `N`. Result: At N=21, 80.4% vs 86.4% safe success, with higher variance for "PSS only".

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does PSS-Social perform at crowd densities substantially beyond 2.33 ped/m², particularly approaching or exceeding real-world peak densities (e.g., >3 ped/m²) in highly dynamic scenarios?  
- **Open Question 2:** Can the proposed density-invariant observation encoding be integrated with attention-based or graph neural network architectures to combine their representational strengths without reintroducing density-dependent instability?  
- **Open Question 3:** How sensitive is zero-shot density generalization to environmental factors such as arena size, shape, and obstacle layout, given that all experiments use a fixed 3m×3m square arena?  
- **Open Question 4:** Does the method’s performance depend critically on the Social Force Model used for pedestrian simulation, and how well does it transfer to scenarios with human-like stochasticity, heterogeneity, or non-cooperative behaviors?

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter values (clipping bounds, zone thresholds, penalty weights, `η(·)` parameters) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- Evaluation confined to a single 3×3 m arena with a specific SFM-based pedestrian model; generalizability to other spaces or crowd dynamics is unknown.
- Training density range [11,16] is narrow; performance at extreme densities (e.g., N>21) is untested.

## Confidence
- **Density-invariant observation encoding stabilizes input distribution:** Medium. Supported by ablations and design rationale, but no direct distribution measurements.
- **Density-adaptive reward scaling prevents freezing:** Medium. Ablation shows improvement, but exact form and sensitivity of `η(·)` are undisclosed.
- **Safe success >99% goal-reaching and 86% collision-free at N=21:** High. Consistent results across seeds and ablations, though seed count unspecified.
- **>60 percentage-point safety margin over learning baselines:** Medium. Depends on baseline implementations; some may be outdated.

## Next Checks
1. **Distribution shift verification:** Record normalized observation statistics during training (N∈[11,16]) and testing (N=21) with and without the K-cap encoder. Confirm that the encoder keeps test statistics within the training envelope.
2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity sweep:** Systematically vary `K_cap` (e.g., 3,5,7,10) and parameters of `η(·)`. Evaluate safe success at N=21 to establish robustness boundaries.
3. **Cross-simulator replication:** Implement PSS-Social in a different crowd simulator (e.g., ORCA) and repeat core experiments. Compare metrics to the paper’s results to assess simulator independence.
