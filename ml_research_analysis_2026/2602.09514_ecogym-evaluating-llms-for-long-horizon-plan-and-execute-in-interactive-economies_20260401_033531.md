# EcoGym: Evaluating LLMs for Long-Horizon Plan-and-Execute in Interactive Economies

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09514
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09514
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** EcoGym introduces three unified economic environments for evaluating LLM agents on long‑horizon planning; testing eleven LLMs shows no single model dominates all scenarios, with Gemini-3-Pro achieving ~11,275 net worth (Vending), GPT-5-Mini ~2,991 income (Freelance), and Claude-Sonnet-4.5 ~1,572 daily active users (Operation), surpassing a human expert baseline of 1,404 DAU in Operation.

## Executive Summary
EcoGym addresses the lack of persistent, generalizable benchmarks for evaluating large language model (LLM) agents on long‑horizon economic planning. It introduces three environments—Vending, Freelance, and Operation—each with an effectively infinite horizon (365 days) and a compact set of atomic actions (4–5 per day). Critical economic mechanics, such as market seasonality, burnout thresholds, and system decay, are hidden, forcing agents to actively discover them through interaction. Success is measured via business‑relevant outcomes: net worth, income, and daily active users. Experiments with eleven LLMs reveal that each model exhibits significant suboptimality in either strategy or execution, and no single model excels across all environments. The analysis further highlights the impact of context window length, memory augmentation, and explicit thinking modes on performance.

## Method Summary
EcoGym provides three simulation environments. In **Vending**, an agent manages a virtual vending machine business, setting prices and ordering inventory to maximize net worth. **Freelance** tasks an agent with earning income as a freelance writer while managing stress and energy to avoid burnout. **Operation** simulates running a digital platform, balancing creator incentives and moderation to maximize daily active users. Agents receive structured daily reports (JSON) and can perform a limited set of discrete actions each simulated day. The horizon is effectively infinite (365 days). Evaluation aggregates a single key performance indicator (KPI) over the full trajectory. The authors run experiments using a sliding context window of 128 steps by default, and they ablate context length, add memory modules (working, symbolic, episodic, Mem0), and test the effect of an explicit thinking mode. The study evaluates eleven LLMs in total. The codebase, full prompts, action schemas, and model version identifiers are publicly released.

## Key Results
- Gemini-3-Pro achieves the highest net worth in Vending, approximately 11,275.
- GPT-5-Mini leads in Freelance income with about 2,991.
- Claude-Sonnet-4.5 attains the highest daily active users in Operation, around 1,572, exceeding the human expert baseline of 1,404.
- No model ranks first across all three environments, indicating environment‑specific specialization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Decoupling action complexity from temporal depth isolates long‑horizon strategic competence.** The environments restrict agents to 4–5 atomic actions per day while offering an effectively infinite horizon (365 days × daily budget). This forces agents to focus on multi‑step coherence, resource timing, and cumulative optimization rather than mastering complex action syntax. Simulations reveal that this design successfully shifts the evaluation focus to long‑term stability and optimization, allowing measurement of an agent’s capacity to plan over ultra‑long contexts.

**Hidden economic mechanics compel active discovery and hypothesis testing.** Critical parameters—such as market seasonality ϕ and elasticity η in Vending, burnout threshold τburnout in Freelance, and system decay coefficients Φsys in Operation—are omitted from observations. Agents must infer causal relationships by designing controlled experiments and correlating outcomes. This mirrors real‑world business environments where the underlying dynamics are not fully disclosed. The appendices provide the mathematical formulations only for the reader, not to the agent.

**Business‑relevant outcome metrics provide valid, generalizable measures of economic viability.** Each environment defines success through a single aggregated KPI (net worth, income, DAU) that compounds over the full trajectory. This avoids reward hacking on proxy metrics and aligns with real commercial objectives. The divergent model rankings across environments suggest these metrics capture distinct planning challenges rather than a single skill.

## Foundational Learning
- **Delayed Credit Assignment** – Actions have compounding, delayed effects (e.g., procurement costs now affect future sales). Agents must propagate reward information backward through time to evaluate decisions.  
  *Quick check:* You spend $500 on inventory on Day 1. It sells gradually over Days 3–7. How do you determine whether that procurement was profitable?

- **State Tracking Under Partial Observability** – Observations are fragmented (daily reports, action feedback). Agents must maintain accurate internal estimates of latent state (cash balance, inventory, stress) to avoid bankruptcy.  
  *Quick check:* Your last payment was 4 days ago and you’ve placed 3 orders since then. How do you compute your current cash balance?

- **Latent Variable Inference via Controlled Experimentation** – To discover hidden mechanics (price elasticity, decay rates), agents must design informative interventions (e.g., vary prices while holding other factors constant) and correlate outcomes.  
  *Quick check:* You raise prices by 10% on Day 20 and sales drop 15%. What additional data would you collect to distinguish elasticity from seasonal effects?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Observation Parser → Internal State Tracker → Hypothesis Tester ↔ Goal Evaluator → Action Budget Manager → task_done

### Critical Path
1. Receive observation → parser → update internal state tracker.  
2. Compare observed outcomes vs. predicted (from current latent model).  
3. If discrepancy persists, design test action to isolate cause.  
4. Else, choose optimization action (procure, price, incentivize).  
5. After max_actions_per_day, call task_done to advance day.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Sliding context window (default 128 steps):** Forces compression; dropping early days may lose long‑term pattern evidence.  
- **Discrete action space:** Simplifies execution but eliminates continuous control (e.g., cannot set exact inventory levels).  
- **Daily action budget:** Limits exploration vs. exploitation; spending actions on research reduces capacity for replenishment.

### Failure Signatures
- **Vending:** Negative net worth + τno_sales ≥ τlimit → bankruptcy.  
- **Freelance:** Stress ≥ Stmax OR Energy ≤ 0 → burnout spiral.  
- **Operation:** DAU < τcollapse for 3 consecutive days → platform collapse.  
- **Common:** Repeating the same ineffective action >5 times suggests stuck in local optimum or context loss.

### First Three Experiments
1. **Baseline stability:** Run 5 seeds with default 128‑step window; compute variance of final metric. High variance indicates environmental stochasticity dominates model decisions.  
2. **Memory ablation:** Disable internal state tracker (force agent to rely only on context). Expect performance drop if horizon exceeds context window.  
3. **Discovery test:** In Vending, deliberately violate seasonal norms (set price high during expected peak). Does agent adjust elasticity estimate or attribute to noise?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What underlying factors cause LLMs to exhibit environment‑specific specialization, with different models achieving SOTA in Vending, Freelance, or Operation? The paper documents specialization but does not investigate its origins or whether it generalizes beyond EcoGym. Resolving this would require controlled fine‑tuning experiments or representational similarity analysis across environments.  
- **Open Question 2:** What mechanisms can bridge the observed trade‑off between strategic planning and execution efficiency, where models consistently fail at one while excelling at the other in long‑horizon tasks? The root causes—reasoning gaps, action‑space limitations, context management, or reward misalignment—remain unclear. A fine‑grained failure‑mode analysis dissecting planning vs. execution stages would help.  
- **Open Question 3:** What principles determine the effectiveness of memory augmentation (working, symbolic, episodic) for long‑horizon economic planning, given the observed model‑ and task‑dependent benefits (or regressions)? No theoretical framework exists to predict when and why specific memory types help. Evidence would come from a systematic evaluation across tasks and models, coupled with metrics on retrieval relevance, retention decay, and conflict resolution.

## Limitations
- LLM‑generated latent dynamics cause non‑determinism; without fixed seeds or stored parameters exact replication is difficult.  
- Proprietary model snapshots (e.g., `gpt‑5.2‑2025‑12‑11`) may be unavailable, potentially altering performance rankings.  
- Memory module implementations are described conceptually but may be missing from the public codebase, making ablations irreproducible.

## Confidence
- Decoupled design isolates long‑horizon planning: **Medium** (The design forces multi-step coherence, but the sliding context window and non‑determinism may limit isolation; see Limitations and Design Tradeoffs.)  
- No model dominates across environments: **High** (Empirical results across eleven LLMs consistently show different models achieving SOTA in each environment, as shown in Key Results.)  
- Metrics proxy general economic competence: **Medium** (While KPIs align with business objectives, the synthetic environments may not fully capture real‑world economic complexity; see Limitations.)  
- Context/memory/thinking analyses: **Low‑to‑Medium** (Ablations yield mixed, model‑dependent outcomes, and memory module implementations may be irreproducible; see Limitations.)
