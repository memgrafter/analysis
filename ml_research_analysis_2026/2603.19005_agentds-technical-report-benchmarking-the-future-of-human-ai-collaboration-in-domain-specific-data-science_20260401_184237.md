# AgentDS Technical Report: Benchmarking the Future of Human-AI Collaboration in Domain-Specific Data Science

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.19005
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19005
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: AI-only agents underperformed human experts; human-AI collaboration yielded strongest solutions.

## Executive Summary
The AgentDS benchmark evaluates AI agents on 17 domain-specific data science challenges across six industries. Results show that AI-only approaches—GPT-4o (quantile 0.143) and Claude Code (0.458)—failed to match top human teams and often scored below the participant median. Human experts contributed strategic problem diagnosis, domain-driven feature engineering, and judgment beyond validation metrics. The strongest solutions emerged from human-AI collaboration, where humans directed strategy and AI accelerated implementation. This demonstrates that current AI cannot autonomously replicate expert performance in complex, multimodal domain tasks, underscoring the essential role of human expertise.

## Method Summary
The benchmark uses synthetic yet realistic datasets per challenge, combining structured tabular data with auxiliary modalities (images, text, JSON/PDFs). Each challenge provides a description.md detailing domain context, schema, and submission format. Performance metrics vary (e.g., Macro-F1, RMSE), normalized into quantile scores (q = (n - r)/(n-1)) for cross-task aggregation. A 10-day competition involved 29 teams (80 participants) with up to 100 submissions per challenge. Two AI-only baselines were evaluated: GPT-4o via direct prompting (single script generation) and Claude Code in autonomous mode (10-minute iterative coding). Submissions were scored via a central API on hidden test sets.

## Key Results
- GPT-4o direct prompting achieved overall quantile score 0.143 (rank 17/29), below the participant median of 0.156.
- Claude Code agentic coding scored 0.458 (rank 10/29), but still trailed top human teams.
- Human-AI collaboration produced the strongest solutions, with humans guiding strategy and AI accelerating implementation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: AI's Domain Reasoning Deficit
- **Claim**: Current AI agents fail to replicate human expert performance because they cannot perform effective domain-specific reasoning.
- **Mechanism**: Agents default to generic machine learning pipelines (e.g., standard preprocessing, gradient-boosted trees) optimized for general tabular patterns, failing to incorporate latent domain knowledge from multimodal signals or context-aware feature engineering.
- **Core assumption**: Domain-specific gains require reasoning beyond inferable training data patterns, needing external contextual knowledge.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "AI-only approaches struggled with domain reasoning, underutilized multimodal signals, and defaulted to generic pipelines." [section 3.1] "Inability to leverage multimodal signals... AI tends to default to familiar patterns..."
- **Break condition**: AI consistently matches top human teams by autonomously identifying, extracting, and utilizing non-tabular modality signals and generating novel domain-informed features.

### Mechanism 2: Human Strategic Value
- **Claim**: Human data scientists provide irreplaceable value through strategic problem diagnosis, injection of non-inferable domain knowledge, and judgment transcending validation metrics.
- **Mechanism**: Humans engage in meta-cognitive tasks: diagnosing structural flaws (e.g., distribution shift), encoding business/clinical/engineering rules absent in data, and exercising skepticism toward overfitted solutions.
- **Core assumption**: Effective domain data science requires a closed-loop process of hypothesizing about data-generating mechanisms, testing, and refining—relying on causal and counterfactual reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "Human contributions included strategic problem diagnosis, domain-driven feature engineering, and judgment beyond validation metrics." [section 3.2] "Strategic problem diagnosis... Encoding domain knowledge that data cannot reveal..."
- **Break condition**: AI autonomously produces post-mortem analyses identifying root causes in problem framing and successfully modifies formulation/objectives for superior generalization.

### Mechanism 3: Collaboration Superiority
- **Claim**: Highest-performing outcomes arise from human-AI collaboration where humans direct strategy and AI accelerates implementation, not from either working alone.
- **Mechanism**: Effective collaboration follows a human-initiated, iterative feedback loop: human diagnoses, hypothesizes, sets objectives; AI implements, runs experiments, provides results. This leverages human strategic reasoning and AI's speed, breadth, and code generation.
- **Core assumption**: Division of labor should be strategic control for humans, tactical execution for AI; reversing this leads to suboptimal solutions.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "Human-AI collaboration produced the strongest solutions, with humans guiding strategy and AI accelerating implementation." [section 3.3] "AI for acceleration, humans for direction... Iterative human-AI feedback loops..." [section 2.5 & 3.1] Teams abandoned autonomous agents for interactive workflows.
- **Break condition**: A fully autonomous AI agent, with equal resources, consistently achieves equal/better performance across all challenges, especially multimodal and complex feature engineering tasks.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Domain-Specific Feature Engineering
- **Why needed here**: The benchmark deliberately makes generic models fail; success requires features encoding domain knowledge (e.g., medical vital sign trends, actuarial rules). Understanding *why* a feature is created is crucial.
- **Quick check**: For the Commerce "Product Recommendation" challenge, beyond standard user-item interactions, what domain-specific visual or textual signal from product images/descriptions might indicate cross-selling potential, and how would you extract it?

### Concept: Multimodal Signal Integration
- **Why needed here**: Challenges provide auxiliary data (images, text, PDFs) where predictive signals are latent. Learners must understand *what* domain-relevant property a model layer captures (e.g., vehicle damage severity).
- **Quick check**: In Insurance "Risk-Based Pricing" (roof images), what latent property about risk is encoded in the image, and how would you hypothesize a relationship between a CNN-extracted feature and the premium?

### Concept: Quantile-Based Cross-Task Evaluation
- **Why needed here**: Different challenges use different metrics (RMSE, Macro-F1, NDCG). Quantile scoring normalizes performance into [0,1] ranks to aggregate across heterogeneous tasks.
- **Quick check**: If your model ranks 5th (n=20) on Challenge A (RMSE=15.2) and 10th (n=25) on Challenge B (Macro-F1=0.72), what is your approximate overall quantile score? How does a non-submission (score 0) affect aggregation?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Benchmark Core (challenge directories) -> Evaluation Engine (submission API) -> Competition Orchestrator (team management, leaderboards); Baseline Runners (GPT-4o/Claude Code scripts) and Analysis Suite (post-competition tools) operate in parallel.

### Critical path
Challenge Design → Data Generation → Metric Calibration → Competition Execution → Baseline Evaluation → Qualitative Analysis. Most critical phase is Data Generation, where synthetic data must embed latent signals accessible only via domain-specific multimodal processing.

### Design tradeoffs
- Synthetic vs. Real Data: Synthetic ensures known upper bounds and clean signal embedding but lacks real-world noise/ambiguity.
- Domain Breadth vs. Depth: 6 domains provide diversity but limit depth per field; scaling tests generalization but increases maintenance.
- Autonomy vs. Collaboration Metric: Competition naturally measures collaboration but doesn't systematically vary interaction patterns (e.g., degree of autonomy).

### Failure signatures
1. Agentic Default: Agent solutions use identical code templates across challenges (e.g., `pd.read_csv() -> XGBoost`), ignoring provided modalities.
2. Multimodal Blindness: No team loads/processes image/text/JSON files unless explicitly prompted; or uses naive methods (e.g., image flattening).
3. Human Overfitting without Validation: Teams excel on public leaderboard but degrade on private test set, indicating over-optimization without robust generalization.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline Replication**: Execute GPT-4o and Claude Code baselines on a simpler challenge (e.g., Retail Banking Fraud). Verify quantile scores match reported ranges and capture generated code for failure analysis.
2. **Multimodal Ablation Study**: For Insurance Risk-Based Pricing, run a top human team's pipeline in three configurations: (a) tabular only, (b) tabular + naive image features (pixel averages), (c) full domain-specific image features (roof material classifier). Measure performance delta.
3. **Collaboration Mode A/B Test**: Recruit 2-3 domain experts to solve 3 challenges under two conditions: (a) full AI assistant, (b) constrained AI assistant (debugging only). Compare solution quality and time-to-submit to isolate AI's value in strategic ideation vs. tactical implementation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How robust are the observed performance gaps between AI agents and human experts when evaluated on real-world, non-synthetic datasets?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Synthetic data limitation "cannot capture the full messiness, ambiguity, and noise of genuine industry datasets."
- **Why unresolved**: Real-world complexities may further disadvantage AI or provide clearer patterns; remains untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Curate and release anonymized real datasets from the six domains; re-evaluate baselines using identical protocols.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Which specific human-AI interaction designs (e.g., human-initiated feedback loops, degree of AI autonomy, prompting strategies) maximize performance in domain-specific data science?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Observational analysis "does not allow controlled experiments"; call for future work to "systematically vary the degree of autonomy, prompting strategies, or human oversight."
- **Why unresolved**: Insights rely on self-reported workflows; causal links between collaboration patterns and outcomes are unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Controlled experiments assigning teams to prescribed collaboration protocols (e.g., fully autonomous vs. human-guided iteration) on benchmark tasks, measuring performance, efficiency, and cognitive load.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What fundamental limitations in current LLM-based agents cause persistent failure in leveraging multimodal signals (e.g., images, text) for domain-specific reasoning?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Agents show "inability to leverage multimodal signals" and "default to generic pipelines" ignoring additional modalities.
- **Why unresolved**: Unclear whether due to inadequate multimodal training data, architectural constraints, or insufficient reasoning over non-tabular features.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Ablation studies removing multimodal components; analysis of agent reasoning traces to identify breakdown points; comparisons with human feature extraction strategies.

## Limitations
- **Synthetic data**: Cannot capture full messiness, ambiguity, and noise of real industry datasets, potentially understating AI challenges or overstating human advantages.
- **Reproduction blockers**: AgentDS evaluation API accessibility, undisclosed baseline prompts, missing participant raw scores, unspecified execution environment, potential dataset version drift.
- **Weak corpus evidence**: Neighboring papers discuss human-AI collaboration broadly but do not directly anchor the specific failure modes (domain reasoning deficit, strategic human value).

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (AI domain reasoning failure)**: Medium. Anchored in abstract/section text, but corpus evidence weak; relies on observed baseline behavior.
- **Mechanism 2 (Human strategic value)**: Medium. Similar anchoring; claims about "judgment beyond metrics" are qualitative from reports.
- **Mechanism 3 (Collaboration superiority)**: High. Directly supported by competition outcomes (human-AI teams outperformed both AI-only and human-only baselines).
