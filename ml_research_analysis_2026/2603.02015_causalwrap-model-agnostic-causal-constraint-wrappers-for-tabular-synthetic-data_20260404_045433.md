# CausalWrap: Model-Agnostic Causal Constraint Wrappers for Tabular Synthetic Data

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.02015
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02015
*   **Reference count:** 18
*   **Primary result:** A post-hoc wrapper that injects partial causal knowledge (trusted/forbidden edges, monotonicity) into any pretrained tabular generator significantly improves causal fidelity metrics (e.g., ATE error) while largely preserving conventional utility.

## Executive Summary
Tabular synthetic data generators are typically trained to match observational distributions, which is insufficient for downstream causal inference tasks requiring structural fidelity. CausalWrap (CW) addresses this by acting as a model-agnostic post-hoc wrapper. It takes a pretrained base generator (e.g., a GAN or diffusion model) and a partial specification of causal knowledge (trusted edges, forbidden edges, monotonicity constraints). CW learns a lightweight, differentiable correction map that transforms the base generator's samples. This map is optimized using an augmented Lagrangian method to minimize a combined loss of conventional distributional utility and differentiable penalties for violating the provided causal constraints. Evaluations across simulated, semi-synthetic, and real-world ICU datasets show CW reduces causal estimation errors (e.g., ATE) by substantial margins while maintaining comparable performance on standard synthetic data quality metrics.

## Method Summary
CausalWrap operates on a frozen, pretrained base tabular generator `Q_base`. It introduces a trainable, lightweight multi-layer perceptron (MLP) correction map `f_ϕ`, initialized near the identity. Samples `x̃ ~ Q_base` are transformed to `x = f_ϕ(x̃)`. The optimization objective balances a utility surrogate (e.g., moment matching between real and corrected data, plus an identity regularization term `||f_ϕ(x̃) - x̃||²`) with a set of causal constraint penalties `Ω_K`. These penalties are: 1) **Forbidden edges (E⁰)**, enforced by computing the residuals of each variable after regressing out its trusted parents (from E⁺) on real data, then penalizing the Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC) between the residuals of the two nodes; 2) **Monotonicity constraints (M)**, enforced by creating a pair of twin samples from the same base input, perturbing the source variable `X_i` by a small `δ`, and applying a hinge loss if the conditional effect on `X_j` has the wrong sign. All penalties are made adaptive via an augmented Lagrangian method (ALM), which introduces dual variables `μ_c` and a growing penalty coefficient `λ`, eliminating the need for manual tuning of a fixed penalty weight.

## Key Results
*   On the ACIC benchmark, CW reduced ATE estimation error by up to 63% (using TV AE as the base generator).
*   On a real MIMIC-IV ICU cohort, CW increased ATE agreement (a proxy for consistency with real-world treatment effects) from 0.00 to 0.38 for TabDDPM and from 0.15 to 0.28 for TV AE.
*   Ablation studies showed monotonicity constraints were the most effective driver of causal fidelity improvements, and the augmented Lagrangian schedule outperformed using a fixed penalty weight.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** A differentiable correction map transforms base generator samples to improve causal fidelity while preserving conventional utility.
*   **Mechanism:** A frozen base generator produces raw samples `x̃ ~ Q_base`. A lightweight MLP `f_ϕ` (identity-initialized) transforms them: `x = f_ϕ(x̃)`. The map is optimized to minimize a combined loss of distributional utility and causal constraint penalties.
*   **Core assumption:** The base generator's output support is compatible with the target causal structure; adjustments can be made without retraining the base.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The [abstract] states CW learns a correction map applied to base samples. [Section 3.2] defines `Q_ϕ := (f_ϕ)# Q_base`. [Section 4.1]'s Algorithm 1 shows the correction is applied before constraint evaluation.
*   **Break condition:** If the base generator's samples lie on a manifold far from the true data manifold, no smooth correction map can bridge the gap without distorting conventional metrics.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Forbidden edges are enforced via HSIC penalties on residuals after regressing out trusted parents, providing a differentiable, direction-aware proxy for conditional independence.
*   **Mechanism:** For each forbidden edge `(a,b) ∈ E⁰`, trusted parents `Pa⁺(b)` are identified. Simple edge models `m̂_j` are fitted on real data. On corrected samples `x`, residuals `x̃_b = x_b - m̂_b(x_Pa⁺(b))` are computed. `HSIC(x̃_a, x̃_b)` penalizes dependence that would violate `a→b` being absent.
*   **Core assumption:** Residualizing on known trusted parents sufficiently captures the conditional dependence; missing confounders can lead to false positives.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [Section 3.3] defines the penalty `Ω_CI(Q) := Σ_(a,b)∈E⁰ w_a,b HSIC(x̃_a, x̃_b; Q)`. It notes residualization acts as a proxy for the conditional-independence structure. [Figure 2] shows gradient flow only updates `f_ϕ`; edge models are frozen.
*   **Break condition:** If a true confounder of `X_a` and `X_b` is missing from `Pa⁺(b)`, its effect remains in `x̃_b`, causing the HSIC penalty to fire spuriously.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Monotonicity constraints are enforced via paired-sample hinge penalties with finite-difference effect estimation.
*   **Mechanism:** For constraint `(i,j,S,σ)` ("`X_j` σ-monotone in `X_i` given `X_S`"), create twin samples from the same base `x̃`. Perturb only `X_i`: `x̃'_i = x̃_i + δ`. Both pass through `f_ϕ` to yield `x, x'`. Penalty = `max(0, -σ·(x'_j - x_j))`. Gradients propagate through both forward passes to update `ϕ`.
*   **Core assumption:** The finite-difference approximation captures the local conditional effect; the constraint holds across the support; `δ` is well-chosen.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [Section 3.3] defines `Ω_mono(Q) := E_̃x~Q [max(0, -σ·∂̂_x_i E_Q[X_j | ...]) ]`. It notes gradients flow from the hinge loss back through both forward passes. [Figure 2] illustrates the twin-sample gradient flow.
*   **Break condition:** If monotonicity is violated only in certain regions, a single `δ` may miss it. `δ` too small → noise; too large → averages over nonlinear regions.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept 1: Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM)**
*   **Why needed here:** To enforce multiple constraints adaptively without manual λ-tuning, while maintaining convergence guarantees. Fixed penalties require finding a λ that balances utility and constraints; ALM eliminates this by introducing dual variables μ and growing λ.
*   **Quick check question:** What is the key difference between pure penalty methods and augmented Lagrangian, and why does ALM avoid the need for λ→∞ ?

**Concept 2: Partial Causal Knowledge (PCK) Representation**
*   **Why needed here:** Real-world experts rarely provide full DAGs; they give partial, uncertain edges (trusted/forbidden) and qualitative constraints. CausalWrap's tuple `K = (E⁺, E⁰, M)` formalizes this into enforceable penalties.
*   **Quick check question:** How would you encode a temporal ordering (e.g., treatment cannot cause pre-treatment covariates) in K?

**Concept 3: Post-hoc vs. Joint Training Paradigm**
*   **Why needed here:** CausalWrap must work with any pretrained base generator (black box). This requires a separation: base stays frozen; only a small correction layer is trained. This introduces a trade-off: the base might be far from causal fidelity, making correction harder vs. being already close, where correction is efficient.
*   **Quick check question:** What are the risks of training a correction map on samples from a base generator that already violates the constraints significantly?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Base generator (frozen) -> f_ϕ (trainable MLP) -> corrected samples x.
Edge models m̂_j (frozen, fitted on real D_n using E⁺) -> residuals for HSIC.
Constraint calculators (CI: HSIC on residuals; Monotonicity: paired-sample hinge) -> penalties Ω_c.
ALM optimizer (μ_c, λ) -> weighted penalized loss L.

**Critical path:**
1. Pre-fit all edge models `m̂_j` on real data `D_n` using trusted parents `E⁺`.
2. For each minibatch:
   a. Sample `x̃ ~ Q_base`.
   b. Compute corrected `x = f_ϕ(x̃)`.
   c. Compute utility surrogate `Û(D_n, {x})` (moment matching + `γ·||f_ϕ(x̃)-x̃||²`).
   d. Compute constraint estimates `Ω_CI` (HSIC on residuals using frozen `m̂`) and `Ω_mono` (paired hinge).
   e. Form ALM loss: `L = Û + Σ μ_c Ω_c + (λ/2) Σ Ω_c²`.
   f. Backpropagate through `f_ϕ` only; update `ϕ`.
3. After inner SGD steps: update `μ_c ← μ_c + λ Ω_c` and `λ ← ρ·λ`.

**Design tradeoffs:**
*   **Correction capacity vs. overfitting:** `f_ϕ` must be expressive enough to fix violations but not so large it memorizes base patterns. Use small MLP (e.g., 2×d hidden) and strong identity regularization.
*   **Constraint budget dilution:** Adding many constraints, especially ones the base already approximately satisfies, wastes gradient budget. Prioritize high-violation, high-impact constraints; consider selection based on initial violation rates.
*   **ALM hyperparameters:** Initial `λ₀` too low slows constraint satisfaction; too high drops utility immediately. Growth rate `ρ` controls aggression. Start with `λ₀=1`, `ρ=1.5` and monitor constraint values.

**Failure signatures:**
1. **Conventional utility collapses** (MMD ↑, TSTR ↓): Over-correction. Reduce `f_ϕ` capacity, increase identity regularization weight, or check for contradictory constraints.
2. **Constraints remain high** after ALM: `λ` not growing enough, HSIC/hinge estimates noisy, or `f_ ϕ` lacks capacity. Increase outer iterations, verify edge models (R² > 0.1), or enlarge `f_ϕ`.
3. **Training unstable** (loss NaN, spikes): HSIC kernel bandwidth issue, learning rate too high, or `λ` growth too aggressive. Use median heuristic for HSIC bandwidth, lower LR (~1e-3), or reduce `ρ`.
4. **Monotonicity fails on test samples:** Perturbation `δ` inadequate for data scale. Normalize columns to unit variance or adapt `δ` per column (e.g., 0.1×std).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Sanity check:** Run CW on Linear-Gaussian SCM (Tier 1) with TabDDPM. Verify ATE error decreases vs. base, constraints near zero, and utility (MMD, TSTR) degrades <5%. Ensure ALM schedule converges smoothly.
2. **Constraint type isolation:** Train with only CI constraints, only monotonicity, and both. Measure ATE error and each constraint's final `Ω`. Expect monotonicity to drive most gains (per Section 6.5). Confirms each penalty is functional.
3. **Knowledge fraction sweep:** From 0% to 100% of true trusted edges (`E⁺`) revealed. Plot ATE error vs. fraction. Anticipate non-monotonic "goldilocks" zone: too few → no guidance; too many → overfit/dilution. Identify optimal fraction for your base+dataset.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Longitudinal extension:** How can CausalWrap be extended to longitudinal data with temporal partial orders as constraints? The current framework handles static tabular data; temporal structures require new constraint representations.
*   **Theoretical strengthening:** Can theoretical guarantees be strengthened to establish an end-to-end link between penalty convergence and conditional distribution matching? Remark 2 notes existing theory does not compose because residualized HSIC is a proxy for CI.
*   **Automated constraint selection:** What is a principled, automated approach for selecting the optimal subset of partial causal knowledge constraints to inject? Ablations show non-monotonic effects and recommend conservative subsets, but no selection method is provided.
*   **Systematic comparison with other methods:** How does CausalWrap compare systematically with other causal-aware synthetic data generation methods such as STEAM and C-DGM across diverse scenarios? A direct benchmark is left to future work.

## Limitations
*   Hyperparameter settings (learning rates, penalty schedule, identity regularization strength) are not fully specified and likely require dataset- and base-generator-specific tuning.
*   The precise composition of the utility surrogate (e.g., which moments, their weighting) and its balance with penalties is underspecified, making perfect replication challenging.
*   Sensitivity to implementation details like HSIC kernel bandwidth and monotonicity perturbation size `δ` is not thoroughly studied, which could affect constraint enforcement and stability.

## Confidence
*   **Causal fidelity improvement (ATE error reduction):** Medium (strong empirical support but appears hyperparameter-sensitive)
*   **Model-agnostic applicability:** High (validated on multiple base generators: CTGAN, TabDDPM, TV AE)
*   **Augmented Lagrangian advantages:** High (supported by theory and ablation showing superiority over fixed penalties)
*   **Utility preservation:** Medium (some degradation observed in certain experiments; trade-off with constraint strength)

## Next Checks
1.  **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Systematically vary key ALM parameters (`λ₀`, `ρ`, `T_inner`) and identity regularization weight `γ` across base-generator/dataset pairs; report mean±std ATE error to quantify robustness.
2.  **Constraint implementation audit:** On synthetic graphs with ground-truth CI and monotonicity, verify the HSIC and hinge penalty values drop to near-zero when constraints hold and rise sharply when violated by the base generator.
3.  **Constraint budget ablation:** Randomly sample increasing fractions (e.g., 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%) of the true partial knowledge set (`E⁰` ∪ `M`). Plot causal fidelity (ATE error) vs. conventional utility (MMD) to identify the optimal constraint density and pinpoint over-constraining thresholds.
