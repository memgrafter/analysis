# A Vision-Language Foundation Model for Zero-shot Clinical Collaboration and Automated Concept Discovery in Dermatology

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10624
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10624
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A dermatology vision-language model trained on over 4 million multimodal data points achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across 20 benchmarks without adaptation, and AI assistance nearly doubles general practitioners' diagnostic accuracy in multinational reader studies.

## Executive Summary
DermFM-Zero is a dermatology-focused vision-language foundation model that eliminates the need for task-specific fine-tuning in diverse clinical settings. Through a two-stage pretraining curriculum—masked latent modeling on 3 million unlabeled images followed by contrastive learning on 1 million curated image-text pairs—it learns robust visual representations aligned with medical semantics. In evaluations across 20 benchmarks, it sets new zero-shot standards. In multinational reader studies (n>1,100), AI assistance with DermFM-Zero nearly doubled general practitioners' top-3 diagnostic accuracy and outperformed board-certified dermatologists in multimodal skin cancer assessment. The model’s interpretable latent space, discovered via sparse autoencoders, enables targeted suppression of artifact-induced biases (e.g., rulers, pen marks), improving robustness by up to 38% AUROC without retraining.

## Method Summary
The model is pretrained in two stages using over 4 million data points from public and private dermatology sources (including 574k private images). Stage 1 employs masked latent modeling on unlabeled images to force the ViT-Large vision encoder to learn fine-grained morphological features. Stage 2 uses bootstrapped contrastive learning to align these frozen visual features with text embeddings from PubMedBERT on 1 million high-quality image-text pairs, creating a shared embedding space. After pretraining, the model is deployed frozen for zero-shot inference via cosine similarity between image and templated text embeddings (e.g., "a photo of {disease}"). For interpretability, sparse autoencoders (SAEs) are trained on the frozen vision encoder’s features to discover monosemantic visual concepts, which are named by matching to the text encoder’s vocabulary. These SAE latents support concept bottleneck models (CBMs) for interpretable diagnosis and enable targeted artifact suppression by zeroing out artifact-associated neurons at inference.

## Key Results
- Zero-shot SOTA across 20 public and private dermatology benchmarks without adaptation.
- In multinational reader studies, AI assistance doubled GPs' top-3 diagnostic accuracy (0.266 → 0.482, p=0.004) and achieved higher accuracy than unassisted dermatologists (0.717 vs. 0.694, p<0.001).
- SAE-discovered concepts enable interpretable diagnostic models and targeted artifact bias mitigation, improving AUROC by up to 38% on artifact-confounded datasets without retraining.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Two-Stage Pretraining for Zero-Shot Generalization
The two-stage curriculum first uses masked latent modeling on 3M unlabeled images to force the vision encoder to capture fine-grained morphological structures (e.g., texture, lesion borders). This is followed by bootstrapped contrastive learning on 1M curated image-text pairs, which aligns the frozen visual features with medical semantics via a PubMedBERT text encoder. This creates a shared embedding space where even unseen condition names (as text embeddings) can be matched to images via cosine similarity. The approach assumes the pretraining data distribution is sufficiently broad (400+ conditions, multiple imaging modalities) to support out-of-distribution generalization. A break condition occurs if a clinical condition or imaging modality is severely underrepresented, causing the semantic bridge to fail.

### Mechanism 2: Sparse Autoencoders for Disentangled Concept Discovery
Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) with 8× overcomplete latents and L1 sparsity are applied to frozen DermFM-Zero features to decompose them into a sparse code. The decoder weights of activated neurons become "concept prototypes" (e.g., "blue globules", "pigment network"), named by matching to the text encoder’s vocabulary. These latents serve as interpretable features for a linear classifier (CBM), maintaining performance while providing human-readable rationales. This relies on the foundation model’s representations being intrinsically well-structured and linearly separable. If the base model’s features are entangled, the SAE will fail to discover monosemantic concepts, as observed with CLIP and MONET baselines.

### Mechanism 3: Targeted Artifact Suppression via Interpretable Neurons
Artifact-associated neurons (e.g., detecting rulers, pen marks, hair) identified by the SAE can be manually suppressed at inference time—by setting their activations to zero—to mitigate spurious correlations. This forces the model to rely on diagnostic features rather than artifact cues, improving robustness without retraining. The intervention assumes artifact signals are consistent and not perfectly confounded with true labels. Suppression may backfire if the artifact is heavily correlated with the diagnosis in pretraining data, as removing it could also discard legitimate predictive signal.

## Foundational Learning

**Vision-Language Contrastive Pretraining (CLIP-style)**
*Why needed:* Enables zero-shot classification by aligning image and text embeddings in a shared space, allowing novel disease classes to be recognized via similarity matching.
*Quick check:* What role does the temperature parameter τ play in InfoNCE loss, and how would a poorly chosen τ affect fine-grained condition discrimination?

**Masked Image Modeling (MIM) / Masked Latent Modeling**
*Why needed:* The initial self-supervised stage enriches the vision encoder’s understanding of local morphology (e.g., texture, structure) before text alignment, providing a robust visual foundation.
*Quick check:* Why does the masked latent modeling objective use both visible and masked patch alignments? What if only masked patch targets were used?

**Sparse Autoencoders (SAE) for Mechanistic Interpretability**
*Why needed:* Moves beyond post-hoc attribution to discover a potentially more faithful, disentangled representation of the model’s internal visual features, enabling targeted interventions.
*Quick check:* What is the purpose of the L1 sparsity penalty in SAE training? How does the 8× latent expansion factor affect the interpretability-reconstruction trade-off?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Image -> Vision Encoder (ViT-Large, Stage 1 MIM + Stage 2 contrastive) -> [CLS] (768-dim) -> (optional: Sparse Autoencoder -> sparse latents (6144-dim) -> concept prototypes) -> Linear Classifier (for CBM). Text -> PubMedBERT -> [CLS] (768-dim) -> used in contrastive loss and for naming SAE concepts. For multimodal fine-tuning: separate vision encoders + Meta-Fusion Transformer.

**Critical path:** Data curation -> Stage 1 MIM pretraining (3M images) -> Stage 2 contrastive pretraining (1M pairs) -> frozen model deployment. For interpretability: extract frozen features -> train SAE -> filter concepts via linear probe -> name concepts -> use for CBM or artifact intervention.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Data Scale vs. Quality:* Stage 2 uses a smaller, filtered subset (1M) for higher semantic quality versus the massive unfiltered Stage 1 set (3M), improving alignment but potentially limiting long-tail coverage.
- *Interpretability vs. Performance:* SAE-CBM approaches black-box linear probe performance on DermFM-Zero but lags on CLIP/MONET, suggesting high-quality pretrained representations are prerequisite for effective automated concept discovery.
- *Zero-shot vs. Fine-tuning:* The model is optimized for zero-shot utility, but multimodal fine-tuning provides clear benefits when task-specific data is available.

**Failure signatures:**
- Poor zero-shot accuracy on rare classes (e.g., DAFFODIL-5) indicates the text embedding for that condition is not well-aligned with its visual features, possibly due to insufficient textual diversity in pretraining.
- Entangled SAE concepts (a neuron activating for multiple unrelated patterns) suggest the base model’s representations are not optimally structured.
- Suppression of artifact neurons causing a significant drop in AUROC on clean test sets indicates the artifact signal was not spurious but a legitimate (if unintended) predictor learned from biased pretraining.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Zero-shot classification sanity check: Run DermFM-Zero on HAM-7 and PAD-6. Expect balanced accuracy >0.74, significantly above BiomedCLIP/CLIP.
2. SAE training and concept validation: Train SAE on 50k diverse Derm1M features. Inspect top-10 activating images for random latents; they should show coherent visual patterns.
3. Artifact intervention prototype: On ISIC-Intervention "Hair" subset, identify top-5 neurons by activation difference between hair/no-hair images. Suppress them (set activation=0) and re-evaluate linear probe AUROC. Expect ~+13% improvement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Prospective clinical validation:** Does DermFM-Zero maintain diagnostic benefits in real-world deployments versus retrospective simulations? Current evidence is from controlled settings.
- **Long-tail generalization:** Can the model’s unsupervised representations generalize to thousands of rare skin conditions beyond the 400+ seen in training? Evaluated only on limited rare disease sets like DAFFODIL-5.
- **Skin tone fairness:** Does AI assistance reduce or exacerbate diagnostic disparities across skin tones? The model’s impact on equity in human-AI collaboration remains unassessed.
- **Impact of interpretability:** Does integrating automated concept discovery and artifact intervention into clinical decision support improve clinician trust, understanding, and decision quality compared to black-box AI? Not yet evaluated.

## Limitations
- **Private data dependence:** ~57% of pretraining data (MoleMap and other private sources) is unreleased, obscuring potential biases and blocking full replication.
- **Undisclosed methodology:** Details of "bootstrapped contrastive learning" purification and domain-aware sampling are omitted, hindering exact reconstruction.
- **Reader study transparency:** Image selection, clinician demographics, and randomization protocols are unspecified, limiting assessment of study robustness.

## Confidence
- **Zero-shot SOTA across 20 benchmarks:** Medium – Public benchmarks are replicable, but private dataset composition may inflate results on undisclosed tests.
- **Clinical partnership effectiveness:** Medium – Large n and p-values support findings, but study design details are sparse and model reproducibility is constrained by private data.
- **SAE interpretability & artifact robustness:** High – Methodology is sufficiently detailed, artifact experiments use public ISIC data, and quantitative gains are clearly reported.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-dataset zero-shot evaluation:** Test on unexposed public datasets (e.g., PH2, DermNet). A marked performance drop versus reported benchmarks would indicate overfitting to the original test suite.
2. **Independent reader study replication:** Recruit new clinicians (GPs n≥100, dermatologists n≥50) to diagnose a balanced subset of original test images with/without AI. Replicating the ~2x GP improvement would strengthen clinical claims.
3. **SAE concept stability audit:** Train 5 SAEs with varied seeds/hyperparameters. Compute Jaccard similarity of top-activated neurons for shared concepts. Low consistency (<0.3) would challenge reliability of automated concept discovery.
