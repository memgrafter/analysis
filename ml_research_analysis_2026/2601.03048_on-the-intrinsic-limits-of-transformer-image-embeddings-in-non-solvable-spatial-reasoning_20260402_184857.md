# On the Intrinsic Limits of Transformer Image Embeddings in Non-Solvable Spatial Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.03048
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03048
- **Reference count**: 24
- **Primary result**: Constant-depth Vision Transformers (ViTs) cannot learn group-homomorphic embeddings for non-solvable spatial transformations (e.g., SO(3)) because such tasks require solving the NC¹-complete Word Problem, while ViTs reside in TC⁰ under the TC⁰ ⊊ NC¹ conjecture.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes a fundamental expressivity barrier for constant-depth Vision Transformers (ViTs) in spatial reasoning tasks involving non-solvable transformation groups (e.g., 3D rotations). The authors prove that ViTs, due to their constant logical depth, belong to the circuit class TC⁰. Representing non-solvable groups requires solving the Word Problem, which is NC¹-complete. Assuming the widely believed complexity separation TC⁰ ⊊ NC¹, ViTs cannot implement the iterated group products needed for a homomorphic visual embedding. The theory is validated empirically using the Latent Space Algebra benchmark, a synthetic dataset spanning abelian, solvable, and non-solvable transformation sequences. Through a recursive linear probing protocol, the study shows that ViT latent spaces suffer catastrophic structural collapse on non-solvable tasks: error accelerates past an identity baseline much earlier than for abelian groups, and directional (cosine) loss diverges rapidly. The results demonstrate an intrinsic computational limit for constant-depth architectures in non-solvable spatial reasoning.

## Method Summary
The authors introduce the Latent Space Algebra (LSA) benchmark: a synthetic dataset generated by rendering 7 Stanford 3D meshes under sequences of spatial transformations from three groups—Level 1 (2D translations, abelian), Level 2 (affine, solvable non‑abelian), and Level 3 (3D rigid + scale, non‑solvable). For each object, random walks of length up to 20 are sampled, producing image sequences. Frozen pretrained vision backbones (ViT‑B/16, DINOv2, ResNet‑50) encode images into feature vectors. A linear probe \(T_\phi\) is trained only on atomic (\(N=1\)) transitions to map \([z_t; e_s]\) to \(z_{t+1}\). At test time, the probe is applied recursively: starting from \(z_0 = E(I_0)\), predictions \(\hat{z}_{t+1} = T_\phi([\hat{z}_t; e_{s_{t+1}}])\) are fed back for \(t=0..n-1\). The predicted final state \(\hat{z}_n\) is compared to the frozen encoder’s output for the true final image \(E(I_n)\). Losses (MSE, cosine) and a normalized ratio versus the identity baseline (\(z_0\) repeated) are tracked. A ratio ≥1.0 indicates structural collapse—the model performs worse than predicting no change. The protocol isolates whether the backbone’s latent space respects group composition.

## Key Results
- **Catastrophic collapse on non‑solvable groups**: For DINOv2 (geometry‑trained), normalized error exceeds the identity baseline at sequence length ~9 (vs ~15 for abelian), and absolute error is 3.0–3.8× higher.
- **Orientation information lost**: Cosine loss diverges far faster than MSE on solvable and non‑solvable groups, showing rapid degradation of directional coherence.
- **Algebraic failure mode**: A probe trained on abelian atomic steps fails to generalize to non‑solvable compositions, confirming that the barrier is algebraic complexity, not action cardinality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Constant‑depth circuits cannot solve the Word Problem**  
Constant‑depth ViTs lie in TC⁰ (constant‑depth, polynomial‑size circuits with majority gates). Non‑solvable groups (e.g., A₅ ⊂ SO(3)) require solving the Word Problem, which is NC¹‑complete. Under the conjecture TC⁰ ⊊ NC¹, TC⁰ circuits cannot compute functions with NC¹ logical depth. Hence ViTs cannot implement the iterated group product needed for non‑solvable transformations.

**Mechanism 2: Homomorphic embedding reduces to the Word Problem**  
Learning a group‑homomorphic visual embedding means that after a sequence of transformations, the latent state equals the product of the individual group actions applied to the initial state. For non‑solvable groups, computing this iterated product is equivalent to the Word Problem. Thus, a model achieving such an embedding would be solving an NC¹‑complete problem, impossible for constant‑depth ViTs under the complexity separation.

**Mechanism 3: Recursive linear probing exposes structural collapse**  
A frozen backbone combined with a linear probe trained on atomic steps tests whether the latent space supports group composition. Empirical results show that on non‑solvable groups, error (MSE and cosine) grows rapidly and surpasses the identity baseline by \(N\approx9\), while abelian groups remain coherent until \(N\approx15\). The faster divergence of cosine loss indicates loss of orientation information, consistent with the theoretical prediction that ViT latents cannot encode non‑solvable group structure.

## Foundational Learning

- **Group Theory (Derived Series & Solvability)**  
  *Why needed*: The paper classifies groups by their derived series; non‑solvability (presence of a perfect non‑abelian subgroup like A₅) maps to NC¹‑completeness.  
  *Quick check*: Why does the existence of a non‑trivial perfect subgroup (H = [H, H] ≠ {e}) in the derived series make the group non‑solvable and computationally harder?

- **Circuit Complexity (TC⁰ vs NC¹)**  
  *Why needed*: ViTs with constant depth belong to TC⁰; non‑solvable group operations require NC¹. The conjectured separation creates the expressivity barrier.  
  *Quick check*: What is the key operational difference between a TC⁰ circuit (constant depth, majority gates) and an NC¹ circuit (logarithmic depth, bounded fan‑in), and why does this matter for iterated non‑commutative operations?

- **Group Homomorphism & Word Problem**  
  *Why needed*: Spatial reasoning is formalized as learning a homomorphic embedding. The Word Problem (testing if a product equals identity) is NC¹‑complete. For non‑solvable groups, predicting composed states is equivalent to the Word Problem.  
  *Quick check*: If an encoder \(E\) satisfies \(E(g·I) = \rho(g)·E(I)\) for a faithful representation \(\rho\), why is predicting \(E\) applied to a long sequence of transformations equivalent to solving the Word Problem for that group?

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:  
Frozen Vision Transformer (ViT) Encoder → Linear Probe (T_φ) → Recursive Prediction Loop → Benchmark Evaluator  
(Detailed: Input image \(I_0\) → Encoder gives \(z_0 = E(I_0)\). For each step \(t\): combine \(z_t\) and action embedding \(e_s\) → \(T_\phi([z_t; e_s]) = \hat{z}_{t+1}\). Repeat. Compare \(\hat{z}_n\) to \(E(I_n)\).)

Critical path:  
1. Generate LSA dataset: render 3D objects under transformations for Levels 1–3, sample random walks up to length 20, store every step.  
2. Train linear probe \(T_\phi\) on atomic (\(N=1\)) transitions (50 epochs, Adam, lr = 1e‑4, batch = 1024).  
3. For \(N = 2..20\), run recursive rollout: start with \(z_0\), apply probe repeatedly with next action to get \(\hat{z}_1..\hat{z}_N\).  
4. Compute losses (MSE, cosine) between \(\hat{z}_N\) and true \(E(I_N)\); also compute identity baseline loss \(\|z_0 - E(I_N)\|\).  
5. Normalize loss by baseline to obtain collapse ratio; track sequence‑length dependence.

Design tradeoffs:  
- **Frozen backbone vs fine‑tuning**: Freezing isolates the intrinsic representation capacity of the pretrained model; fine‑tuning could allow the model to learn a new representation, conflating architectural limits with optimization.  
- **Linear probe vs nonlinear**: A perfect homomorphic embedding should be linearizable; a nonlinear probe might mask failures by learning complex corrections, hiding whether the backbone’s native space is algebraic.  
- **Loss choice**: MSE measures Euclidean fidelity; cosine tests directional preservation. Their divergence reveals which geometric properties are discarded.

Failure signatures:  
- **Baseline Ratio ≥ 1.0**: error worse than identity baseline, indicating complete structural collapse.  
- **Accelerated cosine divergence**: cosine loss grows much faster than MSE, showing loss of orientation information.  
- **Sequence‑length sensitivity gap**: collapse occurs at significantly smaller \(N\) for non‑solvable (≈ 9) than for abelian (≈ 15) in geometry‑aware models.

First 3 experiments:  
1. **Hierarchical Generalization Test**: Train a single linear probe on Level 1 (abelian) atomic steps, then test recursively on Levels 1, 2, 3. Expect poor generalization to Level 3 despite same action set—algebraic complexity, not cardinality, is the barrier.  
2. **Metric Sensitivity Analysis**: Track MSE and cosine loss trajectories per model/level. Supervised models (ViT‑B/16, ResNet‑50) should show extreme cosine/MSE divergence on Level 1 (due to invariance), while DINOv2 shows balanced metrics but rapid Level 3 collapse.  
3. **DINOv2 vs Supervised ViT Contrast**: Compare DINOv2 (geometry‑trained) with supervised ViT‑B/16. DINOv2 avoids immediate Level 1 collapse but exhibits similar Level 3 collapse rate as ViT‑B, isolating architectural limit from training objective.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**  
*How can we design vision architectures that achieve NC¹‑level expressivity for non‑solvable spatial reasoning while preserving stable training dynamics?*  
*Basis*: The conclusion explicitly identifies the need to reconcile logical depth with analog stability; no current solution exists.  
*Unresolved*: The paper establishes a barrier but offers no concrete architectural pathway; extensions like SE(3)‑Transformers are argued to remain within TC⁰.  
*What would resolve*: A novel model family that successfully models non‑solvable transformations (e.g., SO(3)) in the LSA benchmark without collapse, accompanied by theoretical proof of NC¹ expressivity.

**Open Question 2**  
*Can iterative inference mechanisms analogous to Chain‑of‑Thought stabilize error accumulation in continuous visual embedding spaces for long transformation sequences?*  
*Basis*: Section 4.5.1 discusses CoT as a theoretical bypass but highlights "analog stability": small approximation errors compound exponentially.  
*Unresolved*: No empirical investigation into stabilization techniques (e.g., noise injection, corrective feedback).  
*What would resolve*: Training a Vision Transformer with iterative unrolling that maintains homomorphic alignment on Level 3 tasks up to \(N=20\), with subexponential error growth.

**Open Question 3**  
*Does increasing model depth beyond strict constant bounds allow Transformers to escape TC⁰ and capture non‑solvable group dynamics in practice?*  
*Basis*: The theoretical bound applies to constant‑depth circuits; experiments only use fixed backbones (ViT‑B/16, ResNet‑50).  
*Unresolved*: Unclear whether practical depth increases (far beyond constant but still fixed) can provide the logarithmic logical depth required for NC¹‑level computations.  
*What would resolve*: A systematic depth‑sweep evaluation on LSA showing that error accumulation on non‑solvable sequences decreases monotonically with layer count, indicating a transition toward NC¹‑like expressivity.

## Limitations

- The theoretical barrier depends on the unproven conjecture TC⁰ ⊊ NC¹; if equality holds, the expressivity limit collapses.  
- The linear probe assumption may be too strict: a nonlinear probe could still extract group structure from ViT latents, weakening the conclusion.  
- The LSA dataset generation is underspecified (camera parameters, rendering engine), hindering exact replication and potentially introducing hidden biases.

## Next Checks

1. **Nonlinear probe evaluation**: Replace the linear probe with a small MLP and re‑run recursive testing. If collapse persists, it strengthens the claim that ViT latents lack algebraic structure; if collapse disappears, the linear probe is insufficient.  
2. **Exact dataset replication**: Implement the LSA pipeline with precise rendering parameters (e.g., Blender, camera intrinsics) and validate visual injectivity. Reproduce original error trajectories to confirm robustness.  
3. **Depth and recurrence study**: Evaluate ViTs with increased depth or recurrent attention (e.g., Transformer‑XL) to test whether effective logical depth > constant alleviates non‑solvable collapse, as predicted by the TC⁰ vs NC¹ boundary.
