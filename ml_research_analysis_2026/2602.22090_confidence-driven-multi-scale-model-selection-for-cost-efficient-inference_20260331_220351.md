# Confidence-Driven Multi-Scale Model Selection for Cost-Efficient Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.22090
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22090
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Confidence-driven routing reduces computational cost by 36‑60% while maintaining accuracy within 0.35% of the largest model on MMLU and showing generalizability to OOD and open-ended QA.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the high cost of using large language models for every query by proposing a dynamic routing mechanism. A small model first assesses its confidence for each query using two metrics: answer choice probability P(T) and a trained classifier on internal states P(IK). If confidence is high, the small model’s answer is returned; otherwise, the query is escalated to a larger model. This approach minimizes expensive large‑model invocations for queries that can be answered confidently by the small model.

Experiments on the MMLU benchmark with LLaMA models show that the cascade achieves accuracy within 0.35% of the 70B model while reducing computational cost by 36.46% for an 8B→70B setup. When applied to GPT‑4o API calls, token usage is reduced by approximately 60% with minimal performance drop. The method also demonstrates generalizability to out‑of‑distribution data and open‑ended QA tasks, suggesting broad applicability.

## Method Summary
The proposed system employs a cascade of models with increasing size. For each input query, a small model generates a response and computes two confidence scores: P(T) (the probability assigned to the chosen answer) and P(IK) (output of a classifier trained on the model’s internal hidden states). These scores are combined (e.g., via a threshold) to decide whether to accept the small model’s output or route the query to a larger model. The larger model then produces the final answer. Confidence thresholds are tuned on a validation set to balance cost and accuracy. This dynamic allocation reduces the average computational burden by avoiding large models for queries that are confidently answered by the small model.

## Key Results
- On MMLU with LLaMA models, the cascade matches the 70B model’s accuracy within 0.35% while cutting cost by 36.46% (8B→70B cascade).
- When applied to GPT‑4o API, token usage is reduced by approximately 60% with negligible performance drop.
- The approach generalizes to out‑of‑distribution and open‑ended QA tasks, indicating robustness beyond the training distribution.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works because a significant portion of queries are inherently easy and can be answered correctly by a small model with high confidence. The confidence metrics, especially P(IK) which leverages internal representations, effectively discriminate between easy and hard queries. By routing only the uncertain queries to larger models, the system minimizes the use of expensive models without sacrificing overall accuracy. The training of P(IK) on internal states allows it to capture signals beyond output probabilities, improving detection of when the small model is likely to be incorrect.

## Foundational Learning
- **Confidence Calibration**: Why needed: Reliable confidence estimates are crucial for correct routing decisions; miscalibrated confidence leads to misrouting. Quick check: Evaluate Expected Calibration Error (ECE) on a held‑out set; lower ECE indicates better calibration.
- **Model Cascades**: Why needed: They enable adaptive compute allocation, using smaller models for simple queries to save resources. Quick check: Plot cost versus accuracy for different thresholds to verify Pareto improvements.
- **Internal Knowledge Detection (P(IK))**: Why needed: Internal hidden states can provide earlier and more nuanced signals about model certainty than final output probabilities. Quick check: Train a classifier on hidden states and measure its accuracy in predicting whether the model’s answer is correct.
- **Out‑of‑Distribution Robustness**: Why needed: Real‑world deployments often encounter data from shifted distributions; the cascade must remain effective. Quick check: Test on diverse OOD benchmarks (e.g., HANS, BioMMLU) and compare accuracy drops to monolithic models.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Query -> Small Model -> Confidence Assessment (P(T) & P(IK)) -> [if high confidence: Output] ; [if low confidence: Large Model -> Output]
- **Critical path**: For high‑confidence queries: small model inference, confidence computation, output. For low‑confidence queries: small model inference, confidence computation, large model inference, output.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Confidence threshold selection impacts the cost‑accuracy tradeoff (higher threshold increases large model usage, improving accuracy but increasing cost); confidence assessment adds latency; choice of model sizes in the cascade affects overall efficiency.
- **Failure signatures**: Overconfident small model on hard queries (incorrect output accepted); underconfident small model on easy queries (unnecessary large model call); P(IK) classifier miscalibration leading to systematic misrouting.
- **First experiments**: (1) Sweep confidence thresholds on validation set to characterize cost‑accuracy curve; (2) Ablate confidence signals: compare using P(T) alone, P(IK) alone, and their combination; (3) Test different model pairs (e.g., 7B→13B, 13B→70B) to evaluate scalability of cost savings.
