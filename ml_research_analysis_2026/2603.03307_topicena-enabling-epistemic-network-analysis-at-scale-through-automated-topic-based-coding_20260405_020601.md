# TopicENA: Enabling Epistemic Network Analysis at Scale through Automated Topic-Based Coding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03307
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03307
- **Reference count:** 6
- **Primary result:** Replaces manual ENA coding with BERTopic's automated topic modeling to enable scalable network analysis of discourse; granularity must match data scale, medium thresholds balance co-occurrence density, and the method scales to massive corpora while recovering assignment-specific structures and performance-group differences.

## Executive Summary
Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA) traditionally relies on expert manual coding to capture co-occurrence patterns of conceptual elements in discourse. This paper presents TopicENA, an automated pipeline that substitutes BERTopic's neural topic modeling for human coding, thereby scaling ENA to corpora of hundreds of thousands of utterances. Evaluated on the ASAP 2.0 dataset (24,728 essays, 457,002 sentences), the authors show that topic granularity (controlled via BERTopic parameters) and the topic inclusion threshold critically affect network interpretability: coarser topics suit large datasets, while a medium threshold (~0.05) balances multi-concept representation and structural clarity. The approach successfully recovers seven assignment-specific topic clusters and reveals plausible epistemic network differences between high- and low-performing students. TopicENA thus opens ENA to large-scale learning analytics without sacrificing the relational richness central to the method.

## Method Summary
The method consists of a three-stage pipeline. First, essays are segmented into utterance-level units (sentences). Second, BERTopic processes the utterances: sentence embeddings (SBERT) are generated, dimensionality is reduced (UMAP), and density-based clustering (HDBSCAN) yields topics and a document-topic probability matrix; granularity is tuned by UMAP's `n_neighbors`. Third, a threshold (e.g., 0.05) binarizes the probability matrix, assigning each utterance to all topics with probability exceeding the threshold. Finally, an R-based ENA pipeline consumes the binary incidence matrix, computes co-occurrence networks (within utterance windows) for predefined groups (e.g., high/low scoring essays), and visualizes networks (node size = total edge weight, edge thickness = co-occurrence strength). Sensitivity to granularity and threshold is tested on two assignments; the optimized configuration is then applied to the full corpus to assess scalability.

## Key Results
- **Topic granularity must align with dataset scale:** Coarse topics (fewer, broader) yield interpretable networks for large assignments (e.g., 119k utterances), while fine topics (more specific) risk sparse, overlapping networks; for smaller datasets, finer granularity may be appropriate.
- **Topic inclusion threshold critically shapes network density:** A medium threshold (~0.05) optimally balances multi-concept representation and structural clarity; lower thresholds produce overly dense networks, higher thresholds yield degenerately sparse ones.
- **Scalability demonstrated:** The pipeline processes the full ASAP 2.0 corpus (457k utterances) without failure, recovers seven assignment-specific topic clusters, and reveals meaningful performance-group differences in epistemic networks, validating the automated substitution for manual coding.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Coarse-grained topic modeling improves epistemic network interpretability for large datasets by ensuring each topic has sufficient document support for stable co-occurrence patterns.
- **Mechanism:** For large corpora, coarser granularity (higher `n_neighbors` in BERTopic) merges semantically related subtopics into single nodes, preventing overly sparse or dense networks and maintaining meaningful edge strengths.
- **Core assumption:** Epistemic networks rely on observable co-occurrence frequencies; topics with too few documents produce unreliable connection estimates.
- **Evidence anchors:** abstract: "coarse topics work better for large datasets, fine topics for small"; section 5.1: Assignment 5 (119,052 utterances) yielded clearer networks under coarse granularity, while fine granularity produced sparse, overlapping networks.
- **Break condition:** If dataset size is insufficient to provide minimum document count per coarse topic (e.g., <100–200 docs per topic), coarsening may oversimplify semantics and mask distinctions.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The topic inclusion threshold critically controls network density, with a medium range (~0.05) balancing multi-concept representation and interpretable structure.
- **Mechanism:** The threshold converts BERTopic's continuous topic-document probabilities into binary presence. A low threshold assigns many topics per document, inflating co-occurrence counts and creating dense, uninterpretable networks. A high threshold restricts assignments, yielding sparse networks lacking structural signals. A medium threshold filters weakly associated topics while preserving meaningful multi-concept documents.
- **Core assumption:** ENA network clarity depends on an optimal signal-to-noise ratio in co-occurrence matrices; this ratio is non-monotonic with threshold strictness.
- **Evidence anchors:** abstract: "medium thresholds balancing co-occurrence density"; section 5.2: For both Assignment 4 and 5, threshold 0.05 produced clearer group differences than 0.01 (overly dense) or 0.10 (overly sparse).
- **Break condition:** If the underlying topic probability distribution is extremely skewed (most documents have very low max probabilities), even a medium threshold may yield near-zero multi-assignments, leading to degenerate sparsity.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** BERTopic's context-aware embedding-based topic modeling enables TopicENA to scale to massive corpora while maintaining semantic coherence and assignment-specific structure, overcoming LDA's bag-of-words limitations.
- **Mechanism:** BERTopic uses sentence embeddings (SBERT) and density-based clustering (HDBSCAN) to group documents by semantic similarity in a contextual vector space. This preserves meaning across varied phrasing and works robustly with short texts (utterances). The resulting topics are semantically coherent units that replace manual codes, allowing ENA to process datasets 10–100× larger than prior manual-coded studies.
- **Core assumption:** High-quality, contextual topic representations are a sufficient substitute for expert-defined codes in capturing the conceptual dimensions relevant to epistemic co-occurrence.
- **Evidence anchors:** abstract: "successfully scales to massive corpora, recovering assignment-specific topic structures"; section 3.3: BERTopic's design chosen over LDA; section 5.3: On 457,002 utterances, BERTopic recovered 7 topics matching assignments and produced interpretable group difference networks.
- **Break condition:** If the corpus is multilingual or domain-specific with out-of-distribution language for SBERT, topic coherence may degrade, harming network interpretability.

## Foundational Learning
### Epistemic Network Analysis (ENA)
- **Why needed here:** TopicENA extends ENA's core method—modeling co-occurrence of conceptual elements as networks—but replaces manual coding with automated topic units. Understanding ENA's focus on relational structure (not frequency) is essential to grasp why topic co-occurrence, not just topic presence, drives the analysis.
- **Quick check question:** How does an ENA network differ from a simple co-occurrence matrix or word cloud in representing knowledge structure?

### Neural Topic Modeling (BERTopic)
- **Why needed here:** BERTopic is the engine that generates the semantic units (topics) for TopicENA. Its parameters (UMAP, HDBSCAN) directly control granularity, and its probabilistic outputs enable multi-topic assignment. Knowing its embedding-based, context-aware nature explains advantages over LDA.
- **Quick check question:** Why might LDA produce less interpretable topics than BERTopic for sentence-level essay analysis?

### Multi-Topic Assignment via Probability Thresholding
- **Why needed here:** This design choice transforms BERTopic's soft topic distributions into the hard co-occurrence data ENA requires. The threshold is the critical knob that trades off concept multiplicity against network density. Without this step, default single-topic assignment would discard multimodal discourse information.
- **Quick check question:** What would happen to the ENA network if every document were assigned only its single highest-probability topic?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Text Segmentation -> Topic Modeling (BERTopic) -> Threshold Encoding -> ENA Network Construction -> Visualization & Comparison  

**Critical path:** The sequence is linear but with iterative tuning loops between Steps 2–4. The most critical non-linear dependency: threshold choice must be informed by inspecting the distribution of max probabilities from Step 2. Granularity (Step 2) must be set before thresholding.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Granularity vs. Scale:** Finer granularity increases semantic resolution but risks sparse co-occurrence on small datasets; coarser granularity stabilizes large networks but may conflate distinct concepts.
- **Threshold vs. Density:** Lower threshold increases multi-assignment, risking dense, uninterpretable networks; higher threshold risks losing meaningful multi-concept utterances and producing sparse networks.
- **Context vs. Control:** BERTopic's context-awareness improves coherence but reduces direct interpretability of topic-word weights compared to LDA's clear probability distributions.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Degenerate networks:** All nodes isolated (sparse) or all nodes fully connected (dense). Indicates threshold too high or too low, or granularity misaligned with data scale.
- **Topic-semantic mismatch:** Top N words in a topic are incoherent (e.g., mixing unrelated domains). Suggests granularity too fine or embedding model mismatch.
- **No group differences:** Subtract network shows no edges or all edges in same direction. Could mean threshold swamps signal, or grouping variable unrelated to conceptual structure.
- **Memory/time explosion:** BERTopic step fails on large corpus. Requires adjusting HDBSCAN parameters or reducing embedding dimensions.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Granularity Sensitivity Test:** Run BERTopic on a medium-sized assignment (e.g., Assignment 4) with coarse (`n_neighbors=60`), medium (`35`), and fine (`18`) settings. Generate ENA networks for each. Inspect for interpretable group differences and topic coherence. Goal: Find granularity where topics are meaningful *and* networks show expected structural variation.
2. **Threshold Sweep & Density Profiling:** Using a fixed granularity from Exp. 1, vary `topic_inclusion_th` (e.g., 0.01, 0.05, 0.10). For each, calculate average number of topics per utterance and network density. Plot density curve. Choose threshold in the "elbow" region where density is moderate and group differences are visible.
3. **Full-Scalability Stress Test:** Apply chosen granularity and threshold from Exps. 1–2 to the full ASAP 2.0 corpus (all 7 assignments, 457k utterances). Verify: (a) topics align with assignment themes (inspect top words), (b) networks computable within memory limits, (c) subtract networks reveal plausible performance-based differences. Validates parameter configuration at scale.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Parameter tuning across dataset scales:** How do variations in topic modeling parameters (e.g., granularity via UMAP/HDBSCAN settings, inclusion threshold) affect the stability, interpretability, and reproducibility of epistemic networks across different dataset scales? *What evidence would resolve it:* A large-scale experiment manipulating parameters across diverse corpora, quantifying network stability with metrics like Jaccard similarity of edges and interpretability via expert evaluation.
2. **Hybrid manual-automated coding:** Can TopicENA integrate with theory-driven manual coding schemes to produce hybrid epistemic networks that balance automated scalability with conceptual fidelity, and under what conditions does such integration enhance or diminish analytical outcomes? *What evidence would resolve it:* Comparative studies applying pure manual ENA, pure TopicENA, and hybrid approaches (e.g., using manual codes as seeds/constraints) to the same dataset, comparing network structures, statistical power, and alignment with hypotheses.
3. **Multilingual applicability:** Does TopicENA maintain consistent performance and semantic validity when applied to multilingual or cross-cultural text corpora, and how do language-specific linguistic and cultural features influence topic quality and network interpretability? *What evidence would resolve it:* Replication studies using multilingual datasets, assessing topic coherence (e.g., PMI scores), network meaningfulness via cross-linguistic expert validation, and potential need for language-specific model tuning.
4. **Corpus feature moderation:** How do corpus-level characteristics (e.g., domain specificity, text length distribution,genre conventions) moderate the effectiveness of topic granularity and inclusion threshold settings, and can we develop adaptive configuration guidelines? *What evidence would resolve it:* Meta-analytic research applying TopicENA to heterogeneous corpora (e.g., social media, scientific articles, dialogue transcripts), modeling how corpus features predict optimal parameter ranges.
5. **Construct validity against manual coding:** What is the convergent and discriminant validity of topic-based epistemic networks compared to those derived from expert manual coding in representing underlying cognitive or epistemic structures, and what are the implications for theoretical interpretations in learning analytics? *What evidence would resolve it:* Validation studies where a subset of data is manually coded; compare networks from manual coding and TopicENA using graph similarity measures and ability to predict external criteria (e.g., learning outcomes).

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility gaps:** Exact versions and hyperparameters for BERTopic (UMAP/HDBSCAN) and the R ENA pipeline (normalization method) are unreported; preprocessing details (lowercasing, stopwords, embedding model) are unspecified.
- **External validation:** Claims rely solely on the ASAP 2.0 dataset; generalizability to other discourse genres (e.g., discussion forums, interviews) is unproven.
- **Topic coherence assessment:** No quantitative topic quality metrics (e.g., coherence scores) are reported; interpretability is judged via visual inspection only, risking subjectivity.

## Confidence
- **Granularity effect:** Medium (supported by internal experiments but lacking cross-dataset validation)
- **Threshold optimization:** Medium (evidence from two assignments with visual inspection; no automated metric guides selection)
- **Scalability via BERTopic:** Medium (stress test on 457k utterances is compelling, but topic semantic alignment was manually verified; no comparison to alternative neural topic models)

## Next Checks
1. **Parameter specification audit:** Re-run Experiments 1–3 with explicitly logged BERTopic (`n_components`, `min_cluster_size`) and ENA (normalization type) parameters to establish a reproducible baseline.
2. **Normalization sensitivity test:** Apply multiple ENA normalization methods (row-wise, column-wise, global max) to the same binary incidence matrix and quantify changes in group difference significance (e.g., via permutation tests).
3. **Cross-corpus generalization:** Apply the validated TopicENA pipeline to a non-essay discourse dataset (e.g., MOOC discussion posts) and evaluate whether assignment-specific topic structures and meaningful group differences replicate.
