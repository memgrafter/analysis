# MAP-Diff: Multi-Anchor Guided Diffusion for Progressive 3D Whole-Body Low-Dose PET Denoising

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.02012
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02012
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** Improves PSNR by 1.23 dB on Siemens data (43.71 dB vs. 42.48 dB) and outperforms all baselines on external United Imaging scanner (34.42 dB PSNR).

## Executive Summary
Low-dose PET imaging is limited by noise due to reduced radiation. Existing diffusion models produce denoising trajectories that do not align with the progressive nature of dose acquisition. MAP-Diff addresses this by using clinically observed intermediate-dose scans as anchors to regularize the diffusion reverse process. It calibrates anchor timesteps to match real degradation statistics and applies timestep-dependent weighting. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on internal Siemens data and generalizes to an external United Imaging scanner, enabling dose-consistent intermediate reconstructions from ultra-low-dose inputs.

## Method Summary
MAP-Diff builds on a conditional 3D Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) framework. Training utilizes paired multi-dose PET volumes: an ultra-low-dose image as condition and a full-dose image as target. During the diffusion process, the model predicts noise and derives a clean estimate $\hat{x}_0$ at each timestep $t$. The core innovation is an anchor loss that supervises $\hat{x}_0$ toward a pre-assigned intermediate-dose anchor $a(t)$. Anchor assignments are determined by a piecewise mapping over timesteps; the boundaries $\tau_1, \tau_2, \tau_3$ are calibrated beforehand by matching the degradation signature (NMAE, 1-SSIM) of simulated diffusion steps to those of the real intermediate-dose scans. The anchor loss is weighted by $w(t) = (1 - t/T)^p$, which down-weights early high-noise states. This shapes the reverse trajectory to follow a dose-consistent progression, yielding intermediate reconstructions that correspond to realistic intermediate-dose images.

## Key Results
- On internal Siemens data: PSNR 43.71 dB (+1.23 dB vs. 3D DDPM), SSIM 0.986, NMAE 0.103.
- On external United Imaging scanner: PSNR 34.42 dB, NMAE 0.141, outperforming all CNN, GAN, Transformer, and diffusion baselines.
- Provides dose-consistent intermediate reconstructions validated against real intermediate-dose scans.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Anchor trajectory regularization.**  
MAP-Diff constrains the diffusion reverse trajectory to be dose-aligned by imposing supervision from clinically observed intermediate-dose images. The timestep axis is partitioned into zones mapped to anchor doses (e.g., 1/10, 1/4, 1/2, full). At each timestep $t$, the model’s clean estimate $\hat{x}_0(x_t, t|y)$ is supervised toward its corresponding anchor $a(t)$. This shapes the generative path to follow a physically plausible dose progression rather than an unconstrained denoising path. *Evidence:* The paper explicitly states this in the abstract and Section 2.3, and ablations show that removing anchor loss (S1 vs. S4) degrades performance.

**Mechanism 2: Calibration via degradation matching.**  
Anchor timestep boundaries are calibrated so diffusion noise levels statistically match the degradation between real dose pairs. For each real anchor dose $a_j$, a degradation signature (NMAE, 1-SSIM vs. full-dose) is computed. The simulated forward diffusion is run to find timestep $t^*_j$ whose $x_t$ exhibits a matching signature. The final boundaries $\tau_1, \tau_2, \tau_3$ are averages of these matched timesteps across the dataset. *Evidence:* Described in Section 2.3.1. *Break condition:* If the degradation signature is non-monotonic or scanner-specific, matched timesteps may misalign.

**Mechanism 3: Timestep-dependent loss weighting.**  
A weighting function $w(t) = (1 - t/T)^p$ stabilizes training by preventing over-constraining high-noise early states. The anchor loss is small near $t=T$ (high noise) and larger near $t=0$ (low noise), acknowledging that early noisy predictions are less reliable. *Evidence:* Paper Section 2.4 and Table 2 show constant weighting (S8) causes large performance drop (PSNR 41.856 vs. 43.710), confirming the need for adaptive weighting.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Conditional denoiser (εθ(x_t, t|y)) → Compute clean estimate x̂0 → Anchor mapping module (assigns a(t) based on calibrated τ boundaries) → Loss calculator (combines L_noise + weighted L_anch) → Backpropagation. Calibration script (pre-step) determines τ by matching degradation signatures.

**Critical path:**  
1. Preprocess multi-dose dataset, compute body mask M.  
2. Calibration: for each anchor dose, find t* minimizing ||dsim(t) - dclin_j||²; average across training set → τ boundaries.  
3. Initialize εθ; for each training iteration: sample t, compute x_t from forward process, predict noise ε, derive x̂0.  
4. Retrieve anchor a(t) for t using piecewise mapping on τ.  
5. Compute loss L = L_noise + λ · w(t) · ||x̂0 - a(t)||².  
6. Backpropagate, update network weights.  
7. Inference: run reverse diffusion from t=T to 0 using only ULD condition y; optionally record x̂0 at τ1, τ2, τ3.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Anchor density:* More anchors (e.g., 1/20, 1/10, 1/4, 1/2, full) better approximate dose curve but increase memory and may overlap if degradation non-linear; paper ablates anchor count.  
- *Hard vs. soft zoning:* Hard piecewise mapping is simple and stable; soft interpolation could smooth transitions but adds hyperparameters.  
- *Weight function:* Polynomial w(t) is hand-designed; learned weighting might adapt but risks overfitting.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Trajectory drift:* Intermediate outputs at τ show no improvement vs. standard diffusion → λ too low or boundaries mis-calibrated.  
- *Over-smoothing at early stages:* 1/10-dose reconstruction has low SSIM → likely constant weighting (S8) forcing high-noise anchors too strongly.  
- *Cross-scanner performance drop:* If calibrated on Siemens only, boundaries may not align with United Imaging degradation → need recalibration or domain-invariant signatures.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Validate anchor calibration: For a held-out validation set, plot degradation signatures of real anchors (dclin_j) and simulated diffusion (dsim(t)); confirm matched t*_j are monotonic and that dsim(τ_j) ≈ dclin_j.  
2. Test weighting function: Train small models (e.g., 2D slices, few epochs) with constant, linear, polynomial weighting; compare intermediate-stage SSIM/NMAE and training stability. Expect polynomial to stabilize early-stage loss.  
3. Intermediate output sanity check: After minimal training, run inference on a few samples and save x̂0 at τ1, τ2; compute NMAE against real a1, a2; expect descending error (1/10 stage worst, 1/2 best) but better than no anchors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Adaptive anchor selection:** How can anchor selection be made data-driven rather than relying on fixed clinical dose levels? The paper mentions future work on adaptive selection but no mechanism is provided.
- **Diffusion noise model adequacy:** Does Gaussian diffusion corruption adequately model real PET noise statistics across all dose levels? The paper uses Gaussian as a tractable surrogate, but its fidelity to Poisson-Gaussian PET noise is uncertain.
- **Calibration metric sufficiency:** Are NMAE and SSIM sufficient for calibrating diffusion timesteps to real PET dose degradation? These metrics may not capture all aspects of PET degradation (e.g., texture, bias).
- **Optimal weighting schedule:** Is the fixed polynomial weighting w(t) optimal across stages and scanners? The choice is heuristic; learned weighting might improve trajectory regularization.
- **Cross-scanner generalization:** Why does performance drop significantly on the external United Imaging scanner? The root causes (e.g., resolution, scatter differences) and remedies are not analyzed.

## Limitations
- Architectural and hyperparameter details (e.g., 3D U-Net channels, attention heads, λ, p) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- Anchor calibration procedure is vaguely described (search range, metric weighting, averaging), risking boundary misalignment.
- Dataset availability is uncertain (Ultra-Low-Dose PET Challenge may not be public), limiting external verification.
- The method shows a large performance gap between internal Siemens and external United Imaging scanners, indicating limited domain robustness.

If alignment is poor, explore domain-invariant calibration metrics or fine-tuning on target scanner data.
