# INDUCTION: Finite-Structure Concept Synthesis in First-Order Logic

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.18956
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18956
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Introduces a solver-verifiable benchmark for first-order concept synthesis; finds a strong parsimony–generalization gap where low-bloat formulas generalize up to +75 percentage points better.

## Executive Summary
INDUCTION is a benchmark for evaluating first-order logic concept synthesis from finite relational structures. It defines three tasks—FullObs (match target everywhere), Contrastive (discriminate YES/NO worlds), and Existential Completion (reason under missing facts)—with correctness mechanically verifiable via finite model checking. Instance generation uses trap mechanisms and version-space tracking to create difficulty gradients. Results show persistent hard families (e.g., lift-hard patterns) and a strong parsimony–generalization gap: among training-correct formulas, those with minimal syntactic bloat achieve dramatically higher held-out accuracy. Elite models like GPT-5.4 excel in existential completion, while bloat strongly correlates with overfitting, highlighting succinctness as a proxy for abstract conceptualization.

## Method Summary
The benchmark requires models to output a first-order formula φ(x) that matches an implicitly defined target predicate T(x) across multiple finite worlds (domains 5–12 elements, unary predicates P,Q and binary R,S). For FullObs, φ must exactly match T on all worlds; for Contrastive (CI), worlds are labeled YES/NO and φ must match T on YES and fail on NO; for Existential Completion (EC), some ground atoms are unknown and φ must be compatible with T under some completion. Correctness is determined by exhaustive evaluation over each finite domain, grounding quantifiers into propositional circuits and checking with an SMT solver (Z3) for EC. World generation incrementally adds worlds that eliminate candidate hypotheses from a frozen pool (~1500 formulas) to control difficulty; CI builds YES worlds that leave near-miss traps alive and NO worlds that kill them. Metrics include raw validity, budgeted accuracy (AST ≤ gold+Δ), bloat rate, and held-out generalization.

## Key Results
- Parsimony–generalization gap: Among train-valid formulas, those with AST size ≤ gold+1 achieve 76.7–98.0% holdout exact-match, while above-gold solutions drop to 14.4–53.3% (gap up to +75 pp).
- Elite model performance: GPT-5.4 leads in EC (93.5% validity, 64.0% budgeted @Δ=25); GPT-5.2 achieves higher raw CI accuracy (82.5%) but produces more bloated formulas; Grok4 excels in FullObs (50.7%) but with low coverage (~67%).
- Persistent hard families: Lift-hard patterns consistently challenge all models; formula bloat strongly correlates with overfitting to training worlds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Solver-Verifiable Finite Model Checking
Correctness reduces to Boolean evaluation over finite domains. For each world, the formula φ(x) is grounded by substituting all domain elements for free variables, yielding a propositional circuit. The evaluator checks whether the circuit’s output matches the target extension for every object. In EC, an SMT solver (Z3) checks if there exists a truth assignment to unknown atoms such that the match holds. This eliminates semantic ambiguity and makes assessment deterministic. Core assumption: finite domains and closed-world (or controlled partial) semantics ensure tractable exhaustive evaluation. Evidence: Section 2 states evaluation reduces to finite model checking; grounding is standard in finite FOL. Break condition: If φ cannot be parsed or grounding exceeds solver resource limits, correctness cannot be assessed, yielding parse errors or missing outputs.

### Mechanism 2: Difficulty-Controlled Instance Generation via Trap Mechanisms & Version-Space Diagnostics
Instance difficulty is tuned by maintaining a frozen pool of candidate hypotheses (simple shortcuts, near-misses, distractors). For FullObs, each new world must eliminate at least one surviving hypothesis from previous worlds. For CI, YES worlds are built to allow 1–2 near-miss traps to survive; each NO world is crafted to match exactly one such trap, forcing discriminative learning. Version-space metrics track the number of consistent candidates, providing a continuous difficulty axis. Core assumption: Plausible shortcut hypotheses exist in a finite pool that models would consider before converging. By selectively eliminating them, the generator ensures tasks are neither trivial nor infeasible. Evidence: Section 4.2 describes kill tracking; Section 4.3 details trap-based contrastive construction. Break condition: If the hypothesis pool misses actual model shortcuts, some instances may be accidentally too easy or impossible; filters catch trivialities but survivor bands may still be inadequate.

### Mechanism 3: Parsimony-Aware Scoring & Bloat–Generalization Correlation
The benchmark reports budgeted accuracy that rewards formulas close in AST size to the gold template. Analysis shows that among formulas valid on training worlds, those with minimal syntactic bloat generalize far better to held-out worlds. This confirms Occam’s razor: succinct formulas capture the underlying rule without encoding accidental coincidences from training data. Core assumption: Among formulas fitting the training set, syntactic compactness correlates with conceptual purity and robustness. Evidence: Abstract and Section 6.1 demonstrate the gap; Table 2 and within-problem control (Table 3) directly evidence near-monotonic decay in holdout accuracy as AST increases. Break condition: If gold formulas themselves are unnecessarily complex or if models discover genuinely simple but non-canonical equivalents, AST budget may penalize correct solutions, weakening the correlation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Finite model checking**: Why needed: Correctness must be mechanically precise across all domain elements; finite domains allow exhaustive grounding of quantified formulas into propositional circuits for solver evaluation. Quick check: Given domain {a0,a1,a2} and formula `(exists y (R x y))`, list for each x whether ∃y R(x,y) holds. What if some R facts are unknown?
- **Version space**: Why needed: To measure and control difficulty; the generator tracks which candidate hypotheses remain consistent with observed worlds, ensuring each new world is informative (eliminates at least one). Quick check: With candidate concepts {P(x), Q(x), (exists y (R x y))}, after seeing one world where T = {a0}, which survive? What must a second world do to eliminate the rest?
- **AST-based bloat measurement**: Why needed: Raw validity can be achieved by bloated case-splitting formulas that overfit; AST size provides a computable, language-agnostic proxy for complexity. Budgeted scoring uses gold-relative AST thresholds to distinguish compact generalization from patching. Quick check: Compare Gold = `(and (P x) (exists y (R x y)))` (AST 7) with Candidate A = `(or (and (P x) (exists y (R x y))) (and (not (P x)) (exists y (S x y))))` (AST 13) and Candidate B = `(and (P x) (exists y (R x y)) (exists z (S z z)))` (AST 9). Which are compact? Which might overfit?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Gold Formula Selector -> World Generator -> Partial Observer -> Instance Curation -> Model Inference Engine -> Evaluator/Solver -> Metrics Aggregator.
Critical path: Gold formula → world generation (with kill/trap) → filtering → model query → AST parse → grounding/evaluation → metric calculation. EC adds an SMT-based completion quantification step.
Design tradeoffs:
- Generative success vs. difficulty: Tight survivor bands improve discriminative power but reduce problem yield; very high quantifier depth or large domains may cause generation failures.
- AST budget Δ: Small Δ strictly tests parsimony but may penalize equivalent non-canonical formulas; larger Δ captures more valid solutions but risks including bloated overfits.
- Signature size: Limited to unary P,Q and binary R,S to keep grounding tractable; richer signatures increase expressivity but blow up solver time.
- Equality usage: Allowing `(= x y)` enables case-splitting shortcuts; benchmark permits it but measures correlation with bloat.
Failure signatures:
- Parse errors: malformed S-expressions, variable name violations, incorrect arity.
- Missing outputs: model times out or refuses (e.g., Grok4’s 67.2% coverage).
- High bloat, low holdout gen: AST >> gold+25 but matches training; often uses large disjunctions or nested case splits.
- CI NO-fail: formula accidentally matches a NO world, indicating reliance on a surviving trap.
- EC unsolvable: even with best completion, minimum mismatches >1, suggesting structural conflict.
First 3 experiments:
1. Simple band smoke test: Run all models on FullObs `simple` (QD=1, k=4). Expect near-perfect accuracy; verifies parsing and solver integration.
2. Trap effectiveness check (CI core): For a fixed problem, verify trap pool size and that each NO world kills ≥1 survivor. If models still solve easily, survivor band too tight; widen and regenerate.
3. Bloat–generalization pilot: For each model on FullObs `medium`, collect train-correct formulas, bin by `AST(φ) – AST(φ★)`, compute holdout exact-match on 5 new worlds. Expect steep drop as Δ increases; if not, recalibrate Δ or check holdout distribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How does performance scale with increased relational vocabulary size and introduction of constants/functions? Basis: Paper notes future work on richer signatures. Why unresolved: Controlled difficulty relies on limited signature; larger signatures may reveal new failure modes. Resolution: Generate variants with more predicates/higher arity, evaluate models.
- **Open Question 2**: Can we define a more robust simplicity measure than AST that better predicts generalization? Basis: Paper acknowledges AST is only a proxy and does not do equivalence checking. Why unresolved: Different syntaxes express same concept; AST may not capture deeper simplicity. Resolution: Correlate alternative measures (minimal DNF, Kolmogorov approximations) with holdout generalization.
- **Open Question 3**: How to adapt INDUCTION to evaluate abductive or causal reasoning? Basis: Conclusion mentions future work on abductive/causal synthesis. Why unresolved: Current tasks are deductive; abduction/causality require different semantics (e.g., explaining observations via hypotheses not guaranteed true). Resolution: Design new tasks with appropriate solvers for abductive/causal correctness.
- **Open Question 4**: Why are lift-hard patterns consistently difficult? Basis: Paper identifies lift-hard as a hard family but doesn’t explain why. Why unresolved: Could be due to quantifier nesting, relational dependence, or combination. Resolution: Controlled error analysis swapping factors (e.g., flatten quantifiers) to isolate source.
- **Open Question 5**: What is the mechanistic relationship between formula bloat and overfitting? Basis: Paper shows correlation but not causality. Why unresolved: Bloat may be symptom of search limitations or overfitting to spurious correlations. Resolution: Train models with parsimony regularization or inspect search trajectories if accessible.

## Limitations
- Hypothesis pool composition unknown: Exact enumeration and near-miss mutant generation details not specified, hindering reproduction and confidence in difficulty control.
- Prompting details incomplete: Few-shot examples, JSON schema, and decoding parameters used in evaluation are not fully documented; results may depend on these.
- AST measurement ambiguity: Exact node-counting rules (e.g., for equality, nested `and`/`or`) are unspecified, impeding precise bloat quantification.

## Confidence
- Solver-verifiable correctness: High
- Trap-based difficulty control: Medium
- Parsimony–generalization gap: High within trained families; Low for extrapolation beyond provided templates
- "Lift-hard" persistent difficulty: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Reconstruct and stress-test the hypothesis pool: Generate an expanded pool (e.g., 10k formulas) using described tiered approach, re-run trap generation to see if difficulty gradient and survivor band stability persist.
2. AST-budget invariance test: For fixed gold formulas, derive multiple logically equivalent but syntactically diverse correct formulas (e.g., via De Morgan, quantifier pushdown). Test whether parsimony–generalization gap holds across canonical variants.
3. Zero-shot family generalization: Train on all instances from all but one gold template family (e.g., "transitive closure"), test on held-out instances from that unseen family. Determine if low-bloat advantage extends to novel concept structures.
