# LITTA: Late-Interaction and Test-Time Alignment for Visually-Grounded Multimodal Retrieval
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.26683
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26683
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Multi-query retrieval (Q=3 or 5) consistently improves NDCG@10 and Recall@k over single-query baselines, with largest gains in industrial manuals (NDCG@10 from 39.05 to 61.39) and average NDCG@10 rising from 64.2 to 72.0.

## Executive Summary
LITTA tackles visually-grounded document retrieval where complex layouts and weak query-document lexical overlap hinder performance. It introduces a test-time adaptation strategy: generate multiple complementary query variants via an LLM, retrieve candidate pages for each using a frozen late-interaction vision retriever, and fuse results with Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF). Evaluated across computer science, pharmaceutical, and industrial manual domains, LITTA shows robust improvements in retrieval metrics, with accuracy directly tunable via the number of query variants, offering a practical balance for latency-sensitive applications.

## Method Summary
Given an original query, LITTA uses an LLM (Gemma 3N) to produce Q-1 semantically faithful but lexically diverse variants. Each variant is processed independently by a frozen late-interaction retriever (Nemotron ColEmbed V2), which encodes queries and pre-indexed page embeddings, computes MaxSim scores for fine-grained token-region matching, and retrieves top-k candidates. The candidate lists from all variants are pooled and aggregated via RRF, which sums reciprocal ranks to produce a final ranking that emphasizes pages consistently high across variants. This approach avoids retraining, leveraging test-time query diversification to enhance coverage and robustness.

## Key Results
- Multi-query retrieval (Q=3 or 5) improves NDCG@10 and Recall@k over single-query (Q=1) across all domains.
- Industrial manuals show dramatic gains: NDCG@10 increases from 39.05 (Q=1) to 53.67 (Q=3) and 61.39 (Q=5).
- Average NDCG@10 across domains rises from 64.2 to 72.0 with Q=3.
- Accuracy-efficiency trade-off is controlled by the number of query variants Q, with Q=3 often optimal and Q=5 beneficial for difficult domains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Query Diversification Increases Alignment Likelihood**
- Claim: Generating multiple complementary query variants increases the chance that at least one aligns with the evidence page's terminology or visual conventions.
- Mechanism: LLM reformulates the query into diverse variants (e.g., synonyms, abbreviations) processed by the retriever. Variants better matching page tokens yield higher MaxSim scores, surfacing relevant pages missed by original phrasing.
- Core assumption: Evidence page contains lexical/visual cues activatable by a semantically faithful reformulation.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and section 3.3 emphasize improved evidence coverage and reduced sensitivity to phrasing. Corpus evidence weak; assumption inferred from design and results.
- Break condition: Variants lose core intent or evidence is purely visual without text.

**Mechanism 2: Late-Interaction Sensitivity to Lexical Shifts**
- Claim: Late-interaction (MaxSim) scoring allows each variant to match fine-grained visual regions, making system sensitive to small lexical shifts.
- Mechanism: Retriever uses MaxSim to compute per-token maximum similarities. A variant mentioning specific terms (e.g., in table captions) activates different page tokens than broader phrasing, surfacing pages with localized visual evidence.
- Core assumption: Relevant evidence is concentrated in specific visual regions and late-interaction can isolate them with lexical anchors.
- Evidence anchors: Sections 2.1 and 3.4 describe fine-grained interactions. Neighboring papers support late-interaction benefits.
- Break condition: Evidence is purely visual without text or page tokens not discriminative.

**Mechanism 3: RRF Aggregation for Robust Fusion**
- Claim: Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) aggregates ranked lists to emphasize pages consistently ranked highly, reducing sensitivity to single-variant noise.
- Mechanism: RRF scores each page as sum over variants of 1/(k_RRF + rank_i(p)). Pages near top in multiple lists get higher scores; score-agnostic handling of heterogeneous scales.
- Core assumption: Correct evidence page, when retrieved by at least one variant, will be ranked reasonably well by other faithful variants.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and section 3.5 describe RRF's robustness. Section 4.2 shows Recall@k improvements, indicating retrieval of additional relevant pages.
- Break condition: Correct page retrieved by only one variant and ranked low, or all variants rank it low.

- Why needed: Distinguish LITTA from retraining or fine-tuning; gains come from diversifying query input to a fixed model.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
LLM Query Generator -> Frozen Late-Interaction Retriever -> Candidate Pool & RRF Aggregator -> Final Ranked List

**Critical Path:**
1. Original user query arrives.
2. LLM generates Q-1 expanded queries.
3. For each query variant (including original):
   - Encode query tokens.
   - ANN search on index to get top-k candidate pages.
   - Late-interaction (MaxSim) re-ranking of these k candidates.
4. Pool all Q * k candidates (with ranks per variant).
5. Compute RRF score for each pooled page.
6. Sort by RRF, take top-K evidence pages.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Accuracy vs. Latency: Controlled by Q (number of variants) and k (per-variant depth). Q=3 often optimal; Q=5 helps difficult domains but doubles cost.
- Diversity vs. Faithfulness: More diverse variants cover more evidence but risk off-topic queries. LLM prompt design constrains this.
- Fusion Robustness: RRF handles score scale differences but assumes correct pages get decent ranks from multiple faithful variants.

**Failure Signatures:**
- No expansion benefit: NDCG@10 identical for Q=1 vs Q>1. Indicates lexically consistent domain, unfaithful variants, or non-lexically retrievable evidence.
- Recall gain without precision gain: Recall@k improves but Hit@k or NDCG@10 lags. Suggests more relevant pages retrieved but with more noise or lower ranks.
- Degradation with higher Q: Performance drops from Q=3 to Q=5. Likely noisy variants overwhelm fusion, especially with small k.
- Domain-specific variance: Large gains in industrial manuals but smaller in C.S. correlates with terminology heterogeneity and visual evidence concentration.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. Baseline replication: Run single-query retrieval (Q=1) with frozen ColEmbed-v2 on all domains. Verify NDCG@10 matches paper's values.
2. Ablation - Fusion Method: Replace RRF with score averaging or Borda count for Q=3. Compare NDCG@10; expect RRF best.
3. Variant Diversity vs. Quality: Manually inspect Q=3 variants for a subset, categorize faithfulness and diversity, correlate with per-query NDCG@10 improvement.

## Limitations
- LLM-generated variants may lack faithfulness or introduce noise, affecting retrieval quality.
- Mechanism relies on lexical matching to visual tokens; purely visual evidence (e.g., unlabeled diagrams) remains unreachable.
- RRF assumes correct pages achieve decent ranks across multiple faithful variants; if only one variant retrieves it, fusion may fail.
- Domain-specific gains may be confounded by other factors like image quality or layout regularity.
