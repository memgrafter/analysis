# Laya: A LeJEPA Approach to EEG via Latent Prediction over Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16281  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16281  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Laya-S (10% pretraining data) achieves highest mean balanced accuracy (0.597) on EEG-Bench clinical tasks, outperforming LaBraM (0.573) and LUNA (0.560).

## Executive Summary
Laya is the first EEG foundation model based on Latent Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (LeJEPA), addressing the limitation of reconstruction-based self-supervised learning (SSL) methods that overfit to high-variance artifacts. By replacing signal reconstruction with latent-space prediction across temporally masked regions and stabilizing training with Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg), Laya learns representations more aligned with clinical phenotypes. Remarkably, when pretrained on only 10% of the data (Laya-S), it achieves state-of-the-art mean balanced accuracy of 0.597 on the EEG-Bench clinical suite, surpassing LaBraM (0.573) and LUNA (0.560), with particularly strong gains in seizure detection (+10.8%), mild traumatic brain injury classification (+4.1%), and artifact classification (+5.6%). Additional analysis shows enhanced noise robustness and embeddings that clearly demarcate seizure events, demonstrating the promise of latent prediction objectives for neurophysiological representation learning.

## Method Summary
Laya’s pretraining framework follows LeJEPA: raw EEG (resampled to 250 Hz, band-pass 0.5–100 Hz, notch filtered) is split into patches (25 samples each). A dynamic channel mixer then aggregates per-channel patches into a fixed-length sequence of latent brain states (S), using learned queries and Fourier-encoded electrode coordinates to handle variable montages. A transformer encoder processes S in two parallel passes: one over the full sequence to produce target embeddings (Z, with stop-gradient), and one over a temporally masked version (Z_ctx). Both embeddings are projected to lower-dimensional spaces; a lightweight predictor maps the masked projection to predict the target projection at masked positions. The loss combines mean squared error with Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg) applied to a global pooled projection, which stabilizes training by enforcing an isotropic Gaussian distribution over batch embeddings. The model is pretrained on 29,109 hours of diverse EEG data spanning 17 channel topologies and evaluated via linear probing on the EEG-Bench suite (14 clinical/BCI tasks), outperforming reconstruction-based baselines while using only 10% of the pretraining data.

## Key Results
- Balanced accuracy: Laya-S (0.597) > LaBraM (0.573) > LUNA (0.560) on EEG-Bench clinical tasks.  
- Largest gains on seizure detection (+10.8%), mTBI classification (+4.1%), and multiclass artifact classification (+5.6%) compared to prior models.  
- Improved robustness to noise and PCA evidence of seizure-aligned embedding trajectories.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**SIGReg (Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization)** prevents model collapse during latent prediction pretraining. It regularizes the geometry of batch-level latent embeddings by encouraging them to match an isotropic Gaussian distribution via random 1D projections, promoting diversity and full utilization of embedding dimensions without architectural heuristics like stop‑gradients or momentum networks. Evidence: Ablation shows training collapses if the regularization weight λ falls below 0.02 (Appendix D.3). Break condition: λ too low causes collapse; too high may over‑regularize and constrain useful capacity.

**Dynamic channel mixer** provides montage‑agnostic learning of cross‑channel neural patterns. It uses learned query vectors that attend over channel embeddings augmented with Fourier‑encoded electrode coordinates, aggregating distributed activity into fixed‑dimensional latent brain states (S). This decouples spatial processing from temporal modeling, allowing the encoder to handle variable electrode counts and positions. Evidence: Query topographies (Fig 4–5) show diverse spatial attention patterns; a query specialization loss (AAT penalty) encourages diversity. Break condition: if queries become uniform (high AAT similarity), the mixer fails to provide distinct spatial summaries, harming generalization.

**Latent temporal prediction** captures clinically meaningful, low‑variance state dynamics (e.g., seizure onset/offset) better than reconstruction. By training the predictor to map a visible context to the latent embeddings of a masked future segment, the model must encode features predictable over longer gaps, forcing abstraction away from instantaneous noise/artifacts. Evidence: Laya outperforms reconstruction models on seizure detection (+10.8%), shows greater resilience to injected noise at low SNR (Sec 4.4), and its PCA embeddings show clear shifts at seizure boundaries (Sec 4.5). Break condition: masking too trivial (small blocks) may lead to short‑term autocorrelation; if the context window is too short relative to mask size, prediction becomes impossible.

- **Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA):** Moves beyond reconstruction, aligning pretraining with downstream linear probing. *Quick check:* For a linear probe on frozen features, why should pretraining avoid raw signal reconstruction?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Patch Embedder -> Channel Mixer -> Encoder -> Projector -> Predictor

**Critical path:**  
1. Input EEG (B, C, T) -> Patch Embedder (depthwise 1D conv, kernel=stride=25) -> per‑channel patch tokens (B, C, N, E).  
2. Channel Mixer: Fourier‑encoded electrode coordinates added to channel embeddings; learned queries (Nq=16) via cross‑attention produce mixed latent states S (B, N, D).  
3. Parallel passes on S:  
   - Full pass: Encoder(full S) -> Z, stop‑gradient, Projector(Z) -> T.  
   - Masked pass: Temporal mask on S, Encoder(masked S) -> Z_ctx, Projector(Z_ctx) -> P_ctx.  
4. Predictor(P_ctx) -> predicted masked embeddings ˆT.  
5. Loss: MSE(ˆT, T[masked]) + λ · LSIGReg(p_cls), where p_cls is the projected global summary of Z.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Channel mixer complexity vs. flexibility: Learned cross‑attention with queries increases parameters but enables montage‑agnosticism; simpler per‑channel MLP would be faster but montage‑specific.  
- Stop‑gradient necessity: Essential for stability per LeJEPA, but discards predictor gradients; SIGReg loss on p_cls provides alternative gradient to encoder.  
- Masking strategy: Contiguous blocks force long‑range prediction but may be too difficult for short recordings; random patches easier but less semantic.  
- Projector dimensionality: High expansion (D_up >> D) aids stability but adds parameters; must ensure D_proj < predictor input dim.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Collapse: Constant embeddings across batch; variance of p_cls near zero. Fix: increase SIGReg λ.  
- Poor montage generalization: Performance drops on unseen layouts; query affinities become uniform. Fix: adjust query loss weight, verify coordinate encoding.  
- Underfitting masked prediction: Predictor MSE remains high. Causes: mask ratio too high, context too short. Fix: reduce mask ratio or increase input length.  
- Noise sensitivity similar to reconstruction models: Laya loses robustness advantage. Check: SIGReg off, channel mixer not providing clean S.

**First three experiments:**  
1. **Pretraining stability check:** Vary λ (SIGReg weight) across {0, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1} for 1k steps; monitor variance of p_cls and predictor MSE. Target: non‑zero variance, decreasing MSE; failure: λ=0 leads to collapse.  
2. **Linear probe smoketest:** After 1k pretraining steps, freeze encoder and train linear head on a binary clinical task (e.g., Abnormal vs Normal from TUAB). Target: >80% of final paper’s reported performance; failure: ~50% accuracy indicates representation failure.  
3. **Montage generalization probe:** Pretrain on 10‑20 montages only; evaluate linear probe on a dataset with non‑standard montage (e.g., BioSemi128 from HBN) without channel harmonization. Target: <5% drop vs. in‑montage performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What specific neural features do LeJEPA embeddings capture?** The paper (Sec 5.2) admits lack of quantitative analysis linking embeddings to known patterns; resolution requires systematic neurophysiological benchmarks, attribution methods, and synthetic data.  
2. **What scaling laws govern latent prediction in EEG?** Laya‑full underperforms Laya‑S despite more data/compute (Sec 5.1); scaling dynamics remain unclear. Needs large‑scale experiments with representation analysis (e.g., eigenvalue spectra), loss curvature, and performance curves.  
3. **How does the dynamic channel mixer contribute to stability and transferability?** Ablations show necessity but not mechanism (Discussion, Sec 4.6). Requires mixer ablations (replace with alternatives), attention pattern probing, and robustness tests under channel dropout/montage shifts.  
4. **Do EEG‑Bench tasks underestimate clinical relevance?** The benchmark focuses on classification, not retrieval/forecasting/personalization (Sec 5.1). Needs evaluation on forecasting (e.g., seizure prediction), retrieval (matching EEG to cognitive states), and subject‑specific fine‑tuning.

## Limitations
- **Implementation details missing:** Precise Fourier coordinate encoding, predictor architecture hyperparameters (heads, MLP ratio, dropout), SIGReg random projection distribution and Gaussianity test, robust scaling scope, stop‑gradient scope, and linear probing protocol are not fully specified, hampering exact reproduction.  
- **Evaluation limited to classification:** EEG‑Bench focuses on classification tasks and does not cover retrieval, forecasting, or subject‑adaptive scenarios, leaving uncertainty about broader clinical utility.

## Confidence
- SIGReg prevents representation collapse: **High**  
- Channel mixer enables montage‑agnostic representation: **Medium**  
- Latent prediction captures clinically relevant temporal structure: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **SIGReg collapse boundary:** Train Laya‑S with λ ∈ {0, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1} for 1k steps; monitor batch variance of `p_cls` and predictor MSE. Expect λ ≥ 0.02 to avoid near‑zero variance.  
2. **Montage‑agnostic generalization:** Pretrain on 10‑20 montages only, then evaluate linear probe on a dataset with non‑standard montage (e.g., BioSemi128) without channel harmonization. Target: <5% drop vs. in‑montage accuracy.  
3. **Mechanism sanity check (reconstruction vs. latent prediction):** Train two identical models differing only in objective (reconstruction vs. Laya’s latent prediction). Inject controlled Gaussian/EMG noise at increasing SNR; compare seizure detection performance. Expect Laya’s advantage to grow at lower SNR, confirming reduced artifact reliance.
