# FEAT: A Linear-Complexity Foundation Model for Extremely Large Structured Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16513
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16513
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** FEAT achieves strict O(N) scaling for tabular foundation models, matching or exceeding baselines on 11 datasets with up to 40× faster inference at 500k samples.

## Executive Summary
FEAT is a tabular foundation model designed to overcome the quadratic computational complexity and representational degradation that plague existing approaches when handling extremely large structured datasets. It introduces a dual-axis architecture that processes samples along two complementary pathways: a feature-axis using multi-head self-attention for intra-sample feature interactions, and a sample-axis combining adaptive-fusion bidirectional Mamba-2 (AFBM) for local dependencies and convolutional gated linear attention (Conv-GLA) for global memory. This design achieves strict O(N) time complexity while preserving permutation-invariant modeling of samples. Pre-training employs a hybrid structural causal model (SCM) pipeline that generates synthetic data with heavy-tailed noise and heteroscedasticity, paired with a Huber-based reconstruction loss for gradient stability. Evaluated on 11 real-world datasets, FEAT matches or surpasses state-of-the-art baselines in zero-shot classification (e.g., 0.9251 AUC) and regression (e.g., 0.406 RMSE) while delivering up to 40× speedup at 500k samples.

## Method Summary
This synthesis covers the core methodological contributions of FEAT. The architecture begins with a cell embedding stage that transforms raw tabular data (N×D) into a 3D tensor via a value MLP and dynamic orthogonal feature embeddings (S-DFE), ensuring permutation invariance. The heart of the model consists of repeated dual-axis blocks. Each block first applies multi-head self-attention along the feature dimension (O(N·D²)) to capture interactions among features within each sample. Then, along the sample dimension, it stacks three layers of adaptive-fusion bi-Mamba-2 (AFBM) – bidirectional state space models that concatenate forward and backward hidden states – followed by one layer of Conv-GLA, which performs a 1D convolution and gated cumulative attention to accumulate a global covariance memory. This sample-axis pathway operates in O(N) time due to the linear recurrence of SSMs and the gated attention mechanism. Finally, a task-aware prediction module extracts target tokens and routes them to classification, regression, or imputation heads.

Pre-training uses a hybrid SCM to generate synthetic tables that mimic real-world complexities: scale-free causal graphs, prototype-based root initialization, heteroscedastic noise proportional to magnitude, and Kumaraswamy warping for heavy tails. The reconstruction and regression losses employ a Huber function with a tunable threshold to resist outliers, and the total loss is dynamically normalized by the batch size of each sub-task. This pipeline stabilizes training on heavy-tailed distributions and bridges the synthetic-to-real gap, enabling strong zero-shot transfer without fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- Zero-shot classification AUC reaches 0.9251 on benchmark datasets.
- Zero-shot regression RMSE as low as 0.406.
- Inference latency scales linearly with context size, offering up to 40× speedup over quadratic baselines at 500k samples.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** FEAT achieves strict O(N) time complexity for cross-sample modeling while preserving global dependencies.
- **Mechanism:** It replaces quadratic self-attention with a dual-axis architecture. *Feature-axis modeling* uses MHSA (O(N·D²)) within each sample. *Sample-axis modeling* uses linear-complexity operators: three layers of bidirectional AFBM (forward/backward SSMs) followed by one layer of Conv-GLA (1D convolution + gated linear attention). The AFBM layers capture local dependencies without causal masking; Conv-GLA accumulates a global covariance memory with variance-bounded gating.
- **Core assumption:** Structured data samples are permutation-invariant; hidden-state compression and artificial causality in standard SSMs degrade representations for long sequences.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "replaces quadratic attention with hybrid linear encoding... combines adaptive-fusion bi-Mamba-2... and convolutional gated linear attention... strictly O(N) scaling."
  - [section 5.3] "To simultaneously address the linear trap and the causal mask deficit... integrates two complementary mechanisms: AFBM... and Conv-GLA... decouples aggressive local noise filtering from global long-range storage."
  - [section 8.1] Mathematical proof that total sample-axis complexity is O(N·D·(d·d_state + d·K + d²)) → O(N).
  - [corpus] Related work (e.g., "Scaling Laws for Linear Complexity Language Models") confirms the ongoing challenge of scaling linear models, supporting the novelty of FEAT's dual-axis design.
- **Break condition:** If gating in Conv-GLA fails to suppress noise (e.g., gate parameters saturate), accumulated memory variance may no longer be bounded by M (number of informative tokens), reintroducing linear-trap-like degradation for extremely long N.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The adaptive-fusion bi-Mamba-2 (AFBM) eliminates the unidirectional causal bias of standard SSMs, enabling permutation-invariant cross-sample modeling.
- **Mechanism:** AFBM runs two SSM passes per layer: a forward pass (i=1→N) and a backward pass (i=N→1). Their hidden states are concatenated and projected via a learned fusion matrix W_fuse. This allows each sample's representation to depend on both preceding and succeeding samples in the arbitrary input order.
- **Core assumption:** Permutation-invariant tabular samples require symmetric receptive fields; causal SSMs impose harmful positional bias.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.3.2] "AFBM... captures dynamic local dependencies across samples... bidirectional fusion removes the artificial causality... enables permutation-aware feature extraction."
  - [section 8.3] Proof that gradient influence I_fused(i,k) > 0 for all k, i.e., non-zero influence from both past and future samples, mathematically canceling causal phase shift.
  - [abstract] "preserving expressive representations for massive structured sequences."
- **Break condition:** If W_fuse learns a degenerate solution (e.g., W→ ≈ 0, W← dominates or vice-versa), influence becomes effectively unidirectional again. This could occur if the task accidentally benefits from order (e.g., time-series proxy), violating permutation invariance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The hybrid SCM pre-training pipeline with Huber-based loss ensures stable optimization under heavy-tailed, heteroscedastic real-world distributions.
- **Mechanism:** 1) *Hybrid SCM*: Scale-free DAGs, prototype-based root initialization (Dirichlet mixture), heteroscedastic noise (σ² ∝ |x̃|^γ), Kumaraswamy warping for heavy tails. 2) *Loss*: Huber loss (δ-threshold) for reconstruction and regression resists outlier gradients. 3) *Dynamic balancing*: Loss terms normalized by batch size of each task (|B_cls|, |B_reg|, |M|) prevents domination.
- **Core assumption:** Synthetic-only pre-training fails to match empirical noise; static loss functions cause gradient explosions in multi-task, variable-context training.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "hybrid structural causal model pipeline and a stable reconstruction objective... improves robustness to heavy-tailed noise."
  - [section 5.5.1] Detailed SCM steps: "heteroscedastic noise... Kumaraswamy warping... to faithfully emulate heavy-tailed outliers."
  - [section 5.5.2] "Huber mechanism acts as an L2 penalty for minor errors... L1 penalty for severe deviations... dynamically balanced total loss aggregated strictly over its valid samples."
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Real-tabpfn: Improving tabular foundation models via continued pre-training with real-world data" and "MambaTab" signal community recognition of synthetic-real gaps and stability issues.
- **Break condition:** If the Huber threshold δ is poorly calibrated (too high → outliers dominate; too low → model bias), or if the SCM's Kumaraswamy parameters do not match true tail heaviness, gradient stability may not be achieved, especially for extreme multimodality.

## Foundational Learning

- **State Space Models (SSMs)**  
  *Why needed:* FEAT's AFBM and Conv-GLA are built on SSM principles (e.g., Mamba). Understanding discrete-time recurrence (h_t = Āh_{t-1} + Bx_t) and linear complexity is key to grasping how FEAT avoids O(N²).  
  *Quick check:* Why is a standard, unidirectional SSM (like vanilla Mamba) problematic for permutation-invariant tabular data, and how does bidirectional processing solve this?

- **In-Context Learning (ICL) for Tabular Data**  
  *Why needed:* FEAT is a foundation model for ICL; it predicts unlabeled samples from labeled context without fine-tuning. This frames the entire architecture (context encoding → task-aware prediction).  
  *Quick check:* In the ICL formulation (P_θ(y_U | X_U, X_L, y_L)), what roles do X_L/y_L (support set) and X_U (query set) play during inference?

- **Heavy-Tailed Distributions & Robust Losses**  
  *Why needed:* Real-world tabular data often contains outliers. The Huber loss in FEAT's pre-training is critical for stability. Recognizing the difference between L2 (sensitive) and L1 (robust) penalties explains the design.  
  *Quick check:* How does the Huber loss transition between L2 and L1 behavior, and why is this transition δ crucial for training with heavy-tailed synthetic data?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Cell Embedding: Raw X (N×D) → 3D tensor X^(0) (N×D×d) via value MLP + S-DFE (dynamic orthogonal feature IDs).  
Dual-Axis Blocks (×L): Each block:
- Feature-axis: Sample-wise MHSA (O(N·D²)) → intra-sample feature interactions.
- Sample-axis: 3× AFBM (bidirectional SSM fusion) + 1× Conv-GLA (1D conv + gated cumulative attention) → inter-sample dependencies, O(N).
Task-Aware Prediction: Extract target token (label prompt) and feature tokens from final X^(L); route to classification/regression/imputation heads (MLPs with LayerNorm in reg/impute).

### Critical path
Data flow is strictly sequential: Embedding → [Block (Feature-axis → Sample-axis)] repeated L times → Task Routing → Heads. The sample-axis output of layer l (e.g., X^(3)) feeds into the convolution in layer 4's Conv-GLA.

### Design tradeoffs
- *AFBM vs. Conv-GLA:* AFBM provides dynamic, local filtering but state-compressed; Conv-GLA provides explicit global memory but static. Three AFBM layers vs. one Conv-GLA layer is a fixed heuristic tradeoff. Increasing Conv-GLA frequency could improve global memory but may increase noise accumulation.
- *Huber δ:* Balances outlier robustness (high δ) vs. signal preservation (low δ). Requires tuning on a validation SCM batch.
- *S-DFE (dynamic orthogonal IDs):* Enables permutation invariance but introduces stochasticity in feature positioning; must ensure learned projections generalize across random O matrices.

### Failure signatures
- *Latency not linear:* If batch processing or D dimensions dominate, O(N) may break. Monitor per-sample latency scaling in profiling.
- *Zero-shot performance collapse on large N:* Check if validation AUC/RMSE degrades sharply when context N > pre-training max length. Indicates Conv-GLA variance bounding failure.
- *Task interference:* If classification/regression/imputation heads compete, dynamic loss balancing may fail; watch for one loss term dominating early training.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Scaling law validation:** Train FEAT-small (L=2, d=64) on synthetic SCM with varying N (1k to 500k). Plot latency vs. N and performance vs. N. Verify O(N) slope and stable AUC/RMSE.
2. **Ablation of AFBM/Conv-GLA:** Create variants: (a) only AFBM (no Conv-GLA), (b) only Conv-GLA (no AFBM), (c) causal AFBM (remove backward pass). Evaluate on TabArena-CLS/REG to quantify contribution of each component to representation quality.
3. **Huber δ sensitivity:** Pre-train with δ ∈ {0.1, 1.0, 10.0} on a synthetic set with injected 5% extreme outliers. Monitor training loss stability (gradient norms) and final zero-shot performance on a held-out real dataset (e.g., GI-CLS). Expect low δ → instability; high δ → biased convergence.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **How can FEAT's dual-axis architecture be extended to handle multi-modal structured data, where samples may integrate tabular features with other modalities like text or images?**  
   *Basis:* The conclusion states: "In future work, we plan to extend FEAT to multi-modal structured-data settings..."  
   *Why unresolved:* FEAT currently processes only unimodal tabular tensors; multi-modal inputs require novel embedding strategies and architectural modifications to fuse heterogeneous data types effectively.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Design a multi-modal FEAT variant, incorporate modality-specific encoders, and evaluate on benchmarks like MIMIC-IV (clinical notes + tabular) or multimodal recommendation datasets, comparing against unimodal and multimodal baselines.

2. **How does FEAT perform in large-scale industrial applications such as recommendation systems and financial forecasting, which often involve extreme sparsity, non-stationarity, and sub-second latency constraints?**  
   *Basis:* The conclusion mentions exploring applications in "recommendation systems and financial forecasting."  
   *Why unresolved:* Current evaluations use academic/benchmark datasets; industrial scenarios present unique challenges (e.g., billions of samples, high-cardinality categorical features, concept drift) not fully addressed in the paper.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Deploy FEAT in real-world industrial pipelines (e.g., a commercial recommendation platform or trading system) and report accuracy, latency, scalability, and business metrics compared to existing in-production models.

3. **How does FEAT's efficiency and effectiveness scale with feature dimensionality D, given that its feature-axis modeling uses multi-head self-attention with O(D^2) per-sample complexity?**  
   *Basis:* Feature-axis modeling applies MHSA across D features for each sample (Section 5.3.1). The overall complexity includes an O(N·D^2·d) term, but experiments fix D=20 and vary only N.  
   *Why unresolved:* Real-world datasets can have hundreds or thousands of features (e.g., genomics); the paper does not test scalability along the feature axis, leaving open whether FEAT remains efficient and retains performance for high-D data.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Evaluate FEAT on datasets with controlled variations in D (e.g., 10, 100, 1000, 10000), measuring inference latency, memory usage, and predictive performance; compare against baselines and analyze if the dual-axis design adequately captures high-dimensional interactions.

4. **Is the gating mechanism in Conv-GLA robust to distribution shifts, and does it reliably identify informative samples in zero-shot settings across heterogeneous datasets?**  
   *Basis:* The gate gi,j = SiLU(Wg ˜X) is learned during pre-training to suppress uninformative samples (Section 5.3.2), but its behavior on out-of-distribution data is not analyzed.  
   *Why unresolved:* Zero-shot performance indicates general utility, but the gate's decisions may overfit pre-training distributions; no study examines gate activation patterns across diverse domains or their correlation with true sample informativeness.  
   *What evidence would resolve it:* Analyze gate statistics (e.g., activation distributions, sparsity) on multiple test datasets; perform ablations (remove gate, randomize gate) to quantify impact; evaluate on deliberately OOD shifted data to assess robustness.

## Limitations

- **Strict O(N) scaling may break for extreme context lengths** if the gating in Conv-GLA fails to bound variance, leading to linear-trap-like degradation.
- **Permutation‑invariant modeling could be limiting** if the fusion matrix W_fuse degenerates to a unidirectional solution, especially on tasks with latent order.
- **Robust pre‑training depends on careful hyperparameter tuning** (Huber δ, SCM tail parameters); poor calibration can cause outlier domination or biased convergence.

- **Permutation‑Invariant Modeling:** Medium
- **Robust Hybrid Pre‑training:** Medium
