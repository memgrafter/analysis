# Temporal Representations for Exploration: Learning Complex Exploratory Behavior without Extrinsic Rewards

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02008
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02008
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** C-TeC significantly outperforms prior methods in unsupervised exploration, covering 2500±300 unique positions in ant hardest maze and achieving a sum of achievement success rates of ~4.5 in Craftax-Classic.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces C-TeC, a method for unsupervised exploration in reinforcement learning that learns temporal contrastive representations to generate intrinsic rewards. By training separate encoders for state-action pairs and future states with an InfoNCE loss, C-TeC encourages agents to visit states whose futures are unpredictable relative to the replay buffer’s marginal distribution, avoiding degenerate distractions like noisy TV. The approach demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across robotic locomotion, manipulation, and open-world benchmarks, with ablations confirming the importance of separable architectures and representation normalization.

## Method Summary
C-TeC learns two encoders: ϕ(s,a) for state-action pairs and ψ(s_f) for future states. Using an InfoNCE loss on tuples sampled from a replay buffer, it trains these encoders to discriminate true future states from negatives. The intrinsic reward is defined as the negative similarity (e.g., L1 distance) between ϕ(s,a) and ψ(s_f), promoting exploration of states whose future distributions diverge most from the buffer’s marginal. Future states are sampled with a geometric offset Δ ~ Geometric(1-γ_cl) to balance short and long-horizon unpredictability. The policy is updated with standard RL algorithms (e.g., PPO, SAC) using this reward, while the encoders are updated concurrently.

## Key Results
- In ant hardest maze, C-TeC covers 2500±300 unique positions after 500M environment steps, far exceeding prior methods.
- In Craftax-Classic, it achieves a sum of achievement success rates of ~4.5, compared to ~2–3 for baselines.
- Ablations show that separable critic architectures and representation normalization are critical for performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** C-TeC’s exploration signal emerges from a KL-divergence minimization that prioritizes states where the agent’s future trajectory distribution is least aligned with the replay buffer’s marginal future distribution (mode-seeking).
- **Mechanism:** The contrastive critic is trained via InfoNCE to approximate log-odds between conditional future distribution p_T(s_f|s_t,a_t) and marginal p_T(s_f). The intrinsic reward r_int = -C_θ((s,a), s_f) ≈ ||ϕ(s,a) - ψ(s_f)|| then mathemathetizes negative similarity. Taking expectation over p_T(s_f|s_t,a_t) yields a surrogate for -D_KL[p_T(s_f|s_t,a_t) || p_T(s_f)], which is maximized under policy optimization.
- **Core assumption:** The learned contrastive representations accurately capture the pointwise mutual information between (s,a) and s_f, i.e., C_θ → log p_T(s_f|s_t,a_t) - log p_T(s_f).
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "intrinsic reward is defined as the negative similarity... encouraging the agent to visit states whose futures are predictable"  
  [section 4.2] Equation (4)-(5) explicitly derive E[r_int] = -D_KL[p_T(s_f|s_t,a_t) || p_T(s_f)]  
  [section 5.1] Explains mode-seeking behavior: "state-action pairs with spread-out trajectories over states that have actually been seen have higher reward"
- **Break condition:** If the contrastive model collapses to a constant function (all state representations identical), C_θ becomes constant and r_int loses sensitivity, halting learning progress. Indicator: representation variance near zero (Section G shows variance remains non-zero).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Separable encoder architecture (ϕ(s,a) and ψ(s)) is necessary for stable contrastive learning that respects temporal structure, avoiding pitfalls of monolithic critics.
- **Mechanism:** The contrastive objective treats (s,a) pairs and future states from different latent spaces. Two separate networks project into a shared embedding, forcing the similarity metric to compute distances between state-action vectors and state vectors. This separation prevents the model from learning shortcuts (e.g., memorizing specific state IDs) that ignore temporal dynamics.
- **Core assumption:** The factorized parameterization biases the model toward learning a proper metric space where distances reflect temporal transitions rather than exploiting trivial correlations present in a joint embedding.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "avoiding episodic memory mechanisms... our method builds directly on temporal similarities, yielding a simpler yet effective strategy"  
  [section 4.1] "We use two parameterized encoders... ϕ_θ(s_t,a_t) for state-action pairs and ψ_θ(s_f) for future states."  
  [section G.4] Ablation (Fig 14) shows monolithic critic f(s,a,sf) fails; separable architecture is critical.
- **Break condition:** Using a monolithic critic leads to zero intrinsic reward gradients across most states, as the network cannot disentangle (s,a) from sf in the contrastive loss. Empirically, state coverage collapses to near random levels.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Geometric discounting of future state sampling (Δ ~ Geometric(1-γ)) creates a natural curriculum: early training emphasizes nearby transitions (easier learning), while later stages increasingly penalize long-horizon unpredictability, enabling complex exploration without handcrafted curricula.
- **Mechanism:** The future state s_f is sampled as s_{t+Δ} where Δ ~ Geometric(1-γ). The expected reward thus weights all future horizons by γ^Δ, theoretically balancing immediate and distal prediction errors. The contrastive model is trained on this hierarchical distribution, making it sensitive to both local and global temporal patterns.
- **Core assumption:** The geometric distribution with appropriate γ (e.g., 0.99) ensures that both short-term and long-term futures contribute meaningfully to the intrinsic reward, while still focusing on reachable states.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 3] "We sample an offset Δ ~ Geometric(1-γ), then set the future state sf = s_t+Δ."  
  [section G.6] Figure 16 compares geometric vs uniform and γ-scheduled sampling; geometric works robustly across environments.  
  [section 4.2] "We found that using a single sample future state to approximate the expectation works well," suggesting the geometric sampling provides low-variance estimates.
- **Break condition:** If γ is too low (e.g., 0.1), the reward only cares about immediate transitions → myopic exploration; if γ ≈ 1 without normalization, the distribution over Δ becomes too diffuse, making the reward too noisy and potentially encouraging revisiting of distant, unreachable states.

## Foundational Learning

### Contrastive Learning & InfoNCE Loss
- **Why needed:** The foundation of the representation learning. Without understanding how the contrastive loss shapes embeddings, the reward design makes no sense.
- **Quick check:** If all negative examples are semantically identical, how would the model fail, and why does including in-episode negatives help (Section G.9)?

### Intrinsic Motivation Taxonomy
- **Why needed:** To distinguish between prediction-error (RND, ICM), coverage (count-based), empowerment, and C-TeC’s mode-seeking KL approach. Comparing to RND/ICM clarifies why C-TeC succeeds where they fail.
- **Quick check:** Why would maximizing entropy over state representations lead to degenerate noisy TV attraction, while maximizing -D_KL[p_future|past || p_marginal] does not?

### Discounting and Horizon Effects
- **Why needed:** The γ in geometric sampling is distinct from RL discount. It controls the timescale of future sampling, changing what “unpredictable” means. Correct γ selection is critical (Section G.7); understanding its dual role prevents misconfiguration.
- **Quick check:** If γ_cl is set to 0.3 in a maze, what bias is introduced into the exploration signal?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Replay Buffer T -> Sample (s,a) and s_f with geometric Δ -> Compute r_int = -dist(ϕ(s,a), ψ(s_f)) -> Update encoders via InfoNCE -> Update policy with r_int

### Critical path
1. Initialize buffer T with random policy data.
2. For each gradient step: sample batch of (s_t,a_t) from T.
3. For each (s_t,a_t), sample Δ_i ~ Geometric(1-γ_cl) to get s_f = s_{t+Δ_i} within stored trajectory.
4. Compute r_int,i = -||ϕ(s_t,a_t) - ψ(s_f)|| (or dot product variant).
5. Update θ to minimize InfoNCE loss on this batch.
6. Update policy using (s_t,a_t,r_int,t).
7. Add new rollout data to T.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Similarity function:** L1 vs L2. L1 generally better for continuous control (Section G.3), L2 for pixel/discrete (Craftax). This is environment-dependent; quick heuristic: use L1 unless dealing with one-hot/large sparse spaces.
- **Contrastive loss:** InfoNCE is default; symmetric InfoNCE can help but is not necessary (Section G.2). NCE/FlatNCE may harm in complex spaces.
- **Representation normalization:** Must include unit-norm (Section G.1). Without it, representations can drift, changing scale of reward.
- **LogSumExp penalty:** Should be omitted from reward even if present in loss (Equation 6, Section E.4.1); including it causes the reward to inadvertently minimize state entropy.

### Failure signatures
- **Representation collapse:** Variance of ϕ/ψ outputs approaches zero → all states map to same point → reward constant. Check by logging embedding variance every 1M steps.
- **Intrinsic reward saturation:** Mean |r_int| drops to near zero quickly. Could indicate either perfect coverage (good) or representation failure (bad). Cross-check with state visitation count.
- **Noisy TV fixation:** If agent repeatedly visits a single high-variance state (e.g., TV static) without expanding coverage, representations may be ignoring temporal relevance. Check if reward spikes from that state persist.
- **Policy collapse:** Entropy of π drops to near-zero while state coverage remains low. Suggests policy overweighting high r_int from few states (spikes) instead of diverse exploration.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Replication sanity check:** Run C-TeC on ant_hardest_maze with default hyperparameters (γ_cl=0.99, L1 similarity, normalized reps). Should achieve >2000 unique positions after 500M steps (Table 1). Compare against random policy’s ~800 coverage.
2. **Ablation – Monolithic critic:** Replace separable ϕ/ψ with single f(s,a,sf). Expect severe performance drop (<50% coverage of full C-TeC) as per Fig 14. Validates architecture necessity.
3. **Ablation – Representation normalization:** Disable unit-norm on embeddings. Expect rapid coverage drop after first 50M steps (Fig 11), confirming design choice.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can C-TeC be effectively adapted to pixel-based visual observations and partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The conclusion explicitly states "adapting the method to pixel-based and partially observed settings" as future work.
- **Why unresolved:** The current experiments use state vectors as inputs. Pixel-based inputs introduce high dimensionality and partial observability that may break the contrastive learning assumptions about temporal structure.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Developing visual encoders that preserve temporal contrastive properties under partial observability and demonstrating successful exploration on raw-pixel environments.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What specific properties of temporal contrastive representations make them effective for exploration, and how do they differ from other representation learning objectives?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section 5.2 states representations capture "prediction error" but the exact mechanism is not characterized. Section G.4 shows separable architecture is crucial but provides no theoretical justification.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper offers an information-theoretic interpretation assuming perfect representations (Section 5.1), but real representations are imperfect. The link between representation quality and exploration performance is not established.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis linking representation objectives to exploration metrics, or controlled empirical comparisons isolating representation effects.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why is the separable critic architecture (separate encoders for (s,a) and s_f) necessary for effective exploration, and what failure modes occur with monolithic critics?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section G.4 ablation shows monolithic critic results in "poor exploration performance" while separable architecture succeeds, but this is unexplained.
- **Why unresolved:** The empirical finding lacks theoretical foundation. Understanding this could inform architecture design for contrastive exploration methods beyond C-TeC.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analysis of gradient dynamics or loss landscapes comparing monolithic vs separable critics, or proving conditions where separability is necessary for meaningful exploration signals.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Do the state occupancy distributions learned by C-TeC converge to a steady state, or do they continually evolve as suggested by the simplified linear stability analysis in Section J.1?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section J.1 finds "no easily-achievable stable fixed points for general nontrivial MDPs" under strong assumptions (perfect representations, deterministic one-step transitions), implying continual evolution.
- **Why unresolved:** The analysis is highly simplified and may not reflect actual algorithm behavior. The paper explicitly notes "Future work remains to investigate the existence of dynamical steady-states."
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Long-term empirical tracking of state occupancy distributions across diverse environments, or theoretical analysis relaxing the assumptions in J.1.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Under what environmental conditions do forward-looking exploration rewards (C-TeC) outperform backward-looking rewards (ETD), and can they be combined?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section 6.1 and K contrast C-TeC's forward-looking reward (future state distribution) with ETD's backward-looking reward (temporal distance from past). Results are mixed: ETD better in Arm binpick hard, C-TeC better in Crafter.
- **Why unresolved:** The theoretical trade-offs are not analyzed. The paper speculates forward-looking may better capture long-term exploratory value but provides no formal characterization of when each is preferable.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis of exploration objectives in terms of state coverage growth rates, or systematic empirical evaluation identifying environmental properties that favor each approach.

## Limitations
- **Reproduction details:** Specifications for Craftax input preprocessing, action encoding, and policy network are incomplete, risking failed replication.
- **Visual generalization:** Evaluation is limited to state-based and symbolic inputs; performance on raw pixel observations is unknown.
- **Hyperparameter scope:** Systematic sensitivity analysis of γ_cl, similarity metric, and batch construction across diverse environments is lacking.

## Confidence
- **Performance superiority:** C-TeC outperforms prior methods on all reported benchmarks. *(High)*
- **KL-divergence mechanism:** Intrinsic reward approximates mode‑seeking KL under assumptions that contrastive learning recovers PMI. *(Medium)*
- **Separable architecture necessity:** Monolithic critic fails (Fig 14). *(High)*
- **Geometric discount curriculum:** Geometric future sampling yields robust results. *(Medium)*
- **Normalization requirement:** Removing unit norm leads to collapse (Fig 11). *(High)*
