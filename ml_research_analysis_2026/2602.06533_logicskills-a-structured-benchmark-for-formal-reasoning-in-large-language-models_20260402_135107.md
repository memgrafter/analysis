# LogicSkills: A Structured Benchmark for Formal Reasoning in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06533
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06533
- **Reference count:** 26
- **Primary result:** LogicSkills reveals that instruction-tuned LLMs excel at validity assessment but struggle with formal symbolization and countermodel construction, while reasoning-tuned models achieve balanced competence across all three subskills.

## Executive Summary
LogicSkills is a new benchmark designed to dissect logical reasoning in large language models (LLMs) by isolating three fundamental subskills: formal symbolization (translating natural language to first-order logic), countermodel construction (generating a finite model to demonstrate invalidity), and validity assessment (judging entailment). The benchmark uses the two-variable fragment of first-order logic, presented in both English and an artificial Carrollian language, with all items solver-verified for correctness. Evaluations show that conventional instruction-tuned LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini) achieve high validity scores (87–96%) but substantially lower performance on symbolization (~70–80%) and countermodel construction (~10–42%). In contrast, reasoning-tuned models (e.g., o3, Qwen3-32B) perform strongly across all three tasks (>89%). Fine-tuning on symbolization and countermodel yields large in-task gains but no transfer to validity assessment, indicating that these skills are largely disentangled in standard LLMs. The benchmark thus highlights a critical gap: high-level reasoning scores may mask weaknesses in foundational logical abilities.

## Method Summary
LogicSkills generates items from the two-variable fragment of first-order logic (FO2) without identity. A compositional generator produces English and Carrollian sentences paired with canonical FO2 formulas, which are verified using the Z3 solver. Arguments are constructed by sampling premise sets and valid conclusions; invalid distractors share surface features. The final evaluation set contains 1,500 items (600 symbolization, 600 validity, 300 countermodel) with language splits. Models are prompted (prompts in Appendix F) and their raw outputs normalized via an LLM extractor before solver-based verification: symbolization requires logical equivalence, validity requires exact conclusion selection, and countermodel requires a finite structure (domain size 3) satisfying premises and negated conclusion. Fine-tuning experiments use LoRA on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct with 100k task-specific examples for one epoch.

## Key Results
- Instruction-tuned models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Flash) score high on validity (87.0%–96.0%) but drop to ~70–80% on formal symbolization and as low as 10–42% on countermodel construction.
- Reasoning-tuned models (o3, Qwen3-32B) achieve >89% across all three skills, indicating balanced logical competence.
- Fine-tuning improves in-task performance dramatically (symbolization +80.7, countermodel +15.7 to +25.6) but yields negative or negligible transfer to validity assessment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Instruction-tuned LLMs can achieve high validity assessment scores without robustly performing formal symbolization or countermodel construction, suggesting reliance on heuristic or pattern-based judgment rather than systematic logical deduction.
- **Mechanism:** Validity assessment presents premises and multiple-choice conclusions. Models may leverage surface-level cues, statistical regularities in training data, or "common sense" associations to select the most plausible conclusion without explicitly translating premises into a formal logical representation or constructing a falsifying model. The task conflates the subskills but can be solved via alternative, non-symbolic strategies.
- **Core assumption:** High-level composite task success does not guarantee mastery of the underlying constituent skills.
- **Evidence anchors:** Performance gap: GPT-4o 87.0% validity vs. 10.0% countermodel; Gemini 96.0% vs. 13.0%.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Countermodel construction is exceptionally difficult for current LLMs because it requires generating a novel, finite structure that satisfies a precise semantic constraint—a form of constructive model-building that is not reducible to pattern recognition or textual entailment.
- **Mechanism:** The task demands the synthesis of a consistent interpretation (domain, constants, predicate extensions) that makes all premises true and the conclusion false. This is a generative, constrained search problem over a combinatorial space of possible structures. LLMs, trained primarily on linguistic data, lack an innate procedure for systematically exploring this space or verifying semantic satisfaction conditions without external tools.
- **Core assumption:** Model-theoretic reasoning is a distinct and more complex cognitive operation than evaluating given options.
- **Evidence anchors:** Most erroneous outputs (72.9%) are structurally well-formed but fail to falsify the argument—typically because one or more premises are false. Fine-tuning yields large in-task gains for countermodel (+15.7 to +25.6), indicating the skill is learnable but not naturally present.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Reasoning-tuned models (e.g., o3, Qwen3-32B) develop a more integrated and systematic logical skill profile because their training objectives or inference procedures encourage step-by-step symbolic or model-theoretic reasoning, rather than direct end-task prediction.
- **Mechanism:** Techniques like reinforcement learning with process rewards, search augmentation, or explicit chain-of-thought distillation encourage the model to generate intermediate reasoning traces that resemble formal logical manipulations or case analysis. This builds a more generalizable competency that transfers across symbolization, model-building, and validity checking.
- **Core assumption:** The observed balanced performance across all three isolated skills is evidence of a deeper, more algorithmic reasoning process.
- **Evidence anchors:** o3: 99% validity, 98% symbolization, 99% countermodel; Qwen3-32B: >89% all tasks. No significant positive transfer from symbolization/countermodel fine-tuning to validity assessment in base models, but reasoning-tuned models start from a balanced baseline.

## Foundational Learning
### First-Order Logic (FO2 fragment) and Logical Skills Decomposition
- **Why needed here:** The entire benchmark is built on the two-variable fragment of first-order logic. Understanding the expressivity (e.g., quantifiers, predicates) and the decomposition into symbolization, countermodeling, and validity assessment is fundamental to interpreting the results.
- **Quick check question:** What logical form represents "Every rath that chased a tove is mimsy," and which specific skill does translating this sentence test?

### LLM Evaluation Paradigms (Behavioral vs. Mechanistic)
- **Why needed here:** The paper explicitly evaluates behavior (task accuracy) and warns it does not probe internal mechanisms. This distinction is crucial for not over-interpreting the results as evidence of what models "understand" versus what input-output mappings they have learned.
- **Quick check question:** Why does high performance on a validity assessment task not necessarily mean the model is performing first-order logic inference internally?

### Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) and Transfer Learning
- **Why needed here:** Section 5.3 uses LoRA to fine-tune on generated examples for specific skills and measures cross-task transfer (or lack thereof). This is key evidence for the skill isolation claim.
- **Quick check question:** What does the negative transfer result (improving symbolization does not improve validity) suggest about the relationship between these skills in standard LLMs?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Compositional Generator -> Solver-Verification Core -> Argument Constructor -> Task Instantiators -> Evaluation Pipeline

### Critical Path
Sentence Generation -> Formula Synthesis -> Argument Construction -> Task-Specific Formatting -> Model Inference -> Extraction/Normalization -> Solver-Based Verification

### Design Tradeoffs
- **FO2 fragment:** Traded expressivity for decidability and guaranteed finite model checking, limiting claims to this logic.
- **Bilingual design (English/Carrollian):** Traded natural language diversity for isolating syntax/semantic heuristics; Carrollian removes real-world knowledge but retains syntactic complexity.
- **Fixed evaluation set vs. generator:** Uses a static test set for comparability despite a potentially infinite generation space, limiting scope but ensuring reproducibility.

### Failure Signatures
- **Generator failure:** Z3 returns unsat/timeout during initial sentence/argument creation, indicating non-triviality constraints not met.
- **Extraction failure:** LLM extractor mis-parses model output (e.g., misidentifies predicate constants, fails to normalize notation), leading to false negatives; meta-evaluation showed ~1-2% rate.
- **Verification failure:** For symbolization, extracted formula is not logically equivalent to target; for countermodel, proposed structure does not satisfy premises+¬conclusion; for validity, selected option set does not match gold.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Skill Isolation Validation:** Run all models on the three tasks. Confirm the primary pattern: instruction-tuned models show validity > symbolization > countermodel discrepancy, while reasoning-tuned models are balanced.
2. **Bilingual Equivalence Check:** For each model, compare performance on English vs. Carrollian versions of symbolization and validity tasks. Expect minimal difference (<5% avg), supporting the claim that performance reflects logical syntax processing, not semantic knowledge.
3. **Transfer Failure Replication:** Replicate the PEFT experiment (§5.3): fine-tune a small instruction model (e.g., Llama-3.2-3B) on 100k symbolization examples and 100k countermodel examples separately. Measure deltas on all three tasks. Expect large in-task gains but non-positive (or negative) transfer to validity, confirming skill disentanglement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: Are reasoning-tuned models truly performing model-theoretic reasoning?
- **Basis in paper:** The Limitations state: "whether reasoning-oriented models that pass all three tasks are genuinely approximating model-theoretic reasoning or achieving this through more powerful but equally opaque means remains an open question for mechanistic investigation."
- **Why unresolved:** The benchmark evaluates only behavioral correctness, not internal computation; hence it's unclear if models use explicit symbolic manipulation or other strategies.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Mechanistic interpretability studies that reveal whether models construct and manipulate logical forms in a model-theoretic fashion during reasoning.

### Open Question 2: How do instruction-tuned LLMs achieve high validity assessment accuracy despite severe deficiencies in formal symbolization and countermodel construction?
- **Basis in paper:** Results show GPT-4o: validity 87.0%, symbolization ~70-80%, countermodel ~10-42%. The authors note this as surprising, suggesting models may reach valid judgments without robust symbolization or explicit countermodels.
- **Why unresolved:** The dissociation implies use of shortcuts; the precise reasoning pathway is not identified.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation experiments that selectively impair subskills (e.g., via prompting or editing) and measure validity impact; internal probes for implicit logical representations.

### Open Question 3: Why does fine-tuning on formal symbolization and countermodel construction not transfer to improved validity assessment?
- **Basis in paper:** Symbolization fine-tuning (+80.7) yields validity Δ -4.3; countermodel (+15.7) yields -6.4; joint (+84.0,+25.6) yields -5.7. Authors report no significant positive transfer.
- **Why unresolved:** Expected skill integration does not occur; the cause—task interference, different reasoning modes, etc.—is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic variations in fine-tuning (data mix, objectives, duration) and analysis of subskill usage during validity tasks, perhaps via step-by-step outputs.

## Limitations
- Benchmark confined to the two-variable fragment (FO2) and a fixed domain size of three; findings may not extend to richer logics or larger models.
- Evaluation relies on a GPT-4o extractor with a small error rate and uses a static test set that is not publicly available, raising risks of overfitting and hindering independent replication.
- Only a limited set of models (few instruction-tuned and reasoning-tuned) were evaluated; results might not generalize to other architectures or newer releases.
- Fine-tuning experiments used a single base model (Llama-3.2-3B) and one epoch, leaving hyperparameter sensitivity and transferability across models unexamined.
- Claims about heuristic versus systematic reasoning are based solely on behavioral outcomes, without mechanistic probing of model internals.

## Confidence
- **Instruction-tuned LLMs rely on heuristics for validity assessment**: Medium
- **Countermodel construction is exceptionally difficult for current LLMs**: Medium
- **Reasoning-tuned models achieve balanced, systematic competence**: Medium

## Next Checks
- **Broad model replication:** Evaluate at least 10 recent instruction-tuned (e.g., Llama-3.1, Mistral) and reasoning-tuned (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, o3-mini) models on the public LogicSkills set to confirm generalizability of the skill profile.
- **Mechanistic probing:** Use linear probes or activation patching on a high-performing model during validity tasks to test whether internal representations encode formal logical structures (e.g., quantifier scopes).
- **Benchmark extension:** Create versions with identity, three variables, and translations into 5+ natural languages; re-administer all three tasks to verify robustness of conclusions under increased logical and linguistic diversity.
