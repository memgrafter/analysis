# From We to Me: Theory Informed Narrative Shift with Abductive Reasoning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv:** 2603.03320
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03320
- **References:** 12
- **Result:** Outperforms zero-shot LLMs: up to 55.88% improvement in narrative orientation and 40.4% better semantic fidelity (lower KL divergence) on GPT‑4o, while modifying only a median 32.11% of tokens.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the problem of rewriting a story to match a different cultural orientation—specifically, shifting between individualistic and collectivistic perspectives—while preserving its core meaning, a task current LLMs find challenging. It introduces a neurosymbolic framework that combines social science theory with abductive reasoning to automatically extract interpretable rules identifying which narrative elements must change.

Evaluated across GPT‑4o, Llama‑4, Grok‑4, and Deepseek‑R1, the system consistently outperforms zero‑shot baselines. For GPT‑4o on collectivistic‑to‑individualistic shift, it achieves a 55.88% boost in a diagnostic orientation score and a 40.4% reduction in KL divergence (semantic drift), while modifying only a median 32.11% of the tokens. This demonstrates effective, efficient, and theory‑driven narrative transformation.

## Method Summary
The method comprises an offline rule‑learning phase and an online iterative transformation phase. During training, a corpus of stories with known orientation (individualistic or collectivistic) is segmented into chunks. For each chunk, an LLM‑based diagnostic survey answers 40 binary questions, yielding feature confidences. For every feature `f` and confidence level `µ`, the system computes `conf(f,µ)` as the proportion of training chunks with that annotation. These confidence values instantiate rule templates of the form `corpus_sim(X,f)_conf ← q(X,f)_µ`, creating a logic program Π that links the presence of certain chunk features to increased similarity with the target corpus.

For a new story, the system iteratively diagnoses its current chunks (`O_t`). An abductive solver (PyReason) searches for the minimal set `E_t` of alternative chunk‑feature pairs that, when incorporated into Π ∪ `O_t`, maximize the derived `corpus_sim` score via a parsimony function `σ`. The top‑`k` (ψ) pairs are selected as chunks to edit. Each chosen chunk is sent to a fixed‑prompt LLM that rewrites only that segment to exhibit features aligning with the target orientation, using the full story as context. The modified story is re‑diagnosed and the loop repeats up to three times. The output with the highest orientation score is kept. The approach edits only a small fraction of tokens (median 32.11%) and maintains low KL divergence, indicating strong semantic preservation.

## Key Results
- For GPT‑4o on collectivistic → individualistic shifts, the method achieves a **55.88% improvement** in the diagnostic orientation score and a **40.4% reduction** in KL divergence (semantic drift) relative to a zero‑shot baseline.
- Similar improvements are observed for Llama‑4, Grok‑4, and Deepseek‑R1, showing consistent gains across model families.
- The selective editing mechanism changes only a **median 32.11%** of tokens, demonstrating high content preservation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works because of three interacting mechanisms.

**1. Statistical rule extraction from a target corpus.** The system processes a training corpus of stories bearing the desired orientation (e.g., collectivistic) using an LLM‑based diagnostic survey that annotates each chunk on 40 cultural features. For each feature `f` and confidence level `µ`, it computes `conf(f,µ)`—the proportion of training chunks that exhibit that annotation—and instantiates rule templates `corpus_sim(X,f)_conf ← q(X,f)_µ`. This yields a logic program Π where any chunk possessing feature `f` contributes evidence that the story aligns with the target corpus. The core assumption is that a story’s cultural orientation can be approximated by aggregating the presence of features that are statistically prevalent in a corpus of that orientation. Evidence of this mechanism is given in the abstract and Section 5, although direct validation on held‑out corpora is not provided.

**2. Abductive reasoning to pinpoint impactful chunks.** For a test story `s_t`, its chunk‑level diagnoses constitute observations `O_t`. Formulating the abduction problem `<O_t, H, Π>`, the solver searches for the minimal explanation `E_t` (new feature annotations for existing chunks) that, when combined with Π, maximizes the derived `corpus_sim(s_t, target)` under a parsimony function `σ`. The optimization `ϕ_k` returns the top‑`k` (ψ) chunk‑feature pairs, representing the chunks whose feature changes would most improve orientation. The system then edits these chunks, re‑diagnoses, and iterates. The underlying assumption is that the most “suggestive” chunks—those whose transformation yields the largest increase in corpus similarity according to the learned rules—drive the overall narrative shift most efficiently. The formalization appears in Sections 4 and 5, and the abstract highlights that the method correctly identifies key narrative markers.

**3. Feature‑aware guided rewriting by an LLM.** When a chunk is selected, the system prompts an LLM with a fixed template (Appendix C). The prompt provides the chunk, its current feature diagnoses, the target orientation, and the full story context, instructing the model to rewrite *only* that segment so that the story becomes more characteristic of the target culture while preserving surrounding content. The assumption is that an LLM can execute a fine‑grained, feature‑driven edit without collateral changes. Figure 1 shows a successful modification (correctly changing a collectivistic phrase) and a failure (the LLM alters extra text). However, the paper lacks a systematic study of the LLM’s adherence to the one‑segment constraint and its propensity for hallucination or over‑modification.

## Foundational Learning
- **Individualism‑Collectivism Cultural Dimensions** – The binary dimension that defines the transformation task. A 40‑question diagnostic survey operationalizes this theory into computable narrative features (e.g., “Self‑Reliance” for individualism vs. “Group Ambitions” for collectivism). *Quick check:* How would an individualistic vs. collectivistic narrative differ in explaining a protagonist’s success? (e.g., “through personal grit” vs. “with family support”.)  
- **Abductive Reasoning in Annotated Logic** – The core algorithmic paradigm. The problem is cast as an abduction problem: observations `O` (current chunk features), hypothesis space `H` (possible alternative chunk features), and background knowledge `Π` (learned rules). The solver finds the minimal explanation `E` that maximizes a parsimony‑driven increase in `corpus_sim`. *Quick check:* Given `O` and `Π`, what does finding `E` enable? (It explains which chunk features must change to achieve a higher similarity to the target orientation under the rules.)  
- **Token‑Level Chunking & Diagnosis** – Stories are partitioned into “chunks” (subsets of the token sequence). The `diagnosis` function assigns a feature vector to every chunk. This granularity limits LLM calls and enables selective editing. The claim that only a median 32.11% of tokens are modified and the linear bound on LLM calls (Proposition 5.1) depend on this segmentation. *Quick check:* If a story of 100 tokens is split into 5 equal‑size chunks and the abductive step selects 2 chunks, what is the maximum number of LLM calls for that iteration? (Answer: 2, one per selected chunk, though the bound may allow more depending on implementation; the question probes understanding of the pipeline.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
- Diagnostic Survey Engine: LLM‑based module that, given a text chunk or story, outputs scores for 40 cultural features.  
- Logic Program (Π): Knowledge base containing R2 rules (linking chunk features to corpus similarity) and R1 rules (aggregating chunk features into story‑level features).  
- Abductive Solver (ϕ_k): Implemented via PyReason; takes current diagnoses O_t, hypothesis space H (all possible alternative chunk features), and Π; computes the top‑k chunk‑feature pairs E_t that maximize the parsimony function σ (increase in corpus similarity).  
- Chunk Transformer (llm_transform): LLM call with a fixed prompt that rewrites only the selected chunk according to target orientation and the chunk’s diagnosed features.  
- Iterative Controller (Algorithm 1): Orchestrates the loop: diagnose → abduct → transform for up to T_max iterations, then selects the best final story.

**Critical path**  
- **Training**: Corpus (with known orientation) → [Diagnosis] → chunk features → [Rule Extraction] → Π. (One‑time.)  
- **Transformation**: Test story s₀ → Loop t=0..T_max−1: Diagnose s_t → Abduction (E_t via Π) → For each chunk in E_t: llm_transform → s_{t+1}. After loop, pick s_t* with highest orientation score.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Chunk‑selection ψ (top‑k)**: Larger ψ modifies more text, risking semantic drift and incoherence; smaller ψ may not shift orientation enough. The paper conducts a hyperparameter study (Figure 7) to guide selection.  
- **Rule generality vs. specificity**: Rules from a small, genre‑specific corpus may be brittle; a larger, more diverse corpus yields robust rules but may introduce noise.  
- **LLM prompt simplicity**: The fixed prompt is easy to implement but may not consistently enforce segment‑only editing or full feature alignment. More elaborate prompts could improve quality at the cost of increased variability and token usage.

**Failure signatures**  
- **Abductive stage**: Returns chunks that are semantically peripheral (e.g., single adjectives) or proposes feature changes contradictory to the target orientation, often due to noisy rules or inaccurate diagnoses.  
- **Transformation stage**: The LLM modifies text outside the specified chunk, alters core plot elements, or fails to incorporate the target feature (e.g., retains collectivistic pronouns after an I→C instruction).  
- **Iterative stage**: Orientation scores plateau or drop after a few iterations, indicating conflicting modifications or diminishing returns.  
- **Model‑specific biases**: Some base LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek‑R1) exhibit inherent cultural leanings that may reduce observable improvement or distort rule learning.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Rule validation**: Apply Π to a held‑out set of stories with known orientations. The `diagnosis` should yield high `corpus_sim` for each story’s true orientation. Expected discriminative accuracy >85%.  
2. **Chunk identification sanity check**: For a test story known to be strongly collectivistic, run one abductive iteration for C→I. Manually inspect the top‑2 proposed chunks; they should contain unmistakable collectivistic markers (e.g., “we”, “community”, “together”).  
3. **End‑to‑end single‑iteration test**: Construct a 2‑3 sentence story with a single obvious cultural marker (e.g., a solo hero). Run the full pipeline for one iteration and verify: (a) only the sentence containing the marker is altered, (b) the alteration introduces the target feature (e.g., group references for C→I), and (c) the orientation score improves.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Beyond individualism‑collectivism:** The method currently transforms along a single binary dimension. How can it be extended to target specific cultural audiences with multiple intersecting cultural dimensions?  
- **Temporal dynamics:** The learned rules are static. How can a richer temporal rule structure be incorporated to handle non‑linear narratives such as flashbacks?  
- **Diagnosis robustness:** LLM‑based diagnosis may be biased by the model’s own cultural assumptions. How stable and valid are the feature assignments across different LLMs and prompts?  
- **Scalability to long texts and diverse genres:** The dataset consists of short narratives. Does the approach scale to longer forms (e.g., novels) and genres like news or social media?

## Limitations
- **Undisclosed implementation details:** The paper does not specify chunk segmentation boundaries, the exact prompts and LLM configuration for the diagnostic survey, nor the train/test split strategy, hampering exact reproduction.  
- **Annotation normalization ambiguity:** The conversion of 1‑5 survey scores to discrete feature values is only described as “normalized”; the precise mapping is unclear.  
- **Sparse error analysis:** While results are reported, there is little systematic analysis of abductive proposal quality, LLM transformation fidelity (e.g., adherence to segment‑only editing), or statistical significance of improvements.

1. **Rule discriminativity test:** Evaluate the learned logic program Π on a held‑out set of stories with known orientations. The `diagnosis` should assign higher `corpus_sim` to the true orientation of each story. Expect >85% accuracy.  
2. **Chunk proposal sanity check:** For a test story containing a single strong cultural marker (e.g., a collectivistic phrase), run one abductive iteration for the opposite direction. The top‑2 proposed chunks should contain the marker at least 90% of the time.  
3. **Single‑iteration fidelity test:** Transform a minimal story (2–3 sentences) with an obvious marker using one iteration. Verify that only the marked segment is edited, that the edit correctly introduces the target feature, and that the orientation score improves.
