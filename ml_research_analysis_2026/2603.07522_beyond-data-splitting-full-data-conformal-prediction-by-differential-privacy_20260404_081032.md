# Beyond Data Splitting: Full-Data Conformal Prediction by Differential Privacy
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.07522
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07522
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: DP-SCP achieves full-data conformal prediction with differential privacy, yielding sharper prediction sets than split-based approaches while maintaining near-nominal coverage.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DP-SCP, a framework that eliminates the data-splitting bottleneck in differentially private conformal prediction. By leveraging the stability of DP-SGD, DP-SCP uses the entire dataset for both training and calibration, and employs a conservative private quantile estimator (Buffered Right-Endpoint Binary Search) to guarantee valid coverage. Theoretically, any f-DP mechanism yields a coverage lower bound, and under DP-SGD’s stability, asymptotic recovery of the nominal level is achieved. Empirical results on BloodMNIST and California Housing show that DP-SCP produces significantly smaller prediction sets than the standard DP-Split baseline, with gains increasing under stricter privacy budgets.

## Method Summary
DP-SCP rethinks the conventional split-based conformal prediction pipeline in privacy-sensitive settings. It trains a model with DP-SGD on the full dataset and computes conformity scores on the same data. A private quantile estimate is obtained via the Buffered Right-Endpoint Binary Search algorithm, which outputs a threshold that is conservative by design, ensuring coverage never falls below the nominal level. Two variants are proposed: DP-SCP-F for finite-sample guarantees and DP-SCP-A for asymptotic efficiency under stability. This approach avoids the data inefficiency of splitting and exploits the inherent stability of DP training.

## Key Results
- At ε=2.0 on BloodMNIST, DP-SCP-A achieves average set size 1.492 vs. 2.003 for DP-Split and a singleton rate 0.611 vs. 0.385, with coverage ≈0.898 close to the nominal 0.90.
- DP-SCP variants consistently outperform DP-Split across ε ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0}, with larger improvements under stricter privacy budgets.
- The finite-sample variant (DP-SCP-F) maintains coverage strictly above nominal, while the asymptotic variant (DP-SCP-A) approaches nominal coverage more closely.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DP-SCP works because differentially private training mechanisms, especially DP-SGD, exhibit stability: the output distribution changes minimally when a single data point is added or removed. This stability justifies using the same data for both training and calibration, as the scores are not overly sensitive to individual records. The Buffered Right-Endpoint Binary Search algorithm privately estimates the quantile and, by construction, yields a threshold that is slightly higher than the true quantile, guaranteeing no under-coverage. Theoretically, any f-DP mechanism ensures that the score distribution is robust, giving a coverage lower bound f(α). Under DP-SGD’s specific stability condition, this bound tightens to the nominal level asymptotically as the sample size grows.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:  
Data -> DP-SGD Training (full data) -> Trained Model -> Score Computation (full data) -> Scores -> Buffered Right-Endpoint Binary Search (private quantile) -> Threshold -> Prediction Sets

**Critical Path**:  
1. Train model with DP-SGD on entire dataset.  
2. Compute conformity scores for all training points using the trained model.  
3. Run Buffered Right-Endpoint Binary Search on the scores to output a quantile estimate q̂.  
4. For a new test point, compute its score and construct the prediction set as {y : score(x,y) ≤ q̂}.

**Design Tradeoffs**:  
- *Data reuse vs. splitting*: Using all data improves model accuracy and score calibration efficiency but requires stability justification; splitting is simpler but wastes data.  
- *Finite-sample conservatism vs. asymptotic efficiency*: DP-SCP-F guarantees coverage at the cost of larger sets; DP-SCP-A is more efficient but relies on asymptotic arguments.  
- *Private quantile algorithm*: The chosen Buffered Right-Endpoint Binary Search is conservative and simple, but may not be optimal in terms of variance; more sophisticated methods could improve efficiency.

**Failure Signatures**:  
- Coverage dropping below the nominal level (1-α), indicating insufficient conservatism or instability.  
- Excessive prediction set sizes, suggesting over-conservatism or poor model calibration.  
- High variance in coverage across runs, possibly due to insufficient privacy budget or poor tuning of DP-SGD hyperparameters.

**First Experiments**:  
1. **Baseline comparison**: Run DP-SCP (both variants) and DP-Split on BloodMNIST and California Housing across ε = {0.5, 1.0, 2.0} to replicate the reported coverage and set size metrics.  
2. **Stability validation**: Perform ablation by varying the DP-SGD noise multiplier and clipping norm to assess how stability affects DP-SCP's performance, especially the asymptotic variant.  
3. **Finite-sample behavior**: Evaluate DP-SCP-F on datasets of varying sizes (e.g., n=1k, 5k, 10k) to quantify the efficiency loss relative to DP-SCP-A and to confirm that the gap narrows with increasing n.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions. However, based on the limitations, potential directions include extending the stability argument to other DP mechanisms and evaluating performance under distribution shift.

## Limitations
- Finite-sample conservatism of DP-SCP-F may lead to substantially larger prediction sets in small-data regimes.
- The stability argument for reusing data is currently established only for DP-SGD; its applicability to other DP training algorithms (e.g., DP-FW, DP-Adam) remains open.
- The empirical evaluation is limited to two datasets; broader validation across diverse tasks and data distributions is needed to confirm generalizability.

## Confidence
- **High**: Empirical superiority of DP-SCP over DP-Split on BloodMNIST and California Housing for ε ∈ {0.5,1.0,2.0}; theoretical guarantee that any f-DP mechanism yields a coverage lower bound f(α).
- **Medium**: Asymptotic recovery of nominal coverage under DP-SGD's stability condition; practical efficacy of the Buffered Right-Endpoint Binary Search as a conservative private quantile estimator.
- **Low**: Generalization of the stability-and-reuse argument to all f-DP mechanisms; claims about broader applicability beyond the tested regression/classification tasks.

## Next Checks
1. **Mechanism generality test**: Implement DP-SCP using at least two other established DP training algorithms (e.g., DP-FTRL, DP-Adam) on the same benchmarks to validate if the stability argument and empirical gains hold generically.
2. **Small-data regime assessment**: Run DP-SCP and DP-Split on significantly smaller versions of the datasets (e.g., n<5,000 for BloodMNIST) to quantify the finite-sample efficiency loss of DP-SCP-F and confirm DP-SCP-A's asymptotic behavior in practice.
3. **Distribution shift stress test**: Evaluate DP-SCP's coverage and set size under covariate shift (e.g., using a different hospital's data for BloodMNIST) to probe the robustness of the stability assumption and private calibration when the held-out (calibration) data distribution diverges from the training data.
