# Quantum-Audit: Evaluating the Reasoning Limits of LLMs on Quantum Computing

### Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.10092
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10092
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: Top models (e.g., Claude Opus 4.5) exceed human experts (84% vs 74%) but show a 12-point drop on expert-written questions and face challenges in quantum security and false-premise reasoning.

## Executive Summary

Quantum-Audit introduces a systematic benchmark to evaluate large language models' conceptual understanding of quantum computing, going beyond prior focus on code generation. The benchmark comprises 2,700 questions—1,000 written by experts, 1,000 extracted from validated research papers, and 350 each of open-ended and false-premise items—covering seven core topics. Evaluations on 26 models against 43 human experts reveal that frontier models like Claude Opus 4.5 surpass average human performance (84% vs 74%) but show consistent weaknesses: a 12-point accuracy gap between expert-written and LLM-extracted questions, sub-73% scores on quantum security, and below-66% accuracy on false-premise tasks. Agentic/deep research modes and multilingual capabilities yield measurable improvements, yet the results highlight persistent gaps in depth of reasoning and robustness.

The study underscores the need for more nuanced evaluation of LLMs in specialized scientific domains. While top models demonstrate impressive breadth, their performance degrades on complex or recent topics and when faced with erroneous premises, suggesting limitations in genuine comprehension. The introduced benchmark provides a foundation for future work to bridge these gaps through improved training, retrieval augmentation, and reasoning architectures.

## Method Summary

Quantum-Audit’s methodology centers on a carefully designed benchmark with 2,700 questions across four formats. The multiple-choice core (2,000 questions) combines 1,000 items authored by domain experts and 1,000 automatically extracted from peer-reviewed quantum computing papers, ensuring both depth and coverage. An additional 700 questions—350 open-ended and 350 false-premise—probe deeper reasoning and consistency-checking abilities. The evaluation involved 26 LLMs (both open- and closed-source) and a human baseline of 43 quantum computing experts (scores ranging 23–86%, average 74%). Models were tested on overall accuracy, topic-specific performance (seven domains), translational robustness (Spanish/French), and the impact of agentic/deep research modes. The critical path included benchmark construction, baseline testing, selective deeper evaluation of top models, and comparative analysis to identify strengths, failure modes, and the influence of question origin.

## Key Results
- Top models achieved 84% overall accuracy, exceeding the human expert average of 74%, but showed a 12-point drop on expert-written questions compared to LLM-extracted ones.
- Accuracy on quantum security topics fell below 73% for the best models, and false-premise reasoning remained under 66%, indicating weaknesses in advanced and robustness-critical areas.
- Agentic/deep research modes boosted performance by an average of 6.7 points, and top models maintained above 72% accuracy in Spanish and French translations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Question source influences reasoning depth
- **Claim**: The benchmark's question source (expert-written vs. LLM-extracted) creates a systematic performance differential.
- **Mechanism**: Expert-written questions likely probe deeper conceptual reasoning or test less-represented concepts in training data, while LLM-extracted questions may inadvertently align with statistical patterns encountered during pre-training.
- **Core assumption**: Question phrasing and origin influence the depth of reasoning required beyond surface-level recall.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "top models showed an average 12-point accuracy drop on expert-written questions compared to LLM-generated ones."
  - [section 4.1]: "models consistently score lower on expert-written questions... even among top performers... the gap suggests that expert-written questions demand deeper reasoning."
  - [corpus]: Weak direct evidence; neighboring papers (e.g., "LLM-Generated Tips Rival Expert-Created Tips") focus on educational tips rather than benchmark design effects.
- **Break condition**: If models perform equivalently across both sources, the source-based reasoning gap is not a primary factor.

### Mechanism 2: Agentic modes enable external reasoning gains
- **Claim**: Agentic/deep research modes enable tangible accuracy gains by facilitating external information access and multi-step reasoning.
- **Mechanism**: These modes allow models to break down complex queries, search for relevant information, and synthesize answers beyond their static parameterized knowledge.
- **Core assumption**: Improved performance stems from dynamic information retrieval and iterative reasoning rather than additional training.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Agentic/deep research modes improved scores by an average of 6.7 points."
  - [section 4.3]: Shows consistent improvements across frontier models (e.g., Gemini 3 Deep Research +8.6 points).
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like "SciReplicate-Bench" and "MerLean" highlight agentic frameworks for research paper tasks, supporting the utility of external tools.
- **Break condition**: If no improvement is observed or if gains are inconsistent across model families, the value of agentic modes for this domain is limited.

### Mechanism 3: Multilingual transfer is uneven
- **Claim**: Multilingual performance reveals uneven knowledge transfer, with systematic degradation in non-dominant languages.
- **Mechanism**: Specialized technical vocabulary and conceptual mappings may not transfer equally across languages due to training data distribution.
- **Core assumption**: Quantum computing knowledge in LLMs is linguistically anchored, with English-dominant training causing gaps.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "top models maintained above 72% accuracy in Spanish and French... while smaller models show more pronounced degradation."
  - [section 4.5]: "models consistently performing better in French than Spanish" with a visual gap in Figure 5.
  - [corpus]: Weak; neighbors focus on monolingual quantum benchmarks or general LLM multilingual ability, not quantum-specific transfer.
- **Break condition**: If models show no statistically significant difference between languages, linguistic transfer is robust for quantum concepts.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - Question-generation pipeline (expert-written, LLM-extracted from papers, open-ended, false-premise).
  - Model evaluation harness (API access for closed-source, local inference for open-source).
  - Human baseline survey (43 experts, varied experience levels).
  - Multilingual subset (500 questions translated to Spanish/French).
  - Analysis modules (topic-wise scoring, format comparison, agentic mode testing).
- **Critical path:**
  1. Assemble 2,700 questions across formats and topics.
  2. Run all 26 models on the core 2,000 multiple-choice subset.
  3. Select top models for extended evaluation (open-ended, false-premise, multilingual, agentic modes).
  4. Compare against human baseline distributions.
  5. Perform topic-wise and format-wise breakdowns to identify weaknesses.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Multiple-choice efficiency vs. open-ended depth:** MCQ allows scalable scoring but may not assess synthesizing ability; open-ended does but requires manual or complex automated evaluation.
  - **Synthetic (LLM-generated) question volume vs. expert quality:** LLM extraction scales coverage but risks alignment with training data; expert-written ensures depth but is costly.
  - **Breadth (7 topics) vs. depth per topic:** Covering core areas provides general assessment but may miss niche cutting-edge subtopics.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - >10-point drop on expert-written vs. LLM-extracted questions indicates reliance on surface pattern matching.
  - <70% on quantum security suggests inability to keep pace with rapidly evolving research.
  - <66% on false-premise questions shows a tendency to accept and build on erroneous assumptions.
  - Large performance gaps between languages (e.g., >5 points) indicate weak multilingual knowledge transfer.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline MCQ evaluation:** Run all 26 models on QA2000 (1,000 expert + 1,000 LLM-extracted) to establish overall rankings and the expert-written gap.
  2. **False-premise stress test:** Evaluate top 9 models on the 350 false-premise questions to quantify reasoning robustness.
  3. **Agentic mode comparison:** Test Gemini 3, Claude Opus 4.5, and GPT-5.2 with and without research modes on a 500-question balanced subset to measure gains from external reasoning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Why do LLMs exhibit a consistent performance gap between expert-written and LLM-extracted quantum computing questions?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Section 4.1 reports "Models consistently score lower on expert-written questions... with even top performers like Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.2 Pro achieving only 77-78% accuracy compared to nearly 90% on LLM-extracted questions." The authors suggest the gap indicates deeper reasoning demands or less prominent training coverage.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper does not empirically isolate the factors (e.g., abstraction level, phrasing, concept representation) underlying the difficulty difference.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic decomposition of question attributes (e.g., cognitive complexity, term rarity) and controlled experiments with paraphrased questions to identify causal factors.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What specific reasoning failures cause LLMs to accept false premises in quantum computing contexts, and can these be mitigated?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Section 4.2 states "models frequently accept and work within erroneous premises rather than challenging them... even the best achieving only around 65% accuracy" on false premise questions.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper lacks an error taxonomy; it is unclear whether failures stem from lack of knowledge, inability to detect inconsistency, or other cognitive biases.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Fine-grained analysis of model errors on false premise items, testing interventions (e.g., prompting for premise validation, reinforcement learning with corrective feedback).

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Why does performance degrade significantly on quantum security topics compared to foundational concepts, even for top models?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Section 4.1 and 5 note that "top models achieve over 92% accuracy on basic quantum concepts but drop below 75% on quantum security questions... Security questions prove particularly challenging because they address recent attack research, where literature is limited and terminology less standardized."
- **Why unresolved**: The hypothesis about recency and terminology standardization is not validated with quantitative evidence linking question features to model scores.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Correlate question-level difficulty with publication year, citation count, and term overlap with training data; assess performance on older vs. newer security questions.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: To what extent do agentic and deep research modes improve LLM performance on quantum computing tasks across different topics, and what mechanisms drive the improvement?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Section 4.3 reports an average improvement of 6.7 percentage points with these modes, e.g., Gemini 3 Deep Research gain +8.6 points, and Claude Opus 4.5 Research reaching 85.6%. The authors suggest advantages like accessing current information and multi-step reasoning.
- **Why unresolved**: The contribution of retrieval versus reasoning components and topic-specific variation remain unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Ablation experiments that disable external search while keeping reasoning, analyze reasoning traces, and measure topic-wise gains to see if certain areas benefit more.

## Limitations

- Benchmark design confounds question source (expert vs. LLM) with potential difficulty or topic distribution, and human expert scores vary widely (23–86%), adding baseline noise.
- Corpus signals indicate nascent reception (average citations 0.0) with limited external validation of quantum-specific multilingual or agentic effects.
- Causal mechanisms (e.g., "deeper reasoning" for expert questions) remain inferred rather than isolated experimentally.

## Confidence

- **Source-based reasoning gap**: Medium
- **Agentic mode improvements**: High
- **Multilingual knowledge transfer**: Medium

## Next Checks

1. **Control for question difficulty**: Match expert-written and LLM-extracted questions on length, topic, and Bloom’s taxonomy level via expert raters; re-evaluate model performance to isolate source effect.
2. **Agentic generalization test**: Apply agentic/deep research modes to a held-out set drawn from recent (2024) quantum arXiv papers, unseen during benchmark creation.
3. **Native-speaker validation**: Have bilingual quantum experts assess translated (Spanish/French) question fidelity and correlate model performance with their ratings to disentangle language vs. concept-transfer issues.
