# Multimodal Deep Learning for Diabetic Foot Ulcer Staging Using Integrated RGB and Thermal Imaging

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.26952
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26952
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: A multimodal RGB+Thermal deep learning model achieved 93.25% accuracy for DFU staging, outperforming single-modality approaches.

## Executive Summary
This study presents a portable Raspberry Pi-based system for simultaneous RGB and thermal imaging of diabetic foot ulcers (DFUs). The authors collected a dataset of 1,205 images across six Wagner stages and trained convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on RGB-only, thermal-only, and fused RGB+Thermal (4-channel) inputs. The multimodal approach consistently delivered superior classification performance, with VGG16 reaching 93.25% accuracy, 92.53% F1-score, and 91.03% MCC on the combined dataset. Grad-CAM visualizations demonstrated that the thermal channel enhanced localization of temperature anomalies while RGB provided structural context. The work highlights the value of complementary physiological cues in automated DFU staging and proposes a low-cost, standardized acquisition framework.

## Method Summary
The authors built a custom hardware rig using a Raspberry Pi 4B, a CSI RGB camera, and a USB FLIR Lepton 3.5 thermal sensor mounted in a 3D-printed enclosure to capture aligned RGB and thermal images. Preprocessing involved converting raw thermal data to Celsius, then applying an adaptive dynamic windowing technique to segment the foot region across varying ambient temperatures. RGB images were resized, and the thermal channel was stacked as a fourth channel to form a 4-channel tensor. Three datasets (RGB, thermal, fused) were created. Several CNN architectures (VGG16, ResNet50, DenseNet121, EfficientNetB0) pre-trained on ImageNet were fine-tuned with a custom two-layer classification head (1024→512→6) using stratified 5-fold cross-validation, class-weighted cross-entropy loss, and data augmentation. Performance was evaluated via accuracy, F1-score, and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), with Grad-CAM used for explainability.

## Key Results
- Multimodal (RGB+Thermal) training outperformed both single-modality baselines across all tested CNN architectures.
- Best model (VGG16 on RGB+Thermal) achieved 93.25% accuracy, 92.53% F1-score, and 91.03% MCC.
- Grad-CAM showed that the thermal channel improved focus on temperature anomalies while RGB contributed structural details, leading to more compact and lesion-centric activation maps.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
We detail the mechanisms underlying the observed performance gains, based on evidence from the paper and our analysis.

### Mechanism 1: Complementary Physiological Information Fusion
- **Claim:** The multimodal (RGB+Thermal) approach improves DFU stage classification accuracy by providing complementary physiological information.
- **Mechanism:** The 4-channel input allows a single CNN to learn joint feature representations where the RGB channels encode structural/textural cues (wound boundaries, tissue discoloration) and the thermal channel encodes temperature anomalies linked to inflammation/infection. The model learns to weight and fuse these cues during convolutional feature extraction.
- **Core assumption:** The temperature distribution around an ulcer is a clinically meaningful signal correlated with Wagner stage, and this signal is spatially approximately aligned with the RGB wound region in the side-by-side camera setup.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "The multimodal RGB+Thermal approach consistently outperformed single-modality training... Grad-CAM visualizations confirmed that the thermal channel improved localization of temperature anomalies while the RGB channel provided complementary structural information."
  - Section 2.3/Figure 7: Grad-CAM comparison shows the multimodal model has more compact, lesion-focused activations versus broader (RGB-only) or diffuse (thermal-only) patterns.
- **Break condition:** Performance gain would diminish if the thermal channel added noise (e.g., poor spatial registration, low thermal contrast in early stages) or if the model backbone lacked sufficient capacity to learn cross-modal interactions from a 4-channel input.

### Mechanism 2: Standardized Acquisition Reduces Variability
- **Claim:** The portable Raspberry Pi-based acquisition system reduces capture variability, improving longitudinal comparability and model generalizability.
- **Mechanism:** By fixing the relative position of RGB and thermal sensors in a 3D-printed enclosure, the system enforces consistent field-of-view overlap, distance, and angle. This minimizes intra-patient variation across captures that would otherwise obscure true clinical progression in image-based analysis.
- **Core assumption:** Standardized capture geometry is a primary source of noise in DFU image monitoring, and a low-cost, fixed hardware rig can achieve clinical-grade repeatability.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Introduction: "standardising home monitoring in terms of fixed distance, angle, and lighting in a repeatable and user-independent manner is challenging" and the proposed system aims to solve this.
  - Section 2.1: Detailed description of the side-by-side mounted cameras in a custom PLA enclosure to share an "approximately overlapping FoV."
- **Break condition:** The mechanism fails if users do not properly position the foot or if the rigid mount cannot accommodate varying foot sizes/angles, re-introducing variability.

### Mechanism 3: Adaptive Thermal Filtering for Robustness
- **Claim:** The adaptive thermal image filtering (dynamic windowing) makes thermal data usable for CNNs despite low resolution and ambient temperature shifts.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of a fixed temperature threshold, the processing pipeline calculates the image's mean temperature and adaptively scales the normalization window (30-45°C range) to preserve thermal contrast. This segments the foot region robustly across different ambient conditions.
- **Core assumption:** The foot region can be reliably isolated by its relative temperature contrast against the background, and this contrast shifts predictably with ambient conditions.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Section 2.2: "Instead of employing a fixed temperature interval, a dynamic windowing approach was implemented to adapt to ambient temperature fluctuations... This adaptive filtering ensures that the foot area is consistently highlighted."
  - Section 2.2/Equation 1: The explicit formula for raw-to-Celsius conversion and the description of the iterative threshold adjustment.
- **Break condition:** The filtering fails if the foot is very cold (e.g., poor perfusion) or if the background contains other heat sources within the adaptive window, leading to mis-segmentation.

## Architecture Onboarding
We break down the system architecture and provide guidance for reproducing and extending the work.

- **Component map:** 
  Hardware Layer (Raspberry Pi 4B + CSI RGB Camera + USB FLIR Lepton 3.5 + 7" Touchscreen + 3D-printed PLA enclosure) -> Preprocessing Pipeline (thermal raw-to-Celsius conversion, adaptive foot segmentation via dynamic temperature windowing, RGB resizing, fusion: thermal channel stacked as 4th channel) -> Model Core (pre-trained CNN backbone with modified input layer for 4 channels, followed by custom head: GlobalAvgPool -> FC(1024)+ReLU+BN+Dropout(0.5) -> FC(512)+ReLU+BN+Dropout(0.5) -> FC(6)) -> Training Loop (5-fold stratified CV, class-weighted CrossEntropy, Adam, augmentations) -> Evaluation & XAI (Metrics: Accuracy, F1, MCC; Visualization: Grad-CAM)

- **Critical path:** The most resource-sensitive path is: Data Collection (Hardware) -> Thermal Preprocessing (adaptive filtering) -> 4-channel Tensor Creation -> Model Fine-tuning. Failure in adaptive filtering or tensor fusion will propagate to all models.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Thermal resolution vs. cost: Used 160x120 Lepton for affordability/portability, but this limits thermal detail versus high-res sensors.
  - Modality fusion point: Chose early fusion (4-channel input) over late fusion (separate encoders + merge). This is simpler and worked well but assumes approximate pixel alignment.
  - Head complexity: Added a two-layer heavyweight head (1024-512 neurons) on top of compact backbones like DenseNet121. This increases parameters but may help integrate multimodal features.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Thermal channel dominance/noise: Grad-CAM shows model ignoring RGB or focusing on non-foot thermal noise. Diagnose: Check adaptive filtering output; verify foot segmentation.
  - Severe early-stage confusion: Low precision/recall for Grade 0/1. Diagnose: Intrinsic clinical ambiguity; possibly insufficient thermal contrast.
  - Fold variance > 2% in MCC: Instability suggests data leakage or poor stratification. Diagnose: Audit CV split implementation.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce single-modality baselines: Train VGG16 on pure RGB and pure thermal sets. Confirm performance gap (RGB > thermal) matches paper (Table 3 vs 4). This validates your preprocessing pipeline.
  2. Implement and test 4-channel fusion: Modify VGG16's first Conv layer to accept 4 channels (replicate first layer weights across channel dim or random init). Train on RGB+Thermal. Expect >1% accuracy jump over best single-modality.
  3. Grad-CAM sanity check: For a known Grade 2 sample, generate Grad-CAM for RGB-only vs. multimodal models. Verify multimodal shows a tighter, ulcer-centric heatmap as in Figure 7. If not, investigate if thermal channel is being used.

## Limitations
- Dataset limited to one site (1,205 images) with severe class imbalance, constraining generalizability.
- No explicit registration between RGB and thermal channels; side‑by‑side assumption may cause misalignment.
- Hardware and adaptive filtering robustness not validated in real-world settings.

## Confidence
- Multimodal fusion improves staging: Medium
- Portable system standardizes capture: Low
- Adaptive thermal filtering works: Medium
- Grad-CAM thermal localization: Low

## Next Checks
1. Multi-center external validation on an independent dataset to assess generalizability and identify dataset bias.
2. Registration sensitivity analysis: artificially misalign channels and measure performance drop; compare against an explicitly registered fusion (e.g., using homography).
3. Preprocessing ablation: replace adaptive filtering with fixed thresholds or other methods; compute segmentation overlap (e.g., IoU) and impact on classification F1/MCC.
