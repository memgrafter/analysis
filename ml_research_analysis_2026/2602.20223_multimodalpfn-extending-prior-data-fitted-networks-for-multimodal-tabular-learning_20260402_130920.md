# MultiModalPFN: Extending Prior-Data Fitted Networks for Multimodal Tabular Learning

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.20223
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20223
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MultiModalPFN extends TabPFN to images/text via modality projectors, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on multimodal tabular benchmarks and robustness in low-data regimes.

## Executive Summary
TabPFN, a prior-data fitted network for tabular data, cannot inherently process non-tabular modalities like images or text. MultiModalPFN (MMPFN) bridges this gap by introducing lightweight modality projectors that transform frozen image and text embeddings into a tabular-compatible token format. This design preserves TabPFN's powerful in-context learning priors while enabling multimodal integration. The proposed projector—a Multi-Head Gated MLP followed by a Cross-Attention Pooler—mitigates attention imbalance, a key failure mode where token-count disparities cause one modality to dominate. Evaluated on medical and general multimodal benchmarks, MMPFN consistently outperforms existing methods, showing particular strength in data-scarce scenarios and monotonic gains as more modalities are added.

## Method Summary
MMPFN builds on TabPFN by adding per-modality encoders (frozen DINOv2 for images, ELECTRA for text) and a trainable modality projector. The projector first uses a Multi-Head Gated MLP (MGM) to expand a single `[CLS]` embedding from each non-tabular encoder into multiple candidate tokens, promoting diverse representations. A Cross-Attention Pooler (CAP) then condenses these into a fixed-size set of tokens, controlling token count to prevent attention imbalance. These tokens are concatenated with tabular tokens from TabPFN's frozen encoder and fed into TabPFN's backbone, which processes the combined sequence in an in-context learning framework. Only the modality projector, TabPFN backbone, and a lightweight decoder head are fine-tuned, leveraging TabPFN's pretrained tabular prior while adapting to multimodal inputs.

## Key Results
- MMPFN achieves top accuracy on image-tabular tasks: PAD-UFES-20 (85.22%), CBIS-DDSM Mass (74.53%), Calc (75.40%), PetFinder (40.74%).
- On text-tabular tasks, it reaches 47.78% on Airbnb and 66.26% on Cloth.
- Demonstrates robustness in low-data regimes (e.g., using only 10% of CBIS-DDSM Mass data) and monotonic improvement with added modalities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** The modality projector (MGM + CAP) successfully transforms non-tabular embeddings into a set of tabular-compatible tokens that preserve diverse information while fitting into the TabPFN backbone's expected input format.  
**Mechanism:** MGM expands a single `[CLS]` embedding into *N* candidate tokens via separate MLP heads with GLU gating, encouraging specialization. CAP then pools these *N* tokens into a fixed-size *K* set via cross-attention with learned queries, producing a compact, calibrated summary.  
**Core assumption:** TabPFN requires an input structured as a table where each feature is a token; non-tabular modalities must be projected into this token-based feature space without critical information loss.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the projectors "transform non-tabular embeddings into tabular-compatible tokens" and mitigate imbalance. Section 3.2 details MGM as expansion and CAP as condensation. Corpus shows active work on scaling TabPFN, underscoring the input-format challenge.  
**Break condition:** If MGM heads learn redundantly or CAP discards essential information, the backbone cannot utilize the non-tabular signal, leading to degradation akin to a single linear projection.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** MGM+CAP directly mitigates attention imbalance—where a modality with more tokens dominates the self-attention budget—by controlling the effective token count for non-tabular modalities.  
**Mechanism:** The total attention a modality receives is proportional to token count (*N*) times per-token quality (*c*). CAP fixes the output token count (*K*) for non-tabular modalities, balancing *N* across modalities even when raw non-tabular representations are large.  
**Core assumption:** TabPFN's attention is sensitive to raw token counts; imbalance leads to suppression of lower-count modalities regardless of feature quality.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions mitigating attention imbalance. Section 3.4 provides the approximation E[aI] ≈ NI cI / (NI cI + NT cT) and notes token-count imbalance induces attention imbalance. Section 4.3/Fig 2b empirically shows performance degrades with high token ratios and that CAP (fixed output size) maintains robustness.  
**Break condition:** Mitigation fails if the minority modality's per-token quality (*c*) is too low, or if CAP's learned queries fail to extract a representative summary, causing information loss that outweighs balance benefits.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Freezing per-modality encoders and only fine-tuning the modality projector and TabPFN backbone allows MMPFN to reuse TabPFN's strong in-context learning priors while adapting to new modalities, achieving good performance with minimal task-specific data and compute.  
**Mechanism:** TabPFN is pretrained on vast synthetic tabular distributions, learning general-purpose in-context inference weights. The modality projector learns to "translate" frozen non-tabular embeddings into the semantic space expected by these pretrained weights. The decoder head adapts to the specific classification task. This avoids catastrophic forgetting of the tabular prior and reduces need for large multimodal training data.  
**Core assumption:** Representations from large pretrained encoders (DINOv2, ELECTRA) are semantically aligned enough with synthetic tabular features that a lightweight projector can bridge them without retraining the encoders.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states MMPFN extends TabPFN through a "unified pathway" with "lightweight components" and lower training costs. Section 3.3 explains freezing encoders and training projector, backbone, decoder. Section 4.3/Table 5 shows robustness in low-data regimes (10% data), outperforming TIP (which uses self-supervised pretraining), suggesting the frozen prior provides strong regularization.  
**Break condition:** If non-tabular embeddings come from a domain/model with feature space fundamentally misaligned with the tabular prior (e.g., radically different distribution or scale), the lightweight projector cannot perform the translation, and performance suffers compared to a fully trainable multimodal model.

## Foundational Learning
### Amortized Bayesian Inference (TabPFN's Core Paradigm)
**Why needed here:** MMPFN inherits TabPFN's capability for fast, accurate inference on small tabular datasets via its pretrained prior. Understanding this is key to why MMPFN freezes the tabular encoder and backbone.  
**Quick check:** How does a model pretrained on *synthetic* tabular data generalize to *real* small datasets without task-specific fine-tuning of its core weights?

### Attention Mechanism and Token Count Bias
**Why needed here:** The paper's central problem (attention imbalance) and solution (CAP) stem from transformer attention's softmax normalization over tokens; more tokens lead to more attention share.  
**Quick check:** In multi-head attention, if Modality A has 100 tokens and Modality B has 5, what is the expected effect on the attention received by Modality B's tokens, assuming initial per-token relevance is equal?

### Embedding Space Alignment in Multimodal Learning
**Why needed here:** MGM and CAP are fundamentally alignment mechanisms. Negative results with MMD losses suggest alignment is about projecting into a space where the pretrained TabPFN can meaningfully attend, not forcing identical distributions.  
**Quick check:** Why might reducing distributional discrepancy (e.g., with MMD) between tabular and non-tabular embeddings *harm* performance in this framework?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Frozen Image/Text Encoder -> `[CLS]` -> Multi-Head Gated MLP (MGM) -> Cross-Attention Pooler (CAP) -> Concatenate with Frozen Tabular Encoder Tokens -> TabPFN Backbone (in-context) -> Decoder Head -> Prediction

**Critical path:** Non-tabular `[CLS]` embeddings are expanded by MGM into *N* tokens, pooled by CAP into fixed *K* tokens, then concatenated with tabular tokens. The combined sequence is processed by TabPFN's backbone in an in-context setup (training and test rows concatenated), and the decoder predicts test labels.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Memory vs. Information:** Using all patch tokens from ViT is informative but increases memory ~500x vs. `[CLS]` + MGM. MGM+CAP offers a tunable trade-off between token count (*K*) and representational capacity.
- **Freezing vs. Fine-tuning:** Freezing encoders preserves strong pretrained representations and reduces compute, but relies entirely on the modality projector's quality for cross-modal alignment.
- **Fixed *K* vs. Variable *N*:** CAP produces a fixed *K*, enforcing balance. This prevents imbalance but caps maximum useful information from a very rich modality (empirically, performance peaks when *K* ~ number of tabular features).

**Failure signatures:**
1. **Attention Imbalance:** Accuracy drops when non-tabular token count (or raw MGM *N*) vastly exceeds or is vastly exceeded by tabular token count (see Fig 2b). Symptom: one modality dominates attention.
2. **Overcompression:** Poor performance when using single-head (Linear/MLP) projector instead of MGM (Table 4). Symptom: inability to leverage non-tabular signal effectively.
3. **Catastrophic Forgetting (if backbone not frozen appropriately):** If TabPFN backbone weights are heavily retrained, it loses its in-context prior and becomes a standard multimodal transformer, losing low-data robustness.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Ablate Modality Projector:** Replace MGM+CAP with single Linear/MLP projection (Table 4) to validate necessity of multi-head expansion and pooling.
2. **Vary CAP Output Size (*K*):** Systematically change *K* (pooled tokens) and plot accuracy vs. ratio of (non-tabular tokens *K*) / (tabular tokens). Reproduce Figure 2b/S1 to confirm attention imbalance curve and identify optimal balance.
3. **Modality Dropout:** Train with only tabular+image, or tabular+text, or all three. Check for monotonic improvement with added modalities (Figure 4) to validate component additivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** Why does adding MMD-based distribution alignment between tabular and non-tabular embeddings degrade classification accuracy in MMPFN, despite the hypothesis that such alignment should improve multimodal integration?  
**Basis:** "[explicit] while MMD-style losses can reduce distributional gaps, they may also suppress discriminative variations, leading to performance degradation."  
**Why unresolved:** Only an intuitive explanation; no systematic analysis of how alignment regularizers affect discriminative power in multimodal transformers.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments measuring feature discriminability (e.g., mutual information, linear probe accuracy) across varying MMD weights, plus theoretical examination of MMD's impact on decision boundaries under multimodal attention.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** Under what specific data characteristics does training on only 10% of the CBIS-DDSM Mass dataset improve accuracy compared to using the full set?  
**Basis:** "[explicit] Table 5 and accompanying text note: 'Interestingly, on the CBIS-DDSM Mass dataset, performance improved under subsampling.'"  
**Why unresolved:** Authors attribute to synthetic prior but do not investigate dataset-specific factors (e.g., label noise, distribution shift).  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Detailed distributional comparison between full and subset data (label noise estimation, feature statistics), controlled noise injection, and analysis of optimization landscapes to identify overfitting/underfitting in full-data regime.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Why does the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) variant of the modality projector underperform and show instability in low-data regimes compared to MGM?  
**Basis:** "[explicit] Table 4 and text state: 'Within the multi-head family, the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) variant is less effective and less stable across datasets, suggesting that sparse expert routing is hard to exploit in the low-data regime considered here.'"  
**Why unresolved:** No deeper diagnostics; no analysis of expert utilization, routing collapse, or capacity constraints.  
**What evidence would resolve it:** Metrics such as expert load entropy, routing consistency across random seeds, and scaling studies with varying expert counts and data sizes; synthetic multimodal data could isolate impact of sparsity and data scarcity on MoE behavior.

## Limitations
- MGM/CAP hyperparameters (number of heads *N*, output dimension *d*, CAP query count *K*) are dataset-specific and not provided for all datasets.
- MGM MLP head sizes and CAP MLP architecture are underspecified; exact dimensionalities missing.
- Preprocessing pipeline details (image resizing, text cleaning, categorical encoding) are not fully documented; codebase is the only source.
- Evaluation limited to classification; regression tasks not covered.
- Generalization to other modalities (audio, video) unproven.

## Confidence
- Modality projector effectiveness: High
- Attention imbalance mitigation: High
- Frozen encoder + fine-tuning: High
- Overall SOTA accuracy: High
