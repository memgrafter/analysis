# Bi-Orthogonal Factor Decomposition for Vision Transformers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.05328
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05328
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: BFD reveals that ViT attention energy is dominated by content-content and content-position interactions; DINOv2 uses more content-position coupling and preserves a 2D positional manifold, enabling holistic shape processing.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Bi-orthogonal Factor Decomposition (BFD) to disentangle positional and semantic information in Vision Transformer self-attention. By combining ANOVA-style statistical factorization of token activations with SVD of the query-key interaction matrix, BFD isolates orthogonal positional and content factors and decomposes attention into bi-orthogonal communication modes. Applied to supervised ViT and self-supervised DINOv2, the method finds that attention energy is largely mediated by content-based interactions. DINOv2 allocates significantly more energy to content-position coupling and maintains a higher-rank, more diverse mode spectrum across layers. Moreover, DINOv2 preserves a 2D positional manifold in intermediate layers while enriching semantic content, correlating with its superior holistic shape processing. These findings offer a principled understanding of information flow in ViTs and explain advantages of self-supervised training.

## Method Summary
BFD proceeds in three stages. First, token activations from a given layer are factorized into statistically orthogonal components: a global layer bias (µₗ), a positional effect (µP) obtained by averaging over images and subtracting the global mean, and a content residual (µC) per image. Second, for each attention head, the query-key weight product W = W_Q W_Kᵀ is decomposed via truncated SVD into bi-orthogonal singular vectors (U, V) and singular values (Σ). Third, each factor (µₗ, µP, µC) is projected onto the left (query) and right (key) singular vectors; the per-mode energy contributions for all ordered factor pairs are computed, symmetrized into six undirected interactions (LL, PP, CC, LP, LC, PC), and aggregated across heads and layers. This yields interpretable measures of how much attention energy is mediated by each factor combination.

## Key Results
- Attention energy is dominated by content-content and content-position interactions; position-position and layer-layer are negligible.
- DINOv2 allocates significantly more energy to content-position coupling than supervised ViT, and its mode spectrum is higher-rank and more diverse.
- DINOv2 preserves a 2D positional manifold across layers (via PCA of µP), while ViT’s manifold collapses to quasi-1D early; this difference correlates with holistic shape processing capabilities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Factor Isolation via ANOVA‑Style Decomposition
- **Claim**: Token activations can be additively decomposed into statistically orthogonal *positional* and *content* components (plus a global layer bias).
- **Mechanism**: For layer ℓ, compute expectations over the data distribution: µₗ = Eₓ,ₚ[Aₚ] (global mean), µP(p) = Eₓ[Aₚ] – µₗ (positional effect), and µ_c(x,p) = Aₚ – µₗ – µP(p) (content residual). By construction these are pairwise orthogonal in expectation.
- **Core assumption**: Positional information enters linearly and remains recoverable by averaging over images; higher‑order interactions are negligible.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] “ANOVA‑based decomposition statistically disentangles token activations into orthogonal positional and content factors”; [section 2.1] Definition 1 and orthogonality equations.
- **Break condition**: If positional cues are encoded nonlinearly (e.g., multiplicative with content), µP will miss them and µ_c will leak position, violating orthogonality and corrupting downstream attribution.

### Mechanism 2: Bi‑Orthogonal Mode Decomposition of Attention
- **Claim**: The query‑key interaction matrix QKᵀ (or W = W_Q W_Kᵀ) can be decomposed into independent communication channels via SVD, with left/right singular vectors forming bi‑orthogonal bases.
- **Mechanism**: Perform SVD on W = UΣVᵀ. For each mode i, uᵢ (query) and vᵢ (key) are orthonormal, and the interaction is reconstructed as QKᵀ = Σᵢ (A uᵢ) σᵢ (A vᵢ)ᵀ. A mode activates when queries align with uᵢ and keys with vᵢ; orthogonality prevents cross‑talk.
- **Core assumption**: The linear operator W captures the dominant structure of token communication before softmax; its singular vectors are stable and meaningful.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] “SVD of the query‑key interaction matrix QKᵀ exposes bi‑orthogonal modes”; [section 2.2] Definition 2 and reconstruction equation.
- **Break condition**: If W is extremely low‑rank (e.g., one mode dominates), the decomposition fails to reveal a diverse set of channels; or if softmax drastically reshapes interactions, the linear SVD may not reflect actual attention patterns.

### Mechanism 3: Factor‑Projected Energy Attribution
- **Claim**: Projecting the factorized activation components (µₗ, µP, µ_c) onto the query/key modes quantifies the fraction of attention energy mediated by each factor pair (e.g., content‑content, content‑position).
- **Mechanism**: For mode i, compute zQ_µ = (factor matrix) · uᵢ and zK_µ = (factor matrix) · vᵢ. Energy contribution ξᵢ,µ = || zQ_µ σᵢ zK_µ ||². Normalize across modes per head and average over images to obtain ¯ξᵢ,µ. Summing over modes yields the energy share of six symmetric interactions (LL, PP, CC, LP, LC, PC). Barycentric coordinates across three families (L, P, C) reveal mode specialization.
- **Core assumption**: The factorization is orthogonal and the energy contributions add linearly; projections cleanly separate factor influence because factors are mutually exclusive.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] “projecting each mode’s query and key directions onto the three factors and compute the normalized energy”; [section 2.3] Equations for ξ and normalization.
- **Break condition**: If factors leak into each other (non‑orthogonality) or if softmax introduces nonlinear mixing, energy attribution may misallocate contributions.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: ANOVA‑Style Factor Decomposition
- **Why needed here**: BFD’s first stage separates positional and semantic signals; this relies on understanding how to use statistical expectations to isolate effects.
- **Quick check**: Given activations Aₚ from many images, how do you compute the positional effect µP(p)? (Answer: Average Aₚ over images, then subtract the global mean µₗ.)

### Concept: Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) and Bi‑Orthogonal Systems
- **Why needed here**: SVD yields independent communication channels; bi‑orthogonality ensures query and key spaces are cleanly paired.
- **Quick check**: What does it mean for {uᵢ} and {vᵢ} to be bi‑orthogonal? How is this different from a single orthonormal basis? (Answer: uᵢᵀ vⱼ = 0 for i≠j, but uᵢ and vᵢ are not necessarily equal; they are bases for two different spaces.)

### Concept: Self‑Attention in Vision Transformers
- **Why needed here**: BFD analyzes the attention mechanism’s query‑key interactions; this requires knowing how Q and K are derived from token embeddings.
- **Quick check**: In a ViT, how are queries and keys computed from the activation matrix A? (Answer: Q = A W_Q, K = A W_K, where W_Q and W_K are learned linear maps.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Input (activations A) -> Factor decomposition (µₗ, µP, µC) -> Per-head SVD (U, Σ, V) -> Projection (zQ, zK) -> Per-mode energy ξ -> Aggregation (energy fractions, barycentric coordinates) -> Analyses (layer-wise energy, ternary plots, PCA, correlations)

### Critical path
1. Extract activations for target layers/heads from a representative dataset.
2. Compute µₗ, µP, and verify µ_c residuals (optional linear probes).
3. For each head, compute SVD of W_h.
4. For each head and each image, compute factor projections onto U, V; then per‑mode energy ξᵢ,µ(x).
5. Average over images, normalize per interaction, and aggregate across heads if desired.
6. Produce analyses: layer‑wise energy fractions, mode specialization ternary plots, stable rank, etc.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Factor definition**: Linear ANOVA is simple and yields orthogonality by construction. Nonlinear factorization (e.g., with higher‑order terms) could capture richer position‑content interactions but would break clean attribution.
- **SVD target**: Using W = W_Q W_Kᵀ gives data‑independent modes, avoiding content mixing. SVD on the data‑dependent QKᵀ would entangle content and position, undermining the factor attribution.
- **Token selection**: Position factor µP should exclude special tokens (class, registers) to avoid semantic contamination; the paper uses only patch tokens for positional PCA.
- **Normalization**: They normalize energy within each interaction type across modes before averaging across heads. Alternative normalizations (e.g., across heads first) may change relative magnitudes.
- **Number of factors**: Three factors (layer, position, content) are sufficient for their analyses; adding more (e.g., class‑conditional) is possible but reduces statistical power.

### Failure signatures
- **Factor contamination**: Linear probe on µ_c decodes position above chance → positional leakage.
- **Energy imbalance**: Sum of normalized energies over six interactions ≠ 1 → implementation error.
- **Mode indistinguishability**: Ternary plot shows uniform cloud (no clusters) → factors not separated or modes not meaningful.
- **PCA collapse**: For DINOv2, µP PCA should retain a 2D sheet; if it collapses early like ViT, µP computation may be wrong (e.g., including class token).
- **Stable rank inversion**: DINOv2 should show higher stable rank than ViT; if not, check SVD computation or head selection.
- **High variance**: Results should be stable across random data subsets; large fluctuations indicate too few samples.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Factor orthogonality validation**: Train linear probes to predict token coordinates from µP and µ_c. Expected: µP yields high accuracy, µ_c yields chance (as in Figure 3).
2. **Interenergy distribution**: Compute total energy fraction for each of the six interactions per layer; plot layer‑wise curves (Figure 5). Expect content‑content and content‑position dominate, with DINOv2 showing more content‑position.
3. **Mode specialization**: Compute barycentric coordinates for all modes (head×mode) and plot ternary diagrams (Figure 8). Expect clusters near vertices; also per‑layer density plots (Figure 20) to track evolution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: What informational factors (positional, semantic, global) mediate token communication in attention, and how does this vary across layers and training paradigms?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Stated as Question 1 in Introduction (p.2).
- Why unresolved: Attention maps show where attention focuses but not what information is exchanged; prior methods lacked factor attribution to disentangle position, content, and global contributions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Using BFD on ViT and DINOv2, the paper shows content-content and content-position interactions dominate energy; DINOv2 uses more content-position coupling and a richer, higher-rank mode spectrum across layers.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Do attention heads and modes specialize into distinct operators (content-content, content-position, position-position), and is this specialization consistent across layers and models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Question 2 in Introduction (p.2) asks whether heads implement modular roles or distributed computation.
- Why unresolved: Without disentangling factor sources, it remained unclear if heads are functionally differentiated or if all heads contribute similarly to entangled signals.
- What evidence would resolve it: BFD reveals both head-level and mode-level functional specialization, with modes clustering near content, position, or mixed vertices; DINOv2 shows tighter clustering and more content-position operators.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What structural properties of intermediate layers distinguish self-supervised from supervised models, and how do these (positional preservation, content enrichment) enable holistic shape processing?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Question 3 in Introduction (p.2) targets the representational geometry differences underlying self-supervised advantages.
- Why unresolved: The superior shape processing of self-supervised models was known, but the mechanistic link to internal representation structures (e.g., positional manifold shape) was not established.
- What evidence would resolve it: DINOv2 maintains a 2D positional manifold across layers while progressively transforming content (low inter-layer correlation), whereas ViT collapses spatial structure early, limiting hierarchical integration.

## Limitations
- Linear ANOVA factorisation may miss nonlinear position‑content interactions; µC could leak positional information.
- SVD on W = W_Q W_Kᵀ ignores softmax nonlinearity; if attention is low‑rank or heavy‑tailed, singular modes may not capture real communication channels.
- Results are limited to ViT‑B/16 and DINOv2‑B/14 on ImageNet; generalisation to other architectures and datasets is unknown.

## Confidence
- **Factor separation (BFD isolates position vs. content)**: Medium
- **Energy dominance (content‑content & content‑position interactions)**: High
- **Mode functional specialization**: Medium
- **DINOv2 preserves 2D positional manifold & correlates with holistic shape processing**: Medium
