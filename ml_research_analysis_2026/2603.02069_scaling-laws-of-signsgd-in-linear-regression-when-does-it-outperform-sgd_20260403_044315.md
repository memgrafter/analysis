# Scaling Laws of SignSGD in Linear Regression: When Does It Outperform SGD?
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02069
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02069
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Derives a new scaling law for signSGD showing it achieves a steeper compute‑optimal loss‑decay exponent than SGD in noise‑bottleneck regimes for certain feature/target decay parameters, with theory and experiments confirming the advantage.

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes the scaling behavior of signSGD compared to SGD in the power‑law random features (PLRF) model for linear regression. A new scaling law for signSGD is derived, revealing two key effects: drift‑normalization, which accelerates the decay of aligned and distorted feature losses, and noise‑reshaping, which eliminates the N‑dependent decay present in SGD’s quadratic noise term. Under a fixed compute budget and an optimal constant learning rate, signSGD attains a steeper compute‑optimal loss‑decay exponent than SGD in noise‑bottleneck regimes for certain combinations of the spectral exponent α and target exponent β. Moreover, a warmup‑stable‑decay (WSD) learning‑rate schedule further reduces stochastic noise and sharpens the slope in regions where feature decay is fast and target decay is slow. Both theoretical predictions and controlled experiments confirm these advantages, with the compute‑optimal slope and optimal model‑size exponents matching the derived formulas.

## Method Summary
The study places signSGD within the PLRF framework, where the covariance spectrum decays with exponent α and the target coefficients with exponent β. Using a one‑pass, constant learning‑rate setting, the authors derive an implicit integral equation for the population risk by combining a second‑order Taylor expansion with sign‑Gaussian identities. The loss is decomposed into three terms: approximation error (due to finite model size), drift (deterministic contraction), and noise (stochastic variance). These terms are balanced under a fixed FLOP budget to compute optimal exponents for learning rate, model size, and steps. Additionally, the effect of a warmup‑stable‑decay schedule is analyzed by modifying the integral of the learning‑rate curve. Theoretical predictions are validated through numerical experiments that closely follow the PLRF assumptions.

## Key Results
- signSGD exhibits **drift‑normalization** (speedup in aligned/distorted feature decay) and **noise‑reshaping** (eliminates the N‑decay of the quadratic noise term) relative to SGD.
- Under optimal constant learning rate, signSGD achieves a steeper compute‑optimal loss‑decay exponent than SGD in noise‑bottleneck regimes for certain α,β values.
- With warmup‑stable‑decay scheduling, signSGD further reduces stochastic noise and sharpens the compute‑optimal slope in regions with fast feature decay and slow target decay; empirical results confirm the theory.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Drift-Normalization Speedup
The ODE for signSGD contains a drift term proportional to \((4γ/π)(1/√P)(1/λ_i(K)) p_i\), while SGD’s drift is \(2γ λ_i p_i\). The factor \(1/√P\) normalizes the effective stepsize, and the inverse eigenvalue introduces a scaling of \(M^{min(α,0.5)}\). This accelerates the decay of aligned (\(D_{al}\)) and distorted (\(D_{dis}\)) feature losses, yielding higher N‑exponents in those terms. The benefit holds when \(α > 0.5\) and \(β\) is not too large; it diminishes if \(α ≤ 0.5\) or \(β > α+0.5\).

### Noise-Reshaping Simplification
The one‑step quadratic noise in signSGD is \((2γ²/π) wᵀ K^σ K u_i\), which lacks the \(L(k)\) factor present in SGD’s noise term. Consequently, signSGD’s noise does not decay with N and depends only on \(γ₀\) and \(M\) via \(M^{2−min(1,2α)}\). Without time‑dependent second moments, the noise floor remains constant unless \(γ₀\) is decayed. With label noise, an additive \(σγ₀ Tr(diag(K)^{1/2})·√L∞\) term appears, potentially altering the M‑dependence.

### Warmup‑Stable‑Decay Noise Reduction
The warmup‑stable‑decay (WSD) schedule preserves the area under the learning‑rate curve \(F(N) ≈ N\) during an initial “stable” phase, keeping drift velocity comparable to constant‑LR training. Later, a polynomial decay tail reduces the learning‑rate \(f(z)\) for large z, shrinking the integral that contributes to the quadratic noise term. In regimes where drift decays faster than the target (fast feature, slow target; Phase Aa), this shifts the balance from noise‑dominated toward drift‑dominated, lifting the compute‑optimal slope. The analysis shows an increased exponent for the slope in Area Aa*; the benefit requires choosing a decay exponent \(c\) sufficiently large.

**Design tradeoffs**
- **γ₀ scaling**: Choosing constant γ₀ = M^{-e} balances noise (decreases with larger e) against drift velocity (slower with larger e). Too large e pushes the system into a noise‑bottleneck later in training.
- **Scheduling choice**: Warmup‑stable‑decay keeps the drift area F(N) ~ N (so drift not impaired) while polynomially decaying the tail to suppress the quadratic noise term; the decay exponent c must be high enough to attenuate noise without excessively harming drift.

**Failure signatures**
- **Noise plateau**: The noise term γ₀² M^{2−min(1,2α)} becomes constant if γ₀ is over‑decayed, causing compute‑optimal slope to vanish.
- **Approximation dominance**: When M is too large, the approximation error A(M)=M^{-2α+max(0,1-2β)} dominates, and increasing N yields no improvement.
- **Distorted‑drift burst**: In SGD phases III–IV, the distorted drift D_dis can dominate, breaking the drift‑noise balance that favors signSGD; signSGD is less susceptible.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Extension to mini‑batch settings:** The analysis assumes batch size 1; mini‑batch signSGD introduces correlated updates and different noise covariance. The authors leave a rigorous derivation for future work.
2. **Adam scaling without heuristics:** Adam is conjectured to follow the same scaling as signSGD based on a heuristic from Xiao et al. (2025), but a formal reduction under PLRF is lacking; verifying or disproving this conjecture remains open.
3. **Further scheduling improvements:** Learning‑rate schedules might improve compute‑optimal scaling in other (α,β) regions beyond Phase Aa, but current upper/lower bounds are too loose to pinpoint the optimal schedule; a tighter analysis is needed.
4. **Precise mechanism for signSGD advantage:** The heuristic that signSGD outperforms when the projected target decays slower than the gradient magnitude lacks a formal theorem; a rigorous characterization of Uᵀθ∗ (the projected target) in the PLRF model is required.

## Limitations
- The theory assumes a linear model under PLRF with no label noise and Gaussian sketches; extensions to deep networks or realistic data distributions are not covered.
- The compute‑optimal advantage of signSGD is confined to narrow regions of the (α,β) parameter space; outside these, SGD may be equal or better.
- Warmup‑stable‑decay scheduling requires careful selection of warmup length and decay exponent; the paper does not provide a practical tuning guide or test robustness across diverse regimes.

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in the drift‑normalization and noise‑reshaping mechanisms (rigorous derivation under the stated assumptions).
- **High confidence** in the predicted compute‑optimal advantage of signSGD in noise‑bottleneck regimes (theoretically derived and experimentally verified in the PLRF setting).
- **Medium confidence** in the benefits of warmup‑stable‑decay scheduling (analysis relies on bounds and regime‑specific behavior; broader validation needed).
- **Low confidence** in the applicability of these results to deep learning or settings with label noise (no analysis provided).
