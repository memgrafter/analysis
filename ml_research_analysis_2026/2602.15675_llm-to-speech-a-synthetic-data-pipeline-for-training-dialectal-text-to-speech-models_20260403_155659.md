# LLM-to-Speech: A Synthetic Data Pipeline for Training Dialectal Text-to-Speech Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.15675
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15675
- **Reference count**: 3
- **Primary result**: A synthetic pipeline produced NileTTS (38.1h), enabling XTTS v2 fine-tuning that reduced WER by 29.9% (26.8%→18.8%) and CER by 49.4% (8.1%→4.1%), while increasing speaker similarity by 5.9% (0.713→0.755).

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the lack of Egyptian Arabic TTS resources by constructing a fully synthetic data pipeline: LLMs generate dialectal text, NotebookLM synthesizes conversational speech, Whisper transcribes, and ECAPA-TDNN handles diarization, followed by manual quality control. This yields the publicly released NileTTS dataset. Fine-tuning XTTS v2 on NileTTS yields significant improvements in accuracy and speaker similarity compared to the baseline model, which was trained primarily on Modern Standard Arabic and Gulf dialects.

## Method Summary
The pipeline starts with Gemini/Claude instructed to write "PDF-style" reports entirely in Egyptian Arabic across medical, sales, and general domains. NotebookLM converts each report into a ~15-minute dual-speaker (male/female) conversation. Whisper segments and transcribes the audio; ECAPA-TDNN embeddings cluster segments by speaker. Human reviewers validate transcripts and speaker labels, producing 9,521 utterance pairs (38.1 hours). The dataset is split 90%/10% without document overlap. XTTS v2 is fine-tuned with its DV-AE frozen, training the GPT head for up to 30 epochs using AdamW (lr=5e-6), batch size 2, and gradient accumulation 8. The best checkpoint (epoch 8) is selected based on periodic evaluation.

## Key Results
- Word Error Rate reduced by 29.9% relative (26.8% → 18.8%).
- Character Error Rate reduced by 49.4% relative (8.1% → 4.1%).
- Speaker similarity (cosine) increased by 5.9% relative (0.713 → 0.755).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
LLM-generated text provides authentic dialectal content and topical diversity. Prompts explicitly avoid Modern Standard Arabic and request natural Egyptian Arabic, leveraging state-of-the-art LLMs' internalized dialect knowledge. Manual inspection confirmed fluency.
### Mechanism 2
NotebookLM’s podcast-style synthesis yields natural, conversational Egyptian Arabic speech with consistent virtual speakers. Its underlying TTS model generalizes to the dialect and produces audio quality suitable for training.
### Mechanism 3
Fine-tuning a strong multilingual TTS model (XTTS v2) on the high-quality, domain-diverse NileTTS adapts its autoregressive generation to Egyptian Arabic while preserving zero-shot voice cloning, despite the base model being trained mainly on MSA and Gulf dialects.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Dialectal Variation in Arabic**  
  *Why needed*: The entire pipeline depends on distinguishing Egyptian Arabic from MSA and other dialects. Understanding phonological, lexical, and syntactic divergences is essential for evaluating content and synthesis quality.  
  *Quick check*: Name two phonetic differences between Egyptian Arabic and MSA that a TTS system must handle correctly.
- **Concept: Multilingual TTS Architecture (XTTS v2)**  
  *Why needed*: Explains the fine-tuning process. XTTS v2 uses a GPT-style generator over discrete acoustic tokens; knowing this clarifies why the DV-AE is frozen and only the autoregressive head is trained.  
  *Quick check*: What is the functional role of the “DV AE” component in XTTS v2?
- **Concept: Synthetic Data Pipeline Risks**  
  *Why needed*: Fully synthetic creation introduces failure modes such as distribution shift, ASR label noise, and speaker identity leakage. Engineers must anticipate these.  
  *Quick check*: What is “ASR self-consistency bias” and how could it artificially lower WER/CER scores?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
LLM (Gemini/Claude) → NotebookLM → Whisper → ECAPA-TDNN → K-Means → Human QC → Dataset → XTTS v2 Fine-tune
### Critical path
Text generation must produce dialectal content → NotebookLM must deliver natural dual-speaker audio → Whisper must transcribe accurately → ECAPA-TDNN embeddings must cluster by speaker → Human QC must approve → Dataset must be correctly formatted → Fine-tuning must converge. A break at any stage halts progress.
### Design tradeoffs
- Synthetic vs. Real Audio: Scalability and reproducibility of synthetic speech vs. naturalness and diversity of real recordings.
- Automation vs. QC: Heavy automation enables scale but mandatory manual verification limits throughput.
- Speaker Consistency vs. Diversity: Two fixed synthetic voices ensure clean diarization and voice cloning training but severely restrict speaker variability.
### Failure signatures
- Content stage: LLM outputs MSA phrases or unnatural English code-switching.
- Audio stage: NotebookLM outputs metallic artifacts, abrupt cuts, or unbalanced speaker turn-taking.
- Diarization stage: K-means clusters do not separate by gender (e.g., 60/40 split).
- Training stage: Evaluation WER/CER do not improve within the first 10% of training steps.
### First 3 experiments
1. **LLM Dialect Validation**: Manually inspect 100 LLM texts; compute ratio of Egyptian-specific lexemes. Target >95% Egyptian forms.
2. **Diarization Sanity Check**: Run pipeline on a held-out 5-minute dual-speaker file with known speaker boundaries. Target >98% segment-level assignment accuracy.
3. **Baseline Overfit Check**: Fine-tune XTTS v2 for 1 epoch on a 1-hour subset. If WER/CER worsen vs. baseline, data is low-quality; if they improve immediately, data is well-aligned.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- To what extent do TTS models trained on synthetic speech generalize to real human Egyptian Arabic? The evaluation is entirely synthetic; no real-human comparison exists.
- How well do automatic metrics (WER, CER, ECAPA-TDNN similarity) correlate with human perceptual judgments for Egyptian Arabic TTS? A human study is needed.
- Does using the same ASR (Whisper) for dataset transcription and evaluation introduce self-consistency bias that inflates scores?
- Does using the same speaker embedding model (ECAPA-TDNN) for diarization and evaluation introduce model-specific bias?

## Limitations
- Proprietary bottleneck: NotebookLM is closed-source, blocking independent dataset generation.
- In-distribution evaluation: Metrics are computed on a held-out set from the same pipeline, risking overestimation of performance on real speech.
- Limited diversity: Only two synthetic speakers and three domains, likely causing overfitting to these conditions.
- Missing human evaluation: No subjective naturalness or preference ratings.
- ASR noise risk: Whisper transcription errors may propagate into training labels despite manual QC.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (LLM text authenticity): Medium – manual inspection supports but lacks quantitative dialectal metrics.
- Mechanism 2 (NotebookLM synthesis quality): Low – process is opaque; no objective naturalness measures reported.
- Mechanism 3 (Fine-tuning effectiveness): Medium – strong automatic improvements on synthetic test set, but real-world generalizability unproven.

## Next Checks
1. Human perceptual study: Conduct MOS testing with native speakers comparing baseline, fine-tuned, and real human speech.
2. Cross-dataset generalization: Evaluate the fine-tuned model on an external Egyptian Arabic corpus (e.g., MGB-3) to measure out-of-distribution robustness.
3. Ablation study: Train models with varying proportions of synthetic vs. real data and different data volumes to isolate the marginal contribution of synthetic data and detect overfitting.
