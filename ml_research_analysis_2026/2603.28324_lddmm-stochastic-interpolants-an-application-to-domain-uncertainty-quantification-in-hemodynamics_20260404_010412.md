# LDDMM stochastic interpolants: an application to domain uncertainty quantification in hemodynamics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28324  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28324  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** A conditional LDDMM stochastic interpolant framework generates 3D aortic shape variations from a latent descriptor, enabling hemodynamic uncertainty quantification with preserved mesh quality for high-order CFD; biomarker sensitivity varies with lumen size, and OSI shows lower geometric sensitivity.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method to quantify how uncertainties in aortic anatomy—stemming from medical image segmentation—affect hemodynamic biomarkers. It combines stochastic interpolants for generative modeling with diffeomorphic registration (LDDMM) to produce realistic 3D shape variations conditioned on a compact geometric descriptor (centerline points and radii). The framework preserves high-quality hexahedral meshes for efficient discontinuous Galerkin CFD solvers via an elasticity-based volume extension. Applied to synthetic aortas, the study reveals that pressure drop, velocity, and wall shear stress are more sensitive to geometric changes in smaller lumens, while oscillatory shear index depends more on surface shape than diameter. A sample-size analysis confirms statistical consistency between 10 and 100 generated samples.

## Method Summary
Given a template aortic mesh and a dataset of target shapes with associated latent descriptors (centerline points and inscribed sphere radii), the method first computes diffeomorphic registration maps between the template and each target via multilevel ResNet-LDDMM, yielding time-dependent velocity fields. A graph neural network (MeshGraphNet) is then trained to predict the conditional drift for a stochastic interpolant process from the latent descriptor and current shape state, using the LDDMM velocity fields as targets. To generate a new shape, the stochastic differential equation (SDE) with the learned drift is simulated from the template, producing a surface displacement. This displacement is bulk-extended to the volume mesh through an iterative linear elasticity solve with a non-homogeneous Young modulus (based on aspect ratio) followed by an aspect-ratio optimization to preserve hexahedral quality. The resulting volume mesh is used in high-order discontinuous Galerkin CFD simulations to compute hemodynamic biomarkers, whose statistics across generated samples quantify domain uncertainty.

## Key Results
- Biomarkers (pressure drop, velocity magnitude, wall shear stress) show significantly higher sensitivity to geometric variations in smaller aortic lumens; oscillatory shear index (OSI) exhibits lower sensitivity, indicating dependence on surface shape rather than diameter.
- The framework preserves hierarchical hexahedral mesh quality suitable for high-order discontinuous Galerkin solvers in 854 out of 900 generated cases; failures occur for large deformations.
- A sample-size study demonstrates statistical convergence with as few as 10 generated geometries for key biomarker distributions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The conditional drift for the stochastic interpolant between shapes is defined using the LDDMM registration velocity field.
- **Mechanism:** For a template shape \(\Omega_0\) and a target shape \(\Omega_i\), LDDMM computes a diffeomorphic flow \(\phi_{0,i}^t\) and its velocity \(v_{0,i}^t\). The conditional drift is set as \(u_t^{\text{cond}}(x_0, x_i) = v_{0,i}^t(\phi_{0,i}^t(x_0))\), transporting a point \(x_0\) on the template toward its target location along the minimal-energy LDDMM path.
- **Core assumption:** The LDDMM minimal-energy path provides an optimal, smooth, and invertible transport mechanism between shapes, and this deterministic velocity can serve as the conditioned drift for a stochastic process that generates plausible shape variations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "combining stochastic interpolants with diffeomorphic registration"
  - [section 3.2] "the conditional drift is computed from the registration field \(\phi_{0,i}\) between the two realizations"
  - [section 4.2] Application of multilevel ResNet-LDDMM to compute registration maps.
- **Break condition:** The LDDMM flow requires the template and target to be bi-Lipschitz homeomorphic; topological changes cause the flow map to be ill-defined or non-invertible.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A graph neural network (MeshGraphNet) learns the mapping from the latent geometric descriptor (centerline points and radii) to the time-dependent conditional drift required by the stochastic interpolant.
- **Mechanism:** The conditioning variable \(c\) (latent descriptor) and the current state \(I_t\) (coordinates on the template mesh) are encoded using Fourier features and time sinusoidal encodings. These are fed into a MeshGraphNet with multiple message-passing layers to predict the drift \(b_\theta^t(I_t, c)\). The network is trained by minimizing the mismatch between its predicted drift and the LDDMM-based conditional drift \(v_{0,i}^t(\phi_{0,i}^t(m_0))\) on training shape pairs.
- **Core assumption:** The latent descriptor \(c\) sufficiently captures the geometrically relevant variations, and the mapping \(c \mapsto \{ b_\theta^t \}_{t \in [0,1]}\) is learnable from a finite set of registered shape pairs.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] "the time-dependent conditioned vector field \(b_\theta^t[c]\) is approximated using a graph neural network"
  - [appendix A] Details on Fourier feature encoding, MeshGraphNet architecture (processor size 10, hidden dim 128), and Adam optimizer training.
  - [section 5.2] "The conditioning variable \(c\) is defined as a low-dimensional representation... centerline points and radii"
- **Break condition:** If the latent space does not span relevant shape variations (e.g., local aneurysm deformations absent in training), the learned drift cannot generate such perturbations, as shown in Figure 10 (right).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** An elasticity-based volume extension and aspect ratio optimization algorithm preserves the hierarchical hexahedral mesh structure required by the high-order discontinuous Galerkin solver when deforming the template mesh to a generated shape.
- **Mechanism:** The surface deformation \(\Psi_s\) from the stochastic interpolant's final displacement is split into \(N_{\text{steps}}\). For each step, a fictitious linear elastic problem with a non-homogeneous Young modulus \(E(x)\) (square of cell aspect ratio) is solved on the intermediate domain to bulk-extend the displacement. After extension, an optimization reduces badly distorted cells by minimizing an aspect-ratio-based loss \(\mathcal{L}(\Psi)\) over a set of "bad cells" \(\mathcal{I}(\Psi)\).
- **Core assumption:** Solving a linear elastic problem with aspect-ratio-dependent modulus followed by smoothing yields a volume mesh with acceptable element quality and preserved multigrid hierarchy.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.4] "iterative algorithm solving a non-homogeneous linear elastic problem and a smoothing step"
  - [appendix B] Definition of approximate aspect ratio \(gAsp\) and optimization problem (31) for mesh smoothing.
  - [section 4.4] "preserve the internal mesh structure that guarantees solver performance"
- **Break condition:** For very large deformations or highly irregular initial meshes, the elastic extension may still generate inverted or extremely poor-quality cells that smoothing cannot recover, leading to simulation failure (46/900 cases in Table 1).

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Stochastic Interpolants**
- **Why needed here:** Provides the generative modeling framework to bridge a prior (template shape) and a target distribution (shape variations) via a learned time-dependent drift. The method extends this to shapes on different domains by replacing the simple drift with an LDDMM-derived field.
- **Quick check:** What is the role of the noise coefficient \(\sigma_t\) in the SDE (1), and why must it vanish at \(t=0,1\)?

**Concept: LDDMM (Large Deformation Diffeomorphic Metric Mapping)**
- **Why needed here:** Computes the diffeomorphic registration map \(\phi_{0,i}^t\) and velocity field \(v_{0,i}^t\) between template and target shapes, which defines the conditional drift and ensures smooth, invertible deformations consistent with a reproducing kernel Hilbert space norm.
- **Quick check:** How does the LDDMM energy functional (11) balance transformation smoothness and data fidelity?

**Concept: Discontinuous Galerkin (DG) Methods & Mesh Hierarchies**
- **Why needed here:** The CFD solver (ExaDG) relies on high-order DG and geometric multigrid preconditioners, which require a nested hierarchy of high-quality hexahedral meshes. The mesh extension algorithm must preserve this hierarchy.
- **Quick check:** Why are hexahedral meshes preferred for high-order DG methods in this application, and what is the role of the multigrid hierarchy?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Template Mesh + Target Meshes -> ResNet-LDDMM -> Registration Fields (ϕ, v) -> MeshGraphNet Training (with c) -> Learned Drift b_θ -> SDE Solver (Euler-Maruyama) -> Surface Displacement Ψ_s -> Elastic Extension + Smoothing -> Volume Mesh -> ExaDG CFD -> Biomarker Statistics.

**Critical path:**
Dataset -> ResNet-LDDMM registration -> GNN training (drift) -> SDE sampling (shape gen) -> Mesh extension -> CFD -> Statistics

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **GNN architecture:** MeshGraphNet with k=12 nearest neighbors for connectivity (improves info propagation) vs. mesh edges (simpler). Processor depth 10 balances expressivity and training cost.
- **Latent descriptor:** Centerline+radii is natural for aorta but limits local deformations; a more flexible descriptor (e.g., point cloud) would increase GNN complexity.
- **Mesh extension:** Elasticity-based approach preserves hierarchy but may fail for extreme deformations; radial basis function interpolation would be cheaper but less likely to preserve mesh quality.
- **SDE solver:** Euler-Maruyama with fixed timestep (simple) vs. more sophisticated SDE solvers (better accuracy but higher cost).

**Failure signatures:**
1. **Registration failure:** ResNet-LDDMM does not converge or yields non-bijective \(\phi_{0,i}^1\). Symptoms: high Chamfer loss, distorted intermediate shapes.
2. **GNN generalization failure:** Drift error remains high after fine-tuning; generated shapes do not match target radii variations. Correlates with latent space gaps.
3. **Mesh extension failure:** High number of invalid simulations (negative Jacobians, high aspect ratio cells). Symptoms persist after smoothing.
4. **CFD instability:** Simulation diverges due to poor mesh quality even if topologically valid.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Registration validation:** Compute final Chamfer distance \(d_{\text{Chamfer}}(\phi_{0,i}^1(m_0), m_i)\) for a subset of training pairs; expect values ~1-2 mm.
2. **Drift learning error:** Monitor \(\mathbb{E}_{t,\epsilon}[\| b_\theta^t(I_t, c_i) - v_{0,i}^t(\phi_{0,i}^t(m_0)) \|^2]\) on validation set; expect ~1-5% relative L2 error after fine-tuning.
3. **Mesh quality preservation:** For generated meshes, compute min/max Jacobian determinant and aspect ratio; require >95% valid cells with aspect ratio < 10 for DG stability. Validate by running a few CFD cases and checking for NaN/infinite pressure.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the conditional LDDMM stochastic interpolant framework be successfully applied to generate anatomically realistic shapes for other organs (e.g., left atrium, cerebral arteries) and support downstream CFD simulations with comparable mesh quality preservation?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state that "current and future research... includes the application of this approach to different clinical contexts (different organs and pathological conditions, simulation of devices and treatments)" (Conclusions).
- **Why unresolved:** The current study focuses solely on aortic geometries; the method's generalizability to other anatomical structures remains untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Validation on at least one additional organ, demonstrating generation of valid meshes and consistent hemodynamic biomarker statistics compared to patient-specific data.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does enriching the training dataset with shapes that include local geometric variations (e.g., localized dilations or constrictions) enable the model to generate targeted local deformations, and what is the required dataset size and diversity?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors identify that "a limitation of the proposed approach is that it does not allow for the study of local domain uncertainty" and suggest that "this limitation could be addressed by enriching the training dataset with local deformations" (Conclusions).
- **Why unresolved:** The training set only contains global radius variations from SSM; the model fails to produce local changes as shown in Figure 10 (right).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Generate shapes with prescribed local radius changes at specific locations and verify that the output geometries exhibit the intended local modifications without global side effects.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the incorporation of explicit metric constraints (e.g., penalizing deviations of inscribed radii from latent values) during training affect the accuracy of parameter-to-geometry mapping and the reliability of uncertainty quantification?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors note that "imposing metric constraints would improve the correspondence between the latent variables associated with centerline points and radii, and the actual centerline points and radii of the generated shapes" (Conclusions).
- **Why unresolved:** The current training loss does not enforce consistency between latent conditioning variables and resulting geometry properties; hence, radius variations are only approximate.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare distributions of geometric descriptors (e.g., radii at centerline points) for generated shapes with and without metric constraints, and assess improvement in biomarker sensitivity studies.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What is the effect of using multiple template geometries (instead of a single template) on the ability to generate shapes across different topological classes or highly variable anatomical cohorts, and does it improve coverage of the shape space?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors mention that "the current LDDMM stochastic interpolant can be extended to generate shapes in different classes of bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms by considering multiple template shapes [29]" and that "these extensions will be explored in future work" (Conclusions).
- **Why unresolved:** All experiments use a single template aorta; the method may not generalize to shapes that are not bi-Lipschitz to that template (e.g., different branching patterns).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train conditional interpolants with multiple templates from diverse cohorts and evaluate the diversity and realism of generated shapes, including topological variations if present.

## Limitations
- LDDMM registration assumes diffeomorphisms; it fails for topological changes, leading to invalid meshes in some cases.
- The latent descriptor (centerline+radii) may not capture local variations; the model shows poor generalization for unseen local deformations.
- The elasticity-based mesh extension can fail for large deformations, producing invalid volume meshes despite smoothing.
- Results are based on synthetic data; real segmentation uncertainty may follow a different distribution.
- Biomarker analysis uses a small test set (30 geometries) and fixed perturbations, limiting statistical power.

## Confidence
- **Core generative model (shape sampling):** Medium. Works on synthetic data but has notable failure cases and descriptor constraints.
- **Mesh extension (volume deformation):** Medium. High success rate (854/900) but lacks robustness analysis and alternative comparisons.
- **Biomarker sensitivity findings:** Medium-Low. Small test set; findings may not generalize to real patient cohorts.

## Next Checks
1. **Registration robustness:** Test LDDMM on real aortic segmentations with topological variations; quantify non-diffeomorphic transformations via Jacobian determinant statistics.
2. **Generalization to real data:** Apply trained model to patient-specific aortas with multiple expert segmentations; compare generated shape distribution to inter-observer variability (Chamfer distance).
3. **Mesh quality and CFD convergence:** Perform a parameter sweep of elasticity/smoothing on challenging shapes; run CFD on 100+ meshes; verify biomarker stability and solver convergence.
