# Dimensionality Reduction on Riemannian Manifolds in Data Analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05936
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05936
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: Riemannian dimensionality reduction methods achieve near-perfect classification accuracy (96-100%) on spherical datasets and significantly outperform Euclidean baselines on manifold 3D datasets, e.g., 100% vs 54.4% on Sphere Hard.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the problem of dimensionality reduction for data residing on Riemannian manifolds, where traditional Euclidean methods fail to preserve intrinsic geometry. It introduces Riemannian adaptations of Principal Component Analysis (Principal Geodesic Analysis, PGA) and Linear Discriminant Analysis (R-LDA), as well as Riemannian versions of Isomap, ONPP, Laplacian Eigenmaps, and Robust PCA. These methods leverage geodesic distances, tangent space representations, and Fréchet means to capture the manifold structure. Experiments on 12 benchmark datasets show that geometry-aware dimensionality reduction leads to substantial improvements in classification accuracy, particularly on spherical and curved 3D datasets, with Riemannian methods achieving up to 100% accuracy where Euclidean methods drop below 55%.

## Method Summary
The core Riemannian pipeline transforms data from the manifold to a tangent space at the Fréchet mean using logarithmic maps, applies standard linear algebra (PCA, LDA, etc.) in that Euclidean space, and optionally projects back via exponential maps. For graph-based methods (R-Isomap, R-LE), intrinsic geodesic distances are used to construct k‑NN graphs, shortest‑path distances are computed, and then classical MDS or spectral embedding is applied. The Fréchet mean is found by Riemannian gradient descent. The paper provides manifold‑specific formulas for hyperspheres, SPD matrices, and Grassmann manifolds, and reports results on 12 datasets using a 70/30 split with 5‑NN classification.

## Key Results
- On spherical datasets (e.g., Sphere Hard), Riemannian methods achieve 96–100% accuracy, while Euclidean counterparts fall to as low as 54.4%.
- On manifold 3D datasets (Swiss Roll, S‑Curve, etc.), Riemannian methods consistently outperform Euclidean baselines.
- For the Sphere Hard dataset specifically, Riemannian LDA reaches 100% accuracy, demonstrating perfect class separation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Dimensionality reduction on Riemannian manifolds preserves intrinsic data geometry by replacing Euclidean vector arithmetic (subtraction, dot products) with manifold-specific operations (geodesic distances, tangent space projections).
- **Mechanism:** Euclidean methods compute variance, scatter, or neighborhood preservation using Euclidean distances `||x_i - x_j||`. On a manifold `M`, this is invalid. Riemannian methods first map data points to a common tangent space `T_pM` via the logarithmic map `Log_p(x_i)`, where Euclidean linear algebra (PCA, LDA scatter matrices) is performed. The low-dimensional representation is optionally mapped back via the exponential map `Exp_p(v)`. Geodesic distances `d_M(p, q) = inf_γ L(γ)` replace Euclidean distances in graph constructions (e.g., Isomap, Laplacian Eigenmaps).
- **Core assumption:** The tangent space at a central point (typically the Fréchet mean) provides a locally isometric approximation of the manifold, making variance maximization in `T_pM` a valid proxy for variation *along geodesics* on `M`. This holds when data concentration is sufficiently tight relative to manifold curvature.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "exploit geodesic distances, tangent space representations, and intrinsic statistical measures to achieve more faithful low-dimensional embeddings"
  - [section 4, PGA]: "PGA seeks the top k principal geodesic directions... This is equivalent to the trace maximization problem... The solution is obtained by eigen‑decomposition of Σ [computed in tangent space]"
  - [corpus]: Weak direct anchor. Neighbor papers (e.g., "Riemannian Principal Component Analysis") echo the tangent‑space mechanism but do not empirically validate the specific claim of near‑perfect spherical accuracy (96–100%) reported in this paper's experiments.
  - **Unknown:** The precise data concentration thresholds (e.g., von Mises-Fisher concentration κ) for which the single tangent space approximation remains valid on spheres, SPD, and Grassmann manifolds.
- **Break condition:** If data are highly dispersed or the manifold has significant variance in curvature, a single tangent space at the Fréchet mean may become a poor local approximation, causing PCA in `T_pM` to misrepresent global geodesic variation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Replacing Euclidean distances with geodesic distances in neighborhood graphs (e.g., for Isomap, Laplacian Eigenmaps) correctly captures the manifold's intrinsic connectivity, preventing the "short‑circuiting" of graph‑based methods that assume ambient Euclidean flatness.
- **Mechanism:** A k‑NN graph is built using `d_M(x_i, x_j)`. The shortest‑path distance `D_M(i,j)` on this graph approximates the true manifold geodesic. MDS or spectral embedding then preserves these distances. Using Euclidean `||x_i - x_j||` on curved data creates spurious long‑distance edges and distorts local neighborhoods.
- **Core assumption:** The manifold is *globally* well‑approximated by a finite set of local Euclidean patches, and the k‑NN graph with geodesic distances accurately samples these patches to reconstruct the global intrinsic geometry.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "preserving the underlying manifold structure"
  - [section 7, R‑LE]: "Construct the neighborhood N(i) for each x_i based on the manifold distance d_M... This graph encodes local adjacency and ensures that only nearby points influence the embedding, avoiding distortions caused by the curvature"
  - [section 9, R‑Isomap]: "Riemannian Isomap uses geodesic distances to better capture the intrinsic geometry of the manifold"
  - **Unknown:** How manifold topology (e.g., holes, non-trivial fundamental group) affects the choice of k in k‑NN graphs for accurate geodesic approximation and graph connectivity.
- **Break condition:** For manifolds with holes or non‑trivial topology (e.g., a circle `S^1`), a k‑NN graph with fixed `k` may fail to capture essential connectivity (e.g., bridging across a hole) if `k` is too small, or introduce false connections if `k` is too large relative to the manifold's reach.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The Fréchet mean (Karcher mean) provides a geometry‑aware central tendency measure that is invariant to manifold curvature and is the optimal reference for tangent‑space expansions, unlike the Euclidean mean which is biased on curved spaces.
- **Mechanism:** The Fréchet mean `x̄ = argmin_x Σ_i d_M(x, x_i)^2` is computed iteratively via Riemannian gradient descent: `x^{new} = Exp_x( (1/N) Σ_i Log_x(x_i) )`. This point minimizes the sum of squared geodesic distances. Its tangent space `T_{x̄}M` is then the arena for linear operations (PCA, LDA).
- **Core assumption:** The Fréchet mean exists uniquely (requires positive injectivity radius and data concentration) and its associated exponential map is a diffeomorphism on a sufficiently large neighborhood to contain all data logarithms.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4, PGA]: "Once the mean is computed... each point is mapped to the tangent space at x_0 using the logarithmic map"
  - [section 8, R‑LDA]: "Compute the Fréchet mean: x̄ = argmin_x Σ_i dist^2(x, x_i)"
  - [table 2.1 Glossary]: "Fr´echet Mean / Karcher Mean: The point on the manifold that minimizes the sum of squared geodesic distances to all data points"
  - **Unknown:** Convergence guarantees, numerical stability, and sensitivity to initialization for Fréchet mean optimization on manifolds with high curvature or dispersed data.
- **Break condition:** If the data are not sufficiently concentrated (e.g., covering a large portion of a sphere), the Fréchet mean may be non‑unique or lie outside the convexity radius, causing `Log_{x̄}(x_i)` to be undefined for some points or the tangent covariance matrix to poorly reflect manifold structure.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Riemannian Manifold (M, g)**
  - **Why needed here:** All methods operate on data `x_i ∈ M`. You must understand that `M` is a smooth space with a metric `g` defining distances (`d_M`) and angles. Without this, you cannot correctly define `Log`, `Exp`, or `d_M`.
  - **Quick check:** On the sphere `S^2`, what is the dimension of the tangent space `T_pS^2` at any point `p`? What is the Riemannian metric `⟨·,·⟩_p`? (Answer: dim=2; induced by ambient dot product)
- **Concept: Tangent Space `T_pM`**
  - **Why needed here:** This is the *only* place where standard linear algebra (covariance, eigen‑decomposition, projection) occurs. Understanding the isomorphism `T_pM ≅ R^d` and the map `v → Exp_p(v)` is essential to see how "linear PCA" becomes "nonlinear PGA".
  - **Quick check:** For SPD matrices `S_{++}^d`, what is the tangent space at `X ∈ S_{++}^d`? How is the inner product `⟨Z1, Z2⟩_X` defined? (Answer: `T_X S_{++}^d = {Z : Z^⊤ = Z}`; `⟨Z1,Z2⟩_X = tr(X^{-1}Z1 X^{-1}Z2)`)
- **Concept: Fréchet Mean and Optimization on Manifolds**
  - **Why needed here:** The reference point `p` is almost always the Fréchet mean. Computing it requires understanding Riemannian gradient descent (`grad f(p)`) and retractions (`Retr_p`). Failure to compute this accurately propagates errors to all downstream steps.
  - **Quick check:** Write the Riemannian gradient descent update to find the Fréchet mean of points `{x_i}` on a manifold `M`. What is the role of the retraction operation? (Answer: `x^{new} = Retr_x( -α grad f(x) )` with `grad f(x) = Π_x( ∑_i Log_x(x_i) )`; retraction approximates exponential map efficiently while being cheaper to compute.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Manifold ID → Fréchet Mean → Tangent Lift → Linear Core → (Optional) Exp. For graph‑based: Manifold ID → Geodesic distance (via Log) → k‑NN graph → Shortest paths → MDS/Laplacian → Embedding.
- **Critical path:** `Manifold ID → Fréchet Mean → Tangent Lift → Linear Core → (Optional) Exp`. Do not proceed to Linear Core if Fréchet Mean fails to converge or any Log map returns NaN.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Cost vs. fidelity:** Exact geodesics are expensive (e.g., SVD for Grassmann). Approximations via tangent‑space linearization speed up graph construction but lose global accuracy.
  - **Single reference vs. local charts:** Global tangent at Fréchet mean is efficient but may fail on highly non‑convex manifolds; local tangent PCA per patch is more accurate but loses a unified embedding space.
  - **Numerical stability:** Riemannian gradient descent may diverge with large step sizes; use line search or diminishing steps. Matrix log/exp for SPDs can be unstable near singularity; add small regularization.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Fréchet mean does not converge:** Gradient norm remains > tolerance after max iterations. Likely step size too large or data too scattered.
  - **Logarithmic map failure:** `Log_{x̄}(x_i)` returns NaN. Cause: `x_i` outside cut locus of `x̄`. Fix: reduce k in k‑NN or change reference.
  - **Degenerate covariance in tangent space:** Covariance matrix has zero eigenvalues. Expected for PGA (gives small eigenvalues) but breaks LDA if within‑class scatter singular.
  - **Disconnected graph (R‑Isomap/R‑LE):** k‑NN graph has >1 component. Increase k or adjust distance metric.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **PGA on Synthetic Sphere:** Generate 500 points from von Mises‑Fisher on `S^2` with κ=10. Run R‑PGA and Euclidean PCA. Visualize first two principal geodesics vs. chords. Compute reconstruction error using geodesic distance: R‑PGA should be lower.
  2. **R‑LDA on Binary Classification on `S^2`:** Create two well‑separated small‑cap clusters (“Sphere Hard” subset). Apply R‑LDA (3 components) and Euclidean LDA. Train 1‑NN in reduced space. Expect R‑LDA 100% accuracy, LDA ~54.4%. Inspect between‑class scatter directions in tangent space.
  3. **Robustness to Tangent Approximation:** On Swiss Roll manifold, compare R‑ONPP (using Log at mean) vs. Euclidean ONPP. Add increasing noise to the `z` coordinate (vertical). Expect R‑ONPP to degrade more gracefully than Euclidean ONPP as curvature becomes more pronounced.

## Limitations
- Implementation details (Fréchet mean initialization, step size, heat kernel bandwidth *t*) are missing, hindering full reproducibility.
- Theoretical assumptions (data concentration enabling a single tangent space approximation) are not systematically validated; performance may degrade on highly curved or dispersed manifolds.
- Experimental validation covers only 12 benchmarks, mostly synthetic manifolds; generalizability to complex high‑dimensional real data is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Effectiveness (superior accuracy on benchmarks):** High for reported datasets (e.g., 100% vs 54.4% on Sphere Hard) but Medium for broader generalizability due to implementation gaps.
- **Mechanism (tangent space + geodesics):** Medium; theory is established but empirical evidence for core assumptions is incomplete.
- **Generalizability across manifolds/methods:** Medium for tested manifolds (sphere, SPD, Grassmann); Low for graph‑based methods (R‑LE, R‑Isomap) due to missing heat kernel parameter *t*.
