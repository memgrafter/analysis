# Coverage-Aware Web Crawling for Domain-Specific Supplier Discovery via a Web--Knowledge--Web Pipeline

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.24262
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24262
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** The Web–Knowledge–Web pipeline achieved the highest precision (0.165) and F1-score (0.123) among baselines while using 32% fewer pages (144 vs. 213) in the semiconductor equipment sector.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles incomplete supplier mapping in specialized sectors by proposing a Web–Knowledge–Web (W→K→W) pipeline. It iteratively crawls domain-specific web sources, extracts structured supplier information into a knowledge graph using a domain-adapted few-shot LLM prompt, and analyzes the graph’s topology to identify under-represented supplier regions for targeted subsequent crawling. A coverage estimation framework adapted from ecological methods (Chao1) provides a stopping criterion. In a case study on semiconductor equipment (NAICS 333242), the pipeline constructed a type-consistent knowledge graph with 664 entities and 542 relations while using significantly fewer pages than baselines and achieving the best precision and F1-score.

## Method Summary
The W→K→W pipeline operates in iterative cycles. It begins with a set of seed URLs and a focused crawler that fetches pages, extracts text, and filters outbound links. A large language model (GPT-4o-mini) processes each page’s text using a prompt enhanced with a domain glossary, formal relation type constraints, and few-shot examples to extract typed entities and relations. Extracted elements undergo entity resolution (name normalization, blocking, similarity matching) and are merged into a persistent knowledge graph. After each iteration, the graph is analyzed to detect "structural holes"—sectors or geographic regions with anomalously low connectivity, or predicted missing links—which are transformed into new search queries and seed URLs for the next crawl cycle. The process stops when an estimated coverage ratio (adapted Chao1 estimator) exceeds a threshold or a page budget is exhausted.

## Key Results
- Achieved precision 0.165 and F1-score 0.123, outperforming BFS, Focused, and non-feedback W→K baselines.
- Discovered 664 unique supplier entities and 542 relations with 100% type-consistency.
- Used only 144 pages (32% fewer) than the 213-page baseline budget to reach stopping condition.
- Coverage estimation (Chao1) indicated stabilization, though it overestimated relative to a curated ground truth.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The knowledge graph's topology identifies structural holes (underrepresented supplier regions), enabling targeted crawling that reduces wasted requests.
- **Mechanism:** After each crawl iteration, the KG is analyzed for anomalies (e.g., sectors with unexpectedly low company degree, missing intermediate suppliers in supply-chain paths). These gaps generate specific search queries and seed URLs, directing the next crawl toward pages likely to contain undiscovered entities.
- **Core assumption:** Incomplete discovery manifests as sparse regions in the KG; filling these gaps yields disproportionately many new entities.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "uses the knowledge graph’s topology and coverage signals to guide subsequent crawling toward under-represented regions of the supplier space." [section] IV.C: "The core novelty of the W→K→W pipeline is using the KG to identify what is missing and generate targeted crawl seeds." [corpus] Corpus neighbors (e.g., "Neural Prioritisation for Web Crawling") focus on content-based or link-graph prioritization, not KG topology—supporting the novelty of this mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If gap analysis consistently fails to find high-yield seeds (e.g., all sectors have similar degree distributions), the feedback loop loses direction and reverts to unfocused crawling.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Domain-adapted few-shot LLM extraction with glossary and type constraints yields a high-precision KG that serves as reliable memory for gap detection.
- **Mechanism:** The LLM prompt integrates a curated domain glossary (~80 terms), formal relation definitions with endpoint type constraints (e.g., `supplies_to` requires two `COMPANY` nodes), and disambiguating few-shot examples. This reduces type-inconsistent extractions (e.g., misclassifying "supplies product" as company-to-company) and hallucination, producing a cleaner KG.
- **Core assumption:** Extraction errors propagate into gap signals; a noisy KG misleads crawling toward false gaps or away from real ones.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section] IV.B.2: Details of domain glossary, type constraints, and few-shot examples. [section] VII.D.1: "All 542 relations achieve 100% type-consistency" and 66.2% overall precision from annotation. [section] VII.D.2: Over-extraction issues in `partners_with` and `located_in` show residual noise.
- **Break condition:** If type-consistency drops (e.g., due to domain shift), gap signals become unreliable; the system would require prompt retuning or additional supervision.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Ecological coverage estimation (Chao1) provides a stopping criterion that balances discovery effort against diminishing returns, reducing total pages needed.
- **Mechanism:** Treating each crawl iteration or data source as a "capture occasion," Chao1 uses singleton (`f1`) and doubleton (`f2`) entity counts to estimate the total web-discoverable supplier population. The coverage ratio `ˆC = |ˆE| / ˆS` guides stopping (e.g., `ˆC > 0.85`).
- **Core assumption:** The web-discoverable entity population exhibits capture-recapture dynamics (some entities appear across multiple sources/iterations, others are unique), and Chao1's lower bound is informative for open-ended discovery.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "A coverage estimation framework inspired by ecological species-richness estimators (Chao1, ACE) adapted for web-entity populations." [section] V: Formal adaptation of Chao1 to source- or iteration-based entity frequency. [section] VII.B: Chao1 overestimates coverage relative to the curated ground truth but shows stabilization; bootstrap confidence intervals indicate substantial uncertainty.
- **Break condition:** If entity discovery follows a drastically different pattern (e.g., no singletons after initial iterations), Chao1's assumptions fail and estimates become unstable; alternative estimators or heuristics would be needed.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Focused Web Crawling**
  - **Why needed here:** The pipeline’s efficiency depends on prioritizing URLs likely to contain supplier entities. Understanding relevance scoring, politeness policies, and link filtering is essential to grasp the Web→Knowledge phase.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the crawler balance relevance (e.g., directory pages) against novelty (e.g., outbound links) when prioritizing the URL queue?
- **Concept: Knowledge Graph Construction from Noisy Text**
  - **Why needed here:** The KG is the central memory and analysis artifact. Key steps—joint NER/relation extraction, entity resolution, and schema constraints—must be understood to evaluate the pipeline’s accuracy and the reliability of gap signals.
  - **Quick check question:** Why does the paper use a single LLM call for both NER and relation extraction instead of separate pipelines?
- **Concept: Species Richness Estimation (Chao1)**
  - **Why needed here:** The coverage estimator determines when to stop crawling. Understanding its derivation (from capture-recapture) and limitations (sensitivity to singletons) explains the reported coverage trajectories and errors.
  - **Quick check question:** What does a high `f1/f2` ratio imply about the completeness of discovery, and how does it affect the Chao1 estimate?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Seed URLs -> Crawl Engine -> Text Extraction -> LLM Extraction Module -> Entity Resolution -> KG Store -> Gap Analyzer -> Seed Generator (loop)
- **Critical path:**  
  `Seed URLs → Crawl → Text extraction → LLM extraction → Entity resolution → KG update → Gap analysis → New seeds` (loop until coverage threshold or budget exhaustion).  
  *Key handoff:* KG update must complete before gap analysis; gap signals must be ready before next crawl cycle.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **LLM choice (GPT-4o-mini):** Low cost ($0.04/144 pages) but limited precision (66.2%); tradeoff: cheaper than fine-tuning but requires careful prompt engineering.  
  - **Politeness delay (1.5s):** Ethical/sustainable crawling vs. slower iterations; acceptable given low concurrency (10) and small scale.  
  - **Gap detection simplicity:** Heuristics (degree anomaly) and DistMult (supplementary) are interpretable but may miss complex topological patterns; a GNN could improve detection but increases compute.  
  - **Coverage estimator (Chao1):** Non-parametric and lightweight but overestimates coverage when ground truth is sparse; provides lower bound on discoverable population.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Stagnant KG growth:** Few new entities added over 2+ iterations despite gap-guided seeds → gap analysis failing or sources exhausted.  
  - **Type-consistency drop:** Rising violations in `supplies_to` or `produces` → prompt degradation or domain shift (e.g., crawling outside semiconductor equipment).  
  - **Coverage estimate divergence:** `ˆC` plateaus while `f1/f2` ratio remains high → estimator saturation; consider switching to source-based Chao1 or alternative.  
  - **Crawl queue depletion:** Priority queue empties before budget → seed generation too conservative; relax gap thresholds or expand query domains.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Extraction validation:** Run the LLM module on 10 manually annotated pages; compute type-consistency and per-relation precision (as in Table VI). Expect >95% type-consistency; if lower, adjust prompt.  
  2. **Baseline comparison:** Execute BFS, Focused, and W→K (no feedback) on the same 48 seeds with 213-page budget; compare unique companies discovered (Table II). Expect W→K→W to achieve higher precision at equal or lower page count.  
  3. **Coverage projection:** Run 5 iterations, fit Michaelis-Menten to cumulative entities (Figure 3), and check if `S_max` stabilizes. If curve shows no asymptote, increase iterations or re-evaluate seed diversity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can active learning be integrated into the W→K→W pipeline to reduce LLM extraction errors with minimal human annotation?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Page 10, "Future Work" paragraph 1 identifies active learning for extraction as a research direction.
- **Why unresolved:** The current pipeline uses fixed few-shot prompting without uncertainty estimation, so extraction errors propagate without correction.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementing an uncertainty-aware extraction module that identifies high-uncertainty entities/relations for human review, then measuring extraction precision improvement per unit of annotation effort compared to the baseline.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can the pipeline be extended to capture temporal dynamics of supplier networks rather than static snapshots?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Page 10, "Future Work" paragraph 2 calls for temporal dynamics to track supplier entry/exit.
- **Why unresolved:** The current W→K→W iterations produce a single consolidated KG without timestamps or change tracking.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Modifying the pipeline to timestamp entity appearances and relation validations, then validating against historical business registry data or news archives to measure recall of supplier turnover events.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can more expressive graph neural networks (e.g., CompGCN, R-GCN) improve gap detection and link prediction over the current DistMult embeddings?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Page 10, "Future Work" paragraph 3 suggests GNN integration beyond DistMult.
- **Why unresolved:** The pipeline uses DistMult, which treats relations as translations in embedding space, potentially limiting its ability to model complex KG structures for gap analysis.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replacing DistMult with GNN-based encoders in the gap detection module, then comparing (a) precision of predicted missing links against future iterations, and (b) final KG completeness at fixed page budgets.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** To what extent does the pipeline's reliance on web-accessible information bias estimated supplier populations against firms with minimal online presence?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Page 9, "Limitations" section notes "Firms with minimal web presence cannot be discovered by any crawling method."
- **Why unresolved:** The Chao1 estimator only measures coverage of the web-discoverable population, but there may be a substantial non-web-discoverable supplier base that remains invisible to all crawling approaches.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Cross-referencing discovered entities with comprehensive non-web databases (e.g., government business registries, tax records) to estimate the proportion of suppliers with no web footprint, then adjusting coverage estimates accordingly.

## Limitations
- Exact domain glossary contents, few-shot examples, and crawl priority weights are unreported, hindering precise replication.
- The curated ground truth of 195 suppliers is partially opaque; recall estimates are relative to an unverified reference.
- Chao1’s adaptation assumes web entity capture-recapture dynamics, but empirical overestimation suggests instability with sparse singletons.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (KG-guided crawling):** Medium – topology-driven gap detection is conceptually sound but efficacy depends on unreported heuristics and KG noise levels.
- **Mechanism 2 (LLM extraction):** High – type-consistency (100%) and precision (66.2%) are reported, though prompt details are missing.
- **Mechanism 3 (Chao1 stopping):** Medium – adaptive use is novel, but empirical overestimation and bootstrap uncertainty limit reliability.
- **Overall results (precision/F1):** High – metrics are clearly stated; however, recall is uncertain due to ground truth gaps.
