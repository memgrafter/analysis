# Beyond Translation: Evaluating Mathematical Reasoning Capabilities of LLMs in Sinhala and Tamil
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.14517
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14517
- **Reference count**: 6
- **Primary result**: Native-authored math problems reveal LLMs struggle with complex reasoning in Sinhala/Tamil, not just translation quality.

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the assumption that multilingual LLMs can uniformly reason mathematically across languages. By constructing a parallel dataset of 450 word problems natively authored in English, Sinhala, and Tamil (avoiding translation artifacts), researchers evaluated four LLMs across six mathematical reasoning types. While basic arithmetic (Types 1-2) showed robust cross-lingual transfer, complex reasoning tasks like unit conversion (Type 4) and optimization (Type 6) exhibited significant degradation, with some models dropping 20-36% in accuracy. The results suggest that apparent multilingual competence masks underlying reliance on English-centric processing pathways, with performance stability varying dramatically across models.

## Method Summary
The researchers built a parallel dataset of 450 math word problems (150 per language) by having expert authors with mathematical training and fluency in all three languages create problems directly in each language. Problems were categorized using a six-type taxonomy from single-step arithmetic to optimization. Four LLMs (GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3, Gemini 2.5, Claude Sonnet 4) were evaluated using zero-shot prompting across all languages and types. Performance was measured by accuracy in solving each problem, with results aggregated by model, language, and reasoning type.

## Key Results
- Basic arithmetic (Types 1-2) maintained near-identical accuracy across languages (<2% variation)
- Complex reasoning tasks showed significant degradation: Type 4 (unit conversion) dropped up to 20% (ChatGPT), Type 6 (optimization) fell from 88%→52% (ChatGPT, English→Tamil)
- DeepSeek-V3 showed exceptional stability (~94% overall accuracy across languages)
- Gemini 2.5 performed better in Tamil (96%) than Sinhala (90.7%), while other models showed uniform or worse performance in Tamil

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Native Authorship Isolates Reasoning from Translation Artifacts
By having problems natively authored in each language by fluent speakers with mathematical training, the dataset removes confounding variables present in translated benchmarks (e.g., unnatural syntax, inconsistent number formats). This allows performance differences to be attributed to the model's internal reasoning processes in the target language rather than its ability to parse a translated English structure. Core assumption: Differences in performance between natively-authored and translated datasets would correlate with translation-induced noise, not true linguistic reasoning ability.

### Mechanism 2: Granular Taxonomy Reveals Systematic Cross-Lingual Transfer Patterns
The six-type taxonomy exposes that basic arithmetic (Types 1-2) transfers robustly across languages, while linguistically complex types (4: Unit Conflict, 6: Optimization) show significant degradation. This pattern suggests that surface language impacts reasoning stages involving parsing complex linguistic relationships, not pure calculation. Core assumption: The taxonomy's structural definitions are consistent across languages and map to distinct cognitive/processing demands.

### Mechanism 3: Model-Specific Resilience Indicates Architectural/Sensitivity Differences
Varying degradation profiles across models suggest that apparent multilingual competence relates to how a model's internal representations or training data distribution handles non-English linguistic structures. DeepSeek's near-uniform performance implies its reasoning pathways are less language-bound, while ChatGPT's sharp drop in Type 6 implies reliance on language-specific heuristics that fail in Tamil/Sinhala. Core assumption: The models are sufficiently different in architecture/data to produce distinct behavioral signatures.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Controlled Benchmark Design for Multilingual Evaluation**
  - Why needed here: To draw valid causal inferences about language effects, one must control for confounds like translation quality and cultural context.
  - Quick check question: How would translating an English math problem into Tamil potentially introduce artifacts that affect an LLM's performance independently of its "Tamil reasoning" ability?

- **Concept: Reasoning Skill Taxonomy as a Diagnostic Tool**
  - Why needed here: Aggregate accuracy scores mask critical variations. The six-type taxonomy acts as a diagnostic lens.
  - Quick check question: Why might a model perfectly solve multi-step arithmetic in Sinhala but fail on a logical deduction problem in the same language, according to the paper's framework?

- **Concept: Inference of Internal Processing from Behavioral Asymmetries**
  - Why needed here: Without model interpretability access, researchers infer internal mechanisms (e.g., "implicit translation") from patterns of performance asymmetry.
  - Quick check question: The paper states the translation-dependent hypothesis is "an inference from behavioral evidence rather than a mechanistic finding." What would constitute mechanistic evidence?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Dataset Construction (native authorship, cultural adaptation) -> Taxonomy Implementation (problem type classification engine) -> Evaluation Harness (zero-shot prompt templates, answer extraction) -> Analysis Pipeline (performance matrix generation, radar plot visualization)
- **Critical path**: 1) Secure expert authors fluent in English/Sinhala/Tamil with math training. 2) Develop formal problem type definitions and authoring guidelines. 3) Generate 150 unique problem seeds in English, then natively rewrite each in Sinhala/Tamil. 4) Verify semantic equivalence across versions. 5) Run zero-shot evaluations on all 450 items. 6) Aggregate results by (Model, Language, Type) and visualize.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Native vs. Translated Dataset: Native avoids artifacts but is extremely resource-intensive; Translated is scalable but introduces noise. Zero-shot vs. Few-shot: Zero-shot tests inherent capability; few-shot may reveal scaffolding potential but confounds with in-context learning effects. Granular vs. Aggregate Metrics: Granular (type-aware) reveals critical patterns but increases multiple comparison risks; aggregate is simpler but hides failure modes.
- **Failure signatures**: 1) Consistent Type 4/6 drop: Indicates model's reasoning is brittle on linguistically complex operations. 2) Asymmetric language drop: Worse performance in Tamil vs. Sinhala suggests data imbalance or script-specific tokenization issues. 3) Model-specific distortion: Radar plot "shape change" vs. uniform "shrinkage" points to different internal processing strategies.
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Dataset Sanity Check: Confirm semantic equivalence of random sample across languages. 2) Baseline Validation: Run Type 1 problems to confirm basic arithmetic parsing works. 3) Type Isolation: Run full 25-problem set per type sequentially to confirm degradation pattern holds per-type.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Do LLMs genuinely reason mathematically in Sinhala and Tamil, or do they rely on implicit translation to English-like representations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "This raises a fundamental question: do LLMs genuinely reason mathematically across languages, or do they rely on implicit translation to English-like representations before solving problems?" (Introduction)
- Why unresolved: The study shows performance degradation in complex tasks consistent with translation reliance but lacks internal mechanistic evidence to confirm the processing pathway.
- What evidence would resolve it: Probes into model activations, attention patterns, or intermediate representations during reasoning in Sinhala/Tamil versus translated English inputs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the relative contribution of implicit translation, training data coverage, and tokenization differences to the observed cross-lingual performance gaps?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Disentangling these factors remains an important direction for future work." (Conclusion)
- Why unresolved: Current black-box evaluation correlates language with performance drops but cannot isolate individual factors due to confounding variables.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying mathematical training data distribution across languages, analyzing tokenization efficiency for numerical expressions.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do few-shot and chain-of-thought prompting strategies alter the cross-lingual stability of LLMs' mathematical reasoning, particularly for complex problem types?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "We test only zero-shot prompting; few-shot or chain-of-thought approaches might change these patterns." (Limitations)
- Why unresolved: The evaluation protocol was limited to zero-shot, leaving unknown whether advanced prompting enhances or reduces language-based performance gaps.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replication of the study with few-shot examples and chain-of-thought prompts across all models and languages.

## Limitations
- Zero-shot prompting only: Few-shot or chain-of-thought strategies might mitigate observed degradation patterns
- Cannot separate linguistic reasoning from cultural/educational presentation differences
- Relatively small dataset (450 problems) limits generalizability to other mathematical reasoning tasks
- Behavioral inference rather than mechanistic evidence for "implicit translation" hypothesis

## Confidence
- Native dataset's isolating power (Medium-High): Methodological innovation is sound but exact noise quantification unknown
- Taxonomy's explanatory power for cross-lingual effects (Medium): Pattern holds but mechanism remains speculative
- Model-specific resilience patterns indicating architectural differences (Low-Medium): Behavioral signatures are compelling but could reflect training data distribution

## Next Checks
1. **Few-shot benchmarking**: Test whether providing 2-3 examples of each problem type in the target language eliminates the observed degradation patterns, particularly for Types 4 and 6.
2. **Linguistic feature decomposition**: Analyze whether specific linguistic features correlate with performance drops within each type, isolating language effects from reasoning complexity.
3. **Cross-cultural validation**: Have non-native speakers familiar with mathematical conventions evaluate whether the native-authored problems maintain consistent semantic difficulty across languages.
