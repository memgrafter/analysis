# DistillLens: Symmetric Knowledge Distillation Through Logit Lens

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13567
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13567
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Distilling GPT-2-1.5B to GPT-2-340M yields 23.72 average Rouge-L, surpassing teacher (23.52) and standard KD (~20.4).

## Executive Summary
DistillLens addresses a key limitation of standard knowledge distillation: ignoring teacher model intermediate layer "thought processes." It projects hidden states from both teacher and student into the vocabulary space using the Logit Lens and aligns them layer‑wise with a symmetric Jensen‑Shannon divergence (JSD) loss. This dual‑sided penalty prevents overconfidence and underconfidence while preserving high‑entropy information. Combined with a final‑layer reverse KL objective, the method forces the student to mimic the teacher’s full computational trajectory. Experiments on GPT‑2 and Llama models demonstrate consistent improvements on instruction‑following benchmarks, achieving state‑of‑the‑art results—even exceeding the teacher in some cases—and reducing exposure bias.

## Method Summary
Given a larger teacher and a smaller student, DistillLens selects a subset of intermediate layers via a uniform interleaved mapping (e.g., student layers \[2,4,6,8,10\] → teacher layers \[8,16,24,32,40\] in a 40‑layer teacher). For each mapped pair, the hidden states are projected through the respective unembedding matrices and softmaxed to obtain probability distributions over the vocabulary. The symmetric JSD loss is computed between these distributions and averaged to form an intermediate alignment loss \(L_{\text{inter}}\). This is added to a final‑layer reverse KL task loss \(L_{\text{KD}}\) with weight \(\lambda = 1.0\). The student is trained with AdamW, mixed precision, and standard learning rates. The approach requires shared vocabulary and comparable unembedding matrices, and its memory overhead scales with the number of selected layers.

## Key Results
- Distilling GPT‑2‑1.5B → GPT‑2‑340M achieves 23.72 average Rouge‑L, surpassing both the teacher (23.52) and standard KD (~20.4).
- Gains generalize across model families (GPT‑2, Llama), metrics (SBERT similarity), and datasets, with reduced exposure bias.
- Outperforms feature‑based baselines (MSE, FDD) on instruction‑following benchmarks like DollyEval and VicunaEval.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Logit Lens Projection**  
Applying the unembedding matrix to an intermediate hidden state yields a vocabulary‑space probability distribution that reflects the model’s evolving belief about the next token. This creates a common, semantically aligned space for comparing teacher and student at each layer. Break condition: shared vocabulary and similar unembedding matrices are required; otherwise cross‑model projection fails.

**Mechanism 2: Symmetric JSD Loss**  
JSD combines forward and reverse KL, penalizing both overconfidence (student places mass on tokens the teacher avoids) and underconfidence (student fails to match teacher‑favored tokens). Analysis shows linear penalty growth for extreme overconfidence and a bounded penalty for underconfidence, forming a convex basin that drives the student’s intermediate distributions toward the teacher’s while preserving high entropy. If intermediate distributions are already highly peaked, the benefit diminishes.

**Mechanism 3: Uniform Interleaved Layer Mapping**  
By mapping student layers proportionally across the teacher’s depth, the method supervises the student’s entire forward pass, forcing it to replicate the teacher’s full computational trajectory rather than isolated checkpoints. Ablation shows interleaved mapping outperforms using only early or late consecutive layers. Non‑uniform changes in the teacher could make this heuristic misaligned.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Logit Lens**  
  *Why needed:* Transforms abstract hidden states into interpretable vocabulary distributions for alignment.  
  *Quick check:* Given hidden state \(h\) and unembedding matrix \(W_U\), compute logits \(= W_U h\) then apply softmax.

- **Concept: Symmetric vs. Asymmetric Divergence**  
  *Why needed:* Asymmetric KL biases student uncertainty; symmetric JSD provides balanced penalties.  
  *Quick check:* Forward KL (KL(p||q)) penalizes mass where q is high but p is low; reverse KL (KL(q||p)) heavily penalizes q’s mass on tokens absent in p.

- **Concept: Layer Mapping Strategy**  
  *Why needed:* Determines which intermediate layers to align; ensures full trajectory coverage.  
  *Quick check:* For teacher with 12 layers and student with 4 (excluding output), uniform stride maps student layer \(l\) to teacher layer \(\text{round}(l \times 12 / 4)\), i.e., 1→3, 2→6, 3→9.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Frozen Teacher Model → intermediate hidden states; Trainable Student Model → intermediate hidden states; Layer Mapper selects K layer pairs (uniform stride); Logit Lens (apply \(W_U\) + softmax) yields distributions \(p^{(l')}, q^{(l)}\); Symmetric Loss Calculator computes JSD; Task Loss Calculator computes reverse KL on final logits; Loss Combiner sums \(L_{\text{task}} + \lambda \cdot L_{\text{inter}}\); Optimizer updates Student parameters only.

**Critical path:**  
1. Forward pass through both models to obtain hidden states at all layers.  
2. For each mapped layer pair, project via Logit Lens to probability distributions.  
3. Compute average JSD → \(L_{\text{inter}}\).  
4. Compute final‑layer reverse KL → \(L_{\text{task}}\).  
5. Combine: \(L_{\text{total}} = L_{\text{task}} + \lambda \cdot L_{\text{inter}}\).  
6. Backpropagate \(L_{\text{total}}\) and update student.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Training cost vs. performance:** More projected layers (higher K) increase memory/compute (\(O(K \cdot V \cdot d)\)) but improve results; optimal around K=5.  
- **λ tuning:** Balances structural regularization vs. task loss; too high causes instability, too low reduces to standard KD.  
- **Layer selection:** Interleaved pattern (full‑depth coverage) is superior to consecutive early/late layers.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. No improvement over KD: Verify \(L_{\text{inter}}\) is computed; check layer mapping non‑empty; ensure unembedding matrices match.  
2. Training instability: Reduce λ or add gradient clipping.  
3. Poor generalization on new tasks: Over‑regularization; lower K or λ.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Reproduce Main Result: Distill GPT‑2‑1.5B → GPT‑2‑340M on databricks‑dolly‑15k with λ=1.0, 5 interleaved layers; verify Avg Rouge‑L >23.7.  
2. Ablation: Number of Logit Lens Layers (vary K from 0 to 6); expect peak at K=5.  
3. Loss Comparison: Compare JSD against JD, MSE, FDD; expect JSD/JD to outperform asymmetric baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can the computational overhead of Logit Lens projections be reduced without losing alignment performance?
- What techniques enable cross‑architecture distillation when teacher and student have mismatched vocabularies?
- Can DistillLens effectively transfer complex reasoning to significantly smaller student models, and what architectural limits exist?
- What is the causal relationship between intermediate distribution alignment and reduction in exposure bias?

## Limitations
- Requires shared vocabulary; cross‑vocabulary distillation is unsupported.
- The theoretical advantage of symmetric JSD for preserving high‑entropy intermediate information lacks direct empirical validation.
- Uniform interleaved layer mapping is a heuristic; optimality across diverse architectures is unknown.
- Missing reproduction details (exact epochs, prompt templates) hinder faithful verification.

## Confidence
- Logit Lens assumption (shared unembedding matrix): **Medium**
- Symmetric JSD effect on intermediate layers: **Medium**
- Interleaved mapping optimality: **Low**
- Overall result reproducibility: **Medium** (due to missing hyperparameters)

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑vocabulary distillation:** Train GPT‑2 teacher to student with a different tokenizer (e.g., RoBERTa); expect performance drop; test if a learned linear projection recovers alignment.
2. **Entropy measurement:** Track entropy of intermediate distributions during training; compare JSD vs. asymmetric losses to confirm higher entropy preservation.
3. **Dynamic layer mapping:** Replace uniform stride with data‑driven selection (e.g., highest gradient norm of student hidden states) and evaluate on downstream tasks.
