# Calibration-Reasoning Framework for Descriptive Speech Quality Assessment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10175
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10175
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art on QualiSpeech with mean dimension PCC 0.71 and MOS PCC 0.76 (13% improvement over prior SFT methods), while advancing fine-grained artifact detection and temporal localization.

## Executive Summary
Current speech quality assessment models lack interpretability, providing only aggregated MOS scores without characterizing specific artifacts or localizing them temporally. This work introduces a two-stage Calibration-Reasoning framework for Audio LLMs. First, supervised fine-tuning (Calibration) aligns the model to predefined perceptual dimensions and unfreezes the audio encoder to increase sensitivity to low-level speech features. Second, reinforcement learning (Reasoning) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and dimension-specific rewards enhances the accuracy of artifact descriptions and temporal localization. Evaluated on the QualiSpeech benchmark, the framework sets new records for multidimensional correlation and localization metrics while preserving numerical precision.

## Method Summary
The method post-trains an Audio Flamingo 3 base model on the QualiSpeech dataset (12,450 recordings) in two stages. **Calibration** uses supervised fine-tuning with cross-entropy loss on ground-truth dimension scores (1–5 scale for 7 perceptual dimensions). The audio encoder is made trainable to adapt feature extraction, while the LLM learns to produce scalar judgments per dimension. **Reasoning** employs GRPO with dimension-specific rewards to refine descriptive accuracy and temporal localization. For each input, the policy samples a group of candidate responses; rewards are computed per dimension (e.g., exact score match, semantic similarity of artifact descriptions, or an LLM-judge score) and normalized within the group to compute advantages. A KL penalty against a frozen reference model stabilizes training. The model is prompted to output structured information: dimension scores, artifact time intervals, and brief descriptions, enabling automatic reward extraction.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art on QualiSpeech: mean PCC across dimensions 0.71, MOS PCC 0.76 (13% gain over prior SFT methods).
- Improved fine-grained metrics: artifact detection F1, description similarity (GPT-4o correlation), and temporal IoU all surpass previous approaches.
- Ablations confirm: unfreezing the audio encoder yields +0.12 avg PCC; dimension-specific rewards outperform unified rewards; GRPO is necessary for coherent long-form descriptions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Calibration via Supervised Fine-Tuning
- **Claim:** Calibration aligns the audio encoder to perceptual dimensions and equips the LLM to produce accurate dimension-wise scores.
- **Mechanism:** Training with cross-entropy loss on ground-truth dimension scores. The audio encoder is unfrozen to adapt feature extraction to speech quality characteristics; the LLM learns to interpret embeddings and output scalar judgments.
- **Core assumption:** QualiSpeech taxonomy and annotations reliably cover common speech artifacts.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "calibration stage aligns the model to predict predefined perceptual dimensions" and "making audio encoder trainable."
  - Section 3.1: Calibration loss definition; encoder is made trainable.
  - Ablation (Table 1): Unfreezing encoder yields 0.12 avg PCC gain vs frozen.
- **Break condition:** Under-representation of certain artifacts (e.g., codec distortions) in calibration data leads to weak feature learning and poor localization.

### Mechanism 2: GRPO with Dimension-Specific Rewards
- **Claim:** GRPO improves artifact description accuracy and temporal localization without degrading numerical judgments.
- **Mechanism:** For each input, sample a group of candidate responses. Compute multiple reward functions per dimension (e.g., exact score match, description similarity). Normalize advantages within the group and update policy via KL-regularized loss, biasing toward outputs excelling on specific dimensions.
- **Core assumption:** Per-dimension reward functions provide consistent, human-aligned training signals.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "reinforcement learning stage leverages GRPO with dimension-specific rewards to heavily enhance accuracy of descriptions and temporal localization."
  - Section 3.2.1: Reward formulas (4,5) and LLM-judge approach; Figure 2 illustrates dimension-wise scoring.
  - Comparison (Table 1, 3): Dimension-wise variants outperform unified reward on MOS PCC and localization metrics.
- **Break condition:** Sparse examples for a dimension cause noisy reward signals, leading to unstable updates or overfitting.

### Mechanism 3: Structured Output Format
- **Claim:** Enforcing parseable outputs (scores, artifact time intervals, brief descriptions) enables automatic reward computation and keeps the model grounded in verifiable facts, preserving numerical precision.
- **Mechanism:** Prompt templates instruct the model to output dimension scores as a list and artifacts with explicit timestamps. These elements are extracted automatically to compute rewards (exact match, IoU), preventing hallucination from inflating description quality at the expense of score accuracy.
- **Core assumption:** Reference annotations contain accurate scores/time spans; parsing logic correctly identifies model outputs.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: Reasoning-only models suffer from "hallucinated or incorrect dimension-wise predictions"; structured outputs solve this.
  - Sections 3.1 & 3.2: Prompt examples require scores and artifact mentions; reward extraction relies on structure.
  - Ablation: Explicit parsing for dimension-wise rewards yields higher F1 and IoU vs. long-form-only baselines (Table 3).
- **Break condition:** Model deviates from format (e.g., omits timestamps) breaks automatic reward, causing localization drop.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Audio encoder (AF3) -> 128-channel mel-spectrogram features -> LLM decoder (Qwen) with LoRA adapters (rank 64) -> two-stage training (Calibration SFT -> Reasoning GRPO) -> reward modules (Accuracy, Semantic similarity, LLM-judge) -> QualiSpeech dataset (scores, intervals, descriptions).
- **Critical path:**
  1. Load pretrained Audio Flamingo 3.
  2. Calibration: 10k iterations, batch size 8, LR 1.5e-5; cross-entropy loss on dimension scores; unfreeze audio encoder.
  3. Save calibration checkpoint.
  4. Reasoning (GRPO): 200 iterations, group size 4, batch 4, LR 5e-6; compute per-dimension rewards, normalize advantages within group, update policy with KL penalty (λ).
  5. Evaluate on test set using GPT-4o extraction for PCC, F1, IoU, ROUGE-L.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Trainable encoder: +0.12 avg PCC but doubles memory/compute; partial LoRA on encoder may mitigate.
  - Reward choice: LLM-judge most accurate but costly; Accuracy+Semantic is lightweight but less robust to paraphrasing.
  - GRPO hyperparams: larger group size reduces advantage variance but increases memory; lower RL LR mitigates forgetting SFT knowledge.
  - Two-stage vs single-stage: Reasoning-only degrades dimension scores; Calibration-only cannot produce long descriptions.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Calibration loss stagnates: verify encoder unfrozen, prompts include scoring, LR adequate.
  - Reasoning lowers dimension PCC (>0.05 drop): increase KL penalty λ or rebalance reward weights.
  - Poor temporal localization (IoU < 0.4): check timestamp format consistency; may need more SFT examples with explicit intervals.
  - Reward hacking: correct scores but meaningless descriptions; combine accuracy with semantic similarity to penalize incoherence.
  - Out-of-distribution artifacts: performance drops on novel distortions; consider data augmentation or signal-processing rewards.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate Calibration encoder ablation: train with frozen vs trainable encoder. Expected: trainable encoder boosts avg PCC (0.66 vs 0.51 per Table 1).
  2. Compare Reasoning reward strategies: Accuracy+Semantic vs unified sum reward. Expected: dimension-wise rewards improve MOS PCC and artifact F1/IoU.
  3. Ablate GRPO group size (4 vs 8) and KL penalty λ. Monitor loss stability and dev PCC to find sweet spot.

## Limitations
- **Calibration data bias:** QualiSpeech may under-represent some artifacts (e.g., codec distortions), risking weak feature learning.
- **Reward signal noise:** Per-dimension rewards may be unstable for rare artifacts, causing overfitting or collapse.
- **Parsing brittleness:** Strict format reliance breaks reward computation if model omits timestamps; no parsing error analysis provided.
- **Generalization:** Validation only on QualiSpeech (English speech); unknown on unseen distortions, languages, or acoustic domains.

## Confidence
- **Calibration alignment (SFT): High** – Strong ablation: unfreezing encoder yields +0.12 PCC (Table 1); mechanism standard and validated.
- **GRPO with dimension rewards: Medium** – Comparative gains shown (Tables 1,3), but implementation details (advantage normalization, KL schedule) unspecified; replication non-trivial.
- **Structured output grounding: Medium-Low** – Parsing-based rewards plausible and ablations show benefit, but no format adherence error analysis; reward hacking not fully ruled out.
