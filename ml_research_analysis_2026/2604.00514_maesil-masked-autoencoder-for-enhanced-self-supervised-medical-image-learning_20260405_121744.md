# MAESIL: Masked Autoencoder for Enhanced Self-supervised Medical Image Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2604.00514
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00514
- **Reference count**: 16
- **Primary result**: MAESIL achieves PSNR 30.28, SSIM 0.98, and LPIPS 0.26 on 3D CT reconstruction, significantly outperforming AE, VAE, and VQ-VAE baselines.

## Executive Summary
Self-supervised learning for 3D medical CT scans often suffers from the loss of volumetric context when processing slices independently. MAESIL tackles this by introducing "superpatches"—large 3D chunks that efficiently capture global structure while managing computational cost—combined with a novel dual-masking strategy. Pre-trained on three public CT datasets (BTCV, LIDC-IDRI, TotalSegmentator), the method demonstrates superior 3D representation learning, as evidenced by strong reconstruction metrics across anatomical views.

## Method Summary
Input CT volumes are standardized to 512×512×512 and partitioned into 64 non-overlapping 128×128×128 superpatches. Each superpatch is tokenized into 4,096 local patches (8×8×8), embedded via 3D convolution, and enriched with positional encodings. A dual-masking strategy is applied: (1) plane-wise masking randomly removes 75% of patches per 2D slice, and (2) axis-wise masking removes a contiguous 50% of slices along the Superior-Inferior axis. The encoder processes only visible tokens, while the decoder (Transformer) combines encoded embeddings with learnable mask tokens to reconstruct all patches. Training uses MSE loss on patch pixel values across combined datasets.

## Key Results
- PSNR: **30.28**
- SSIM: **0.98**
- LPIPS: **0.26**
- Outperforms AE, VAE, and VQ-VAE on reconstruction metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Superpatches preserve 3D structural context by modeling volumes at a chunk level, avoiding the fragmentation of slice-wise processing. The dual-masking scheme forces the model to learn both local (within-slice) and global (across-slices) dependencies: plane-wise masking prevents reliance on neighboring pixels within a slice, while axis-wise masking enforces understanding of longitudinal relationships. This structured sparsity ensures the model must infer missing data from volumetric cues, leading to robust 3D representations that generalize across views.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: CT Volume -> Superpatch Splitting -> Tokenization (3D conv + pos enc) -> Dual-Masking -> Encoder (ViT) -> Decoder (Transformer) -> Reconstruction Head -> Predicted Patches

**Critical path**: Data flows from volume splitting to tokenization; masking selects visible tokens for encoder; decoder fuses encoded visible tokens with mask tokens; reconstruction head predicts patch pixel values.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Superpatch size: larger (e.g., 256³) improves context but increases memory; smaller (e.g., 64³) reduces cost but may lose global structure.
- Masking ratios: higher visible token ratio eases optimization but reduces learned representation richness; lower ratios increase difficulty but risk trivial solutions.
- Encoder-decoder sharing: shared parameters reduce count but may limit capacity for distinct tasks.

**Failure signatures**:
- Trivial learning: mask pattern not properly structured (e.g., uniform random); diagnose via visualization—should see contiguous slice removal plus per-slice random holes.
- Poor volumetric understanding: high error in slices distant from visible regions; diagnose by measuring slice-wise reconstruction error decay from visible slices.
- Blurry artifacts: high LPIPS relative to PSNR/SSIM; diagnose by inspecting reconstruction sharpness and pattern coherence across views.

**First experiments**:
1. Implement superpatch splitting and tokenization; verify output shape (64 tokens per volume, each 4,096 patches).
2. Implement dual-masking; visualize masked patterns to confirm plane-wise (75% per slice) and axis-wise (contiguous 50% slices) structure.
3. Train a minimal model (e.g., 2-layer encoder/decoder) on a small subset; confirm loss decreases and basic reconstruction appears.
