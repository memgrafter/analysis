# NCTB-QA: A Large-Scale Bangla Educational Question Answering Dataset and Benchmarking Performance

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05462
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05462
- **Reference count:** 11
- **Primary result:** Fine-tuning BERT on NCTB-QA yields a 313% relative F1 improvement (0.150 to 0.620), demonstrating the critical role of domain adaptation in low-resource Bangla educational QA.

## Executive Summary
NCTB-QA is a newly created dataset of 87,805 question-answer pairs derived from Bangladeshi textbooks, uniquely balanced between answerable (57.25%) and unanswerable (42.75%) instances and featuring adversarially designed distractors. The authors benchmark encoder-only transformers (BERT, RoBERTa, ELECTRA) on this extractive QA task. Results show that domain-specific fine-tuning leads to dramatic improvements: BERT’s F1 score rises from 0.150 in zero-shot to 0.620 after fine-tuning—a 313% relative gain—and semantic similarity (BERTScore) increases significantly across all models. The work underscores the importance of tailored adaptation for robust performance in low-resource educational QA and points to future directions such as chain-of-thought reasoning, monolingual pre-training, and multimodal extensions.

## Method Summary
The task is extractive question answering with answerability classification, following the SQuAD 2.0 format. Inputs are context passages from NCTB textbooks and questions; answers are either a span from the context or marked as unanswerable. The dataset splits (80/10/10) are context-separated to prevent leakage. Models are encoder-only transformers (BERT-base-cased, RoBERTa-base, ELECTRA-base-discriminator) pretrained on multilingual corpora. They are fine-tuned with a standard QA head predicting start and end token logits; unanswerable cases are handled by predicting an empty span. Training uses AdamW, linear warmup and decay, mixed precision, gradient accumulation (effective batch size 32), for 2 epochs. Hyperparameters: max sequence length 384, document stride 128, n_best_size 3, max_answer_length 30, null_score_diff_threshold 0.0. Evaluation uses Exact Match, F1, and BERTScore.

## Key Results
- BERT achieves a 313% relative F1 improvement (0.150 → 0.620) after fine-tuning.
- All models show significant gains in semantic answer quality (BERTScore) post fine-tuning.
- The balanced dataset with adversarial unanswerables yields high unanswerable F1 (0.82–0.98) and reduces hallucinations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Domain Adaptation via Fine-tuning**  
Domain-specific fine-tuning adapts pretrained multilingual transformers to Bangla educational text, enabling accurate span extraction and answerability prediction. The pretrained models are fine-tuned on NCTB-QA’s extractive QA task, learning to map (question, context) pairs to start/end positions or an empty string. Gradients shift the model’s representations toward Bangla linguistic patterns (e.g., SOV order, agglutination) and educational domain semantics.  
*Evidence:* BERT’s F1 jumps from 0.150 (zero-shot) to 0.620 (fine-tuned), a 313% relative improvement (Table 5).  
*Break condition:* Minimal gain (<5% relative F1) would suggest fundamental misalignment or insufficient data quality.

**Adversarial Unanswerables for Robust Abstention**  
Adversarially designed unanswerable questions with plausible distractors force models to develop genuine contextual reasoning rather than superficial pattern matching. The construction ensures the context contains information related to the answer type but not the correct answer. Training on these teaches the model to recognize the absence of a precise answer, improving abstention and reducing hallucinations.  
*Evidence:* All models achieve high F1 on unanswerable questions (0.82–0.98 range) after fine-tuning (Table 6).  
*Break condition:* Low unanswerable F1 (<0.7) despite fine-tuning would indicate distractors are either too easy or too hard.

**Balanced Distribution to Prevent Bias**  
The dataset’s balanced distribution (57.25% answerable, 42.75% unanswerable) prevents the model from learning a “always answer” heuristic and ensures both extraction and answerability detection are trained adequately. During training, the loss receives gradients from both types, with unanswerables targeting an empty span.  
*Evidence:* High unanswerable F1 values confirm the balance supports learning both capabilities.  
*Break condition:* Artificially skewing the training set to >90% answerable would likely cause unanswerable performance to collapse, confirming the importance of balance.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input Pipeline → Model Backbone → Task Head → Training Loop → Inference Engine

**Critical path:**  
1. Data preprocessing (span fixing, windowing)  
2. Model initialization (pretrained weights)  
3. Fine-tuning loop (forward, loss, backward)  
4. Test set prediction  
5. Metric computation (EM/F1/BERTScore) with breakdowns.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Context window vs. answer locality:** Larger windows reduce fragmentation but increase memory; windowing with stride risks splitting answer spans.  
- **Extractive vs. generative:** Extractive models are constrained to context spans; they cannot generate novel answers, limiting scope but simplifying training.  
- **Memory vs. batch size:** Small per-GPU batches (2) with high gradient accumulation (16 steps) simulate batch 32, slowing iteration but fitting 12GB VRAM.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High confidence wrong answers on unanswerables → poor abstention.  
- Poor performance on long answers (>30 tokens) due to truncation.  
- Positional bias (answers only from early context).  
- Catastrophic forgetting on other Bangla tasks.

**First three experiments:**  
1. **Zero-shot sanity check:** Run pretrained models without fine-tuning to verify baseline F1 ~0.15 for BERT.  
2. **Fine-tuning replication:** Train each model with exact hyperparameters (Table 4) and validate gains (BERT F1 ~0.62).  
3. **Adversarial ablation:** Remove only adversarially crafted unanswerables from training; expect drop in unanswerable F1, confirming their role.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **CoT annotations usage:** The dataset includes chain-of-thought reasoning annotations not used in the study. Future work should employ generative models to produce CoT steps and assess impact on accuracy and interpretability.  
- **Monolingual vs. multilingual pre-training:** Could monolingual Bangla pre-training or adapters outperform the multilingual baselines?  
- **Multimodal extension:** How to incorporate textbook visuals (diagrams, tables) and which vision-language architectures work best for Bangla educational QA?  
- **Multi-hop and abstractive reasoning:** The extractive scope excludes higher-order reasoning. Extending the dataset to support multi-context, generative answers is needed.

## Limitations
- Dataset not publicly available, hindering external validation.  
- Preprocessing details (e.g., span fixing heuristics) lack full specification, risking inconsistent implementations.  
- Educational domain specificity may limit generalization to other Bangla text types (e.g., news).

## Confidence
- Fine-tuning efficacy: **High** (direct experimental evidence from Table 5).  
- Adversarial unanswerables: **Medium** (performance inferred, no direct ablation).  
- Dataset balance: **Medium** (correlation observed, no controlled experiment).
