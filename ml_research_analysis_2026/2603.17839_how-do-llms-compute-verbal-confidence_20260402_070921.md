# How do LLMs Compute Verbal Confidence

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17839  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17839  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** LLMs compute verbal confidence via cached retrieval at the post-answer-newline token, using a self-evaluation richer than token log-probabilities.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how large language models (LLMs) generate verbal confidence statements—such as “I’m 90% sure”—when answering questions. Using mechanistic interpretability methods on two models (Gemma 3 27B and Qwen 2.5 7B) with TriviaQA data, the authors test whether confidence is computed just-in-time at the moment it is requested or cached earlier during answer generation. They also examine whether confidence reflects simple token log-probabilities or a more sophisticated internal evaluation. Convergent evidence points to a cached retrieval mechanism: confidence representations arise early at a structural “post-answer-newline” (PANL) token, before the explicit confidence-colon (CC) token, and these representations account for variance beyond what token log-probabilities alone explain.

## Method Summary
The authors apply causal mediation analysis and representation-based probing to trace the computation of verbal confidence. Experiments are conducted on Gemma 3 27B and Qwen 2.5 7B using prompts from TriviaQA that require the model to output an answer followed by a confidence statement. Key techniques include activation steering (intervening on activations to alter confidence), causal patching (swapping activations between correct and incorrect runs), noising and swap experiments (to identify critical layers), attention blocking (masking specific attention patterns), and linear probing (training classifiers to decode confidence from activations). These methods allow the authors to isolate when and where confidence information becomes available in the model’s forward pass.

## Key Results
- Confidence representations are cached during answer generation, not computed de novo at the confidence token; patching PANL activations strongly influences final confidence, while patching CC has weaker effects.
- The post-answer-newline (PANL) token emerges as the early locus of confidence information, with decodable signals appearing in layers 21–25, earlier than the confidence-colon (CC) token (layers 30–35).
- Variance partitioning shows PANL activations explain substantial variance in verbal confidence beyond token log-probabilities (logprobs alone yield cross-validated R² = 0.084), indicating a richer self-evaluation signal.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that confidence is not a simple readout of answer fluency or token likelihood. Activation steering at PANL shifts confidence ratings, and noising experiments show PANL activations carry information necessary for accurate confidence expression. Attention blocking reveals a causal information flow: answer tokens influence PANL, which then influences CC, ruling out a model that computes confidence only at CC. Linear probes trained on PANL activations predict confidence earlier and better than probes on CC activations, and the variance explained by PANL remains after controlling for log-probabilities. Together, this convergent evidence supports a mechanism where the model internally assesses answer quality during generation and stores a confidence representation at a structural checkpoint (PANL), later retrieving it to produce the verbal statement.

## Foundational Learning
Key concepts needed to understand the paper’s approach:

- **Activation steering**: Intervening on specific activation vectors to causally test their effect on outputs. *Why needed*: Directly tests whether manipulating PANL/CC changes confidence. *Quick check*: Can you describe how adding a steering vector at PANL alters confidence predictions?
- **Causal patching**: Swapping activations from a correct-generation run into an incorrect-generation run to identify critical components. *Why needed*: Isolates which activations are necessary for accurate confidence. *Quick check*: What does it mean if patching layer 20 fixes confidence errors?
- **Linear probing**: Training a simple linear model (e.g., regression) on frozen network activations to predict a target property (e.g., confidence). *Why needed*: Diagnoses when confidence information becomes linearly decodable. *Quick check*: How would you set up a probe to test if PANL activations encode confidence?
- **Attention blocking**: Masking or modifying attention patterns between specific tokens. *Why needed*: Traces the flow of information from answer tokens to confidence tokens. *Quick check*: What would blocking attention from answer to PANL imply about confidence computation?
- **Token log-probabilities**: The log-likelihood of each token under the model’s distribution. *Why needed*: Baseline measure of output fluency; compared against richer confidence signals. *Quick check*: Why might high log-prob not correlate with accurate confidence?
- **Variance partitioning**: Decomposing the variance in a target (confidence) into components explained by different factors (e.g., logprobs, PANL activations). *Why needed*: Quantifies the added value of internal representations beyond surface statistics. *Quick check*: How does a low R² for logprobs support the “richer evaluation” claim?

## Architecture Onboarding
Understanding the model components relevant to confidence computation:

- **Component map**: Answer tokens → [early layers (21–25) encode confidence at PANL] → [later layers (30–35) read out at CC] → Confidence colon token and value.  
- **Critical path**: The computation hinges on the PANL token as a bottleneck: answer generation activates PANL, which then propagates to CC. Deviating from this path (e.g., by blocking attention) disrupts confidence accuracy.  
- **Design tradeoffs**: The study uses structured prompts (answer + newline + confidence) which may make PANL an artifact of template formatting; results may not hold for free-form generations. Models differ in size (27B vs 7B) but show consistent patterns, suggesting robustness to scale within this range.  
- **Failure signatures**: If confidence were computed just-in-time at CC, then (a) patching CC should fully control confidence, (b) linear probes on CC should decode confidence earlier than PANL, and (c) blocking attention from PANL to CC should break confidence—none of these are observed.  
- **First three experiments to run**:  
  1. Apply activation steering at PANL (layers 21–25) and measure shifts in verbal confidence across many examples.  
  2. Train linear probes on activations from each layer for both PANL and CC positions; compare decoding accuracy over layers.  
  3. Block attention from answer tokens to PANL and from PANL to CC separately; assess impact on confidence correlation with accuracy.

## Limitations
- Scope limited to two model architectures (Gemma, Qwen) and a single QA dataset (TriviaQA); findings may not generalize to other models, domains, or prompt formats.
- The PANL token is a structural cue induced by the prompt template; its role could be confounded by training on formatted responses rather than reflecting a universal confidence mechanism.
- While PANL activations explain variance beyond log-probabilities, the exact semantic features of the “richer evaluation” (e.g., consistency checks, hypothetical reasoning) remain unidentified.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-model and cross-task replication**: Repeat key experiments (activation patching, linear probing) on at least three additional model families (e.g., Llama 3, Mistral, GPT-2) and in non-QA tasks (e.g., summarization, code generation) to test robustness of PANL/CC dynamics.
2. **Direct feature identification**: Use iterative-trained linear probes or differentiable mask searches on PANL activations to isolate specific features (e.g., answer consistency across multiple rollouts, counterfactual queries) that correlate with verbal confidence beyond log-probabilities.
3. **Format artifact control**: Evaluate models with modified prompts that remove explicit newlines before confidence or use alternative templates (e.g., confidence expressed mid-sentence) to determine whether PANL’s role persists or is a prompt-format side effect.
