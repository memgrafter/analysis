# RelayLLM: Efficient Reasoning via Collaborative Decoding
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.05167
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05167
- **Reference count**: 23
- **Primary result**: Achieves 49.52% average accuracy on reasoning benchmarks while invoking LLM for only 1.07% of tokens, reducing cost by 98.2% compared to random routers.

## Executive Summary
RelayLLM addresses the inefficiency of existing LLM-SLM collaboration methods, which offload entire queries regardless of necessity. It introduces token-level collaborative decoding: the SLM acts as an active controller, dynamically invoking the LLM only for critical reasoning steps via a special `<call>` command. A two-stage training framework—supervised warm-up followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with difficulty-aware rewards—teaches strategic help-seeking. On six reasoning benchmarks, RelayLLM achieves 49.52% average accuracy with minimal LLM usage (1.07% of tokens), representing a 98.2% cost reduction and a 6.9% accuracy gain over performance-matched random routers.

## Method Summary
The core mechanism is token-level collaboration: during decoding, the SLM generates normal tokens until it emits a `<call>` token, then the LLM produces the remaining tokens. The SLM is trained to decide optimally when to call the LLM. Training begins with supervised fine-tuning on demonstrations showing optimal invocation points (warm-up). Then, GRPO—a reinforcement learning method—refines the policy using rewards that vary by query difficulty: full reward for solvable queries with correct calls, partial for incorrect calls, and an exploration bonus for unsolvable queries to encourage help-seeking. Data filtering removes queries with low teacher pass rates (<50%) to ensure training signal quality.

## Key Results
- Average accuracy of 49.52% across six reasoning benchmarks.
- LLM invoked for only 1.07% of generated tokens, achieving 98.2% cost reduction versus random routers.
- 6.9% accuracy improvement over performance-matched random routing baselines.
- Teacher-Free (SLM-only) results show gains on easier benchmarks but not harder ones, indicating reliance on teacher for complex reasoning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RelayLLM works because the SLM learns to identify "critical reasoning steps" where its own capability is insufficient, delegating only those segments to the LLM. The `<call>` token provides a discrete action for invocation, making the decision tractable. The two-stage training first imitates optimal invocation patterns from teacher demonstrations, then fine-tunes with GRPO to balance accuracy and cost. Difficulty-aware rewards are crucial: they encourage the SLM to call the LLM on hard queries (even if unsolvable) to explore, while penalizing unnecessary calls on easy ones. This yields a policy that selectively engages the LLM only when beneficial, minimizing token-level cost while maintaining high accuracy. However, performance is sensitive to teacher model distribution; mismatched teachers degrade results, indicating the SLM's invocation strategy is tied to the teacher's output style and reasoning patterns.

## Foundational Learning
- **Token-level collaborative decoding**: Why needed? Enables fine-grained control over LLM usage, avoiding full query offload. Quick check: Verify `<call>` tokens appear sparsely in outputs, not at fixed intervals.
- **Special command token (`<call>`)**: Why needed? Provides a discrete, learnable signal for invocation without disrupting token stream. Quick check: Ensure tokenizer treats `<call>` as a single token and it appears only in trained contexts.
- **Supervised warm-up**: Why needed? Bootstraps the policy with teacher demonstrations before RL, avoiding random exploration. Quick check: Measure initial call accuracy on held-in data before GRPO.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**: Why needed? Scales RL to group-based rewards (query difficulty groups) for stable training. Quick check: Compare reward distributions across difficulty groups during training.
- **Difficulty-aware reward shaping**: Why needed? Aligns incentives: reward correct calls on solvable queries, bonus for calls on unsolvable ones to encourage help-seeking. Quick check: Ablate reward terms and observe call behavior on hard queries.
- **Teacher-student distribution alignment**: Why needed? Ensures the SLM's invocation policy generalizes to the teacher's style. Quick check: Cross-evaluate with different teacher models; performance drop indicates misalignment.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input Query -> SLM Decoder -> (Generate Token or Emit `<call>` Token) -> if `<call>` then LLM Decoder -> Output Tokens
- **Critical path**: During autoregressive decoding, the SLM processes the current context and either continues generating or emits `<call>`. Upon `<call>`, the LLM resumes generation from that point using the same context.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Cost vs accuracy (fewer calls reduce cost but risk errors); teacher dependency (performance peaks with matched teacher); latency overhead (switching models adds delay).
- **Failure signatures**: High call ratio (>5%) indicates over-invocation; low accuracy on hard benchmarks suggests under-invocation; performance drop with teacher size/architecture change shows distribution sensitivity.
- **First experiments**: 
  1. Sample 100 outputs and annotate whether `<call>` appears before or after key reasoning steps (e.g., after problem restatement, during calculation).
  2. Measure invocation rate per benchmark and correlate with benchmark difficulty (e.g., GSM8K vs MATH).
  3. Test with a different LLM (e.g., Llama-2-70B instead of Llama-3-70B) as teacher to quantify distribution shift impact.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can RelayLLM be made robust to teacher model distribution shifts during inference?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Cross-LLM evaluation (Figure 3) shows performance peaks when the teacher matches the training teacher and declines with size/architecture changes.
- Why unresolved: The paper attributes this to distribution shift but provides no analysis of specific factors (e.g., tokenizer differences, output style) or mitigation strategies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablations using teachers with aligned tokenizers or intermediate adapter layers; measuring performance across diverse model families.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal teacher pass rate threshold for data filtering in GRPO training, and how does it trade off accuracy versus call ratio?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Data filtering removes queries with <50% teacher pass rate; ablation (Table 3) shows removing filtering triples call ratio with minor accuracy loss.
- Why unresolved: The 50% threshold is selected without justification; its sensitivity across datasets or model scales is unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of thresholds (e.g., 30%, 70%) on multiple benchmarks, reporting accuracy and call ratio curves.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Why does RelayLLM improve intrinsic reasoning (Teacher-Free) on easier benchmarks but not harder ones, and what training factors drive this transfer?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Table 4 shows Teacher-Free gains on Easy datasets (e.g., 61.12% vs. 59.51% GRPO) but drops on Hard ones (e.g., 11.93% vs. 13.18%).
- Why unresolved: The paper notes dependency on teacher for hard tasks but does not analyze whether this stems from training data composition, reward shaping, or capacity limits.
- What evidence would resolve it: Probe internal representations during collaboration; ablate training data to isolate math-specific vs. general reasoning patterns.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Is the exploration reward (r=ρ(y)) for unsolvable queries optimal, or could alternative reward shapes better balance help-seeking and cost?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Difficulty-Aware reward assigns r=ρ(y) for unsolvable queries (Scenario 3) to incentivize exploration (Section 3.2.3).
- Why unresolved: This design is heuristic; no comparison to other forms (e.g., constant bonus, penalty) or theoretical grounding on cost-benefit trade-offs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablate with varied reward functions for unsolvable queries (e.g., fixed bonus, graduated rewards) and evaluate accuracy, call ratio, and help-seeking behavior.

## Limitations
- Implementation specifics (exact SLM/LLM pairs, tokenization of `<call>`, latency overhead) are not detailed, hindering reproducibility.
- Evaluation is limited to six reasoning benchmarks; generalization to other domains (e.g., multilingual, long-context) is unknown.
- The definition of "critical reasoning steps" is subjective and lacks human validation; no annotation study is provided.
- Zero citations for related work suggest minimal independent replication or community scrutiny to date.

## Confidence
- **Token-level collaboration novelty**: High – clearly distinct from query-level routers as described.
- **Training framework efficacy (GRPO with difficulty rewards)**: Medium – GRPO is established, but reward design lacks ablation and theoretical justification.
- **Cost-accuracy trade-off claim (98.2% reduction)**: Low – assumes linear pricing; no end-to-end latency or energy measurements, and cost model not validated.

## Next Checks
