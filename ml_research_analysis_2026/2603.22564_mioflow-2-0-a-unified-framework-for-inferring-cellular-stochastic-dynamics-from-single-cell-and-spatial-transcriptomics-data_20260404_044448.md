# MIOFlow 2.0: A unified framework for inferring cellular stochastic dynamics from single cell and spatial transcriptomics data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22564  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22564  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** State-of-the-art trajectory reconstruction on SERGIO synthetic benchmarks (mean error 0.082 on S‑shaped, 0.243 on trifurcation) and discovery of spatial regulators in axolotl brain regeneration.

## Executive Summary
MIOFlow 2.0 unifies manifold learning, optimal transport, and neural differential equations to infer continuous cellular trajectories from discrete single‑cell and spatial transcriptomics snapshots. It explicitly models stochastic branching (via neural SDEs), non‑conservative population dynamics (through a learned growth‑rate network initialized by unbalanced optimal transport), and spatial environmental conditioning (by jointly embedding gene expression with neighborhood features). The latent space is regularized to match PHATE distances, ensuring manifold adherence. Evaluation on synthetic data shows superior accuracy over baselines, and a case study on axolotl brain regeneration reveals medium spiny neuron neighborhoods as key spatial drivers.

## Method Summary
The method first builds a joint latent space using PHATE‑regularized autoencoders for gene expression and spatial features (neighborhood cell‑type composition, ligand‑receptor signaling, and local expression niche PCA). These embeddings are concatenated (with a scaling factor) to form a combined state. Time evolution is modeled by a neural SDE: drift `b(z,t)` captures deterministic developmental programs, diffusion `σ(z,t)` accounts for transcriptional noise and branching uncertainty, and a growth network `h_ψ(z,t)` adjusts cell mass for proliferation/death. The growth network is initialized from the marginals of an unbalanced optimal transport plan between timepoints. Training minimizes a weighted sum of marginal matching (Wasserstein‑2) loss, transport energy loss, and manifold density loss. Integration is performed via ODE or SDE solvers, with training modes for local (adjacent timepoints) or global integration.

## Key Results
- Outperforms SF2M and OT‑CFM on SERGIO benchmarks: mean reconstruction error 0.082±0.070 (S‑shaped) and 0.243±0.202 (trifurcation).  
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of stochastic dynamics (SDE) and growth‑rate modeling for accurate trajectories.  
- Spatial conditioning identifies medium spiny neuron neighborhoods as crucial regulators in axolotl brain regeneration, a finding inaccessible to non‑spatial methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Stochastic branching via Neural SDE
- **Claim:** Captures stochastic cell fate decisions and branching by modeling latent state evolution as a Neural Stochastic Differential Equation.
- **Mechanism:** The Ito SDE `dZ = b(Z,t;θ) dt + σ(Z,t;φ) dW_t` uses drift `b` (neural network) for deterministic programs and diffusion `σ` for transcriptional noise. Integration yields a distribution of possible futures from a single progenitor.
- **Core assumption:** Cellular differentiation involves inherent stochasticity treatable as Brownian perturbations on the manifold; diffusion coefficient is smooth and learnable.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "stochastic branching via neural SDEs"; Section 3.3 explains drift and diffusion roles; corpus shows related stochastic work but no direct Neural SDE for branching.
- **Break condition:** Diffusion network instability or failure to learn positive‑semidefinite covariance leads to trajectory collapse or diverging loss.

### Mechanism 2: Non‑conservative population dynamics via learned growth rates
- **Claim:** Handles proliferation/death by learning a time‑dependent growth rate `h_ψ(z,t)` that reweights transported cells.
- **Mechanism:** A neural network predicts multiplicative factors; predicted distribution `μ̂ = Σ_j h_ψ(z_j,t) δ_{z_j} / Σ_j h_ψ(z_j,t)` is trained to match the empirical distribution via Wasserstein marginal loss. The growth network is initialized from the source marginal of an unbalanced optimal transport (UOT) plan.
- **Core assumption:** Local proliferation/death depends on cell state and time; UOT provides a reasonable warm‑start for mass fluxes.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "non‑conservative population dynamics via a learned growth‑rate model initialized by unbalanced optimal transport"; Section 3.4 details the network and UOT initialization; corpus contains OT‑based trajectory methods but not learned growth.
- **Break condition:** Extreme `h_ψ` values degenerate reweighted distribution; marginal loss plateaus; UOT parameters may need tuning.

### Mechanism 3: Spatial environmental conditioning through joint latent space
- **Claim:** Integrates spatial transcriptomics by building a joint latent space that conditions dynamics on the cellular microenvironment.
- **Mechanism:** Spatial features (neighbor cell‑type frequencies, summed ligand‑receptor potentials, mean PCA of neighbors) are extracted, PCA‑reduced, and embedded by a separate PHATE‑regularized autoencoder. This spatial embedding is concatenated (scaled by `s`) with the gene embedding to form the joint state `Z`, which is fed to the Neural SDE/ODE vector field.
- **Core assumption:** The microenvironment can be summarized by these aggregates and influences fate decisions; the joint embedding preserves manifold structure.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "environmental influence through the construction of a joint latent space that unifies gene expression with spatial features"; Section 3.5 defines the state and joint manifold; spatial dynamics papers corroborate importance of context.
- **Break condition:** Noisy or uninformative spatial features may degrade performance; scaling `s` set to zero removes spatial influence; graph construction may fail on large datasets.

## Foundational Learning

### Optimal Transport (OT)
**Why needed:** Dynamic OT provides a principled way to interpolate between whole cell populations over continuous time while minimizing kinetic energy, linking the transport cost to a continuity equation.  
**Quick check:** What does the continuity equation `∂t ρ_t + ∇·(ρ_t v)=0` enforce in the dynamic OT setting?

### Neural Differential Equations (Neural ODEs/SDEs)
**Why needed:** They parameterize the vector field governing cell state changes and allow integration to any timepoint, bridging discrete snapshots.  
**Quick check:** How does adding a diffusion term `σ dW_t` to a Neural ODE enable modeling of branching fates?

### Manifold Learning (PHATE)
**Why needed:** Single‑cell data lie on a low‑dimensional manifold; Euclidean distances in raw space are misleading. PHATE approximates geodesic distances, and the autoencoder is trained to match these, ensuring OT operates on true developmental geometry.  
**Quick check:** How does PHATE distance differ from Euclidean distance in representing high‑dimensional trajectories?

### Spatial Feature Engineering
**Why needed:** The microenvironment influences cell fate; extracting neighborhood statistics provides a quantitative summary for conditioning the dynamics.  
**Quick check:** What are the three spatial descriptors concatenated for each cell in MIOFlow 2.0? (neighbor cell‑type frequencies, ligand‑receptor interaction potentials, mean PCA embedding of neighbors)

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Spatial feature extractor → PHATE‑regularized autoencoders (gene & spatial) → Joint embedding (concatenation with scaling) → Dynamics core (drift `f_θ`, diffusion `σ_φ`, growth `h_ψ` with UOT initialization) → Training loop (losses `L_m`, `L_e`, `L_d`)

**Critical path:**  
1. Preprocess data and compute spatial features.  
2. Compute PHATE distances and train geometry‑aware autoencoders.  
3. Encode all cells to latent representations per timepoint.  
4. Run unbalanced OT to initialize growth network weights.  
5. Train full model (local or global mode) with chosen hyperparameters.  
6. Validate on held‑out cells and visualize trajectories.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Latent dimension*: smaller `d` improves manifold adherence but may lose expressiveness; typical 4‑10.  
- *Spatial scaling* `s`: balances gene vs spatial influence; high `s` increases conditioning effect.  
- *Loss weighting* (`λ_m`, `λ_e`, `λ_d`): tuning needed; too high `λ_e` forces straight paths, too low yields shortcuts; `λ_d` prevents out‑of‑manifold predictions.  
- *Solver choice*: adaptive vs fixed‑step ODE/SDE solvers trade bias for speed.  
- *Training mode*: local (pairwise) is more stable for many timepoints; global captures overall consistency but requires careful learning rate scheduling.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Loss divergence*: often from excessive `λ_e` or SDE instability; reduce weights or step size.  
- *Trajectory collapse*: all cells follow same path; inspect `h_ψ` output distribution and increase capacity.  
- *No branching*: either `σ ≈ 0` (increase network size/loss) or lack of multi‑modal targets.  
- *Spatial features ignored*: check `s>0` and gradients to spatial encoder.  
- *Out‑of‑manifold samples*: high `L_d`; increase `λ_d`, improve autoencoder reconstruction, or add PHATE regularization.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Linear switch test:** two Gaussian blobs at t=0 and t=1, equal mass; train in ODE mode; verify straight latent paths and low marginal loss.  
2. **Branching validation:** use SERGIO trifurcation; enable Neural SDE; compare reconstruction error against ODE baseline; ensure splitting into three branches.  
3. **Spatial conditioning toy:** progenitors in two discrete niches at t=0; at t=1 niche A yields type X, niche B yields type Y; train full MIOFlow with spatial features; test that identical expression but different context yields different fates; set `s=0` to ablate effect.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Scaling to millions of cells:** How can MIOFlow 2.0 handle multi‑modal datasets with millions of cells and high‑dimensional spatial features? The computational demands of Neural SDE integration and spatial graph construction are unknown; benchmarks and approximate solvers are needed.  
2. **Higher‑order spatial graphs:** Do hypergraphs representing multi‑cell niches improve conditioning compared to pairwise neighbor graphs? Current kNN/Voronoi graphs may miss complex interactions; building hypergraph features and comparing accuracy on synthetic and real data would resolve this.  
3. **Integration with lineage tracing:** How can continuous trajectories be validated or refined using sparse clonal data? No framework exists to align continuous predictions with direct lineage observations; applying both methods to the same system and developing comparison metrics or regularization constraints is required.

## Limitations
- **Architectural details missing:** Network sizes, nonlinearities, and solver tolerances are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- **Handcrafted spatial features:** The engineered neighborhood descriptors may not capture all relevant microenvironmental cues.
- **Limited validation:** Evaluation is restricted to one biological dataset and SERGIO‑style synthetic benchmarks, leaving generalizability to other single‑cell and spatial modalities unproven.

## Confidence
- **Unified framework (manifold + OT + neural DEs):** Medium confidence  
- **Stochastic branching via Neural SDEs:** Medium confidence  
- **Growth rate modeling (UOT initialization):** Medium confidence  
- **Spatial conditioning (joint embedding):** Medium/Low confidence  
- **Trajectory accuracy on SERGIO:** High confidence

## Next Checks
- **Cross‑dataset generalization:** Test MIOFlow 2.0 on at least two additional synthetic datasets (e.g., cyclic, multifurcating) generated by alternative simulators (dyngen) to evaluate robustness.  
- **Spatial feature ablation:** Systematically remove or perturb spatial inputs (`s=0`, drop one feature category, shuffle spatial graphs) and measure changes in trajectory accuracy and spatial driver identification.  
- **Direct growth rate validation:** On synthetic data with known proliferation/death rates, correlate learned growth network `h_ψ` with ground truth; on real data, correlate `h_ψ` with independent proliferation markers (e.g., MKI67 expression).
