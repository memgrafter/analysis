# Gloss-Free Sign Language Translation: An Unbiased Evaluation of Progress in the Field

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13240
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13240
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Many reported performance gains in recent gloss-free SLT models significantly diminish when evaluated under a unified, standardized framework.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical reproducibility crisis in gloss-free sign language translation (SLT). By re-implementing five recent state-of-the-art models within a single, controlled codebase—standardizing preprocessing, video encoders, and training setups—the authors isolate the true contribution of each method's proposed pretraining strategy. The evaluation reveals that several models' previously reported improvements are not robust to fair comparison, with some scores dropping dramatically. The study also uncovers widespread inconsistencies in evaluation metrics and dataset conventions that further complicate cross-paper comparisons. The released codebase aims to establish a much-needed standard for future SLT research.

## Method Summary
The core method is a large-scale controlled re-evaluation. The authors selected five recent gloss-free SLT models (GFSLT-VLP, SignCL, FLa-LLM, Sign2GPT, C²RL) and re-implemented them from scratch within a unified framework. This framework fixed all variables except the models' core pretraining objectives: it used the same video backbone (Video Swin Transformer), identical frame sampling, consistent text tokenization, and a shared training schedule and optimization protocol. Each model was pretrained on the same large, combined video-text corpus before being fine-tuned and evaluated on the Phoenix-2014T and CSL-Daily benchmark datasets. Additionally, the authors conducted experiments to quantify the impact of different metric libraries and dataset-specific preprocessing conventions (like punctuation handling).

## Key Results
*   Under standardized conditions, the reported gains of many models collapse. For instance, on Phoenix-2014T, C²RL's BLEU-4 drops from the originally reported 26.75 to 21.41, while GFSLT-VLP's score remains relatively stable (21.44 vs. 21.97).
*   Evaluation inconsistencies cause significant score variations. Appending punctuation to Phoenix-2014T test sentences inflates BLEU scores, and the choice of metric library (nlg-eval vs. sacreBLEU) introduces a difference of 1.25 BLEU-4 on CSL-Daily.
*   The study finds that some pretraining strategies that perform well during the pretraining phase (e.g., C²RL's multi-task approach) do not translate to superior final fine-tuned performance when other variables are controlled, suggesting issues like overfitting or poor representation transfer.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's primary mechanism is **experimental control through standardization**. By holding constant all implementation details unrelated to a model's core algorithmic contribution—such as the visual backbone, frame sampling rate, tokenizer, batch size, and optimizer settings—the study removes confounding factors that can inflate or deflate reported numbers. This allows for a direct, apples-to-apples comparison of the intrinsic value of each model's pretraining objective. The second mechanism is **forensic evaluation auditing**, where they deliberately vary known points of inconsistency (punctuation rules, metric libraries) to expose their quantitative impact on reported metrics, demonstrating why published scores are often not directly comparable.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Cross-Dataset Standardization:** The principle that model comparisons must use identical preprocessing, splits, and evaluation code. *Why needed:* Prevents inflated results from cherry-picked data handling. *Quick check:* Are the exact tokenization and filtering steps applied to the test set explicitly documented and identical across all compared experiments?
*   **Metric Consistency:** Using the same metric implementation (library, version, tokenization) for all evaluations. *Why needed:* Different implementations of BLEU, CHRF, etc., produce numerically different scores. *Quick check:* Is the exact command or function call (with version) for calculating every metric provided?
*   **Pretraining Strategy Analysis:** Isolating the effect of a model's pretraining objective from its architectural and training hyperparameters. *Why needed:* To understand if gains come from the novel idea or better-tuned training defaults. *Quick check:* In an ablation, is the only change between runs the model's pretraining objective, with all other hyperparameters frozen?
*   **Reproducibility Through Unified Codebase:** Implementing all competing methods in a single, shared codebase. *Why needed:* Eliminates variance from different codebases, libraries, and accidental optimizations. *Quick check:* Does the repository contain all five model implementations under the same training and evaluation scripts?
*   **Benchmark Dataset Conventions:** Explicitly stating and controlling for dataset-specific rules (e.g., whether to include punctuation, casing). *Why needed:* Many papers follow unreported conventions, making scores incomparable. *Quick check:* Is the text normalization pipeline (lowercasing, punctuation removal/addition) applied to both training references and model outputs specified in detail?
*   **Overfitting Diagnosis in Transfer Learning:** Recognizing that strong pretraining loss reduction does not guarantee better downstream task performance. *Why needed:* Highlights the risk of overfitting to the pretraining distribution or task. *Quick check:* Are both pretraining and fine-tuning validation curves plotted and analyzed for all models?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Input Video -> Video Backbone (e.g., Video Swin) -> Temporal Pooling/Frame Sampling -> Visual Features -> Cross-Modal Fusion (e.g., Transformer) -> Text Decoder (e.g., Transformer) -> Output Tokens -> Text Detokenizer.  
For pretraining: This pipeline is trained with an auxiliary objective (e.g., masked token modeling, translation) on a large video-text corpus, producing a checkpoint that is then fine-tuned end-to-end for the SLT task.

**Critical Path:** The evaluation's validity hinges on the **Unified Codebase** node. From this single source of truth, two parallel paths diverge: 1) The **Standardized Fine-Tuning** path (fixed hyperparams, data) to generate final results, and 2) The **Controlled Ablation** path (systematically varying one factor like backbone or metric) to diagnose sources of variance. The output is a set of comparable performance numbers and a diagnostic report.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The study trades **absolute peak performance** for **comparative validity**. By not allowing each model its own optimal hyperparameters (learning rate, augmentation), they may underperform each model's published best score. This is a necessary tradeoff for isolation, but it means the resulting scores are not necessarily the state-of-the-art achievable for each architecture, just a fair comparison of their core ideas.

**Failure Signatures:** 1) A model's performance degrades significantly under standardization despite strong original claims. 2) High variance in results when changing only the metric library or tokenization script, indicating scores are brittle to unreported details. 3) Large gap between pretraining loss (which may improve) and fine-tuned BLEU (which does not), signaling poor transfer.

**Exactly 3 First Experiments:** 1) **Re-run all 5 models on a third dataset** (e.g., How2Sign) using the same unified pipeline to test if the "diminished gains" pattern holds across languages and domains. 2) **Systematic Backbone Ablation:** For one model (e.g., C²RL), re-train with 3 different modern video backbones (Video Swin, InternVideo, CLIP-ViT) to quantify how much of the variance is due to the visual encoder choice. 3) **Metric Decomposition:** On a fixed subset of 100 test sentences, generate outputs from all models. Calculate BLEU using 1) original paper's likely settings, 2) standardized sacreBLEU, 3) standardized sacreBLEU with punctuation removed. Report the delta between each pair to isolate the contribution of tokenization vs. library.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Which implementation details (e.g., video backbone, frame sampling, batch size, dropout) most significantly contribute to inflated reported performance gains in gloss-free SLT methods?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The abstract and Section 5.3 state that reported gains "diminish when models are evaluated under consistent conditions," implicating choices like backbones and training optimizations. Specific examples include C²RL's BLEU-4 drop from 26.75 to 21.41 and FLa-LLM's underperformance.
- **Why unresolved:** The study standardized components but did not systematically isolate each variable (e.g., batch size, frame sampling) through controlled ablations across all methods, leaving the relative impact of each detail unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A factorial experimental design varying one implementation factor at a time (e.g., batch size, backbone) while holding others constant, measuring performance delta on multiple datasets.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How do dataset-specific preprocessing conventions (e.g., punctuation handling) and evaluation library discrepancies (e.g., nlg-eval vs. sacreBLEU) systematically affect cross-dataset and cross-paper comparability of SLT metrics, and what standardized protocol can mitigate this?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5.4 explicitly documents that appending punctuation to Phoenix-2014T sentences inflates BLEU scores and that sacreBLEU vs. nlg-eval yields 1.25 BLEU-4 difference on CSL-Daily, with these details often unreported.
- **Why unresolved:** The field lacks a unified evaluation standard; the paper notes these factors are "rarely stated" and must be inferred, preventing fair comparison of published results.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and community adoption of a canonical SLT evaluation toolkit with forced, versioned preprocessing rules (e.g., tokenization, punctuation) and mandatory disclosure of all metric calculation parameters.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Why do certain pretraining strategies (e.g., lightweight translation in FLa-LLM, multi-task pretraining in C²RL) exhibit strong initial gains but fail to transfer to improved fine-tuning performance under standardized conditions, suggesting overfitting or poor representation transferability?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section 5.3 shows that while multi-task pretraining (C²RL) yields better pretrained checkpoints, fine-tuning quickly plateaus (Figure 6b), and FLa-LLM's Light-T objective underperforms the baseline. The authors hypothesize overfitting due to model capacity or missing regularization (e.g., dropout).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper did not analyze the learned representations (e.g., via probing) or systematically vary fine-tuning regularization (dropout, weight decay) to diagnose the transfer gap. The role of pretraining dataset size vs. task design is also unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Representation analysis (e.g., linear probing, similarity metrics) comparing pretrained features across methods, coupled with fine-tuning experiments that explicitly vary regularization and capacity to isolate the cause of plateauing.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How do intrinsic dataset characteristics (e.g., domain, signer variability, video quality) affect optimal hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate, training duration) and convergence dynamics in gloss-free SLT, and can these be predicted a priori?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section 5.2 notes CSL-Daily required a lower learning rate (10⁻³ vs. 10⁻²) and converged earlier (within 50 epochs) than Phoenix-2014T. The authors hypothesize this is due to CSL-Daily's larger size, cleaner lab videos, and repeated sentences. However, this was not formally analyzed.
- **Why unresolved:** Only two datasets were compared; the relationship between dataset properties (annotated in Table 1) and optimal training configurations was not studied systematically. No model or rule for hyperparameter selection based on dataset traits exists.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A meta-analysis across 5–10 diverse SLT datasets (varying in domain, signer count, hours) correlating metadata (e.g., signer diversity, repetition rate) with optimal learning rates, convergence speed, and final performance via regression or scaling laws.

## Limitations
*   **Scope:** Findings are based on re-implementing only 5 recent models and evaluating on only 2 popular datasets (Phoenix-2014T, CSL-Daily), limiting generalizability to other architectures (e.g., newer MLLMs) or sign languages/datasets.
*   **Reconstruction Fidelity:** The unified codebase controls major factors but may not replicate all original training nuances (e.g., exact data augmentation, hyperparameter search strategies), potentially misattributing some performance differences.
*   **Metric Audit Depth:** While inconsistencies are documented, the root causes (e.g., specific tokenization differences, case handling rules) are not exhaustively traced for every evaluation library across all historical papers.

## Confidence
*   **High:** The central claim that many previously reported gains diminish under standardized evaluation is strongly supported by consistent results across 5 models and 2 datasets.
*   **Medium:** The assertion that dataset conventions and library choice cause significant score variation is demonstrated experimentally, though the analysis is limited to two datasets and a subset of common libraries.
*   **Low:** The inference that these findings explain the entire field's progress is weak due to the narrow model/dataset scope and the rapid emergence of new large multimodal model (MLLM)-based approaches that were not evaluated.

## Next Checks
1.  **Replicate with additional models:** Apply the unified codebase to other recent gloss-free methods, particularly emerging MLLM-based approaches from the reference corpus, to test the robustness of the "diminished gains" pattern.
2.  **Cross-dataset verification:** Evaluate the 5 reimplemented models on at least one additional, structurally different dataset (e.g., ASLLVD, How2Sign) to assess generalizability of the performance collapse.
3.  **Metric decomposition audit:** On a shared test subset, systematically compare scores from all relevant metric libraries (nlg-eval, sacreBLEU, tokenizer-based) under every documented preprocessing variant (punctuation on/off, casing) to build a complete map of score variance sources.
