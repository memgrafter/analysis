# When both Grounding and not Grounding are Bad -- A Partially Grounded Encoding of Planning into SAT (Extended Version)

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19429
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19429
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: Partially grounded SAT encoding improves coverage over lifted planner LiSAT in 5/9 domains, with linear formula scaling

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the scalability bottleneck in SAT-based planning by introducing a partially grounded encoding that balances the trade-off between fully grounded (exponential) and fully lifted (quadratic) representations. The method keeps actions lifted but grounds only state predicates using Partially Lifted Mutex Groups (PLMGs), yielding formulas whose size scales linearly with plan length. Empirically, the best variant outperforms the state-of-the-art lifted planner LiSAT in length-optimal planning for several IPC domains, achieving higher coverage and solving more instances. The encoding is competitive in satisficing planning, especially in domains where grounding is costly.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is to encode planning problems into SAT by lifting actions (avoiding quadratic blowup) while partially grounding predicates via PLMGs. PLMGs are groups of mutually exclusive facts that can be grounded without reintroducing full state-space explosion. Three encodings are presented: one with binary object encoding, one with predicate pruning (removing predicates not in preconditions), and a hybrid. The formulas grow linearly with plan length rather than quadratically, and the encodings are designed for sequential plans but could be extended to support parallelism and richer PDDL features.

## Key Results
- Best encoding (binary object encoding with predicate pruning) outperforms LiSAT in 5 of 9 benchmark domains for length-optimal planning
- Coverage improves by ≥20% in Logistics, Pipesworld, and Rover domains
- Formula size (variables and clauses) scales linearly with plan length, verified empirically
- In satisficing planning, performance is competitive, particularly in hard-to-ground domains

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by selectively grounding only the predicates that participate in mutex constraints (PLMGs), thus avoiding the exponential blowup of full grounding while keeping the formula smaller than a fully lifted encoding. Actions remain lifted, so their representation does not grow with plan length. The PLMG selection greedily maximizes the number of grounded facts, and predicate pruning further reduces formula size by removing irrelevant predicates. This targeted grounding preserves enough structure for the SAT solver to reason efficiently, while the linear scaling enables solving longer plans.

## Foundational Learning
- **Partially Lifted Mutex Groups (PLMGs)**: Groups of mutually exclusive facts that can be grounded without reintroducing full state-space explosion. Needed to enable selective grounding while controlling formula size. Quick check: Verify that PLMGs cover a maximal set of mutually exclusive facts without overlap.
- **Binary Object Encoding**: Encodes object identities as binary variables to reduce the number of ground facts. Needed to further compress the formula. Quick check: Ensure binary encoding preserves the ability to represent all necessary state transitions.
- **Predicate Pruning**: Removes predicates not appearing in any precondition, reducing formula size. Needed to eliminate irrelevant variables. Quick check: Confirm pruned predicates do not affect goal achievement or causal dependencies.
- **Sequential Plan Assumption**: Encodings assume actions are executed one at a time. Needed to simplify transition constraints. Quick check: Validate that no two actions in the same timestep interfere in the domain.
- **Linear Scaling Verification**: Empirical validation that formula size grows linearly with plan length. Needed to confirm theoretical advantage over lifted encodings. Quick check: Plot formula size vs. plan length on benchmark instances.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Actions (lifted) -> PLMGs (grounded predicates) -> Binary encoding & pruning -> SAT formula -> Solver
- **Critical Path**: PLMG computation → Encoding generation → SAT solving → Plan extraction
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Partial grounding vs. formula size (grounding reduces variables but may miss some states); lifted actions vs. action schema replication (lifting saves space but may lose context)
- **Failure Signatures**: Formula too large (if PLMGs miss mutexes); unsolvable (if pruning removes needed predicates); solver timeout (if formula too complex despite pruning)
- **First Experiments**: 1) Run encoding on a small domain, verify linear scaling; 2) Compare coverage with and without predicate pruning; 3) Test effect of alternative PLMG selection heuristics on formula size

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can action parallelism be integrated into the partially grounded encodings to improve scalability, especially in satisficing planning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "While our encodings target sequential plans, future work could explore integrating action parallelism. This may improve scalability and broaden the applicability of partially grounded encodings, especially in satisficing settings."
- Why unresolved: The current encodings assume sequential action execution. Parallelism could reduce plan length and formula size but requires modifying transition constraints to allow multiple concurrent actions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement an encoding supporting parallel actions (e.g., by quantifying over action sets per timestep), then empirically evaluate coverage and runtime on satisficing benchmarks, comparing against sequential versions and state-of-the-art lifted planners.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the encodings be extended to support negative preconditions and conditional effects without losing linear scaling?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "The encodings may also be extended to support negative preconditions and conditional effects."
- Why unresolved: The current STRIPS-based encoding assumes only positive preconditions and unconditional effects. Negative preconditions and conditional effects introduce additional logical constraints (e.g., universal quantification or nested implications) that may disrupt linear growth.
- What evidence would resolve it: Modify the SAT encoding to incorporate these features (e.g., using additional variables for conditional effects), then analyze formula size growth theoretically and test on domains requiring these constructs (e.g., those with PDDL :negative-preconditions or :conditional-effects).

### Open Question 3
- Question: Under what conditions does predicate pruning—removing predicates not appearing in any precondition—risk eliminating all optimal plans?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Predicate pruning removes entire predicates not in preconditions, but the paper notes it may over-delete facts that appear only in the goal. The authors do not analyze when this compromises optimality.
- Why unresolved: Pruning is applied heuristically; if a goal-only predicate is part of a mutex group that influences state transitions (e.g., via invariants), its removal could make some goals unsatisfiable or force suboptimal detours.
- What evidence would resolve it: Identify domains where goal-only predicates interact with PLMGs (e.g., via exactly-one mutexes). Compare optimal plan coverage and lengths with and without pruning, and develop a theoretical criterion (e.g., based on dependency graphs) for when pruning is safe.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Does the greedy selection of PLMGs (maximizing covered facts) always yield the most compact encoding, or could domain-specific PLMG selection improve performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses Helmert’s greedy PLMG selection, stating non-greedy alternatives usually have negligible or negative impact. However, this is based on standard benchmarks; domain structures might benefit from alternative strategies.
- Why unresolved: Greedy selection is myopic; in some domains, selecting PLMGs that cover fewer facts but reduce causal constraints (e.g., by breaking larger mutex groups) might yield simpler formulas or fewer variables.
- What evidence would resolve it: Design alternative PLMG selection heuristics (e.g., minimizing total counted variables, prioritizing predicates with high arity). Run experiments on diverse domains, measuring formula size (variables/clauses) and solver runtime, to see if any heuristic consistently outperforms greedy.

## Limitations
- Experimental evaluation covers only 9 IPC benchmark domains, limiting generalizability
- No formal proof of linear scaling; claims are empirically supported but not rigorously bounded
- Overhead of computing PLMGs is not quantified or reported
- No scalability analysis beyond tested instance sizes
- Effectiveness for non-temporal domains or domains with different structural properties is unclear

## Confidence
- **High**: Theoretical framework and encoding design are sound and well-justified
- **Medium**: Empirical performance claims are supported by benchmarks but lack statistical testing and broader domain coverage
- **Low**: Claims about scalability and overhead costs are not formally proven or fully quantified
