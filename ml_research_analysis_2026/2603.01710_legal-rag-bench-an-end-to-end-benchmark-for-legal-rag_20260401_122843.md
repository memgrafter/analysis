# Legal RAG Bench: an end-to-end benchmark for legal RAG
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01710
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01710
- **Reference Count:** 12
- **Primary Result:** A domain-specific embedding model (Kanon 2) is the primary driver of legal RAG performance, improving correctness by 17.5 points and retrieval accuracy by 34 points over generalist models; most hallucinations are triggered by upstream retrieval failures.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Legal RAG Bench, the first robust end-to-end benchmark for evaluating legal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems. It addresses a critical gap by providing a curated dataset of 4,876 legal passages and 100 complex, passage-specific questions with reference answers. The evaluation employs a full factorial experimental design and a hierarchical error decomposition taxonomy to isolate the contributions of the retrieval and generation components. The central finding is that the choice of embedding model dominates end-to-end performance, with a legal-specific embedder (Kanon 2) yielding massive gains. Furthermore, the analysis reveals that a significant portion of hallucinations are causally linked to retrieval failures, establishing retrieval quality as the primary ceiling on system capability.

## Method Summary
The authors construct Legal RAG Bench using passages from the Victorian Criminal Charge Book and hand-crafted legal questions requiring precise evidence. The core methodology is a full factorial experiment testing every combination of three embedding models (Kanon 2, Gemini Embedding 001, Text Embedding 3 Large) and two frontier LLMs (Gemini 3.1 Pro, GPT-5.2). A barebones LangChain pipeline with default hyperparameters processes all combinations. End-to-end performance is measured via two key metrics: **correctness** (answer agreement with a gold reference) and **groundedness** (answer support in retrieved passages). A hierarchical error taxonomy classifies failures into **Hallucination** (ungrounded answer), **Retrieval Error** (relevant passage not retrieved), or **Reasoning Error** (relevant passage retrieved but answer still incorrect). Statistical analysis uses a linear probability model with question fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors to identify main effects and interactions.

## Key Results
*   The embedding model is the primary performance driver; the domain-specific Kanon 2 Embedder improved average correctness by 17.5 points, groundedness by 4.5 points, and retrieval accuracy by 34 points over generalist models.
*   Most hallucinations (ungrounded answers) are triggered by upstream retrieval failures; improving retrieval with Kanon 2 reduced the hallucination rate by 6.75 points.
*   A statistically significant **LLM × Embedder interaction** exists for groundedness, meaning an LLM's tendency to stay grounded depends critically on retriever quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Embedding Model Dominance
*   **Claim:** The choice of embedding model is the primary driver of end-to-end legal RAG performance, dominating the impact of the generative LLM.
*   **Mechanism:** Domain-specific embedding models (e.g., Kanon 2) retrieve more legally relevant passages. This higher retrieval accuracy provides the LLM with a better contextual foundation, directly increasing the probability of a correct and grounded answer. The LLM's role becomes primarily synthesis rather than compensation for poor retrieval.
*   **Core Assumption:** A retrieved relevant passage is a necessary (though not sufficient) condition for a correct, grounded answer. If relevant evidence is not retrieved, the LLM's reasoning capacity cannot salvage the outcome.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract and Section 5.1 state embedding choice dominates performance, citing large delta improvements with Kanon 2. The experimental data is primary evidence.

### Mechanism 2: Retrieval-Triggered Hallucinations
*   **Claim:** A significant portion of observed "hallucinations" are causally triggered by upstream retrieval failures, not intrinsic LLM fabrication.
*   **Mechanism:** When the retriever fails (`r_ei = 0`), the LLM must generate from parametric knowledge or irrelevant context. Even if it attempts to stay grounded to the provided irrelevant context (`g_ei = 1`), the answer will be incorrect (`c_ei = 0`). This manifests as a "Retrieval error," not a pure "Hallucination." Pure hallucinations (`g_ei = 0`) may still occur but are reduced when retrieval provides relevant material.
*   **Core Assumption:** LLMs have limited capacity to detect context relevance and will preferentially invent facts when context is unhelpful.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Section 4 and 5.2, particularly Figure 2, showing error distribution shifts from "Retrieval errors" to "Reasoning errors" with better retrievers. Kanon 2's 6.75-point reduction in hallucinations.

### Mechanism 3: LLM-Retriever Interaction
*   **Claim:** The relationship between embedding model and LLM performance is not purely additive; specific combinations exhibit interaction effects, particularly for groundedness.
*   **Mechanism:** An LLM's groundedness tendency depends on context quality. A high-quality retriever provides clearly relevant context, reducing hallucinations even in a more creative LLM. Conversely, a poor retriever's noisy context may trigger hallucinations even in a conservative LLM. This creates non-linear effects where the "best" LLM depends on the retriever.
*   **Core Assumption:** LLMs modulate generation strategy (e.g., confidence, reliance on context) based on implicit cues about context quality.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Section 5.3, Table 3 (significant interaction for Groundedness, χ²(2)=8.12, p=0.017) and Table 4 (switching LLMs harms groundedness with weak embedders but helps/neutral with Kanon 2).

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Full Factorial Experimental Design**
    *   **Why needed here:** Essential to disentangle main effects (performance of a component averaged across others) from interaction effects (performance depends on specific pairings). Without testing every combination, one cannot claim an embedding model is "better" independently of the LLM.
    *   **Quick Check:** If Retriever A+LLM X outperforms Retriever B+LLM Y, what two alternative explanations exist that a full factorial design would distinguish?
*   **Concept: Hierarchical Error Decomposition (RAG Taxonomy)**
    *   **Why needed here:** Standard accuracy masks the source of failure. This taxonomy (Hallucination, Retrieval Error, Reasoning Error) forces causal attribution of an incorrect answer to either the retriever or generator, enabling targeted improvement.
    *   **Quick Check:** For a RAG answer that is **ungrounded and incorrect**, which error type(s) are definitively ruled out? Which could be the cause?
*   **Concept: Domain-Adapted Embedding Models**
    *   **Why needed here:** The finding that a legal-specific embedder massively outperforms generalists highlights that semantic similarity in specialized domains (law) requires models trained on that domain's unique text distribution.
    *   **Quick Check:** What is a risk of using a generalist embedding model on a highly specialized legal corpus, even if the corpus is in English?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Corpus → Retriever → Generator → Evaluator → Error Decomposer
    Corpus: 4,872 semantically chunked passages. Retriever: Embedding model + vector search, outputs Top-K passages. Generator: Frontier LLM, inputs question + passages, outputs answer. Evaluator: GPT-5.2 "high reasoning mode" judge, scores correctness and groundedness. Error Decomposer: Logic module using retrieval accuracy flags to classify failure type.
*   **Critical path:**
    Question → Embedding → Vector Search → Retrieved Passages → LLM Prompt → Answer → Judge Scoring → Error Classification. Retrieval step is the performance bottleneck for both latency and correctness.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Chunking: Semantic (`semchunk`) vs. fixed-size. Larger chunks preserve context but increase noise; smaller chunks may break concepts.
    *   Retrieval k-value: More passages increase gold passage recall but add noise and context load.
    *   Judge Model: Frontier model (GPT-5.2) is expensive but claimed reliable; cheaper domain-tuned model is a trade-off.
    *   Evaluation Scope: End-to-end metrics vs. isolated retrieval metrics (e.g., MRR); paper argues end-to-end is what matters for builders.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   High Retrieval Error rate + High Incorrect/Groundless: Indicates embedder failure; improve embedding model or corpus preprocessing.
    *   High Reasoning Error rate (with good retrieval): Indicates LLM failure on reasoning from provided text; improve LLM, prompt, or context.
    *   High Hallucination rate with poor retrieval: Classic "garbage in, garbage out"; fix retrieval first.
    *   High Hallucination rate with good retrieval: Suggests LLM ignoring context; needs prompt engineering or a less creative LLM.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Baseline Retrieval Sweep:** Fix one LLM (e.g., Gemini 3.1 Pro), run all three embedders. Isolates the **embedder main effect** on retrieval, correctness, groundedness.
    2.  **Baseline Generator Sweep:** Fix one embedder (e.g., Kanon 2), run both LLMs. Isolates the **LLM main effect** on correctness/groundedness (retrieval accuracy should be constant).
    3.  **Full Factorial Run & Interaction Test:** Run all 6 combinations. Fit the linear probability model. Test for significant interaction term (`δ_el`), especially for Groundedness. Confirms whether LLM performance is embedder-dependent.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Generalizability Across Domains:** Does embedding dominance generalize to legal domains beyond Victorian criminal law (e.g., contracts, civil procedure, other jurisdictions)?
*   **Causal Mechanism for Reduced Hallucinations:** What is the precise causal mechanism by which improved retrieval quality reduces hallucinations? The paper shows correlation but not the LLM's internal decision process.
*   **LLM Portfolio Effects:** How does including a broader range of frontier LLMs (e.g., Claude, Llama) affect the observed main effects and interactions? Current conclusions are based on only two LLMs.
*   **Hyperparameter Confounding:** To what extent do default hyperparameter choices (chunk size, retrieval *k*, LLM settings) confound the measured main effects? Fixed defaults isolate components but may obscure optimal configuration shifts.

## Limitations
*   **Hyperparameter Opacity:** Exact Langchain defaults (retrieval *k*, chunk size, LLM sampling) are unreported, risking metric divergence in reproduction.
*   **Judge Implementation:** The GPT-5.2 "high reasoning mode" and precise evaluation rubric are unspecified; the claimed 99% judge accuracy is unverified.
*   **Chunking Nuance:** The `semchunk` algorithm and Kanon legal tokenizer specifics are not detailed, making corpus preprocessing a key unknown.
*   **Interaction Generalizability:** The significant LLM×Embedder interaction is based on only two LLMs; its validity across other model families is uncertain.

## Confidence
| Claim Cluster | Confidence | Basis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Embedding Model Dominance** | High | Supported by large, consistent effect sizes (e.g., +34 pts retrieval accuracy) across metrics; only hyperparameter tweaks might slightly modulate magnitude. |
| **Retrieval-Triggered Hallucinations** | Medium | Error decomposition logic is sound and data-backed (Figure 2), but depends on correct `r_ei`/`g_ei` labeling and judge consistency. |
| **LLM-Retriever Interaction Effects** | Medium | Statistically significant interaction for groundedness is reported (p=0.017), but effect size/direction is model-pair specific; replication with more LLMs is needed. |

## Next Checks
1.  **Replicate Baseline Retrieval Sweep:** Run the benchmark with a single LLM (Gemini 3.1 Pro) across all three embedders. Confirm the claimed main effect magnitudes for retrieval accuracy, correctness, and groundedness.
2.  **Validate Judge Implementation:** Obtain or reconstruct the exact GPT-5.2 judge prompt and "high reasoning mode" settings. Measure inter-annotator agreement against a gold-labeled subset to verify the 99% accuracy claim.
3.  **Test Chunking Sensitivity:** Re-process the corpus using alternative chunking strategies (e.g., fixed-size with legal tokenizer, different `semchunk` parameters). Re-run a minimal factorial (Kanon 2 + GPT-5.2) to measure the stability of end-to-end metrics.
