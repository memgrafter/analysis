# GeoPT: Scaling Physics Simulation via Lifted Geometric Pre-Training
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.20399
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20399
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GeoPT reduces labeled physics data needs by 20–60% and speeds convergence by up to 2× via dynamics-lifted geometric pre-training.

## Executive Summary
Neural simulators require large amounts of labeled physics data, which is expensive to generate. Pre-training on static geometry alone fails because it ignores dynamics. GeoPT introduces a dynamics-lifted geometric pre-training approach: it augments geometry with synthetic velocity fields and uses transport trajectories as self-supervision, creating a dynamics-aware prior without needing physics labels. Pre-trained on 1.3 million samples from diverse geometries, GeoPT fine-tuned on industrial benchmarks (aerodynamics, hydrodynamics, crash simulation) consistently reduces labeled data requirements by 20–60% and speeds convergence by up to 2×, while also generalizing to radiosity simulation. This bridges the gap between geometry and physics, enabling scalable neural simulation.

## Method Summary
GeoPT employs a two-stage training paradigm. In pre-training, a Transformer-based neural operator (Transolver) learns to predict geometric features (vector distance) along synthetic transport trajectories. For each 3D geometry, the method samples numerous random velocity fields and computes particle trajectories that advect along the velocity but stick to boundaries, generating a sequence of geometric feature targets. This lifted supervision forces the model to internalize how geometry and dynamics couple. For fine-tuning, the random velocities are replaced with task-specific velocity fields `V_S` that encode simulation settings (e.g., flow direction for aerodynamics, impact direction for crash). The same model is then fine-tuned with labeled physics data, requiring far fewer samples than training from scratch. Pre-training data comprises 1.3 million samples from 13,000 ShapeNet geometries (cars, airplanes, watercraft), using 100 synthetic velocities per geometry and 2-step trajectories.

## Key Results
- GeoPT reduces labeled data needs by 20–60% across four industrial benchmarks (DrivAerML, NASA-CRM, HullBot, Car-Crash) compared to training from scratch.
- Convergence speed improves by up to 2× during fine-tuning.
- The approach generalizes beyond fluid simulation to radiosity (light transport) and multi-physics (crash) tasks.
- Native geometric pre-training (static) fails to provide benefits, often underperforming scratch training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** Native geometric pre-training fails because static supervision (e.g., predicting SDF or vector distance) teaches representations that aggregate points based on static shape cues (e.g., left/right symmetry). This misaligns with physics tasks where aggregation must reflect dynamic correlations induced by velocity fields and boundary interactions (e.g., front-back asymmetry in aerodynamics). Pre-trained models thus remain "stuck" in static geometry space and cannot adapt to the higher-dimensional geometry-dynamics coupling.

**Mechanism 2:** Lifting the pre-training space by augmenting geometry with synthetic velocity fields and supervising on transport trajectories creates a dynamics-aware prior. For each geometry, uniformly sampled per-point velocities generate particle trajectories that stick to boundaries. The model predicts geometric features along these trajectories, forcing it to learn how boundaries modulate dynamics and how distant points become coupled through shared trajectories. The transport equation’s mass-conservation structure provides a universal inductive bias that matches many downstream physics (e.g., advection-dominated flows).

**Mechanism 3:** The lifted pre-training yields a unified interface where fine-tuning simply replaces the random velocities `V` with task-specific velocities `V_S` derived from simulation settings `S`. The pre-trained model already encodes the mapping from `(G, V)` to feature trajectories. Conditioning on a specific `V_S` prompts the model to align its internal correlation patterns with the task’s dynamics, enabling rapid adaptation with minimal labeled data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Transport Equation (Advection with Boundaries):** Needed to understand the synthetic dynamics (dx/dt = v * 1_G), which models particle advection with sticking at boundaries. Quick check: Given G and v, what is the trajectory of a particle starting on the surface? (It stays fixed: x(t)=x₀.)
- **Neural Operator (Function-Space Learning):** GeoPT uses a Transformer-based operator (Transolver) to map discretized mesh points and conditions to field values, generalizing across geometries. Quick check: Why is a Transformer suitable for irregular meshes? (Attention treats mesh points as tokens, independent of grid structure.)
- **Self-Supervised Pre-Training Paradigm:** GeoPT’s lifted objective predicts features along synthetic trajectories, unlike native SSL which reconstructs within the input space. Quick check: What distinguishes lifted from native pre-training? (Lifted predicts `h_G(xt)` along a trajectory; native predicts `h_G(x)` at a static point.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Geometry Encoder + Dynamics Condition Input -> Backbone (Transolver) -> Trajectory Prediction Head

**Critical path:**
1. Pre-training data generation: For each geometry G, sample N tracking points and N_dyn velocity fields; compute 2-step vector distance trajectories (h_G at each step).
2. Model forward: Input (x, G, V) → Transolver → predict trajectory features F_θ(x; G, V).
3. Pre-training loss: L2 between predicted and target trajectories.
4. Fine-tuning: Replace V with task-specific V_S; loss becomes L2 between predicted and physics fields u(x).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Velocity sampling distribution: Uniform ball (radius v_max) offers diversity but may not match downstream V_S distributions; tradeoff between generality and domain alignment.
- Trajectory steps τ: More steps capture longer-range coupling but increase discretization error and supervision length; paper finds τ=2 optimal.
- Geometry feature h_G: Vector distance outperforms SDF but is computationally heavier (ray-triangle intersections).

**Failure signatures:**
- Pre-training: Test MSE on leave-out geometries fails to decrease → check trajectory boundary sticking, velocity diversity, or data bugs.
- Fine-tuning: No improvement over scratch → verify V_S configuration (norm/direction) and pre-training diversity; sweep velocity parameters.
- Inference: Out-of-memory on large meshes → use subset inference and adjust batch size.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline replication:** Train Transolver from scratch on a small downstream task (e.g., 20 DrivAerML samples). Expect relative L2 ~0.37 at 200 epochs.
2. **Native pre-tuning test:** Pre-train with static geometry supervision, then fine-tune. Expect worse than scratch (e.g., 0.20 vs 0.093 with 100 samples).
3. **GeoPT validation:** Pre-train with lifted supervision (τ=2, 100 velocities/geometry), fine-tune with configured V_S (e.g., norm 0.3 for DrivAerML). Expect ~20–40% data reduction (e.g., 40 samples match scratch 100).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scaling laws:** How does GeoPT’s performance evolve with larger pre-training datasets (10M, 100M geometries) and larger models (100M, 1B parameters)?
2. **Generality to other PDEs:** Does GeoPT transfer to physical systems dominated by PDEs other than transport, such as Schrödinger or Maxwell equations?
3. **Multi-material representation:** Can a single per-point velocity parameterization adequately represent multiple material properties in heterogeneous simulations without collapse?
4. **Free-flight trajectories:** Would incorporating trajectories without boundary sticking enable GeoPT to improve regular-grid turbulence simulation?

## Limitations
- Native pre-training failure may only apply to tasks with strong dynamic coupling; weakly dynamic physics (e.g., static elasticity) might still benefit.
- Synthetic uniform velocity distribution may not match downstream distributions, risking reduced pre-training efficacy.
- Pre-training dataset restricted to ShapeNet car/airplane/watercraft; generalization to other geometry families (e.g., buildings, medical models) remains untested.

## Confidence
- **Native geometric pre-training fails because it ignores dynamics.** Confidence: High (empirical underperformance of native pre‑training; visual evidence in Fig. 3a).
- **Lifted pre‑training with synthetic velocities yields a dynamics‑aware prior.** Confidence: High (consistent 20–60% data reduction and up to 2× speed‑up across four industrial benchmarks).
- **Unified interface via task‑specific V_S enables simple fine‑tuning.** Confidence: Medium (validated on advection‑dominated tasks; extension to multi‑phase flow or pressure‑driven systems is uncertain).

## Next Checks
1. **Nonlinear dynamics stress test:** Apply GeoPT to turbulent fluid simulations (high Reynolds number). A substantial drop in data‑efficiency gains would reveal limitations of linear advection pre‑training.
2. **Velocity distribution ablation:** Pre‑train with task‑aligned velocity distributions (e.g., norms derived from simulated flight speeds) versus uniform; compare downstream sample complexity to quantify distributional robustness.
3. **Cross‑category generalization:** Pre‑train on non‑ShapeNet geometries (e.g., S3DIS indoor scenes, ABC machinery parts) and fine‑tune on DrivAerML. Performance degradation would measure the geometry coverage needed for effective transfer.
