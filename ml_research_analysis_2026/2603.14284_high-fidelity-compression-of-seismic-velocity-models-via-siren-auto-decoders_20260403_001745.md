# High-Fidelity Compression of Seismic Velocity Models via SIREN Auto-Decoders

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.14284
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14284
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Compresses 70×70 seismic velocity maps (4,900 points) into 256-dimensional latent vectors at 19:1 ratio with average PSNR 32.47 dB and SSIM 0.956.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a SIREN auto-decoder framework for compressing seismic velocity models. The method represents each velocity map with a compact 256-dimensional latent vector and a shared SIREN decoder, achieving a 19:1 compression ratio while preserving high fidelity (average PSNR 32.47 dB, SSIM 0.956). The implicit neural representation enables zero-shot super-resolution up to 280×280 and smooth latent space interpolation, providing resolution-independent analysis capabilities for seismic data.

## Method Summary
The proposed auto-decoder architecture employs a shared SIREN (Sinusoidal Representation Networks) decoder that maps normalized coordinates and a per-sample latent code to velocity values. Each training sample is associated with an optimizable latent vector of dimension 256. The network consists of four hidden layers with 512 units each, using sinusoidal activations with ω₀=30. Training minimizes a combined loss: mean squared error (MSE) between predicted and ground truth velocities, plus an L₂ regularization term (λ=10⁻⁴) on latent codes to encourage a smooth latent manifold. Optimization uses Adam with a ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler. After convergence, only the decoder parameters (≈1.05M) and the latent codes need storage per sample, yielding significant compression. The continuous nature of the implicit representation allows querying at arbitrary resolutions without retraining, facilitating super-resolution and interpolation.

## Key Results
- Achieves 19:1 compression ratio on 1,000 seismic velocity models from five geological families.
- Reconstruction quality: average PSNR 32.47 dB, SSIM 0.956.
- Enables zero-shot super-resolution up to 280×280 and smooth latent space interpolation, maintaining plausible intermediate structures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Spectral bias mitigation via SIREN
SIREN's periodic sinusoidal activations enable the network to capture high-frequency details such as sharp geological interfaces and faults, which standard ReLU MLPs often smooth due to spectral bias. The initialization and activation function analytically support representation of high-bandwidth signals, making it suitable for seismic velocity fields with discontinuities.

### Mechanism 2: Auto-decoder compression through shared decoder and latent codes
A single SIREN decoder is shared across all training samples, while each velocity model is represented by a separate latent vector. During training, both decoder parameters and latent codes are optimized jointly. This separation exploits the low-dimensional manifold structure of the dataset, allowing compact storage: only the decoder (1.05M parameters) and a 256-dimensional latent code per sample are needed, achieving 19:1 compression without sacrificing fidelity.

### Mechanism 3: L² regularization promotes smooth latent manifold
Adding λ∥z_i∥²₂ (λ=10⁻⁴) to the loss encourages latent codes to have small, similar norms. This regularization organizes the latent space into a smooth, continuous manifold where linear interpolations between codes produce physically plausible intermediate velocity models. It also prevents overfitting and supports meaningful generative operations like interpolation.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
- **Coordinate grid generator**: Produces normalized (x,z) coordinate pairs for each 70×70 sample.
- **Latent code bank**: Stores a 256-dimensional latent vector z_i for each training sample; initialized randomly and optimized end-to-end.
- **SIREN decoder**: A 4-hidden-layer MLP (512 units per layer) with sin(ω₀·) activations (ω₀=30); takes concatenated (x,z,z_i) and outputs velocity.
- **Loss function**: MSE(V_i, f_θ(x,z,z_i)) + λ∥z_i∥², with λ=10⁻⁴.
- **Optimizer**: Adam (β₁=0.9, β₂=0.999) with ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler.
- **Evaluation metrics**: PSNR and SSIM computed on denormalized velocity fields.

### Critical path
1. Load and normalize OpenFWI velocity maps; precompute coordinate grid for 70×70 resolution.
2. Initialize latent codes from N(0,0.01²) and SIREN decoder with specialized weight initialization.
3. Implement training loop: for each batch, expand latents and coordinates, forward through decoder, compute loss, backpropagate.
4. Periodically evaluate on validation set (using fixed latent codes) to monitor PSNR/SSIM.
5. After convergence, store decoder parameters and optimized latent codes; inference simply queries decoder at desired coordinates.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Latent dimension (d=256)**: Balances compression (19:1) and quality; smaller d degrades PSNR, larger d reduces compression.
- **SIREN vs. Fourier features**: SIREN chosen for differentiability and high-frequency capture; Fourier features might simplify but could impair derivative preservation.
- **Regularization (λ=1e-4)**: Empirically chosen; larger values over-smooth latent space, smaller risk overfitting and rough interpolation.
- **Network size (512 units, 4 layers)**: Sufficient for 70×70; larger networks may improve quality but increase memory and overfitting risk.
- **Training epochs (3000)**: Adequate convergence; fewer underfit, more yield diminishing returns.

### Failure signatures
- Loss plateaus at high value: check learning rate, weight initialization, or model capacity.
- Validation PSNR much lower than training: overfitting; increase λ, add dropout, or collect more data.
- Interpolation produces jumps or artifacts: latent space not smooth; increase λ or verify decoder capacity.
- Super-resolution shows ringing: ω₀ may be too high for fine details; consider adjusting frequency or multi-scale encoding.
- Out-of-memory errors: reduce batch size or use mixed precision; memory scales with hidden units.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Single-sample overfitting**: Train on one FlatVel model until PSNR > 40 dB to confirm decoder can exactly represent a sample and pipeline works.
2. **SIREN vs. ReLU baseline**: Replace SIREN with ReLU MLP of same size, train on same subset; expect lower PSNR on faulted models, demonstrating spectral bias mitigation.
3. **Latent space interpolation**: Choose two models from different families (e.g., FlatFault and CurveFault), interpolate at α={0.25,0.5,0.75}, and visualize; check for plausible intermediate structures and absence of catastrophic artifacts.

## Limitations
- Dataset size and diversity are limited (1,000 models from five families); performance degrades when scaling to 40,000 models.
- Super-resolution evaluation lacks high-resolution ground truth; results rely on visual plausibility only.
- Interpolation lacks quantitative geological validation; assessment is qualitative.

## Confidence
- **SIREN captures high-frequency details**: Medium (theoretical support but limited ablation).
- **Auto-decoder achieves compression**: Medium (validated on 1k, scalability uncertain).
- **L² regularization yields smooth latent space**: Medium (interpolation looks smooth but unquantified).
- **High-fidelity reconstruction (PSNR/SSIM)**: High (concrete metrics on test set).
- **Zero-shot super-resolution**: Low (no ground truth evaluation).
