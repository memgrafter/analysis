# Bias Detection and Rotation-Robustness Mitigation in Vision-Language Models and Generative Image Models

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.08860
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08860
*   **Reference count:** 0
*   **Primary result:** Lightweight LoRA fine-tuning on 24 rotation-augmented samples achieved 100% rotation invariance in Llava-1.5-7B, eliminating observed semantic drift and demographic bias without performance loss.

## Executive Summary
This thesis investigates severe biases and instabilities in vision-language models (VLMs) and generative models when faced with simple geometric rotations. It introduces systematic rotation testing and a structured qualitative annotation scheme to expose failures like human-to-animal misclassification and demographic drift. The core contribution is a highly efficient mitigation strategy: fine-tuning a VLM with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) on a tiny dataset of just 24 rotated images. This intervention successfully enforced rotation invariance and eliminated all documented failure modes in the Llava-1.5-7B model. Complementary analyses reveal catastrophic vulnerabilities in other systems, including a 98.48% accuracy drop for a ResNet-50 under adversarial attack and profound semantic degradation in Gemma-3. The work demonstrates that targeted, low-cost interventions can correct orientation-driven bias, though significant open questions about generalizability and root causes remain.

## Method Summary
The detection methodology involves a systematic rotation probe: a set of base images is rotated through cardinal angles (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) and fed to a VLM with a fixed prompt. Human annotators then apply a four-label scheme (Identity, Demographic, Scene Stability, Bias Flag) to quantify semantic drift and harmful outputs. For mitigation, a lightweight LoRA fine-tuning protocol is employed. A minimal training set is constructed from 4 base images, each augmented with multiple rotations (totaling 24 samples). The target text for each base image is held constant regardless of rotation. LoRA adapters (rank 8, alpha 16) are applied to the `q_proj` and `v_proj` layers of the LLM backbone (Vicuna-7B in LLaVA). After brief training (3 epochs), the fine-tuned model is re-evaluated on all rotations to confirm consistent, bias-free descriptions.

## Key Results
*   **Mitigation Success:** The LoRA-adapted Llava-1.5-7B model achieved 100% rotation invariance, producing identical descriptions across all tested angles and completely eliminating failure modes such as human-to-animal misclassification and demographic drift.
*   **Severe Vulnerabilities:** A ResNet-50 ethnicity classifier's accuracy plummeted by 98.48% under a PGD attack, with the underrepresented "Other" class showing only 13.61% recall. The Gemma-3 VLM exhibited catastrophic semantic drift and dehumanization (e.g., labeling humans as animals) at 90-degree rotations.
*   **Efficiency:** The successful intervention required only 24 training samples and minimal parameter updates, proving that targeted fine-tuning can resolve orientation-induced bias without sacrificing general performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Systematic rotation perturbations reveal hidden semantic instability and demographic bias in VLMs not apparent from standard evaluation.
*   **Mechanism:** Geometric rotation forces the vision encoder to process identical pixel semantics under altered spatial configurations. Divergent textual outputs across angles indicate a lack of rotation-equivariance in the model's internal representations, exposing fragile grounding between visual features and semantic concepts. Drift manifests as identity substitution, attribute reassignment, or scene hallucination.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes "catastrophic semantic drift and human dehumanization at 90-degree rotations" for Gemma-3. Section 5.4 provides specific cases (e.g., human→"proboscis monkey"). The approach is novel; neighboring work focuses on text-image bias, not geometric robustness.
*   **Break condition:** If a model is inherently rotation-equivariant (via augmentation/architecture), the probe yields no drift, potentially missing other bias types. It tests spatial consistency, not fixed-orientation fairness.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Targeted LoRA fine-tuning on a tiny set of rotation-augmented examples can enforce rotation invariance and eliminate observed semantic drift without degrading performance.
*   **Mechanism:** Low-Rank Adaptation injects trainable low-rank matrices (`ΔW ≈ BA`) into attention layers (`q_proj`, `v_proj`). Training on just 24 samples (4 images × 6 rotations) provides a strong signal that textual output must be rotation-invariant. This "nudges" the vision encoder's feature space toward equivariance and aligns the LLM decoder to ignore orientation cues.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states the adapted model achieved "100% rotation invariance" and "completely eliminated observed failure modes." Section 7.4 confirms identical/near-identical descriptions and eliminated demographic drift post-tuning. This is an original empirical finding.
*   **Break condition:** Fails if the root cause is missing fundamental knowledge rather than misalignment. May not generalize to other transforms (scaling, occlusion) or to VLMs with frozen vision encoders incompatible with LoRA.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** A structured qualitative annotation scheme (Identity Stability, Demographic Stability, Scene Stability, Bias Flag) effectively quantifies semantic degradation.
*   **Mechanism:** Human annotators rate each output across four binary dimensions, converting abstract "drift" into measurable failure rates. A high `BIAS_flag` incidence correlates with unsafe outputs (dehumanization, hallucination). The scheme isolates orientation-sensitivity from baseline error by separating stability (did meaning change?) from correctness (is the meaning accurate?).
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "qualitative annotation across identity, demographic, and scene stability." Section 5.2.4 defines the four-label scheme. Section 5.2.5 applies it to show `BIAS_flag=1` for catastrophic cases.
*   **Break condition:** Labor-intensive and subjective; inter-annotator agreement not reported. May miss subtle drift. Automated proxies (e.g., CLIP-similarity) could be faster but less aligned with human safety judgments.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Vision-Language Model Architecture (e.g., LLaVA)**
    *   **Why needed:** Understanding the pipeline (ViT encoder → projector → LLM decoder) is essential to diagnose where rotation noise propagates and where to apply interventions like LoRA.
    *   **Quick check:** If an image is rotated, at what stage (vision encoder feature extraction or LLM textual generation) does the semantic drift originate, and how could you isolate this?
*   **Concept: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**
    *   **Why needed:** The core mitigation uses LoRA for efficient fine-tuning. Knowing that LoRA approximates weight updates as low-rank matrices (`ΔW ≈ BA`) explains why rank-8 adapters on attention projections can modify behavior with minimal parameters.
    *   **Quick check:** Why apply LoRA to `q_proj` and `v_proj` layers specifically in a VLM? What role do these projections play in fusing visual and linguistic information?
*   **Concept: Adversarial and Geometric Robustness Evaluation**
    *   **Why needed:** The paper frames rotation as a non-adversarial geometric robustness test. Understanding FGSM/PGD contrasts *optimized* noise attacks with *structured* geometric transforms, showing bias can emerge from simple, natural corruptions.
    *   **Quick check:** How does the information loss in a 90° rotation (disorienting perspective) differ in mechanism from an FGSM ε=0.1 perturbation (pixel-level noise), and why might both trigger similar semantic drift in VLMs?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Input Image → CLIP ViT-L/336px (vision encoder) → Linear Projector → [IMG] tokens + Text Tokens → Vicuna-7B (LLM backbone with LoRA on q_proj/v_proj) → Generated Text.
*   **Critical path for rotation failure:** 1. Rotated image → ViT patches reordered → altered spatial attention patterns. 2. Altered patch embeddings → distorted multimodal token sequence via projector. 3. LLM's first self-attention mixes corrupted visual tokens with prompt → corrupted visual context. 4. Decoder generates text based on corrupted context → semantic drift (e.g., "human"→"monkey").
*   **Design tradeoffs:** 
    *   **LoRA rank (r=8):** Low rank = efficient but may underfit if major realignment is needed. Success suggests minor drift correction is feasible.
    *   **Data volume (24 samples):** Extremely small; trades off risk of memorizing specific appearances vs. learning a general invariance principle. Success implies the base model knows correct descriptions but relies on orientation shortcuts.
    *   **Adapter location:** Targeting `q_proj`/`v_proj` affects earliest attention fusion. Alternative: apply to projector or output embeddings.
*   **Failure signatures:** 
    *   **Pre-mitigation:** `BIAS_flag=1` cases (human→animal), `DEM_stable=0` with `ID_stable=1` (ethnicity drift), `SCENE_stable=0` (scene hallucination).
    *   **During fine-tuning:** Non-converging loss or dropping 0° accuracy indicates catastrophic interference or learning wrong invariance.
    *   **Deployment:** Invariance on cardinal rotations does not guarantee generalization to arbitrary angles (e.g., 30°, 135°).
*   **First 3 experiments:** 
    1. **Baseline rotation probe on Llava-1.5-7B:** Run 4 repeated images through 0°/90°/180°/270°, annotate with 4-label scheme. Expected: `BIAS_flag=1` for some angles.
    2. **Adversarial comparison on ResNet-50:** Train ResNet-50 on UTKFace, run FGSM/PGD (ε=0.1). Expected: >98% accuracy drop, especially "Other" class.
    3. **Rotation-augmented LoRA fine-tuning:** Take 4 images, generate 6 rotations each (24 pairs), fine-tune Llava-1.5-7B with LoRA (r=8, α=16) for 3 epochs. Re-run experiment (1). Expected: `BIAS_flag=0`, identical descriptions across rotations, no 0° accuracy loss.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   Can adversarial debiasing using a Gradient Reversal Layer effectively reduce demographic disparities and differential robustness in ResNet-50 ethnicity classification? (Listed as RQ2 but not experimentally addressed).
*   What underlying mechanisms cause Gemma-3 to exhibit catastrophic semantic drift under image rotation? (Assessment is observational; no analysis of internal representations or training data).
*   Does the rotation-augmented LoRA fine-tuning mitigation approach generalize to other VLMs beyond Llava-1.5-7B? (Only validated on one model; Gemma-3 was evaluated but not mitigated).
*   How can the severe underperformance on the “Other” ethnicity class in UTKFace (13.61% recall) be remedied without sacrificing performance on majority groups? (Dataset imbalance noted, but no balancing/augmentation strategies tested).
*   What debiasing techniques can effectively mitigate the systematic generative biases in Stable Diffusion (age inflation, ethnicity blending, distortions) identified in Chapter 6? (Bias documented but mitigation left as open problem).

## Limitations
*   **Data and prompt opacity:** Exact images, prompts, and rotation angles used are undisclosed, hindering replication.
*   **Annotation subjectivity:** The qualitative scheme lacks reported inter-annotator agreement; potential labeler bias not quantified.
*   **Generalizability concerns:** LoRA fine-tuning tested only on Llava-1.5-7B with cardinal rotations; unknown for other models, architectures, or geometric transforms.
*   **Overfitting risk:** Using only 24 samples may cause memorization of specific appearances rather than learning true rotation invariance.
