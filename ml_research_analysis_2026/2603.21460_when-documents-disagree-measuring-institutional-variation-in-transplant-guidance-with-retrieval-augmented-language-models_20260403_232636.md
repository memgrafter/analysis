# When Documents Disagree: Measuring Institutional Variation in Transplant Guidance with Retrieval-Augmented Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.21460
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21460
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** 20.8% of non-absent pairwise comparisons among 102 handbooks from 23 U.S. centers show clinically meaningful divergence, with severe coverage gaps (96.2% absence) especially in reproductive health.

## Executive Summary
This study introduces a framework to quantify heterogeneity in solid-organ transplant patient education handbooks across U.S. centers. Using retrieval-augmented language models (RAG), the authors generate center-specific answers to a benchmark of 1,115 patient questions from each handbook. Pairwise comparisons of these answers, classified by an LLM judge into five labels, reveal that 20.8% of non-absent comparisons are clinically divergent or contradictory. The variation is concentrated in monitoring & follow-up and lifestyle topics. Coverage gaps are severe, with 96.2% of question-handbook pairs marked as not addressed, particularly in reproductive health (95.1% absence). Center-level divergence profiles are stable, suggesting systematic institutional differences.

## Method Summary
The pipeline processes 102 PDF handbooks from 23 U.S. transplant centers. Each handbook is parsed (LlamaParse), chunked at section boundaries, and indexed with BM25 and dense vectors (BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5). For a standard set of 1,115 patient questions, the system retrieves the top-5 passages per handbook via hybrid retrieval (RRF fusion + cross-encoder rerank) and generates answers using Qwen3-14B, constrained to the context. Answers that lack relevant content are labeled `NOT ADDRESSED`. Non-absent answer pairs are compared by an LLM judge (Qwen3-14B) using a five-label taxonomy (ABSENT, CONSISTENT, COMPLEMENTARY, DIVERGENT, CONTRADICTORY). Aggregation metrics (divergence rate, consistency rate) are computed across questions, topics, organs, and centers.

## Key Results
- 20.8% of non-absent pairwise comparisons are **DIVERGENT** or **CONTRADICTORY**.
- Divergence is highest in **monitoring & follow-up** and **lifestyle** topics.
- **Coverage gaps** are severe: 96.2% of question-handbook pairs are `NOT ADDRESSED`; reproductive health has 95.1% absence.
- **Center-level divergence profiles** are stable (range 0.139–0.255), reflecting systematic institutional differences.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Document-Grounded Question Answering as a Proxy for Center Guidance
Retrieval-augmented language models generate answers by retrieving relevant passages from a center’s handbook and prompting an LLM (Qwen3-14B) to answer only from that context. This produces a faithful representation of the center’s stated guidance. Evidence: The abstract and Section 2.3 describe the RAG setup with a `NOT ADDRESSED` fallback. **Break condition:** Retrieval failures cause false absences; context non-adherence causes hallucinations.

### Mechanism 2: Structured Pairwise Comparison via LLM Judge
An LLM judge classifies every pair of non-absent answers into one of five clinically meaningful labels. This transforms free-text answers into a structured comparison matrix. Evidence: Abstract and Section 2.4.2 detail the five-label taxonomy and judge setup. **Break condition:** The judge may misclassify nuanced clinical differences (e.g., confusing `COMPLEMENTARY` with `DIVERGENT`), or exhibit systematic biases.

### Mechanism 3: Aggregation Framework to Quantify Heterogeneity Dimensions
Aggregating pairwise labels across questions, topics, organs, and centers yields stable metrics (`R_div(g)`, `R_con(g)`). These reveal where variation concentrates (e.g., topic-level divergence) and that center-level profiles are consistent. Evidence: Abstract, Section 2.4, and Section 3.5. **Break condition:** Aggregation may be skewed by a few volatile questions; pairwise comparisons are not independent.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**: Needed to ground LLM answers in each center’s specific handbook. *Quick check:* If the retriever misses relevant passages, which metric (absence or divergence) is most directly inflated?
- **Concept: LLM-as-a-Judge for Text Comparison**: Needed to apply the five-label taxonomy at scale. *Quick check:* What distinguishes a `COMPLEMENTARY` from a `DIVERGENT` label, and why might an LLM struggle?
- **Concept: Multi-Level Aggregation of Pairwise Metrics**: Needed to summarize heterogeneity across dimensions. *Quick check:* Can a topic have high `R_div` but low `%Div`? What does that pattern imply?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** PDF Parsing (LlamaParse) -> Chunking (section-aware) -> Dual Indexing (BM25 + FAISS) -> Hybrid Query (RRF + rerank) -> Generation (Qwen3-14B) -> Comparison (LLM judge) -> Aggregation.

**Critical path:** Retrieval quality (Stage 4) and LLM judge reliability (Stage 6) are linchpins. Retrieval errors propagate to absence rates and pairwise comparisons; judge biases affect all divergence metrics.

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- Hybrid retrieval improves recall but adds latency.
- Strict context grounding prevents hallucination but may undercount sparse coverage.
- Single LLM judge ensures consistency but may have blind spots.

**Failure signatures:**
1. Artificially high absence rate (>98%) for a handbook: likely parsing/indexing error.
2. Suspiciously high `CONSISTENT` rate for complex questions: retriever returning generic text.
3. Center with very few non-absent pairs (<100): its metrics are unreliable.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Retrieval Sanity Check:** Manually verify 100 `NOT ADDRESSED` pairs to estimate false negative rate.
2. **Judge Validation:** Have clinicians re-label 200 pairwise comparisons; compute Cohen’s κ with LLM judge.
3. **Center Profile Robustness:** Recompute each center’s divergence rate on random 80% subsets; correlate with full-set ranks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **LLM judge bias:** How much do systematic biases in the LLM judge inflate/deflate divergence estimates? (Limitations: no full human validation.)
2. **Non-textual content:** How much do coverage gap estimates change when tables/figures are incorporated? (Currently only text is processed.)
3. **Institutional drivers:** What factors explain stable center-level divergence profiles? (No analysis of covariates like volume, demographics.)
4. **Generalizability:** Do similar patterns exist in non-U.S. or non-English handbooks? (Corpus limited to U.S. English.)

## Limitations
- **Handbook access:** Exact corpus not publicly available, hindering replication.
- **LLM judge reliability:** Limited human validation; potential classification biases.
- **Retrieval-induced absences:** High `NOT ADDRESSED` rates may overstate coverage gaps due to recall errors.

## Next Checks
1. **Retrieval recall audit:** Sample 100 `NOT ADDRESSED` pairs, manually search handbooks, estimate false negative rate.
2. **Judge agreement study:** Have 2–3 clinicians label ~200 pairwise comparisons; compute Cohen’s κ vs. LLM judge.
3. **Profile sub-sampling:** For each center, recompute divergence metrics on 10 random 80% subsets; report intra-center variance and correlation with full-set ranks.
