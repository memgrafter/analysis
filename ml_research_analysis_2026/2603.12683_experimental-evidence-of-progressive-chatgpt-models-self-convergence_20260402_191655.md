# Experimental evidence of progressive ChatGPT models self-convergence

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12683
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12683
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Successive ChatGPT versions show a dramatic increase in the repetition of long text patterns (SPR) among paraphrases, even at maximum stochastic settings, indicating progressive "self-convergence" likely caused by training on increasingly AI-generated data.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether newer ChatGPT versions lose the ability to generate diverse text due to training on data increasingly contaminated by AI-generated content. By analyzing the Similarity Percentage Ratio (SPR) of paraphrases produced by seven model versions from two prompt sets, the authors find a clear, progressive increase in SPR for longer patterns (length ≥15). This effect is most pronounced in the latest versions (5.1/5.2), where SPR is 20–30 times higher than in older models like 3.5 Turbo. Critically, the typical spread between deterministic (temperature 0) and stochastic (temperature 1) outputs collapses in newer models, suggesting a fundamental reduction in output diversity at the model weight level, not just in sampling. The findings point to "self-convergence," where models trained on synthetic data replicate similar structures despite being prompted for variation.

## Method Summary
The experiment uses a corpus of human-written literary summaries (CliffNotes and SparkNotes). For each of 30 chapters, a fixed prompt requests paraphrases from seven ChatGPT versions (3.5 Turbo, 4, 4o, 4.1, 5, 5.1, 5.2) at temperatures 0 and 1. The ARPaD algorithm extracts all non-overlapping repeated patterns of lengths 3 to 20 within each set of paraphrases for a given chapter and model. The Similarity Percentage Ratio (SPR) for a pattern length \( l \) measures the fraction of a paraphrase covered by these repeated patterns. Mean SPRs are aggregated and compared across model versions, temperatures, and pattern lengths.

## Key Results
- SPR for long patterns (length ≥15) increases monotonically and dramatically across versions, with 5.1/5.2 showing 20–30× higher SPR at temperature 1 than 3.5 Turbo.
- The difference between temperature 0 and temperature 1 outputs (δSPR) plateaus or decreases for long patterns in newer models (5.1/5.2), indicating a loss of stochasticity's effect.
- The trend is progressive, with each successive model version exhibiting higher similarity, suggesting a compounding effect of synthetic data in training corpora over time.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Training on increasingly AI-generated internet data reduces output diversity in newer ChatGPT versions.
- **Mechanism:** Synthetic data from prior model outputs, which already contain repeated high-probability token sequences (long n-grams), floods the training corpus. Newer models learn these spurious correlations, causing even stochastic sampling (temp=1) to favor the same long patterns, collapsing the distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract cites "infiltration of internet by artificial data"; Section 6 links results to "gradually lowering quality of the training datasets" and "internet contamination by repeated text...created by LLMs."
- **Break condition:** Future models trained on purified human data show a restored, high δSPR for long patterns at temp=1.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The temperature parameter's effect on output variance diminishes in newer models, indicating a loss of stochasticity at the architectural/weight level.
- **Mechanism:** Overfitting to synthetic data's mode sharpens the model's internal probability distributions (lower entropy). Even with temperature scaling (T=1), Top-P/K filtering selects from a narrowed high-probability set containing the same long patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 5, Fig 5 & 6 show δSPR plateauing for longer patterns in newer models; Section 6 states newer versions "cannot differentiate between deterministic and stochastic" as well as older ones.
- **Break condition:** A future model shows a sustained increase in δSPR for long patterns (l>10), returning to the parabolic decline curve of pre-5.0 models.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Self-convergence is progressive and version-dependent, not a one-time collapse.
- **Mechanism:** Each model generation is trained on a corpus containing proportionally more synthetic data from its predecessors. This compounds the "synthetic signature" (repeated long patterns) across iterations, even without recursive training on a model's own outputs.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes "progressive 'self-convergence'"; Section 1/4 show monotonic SPR increase from 3.5 Turbo to 5.2; Section 5, Fig 8 shows 20–30× SPR increase for l≥15.
- **Break condition:** A future model version shows a decrease in long-pattern SPR compared to its immediate predecessor.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Token Probability Distribution & Temperature**
  - **Why needed:** The phenomenon is defined by temperature=1 failing to produce diverse outputs. Understanding how temperature scales logits into probabilities explains why a model with low-entropy logits ignores the "stochastic" setting.
  - **Quick check:** If a model's next-token logits for a context have very low entropy (few tokens dominate), setting T=1 vs T=0 yields similar high-probability token selections, leading to similar output sequences.
- **Concept: N-gram / Pattern Repetition in Text**
  - **Why needed:** SPR measures exact repeated word sequences (patterns). Interpreting why long patterns (l=10+) are the key signal requires understanding that random human text has an extremely low probability of long exact repeated phrases between independent paraphrases.
  - **Quick check:** The chance of two human writers independently producing the same 10-word sequence in paraphrasing the same source is negligible; high SPR for l=10+ between human paraphrases would indicate plagiarism or non-independent generation.
- **Concept: Training Data Contamination**
  - **Why needed:** The causal chain hinges on synthetic data becoming a significant, diversity-degrading component of the training set. This distinguishes volume (more data) from diversity (varied data).
  - **Quick check:** The key difference is that synthetic data, especially from converged models, contains systematically repeated high-probability long sequences. A corpus contaminated with such text teaches the model to reproduce these sequences as canonical, whereas human-generated repetitive boilerplate may not encode the same "learned pattern" structure at the token level.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Source Texts (CliffNotes/SparkNotes) -> Paraphrase Generation Engine (ChatGPT API, fixed prompt) -> Pattern Detection Module (ARPaD algorithm for non-overlapping patterns l=3-20) -> Similarity Metric Engine (SPR calculation per pattern length) -> Analysis & Visualization (mean SPR aggregation, heatmaps, δSPR curves)
- **Critical path:** Corpus selection -> API calls (rate-limited, requires inter-call gap) -> Pattern extraction (computationally intensive for long l) -> SPR aggregation -> Cross-version trend analysis.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Pattern length upper bound (20):** Longer patterns are rarer and more discriminative but become statistically unstable in short texts; shorter patterns may miss the convergence signal.
  - **Temperature values:** Used 0 and 1 because >1 produced nonsense in older models, limiting comparability across versions.
  - **Control dataset:** Two independently written study guide series (CliffNotes vs SparkNotes) assume a "human baseline" low-SPR relationship for same-source paraphrases.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - API changes/rollbacks alter model behavior independently of training data.
  - Unintended prompt variation across API calls (the "one paraphrase per round with gap" rule mitigates but does not eliminate cache effects).
  - ARPaD algorithm bugs for very long patterns in repetitive text.
  - The chosen human corpus (literature summaries) might have idiosyncratic stylistic repetition that interacts with model training.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Reproduce Long-Pattern SPR Trend:** Run the exact pipeline on the provided CliffNotes dataset for models 3.5 Turbo → 5.2. Verify the 20–30× SPR increase for l=15+ at temp=1.
  2. **Control for Model Size:** Compare models with the same knowledge cutoff (4o vs 4.1; 5 vs 5.1). If SPR differs significantly (as seen for 5 vs 5.1), implicates factors beyond scale (likely data contamination timing/fine-tuning).
  3. **Test the "Human Noise" Baseline:** Calculate SPR between the two human study guides (CliffNotes vs SparkNotes) for the same chapters. Establish true baseline for "different human authors"; should be near-zero for l>5.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Do other major LLM families (e.g., Claude, Gemini, Llama) exhibit the same progressive self-convergence pattern observed across ChatGPT versions?
- **Open Question 2:** Is the increase in SPR with model version consistent across text genres beyond literary summaries (e.g., technical manuals, social media, creative fiction)?
- **Open Question 3:** What specific changes in training data composition or fine-tuning procedures for newer ChatGPT versions explain the abnormal convergence of stochastic (temp=1) and deterministic (temp=0) outputs?
- **Open Question 4:** What fraction of the pretraining corpora for each ChatGPT version consists of AI-generated text, and how does this quantify the reported SPR increases?
- **Open Question 5:** Does the use of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) in deployed LLMs interact with self-convergence, potentially accelerating it?

## Limitations
- Unmeasured confounders: Model versions differ in architecture, tokenization, and safety tuning beyond training data; SPR trend could be influenced by these factors.
- Corpus specificity: Using only literature summaries limits generalizability; domain-specific patterns might artificially inflate SPR.
- Algorithmic artifacts: ARPaD’s pattern extraction for very long sequences (l≥15) may be sensitive to text length or repetition handling, potentially exaggerating differences.
- Baseline ambiguity: The human-human SPR baseline is not directly reported; if not near-zero for long l, the metric’s interpretability weakens.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Synthetic Data Contamination):** Medium – Plausible and consistent with related work, but lacks direct evidence of data composition shifts.
- **Mechanism 2 (Temperature失效):** Medium – δSPR plateau is observed, but could partly stem from sampling algorithm changes (e.g., top-p adjustments).
- **Mechanism 3 (Progressive Convergence):** Low-Medium – Monotonic SPR increase is shown, but version comparisons are not isolated from architectural jumps (e.g., 3.5 Turbo → 4 → 4o).
