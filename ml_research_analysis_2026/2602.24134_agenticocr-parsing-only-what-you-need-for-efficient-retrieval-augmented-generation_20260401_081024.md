# AgenticOCR: Parsing Only What You Need for Efficient Retrieval-Augmented Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.24134
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24134
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: On MMLongBench-Doc, AgenticOCR-8B with Evidence+OCR attains 66.4% accuracy, surpassing human experts (65.8%) and prior SOTA; on FinRAGBench-V, it reaches 78.6%, beating all prior agentic frameworks.

## Executive Summary
AgenticOCR addresses the bottleneck of page-level retrieval in visual RAG for complex documents, where passing entire pages dilutes the generator’s attention and wastes tokens. It converts OCR into a query-driven, agentic system that thinks with images, autonomously localizes relevant regions, and performs on-demand decompression via a unified zoom-and-OCR tool. Training combines supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) with Gemini‑distilled trajectories and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) reinforcement learning, using rewards for dual‑recall and penalties for over‑prediction, overlap, and lazy full‑page parsing. On MMLongBench‑Doc, AgenticOCR‑8B achieves 66.4% accuracy, exceeding human expert performance and the prior SOTA; on FinRAGBench‑V, it reaches 78.6%. The method reduces visual token load and improves signal‑to‑token ratio, demonstrating that selective, instruction‑following parsing boosts both efficiency and accuracy in long‑form document understanding.

## Method Summary
AgenticOCR transforms OCR into a query‑driven agent that selectively parses only the most relevant document regions. The pipeline begins with a visual retriever that fetch candidate page images. For each retrieved page, a VLM‑based agent reason over the page image and the query, then invokes a unified `image_zoom_and_ocr_tool` to crop, rotate‑correct, and extract text from specific bounding boxes. The tool accepts a semantic type (region, text, table, image, equation) to adjust its processing granularity. The agent’s policy is trained in two stages: first, supervised fine‑tuning on high‑quality Gemini‑distilled trajectories via rejection sampling; second, GRPO reinforcement learning with a reward that combines dual‑recall (Recallmin and RecallEM) and penalties for over‑prediction, overlapping boxes, and oversized regions (>85% page). The extracted evidence (cropped images and OCR text) is interleaved with low‑resolution page images and fed to a powerful generator (e.g., Gemini‑2.5‑Pro) to produce the final answer. This selective parsing reduces extraneous context and token waste while preserving critical evidence.

## Key Results
- **MMLongBench‑Doc**: AgenticOCR-8B + Evidence+OCR = 66.4% accuracy, exceeding human experts (65.8%) and DocLens‑Gemini‑2.5‑Pro (66.1%).
- **FinRAGBench‑V**: achieves 78.6%, beating all prior agentic frameworks.
- Demonstrates reduced visual token load and improved signal‑to‑token ratio compared to full‑page pipelines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Selective Parsing for Signal‑to‑Token Ratio (Mechanism 1)
- **Claim**: AgenticOCR reduces extraneous visual context and token waste by selectively parsing only query‑relevant regions, thereby improving the signal‑to‑token ratio for the generator.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of delivering entire pages, the agent analyzes the page image and query, then invokes the `image_zoom_and_ocr_tool` to crop and parse specific regions. This decouples retrieval granularity from rigid page‑level chunking.
- **Core assumption**: The generator’s attention is limited and diluted by irrelevant visual content; compressing full pages into fixed token budgets sacrifices fine‑grained details and increases hallucination risk.
- **Evidence**: [abstract] “delivering entire pages to the generator introduces excessive extraneous context. This not only overloads the generator’s attention mechanism but also dilutes the most salient evidence.” [section 1] “page‑level chunking and retrieval is a natural starting point… it creates a critical bottleneck: delivering entire pages to the generator introduces excessive extraneous context.” The corpus (e.g., VDocRAG, VisRAG) similarly identifies this inefficiency.
- **Break condition**: Benefit diminishes if the generator has sufficiently large context windows and strong internal filtering, or for queries requiring holistic page understanding (e.g., overall layout or cross‑section relationships).

### Unified Zoom‑and‑OCR Tool (Mechanism 2)
- **Claim**: The unified `image_zoom_and_ocr_tool` enables flexible, on‑demand decompression of visual information by combining cropping, rotation correction, and element‑specific OCR into a single atomic operation.
- **Mechanism**: The tool accepts a normalized bounding box, rotation angle, and semantic type (region/text/table/image/equation). The `region` type triggers a two‑stage layout analysis + OCR on the cropped area, while element types bypass layout detection for direct recognition. This mimics human reading: decide where to look, how to orient, and what to parse.
- **Core assumption**: Different document elements require different processing granularity; a one‑size‑fits‑all full‑page OCR is inefficient and error‑prone for localized queries.
- **Evidence**: [section 3.1] “For flexible parsing, we design a single but versatile tool that unifies region localization, geometric correction, and content recognition into an atomic operation.” The tool’s behavior depends on the specified type τ. Prior agentic multimodal models (DeepEyes, Thyme) use image zoom tools; AgenticOCR adapts this specifically for document parsing.
- **Break condition**: If documents are already digitized with perfect OCR and no rotation issues, the tool’s rotation and region capabilities provide less benefit. Also, if the underlying OCR model (MinerU) performs poorly on certain elements (e.g., handwritten annotations), outputs may be unreliable.

### Two‑Stage Training with GRPO (Mechanism 3)
- **Claim**: The two‑stage training (SFT with Gemini‑distilled trajectories + GRPO RL) yields an agent that learns precise, minimal evidence extraction while avoiding common failure modes (spurious boxes, overlap, lazy full‑page parsing).
- **Mechanism**: SFT via rejection sampling on ViDoRe‑v3 creates a cold‑start policy with high‑quality tool‑use demonstrations. GRPO RL then optimizes a reward combining dual‑recall (Recallmin and RecallEM) and penalties for over‑prediction, overlapping boxes, and oversized regions (>85% page). This shapes the policy to extract minimal, accurate sets of regions.
- **Core assumption**: Imitation learning alone cannot optimize for spatial precision and minimalism; reinforcement learning with fine‑grained rewards is needed to refine behavior beyond the demonstrations.
- **Evidence**: [section 3.2] “Direct RL training often fails due to sparse rewards and unstable exploration. We therefore employ a cold‑start phase using high‑quality trajectories distilled from Gemini via rejection sampling.” [section 3.3] “We apply Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a tailored reward function that jointly optimizes coverage, localization accuracy, and behavioral constraints.” Reward definition (Eq. 5) and penalties (Eq. 6) explicitly encode the desired behavior.
- **Break condition**: If the SFT data is not representative of the target distribution, RL may overfit to spurious patterns. The reward function might not capture all aspects of good evidence extraction (e.g., semantic completeness), leading to suboptimal policies.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**:
  1. **Visual Retriever** (e.g., Qwen3‑VL‑Reranker‑8B): Given a query and document pool, returns top‑k relevant page images.
  2. **AgenticOCR Module**: For each retrieved page, a VLM (Qwen3‑VL‑based) reasons and calls the `image_zoom_and_ocr_tool` to produce evidence items (cropped images, OCR text, model comments). It also decides if the page is irrelevant.
  3. **Generator** (e.g., Gemini‑2.5‑Pro): Receives interleaved low‑resolution page images, cropped evidence images, and OCR texts, then generates the final answer.
  4. **Tool**: `image_zoom_and_ocr_tool` that performs crop, rotation, and OCR based on the specified type.

- **Critical path**: Query → Retriever → {Page images} → AgenticOCR (per page: think → tool call(s) → evidence extraction) → Evidence groups (with bounding boxes) → Formatted input (low‑res pages + evidence images + OCR) → Generator → Answer.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Unified tool vs multiple tools**: A single tool simplifies training and reduces policy space but may be less expressive (e.g., cannot adjust contrast). However, it covers the needed operations for document parsing.
  - **SFT cold‑start vs RL from scratch**: Imitation provides a stable initial policy but may not optimize for precision; RL refines but requires careful reward design and can be unstable.
  - **Evidence granularity**: Cropping too small might lose context (e.g., table headers); cropping too large wastes tokens. The model must balance.
  - **Token budget handling**: Gemini’s fixed per‑image token allocation means multiple crops can increase total tokens; using a generator with fine‑grained control (Qwen3‑VL) achieves the intended savings.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Spurious predictions**: many boxes with low IoU to ground truth (triggers over‑prediction penalty).
  - **Redundant overlap**: multiple boxes heavily overlapping (triggers overlap penalty).
  - **Lazy parsing**: using `region` type with bbox covering >85% of page (triggers oversized penalty).
  - **Rotation errors**: applying wrong angle, leading to garbled OCR.
  - **Page‑level misclassification**: marking relevant pages as irrelevant (false negative) or irrelevant as relevant (false positive) — affects recall/precision trade‑off.
  - **Token inflation**: When using Gemini, multiple crops may exceed expected token count due to Pan & Scan preprocessing.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Single‑page evidence extraction** (Table 2): Evaluate AgenticOCR’s ability to localize relevant regions on positive and negative samples, using page accuracy and element‑level recall/precision.
  2. **Full pipeline evaluation** on MMLongBench‑Doc and FinRAGBench‑V (Table 1): Compare input configurations (Page, Page+OCR, Evidence, Evidence+OCR) to demonstrate benefit of agentic extraction.
  3. **Ablation of training stages and reward components**: Analyze impact of SFT only vs SFT+RL, and effect of each penalty term (over‑prediction, overlap, oversized) on final performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly enumerate open questions, but the limitations and proposed next checks imply several directions for future research:
- How does AgenticOCR generalize to non‑English and low‑resource languages, given the English‑biased training data?
- Can the system reliably process handwritten annotations or highly specialized layouts that challenge the OCR component?
- How can the pipeline be extended to incorporate cross‑page evidence for queries requiring holistic document understanding?
- What is the impact of OCR backend choice on overall performance, and can fallback mechanisms mitigate brittleness?
- How does token reduction via selective parsing correlate with accuracy gains across different generator architectures and context window sizes?

## Limitations
- Generalization gap: Training data is English‑heavy; performance may drop on low‑resource languages, degraded scans, or highly specialized layouts (e.g., handwritten annotations).
- OCR brittle dependency: The tool’s accuracy hinges on MinerU; errors in rotated text, tables, or dense layouts propagate without fallback.
- Single‑page processing: Current pipeline handles retrieved pages independently, potentially missing cross‑page evidence needed for holistic queries.

## Confidence
- Selective parsing improves signal‑to‑token ratio & accuracy: **Medium‑High** (benchmark gains and token reduction shown with Qwen3‑VL, but correlation per query not detailed).
- Unified tool design: **Medium** (logical design and strong results, yet no direct ablative comparison against separate cropping/OCR).
- SFT+GRPO training yields precise extraction: **Medium‑High** (ablations confirm RL and each penalty term contribute, but reward may not capture all semantic completeness).

## Next Checks
1. **Domain & language stress test**: Evaluate on non‑English legal/historical documents to measure recall drops and failure modes.
2. **OCR swap ablation**: Replace MinerU with Tesseract/PaddleOCR on a held‑out set; quantify accuracy delta to isolate OCR dependence.
3. **Token‑accuracy correlation**: For 100 random MMLongBench‑Doc queries, compute exact visual tokens (Qwen3‑VL tokenizer) for Evidence+OCR vs full‑page, then correlate reduction with accuracy gain.
