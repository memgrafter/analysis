# Missing-by-Design: Certifiable Modality Deletion for Revocable Multimodal Sentiment Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16144
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16144
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MBD achieves 89.9% binary accuracy on CMU-MOSI with all modalities and robust performance under missing data, while enabling certifiable modality deletion with 270× speedup over retraining.

## Executive Summary
The Missing-by-Design (MBD) framework introduces a unified approach to multimodal sentiment analysis that simultaneously ensures robustness to missing modalities and provides verifiable modality deletion for privacy compliance. It achieves this by decomposing modality embeddings into sample‑invariant properties (capturing modality‑level characteristics) and sample‑specific signals, which stabilizes fusion and guides generative reconstruction. A post‑training parameter‑surgery pipeline, driven by saliency and calibrated Gaussian noise, enables efficient and certifiable removal of a chosen modality backed by a machine‑verifiable Deletion Certificate. On CMU‑MOSI, MBD outperforms recent baselines by 1–2 percentage points under full modality conditions and by 3–8 points when modalities are missing. For audio deletion with privacy budget ε ≤ 1, white‑box attack success drops from 78.4% to ≤ 1.9% while accuracy degrades less than 1.5%; the entire surgery completes in 39 seconds versus 2.9 hours for full retraining—a 270× speedup.

## Method Summary
MBD operates in two phases: training and surgery. During training, it learns a property‑aware representation for each modality. A decomposition operator splits the modality embedding into sample‑specific and sample‑invariant parts; batched averages of the latter form a modality‑level property prior enforced via orthogonality, invariance, and alignment losses. Generators reconstruct missing modalities from available ones and the property prior, while a contrastive back‑translation loss ensures the fused embedding preserves modality‑specific information. After training, if a modality must be deleted (e.g., for privacy), the framework performs parameter surgery: it selects candidate parameters via saliency (reconstruction gradients for the target modality) and an importance proxy, then applies either deterministic zeroing (for strong deletion) or calibrated Gaussian noise (for DP‑like indistinguishability). The result is a modified model and a Modality Deletion Certificate (MDC) containing all metadata needed to verify that the model is indistinguishable from one never trained on the deleted modality.

## Key Results
- Under full modality presence, MBD achieves **89.9% binary accuracy** on CMU‑MOSI, surpassing recent baselines by 1–2 points.
- With missing modalities, it obtains **78.7% average accuracy** across fixed missing patterns and **80.9%** across variable missing rates, beating competitors by 3–8 points.
- For audio deletion with ε ≤ 1, **white‑box attack success falls from 78.4% to ≤ 1.9%** while accuracy degrades < 1.5%; surgical deletion runs in **39 seconds** vs 2.9 hours for retraining (**270× speedup**).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Property‑Aware Representation Decomposition
- **Claim:** Separating modality embeddings into sample‑invariant properties and sample‑specific signals stabilizes fusion under missing data and guides reconstruction.
- **Mechanism:** A decomposition operator (`DE_m`) splits each modality embedding `X^m_i` into `Σ^m_i` (sample‑specific) and `µ^m_i` (sample‑invariant). Batch‑averaged `¯µ_m` serves as a modality‑level property prior `P^m`, enforced via orthogonality (`L_or`), invariance (`L_inv`), and alignment penalties (`L_app`). This disentangles persistent modality characteristics from transient content.
- **Core assumption:** Modality‑level properties are approximately constant within a batch and distinguish one modality from another at a distributional level, not just per‑sample.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract and section 3.3 define decomposition and losses; ablation studies (Sec. 5.1) demonstrate robustness gains when property losses are included.
- **Break condition:** If `L_inv` or `L_app` fail to converge, the learned `P^m` does not capture stable modality properties, leading to degraded reconstruction and fusion under missing modalities.

### Mechanism 2: Generator‑Based Reconstruction with Back‑Translation
- **Claim:** Dedicated generators `G_m` reconstruct missing modalities from available ones and property embeddings, while a back‑translation network `B_m` with contrastive loss (`L_con`) preserves modality‑specific signals in the fused representation.
- **Mechanism:** For missing modality `m`, `G_m` takes other modalities and `P^m` to produce `ˆX^m_i`. Reconstruction loss `L_re` trains generators. The fused embedding `Z_i` is fed to `B_m` to recover `Σ^m_i`; contrastive loss `L_con` aligns `˜X^m_i` with the true `Σ^m_i` within a batch, encouraging `Z_i` to retain modality‑specific information.
- **Core assumption:** The fused space can be reliably reverse‑mapped to modality‑specific components, and contrastive alignment prevents collapse to modality‑common factors.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract and section 3.2 describe the generator and back‑translation; reconstruction quality metrics (Sec. 4.10) show low `L_re` and effective contrastive alignment.
- **Break condition:** If `L_con` is mis‑tuned (e.g., temperature τ improper), `Z_i` loses modality‑specific signals, harming performance when only a subset of modalities is available.

### Mechanism 3: Certifiable Parameter Surgery via Saliency and Gaussian Calibration
- **Claim:** Saliency‑driven candidate selection combined with a calibrated Gaussian update achieves modality deletion with a verifiable privacy‑utility trade‑off, avoiding full retraining.
- **Mechanism:** For deletion of modality `m*`, compute per‑parameter saliency `s^(m*)_q` (mean absolute reconstruction gradient on a calibration batch `B`) and SwiftPrune‑inspired importance proxy `L_q`. Select candidates `I_m*` by thresholding (`η_s`, `η_L`) and sort by `L_q`. Apply deterministic zeroing or Gaussian noise (`σ` set by DP‑like formula) to selected parameters. The Modality Deletion Certificate (MDC) records metadata for verification.
- **Core assumption:** Parameters with high reconstruction gradient for the target modality are primary carriers of that modality’s information; modifying them sufficiently increases the model’s reliance on other modalities. The sensitivity bound `∆` and Gaussian calibration provide indistinguishability from a model never exposed to `m*`.
- **Evidence anchors:** The abstract and section 3.7–3.8 detail the selection thresholds, noise calibration, and certificate contents; empirical deletion efficacy (ASR drop) and speedup are reported in Sec. 4.12.
- **Break condition:** If `η_s` is too low or `η_L` too high, irrelevant parameters are modified, causing utility loss. If `σ` is poorly calibrated, either deletion fails (ASR stays high) or utility drops beyond 1.5%.

## Foundational Learning

### Property Embedding & Decomposition
**Why needed:** Captures modality‑level invariances that persist even when samples are incomplete, enabling reconstruction and stable fusion.
**Quick check:** If `L_inv` is minimized, what happens to the variance of `µ^m_i` across a batch? Should it increase or decrease, and why?
- *Answer:* Variance should decrease because `L_inv` encourages all sample‑invariant parts `µ^m_i` to be close to the batch mean `¯µ_m`, making them converge to a shared property vector.

### Contrastive Learning for Modality‑Specific Retention
**Why needed:** Ensures the fused representation retains unique modality information rather than collapsing to common factors, crucial for performance when only subsets are available.
**Quick check:** Why is a *contrastive* loss used instead of a simple MSE between `B_m(Z_i)` and `Σ^m_i`? What would be the risk of MSE?
- *Answer:* MSE would drive all back‑translated outputs toward a single centroid, losing diversity. Contrastive loss retains relative distinctions within a batch, preserving modality‑specific information.

### Differential Privacy (DP) Gaussian Mechanism for Indistinguishability
**Why needed:** Provides a mathematical guarantee that the modified model is indistinguishable from one never trained on the deleted modality, enabling a verifiable privacy certificate.
**Quick check:** If the ℓ₂‑sensitivity `∆` is overestimated, what happens to the privacy budget `ε_mod` and expected utility loss?
- *Answer:* Overestimating `∆` leads to larger noise `σ` (since `σ` scales with `∆`), which worsens utility while making the privacy guarantee looser (higher `ε_mod` for the same `δ_mod`).

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Backbone: frozen modality encoders → X^m_i.  
Property pathway: DE_m splits X^m_i → (Σ^m_i, µ^m_i); batch‑average µ^m_i → P^m.  
Reconstruction pathway: G_m(X_{\m}, P^m) → ˆX^m_i.  
Fusion pathway: F → Z_i, then C → ŷ_i.  
Back‑translation pathway: B_m(Z_i) → ˜X^m_i (contrastive with Σ^m_i).  
Surgery pipeline (post‑training, on deletion request for m*): calibration batch B → compute saliency s_q^{(m*)} and importance L_q → select candidate parameters → apply zeroing or Gaussian noise → generate MDC.

### Critical Path
1. Train full MBD (minimize L_task + αL_re + βL_pe + γL_con) until validation convergence.  
2. Upon deletion request for modality m*:  
   a. Draw calibration batch B (disjoint from train/test).  
   b. Compute row‑activation sums S on B for all parameters → χ_q, L_q; compute saliency s_q^{(m*)} by back‑propagating L_re for m* on B.  
   c. Threshold (η_s=0.1, η_L=0.05) → candidate set C_sel; sort by L_q; take top‑k where k = r·|W|, r=0.03.  
   d. Compute sensitivity Δ (bound using parameter norms); set σ via Eq. (17) for target (ε_mod, δ_mod).  
   e. Apply operation: zero if ε_mod ≤ 1 else add N(0,σ²).  
   f. Generate MDC JSON with m*, C_sel, seed, σ, (ε_mod, δ_mod), SHA‑256 digest of W'.  
3. Deploy modified model W'; retain MDC for audit.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Property embedding dimension d_p:** Larger increases capacity to capture modality properties but risks overfitting; default d_p=128.  
- **Surgery budget r:** Larger r modifies more parameters → stronger deletion but higher utility loss; default r=0.03 balances.  
- **Calibration batch size |B|:** Larger gives more stable s_q, L_q estimates but increases prep time; default |B|=5000, stable from |B|≥2000.  
- **Thresholds η_s, η_L:** Control candidate set purity. Tighter η_s selects only highly modality‑specific params; looser η_L admits more params but risks harming general capacity; defaults 0.1/0.05.

### Failure Signatures
- **High reconstruction loss L_re** during validation: generators or property embeddings are ineffective.  
- **Surgery causes >1.5% binary accuracy drop (Acc2):** r, η_L too aggressive or σ miscalibrated.  
- **Attack success rate (ASR) remains high post‑surgery** (e.g., >20% for ε_mod ≤ 1): saliency computation failed or candidate set too small; η_s too high.  
- **MDC verification fails** (hash mismatch or missing fields): certificate generation bug or parameters altered after surgery.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Property pathway sanity check:** Train MBD with/without L_pe (property loss) and evaluate on fixed missing patterns (e.g., {v,a}). Expect >5‑point gap in binary accuracy in favor of full MBD (Table 5 confirms).  
2. **Reconstruction quality under missing data:** For test samples with audio missing, compute L_re for audio generator. Should be low (<0.02 relative to from‑scratch reference per section 4.10).  
3. **Surgery efficacy:** Delete audio modality with ε_mod=1. Run white‑box attack (ResNet on intermediate activations) to recover audio presence. Expected ASR ≤1.9% (Table 7). Also check MDC integrity by parsing JSON and verifying SHA‑256 digest of released W'.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does MBD's privacy‑utility trade‑off generalize to datasets with balanced demographic representations, and does deletion induce disparate impacts across groups?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] 4.13 Discussion: "Our experiments use publicly available benchmarks collected under prior review protocols. These datasets lack detailed demographic annotations (e.g., race, dialect, disability), making post‑deletion fairness audits infeasible."
- **Why unresolved:** Current benchmarks do not contain the necessary metadata to measure fairness; thus, potential biases in deletion outcomes remain unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate MBD on new or enriched datasets with comprehensive demographic labels, computing group‑wise utility and privacy metrics (e.g., accuracy disparity, attack success rate differences) after modality deletion.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can more sophisticated attacks or significant distribution shifts recover deleted modality information from models modified by MBD, even when (ε_mod, δ_mod) ≤ (1, δ)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] 4.13 Discussion: "Although the framework enforces (ε_mod, δ_mod)‑indistinguishability, current validation focuses on representative leakage tests. Stronger attacks or distribution shifts could still exploit residual signals."
- **Why unresolved:** The empirical validation uses standard white‑box/black‑box attacks on in‑distribution test data; robustness to adaptive or out‑of‑distribution attacks is not established.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Design and deploy advanced reconstruction attacks (e.g., generative model inversion, transfer attacks) under covariate shift; measure modality recovery rates post‑surgery across diverse perturbations.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can theoretical bounds for modality‑level indistinguishability be tightened beyond the current ℓ₂‑sensitivity‑based analysis to provide stronger certified deletion guarantees?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] 5 Conclusion: "Future work will investigate tighter theoretical bounds for modality indistinguishability..."
- **Why unresolved:** The current sensitivity analysis uses conservative worst‑case bounds (e.g., on parameter changes) that may overestimate noise needed, harming utility.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop refined sensitivity calculations that account for network architecture specifics (e.g., activation distributions, modality‑specific pathways) and prove tighter (ε, δ) guarantees under realistic assumptions.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does MBD scale to systems with more than three modalities (e.g., adding physiological or environmental sensors) and to larger model families (e.g., multimodal large language models)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] 5 Conclusion: "Future work will investigate... extensions to additional modality combinations and larger model families..."
- **Why unresolved:** All experiments use three fixed modalities (text, audio, visual) with frozen feature extractors on relatively small models; interactions in higher‑modality settings and scalability to giant models are untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend MBD to benchmarks with ≥4 modalities (if available) and to transformer‑based multimodal LLMs; measure deletion efficacy, utility retention, and computational overhead as modality count and model size increase.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Can adaptive calibration strategies during surgery (e.g., dynamically adjusting noise scale or candidate selection based on real‑time utility impact) improve the privacy‑utility trade‑off beyond static hyperparameters?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] 5 Conclusion: "Future work will investigate... adaptive calibration strategies that further minimize utility loss while strengthening deletion guarantees."
- **Why unresolved:** Current surgery uses fixed thresholds (η_s, η_L, r) and a single calibration batch; it does not adapt to modality‑specific sensitivity or task‑critical parameters.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop closed‑loop surgery where post‑modification utility is monitored and calibration parameters (e.g., noise σ, deletion fraction) are tuned per modality and model, demonstrating improved trade‑offs on benchmark suites.

## Limitations
- **Dataset scope:** Evaluation only on CMU‑MOSI (English speech) may not generalize to other languages, modalities, or affective datasets.
- **Assumption of batch‑level property invariance:** The property embedding relies on `¯µ_m` being stable within a batch; small or heterogeneous batches could destabilize training or deletion.
- **Surgery hyperparameter sensitivity:** Default thresholds (`η_s=0.1`, `η_L=0.05`) and budget `r=0.03` are tuned for this setup; other models or modalities may require different tuning, risking utility loss or failed deletion.
- **Certification scope:** The Modality Deletion Certificate provides parametric indistinguishability but does not guarantee task‑level privacy (e.g., reconstruction attacks on `Z_i`).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross‑dataset evaluation:** Train and delete MBD on other multimodal sentiment datasets (e.g., MOSEI, IEMOCAP) to assess generalizability of robustness and surgery thresholds.
2. **Sensitivity analysis of surgery budget `r` and thresholds:** Systematically vary `r`, `η_s`, `η_L` and measure task accuracy and deletion success (ASR) to produce a reliability profile for different operating points.
3. **Information‑theoretic audit of `Z_i` post‑surgery:** Use mutual information estimators or linear probes to quantify how much of each modality’s specific signal remains in the fused representation after deletion, checking alignment with the privacy guarantee.
