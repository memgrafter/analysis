# Early Risk Stratification of Dosing Errors in Clinical Trials Using Machine Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22285
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22285
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A multimodal ML framework using pre-initiation trial data achieves an AUC-ROC of 0.862; after probability calibration, model outputs enable robust four-category risk stratification with observed dosing error rates monotonically increasing from 0.62% (low) to 18.80% (very high).

## Executive Summary
This study develops a machine learning framework to prospectively stratify clinical trials by their risk of experiencing high dosing errors, using only data available before patient enrollment. Analyzing 42,112 interventional trials from ClinicalTrials.gov, the authors integrate structured trial characteristics (e.g., phase, intervention type) with free-text protocol summaries using a late-fusion model that combines XGBoost and ClinicalModernBERT. The model's predicted probabilities are then calibrated to true event frequencies. This approach yields strong discrimination (AUC-ROC 0.862) and, critically, produces well-calibrated risk estimates that reliably separate trials into four risk categories with a more than 30-fold difference in observed error rates. The work demonstrates that upstream trial design decisions contain actionable signals for operational risk mitigation.

## Method Summary
The study uses a retrospective cohort of 42,112 completed or terminated interventional trials with results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov before September 2025. Trials are labeled as positive if their lower bound Wilson confidence interval for dosing error rate (derived from MedDRA-mapped adverse events) exceeds 0.01%. A chronological split by completion date creates training (70%), validation (15%), and test (15%) sets to ensure prospective simulation. Two unimodal models are trained: XGBoost on 30 pre-initiation structured features and ClinicalModernBERT on 10 concatenated free-text fields. A late-fusion model combines their predicted probabilities via a weighted average, with the fusion weight optimized on the validation set. Calibration (isotonic regression for XGBoost, Platt scaling for neural models) is fit on validation predictions to convert raw scores into reliable probability estimates. Final evaluation uses AUC-ROC, Brier score, and stratification into four risk categories (<2%, 2–5%, 5–10%, ≥10% predicted probability), checking for monotonic increases in observed error rates.

## Key Results
- The late-fusion model achieves an AUC-ROC of 0.862, outperforming both unimodal XGBoost (0.848) and ClinicalModernBERT (0.855).
- Probability calibration reduces the Brier score from 0.090 to 0.041 on the test set, indicating excellent alignment between predicted probabilities and observed frequencies.
- Calibrated outputs stratify trials into four risk groups with monotonically increasing observed dosing error rates: low (<2% predicted) 0.62%, medium-low (2–5%) 3.14%, medium-high (5–10%) 8.72%, and very high (≥10%) 18.80%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Multimodal integration of structured trial features and protocol text captures complementary risk signals, improving discrimination over unimodal models.
**Mechanism:** Structured features (e.g., enrollment count, intervention types) encode trial design and operational characteristics, while protocol text (e.g., brief summary, intervention descriptions) contains nuanced semantic information about procedures and eligibility. A late-fusion model combines their predicted probabilities, allowing each modality to contribute independently without architectural complexity.
**Core assumption:** The information sources are partially independent; errors in one modality are not perfectly correlated with the other.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "multimodal integration... yielded performance gains without requiring complex architectures"
- [section 2.5] LateFusion model achieved highest AUC-ROC (0.862) vs. XGBoost (0.848) and ClinicalModernBERT (0.855).
- [corpus] Neighbors show similar multimodal gains in other risk stratification tasks (e.g., heart failure, cancer), but direct evidence for dosing errors is limited to this paper.
**Break condition:** If structured and text features are highly redundant or one modality contains systematic noise that dominates the signal.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Post-hoc probability calibration transforms poorly calibrated model outputs into reliable risk estimates, enabling robust stratification into interpretable probability bands.
**Mechanism:** Raw model outputs (e.g., logits from XGBoost or BERT) are poorly calibrated. Calibrators (Platt scaling, isotonic regression) are fitted on validation data to map these outputs to estimated probabilities that match empirical frequencies. This allows predefined thresholds (e.g., 2%, 5%, 10%) to reliably correspond to increasing observed error rates.
**Core assumption:** The calibration set is representative of the test distribution; the relationship between raw scores and true probabilities is stable.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract] "calibrated outputs enabled robust stratification... observed dosing error rates monotonically increasing"
- [section 2.6 & Table 3] Calibration reduces Brier score dramatically (e.g., LateFusion: 0.090 → 0.041) and yields monotonic risk increases (0.62% → 18.80% across categories).
- [corpus] Neighbors confirm calibration is standard for reliable risk categories in clinical ML, but specific dosing error application is unique.
**Break condition:** If the validation set is small or unrepresentative, causing overfitting in calibration (e.g., isotonic regression) or if the underlying data distribution shifts.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Pre-initiation data encodes upstream design and planning decisions that are causally linked to dosing error susceptibility, enabling *prospective* risk assessment.
**Mechanism:** Features like `phases`, `interventionTypes`, `armGroupTypes`, and protocol text describe trial architecture before patient enrollment. These design choices (e.g., complexity of dosing regimens, blinding, number of arms) may create conditions prone to protocol deviations, misunderstandings, or operational challenges that manifest as dosing errors during conduct.
**Core assumption:** Dosing errors are not purely random but are influenced by trial design characteristics observable at the planning stage.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [introduction] "dosing error risk can be anticipated... using information available prior to trial initiation"
- [section 4.1] "aspects of dosing error risk are encoded in upstream trial design and protocol characteristics"
- [corpus] Corroborated by neighboring studies showing pre-trial features predict other operational risks (e.g., trial failure, recruitment issues), but direct causal pathways for dosing errors require domain validation.
**Break condition:** If dosing errors are predominantly driven by site-level factors, patient idiosyncrasies, or post-initiation events not captured in pre-trial data.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data extraction (ClinicalTrials.gov → structured features + protocol text) -> unimodal models (XGBoost on structured, ClinicalModernBERT on text) -> late fusion (weighted average of probabilities) -> calibration (Platt/isotonic) -> risk stratification (thresholding into 4 categories).
- **Critical path:** 1) Extract pre-initiation features only. 2) Train unimodal models. 3) Optimize fusion weight on validation set. 4) Fit calibrators on validation set predictions. 5) Apply calibrators to test set and stratify.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Late-fusion simplicity vs. potential performance of early-fusion (joint representation). Calibration adds a step but is necessary for interpretability. Using pre-initiation data limits information but ensures real-world feasibility.
- **Failure signatures:** Non-monotonic observed error rates across predicted risk groups; high Brier score (>0.1) after calibration; poor calibration (reliability diagram deviates from diagonal); overlap in feature distributions between risk groups.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Train unimodal XGBoost and ClinicalModernBERT; compare AUC-ROC to establish individual modality performance.
  2. Implement LateFusion via weighted average; optimize fusion weight on validation AUC to see if fusion improves over unimodal.
  3. Apply calibration to LateFusion outputs; evaluate Brier score reduction and check monotonicity of observed error rates across predefined risk thresholds (2%, 5%, 10%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** Do more sophisticated multimodal neural architectures (beyond simple late-fusion) improve AUC-ROC and Brier score for dosing error risk stratification when integrating structured trial data and protocol text?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "although a multimodal modeling strategy was adopted, the integration of structured and unstructured information was implemented using a deliberately simple late-fusion approach. More sophisticated multimodal architectures may warrant further investigation and could potentially yield additional performance gains."
  - **Why unresolved:** The study only evaluated simple late-fusion; more complex architectures like cross-attention transformers or joint embedding spaces were not tested.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic comparison of various multimodal fusion strategies (early, late, hybrid) on the same dataset with proper statistical testing of performance differences.
- **Question:** Can full protocol documents be effectively processed using current NLP techniques to provide additional predictive signal beyond the structured fields and short text fields currently used?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "while full protocol documents were collected and converted into machine-readable text, the present study did not exploit this source of information... Although addressing these challenges represents an exciting direction for future research, it was beyond the scope of the present study."
  - **Why unresolved:** Full protocols were downloaded but not used due to challenges with length, heterogeneity, and complex structure.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and evaluation of NLP pipelines (e.g., document chunking, hierarchical encoding, or retrieval-augmented generation) that can extract relevant dosing-related information from full protocols and demonstrate improved risk prediction when combined with existing features.
- **Question:** How does the model's performance vary across therapeutic areas, and are the current fixed risk thresholds (2%, 5%, 10%) optimal for all conditions?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper uses MedDRA terms to identify dosing errors and applies uniform thresholds across all trials, but acknowledges that dosing error prevalence may vary by therapeutic area through their use of condition-specific MedDRA mapping.
  - **Why unresolved:** Results are presented aggregated across all trials without stratified analysis by condition or therapeutic class; thresholds are set based on overall prevalence without validation across subgroups.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Subgroup analysis by therapeutic area showing calibration curves and risk category enrichment; threshold optimization studies that test whether area-specific thresholds improve clinical utility and maintain monotonic risk stratification.

## Limitations
- **Label reliability:** Dosing errors derived from adverse events with Wilson CI >0.01% likely undercount true errors, weakening ground truth.
- **Data bias:** Dataset over-represents trials with posted results (ClinicalTrials.gov), skewing toward larger, industry-sponsored studies.
- **Feature gaps:** Structured features omit protocol details; text truncation at 8192 tokens may discard salient information.

## Confidence
- **Multimodal fusion improves AUC:** High (0.862 vs 0.855 unimodal).
- **Calibration yields robust stratification:** High (Brier 0.041; monotonic rates 0.62%→18.80%).
- **Pre-initiation data enables prospective risk assessment:** Medium (observational support, causal link unproven).
- **Model generalizes:** Low (only internal temporal validation).
