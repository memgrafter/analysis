# Sentipolis: Emotion-Aware Agents for Social Simulations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.18027  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18027  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Sentipolis mitigates emotional amnesia in LLM-based social simulations via continuous PAD emotion states, dual-speed dynamics, and emotion-memory coupling, yielding >150% improvement in emotional continuity and significant gains in communication and empathy.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Sentipolis, a framework to address "emotional amnesia" in LLM-based social simulations, where agents lack persistent emotional states leading to inconsistent long-term behavior. Sentipolis integrates a continuous Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance (PAD) emotion representation, dual-speed emotion dynamics (fast per-turn updates and slow reflective updates with decay), and emotion-memory coupling that tags events with PAD-derived descriptors. Evaluations across multiple LLMs and three independent evaluators show substantial improvements in emotional continuity (over 150% gain), communication (+4% to +70%), and empathy (+21% to +28%). Gains are capacity-dependent: larger models (e.g., GPT-5.2) show increased believability while smaller ones (e.g., GPT-4o-mini) decline, interpreted as a human-like trade-off between emotional expression and norm adherence. Network-level analysis reveals emergent relationship dynamics with high reciprocity (+0.87) and temporally stable community structures (NMI >0.75) without explicit enforcement.

## Method Summary
Sentipolis builds upon the modular agent architecture of Generative Agents, adding a dedicated emotion subsystem. Each agent maintains a continuous PAD vector (values in [-1,1]) that evolves via two mechanisms: fast updates after each dyadic conversation using an appraisal prompt, and slow reflective updates triggered when accumulated poignancy exceeds a threshold (150), both incorporating exponential decay (half-life 120 minutes per 20-minute simulation step). Memories are stored with PAD-derived tags and retrieved with semantic enrichment: raw PAD values are mapped to a Plutchik label via k-nearest neighbors (k=3) on the MSP-Podcast corpus (264k PAD-label pairs), then an LLM generates an emotion paragraph incorporating the label, agent profile, and retrieved memories, which is injected into the agent's prompt. The simulation runs 25 agents over 36 time steps (20-minute stride) starting 2025-02-13 08:00. Baseline comparisons omit the emotion module while keeping other components identical. Evaluation uses SotopiaEval rubrics (Empathy, Appropriateness, Continuity, Believability, Communication, Social Rules) assessed by three judges (Claude Sonnet 4.5, GPT-5.1, DeepSeek-V3.2), and network metrics derived from directed weighted relationship graphs (edge weight threshold 0.2).

## Key Results
- Emotional continuity (CON) improved by over 150% relative to the baseline.
- Communication (COM) and empathy (EMP) increased by +4% to +70% and +21% to +28%, respectively, with variations across model capacities.
- Network analysis showed high weighted reciprocity (rw = 0.87), moderate modularity (Q ≈ 0.22), and stable community structures over time (NMI > 0.75), indicating emergent social dynamics.
- Believability (BEL) increased for larger models (e.g., GPT-5.2) but decreased for smaller ones (GPT-4o-mini), highlighting capacity-dependent trade-offs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Sentipolis works because it directly combats emotional amnesia through three synergistic mechanisms. First, continuous PAD representation captures nuanced emotional states, avoiding the lossiness of discrete labels. Second, dual-speed dynamics model both immediate emotional reactions and slower mood shifts, with decay preventing runaway emotional accumulation. Third, emotion-memory coupling ensures that emotionally salient events are stored and retrieved, creating a feedback loop where past emotions influence present cognition. The semantic enrichment step bridges raw PAD vectors to natural language, making the emotional state interpretable by the LLM. Together, these mechanisms create persistent, dynamic emotional states that evolve realistically over long horizons, thereby improving continuity, empathy, and communication.

## Foundational Learning
- **PAD emotion model:** A continuous three-dimensional representation (Pleasure, Arousal, Dominance) used to quantify emotional states. *Why needed:* Enables fine-grained, differentiable updates and avoids categorical limitations. *Quick check:* Ensure agents' PAD vectors remain within [-1,1] and show gradual changes rather than discrete jumps.
- **Dual-speed emotion dynamics:** Combines fast per-turn updates (reactive) with slow reflective updates (mood-level) and exponential decay. *Why needed:* Captures multi-timescale emotional processes observed in humans. *Quick check:* Plot PAD trajectories: after each conversation, immediate shift should occur, and over many steps without events, a slow drift back toward neutral due to decay.
- **Emotion-memory coupling:** Memories are tagged with PAD values and retrieved with emotional bias. *Why needed:* Emotional states color memory encoding and recall, essential for continuity. *Quick check:* Verify that retrieved memories for an agent in a given emotional state have PAD values congruent with that state (e.g., high arousal events retrieved when arousal is high).
- **Semantic enrichment via KNN mapping:** Maps PAD to discrete Plutchik labels using the MSP-Podcast corpus, then generates a natural language emotion paragraph. *Why needed:* Bridges numerical emotions to LLM-understandable concepts. *Quick check:* Sample generated paragraphs; they should coherently describe the agent's emotional state in context of recent memories.

## Architecture Onboarding
The Sentipolis agent architecture extends Generative Agents with an emotion subsystem. The component map can be described as:

Scheduler -> Agent turn -> Cognition (LLM) + Memory (retrieval) + Emotion (state update) + Social (interaction) + Movement (location change). The Emotion module interacts bidirectionally with Memory (tags events, influences retrieval) and Cognition (injects emotion paragraph). The fast update runs after each conversation, while the slow update runs during reflection phases when poignancy threshold is exceeded.

Critical path per simulation step (20 minutes):
1. Scheduler activates agents in order.
2. For active agent: retrieve relevant memories (with emotional bias) from Memory.
3. Emotion module compiles current PAD state, retrieved memories, and profile to generate an emotion paragraph (via semantic enrichment pipeline).
4. Cognition module receives prompt (including emotion paragraph) and produces an action (dialogue or movement).
5. Social/Movement modules execute the action, potentially triggering a dyadic conversation.
6. After a conversation, fast emotion update: appraisal prompt modifies PAD based on interaction outcome.
7. The agent's poignancy accumulator is updated; if it exceeds 150, a reflection is scheduled for the next step, which triggers slow emotion update (aggregating recent events and applying decay).
8. The interaction (conversation) is stored in Memory with the post-conversation PAD tags.

Design tradeoffs:
- Continuous PAD vs discrete emotions: continuous allows smoother dynamics but requires LLM to interpret numbers; semantic enrichment adds complexity.
- Dual-speed parameters (decay half-life, poignancy threshold) control emotional inertia; shorter half-lives cause faster forgetting, longer thresholds cause fewer reflections.
- Emotion-memory coupling increases retrieval relevance but may introduce bias toward extreme emotions; tuning retrieval size needed.
- Capacity dependence: larger models handle the enriched prompt and complex update logic better; smaller models may overemphasize emotional tags, reducing believability.

Failure signatures:
- Emotional amnesia persists: low continuity (CON) scores; PAD trajectories revert to neutral between interactions.
- Believability drop in small models: high COM/EMP but BEL decreases; dialogues become overly emotional or persona-inconsistent.
- Network metric divergence: weighted reciprocity far from 0.87 or modularity >0.3 likely indicates incorrect graph construction (e.g., edge threshold misapplied).
- Social rule penalties exceed mild range (e.g., SOC decrease >3.0): suggests emotion coupling overrides norm adherence; reflection prompts may need rebalancing.
- Low inter-evaluator Spearman (<0.8): indicates rubric ambiguity; prompts for judges require refinement.

First experiments (to run when onboarding):
1. Prompt Variation Test: Implement three variants of fast/slow update prompts (minimal, detailed, example-injected) to measure impact on emotional continuity and believability; identify the minimal prompt that yields parity with reported results.
2. Hyperparameter Sweep: Vary decay half-life (60–240 min) and poignancy threshold (100–300) on a single model (e.g., GPT-5.2) to map sensitivity and find robust settings.
3. Small-Model Stress Test: Deploy Sentipolis on models with ≤3B parameters (e.g., GPT-4o-mini) using the exact same prompts and hyperparameters as for larger models to isolate whether believability drop stems from emotional over-expression or generic capability limits.

## Limitations
- Exact prompt templates for emotion updates and semantic enrichment are undisclosed, hindering precise replication.
- Baseline implementation specifics (e.g., memory retrieval, reflection, scheduling parity) are ambiguous, complicating attribution of gains.
- Hyperparameters (decay half-life, poignancy threshold, retrieval size) lack sensitivity analysis or justification.
- Evaluation aggregation methodology (per-agent scoring) is unspecified, risking metric inconsistency.
