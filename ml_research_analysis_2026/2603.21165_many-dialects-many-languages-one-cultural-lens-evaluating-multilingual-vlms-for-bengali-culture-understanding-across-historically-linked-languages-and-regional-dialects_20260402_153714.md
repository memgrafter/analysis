# Many Dialects, Many Languages, One Cultural Lens: Evaluating Multilingual VLMs for Bengali Culture Understanding Across Historically Linked Languages and Regional Dialects

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.21165
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21165
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Evaluating only standard Bangla overestimates model capabilities; dialectal variation causes significant performance drops (especially in caption generation), while missing cultural knowledge is the main bottleneck.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal evaluation benchmarks for Bengali culture have historically relied solely on standard Bangla, ignoring regional dialects and historically linked languages, leading to inflated assessments of vision-language model (VLM) performance. To address this gap, the authors introduce **BANGLAVERSE**, a culturally grounded benchmark of 1,152 manually curated images spanning nine domains (e.g., Food, Media & Movies, Politics). Using a two-stage pipeline—Gemini-2.5-Flash for multilingual translation and a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct for dialect generation—they expand the dataset into four languages (Bangla, English, Hindi, Urdu) and five Bangla dialects, yielding approximately 32.3K VQA and captioning artifacts. Key findings reveal that dialectal robustness is substantially weaker for free-form captioning than for answer-constrained VQA, historically linked languages (Hindi, Urdu) preserve descriptive meaning but lag in reasoning, and the dominant bottleneck is missing cultural knowledge rather than visual grounding. Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting consistently improves VQA accuracy, while few-shot prompting harms it but marginally benefits caption quality.

## Method Summary
BANGLAVERSE was constructed through a meticulous multi-stage process. First, native speakers manually curated 1,152 culturally relevant images from news outlets, Wikipedia, and books, and wrote captions and VQA pairs (with plausible distractors), ensuring high inter-annotator agreement (Cohen’s κ = 0.87). Next, Gemini-2.5-Flash translated Bangla annotations into English, Hindi, and Urdu. For dialect generation, the authors fine-tuned Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct on the BanglaDial dataset using LoRA to convert standard Bangla into five regional dialects (Barishal, Chittagong, Noakhali, Rangpur, Sylhet). Generated artifacts underwent post-verification via Gemini-2.5-Flash to check semantic fidelity, with human validation on a 400-sample subset confirming high quality (94% flawless). Finally, evaluation employed BERTScore-F1 for caption semantics, an LLM-as-a-Judge (Gemini) for multi-aspect caption quality, and exact-match accuracy for VQA.

## Key Results
- Standard Bangla evaluation overestimates capabilities; dialectal variation leads to notable performance drops, particularly in caption generation (e.g., GPT-4.1-mini’s caption quality declines by 14.84 points).
- Historically linked languages (Hindi, Urdu) achieve competitive caption scores but lag in VQA, indicating better preservation of descriptive meaning than reasoning.
- The primary bottleneck is missing cultural knowledge; knowledge-intensive domains such as Media & Movies and Politics exhibit the highest difficulty scores (>106). CoT prompting consistently improves VQA, especially in these domains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Dialectal variation causes significant performance drops in VLMs, especially for caption generation.
- **Mechanism**: Models trained on standard Bangla encounter distribution shifts in phonology, lexicon, and syntax when processing dialectal inputs, degrading representation and generation quality.
- **Core assumption**: Standard-language-centric pretraining yields brittle cultural-linguistic understanding.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract (“performance drops under dialectal variation, especially for caption generation”); Section 5 (RQ1) (“dialectal robustness is substantially weaker for free-form generation than for answer-constrained reasoning”); corpus (Neighbors, ANCHOLIK-NER) highlighting scarcity of dialect resources.
- **Break condition**: If VLMs are exposed to diverse dialectal data during pretraining or fine-tuning, the performance gap narrows.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting improves VQA accuracy by scaffolding intermediate reasoning.
- **Mechanism**: CoT forces the model to verbalize visual observations and cultural associations before selecting an answer, reducing hallucination and improving knowledge retrieval.
- **Core assumption**: Explicit reasoning bridges the gap between visual perception and cultural knowledge.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract (“CoT prompting consistently improves VQA”); Section C.2 (“CoT yields the largest gains in knowledge-heavy domains”); corpus (CultureScope) indirectly supports CoT value for structured reasoning.
- **Break condition**: For tasks with minimal reasoning depth, CoT may add overhead without performance gain.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The dominant bottleneck in Bengali cultural understanding is missing cultural knowledge, not visual grounding.
- **Mechanism**: Models can detect visual entities but lack fine-grained factual associations (e.g., specific dishes, historical personalities), leading to errors in knowledge-intensive domains.
- **Core assumption**: Visual recognition is sufficient; cultural semantics must be retrieved from memory.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract (“main bottleneck is missing cultural knowledge, not visual grounding”); Table 3 (knowledge-intensive domains have highest difficulty scores); CoT helps most in those domains (Section C.2); corpus (WorldCuisines, Do You Know About My Nation?) similarly identify knowledge gaps.
- **Break condition**: With enriched cultural training data, knowledge gaps shrink significantly.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM)**: Why needed: BANGLAVERSE evaluates VLMs; understanding their architecture (image encoder + LLM) is essential. Quick check: How does a VLM fuse visual features with textual generation?
- **Dialectal Variation**: Why needed: The core contribution is multidialectal evaluation; knowing linguistic variation is key to interpreting results. Quick check: What distinguishes a dialect from a standard language in terms of vocabulary and syntax?
- **Chain-of-Thought Prompting**: Why needed: CoT is a primary experimental condition; grasping its mechanism is necessary to understand performance differences. Quick check: Why might CoT improve performance on knowledge-intensive VQA?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: 
  Data collection → Human annotation → Multilingual expansion (Gemini-2.5-Flash) → Dialect generation (fine-tuned Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct) → Post-verification (Gemini + human) → Evaluation (BERTScore, LLM-as-a-Judge, accuracy)
- **Critical path**: Annotation → Dialect model fine-tuning → Artifact generation → Verification → Evaluation.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Manual curation vs. scale: 1,152 images ensure quality but limit coverage.
  - Automatic translation vs. authenticity: Gemini preserves meaning but may lose nuance; human verification mitigates.
  - Dialect model: Fine-tuning on BanglaDial yields native-like outputs but depends on dialect data availability.
  - Metrics: BERTScore captures semantics; LLM-as-a-Judge enables multi-aspect scoring but may inherit judge biases.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Sudden performance drop in dialect settings indicates poor generalization to regional forms.
  - High LLM-as-a-Judge scores but low human agreement suggest misalignment of the judge model.
  - Caption quality consistently lower than VQA points to challenges in free-form cultural generation.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Zero-shot evaluation across 4 languages (Bangla, English, Hindi, Urdu) to establish baseline cross-lingual transfer.
  2. Compare standard Bangla vs. five dialect performances to quantify robustness to linguistic variation.
  3. Ablate prompting strategies (zero-shot, few-shot, CoT) to identify the most effective inference approach for each task.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Scaling BANGLAVERSE to more images and additional modalities (video, audio)** while maintaining annotation quality and cultural authenticity. Current work does not address a scalable methodology; resolving requires case studies on scaling culturally grounded benchmarks and protocols for multi-annotator validation.
2. **Community-driven refinement of dialectal annotations** to capture fine-grained regional nuances. The paper lacks concrete strategies for engaging native speakers, iterating on feedback, and measuring refinement impact on evaluation fairness.
3. **Why dialectal variations cause a much larger performance drop in caption generation than in VQA**. Possible factors include open-ended nature of captioning, differential exposure to dialectal data, or models’ reliance on surface forms; evidence needed includes comparative error analysis and representation probing.
4. **Why historically linked languages (Hindi, Urdu) preserve Bengali cultural meaning in captions but underperform in VQA**. The divergence may stem from translation quality, shared descriptive lexicon, or task-specific knowledge requirements; evidence needed includes error breakdowns and cross-linguistic semantic analysis.

## Limitations
- Human validation of generated dialect artifacts and LLM-as-a-Judge outputs is limited (400 samples), potentially affecting reliability.
- Benchmark scale (1,152 images) is modest, restricting coverage; the paper does not resolve how to scale to larger volumes or additional modalities.
- Findings may not generalize to other language-dialect clusters due to unique historical and political contexts.

## Confidence
- Dialect robustness claim: **Medium** confidence (strong evidence from Table 5 and Section 5, but break condition not tested).
- CoT effectiveness claim: **Medium** confidence (consistent VQA gains, but indirect support and lack of reasoning depth ablation).
- Knowledge bottleneck claim: **High** confidence (robustly supported by domain difficulty scores, CoT gains, and prior work alignment).

## Next Checks
1. **Dialect quality verification**: Recruit native speakers for each dialect to rate a stratified sample (n=100 per dialect) of captions/VQA on fluency and cultural authenticity (3-point scale); compare with human-annotated BanglaDial.
2. **Judge model bias audit**: For 200 instances, collect human evaluations of caption quality across all linguistic variants; compute Spearman’s correlation with LLM-as-a-Judge scores and analyze variance by language/dialect.
3. **Cross-linguistic replication**: Apply the BANGLAVERSE pipeline to a historically linked low-resource pair (e.g., Bengali-Assamese) to test if the dialect robustness gap and knowledge bottleneck patterns hold.
