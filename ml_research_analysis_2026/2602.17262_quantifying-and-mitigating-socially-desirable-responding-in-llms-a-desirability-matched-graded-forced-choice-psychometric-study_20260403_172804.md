# Quantifying and Mitigating Socially Desirable Responding in LLMs: A Desirability-Matched Graded Forced-Choice Psychometric Study

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17262
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17262
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Desirability-matched graded forced-choice (GFC) questionnaires substantially attenuate socially desirable responding in LLMs compared to Likert scales, while largely preserving the ability to recover ground-truth personality traits.

## Executive Summary
Self-report questionnaires used to evaluate large language models (LLMs) are vulnerable to socially desirable responding (SDR), where models inflate scores toward an idealized profile. This study quantifies SDR by comparing IRT-estimated latent traits from the same inventory administered under honest versus fake-good instructions. To mitigate SDR, the authors construct a graded forced-choice (GFC) inventory with statement pairs matched on social desirability across the Big Five domains via constrained optimization. Evaluating nine instruction-tuned LLMs on synthetic personas, they find that Likert scales show consistently large SDR, while GFC reduces SDR to near-zero levels with acceptable trait recovery. The results reveal a model-dependent trade-off between SDR mitigation and trait signal preservation.

## Method Summary
The study first estimates item-level social desirability scores for a 98-item IPIP Big Five pool using GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro. Using these scores, a 30-block graded forced-choice (GFC) inventory is constructed via mixed-integer optimization to minimize within-pair desirability gaps while balancing trait domains and keying. Fifty synthetic personas are generated from a multivariate normal distribution based on human Big Five correlations. Nine LLMs are prompted to respond to both a 60-item 7-point Likert version and the 30-pair GFC version under "HONEST" and "FAKE-GOOD" instructions. Responses are analyzed with IRT: a multidimensional graded response model (GRM) for Likert and an ordinal Thurstonian model for GFC. Socially desirable responding is quantified as a direction-corrected Cohen’s *dz* on IRT-estimated latent traits (θ_fake – θ_honest), and ground-truth recovery is the Pearson correlation between honest θ and persona targets.

## Key Results
- Likert questionnaires produce consistently large, positive SDR effect sizes (dz) across all nine LLMs.
- Desirability-matched GFC reduces SDR substantially, with effect sizes often near zero.
- GFC maintains acceptable convergent validity with persona targets (r ≥ 0.50 for most models), indicating preserved trait signal.
- A model-dependent SDR–recovery trade-off exists: models with large Likert SDR show the greatest attenuation with GFC, but some experience moderate recovery loss.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Item Response Theory (IRT) latent trait estimation provides a common, format-agnostic metric to quantify socially desirable responding (SDR) as an instruction-induced shift.
- **Mechanism:** IRT models (GRM for Likert, ordinal Thurstonian for GFC) estimate continuous latent traits (θ) from ordinal responses. By fitting a single model across all LLMs and both instruction conditions (HONEST vs. FAKE-GOOD), the same latent scale is used. The SDR effect size (direction-corrected Cohen’s *dz*) is computed on these latent shifts, isolating response distortion from format-specific scaling.
- **Core assumption:** True latent traits for a given persona are stable across instruction conditions; observed shifts in IRT estimates primarily reflect SDR rather than changes in the underlying persona.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states SDR is computed from IRT-estimated latent scores. Section 3.3 details the multidimensional GRM and ordinal Thurstonian IRT models, emphasizing a "single, coherent measurement backbone" for cross-format comparison. Model fit diagnostics (e.g., item discrimination >0, ordered thresholds) support reliable latent estimates.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Desirability-matched graded forced-choice (GFC) attenuates SDR by preventing LLMs from uniformly selecting the socially preferred option within a pair.
- **Mechanism:** Cross-domain statement pairs are constructed via constrained optimization to minimize within-pair desirability gaps (using LLM-estimated item desirability scores *sj*). When an LLM tries to "fake good," it cannot consistently choose the more desirable statement because both options in a pair are equally desirable on average (mean gap 0.03 on 1–9 scale, max 0.18). The forced-choice comparison forces trade-offs, disrupting a global shift toward socially desirable items.
- **Core assumption:** LLM SDR under Likert operates by endorsing items based on their absolute social desirability. Matching desirability within pairs removes this simple heuristic, forcing the model to reveal its baseline trait preferences.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.2 describes the two-stage mixed-integer optimization achieving tight desirability matching. Section 5.2 reports that desirability-matched GFC substantially attenuates SDR across most models, with reduction strongest for models showing largest Likert SDR.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** GFC largely preserves ground-truth recovery (correlation with persona targets) while reducing SDR, creating a model-dependent SDR–recovery trade-off.
- **Mechanism:** Because GFC uses comparative judgments modeled by Thurstonian IRT, the resulting latent traits are on a normative scale, not ipsative. The desirability-matched pairs ensure that the comparison signal still reflects trait-relevant variance (e.g., an extravert vs. introvert statement pair). Thus, IRT scoring can recover rank-order trait differences from personas, even though absolute score levels are less distorted by SDR.
- **Core assumption:** The trait-relevant signal in Big Five items is orthogonal or only weakly correlated with social desirability after matching; sufficient cross-domain variability remains for IRT to estimate traits.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 5.3 notes most GFC points remain in acceptable-to-strong convergent-validity bands (r ≥ 0.50). Figure 4 shows the SDR–recovery trade-off: switching from Likert to GFC shifts models left (lower SDR) with moderate recovery loss.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Item Response Theory (IRT)**
  - **Why needed:** IRT is the measurement backbone that enables latent trait estimation from ordinal responses (Likert or forced-choice) and allows a common scale for comparing SDR across formats. Without IRT, raw scores are format-specific and ipsative in forced-choice.
  - **Quick check:** Why can't we simply compare mean Likert scores between HONEST and FAKE-GOOD conditions to quantify SDR?
- **Concept: Social desirability as a response style**
  - **Why needed:** The study treats SDR as a systematic distortion where respondents (LLMs) shift toward normatively favorable items. Understanding this as a *style*—separate from the trait itself—is key to designing mitigation (matching desirability).
  - **Quick check:** If an LLM consistently rates all items as "Very Accurate" in FAKE-GOOD, what does that tell us about the limitation of raw scores?
- **Concept: Forced-choice psychometrics & Thurstonian IRT**
  - **Why needed:** Standard forced-choice scoring produces ipsative data; Thurstonian IRT (modeling latent utilities for each statement and their differences) is essential to recover normative trait scores from comparative judgments, which is the core of the GFC mitigation.
  - **Quick check:** Why is it problematic if we score a forced-choice inventory by simply counting how often each trait’s statement is chosen?
- **Concept: Direction-corrected effect size (Cohen’s dz)**
  - **Why needed:** SDR is directional for Big Five traits (high desirability for Agreeableness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Openness; low for Neuroticism). Direction-correction aligns effect sizes to a common "toward desirability" metric for comparison across traits and models.
  - **Quick check:** How does direction-correction avoid canceling out positive and negative SDR across traits when averaging?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Desirability Rater LLMs (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) -> Optimization Engine (ompr/ROI with Gurobi) -> Stimulus Generator (persona descriptions, instruction injection) -> Target LLM (e.g., GPT-5, Claude) -> IRT Scorer (Stan models: GRM for Likert, ordinal Thurstonian for GFC) -> Metric Calculator (SDR dz, recovery r).
- **Critical path:** Desirability estimation -> GFC pair selection -> LLM response collection -> IRT model fitting (pooled across conditions/formats) -> latent trait extraction -> SDR/recovery computation. The desirability matching step is the keystone; without it, GFC cannot attenuate SDR.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - SDR vs. Recovery: Tighter desirability matching (smaller within-pair gaps) reduces SDR but may reduce trait variance, lowering recovery.
  - Format equivalence: GFC uses 30 pairs vs. 60 Likert items; this imbalance may advantage Likert in recovery. A fairer comparison would match number of responses (e.g., 60 Likert items vs. 60 GFC items from 60 pairs).
  - Human-LLM comparability: Human SDR benchmarks use heterogeneous instruments; direct comparison requires a desirability-matched GFC administered to humans with the same Thurstonian IRT.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High SDR in GFC (|dz| > 0.5) => Desirability matching failed; pairs have substantial desirability gaps.
  - Low recovery in GFC (r < 0.50) => Pairs are too desirability-matched; trait signal is lost, or IRT model mis-specified.
  - Inconsistent LLM desirability ratings (low ICC between rater LLMs) => Unreliable sj inputs, leading to poor pair selection.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Validate desirability matching:** After constructing the GFC inventory, compute all within-pair desirability gaps (Δsd). Confirm max gap ≤ 0.2 and mean ≈ 0 (as in paper: max 0.18, mean 0.03).
  2. **Check IRT model convergence & fit:** For each response format, inspect Stan diagnostics (R-hat < 1.01, ESS > 400). Examine item discrimination parameters (aj > 0) and ordered thresholds (κ strictly increasing). Low discrimination or reversed thresholds indicate poor item functioning.
  3. **Replicate SDR–recovery pattern:** For a single LLM, compute dz and r for both formats. Expect: (a) Likert dz large and positive; (b) GFC dz near zero; (c) GFC r moderately high (≥0.5). If GFC dz remains large, revisit desirability scores; if GFC r drops below 0.5, examine pair diversity (domain/keying balance).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does SDR in LLMs compare to human SDR when using the same desirability-matched GFC inventory and IRT scoring? *Basis:* Human reference comes from heterogeneous meta-analysis; a direct, design-matched human–LLM study is needed.
- **Open Question 2:** What aspects of model training (e.g., RLHF, safety fine-tuning) drive model-specific SDR magnitudes and the SDR–recovery trade-off under forced-choice? *Basis:* Results show model-dependent SDR but do not connect it to documented training details.
- **Open Question 3:** Does desirability-matched GFC retain its SDR-mitigation advantage when response effort (number of judgments) is equated with Likert? *Basis:* The current comparison confounds format with total response volume (60 Likert items vs. 30 GFC pairs).
- **Open Question 4:** Do other psychometric constructs (e.g., values, cognitive styles) exhibit similar SDR patterns and mitigation via desirability-matched forced-choice? *Basis:* Study focuses solely on Big Five; generalizability to other inventories is unknown.
- **Open Question 5:** Why do some models retain non-negligible SDR under desirability-matched GFC, and can residual SDR be further reduced? *Basis:* Results show model-dependent residual SDR without mechanistic explanation.

## Limitations
- Persona generation details (52 markers, stanine mapping) are unspecified; approximations may affect realism.
- API parameters (temperature, top_p) unreported; could influence response variability.
- IRT pooling strategy ambiguous; impacts latent scale comparability.
- Format imbalance (60 Likert vs 30 GFC) may bias recovery.
- Sample limited to 9 high-capability models; generalizability uncertain.

## Confidence
- **IRT metric:** High – theory solid, model fit good.
- **GFC attenuates SDR:** Medium – empirical support but depends on matching quality; gaps >0.2 break mitigation.
- **GFC preserves recovery:** Medium – recovery mostly ≥0.5, but trade-offs observed; sensitive to pair diversity and format equivalence.

## Next Checks
1. **Desirability gaps:** Recalc within-pair gaps; confirm max ≤0.18, mean ≈0.03. Else re-optimize.
2. **IRT fit:** Refit models; check R-hat <1.01, ESS >400, discrim >0, thresholds ordered.
3. **Replication:** Pick 3 LLMs (low/med/high SDR), rerun. Expect: Likert dz >0.5, GFC dz ≈0, GFC r ≥0.5.
