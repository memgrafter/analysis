# Faster, Cheaper, More Accurate: Specialised Knowledge Tracing Models Outperform LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02830
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02830
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Specialized Knowledge Tracing models achieved up to 73% accuracy versus 58–66% for LLMs, with orders of magnitude lower latency (sub-second vs. minutes) and deployment cost (<$2 vs. $1,200–$25,000 annually for 100k students).

## Executive Summary
This study benchmarks specialized Knowledge Tracing (KT) models against state-of-the-art Large Language Models (LLMs) for predicting student future responses on an educational platform. KT models (DKT, SAKT, and a custom LLM-KT hybrid) consistently outperformed all evaluated LLMs (including GPT-4o-mini, Gemini-2.5-flash-lite, and fine-tuned Llama-1B) on primary metrics of accuracy and F1 score. The superiority stems from KT's architecture, which is explicitly designed to model evolving student knowledge states over time, combined with extreme parameter efficiency. Consequently, KT models deliver sub-second inference at negligible cloud cost, making them the practical choice for scalable, real-time prediction in EdTech systems, while general-purpose LLMs prove prohibitively slow and expensive despite their broader capabilities.

## Method Summary
The task is binary classification: predict whether a student will answer a future question correctly, given their history of up to 49 prior question-response pairs. Inputs include historical and target question/construct IDs, text, correctness labels, and optional metadata like misconception explanations. Models compared include classic KT architectures (DKT using RNNs, SAKT using self-attention), a custom "LLM-KT" hybrid (using frozen Qwen embeddings fed to a small temporal transformer), and various LLMs via zero/few-shot prompting or LoRA fine-tuning. Evaluation on a real-world math platform dataset (12.8k training, 1.6k validation students) uses Accuracy and macro-F1 as primary metrics, with secondary measurement of inference latency per student and extrapolated annual deployment cost for 100k students.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy/F1 Superiority:** Best KT model (LLM-KT) achieved 72.8% accuracy; SAKT 72.7%. Top LLM (fine-tuned Llama-1B) reached 71.0%, while others ranged 58–66%.
- **Latency Advantage:** KT models processed a student's history in ~0.1 seconds; LLMs ranged from 3 seconds (GPT-4o-mini) to over 3,000 seconds (Qwen2.5-7B).
- **Cost Advantage:** Annual deployment cost for KT models was estimated <$2 vs. $1,200–$25,000 for LLM-based solutions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Explicit Temporal Modeling
KT models (DKT, SAKT) process the full sequence of past interactions as a time-ordered series, updating a latent knowledge state that directly informs the next prediction. Their architectures (RNN or self-attention) are built to capture dependencies across a student's trajectory. LLMs lack this intrinsic temporal mechanism; they treat each prediction as an isolated reasoning task over a static prompt, leading to superficial pattern matching without a evolving state representation. Evidence: KT models are defined by sequential input processing [section 3.4]; LLM prompts provide history as text without enforcing temporal updates. Break condition: If interactions are non-sequential or extremely sparse, the temporal advantage may diminish; LLMs could gain an edge with rich meta-cognitive cues.

### Mechanism 2: Parameter Efficiency
KT models use sub-million parameters in compact architectures (simple embeddings + shallow recurrent/attention layers), enabling CPU-friendly, sub-second inference. LLMs have billions of parameters, requiring expensive matrix operations often on GPUs, yielding minute-scale latency and high API/compute costs. The paper's cost analysis shows a 600–12,000× difference. Evidence: Latency figures [section 4.2–4.3] show DKT: 0.09s, SAKT: 0.10s; GPT-4o-mini: 3.1s, Qwen2.5-7B: 3298.8s. Annual cost extrapolation [abstract]. Break condition: If budget and latency constraints are relaxed (e.g., offline small-cohort analysis), LLMs become more viable.

### Mechanism 3: Clean, Discrete Signal
KT models use discrete, domain-specific IDs (question, construct) as inputs, providing a noise-free, direct mapping from interaction patterns to outcomes. LLMs must parse verbose natural language prompts (question text, descriptions, explanations), introducing variability and potential misinterpretation of subtle pedagogical nuances. Evidence: DKT/SAKT use only IDs [section 3.4]; LLMs receive full text prompts. Even LoRA-fine-tuned Llama-1B (71.0%) trailed SAKT (72.7%) and LLM-KT (72.8%), showing text-based fine-tuning cannot fully recover the simplicity of ID-based modeling [section 4.1]. Break condition: If question text is minimal or IDs are unavailable, LLMs might gain; if required reasoning extends beyond ID correlations, LLMs could parlay.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Raw Student Logs -> Data Pipeline (filtering, windowing) -> Feature Encoder (ID embeddings for KT; text prompts for LLMs) -> Model Serving (KT on CPU; LLM via API/GPU) -> Monitoring (accuracy, latency, cost)

**Critical path:**
1. New student response arrives → update history buffer.
2. For each pending future question, format input (ID sequence or text prompt).
3. Run model inference → obtain binary prediction.
4. Log prediction and performance metrics.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Accuracy vs. Efficiency:* KT models trade potential LLM reasoning gains for massive speed/cost wins.
- *Structured vs. Unstructured Inputs:* IDs provide clean signal but require maintained mappings; text prompts are flexible but noisy and slow.
- *In-house vs. API:* Hosting KT models on commodity CPUs avoids vendor lock-in and extreme costs; LLM APIs simplify scaling but are expensive.

**Failure signatures:**
- *Accuracy drop:* Model performance falls below dataset bias (66.5%) on validation.
- *Latency increase:* Per-student inference exceeds expected thresholds (e.g., >1s for KT, >API baseline for LLMs).
- *Cost spike:* Monthly bills exceed forecast; often due to unbatched requests or prompt bloat.
- *Format violation:* LLM outputs non-YES/NO responses; requires prompt tightening or parsing fallback.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline replication:** Train DKT and SAKT on the provided dataset; verify accuracy ~72% and latency <0.25s on CPU.
2. **LLM API smoke test:** Send 10 prompts to GPT-4o-mini and Gemini-2.5-flash-lite; measure latency, cost, and format adherence; confirm accuracy ~60%.
3. **Hybrid KT implementation:** Build the custom LLM-KT (Qwen embeddings cached + small transformer); compare its accuracy/latency against DKT/SAKT to isolate the effect of text-based features.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** What is the upper bound of predictive performance achievable by LLMs on student response prediction when given optimal prompting, fine-tuning, and architecture? *Basis:* The paper compares a fixed set of LLMs with standard LoRA and prompting. *Why unresolved:* No exploration of advanced prompting (e.g., chain-of-thought), larger/recent LLMs, or exhaustive hyperparameter tuning. *What evidence would resolve it:* Comprehensive ablation testing state-of-the-art LLMs with varied strategies, benchmarked against KT ceilings (~73%).

**Open Question 2:** To what extent can hybrid models that integrate LLMs with knowledge tracing architectures close the performance gap while maintaining scalability? *Basis:* The paper's "LLM-KT" uses a frozen LLM only as a feature extractor, not for temporal reasoning. *Why unresolved:* True end-to-end hybrids (e.g., LLM as sequence model) are not evaluated. *What evidence would resolve it:* Direct comparison of multiple hybrid architectures (LLM-as-encoder, LLM-as-sequence-model) against pure KT and pure LLMs, measuring accuracy-latency-cost trade-offs.

**Open Question 3:** Are KT models' superior performance and efficiency consistent across diverse educational domains and varying dataset characteristics? *Basis:* Study uses one math platform dataset with specific properties (~65% bias, 4k questions). *Why unresolved:* Extrapolation to other subjects (history, language) or noisier/sparser data lacks evidence. *What evidence would resolve it:* Replication across multiple public KT datasets from different subjects (ASSISTments, Duolingo, MOOCs) with varying sparsity and taxonomies.

**Open Question 4:** Can the temporal student modeling capability of KT models be distilled or transferred into a smaller, more deployable LLM without significant loss of accuracy? *Basis:* Fine-tuned Llama-1B (71.0%) trailed SAKT (72.7%) but had high latency (~1600s). *Why unresolved:* No exploration of distillation, quantisation, or other compression techniques on KT models for LLM transfer. *What evidence would resolve it:* Apply model compression (distillation, quantisation) to best KT models or the LLM-KT hybrid; evaluate if compressed LLM-based model matches KT accuracy with lower latency.

**Open Question 5:** What specific architectural or inductive biases in KT models are irreplaceable by general LLM architectures for this task? *Basis:* Empirical gap exists but models differ in architecture (RNN/Transformer vs. LLM) and scale (0.6M vs. 1B+ params). *Why unresolved:* The contribution of temporal architecture vs. discrete representation vs. model scale is not isolated. *What evidence would resolve it:* Ablation controlling for scale/architecture: (a) tiny Transformer on IDs vs. text; (b) large Transformer on IDs; (c) RNN on text embeddings.

## Limitations
- Missing hyperparameters and training details for KT models prevent exact replication.
- Data preprocessing specifics (window size, filtering, embedding pooling) are undisclosed.
- LLM evaluation uses a single prompt template; performance may be sensitive to prompt engineering.
- Latency/cost measurements depend on one Azure instance type and current API pricing.
- Generalizability is limited to a single math dataset; other subjects or sparser patterns may weaken KT's advantage.
- Fine-tuning was only tested on a 1B-parameter LLM; larger fine-tuned LLMs could narrow the accuracy gap.

## Confidence
- **Accuracy/F1 superiority of KT models:** High. Consistent gaps across multiple LLM baselines with clear metrics.
- **Latency and cost superiority:** High. Orders-of-magnitude differences under defined, reproducible conditions.
- **Proposed mechanisms (temporal modeling, parameter efficiency, discrete ID signal):** Medium. Explanations align with architectural differences but are not experimentally isolated; break conditions indicate potential edge cases.
