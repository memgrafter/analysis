# Ontology-Based Knowledge Modeling and Uncertainty-Aware Outdoor Air Quality Assessment Using Weighted Interval Type-2 Fuzzy Logic

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.19683
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19683
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** A hybrid framework combining weighted interval type-2 fuzzy logic with a semantic OWL ontology achieves 78% F1-score and reduces MAE to 0.242 by explicitly modeling uncertainty and health-impact weights in AQI classification.

## Executive Summary
Traditional Air Quality Index (AQI) computation uses crisp thresholds that misclassify values near regulatory boundaries due to inherent measurement uncertainty and gradual pollution transitions. This paper proposes a hybrid framework that integrates weighted interval type-2 fuzzy logic (IT2FL) with an ontology-based semantic model. IT2 fuzzy sets model second-order uncertainty via a footprint of uncertainty (FOU), while IT2-FAHP assigns importance weights to pollutants based on health impact. An OWL ontology extends the SSN standard to represent pollutants, AQI categories, health risks, and governance actions, with SWRL rules enabling explainable inference. Validated on CPCB data, the system outperforms baseline approaches, offering a robust, interpretable solution for outdoor air quality monitoring.

## Method Summary
The framework processes CPCB air quality data (PM2.5, PM10, CO, O3, NO2, SO2, NH3) through several stages. First, trapezoidal IT2 membership functions map pollutant concentrations to interval membership degrees across six AQI linguistic categories, explicitly capturing boundary uncertainty. A rule base of IF-THEN statements combines these fuzzy sets. Pollutant weights are derived via IT2-FAHP using expert pairwise comparisons, yielding a dominance order (PM2.5 > PM10 > CO > O3 > NO2 > SO2 > NH3). During inference, each rule’s firing interval is multiplied by the highest-priority pollutant’s weight. The weighted outputs undergo Karnik-Mendel type-reduction and defuzzification to produce a crisp AQI. This numerical result populates an OWL ontology (extending SSN), where SWRL rules propagate knowledge to infer health impacts and recommended mitigation actions (e.g., industrial shutdown). Performance is evaluated via classification metrics (precision, recall, F1) and error measures (MAE, RMSE), with semantic correctness validated through SPARQL queries.

## Key Results
- 78% precision, 78% recall, 77% accuracy, and 78% F1-score on AQI category prediction.
- MAE of 0.242 and RMSE of 0.525 between predicted and ground-truth AQI categories.
- The system outperforms classic ontology-based and type-2 fuzzy indoor approaches, demonstrating effective uncertainty management and explainable decision support.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Interval Type-2 fuzzy sets explicitly model uncertainty in pollutant concentration boundaries, improving AQI classification reliability near regulatory thresholds.
- **Mechanism:** Trapezoidal IT2 membership functions define an upper and lower membership degree (Footprint of Uncertainty, FOU) for each pollutant-AQI category pair. A crisp pollutant value yields an interval membership `[µ_lower, µ_upper]` instead of a single value, capturing sensor noise, measurement vagueness, and gradual transitions between classes (e.g., Moderate/Poor boundary).
- **Core assumption:** Real-world air quality transitions are inherently gradual; crisp thresholds cause misclassification near breakpoints.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "Interval Type-2 fuzzy sets are used to model uncertainty near AQI class boundaries."
  - Section 4.2: "The region enclosed between the UMF and LMF constitutes the footprint of uncertainty (FOU), which explicitly captures the imprecision associated with pollutant concentration boundaries."
- **Break condition:** Poorly calibrated FOUs (e.g., parameters in Table 1 not matching local pollutant distribution) lead to overly broad intervals and ambiguous classifications.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** IT2-FAHP assigns health-impact-aware weights to pollutants, prioritizing high-toxicity pollutants during aggregated AQI reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** Experts use a linguistic scale to build a fuzzy pairwise comparison matrix. IT2-FAHP computes fuzzy geometric means, defuzzifies (DTraT method), checks consistency, and normalizes to yield crisp weights. During weighted rule firing, the pollutant with the highest weight in a rule’s antecedents multiplies the rule’s firing interval, ensuring dominant health impact representation.
- **Core assumption:** Pollutants contribute unequally to health outcomes; equal weighting underrepresents high-risk pollutants like PM2.5.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "pollutant importance weights are determined using Interval Type-2 Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (IT2-FAHP) to reflect their relative health impacts."
  - Section 4.4: "PM2.5 has been identified as the most harmful pollutant... dominance order: PM2.5 > PM10 > CO > O3 > NO2 > SO2 > NH3."
- **Break condition:** An inconsistent pairwise matrix (CR ≥ 0.1) or biased expert judgments misrepresent health priorities, skewing AQI toward less critical pollutants.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** An OWL ontology (extending SSN) with SWRL rules enables semantic reasoning that maps raw pollutant data to explainable AQI categories, health risks, and governance actions.
- **Mechanism:** The ontology models `Sensor`, `Observation`, `Pollutant`, `AirQualityIndex`, `HealthImpact`, and `RegulatoryAction`. SWRL rules infer implicit knowledge: e.g., if `hasPM10Category = Severe`, then `hasAQICategory = Severe` and `hasRecommendedAction = IndustrialShutdown`. SPARQL queries validate inferred triples, linking numeric AQI to policy and health semantics.
- **Core assumption:** Structured, machine-interpretable knowledge representation enhances transparency, interoperability, and decision traceability compared to black-box outputs.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "An OWL-based air quality ontology extending the Semantic Sensor Network (SSN) ontology... Semantic reasoning is implemented using SWRL rules... infer AQI categories, health risks, and recommended mitigation actions."
  - Section 5.3: Example SWRL rule shows chained inference from `hasPM10Category(PM10_Severe)` to `hasRecommendedAction(IndustrialShutdown)`.
- **Break condition:** Incomplete SWRL rules (e.g., missing meteorological interactions) or flawed SSN alignment cause reasoning failure to capture real-world complexity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Interval Type-2 Fuzzy Sets (IT2FS)**
  - *Why needed:* To model second-order uncertainty—uncertainty about membership functions themselves—arising from sensor noise, expert vagueness, and spatial variability. IT2FS uses a 3D membership volume (UMF/LMF) rather than a 2D curve.
  - *Quick check:* What is the Footprint of Uncertainty (FOU), and how does it differ between Type-1 and Type-2 fuzzy sets?

- **Concept: Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (FAHP)**
  - *Why needed:* To systematically derive pollutant importance weights from expert judgments while accommodating linguistic vagueness via trapezoidal IT2 fuzzy numbers, avoiding crisp pairwise comparisons that lose nuance.
  - *Quick check:* How does the defuzzification method (DTraT, Eq. 7) convert an IT2 fuzzy number into a crisp value for consistency checking?

- **Concept: OWL Ontologies & SWRL Rules**
  - *Why needed:* To create a formal, shared model of air quality knowledge (pollutants, stations, AQI, actions) and automate inference via rule-based reasoning, enabling queries that connect measurements to policy outcomes.
  - *Quick check:* What is the role of the SSN ontology’s `Observation` and `FeatureOfInterest` classes in structuring air quality data?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Data Preprocessing -> IT2 Fuzzification -> Rule Base -> Weight Evaluation (IT2-FAHP) -> Weighted Inference (KM algorithm) -> Ontology Population -> Semantic Reasoning (SWRL) -> Validation (SPARQL)

- **Critical path:** Raw CPCB data → IT2 fuzzification → weighted rule firing → crisp AQI → ontology population → SWRL inference → actionable output (`hasRecommendedAction`). Any break in fuzzy inference or rule completeness halts semantic propagation.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Computational cost vs. accuracy:* IT2FS + KM algorithm is slower than Type-1 but necessary for boundary uncertainty. Ontology reasoning (Pellet) adds overhead but enables explainability.
  - *Expert dependency vs. adaptability:* IT2-FAHP relies on expert pairwise comparisons; automated weighting (e.g., from SHAP values) could reduce bias but lose health rationale traceability.
  - *Generalization vs. specificity:* CPCB-specific breakpoints limit direct transfer to EPA/WHO AQIs without remapping membership parameters.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - High MAE/RMSE despite fuzzy modeling → misconfigured FOUs (Table 1 parameters not matching local distribution).
  - Low ontology reasoning coverage → insufficient/contradictory SWRL rules or poor data population.
  - Inconsistent IT2-FAHP weights (e.g., NH3 > PM2.5) → erroneous pairwise matrix or failed consistency check.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. *Membership calibration test:* Plot pollutant concentration histograms against UMF/LMF bounds (Table 1). Adjust parameters if >20% of values fall completely outside all FOUs.
  2. *Weight sensitivity test:* Vary IT2-FAHP input judgments ±1 linguistic scale unit (Table 4) and observe AQI classification changes for boundary cases. Ensure PM2.5 weight remains dominant (>0.3).
  3. *Reasoning completeness test:* Execute SPARQL Query 2 to list all observations triggering `ConstructionBan`. Manually verify a sample that should trigger but doesn’t—likely a missing SWRL antecedent (e.g., `hasWeatherType(Wind)` condition).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can the fuzzy rule base and membership functions be automatically adapted to regional and temporal variations without expert intervention?** Future work proposes adaptive learning to refine rules and parameters from streaming sensor data, but the current framework relies on static expert definitions.
- **What architectural modifications are needed to integrate large language models (LLMs) with the ontology-fuzzy system for real-time natural language decision support?** Challenges include semantic alignment, real-time performance, and preserving explainability in a generative AI layer.
- **How can the framework be systematically generalized to other international AQI standards (e.g., WHO, US EPA) while maintaining accuracy and regulatory compliance?** The adaptation process is unspecified; different standards have distinct breakpoints and category structures requiring a clear reconfiguration methodology.
- **Are the static pollutant weights from IT2-FAHP robust across diverse geographical and seasonal contexts, or do they require dynamic adjustment based on real-time factors?** Weings are derived from general health impacts and ignore local factors like emission sources or meteorology, potentially biasing AQI outcomes in heterogeneous environments.

## Limitations
- **Incomplete semantic rule base:** The exact SWRL rule set for full health/governance propagation is not fully enumerated, blocking full replication of the reasoning layer.
- **Ambiguous weighted aggregation:** The precise method for combining multiple weighted rule outputs before type-reduction is unspecified (per-rule weighting vs. global aggregation), creating a critical gap in the fuzzy inference pipeline.
- **Local parameter dependency:** Membership functions and expert weights are calibrated for CPCB/Indian data; generalization to other regulatory frameworks (EPA, WHO) requires remapping and is unvalidated.

## Confidence
- **Performance metrics (78% F1, MAE 0.242): High.** Values are explicitly reported from experiments on a specified dataset; replication depends on implementing the ambiguous aggregation step.
- **Uncertainty modeling via IT2FS near boundaries: Medium.** The mechanism is theoretically sound, but lacks ablation isolating the quantitative contribution of FOU width versus Type-1 fuzzy logic.
- **Health-impact weighting via IT2-FAHP: Medium.** The process is detailed and weights align with known toxicity, but sensitivity to expert judgments is unexplored.
- **Semantic reasoning for explainability: Low.** While ontology structure and one SWRL example are shown, completeness and soundness of the full rule base for all categories and effects are unverified.
