# P-GenRM: Personalized Generative Reward Model with Test-time User-based Scaling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12116
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12116
- **Reference count:** 11
- **Primary result:** P-GenRM sets new state-of-the-art on PersonalRewardBench (avg accuracy +2.31%) and achieves up to 75.92% on Chatbot Arena-Personalized, while outperforming much larger models on sparse-feedback QA (0.638 Spearman vs. Qwen3-235B-A22B's 0.619).

## Executive Summary
P-GenRM addresses the challenge of personalizing large language model (LLM) alignment to individual user preferences. Existing reward models often fail because they oversimplify user preferences into static criteria and generalize poorly to new users with limited feedback. P-GenRM transforms hybrid preference signals (historical selections and explicit criteria) into structured evaluation chains that infer scenario-specific personas and adaptive scoring rubrics. It further introduces a dual-granularity test-time scaling mechanism: at the individual level, it aggregates multiple generated scoring schemes; at the prototype level, it incorporates scores from similar users via learned user prototypes. Trained through a three-stage framework (SFT, RL, curriculum), P-GenRM establishes a new state-of-the-art on the PersonalRewardBench benchmark, with test-time scaling yielding additional gains. Notably, on out-of-distribution sparse-feedback QA, it surpasses much larger models, demonstrating effective generalization.

## Method Summary
P-GenRM’s training pipeline consists of three stages. First, **SFT (Persona-guided Scoring Induction)** constructs a dataset of Structured Evaluation Chains (SECs) by prompting a strong LLM (o3) to generate a persona `P(u)_t` and a weighted scoring rubric `S(u)_t` from user history and explicit criteria. The model is fine-tuned to produce such chains via next-token prediction, with rejection sampling to ensure the final extracted scores match the ground-truth preference label. Second, **RL (Criteria-based Reasoning Enhancement)** refines the model using the GRPO algorithm with a mixed reward: a process reward (`PR`) from an LLM-as-a-judge assesses how well the chain covers the user’s preferences, while an outcome reward (`OR`) evaluates the correctness of the extracted score. Third, **Curriculum Learning** gradually increases the proportion of hard negatives (samples where the model’s score difference is small) and eventually disables the process reward to focus on outcome accuracy.

For personalization and generalization, P-GenRM builds an offline prototype system. User preference embeddings derived from `P(u)_t` are clustered (K-means, k=50) into prototypes, which are refined via a history-aware attentive mechanism that weights relevant past interactions. At test time, for a query, the model generates `m` individual scoring schemes (parallel sampling) and retrieves `n` similar users from the same prototype to incorporate their scores. The final reward is the average of all `m+n` extracted scores, reducing noise and leveraging group consensus.

## Key Results
- **State-of-the-art on PersonalRewardBench:** P-GenRM improves average accuracy by 2.31% over prior methods.
- **Test-time scaling gains:** Adding user-based scaling yields an extra 3% improvement, reaching up to 75.92% on Chatbot Arena-Personalized.
- **Strong generalization on sparse feedback:** On out-of-distribution LaMP-QA, it achieves 0.638 Spearman correlation, outperforming much larger models such as Qwen3-235B-A22B (0.619).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Transforming hybrid preference signals into structured, scenario-specific evaluation chains (personas + weighted rubrics) improves scoring accuracy over static criteria.
- **Mechanism:** P-GenRM uses a three-stage training process (SFT, RL, curriculum) to generate an explicit "evaluation chain" from user history and explicit cues. This chain first infers a context-aware `P(u)_t` (persona/core preferences) and then derives `S(u)_t` (a weighted rubric). The final score is computed by applying this rubric to candidate responses.
- **Core assumption:** User preferences are dynamic and context-dependent; a static, fixed set of evaluation dimensions cannot capture this variability within or across users.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "transforms hybrid preference signals into structured evaluation chains that infer scenario-specific personas and adaptive scoring rubrics."
  - [section 4.1] Describes the Persona-guided Scoring Induction (SFT) stage that builds the SEC dataset and trains the model to produce `[P(u)_t ; S(u)_t]`.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers like "SynthesizeMe!" and "CoPL" also move toward dynamic/user-specific preferences, suggesting this is an active research direction.
- **Break condition:** If the generated evaluation chains become overly complex, inconsistent, or fail to align with ground-truth human judgments on scenario-specific data, the mechanism breaks down.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Clustering users into prototypes and refining them with historical attention creates transferable preference priors, enabling generalization to new users with sparse feedback.
- **Mechanism:** User preference embeddings `P(u)_t` are clustered offline (K-means). During test-time, a new user is assigned to the nearest prototype. During prototype refinement (offline), each prototype's representation `aj` is updated using a History-aware Attentive mechanism that weighs historical records relevant to the current query, followed by a discriminative loss with regularization (Eq 6-7). This makes prototypes effective, smooth priors.
- **Core assumption:** Users with similar preference embeddings exhibit sufficiently similar evaluation patterns that a prototype's refined prior can guide scoring for an unseen member of that cluster, even with little personal history.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "clusters users into User Prototypes... enhances generalization to unseen users through prototype-based transfer."
  - [section 4.2.1] Details the History-aware Attentive Prototype Refinement with equations and loss function.
  - [section 5.4] Outperforms much larger models on LaMP-QA with sparse feedback, demonstrating generalization.
- **Break condition:** If the user embedding space is not semantically meaningful, or if prototype clusters are too coarse (mixing incompatible preferences) or too fine (no meaningful similarity), the transfer fails.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Dual-granularity test-time scaling (aggregating multiple individual schemes + incorporating similar users' scores) reduces noise in inferred preferences and boosts performance.
- **Mechanism:** At test-time for user `u`, P-GenRM performs `m` parallel samplings to generate multiple individual scoring schemes `S_i^t,x`. Separately, it finds `n` similar users in the same prototype and incorporates their scores `(S_i^t)^(uw)`. The final score `s_i^t` is the average of all `m+n` extracted scores (Eq 8-9).
- **Core assumption:** (1) Multiple samples from the same user's conditional distribution explore a range of plausible preference interpretations, and averaging reduces stochastic noise. (2) Scores from similar users provide a consensus signal that corrects individual overfitting or hallucination.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "dual-granularity scaling mechanism: at the individual level... at the prototype level... reduces noise and enhances generalization."
  - [section 4.2.2] Formalizes the two scaling operations.
  - [Table 2] Shows consistent gains from adding both individual (Ind) and prototype (Pro) scaling, with optimal combinations (e.g., Ind-16, Pro-8) outperforming larger Ind alone (Ind-32).
- **Break condition:** If individual sampling does not produce meaningful diversity (mode collapse), or if similar users' preferences are actually divergent from the target user's, aggregation introduces harmful noise instead of signal.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Generative Reward Models (GenRM)**  
**Why needed here:** P-GenRM builds directly on the GenRM paradigm, which uses LLMs to generate textual evaluation chains (process + outcome) rather than output a scalar. Understanding GenRM's test-time scalability (via sampling/aggregation) is prerequisite to grasping P-GenRM's dual-granularity scaling.  
**Quick check question:** How does generating multiple reasoning traces and averaging their extracted scores provide robustness compared to a single forward pass outputting a score?

**Concept: User Preference Embedding & Clustering**  
**Why needed here:** The entire prototype system relies on embedding user preference states (`P(u)_t`) into a vector space where similarity implies shared evaluation criteria. K-means clustering is the offline operation that creates the prototypes.  
**Quick check question:** What are the risks if the embedding model (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) does not preserve the semantic nuances of user preferences in the vector space?

**Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) & GRPO**  
**Why needed here:** The Criteria-based Reasoning Enhancement stage uses the GRPO algorithm with a mixed reward (process + outcome) to refine the model's ability to generate high-quality evaluation chains even when explicit criteria are missing. This is a core training technique.  
**Quick check question:** Why is a process reward (`PRt`) necessary alongside an outcome reward (`ORt`) for this subjective, generative task?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input Encoder -> Preference Analyzer (P(u)_t generator) -> Scoring Scheme Generator (S(u)_t generator) -> Extract function -> Prototype Database -> Test-time Scaling Controller

**Critical path:**  
User input (qt, H(u)_t, E(u)) -> generate [P(u)_t, S(u)_t] via SFT/RL-trained model -> extract individual score -> retrieve similar users from Prototype DB (based on P(u)_t embedding) -> generate their scores for same query/response pairs -> aggregate all scores (individual + prototype) -> final personalized reward.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Expressiveness vs. Noise:* Longer evaluation chains capture nuance but are harder to generate consistently and parse.
- *Compute vs. Personalization:* More individual samples (m) and more similar users (n) improve robustness but increase inference cost.
- *Prototype granularity:* Too few prototypes risk averaging out idiosyncrasies; too many harm generalization for sparse-feedback users.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Incoherent `P(u)_t`: Generic, contradictory, or ungrounded personas.
- Rubric `S(u)_t` mismatch: Derived weights/criteria illogical or inconsistent with persona.
- Prototype misassignment: User clustered with a prototype of opposite preferences.
- Scaling degradation: Increasing m or n reduces performance, indicating lack of diversity or flawed similarity.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Validate Persona Extraction:** Use a labeled subset with ground-truth personas; measure semantic similarity (e.g., BERTScore) between model-generated `P(u)_t` and ground-truth. Run LLM-as-a-judge to assess plausibility given history.
2. **Test Individual-Level Scaling in Isolation:** For a fixed user, vary m=1,4,8,16 without prototype scaling. Plot accuracy vs. m and check rubric consistency (e.g., Jaccard similarity of top criteria across samples).
3. **Validate Prototype Assignment & Utility:** For a held-out user with sparse history, manually compare nearest prototype’s preferences with user’s known choices. Ablate: compare (Ind-8, Pro-0) vs. (Ind-8, Pro-4); significant increase confirms prototype utility.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper leaves several key questions unresolved:
1. How does the optimal number of user prototypes scale with larger and more diverse user populations, and what is the theoretical relationship between prototype count and personalization performance?
2. What is the minimal number of historical preference interactions required for P-GenRM to achieve effective personalization, and how does performance degrade with fewer or noisier interactions?
3. How well does P-GenRM generalize across fundamentally different domains (e.g., from QA to coding or creative generation) where preference dimensions and evaluation rubrics may differ significantly?
4. What is the trade-off between the interpretability of structured evaluation chains and the accuracy of reward scores, compared to opaque scalar reward models?
5. How stable are the learned user prototypes over time as user preferences drift, and what is the optimal frequency for updating the prototype clustering?

## Limitations
- **Missing implementation details:** The paper lacks critical specifics for faithful reproduction, including the exact prompt and filtering method for SEC data generation, hyperparameters for prototype refinement, GRPO training settings, and the judge model for process reward.
- **Sensitivity to embedding/clustering:** The prototype mechanism depends heavily on the quality of user preference embeddings and the choice of cluster count; poor embedding semantics or suboptimal k can break transfer.
- **Generalizability concerns:** Results are demonstrated on a limited set of datasets (chat, QA). Performance on other domains or with drastically different user populations remains unverified.

## Confidence
- **High:** Evaluation chains (SFT/RL) capture user preferences; reported SOTA results on benchmarks.
- **Medium:** Prototype-based transfer for sparse feedback (sensitive to embedding/clustering); Dual-granularity scaling reduces noise (requires careful tuning).

## Next Checks
