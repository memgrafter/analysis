# From High-Dimensional Spaces to Verifiable ODD Coverage for Safety-Critical AI-based Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.02198
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02198
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: Proposes a structured method to transform abstract ODD definitions into verifiable coverage metrics; applied to VerticalCAS case study, reduced state space by 59.7% and identified 2.62% initial coverage in constrained space.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical regulatory gap: how to prove complete Operational Design Domain (ODD) coverage for AI-based safety-critical systems, as mandated by aviation authorities like EASA. It introduces a structured engineering process that converts continuous ODD parameters into discrete bins, applies constraint-based filtering to exclude implausible states, and then iteratively tests coverage. Using a VerticalCAS collision avoidance case study with 1.97 million simulation points, the method demonstrates a 59.7% reduction in the relevant state space and pinpoints existing coverage gaps (2.62%), enabling systematic scenario generation to drive toward completeness.

## Method Summary
The method bridges abstract ODD definitions to verifiable completeness evidence through three core steps. First, continuous ODD parameters are discretized into bins; bin resolution is tied to a criticality measure, transforming the high-dimensional space into a finite Cartesian product of bins. Second, domain knowledge and physical models define constraints (e.g., kinematic limits, logical exclusions) that prune the theoretical bin combinations to a safety‑relevant subset. Third, a scenario database is mapped onto the constrained bin space, and coverage is computed as the fraction of covered relevant bins. If coverage is incomplete, the missing bin combinations are reverse‑engineered into new scenario parameters, executed, and the process repeats until the constrained space is exhaustively covered.

## Key Results
- Reduced unconstrained state space by 59.7% (from 195,200 to 78,688 bin combinations) through two manually defined constraints.
- Initial coverage of only 2.62% within the constrained space, revealing significant gaps in the existing test dataset.
- Demonstrated an iterative loop that can systematically target missing bin combinations to incrementally increase coverage toward completeness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Discretization
- **Claim:** Discretizing continuous ODD parameters into finite bins transforms an intractable high-dimensional space into a combinatorially finite set of states that can be exhaustively evaluated for coverage.
- **Mechanism:** Each parameter is divided into bins based on a criticality measure (uniform or varying). The Cartesian product of all bins defines the discrete state space. A bin combination is "covered" if at least one executed scenario falls within its ranges.
- **Core assumption:** The chosen bin resolution is sufficient to capture safety‑relevant variations without being computationally prohibitive.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "integrates parameter discretization... into a structured, multi-step ODD coverage verification process."  
  [section 3] "Continuous parameters make exhaustive coverage... infeasible. The bin size is determined by the criticality c of each parameter... The overall discretized parameter space is then given by the Cartesian product B = B1 × B2 × ... × Bn."
- **Break condition:** If bin sizes are too coarse, safety‑critical sub‑ranges may be masked; if too fine, the combinatorial explosion makes analysis infeasible or requires unrealistic scenario volumes.

### Mechanism 2: Constraint Filtering
- **Claim:** Constraint-based filtering excludes implausible or low‑criticality parameter combinations from the theoretical state space, drastically reducing the verification burden while focusing on relevant safety regions.
- **Mechanism:** Domain knowledge or analytical models define logical/mathematical constraints (e.g., τ‑dependent h limits, removal of diverging vertical encounters). These constraints prune the Cartesian product space to a "relevant" subset.
- **Core assumption:** The defined constraints accurately capture all non‑critical or physically impossible states without inadvertently omitting safety‑critical edge cases.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "constraint‑based filtering to exclude implausible or low‑criticality states."  
  [section 4] "Constraints can be introduced to exclude certain parameter combinations... Both constraints further reduced the state space... by 59.7%."
- **Break condition:** Mis‑specified constraints (too broad/too narrow) either retain irrelevant states, wasting effort, or remove critical states, creating false confidence in coverage.

### Mechanism 3: Iterative Loop
- **Claim:** An iterative loop, where uncovered bin combinations are translated into new scenario parameters and re‑executed, systematically drives the coverage metric toward completeness.
- **Mechanism:** After coverage testing, any missing bin combinations in the *relevant* constrained space are identified. These are inverse‑mapped to continuous parameter values to generate new test scenarios. The dataset is augmented, and coverage analysis repeats until the relevant space is fully covered.
- **Core assumption:** New scenarios can be realistically generated for any uncovered bin combination and that the underlying system's behavior is deterministic enough for the same bin to be consistently covered by a single instance.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract] "identifying untested regions for iterative scenario generation until completeness is achieved."  
  [section 3] "If uncovered regions remain, the analysis produces a list of missing bin combinations... By translating these combinations back into physical values, new scenarios can be created..."  
  [section 4] "Iteratively generating and executing such scenarios allows coverage to be systematically increased, ultimately achieving complete coverage."
- **Break condition:** Scenario generation fails to produce valid/executable cases for certain bins, or the system exhibits non‑determinism requiring multiple runs per bin to confirm coverage.

## Foundational Learning

### Operational Design Domain (ODD)
**Why needed here:** The entire method is built upon a formally defined ODD. Understanding its role as the boundary of safe operation (per EASA) is prerequisite to defining the parameter space and constraints.
**Quick check:** What is the difference between an ODD's *operational envelope* (parameter ranges) and its *operational conditions* (combinations/interactions), and why does EASA require proof of completeness for the latter?

### Curse of Dimensionality in Coverage
**Why needed here:** The method's primary motivation is combating exponential state‑space growth. Recognizing why brute‑force or naive sampling fails for completeness is essential to appreciating discretization and constraint steps.
**Quick check:** If you have 10 ODD parameters each with just 10 bins, how many total bin combinations exist? Why is checking even 1% of them likely insufficient for completeness?

### Cartesian Product Coverage Metric
**Why needed here:** The core completeness criterion is rcov = |B_relevant, covered| / |B_relevant| = 1. This requires understanding that coverage is defined over *joint* parameter bins, not marginal coverage of each parameter alone.
**Quick check:** A test suite covers all values of parameter A and all values of parameter B individually. Is it guaranteed to cover all (A,B) bin combinations? Explain why or why not.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
ODD Definition Module → Binning Engine → Constraint Definition Module → Coverage Analyzer → (if rcov < 1) → Scenario Parameter Generator → new scenario execution → back to Coverage Analyzer; Iteration Controller orchestrates the loop.

### Critical Path
ODD Definition → Binning Engine → Constraint Module → (defines B_relevant) → Coverage Analyzer (on scenario DB) → if rcov < 1 → Scenario Generator → new scenario execution → back to Analyzer.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Bin size vs. Resolution/Compute:** Finer bins increase resolution but multiply |B|. Requires balancing safety granularity with computational feasibility.
- **Constraint strictness vs. Safety risk:** Aggressive constraints greatly reduce |B_relevant| but risk omitting low‑probability, high‑consequence states. Requires conservative domain expertise.
- **Independence assumption:** The Cartesian product metric assumes parameter independence for coverage definition, which may overstate needed coverage if strong statistical dependencies exist.

### Failure Signatures
- **Persistently low coverage** despite many scenarios: Indicates B_missing contains physically plausible but untested multi‑parameter interactions. Triggers deeper constraint review or dimension reduction.
- **Unexpected bin combinations in B_missing:** May reveal flawed constraints (too strict) or an incomplete ODD definition (missing a controlling variable).
- **Non‑converging iteration:** Generator cannot produce valid scenarios for certain B_missing bins. Suggests constraints are insufficient or generation logic needs physical plausibility checks.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Synthetic sanity check:** Use a 3‑parameter ODD with known ground truth constraints. Inject a synthetic dataset covering only a subset. Verify the analyzer correctly identifies B_missing and that generated scenarios fill the gaps.
2. **Replicate VerticalCAS case study:** Follow the paper's exact bin sizes and constraints on the provided 1.97M‑point dataset. Reproduce Table 2 metrics. This validates the pipeline's implementation.
3. **Sensitivity analysis on binning:** Take one key parameter (e.g., τ). Halve and double its bin count while keeping others fixed. Observe impact on |B|, |B_relevant|, and computed coverage. Quantify the trade‑off between resolution and combinatorial explosion.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can complex dependency structures (e.g., beyond simple kinematic constraints) be effectively modeled and integrated into the coverage metric for very high‑dimensional ODDs?
- **Basis in paper:** The conclusion explicitly states that "Future work will focus on modeling dependency structures in larger ODDs." Section 5 also notes that for larger ODDs, "advanced stages...become essential" and that vine copulas may become relevant.
- **Why unresolved:** The current case study relies on simple, deterministic constraints because the VerticalCAS state space is low‑dimensional. The paper acknowledges that for higher‑dimensional, more stochastic ODDs, methods like vine copulas are needed but not yet integrated or validated within their proposed engineering workflow.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A follow‑up study applying the complete method (including copula‑based dependency modeling) to a significantly higher‑dimensional ODD (e.g., >10 parameters) from a different safety‑critical domain. This would require demonstrating that the dependency model computationally reduces the feasible space without eliminating safety‑critical combinations, and that the resulting coverage metric remains valid and certifiable.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the quantitative impact of using non‑uniform, simulation‑derived criticality distributions (instead of the assumed uniform distribution) on the binning strategy and the resulting completeness metric?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper explicitly assumes uniform criticality for demonstration ("a uniform criticality distribution is assumed") and notes that "in a more detailed process, simulation‑based analyses could also be used to determine the distribution of criticality." The methodological step of "criticality‑based dimension reduction" is introduced but not quantitatively explored with real criticality data.
- **Why unresolved:** The uniform assumption is a major simplification. Criticality is likely highly non‑uniform across parameter ranges (e.g., small τ with large h is far more critical). Using uniform bins may either miss critical gaps (bins too large in critical zones) or be computationally wasteful (bins too small in non‑critical zones). The sensitivity of the coverage result to this binning choice is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study using the same VerticalCAS dataset but applying different criticality‑informed binning schemes (e.g., finer bins in high‑criticality regions derived from a surrogate safety model or simulation). Comparing the number of required bins, the constrained state space size, and the identified coverage gaps would show how robustness and efficiency depend on the criticality model.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the constraint definition step be systematically supported or automated to reduce reliance on explicit domain expert knowledge, and what are the risks of over‑constraining the ODD?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper states that "defining appropriate constraints is generally non‑trivial and requires significant domain knowledge." It shows two manually defined constraints (τ‑dependent h envelope, diverging encounter exclusion). The process flow lists "Constraints Definition" as a core step but does not specify a method beyond expert analysis.
- **Why unresolved:** Manual constraint definition does not scale to complex ODDs and introduces subjectivity. An automated or semi‑automated method (e.g., learning constraints from simulation data of near‑misses or failures) could improve consistency. However, such automation risks inadvertently excluding valid but rare safety‑critical edge cases (over‑constraining), which would directly violate the completeness requirement.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and testing of an algorithm that proposes candidate constraints (e.g., logical rules, boundary functions) based on clustering of failed/scarce scenarios. Validation would involve comparing coverage metrics and safety argumentation strength between manually defined constraints and algorithmically suggested ones, ensuring that no known safety‑critical scenarios are excluded and that the final constrained space is provably a subset of the true ODD.

## Limitations
- Criticality‑based bin sizing lacks a systematic derivation; choosing bin counts remains heuristic.
- Constraint definition relies on domain expert knowledge with no guidance on ensuring completeness or avoiding over‑pruning.
- Parameter independence assumption in the Cartesian product metric may misrepresent true ODD interactions; the paper’s optional dependency modeling is not demonstrated.
- Scalability to high‑dimensional ODDs (>10 parameters) or fine binning can cause combinatorial explosion despite constraints.
- The case study reports low initial coverage (2.62%) and does not show full convergence, leaving iterative effectiveness unproven.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Discretization): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (Constraint Filtering): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (Iterative Loop): Medium
