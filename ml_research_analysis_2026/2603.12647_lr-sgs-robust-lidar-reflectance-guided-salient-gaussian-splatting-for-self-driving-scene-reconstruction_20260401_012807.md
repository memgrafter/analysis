# LR-SGS: Robust LiDAR-Reflectance-Guided Salient Gaussian Splatting for Self-Driving Scene Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12647
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12647
- Reference count: 37
- Primary result: LR-SGS achieves state-of-the-art performance on the Waymo Open Dataset, surpassing OmniRe by 1.18 dB PSNR on Complex Lighting scenes, while using fewer Gaussians (2.51M vs 2.74M), shorter training time (59m25s vs 67m11s), and higher FPS (36.95 vs 30.55).

## Executive Summary
LR-SGS tackles the underutilization of LiDAR reflectance in self-driving scene reconstruction by introducing a lighting-invariant reflectance channel and structure-aware Salient Gaussians. The method calibrates raw LiDAR intensity into reflectance and attaches it to each Gaussian, providing a stable supervisory signal under varying illumination. Salient Gaussians are initialized from geometric and reflectance feature points extracted from the LiDAR point cloud and employ a compact anisotropic parameterization to efficiently capture edges and planes. A joint RGB-reflectance gradient consistency loss aligns cross-modal boundaries, sharpening details. Experiments on the Waymo Open Dataset demonstrate state-of-the-art novel view synthesis, particularly in the challenging Complex Lighting scenes, with improved efficiency over prior arts like OmniRe.

## Method Summary
LR-SGS processes synchronized multi-camera RGB sequences and LiDAR point clouds (with intensity). It first calibrates LiDAR intensity to a lighting-invariant reflectance (Eq. 1-2) and projects it to each camera to obtain sparse reflectance images and their gradients. Feature points are extracted: geometric edges (high smoothness), geometric planes (low smoothness), and reflectance edges (high reflectance gradient). These points initialize Salient Gaussians, which have a compact anisotropic covariance: one dominant scale (σ∥) along a key direction (edge or planar normal) and a shared secondary scale (σ⊥). Non-Salient Gaussians are initialized from SfM points. During rendering, each Gaussian outputs color, depth, and reflectance via α-blending. The loss combines RGB reconstruction (L1 + D-SSIM), LiDAR depth and reflectance (L1) including reflectance gradient supervision, and a novel joint gradient consistency loss (L_joint) that aligns grayscale RGB gradients with reflectance gradients in direction and magnitude. An adaptive Salient Transform upgrades or downgrades Gaussians based on linearity/planarity metrics, and density control splits Gaussians according to their type. Training proceeds for 30k iterations with a scene graph handling background, dynamic objects, and sky.

## Key Results
- Surpasses OmniRe by 1.18 dB PSNR on Complex Lighting scenes.
- Uses fewer Gaussians: 2.51M vs OmniRe’s 2.74M.
- Shorter training time: 59m25s vs 67m11s.
- Higher rendering FPS: 36.95 vs 30.55.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Reflectance Channel as Lighting-Invariant Supervision
- **Claim:** Attaching calibrated LiDAR reflectance provides a stable supervisory signal under complex lighting, reducing RGB reconstruction artifacts.
- **Mechanism:** Raw LiDAR intensity is calibrated using distance and incident angle (Eq. 1-2). Each Gaussian carries a reflectance attribute supervised by L1 loss against the projected reflectance image (L_fle) and its gradient (L'_fle). Reflectance is approximately independent of lighting variations, anchoring material boundaries where RGB may be corrupted.
- **Core assumption:** Calibrated reflectance remains constant for the same surface material under varying camera lighting.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Sec III.A describe calibration and loss; neighbors use depth/geometry but not reflectance as material channel.
- **Break condition:** fails for specular/absorbing materials or calibration errors due to noisy normals.

### Mechanism 2: Structure-Aware Salient Gaussians
- **Claim:** Salient Gaussians, initialized from LiDAR geometric and reflectance feature points, efficiently capture edges and planes with fewer parameters than isotropic Gaussians.
- **Mechanism:** Salient Gaussians use compact anisotropy: a dominant scale (σ∥) along a key direction (edge/flattened normal) and a shared secondary scale (σ⊥). Initialization selects high-confidence points: geometric edges (high smoothness variation), geometric planes (low smoothness), and reflectance edges (high reflectance gradient). This builds an initial scaffold aligned with scene structure.
- **Core assumption:** LiDAR feature points correlate strongly with perceptual edges/planes in RGB.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Sec III.B detail feature extraction and covariance; corpus shows neighbors lack such feature-guided init.
- **Break condition:** In low-density or featureless regions (smooth dark surfaces), Salient Gaussians may be poorly initialized, relying on later Salient Transform.

### Mechanism 3: Joint RGB-Reflectance Gradient Consistency Loss
- **Claim:** The joint loss L_joint aligns cross-modal boundaries, sharpening material edges and reducing blur in thin structures.
- **Mechanism:** Rendered RGB is converted to grayscale C^g. Both C^g and rendered reflectance F are smoothed, then gradients computed via Scharr operator. L_joint minimizes direction mismatch (1 - cos similarity of normalized gradients) and magnitude mismatch (L1 on normalized magnitudes). This forces co-located high gradients in reflectance and grayscale to coincide.
- **Core assumption:** True material/geometric boundaries produce high gradients in both modalities; alignment improves RGB fidelity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions cross-modal boundary alignment; Sec III.D.3 defines L_joint with L_dir and L_val; neighbors use depth/point losses, not cross-modal gradient alignment.
- **Break condition:** Noisy reflectance gradients or loss of color-defined boundaries in grayscale conversion could misalign or over-smooth details.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** LiDAR+RGB → Preprocessing (reflectance calibration & feature extraction) → Salient Gaussian initialization (from LiDAR features) / Non-Salient initialization (from SfM) → Forward rendering (α-blending) → Loss computation (RGB, depth, reflectance, reflectance-gradient, joint) → Optimization → Adaptive control (Salient Transform, density control) → Final scene.

- **Critical path:** Reflectance calibration → Feature point extraction → Salient Gaussian initialization → Rendering → Multi-loss backpropagation → Salient Transform & density control iterations.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Salient Gaussian parameter reduction vs. expressiveness for complex structures.  
  - Early strong LiDAR prior vs. risk of overfitting to sparse/noisy points in uncovered regions.  
  - Cross-modal gradient loss improves boundaries but adds computational cost.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Blurry boundaries → check L_joint weights or reflectance calibration.  
  - Lack of structural details → monitor Salient Gaussian count; adjust thresholds or feature extraction.  
  - Training instability → reduce learning rates, ensure reflectance normalization.  
  - Floating sky artifacts → inadequate sky modeling.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Ablate Salient Gaussians:** Train without feature-guided initialization or Salient Transform (only Non-Salient from raw LiDAR/SfM). Expected: lower PSNR/SSIM, especially at edges; more Gaussians; slower convergence.  
  2. **Ablate Reflectance Channel:** Remove all reflectance-related losses and attribute. Expected: significant PSNR drop in Complex Lighting scenes; blurrier boundaries; higher LPIPS.  
  3. **Ablate Joint Loss:** Train with only L_rgb and L_depth (drop L_joint). Expected: degradation in boundary consistency; vehicles appear blurrier; reflectance rendering less accurate.

## Limitations
- Scene graph dynamics: How object masks associate with Gaussian nodes and represent per-frame motions (rigid transforms vs. deformation) is unspecified, hindering dynamic scene reconstruction.  
- Optimizer configuration: Missing learning rates and schedules for Gaussian attributes and sky model; reliance on 3DGS defaults may not match reported training time/speed.  
- Sky model definition: The composition equation uses *C_sky* but its representation (learned per-view, constant, etc.) and optimization are not described.
