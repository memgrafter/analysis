# DongYuan: An LLM-Based Framework for Integrative Chinese and Western Medicine Spleen-Stomach Disorders Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.28191
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28191
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** Developed an LLM framework (SSDF-Core-DPO) that achieved state-of-the-art performance on a new ICWM spleen-stomach benchmark, significantly outperforming baselines and improving consultation efficiency when augmented with a navigation model.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the lack of data and models for integrative Chinese and Western medicine (ICWM) diagnosis of spleen-stomach disorders. The authors curated three specialized datasets (syndrome, dialogue, preference pairs) and developed "DongYuan," a framework featuring a core LLM (SSDF-Core) fine-tuned via supervised learning and preference optimization, and a lightweight consultation navigation model (SSDF-Navigator). Evaluated on a new benchmark (SSDF-Bench), the best model, SSDF-Core-DPO, substantially outperformed 12 mainstream medical and general LLM baselines. When combined with the Navigator, it showed improved critical symptom recall and dialogue efficiency in multi-turn consultations.

## Method Summary
DongYuan is a two-component system. The **SSDF-Core** is based on Qwen3-14B and undergoes a two-stage training pipeline: first supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a mix of general, medical, and ICWM dialogue/syndrome data to establish clinical skills, followed by Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) on expert-annotated preference pairs to refine reasoning for integration, safety, and specificity. The **SSDF-Navigator** is a separate transformer-based model trained to predict the next most informative symptom inquiry. It uses a hybrid loss combining behavior cloning with an offline RL reward based on information gain (conditional entropy reduction). During multi-turn consultation, the Navigator is conditionally activated via a standardized symptom mapping; it proposes candidate next symptoms, and the Core selects the final inquiry from the union of its own generation and the Navigator's top candidates.

## Key Results
*   SSDF-Core-DPO achieved a total score of **0.8241** on the SSDF-Bench, outperforming 12 baselines by up to **22.6%**.
*   It demonstrated superior performance on the core ICWM syndrome differentiation task (DDSSD).
*   The combination of SSDF-Core-DPO and SSDF-Navigator improved critical symptom recall by **7.96%** and dialogue efficiency by **7.6%**.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Two-Stage Training Pipeline (SFT → DPO) for Robust ICWM Reasoning
The mechanism posits that first training the model on high-quality ICWM demonstration data (SFT) builds foundational clinical skills, while subsequent DPO on expert preference pairs calibrates the model's outputs towards nuanced, integrated TCM/WM reasoning and safety. The small DPO coefficient (β=0.1) is intended to prevent catastrophic forgetting of the SFT-acquired skills.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The paper explicitly describes this pipeline in the abstract and methodology section, detailing the datasets (SSDF-Syndrome, SSDF-Dialogue, SSDF-PD) and hyperparameters. The corpus supports the general use of SFT and DPO for medical LLM alignment but does not provide direct evidence for this specific ICWM design.
*   **Break condition:** Failure would be indicated by a drop in general medical knowledge scores after DPO (catastrophic forgetting) or a lack of improvement on the core differentiation task, suggesting the preference pairs failed to encode superior clinical judgment.

### Mechanism 2: Hybrid Training for Efficient Symptom Inquiry Policy
The SSDF-Navigator learns a policy for the next symptom question via a hybrid of Behavior Cloning (BC) and offline Reinforcement Learning (RL). The BC loss imitates expert trajectories. The RL reward is a manually shaped information-gain signal (normalized conditional entropy of symptom transitions) penalized by a repetition factor. This design aims to replicate expert strategic efficiency without requiring online patient interaction.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The methodology fully specifies the state representation (sliding window of standardized symptoms), reward computation (Eqs. 3-7), and hybrid loss (Eqs. 8-12). The high coverage (92%) of the core symptom list in the dialogue corpus suggests the standardization is comprehensive.
*   **Break condition:** The mechanism degrades if the standardized symptom mapping discards critical TCM semantic nuances or if the offline transition statistics are biased, leading to a rigid or suboptimal inquiry policy. This would manifest as lower critical recall or unnatural dialogue flows.

### Mechanism 3: Candidate-Based Coordination for Synergistic Consultation
A coordination module merges the strategic roadmap from the Navigator with the contextual understanding of the Core. When the Core's last question maps to a standard symptom, the Navigator generates top-5 candidate next symptoms from its learned policy. The Core then selects the final question from the union of these candidates and its own generation. This allows the Navigator's global view to propose options while the Core applies deep contextual reasoning to choose.
*   **Evidence anchors:** The abstract and section II.C.3 describe this synergy and the conditional activation logic. The ablation results (improved efficiency and recall) provide empirical support for the coordination's effectiveness.
*   **Break condition:** Coordination fails if the symptom mapping is inaccurate (preventing Navigator activation) or if the Core consistently ignores high-value Navigator candidates. This would be evident in ablation studies showing no performance gain when adding the Navigator.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**
    *Why needed here:* Enables alignment with complex expert preferences (integration, safety) without training a separate reward model, which is data-intensive. It directly optimizes the policy on chosen/rejected response pairs.
    *Quick check question:* How does using a fixed reference model (the SFT model) in the DPO loss prevent policy collapse to high-reward but low-diversity responses?
*   **Concept: Offline Reinforcement Learning with Reward Shaping**
    *Why needed here:* The Navigator must learn a questioning policy from static expert dialogues without real-time interaction. The manually designed information-theoretic reward provides a dense learning signal where environmental feedback (diagnostic success) is delayed or absent.
    *Quick check question:* Why compute conditional entropy `H(S|s_i)` over the *next* symptom given the *current* state, instead of the entropy of the full future trajectory?
*   **Concept: Multi-turn Consultation as a Partially Observable MDP (POMDP)**
    *Why needed here:* The true patient syndrome is hidden (partial observation). The Navigator's state (symptom history) and action (next inquiry) frame the consultation as a sequential decision problem where questions are taken to reduce uncertainty about the hidden state.
    *Quick check question:* If patient responses are mostly confirm/deny and have limited impact, why model it as an MDP rather than a static decision tree based on initial symptoms?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Raw Clinical Records -> SSDF-Syndrome, SSDF-Dialogue, SSDF-PD Datasets -> SSDF-Core (SFT -> DPO) & SSDF-Navigator (BC+RL) -> Coordination Module -> SSDF-Bench Evaluation
### Critical Path
1.  Curate datasets from real records with expert annotation.
2.  Train SSDF-Core-SFT on mixed data (4k samples) for 3 epochs.
3.  Train SSDF-Core-DPO on SSDF-PD (12.8k pairs) with SFT data for 2 epochs.
4.  Extract symptom sequences from SSDF-Dialogue; train Navigator with hybrid BC+RL loss.
5.  Implement candidate-based coordination logic (symptom mapping, candidate generation/merging).
6.  Evaluate integrated system on SSDF-Bench (500 samples) across 6 tasks.
### Design Tradeoffs
*   **Standardized symptom list vs. free-text:** Mapping to 83 symptoms enables Navigator training but may lose TCM-specific nuance (e.g., "赋" vs. "chest tightness"). Favors computational tractability over clinical fidelity.
*   **Hybrid BC+RL vs. pure BC:** RL reward encourages efficiency but requires careful shaping to avoid deviating from safe expert behavior. Adds complexity for a performance gain.
*   **Candidate-based selection vs. Navigator control:** Merging candidates preserves the Core's contextual authority but limits the Navigator's strategic imposition. Simpler coordination but may dilute guidance.
### Failure Signatures
*   **Navigator misactivation:** Low rate of successful symptom mapping (`q_{t-1}` to standard symptom) prevents Navigator engagement; check mapping accuracy on a validation dialogue set.
*   **DPO misalignment:** Drop in basic knowledge scores (TCM-BC/WM-BC) after DPO suggests catastrophic forgetting. Minimal gain on DDSSD indicates ineffective preference learning. Monitor validation task-specific scores during training.
*   **Coordination breakdown:** No performance difference between standalone Core and Core+Navigator on MPC metrics suggests Navigator candidates are ignored or ineffective. Log candidate selection rates in the merging step.
### First 3 Experiments
1.  **Ablation of training stages:** Compare base model, SSDF-Core-SFT, and SSDF-Core-DPO on SSDF-Bench total and DDSSD scores. Expect progressive gains, with DPO showing largest DDSSD improvement.
2.  **Navigator contribution:** Run SSDF-Core-DPO with and without Navigator on the MPC task. Measure `Recall_crit` and `Eff_dia`. Expect significant increases in both.
3.  **Judge model validation:** Use perturbed vs. clean expert responses to compute score drop (`S_drop`) for the LLM-as-a-Judge. Expect `S_drop` >40% for critical error dimensions (Hallucination-Free, Safety/Efficacy), confirming judge sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*Question:* How can the DongYuan framework be effectively extended to incorporate multimodal data (e.g., medical images, tongue/physical inspection data) to improve ICWM diagnosis?
*Basis in paper:* [explicit] The authors explicitly state in the conclusion: "SSDF-Core currently relies solely on text input and does not incorporate multimodal data that are valuable in ICWM diagnosis."
*Why unresolved:* Multimodal signs (e.g., tongue coating, pulse quality) are fundamental to TCM syndrome differentiation. The current text-only approach limits diagnostic completeness.
*What evidence would resolve it:* Development and evaluation of a multimodal variant of SSDF-Core that fuses textual consultation with image features. Performance would need to be benchmarked on SSDF-Bench or a new multimodal ICWM benchmark, showing statistically significant improvement in syndrome differentiation accuracy and/or critical symptom recall.

### Open Question 2
*Question:* To what extent does the SSDF-Navigator's fixed-length sliding window approach optimally balance historical context retention with the risk of discarding clinically relevant but temporally distant symptoms?
*Basis in paper:* [inferred] The SSDF-Navigator uses a fixed-length window `L` to model state (`state_t = [s_{t-L+1}, ..., s_t]`). The paper does not investigate the sensitivity of performance to the choice of `L` or explore dynamic mechanisms.
*Why unresolved:* An inappropriate window size could lose key diagnostic context (if too short) or introduce noise (if too long). The optimal window may depend on disease complexity.
*What evidence would resolve it:* An ablation study varying window length `L` on the MPC task, measuring impact on `Recall_crit` and `Eff_dia`. Comparing the fixed window against a learned attention mechanism or a clinically-guided gating strategy.

### Open Question 3
*Question:* How robust and clinically aligned is the LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation paradigm (used in SSDF-Bench) compared to assessments from a panel of expert ICWM physicians?
*Basis in paper:* [explicit] The authors validate their judge model against perturbed data and compute a Spearman correlation with expert ratings on a small sample (n=50). They do not provide a full-scale, multi-expert human evaluation benchmark.
*Why unresolved:* Correlation on 50 samples may not capture the full nuance of real clinical expert panels. The judge's scoring may not perfectly mirror expert judgment on complex, borderline cases.
*What evidence would resolve it:* A large-scale study where a diverse set of ICWM expert panels independently score a substantial subset (e.g., 200-500) of SSDF-Bench's subjective evaluation items. Reporting inter-expert agreement and correlation between LLM-judge and aggregated expert consensus scores.

### Open Question 4
*Question:* Does the two-stage training paradigm (SFT followed by DPO) for SSDF-Core generalize to other disease domains within ICWM, or is its effectiveness specific to the spleen-stomach disorder data?
*Basis in paper:* [inferred] The paper demonstrates success on spleen-stomach disorders. The methodological contribution is framed as a general "blueprint," but this claim is not tested. The unique integration logic may vary across domains.
*Why unresolved:* The curated datasets are highly specific to spleen-stomach disorders. The DPO preferences and Navigator's symptom space are tailored to this domain. Transferring would require reconstructing analogous datasets for a new domain.
*What evidence would resolve it:* Applying the same DongYuan pipeline to a different, well-defined ICWM disease domain (e.g., "ICWM insomnia diagnosis"). Successful replication—achieving SOTA on a new domain-specific benchmark and demonstrating improved consultation efficiency with a retrained Navigator—would confirm generalizability.

## Limitations
*   The standardized symptom mapping for the Navigator may discard nuanced TCM symptom semantics, potentially losing diagnostic differentiators.
*   The quality and inter-rater reliability of the SSDF-PD preference annotations are unreported, posing a risk of DPO misalignment.
*   The entropy-based information-gain reward for the Navigator lacks direct clinical validation and may induce unnatural inquiry sequences.
*   The framework is evaluated only on spleen-stomach disorders; its generalizability to other ICWM domains is untested.
*   Heavy reliance on LLM-as-a-judge for subjective evaluation, with limited large-scale human correlation data.

## Confidence
*   **Dataset & Benchmark Construction:** High (expert annotation with reported Kappa >0.82).
*   **Core Model Training (SFT->DPO) Pipeline:** Medium (ablation shows improvement, but DPO stability and catastrophic forgetting are not robustly checked across hyperparameters).
*   **Navigator Training (BC+RL) & Reward Design:** Medium (novel design with clear specification, but clinical validity of the entropy-based reward is assumed, not evidenced).
*   **Coordination Mechanism (Candidate Merging):** Medium (gains are observed in results, but the specific selection logic's robustness is underspecified).
*   **Overall Claim of Framework Superiority:** Medium (strong results on a new benchmark, but benchmark design and judge validation have limitations).

## Next Checks
1.  **Symptom Mapping Audit:** Sample 200 dialogue turns; have two independent TCM/WM experts map them to the 83-standard symptom list. Compute coverage and agreement versus the authors' mapping to identify potential semantic loss.
2.  **DPO Robustness Sweep:** Vary the DPO hyperparameter β (e.g., {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}) and number of DPO epochs (e.g., {1,2,3}) on a validation set. Track both the core DDSSD score and basic knowledge scores (TCM-BC/WM-BC) to detect catastrophic forgetting or ineffective alignment.
3.  **Navigator Generalizability Test:** Construct a test set with rare or out-of-distribution symptom combinations not prevalent in the training dialogues. Compare Navigator-guided vs. standalone Core on `Recall_crit` and `Eff_dia` to assess if the learned policy overfits to common trajectories.
