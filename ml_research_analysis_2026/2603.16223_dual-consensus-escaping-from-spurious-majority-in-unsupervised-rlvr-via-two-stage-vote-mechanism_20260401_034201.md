# Dual Consensus: Escaping from Spurious Majority in Unsupervised RLVR via Two-Stage Vote Mechanism

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.16223  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16223  
- **Reference count**: 20  
- **Primary result**: DCRL improves Pass@1 over majority-vote unsupervised RLVR methods, reaching 79.2% on MATH-500 with Qwen3-8B-Base, comparable to supervised GRPO (82.0%) without using labels.

## Executive Summary
Unsupervised reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) for large language models often converges to spurious majority answers, limiting reasoning gains. This paper proposes Dual Consensus Reinforcement Learning (DCRL), a two-stage vote mechanism that generates reliable learning signals without supervision. By first producing anchor responses that reflect dominant patterns, then applying temporary unlearning to create an explorer that generates diverse outputs, DCRL selects final pseudo-labels via the harmonic mean of both distributions. Adaptive sampling further stabilizes training based on consensus rates. Evaluations on eight benchmarks with models up to 8B parameters show consistent Pass@1 improvements over majority-vote baselines.

## Method Summary
DCRL operates in two phases. First, a model fine-tuned with RLVR generates *anchor responses* that capture high-reward but potentially spurious patterns. Then, a temporary unlearning step distills the model into an *explorer* that produces diverse, lower-reward candidates to break from dominant modes. For each query, responses from both stages are aggregated, and a pseudo-label is selected using the harmonic mean of their reward distributions to balance reliability (anchors) and diversity (explorer). Training updates use these pseudo-labels, with adaptive sampling that modulates the proportion of anchor vs. explorer responses based on their consensus rate to maintain stability.

## Key Results
- DCRL improves Pass@1 over majority-vote unsupervised RLVR on all eight tested benchmarks.
- On MATH-500, DCRL achieves 79.2% with Qwen3-8B-Base vs. 78.3% for TTRL, matching supervised GRPO (82.0%) despite zero labels.
- Adaptive sampling stabilizes training and prevents collapse to spurious modes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The two-stage mechanism escapes spurious majorities by decoupling reliability from exploration. Anchor responses provide a high-reward starting point, but alone they reinforce dominant (potentially incorrect) patterns. The explorer, created via unlearning, introduces alternative solutions that may have lower immediate rewards but higher long-term diversity. Using the harmonic mean of both reward distributions for selection prioritizes responses that are consistently good across both stages, filtering out extremes and avoiding overfitting to either spurious anchors or noisy explorer outputs. Adaptive sampling further ensures the model does not over-rely on one distribution, maintaining a balance that supports stable self-improvement.

## Foundational Learning
- **Unsupervised RLVR**: Training LLMs with RL using automatically verifiable rewards without human labels. *Why needed*: Enables self-improvement when labeled data is scarce. *Quick check*: Does the method avoid manual annotation?
- **Spurious Majority**: When models converge to high-frequency but incorrect answers due to reward over-optimization. *Why needed*: Undermines true reasoning progress. *Quick check*: Are diverse correct answers present in the output distribution?
- **Two-Stage Voting**: Generating signals from separate anchor and explorer models then combining them. *Why needed*: Balances exploitation and exploration. *Quick check*: Does combining distributions improve robustness over single-stage?
- **Harmonic Mean Selection**: Choosing pseudo-labels by harmonic mean of reward scores from both stages. *Why needed*: Penalizes extreme scores, favoring consensus. *Quick check*: Is performance better than using arithmetic mean or max?
- **Temporary Unlearning**: Distilling a model to forget specific patterns before generating explorer outputs. *Why needed*: Breaks adherence to spurious modes. *Quick check*: Does the explorer show higher diversity than the anchor?
- **Adaptive Sampling**: Adjusting the mix of anchor and explorer responses during training based on consensus rate. *Why needed*: Prevents training instability from distribution shifts. *Quick check*: Does varying the mix correlate with improved convergence?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Initial RLVR Model -> Anchor Response Generation -> Temporary Unlearning -> Explorer Model -> Dual Consensus Selection (Harmonic Mean) -> Adaptive Sampling -> Training Update

**Critical path**:  
1. Train initial model with RLVR to produce anchor responses.  
2. Apply unlearning to create explorer model.  
3. For each query, gather responses from both models and compute harmonic mean rewards.  
4. Select pseudo-labels based on harmonic mean ranking.  
5. Compute adaptive sampling weights from consensus rate.  
6. Update model with chosen pseudo-labels and sampling weights.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Reliability vs. Diversity: Anchors ensure high reward but risk spurious modes; explorers add diversity but may be noisy. Harmonic mean mediates.  
- Stability vs. Exploration: Adaptive sampling maintains stability but may slow adaptation if thresholds are mis-set.  
- Computational Cost: Two models and harmonic scoring increase inference overhead vs. single-stage voting.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Low consensus rate between anchor and explorer distributions, indicating poor harmonic mean utility.  
- Degraded Pass@1 when adaptive sampling weights become too skewed toward one model.  
- Explorer outputs remain similar to anchors despite unlearning, suggesting ineffective unlearning.

**First three experiments**:  
1. Compare reward distributions of anchor and explorer models: verify explorer shows lower average reward but higher diversity (e.g., higher entropy).  
2. Ablate selection method: replace harmonic mean with arithmetic mean and max reward, measure Pass@1 changes.  
3. Test adaptive sampling sensitivity: vary consensus rate thresholds and plot Pass@1 vs. anchor/explorer proportion.

## Limitations
- Performance gains are modest (e.g., +0.9% on MATH-500); practical impact uncertain.
- No statistical reporting (variance, error bars); reliability of improvements unknown.
- Evaluations limited to models up to 8B parameters; scalability untested.

## Confidence
- Unsupervised RLVR suffers from spurious majority: Medium (plausible but direct evidence limited).
- DCRL improves Pass@1 over majority-vote: High (explicit results on eight benchmarks).
- DCRL matches supervised GRPO: Medium (gap remains; unsupervised vs. supervised mismatch).
- DCRL escapes spurious modes via two-stage consensus: Medium (mechanism plausible, causal proof lacking).
