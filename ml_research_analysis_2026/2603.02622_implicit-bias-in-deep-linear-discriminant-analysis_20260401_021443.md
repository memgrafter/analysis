# Implicit Bias in Deep Linear Discriminant Analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.02622
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02622
- Reference count: 3
- Primary result: Training an L-layer diagonal linear network with balanced initialization under the Deep LDA objective automatically conserves the sum of the (2/L) powers of the per-feature weights, i.e., ∑ w_i^{2/L} = constant, and greater depth amplifies multiplicative penalties on smaller weights, accelerating their elimination.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores the implicit bias of Deep Linear Discriminant Analysis (Deep LDA), a scale-invariant metric learning objective. The authors analyze gradient flow on L-layer diagonal linear networks under balanced initialization, showing that depth converts additive gradient updates into multiplicative ones. This mechanism yields an automatic conservation law: the (2/L)-quasi-norm of the weight vector remains constant throughout training. Theoretical derivations prove ∑ w_i^{2/L} = C for any depth L. Simulations across depths 1 to 20 confirm the conservation and reveal that deeper networks impose harsher penalties on features with small initial weights, driving them to zero faster—a sparsity-like phenomenon. The study uncovers how architecture depth influences optimization dynamics for scale-invariant losses.

## Method Summary
The study uses continuous-time gradient flow on an L-layer diagonal linear network. Each layer k applies a diagonal scaling matrix with entries u_i^{(k)} > 0 to feature i; the effective weight is the product across layers, w_i = ∏_{k=1}^L u_i^{(k)}. The loss is the Rayleigh quotient L(w) = (w^⊤ S_w w)/(w^⊤ S_b w), where S_w (intra-class scatter) and S_b (inter-class scatter) are symmetric positive semi-definite matrices generated randomly. Balanced initialization ensures that for each feature i, all u_i^{(k)}(0) are equal. Under these conditions, the chain rule and gradient flow u_i^{(k)} du_i^{(k)}/dt = -w_i ∂L/∂w_i force the squared layer weights to remain equal across layers, leading to u_i^{(k)}(t) = w_i(t)^{1/L} and the multiplicative update dw_i/dt = -L·w_i^{2-2/L} ∂L/∂w_i. Scale-invariance of L(w) (w^⊤∇L = 0) then guarantees d/dt (∑ w_i^{2/L}) = 0, i.e., conservation of the (2/L)-quasi-norm. Numerical experiments with d=5 and synthetic S_w, S_b validate both the conservation law and the accelerated elimination of weak features as L increases.

## Key Results
- The (2/L)-quasi-norm ∑ w_i^{2/L} is exactly conserved during gradient flow for any depth L under balanced initialization.
- The effective update becomes multiplicative with exponent (2-2/L); this exponent decreases as L grows, making the penalty on smaller weights more severe.
- Deeper architectures (L up to 20) cause weak features (small initial w_i) to vanish faster than in shallow networks, exhibiting a sparsity-like effect.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Depth transforms additive updates into multiplicative ones
For an L-layer diagonal network, ∂w_i/∂u_i^{(k)} = w_i / u_i^{(k)}. Substituting into gradient flow gives u_i^{(k)} du_i^{(k)}/dt = -w_i ∂L/∂w_i. Balanced initialization (all u_i^{(k)}(0) equal) forces all u_i^{(k)}(t) equal, yielding w_i = (u_i^{(k)})^L and dw_i/dt = -L·w_i^{2-2/L} ∂L/∂w_i—a multiplicative update. Break condition: Unbalanced initialization preserves equality of (u_i^{(k)})^2 across layers but not u_i^{(k)} = w_i^{1/L}, altering the dynamics.

### Mechanism 2: Scale-invariance forces quasi-norm conservation
Deep LDA’s Rayleigh quotient is scale-invariant: L(αw)=L(w) ⇒ w^⊤∇L=0. Using the multiplicative update, d/dt (w_i^{2/L}) = (2/L) w_i^{2/L-1} dw_i/dt = -2 w_i ∂L/∂w_i. Summing over i and applying w^⊤∇L=0 yields d/dt (∑ w_i^{2/L}) = 0, proving conservation. Break condition: Any non-scale-invariant modification (e.g., L2 regularization) breaks w^⊤∇L=0 and destroys conservation.

### Mechanism 3: Depth amplifies penalties on weak features, promoting sparsity
From dw_i/dt = -L·w_i^{2-2/L} ∂L/∂w_i, the factor w_i^{2-2/L} grows larger for small w_i as L increases (since 2-2/L < 2). Thus, for a fixed gradient signal, the decay rate of a small weight is faster at greater L, accelerating elimination of weak features. Break condition: If ∂L/∂w_i for a weak feature becomes zero prematurely (e.g., data already linearly separable), the weight may stop decaying before reaching zero.

## Foundational Learning

### Gradient Flow Analysis
Continuous-time dynamics replace discrete updates to derive exact conservation laws without step-size discretization artifacts. Quick check: How does the continuous-time assumption change the derivation of weight equality across layers compared to a discrete update with fixed learning rate?

### Quasi-Norms and Homogeneous Functions
The conserved quantity is a quasi-norm; scale-invariance (degree 0 homogeneity) of L(w) implies orthogonality w^⊤∇L=0, which directly yields quasi-norm conservation. Quick check: Why does scale-invariance imply w^⊤∇L=0 and how does this link to quasi-norm conservation?

### Diagonal Linear Networks (DLNs) as a Proxy
DLNs decouple feature paths, removing cross-feature interactions and isolating the effect of depth on per-feature dynamics. Quick check: What would change if the network had non-diagonal (fully connected) layers? Why is the diagonal restriction critical for the proof?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
- L-layer diagonal linear network: each layer k applies a diagonal matrix U^{(k)} with entries u_i^{(k)}; the effective weight per feature is w_i = ∏_{k=1}^L u_i^{(k)}.
- Deep LDA loss: L(w) = (w^⊤ S_w w)/(w^⊤ S_b w), with S_w and S_b symmetric positive semi-definite scatter matrices.
- Balanced initialization: for each feature i, all u_i^{(k)}(0) are equal.

### Critical Path
1. Initialize all u_i^{(k)}(0) identically across layers for each feature i.
2. Gradient flow enforces that (u_i^{(k)})^2 remain equal across layers k for each i.
3. Consequently, u_i^{(k)}(t) = w_i(t)^{1/L}, converting additive layer updates into multiplicative updates on w_i.
4. Scale-invariance (w^⊤∇L=0) guarantees d/dt (∑ w_i^{2/L}) = 0.
5. Depth L determines the exponent in dw_i/dt, making the penalty on small w_i more severe as L increases.

### Design Tradeoffs
- *Simplicity vs. Generality:* Diagonal linear networks remove non-linearities and interactions, enabling clean theory but limiting applicability to real deep networks.
- *Continuous vs. Discrete:* Gradient flow provides exact conservation; discrete steps with finite learning rate may cause drift or oscillations.
- *Balanced init assumption:* Crucial for simplification; unbalanced initialization preserves a different conservation law (∑(u_i^{(k)})^2 constant per feature) but not the (2/L)-quasi-norm.

### Failure Signatures
- The quasi-norm ∑ w_i^{2/L} drifts from constancy: indicates unbalanced init, non-diagonal architecture, or non-scale-invariant loss.
- Weak features do not decay faster with increased L: suggests ∂L/∂w_i vanishes early or the network is not purely diagonal.
- Layer weights u_i^{(k)} diverge from equality: violates balanced initialization or non-linearity broke the product structure.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Verify quasi-norm conservation:** Train L-layer diagonal linear networks (L∈{1,5,20}) with balanced init on synthetic data; plot ∑ w_i^{2/L} over time; it should be flat.
2. **Depth vs. sparsity:** Track individual w_i(t) for various L; expect smaller initial weights to decay to zero faster as L increases.
3. **Init sensitivity:** Repeat experiment 1 with unbalanced initialization; the quasi-norm should drift, confirming the necessity of balanced init.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does stochastic gradient descent preserve the (2/L)-quasi-norm conservation? Theory assumes continuous flow; SGD introduces noise and may break conservation.
2. Can the quasi-norm conservation be extended to deep networks with non-linear activations? The multiplicative mechanism relies on linearity; non-linearities may alter dynamics.
3. How does unbalanced initialization affect the conservation law? Balanced init is central; unbalanced could lead to different behaviors.
4. Do the findings generalize to real-world datasets? Experiments used synthetic scatter matrices; real-world complexity may affect implicit bias.
5. Under what conditions does greater depth fail to eliminate weak features? Real-world factors (e.g., feature correlations) might weaken the amplification effect.

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis is restricted to diagonal linear networks and balanced initialization; these assumptions may not hold in practical deep learning.
- Conservation proved only for continuous gradient flow; stochastic optimizers and discrete steps may cause deviations.
- Empirical validation uses synthetic data only; generalization to real datasets remains uncertain.

## Confidence
- Depth transforms additive updates into multiplicative ones: **High** (rigorous derivation, simulations confirm).
- Scale-invariance enforces (2/L)-quasi-norm conservation: **High** (proven orthogonality, empirical constancy).
- Depth amplifies penalties on weak features (sparsity-like behavior): **Medium** (theoretically plausible, simulations support but not formally proved).
- Extension to non-diagonal/nonlinear networks: **Low** (unaddressed).
- Conservation under realistic training (SGD, real data): **Low** (open questions).

## Next Checks
