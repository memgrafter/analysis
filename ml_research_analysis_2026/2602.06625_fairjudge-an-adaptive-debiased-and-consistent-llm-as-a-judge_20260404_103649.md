# FairJudge: An Adaptive, Debiased, and Consistent LLM-as-a-Judge

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06625
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06625
- **Reference Count:** 16
- **Primary Result:** FairJudge-8B achieves state-of-the-art Agreement and macro-F1, outperforming much larger models (e.g., Qwen2.5-72B, DeepSeek-V3-671B) and reaches 65.52% pointwise-pairwise consistency, significantly above prior baselines.

## Executive Summary
Existing LLM-as-a-Judge systems are limited by their lack of adaptivity to changing rubrics, susceptibility to non-semantic biases (e.g., answer order, length), and inconsistency between pointwise scoring and pairwise ranking. FairJudge addresses these by reformulating judging as a learnable, conditional policy that explicitly incorporates the evaluation rubric and mode. It is trained on a high-quality, perturbed dataset (FairJudge-16K) through a curriculum: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for rubric adherence, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for debiasing via invariant learning, and Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to enforce cross-mode consistency. Evaluations show FairJudge-8B surpasses larger models in accuracy and, critically, achieves far higher consistency between scoring protocols while maintaining strong multimodal and efficient "Fast" inference capabilities.

## Method Summary
FairJudge constructs a curated dataset, FairJudge-16K, from existing judge data by canonicalizing records to expose rubrics and reasoning, then injecting structured perturbations (e.g., shuffling answer order, varying length) to create bias-sensitive pairs and cross-mode (pointwise/pairwise) aligned instances. The judge model is trained in three stages: 1) SFT teaches the model to follow provided rubrics and generate structured judgments; 2) DPO uses preference pairs where the "chosen" response is rubric-adherent and invariant to non-semantic perturbations, while the "rejected" exhibits bias; 3) GRPO samples multiple judgments per instance under different modes and applies a group-relative reward that scores the consistency between pointwise scores and derived pairwise preferences, directly optimizing for stable cross-mode behavior. Inference supports both a full reasoning mode and a faster decision-only mode enabled by the learned consistency.

## Key Results
- FairJudge-8B outperforms significantly larger models (e.g., 72B, 671B) on multiple benchmarks in Agreement and macro-F1.
- Achieves 65.52% consistency between pointwise and pairwise judgments, a large improvement over ~54% for strong baselines.
- Fast inference mode yields a 12–13× speedup with only a minor performance drop, demonstrating practical efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Policy-Based Conditional Modeling
- **Claim:** Modeling judging as a conditional policy π_judge(y|x, c, m) enables adaptive, debiased, and consistent behavior by making evaluation context (rubric c, mode m) an explicit input.
- **Mechanism:** This reframes judging from a fixed function to a controllable policy, allowing the system to learn adjustments based on rubrics (adaptivity), ignore spurious cues (debiasing), and align outputs across modes (consistency) through targeted training objectives.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] states judging is modeled as a "learnable and regularized policy"; [section 3.1] defines output y as a sample from the policy conditioned on setting.
- **Break condition:** Poorly specified rubrics or modes in training could prevent the policy from disentangling context from content.

### Mechanism 2: Structured Perturbations for Debiasing
- **Claim:** Structured perturbations in FairJudge-16K explicitly teach invariance to non-semantic cues (e.g., answer order, length) by presenting semantically equivalent evaluations with spurious variations.
- **Mechanism:** Contrastive DPO pairs are built where the chosen judgment is rubric-adherent and robust to perturbations, while the rejected one shows bias, optimizing the policy to ignore superficial factors.
- **Evidence:** [section 3.2] describes perturbations along non-semantic dimensions to constrain invariance; [section 3.4] details constructing preference pairs for this purpose.
- **Break condition:** If perturbations inadvertently alter semantic meaning or rubrics are ambiguous, debiasing may corrupt true quality signals.

### Mechanism 3: GRPO for Cross-Mode Consistency
- **Claim:** Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a consistency reward enforces cross-mode consistency (pointwise vs. pairwise) by directly optimizing for logically equivalent judgments.
- **Mechanism:** For an instance, multiple judgments are sampled under different modes; a reward function (Eq. 7) scores consistency between pointwise scores and pairwise preferences. GRPO's group-relative advantage amplifies consistent outputs.
- **Evidence:** [section 3.4] introduces consistency-oriented rewards; Table 1 ablation shows removing GRPO causes the largest F1 drop (72.18 → 69.45); Table 4 shows 65.52% consistency vs. ~54% in baselines.
- **Break condition:** An overly strong consistency reward could cause overfitting to superficial mode-matching at the cost of nuanced rubric adherence.

## Foundational Learning
- **Policy-based modeling vs. function-based judging**
  - *Why needed:* FairJudge's core innovation is treating judging as a learnable policy; this shift explains the need for staged training (SFT/DPO/GRPO) to shape behavior.
  - *Quick check:* How would you prompt a static LLM to be consistent across pointwise and pairwise modes, and why might that fail vs. a policy-based approach?

- **Rubric conditioning as a form of context window**
  - *Why needed:* Adaptivity requires explicit rubric conditioning; SFT teaches the model to parse and follow these structured criteria.
  - *Quick check:* In SFT data (Figure 2, left), what if the rubric field is omitted or poorly written, and how would that affect downstream DPO/GRPO?

- **Preference learning (DPO/GRPO) for behavioral alignment**
  - *Why needed:* DPO mitigates bias via relative preferences; GRPO extends this to group-consistent preferences across modes, moving beyond SFT's imitation.
  - *Quick check:* Why is DPO insufficient for cross-mode consistency, requiring GRPO's group-relative reward design?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Raw evaluation records -> canonicalization (expose rubric/reasoning) -> perturbation injection (debiasing) -> cross-mode pairing (consistency) -> SFT (rubric adherence) -> DPO (bias mitigation) -> GRPO (cross-mode consistency) -> Inference (Full/Fast modes) -> Evaluation (FairJudge-Benchmark-1K, PandaLM/JudgeLM, multimodal).

- **Critical path:**
  Ensure high-quality canonicalized records (JudgeLM data) with explicit rubrics. SFT must stabilize format and rubric following; unstable SFT harms DPO/GRPO. DPO perturbations must preserve semantic equivalence; validate via spot-checks. GRPO reward (Eq. 7) must correctly map pointwise scores to pairwise preferences. FairJudge-Benchmark-1K consistency is a key milestone.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Information density vs. scalability: FairJudge-16K is rich but small; may limit diversity.
  - Consistency vs. adaptivity: GRPO's pressure could reduce flexibility on novel rubrics.
  - Efficiency vs. interpretability: Fast mode sacrifices reasoning trace for speed; acceptable if consistency holds.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low SFT agreement: Model fails to follow rubrics; revisit data canonicalization or SFT hyperparameters.
  - DPO fails to reduce bias: Perturbations too subtle or rubrics not enforced; check chosen/rejected pairs for artifacts.
  - GRPO degrades F1: Consistency reward overfits; check tie/preference misclassifications in reward calculation.
  - Poor multimodal performance: Judge training harmed perception; verify multimodal backbone initialization (Qwen3-VL).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Rubric adaptivity stress test:** Systematically paraphrase rubrics on 500 held-out instances; measure judgment appropriateness. Target: >80% adaptation.
  2. **Bias robustness probe:** Swap answer order/length; measure judgment flip rate. Target: <5% flip on biased baselines.
  3. **Cross-mode consistency check:** Generate pointwise scores and pairwise preferences for 500 instances; compute consistency % (Eq. 8). Target: >65%.

## Limitations
- Policy formulation novelty may be incremental; prior work on conditional evaluation exists.
- Debiasing via perturbations assumes non-semantic variations are cleanly separable; semantic equivalence validation is weak.
- GRPO's consistency reward risks over-optimization at the cost of nuanced rubric adherence.
- FairJudge-16K's small scale may not capture the full distribution of real-world judging scenarios.
- Multimodal evaluation lacks detail; potential data contamination not ruled out.
