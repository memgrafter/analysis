# Towards Better Evolution Modeling for Temporal Knowledge Graphs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.08353
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08353
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces the first bias-corrected TKG evolution benchmark with four datasets and two new tasks; the co-occurrence baseline's performance drops tenfold, confirming the benchmark's effectiveness.

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals that existing temporal knowledge graph (TKG) forecasting benchmarks suffer from a co-occurrence shortcut, where simple count-based methods achieve near-state-of-the-art performance by exploiting dataset biases rather than learning temporal evolution. To resolve this, the authors present a new TKG evolution benchmark comprising four bias-corrected datasets (FinWiki, ICEWSWiki, YAGO130K, WIKI500K) that incorporate both timestamp and time-interval knowledge enriched with textual semantics. They propose two novel tasks—generative knowledge forecasting (predicting all future facts for an entity) and knowledge obsolescence prediction (predicting when a fact ends)—to properly evaluate knowledge formation and decay. Extensive experiments show that the co-occurrence baseline's performance plummets tenfold on the new benchmark, demonstrating its robustness against shortcuts. LLM-based models, which leverage textual annotations, achieve the best results, underscoring the importance of semantic understanding for evolution modeling.

## Method Summary
The benchmark construction follows a rigorous pipeline: source datasets (FinDKG, ICEWS, YAGO, Wikidata) are aligned to Wikidata, filtered via k-core to ensure dense subgraphs, split chronologically, and enriched with textual short descriptions. This yields four bias-corrected datasets containing both timestamp and aligned time-interval facts. Two tasks are defined: (1) generative forecasting, where for each entity at a given timestamp the model must generate all (relation, object) pairs that will hold at the next timestamp; and (2) obsolescence prediction, which predicts the end time of interval facts. Baselines include time embedding models (TNT, HGE), dynamic embedding models (DE-Simple, ATiSE, TA-dismult), and the LLM-based TKG-ICL. A simple co-occurrence baseline builds dictionaries from training data to rank candidate facts.

## Key Results
- The co-occurrence baseline's performance drops tenfold on the new benchmark (e.g., Recall@50 ~0.0001 on YAGO130K), whereas it previously achieved near-SOTA on legacy datasets.
- Best generative forecasting Recall@50: 0.1697 on YAGO130K (TKG-ICL-GPT4o).
- Best obsolescence prediction: MAE 1.72 on YAGO130K, accuracy 24.91%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper explains the effectiveness of the new benchmark through three key mechanisms:

1. **Co-occurrence Shortcut in Existing Benchmarks**  
   Existing TKG forecasting data contain high train-test overlap (many test facts appear in training) and small entity preference sets (few objects per (subject, relation) pair). This allows models to memorize static co-occurrences and ignore temporal dynamics. The shortcut is broken when datasets have low overlap or large preference sets, causing co-occurrence performance to collapse.

2. **Oversimplified Task Formulation**  
   The traditional task (predicting object given subject and relation) provides redundant query information, reducing the problem to ranking memorized objects for a known (subject, relation) pair. The new generative forecasting task removes the relation from the query, requiring models to generate both relation and object from historical patterns, thus forcing genuine evolution modeling.

3. **Neglect of Semantics and Time-Interval Knowledge**  
   Prior benchmarks lack textual semantics (entities/relations are opaque IDs) and mis-handle time-interval facts (repeating them across intervals), creating artificial duplication. Enriching data with disambiguating text and correctly aligning interval facts enables models to capture causal triggers (e.g., a hiring event ending an employment interval).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Source Data → Entity Alignment → k-core Filtering → Temporal Split → Annotation Enrichment → Bias-corrected Datasets → (Generative Forecasting, Obsolescence Prediction) → Evaluation with Baselines (TNT, HGE, DE-Simple, ATiSE, TA-dismult, TKG-ICL).
- **Critical path**: 
  1. Apply k-core filtering and temporal popularity-based selection to reduce train-test overlap and diversify preference sets.
  2. Align timestamp and time-interval facts to capture mutual evolution triggers.
  3. Enrich entities/relations with textual short descriptions to provide semantic disambiguation.
  4. Design tasks that eliminate redundant query information (generative) and model interval endpoints (obsolescence).
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Larger k-core yields denser but smaller graphs, trading off preference set diversity for scale.
  - Textual annotations prevent ID leakage but require filtering to remove time-related phrases and resolve ambiguity.
  - Generative forecasting is more realistic but harder to evaluate (ranking all (r,o) candidates).
- **Failure signatures**: 
  - Co-occurrence baseline achieves >50% Hits@10 on old datasets but <2% on the new ones.
  - LLM-based models (TKG-ICL) fail without textual annotations (Recall@50 ~0.0004) but succeed with them (~0.1697 on YAGO130K).
  - Models that excel at generative forecasting (e.g., DE-Simple) often perform poorly on obsolescence prediction (high MAE).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Are there remaining exploitable biases in the proposed benchmark that simple heuristics could leverage?
2. How can timestamp and time-interval knowledge be jointly modeled to capture their mutual influence on evolution?
3. What architectures are best suited for generative knowledge forecasting, which requires generating multiple future facts without redundant query information?
4. Can non-LLM models achieve LLM-level performance on TKG evolution tasks, perhaps via semantic distillation or better representations?
5. How can one-step evolution modeling be extended to multi-step forecasting to capture long-term dynamics?

## Limitations
- Incomplete reproduction specifications (e.g., prompt formats, negative sampling strategies, interval handling) for baselines.
- k-core filtering may bias datasets toward high-degree entities, potentially overlooking tail entities.
- The proposed tasks are simplified (single-step, fixed intervals) compared to real-world long-horizon evolution, and LLM-based solutions are computationally expensive.

## Confidence
- **High**: Existing TKG forecasting benchmarks suffer from a co-occurrence shortcut; the new benchmark and tasks effectively evaluate evolution modeling.
- **Medium**: The importance of textual semantics and time-interval alignment for evolution understanding.
