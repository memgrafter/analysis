# AnyThermal: Towards Learning Universal Representations for Thermal Perception

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06203  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06203  
- **Reference count**: 36  
- **Primary result**: AnyThermal achieves state-of-the-art zero‑shot performance: 81.11% R@1 on cross‑modal VPR, 53.47% mIoU on thermal segmentation, and 0.0883 AbsRel on monocular depth.

## Executive Summary
Thermal perception models are typically trained for narrow tasks on small datasets, limiting their utility. AnyThermal overcomes this by distilling robust, task‑agnostic features from a frozen DINOv2 RGB foundation model into a thermal encoder. It uses a diverse multi‑environment RGB‑thermal dataset (TartanRGBT) collected via a new open‑source platform. After distillation, lightweight task‑specific heads are attached for zero‑shot transfer to cross‑modal place recognition, thermal segmentation, and monocular depth estimation. The approach yields significant gains on multiple benchmarks, outperforming prior works by up to 36% relative improvement.

## Method Summary
AnyThermal employs knowledge distillation between two DINOv2 ViT‑B/14 models. The teacher (frozen) processes RGB images, while the student (trainable) processes thermal images. A contrastive loss aligns the CLS token embeddings of corresponding RGB‑thermal pairs, requiring no labels. After distillation, the student backbone is frozen and paired with task‑specific heads: a two‑layer MLP for segmentation (trained with Dice loss), a SALAD head for VPR (triplet margin loss), and a MiDaS‑style architecture for depth (using multiscale patch features). These heads are trained on their respective datasets while the backbone remains fixed.

## Key Results
- **Cross‑modal place recognition**: AnyThermal‑VPR reaches 81.11% R@1 on urban MS2 (vs. 76.97% for SALAD).  
- **Thermal segmentation**: Achieves 53.47% mIoU on MF‑Net (vs. 51.95% for MCNET).  
- **Monocular depth estimation**: Attains 0.0883 AbsRel on MS2 (vs. 0.0905 for DINOv2), with up to 36% relative improvement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Distillation transfers the rich semantics of a large RGB foundation model to the thermal domain, bridging the modality gap via contrastive alignment on CLS tokens. The frozen DINOv2 teacher provides stable, high‑quality targets. Using a highly diverse RGB‑thermal training set (TartanRGBT) exposes the student to varied environments, yielding environment‑agnostic features. The CLS token captures global context while the patch tokens retain spatial information, supporting both retrieval and dense tasks. Lightweight task‑specific heads then adapt these universal features to downstream objectives with minimal data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge Distillation**: Enables a smaller student to mimic a large teacher without label dependence. *Why needed*: Bootstrap thermal encoder with powerful RGB features. *Quick check*: Student CLS embeddings should correlate with teacher’s on paired inputs.  
- **Contrastive Learning**: Aligns positive pairs and separates negatives in a shared space. *Why needed*: Overcome domain shift between RGB and thermal. *Quick check*: High similarity for true pairs, low for negatives.  
- **Foundation Models**: Large‑scale pretrained vision models (e.g., DINOv2) contain rich hierarchical representations. *Why needed*: Provide a strong starting point for thermal features. *Quick check*: Teacher achieves high accuracy on standard RGB benchmarks.  
- **Multi‑environment Data**: Training across diverse scenes (indoor, aerial, off‑road, urban) prevents overfitting. *Why needed*: Promote universal perception. *Quick check*: Feature embeddings remain consistent across domains in PCA/t‑SNE plots.  
- **Task‑Agnostic Feature Extraction**: Using a single backbone for multiple downstream tasks. *Why needed*: Reduce redundancy and enable zero‑shot transfer. *Quick check*: Linear probes on frozen features yield reasonable accuracy on several tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
RGB Input -> DINOv2 Teacher (frozen) -> teacher_CLS  
Thermal Input -> AnyThermal Student -> student_CLS  
ContrastiveLoss(teacher_CLS, student_CLS) updates student  
After distillation: AnyThermal Backbone (frozen) -> Task Heads  
- VPR: SALAD head -> global descriptor  
- Segmentation: MLP on patch tokens -> pixel labels  
- Depth: MiDaS‑style decoder on multiscale patch features -> depth map  

**Critical Path**  
1. Load synchronized RGB‑thermal pair.  
2. Preprocess thermal to 3‑channel 8‑bit (min‑max, CLAHE, bilateral filter).  
3. Forward pass through teacher (RGB) and student (thermal).  
4. Compute contrastive loss on CLS tokens.  
5. Backpropagate to update student.  
6. Repeat until convergence.  
7. Freeze student backbone.  
8. For each task, attach corresponding head and train head only on task‑specific data.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *Frozen teacher*: Stable targets, lower compute, but cannot adapt to thermal distribution.  
- *CLS token alignment*: Simplifies training and yields global semantics, but loses spatial details needed for dense predictions.  
- *Universal backbone*: Enables zero‑shot transfer with a single model, but may be suboptimal for tasks requiring specialized features; lightweight heads attempt to compensate.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Low VPR R@1 on in‑distribution queries indicates poor RGB‑thermal alignment.  
- Segmentation mIoU near random suggests backbone lacks spatial information or head not learning.  
- Depth AbsRel significantly higher than teacher’s RGB‑based value implies mismatch in feature hierarchy or insufficient depth data diversity.

**First Experiments**  
1. Visualize CLS embeddings (t‑SNE) of paired vs. unpaired RGB‑thermal samples to confirm alignment.  
2. Train VPR head on a small subset and evaluate R@1 on a held‑out set; expect >70% R@1.  
3. Run segmentation head on a few validation images and compute per‑class IoU; check for reasonable structure.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**  
- **Question**: How can accurate ground truth for thermal depth and odometry be obtained to enable proper benchmarking of thermal perception models?  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors state in Section V‑E that the dense depth and odometry from stereo‑RGB are insufficient for benchmarking tasks like odometry or depth estimation, and future work includes adding GPS and LiDAR.  
- **Why unresolved**: Current evaluations rely on proxy ground truth from RGB‑based methods, which may not accurately represent thermal sensor performance, leading to potentially biased assessments of model capabilities.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Collect a thermal dataset with synchronized high‑accuracy LiDAR and GPS, and re‑evaluate AnyThermal and baselines on this ground truth to assess true performance gaps and generalization limits.

**Open Question 2**  
- **Question**: Does distilling from newer RGB foundation models (e.g., DINOv3, SAM) significantly improve thermal feature quality beyond DINOv2?  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] In the Conclusion and Future Work, the authors suggest distilling stronger backbones leveraging newer visual foundation models.  
- **Why unresolved**: AnyThermal was only distilled from DINOv2; it is unknown whether recent advancements in RGB foundation models translate to meaningful gains in thermal representation learning.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Train AnyThermal variants using DINOv3, SAM, or other recent models as teachers, and compare performance on the same downstream tasks (VPR, segmentation, depth) across diverse environments to quantify improvements.

**Open Question 3**  
- **Question**: What specific dimensions of dataset diversity (e.g., environmental conditions, sensor setups, scene semantics) are most critical for effective knowledge distillation in thermal perception?  
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The authors claim diversity is more important than scale based on experiments showing TartanRGBT (diverse) helps while other urban datasets saturate performance (Section VI‑D, Fig. 8). However, they do not analyze what makes TartanRGBT diverse or which factors drive gains.  
- **Why unresolved**: Without understanding which diversity aspects matter, future data collection may be inefficient, and it is unclear if the current diversity in TartanRGBT is optimal or sufficient for universal thermal representations.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conduct an ablation study decomposing diversity factors (e.g., time of day, weather, scene types, platform motion) by curating controlled subsets of TartanRGBT and measuring downstream task performance to isolate key drivers.

**Open Question 4**  
- **Question**: Is the CLS token‑based contrastive loss optimal for distilling thermal features, or would multi‑level or patch‑level losses improve performance on tasks requiring fine‑grained details like segmentation?  
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The authors use CLS token contrastive loss for simplicity and to relax alignment constraints (Section III‑B), but they do not compare with alternative loss formulations (e.g., patch‑wise, feature pyramid matching).  
- **Why unresolved**: Different downstream tasks may benefit from different feature granularities; CLS tokens capture global semantics but might lose local details crucial for pixel‑level tasks like segmentation or depth estimation.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Train AnyThermal with alternative distillation losses (e.g., patch‑wise contrastive, multi‑layer feature alignment) and evaluate on segmentation and depth benchmarks to identify performance trade‑offs and optimal loss choices per task.

## Limitations
- **Missing hyperparameters** for knowledge distillation (batch size, learning rate, temperature, epochs) hinder exact replication.  
- **Underspecified task head architectures** (e.g., segmentation MLP dimensions, depth head feature aggregation) leave implementation gaps.  
- **Reliance on a frozen DINOv2 teacher** may limit thermal‑specific adaptation; the balance between RGB bias and generalizable features is unclear.

## Confidence
- **Distillation yields universal thermal features**: **Medium**  
- **Zero‑shot SOTA on VPR, segmentation, depth**: **Medium**  
- **TartanRGBT dataset diversity drives robustness**: **Medium**
