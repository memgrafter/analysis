# ILION: Deterministic Pre-Execution Safety Gates for Agentic AI Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13247
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13247
- **Reference Count:** 16
- **Primary Result:** Achieves F1=0.8515 on ILION-Bench v2 with 143 µs latency, outperforming a key baseline by 4.3 F1 points while reducing false positives fourfold.

## Executive Summary
ILION introduces a deterministic, rule-based safety gate to address the critical gap in verifying whether autonomous AI agents act within their authorized operational scope. Unlike text-safety systems, it operates as a pre-execution filter without requiring training data. Evaluated on the authors' ILION-Bench v2 benchmark (380 scenarios), it demonstrates high effectiveness (F1=0.8515) and exceptional speed (143 µs mean latency), significantly outperforming a representative baseline in both accuracy and false positive rate.

## Method Summary
The proposed system is a cascade of five independent, deterministic checks applied before an agent executes any action. It requires no model training or fine-tuning, relying instead on explicitly defined policy rules, action validation, identity verification, scope enforcement, and a final execution gate. Each component makes a binary decision; the action is permitted only if all components approve. This design ensures predictability, minimal latency, and independence from training data distribution.

## Key Results
- F1 score of 0.8515 on ILION-Bench v2 with 91.0% precision and a 7.9% false positive rate.
- Mean latency of 143 microseconds per gate evaluation.
- Outperforms Lakera Guard (F1=0.8087) by 4.3 F1 points while operating ~2000× faster and with a four-times lower false positive rate.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ILION works by decoupling safety verification from the generative process. Its deterministic, rule-based cascade ensures that every potential action is checked against a predefined, authorized operational scope before execution. This eliminates variability from model-based classifiers, prevents harmful actions at the point of decision, and achieves near-instantaneous evaluation because it involves no neural network inference. The design assumes the operational scope policies are precise and comprehensive; the gate's reliability is directly tied to the quality of those upstream definitions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Deterministic Rule Systems:** Why needed? To guarantee reproducible, explainable safety decisions without training data biases. Quick check: Verify that identical inputs always produce identical pass/fail outputs.
- **Operational Scope Definition:** Why needed? To formally specify the boundary of permitted agent behaviors. Quick check: Ensure policies are exhaustive, unambiguous, and machine-readable.
- **Pre-Execution Verification:** Why needed? To block actions before they cause harm, unlike post-hoc content filters. Quick check: Confirm the gate sits between the agent's decision and tool execution.
- **Action Canonicalization:** Why needed? To map diverse agent outputs to a standard set of validatable actions. Quick check: Test that synonymous or parameter-variant expressions are normalized correctly.
- **Identity & Context Validation:** Why needed? To ensure actions are authorized for the current agent session and environment. Quick check: Validate that session tokens, role claims, and resource contexts are rigorously checked before scope evaluation.
- **Cascade Failure Isolation:** Why needed? To pinpoint precisely why an action was blocked. Quick check: Each component returns a specific, non-overlapping failure reason.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component Map:
Policy Parser -> Action Validator -> Scope Enforcement -> Identity Verifier -> Execution Gate

Critical Path: An agent's proposed action is first parsed into a canonical form. The Action Validator checks if it is a known, structurally valid operation. The Scope Enforcement module checks if this action is within the rules for the agent's current role. The Identity Verifier confirms the request originates from an authenticated session with appropriate privileges. Finally, the Execution Gate permits or denies the action based on all prior approvals. All five must succeed.

Design Tradeoffs: Speed (deterministic rules) vs. Scope Expressivity (complex policies can become hard to author/maintain). Independence from training data vs. Inability to learn novel threat patterns implicitly. High precision (low false positives) may come at the cost of some recall if policies are overly restrictive.

Failure Signatures:
1.  **False Positive:** A safe, within-scope action is blocked. Signature: Gate denies with a specific policy violation reason that appears valid but contextually inappropriate.
2.  **False Negative:** An unsafe or out-of-scope action is permitted. Signature: Gate approves, but the action leads to an undesired state, indicating a gap in the defined policies or validation logic.
3.  **Cascade Break:** The gate becomes unresponsive or crashes. Signature: Agent action hangs or times out, indicating a bug in a component (e.g., parser crash on malformed input).

First Three Onboarding Experiments:
1.  **Policy Coverage Stress Test:** Feed the gate a large corpus of borderline actions (e.g., "read file" vs. "read sensitive file") to measure false positive/negative rates and identify ambiguous policy zones.
2.  **Concurrent Load Test:** Simulate 100+ concurrent agent requests to measure latency distribution, throughput, and identify race conditions or resource contention in the gate.
3.  **Policy Mutation Robustness:** Slightly rephrase valid policies (e.g., "delete user data" -> "remove user information") to check if the gate's interpretation is robust to synonymous wording or fails catastrophically.

## Limitations
- Performance claims are solely on ILION-Bench v2, a proprietary benchmark, raising risks of overfitting and lacking validation on standardized, third-party suites.
- Real-world generalization is unknown; performance under multi-agent concurrency, adversarial prompts, network latency, or with novel action chains is not evaluated.
- System reliability is fundamentally dependent on the precision of upstream, human-defined operational scope policies, which is a separate major failure domain.

## Confidence
- **Performance on ILION-Bench v2:** Medium (high confidence in internal numbers, low in generalizability due to non-independent benchmark).
- **Deterministic & Ultra-Fast Operation:** High (intrinsically plausible for a rule-based system).
- **Superiority over Baselines (e.g., Lakera Guard):** Low (comparison limited to one baseline on a proprietary benchmark; newer LLM-based safety systems not evaluated).
- **Solving the "Verification Gap":** Very Low (broad conceptual claim; robust verification for diverse, multi-step agentic behaviors remains an unproven hypothesis).

## Next Checks
1.  **Independent Benchmark Replication:** Re-implement ILION's gate logic and evaluate it on a standardized, third-party agent safety benchmark (e.g., a subset of AgentBench) to compare results against published baselines.
2.  **Adversarial Robustness & Edge Case Testing:** Conduct a red-teaming exercise with prompts designed to probe authorization boundaries, including ambiguous policy phrasing, action chaining, and attempts to exploit identity/context validation.
3.  **End-to-End System Integration & Scaling Test:** Deploy ILION as middleware in a production-grade agent framework (e.g., LangChain) to measure latency, error rates, and failure modes under realistic multi-turn workflows and concurrent request loads.
