# CUA-Suite: Massive Human-annotated Video Demonstrations for Computer-Use Agents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24440
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24440
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Introduces CUA-Suite (55h video, 3.6M UI elements) to address the continuous video gap; current models like OpenCUA-32B achieve only 37.7% success on professional desktop apps, with spatial grounding as the primary bottleneck.

## Executive Summary
CUA-Suite is a unified ecosystem designed to overcome the data bottleneck for computer-use agents (CUAs) by providing massive, high-quality human demonstrations. It comprises three core resources: VideoCUA (55 hours of 30 fps expert task video with dense reasoning annotations), GroundCUA (56K annotated screenshots with 3.6M UI elements), and UI-Vision (a grounding and planning benchmark). The paper establishes that continuous video, not sparse screenshots, is critical for capturing temporal dynamics in professional desktop applications. Evaluation of foundation models reveals severe performance drops on complex apps (e.g., <15% on Krita), pinpointing spatial grounding as the chief failure mode. The suite publicly releases all data and models to catalyze research in generalist CUAs, continuous spatial control, and visual world models.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates foundation action models on a new, large-scale dataset of continuous human-computer interactions. VideoCUA provides the primary training and evaluation data: 10,000 expert tasks across 87 applications, recorded at 30 fps, yielding ~6 million frames. Each action step includes kinematic cursor traces and four layers of manual reasoning annotations (observation, thought, action description, reflection). For evaluation, a stratified sample of 256 tasks is used. Preprocessing extracts keyframes at temporal midpoints between actions to prevent cursor leakage, filters out `moveTo` actions, and normalizes coordinates. Models (OpenCUA-7B/32B) predict the next action given the task instruction, a history of five prior actions, and the current keyframe. Performance is measured via pixel distance (mean/median) and success rates within 20 px/50 px thresholds. A human evaluation on 576 sampled steps provides stepwise accuracy (action + grounding correctness). GroundCUA and UI-Vision support grounding analysis and benchmarking.

## Key Results
- **Scale:** VideoCUA is 2.5× larger than prior continuous video efforts (e.g., ScaleCUA) with 55 hours of 30 fps video.
- **Model Performance:** OpenCUA-32B achieves only 37.7% success within 50 px on the evaluation sample; performance on specialized apps like Krita and Darktable falls below 15%.
- **Bottleneck Identification:** Spatial grounding accuracy is 52.4%, while action-type correctness is 85.9%, confirming grounding as the primary failure mode.
- **Human Evaluation:** Stepwise accuracy is a modest 57.6%, indicating substantial room for improvement even from an expert human standard.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Continuous video demonstrations preserve the temporal dynamics essential for learning robust CUAs.
- **Mechanism:** Recording at 30 fps captures every frame of interaction, including intermediate cursor movements and visual feedback between discrete actions. This dense temporal signal enables models to learn continuous spatial control and world models that predict state transitions, which are critical in professional apps with complex, non-linear dynamics.
- **Core Assumption:** Isolated screenshots fail to encode the sequential causality and transient states (e.g., loading indicators, dynamic menus) inherent in desktop workflows.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [Abstract]: "continuous video, not sparse screenshots, is the critical missing ingredient." [Section 3.1]: "By preserving the complete visual state at every frame, the dataset encodes the full temporal dynamics of human interaction."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Dense, multi-layered reasoning annotations provide causal links between visual states and actions, enabling intent-driven behavior.
- **Mechanism:** Each keyframe receives ~497 words of supervision across four layers (observation, thought, action description, reflection). This structured chain-of-thought (CoT) teaches the intermediate cognitive steps that connect a high-level goal to a specific low-level action, moving beyond simple imitation learning.
- **Core Assumption:** Explicit reasoning traces improve generalization by grounding actions in context and intent, rather than just mimicking coordinate patterns.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [Abstract]: "multi-layered reasoning annotations." [Section 3.4.1]: Format τt = (s_t, o_t, r_t, d_t, a_t, s_{t+1}, ref_t) provides "the dense supervision signal that recent work identifies as critical."

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A unified ecosystem (VideoCUA + GroundCUA + UI-Vision) aligns data distribution across perception, action, and evaluation, avoiding fragmentation.
- **Mechanism:** VideoCUA supplies raw interaction traces; GroundCUA provides pixel-precise element annotations for spatial grounding; UI-Vision offers a diagnostic benchmark. Together they form a cohesive pipeline where grounding annotations correspond directly to video frames, and the benchmark tests capabilities derived from the same data distribution.
- **Core Assumption:** Progress requires consistency across training data (trajectories + grounding) and evaluation (planning/grounding benchmarks) to prevent distribution shifts that obscure failure modes.
- **Evidence Anchors:** [Abstract]: "CUA-Suite further provides two complementary resources: UI-VISION... and GroundCUA." [Section 3]: "By unifying all three resources... forming a superset of information that can be losslessly transformed into the formats required by existing agent frameworks."

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Visual GUI Grounding**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper identifies spatial grounding as the primary bottleneck (52.4% accuracy). Accurate localization of UI elements (e.g., "Save button") is a prerequisite for correct action execution.
  - **Quick check:** How would you design an evaluation to distinguish a model that understands *what* "Save" means from one that merely localizes a button based on visual salience?
- **Concept: Temporal Dynamics in GUI Interaction**
  - **Why needed here:** Professional desktop apps exhibit complex state changes (e.g., modal dialogs, progress bars, canvas updates) that occur between discrete actions. Sparse screenshots miss these transitional states.
  - **Quick check:** Why might a model trained only on screenshots fail at a drag-and-drop operation that requires tracking a cursor's continuous path?
- **Concept: Evaluation Metrics for Computer-Use Agents**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper uses multiple metrics (pixel distance, success@thresholds, human stepwise accuracy) to dissect failures in coordinate prediction vs. action sequencing vs. grounding.
  - **Quick check:** What are the limitations of using "success within 50 pixels" as the sole metric? When might a 10-pixel error still represent a catastrophic failure?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** VideoCUA (55h video + traces + reasoning) -> GroundCUA (56K screenshots, 3.6M element boxes) -> UI-Vision (450-task grounding/planning benchmark). The first two share an annotation backbone; the third is derived from them.

**Critical Path:**
1.  Download VideoCUA and preprocess: extract keyframes at temporal midpoints between actions, filter out `moveTo` actions, adjust `click` actions post-`moveTo` to use the preceding keyframe, and normalize coordinates.
2.  For evaluation, construct model inputs from the 256-task sample: (instruction, last 5 ground-truth actions in natural + code format, current keyframe).
3.  Run OpenCUA-7B/32B to predict next action. For coordinate actions, compute Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth absolute pixels. Calculate mean/median distances and success@20px/@50px.
4.  Sample 49 tasks for human evaluation; annotators judge grounding and action correctness per step.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- 30 fps vs. storage: 30 fps (~6M frames) captures fine detail but increases data size; a lower rate might omit critical micro-movements.
- Keyframe at action midpoints: Prevents cursor leakage (model seeing cursor position) but may skip fast visual feedback occurring just before/after an action.
- Human curation vs. scale: Ensures high-quality, expert annotations and reasoning but limits total hours (55h) compared to potential web-scale synthetic data generation.

**Failure Signatures:**
- **Cursor leakage:** Using raw video frames (not midpoints) in training allows the model to see the cursor, inflating performance unrealistically.
- **Domain overlap:** Some UI-Vision benchmark tasks might appear in VideoCUA training data, invalidating evaluation if splits are not rigorously separated.
- **Coordinate normalization errors:** Mismatches between training and inference resolution (e.g., training at 1920x1080, inferring at 2560x1440) cause systematic pixel offset failures.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Baseline Replication:** Run OpenCUA-32B on the published 256-task sample using the described preprocessing. Report success@50px and human stepwise accuracy to verify Table 3 (37.7% & 57.6%).
2.  **Temporal Density Ablation:** Train identical model architectures on VideoCUA data formatted as (a) full 30 fps frames vs. (b) sparse keyframes (one per action). Evaluate both on UI-Vision action prediction to measure the impact of continuous temporal signal.
3.  **Reasoning Supervision Ablation:** Train models using (a) only state-action pairs (s_t, a_t) vs. (b) full multi-layer annotations (s_t, o_t, r_t, d_t, a_t, s_{t+1}, ref_t). Compare performance on UI-Vision and human stepwise accuracy to quantify the value of dense CoT.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why do current foundation action models show such a dramatic performance drop (e.g., <15% success) on specialized, canvas-based desktop applications like Krita and Darktable, compared to more web-like interfaces?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper states: "specialized tools like Krita and Darktable score below 15%" and describes these tools as having "dense toolbars, non-standard widget layouts, and domain-specific iconography." It explicitly identifies spatial grounding as the primary bottleneck.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper identifies the symptom (spatial grounding failure) and suggests potential causes (visual complexity, multi-panel layouts) but does not isolate the specific factors—e.g., whether the issue is due to a lack of similar training data, inherent visual confusion in UIs, or the models' inability to process dense, overlapping interactive elements.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A controlled ablation study using CUA-Suite data that (1) measures performance after fine-tuning on subsets containing only canvas-based vs. standard UI patterns; (2) includes error analysis that categorizes spatial errors (e.g., cross-panel confusion vs. icon similarity) at a pixel- and element-level across different application categories; and (3) compares feature importance from model probes to determine whether failures stem from visual recognition, layout parsing, or spatial reasoning.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does CUA-Suite's focus on open-source applications with permissive licenses adequately represent the UI/UX diversity and complexity of closed-source, commercially dominant software (e.g., full Microsoft Office, Adobe Creative Suite), or does it introduce a domain shift that limits generalizability?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state they selected 87 open-source applications because of licensing and to ensure broad release. They claim similarity between open-source (e.g., LibreOffice) and closed-source (e.g., MS Office) counterparts "makes the dataset broadly applicable" but provide no direct validation of this claim through cross-domain experiments.
- **Why unresolved:** The assumption that UI similarity between open- and closed-source apps holds universally is untested. Critical differences in proprietary design languages, accessibility implementations, and feature density could make models trained on CUA-Suite ineffective on commercially dominant closed-source tools where users most need automation. The paper’s evaluation itself is confined to the CUA-Suite apps.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An external evaluation benchmark (e.g., extension of UI-Vision or a new suite) that tests models trained on CUA-Suite on a matched set of closed-source commercial applications. The evidence would need to show (a) performance correlations or gaps between open- and closed-source equivalents and (b) analysis of which UI characteristics most predict transfer success/failure.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What is the optimal granularity and structure of reasoning annotations (e.g., the 4-layered CoT format in VideoCUA) for training effective action prediction, and how does annotation cost scale with task complexity and model capability?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper introduces 4-layered annotations (observation, thought, action description, reflection) averaging 497 words per step. It claims this provides the "dense supervision signal" needed for capable GUI agents but does not analyze the marginal utility of each layer or the annotation efficiency.
- **Why unresolved:** While the CoT format is presented as beneficial, there is no ablation study showing which annotation layers contribute most to performance gains, whether simpler annotations suffice for simpler tasks, or how much annotation overhead is justified by improved outcomes. The cost of generating such dense, manual CoT for every frame in continuous video is high and not justified by empirical returns.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments that train models with subsets of the annotation layers (e.g., only action descriptions vs. full CoT) and with robotically generated vs. human-written CoT, measuring action prediction accuracy and stepwise success. This would determine the cost/benefit curve of annotation depth and inform whether future datasets can be more efficiently curated.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can continuous video demonstrations (as provided in VideoCUA) unlock improvements in temporal reasoning and long-horizon planning beyond what is achievable with sparse screenshots, and if so, what architectural or training paradigm changes are required to leverage this signal?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper posits that "continuous video, not sparse screenshots, is the critical missing ingredient" and that VideoCUA preserves "full temporal dynamics." It suggests support for "visual world models" and "continuous spatial control." However, the action prediction evaluation still uses keyframes extracted from video, not raw continuous video input.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates a bottleneck but does not empirically validate that the video format itself provides a measurable advantage over frame-based datasets when used with current model architectures. The evaluation framework filters video into keyframes, sidestepping the question of whether processing the full 30 fps stream with stateful models (e.g., video transformers, world models) yields better performance on temporal tasks like wait detection, cursor trajectory prediction, or multi-step procedure understanding.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A direct comparative study where state-of-the-art models are trained either (a) on the keyframe/extracted action format used in the paper or (b) on raw continuous video with architectures designed for temporal modeling (e.g., video-pretrained VLMs, diffusion-based world models). Performance would be measured on tasks requiring temporal understanding, such as predicting intermediate states, detecting failed subtasks, or planning sequences with timing constraints.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** How effective are the dense, pixel-precise UI element annotations in GroundCUA for training universal screen parsing models that can generalize to unseen, dynamic, or non-standard desktop interfaces (e.g., games, real-time data visualizations, custom canvas-based tools)?
- **Basis in paper:** GroundCUA provides 3.6M element annotations with bounding boxes and categories. The paper claims it targets the "spatial grounding bottleneck" and supports "generalist screen parsing." However, the benchmark UI-Vision focuses on predefined, static UIs from the same 87 applications.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper asserts that pixel-precise annotations cover "almost every visible element, including small icons and controls," but this is demonstrated only on recorded, deterministic workflows. It is unclear whether the annotation schema and models trained on GroundCUA can handle UIs with: (a) dynamically generated graphics, (b) non-rectangular or animated elements, (c) overlapping transient tooltips/notifications, or (d) applications not in the training distribution. The generalizability to truly novel visual interfaces remains an assumption.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A zero-shot or few-shot evaluation of a model trained exclusively on GroundCUA on a separate, curated suite of "challenge" applications—such as video games, scientific visualization tools, or heavy-use-of-GL-canvas apps—that explicitly test robustness to non-standard, dynamic, or procedurally generated UIs. Success would be measured by element detection recall and precise localization accuracy in these unseen domains.

## Limitations
- **Causal attribution gap:** The paper shows current models perform poorly and presents a new dataset, but does not prove that the continuous video or reasoning annotations are *necessary* or *sufficient* for improvement (no direct ablation vs. sparse data).
- **Generalization scope:** Evaluation is restricted to 87 open-source applications and a 256-task sample; robustness to unseen UI paradigms, closed-source commercial software, or dynamic interfaces is not established.
- **Reproducibility details:** Preprocessing parameters (exact midpoint extraction, `moveTo` filtering thresholds, stratification criteria for the 256-task sample) are described conceptually, risking implementation variance that could affect reported metrics.

## Confidence
- **Scale (55h video, 3.6M elements):** High
- **Spatial grounding as primary bottleneck:** High (empirical: 52.4% vs. 85.9% on action type)
- **Continuous video as critical missing ingredient:** Medium (theoretically sound, but no direct comparative experiment with sparse data)
- **Unified ecosystem value:** Medium (cohesive design, but no quantitative training/evaluation benefit demonstrated)
- **Generalizability to closed-source apps:** Low (claim based on analogy, not empirical validation)
- **Optimal annotation structure:** Low (no ablation on annotation layer utility)
