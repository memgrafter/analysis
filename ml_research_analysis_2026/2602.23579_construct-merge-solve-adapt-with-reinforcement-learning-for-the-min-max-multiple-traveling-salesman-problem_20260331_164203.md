# Construct, Merge, Solve & Adapt with Reinforcement Learning for the min-max Multiple Traveling Salesman Problem
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23579  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23579  
- **Reference count:** 21  
- **Primary result:** RL-CMSA significantly outperforms a state-of-the-art hybrid genetic algorithm (HGA) on min-max mTSP instances, achieving better average objective values, higher best-run frequencies, and often faster runtimes, especially as problem size and number of salesmen increase.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RL-CMSA, a metaheuristic for the min-max Multiple Traveling Salesman Problem (mTSP) that integrates reinforcement learning into a Construct–Merge–Solve–Adapt framework. The method iteratively constructs candidate solutions via a Q-guided probabilistic clustering, merges their routes into a compact pool, solves a restricted set-covering MILP to select m routes, and refines the result via inter-route moves. Pairwise Q-values are updated based on the current best solution, biasing future clustering toward promising city groupings. Extensive computational experiments on random and TSPLIB instances show RL-CMSA consistently matches or improves upon the HGA baseline, with statistically significant advantages in most settings, particularly for larger instances.

## Method Summary
RL-CMSA is a hybrid algorithm that alternates between construction, merging, solving, improvement, learning, and adaptation. It starts with all pairwise Q-values set to 0.5. In each iteration, it repeatedly constructs *nsolutions* candidate solutions: non-depot cities are probabilistically assigned to *m* clusters using a seeding biased by Q-values and an assignment cost that balances proximity and mean Q-compatibility. Each cluster is turned into a route via greedy insertion followed by intra-route local search (2-opt, Or-opt). All routes are merged into a candidate pool *R_cand*, deduplicated by city set, and pruned if longer than the current incumbent. A set-covering MILP selects exactly *m* routes from *R_cand* covering all cities, minimizing the maximum route length (bounded by incumbent). The MILP output is improved by inter-route moves (removing duplicates, shifting, swapping) to ensure feasibility and possibly enhance quality. The Q-values are then updated: for each city pair, if the two cities co-occur in the best solution, Q is decreased (reinforce); if they co-occur in the pool but not in the best, Q is increased (discourage). Finally, routes not in the best solution age and are removed once age reaches *agemax*, while new best routes are added with age 0. The loop runs until a time limit (n seconds). Parameters are tuned via i-Race.

## Key Results
- RL-CMSA achieves lower mean min-max tour lengths than HGA on randomly generated instances with n=50, 100, 200 and varying *m* (as % of n), with performance gaps widening for larger *n* and *m*.
- RL-CMSA finds the best-known solution more frequently (#b) than HGA, particularly for larger instances (n=200) and moderate *m* (e.g., 5%, 10% of n).
- On TSPLIB benchmarks (eil51, berlin52, eil76, rat99), RL-CMSA matches or improves HGA’s best objectives while often requiring less wall-clock time.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Q-guided clustering steers construction toward promising groupings
- **Claim:** Reinforcement learning biases probabilistic clustering toward city pairs that co-occur in high-quality solutions, improving route construction.
- **Mechanism:** During the Cluster stage of Construct, cluster centers are seeded using a k-means++–like procedure weighted by squared distance to the depot and learned pairwise Q-values. Remaining cities are assigned to clusters via a weighted cost that includes mean Q-compatibility with current cluster members. High Q-values (near 0) indicate pairs that should be grouped; low Q-values (near 1) indicate pairs to separate.
- **Core assumption:** Co-occurrence of city pairs in historically good routes correlates with their optimal assignment to the same salesman’s tour in new solutions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “probabilistic clustering guided by learned pairwise q-values”; Section 3.1 pseudocode (Algorithm 1) shows Q-values used in seeding (line 10) and assignment (lines 24, 26).
- **Break condition:** If a convergence proxy for Q-values plateaus, all Q-values are reset to 0.5 to discard accumulated preferences and avoid premature convergence.

### Mechanism 2: Compact pool with age management enables efficient high-quality recombination
- **Claim:** A compact, age-managed pool of diverse routes enables efficient recombination via a restricted set-covering MILP, balancing solution quality and computational cost.
- **Mechanism:** After constructing solutions, all distinct routes (by visited-city signature) are merged into a candidate pool *R_cand*. Routes longer than the incumbent are pruned. A set-covering MILP selects exactly *m* routes from *R_cand* covering all cities while minimizing the maximum length. This exact subproblem leverages the pool of high-quality building blocks without the full-scale optimization burden.
- **Core assumption:** High-quality routes often consist of city subsets that appear in optimal or near-optimal solutions; recombining such “building blocks” yields competitive full solutions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes “merges routes into a compact pool, solves a restricted set-covering MILP”; Section 3.2 details merging and pruning; Section 3.3 formulates the MILP.
- **Break condition:** The pool size is bounded by an aging policy: routes not in the current best solution have their age incremented and are removed when age >= *agemax*. If the pool becomes too small or homogenous, the Construct phase may fail to generate novel routes.

### Mechanism 3: Iterative Q-update creates a feedback loop aligning clustering with promising route structures
- **Claim:** Q-value updates reinforce co-occurrences from the current best solution and discourage co-occurrences absent from it, creating a feedback loop that gradually aligns clustering with promising route structures.
- **Mechanism:** After Improvement, pairwise co-occurrence counts are computed for the candidate pool (*S_cand*) and the best solution (*S_best*). For pairs present in the pool, Q-values are nudged: decreased (toward 0) if the pair appears in *S_best* (reinforce), or increased (toward 1) if absent (discourage). The update rule is *Qij ← Qij − lrate·Qij* (reinforce) or *Qij ← Qij + lrate·(1−Qij)* (discourage).
- **Core assumption:** The best solution found so far represents a locally optimal assignment of city pairs; strengthening its pattern improves future constructions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states “Q-values are updated based on co-occurrences in high-quality solutions”; Section 3.5 gives the explicit update rule and convergence proxy.
- **Break condition:** If the convergence proxy (average absolute change of Q-values) does not improve over a window, all Q-values are reset to 0.5 to clear the learned signal.

## Foundational Learning
- **min-max Multiple Traveling Salesman Problem (mTSP):** The optimization problem where the goal is to minimize the longest tour among *m* salesmen, each starting/ending at a single depot and covering all cities exactly once. This objective complicates standard TSP heuristics because improving the maximum route often requires balancing loads.

- **Set-covering formulation for route selection:** The Solve phase uses a MILP that selects exactly *m* routes from a pool to cover all cities, minimizing the maximum route length. Overlapping coverage is allowed in the MILP to ensure feasibility, and duplicates are later removed by the Improve phase.  
  *Quick check:* In the MILP, why is it allowed for a city to be covered by more than one route, and how is feasibility restored later?
- **Q-learning for pairwise preferences:** The Learn phase updates Q-values, which represent the desirability of two cities being in the same route. Values near 0 indicate strong preference for co-occurrence; values near 1 indicate preference for separation.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Construct -> Merge -> Solve -> Improve -> Learn -> Adapt
- **Critical path:** The loop’s backbone is Construct → Merge → Solve → Improve → Learn → Adapt, with the output of each phase feeding the next. The best solution found (R_best) influences learning and adaptation.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Pool size vs. solver cost: Larger candidate pool increases chance of high-quality recombinations but makes the MILP slower.
  - Exploration vs. exploitation: The *d_construct_rate* and *d_improve_rate* control randomness; lower values increase diversity, higher values intensify around current best.
  - Learning rate (*lrate*): Balance between quick adaptation and stability; too high may cause oscillations, too slow may delay convergence.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Frequent Q-value resets indicate learning stagnation or premature convergence.
  - Pool depletion (*R_cand* becoming too small) reduces diversity for the MILP, degrading solution quality.
  - Deterministic clustering (high *d_construct_rate* with stale Q-values) yields near-identical new solutions, limiting exploration.
  - MILP struggles (infeasible or slow) if pool is poorly constructed or too large.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Parameter tuning validation:** Use i-Race on 200-node random instances to confirm that tuned parameters (*nsolutions*, *agemax*, *lrate*, etc.) yield lower mean objective than defaults within the time limit.
  2. **Baseline comparison replication:** Run RL-CMSA and HGA on random instances (n=50/100/200, m=% values) with 40 runs each. Verify that RL-CMSA achieves better or equal mean objective, higher #b (best-run frequency), and often faster time to best, especially for n≥100 and m≥5% of n.
  3. **Search dynamics analysis:** For a large instance (e.g., n=200, m=10%), generate a solution space map (STN) and structural distance violin plots. Confirm that RL-CMSA converges to a narrower, higher-quality region than HGA.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

   *Basis:* Future work mentions enriching the route pool with additional large-scale neighborhoods. Unresolved because the current method only uses local moves and set-covering recombination.
2. **Can reinforcement learning based on higher-order route features be more effective?**  
   *Basis:* Future work suggests extending the RL scheme beyond pairwise co-occurrences. Higher-order structures (triplets, sequences) are not considered.
3. **How does RL-CMSA perform on a wider range of benchmarks?**  
   *Basis:* Future work plans evaluation on broader and larger benchmark families. Current experiments limited to random circular instances and four small TSPLIB cases.
4. **Can the framework handle additional constraints (e.g., capacities, time windows)?**  
   *Basis:* Future work aims to extend to more general routing settings with extra constraints. The current algorithm assumes only a single depot and metric distances, without capacities or time windows.

## Limitations
- The big-M constant for the MILP is unspecified; choosing an arbitrary large value may cause numerical issues or weaken solver performance.
- The exact computation of `twoClosestPoints(u, C_j, D)` is ambiguous, particularly regarding depot handling and tie-breaking.
- The algorithm’s dependence on CPLEX may hinder reproducibility with open-source solvers, which could be slower or less robust.
- Experimental evaluation covers only random circular instances and a few small TSPLIB cases; performance on larger, clustered, or real-world data remains untested.

## Confidence
- **Claim:** RL-CMSA outperforms HGA on min-max mTSP. **Confidence:** Medium (strong empirical evidence but no independent replication; solver-dependent).
- **Claim:** Q-guided clustering improves construction. **Confidence:** Medium (plausible but not isolated via ablation; could be conflated with other components).
- **Claim:** Restricted set-covering MILP yields effective recombinations. **Confidence:** Medium (practical success but the specific role of pool management not disentangled).
