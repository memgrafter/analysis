# Seeing with You: Perception-Reasoning Coevolution for Multimodal Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28618
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28618
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PRCO improves average accuracy by 7.65 points over Qwen2.5‑VL‑3B and by 7.18 points over the 7B variant, surpassing prior RL‑tuned methods on eight benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Multimodal reasoning models trained with reinforcement learning using only final‑answer rewards suffer from blurred credit assignment between perception and reasoning, limiting gains in visual evidence extraction. PRCO (Perception‑Reasoning Coevolution) addresses this by coevolving two roles within a shared policy: an Observer that produces question‑conditioned evidence captions and a Solver that predicts answers using those captions. The Observer receives a utility reward derived from the Solver’s success (modulated by a leakage detector to prevent answer shortcuts), while the Solver uses verifiable outcome rewards. A caption‑first warmup period ensures the Solver initially relies on captions, preserving a high‑variance learning signal for the Observer. Across eight challenging benchmarks, PRCO yields average improvements of over 7 points and reduces perception errors by 39.2% on WeMath, establishing a new state‑of‑the‑art for open‑source multimodal reasoning.

## Method Summary
PRCO employs a single multimodal LLM (e.g., Qwen2.5‑VL) as a shared policy πθ that acts in two modes: Observer and Solver. For a given image *I* and question *q*, the Observer generates an evidence caption *c* conditioned on *I* and *q*. The Solver then predicts an answer *â* conditioned on *c* (and optionally *I*). Training follows group‑relative policy optimization (GRPO). For each sample, *G<sub>O</sub>* captions are sampled; for each caption, *G<sub>S</sub>* answers are sampled. The Solver reward *r<sub>S</sub>* combines a verifiable correctness score (weight 0.9) and a format score (0.1). The Observer reward *r<sub>O</sub>* is the mean of the Solver rewards for that caption multiplied by *(1−Ileak)*, where *Ileak* is a binary classifier that flags captions containing the final answer. Advantages are computed separately per role: Observer advantage is *r<sub>O</sub>* minus the mean of all *r<sub>O</sub>* values; Solver advantage (for a selected caption with non‑zero variance) is *r<sub>S</sub>* minus the mean of the corresponding *r<sub>S</sub>* group. To maintain a discriminative utility signal, the Solver is warmed up for 40 steps without image access (*I<sub>S</sub>* = ∅), forcing reliance on captions; after warmup the image is reintroduced. The shared policy is updated with the combined GRPO loss, effectively decoupling perception and reasoning updates while enabling parameter sharing.

## Key Results
- Average accuracy gains of **+7.65** (3B) and **+7.18** (7B) points over base Qwen2.5‑VL models on eight benchmarks.
- Outperforms prior open‑source RL‑tuned methods (DAPO, VPPO) across all evaluated datasets.
- On WeMath, perception errors are reduced by **39.2%** relative to the base model, while GRPO achieves only a 7.6% reduction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Separating perception and reasoning into distinct roles (Observer and Solver) with role‑specific rewards decouples gradient updates, enabling targeted improvement in visual evidence extraction.
- **Mechanism:** The shared policy πθ executes two roles. The Observer generates a question‑conditioned caption *c* from image *I* and question *q*. The Solver produces an answer conditioned on *c* (and optionally *I*). The Observer’s reward *r<sub>O</sub>* is a utility signal derived from the Solver’s verifier success, *not* from direct answer correctness. The Solver uses a verifiable outcome reward *r<sub>S</sub>*. Group‑relative advantages are computed separately for each role’s rollouts, so policy gradients for perception tokens (Observer) and reasoning tokens (Solver) are isolated.
- **Core assumption:** Token‑level credit assignment blurs when a single final‑answer reward updates the entire sequence; isolating the optimization objectives for perception and reasoning prevents the reasoning gradient from dominating and allows perception‑specific improvements.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract]: “PRCO employs role‑specific reward signals: the Solver is optimized using verifiable outcome rewards on the final answer, while the Observer receives a utility reward derived from the Solver’s downstream success.”  
  [section 2.2]: “We therefore compute relative advantages separately for the two roles and optimize the shared policy over the combined rollouts.”  
  [section 3.3]: “Removing Observer updates yields consistent drops… This confirms that utility‑driven evidence extraction provides complementary benefits… by strengthening the intermediate evidence signal.”
- **Break condition:** If role‑specific gradients are not properly isolated (e.g., due to shared advantage computation or incorrect grouping), perception gains would mirror those of standard outcome‑only RLVR (minimal, as shown in Fig. 1).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Observer’s utility reward, calculated from the Solver’s verifier success *and* filtered by an answer‑leakage suppressor, trains the Observer to produce *evidence captions* rather than *answer hints*.
- **Mechanism:** For a caption *c*, reward *r<sub>O</sub>* = *(1 − Ileak(q, c)) * E<sub>ˆa~π<sub>θ</sub></sub>[V(ˆa, a)]*. *Ileak* is a binary classifier flagging if *c* contains the final answer. The expectation is approximated by sampling multiple Solver answers conditioned on *c*. This encourages captions that are useful for solving *and* are not answer shortcuts.
- **Core assumption:** A caption that directly states the answer will be flagged by *Ileak* and receive zero reward, even if the Solver would succeed; conversely, a caption with genuine visual evidence, even if imperfect, can still lead to Solver success and yield positive utility.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 2.3]: “Directly verifying the intrinsic quality of an evidence caption is difficult. We therefore train the Observer through the downstream utility that its caption provides to the Solver. A key failure mode is answer shortcutting… To suppress this behavior, we use an auxiliary LLM‑based leakage checker.”  
  [section A.4]: “Removing the leakage checker also degrades the overall average, indicating that suppressing answer leakage is important for learning useful intermediate evidence.”  
  [section 3.3 (ablation)]: “PRCO w/o Leakage Checker… underperforms full PRCO‑7B… Without leakage checking, the Observer is more likely to exploit answer shortcutting… This weakens the utility reward as a learning signal for evidence extraction.”
- **Break condition:** If the leakage checker is inaccurate (high false positives/negatives) or if the Solver’s verifier is unreliable, the utility signal becomes noisy and the Observer may optimize for incorrect objectives (e.g., captions that hack the leakage checker but remain useless).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A caption‑first warmup period (Solver trained *without* image access) ensures the Solver initially learns to rely on the Observer’s caption, preserving a high‑variance utility signal for the Observer before the Solver regains image input.
- **Mechanism:** During the first *W* training steps, the Solver’s rollout uses *I<sub>S</sub>* = ∅ (no image). After warmup, *I<sub>S</sub>* = *I* (image provided). This prevents early Solver behavior from ignoring captions and solving directly from the image, which would collapse the variation in *r<sub>O</sub>* across different captions and weaken the Observer’s learning signal.
- **Core assumption:** If the Solver has access to the image too early, it will bypass the caption, making the Observer’s utility reward insensitive to caption quality (low variance). A period of caption‑only conditioning forces the Solver to learn to extract information from *c*, thus maintaining a discriminative utility signal for the Observer.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [section 2.4]: “We find that if the Solver receives both the image and the caption too early, it tends to ignore the caption and solve directly from the image, which can drown out the learning signal for improving captions. Therefore, we first warm up the Solver to solve using only captions…”  
  [section 3.3]: “Removing warmup degrades performance… Without warmup, the Solver can rely on raw visual inputs too early, which weakens the learning signal for the Observer.”  
  [Fig. 9]: “Without warmup, the standard deviation [of caption rewards] decreases rapidly. Solver outcomes then become largely insensitive to which caption is provided.”
- **Break condition:** If warmup duration *W* is too short, the Solver may still learn to ignore captions early; if too long, the Solver may never recover sufficient image‑grounded reasoning, harming final performance (as shown by *PRCO w/ I<sub>S</sub>=∅* in Table 5).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)** – *Why needed*: PRCO is an RLVR method; understanding outcome rewards, group‑relative advantage (GRPO/DAPO), and the standard final‑answer‑only loop is prerequisite to seeing what PRCO changes. *Quick check*: In standard GRPO, how is the advantage computed for a group of sampled responses to the same prompt, and what does this normalization aim to achieve?
- **Concept: Credit Assignment in Sequence Generation** – *Why needed*: PRCO’s central thesis is that final‑answer rewards blur credit assignment between perception (caption tokens) and reasoning (answer tokens). Understanding token‑level vs. sequence‑level rewards, and why separating them might help, is key. *Quick check*: If a model generates both an image caption and a final answer, and only the final answer is correct, why might a standard RL update fail to improve the caption quality?
- **Concept: Multimodal Grounding and Evidence Extraction** – *Why needed*: The Observer’s task is to produce a “question‑conditioned evidence caption.” This requires understanding what constitutes useful visual evidence for a given question, distinct from full scene description or answer synthesis. *Quick check*: For a question like “What is the length of the red segment?”, what makes a caption useful versus unhelpful or misleading for a downstream solver?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Shared policy πθ → Observer → evidence caption c; c → Solver → answer â; Leakage Checker (Ileak) scores (q,c); Verifier (V) scores (â,a); Rollout Sampler produces G<sub>O</sub> captions and per‑caption G<sub>S</sub> answers; Advantage Compute separates group statistics for Observer and Solver; Policy Optimizer applies GRPO to update πθ.

**Critical path**  
1. For each training sample *(I, q, a)*, sample *G<sub>O</sub>* Observer captions *{c<sub>k</sub>}*.  
2. For each caption *c<sub>k</sub>*, sample *G<sub>S</sub>* Solver answers *{â<sub>k,g</sub>}* (with *I<sub>S</sub>* = ∅ during warmup, then *I*).  
3. Compute Solver rewards *r<sub>S</sub><sub>k,g</sub>* via verifier + format.  
4. Compute Observer rewards *r<sub>O</sub><sub>k</sub>* = *(1 − Ileak(q, c<sub>k</sub>)) * mean(r<sub>S</sub><sub>k,g</sub>)*.  
5. Compute group‑relative advantages: *Â<sub>O</sub><sub>k</sub>* = *r<sub>O</sub><sub>k</sub>* − mean({r<sub>O</sub>}); *Â<sub>S</sub><sub>k,g</sub>* = *r<sub>S</sub><sub>k,g</sub>* − mean({r<sub>S</sub><sub>k,g</sub>})* for the selected caption.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Shared vs. Separate Policies*: Sharing reduces parameters and enables transfer but may cause interference; separate policies would cleanly decouple but double size and hinder knowledge sharing.  
- *Rollout Budget Allocation*: Fixed total rollouts must be split between *G<sub>O</sub>* and *G<sub>S</sub>*. Increasing *G<sub>S</sub>* yields larger gains beyond a basic *G<sub>O</sub>* threshold.  
- *Leakage Checker Cost*: Using a large external LLM (e.g., Qwen3‑VL‑8B) adds inference overhead; a smaller fine‑tuned classifier could be cheaper but risk performance.  
- *Warmup Duration*: Heuristic (40 steps); too short fails to force caption reliance, too long prevents recovery of image‑grounded reasoning.

**Failure signatures**  
- *Observer leakage*: High *Ileak* frequency or captions directly stating the answer.  
- *Solver caption neglect*: Solver accuracy unchanged when removing captions, indicating ignoring the evidence.  
- *Collapsed Observer variance*: Standard deviation of *r<sub>O</sub>* near zero, making Observer gradient vanish.  
- *Role confusion*: Ambiguous prompts causing the policy to mix behaviors; monitor role‑specific format adherence.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Validate gradient decoupling**: Log gradient norms separately for caption vs. answer tokens during training; confirm both roles receive meaningful updates and are not dominated by one.  
2. **Ablate caption‑first warmup**: Train with *I<sub>S</sub>* = *I* from step 0. Measure (a) variance of Observer rewards over time, (b) final accuracy, (c) proportion of Solver answers that are correct even when given a wrong caption. Expect low variance and smaller gains without warmup.  
3. **Test rollout group size tradeoff**: Fix total rollout budget (e.g., *G<sub>O</sub>* × *G<sub>S</sub>* = 32). Vary *G<sub>O</sub>* ∈ {2,4,8} and set *G<sub>S</sub>* = total/*G<sub>O</sub>*. Plot overall accuracy vs. *G<sub>O</sub>*. Expect peak at *G<sub>O</sub>*=4, *G<sub>S</sub>*=8 (as in Fig. 8), confirming Solver‑side diversity is more critical beyond a basic Observer diversity threshold.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does PRCO generalize to open‑ended multimodal generation tasks where reward signals are less well‑defined compared to tasks with concise, verifiable answers?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] “Our current study focuses on multimodal reasoning benchmarks with concise and verifiable answers. Further evaluation is needed to determine how well PRCO generalizes to more open‑ended generation settings. Extending the framework to broader multimodal generation tasks is an important direction for future work, since reward signals in these settings are often less well defined.”
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only evaluates on benchmarks with short, answerable questions; open‑ended tasks require different reward designs and may break the assumption of verifiable outcomes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Extend PRCO to tasks like image captioning, visual storytelling, or embodied planning and measure performance compared to baselines; design proxy reward models for open‑ended outputs.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does the auxiliary supervision for answer leakage detection introduce noise or computational overhead that could be mitigated?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] “In addition, the Observer is trained with auxiliary supervision for leakage detection and answer verification. Although this auxiliary supervision is helpful in our setting, it may also introduce additional noise and computational overhead.”
- **Why unresolved:** The paper uses a separate model (Qwen3‑VL‑8B‑Instruct) for leakage checking, adding computational cost. The impact on training stability and final performance is not deeply analyzed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablate the leakage checker and measure changes in training dynamics, final accuracy, and computational cost; explore alternative, cheaper leakage detection methods.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** For visual inputs with complex global structure, fine‑grained spatial relations, or geometric details, are short captions sufficient intermediate representations, or are richer representations needed?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] “Finally, representing visual evidence as short captions is inherently lossy. Important aspects of the input, such as global structure (e.g., layout and texture), fine‑grained spatial relations, and geometric details that are difficult to compress faithfully into text, may be only partially preserved. Future work could therefore explore richer intermediate representations for visual inputs that cannot be adequately captured by captions.”
- **Why unresolved:** PRCO relies on captions as the sole evidence channel between Observer and Solver. For tasks requiring precise spatial or structural information, captions may be inadequate, but this is not systematically evaluated.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Test PRCO on tasks where spatial precision is critical (e.g., architectural plans, circuit diagrams) and compare caption‑based evidence to alternatives like spatial token sequences, scene graphs, or coordinate annotations; measure impact on final accuracy.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What are the dynamics and stability of the coevolution process between Observer and Solver? Could the mutual feedback loop lead to collapse or oscillations over longer training?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper uses a coevolutionary training where Observer’s utility reward comes from Solver’s success and Solver’s reward is independent, creating a feedback loop.
- **Why unresolved:** Training curves (Fig. 3) show consistent gains, but long‑term stability beyond 200 steps or with different hyperparameters is not explored. Theoretically, such coupled RL processes can diverge.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Run longer training runs and monitor metrics like caption reward variance, solution diversity, and performance on held‑out data; analyze theoretical conditions for convergence in dual‑role RL.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Does parameter sharing between Observer and Solver in a single policy help or hinder learning, especially as the two roles may require different optimization dynamics?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] PRCO uses a shared policy πθ for both roles with role‑specific prompts, assuming a single set of parameters can optimize both objectives.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not compare against separate policies for Observer and Solver. Sharing may cause interference if optimal updates for perception and reasoning conflict.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement a variant with separate policies and compare sample efficiency, final performance, and parameter norms; analyze gradient conflicts using similarity metrics.

## Limitations
- Under‑specified reward details (verifier normalization, leakage prompt) may cause replication gaps.
- Heuristic hyperparameters (warmup steps, rollout *G<sub>O</sub>*/ *G<sub>S</sub>* counts) could be suboptimal for other datasets.
- Dependency on a large external LLM for leakage adds computational cost and potential bias.

- Utility reward + leakage suppression: Medium
- Caption‑first warmup: Medium
- Overall performance gains: High
