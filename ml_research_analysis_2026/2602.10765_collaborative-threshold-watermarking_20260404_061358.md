# Collaborative Threshold Watermarking

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10765
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10765
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** (t,K)-threshold watermarking enables only coalitions of ≥t clients to verify model provenance with minimal accuracy loss and strong robustness.

## Executive Summary
Federated learning (FL) model watermarking faces two key challenges: watermark dilution as the number of clients grows, and the ability of any single client to verify or remove the watermark. This paper introduces (t, K)-threshold watermarking, a collaborative scheme that enables only coalitions of at least t clients to verify model provenance without revealing the secret key. The approach combines Shamir secret sharing to split the watermark key, secure aggregation to hide individual contributions during embedding, and an adaptive scaling factor to maintain stable embedding strength across heterogeneous client updates. Evaluations on image classification with ResNet-18 across CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny ImageNet, with up to 128 clients, show that the proposed watermark remains detectable (z-score ≥4, false positive rate ≈3.2×10⁻⁵) while a baseline per-client watermark fails beyond 16 clients. With a watermark strength of c=0.025, the scheme incurs negligible accuracy loss (less than 0.25 percentage points). The watermark also demonstrates robustness against fine-tuning (≤20% of training data), 4-bit quantization, 90% pruning, and adaptive fine-tuning; only knowledge distillation with sufficient data can reliably remove it, albeit at high computational cost.

## Method Summary
The protocol begins with a trusted dealer (or a distributed key generation ceremony) sampling a secret watermark vector τ from a Gaussian distribution and applying Shamir's (t, K) secret sharing to produce shares {s_k} for each client. Public Lagrange coefficients {λ_k} are derived from publicly chosen evaluation points. Each client computes an additive embedding share w_k = λ_k s_k, which satisfies Σ w_k = τ. During each training round, a client trains locally to obtain a model θ_local, computes the update Δθ = θ_local - θ_global, and tracks an exponential moving average (EMA) of the squared update norm: ema ← β·ema + (1-β)·||Δθ||². The client then calculates a local scaling factor scale_k = c · (||Δθ||² / ema). All clients securely aggregate their scale_k values to obtain a global scale_total = Σ scale_k. The client then embeds the watermark by modifying its local model: u = θ_local + (scale_total / |S_r|) · w_k, where S_r is the set of participating clients. The server aggregates the watermarked updates as usual to produce the next global model θ_global. Verification is performed by any coalition of at least t clients: each member i computes the inner product ⟨θ, s_i⟩ locally, and these values are securely aggregated with weights λ_i to obtain ⟨θ, τ⟩ = Σ λ_i ⟨θ, s_i⟩ without ever reconstructing τ. A one-sided z-test compares the resulting cosine similarity against a distribution from unwatermarked models; if the z-score exceeds 4 (corresponding to a false positive rate of ≈3.2×10⁻⁵), the model is claimed as watermarked.

## Key Results
- The threshold watermark remains detectable (z-score ≥4) across increasing numbers of clients up to 128, while a baseline per-client watermark fails beyond 16 clients.
- With watermark strength c=0.025, the scheme incurs minimal accuracy degradation: −0.12 percentage points on CIFAR-10, −0.21 pp on CIFAR-100, and −0.25 pp on Tiny ImageNet.
- The watermark persists (z≥4) under fine-tuning with ≤20% of training data, 4-bit quantization, 90% pruning, and adaptive fine-tuning; only knowledge distillation with adequate data reliably removes it, and even then only at substantial computational cost.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Shamir secret sharing enables (t, K)-threshold verification by splitting the watermark key τ into shares such that any coalition of size <t cannot reconstruct τ.
- **Mechanism:** A dealer (or DKG) samples τ and uses Shamir's (t, K) scheme to generate shares {s_k}. Each client derives an additive embedding share w_k = λ_k s_k using public Lagrange coefficients λ_k, ensuring Σ w_k = τ. During verification, a coalition of ≥t clients combines their shares via polynomial interpolation to compute ⟨θ_s, τ⟩ = Σ_{i∈S} λ_i ⟨θ_s, s_i⟩ without ever reconstructing τ in the clear.
- **Core assumption:** Shamir's information-theoretic secrecy holds: the joint distribution of <t shares is independent of τ.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "clients collaboratively embed a shared watermark using Shamir secret sharing, so only coalitions of at least t clients to verify without revealing the key."
  - [section 2.4] Formal definition of Shamir sharing and reconstruction.
  - [section 4.1] Algorithm 1 and derivation of additive shares w_k.
  - [corpus] Neighbors focus on watermarking but do not directly challenge Shamir's secrecy properties; assumption stands unless cryptographic breakthrough occurs.
- **Break condition:** If an adversary controls ≥t client shares or breaks the underlying finite field polynomial reconstruction.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Adaptive scaling via exponential moving average (EMA) of update norms stabilizes watermark embedding strength across heterogeneous client updates and dynamic training rounds.
- **Mechanism:** Each client k maintains ema^{(r)}_k = β·ema^{(r-1)}_k + (1-β)·||Δθ^{(k)}_r||². The per-client scaling factor is scale_k = c·||Δθ^{(k)}_r||² / ema^{(r)}_k. Clients submit scale_k via secure aggregation to obtain a global scaletotal = Σ scale_k. The watermarked update is u^{(k)}_r = θ^{(k)}_r + (scaletotal / |S_r|)·w^{(S_r)}_k, preserving additive structure.
- **Core assumption:** The ratio ||Δθ_r||² / ema_r normalizes local update magnitudes relative to recent training dynamics, making embedding strength robust to varying local data and optimizer states.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2] Equations (4)-(6) and description of EMA tracker and global scaling.
  - [section 5.3] Table 1 shows minimal accuracy drop (e.g., -0.12pp on CIFAR-10 at c=0.025), indicating scaling preserves utility.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "Robust Client-Server Watermarking" use fixed scaling; this adaptive approach is novel to this paper.
- **Break condition:** If clients maliciously report manipulated scale_k values that pass secure aggregation but distort global scaletotal, or if EMA tracking fails under non-IID data causing unstable embeddings.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Reconstruction-free verification computes the watermark test statistic directly from Shamir shares without reconstructing τ, preserving secrecy even under repeated checks.
- **Mechanism:** A verifying coalition S (|S|≥t) computes the inner product ⟨θ_s, τ⟩ = Σ_{i∈S} λ_i ⟨θ_s, s_i⟩ via secure aggregation of locally computed ⟨θ_s, s_i⟩. The z-score is z = (cos(θ_s, τ) - μ) / σ, where μ, σ are precomputed from unwatermarked models (Fig. 2). Verification accepts if z ≥ 4 (FPR ≈ 3.2·10⁻⁵).
- **Core assumption:** The distribution of cosine similarities cos(θ_s, τ) for unwatermarked models is approximately normal N(μ, σ²), calibrated empirically.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3] Equation (8) and (9), description of reconstruction-free inner product.
  - [section 5.1] Figure 2 validates normality across datasets with fitted normal curves.
  - [section 5.2] Figure 3a shows z≥4 sustained up to K=128, confirming detectability.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "FLClear" use visual triggers; this white-box inner-product method is distinct and not directly compared.
- **Break condition:** If the coalition size <t, or if the cosine similarity distribution significantly deviates from normality (e.g., due to distribution shift), causing inflated false positives/negatives.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Shamir Secret Sharing**  
  **Why needed:** Forms the cryptographic backbone for threshold verification; splits τ so that only ≥t clients can jointly compute ⟨θ_s, τ⟩.  
  **Quick check:** How does the Lagrange interpolation formula τ = Σ λ_i s_i allow reconstruction without revealing τ during verification?

- **Concept: Secure Aggregation**  
  **Why needed:** Allows clients to embed additive shares w_k and report scale_k without the server learning individual values, preserving τ secrecy from an untrusted server.  
  **Quick check:** In the secure aggregation step for scaletotal, what information does the server learn, and why is this sufficient for the protocol?

- **Concept: Z-score Hypothesis Testing**  
  **Why needed:** Provides a statistically calibrated detection threshold (z≥4) to distinguish watermarked models from random chance with controlled false positive rate.  
  **Quick check:** Why is the one-sided z-test appropriate here, and what does the threshold z=4 imply about the false positive rate?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Dealer/DKG -> Client Embedding -> Server Aggregation -> Verification

**Critical path:** SETUP (τ sharing) → [Round r: LOCAL TRAIN → EMA update → scale_k calc → SECAGG(scaletotal) → u^{(k)}_r embed → SECAGG(θ_r)] × T → Verify (inner product via shares)

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Watermark strength (c) vs. accuracy: higher c increases z-scores but degrades task accuracy.
- Threshold (t) vs. robustness: lower t simplifies verification for small coalitions but increases risk of unauthorized reconstruction; higher t requires larger coalitions but better protects against small-group collusion.
- EMA decay (β) vs. adaptability: higher β smooths scaling more aggressively but may lag behind sudden norm changes.

**Failure signatures:**
- z-score < 4 on a known watermarked model (embedding failure or over-aggressive removal).
- Significant accuracy drop (>1%) at moderate c (poor utility preservation).
- Verification fails when coalition size < t (expected) or when ≥t but shares inconsistent (protocol deviation).
- High variance in z-scores across seeds (unstable training/embedding).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Scalability test:** Vary K ∈ {4,8,16,32,64,128} with fixed t, c. Measure z-score. Expect baseline to collapse at K≥16; proposed method should maintain z≥4.
2. **Fidelity test:** Vary c ∈ {0.025,0.05,0.075,0.1} at fixed K (e.g., 32). Record test accuracy and z-score. Identify c that yields minimal accuracy loss while keeping z≥4.
3. **Robustness - Fine-tuning:** Fine-tune a watermarked model on p% ∈ {1,5,10,20} of training data for 100 epochs. Plot accuracy vs. z-score. Expect z≥4 until accuracy degrades substantially.

## Limitations
- Finite field representation is unspecified: Shamir secret sharing is defined over a finite field, but the paper treats the continuous Gaussian watermark τ without clarifying modulus, quantization, or handling of large Lagrange coefficients for K>20, raising feasibility concerns.
- Threshold t is not disclosed in experiments; the exact value used affects security guarantees and is necessary for reproducibility and security assessment.
- The EMA scaling formula shown (multiply vs. divide) appears ambiguous and potentially inconsistent with the reported negligible accuracy impact; clarification is needed.
- The verification requires white-box access to model parameters to compute inner products with secret shares, limiting applicability to scenarios where model weights are exposed (e.g., not black-box APIs).
- Security relies on the assumption that at most t-1 clients are compromised; a coalition of size ≥t can reconstruct the watermark key and potentially remove the watermark or impersonate the owner.

## Confidence

- Adaptive EMA scaling: Low

## Next Checks
1. **Finite‑field implementation:** Build a Shamir sharing scheme over a large prime (e.g., 2^61−1), quantize τ into fixed‑point integers, compute Lagrange coefficients modulo p, and verify Σ w_k = τ exactly. Then test watermark detectability as K scales to 128 to assess overflow/precision issues.
2. **EMA scaling clarification:** Reverse‑engineer the intended scaling equation by grid‑searching over c and β while monitoring per‑round scale_total and final test accuracy. If the reported <1 % drop occurs only when ema appears in the denominator (division), flag the discrepancy.
3. **Adaptive fine‑tuning recreation:** Reconstruct the missing attack algorithm (likely accumulating gradients over recent checkpoints to estimate τ') and evaluate whether it reliably removes the watermark at low data fractions; compare against standard fine‑tuning to isolate the effect.
