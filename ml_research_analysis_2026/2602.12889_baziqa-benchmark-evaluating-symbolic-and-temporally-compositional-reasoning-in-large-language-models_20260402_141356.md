# BaziQA-Benchmark: Evaluating Symbolic and Temporally Compositional Reasoning in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12889
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12889
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: LLMs exceed random baseline (25%) but peak at only 36.7% macro-average accuracy (DeepSeek-Chat-V3), indicating substantial unsolved difficulty.

## Executive Summary
BaziQA-Benchmark introduces a standardized test from professional Bazi astrology competitions to evaluate symbolic and temporally compositional reasoning in LLMs. The benchmark provides 200 fixed-chart multiple-choice questions, evaluated multi-turn and with a Structured Reasoning Protocol (SRP) that constrains inference order. All tested models significantly exceed the 25% random baseline but achieve low absolute accuracy, with a macro-average peak of 36.7% (DeepSeek-Chat-V3). Performance varies by competition year and reasoning domain: static symbolic domains (e.g., personality, family relations) yield higher accuracy than temporally compositional ones (e.g., annual fortune). SRP produces mixed effects, improving stability in some contexts but degrading accuracy in others, highlighting sensitivity to reasoning order. Systematic failures occur on precise temporal localization and multi-condition symbolic judgments, and moderate pairwise model agreement suggests non-trivial ensemble headroom alongside shared intrinsic challenges.

## Method Summary
BaziQA-Benchmark evaluates LLMs on symbolic reasoning using 200 multiple-choice questions derived from professional Bazi astrology competitions. Each question is based on a fixed natal chart (pre-computed and formatted textually with elements like Heavenly Stems, Earthly Branches, Ten Gods, and luck cycles) and requires reasoning about temporal interactions (e.g., decadal and annual cycles). Models are tested in zero-shot or few-shot settings without fine-tuning, using two protocols: (1) **multi-turn**, where the chart is provided once and then 5 questions are asked sequentially in one conversation; and (2) **Structured Reasoning Protocol (SRP)**, which enforces a three-step scaffold (Quantitative Scan → Severity Grading → Event Mapping) via prompts. Accuracy is measured against official answer keys, with macro-averaged scores across competition years and domain-specific breakdowns. Multiple runs per model-year are conducted to account for decoding stochasticity.

## Key Results
- Low absolute accuracy: models peak at 36.7% macro-average (DeepSeek-Chat-V3), far below human-level.
- Performance varies by domain and year: static symbolic domains (e.g., personality, family) outperform temporally compositional ones (e.g., annual fortune); year-wise differences correlate with temporal compositional density.
- SRP yields mixed effects: improves stability in some contexts but degrades accuracy in others; systematic failures in temporal localization and multi-condition symbolic judgments; moderate pairwise agreement suggests both shared and model-specific error modes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Structured Reasoning Protocol (SRP) changes accuracy by constraining inference order.** SRP decomposes Bazi inference into "Quantitative Scan → Severity Grading → Event Mapping," forcing models to consider global chart properties before local interactions. This can reduce noise from mis-prioritization but may hurt if the imposed order is suboptimal. Evidence: SRP's mixed effects (improving stability in some contexts, degrading in others) as reported in the abstract and section II.E. Break condition: if models already follow the order internally, SRP may force redundant steps.

**Mechanism 2: Year-wise performance variation driven by temporal compositional density.** Questions from years requiring integration of more temporal layers (natal + decadal + annual) create combinatorial interactions that models struggle with. Core assumption: Bazi's temporal precedence rules are not trivially learnable. Evidence: Non-monotonic year-wise performance and authors' interpretation that "temporal composition remains a major challenge" (section IV.B). Break condition: if year differences are due to uncontrolled factors like wording shifts.

**Mechanism 3: Fixed symbolic chart representation isolates inference from chart derivation.** By providing a pre-formatted chart, the benchmark ensures all models operate on identical inputs, attributing accuracy differences to inference rather than calendrical conversion. Evidence: Design explicitly separates derivation from inference (section II.C). Break condition: if the fixed representation loses critical cues or introduces biases.

## Foundational Learning
- **Symbolic Chart Literacy (Bazi-specific)**: Why needed: To understand the task, an engineer must grasp basic Bazi elements (Heavenly Stems, Earthly Branches, Ten Gods, palaces) and their static relations. Quick check: "In a Bazi chart, what does the 'Day Master' represent, and why is its 'strength' a foundational quantitative assessment?"
- **Temporal Layering in Symbolic Systems**: Why needed: The benchmark's key difficulty is temporal composition; an engineer must understand how a natal chart interacts with decadal luck cycles and annual influences, and that these layers can override each other. Quick check: "If a natal chart indicates a weak Day Master but the current annual cycle brings strong supportive elements, which layer typically takes precedence in Bazi interpretation for that year's fortune?"
- **Evaluation Protocol Sensitivity**: Why needed: The paper shows accuracy depends heavily on evaluation protocol (multi-turn vs. SRP). An engineer must appreciate that benchmark scores are conditional measurements. Quick check: "Why might enforcing a specific reasoning order (SRP) improve accuracy for 'annual fortune' questions but hurt it for 'personality' questions?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input Formatter → (optional SRP Wrapper) → Inference Engine → Scoring Module → Aggregation & Analysis

**Critical path**: For each of 200 chart-question sets: load fixed chart context, then sequentially present 5 questions in a conversation (multi-turn) or via SRP prompts; extract model's chosen option; compare to answer key; aggregate accuracies across subjects, years, domains.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- SRP vs. Freedom: SRP provides diagnostic clarity on reasoning order sensitivity but may force unnatural or inefficient reasoning paths, under-estimating capability.
- Multi-turn context: Re-using chart and prior Q&A reflects real evaluation but risks contextual accumulation (model "remembers" earlier answers), an uncontrolled confound.
- Domain labeling: Heuristic text-matching for domain tags is efficient but error-prone; domain analysis is exploratory.

**Failure signatures**:
- Temporal mislocalization: model picks correct outcome but attributes it to wrong temporal layer.
- Multi-condition symbolic conflict: model fails to apply precedence rules when conditions conflict.
- SRP degradation pattern: accuracy drops when SRP forces "Severity Grading" before seeing all evidence.
- Year-specific collapse: model performs normally on some years but fails on others (e.g., 2022/2024), suggesting high temporal compositional density.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Baseline replication: run all models in multi-turn to confirm paper's macro-average accuracy and year-wise variation.
2. SRP A/B test: for DeepSeek-Chat-V3, run all questions with and without SRP; compute delta per question; analyze if SRP helps more on annual fortune domain and reduces variance.
3. Temporal density probe: annotate each question with number of temporal layers (1: natal only; 2: +decadal; 3: +annual); plot accuracy vs. layer count; test correlation with temporal compositional density.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: What underlying mechanisms in LLMs cause systematic failures on precise temporal localization and multi-condition symbolic judgments in Bazi reasoning? Basis: paper identifies failures but doesn't diagnose root causes. Unresolved because benchmark reveals modes but can't distinguish between temporal reasoning deficits, symbolic rule application, error propagation, or representation limits. Needed evidence: controlled ablations isolating temporal vs. symbolic, fine-grained error analysis tracing to inference steps, probing internal representations.
- **Open Question 2**: Why does SRP produce heterogeneous effects and what does this sensitivity reveal about model reasoning strategies? Basis: SRP yields mixed effects, domain-specific variations. Unresolved because paper doesn't explain why fixed order aligns with some questions but misaligns with others. Needed evidence: step-level accuracy under SRP, comparing reasoning traces with/without SRP, correlating SRP effects with question features.
- **Open Question 3**: What architectural or training factors explain domain-specific performance heterogeneity? Basis: no single model dominates all domains; variation in strengths. Unresolved because evaluation compares model families without ablative controls for architecture, pretraining, fine-tuning. Needed evidence: controlled experiments with models of similar scale but different pretraining, targeted fine-tuning, probing for domain-specific knowledge acquisition.
- **Open Question 4**: What do moderate pairwise model agreement rates imply about distribution of failure modes? Basis: moderate agreement implies shared failure modes but also distinct subsets. Unresolved because agreement analysis at question level without decomposing why models agree/disagree. Needed evidence: disagreement analysis clustering by features, human expert annotations of error types, ensemble experiments.

## Limitations
- Missing prompt templates and exact chart formatting hinder exact replication.
- SRP's textual constraints are unspecified, risking inconsistent implementation.
- Year-wise and domain results may be confounded by uncontrolled factors (wording shifts, heuristic labels).

## Confidence
- Low absolute accuracy (max 36.7%): High
- Performance varies by year and domain: High
- SRP has mixed effects on accuracy/stability: Medium
- Temporal compositional density drives difficulty: Low
- Systematic failures in temporal localization and multi‑condition judgments: High
- Moderate pairwise model agreement suggests ensemble headroom: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **SRP reproducibility test**: Implement SRP as described and run multi-turn vs. SRP across three model families (e.g., GPT-4, Claude, Llama). Expected: SRP improves stability for static domains but may reduce accuracy for temporally compositional ones.
2. **Temporal density probe**: Manually annotate each question with number of temporal layers (1,2,3). Perform mixed-effects logistic regression of correctness on layer count. Significant positive coefficient supports Mechanism 2.
3. **Chart representation ablation**: Convert fixed symbolic chart back to raw birth data (e.g., date‑time strings) and evaluate models with this input instead. Substantial accuracy drop would confirm isolation effectiveness; negligible drop would challenge design premise.
