# Recycling Failures: Salvaging Exploration in RLVR via Fine-Grained Off-Policy Guidance

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.24110
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24110
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: SCOPE achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy of 46.6% on in-distribution math benchmarks and 53.4% on out-of-distribution tasks, while increasing output diversity by 13.5% relative to GRPO.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) typically uses sparse binary rewards that treat any partially correct rollout as a total failure, discarding valuable intermediate reasoning steps and causing exploration collapse. SCOPE (Step-wise Correction for On-Policy Exploration) resolves this by leveraging a Process Reward Model to identify the first erroneous step in a failed rollout, preserving the correct on-policy prefix and generating a rectified suffix via off-policy supervised learning. A distribution-aware selection filters outlier corrections, and a hybrid objective updates the policy on the prefix with reinforcement learning and on the suffix with supervised fine-tuning. This approach significantly improves accuracy, diversity, and training stability across multiple math benchmarks.

## Method Summary
SCOPE modifies the standard RLVR pipeline by salvaging failed rollouts. After generating a trajectory, a verifier provides a binary reward; if the reward is zero, a Process Reward Model (PRM) identifies the earliest step where the reasoning became incorrect. The trajectory is split into a correct on-policy prefix and an erroneous suffix. The prefix is retained for policy optimization, while the suffix is replaced by a corrected sequence generated off-policy (e.g., via a supervised model or beam search). A distribution-aware filter removes highly atypical corrections. The final training signal combines an on-policy reinforcement loss (e.g., GRPO) for the prefix and a supervised cross-entropy loss for the corrected suffix.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy**: Average 46.6% on in-distribution benchmarks (AIME24/25, AMC, MATH-500, Minerva, Olympiad) and 53.4% on out-of-distribution tasks (ARC-c, GPQA*, MMLU-Pro), setting a new state-of-the-art for Qwen2.5-Math-7B.
- **Diversity**: Output diversity increased by 13.5% relative to GRPO, measured by entropy or uniqueness of generations.
- **Efficiency**: Dramatically reduces discard of near-miss trajectories and maintains higher policy entropy throughout training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SCOPE succeeds by converting sparse binary feedback into fine-grained corrective guidance. The PRM provides step-level error localization, enabling the system to isolate exactly where reasoning derailed. By preserving the on-policy prefix, SCOPE retains the model’s own exploratory efforts and avoids catastrophic forgetting of useful reasoning patterns. The off-policy rectification supplies a correct suffix without requiring additional on-policy rollouts, improving sample efficiency. The hybrid objective balances reinforcement learning (which encourages exploration on the prefix) and supervised learning (which provides a reliable target for the suffix). Distribution-aware selection prevents outlier corrections from destabilizing training, leading to more stable convergence and higher final performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**: Training paradigm where rewards are computed by an automatic verifier (e.g., checking math answer correctness) rather than human labelers or learned reward models.
  - *Why needed*: Enables scalable reinforcement learning on tasks with objective correctness criteria.
  - *Quick check*: Is the reward signal derived from a deterministic verifier (e.g., unit test, math expression evaluator)?

- **Process Reward Model (PRM)**: A model that predicts a reward for each step of a reasoning process, indicating whether that step is correct.
  - *Why needed*: Overcomes sparsity of final-answer rewards by providing dense, step-wise feedback.
  - *Quick check*: Is the model trained to output a scalar per step (often using synthetic or human-annotated step labels)?

- **On-policy vs. Off-policy Data**: On-policy data is generated by the current policy; off-policy data comes from previous policies or external sources.
  - *Why needed*: SCOPE mixes on-policy prefixes (preserving exploration) with off-policy rectified suffixes (efficient correction).
  - *Quick check*: Are the corrected suffixes generated by a different model or a snapshot of the policy?

- **Exploration Diversity**: Variety of reasoning paths explored by the policy during training.
  - *Why needed*: Sparse rewards often cause mode collapse; diversity improves coverage of solution space and robustness.
  - *Quick check*: Measure entropy of generated trajectories or count unique solution strategies.

- **Hybrid Objective**: Loss function combining reinforcement learning (e.g., policy gradient) and supervised learning (e.g., cross-entropy).
  - *Why needed*: Optimizes the policy on both retained exploratory prefixes (via RL) and guaranteed-correct suffixes (via SL).
  - *Quick check*: Does the training objective include both a policy gradient term and a maximum likelihood term?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
Policy -> Rollout -> Verifier (binary) & PRM (step rewards)
PRM -> Error localization -> Split: Prefix (on-policy) + Suffix (to rectify)
Prefix + Off-policy Rectifier -> Corrected suffix
Prefix + Corrected suffix -> Combined trajectory
Combined trajectory -> Hybrid loss -> Policy update

**Critical path**: Generate rollout → check binary reward → if failed, use PRM to locate first error → keep prefix → generate corrected suffix off-policy → combine → compute hybrid loss → update policy.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- PRM accuracy vs. availability: A high-fidelity PRM is essential but may require expensive annotation or synthetic data.
- Off-policy vs. on-policy suffix generation: Off-policy is more sample-efficient but risks distribution shift; on-policy would need extra rollouts.
- Hybrid loss weighting: Balancing RL and SL terms affects stability and final performance; requires tuning.

**Failure signatures**:
- PRM misidentifies the first error, leading to prefixes that contain mistakes or corrections that don’t fix the root cause.
- Off-policy suffix distribution mismatch causes policy gradient estimates to become biased or high-variance.
- Over-weighting the supervised suffix component causes the policy to forget how to generate correct prefixes (catastrophic forgetting).

**First experiments**:
1. PRM Sanity Check: Evaluate PRM’s step-level error localization accuracy on a held set of trajectories with known mistakes.
2. Prefix Preservation Test: Compare the correctness of prefixes from failed rollouts against prefixes generated anew by the current policy.
3. Hybrid Loss Ablation: Train with (a) only on-policy RL (no suffix correction), (b) only supervised suffix learning (no prefix retention), and (c) full hybrid to disentangle contributions.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability**: Results are demonstrated only on Qwen2.5-Math-7B; scalability across model architectures and sizes is unvalidated.
- **PRM Dependency**: SCOPE’s effectiveness hinges on a high-quality Process Reward Model; performance may degrade significantly with imperfect or biased PRMs, a risk not stress-tested.
- **Component Analysis**: The relative contribution of on-policy prefix optimization versus supervised suffix rectification is not isolated; hyperparameter sensitivity (e.g., selection thresholds) remains unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Generalizability claim**: Medium (limited to one model family)
- **PRM robustness**: Medium (no stress tests reported)
- **Hybrid objective understanding**: Low (component contributions not disentangled)

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-architecture replication**: Implement SCOPE on at least two other model families (e.g., Llama3.1, Gemma2) using identical training data and PRMs to verify portability.
2. **Component isolation ablation**: Train separate models using only (a) on-policy prefix optimization, (b) supervised suffix fine-tuning, and (c) varied hybrid weighting schedules to quantify each factor’s impact.
3. **PRM robustness stress test**: Inject controlled noise (e.g., random step mislabeling) into the PRM and compare SCOPE’s degradation curve against GRPO and whole-trajectory replacement baselines.
