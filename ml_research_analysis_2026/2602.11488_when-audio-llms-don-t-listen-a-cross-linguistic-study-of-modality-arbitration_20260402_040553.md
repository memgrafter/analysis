# When Audio-LLMs Don't Listen: A Cross-Linguistic Study of Modality Arbitration

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.11488
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11488
- **Reference count**: 19
- **Primary result**: Audio-LLMs exhibit a 10–26× higher Text Dominance Ratio than text-text cascade baselines, confirming a strong modality-specific arbitration asymmetry.

## Executive Summary
Audio-LLMs exhibit a "text dominance" bias, selectively ignoring correct audio evidence in favor of conflicting text even when explicitly instructed to listen. This study introduces ALME, a large-scale cross-lingual dataset of controlled audio-text conflicts, and the Text Dominance Ratio (TDR) metric to quantify this bias. Results across four models reveal that the bias is architectural—not merely a failure of audio comprehension—and varies dramatically by language (2–4× higher for CJK/Arabic vs. European). The bias can be dramatically reduced (by 80%) via adversarial prompt framing or by fine-tuning the LLM core, but is exacerbated by adapter-only audio integration, indicating the arbitration bottleneck resides in the LLM’s reasoning, not the audio encoder.

## Method Summary
The authors construct ALME, a dataset of 57,602 synthetic conflict stimuli spanning eight languages, where audio and text inputs provide opposing answers to the same question. The key metric, Text Dominance Ratio (TDR), measures the proportion of times the model follows the text despite being instructed to follow the audio. A critical control is the text-text cascade baseline (Cascade-B), which replaces audio with its perfect transcript, isolating modality-specific arbitration from general instruction-following failures. Experiments test prompt interventions, fine-tuning ablations (adapter-only vs. LLM LoRA), and cross-linguistic generalizability.

## Key Results
- Gemini 2.0 Flash (16.6% TDR) and GPT-4o (23.2% TDR) show 10–26× higher TDR than the cascade baseline (1.6%, 0.9%).
- Adversarial prompt framing ("deliberately corrupted" transcript) reduces TDR by 80% (to 3.8%).
- LLM LoRA fine-tuning halves TDR (49.4%→25.5%); adapter-only fine-tuning increases it (49.4%→75.9%).
- TDR is 2–4× higher for CJK/Arabic languages than for European languages in three of four models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Arbitration Accessibility Asymmetry
**Claim**: Text dominance arises from an asymmetry in how easily the model can use competing representations at decision time; text tokens are directly accessible to the LLM, while audio-derived tokens must be projected, creating a bottleneck.
**Mechanism**: The LLM’s reasoning operates primarily on textual tokens. When audio and text conflict, the audio signal must be "translated" via a projection layer into the LLM’s semantic space, a less direct and costlier pathway. The model defaults to the path of least resistance—using the already-present text—even if audio comprehension is adequate.
**Core assumption**: The LLM’s internal attention and feed-forward layers favor native textual representations over projected audio ones during conflict resolution.
**Evidence anchors**:
- Abstract: Text dominance reflects "arbitration accessibility" not just information content.
- Section 5.1: (1) High audio accuracy co-occurs with high TDR; (2) Forcing transcription increases TDR; (3) Reducing text trust lowers TDR more than improving audio; (4) LLM LoRA reduces TDR while adapter-only increases it.
- Corpus: Weak; "LiSTEN" finds reliance on lexical over acoustic cues for emotion, a related bias, but low FMR (0.568) and zero citations.

### Mechanism 2: Training Data Composition Prior
**Claim**: Overwhelmingly text-based pretraining conditions the model to treat text as the primary evidence modality, creating a persistent prior that audio fine-tuning does not override.
**Mechanism**: Standard pretraining on pure text establishes strong weights for textual reasoning. Audio-LLM training often adds an audio encoder without sufficiently retraining the LLM to reweight its arbitration heuristics, so the model defaults to its text-honed reasoning unless explicitly prompted to distrust text.
**Core assumption**: The LLM’s weights encode a robust textual prior resistant to simple adapter-based audio integration.
**Evidence anchors**:
- Abstract: Bias is about "how easily the model can reason" over representations.
- Section 5.1: Authors acknowledge training data composition as a plausible untested factor.
- Corpus: No direct anchor; neighbors focus on architecture or specific tasks.

### Mechanism 3: Representational Alignment Cost
**Claim**: The audio-to-text projection pathway is inherently lossy, producing representations that are poorly aligned with the LLM’s pre-trained token space, making audio-derived evidence less competitive.
**Mechanism**: Audio encoders output continuous embeddings that must be projected into the LLM’s discrete token space. This projection introduces noise or misalignment. In conflict, the "imperfect" projected audio tokens are easily outvoted by clean, high-confidence text tokens.
**Core assumption**: The adapter layer is a misaligning translator; LLM attention undervalues projected tokens when direct text exists.
**Evidence anchors**:
- Section 4.6: Adapter-only fine-tuning *increases* TDR (49.4%→75.9%) while degrading audio-only accuracy, suggesting misalignment worsens arbitration.
- Section 4.1: Cascade-B TDR (1.6%) is near-zero even with perfect ASR transcripts, implicating the modality transition itself.
- Corpus: Weak; "LiSTEN" uses soft token embeddings to improve alignment, indirectly acknowledging the issue.

## Foundational Learning
- **Modality Arbitration**: The process by which a model resolves conflicts between evidence from different input modalities. *Why needed*: Distinguishes *comprehension* (extracting meaning from audio) from *arbitration* (choosing between competing sources). *Quick check*: If a model scores 95% on audio-only QA but 40% TDR in conflict, the bottleneck is arbitration, not audio understanding.
- **Text Dominance Ratio (TDR)**: P(model follows text | instructed to follow audio & conflict exists). *Why needed*: Isolates modality-specific preference by controlling for reliability and comparing to a cascade baseline. *Quick check*: How does TDR differ from "Text Influence Rate" (TIR) in MCR-BENCH, and what unique insight does TDR provide?
- **Cascade Baseline**: Replaces audio with its perfect text transcript, creating a text-text conflict control. *Why needed*: Separates general instruction-following failures from modality-specific arbitration asymmetry. *Quick check*: If multimodal TDR=20% and cascade TDR=2%, what does the 18% gap represent?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Audio Encoder -> Multimodal Projection/Adapter -> Frozen/Fine-tuned LLM
**Critical path**: The LLM’s conflict-resolution dynamics (attention/FFN layers) are the primary bottleneck, not the audio encoder. Evidence: (1) Adapter-only FT hurts; LLM LoRA helps. (2) Cascade baseline (no audio path) has near-zero TDR. (3) Prompt interventions affecting LLM trust are effective.
**Design tradeoffs**:
- **Adapter-only training**: Low compute but risks misaligning projected audio with the frozen LLM, increasing TDR and degrading audio-only accuracy.
- **LLM LoRA/full FT**: Directly reshapes arbitration logic, reduces TDR, but high compute and risks catastrophic forgetting or degraded audio-only performance (~25pp drop).
- **Prompt engineering**: Zero-cost, effective (~80% TDR reduction), but fragile to phrasing and may not generalize.
**Failure signatures**:
- TDR >30% (European) or >60% (CJK/Arabic).
- Large multimodality gap: Multimodal TDR >> Cascade TDR.
- TDR increases with explicit transcription prompts.
- Adapter-only FT increases TDR and reduces audio-only accuracy.
**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Reproduce the arbitration gap**: Run ALME conflict and cascade-B conditions on a target model; verify 10–26× higher TDR in multimodal.
2. **Prompt intervention test**: Apply baseline, adversarial, explicit-ignore, and audio-first prompts; expect ~80% TDR reduction with adversarial framing.
3. **Component ablation**: On separable architectures (e.g., Ultravox), run adapter-only vs. LLM LoRA fine-tuning on conflict subset; expect adapter-only to increase TDR, LLM LoRA to decrease it.

## Limitations
- Mechanistic evidence is indirect; lacks direct measurement of computational costs or attentional weights given to audio vs. text streams during decoding.
- Root cause of cross-linguistic variation (2–4× higher TDR for CJK/Arabic) is unknown—could be tokenization, audio-text semantic gap, or data bias.
- ALME’s synthetic, perfectly balanced conflicts may not generalize to natural, noisy audio-text disagreements.

## Confidence
- **Core phenomenon (text dominance exists)**: High
- **Arbitration asymmetry (not just comprehension failure)**: High
- **LLM reasoning is primary bottleneck**: Medium
- **Prompt framing can mitigate bias**: Medium-High
- **Cross-linguistic variation is real**: High (explanation: Low)
- **Specific mechanistic explanations (all three)**: Low to Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Measure audio-text alignment directly**: Extract embeddings from audio encoder and LLM for matched audio/text; compute representational similarity (e.g., CCA) and correlate with TDR across model variants. Tests Mechanism 3.
2. **Train balanced multimodal model from scratch**: Train an audio-LLM on large, perfectly balanced multimodal data (no pure-text pretraining); compare TDR to standard text-heavy pretrained model. Tests Mechanism 2.
3. **Disambiguate cross-linguistic variation**: For high-TDR languages, analyze tokenization sparsity, audio-text embedding distance, and training data language balance; perform regression to identify strongest TDR predictor. Tests linguistic, alignment, and data hypotheses.
