# Safe Fairness Guarantees Without Demographics in Classification: Spectral Uncertainty Set Perspective

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.11785  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11785  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: SPECTRE achieves the highest average worst-group accuracy and smallest interquartile range among compared methods, with 20–40% gains on the most disadvantaged groups and only a 1–4% reduction in overall accuracy versus the best baseline, while providing out-of-sample generalization bounds.

## Executive Summary
SPECTRE is a fairness-enhancing classifier that operates without access to sensitive attribute data during training. It addresses the over-pessimism and outlier sensitivity of existing robust risk minimization (RRM) approaches by shaping the uncertainty set of a minimax risk classifier (MRC) through spectral adjustment of a random Fourier feature (RFF) mapping. By truncating the RFF spectrum, only the most response-relevant frequencies are retained, and a confidence parameter constrains the worst-case distribution’s deviation from the empirical distribution. Experiments on U.S. Census (ACS), COMPAS, and German Credit data show SPECTRE significantly improves worst-group accuracy while maintaining competitive overall accuracy. The framework also yields out-of-sample generalization bounds for both group and overall errors, enabling safer real-world deployment.

## Method Summary
SPECTRE first transforms input features via a random Fourier feature mapping parameterized by a bandwidth σ. For each σ, frequency vectors ω are sampled from a Gaussian distribution. Each labeled example (x, y) is mapped to Φ(x, y) = e_y ⊗ √(2/D)[cos(ωᵀx), sin(ωᵀx), …], where e_y is a one-hot encoding and D is the number of RFF components. A minimax risk classifier is then trained under an uncertainty set U = {p : |E_p[Φ] − E_̃p_N[Φ]| ≤ λ}, where λ = λ₀ · √(var(Φ)/N) scales with feature variance and sample size. The optimization uses the 0–1 loss directly. Hyperparameters σ and λ₀ are tuned in two stages on a validation set using strategies such as maximizing overall accuracy (ACC), minimizing worst-class error (WCE), or a trade-off (WCE+T+A, TOPN+WCE). The final model is retrained on the combined training and validation data with the selected hyperparameters.

## Key Results
- SPECTRE achieves the highest average worst-group accuracy and the smallest interquartile range across 20 U.S. states from the American Community Survey, plus COMPAS and German Credit datasets, outperforming state-of-the-art methods—including those that use sensitive attributes.
- Gains reach 20–40% in accuracy for the most disadvantaged groups, with overall accuracy reductions limited to 1–4% compared to the best baseline.
- The method provides out-of-sample generalization bounds for both group and overall errors, facilitating safe deployment when a subset of training data includes sensitive attributes.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core innovation lies in spectral truncation of the RFF mapping. By adjusting the bandwidth σ, SPECTRE controls which frequencies are preserved, effectively filtering out noise and irrelevant variations that could cause the MRC to overfit to outliers or minority patterns. The resulting feature representation is more robust. The uncertainty set is defined element-wise on feature expectations, with λ scaling adaptively with the empirical feature variance and dataset size. This prevents the uncertainty set from becoming arbitrarily large—a key source of pessimism in conventional RRM—while still covering plausible distribution shifts. The minimax optimization over this set thus focuses on meaningful worst-case scenarios rather than pathological ones. Direct use of the 0–1 loss aligns the training objective with the evaluation metric, avoiding bias introduced by surrogate losses.

- **Minimax Risk Classifier (MRC)**: Optimizes the worst-case risk over an uncertainty set; forms the decision engine in SPECTRE. *Quick check*: Write the minimax objective min_f sup_{p∈U} R(f, p) and explain how U is constructed.
- **Uncertainty Sets**: Define the set of distributions around the empirical one; their size and shape determine robustness. *Quick check*: Interpret the set {p : |E_p[Φ] − E_̃p_N[Φ]| ≤ λ} in terms of feature moments.
- **Spectral Truncation**: Adjusting the RFF spectrum via σ to keep only informative frequencies; reduces model variance. *Quick check*: Explain how σ controls the Gaussian kernel bandwidth and thus the frequency distribution.
- **0–1 Loss**: The true classification loss; SPECTRE optimizes it directly, avoiding relaxation errors. *Quick check*: Contrast the 0–1 loss with common surrogates (hinge, logistic) and discuss optimization challenges.
- **Group Fairness**: Goal is to maximize worst-group accuracy; requires group labels only for evaluation/tuning, not training. *Quick check*: Define worst-group accuracy and why it captures a notion of fairness.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Data → RFF Transform (σ) → MRC Training (λ₀) → Validation Tuning (σ, λ₀) → Final Classifier

**Critical path**:  
1. Split data into train (50%), validation (20%), test (30%); standardize features.  
2. For each σ candidate, sample RFF frequencies ω once and transform train/val sets.  
3. For each (σ, λ₀) pair, train MRC on transformed train set with λ = λ₀·√(var(Φ)/N).  
4. Evaluate on validation set using overall and worst-group accuracy (requires group labels in val).  
5. Select (σ*, λ*₀) via chosen tuning strategy (e.g., WCE).  
6. Retrain MRC on train ∪ val with selected hyperparameters.  
7. Report test performance.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- λ₀: larger values increase robustness but may degrade overall accuracy and cause numerical instability.  
- σ: too small limits model capacity; too large introduces noise and overfitting.  
- Tuning strategy: ACC optimizes overall accuracy, WCE optimizes fairness, WCE+T+A balances them, TOPN+WCE seeks a robust compromise.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Overall accuracy near random: σ too large or λ₀ too high.  
- Large gap between overall and worst-group accuracy: λ₀ too small or σ too small.  
- Non-convergence or LP solver errors: ill-conditioned Φ or extreme λ values.

**Three first experiments**:  
1. **Baseline reproduction**: Run SPECTRE with WCE+T+A strategy on ACS states, COMPAS, German Credit; compare worst-group accuracy and IQR to LR, XGB, and MRC without spectral adjustment.  
2. **Ablation over λ₀**: Fix σ = σ_scale, sweep λ₀ ∈ [0.01, 1.0]; plot worst-group vs. overall accuracy to visualize the fairness-accuracy tradeoff curve.  
3. **Sensitivity to tuning strategy**: For each strategy (ACC, WCE, WCE+T+A, TOPN+WCE), record selected (σ, λ₀) and corresponding test metrics; analyze how strategy choice impacts fairness and accuracy.

## Limitations
- The training algorithm for MRC relies on the MRCpy library or the details of [40]; incorrect reimplementation could undermine results.
- The tolerance parameter in the WCE+T+A tuning strategy is unspecified, introducing ambiguity in reproduction.
- Randomness in RFF frequency sampling is not fully controlled; different seeds may yield variable performance.
- Scalability is limited by the linear programming formulation, which grows with N and D (e.g., D=600), causing memory/time issues on large datasets.
- Generalization bounds require a validation subset with known sensitive attributes and at least 40 samples per group, which may not hold in practice.
- Experimental validation is restricted to three datasets; broader generalizability remains uncertain.

## Confidence
- Fairness improvement without demographics: **Medium** – strong empirical evidence but contingent on correct MRC implementation and hyperparameter choices.
- Mitigation of pessimism in robust risk minimization: **Medium** – theoretical argument is plausible but lacks extensive empirical robustness checks.
- Out-of-sample generalization guarantees: **Medium** – derivation appears sound but depends on strong assumptions (e.g., availability of sensitive labels, correct λ tuning).
