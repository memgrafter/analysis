# GPT4o-Receipt: A Dataset and Human Study for AI-Generated Document Forensics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11442
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11442
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Humans show larger visual realism gaps between AI and real receipts (1.87 points) but achieve lower binary detection F1 (0.852) than top multimodal LLMs (e.g., Claude F1 0.975) because the dominant forensic signal—arithmetic incoherence—is invisible to visual inspection but verifiable by LLMs.

## Executive Summary
This study introduces GPT4o-Receipt, a benchmark of 1,235 receipt images (935 AI-generated by GPT-4o, 300 authentic), to compare human and AI-based detectors for financial document forensics. The core finding is a performance paradox: human annotators excel at distinguishing visual realism yet are outperformed in binary detection by state-of-the-art multimodal LLMs. The reason is that the strongest forensic signal is arithmetic incoherence—errors in sums, taxes, or rounding—which humans rarely spot by inspection but LLMs systematically detect through reasoning. Additionally, LLM detectors differ markedly in calibration (recall vs. false positive trade-offs), meaning high accuracy alone is insufficient for practical selection. Top models like Claude and Gemini also retain high performance even when arithmetic errors are removed, suggesting reliance on complementary factual and visual channels.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed GPT4o-Receipt by generating 935 receipts using a GPT-4o pipeline (text description + GPT-Image-1 rendering) and collecting 300 authentic receipts from public datasets. Five multimodal LLMs (Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.5 Flash, GPT-5 Nano, Grok 4, LLaMA 4 Scout) were evaluated in a zero-shot setting with structured JSON prompts requesting scores for visual realism (1–5) and binary checks for arithmetic and factual consistency. Thirty human annotators assessed the same images via a crowdsourcing platform, answering questions about typography, layout, and artifacts, and assigning an overall visual realism score. Human binary detection metrics were derived by applying a threshold (score ≤3 → AI-generated) to the mean scores. Performance was measured using accuracy, F1, recall, and false positive rate (FPR), with further analysis via threshold sweeps, bootstrap confidence intervals, and a simulated adversarial hardening that corrected arithmetic errors.

## Key Results
- Human annotators exhibit a large visual realism gap between AI and authentic receipts (1.87 out of 5) but achieve a lower binary detection F1 (0.852) than top LLMs (Claude 0.975, Gemini 0.890, GPT-5 Nano 0.875).
- Arithmetic incoherence is the dominant forensic signal: Claude detects arithmetic errors in 97.2% of AI-generated receipts, and this channel is invisible to casual human visual inspection.
- LLM detectors show starkly different calibration: Grok 4 achieves near-perfect recall (0.999) but a catastrophic FPR (0.903), while LLaMA 4 Scout has very low FPR (0.017) but near-zero recall (0.114), making simple F1 scores misleading for operational use.
- After adversarial hardening (correcting arithmetic errors), Claude and Gemini retain >94% of their original F1 by leveraging non-arithmetic signals like factual inconsistencies and residual visual artifacts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Human visual detection is outperformed by top LLM detectors despite superior visual discrimination because the primary forensic signal (arithmetic incoherence) is semantically encoded and invisible to casual visual inspection.
**Mechanism:** GPT-4o’s image generation treats numbers as visual patterns, not computed values, producing receipts where line-item sums do not match subtotals. Humans assess only pixel-level visual quality; LLMs can perform explicit arithmetic verification across fields.
**Core assumption:** Arithmetic errors are prevalent in GPT-4o-generated receipts and form the dominant detection channel.
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Abstract: "the dominant forensic signals in AI-generated receipts are arithmetic errors—invisible to visual inspection but systematically verifiable by LLMs."
- Section VI.E: "arithmetic errors in AI-generated receipts are visually imperceptible… human annotators achieve high visual sensitivity (gap = 1.87) but can recover only a fraction of the forensic signal available to LLMs."
- Corpus: Neighbors (e.g., `SeqXGPT`, `VisNumBench`) indicate LLMs systematically fail numerical reasoning, consistent with generation artifacts being numerically incoherent.
**Break condition:** If GPT-4o (or future generators) enforces arithmetic consistency during rendering, this mechanism vanishes; detection would then rely on visual/factual signals alone.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** LLM detector selection cannot rely on aggregate metrics like F1 because models exhibit qualitatively different calibration (recall vs. false positive rate trade-offs), making some practically unusable despite high accuracy.
**Mechanism:** Models adopt distinct decision thresholds: Grok 4 flags nearly everything (high recall, catastrophic FPR); LLaMA 4 Scout flags almost nothing (low FPR, near-zero recall); Claude and Gemini balance both.
**Core assumption:** Operational constraints (e.g., cost of false positives) require joint optimization of recall and FPR, not a single metric.
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Section V.B: "Grok 4… achieves near-perfect recall (0.999)… resulting in an FPR of 0.903. LLaMA 4 Scout… FPR of 0.017 but detects only 11.4%."
- Section VII.C: "model selection should treat recall and false positive rate as joint constraints… no single current model provides a universally satisfactory operating point."
- Corpus: Limited direct evidence; neighboring work focuses on document understanding rather than detector calibration.
**Break condition:** If future detectors achieve uniformly high recall with low FPR, calibration differences may narrow but are unlikely to disappear entirely due to inherent model biases.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Top detectors (Claude, Gemini) are resilient to adversarial hardening that removes arithmetic errors because they leverage complementary forensic channels (factual inconsistencies, visual artifacts).
**Mechanism:** Even when arithmetic verification yields no error, these models still detect >94% of AI receipts via other dimensions (address errors, item-store mismatches, residual visual flaws).
**Core assumption:** Multi-dimensional forensic signals (visual, arithmetic, factual) are jointly necessary but not individually sufficient for robust detection.
**Evidence anchors:**  
- Section VII.E: "Claude Sonnet 4 retains 94.2% (0.975 → 0.918)… Gemini 2.5 Flash retains 94.5%… all three leverage factual consistency signals."
- Section VII.E: "the longer-term challenge… to develop classifiers that integrate visual, semantic, and structural signals in ways that are jointly difficult to satisfy."
- Corpus: Neighbors (e.g., `AIForge-Doc`, `DocTamper`) show single-signature detectors degrade out-of-distribution, supporting need for multi-channel fusion.
**Break condition:** An adversary that post-processes images to ensure numerical, factual, *and* visual consistency would likely defeat current detectors, requiring fundamentally new forensic invariants.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Forensic Signal Dimensions**  
  *Why needed here:* The paper decomposes detection into visual realism, arithmetic integrity, and factual consistency. Understanding this trichotomy is essential to interpret why humans and LLMs diverge.  
  *Quick check question:* Which dimension is *invisible* to human annotators but trivially verifiable by LLMs?

- **Concept: Detector Calibration**  
  *Why needed here:* F1 alone masks critical operational trade-offs (recall vs. FPR). A model with near-perfect recall may be unusable if its FPR is 90%.  
  *Quick check question:* Why is Grok 4’s F1 of 0.873 misleading for practical deployment?

- **Concept: Adversarial Hardening Simulation**  
  *Why needed here:* The paper estimates robustness by simulating forgeries with corrected arithmetic. This isolates the contribution of non-arithmetic signals.  
  *Quick check question:* What does it imply when a detector retains >94% F1 after arithmetic hardening?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Dataset layer -> Evaluation layer -> Human study layer -> Analysis layer
- **Critical path:**  
  Data collection -> LLM batch evaluation -> Human annotation -> Score thresholding (human) -> Performance metric computation -> Calibration & robustness analysis.
  *Key dependency:* LLM evaluations must complete before human detection metrics can be compared; human threshold selection (≤3) is applied post-hoc.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Ground truth:** Filename-based labels ensure scalability but assume no corruption in the generation pipeline.  
  - **Human binary detection:** Inferred from continuous visual scores rather than direct binary judgment, introducing threshold sensitivity.  
  - **LLM prompting:** Structured JSON output enables automation but may constrain model reasoning; free-form reasoning was not collected.  
  - **Dataset balance:** 75.7% AI reflects forensic prevalence but may bias models; majority-class baseline F1=0.862 sets a low bar.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **LLM JSON parsing errors** (2/6,175 assessments failed) requiring repair logic.  
  - **Low human inter-rater agreement** (Cohen’s κ=0.523) indicates subjective visual judgment; threshold choice greatly affects results.  
  - **Model version drift:** Results tied to Feb 2026 API models; future updates may change rankings.  
  - **Domain shift:** AI receipts are English/North American; performance may drop on other formats/cultures.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Replicate LLM evaluation:** Run the LangGraph pipeline on 50 sampled images, verify JSON schema compliance and error rates.  
  2. **Human threshold sweep:** Recompute human detection metrics across thresholds ≤1–≤4 to confirm Figure 6 stability; identify optimal F1 point.  
  3. **Arithmetic hardening check:** For 100 AI receipts, manually verify sum/tax errors; compute what fraction of Claude’s detections rely *solely* on arithmetic vs. other signals.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How do detection performance and error patterns differ for AI-generated receipts from models other than GPT-4o, such as Stable Diffusion or DALL-E 3?
- **Open Question 2:** How effective are human and LLM detectors at identifying AI-generated receipts in non-English contexts, such as those with non-Latin numerals or diverse cultural formats?
- **Open Question 3:** Can forensic detection methods be made resilient against adversarially-hardened forgeries where arithmetic and factual inconsistencies are corrected, and what alternative signals remain exploitable?
- **Open Question 4:** To what extent do inferred human detection metrics from visual quality scores align with direct binary human judgments, and how does this impact the human-LLM performance comparison?

## Limitations
- **Prompt and hyperparameter opacity:** Exact LLM prompts, temperature, and verdict thresholding are unreported, risking replication deviations.
- **Human study design:** Annotator recruitment criteria, image display conditions, and aggregation methods lack full specification, limiting generalizability.
- **Temporal and domain constraints:** Results are tied to February 2026 model versions and North American receipt formats; performance may degrade with model updates or non-English documents.
- **Ground truth assumption:** Filename-based labels assume no generation pipeline errors; potential label noise is unquantified.

## Confidence
- **Arithmetic as dominant forensic signal:** Medium. Evidence is strong within the studied GPT-4o pipeline, but the break condition (generator fixes arithmetic) is plausible and untested.
- **Calibration differences matter:** High. Recall/FPR trade-offs are empirically stark and threshold-independent; operational relevance is clear.
- **Multi-channel robustness:** Medium. Post-hardening retention (>94%) suggests complementary signals, but adversarial hardening is simulated, not live; unknown if other generators would produce similar residual artifacts.

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt replication audit:** Using the paper’s referenced LangGraph workflow, run the LLM evaluation with author-supplied exact prompts (if released) and compare detection metrics to reported values; variance >2% would indicate prompt sensitivity.
2. **Human threshold sensitivity:** Recompute human binary detection F1 across thresholds ≤1–≤4 on the released human scores; if optimal F1 shifts >0.05 from the reported ≤3, the human-LLM gap conclusion is threshold‑dependent.
3. **Arithmetic signal isolation:** Manually verify sum/tax errors in a random sample of 100 AI receipts; compute the fraction of Claude’s detections that *solely* rely on arithmetic (no other errors). If <80%, other signals contribute significantly even without hardening.
