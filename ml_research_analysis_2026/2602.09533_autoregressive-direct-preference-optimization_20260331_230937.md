# Autoregressive Direct Preference Optimization

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID:** 2602.09533  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09533  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** ADPO, which explicitly incorporates autoregressive assumptions before the Bradley‑Terry model, achieves 91.74% accuracy on GSM8K with Qwen-3-8B, outperforming DPO (86.96%).

## Executive Summary

DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) relies on a sequence‑level reward derived from the Bradley‑Terry model, but it only assumes autoregressive structure after the objective is formed. This limits the granularity of credit assignment during training. ADPO (Autoregressive DPO) moves the autoregressive assumption earlier by defining reward energies over prefix closures. The resulting loss aggregates per‑prefix log‑sigmoid terms outside the nonlinearity and introduces two independent length measures: token length μ and feedback length μ′. Experiments on GSM8K, MATH500, and a conversational benchmark show ADPO consistently surpasses DPO and cDPO, with a notable gain of 4.78 percentage points on GSM8K using Qwen‑3‑8B. Moreover, analyses of granularity families indicate that finer feedback (smaller window or more segments) generally improves performance, validating the theoretical framework.

## Method Summary

ADPO constructs prefix closures for each chosen and rejected sequence under an autoregressive decomposition. For every prefix, a reward energy is computed as the difference of log‑probabilities between the current policy and a reference model. This energy feeds into a Bradley‑Terry–style pairwise comparison at the prefix level. The final loss sums the resulting log‑sigmoid terms across all prefixes, with two tunable parameters: μ controls the maximum prefix length considered, while μ′ determines over how many subsequent tokens the feedback from a given prefix is propagated. This design yields a loss that is both autoregressive and granular, enabling token‑level credit assignment while retaining the simplicity of DPO’s original pairwise formulation.

## Key Results

- ADPO achieves **91.74%** on GSM8K (Qwen‑3‑8B), significantly higher than DPO’s **86.96%**.  
- Across GSM8K, MATH500, and a conversational benchmark, ADPO consistently outperforms both DPO and cDPO.  
- Finer feedback granularity (e.g., smaller window size or more segments) correlates with improved performance, confirming the benefit of the dual length measures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

The core mechanism is the early integration of autoregressive structure: ADPO applies the Bradley‑Terry model to *prefix-level* reward energies before aggregating them. This shifts the summation of per‑prefix losses outside the log‑sigmoid, converting a sequence‑level preference signal into a sum of token‑level contributions. Gradients can therefore influence earlier tokens directly, providing finer credit assignment. The dual length measures (μ and μ′) decouple the prefix length from the feedback propagation length, allowing adaptive granularity without altering the underlying generative model. This alignment with the true autoregressive process yields more stable and effective policy updates, explaining the observed performance gains.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input (prompt, chosen, rejected) -> Prefix Splitter (creates prefix closures with window size μ) -> Energy Calculator (computes energy for policy and reference) -> Loss Aggregator (sums per‑prefix log‑sigmoid terms weighted by μ′) -> KL Divergence Module (adds reference penalty) -> Optimizer (updates policy parameters).

**Critical path:**  
1. For each (chosen, rejected) pair, generate all prefixes up to the full sequence length.  
2. For every prefix, compute the reward energy difference (policy vs. reference).  
3. Apply the log‑sigmoid to obtain a per‑prefix preference loss.  
4. Aggregate these losses according to μ′; feedback from a prefix may be spread over subsequent tokens.  
5. Add the KL divergence term relative to the reference model.  
6. Backpropagate the total loss to update the policy.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Granularity vs. cost:* Smaller μ′ (more prefixes) improves credit assignment but increases computation and memory roughly quadratically with length.  
- *Static vs. adaptive segmentation:* Fixed window sizes are simple but may not adapt to content; adaptive schemes (e.g., based on syntactic boundaries) add complexity but could yield better performance.  
- *Dual length parameters:* μ and μ′ introduce extra hyperparameters; inappropriate values can cause vanishing gradients or over‑regularization.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Training instability (loss spikes) when μ′ is too large, producing many tiny gradient contributions.  
- Mode collapse if the KL weight is too high, keeping the policy overly close to reference and ignoring preferences.  
- Poor performance on long sequences if prefix generation truncates prematurely, weakening the reward signal at later tokens.  
- Reward hacking where the policy exploits per‑prefix loss by generating unnatural prefixes that artificially minimize energy differences.

**First experiments:**  
1. *Baseline replication:* Train ADPO and DPO on GSM8K and MATH500 with the same backbone (e.g., Qwen‑3‑8B) to verify the reported accuracy gap.  
2. *Granularity ablation:* Systematically vary μ′ (window size or segment count) and plot performance vs. training cost to identify the optimal regime.  
3. *Efficiency profiling:* Measure per‑step runtime, memory usage, and total training time of ADPO relative to DPO, isolating the overhead of prefix closure computation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What are the effects of using alternative divergence metrics (e.g., Jensen‑Shannon, α‑divergences) in ADPO?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state in the Limitations: "future research could investigate more general divergence metrics as alternatives to KL divergence."  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only experiments with KL divergence; no evaluation of other divergences is provided.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically replace KL with various f‑divergences in ADPO, then benchmark on standard alignment tasks (e.g., GSM8K, MT‑Bench) to compare reward, KL to reference, training stability, and final accuracy.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How should the feedback length measure μ′ be chosen relative to the token length μ for optimal ADPO performance?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section 5.3 distinguishes μ and μ′ and shows that finer granularity (larger μ′) generally improves results, but no guideline for selecting μ′ is offered.  
- **Why unresolved:** μ and μ′ are theoretically independent; their practical relationship may depend on data, model, and task, leaving the optimal choice unclear.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct controlled experiments varying μ′ across multiple values for different models and datasets, analyze performance trends, and derive a heuristic or theoretical bound linking μ′ to μ and sequence characteristics.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the prefix‑splitting strategy ξ be learned adaptively rather than using fixed static or adaptive heuristics, and would such learning improve ADPO?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section 5.4 introduces static and adaptive families but notes that "the choice of ξ determines the learning behavior," implying it is a design choice.  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper uses hand‑crafted compositions (ξ) for splitting; learning ξ from data to adapt to response complexity is unexplored.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop a differentiable segmentation module that outputs ξ and train it end‑to‑end with ADPO, then compare its performance against fixed ξ strategies on reasoning and conversation benchmarks.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What is the essential theoretical and practical difference between applying the autoregressive assumption before versus after the Bradley‑Terry model in DPO variants?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper contrasts ADPO (autoregressive before BT) with prior work (autoregressive after BT), yielding a loss with summation outside the log‑sigmoid, but the deeper implications are not analyzed.  
- **Why unresolved:** It is unclear whether ADPO's gains stem solely from this summation shift or reflect a more fundamental difference in implicit reward modeling and credit assignment.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis comparing the two orderings (e.g., derived reward equivalence classes, gradient behavior) and empirical ablations that isolate the effect of summation placement while controlling other factors.

## Limitations

- Evaluation is limited to GSM8K, MATH500, and a single conversational benchmark; the generalizability of ADPO to other domains remains unknown.  
- No computational cost analysis is reported; the prefix closure mechanism may introduce non‑trivial training overhead in time and memory.  
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (e.g., window size, segment count, μ vs. μ′) is not thoroughly explored; optimal settings may be highly task‑specific.
