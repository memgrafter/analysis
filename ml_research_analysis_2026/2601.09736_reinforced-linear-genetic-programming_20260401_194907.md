# Reinforced Linear Genetic Programming

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.09736
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09736
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** RLGP underperforms LGP baseline on CartPole (mean 207 vs 454) and fails to solve MountainCar (mean -130 vs -126).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Reinforced Linear Genetic Programming (RLGP), a hybrid approach that combines Linear Genetic Programming (LGP) with Q-Learning to automate the assignment of internal registers to external actions. The authors develop a modular Rust framework named `linear-gp` to support systematic experimentation, separating evolutionary operators (core, breed, mutate) from fitness evaluation. The method replaces LGP's manual register-action mapping with a Q-Table that learns from environmental rewards during program execution.

Empirical evaluation on OpenAI Gym benchmarks (CartPole-v1 and MountainCar-v0) reveals that RLGP significantly underperforms the standard LGP baseline. While LGP achieves mean scores of 454 on CartPole and solves MountainCar with a mean of -126, RLGP reaches only 207 on CartPole and fails to solve MountainCar (-130). RLGP plateaus early on CartPole and cannot solve MountainCar, suggesting that the Q-Learning integration may hinder strategic exploration. The paper hypothesizes that the register-based state representation is insufficient for effective Q-Learning, but further analysis is needed.

## Method Summary
The method extends the standard Linear Genetic Programming (LGP) algorithm by incorporating a tabular Q-Learner that dynamically maps the program's internal registers to environment actions. In LGP, programs consist of linear instruction sequences operating on a set of registers; traditionally, the mapping from a designated "winning" register to an action is manually defined. RLGP replaces this with a Q-Table where states correspond to the index (and possibly value) of the winning register, and actions correspond to environment actions. During fitness evaluation, an ε-greedy policy selects an action based on the current Q-Table, and the Q-Table is updated using the standard Q-Learning rule after each step if consecutive winning registers differ. The evolutionary loop (selection, crossover, mutation) remains unchanged, but the fitness evaluation subroutine now includes the RL interaction.

The authors implement the entire system in Rust as the `linear-gp` crate, which is deliberately modular: the `Core` engine coordinates generations, while `Breed`, `Mutate`, and `Fitness` are pluggable components. Separate fitness implementations exist for classification (Iris) and Gym environments, with the RLGP variant adding the Q-Learning logic. Hyperparameters for both LGP and RLGP (e.g., population size, instruction limits, Q-Learning rates) are optimized using Optuna, though the search process is not fully detailed. The reproduction plan involves cloning the `linear-gp` and `gym-rs` repositories, running baseline LGP with provided hyperparameters (Listings 1,3) and RLGP with its own (Listings 2,4), and aggregating results over 100 independent runs.

## Key Results
- On CartPole-v1, LGP attains a mean cumulative reward of 454 (near the maximum 500), while RLGP reaches only 207, plateauing early.
- On MountainCar-v0, LGP solves the task with a mean of -126 (above the -110 threshold), whereas RLGP fails with a mean of -130, indicating it does not learn a successful policy.
- RLGP's performance is consistently below LGP across both environments, suggesting the Q-Learning integration introduces an exploration or representation challenge.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Q-Learning for Automatic Register-Action Mapping
RLGP automates the manual mapping by introducing a Q-Table that learns associations between the LGP program's internal "winning" register and external actions. During evaluation, an ε-greedy policy selects a winning register (the one with highest value) from the program's registers; the action is then chosen according to the Q-Table for that register state. The Q-Table is updated using the standard Q-Learning rule with the environment's reward, thereby aligning long-term action values with the register states. The core assumption is that the genetically evolved registers form a Markovian state representation suitable for Q-Learning. Evidence includes the abstract and Section 3.3's formal description. Performance plateaus (e.g., RLGP on CartPole) indicate the assumption may be violated or exploration is insufficient.

### Mechanism 2: Modular Rust Framework for Systematic Experimentation
The `linear-gp` framework's modular engine design separates evolutionary operators from fitness evaluation, enabling fair comparison between LGP and RLGP. The `Core` engine drives the generational loop, while `Breed`, `Mutate`, and `Fitness` are pluggable components. The `Fitness` engine encapsulates the evaluation logic: for RLGP it includes the Q-Learning step inside the environment interaction loop. This isolation ensures that differences in performance stem solely from the fitness evaluation (i.e., presence of Q-Learning) rather than other evolutionary components. Evidence is the engine definitions in Section 3.1 and the abstract's mention of the framework. A break condition arises if hyperparameters optimized for LGP prove detrimental to RLGP, as observed.

### Mechanism 3: Exploration Challenges in Dual Adaptive Loops
The underperformance of RLGP is attributed to the Q-Learning component creating an inefficient exploration-exploitation trade-off, particularly on tasks requiring strategic exploration like MountainCar. The ε-greedy policy selects actions based on the Q-Table; if the Q-Table updates are poorly correlated with the program's evolving registers or if ε decays too quickly, the system may converge prematurely to a suboptimal policy. This combines two adaptive loops (evolution and Q-Learning) that may interfere with each other. The core assumption is that the winning register provides a stable state representation; evidence includes Section 4.2's discussion of exploration issues and the performance gap (CartPole: 454 vs 207; MountainCar: -126 vs -130). The break condition is when RLGP fails to reach solution thresholds across many configurations, while LGP succeeds.

## Foundational Learning

- **Linear Genetic Programming (LGP) Program Representation**  
  *Why needed:* To understand the base algorithm being augmented. RLGP operates on LGP's linear instruction sequence (`R[y] ← R[y] op R[x]`) and register set. The "winning" register concept in Q-Learning depends on this structure.  
  *Quick check:* If an LGP program has `R[1] ← R[1] + R[0]` followed by `R[2] ← R[1] * R[0]`, what are the source and target register indices for the second instruction, and what is the net effect on `R[2]` if `R[1]=5` and `R[0]=2` after the first instruction?

- **Q-Learning (ε-greedy & Tabular)**  
  *Why needed:* RLGP's core augmentation is a tabular Q-Learner that maps program registers to actions. Understanding state, action, reward, update rule, and exploration (ε) is essential to analyze the hybrid mechanism.  
  *Quick check:* In the update rule `Q(s,a) += α(r + γ max_a' Q(s',a') - Q(s,a))`, what is the role of γ (gamma) and what happens if γ is set to 0?

- **Hyperparameter Optimization (Optuna)**  
  *Why needed:* The paper's conclusions rely on hyperparameters found via Optuna. Potential performance issues may stem from searching LGP-optimal params and then wrapping Q-Learning constants, rather than joint optimization.  
  *Quick check:* If a hyperparameter search for LGP finds `max_instructions=23` and `n_extras=1` for CartPole, why might these same values be suboptimal for RLGP, which adds a Q-Table of size (n_registers × n_actions)?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**  
linear-gp crate: Engines (core, breed, mutate, fitness, generate), program representation (Instruction, Program), configuration (Parameters). gym-rs port: Wrapper for OpenAI Gym environments (CartPole, MountainCar). q_learning module: Q-Table struct, ε-greedy selector, update logic.

**Critical Path**  
core::run() → generate::individual() → fitness::evaluate() → (for RLGP: q_learning::step() inside evaluation loop) → core::selection() → breed::crossover() / mutate::replace().

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Simplicity vs. Performance: Adding a separate Q-Table increases memory and introduces a second learning loop, complicating the evolutionary signal.
- Generality vs. Efficiency: The modular Fitness engine is flexible but may not allow deep integration (e.g., using Q-values to influence mutation rates).
- Safety vs. Speed: Rust’s memory safety prevents crashes but may restrict certain pointer-based LGP optimizations.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Early plateau: Mean/median fitness stabilizes at a suboptimal value within 10–20 generations.
- Action collapse: The ε-greedy policy drives all programs to select the same single register-action pair, eliminating diversity.
- Register misuse: Q-Table updates occur for registers that are rarely the "winning" register, leading to many unreachable Q-states.

**First 3 Experiments**  
1. Baseline validation: Run LGP on Iris dataset with only reproduction, then recombination, then mutation to verify fitness, breed, and mutate engines.
2. Gym integration check: Run LGP on CartPole with minimal instructions to confirm gym-rs integration and basic program execution.
3. Q-Learning smoke test: Run RLGP on CartPole with a fixed program and tiny population to observe Q-Table updates and ensure ε exploration works.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Why does integrating Q-Learning into LGP lead to underperformance?** The paper shows RLGP underperforms on both tasks, but the cause (ε-greedy policy, state representation, or algorithmic interference) is unresolved. Ablation studies or exploration analysis would clarify.
- **Did separate hyperparameter optimization create an unfair comparison?** LGP and RLGP were tuned separately; LGP-optimal parameters may be suboptimal for RLGP, but the impact is unquantified. Joint optimization is needed.
- **How does the differing number of evaluation trials affect MountainCar results?** LGP uses 5 trials vs. RLGP's 100, potentially biasing variance; replication with equal trials is required.

## Limitations
- Separate hyperparameter optimization may yield suboptimal settings for RLGP's dual-loop learning.
- The state representation (winning register) for Q-Learning may not be Markovian, undermining learning stability.
- Performance comparisons lack statistical significance testing and could be influenced by variance from few trials.

## Confidence
- **Automation via Q-Learning:** Medium
- **Modular Rust framework:** High
- **Q-Learning as performance bottleneck:** Medium

## Next Checks
1. Hyperparameter sensitivity: Exhaustively vary RLGP’s Q-Learning parameters (α, γ, ε-decay) while keeping evolutionary constants fixed to see if performance gaps close.
2. State-space coverage: Log all (winning_register, action) pairs during evaluation; compute entropy and visitation frequency to detect premature convergence or poor state abstraction.
3. Ablation of Q-Learning: Replace the Q-Table with a random policy (ε=1) or a fixed mapping, keeping all other components unchanged, to quantify Q-Learning's net contribution.
