# Tight Bounds for Logistic Regression with Large Stepsize Gradient Descent in Low Dimension

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12471
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12471
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** For separable data in 2D, with sufficiently large step size η, the transition time τ (to stability) satisfies τ ≤ O(n/γ + log(1/γ)/γ²) independent of η; for T ≥ Ω(n/γ + 1/γ²) iterations, the logistic loss is O(1/(ηT)). A matching lower bound confirms tightness up to logarithmic factors.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates gradient descent (GD) for logistic regression under linear separability, focusing on how quickly the iterates enter a stable monotonic phase when using a large constant step size. In the two-dimensional setting, the dynamics can be decomposed along the max-margin direction and its orthogonal complement. A key phenomenon—oscillations, where the orthogonal component changes sign while the loss remains above a threshold—triggers exponential growth of the projection onto the max-margin direction. By combining exponential growth during oscillations with polynomial growth between them, the authors derive a tight bound on the number of iterations required to reach stability that does not depend on the step size η. They also provide a lower bound construction that matches the upper bound up to logarithmic factors, establishing the optimal iteration complexity in this regime.

## Method Summary
Under the assumptions of linear separability with margin γ, two-dimensional inputs, and a sufficiently large constant step size η, the analysis proceeds by decomposing the weight vector \( w_t \) into components parallel and orthogonal to the max-margin separator \( w^* \). In two dimensions the orthogonal component is scalar, enabling a precise characterization. The authors define an “oscillation” as an iteration where \( w_t \) is sufficiently aligned with \( w^* \) (above a threshold), the loss is still large, and the orthogonal component flips sign. They show that during an oscillation, the parallel component grows by at least a factor \( 1+γ^2 \). Between oscillations, as long as a future oscillation is anticipated, the parallel component grows at a polynomial rate \( 1+γ^2/2 \). Oscillations can only occur while the parallel component is at most \( η/γ \). Starting from an initial step that guarantees a linear increase, the total number of oscillations is bounded by \( O(\log(1/γ)/γ^2) \). The time between consecutive oscillations is bounded by \( O(n/γ + 1/γ^2) \) (Lemma 2.6), leading to an overall transition time \( τ = O(n/γ + \log(1/γ)/γ^2) \). Once within a stable region, smoothness of the logistic loss yields an \( O(1/(ηT)) \) convergence rate.

## Key Results
- Transition time bound: \( τ ≤ O\!\big(\frac{n}{γ} + \frac{\log(1/γ)}{γ^2}\big) \), independent of step size \( η \), for \( η ≥ η_0 \) (sufficiently large).
- After \( T ≥ Ω\!\big(\frac{n}{γ} + \frac{1}{γ^2}\big) \) iterations, logistic loss satisfies \( F(w_T) = O\!\big(\frac{1}{ηT}\big) \).
- A lower bound construction in 2D exhibits \( τ = Ω\!\big(\frac{n}{γ} + \frac{\log(1/γ)}{γ^2}\big) \), matching the upper bound up to logarithmic factors, demonstrating tightness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

Assumptions: The analysis assumes a linearly separable dataset in two dimensions with positive margin γ, and a constant step size η that is sufficiently large (η ≥ η_0). These assumptions drive the mechanisms described below.

### Mechanism 1
When GD jumps over the sublevel set \( \{F(w) ≤ O(1/η)\} \) (an oscillation), the component of the weight vector along the max-margin direction \( w^* \) grows by at least a factor \( (1+γ^2) \). During an oscillation the orthogonal component \( \tilde{w}_t \) changes sign; gradients from points with negative margins in the orthogonal subspace are aligned so that \( \langle w_{t+1}-w_t, w^* \rangle ≥ γ^2 \cdot \hat{w}_t \). This relies on linear separability with margin \( γ \) and step size \( η \) large enough. Lemma 2.2 formalizes this.

### Mechanism 2
Between oscillations, provided a future oscillation exists, the parallel component \( \hat{w}_t \) increases at a polynomial rate: \( \hat{w}_{t+1} ≥ (1+γ^2/2) \hat{w}_t \). This follows from the fact that before the oscillation the set of points with negative margins in the orthogonal plane does not change sign, and the gradient contribution from those points is smaller than at the oscillation step. Lemma 2.7 makes this precise under the 2D assumption.

### Mechanism 3
The transition time \( τ \) is bounded by \( O(n/γ + \log(1/γ)/γ^2) \), independent of \( η \). Oscillations can only occur while \( \hat{w}_t ≤ η/γ \) (Lemma 2.3). Starting from an initial value \( \hat{w}_1 = Ω(ηγ) \) (Lemma 2.1), each oscillation multiplies \( \hat{w} \) by \( (1+γ^2) \) and between oscillations it grows by \( (1+γ^2/2) \). Hence after \( O(\frac{1}{γ^2}\log(η/γ)) \) steps, \( \hat{w}_t \) would exceed \( η/γ \), precluding further oscillations. Moreover, Lemma 2.6 guarantees that within \( O(n/γ + 1/γ^2) \) steps after any time before τ, either an oscillation occurs or stability is reached. Combining these yields the claimed bound, and after stability the loss decreases smoothly.

## Foundational Learning
### Maximum margin separator and margin \( γ \)
- **Why needed here:** The direction \( w^* \) defines the coordinate system; its margin \( γ \) sets the scale for separation and appears in all bounds. Progress is measured via \( \hat{w}_t = \langle w_t, w^* \rangle \).
- **Quick check:** For a separable dataset with margin \( γ = 0.2 \), the minimum possible value of \( \langle w^*, x_i \rangle \) over all \( i \) is at least 0.2.

### Parallel/orthogonal decomposition of \( w_t \)
- **Why needed here:** In \( d=2 \), the weight vector splits into \( \hat{w}_t w^* + \tilde{w}_t v^* \) with \( \langle v^*, w^* \rangle = 0 \). The oscillation definition depends on sign changes of \( \tilde{w}_t \), and the loss reduces to a function of two scalars, simplifying dynamics.
- **Quick check:** If \( w^* = (1,0) \), \( v^* = (0,1) \), and \( w_t = (3,-1) \), then \( \hat{w}_t = 3 \) and \( \tilde{w}_t = -1 \).

### Gradient potential \( G(w) \)
- **Why needed here:** \( G(w) = \frac{1}{n}\sum \frac{1}{e^{\langle w , x_i \rangle}+1} \) approximates the gradient norm and links the loss level to progress in the \( w^* \) direction: \( \hat{w}_{t+1} - \hat{w}_t = η \langle -\nabla F(w_t), w^* \rangle ≥ η γ G(w_t) \). Bounding \( G(w_t) \) controls growth.
- **Quick check:** When all \( \langle w, x_i \rangle \) are large positive, \( G(w) \) is approximately 0 because each term is near 0.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Dataset (x_i, y_i) → compute projections a_i^t = ⟨w_t, x_i⟩ → partition into D_t^+ and D_t^- → compute gradient potential G(w_t) → update w_{t+1} = w_t - η∇F(w_t) → iterate.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Prove \( \hat{w}_t \) is strictly increasing and lower‑bounded (Lemma 2.1).
  2. Show if \( |\tilde{w}_t| ≤ \frac{γ}{2}\hat{w}_t \) then stability is already achieved (Lemma A.1).
  3. Define oscillations; prove during an oscillation \( \hat{w} \) grows exponentially (Lemma 2.2) and oscillations require \( \hat{w}_t ≤ η/γ \) (Lemma 2.3).
  4. Establish auxiliary bounds: Lemma 2.4 (progress of margins for i∈D_t^-), Lemma 2.5 (\( |\tilde{w}_t| ≤ η \)), Lemma 2.6 (time to next oscillation or stability is \( O(n/γ + 1/γ^2) \)), Lemma 2.7 (polynomial growth between oscillations).
  5. Combine to bound total number of oscillations and thus \( τ \) (Lemma 2.8).
  6. Prove final loss rate under stability using smoothness (Theorem 2.1).
  7. Construct lower‑bound datasets to show tightness (Section 3).
- **Design tradeoffs:** The analysis heavily exploits dimension 2: orthogonal component is scalar, making the set \( \{ \hat{w} ≥ λ, |\tilde{w}| ≥ \frac{γ}{2}\hat{w} \} \) disconnected, forcing jumps. For \( d>2 \) this set is connected, breaking the oscillation argument. The threshold \( 1/(8η) \) is chosen for convenience; larger thresholds affect constants. The requirement \( η ≥ η_0 \) (with \( η_0 \) depending on \( n \) and \( γ \)) ensures sufficient per‑step progress; for smaller \( η \) the bound likely incurs \( η \)-dependence.
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - Higher dimensions (\( d>2 \)): Lemma 2.6 may fail because \( \tilde{w}_t \) can avoid sign change while outside the stable region; numerical hints suggest possible η‑independence but proof lacking.
  - Non‑separable data (\( γ=0 \)): Exponential growth factors vanish; GD may never stabilize.
  - Small step size (\( η < η_0 \)): Linear/exponential growth rates degrade; τ may become \( Ω(η/γ^2) \) as in prior work.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce Figure 1(a): Run GD on MNIST (binary, \( n=256 \)) with large \( η \) ranging from \( 2^2 \) to \( 2^9 \). Plot loss and record \( τ \) (first step with \( F ≤ 2/η \)). Verify \( τ \) does not increase as \( η \) grows.
  2. Synthetic 2D test: Generate separable datasets with \( n=100 \), \( γ=0.1 \), \( d=2 \). Use large \( η \) (e.g., 1000). Measure \( τ \) and compare to predicted \( O(n/γ + 1/γ^2) ≈ 1100 \). Sweep \( n \) and \( γ \) to confirm scaling.
  3. Higher‑dimensional check: For \( d ∈ \{3,5,10,27\} \), generate random separable datasets with \( n=d \) and fixed \( γ=0.1 \). Increase \( η \) and observe worst‑case \( τ \) vs. \( η \). If \( τ \) remains roughly constant as \( η \) grows, it supports the conjecture that the bound may extend beyond \( d=2 \).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper highlights the following open questions for future research:
- Extending the η‑independent transition time bound to higher dimensions (d > 2).
- Determining the iteration complexity for step sizes below the threshold η_0.
- Understanding the behavior of gradient descent under non‑separable data (e.g., zero margin or imperfect separation).

## Limitations
- Results are restricted to two dimensions; extension to higher dimensions remains an open problem.
- The step size must be sufficiently large (\( η ≥ η_0 \)), and the iteration bound for smaller \( η \) is unknown.
- The analysis assumes exact linear separability with a positive margin; non-separable data are not covered.

## Confidence
- Upper bound on \( τ \) (Theorem 2.1): **High** (the proof is constructive and covers all large‑η regimes)
- Oscillation dynamics (Lemmas 2.2, 2.7): **High** (the mechanisms are rigorously established for the 2D case)
- Lower bound tightness (Section 3): **Medium** (the construction matches the upper bound up to logarithmic factors but is restricted to 2D; it is unknown whether it extends to higher dimensions or other step sizes)

## Next Checks

2. **Lower‑bound test**: Implement the dataset construction from Section 3; compute \( τ \) for different \( n,γ \) and confirm the \( Ω(n/γ + \log(1/γ)/γ^2) \) scaling.
