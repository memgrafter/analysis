# TopoCurate:Modeling Interaction Topology for Tool-Use Agent Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.01714
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01714
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: A topological curation method that improves tool-use agent training, yielding +4.2% (SFT) and +6.9% (RL) over state-of-the-art on BFCLv3 and Tau2 Bench.

## Executive Summary
Standard training for tool-use agents relies on simple outcome-based filtering (e.g., selecting only successful trajectories for SFT, tasks by pass-rate for RL). This approach ignores critical interaction dynamics such as error recovery, strategic diversity, and efficiency, leading to covariate shift and mode collapse in supervised fine-tuning and vanishing gradients in reinforcement learning due to low-signal tasks. TopoCurate addresses this by modeling the interaction topology: it projects multiple rollout trajectories for a task into a unified directed acyclic graph (DAG) via a semantic quotient, merging equivalent action-observation states. From this structural representation, it derives dual selection mechanisms—topological metrics for SFT trajectory ranking and task curation for RL—that prioritize informative and diverse interactions. Evaluations on BFCLv3 and Tau2 Bench show consistent and significant gains over strong baselines.

## Method Summary
TopoCurate’s pipeline first aggregates multi-turn interaction turns (reasoning, action, observation) from multiple rollouts per task. It defines semantic equivalence between turns using similarity thresholds on tool actions and observation embeddings, merging equivalent turns to build a quotient topology—a DAG where nodes are equivalence classes and edges are state transitions. State success potentials (Φ) are estimated from visit frequencies. For SFT, each trajectory is scored on three topological metrics: Reflective Recovery (sum of potential recoveries after dips), Semantic Efficiency (geodesic vs. actual path length), and Distributional Diversity (inverse popularity weighted by local accuracy). Trajectories are selected by a weighted combination of normalized scores. For RL, tasks are characterized by Error Branch Ratio (fraction of children with Φ below a failure threshold) and Strategic Heterogeneity (unique successful chain ratio). Tasks are sampled with probability proportional to exp((Vstruct + α·Vdiv)/τ). Selected data then trains the agent via standard SFT and GRPO.

## Key Results
- Gains: +4.2% average Pass@1 improvement for SFT and +6.9% for RL over state-of-the-art baselines on BFCLv3 and Tau2 Bench.
- Ablations: Each topological metric (Reflection, Efficiency, Diversity for SFT; Structure, Diversity for RL) contributes positively to accuracy; removing Reflection harms Telecom by 1.3%, and removing Structure harms Telecom by 3.6%.
- Analysis: Selected trajectories show higher reflection rates, better token efficiency, and greater strategic plasticity (higher unique chain ratio) compared to outcome-filtered baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Quotient Topology Construction
*Claim*: Projecting multi-trial rollouts into a unified semantic quotient topology reveals the causal structure of agent-environment interaction, making explicit the bifurcations between effective strategies and failure modes.  
*Mechanism*: Define a complete interaction turn as (reasoning, action, observation). Merge turns that are semantically equivalent according to similarity thresholds on tool actions and observational results. This yields a directed acyclic graph (DAG) where nodes represent equivalence classes and edges represent state transitions. The graph compactly encodes all explored paths for a task.  
*Core assumption*: Semantic equivalence of (action, observation) pairs accurately captures the relevant state for decision-making, and merging does not lose critical distinctions.  
*Evidence anchors*: [abstract]: "projects multi-trial rollouts into a unified semantic quotient topology by merging equivalent action-observation states"; [section 4.2]: Detailed algorithm and equations (3) defining equivalence with thresholds.  
*Break condition*: Thresholds too low cause over-merging and obscure decision points; thresholds too high leave sparse graphs. Embedding model failure yields semantically incorrect topology.

### Mechanism 2: SFT Trajectory Selection via Topological Metrics
*Claim*: Selecting trajectories with high Reflective Recovery, Semantic Efficiency, and Distributional Diversity mitigates covariate shift and mode collapse in SFT by aligning the training distribution with a robust, efficient, and diverse expert policy.  
*Mechanism*: For each trajectory, compute: (1) Reflective Recovery (Sref) sums potential recovery after performance dips; (2) Semantic Efficiency (Seff) penalizes redundant loops via geodesic vs. actual path length; (3) Distributional Diversity (Srare) upweights rare but successful branches. Combine normalized scores with weights to get selection probability.  
*Core assumption*: These metrics correlate with process quality (error recovery, token efficiency, strategic diversity) that outcome-only filtering ignores.  
*Evidence anchors*: [abstract]: "trajectories demonstrating reflective recovery, semantic efficiency, and strategic diversity to mitigate covariate shift and mode collapse"; [section 4.3]: Equations (4)-(6) define metrics and link to KL minimization; [section 5.3]: Ablation shows each metric individually contributes to Pass@1 gains (e.g., removing Reflection hurts Telecom by 1.3%).  
*Break condition*: Candidate pool lacks high-scoring trajectories on any metric, causing overemphasis on low-variance metrics. Poor estimation of success potential Φ(v) due to sparse data makes metrics unreliable.

### Mechanism 3: RL Task Selection via Structural Complexity
*Claim*: Selecting tasks with high Error Branch Ratio and Strategic Heterogeneity maximizes the gradient Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) in sparse-reward RL, addressing vanishing gradients and accelerating convergence.  
*Mechanism*: For each task, compute (1) Error Branch Ratio (Vstruct): fraction of decision nodes with failure branches (Φ(u) < ϵ_fail); (2) Strategic Heterogeneity (Vdiv): ratio of unique successful tool sequences. Sample tasks with probability proportional to exp((Vstruct+αVdiv)/τ).  
*Core assumption*: These topological metrics are proxies for Fisher information and gradient SNR; tasks with sharp decision boundaries and multiple valid paths provide high-contrast learning signals.  
*Evidence anchors*: [abstract]: "selects tasks with high error branch ratios and strategic heterogeneity, maximizing gradient Signal-to-Noise Ratio"; [section 4.4]: Equations (9)-(11) connect Vstruct to advantage variance and Vdiv to policy entropy; [section 5.3]: Ablation shows structure metric is dominant (removing it drops Telecom performance by 3.6%).  
*Break condition*: Tasks with extremely high error branch ratios may be too difficult (no successful trajectories, zero gradient). Tasks with high Vdiv but low success probability may yield noisy signals if policy cannot reach success.

### Concept: Covariate Shift and Mode Collapse
*Why needed*: TopoCurate's SFT metrics directly target these issues: covariate shift from fragile trajectories and mode collapse from solution homogeneity. These are standard challenges in distribution matching.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:
Trajectory Pool -> Topology Builder -> Metric Calculator -> SFT Selector & RL Selector -> Training Pipeline

Critical path:
1. For each task, aggregate all its trajectories.
2. Build quotient topology: compare all turns pairwise (or via LSH) to merge equivalent states (action+observation similarity) into a DAG.
3. Compute node potentials (success rates) from aggregated data.
4. For SFT: for each trajectory, compute Sref (sum of recoveries along path), Seff (geodesic/length), Srare (inverse popularity weighted by local accuracy). Normalize and combine to get weight. Select top trajectories within budget.
5. For RL: for each task, compute Vstruct (fraction of failure branches across decision nodes) and Vdiv (Unique Chain Ratio). Form task sampling distribution.
6. Train SFT on selected trajectories; then train RL on tasks sampled from P_select.

Design tradeoffs:
- Merging thresholds: Strict thresholds preserve distinctions but yield sparse graphs; lenient thresholds create dense graphs but risk merging distinct states. Tradeoff between granularity and statistical robustness.
- Metric weights: λ_ref, λ_eff, λ_rare for SFT; α, τ for RL. Tuning required; paper shows moderate sensitivity but defaults work well.
- Topology construction cost: O(N log N) with LSH but still heavy; can be amortized over multiple training runs? Or use approximate merging.
- Potential estimation: Requires sufficient visits per node; poor estimation leads to noisy metrics. May need smoothing.

Failure signatures:
- SFT selection: All selected trajectories have nearly identical topological patterns (low diversity). Indicates Srare weight too low or candidate pool lacks diversity.
- RL task selection: Selected tasks have very low success rates (<5%) despite high Vstruct. May cause policy to fail to learn; need to enforce a minimum success threshold.
- Topology builder: Graph has many isolated nodes or extremely dense components. Check distribution of node degrees and component sizes.
- Metric instability: Large variance in metrics across random subsets of trajectories. Indicates insufficient data for robust estimation; consider increasing trajectory pool or relaxing thresholds.

First 3 experiments:
1. Sanity check: Topology construction. For a small task pool (e.g., 10 tasks), manually inspect the DAGs. Verify that nodes with similar (action, observation) are merged, and that critical decision points (e.g., error branches) appear as distinct nodes.
2. Metric correlation study: Compute all topological metrics for a large candidate pool. Correlate them with outcome-based metrics (pass rate). Expect weak correlation, confirming they capture orthogonal information.
3. Ablation on synthetic control: Create a synthetic task with known properties (e.g., one trajectory with explicit error recovery, another without). Verify that Reflective Recovery score discriminates them. Similarly, for RL tasks, create tasks with high vs. low error branch ratios and check that they yield different gradient variances in a simple policy gradient run.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
*Question*: What is the effect of the similarity thresholds on the quotient topology and agent performance?  
*Basis*: The paper fixes thresholds (δtool≈0.95, δresult≈0.90) but does not justify them (Sec 4.2).  
*Why unresolved*: Thresholds control state abstraction; inappropriate values could drastically alter graph structure and harm learning.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Systematically vary thresholds and measure downstream SFT/RL success rates and topology metrics.

### Open Question 2
*Question*: Does TopoCurate generalize to tool-use domains outside Retail/Airline/Telecom/BFCL?  
*Basis*: Evaluation only on those benchmarks; no other domains tested.  
*Why unresolved*: Topological metrics were designed for specific benchmark dynamics; other settings may require different definitions of recovery, efficiency, or diversity.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Apply TopoCurate to other benchmarks (e.g., WebShop, robotic manipulation) and compare against outcome-based baselines.

### Open Question 3
*Question*: How does topology construction scale in time and memory with the number of trajectories?  
*Basis*: The paper mentions O(N log N) LSH but provides no empirical scaling results; experiments used only a few thousand trajectories.  
*Why unresolved*: Large-scale data could be computationally prohibitive, and approximate merging might degrade topology quality, affecting agent performance.  
*What evidence would resolve it*: Benchmark runtime and memory usage for increasing dataset sizes (up to millions) and monitor any change in agent accuracy.

## Limitations
- Unspecified thresholds and hyperparameters (δtool, δresult, ϵfail, τ, λ, α) with no sensitivity study.
- Reliance on diverse multi-trial rollouts; low diversity may collapse metrics to outcome filtering.
- No runtime analysis; topological graph construction could be costly for large rollout sets.

## Confidence
- Topology construction: Medium (sensitivity to equivalence thresholds, no robustness analysis).
- SFT metrics: Medium (ablations support impact but not direct link to covariate shift/mode collapse).
- RL task selection: Medium (theoretical SNR connection not measured; task difficulty balance unclear).
- Overall gains: High (consistent benchmark improvements).
