# TraderBench: How Robust Are AI Agents in Adversarial Capital Markets?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00285
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00285
- **Reference count:** 6
- **Primary result:** Most AI trading agents (8 of 13) use fixed, non-adaptive strategies, showing negligible performance variation across progressive adversarial market transforms, while performance-grounded scoring eliminates judge variance plaguing rubric-based evaluation.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces TraderBench, a benchmark for evaluating AI trading agents that combines static, expert-verified tasks with adversarial trading simulations. It addresses two key flaws in existing evaluations: the static nature of benchmarks that miss dynamic market interactions, and the high variance introduced by using LLMs as judges. TraderBench scores crypto trading purely on realized performance metrics (Sharpe ratio, returns, drawdown), removing subjective judgment. Evaluating 13 models reveals that the majority employ inert strategies unaffected by adversarial data manipulation, extended thinking improves knowledge retrieval but not trading, and rubric-based scores are highly judge-dependent while performance scores are not. The findings indicate current agents lack genuine market adaptation and demonstrate the superiority of objective, performance-grounded evaluation for safety-critical financial tasks.

## Method Summary
TraderBench employs a two-agent architecture: an Evaluator Agent orchestrates tasks and applies scoring, while a Candidate Agent (the system under test) uses an LLM integrated with six Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers. These servers provide access to SEC filings, market data, a code sandbox, options pricing, a trading simulator, and risk metrics. Evaluation covers four sections: Knowledge Retrieval (tool-based fact extraction), Analytical Reasoning (self-contained calculations), Options Trading (derivatives P&L, Greeks, strategy), and Crypto Trading. The Crypto section is core: it runs historical data through four progressive adversarial transforms (Baseline → Noisy → Meta → Adversarial) and scores only on realized trading performance (a weighted sum of return, Sharpe, win rate, and drawdown penalty). Other sections use GPT-5.2 with a structured rubric. Each model is evaluated once with a fixed task sample (≈50 tasks, seed 42) and a 6-hour timeout.

## Key Results
- **Fixed strategies dominate:** 8 of 13 models show near-identical crypto scores (~33) with under 1-point variation across all four adversarial transforms, indicating non-adaptive, likely inertial strategies.
- **Extended thinking mismatch:** Chain-of-thought improves Knowledge Retrieval scores by 26 points but has negligible impact on trading performance (+0.3 in crypto, -0.1 in options).
- **Judge variance is severe:** Rubric-based Knowledge Retrieval scores vary by up to 29 points across different LLM judges, whereas crypto scores (performance-based) vary by only 0.3 points.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Performance-Grounded Scoring Eliminates Judge Variance
- **Claim:** Scoring via realized trading metrics (Sharpe, returns, drawdown) removes reliance on subjective LLM judgment.
- **Mechanism:** Objective mathematical metrics derived from simulation outcomes are unambiguous and directly comparable across runs and judges.
- **Core assumption:** Performance metrics are invariant to the evaluator's identity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "scored purely on realized performance—Sharpe ratio, returns, and drawdown—eliminating judge variance entirely."
  - [section 5.5] Crypto scores vary by 0.3 points across three judges; Knowledge Retrieval varies by 28.8 points.
- **Break condition:** Changes to the simulation environment (slippage, data feed) between evaluations could reintroduce incomparability.

### Mechanism 2: Adversarial Transforms Expose Adaptive vs. Fixed Strategies
- **Claim:** Progressively degrading signal quality in price data separates adaptive strategies from fixed/inert ones.
- **Mechanism:** A model that adapts will maintain performance as inputs are manipulated (noisy, false signals); a fixed strategy's score is tied to the baseline data's properties.
- **Core assumption:** Adaptive strategies modify decision logic based on input quality; fixed strategies do not.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "8 of 13 models maintained nearly identical scores (~33) with under 1-point variation, indicating fixed, non-adaptive strategies."
  - [section 5.4] Flat score profiles across transforms suggest fixed strategies.
- **Break condition:** A random strategy might also show low variance, creating a false positive for robustness.

### Mechanism 3: Sub-Scoring Reveals Conceptual-Computational Gap in Options
- **Claim:** Decomposing options scoring highlights a universal gap where models handle high-level strategy but fail at precise quantitative calculation (Greeks).
- **Mechanism:** Separate sub-scores for conceptual tasks (P&L, strategy) vs. computational tasks (Greeks within tight tolerance) isolate this competence mirage.
- **Core assumption:** Safe options trading requires accurate Greeks; a large disparity indicates a critical failure masked by aggregate scores.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "a 54-point quantitative-vs-qualitative gap in options persists."
  - [section 5.3] Conceptual scores (80–93) consistently exceed computational scores (18–53); mean difference 54 points.
- **Break condition:** If models used perfect tool calls for Greeks, the gap might close, pointing to tool-interface rather than calculation issues.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Performance-Grounded vs. Rubric-Based Evaluation**
  - *Why needed:* Central to TraderBench's design; explains why crypto scores are reliable while Knowledge Retrieval scores are not.
  - *Quick check:* Why does changing the LLM judge cause a 29-point swing in Knowledge Retrieval scores but a 0.3-point swing in Crypto Trading scores?
- **Concept: Adversarial Data Transforms as a Robustness Test**
  - *Why needed:* To correctly interpret crypto leaderboard; a score of 32 could mean "robustly bad" or "inaction robust."
  - *Quick check:* What is the practical difference between a model that scores 33 on all four transforms and one that scores 50 on baseline but 22 on adversarial?
- **Concept: Tool-Use Planning vs. Internal Reasoning**
  - *Why needed:* Knowledge Retrieval results show strong reasoning (Analytical Reasoning) does not guarantee effective tool use.
  - *Quick check:* Why does Qwen3-32B score 84.0 on Analytical Reasoning but only 14.4 on Knowledge Retrieval?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Evaluator Agent -> (A2A task send) -> Candidate Agent (LLM + MCP servers) -> (A2A response) -> Evaluator Agent (applies scoring evaluator). MCP servers: SEC EDGAR, Yahoo Finance, Sandbox, Options, Trading Sim, Risk.
- **Critical path:** Task generation → A2A task dispatch → Candidate LLM reasoning → MCP tool call(s) → Candidate response → A2A response → Evaluator scoring (LLM rubric or performance metric).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Equal 25% section weighting prioritizes breadth; re-weighting changes the "well-rounded agent" premise.
  - Fixed LLM judge (GPT-5.2) ensures consistency but limits generalizability; judge reliability is a known limitation.
  - Fixed Candidate Agent architecture (same MCP access for all) isolates LLM capability but hides gains from model-specific agent designs.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Fixed crypto strategy:** Near-identical scores (~32-34) across Baseline, Noisy, Meta, Adversarial transforms.
  - **Tool-use failure:** High Analytical Reasoning score but very low Knowledge Retrieval score (<20).
  - **Conceptual-computational gap:** Options sub-scores show >40-point difference between P&L/Strategy and Greeks/Risk.
  - **Judge dependency:** >10-point score swings on rubric sections with different judges.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline vs. Adversarial Drift:** Evaluate agent on Baseline and Adversarial crypto transforms only. >5-point drop indicates signal sensitivity; <1-point difference suggests potential inaction.
  2. **Tool-Use Isolation:** Run Knowledge Retrieval tasks with MCP servers disabled. Score should plummet from 50+ to ~10 if tool use is primary driver.
  3. **Judge Sensitivity Check:** Re-score Options Trading responses with two different LLM judges. >5-point spread on overall Options score indicates unreliable metric.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Why do most AI trading agents adopt fixed, non-adaptive strategies in adversarial crypto markets, and what architectural modifications could enable genuine adversarial resilience without sacrificing baseline performance?
  - *Basis:* 8 of 13 models score ~33 on crypto with <1-point variation across transforms. No model achieves both high baseline and minimal adversarial degradation.
  - *Why unresolved:* The pattern is identified but causes (adaptation limits, incentive misalignment, architecture constraints) are not investigated.
  - *What evidence would resolve:* Ablations on agent policy, reward functions; mechanistic analysis of decision trajectories; experiments with modified evaluation incentives.
- **Open Question 2:** What evaluation protocols minimize judge-dependent variance in rubric-based financial tasks while maintaining scalability, and how should these be standardized for safety-critical deployment?
  - *Basis:* Knowledge Retrieval varies up to 29 points across judges. Authors argue for performance metrics, multi-judge protocols, and judge calibration.
  - *Why unresolved:* No concrete, tested protocol is prescribed; trade-offs between cost, scalability, and reliability are unexplored.
  - *What evidence would resolve:* Systematic comparison of aggregation methods (voting, averaging, human adjudication); calibrated judge models fine-tuned on expert rubrics; extension of performance scoring to more tasks.
- **Open Question 3:** What is the primary bottleneck behind the universal conceptual-vs-computational gap in options trading, and can targeted interventions in tool interfacing or internal numerical reasoning close this gap?
  - *Basis:* Universal 54-point gap between conceptual (P&L, strategy) and computational (Greeks) sub-scores. Suggested cause: interface failure in parameterizing external calls or parsing outputs.
  - *Why unresolved:* Relative contributions of tool-use errors vs. intrinsic calculation limitations are not disentangled.
  - *What evidence would resolve:* Error classification (parameterization, parsing, conceptual); ablations without tool access; fine-tuning on numerical precision or tool-calling reliability.
- **Open Question 4:** Why does extended thinking improve knowledge retrieval but yield negligible gains in trading performance, and does this indicate a fundamental limitation of current reasoning paradigms for dynamic market adaptation?
  - *Basis:* Extended thinking helps retrieval (+26 points) but has zero impact on trading (+0.3 crypto, -0.1 options). Authors conclude chain-of-thought improves information synthesis but fails for real-time adaptation.
  - *Why unresolved:* Reasons (real-time constraints, architecture not leveraging traces, reasoning unsuited for decisions) are speculative.
  - *What evidence would resolve:* Effect of thinking time under varying pressures; analysis of thinking traces informing tool use; comparisons with architectures integrating reasoning into action selection (e.g., tree search).
- **Open Question 5:** How can benchmarks provide statistically reliable comparisons given the single-run evaluation design without confidence intervals, and what sampling strategies ensure robustness across model stochasticity and task variants?
  - *Basis:* Each model evaluated in a single run; no confidence intervals reported despite model generation stochasticity and task sampling.
  - *Why unresolved:* Required number of seeds or tasks for stable estimates in noisy trading simulations is unknown.
  - *What evidence would resolve:* Multi-seed evaluations (10–30 seeds) to compute distributions and confidence intervals; determining minimal seeds for desired power; assessing variance from model vs. task sampling.

## Limitations
- MCP server implementation specifics (data feeds, slippage models, no-lookahead enforcement) are unspecified, impacting score validity.
- Exact crypto transform algorithms (especially coordinated false signals) are not detailed, risking replication difficulty.
- Full scoring rubrics and normalization functions for Knowledge Retrieval/Analytical Reasoning are not provided.
- Candidate agent's system prompts, tool-use policy, and LLM generation parameters are absent, creating a major variance source.

## Next Checks
1. **Judge invariance replication:** Re-run Crypto evaluation for 3 models using three different LLM judges for rubric sections. Confirm crypto scores remain stable (<1 pt spread) while Knowledge Retrieval scores vary wildly (>20 pts).
2. **Transform definition stress test:** Implement the four crypto transforms and run a simple known strategy (e.g., 50-day MA crossover) on all four. Measure performance drop from Baseline to Adversarial; expect >50% degradation for this non-adaptive strategy.
3. **Options sub-scoring granularity audit:** For 2 models, manually audit the four Options sub-scores. Verify the reported large gap holds and that the "Greeks" sub-score is the primary driver of the low computational score, not a single outlier task.
