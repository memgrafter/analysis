# Evolving Programmatic Skill Networks

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2601.03509
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03509
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PSN unlocked diamond tools in all MineDojo runs (mean 51 iterations) versus Voyager’s 1/3 success (102 iterations), and achieved higher cumulative reward on Crafter.

## Executive Summary
Continual skill acquisition in open-ended environments struggles with credit assignment across compositions and uncontrolled library growth. The Programmatic Skill Network (PSN) addresses this by representing skills as executable symbolic programs in a directed invocation graph. Three core mechanisms—trace-based fault localization (REFLECT), maturity-aware update gating, and canonical refactoring—enable stable, compositional learning. Evaluated on MineDojo and Crafter, PSN demonstrates superior long-horizon progress, robustness to forgetting, and compact skill repertoires compared to prior methods.

## Method Summary
PSN maintains a network of programmatic skills (e.g., JavaScript for Minecraft) with explicit pre/postconditions and invocation links. The learning cycle proceeds: a hybrid planner (backward-chaining with LLM fallback) selects or synthesizes skills; execution produces a trace; on failure, REFLECT uses the trace and LLM to perform symbolic backpropagation, identifying faulty components and generating patches applied in dependency order; on success, an online refactor scans for redundancy patterns (e.g., duplicates, common subskills) and applies deterministic rewrites validated by rollback on recent tasks. Skill reliability values drive a maturity gate that modulates update frequency, preserving stable behaviors while allowing plasticity.

## Key Results
- On MineDojo’s tech tree, PSN unlocked diamond tools in all 3 runs (mean 51 iterations) vs. Voyager (1/3 runs, 102 iterations); it also achieved obsidian in 1 run (77 iterations).
- On Crafter, PSN attained higher cumulative reward and better survival stability than baseline methods.
- Ablations confirm: the optimizer is critical for deep task success; maturity gating prevents oscillatory forgetting; refactoring curbs library explosion, causing repertoire to plateau rather than grow linearly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: REFLECT (Symbolic Fault Localization)
- **Claim:** Enables effective credit assignment over hierarchical skill compositions by propagating failure signals along symbolic invocation links.
- **Mechanism:** Given an execution trace and task feedback, REFLECT performs (1) top-down symbolic differentiation to assign responsibility from failing skills to subskills (like backprop), then (2) bottom-up gradient application to edit components in a dependency-respecting order. This isolates faults without globally overwriting reliable skills.
- **Core assumption:** Failures can be localized to specific components within the programmatic structure and invocation graph.
- **Break condition:** Shallow traces or poor network modularization may misassign credit to overly coarse components, leading to ineffective repairs.

### Mechanism 2: Maturity-Aware Update Gating
- **Claim:** Stabilizes reliable skills while preserving plasticity for uncertain ones, mitigating catastrophic forgetting and oscillations.
- **Mechanism:** Each skill maintains a reliability estimate V(s) (Laplace-smoothed success rate minus an uncertainty term). Update probability follows a sigmoid centered at a maturity pivot (0.6): high-V(s) skills are rarely updated, low-V(s) skills updated frequently.
- **Core assumption:** A scalar reliability value predicts future stability; skills above a threshold benefit more from preservation than further optimization.
- **Break condition:** Noisy reliability estimates in sparse-data regimes may prematurely freeze improving skills or mislabel unstable ones as mature.

### Mechanism 3: Canonical Structural Refactoring
- **Claim:** Maintains network compactness and promotes reuse via semantics-preserving rewrites under rollback validation.
- **Mechanism:** After successes, a refactor module scans a candidate set (parents, children, top-5 semantically similar skills) for five redundancy patterns (e.g., parametric coverage, common subskills). Deterministic rewrites (merge, abstract, extract) are proposed and validated on a sliding window of recent tasks; if success rate drops >20%, the change is rolled back.
- **Core assumption:** Redundant structures consistently emerge and can be captured by fixed patterns; rollback prevents performance degradation from premature reorganizations.
- **Break condition:** The fixed pattern set may miss some redundancies; validation might reject beneficial but initially disruptive reorganizations if the window is too narrow or tasks too heterogeneous.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Programmatic Skill Representation
- **Why needed:** Skills as executable symbolic programs (with control flow, parameters, pre/postconditions) enable precise trace recording, symbolic manipulation, and deterministic execution—prerequisites for REFLECT and refactoring.
- **Quick check:** How does a programmatic representation differ from a black-box policy or natural language description in terms of editability and trace fidelity?

### Concept: Compositional Network with Invocation Links
- **Why needed:** Explicit invocation edges form a directed graph that allows trace-based credit assignment (propagation) and backward-chaining reuse. Without these links, symbolic backprop and redundancy detection would not be possible.
- **Quick check:** If two skills are semantically similar but not directly connected, under what conditions might refactoring still merge them?

### Concept: Multi-Timescale Continual Learning Dynamics
- **Why needed:** PSN operates on coupled timescales: fast per-execution repair (REFLECT), intermediate stabilization (maturity gating over 10–50 executions), and slow structural changes (refactoring every 5–10 successes). Understanding their interplay predicts network evolution and stability.
- **Quick check:** What would likely happen if refactoring occurred after every success instead of only after convergence?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**  
  Hybrid Planner -> PSN Manager -> (Skill Optimizer on failure; Online Refactor on success)  
  Value Estimator feeds Planner and Optimizer.

- **Critical path:** Task → Plan (backward-chain or LLM forward) → Synthesis (CODEGEN) → Execution & Trace → If success → candidate Refactor; if failure → Optimizer (REFLECT → Patch) → Updated Network.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Symbolic precision vs. LLM flexibility: LLM synthesis/repair introduces non-determinism; architectural scaffolding (traces, gating) imposes stability.  
  - Stability vs. plasticity: Maturity gating trades off preserving reliable skills vs. adapting new contexts; pivot (0.6) and sharpness (γ=5.0) are tunable.  
  - Redundancy vs. generalization: Aggressive refactoring risks over-abstraction; conservative refactoring allows bloated libraries; five canonical cases are a designed compromise.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Oscillatory forgetting: Without maturity gating, skills repeatedly modified after downstream failures never stabilize.  
  - Library explosion: Without refactor, skill count grows linearly, bloating the repertoire and increasing planning failure rates.  
  - Brittle skills: Without the optimizer, skills pass initial tests but fail under compositional pressure.  
  - High refactor rejection: Rollback rate > expected may indicate overly aggressive candidate selection or unstable task distributions.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. MineDojo Tech Tree Mastery: Measures progress through wood → stone → iron → diamond → obsidian. PSN unlocked diamond in all runs vs. Voyager’s 1/3.  
  2. Crafter Cumulative Reward: Tests dense-reward continual learning; PSN shows steadily increasing reward while baselines plateau or die.  
  3. Ablation of Optimizer: Removing symbolic optimization degrades performance on deep tasks, proving the optimizer’s critical role.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Theoretical guarantees:** What convergence, optimality, or stability guarantees exist for symbolic projection and update rules in REFLECT and refactoring? The paper acknowledges a lack of formal analysis.
2. **Backend robustness:** How do PSN’s learning dynamics transfer to different code-generation backends (e.g., non-LLM synthesizers)? The claim of architecture-driven behavior is untested.
3. **Neural-symbolic analogy:** Does the structural parallel to neural training (backprop, learning rate, NAS) yield predictive power or novel algorithmic insights for programmatic learning? The analogy remains partial and unoperationalized.
4. **Refactoring strategy comparison:** What is the isolated contribution of online, rollback-validated refactoring versus offline or less constrained approaches? The ablation contrasts online PSN with offline Voyager refactoring but not other online variants.

## Limitations
- **Unknown implementation details:** Exact LLM prompt templates, skill value uncertainty update rules, semantic embedding function for refactor, and rollback inverse operations are undisclosed, hindering faithful reproduction.
- **Generalizability:** Evaluation limited to Minecraft (MineDojo) and Crafter; performance in algorithmically distinct domains (e.g., robotics, web automation) is unknown.
- **Theoretical foundation:** No formal guarantees for convergence, stability, or optimality of the continual learning dynamics.

## Confidence
- **Results (High):** Benchmarks and ablations are clearly reported and consistent with stated mechanisms.
- **Mechanisms (Medium):** Logically sound and supported by ablations, but depend on undisclosed prompt/embedding design choices that affect reliability.
- **Scalability/Generality (Low):** No evidence beyond tested environments; computational cost of LLM-based trace analysis at scale is uncharacterized.

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt Sensitivity Test:** Systematically vary REFLECT prompt phrasing on held-out failing traces to measure edit validity and success rate.
2. **Embedding Ablation:** Replace the undisclosed semantic embedding with a random or token-overlap baseline; measure impact on refactor trigger rate and repertoire plateauing.
3. **Cross-Domain Transfer:** Pre-train PSN on Crafter, then zero-shot transfer to a new Minecraft subtask (e.g., farming) to test compositional generalization beyond the original curriculum.
