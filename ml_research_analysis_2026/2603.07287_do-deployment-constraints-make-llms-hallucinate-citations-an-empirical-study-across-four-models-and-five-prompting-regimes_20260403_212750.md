# Do Deployment Constraints Make LLMs Hallucinate Citations? An Empirical Study across Four Models and Five Prompting Regimes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv: 2603.07287
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07287
- References: 23
- Primary result: LLMs generate many unverifiable citations under deployment-constraint prompts, with no model exceeding 47.5% verifiable rate and temporal constraints causing the steepest declines.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how deployment constraints—temporal limits, survey breadth, and non-disclosure policies—affect the quality of citations generated by large language models (LLMs) in academic writing. Evaluating four models (Claude Sonnet, GPT-4o, LLaMA 3.1-8B, Qwen 2.5-14B) across five prompting regimes on 144 claims, the authors find that citation verifiability plummets under constraints: no model achieved a existence rate above 47.5%, and temporal restrictions reduced rates most dramatically (e.g., Claude Sonnet from 38.1% to 11.9%). A three-way taxonomy (Existing, Unresolved, Fabricated) reveals that the majority of citations fall into the Unresolved category, which manual audits suggest mostly comprises fabricated references despite appearing plausible. Proprietary models consistently outperformed open-weight ones by a large margin, and combined constraints produced near-zero verifiability for most models. The results highlight a critical risk: LLMs maintain surface-level format compliance while generating substantively unreliable citations.

## Method Summary
The authors generated citations for 144 claim prompts across six domains (including software engineering and computer science) using four LLMs under five conditions: Baseline (5 citations per claim), Temporal (year-window restrictions), Survey (8 citations with categorical organization), Non-Disclosure (avoid claiming memorized access), and Combo (all constraints). Generation was deterministic (temperature=0) and closed-book (no retrieval). Citations were parsed into structured fields (title, authors, venue, year, DOI, URL) and verified via Crossref and Semantic Scholar. A weighted metadata similarity score (title 0.60, authors 0.20, year 0.15, venue 0.05) determined three-way labels: Existing (≥0.85), Fabricated (<0.60), Unresolved (0.60–0.85). Metrics were aggregated per condition, with confidence intervals computed via cluster bootstrap over the 144 claims. The study's replication package includes prompts, outputs, and analysis code.

## Key Results
- No model exceeded 47.5% citation existence rate; under temporal constraints, rates dropped as low as 11.9% (Claude Sonnet). Format compliance remained high (>95% parsing success).
- Unresolved citations dominated (36–61%), and a manual audit of 35 Unresolved samples found 16 were actually Fabricated, 4 were Existing, and only 15 remained truly unresolved.
- Proprietary models (Claude, GPT-4o) consistently outperformed open-weight ones (LLaMA, Qwen) by an average of 22.9 percentage points, a gap that widened under Survey prompting (+31.0 pp).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
*Mechanism 1: Constraints force generation from weaker memory traces.* Deployment constraints restrict the parametric knowledge distribution a model can draw upon, shifting generation toward unreliable, fabricated patterns. Evidence: Temporal constraints caused steep declines in existence rates (e.g., Claude −0.261) with minimal direct violations, indicating obedience but inability to produce verifiable references within the window. The model’s internal knowledge contains gaps; constraints amplify gap-filling heuristics (e.g., paraphrasing real titles) that yield well-formed but unverifiable citations.

*Mechanism 2: Proprietary–open-weight gap is structural.* Proprietary models consistently outperform open-weight ones, likely due to undisclosed data curation, scale, or fine-tuning. The gap persists across all constraint regimes and widens under Survey breadth (suggesting superior handling of breadth pressure). Evidence: Baseline existence rates: Claude 0.381 vs. Qwen 0.090; gap CI [0.191, 0.266].

*Mechanism 3: “Unresolved” is a high-risk reservoir.* The verification pipeline’s 0.60–0.85 score band captures citations that evade automated checks; manual audits reveal many are actually fabricated (16/35 sampled Unresolved were Fabricated). This indicates the pipeline’s similarity scoring under a 0.85 threshold lacks discriminative power, as fabricated citations can achieve moderate scores by mimicking real metadata fragments. Reclassifying using audit rates increases estimated fabrication to 0.33–0.75.

## Foundational Learning
- **Three-way citation verification taxonomy (Existing, Unresolved, Fabricated)**: Binary labels hide the risk of near-miss fabrications; Unresolved likely contains many spurious citations that pass partial similarity checks. Needed to assess true citation quality.
- **Deterministic closed-book citation generation**: Isolates constraint effects by removing retrieval augmentation; models scenarios where LLMs act as knowledge engines. Temperature=0 ensures reproducibility but may not capture stochastic hallucination modes.
- **Deployment constraints as experimental stressors**: Simulate real-world pressures (recent literature, breadth, compliance) that trigger different hallucination modes. Help disentangle constraint-specific effects.

## Architecture Onboarding
Claim → Prompt Templates → Deterministic Inference Engine → Citation Parser → Verification Pipeline → Aggregation & Bootstrap → Audit Interface → Metrics.

Critical path: A claim with condition-specific prompt is sent to the model; output is parsed into structured reference fields; these are looked up via Crossref/SS; similarity scores assign labels; per-claim fractions are aggregated and bootstrapped to produce CIs.

Design tradeoffs: Deterministic decoding (temp=0) ensures reproducibility but may miss stochastic hallucination modes. Thresholds (0.85/0.60) are fixed; changing them reclassifies citations and affects overall rates. Database coverage (Crossref + SS) may miss some venues, inflating Unresolved/Fabricated.

Failure signatures: High format compliance but low existence under Temporal; high Unresolved fraction under Combo; proprietary–open-weight gap widest under Survey; DOI completeness drops under Non-Disclosure.

First 3 experiments:
1. Baseline replication: Run all claims in Baseline condition; expect ~0.38 Existing for Claude, ~0.09 for Qwen, and ~0.46–0.56 Unresolved.
2. Temporal stress test: Apply Temporal condition; expect steep Existing drop (Claude to ~0.12) with negligible direct temporal violations.
3. Threshold sensitivity sweep: Vary Existing threshold (0.80–0.90) and Unresolved lower bound (0.55–0.65) on a stratified sample to assess stability of main conclusions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does varying the phrasing of the same claim affect citation hallucination rates across models and deployment constraints? The study used a single fixed phrasing; paraphrases may alter behavior.
2. To what extent does retrieval-augmented generation reduce citation hallucination under deployment constraints compared to closed-book settings? This was not evaluated.
3. How effective is an integrated, real-time verification pipeline at reducing the use of hallucinated citations in actual SE writing workflows? The pipeline was offline; practical utility in live drafting is unknown.
4. What proportion of LLM-generated citations that pass existence verification actually support the claims they are attached to? The authors did not check claim alignment.

## Limitations
- Verification depends on external scholarly databases (Crossref, Semantic Scholar) whose coverage and APIs may change, potentially affecting labels.
- Manual audit of Unresolved used a small sample (n=35), limiting precision of the estimated Fabricated proportion.
- Results apply only to the specific model versions and deterministic decoding; newer models or stochastic sampling might differ.

## Confidence
- **Proprietary models consistently outperform open-weight ones**: High
- **Deployment constraints drastically reduce citation verifiability**: High
- **Unresolved category hides many fabrications**: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Expand the manual audit of Unresolved citations to a stratified sample of ~200 across models and conditions to tighten confidence intervals on the Fabricated proportion.
2. Replicate the experiment with the latest model versions (e.g., GPT-4.5, Claude 3.5, LLaMA 3.2, Qwen 2.6) to test temporal robustness of the proprietary–open-weight gap and constraint effects.
3. Perform a threshold sensitivity analysis: vary similarity score cutoffs for Existing (0.80, 0.85, 0.90) and Unresolved lower bound (0.55, 0.60, 0.65) to evaluate stability of conclusions.
