# TACTIC for Navigating the Unknown: Tabular Anomaly deteCTion via In-Context inference

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.14171
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14171
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** A discriminative in-context model achieving SOTA on 38 ADBench datasets (avg. AUCROC 90.43% clean, 80.57% noisy), directly outputting calibrated anomaly probabilities without post-hoc thresholding.

## Executive Summary
TACTIC is a novel framework for tabular anomaly detection that uses in-context learning with a transformer backbone. Unlike prior score-based methods (e.g., TabPFN), it is pretrained as a discriminative predictor to directly output calibrated anomaly probabilities for query points conditioned on an unlabeled context set. It addresses the instability of thresholding and performance degradation in noisy contexts by pretraining on a mixture of synthetic priors—classification-based (TabPFN-style) and Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)-based—with explicit contamination of the context set. Evaluated on 38 ADBench datasets, TACTIC ranks first under both clean and noisy settings, demonstrating robustness across local, global, and cluster anomaly types.

## Method Summary
TACTIC follows a pretrain-then-infer paradigm without any dataset-specific tuning at inference. During pretraining, it is trained on vast quantities of synthetic tabular tasks generated from two priors: (1) a classification-based prior that creates multi-class data where subsets of classes are designated anomalies, and (2) a GMM-based prior where nominal data comes from a Gaussian mixture and anomalies are injected via local (inflated covariance), cluster (shifted means), or global (uniform support) mechanisms. Crucially, the context set for each synthetic task is contaminated with unlabeled anomalies (5–30% of nominal count) to build robustness. A transformer encoder processes the concatenated context and query tokens, and a decoder MLP produces two logits per query, converted to an anomaly probability via softmax. The model is trained with cross-entropy loss. At inference, a frozen pretrained model takes a new dataset's unlabeled context and query set, performing a single forward pass to yield calibrated probabilities for all queries.

## Key Results
*   Under clean contexts, TACTIC achieves an average AUCROC of 90.43% and F1-score of 65.52% across 38 ADBench datasets, ranking first.
*   Under noisy (contaminated) contexts, TACTIC-N (noisy-pretrained variant) achieves an average AUCROC of 80.57% and F1-score of 28.74%, also ranking first.
*   It demonstrates exceptional robustness to local anomalies (>99% AUCROC) and strong performance on global anomalies, while the GMM prior specifically aids in handling cluster anomalies.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** TACTIC directly outputs calibrated anomaly probabilities in a single forward pass by learning a discriminative mapping from context to query labels, eliminating the need for post-hoc thresholding.
*   **Mechanism:** A transformer encoder processes the context set (unlabeled features) and query points jointly via attention. A decoder MLP then produces a 2-dimensional output per query, transformed into a probability via softmax. The model is pretrained with cross-entropy loss on synthetic datasets where ground-truth anomaly labels are known.
*   **Core assumption:** The inductive biases from the synthetic pretraining prior enable the model to infer a decision boundary between nominal and anomalous points solely from unlabeled context structure, without requiring labels in the context at inference.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [abstract]: "In contrast to typical score-based approaches... the proposed model is trained as a discriminative predictor, enabling unambiguous anomaly decisions in a single forward pass."
    *   [section 2]: "TACTIC directly focuses on modeling the probability ˆpθ(y = anomaly | x, Dctx)... The prediction is conditioned on the features of x and the entire context Dctx."
    *   [corpus]: Neighboring works (e.g., ICLAD, ICAD-LLM) also explore in-context AD but often rely on score-based outputs or multi-stage processes, highlighting TACTIC's discriminative endpoint as a distinctive design choice.
*   **Break condition:** If the context distribution is extremely sparse or high-dimensional (d >> samples) such that no coherent density manifold can be attended to, the discriminative mapping may collapse to trivial probabilities.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Pretraining on a mixture of classification-based (TabPFN-style) and GMM-based synthetic priors with explicit anomaly injection provides complementary inductive biases that generalize across local, global, and cluster anomaly types.
*   **Mechanism:** Two data-generating processes create synthetic training tasks:
    *   *Type I (Classification-based):* Uses SCMs/BNNs to generate multi-class data; subsets of classes are labeled anomalies, creating mode-level shifts.
    *   *Type II (GMM-based):* Samples nominal data from a Gaussian mixture; anomalies are added via local (inflated covariance), cluster (shifted means), or global (uniform support) mechanisms.
    * These are mixed during pretraining, exposing the model to diverse anomaly generation mechanisms.
*   **Core assumption:** A finite mixture of these priors approximates the manifold of real-world anomaly structures; the model learns to condition its decision rule on the observed context's latent structure rather than overfitting to one anomaly type.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [section 3]: "We employ a mixture of synthetic priors, combining TabPFN-like generative processes with newly designed Gaussian mixture models augmented with specially designed anomalies."
    *   [section 4.5]: Ablation shows removing either prior degrades performance, with the classification-based prior having a stronger impact on robustness.
    *   [corpus]: Related work (e.g., TabADM, Diffusion-Scheduled DAEs) uses single generative mechanisms (diffusion); TACTIC's mixture prior is a response to heterogeneity in ADBench.
*   **Break condition:** If real-world anomalies arise from a generative process completely outside the support of the synthetic priors (e.g., adversarial perturbations not resembling any of the three types), performance may degrade significantly.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Context contamination during pretraining (injecting unlabeled anomalies into the context set) builds robustness to realistic noisy contexts by teaching the model to identify the nominal manifold despite corruption.
*   **Mechanism:** During synthetic dataset generation, the context set Dctx is constructed with a sampled anomaly rate (0.05–0.3 times nominal count). The model must still predict labels for query points, learning to ignore atypical context points when inferring normality.
*   **Core assumption:** Anomalies in the context are a minority and can be treated as latent variables; the model learns to approximate the posterior over nominal data distribution p(x_nominal | Dctx) even when Dctx contains outliers.
*   **Evidence anchors:**
    *   [section 3]: "Explicitly varying the proportion of anomalies in the context during pretraining encourages robustness and prevents collapse when anomalies are present in Dctx."
    *   [section 4.2 & 4.3]: TACTIC-N (noisy pretraining) substantially outperforms TACTIC-C (clean pretraining) under contaminated contexts, and degrades more gracefully as noise increases (Figure 6).
    *   [corpus]: The "noisy context" setting is acknowledged in ADBench-related work, but many baselines assume clean training data; TACTIC's explicit contamination pretraining addresses this gap.
*   **Break condition:** If the anomaly rate in the context exceeds the pretraining range (e.g., >30% of nominal count) or anomalies are not independent (e.g., clustered corruption), the model may misinterpret the nominal manifold.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: In-Context Learning (ICL)**
    *   *Why needed here:* TACTIC relies on a frozen pretrained transformer that adapts to any new tabular dataset at inference solely by attending to the provided context, avoiding per-dataset optimization. Understanding ICL is crucial to grasp why no fine-tuning is required.
    *   *Quick check question:* Given a context of unlabeled mixed data, what inductive biases allow the model to infer which points are anomalous without ever seeing anomaly labels during inference?

*   **Concept: Synthetic Prior Design for Anomaly Detection**
    *   *Why needed here:* The model's capabilities are entirely determined by the distribution p(D) used in pretraining. Knowing how the priors encode local/global/cluster anomalies explains TACTIC's strengths and limitations.
    *   *Quick check question:* How do the GMM-based mechanisms (inflated covariance, shifted means, uniform support) correspond to the three canonical anomaly types, and why might a classification-based prior help with cluster anomalies?

*   **Concept: Discriminative vs. Generative Modeling in AD**
    *   *Why needed here:* Traditional AD methods (e.g., density estimation) produce scores requiring thresholding; TACTIC's discriminative output is calibrated and decision-ready. This shift is central to its practical utility.
    *   *Quick check question:* Why does modeling p(anomaly | x, Dctx) directly lead to better F1 scores compared to ranking by p(x | Dctx) (as in uTabPFN), especially under class imbalance?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Input encoder -> Transformer backbone -> Decoder MLP -> Output (Softmax)

*   **Critical path:**
    Context tokens and query tokens are embedded and concatenated. The Transformer encoder processes the full sequence, with queries attending only to context tokens. The final hidden state of each query token is extracted and passed through the decoder MLP, which projects to 2 logits. A softmax over these logits yields the calibrated anomaly probability for that query point. The entire process for all queries is completed in a single forward pass.

*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   *Model capacity vs. speed:* 12 layers/4 heads balances expressivity and inference time. Larger models may capture more complex anomalies but increase compute.
    *   *Prior mixture ratio (0.3 GMM, 0.7 classification):* Empirically tuned; shifting toward GMM improves handling of synthetic anomalies but may hurt real-world generalization.
    *   *Context size:* Fixed during pretraining (likely 512–1024 tokens). Very large contexts may require truncation or hierarchical attention, which could lose information.

*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Calibration collapse:** Output probabilities cluster near 0 or 1 even when context is ambiguous → may indicate overconfidence from insufficiently diverse pretraining.
    *   **Context dependence:** Small changes in context composition (e.g., adding a few nominal points) cause large output swings → model has not learned a stable decision rule.
    *   **Anomaly type mismatch:** Performance drops sharply on an anomaly type not represented in the GMM prior (e.g., collective anomalies) or when cluster anomalies are highly overlapping with nominal clusters.

*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Clean vs. noisy context evaluation:** Run TACTIC-C and TACTIC-N on ADBench with 0% and dataset-native anomaly rates in context. Expect TACTIC-C to win clean, TACTIC-N to win noisy.
    2.  **Ablation of priors:** Retrain with only GMM prior and only classification prior; compare AUCROC/F1 on ADBench clean/noisy. Expect classification prior removal to hurt most.
    3.  **Synthetic anomaly type stress test:** Generate pure local/global/cluster anomaly datasets. Verify TACTIC achieves >99% AUCROC on local, strong on global, but lower on cluster.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Open Question 1:** What is the optimal mixture proportion of classification-based and GMM-based priors for pretraining TACTIC, and does this proportion vary across different anomaly detection scenarios?
*   **Open Question 2:** How robust is TACTIC to context contamination that is not comprised of anomalies, such as feature noise, missing values, or covariate shift?
*   **Open Question 3:** Does TACTIC maintain high detection performance when the query set contains extremely low anomaly prevalence (e.g., <0.1%), which is common in domains like fraud or network intrusion detection?
*   **Open Question 4:** Are the anomaly probabilities produced by TACTIC well-calibrated, especially when the context contains anomalies?

## Limitations
*   Synthetic prior specifications (GMM covariance, TabPFN generator version) are underspecified, hindering exact replication.
*   Evaluation splits and F1 thresholding (likely 0.5) are not thoroughly documented; deviations could alter reported metrics.
*   The model may degrade on anomaly types completely outside its synthetic prior support (e.g., adversarial, collective, or concept-drift anomalies).

## Confidence
*   Direct discriminative output: **Medium**
*   Mixture prior generalization: **Medium-High**
*   Contamination robustness (within 5–30% range): **Medium**
