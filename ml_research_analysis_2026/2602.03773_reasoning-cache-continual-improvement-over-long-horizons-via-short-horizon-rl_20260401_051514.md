# Reasoning Cache: Continual Improvement Over Long Horizons via Short-Horizon RL

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03773
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03773
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A 4B model trained with a 16k token budget using Reasoning Cache achieves 70% on HMMT 2025 with 512k test tokens, a 30-point gain over its 16k-token performance and outperforms larger specialized models.

## Executive Summary
Standard reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models (LLMs) fails to improve reasoning when test-time token budgets exceed the training horizon. Reasoning Cache (RC) solves this by iteratively generating reasoning traces, compressing each into a summary cache, and conditioning subsequent generation on that cache. Training uses outcome-reward RL coupled with a summary replay buffer to improve the quality of summary-conditioned generation. This enables a 4B model (RCT-4B) trained with a 16k budget to extrapolate performance gains over more than an order of magnitude longer test horizons (512k tokens), while maintaining constant computational throughput.

## Method Summary
RC introduces an iterative decoding loop: first, a reasoning trace is generated; second, a summary of that trace is produced and stored in a cache; third, the next generation step is conditioned on this cache rather than the full trace. During training, outcome-reward RL is applied not to the full long-horizon trace (which is infeasible) but to the summary-conditioned generation steps. A summary replay buffer stores past (summary, outcome) pairs to continually refine the summarization policy, preventing catastrophic forgetting of earlier summaries. This decouples training horizon from test horizon, allowing the model to improve indefinitely as it generates longer traces at test time.

## Key Results
- On HMMT 2025, RCT-4B (4B parameters, 16k training budget) improves from 40% at 16k test tokens to 70% at 512k test tokens, surpassing the base model with RC (60% at 512k) and the specialized Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507.
- On IMO-AnswerBench, accuracy rises from 41% to 58% at 256k tokens, outperforming larger models like Qwen3-30B.
- Transfer to FrontierScience yields a 24%→34% gain at 256k tokens, and RC decoding integrates more effectively with test-time scaffolds (RSA, DSM Agent).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Summary Cache Conditioning:** Compressing a long reasoning trace into a fixed-size summary prevents context window overflow and focuses subsequent steps on high-level progress, avoiding distraction from intermediate details that may be irrelevant or noisy.
- **Summary Replay Buffer:** Storing past summaries and their outcomes allows RL to update the summarization policy based on long-term reward signals, not just immediate token-level rewards. This prevents the summary quality from drifting or degenerating as horizons lengthen.
- **Outcome-Reward RL on Short Steps:** By training on the short step from summary to next trace segment (rather than the entire long trace), credit assignment remains tractable while still optimizing for the final outcome, enabling learning that scales with horizon length.
- **Bounded Context Through Iteration:** Each iteration only needs to attend to the current summary, not the full trace, so memory and compute per step remain constant regardless of total horizon, avoiding autoregressive slowdown.

## Foundational Learning
- **Outcome-Reinforcement Learning:** Why needed: Standard token-level RL fails for long-horizon reasoning; outcome-reward RL provides a scalar signal for the entire trace, allowing the summary policy to optimize for final correctness. Quick check: Train with only token-level rewards vs. final outcome reward on a medium-horizon task; outcome reward should yield better long-horizon generalization.
- **Summary Replay Buffer:** Why needed: Without a buffer, RL updates from recent summaries overwrite earlier learning, causing summary quality to collapse as horizon extends. Quick check: Ablate the replay buffer; performance on long horizons should degrade sharply while short-horizon performance remains similar.
- **Iterative Decoding with Cache:** Why needed: Standard autoregression cannot exceed context limits; iterative summarization creates a bounded loop. Quick check: Implement a non-iterative baseline that prepends full traces (where possible); throughput should degrade with horizon while RC remains constant.
- **Horizon Extrapolation:** Why needed: Real-world reasoning often requires many more steps than feasible for training. Quick check: Train with a fixed budget (e.g., 16k) and test at multiples (e.g., 32k, 64k, 128k); a successful method shows monotonic or improving accuracy with scale.
- **Scaffold Integration (RSA/DSM):** Why needed: External scaffolds provide additional reasoning structure; RC’s summary cache likely provides a cleaner interface for such tools. Quick check: Combine RC with a simple scaffold (e.g., chain-of-thought prompting) vs. standard RL; RC should show a larger performance delta, indicating better synergy.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map (Plain Text)**
Input Problem -> Trace Generator (initial) -> Summarizer -> Summary Cache -> Conditioned Generator -> [Next Trace Segment] -> (Repeat until solution)  
Training: (Summary, Outcome) -> Replay Buffer -> RL Update for Summarizer & Conditioned Generator

**Critical Path**
1. Problem is given.
2. Trace Generator produces initial reasoning segment.
3. Summarizer compresses trace into summary; summary stored in cache.
4. Conditioned Generator reads cache and produces next trace segment.
5. Loop from step 3 until answer is produced or horizon limit.
6. Final outcome (correct/incorrect) is computed.
7. (Summary, outcome) is added to replay buffer; RL updates parameters using buffer samples.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- Summary Fidelity vs. Compression: Shorter summaries save context but risk losing critical details; longer summaries approach full trace, losing bounded-context benefit.
- Summarization Cost: Calling theSummarizer repeatedly adds latency; assumes this cost is negligible compared to final long-horizon generation.
- Replay Buffer Size: Larger buffers preserve more historical summaries but increase memory and sampling overhead.

**Failure Signatures**
- Summary Degeneration: Summaries become generic or repeated, causing subsequent steps to loop or diverge.
- Cache Pollution: A single erroneous summary cascades, leading to incorrect final answers despite correct earlier traces.
- Replay Buffer Overfitting: The buffer overfits to a narrow set of summary patterns, failing on novel problem structures.

**First Three Experiments**
1. **Summary Quality Correlation:** Generate traces of varying lengths, compute summary embeddings (e.g., via SBERT) vs. full-trace embeddings, and correlate with final accuracy. Expect: higher fidelity correlates with higher accuracy, but beyond a point compression dominates.
2. **Replay Buffer Ablation:** Train RC with and without a replay buffer on HMMT; evaluate at 16k, 128k, 512k tokens. Expect buffer-less RC shows gains at 16k but collapses at 512k.
3. **Cross-Domain Transfer:** Take RCT-4B trained only on HMMT-like data, apply RC decoding to a held-out math benchmark (e.g., MATH). Test at 256k tokens. Expect moderate transfer gain (e.g., +10-15%) but lower than in-domain, indicating domain specificity.

## Limitations
- Evaluations are on a narrow set of challenging benchmarks (HMMT 2025, IMO-AnswerBench, FrontierScience); generalization to open-ended or non-mathematical reasoning is unproven.
- The summary mechanism risks information loss; the extent to which complex, multi-faceted reasoning is preserved is not quantified.
- Comparisons to baselines like Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 lack full specification of training data and prompt formats, reducing confidence in direct superiority claims.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** Empirical results on reported benchmarks (specific accuracy improvements at specific token budgets) are concrete and reproducible from the described setup.
- **Medium Confidence:** The claim that RC enables "extrapolation over reasoning horizons more than an order of magnitude beyond training" is supported by the 16k→512k jump but assumes linear or predictable generalization beyond tested ranges.
- **Low Confidence:** The assertion that RC integrates better with test-time scaffolds (RSA, DSM Agent) is based on combined performance numbers without isolating the integration mechanism; could be confounded by other factors.
- **High Confidence:** Ablation demonstrating the summary replay buffer's necessity for long-horizon gains is a strong causal signal.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the summary replay buffer ablation on at least two benchmarks (HMMT, IMO-AnswerBench) to confirm the buffer's causal role in long-horizon improvements.
2. Test RCT-4B with RC decoding on two novel held-out reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MATH, GPQA) to evaluate generalizability of the 512k-token extrapolation claim.
3. Conduct a summary content analysis: measure compression rate, semantic fidelity (e.g., via embedding similarity between summary and full trace), and correlate these metrics with final accuracy to diagnose failure modes.
