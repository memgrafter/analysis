# Sonny: Breaking the Compute Wall in Medium-Range Weather Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv: 2603.21284
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21284
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: Sonny achieves competitive medium-range forecasting skill with only 20.5M parameters, trains in 5.5 days on a single NVIDIA A40, and EMA reduces RMSE by an average of 3.34% (peak 4.75% at Day 3).

## Executive Summary
Sonny addresses the prohibitive compute costs of existing AI weather models by introducing a hierarchical transformer architecture tailored for medium-range forecasting. Its core innovation is the two-stage StepsNet, where a narrow slow path first models large-scale atmospheric dynamics (U, V, Z, P) in a compressed representation, followed by a full-width fast path that integrates thermodynamic variables (T, Q). Training incorporates a randomized dynamics objective (δt ∈ {6,12,24} h) and Exponential Moving Average (EMA) to stabilize long autoregressive rollouts without a separate fine-tuning stage. Evaluated on WeatherBench2, Sonny matches operational baselines with just 20.5M parameters, achieves a 3.34% average RMSE reduction (4.75% at Day 3) via EMA, and shows particular strength in tropical regions at extended lead times. The model trains in 5.5 days on a single A40 GPU and demonstrates accurate track and intensity predictions for Typhoon Nanmadol and Winter Storm Elliott at 5-day leads.

## Method Summary
Sonny was trained on the WeatherBench2 dataset at 1.5° resolution with 6-hourly timesteps. The training period is 1979–2018, with validation in 2019 and test in 2022. Inputs comprise 70 atmospheric channels: 5 surface variables (T2m, MSLP, SP, U10, V10) and 5 atmospheric variables (U, V, T, Q, Z) each on 13 pressure levels.

The architecture begins with a Variable-Aware Embedding that splits channels into a Dynamics group (U, V, Z, P) and a Thermodynamics group (T, Q). These are linearly projected to separate embedding dimensions (d₁ for Dynamics, d₂ for Thermo). The two-stage StepsNet then processes the Dynamics embeddings through a "slow path" of N₁ Transformer blocks (hidden dimension d₁) to capture large-scale dynamics. The slow path output is concatenated with the raw Thermodynamics embeddings (dim d₂) along the channel dimension, yielding a full-width tensor of dimension d = d₁ + d₂. This tensor passes through a "fast path" of N₂ Transformer blocks (hidden dimension d) to model cross-group interactions.

A randomized time interval δt (uniformly sampled from {6,12,24} hours) is embedded and injected via adaLN-Zero modulation in every Transformer block of both paths, enabling the model to directly generate forecasts over varying lead times during training. The final LayerNorm and linear layer predict the state change Δδt over the sampled interval; during iterative inference, δt is fixed to 6 hours and the model is rolled out autoregressively up to 10 days.

Training uses pressure-weighted (weight = pressure/1000) and latitude-weighted (cosine of latitude) L2 loss on Δδt. Optimization employs AdamW (lr=5e-4, β₁=0.9, β₂=0.95, weight decay=1e-5) with a batch size of 16 and mixed precision. An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the model weights with decay 0.9 is maintained throughout training and used for evaluation, avoiding a costly fine-tuning phase. The total parameter count is 20.5M. Training converges in approximately 5.5 days on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU, and inference latency measures 125.48 ms.

## Key Results
- On WeatherBench2, Sonny (20.5M parameters) achieves competitive RMSE and ACC against operational baselines across 10-day lead times.
- Applying EMA reduces average RMSE by 3.34% compared to the non-EMA variant, with a peak reduction of 4.75% at Day 3. Sonny with EMA shows a particular advantage over FastNet in tropical regions at extended lead times (beyond Day 4).
- The model is highly efficient: training completes in 5.5 days on a single NVIDIA A40, inference latency is 125.48 ms. Case studies on Typhoon Nanmadol (2022) and Winter Storm Elliott (2022) demonstrate accurate track and intensity predictions at 5-day lead times.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Mechanism 1**: Sonny's hierarchical two-stage StepsNet architecture decouples the modeling of large-scale atmospheric dynamics from thermodynamic interactions. A narrow "slow path" processes only Dynamics variables (U, V, Z, P) with reduced hidden dimension d₁, capturing the primary kinematic backbone efficiently. The resulting features are then fused with raw Thermodynamics embeddings (T, Q) and processed by a full-width "fast path" to model their nonlinear coupling. This split exploits the physical hierarchy where dynamics drive the large-scale structure that thermodynamics subsequently follow. Evidence: [abstract], [section 2.5], and comparisons to neighbor architectures that do not use variable-split hierarchical design. Break condition: If the Dynamics-only bottleneck loses critical information needed for thermodynamic coupling (e.g., leading to poor T850/Q skill), the hierarchical advantage diminishes.
- **Mechanism 2**: Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of model weights during training stabilizes long iterative rollouts by smoothing the optimization landscape, eliminating the need for a separate fine-tuning phase. EMA maintains a shadow copy θ_ema = α·θ_ema + (1−α)·θ with decay α=0.9, acting as implicit ensembling and regularization. This produces weights that lie in flatter minima and are more robust to error accumulation in autoregressive forecasting. Evidence: [abstract] and [section 2.4] describe the approach; [section 3.1] (Figure 2) quantifies a consistent 3.34% average RMSE reduction (4.75% at Day 3) for Sonny EMA over Sonny without EMA; references [18-20] support EMA theory. Break condition: The decay rate is sensitive; too high causes underfitting, too low yields insufficient stabilization.
- **Mechanism 3**: A randomized dynamics forecasting objective (δt sampled uniformly from {6,12,24} hours) forces the model to learn a general representation of atmospheric evolution rather than overfitting to a fixed 6-hour transition. By training on multiple time intervals, the network captures underlying continuous-time tendencies, enabling direct inference at any of these lead times without retraining. Core assumption: Atmospheric dynamics are approximately Markovian and consistent across sub-daily scales. Evidence: [abstract], [section 2.2] details the sampling; external papers (e.g., Scaling transformer neural networks) corroborate randomized interval training for large weather models. Break condition: If dynamics are significantly non-Markovian at the sampled intervals, or the model cannot disentangle interval-specific scaling, performance may degrade for non-training intervals.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:
1. **Input**: 6-hourly state X₀ (4 surface + 5 atmospheric vars on 13 levels) at 121×240 resolution. (Actual channel count is 70; see Method Summary.)
2. **Variable-Aware Embedding**: Splits channels into Dynamics (U, V, Z, P) and Thermodynamics (T, Q). Each group is independently linearly projected to its embedding dimension (d₁ for Dynamics, d₂ for Thermo).
3. **Step 1 (Slow Path)**: Dynamics embeddings are processed by N₁ Transformer blocks with hidden dimension d₁. Output y₁.
4. **Fusion**: y₁ is concatenated with the raw Thermodynamics embeddings E_thermo (dim d₂) along the channel dimension, yielding a tensor of dimension d = d₁ + d₂.
5. **Step 2 (Fast Path)**: The fused tensor is processed by N₂ Transformer blocks with hidden dimension d, capturing cross-group interactions.
6. **Randomized Conditioning**: The sampled time interval δt is embedded and injected via adaLN-Zero in every Transformer block of both steps (Step 1 uses projection to d₁, Step 2 to full d).
7. **Output Head**: Final LayerNorm followed by a linear layer produces the predicted state change Δδt (shape matching input channels). An unpatchify operation (if patching is used) reconstructs the full-resolution field; the model is applied autoregressively with δt=6h for multi-step forecasts.

**Critical path**: The Step 1 (Slow Path) is the primary compute bottleneck due to its depth (N₁ blocks) operating on the Dynamics-only stream. Its narrow channel width (d₁) keeps FLOPs manageable. The full-width Step 2, while shorter (N₂ blocks), is more expensive per block but overall less dominant.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Narrow slow path vs. Information Bottleneck*: Reducing d₁ saves compute but risks losing critical dynamical information needed for accurate thermodynamic coupling.
- *Variable Split vs. Physical Realism*: The hard split into Dynamics/Thermodynamics is physically motivated but may discard early cross-variable couplings (e.g., latent heat release from condensation immediately affecting dynamics).
- *Randomized δt vs. Training Stability*: Training on multiple intervals improves flexibility but complicates the loss landscape, requiring careful balancing (e.g., per-variable loss weighting already used).

**Failure signatures**:
- **Tropical degradation**: If the Dynamics-only path fails to capture moist-convective drivers, skill in T850/Q drops beyond day 4 in the Tropics (as seen in FastNet's failure).
- **Rapid error growth at long leads**: Without proper EMA (or with wrong decay), RMSE curves steepen after day 5–7, indicating instability in iterative rollout.
- **Over-smoothing of synoptic features**: Blurry or missing small-scale intense systems (e.g., hurricane eyes).

**First experiments**:
1. **Single-step overfit**: Train on a small subset (few batches) to verify the model can achieve near-zero loss, confirming gradient flow and model capacity.
2. **EMA impact test**: Train two models for a few epochs with and without EMA; compare validation RMSE to confirm EMA stabilizes training as claimed.
3. **Variable grouping sanity**: Swap a key variable (e.g., move T2m from Thermodynamics to Dynamics) and evaluate performance; a significant drop would indicate the chosen split's importance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1 (Multi-path uncertainty)**: How can multi-path inference from randomized time intervals be used to estimate forecast uncertainty in Sonny? The authors propose this in Section 4 but it remains unimplemented. Evidence needed: Implement multi-path sampling during rollout, compute ensemble statistics (spread-skill, reliability), and validate calibration against reference ensembles.
- **Open Question 2 (Coupled Earth system)**: Does integrating land-surface and oceanic variables improve forecast robustness? The paper lists this as future work. Evidence needed: Extend inputs with land (soil moisture, temperature) and ocean (SST, salinity) fields, retrain Sonny, and evaluate changes in RMSE/ACC, especially for surface variables and tropical cyclone intensity.
- **Open Question 3 (High-resolution scaling)**: How does Sonny's efficiency-accuracy trade-off hold at higher spatial resolutions (e.g., operational ~0.25°)? Only 1.5° results are reported. Evidence needed: Train and evaluate Sonny at multiple resolutions (0.25°, 0.5°) on WeatherBench2 or similar datasets, measuring convergence time, memory usage, and skill relative to baselines like Fengwu or Pangu-Weather.
- **Open Question 4 (Optimal variable grouping)**: Is the variable-aware split into Dynamics (U,V,Z,P) and Thermodynamics (T,Q) optimal? No ablation studies compare alternative groupings. Evidence needed: Train Sonny variants with different partitions, compare RMSE/ACC, training stability, and FLOPs.
- **Open Question 5 (EMA mechanism)**: What is the mechanistic reason behind EMA's effectiveness in stabilizing medium-range rollouts without fine-tuning? The improvement is empirical; underlying dynamics (e.g., gradient smoothing, chaotic divergence mitigation) are unknown. Evidence needed: Analyze training dynamics (weight trajectory smoothness, gradient norms), vary decay rates, and correlate EMA-induced changes with stability metrics (error doubling time, Lyapunov exponents) in long rollouts.

## Limitations
- **Exact variable grouping ambiguous**: The specific assignment of surface variables to Dynamics or Thermodynamics (the meaning of “P”) is not clearly defined, preventing faithful replication.
- **Hyperparameter gaps**: Key architectural details (patch size, depths N₁/N₂, dimensions d₁/d₂, MLP ratio, positional embedding type) and training specifics (LR warmup/decay schedule, gradient clipping, data augmentation) are missing, requiring guesses that may deviate from the original.
- **Loss weighting unclear**: The pressure and latitude weighting formulas are incompletely described (e.g., exact normalization), risking significant performance differences if implemented incorrectly.
