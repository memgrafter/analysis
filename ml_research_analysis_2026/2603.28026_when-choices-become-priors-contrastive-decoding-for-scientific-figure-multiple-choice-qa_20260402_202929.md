# When Choices Become Priors: Contrastive Decoding for Scientific Figure Multiple-Choice QA

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28026
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28026
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** SCICON, a training-free contrastive decoding method, consistently improves accuracy on scientific figure multiple-choice QA by suppressing choice-induced text priors (e.g., +0.91% to +6.73% absolute gains over greedy decoding across three benchmarks).

## Executive Summary
Scientific figure multiple-choice QA is uniquely vulnerable to **choice-induced prior bias**, where the semantic plausibility of answer text itself steers models away from figure-grounded reasoning. This paper introduces **SCICON**, a simple yet effective training-free decoding strategy that explicitly discounts this bias. For each candidate answer, SCICON computes two scores: one from the full multimodal input (figure + question + choices) and another from a text-only input (question + choices). The final score subtracts a scaled text-only logit from the multimodal logit, forcing the model to rely on visual evidence not already captured by the textual prior. Evaluated on MAC, SciFIBench, and MMSci with Qwen 4B/9B and Phi-3.5 backbones, SCICON yields consistent accuracy improvements over greedy decoding and prior contrastive methods (VCD, ICD). Analysis confirms gains stem primarily from recovering gold answers when visual evidence contradicts a misleading text prior, with minimal computational overhead.

## Method Summary
SCICON is a training-free inference-time intervention for multiple-choice QA with vision-language models. Given an image, a question, and a set of candidate answers \( C = \{c_1, ..., c_K\} \), the method scores each candidate \( c \) via:
\[
l_{sc}(c) = l_{mm}(c) - \alpha \cdot l_{txt}(c)
\]
where \( l_{mm}(c) \) is the logit from the full multimodal forward pass (image + question + choices) and \( l_{txt}(c) \) is the logit from a text-only forward pass (question + choices only, with image tokens discarded). The hyperparameter \( \alpha \) (default 0.5) scales the subtraction strength. The candidate with the highest \( l_{sc} \) is selected. This asymmetric contrast explicitly removes preference stemming from the answer text alone, promoting decisions based on additional visual evidence. The method requires only one extra text-only forward pass per example, avoiding the cost of a second image-processing pass used in prior contrastive decoding schemes.

## Key Results
- SCICON improves accuracy over greedy decoding on all three benchmarks: MAC (Qwen-9B: 82.26% vs. 81.35%), SciFIBench (Qwen-9B: 58.00% vs. 55.10%), and MMSci (Qwen-9B: 52.14% vs. 46.54%).
- Gains are larger on Phi-3.5-vision-instruct (e.g., +6.73% on MAC) than on Qwen models, suggesting greater susceptibility to choice priors in some architectures.
- SCICON outperforms Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) and Instruction Contrastive Decoding (ICD) in most settings, while being more computationally efficient.
- Error analysis shows improvements primarily come from **corrected cases** where the gold answer was initially ranked lower due to a strong distractor prior but possesses a positive visual evidence margin (\( l_{mm}(y^*) - l_{txt}(y^*) \)).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Explicit Prior Subtraction via Dual Forward Passes
**Claim:** SCICON reduces choice-induced prior bias by computing a contrastive score that subtracts the text-only answer preference from the image-conditioned answer score.  
**Mechanism:** The method performs two forward passes per candidate: one with the full multimodal input yielding logits \( l_{mm}(c) \), and one with text-only input yielding \( l_{txt}(c) \). The final score is \( l_{sc}(c) = l_{mm}(c) - \alpha \cdot l_{txt}(c) \). This explicitly discounts any candidate preference already present in the text-only distribution, forcing reliance on image evidence.  
**Core assumption:** The text-only forward pass \( p_{txt}(c|q,C) \) accurately captures the choice-induced prior—the model’s inherent preference for certain options based solely on question and choice text.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "scores each candidate by subtracting a text-only option score from its image-conditioned counterpart."  
- [section 3.3] Formal definition of \( l_{mm} \), \( l_{txt} \), and scoring rule.  
- [corpus] Neighboring work (e.g., Balepur et al. 2024) discusses "choices-only" bias in MCQA, supporting the premise that answer text alone can drive predictions.

### Mechanism 2: Gold-Answer Recovery via Visual Evidence Amplification
**Claim:** SCICON’s primary effect is to recover correct answers \( y^* \) that were incorrectly ranked lower than distractors due to a strong misleading text prior, by amplifying the relative advantage provided by visual evidence.  
**Mechanism:** When the correct answer has a higher \( l_{mm}(y^*) - l_{txt}(y^*) \) (visual evidence margin) than competing distractors, subtracting \( \alpha \cdot l_{txt}(c) \) reduces the score of distractors more if they have higher \( l_{txt} \) values. This increases the rank of \( y^* \) if its visual evidence margin is sufficiently positive.  
**Core assumption:** In failure cases, the correct answer’s multimodal logit \( l_{mm}(y^*) \) is already higher than its text-only logit \( l_{txt}(y^*) \) by a meaningful margin, but the distractor’s multimodal logit is inflated by an even higher text-only logit.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [section 5.1] Table 5 shows corrected cases have large positive "Gold uplift" and "Visual evidence margin" for the gold answer, while harmed cases have near-zero or negative margins.  
- [section 3.2] Table 1 shows correct predictions have higher Jensen-Shannon divergence and lower cosine similarity between \( p_{mm} \) and \( p_{txt} \), indicating greater departure from the text prior.  
- [section 5.2] Table 6 shows corrected cases have very low "Text-prior gold hit" (text-only branch rarely ranks gold first), while harmed cases have high "Text-prior gold hit" and high "Prior alignment".

### Mechanism 3: Inference-Time Efficiency via Asymmetric Contrast
**Claim:** SCICON provides a more computationally efficient contrastive decoding approach compared to VCD and ICD by requiring only one additional text-only forward pass instead of a second full multimodal pass.  
**Mechanism:** The contrast is asymmetric: the multimodal branch \( l_{mm} \) uses the full input (image tokens + text), while the contrasting branch \( l_{txt} \) discards only the visual tokens, reusing the text prompt. This avoids the cost of processing a second image (even a degraded one) and the associated visual token computations.  
**Core assumption:** The text-only forward pass \( p_{txt} \) is a sufficiently accurate and cheaper proxy for the choice-induced prior, and the key signal for debiasing comes from contrasting against this text-only distribution, not from contrasting two different image states.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [section B (Inference-Time Complexity Analysis)] Theoretical cost analysis showing \( C_{SCICON} = O((L_q + L_v)^2 d + L_q^2 d) \) vs. \( C_{VCD/ICD} \approx O(2(L_q + L_v)^2 d) \), since \( L_q^2 < (L_q + L_v)^2 \) for \( L_v > 0 \).  
- [conclusion] "SCICON offers a more computationally efficient alternative... by requiring only one additional text-only prefill pass instead of a second multimodal pass."

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Choice-Induced Prior Bias**  
  *Why needed:* Understanding this core failure mode is prerequisite to motivating SCICON’s design. It refers to the phenomenon where the semantic plausibility and domain-specific phrasing of answer choices themselves act as a prior, leading models to select options that are textually reasonable but not visually supported by the figure.  
  *Quick check:* If a model consistently picks an answer because "it sounds like a valid scientific statement" even when the figure contradicts it, what specific bias is occurring?  
- **Concept: Contrastive Decoding (General Form)**  
  *Why needed:* SCICON is an instance of a broader class of inference-time methods that adjust output distributions by contrasting scores from different input conditions. Understanding the general pattern \( score = original\_score + \alpha \cdot (original\_score - contrastive\_score) \) is key to comparing SCICON to VCD/ICD.  
  *Quick check:* In the standard contrastive decoding formula \( s(y) = l(y) + \alpha \cdot (l(y) - l'(y)) \), what does the term \( (l(y) - l'(y)) \) represent?  
- **Concept: Scientific Figure QA Specificity**  
  *Why needed:* The nature of scientific figures (trends, multi-panel layouts, symbolic notation) and their MCQA setting (with expert-level distractors) makes them uniquely vulnerable to this bias compared to general-domain VQA. This context explains why a text-prior subtraction is particularly valuable here.  
  *Quick check:* Why might "scientifically plausible phrasing" in choices be a stronger prior in a biology figure QA task than in a photo-based "what is this object?" QA task?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input Processor → Dual Forward Pass Engine → Contrastive Scorer → Decision Module → Coefficient Manager  
- **Critical path:** Input → Text-Only Prompt → Forward Pass 1 (\( l_{txt} \)) **AND** Full Multimodal Prompt + Figure → Forward Pass 2 (\( l_{mm} \)) → Contrastive Subtraction → Argmax → Prediction.  
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Simplicity vs. Optimality:* Fixed linear subtraction is simple and training-free but uses a single global \( \alpha \) instead of a dynamic, per-example weight.  
  - *Efficiency vs. Contrast Strength:* Text-only branch is cheaper than a second multimodal pass with distorted image/instruction but may provide a less targeted contrast for non-choice-related hallucinations.  
  - *Generality vs. Specialization:* Pattern extends to MCQA with fixed candidates; open-ended generation requires different formulation.  
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **Over-subtraction:** Using large \( \alpha \) when \( l_{txt}(y^*) \) is high and \( l_{mm}(y^*) - l_{txt}(y^*) \) is low/negative.  
  - **Incorrect logit extraction:** Flawed scoring pipeline yields near-identical \( l_{mm} \) and \( l_{txt} \) distributions (low JS divergence).  
  - **Weak visual evidence:** When gold answer has little visual evidence margin, subtraction removes useful signal.  
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Replication & Baseline Comparison:** Implement SCICON (\( \alpha=0.5 \)) on one benchmark (e.g., MAC) with one backbone (Qwen-3.5-4B). Verify it outperforms greedy decoding and matches paper’s main result.  
  2. **Ablation on \( \alpha \):** Sweep \( \alpha \) (0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9) on the same setup. Confirm performance stability across a range.  
  3. **Error Analysis Partition:** Classify test examples into "corrected" (baseline wrong, SCICON correct) and "harmed" (baseline correct, SCICON wrong). Compute per-group averages of "Visual evidence margin" and "Text-prior gold hit". Replicate the pattern: corrected cases should show high visual margin and low text-prior gold hit; harmed cases the opposite.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Dynamic \( \alpha \) adaptation:** How can the subtraction weight be adapted per example or dataset to match the varying degree of choice-induced prior bias?  
- **Extension to generative QA:** Does the contrastive decoding principle extend effectively to open-ended generative scientific QA without predefined candidates?  
- **Performance vs. number of choices:** How does SCICON perform across varying numbers of answer choices (K), especially given MMSci’s highly variable candidate counts (2 to 26)?  
- **Model-specific variability:** What underlying model properties (scale, architecture, pre-training data) determine the magnitude of SCICON’s improvement, and why are gains larger on Phi-3.5 than on Qwen?  
- **Predicting failure modes:** Under what conditions does subtracting the text-only prior harm performance, and can these failure modes be predicted a priori to avoid incorrect corrections?

## Limitations
- **Prompt/template ambiguity:** Exact multimodal prompt format and per-candidate scoring procedure for Qwen-VL and Phi-3.5 are not fully specified, risking inconsistent reproduction.  
- **Baseline implementation gaps:** Hyperparameters for VCD/ICD (e.g., distortion strength) are inherited from other papers and may not be optimally adapted to scientific figure QA.  
- **Text-only prior assumption:** SCICON assumes \( p_{txt}(c|q,C) \) accurately captures choice-induced bias; if the text-only branch is already accurate or visual evidence is weak, subtraction can harm performance.

## Confidence
- **Accuracy improvements:** High – consistent gains across three benchmarks and two model families, with statistical significance typically reported.  
- **Mitigation of choice-prior bias:** Medium – supported by distribution divergence (Jensen-Shannon) and lower cosine similarity for corrected cases, but relies on the text-only branch as a perfect prior proxy.  
- **Gold-answer recovery via visual margin:** Medium-High – strong correlation in analysis tables: corrected cases show large positive visual evidence margin; harmed cases show the opposite.  
- **Inference-time efficiency:** High – asymmetric contrast clearly avoids a second image pass; provided complexity analysis is sound.

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt template verification:** For a single example, compute \( l_{mm}(c) \) and \( l_{txt}(c) \) using both a plausible template (e.g., separating `<image>`) and a naive template. Expect meaningful distributional difference (JS divergence >0.1); if not, the scoring pipeline is flawed.  
2. **Baseline replication of VCD/ICD:** Using original VCD/ICD distortion parameters (e.g., Gaussian blur sigma=1.0 for VCD), re-implement these baselines on one benchmark (e.g., MAC). Confirm SCICON’s reported margins remain statistically significant.  
3. **Ablation on visual evidence margin:** Split the test set by the gold answer’s \( l_{mm} - l_{txt} \) margin (top vs. bottom quartile). Measure SCICON’s accuracy delta relative to greedy; expect negligible or negative gain in the low-margin group, confirming the boundary condition.
