# Multimodal Survival Analysis with Locally Deployable Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22158  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22158  
- **Reference count:** 33  
- **Primary result:** Best configuration (late fusion, calibration correction, CoxPH) achieves combined time-dependent concordance 0.775 and integrated Brier score 0.126 on TCGA.

## Executive Summary
This work tackles multimodal survival analysis under computational and privacy constraints that preclude cloud-based LLMs. It proposes distilling a compact, locally deployable LLM from a larger teacher model, jointly learning calibrated survival curves and verbalized prognosis text. Multimodal fusion integrates pathology reports, tabular covariates, and gene expression. On the TCGA cohort, the approach outperforms BERTSurv and unimodal baselines, achieving a time-dependent concordance of 0.775 and an integrated Brier score of 0.126 in its best configuration. The framework enables local deployment while reducing hallucinations and miscalibration risks inherent in base LLMs.

## Method Summary
The method employs a teacher-student distillation framework. A large teacher LLM (DeepSeek-R1 Distill Qwen-32B) is prompted to produce numeric survival probabilities at 1/3/5 years and an explanation. A compact student LLM (DeepSeek-R1 Distill Qwen-1.5B) is trained to replicate these outputs while directly predicting survival via a dedicated head. Inputs are processed through separate branches: pathology reports (LLM encoder yielding z_text), tabular covariates (min-max scaled and one-hot encoded), and gene expression (autoencoder yielding z_ge). Two fusion strategies are explored: early concatenation of all modality embeddings, or late gated heads that weight modality-specific projections. Survival heads support either discrete-time hazards (30 bins) or Cox proportional hazards. The total loss combines survival head loss, gene autoencoder reconstruction loss, and text generation loss with upweighted numeric spans. An optional calibration correction masks inconsistent teacher text. At test time, the survival curve from the head and the verbalized probability are blended using a λ tuned on validation.

## Key Results
- Outperforms BERTSurv and unimodal baselines on the TCGA cohort.
- Best configuration (late fusion, calibration correction, CoxPH) yields C<sub>td</sub>=0.775 and IBS=0.126.
- Framework enables local deployment and reduces hallucinations/miscalibration compared to the base teacher LLM.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Distillation transfers complex pattern recognition from the large teacher to the small student, preserving performance while meeting local deployment constraints. Multimodal fusion (early or late) flexibly integrates heterogeneous data: text from pathology reports, structured covariates, and high-dimensional gene expression. Calibration correction filters out inconsistent teacher text, improving reliability. The blending of the survival head output with the verbalized probability leverages the discrimination of the former and the calibration tendencies of the latter, with λ balancing the trade-off. Joint training on survival and text objectives encourages consistency between numeric predictions and natural language descriptions.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
x_text (pathology + patient snippet) -> LLM encoder -> z_text  
x_ge (gene expression) -> Gene Autoencoder -> z_ge  
x_cov (tabulated covariates) -> Covariate Processor -> h_cov  

Fusion strategies:  
- Early: concat(z_text, h_cov, z_ge) -> Fused -> Survival Head (discrete/Cox) & Text Generation Head  
- Late: z_text -> T_proj; h_cov -> C_proj; z_ge -> G_proj -> concatenated projections -> Gating Network -> weighted sum -> Survival Head; Text Generation uses LLM decoder (may condition on fused survival representation)  

Teacher model: generates target numeric probabilities (via prompt) and target text.  

Training losses: L_surv + α L_AE + β L_text (with span/number upweighting). Optional calibration correction masks inconsistent teacher text. Inference: blend S(t) and S_v(t) with λ.

### Critical Path
1. **Data preprocessing:** Extract modalities from TCGA, split (70/10/20), scale/code, train gene autoencoder.  
2. **Teacher labeling:** Prompt teacher LLM to obtain numeric probs and explanations for training samples.  
3. **Student training:** Forward pass through student, compute total loss, backpropagate for frozen LLM (last 18 layers unfrozen) and trained heads.  
4. **Validation:** Tune λ for blending, possibly α/β, and select fusion/head type.  
5. **Test evaluation:** Report C<sub>td</sub> and IBS, qualitative text analysis.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Early vs Late Fusion:** Early fusion forces a single representation, potentially losing modality-specific nuances; late fusion allows specialized processing but increases parameters and may suffer from misaligned scales.  
- **Discrete-time vs CoxPH:** Discrete-time offers flexibility at cost of many parameters; CoxPH is efficient but relies on proportional hazards assumption.  
- **Calibration Correction:** Adds preprocessing complexity but removes noisy text supervision.  
- **Hyperparameters (α, β, λ):** Require validation; imbalanced weighting can destabilize training.  
- **Computational cost:** Student LLM still non-trivial; autoencoder and survival heads add overhead.

### Failure Signatures
- Incoherent or missing «VPROB»‑delimited probability in generated text.  
- Teacher’s numeric probabilities poorly calibrated (high Brier score, reliability diagram).  
- Sudden degradation in C<sub>td</sub> on specific cancer types or high-censorship subsets.  
- Training divergence when α or β exceed certain thresholds.  
- Blending λ on validation fails to improve test calibration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How stable are the performance metrics (C<sub>td</sub> and IBS) across different random splits and seeds?
- How does the calibration of the teacher model compare to that of the student model, and how does the blending strategy affect calibration?
- Does the proposed framework generalize to independent cancer cohorts beyond TCGA?

## Limitations
- Exact teacher prompt templates are omitted, affecting reproducibility of numeric probability extraction.  
- Format of the patient‑information snippet appended to pathology reports is unspecified.  
- Evaluation restricted to a single dataset (TCGA); no external validation.  
- Qualitative assessment of text fidelity; no quantitative hallucination metrics.  
- Potential sensitivity to random seeds and hyperparameter choices.
