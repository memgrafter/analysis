# Personalized Multi-Agent Average Reward TD-Learning via Joint Linear Approximation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02426
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02426
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A single-timescale algorithm (PMAAR-TD) for cooperative multi-agent TD learning in heterogeneous environments achieves O(1/√(TK)) convergence with linear speedup in agents K and local steps L, and outperforms baselines on Acrobot and CartPole with strong heterogeneity.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces PMAAR-TD, a single-timescale algorithm for cooperative multi-agent temporal difference learning under average reward in heterogeneous environments. Agents share a low-dimensional linear subspace for their value functions but maintain agent-specific heads to capture local dynamics. The method uses projected updates and QR decomposition to maintain orthonormality of the shared subspace and filter misaligned signals. Theoretically, it achieves a convergence rate of O(1/√(TK)) with linear speedup in the number of agents K and local steps L under Markovian sampling. Empirically, on Acrobot and CartPole with engineered heterogeneity (e.g., mirrored actions), PMAAR-TD converges faster and to higher rewards than single-agent TD, uniform federated TD, and two-timescale baselines, with lower variance across random seeds.

## Method Summary
PMAAR-TD has each agent maintain a local estimate of a shared orthonormal basis matrix B (defining a low-dimensional linear subspace) and an agent-specific weight vector ωₖ. At each step, agents compute TD errors and gradients from local transitions. The update for B is performed via a projected gradient step, followed by QR decomposition to re-orthonormalize B and thus filter out signals misaligned with the current subspace. The agent-specific heads ωₖ are updated via local projected gradients. All updates occur at the same timescale (single-timescale), avoiding the need to tune multiple step sizes. This joint estimation allows the shared representation to be informed by all agents while permitting personalization to each agent’s dynamics.

## Key Results
- Convergence rate O(1/√(TK)) with linear speedup in number of agents K and local steps L.
- Outperforms single-agent TD, uniform federated TD, and two-timescale baselines on Acrobot and CartPole with strong heterogeneity (mirrored actions).
- Achieves lower variance across random seeds compared to baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method succeeds by separating shared representation learning (the subspace B) from personalization (the heads ωₖ). The orthonormality constraint on B, enforced via QR decomposition after each update, ensures the subspace remains well-conditioned and that gradients are only integrated along directions consistent with the current basis. This filters out misaligned signals that could destabilize learning. The single-timescale update simplifies implementation and avoids the slowest eigenvalue problem of two-timescale methods. Pooling data across agents to learn B reduces sample complexity, while agent-specific ωₖ adapt to local transition dynamics or reward structures, yielding both efficiency and adaptability.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Agents (K) → Local environment interactions → Compute TD errors and gradients → Joint update: 
  - Update shared subspace B via projected gradient + QR re-orthonormalization.
  - Update agent-specific ωₖ via local projected gradient.
Repeat.

### Critical Path
The critical path is the joint update of B and ωₖ at each time step. The orthonormalization of B via QR decomposition is a key computational step that ensures the subspace remains valid and filters misaligned signals, directly impacting convergence.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Single-timescale vs two-timescale:** Single-timescale simplifies implementation but may require more careful step size tuning; two-timescale can separate learning rates but introduces an extra hyperparameter and may suffer from slowest eigenvalue issues.
- **Orthonormality constraint:** Enables theoretical guarantees and stable learning but adds computational overhead (QR decomposition each step).
- **Personalization:** Allows adaptation to local dynamics but requires maintaining agent-specific parameters; may reduce sharing benefits if agents are too dissimilar.

### Failure Signatures
- Performance collapse when linear realizability fails (true value function not in linear span of features).
- Increased variance or divergence when Markovian mixing is slow or features are unbounded.
- Excessive computation time when shared subspace dimension d is large due to QR decomposition cost.

### First Experiments
1. **Toy Heterogeneous MDP:** Implement a two-agent MDP with mirrored actions to verify that B captures common structure and ωₖ adapts to mirroring.
2. **Baseline Ablation:** Compare PMAAR-TD against independent per-agent TD and uniform federated TD (no personalization) on a small problem to isolate the effect of joint subspace learning and personalization.
3. **Orthonormality Ablation:** Disable QR re-orthonormalization (update B without projection) and measure performance degradation to confirm the importance of orthonormality.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely on strong assumptions (Markovian sampling, bounded features/rewards, linear subspace realizability, positive definite covariance) whose validity in complex, non-stationary, or high-dimensional real-world tasks is unknown.
- Empirical evaluation is confined to two classic control tasks (Acrobot, CartPole) with simple engineered heterogeneity (mirrored actions); performance on more complex domains or with diverse heterogeneity sources is not demonstrated.
- The computational overhead of QR decomposition at each step, especially in high-dimensional subspace settings, is not analyzed relative to the reported speedup.
