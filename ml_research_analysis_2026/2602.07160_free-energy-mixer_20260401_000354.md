# Free Energy Mixer
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.07160
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07160
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: FEM introduces a variational free-energy read that enables per-channel value-aware selection from a lossless KV cache, achieving state-of-the-art improvements on MAD (80.2% avg, +3.8), Open LLM Leaderboard (avg ranks 2.06/1.81 with multiple top-1), ImageNet-1K (76.70% top-1), and time-series benchmarks while matching parameter budgets and incurring minimal overhead.

## Executive Summary
The paper identifies a fundamental limitation in standard attention: the convex averaging of values across tokens prevents per-channel selection, blocking even simple tasks like per-channel argmax. The proposed Free Energy Mixer (FEM) resolves this by treating the selection prior (from softmax, linear attention, RNNs, or SSMs) as a distribution and applying a value-driven, per-channel log-linear tilt to obtain a posterior via a variational free-energy read. This mechanism introduces cross-token competition that is sensitive to per-channel values. Linearized temperature learning (LTL) further allows dynamic entropy control without recomputing expensive log-sum-exp operations, preserving the asymptotic complexity of the underlying prior. Combined with a two-level gated structure and optional low-rank convolution for local conditioning, FEM seamlessly integrates into existing architectures. Extensive experiments across language modeling, vision, and time-series forecasting show consistent gains at matched parameter budgets, with notable improvements on the MAD benchmark (80.2% average) and competitive results on the Open LLM Leaderboard and ImageNet-1K.

## Method Summary
FEM replaces the read operation of an attention module with a variational free-energy computation. Given a prior selection distribution \(p_t(i)\) (e.g., from masked softmax), FEM computes two auxiliary quantities per channel: the prior mean \(\mu_t = \sum_i p_t(i) v_{i,j}\) and a high-temperature branch \(F^{\text{max}}_t = \frac{1}{\beta_{\text{max}}} \log \sum_i p_t(i) \exp(\beta_{\text{max}} v_{i,j})\). A learned temperature gate \(\lambda_t \in [0,1]\) interpolates between them: \(\tilde{eF}_t = (1-\lambda_t)\odot \mu_t + \lambda_t \odot F^{\text{max}}_t\). An outer positive gate \(g_t\) then scales the output: \(o_t = g_t \odot \tilde{eF}_t\). The interpolation effectively realizes the free energy \(F(\beta)\) for some \(\beta\) hidden within \([0,\beta_{\text{max}}]\), allowing per-channel entropy control without explicit \(\beta\) computation. An optional time-decay conditioner (TDC), a low-rank causal convolution, modulates the prior parameters and the two gates, providing local context sensitivity while adding only \(O(T H_c)\) overhead. The overall complexity remains identical to the underlying prior: \(O(T^2)\) for softmax attention, \(O(T)\) for linear/SSM-based priors.

## Key Results
- **MAD benchmark**: FEM-SM achieves 80.2% average score, exceeding the previous best by 3.8 points.
- **Language models**: At 1.3B and 340M parameter scales, FEM attains average ranks of 2.06 and 1.81 on the Open LLM Leaderboard, with multiple top-1 placements.
- **Image classification**: On ImageNet-1K, FEM reaches 76.70% top-1 accuracy with fewer parameters than comparable models.
- **Time-series**: FEM matches or improves MSE scores across multiple forecasting datasets.
- **Efficiency**: Gains are realized at matched parameter budgets with minimal computational overhead compared to the prior.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core innovation of FEM is a variational reparameterization of the attention read that converts the convex averaging limitation into a value-aware posterior selection. For each output token \(t\) and channel \(j\), the prior \(p_t(i)\) is tilted by the value evidence \(v_{i,j}\) to produce a posterior \(q_t(i) \propto p_t(i) \exp(\beta v_{i,j})\). By Donsker–Varadhan duality, maximizing expected value under a KL budget \(\beta\) yields the free energy \(F(\beta) = \frac{1}{\beta} \log \sum_i p_t(i) \exp(\beta v_{i,j})\). As \(\beta\) increases, \(q_t\) concentrates on indices with high \(v_{i,j}\), enabling per-channel argmax—something impossible with a simple convex combination. This mechanism introduces cross-token competition that is sensitive to the actual values, breaking the channel-synchronization barrier.

The linearized temperature learning (LTL) trick makes the mechanism practical. Rather than computing \(F(\beta)\) for varying \(\beta\), FEM pre-computes two branches: the prior mean \(\mu_t\) (effectively \(F(0)\)) and a high-temperature branch \(F^{\text{max}}_t\) at a fixed \(\beta_{\text{max}}\). A learned gate \(\lambda_t\) interpolates between them, and due to the monotonicity of \(F(\beta)\), any \(\lambda_t\) corresponds uniquely to some effective \(\beta_{\text{hid}}\). This avoids repeated log-sum-exp evaluations, preserving the asymptotic time and memory cost of the prior. The outer multiplicative gate \(g_t\) further modulates the output magnitude, while a lightweight time-decay conditioner (TDC) injects position-specific adaptations into \(p_t\), \(\lambda_t\), and \(g_t\) with negligible overhead \(O(T H_c)\).

Empirical evidence confirms the design: synthetic argmax tasks achieve near-perfect per-channel index accuracy; ablations on the MAD benchmark show that both the LSE mixing (+L) and temperature learning (+T) contribute significantly, especially on compression and fuzzy recall subsets; and large-scale language experiments demonstrate consistent gains without extra compute.

## Foundational Learning
- **Causal masked softmax attention**
  - *Why needed*: FEM's prior is defined relative to a masked selection distribution; understanding convex averaging reveals the channel-synchronization bottleneck that FEM overcomes.
  - *Quick check*: “What set of values can a single attention head produce from \(\{v_i\}\)? (Answer: any convex combination; per-channel argmax is generally outside that convex hull.)”
- **Donsker–Varadhan (DV) variational representation**
  - *Why needed*: FEM’s free energy \(\frac{1}{\beta}\log \sum_i p(i)\exp(\beta f)\) stems from the dual of KL-constrained reward maximization, linking selection to an information budget.
  - *Quick check*: “State the dual form of the KL-constrained reward maximization that yields FEM; what role does \(\beta\) play?”
- **Log-sum-exp trick and numerical stability**
  - *Why needed*: Computing \(F = \frac{1}{\beta}\log \sum_i p(i)\exp(\beta v)\) stably requires subtracting a per-channel maximum to avoid overflow; gradients then yield the posterior \(q\).
  - *Quick check*: “Given \(v_{\text{max}}\) and \(\sum \exp\), how to compute \(F\) stably and obtain \(\partial F/\partial v\)?”

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Prior selector → LTL module → Temperature gate λ_t → Outer gate g_t → Output (with Time-Decay Conditioner feeding context to prior, λ_t, and g_t)

### Critical path
Input → Compute prior selection \(p_t\) (from any masked scores) + Value bank \(V\) → one-pass LSE to get \(\mu_t\) and \(F^{\text{max}}_t\) → derive λ_t and g_t from context features → \(\tilde{eF}_t = (1-\lambda_t)\odot \mu_t + \lambda_t \odot F^{\text{max}}_t\) → \(o_t = g_t \odot \tilde{eF}_t\) → Output. Complexity matches the prior: \(O(T^2)\) for softmax; \(O(T)\) for linear/SSM.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Value width vs prior width**: With fixed parameter budget, allocate hidden dimensions between value path and prior; common splits: value dimension \(d = D/2\) with prior expansion ratio \(r=4\), or \(d = 2D/3\) with \(r=2\).
- **β_max choice**: Must be large enough to achieve desired sparsity but small enough to avoid overflow; typically initialized via \(\text{softplus}(0) \approx 1\) and learned.
- **Convolution rank \(H_c\)**: Balances local adaptivity vs. overhead; default \(H_c = d/16\).
- **Gating saturation**: If λ_t collapses to 0 or 1, FEM degenerates to mean or hard selection; initialize λ MLP with zero bias and consider temperature annealing.

### Failure signatures
- **Unstable training**: λ_t or g_t explode, LSE overflow. Monitor β_max, use per-channel max subtraction inside LSE, clip gradients.
- **No gain on standard LM**: FEM collapses if channels are already aligned; inspect λ_t statistics—mean near zero indicates unnecessary capacity.
- **Memory blow-up**: The LSE branch materializes \(\exp(\beta_{\text{max}} v)\); for large \(d\) and long \(T\) memory scales with \(T \cdot d\). Use micro-batching, checkpointing, or mixed precision.
- **Poor generalization from TDC**: Local conditioner may overfit training context lengths; evaluate with longer sequences.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Toy channel-wise argmax**: Synthetic data with \(T=128, D=512\), margin \(\Delta=1\), Gaussian noise \(\sigma=0.05\). Train a single-layer Transformer where FEM replaces attention (no outer gate/TDC). Expect near 100% per-channel index accuracy for FEM vs ~0.8% random baseline.
2. **MAD benchmark ablation**: Using official MAD code, disable modules (C/L/T/G) to isolate contributions of LSE mixing (+L) and temperature (+T), focusing on compression and fuzzy recall subsets.
3. **Small-scale LM parity check**: Train a 340M parameter decoder-only model (e.g., 24 layers, hidden 1024) with FEM-SM. Use paper hyperparameters (AdamW lr=4e-4, cosine decay, weight decay 0.1, gradient clip 1.0, context 4096). Compare perplexity and downstream task accuracy (ARC, HellaSwag) against a matched Transformer baseline on the same data and context length. Monitor λ_t distribution to ensure it is not collapsed.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Scalability to very large models and long contexts**: It is unknown whether FEM's value-driven posterior selection and numerical stability remain favorable at scales beyond 10B parameters and context lengths >100k tokens, due to limited computational resources in the study.
- **Representational limits of Linearized Temperature Learning (LTL)**: LTL approximates temperature control by interpolating two branches; it is unclear whether this restricts flexibility compared to explicit per-channel temperature optimization or richer parameterizations.
- **Cross-channel dependencies**: FEM assumes channel-wise independence in the posterior; for tasks requiring inter-channel coordination this might hinder performance. Exploring coupled gating could be beneficial.
- **Memory/compute overhead at extreme lengths**: The per-channel LSE adds \(O(DT)\) work which may become significant for very long sequences; algorithmic optimizations (e.g., approximate LSE) might be needed to preserve throughput.

## Limitations
- **Code and hyperparameter opacity**: The repository link is not provided, leaving exact implementations, training schedules, and evaluation scripts for MAD and LLM benchmarks inaccessible, which hinders faithful replication.
- **Task scope of benefits**: FEM’s advantages are demonstrated on tasks needing per-channel selection, but the boundary conditions (e.g., when channels are highly correlated or the prior is already optimal) are not fully characterized.
- **Practical memory overhead**: Although asymptotically matched, the high-temperature LSE branch materializes \(\exp(\beta_{\text{max}} v)\), which can strain memory for large hidden dimensions and long contexts; stability at scale remains to be fully validated.
