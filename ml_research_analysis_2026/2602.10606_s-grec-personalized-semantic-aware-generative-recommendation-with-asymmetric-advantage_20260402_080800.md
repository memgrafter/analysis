# S-GRec: Personalized Semantic-Aware Generative Recommendation with Asymmetric Advantage

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.10606
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10606
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: S-GRec achieves +1.19% GMV and +1.55% GMV-Normal in large-scale A/B tests, with no real-time LLM inference; sparse sampling (5% data) retains >99% performance.

## Executive Summary
Generative recommendation using LLMs faces three key challenges: weak behavioral supervision, misalignment with business objectives, and prohibitive serving costs. This paper introduces S-GRec, a framework that decouples a lightweight online generator from an offline LLM-based Personalized Semantic Judge (PSJ). PSJ produces interpretable aspect scores and learns user-conditional aggregation from pairwise feedback to yield stable semantic rewards. These semantic rewards are fused with business rewards (e.g., eCPM) via Asymmetric Advantage Policy Optimization (A2PO), which activates semantic signals only when they are directionally consistent with business goals. Large-scale A/B tests on an e-commerce platform show statistically significant GMV gains (+1.19% GMV, +1.55% GMV-Normal) while eliminating real-time LLM costs. Furthermore, a sparse sampling strategy using only 5% of the data for PSJ annotations retains over 99% of the full-performance.

## Method Summary
S-GRec comprises an offline Personalized Semantic Judge (PSJ) and an online generator. PSJ leverages an LLM to compute interpretable aspect scores for candidate items and refines them into semantic rewards by learning user-conditional aggregation from pairwise feedback, thereby providing dense supervision aligned with user preferences. The online generator is a lightweight model trained via policy gradient. The training objective uses Asymmetric Advantage Policy Optimization (A2PO), which combines the semantic reward from PSJ with a business reward (e.g., eCPM) at the advantage level. A key mechanism is directional consistency gating: the semantic component of the advantage is only included when its gradient aligns with that of the business reward, preventing business metric degradation. To reduce PSJ inference costs, S-GRec employs sparse sampling, annotating only a small fraction (5%) of the data with PSJ while maintaining high generator performance.

## Key Results
- +1.19% GMV and +1.55% GMV-Normal increase in large-scale A/B tests.
- No real-time LLM inference required; only a lightweight generator is served.
- Sparse sampling with 5% of data yields >99% of the performance achieved with full data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
S-GRec succeeds by decoupling expensive LLM-based semantic evaluation from real-time serving. The PSJ delivers stable, interpretable semantic rewards through user-conditional aggregation, addressing weak supervision. A2PO’s asymmetric gating ensures semantic improvements contribute only when they do not conflict with business objectives, preserving revenue alignment. This design avoids real-time LLM costs while harnessing their offline semantic understanding, and the fusion mechanism safeguards business metrics.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Offline PSJ → semantic rewards → A2PO (fuses with business rewards) → Online Generator (lightweight). Business reward (e.g., eCPM) computed from logs or online signals.
- **Critical Path**: PSJ processes offline data to produce semantic rewards; during training, A2PO combines these with business rewards to compute advantage; generator updates via policy gradient; at serving, only the generator runs.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Decoupling enables cost-effective serving but introduces distribution shift between PSJ and generator; asymmetric gating protects business metrics but may suppress useful semantic signals when business reward is noisy; sparse sampling reduces cost but risks underrepresenting rare aspects.
- **Failure Signatures**: GMV degradation despite semantic improvements indicates gating failure; performance drop when sampling strategy mismatches data distribution; generator overfitting to PSJ biases.
- **First Experiments**:
  1. Validate PSJ aspect scores against human judgments on a held-out set.
  2. Run A/B test with A2PO gating disabled to isolate effect on business deviation (GMV vs. long-term engagement).
  3. Evaluate generator performance across sparse sampling ratios (1%, 5%, 10%, 50%) and strategies (random, importance sampling based on PSJ confidence).

## Limitations
- **Generalizability**: Results are from a single e-commerce platform; applicability to other domains (news, video) is unverified.
- **Sparse Sampling Transparency**: The specific 5% sampling strategy and its robustness to distribution shifts are unclear.
- **Business Alignment Assumption**: Directional consistency gating assumes eCPM is a reliable proxy for long-term value, which may not hold if user satisfaction diverges from immediate revenue.

## Confidence
- Online Gains (+1.19% GMV): High
- Sparse Sampling Efficiency: Medium
- Asymmetric Advantage & Business Safety: Medium-Low
