# WaveFormer: Wavelet Embedding Transformer for Biomedical Signals

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12189
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12189
- **Reference count:** 24
- **Primary result:** WaveFormer achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on 7 of 8 biomedical signal classification datasets, with the largest improvement (+8.0 percentage points) on the longest sequence (MotorImagery, 3000 timesteps) and a mean accuracy drop of 3.1pp when its wavelet-enhanced embedding is ablated.

## Executive Summary
WaveFormer is a transformer architecture designed for biomedical signal classification that explicitly incorporates frequency-domain knowledge through Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT). It introduces two novel components: a wavelet-enhanced embedding that fuses raw temporal patches with multi-channel DWT coefficients, and a Dynamic Wavelet Positional Encoding (DyWPE) that generates signal-aware positional embeddings via mono-channel DWT and dynamic modulation. Evaluated on eight diverse datasets (sequence lengths 50–3000, channels 1–144), WaveFormer attained the highest test accuracy on seven datasets. Ablation studies confirm the wavelet embedding as the largest contributor to performance (+3.1pp), followed by DyWPE (+2.6pp) and relative positional bias (+1.8pp). Accuracy gains positively correlate with sequence length (r=0.741), demonstrating the method's particular strength on longer recordings.

## Method Summary
WaveFormer processes a multivariate time series by first applying a 1D convolution to extract non-overlapping patches, converting the raw sequence of length \(L\) into \(N = L/p\) tokens. Each token is formed by fusing two parallel representations: (A) a raw patch capturing local temporal patterns, and (B) a wavelet-derived feature obtained by applying multi-channel DWT to each input channel, combining approximation and detail coefficients, and projecting to the same dimension. This dual-path embedding explicitly encodes multi-scale frequency information. Positional information is added via DyWPE: the input channels are projected to a mono-channel summary, decomposed via DWT into multiple scales, modulated by learned gates per scale, and reconstructed via inverse DWT to produce a positional signal that is then projected to token space. The resulting sequence is processed by a standard transformer encoder with T5-style bucketed relative positional bias. A classification head uses the [CLS] token to predict class logits. The design targets biomedical signals whose discriminative features often span multiple frequency bands (e.g., EEG rhythms), making explicit wavelet integration beneficial.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art accuracy on 7 of 8 UEA biomedical datasets.
- Ablation: Removing wavelet-enhanced embedding reduces mean accuracy by 3.1 percentage points; removing DyWPE reduces by 2.6pp; removing relative positional bias reduces by 1.8pp.
- Accuracy gain relative to baselines strongly correlates with sequence length (r = 0.741), with the largest gain (+8.0pp) on the MotorImagery dataset (3000 timesteps).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Explicitly fusing raw time-domain patches with multi-channel DWT coefficients during embedding construction creates tokens that encode multi-scale frequency patterns, complementing local temporal patterns and improving feature representation for classification.
- **Mechanism:** The input signal undergoes two parallel paths: (A) standard 1D convolution on raw segments yields patches capturing local temporal morphology; (B) multi-channel DWT independently decomposes each channel into approximation (low-frequency trends) and detail (high-frequency rhythms) coefficients, which are linearly combined. These wavelet-derived features are concatenated with raw patches along the embedding dimension, producing hybrid tokens.
- **Core assumption:** Biomedical signals contain discriminative information distributed across distinct frequency bands (e.g., EEG rhythms), and explicitly representing these bands during token creation is more effective than expecting later attention layers to implicitly discover them from raw amplitudes alone.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "multi-channel Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) extracts frequency features to create tokens containing both time-domain and frequency-domain information."
  - Section 3.2: Equations (1)-(4) detail the DWT application and fusion; states that raw patches capture local temporal patterns and fusion produces "dual-path feature representation."
  - Neighbor papers (e.g., PhysioWave, DyWPE) confirm a trend of integrating wavelets into transformers, but WaveFormer's specific dual-path embedding fusion is novel.
- **Break condition:** If the signal’s discriminative features are exclusively low-frequency with minimal high-frequency detail, the detail coefficient branch may add noise or redundant parameters without performance gain, potentially degrading efficiency.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Generating positional encodings from the signal itself via mono-channel DWT and dynamic gating (Dynamic Wavelet Positional Encoding, DyWPE) creates position embeddings that are sensitive to local temporal structure and non-stationarities, providing more meaningful positional bias than fixed or learned index-based encodings.
- **Mechanism:** The input channels are projected to a mono-channel summary. This summary is decomposed via J-level DWT into multi-scale coefficients. A gating mechanism (Eq. 7) modulates these coefficients using learned embeddings tied to the scale and a learned channel projection weight. The modulated multi-scale coefficients are reconstructed via inverse DWT to produce a full-length positional encoding vector \(P_{DyWPE}\), which is added to the token embeddings.
- **Core assumption:** The optimal "meaning" of a position depends on local frequency content (e.g., a position in a high-frequency segment vs. a low-frequency segment should have different semantic context); therefore, positional information should adapt to the signal.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "mono-channel DWT with dynamic modulation generates signal-aware position embeddings."
  - Section 3.3: Details channel projection, DWT decomposition, dynamic gating, and IDWT reconstruction; explicitly states the goal is to generate "position embeddings that reflect local frequency content."
  - Strong anchor: The DyWPE paper (arXiv:2509.14640) is direct prior work by the same authors.
- **Break condition:** If the signal is perfectly stationary with no local variation in frequency content (e.g., pure sine wave or white noise), the dynamic modulation would produce outputs nearly identical to a fixed encoding, nullifying its adaptive benefit while adding computational overhead.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Augmenting the attention scores with relative positional biases computed via bucketed learned parameters (Relative Positional Encoding, RPE) complements DyWPE by efficiently capturing fine-grained local temporal dependencies within a configurable range, enhancing the model's sensitivity to short-term order.
- **Mechanism:** For each attention head, a small set of learned bias vectors \(b_{ij}\) is indexed by a bucketed relative distance \(\text{bucket}(j-i)\) between token positions. This bias is added to the raw attention score \(QK^T / \sqrt{d_k}\) before softmax. Bucketing groups large relative distances logarithmically to maintain parameter efficiency.
- **Core assumption:** While DyWPE provides a signal-aware notion of absolute/contextual position, the precise relative offset between two tokens (e.g., "5 steps ago" vs "10 steps ago") carries distinct information for modeling local dynamics, and this relationship can be effectively modeled with a compact, shared-parameter scheme.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: Mentions the architecture is "augmented with relative positional encoding in the attention mechanism."
  - Section 3.4: Describes the bucketing formula (Eq. 13) and states: "We adopt T5-style bucketing RPE... as it provides the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off." Ablation shows its individual contribution (1.8pp drop when removed).
  - Bucketing RPE is a known technique (referenced as T5-style) and used in other models (ConvTran).
- **Break condition:** If the task relies exclusively on very long-range, global dependencies and is insensitive to precise local ordering (e.g., classification based on a single global trend), the fine-grained bucketing parameters may not be utilized effectively, making RPE's contribution negligible compared to its added parameter count.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT)
- **Why needed here:** DWT is the core mathematical operation enabling multi-scale frequency decomposition. Understanding approximation vs. detail coefficients, downsampling, and the choice of wavelet family (e.g., Daubechies) is essential to grasp why and how frequency features are extracted.
- **Quick check question:** What is the conceptual difference between the approximation coefficients (cA) and detail coefficients (cD) at level J in a multi-level DWT of a biomedical signal?

### Concept: Self-Attention and Positional Encoding in Transformers
- **Why needed here:** The entire WaveFormer architecture is built on the transformer encoder. Understanding the permutation-invariance of self-attention and the necessity of positional information is fundamental to seeing why both DyWPE and RPE are needed.
- **Quick check question:** Why can't a standard transformer model sequential order from raw time series data without an explicit positional encoding mechanism?

### Concept: Patch-Based Tokenization for Time Series
- **Why needed here:** WaveFormer uses a convolution-based patchify step to convert the long 1D sequence into a shorter sequence of N tokens. This design choice directly impacts the sequence length fed to attention, influencing computational cost and the scale of patterns each token represents.
- **Quick check question:** How does increasing the patch size p affect the number of tokens N and the temporal resolution of the input representation to the transformer?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
1. **Input:** Multivariate time series X shape [L, C].
2. **Patching (Conv1D):** 1D convolution with kernel size=p, stride=p. Output shape [N, d/2] where N = L/p.
3. **Wavelet Path (Multi-channel DWT):** Per-channel DWT → cA and cD → linear combination cA + α*cD → Conv1D projection → shape [N, d/2].
4. **Fusion:** Concatenate Raw Patches & Wavelet Path → shape [N, d].
5. **Dynamic Wavelet Positional Encoding (DyWPE):** Channel projection → mono-channel signal → J-level DWT → dynamic gating per scale → IDWT → shape [N, d].
6. **Final Embedding:** Sum of Fusion output and DyWPE → shape [N, d]. (Prepended class token → shape [N+1, d]).
7. **Transformer Encoder:** L layers of Multi-Head Self-Attention (with Bucketing RPE) + Feed-Forward Network, each with residual connections & LayerNorm.
8. **Classification Head:** Extract [CLS] token → two-layer MLP (with GELU & Dropout) → K-class logits.

### Critical path
The minimal execution path for a forward pass: Input → Patching → (Parallel: Raw Conv & Multi-Channel DWT Path) → Fusion → DyWPE → Sum → Transformer Encoder (MHSA+RPE + FFN repeated L times) → [CLS] extraction → MLP Head → Output. The wavelet computations (steps 3 & 5) are the core novel branches inserted into the standard transformer flow.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Wavelet Level (J):** Higher J provides finer frequency resolution but reduces the length of coefficient vectors (downsampling), potentially losing temporal precision in the positional encoding. Paper uses a fixed level without tuning.
- **Patch Size (p):** Larger p reduces sequence length N (lower compute) but each token aggregates more raw timesteps, potentially blurring fine temporal patterns. Smaller p preserves resolution but increases attention cost O(N^2). WaveFormer inherits this classic trade-off.
- **Embedding Dimension (d):** Larger d increases model capacity and cost. The split d/2 for raw vs. wavelet paths is a fixed architectural decision; making this split learnable could be an extension.
- **DyWPE Complexity:** The J-level DWT/IDWT adds overhead. The gating mechanism introduces parameters W_g, W_v, e per scale. The paper claims this is lightweight compared to attention.

### Failure signatures
1. On short sequences (<~500 timesteps): The performance advantage over baselines diminishes or reverses (per Fig 4b). Likely cause: insufficient signal length for meaningful multi-scale wavelet decomposition; overhead of wavelet branches hurts more than helps.
2. On single-channel, high-frequency periodic signals: If the wavelet basis is poorly matched to the dominant periodicity (e.g., using a low-iteration Daubechies for a very high-frequency rhythm), the extracted coefficients may not align with discriminative bands, reducing the embedding fusion benefit.
3. Training instability with high dropout or aggressive gating: The dynamic gating in DyWPE (Eq. 7) uses tanh and σ. If initialized poorly, gradients could vanish/explode, especially with the ⊗ (element-wise product with c'). Monitor training loss curves for sudden spikes.
4. High-dimensional, short sequences (e.g., FaceDetection: 144 channels, 62 steps): The multi-channel DWT runs independently per channel. With few timesteps, wavelet decomposition yields very few coefficients, making the wavelet path less informative. Paper shows negative gain (-0.48pp) on this dataset.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Ablation: Remove Wavelet-Enhanced Embedding.** Replace dual-path embedding with standard single-path (raw only) patching. Expected: Significant accuracy drop (~3.1pp mean), confirming the core contribution of explicit frequency features in tokens.
2. **Ablation: Remove DyWPE.** Replace DyWPE with a standard learned absolute positional embedding of same dimension. Keep all else (including RPE). Expected: Moderate accuracy drop (~2.6pp), demonstrating the value of signal-aware positioning.
3. **Sequence Length Correlation Replication.** Train full WaveFormer and best baseline (e.g., ConvTran) on datasets of varying lengths (including created subsets of long sequences). Plot accuracy difference vs. sequence length. Expected: Replication of positive correlation (r ~0.74), with gains growing for L > 1000. This tests the hypothesis that multi-scale decomposition's value scales with temporal structure.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do choices of wavelet family (e.g., Haar, Daubechies) and decomposition levels affect WaveFormer's performance across different biomedical signal types?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The methodology uses a fixed Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) implementation without exploring hyperparameters of the wavelet transform itself (e.g., mother wavelet, decomposition depth J). The paper states that DWT "naturally decomposes signals" but does not investigate whether optimal wavelet parameters are dataset-specific.
- **Why unresolved:** The ablation studies isolate architectural components (embedding, DyWPE, RPE) but do not vary the underlying wavelet parameters. Performance may be sensitive to these choices, especially given the diversity of signals (EEG, HAR, Audio) with different spectral characteristics.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments varying wavelet family and decomposition levels on a subset of datasets, measuring accuracy and computational cost. Analysis of which wavelet properties (e.g., regularity, support length) correlate with performance for specific signal modalities.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the minimum sequence length threshold at which wavelet-enhanced embeddings provide a statistically significant advantage over raw-patch embeddings?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Figure 4b shows a positive correlation (r=0.741) between sequence length and WaveFormer's accuracy gap over baselines, with largest gains on sequences >1000 timesteps. The paper notes that shorter sequences (<500 timesteps) show "minimal gains."
- **Why unresolved:** While the correlation is established, the exact threshold where wavelet features become beneficial is not quantified. The FaceDetection dataset (62 steps, 144 channels) showed a negative gain (-0.48pp), suggesting other factors (channel count) interact with length.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Fine-grained analysis on datasets or synthetic sequences spanning a continuous range of lengths (e.g., 50–2000 steps) to identify the breakpoint where wavelet embedding consistently outperforms raw patches. Statistical testing (e.g., piecewise regression) to confirm the threshold.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the assumption of channel independence in multi-channel DWT (via grouped convolution) limit performance for signals with strong inter-channel frequency couplings?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The wavelet-enhanced embedding applies DWT independently per channel (equations 1–3), treating channels as separate univariate signals. The introduction acknowledges "complex inter-channel dependencies" in biomedical signals, but the embedding does not model cross-channel frequency interactions.
- **Why unresolved:** The success on multivariate datasets suggests independence may be sufficient, but the paper does not analyze whether joint wavelet decomposition (e.g., multichannel wavelet packets) could capture cross-channel spectral relationships. The moderate improvement on high-channel datasets (vs. sequence length) hints at potential ceiling effects.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare the current grouped DWT approach against architectures that jointly decompose multi-channel signals (e.g., multidimensional wavelet transforms or cross-channel attention in the embedding path) and measure performance change on datasets with known inter-channel frequency coupling (e.g., EEG with coherence across regions).

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How does Dynamic Wavelet Positional Encoding (DyWPE) compare to alternative signal-aware positional encodings (e.g., Fourier-based, data-driven learned) in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] DyWPE is a core contribution, but the paper only compares it to standard learnable positional encoding and relative positional bias (RPE) in ablation studies. It does not benchmark against other signal-aware methods likeLearned Positional Encoding from signal statistics or Fourier-based approaches.
- **Why unresolved:** The relative positional encoding (RPE) is adopted from prior work (T5-style bucketing) and combined with DyWPE, but DyWPE's unique contribution beyond RPE is not isolated against other content-aware schemes. The paper claims DyWPE is "signal-aware" but does not demonstrate its superiority over, for example, a CNN-based positional encoding or attention with Fourier bias.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement alternative signal-aware positional encodings (e.g., using short-time Fourier transform, learned linear projections of local windows) within WaveFormer's framework and compare accuracy, training stability, and inference speed across the eight datasets. Ablation that removes DyWPE but adds another signal-aware method would isolate its specific value.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** To what extent does WaveFormer's frequency-domain processing generalize beyond biomedical signals to other long-sequence time series domains (e.g., finance, IoT sensor networks)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The abstract states WaveFormer provides "a principled framework for incorporating frequency-domain knowledge into transformer-based time series classification," implying general applicability. However, all experimental datasets are biomedical (EEG, HAR, heartbeat).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not test on non-biomedical datasets, so the generalization claim remains speculative. Biomedical signals have known multi-scale frequency patterns (e.g., neural rhythms), but other domains may have different spectral characteristics (e.g., volatility clusters in finance) where wavelet benefits may differ.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluation on standard non-biomedical multivariate time series benchmarks (e.g., from UCI repository, forecast datasets like Electricity, Traffic) to measure accuracy gains and analyze if the sequence-length correlation holds. Failure cases would indicate domain-specific assumptions in the wavelet integration.

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters (patch size, DWT level J, wavelet family, training schedule) are not reported, hindering exact replication.
- Architectural ambiguities (handling of length mismatch in multi-channel DWT, precise DyWPE gating mechanism) risk implementation divergence.
- Performance gains are limited to sufficiently long sequences (>~500 timesteps) and may degrade on high-dimensional, short recordings (e.g., FaceDetection: 144 channels, 62 steps).

## Confidence
- **Wavelet-Enhanced Embedding:** Medium (strong ablation evidence but unreported wavelet choices and conditional performance).
- **Dynamic Wavelet Positional Encoding (DyWPE):** Medium-High (prior work anchors method, ablation shows significant contribution; slight uncertainty due to unspecified gating details).
- **Relative Positional Bias:** Medium (standard technique, incremental gain, but contribution is smallest and not deeply justified).
