# Broken Chains: The Cost of Incomplete Reasoning in LLMs
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.14444
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14444
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: Token budget truncation can actively degrade reasoning performance; code-based reasoning is more robust than natural language, hybrid underperforms, and robustness varies greatly across models.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how the format of reasoning (code, natural language, hybrid, or none) and the amount of tokens allocated to reasoning affect the performance of four frontier LLMs on mathematical benchmarks. By systematically truncating reasoning traces to 10%, 30%, 50%, and 70% of an optimal token budget, the authors uncover several counterintuitive findings: (1) truncated chain-of-thought can hurt accuracy compared to having no explicit reasoning at all; (2) code-based reasoning degrades more gracefully than natural language comments under tight budgets; (3) mixing code and comments underperforms single-modality reasoning; and (4) model-to-model differences are stark, with Grok maintaining high accuracy even at 30% budget while other models collapse. The study highlights the fragility of explicit reasoning and suggests that both modality choice and token budgeting critically determine the robustness of LLM reasoning.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluated GPT-5.1, Gemini 3 Flash, DeepSeek-V3.2, and Grok 4.1 on AIME, GSM8K, and HMMT. They defined five reasoning conditions enforced via system prompts: `code-only` (reason solely in executable code), `comments-only` (reason solely in natural language comments), `both` (hybrid), `nothing` (direct answer without explicit CoT), and `standard CoT`. For each model-dataset-condition pair, they first measured the optimal token count `T_opt` by allowing unconstrained generation. They then re-ran the test set with hard token limits set to 10%, 30%, 50%, and 70% of `T_opt`. Accuracy was computed by exact match of the final answer, with code outputs parsed from execution results. This factorial design enabled comparison of how reasoning format and truncation severity interact.

## Key Results
- Truncated CoT can actively harm performance: DeepSeek-V3.2 achieved 53% accuracy with no reasoning but only 17% with truncated CoT at a 50% token budget.
- Code-based reasoning is more robust than natural language comments: at a 30% budget, Gemini’s comments-only accuracy dropped to 0% while code-only maintained 43–47%.
- Hybrid reasoning underperforms single-modality approaches, and model robustness diverges sharply: Grok retained 80–90% accuracy at 30% budget across conditions, whereas OpenAI and DeepSeek models fell to 7–27%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Truncated chain-of-thought (CoT) can actively harm performance compared to having no explicit reasoning trace at all.
**Mechanism:** When a model's reasoning chain is cut short due to a token budget, it may be left in an inconsistent intermediate state—with variables declared but not resolved, or premises established but not concluded. Forced to produce a final answer from this state, the model must either hallucinate a completion contradicting its partial reasoning or guess, both degrading accuracy. The "nothing" condition bypasses this failure mode by allowing a direct, unburdened answer.
**Core assumption:** The model's internal state during generation is path-dependent; an incomplete reasoning trajectory leaves it in a misaligned state for final answer generation.
**Evidence anchors:** 
- The DeepSeek-V3.2 result (53% vs 17% at 50% budget) demonstrates the harm.
- The effect appears novel; neighboring papers focus on CoT benefits or efficiency, not the active harm of truncation.
**Break condition:** The harm manifests when the truncation point occurs before a critical logical closure or computational resolution, creating a contradiction between the partial trace and the required answer.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Code-based reasoning degrades more gracefully under token constraints than natural language (comments).
**Mechanism:** Programming language syntax has inherent structural semantics. A truncated `for` loop, `if` statement, or function call still conveys algorithmic intent (iteration, branching, abstraction) even if incomplete. This "syntactic redundancy" preserves some reasoning value. In contrast, natural language relies on sequential logical connectives ("therefore", "because"); truncating mid-sentence often destroys the logical proposition entirely.
**Core assumption:** Meaning in code is partially encoded in its grammatical structure, not just token sequence, making it more resilient to interruption.
**Evidence anchors:** 
- Gemini's comments-only collapse to 0% at 30% budget while code-only maintains 43–47%.
- Indirect support from literature on code's formal properties, though the truncation robustness aspect is novel.
**Break condition:** Truncation removes a critical syntactic anchor (e.g., the colon in a loop header) or isolates a code token without its operational context, rendering it meaningless.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Hybrid reasoning (using both code and comments) underperforms single-modality reasoning under token budgets.
**Mechanism:** Switching between modalities incurs a coordination cost. Each transition requires the model to (a) summarize computational or logical state from one format into the other, and (b) re-encode that summary for forward progress. These translation steps consume tokens without directly contributing to problem-solving. Single-modality chains avoid this overhead, dedicating all tokens to forward computation within a consistent representational system.
**Core assumption:** There is a non-trivial cognitive/computational cost to interleaving distinct symbolic systems within a single generative sequence.
**Evidence anchors:** 
- Grok achieves 90% with code-only or comments-only at 30% budget but only 80% with both.
- No direct corpus support; this is a novel interaction effect observed in the experiments.
**Break condition:** The overhead of modality switching exceeds the marginal benefit of using the "best" tool for each micro-step, especially under tight token budgets where every token must yield high reasoning value.

## Foundational Learning
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** Understanding that CoT is a generated intermediate trace is prerequisite because the entire study investigates its robustness and fragility when truncated.  
  *Quick check:* If a model's CoT is cut off mid-reasoning, why might its final answer be worse than if it had not tried to reason at all?
- **Token Budgeting as an Experimental Lever:** The core independent variable is the *proportion* of the optimal token count. This requires understanding that tokens are a constrained resource (cost/latency) and that "optimal" is measured per-condition.  
  *Quick check:* Why is it insufficient to just compare "short" vs "long" CoT without normalizing by a per-condition optimal length?
- **Modalities as Representational Formats:** The study isolates three distinct formats: executable code, natural language (comments), and hybrid. The hypothesis is that the format itself affects robustness.  
  *Quick check:* What is the key hypothesized property of *code* that might make it more robust to truncation than natural language?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Models: Four frontier LLMs (GPT-5.1, Gemini 3 Flash, DeepSeek-V3.2, Grok 4.1) queried via API with controlled generation. Datasets: Three math benchmarks (GSM8K, AIME, HMMT). Reasoning Conditions: Five enforced output formats via system prompts: `code-only`, `comments-only`, `both`, `nothing`, `standard CoT`. Token Ablation Engine: A wrapper that (1) runs unconstrained generation to measure `T_opt` per model-dataset-condition, (2) re-runs with hard token limits at 10%, 30%, 50%, 70% of `T_opt`. Evaluation Harness: Answer parser/normalizer for each condition and exact-match scorer.

**Critical path:**
1. For each model and dataset, determine unconstrained `T_opt` for each of the 5 reasoning conditions.
2. For each condition, run the full test set at each of the 4 budget levels (10%, 30%, 50%, 70% of `T_opt`).
3. Aggregate exact-match accuracy per (model, dataset, condition, budget) cell.
4. Analyze cross-condition and cross-model comparisons.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Fixed % budgets vs absolute tokens: Using % of `T_opt` normalizes for condition-specific baseline lengths but may obscure absolute token amounts.
- Modality enforcement: Relies on prompt compliance; a model might ignore the instruction. Mitigation: strict parsing and discard non-compliant outputs.
- Benchmark selection: Math benchmarks provide clean, verifiable answers but findings may not generalize to other reasoning tasks.

**Failure signatures:**
- Paradoxical drop: Accuracy in a truncated reasoning condition falls *below* the `nothing` condition for the same model/budget.
- Comment collapse: Comments-only accuracy plummets to near-zero at moderate budgets (e.g., 30%) while code-only remains robust.
- Hybrid penalty: `both` condition never reaches the peak of the best single-modality condition at any budget.
- Model bifurcation: Some models (e.g., Grok) maintain high accuracy at 30% budget; others (OpenAI, DeepSeek) crash to near-zero on reasoning conditions.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replicate the "truncation paradox": On DeepSeek-V3.2 + AIME, compare `nothing` vs truncated `CoT` (50% budget). Expect `nothing` (~53%) > truncated `CoT` (~17%).
2. Test code vs. comment gracefulness: On Gemini + GSM8K, compare `code-only` and `comments-only` at 30% budget. Expect `code-only` (~43–47%) >> `comments-only` (~0%).
3. Validate hybrid underperformance: On any model/dataset, compare `both` against best of `code-only`/`comments-only` at 30% and 50% budgets. Expect `both` to be strictly worse.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What architectural or training differences contribute to Grok's superior robustness to token truncation compared to other frontier models?
- Do the observed patterns of reasoning modality degradation under token constraints generalize beyond mathematical reasoning to other domains like coding or commonsense?
- Can adaptive token allocation strategies that dynamically choose reasoning modality based on problem characteristics mitigate the performance drop from truncation?
- Is it possible to fine-tune reasoning-specialized models to become truncation-robust without compromising their performance when full reasoning budgets are available?

## Limitations
- Exact system prompts for modality enforcement are unspecified, risking non-compliance and condition contamination.
- Strict token limit enforcement and access to all four frontier models are required but not guaranteed by APIs.
- The study is limited to three mathematical benchmarks; generalization to other reasoning domains is unknown.

## Confidence
- Truncated CoT can actively harm performance: Medium
- Code-based reasoning degrades more gracefully: Medium–High
- Hybrid reasoning underperforms: Medium
- Model-dependent robustness: High

## Next Checks
1. Replicate truncation paradox across models and datasets: Test `nothing` vs truncated `CoT` (50% budget) on GPT-5.1 and Grok 4.1 using GSM8K and HMMT. Validates generalizability beyond DeepSeek-AIME.
2. Analyze truncation states and modality adherence: For a stratified sample of truncated outputs (e.g., Gemini at 30% for code/comments), annotate (a) modality purity and (b) syntactic/logical completeness at cut-off point. Correlate with correctness to test break conditions.
3. Forced-switching experiment: For a fixed token budget, force models to switch between code and comments at regular intervals (e.g., every 10 tokens). Compare to single-modality baselines. This isolates the coordination cost of switching from endogenous switching decisions.
