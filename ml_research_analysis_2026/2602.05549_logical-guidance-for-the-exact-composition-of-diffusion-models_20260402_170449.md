# Logical Guidance for the Exact Composition of Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.05549
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05549
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: LOGDIFF enables exact compositional generation for diffusion models via recursive Boolean guidance, achieving >97% conformity on image benchmarks and improving molecular docking scores up to 73.20.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion models have demonstrated remarkable generative capabilities, yet they lack a principled mechanism for conditioning on complex logical queries over multiple attributes. Existing approaches resort to heuristic guidance, which often fails to guarantee exact satisfaction of intended Boolean constraints. LOGDIFF closes this gap by introducing an exact Boolean calculus that recursively constructs a guidance signal from atomic conditional scores and posterior probability estimates. Exactness is assured under the conditions that conjunctions involve conditionally independent subformulas and disjunctions involve either conditionally independent or mutually exclusive ones. Experiments on image (CMNIST, Shapes3D, CelebA) and molecular generation tasks show conformity rates of 97–98%, significant improvements over constant baselines, better diversity preservation, and state‑of‑the‑art dual‑target docking scores. The framework integrates seamlessly with standard diffusion samplers and scales efficiently.

## Method Summary
LOGDIFF modifies the reverse diffusion process by adding a logical guidance term computed recursively from the query’s Boolean formula. For each atomic predicate \(c_i\), the score \(s_t(c_i, x_t)\) is derived from the conditional diffusion model’s noise prediction: \(s_t(c_i, x_t) \propto \epsilon_\theta(x_t, c_i) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \emptyset)\). The posterior probability \(b_\pi(c_i) \approx p_t(c_i|x_t)\) is estimated either by a noise‑aware classifier trained on noisy samples or directly from the diffusion model. The recursive combination follows rules: negation flips and scales by posterior; conjunction multiplies posteriors and combines scores under conditional independence; disjunction uses OR‑CI (denominator \(\pi_\psi + \pi_\chi - \pi_\psi\pi_\chi\)) for conditionally independent subformulas or OR‑ME (denominator \(\pi_\psi + \pi_\chi\)) for mutually exclusive ones. The recursion returns a guidance vector \(g_\psi\) that is added to the unconditional score before each sampler step: \(\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \emptyset) + w \cdot g_\psi\). Exactness holds provided the query’s circuit respects the CI/ME constraints. Implementation requires training a single conditional diffusion model (e.g., with classifier‑free guidance) and optionally separate noise‑aware classifiers for atomic predicates.

## Key Results
- **Conformity**: On CMNIST, Shapes3D, and CelebA, LOGDIFF achieves 97–98% samples satisfying OR‑ME/OR‑CI queries, outperforming constant baselines by more than 20 percentage points.
- **Diversity & Quality**: LOGDIFF preserves higher sample entropy under strong guidance and lowers FID for NOT queries compared to baselines.
- **Molecular Generation**: For the GRM5‑RRM1 target pair, AND queries yield docking scores up to 73.20, and the method enables selective generation of compounds that satisfy \(A \land \neg B\), demonstrating broad applicability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method rests on an exact computation of the gradient of \(\log p(\psi|x_t)\), the log‑probability of the logical formula given the current sample. Under the assumptions that subformulas in a conjunction are conditionally independent given the rest (\(\phi \perp \chi \mid \psi_{\text{rest}}\)) and subformulas in a disjunction are either conditionally independent or mutually exclusive, the joint probability factorizes such that its gradient decomposes into atomic gradients and posterior probabilities. Specifically, for a node with children \(\phi\) and \(\chi\), the posterior \(b_\pi(\psi)\) is computed from the children’s posteriors using the appropriate combination (product for AND, sum‑product for OR‑CI, simple sum for OR‑ME). The scores \(s_t\) propagate from leaves to the root. This exact decomposition ensures that when the diffusion samples from the modified score, it converges to the distribution \(p(x|\psi=1)\) under the CI/ME conditions. The use of the base model’s conditional predictions for atomic scores and the careful weighting by posteriors avoids heuristic approximations and yields precise control.

- **Boolean Logic Composition**: Combining multiple conditions using AND, OR, NOT. *Why needed*: The goal is to satisfy arbitrary logical formulas over atomic predicates during generation. *Quick check*: Given a sample, how do you evaluate whether it satisfies a formula like \((A \lor B) \land \neg C\)?
- **Conditional Independence**: Two events are conditionally independent given a third if their joint probability factorizes. *Why needed*: Exact conjunction rule requires subformulas to be conditionally independent; otherwise guidance becomes approximate. *Quick check*: If attributes "wearing glasses" and "has beard" are marginally independent, are they conditionally independent given gender? Test from data.
- **Mutual Exclusivity**: Two events cannot both be true simultaneously. *Why needed*: For disjunctions of mutually exclusive subformulas, a simpler OR‑ME rule applies, preserving exactness. *Quick check*: In digit classification, are "digit=1" and "digit=3" mutually exclusive? Yes, because a single image cannot have two digit labels.
- **Posterior Probability Estimation**: Estimating \(p_t(c_i|x_t)\) at diffusion timestep \(t\). *Why needed*: These posteriors weight the atomic scores when combining them recursively. *Quick check*: How would you design a noise‑aware classifier to predict an attribute on a noisy image \(x_t\)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Conditional Diffusion Model (\(\epsilon_\theta\)) → Atomic Score Computation (\(s_t(c_i) = \epsilon_\theta(x_t,c_i) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t,\emptyset)\))  
Noise‑aware Classifiers (\(b_\pi(c_i)\)) → GetLogicalScore(\(\psi, x_t\)) (recursive combination using Table 1) → Total Guidance \(g_\psi\)  
Unconditional Score (\(\epsilon_\theta(x_t,\emptyset)\)) + \(w \cdot g_\psi\) → Diffusion Sampler (e.g., DDIM) → \(x_{t-1}\)

**Critical path**  
1. Initialize \(x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\).  
2. For \(t = T, \dots, 1\):  
   a. Compute \(\epsilon_\theta(x_t,\emptyset)\) and \(\epsilon_\theta(x_t,c_i)\) for all atomic \(c_i\).  
   b. Estimate \(b_\pi(c_i|x_t)\) using classifiers or diffusion‑based estimator.  
   c. Traverse the AST of the Boolean query \(\psi\): recursively combine (\(b_\pi, s_t\)) from children according to node type (NOT, AND, OR‑CI/OR‑ME) to obtain (\(g_\psi, b_\pi(\psi)\)).  
   d. Form combined noise prediction: \(\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(x_t,\emptyset) + w \cdot g_\psi\).  
   e. Perform sampler step to get \(x_{t-1}\).  
3. Output \(x_0\).

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Guidance scale \(w\)**: Larger values increase conformity but risk mode collapse and reduced diversity; requires tuning per query.  
- **Posterior estimator choice**: Separate noise‑aware classifiers can be accurate but need extra training; diffusion‑based estimators are simpler but may be less reliable at high noise.  
- **Relationship determination**: Misclassifying a disjunction as CI when subformulas are ME (or vice versa) breaks exactness; requires domain knowledge or empirical validation.  
- **Base model capacity**: Atomic predicates must be well represented by the conditional diffusion model; out‑of‑distribution predicates yield noisy scores.

**Failure signatures**  
- **Low conformity**: Independent classifier shows few samples satisfy the query. Likely causes: inaccurate posteriors (e.g., all \(b_\pi\) near 0.5) or incorrect circuit compilation (wrong OR rule).  
- **Mode collapse**: Samples show little variation (e.g., all same digit/color). Often due to too high guidance scale or using OR‑CI for mutually exclusive attributes.  
- **Blurry/unnatural samples**: FID spikes or artifacts indicate the guidance term is too noisy, possibly from unstable posterior estimates near 0 or 1.  
- **Logical inconsistency**: For AND queries, samples satisfy only one conjunct; suggests conjunction rule misapplied (subformulas not CI) or poor atomic score resolution.

**Three first experiments**  
1. **Atomic predicate verification**: For each atomic attribute \(c_i\), generate samples conditioned solely on \(c_i\) and measure conformity via an independent classifier. Validates the base model and classifier.  
2. **OR‑CI disjunction test**: Query with two independent attributes (e.g., “red OR pink” in CMNIST) using OR‑CI rule. Expect near‑100% conformity and a color distribution matching the independent mixture, contrasting with the constant baseline that collapses to a single color.  
3. **Guidance scale sweep**: Fix a moderately complex query (e.g., “digit 1 AND (red OR pink)”). Sweep \(w\) across a range (e.g., 0.5–5.0) and plot conformity, mean joint entropy, and FID. Identify the tradeoff curve and choose a working point balancing conformity (>90%) with acceptable diversity.

## Limitations
- Requires explicit knowledge of conditional independence or mutual exclusivity relationships among subformulas; automatically determining these for arbitrary predicate sets is not addressed.
- Posterior probability estimation is critical and sensitive; the paper omits training details of the noise‑aware classifier, hindering replication.
- Molecular experiments rely on the TargetDiff model; without full implementation details, independent validation of docking score improvements is difficult.

## Confidence
- **Theoretical Exactness**: High. Proofs are rigorous under stated assumptions, but practical use depends on correctly identifying CI/ME.
- **Empirical Superiority**: Medium. Benchmarks show strong results, but they rely on proper implementation and accurate posteriors; sensitivity analyses indicate potential fragility.
- **Diversity Preservation**: Medium. Reported advantages hold when guidance is tuned; high guidance scales can still collapse diversity if posteriors are noisy.
