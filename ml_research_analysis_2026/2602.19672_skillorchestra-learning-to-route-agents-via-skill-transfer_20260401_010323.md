# SkillOrchestra: Learning to Route Agents via Skill Transfer

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.19672
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19672
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** Replaces end-to-end RL with a reusable skill-centered approach, achieving up to 22.5% higher accuracy and 700x lower training cost versus state-of-the-art orchestrators.

## Executive Summary
SkillOrchestra tackles ineffective agent orchestration in compound AI systems by moving away from black-box end-to-end reinforcement learning. It constructs a reusable "Skill Handbook" from historical execution traces, automatically inducing fine-grained skills and building competence profiles for each available agent. At inference, it matches the inferred skill demands of a new task to these profiles under an explicit cost-performance trade-off. Evaluated on ten benchmarks, this method significantly improves accuracy over RL-based baselines while drastically cutting training costs. It also demonstrates strong Pareto efficiency (accuracy vs. inference cost), mitigates routing collapse, and successfully transfers its routing logic across different underlying orchestrator models without retraining.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is a shift from monolithic policy learning to a two-phase, reusable skill-based routing framework. First, it processes historical execution traces to decompose agent behaviors into a handbook of atomic "skills" and to generate a quantitative competence profile for each agent, indicating its proficiency and cost for each skill. Second, for a new query, it decomposes the task into required skills, then selects the optimal agent by solving a constrained optimization problem that explicitly balances the expected performance (based on competence profiles) against inference cost.

## Key Results
*   Achieves up to 22.5% absolute accuracy improvement over state-of-the-art RL-based orchestrators across ten benchmarks.
*   Reduces training cost by 700× versus Router-R1 and 300× versus ToolOrchestra.
*   Lies on the Pareto frontier, delivering higher accuracy at lower inference cost than all compared baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SkillOrchestra works because it replaces the brittle, sample-inefficient process of learning a single end-to-end routing policy with a more interpretable and reusable matching problem. By building explicit, fine-grained skill and competence representations from past data, it decouples the discovery of *what* needs to be done (skill decomposition) from the decision of *who* should do it (agent matching). The explicit cost-performance objective during routing prevents the system from defaulting to expensive, overused agents (mitigating routing collapse) and allows for principled trade-offs. This structure also enables the routing logic itself to be applied (transferred) to different backbone models or agent pools simply by updating their competence profiles.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Skill Decomposition & Induction:** Why needed: Breaks complex agent behaviors into reusable, atomic capabilities for fine-grained matching. Quick check: Can induced skills be semantically interpreted and recombined to describe novel tasks?
*   **Agent Competence Profiling:** Why needed: Quantifies each agent's proficiency and cost for specific skills, enabling data-driven selection. Quick check: Do profile scores correlate with actual agent performance on held-out skill-specific tasks?
*   **Cost-Performance Trade-off Optimization:** Why needed: Formalizes the orchestration decision as an optimization problem, avoiding heuristic or purely accuracy-maximizing choices. Quick check: Does varying the cost constraint in the objective lead to predictable accuracy-cost trade-offs?
*   **Execution Trace Mining:** Why needed: Provides the empirical data source for learning skills and profiles without manual labeling. Quick check: Is the system sensitive to the diversity and quality of the provided trace dataset?
*   **Transfer Learning for Orchestration Logic:** Why needed: Allows the routing framework to be applied to new agent sets or backbone models without full retraining. Quick check: Can the router trained on one model family (e.g., Llama-based agents) be applied to another (e.g., GPT-based agents) with only profile re-computation?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Execution Traces -> Skill Induction Module & Agent Profiler -> Skill Handbook & Competence Database -> Skill Demand Decomposer & Router -> Selected Agent Execution

**Critical Path:** The primary flow begins with ingesting historical traces. The Skill Induction Module extracts the skill taxonomy, while the Agent Profiler populates the Competence Database. For a new query, the Skill Demand Decomposer identifies required skills, the Router solves the cost-performance optimization using the Handbook and Database, and the top-ranked agent executes.

**Design Tradeoffs:** 
*   **Skill Granularity:** Finer skills improve matching precision but increase handbook size and risk of overfitting to trace noise. Coarser skills improve robustness but reduce specificity.
*   **Trace Dependency:** System performance is fundamentally gated by the coverage and quality of historical traces; exceptional or novel agent behaviors absent from traces cannot be properly assessed.
*   **Optimization Complexity:** The routing objective's formulation (e.g., knapsack-like constraints) balances solution optimality with computational overhead at inference time.

**Failure Signatures:** 
*   **Cold-Start Failure:** New tasks or agents with no relevant trace data receive empty or default profiles, leading to random or failing selections.
*   **Skill Induction Failure:** Poorly defined, overlapping, or semantically meaningless skills in the Handbook cause the matching process to be misaligned with true task requirements.
*   **Profile Staleness:** Agent updates or new tools are not reflected in profiles until sufficient new execution traces are processed, causing suboptimal routing during transition periods.

**First Three Experiments:**
1.  **Zero-Shot Domain Transfer:** Implement SkillOrchestra using trace data from standard agent benchmarks (e.g., WebArena, AgentBench) and evaluate its routing performance on a fundamentally different domain (e.g., a scientific literature investigation suite) without any traces from that new domain.
2.  **Skill Handbook Quality Ablation:** Conduct a human or LLM-based evaluation to score the interpretability, coherence, and coverage of the automatically induced skills. Then, correlate these quality metrics with downstream orchestration accuracy to validate the core assumption that a better handbook yields a better router.
3.  **Trace Robustness Stress Test:** Systematically corrupt the training trace set by injecting noise (e.g., random action swaps, missing steps) or removing traces for a subset of "critical" skills. Measure the degradation in routing accuracy and identify which types of trace damage are most catastrophic.

None.

## Limitations
*   **Generalization Scope:** Evaluation is limited to 10 specific agent orchestration benchmarks; performance on novel, real-world, or radically different task domains is unproven.
*   **Skill Induction Black Box:** The automatic induction of "fine-grained skills" from traces is a critical but under-scrutinized component; the semantic quality and granularity of these skills are major unknowns.
*   **Cold-Start Problem:** The system is heavily dependent on existing execution traces and may fail for new agents or task types with no historical data, a significant practical limitation.

## Confidence
*   **High Confidence:** Reported accuracy improvements and training cost reductions *on the presented 10 benchmarks* are likely valid due to controlled comparison.
*   **Medium Confidence:** Claims of effective "skill transfer" and mitigation of "routing collapse" are supported by internal analyses but require external validation on broader task sets.
*   **Low Confidence:** The broad claim of universal "higher accuracy at lower inference cost than all compared baselines" is not fully proven, as it depends on specific baseline implementations and cost metrics used in the study.

## Next Checks
1.  Reproduce and evaluate SkillOrchestra on at least 2-3 entirely new benchmark suites (e.g., from embodied AI or scientific tool use) unseen during development to test generalization.
2.  Conduct a human or automated evaluation to directly assess the interpretability and coverage of the automatically induced skill handbook, then correlate handbook quality with orchestration performance.
3.  Measure performance in cold-start scenarios (no traces for a new agent/task) and under controlled trace corruption (noise, missing data) to determine sensitivity and failure modes.
