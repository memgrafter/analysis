# Comparative Analysis of 3D Convolutional and 2.5D Slice-Conditioned U-Net Architectures for MRI Super-Resolution via Elucidated Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14667
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14667
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Primary result:** The 3D volumetric EDM U-Net achieves 37.75 dB PSNR, 0.997 SSIM, and 0.020 LPIPS on a held-out test set, significantly outperforming the 2.5D slice-conditioned variant (35.82 dB PSNR) and off-the-shelf EDSR/Swin2SR baselines (35.57 dB PSNR).

## Executive Summary
This work tackles MRI super-resolution to reduce reliance on high-field scanners. It adapts the Elucidated Diffusion Model (EDM) framework with two U-Net backbones: a full 3D convolutional U-Net processing volumetric patches and a 2.5D slice-conditioned U-Net operating per-slice with adjacent context. Both are trained on the NKI cohort of FOMO60K. On a held-out test set (5 subjects, 993 slices), the 3D model yields superior quantitative results across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, demonstrating the advantage of 3D volumetric processing for MRI super-resolution.

## Method Summary
The task is 2× in-plane super-resolution from low-resolution (LR) to high-resolution (HR) brain T1-weighted sagittal slices/volumes. Data: NKI FOMO60K dataset, split 59 subjects for training (100 sessions) and 5 subjects for testing (6 sessions, 993 slices). Preprocessing: intensity normalization to [0,255] using 1st–99th percentile, then to [-1,1]; LR created by block-averaging HR (factor 2). Two EDM-based models: (1) 3D volumetric U-Net processes 3D patches (LR size 32³, HR size 32×64×64) with 4-level encoder/decoder, 3D convolutions (3×3×3), residual blocks, and multi-head self-attention at deepest level; uses Euler sampler with 20 steps; 50.7M parameters. (2) 2.5D slice-conditioned U-Net processes each target LR slice together with one adjacent LR slice (both upsampled to HR resolution) and a noisy HR slice as 3 channels; a 2D U-Net with channels [64,64,128,256], residual blocks, attention; uses Heun solver (1-step); 51.1M parameters. Training: AdamW (lr=1e-4, weight decay=1e-2), σ sampled from log-normal (lnσ ~ N(-1.2,1.2²)), loss MSE. Batch/accumulation: 3D batch=2, 4 accum steps, 8 patches/volume; 2.5D batch=4, 8 accum steps. Evaluation metrics: PSNR↑, SSIM↑, LPIPS↓.

## Key Results
- 3D EDM U-Net: **37.75 dB PSNR**, **0.997 SSIM**, **0.020 LPIPS**.
- 2.5D EDM U-Net: **35.82 dB PSNR**.
- Off‑the‑shelf EDSR/Swin2SR: **35.57 dB PSNR**.
All differences are statistically significant, with the 3D model surpassing all comparators.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The 3D volumetric U-Net processes the entire volume (in patches) and therefore inherently models inter‑slice dependencies and full 3D anatomical structure, leading to more consistent and accurate reconstructions. The EDM framework’s noise conditioning and preconditioning stabilize training and improve sample fidelity for both architectures. In contrast, the 2.5D approach treats each slice independently with only a single adjacent slice for context, limiting its ability to capture volumetric correlations and resulting in lower quantitative performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Diffusion Models**: Generative models that reverse a gradual noising process. Needed to learn complex distributions and produce high‑quality samples. Quick check: Loss is MSE between predicted and added noise at various σ.
- **Elucidated Diffusion Model (EDM)**: Framework with explicit σ conditioning and preconditioning to stabilize training/sampling. Quick check: Network input includes normalized σ and uses c_in/c_noise/c_skip/c_out functions.
- **U‑Net**: Encoder‑decoder with skip connections for preserving spatial details. Needed for multi‑scale feature aggregation in image-to-image tasks. Quick check: Model has symmetric encoder/decoder with residual blocks and skip connections.
- **3D Convolutional Networks**: Convolutions across all three dimensions; essential for volumetric data like MRI. Quick check: Convolutional kernels are 3×3×3 and applied with depth stride.
- **2.5D Slice‑Conditioned Networks**: 2D convolutions plus extra slice context; a memory‑efficient approximation of 3D. Quick check: Input includes multiple slices from the same volume.
- **Super‑Resolution Metrics**: PSNR (pixel‑wise fidelity), SSIM (structural similarity), LPIPS (perceptual similarity). Needed to quantify reconstruction quality. Quick check: Metrics computed in same normalized space as training or after denormalizing consistently.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**  
LR patch/slice + σ → Preconditioning (c_in, c_noise) → U‑Net backbone (either 3D or 2.5D) → Preconditioning (c_skip, c_out) → Prediction (denoised HR) → MSE loss w.r.t. HR → Backpropagation.

**Critical path:** 1) Sample σ from log‑normal; 2) Corrupt HR with Gaussian noise; 3) Precondition inputs; 4) Forward through U‑Net; 5) Rescale output; 6) Compute MSE loss with clean HR; 7) Backpropagate and update.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- 3D vs 2.5D: 3D captures full volumetric context but needs heavy memory (solved by patches); 2.5D reduces memory at cost of slice‑wise consistency.  
- Patch‑based 3D training enables fitting large volumes but may miss global context; overlapping inference with blending mitigates border artifacts.  
- solver choice (Euler 20 steps vs Heun 1 step) trades sampling fidelity for speed.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **OOM** in 3D: caused by too large patch/batch; remedy: lower batch, increase grad‑accum, use checkpointing.  
- **Blurry outputs**: underfitting due to insufficient capacity, wrong σ distribution, or mis‑preconditioning; check loss trend and visual sanity.  
- **Slice inconsistency** in 2.5D: seams between slices; indicates inadequate context; may require stronger cross‑slice conditioning.  
- **Training instability**: spikes in loss often from incorrect σ sampling or preconditioning implementation.

**First experiments (3):**  
1. Sanity‑check training on <1⁠% of data (1 subject) for 1 epoch: ensure loss decreases without crashes.  
2. Visualize LR/HR pairs and early model outputs to verify preprocessing (normalization, downsampling) and network plausibility.  
3. Test effect of noise sampling: vary σ distribution (e.g., narrower log‑normal) or number of sampler steps (Euler steps) and observe PSNR changes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions. However, based on the identified limitations and next checks, the following questions arise:
- How does the exact test split composition (subject/session IDs) affect the reported quantitative results and potential data leakage?
- What is the impact of the 3D patch sampling protocol (spatial coverage, number of patches per volume) on training dynamics and final performance?
- Can the 2.5D slice‑conditioned architecture be improved to better capture volumetric correlations, perhaps by incorporating more adjacent slices or cross‑slice attention mechanisms?

## Limitations
- Test set composition: exact subject/session IDs for the 5 test subjects (6 volumes, 993 slices) are not disclosed, preventing exact replication of the evaluation split and risking data leakage.  
- 3D patch sampling protocol: the description “random 3D patches” lacks details on spatial coverage, sampling density, and use of replacement, which affect training dynamics and volumetric representation.  
- 2.5D adjacent slice selection: the rule for choosing the conditioning slice (e.g., immediate neighbor, random) is unspecified, impacting contextual consistency and per‑slice performance.

## Confidence
- **Framework adaptation (EDM + U‑Nets):** High – architecture details and conditioning are clearly described and follow established EDM design.  
- **Quantitative results (3D > 2.5D > baselines):** Medium – results rely on an undisclosed test split and unresolved implementation details (patch sampling, adjacent slice selection).  
- **Architectural comparison rationale (volumetric context matters):** Medium – the reasoning is plausible but the claimed superiority may vary with exact data split and sampling specifics.
