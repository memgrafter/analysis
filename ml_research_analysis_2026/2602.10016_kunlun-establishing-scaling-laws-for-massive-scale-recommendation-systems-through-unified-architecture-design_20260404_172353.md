# Kunlun: Establishing Scaling Laws for Massive-Scale Recommendation Systems through Unified Architecture Design
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10016  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10016  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Kunlun doubles scaling efficiency and increases Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU) from 17% to 37% on NVIDIA B200 GPUs through a unified architecture with systematic model-efficiency co-design, delivering a 1.2% topline metric improvement in Meta Ads deployments.

## Executive Summary
Massive-scale recommendation systems face a critical challenge: scaling model compute does not yield predictable quality improvements due to poor hardware utilization and inefficient modules. Kunlun addresses this by introducing a unified architecture that jointly models sequential user history and non-sequential context features. It combines low-level kernel optimizations (Generalized Dot-Product Attention, Hierarchical Seed Pooling, Sliding Window Attention) with high-level computation reallocation (Computation Skip, Event-level Personalization). This co-design dramatically improves hardware efficiency (MFU) and enables reliable scaling laws, where quality (Normalized Entropy) improves linearly with log compute. The system has been deployed in Meta’s ads models, demonstrating real-world gains.

## Method Summary
Kunlun processes heterogeneous inputs by splitting them into non-sequence features (dense projections, sparse embeddings) and sequence features (temporal embeddings, MLP fusion). Its core building block is the Kunlun Layer, repeated L times. Each layer contains two sub-modules: a Transformer Block and an Interaction Block. The Transformer Block first applies Generalized Dot-Product Attention (GDPA) to transform the sequence using weights derived from non-sequence summaries, followed by Sliding Window Self-Attention for local refinement. The Interaction Block generates new non-sequence representations by combining them with a compressed sequence summary from Hierarchical Seed Pooling (HSP) via a Mixture of Wukong Experts. A high-level Computation Skip pattern alternates between processing self-attention and HSP/PFFN across layers to eliminate redundancy. Event-type-specific configurations (dimension, depth, window) further optimize compute allocation.

## Key Results
- Increased Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU) from 17% to 37% on NVIDIA B200 GPUs.
- Achieved 2× scaling efficiency over prior state-of-the-art methods (e.g., InterFormer) as measured by the scaling coefficient η in NE(C)=NE₀−η·log(C/C₀).
- Deployed in major Meta Ads models, yielding a 1.2% improvement in topline business metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Generalized Dot-Product Attention (GDPA)
**Claim:** GDPA increases MFU by transforming the Personalized FeedForward Network (PFFN) from a memory-bound two-layer MLP into a fused, compute-bound attention-style operation.  
**Mechanism:** GDPA reinterprets the PFFN’s weight generation `f(X_sum)` as key/value projections in an attention framework, with sequence tokens as queries. This enables a single block-wise kernel (inspired by FlashAttention) that fuses all matrix multiplications and activations, eliminating intermediate storage and maximizing GPU compute occupancy. It overcomes sequential dependencies and irregular tensor shapes that previously limited hardware utilization to 3–15%.  
**Core assumption:** The fundamental computation in PFFN (sequence transformed by context-generated weights) is mathematically equivalent to a dot-product attention pattern, and fusing these operations into one kernel reduces memory bandwidth bottlenecks.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] MFU increase from 17% to 37% and GDPA listed as a low-level optimization.  
- [Section 4.3.1] GDPA reformulation enables entire PFFN fusion into a single kernel, achieving up to 6× MFU improvement for the PFFN block.  
**Break condition:** For extremely short sequences (`T << 100`) or tiny embedding dimensions (`d < 32`), the attention-style kernel overhead may outweigh benefits, reducing MFU versus original PFFN.

### Mechanism 2: Hierarchical Seed Pooling (HSP)
**Claim:** HSP with SumKronLinear improves model quality (Normalized Entropy) over traditional Pooling by Multihead Attention (PMA) by providing more stable and expressive sequence summarization for global interaction.  
**Mechanism:** HSP uses a three-stage hierarchical process: (1) initialize overcomplete learnable seed embeddings, (2) perform seed-level attention to aggregate sequence information, (3) compress seeds to final tokens using SumKronLinear. This contrasts with PMA’s single-stage random query attention. The hierarchical approach provides better initialization, and the Kronecker-based SumKronLinear captures joint sequence-embedding correlations with far fewer parameters, yielding more informative summaries for the Global Interaction module.  
**Core assumption:** Hierarchical summarization (seed aggregation then compression) and a parameter-efficient compressor (SumKronLinear) produce higher-quality condensed representations than a single-step random query attention, especially for long sequences.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [Section 4.3.2] HSP improves model quality, achieving 0.08% NE gain over PMA.  
- [Table 1] Ablation shows `w/o HSP (use PMA)` results in a `+0.08%` NE degradation.  
**Break condition:** For very short sequences (`T < 50`), the overhead of additional seed-level attention and compression may not justify the NE benefit, making standard PMA more efficient.

### Mechanism 3: Computation Skip (CompSkip)
**Claim:** CompSkip via an every-other-layer pattern doubles scaling efficiency by adaptively reallocating compute away from redundant or lower-impact operations (HSP/PFFN) towards more essential ones (self-attention), maintaining model quality while significantly reducing FLOPs.  
**Mechanism:** CompSkip implements a predetermined alternating pattern: even layers skip self-attention but compute fresh HSP summaries; odd layers skip HSP (reusing previous summaries) and PFFN but compute self-attention. This exploits presumed layer redundancy where HSP summaries need not be recomputed every layer and PFFN transformations are less critical than local sequence refinement via attention. The pattern ensures each layer contributes either local or global refinement without redundancy.  
**Core assumption:** In deep recommendation models, HSP summaries from one layer remain sufficiently informative for the next layer’s global interaction, and PFFN transformations can be skipped in favor of sustaining self-attention, which is more critical for capturing sequential dependencies.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [Section 4.4.1] CompSkip delivers 43.1% FLOPs reduction and 35% QPS improvement through the every-other-layer pattern while maintaining NE neutrality.  
- [Table 1] `w/o CompSkip` shows `-35%` QPS degradation and `+43.1%` FLOPs increase.  
**Break condition:** For very shallow models (`L <= 2`) or short sequences (`T < 100`), the redundancy assumption breaks; each layer’s HSP summary and PFFN transformation become critical, and skipping them would cause significant NE degradation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Normalized Entropy (NE) & Scaling Laws**  
  **Why needed here:** NE is the primary quality metric and central to the claimed scaling laws. Understanding `NE(C) = NE₀ - η·log(C/C₀)` and that `η` is the scaling coefficient (improvement per log compute) is essential to interpret the "2× scaling efficiency" claim.  
  **Quick check:** If Model A has η=0.5 and Model B has η=1.0, what does 1.0 imply about B's NE improvement for a 10× compute increase compared to A?

- **Concept: Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU)**  
  **Why needed here:** MFU is the key hardware efficiency metric. The paper’s core argument is that poor MFU (17%) prevented predictable scaling. Understanding MFU as `(Achieved FLOPs * Throughput) / (Peak FLOPs * Num GPUs)` explains why kernel fusion (GDPA) and operation skipping (CompSkip) matter—they push MFU higher.  
  **Quick check:** If a model’s operations are memory-bound (e.g., many small matmuls), would you expect its MFU to be closer to 5% or 60%? Why?

- **Concept: Heterogeneous Feature Spaces in Recommendation**  
  **Why needed here:** The entire efficiency problem stems from mixing sequential (user history) and non-sequential (user demographics, ad metadata) features, leading to irregular tensors, small embedding dimensions, and memory-bound ops. This context is necessary to understand *why* LLM-style scaling didn’t directly apply.  
  **Quick check:** Why would jointly modeling a 1000-length sequence and 500 discrete categorical features likely result in lower MFU than modeling a 1000-token text sequence alone?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input Layer → (Non-Sequence: Dense proj, Sparse emb) & (Sequence: MLP fusion, ROTE temp emb) → Kunlun Layer (xL): [Transformer Block: GDPA → SlidingWindowMHA] + [Interaction Block: WeightGen → HSP → Global Interaction (Mixture of Wukong experts)] → Output: Final non-seq → MLP → Sigmoid.

- **Critical path:** Non-sequence features `X` and sequence `S` enter layer → `WeightGen(X_sum)` creates `W` → `GDPA(S, W)` transforms `S` → `SlidingWindowMHA` refines `S` → `HSP` compresses `S` → `GlobalInteraction([X | HSP_output])` produces new `X` and `S` for next layer. Bidirectional flow is key: `X` guides `S` via `W`, `S` informs `X` via concatenation.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **GDPA vs. original PFFN:** GDPA trades a slight NE cost when removed (+0.04% in Table 1) for massive MFU/QPS gains.  
  - **HSP vs. PMA:** HSP gives better NE (+0.08%) at cost of QPS (-8.8% vs PMA). Quality-critical systems prefer HSP.  
  - **Sliding Window:** Accepts potential loss of very long-range dependencies (unimportant per locality bias) for 31.1% QPS gain.  
  - **CompSkip:** Sacrifices theoretical maximum capacity (by skipping modules) for massive FLOP/QPS reduction with minimal NE loss ("maintaining NE neutrality"), enabling effective scaling.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Unpredictable scaling: NE curve deviates from power-law (log-linear) as compute increases.  
  - Low MFU (<20%): Indicates memory-bound operations persist (e.g., if GDPA not applied, or shapes remain jagged).  
  - High NE variance across event types: Suggests Event Personalization configuration is incorrect (e.g., too much compute wasted on low-signal events).  
  - QPS plateaus early: Could indicate kernel fusion not fully implemented or excessive synchronization from irregular shapes.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline Comparison at Multiple Scales:** Run Wukong, InterFormer, and Kunlun at ~6, 60, 180 GFLOPs. Expected: Kunlun’s NE gains should widen with scale (e.g., 0.31%→0.79% vs InterFormer 0.31%→0.50%), proving superior scaling efficiency.  
  2. **Component Ablation:** Systematically remove GDPA, HSP, Sliding Window, CompSkip, Event Personalization. Expected: GDPA removal drops MFU 3% and QPS 8%; HSP removal degrades NE 0.08%; CompSkip removal increases FLOPs 43% and drops QPS 35%. Validates each mechanism’s isolated contribution.  
  3. **Scaling Law Curve Fit:** Train Kunlun models across a range of FLOPs (e.g., 10 to 200 GFLOPs). Plot `NE` vs `log(Compute)`. Expected: Strong linear fit (high R²) with slope `η` approximately 2× that of InterFormer, confirming predictable power-law and 2× efficiency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Does the predictable power-law scaling hold for compute budgets significantly larger than those evaluated (e.g., >1000 GFLOPs), or do new bottlenecks (e.g., memory, communication) cause deviations?** The experiments only covered up to 180 GFLOPs and claimed predictable scaling based on that range; they did not test extreme scales.
- **Do Kunlun’s architecture and scaling benefits generalize to recommendation tasks beyond CTR prediction, such as conversion prediction or multi-task learning?** The paper exclusively reports results on CTR prediction for Meta Ads, with no experiments on other tasks or datasets.
- **How does Kunlun compare to sequence-only architectures like HSTU when the set of non-sequential context features is reduced or absent?** The paper did not include HSTU due to a "fundamental mismatch in design assumptions" and did not evaluate Kunlun under context-sparse regimes.
- **Can per-event configuration choices (model dimension, attention heads, number of layers, window size) be automatically optimized rather than manually set based on heuristics?** The paper describes manual importance-based allocation but no systematic method to learn these configurations.

## Limitations
- **Proprietary data & setup:** Core results rely on Meta’s internal Ads datasets (feature distributions, sequence lengths, event types) which differ significantly from public benchmarks; scaling law behavior may not transfer.
- **Underspecified architectural details:** Exact configuration of the "Mixture of Wukong experts" (expert count, inner architecture), WeightGen MLP dimensions, and SumKronLinear initialization/training are not provided, requiring reverse-engineering that may introduce variance.
- **Unknown training regime:** Learning rate schedule, optimizer settings, batch size, and number of training epochs beyond "single epoch" are undisclosed and heavily influence convergence and final NE.
- **MFU measurement ambiguity:** Precise protocol for MFU calculation (forward vs. full training FLOPs, throughput averaging window) is unclear, making direct comparison to reported 17%→37% difficult without identical protocols.

## Confidence
- **GDPA MFU gain (17%→37%):** Medium. Evidence is internal (Section 4.3.1) with strong ablation, but lacks independent replication on different hardware/datasets.
- **HSP NE improvement (+0.08% over PMA):** Medium. Supported by Table 1 ablation, but marginal gain could be sensitive to seed count/kron rank choices not fully explored.
- **CompSkip efficiency (43.1% FLOPs reduction, NE-neutral):** Medium. Internal validation is robust, but assumes a fixed every-other-layer pattern; optimal skipping may vary with model depth/sequence length.
- **Predictable scaling laws & 2× efficiency:** High for existence of power-law fit (explicit NE(C) curves); Medium for the "2×" claim relative to SOTA, as SOTA baselines (InterFormer) details are not fully described.
- **Production topline improvement (1.2%):** High. Empirical A/B test result from deployment, though specific business metric definition and confidence intervals are undisclosed.

## Next Checks
1. **Scaling law validation on public data:** Train Kunlun-style models (with GDPA, HSP, CompSkip) on a large public CTR dataset (e.g., Avazu or a large Criteo subset). Vary model scale (L=2~8, d=64~256) to compute per-sample FLOPs. Fit NE vs. log(FLOPs) and compare slope η to a baseline model (e.g., standard Transformer with PFFN+PMA). Check for linearity (R²>0.95) and whether η improves by ~2×.
2. **Component ablation under fixed compute budget:** Fix total model FLOPs (e.g., 100 GFLOPs). Build 5 models: (a) baseline (PFFN+PMA), (b)+GDPA, (c)+HSP, (d)+CompSkip, (e) full Kunlun. Train each to convergence on the same dataset. Verify: (b) MFU increases >15% with ≤0.04% NE change, (c) NE improves ≥0.07% vs (a), (d) FLOPs ↓≥40% with ≤0.02% NE change vs (c), (e) combines all gains.
3. **MFU measurement protocol standardization:** On a single GPU type (e.g., H100), implement GDPA both as fused Triton kernel and as naive separate PyTorch ops. Run identical batch/length/dim configuration. Measure: (i) peak SM utilization via Nsight, (ii) achieved throughput (samples/sec), (iii) total training FLOPs (forward+backward) via analytical count. Compute MFU = (FLOPs × throughput)/(peak TFLOPS). Confirm fused version achieves ≥30% MFU and that naive version remains <20%, reproducing the claimed gap.
