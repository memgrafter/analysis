# PA3: Policy-Aware Agent Alignment through Chain-of-Thought

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv:** 2603.14602
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14602
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** PA3 achieves 53.75% average pass@1 on τ-Bench (Airline 42.00, Retail 65.51) while using 40% fewer words than in-context baselines.

## Executive Summary
Business policy adherence for conversational assistants is challenging when policies are lengthy; including them in prompts causes latency and context degradation. PA3 introduces a multi-stage alignment method that teaches models to recall only relevant policies during chain-of-thought reasoning, without any in‑context policy injection. The approach features a generate‑branch‑evaluate‑refine pipeline to produce high‑quality policy‑grounded CoTs, a novel PolicyRecall reward (Jaccard) combined with hallucination and length penalties in GRPO, and three training stages: continual SFT, CoT‑augmented SFT, and RL fine‑tuning.

On τ‑Bench, PA3 surpasses a no‑policy baseline by 16 points and matches in‑context baselines while reducing word count by 40%. It also reaches 71.43% accuracy on a policy knowledge QA test.

## Method Summary
PA3’s pipeline begins with data preparation: the APIGen dataset (4.8k cleaned trajectories) is used to generate reasoning traces. For each assistant turn, deepseek‑r1 produces an initial CoT, which four Claude‑3.5‑v1 evaluators score on rubrics (Completeness, Atomicity, Faithfulness, Style). CoTs failing thresholds are refined up to four rounds via an error‑summary summarizer. Only passing CoTs are retained.

Training proceeds in three stages. Stage 1 (PA1) performs continual supervised fine‑tuning of Qwen2.5‑Instruct‑32B on GFC plus APIGen with policies included in the prompt. Stage 2 (PA2) continues from PA1, but removes policies from the context and instead appends the filtered CoT to the assistant’s response. This forces the model to learn to recall applicable policies internally. Stage 3 (PA3) applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on 900 held‑out APIGen trajectories using a composite reward: format (binary), turn‑level correctness, PolicyRecall (Jaccard between required and recalled policies), hallucination penalty, and length penalty. The final model yields accurate, concise policy‑adherent responses without needing in‑context policies.

## Key Results
- PA3 attains 53.75% average pass@1 on τ‑Bench (Airline 42.00, Retail 65.51), improving over the no‑policy baseline by 16 points and matching in‑context methods while reducing word count by 40%.
- Policy knowledge QA accuracy reaches 71.43%, indicating successful internalization of business rules.
- Ablations confirm the value of the rubrics (filtered CoTs) and each reward component; the length penalty reduces token usage without harming correctness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Internalization of Policies via CoT Recall**  
Training removes policies from the prompt during Stage 2, requiring the model to mention only the relevant policies inside its thinking block. This creates a direct mapping from user intent to policy clauses, embedding that knowledge in the model’s parameters. At inference, the model generates a CoT that recalls from memory the applicable policies and grounds its response accordingly. Evidence includes the explicit description of Stage 2 and the observation that SFT alone yields limited gains on the out‑of‑distribution Airline domain, which improve after RL fine‑tuning. The break condition: if policies are too complex or out‑of‑distribution, recall may be incomplete, lowering PolicyRecall reward and pass@1.

**High‑Quality CoT Data via Rubric‑Based Filtering**  
The generate‑branch‑evaluate‑refine pipeline uses four rubrics (Completeness ≥9, Atomicity ≥7, Faithfulness =10, Style ≥6) to filter CoTs. This eliminates noisy, hallucinated, or irrelevant reasoning traces that could mislead the model during SFT. The iterative refinement with error summaries progressively improves rubric scores. Ablation shows that using unfiltered CoTs degrades performance (48.80 → 53.75). Break condition: overly strict thresholds discard valid CoTs, reducing training diversity; overly lax thresholds let noise through.

**Reward Design for Precise and Concise Recall**  
GRPO optimizes the model with a PolicyRecall reward equal to the Jaccard index between required policies (A) and recalled policies (B) extracted from the thinking block. This splits truthfully recalled policies (A∩B) from over‑recall (B\A). A hallucination penalty penalizes policies mentioned that aren’t in A. A length penalty softens beyond 100 words and hard beyond 250 words. Ablations confirm each component’s contribution: adding hallucination penalty improves airline performance; length penalty yields large gains and token reduction. Mis‑weighting can cause over‑recall or excessive brevity.

*Why needed here:* The final stage uses GRPO to fine‑tune on objectives (PolicyRecall, hallucination, length) that are not easily captured by standard supervised learning. Understanding group‑relative advantage and verifiable reward design is essential.

*Why needed here:* The CoT quality pipeline relies on multiple LLM judges scoring against rubrics. This replaces human annotation and scales data generation.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
CoT Generation Pipeline: Generate (deepseek‑r1) -> Evaluate (Claude‑3.5‑v1) on four rubrics -> if all threshold met -> output; else -> Summarize error -> Refine (deepseek‑r1) -> loop (up to 4 rounds).  
Training Pipeline: Qwen2.5‑Instruct‑32B -> Stage1 SFT (PA1) -> Stage2 SFT (PA2) -> Stage3 GRPO (PA3).

**Critical path:**  
1. Clean APIGen (remove hallucinated tool calls) → 4.8k trajectories; split 900 for GRPO.  
2. Run CoT generation pipeline on all 4.8k trajectories (compute‑intensive).  
3. Stage 1 SFT (Qwen2.5‑Instruct‑32B → PA1).  
4. Stage 2 SFT (PA1 → PA2).  
5. Stage 3 GRPO (PA2 → PA3).  
6. Evaluate on τ‑Bench and QA test.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- CoT generation cost: multi‑round refinement improves quality but requires many LLM calls; could reduce rounds or use cheaper evaluators.  
- Reward scaling: R_final = R_format + R_turn + R_policy − P_hallucination − P_policy_len; ranges are heuristic (e.g., R_format∈{0,1}, R_turn∈[-3,3]). Tweaking weights alters balance between accuracy and conciseness.  
- Training data size: Only 4.8k trajectories; limited coverage. Synthetic augmentation possible but risks noise.  
- Model size: Base model is 32B; larger models may internalize better but increase cost. Method is model‑agnostic but hyper‑parameters may need adjustment.  
- Length penalty thresholds: L_soft=100, L_hard=250 (words). Too low may truncate valid reasoning; too high may not curb verbosity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low QA accuracy (<50%) on policy knowledge test: model failed to internalize policies; revisit Stage 2 or CoT quality.  
- High hallucination rate during GRPO (P_hallucination near 1): model invents policies; strengthen hallucination penalty or improve CoT filtering.  
- Low PolicyRecall Jaccard (near 0): model not recalling relevant policies; need more CoT training data or adjust reward scaling.  
- Excessive CoT length (frequent length penalty activation): model verbose; check if reasoning genuinely requires more steps or if penalty thresholds are too high.  
- Large performance gap between airline (OOD) and retail (in‑distribution): suggests generalization issues; need more diverse data or domain adaptation.  
- Many CoTs discarded during generation (low acceptance rate): thresholds too strict or base generator weak; consider relaxing thresholds or stronger generator.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **QA Knowledge Test:** Run the 500 yes/no questions on PA2 and PA3. Expected: PA3 ~71.4% > PA2 ~69%. Validates policy internalization.  
2. **CoT Filtering Ablation:** Train a PA2 variant with unfiltered (first‑round) CoTs and compare τ‑Bench pass@1 to filtered PA2. Expected: filtered PA2 higher, confirming pipeline value.  
3. **Policy Override Test:** Use the synthetic override dataset to evaluate if the model can follow in‑context overridden policies. Expected PA3 ~53%; lower scores indicate need for better instruction‑following integration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1:** Does PA3 truly generalize to new domains beyond τ‑Bench?  
*Basis:* Conclusion states: "SFT improves policy recall on familiar domains but is prone to memorization; RL improves task performance over more OOD data, though whether this constitutes true generalization beyond the evaluated domains remains an open question for future work."  
*Why unresolved:* Evaluation is limited to τ‑Bench's airline and retail domains; transfer to other business domains with different policy structures is unknown.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Evaluate PA3 on additional diverse policy‑based benchmarks and measure performance consistency.

**Open Question 2:** Can compressing reasoning traces into latent tokens maintain policy adherence while reducing output token cost?  
*Basis:* Limitations note: "Future work could compress these reasoning traces into latent tokens, following recent approaches (Cheng and Durme, 2024), although this direction is beyond the scope of this work."  
*Why unresolved:* CoT generation increases output tokens; latent compression could lower cost but might lose crucial policy information.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Implement a latent token compression method, train a model with it, and compare τ‑Bench accuracy and token efficiency against original PA3.

**Open Question 3:** How can models adapt to dynamic policy updates without full retraining?  
*Basis:* Limitations state: "training on a fixed set of policies risks overfitting to that specific version, reducing reliability when policies change and requiring frequent retraining. One potential solution is to incorporate additional synthetic policy‑override data during training... We leave this direction for future work."  
*Why unresolved:* Real‑world policies evolve; the current model likely overfits to training versions and may fail when policies are updated, making maintenance costly.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Create a benchmark with policy overrides (like τ‑overrule), evaluate model accuracy on overridden policies, then train with synthetic override data and measure improvement without full retraining.

**Open Question 4:** Does incorporating instruction‑following data improve policy overriding performance?  
*Basis:* Appendix C: "We believe that incorporating instruction‑following datasets during SFT (Stage 2) and GRPO training (Stage 3) would substantially improve the model’s ability to override policies when required. We leave this direction for future work."  
*Why unresolved:* PA3's override accuracy on synthetic τ‑overrule is only 53.33%, barely above random, indicating difficulty in following conflicting policies.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Augment Stage 2 SFT and Stage 3 GRPO with instruction‑following datasets, then re‑evaluate on τ‑overrule for statistically significant gains.

## Limitations
- Unreleased code/data/models and reliance on specific LLM versions (deepseek‑r1, Claude‑3.5‑v1) may hinder faithful reproduction; minor prompt variations could alter outcomes.
- Training data is limited to 4.8k APIGen trajectories, which may restrict coverage and cause overfitting to the specific policy set.
- Reward components and length thresholds are heuristic; their fixed ranges may not generalize to new domains or longer policies.
