# CLARIN-PT-LDB: An Open LLM Leaderboard for Portuguese to assess Language, Culture and Civility

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12872
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12872
- Reference count: 7
- Primary result: CLARIN-PT-LDB, a HuggingFace leaderboard with 10 PT-PT benchmarks, shows that European Portuguese fine-tuning (Gervásio 70B) improves cultural alignment (39.76% vs 25.69% for base Llama 70B) and achieves a safeguards score of 86.9% on DoNotAnswer-PT.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the lack of evaluation resources for European Portuguese (PT-PT) large language models, especially for cultural alignment and safeguards, by introducing CLARIN-PT-LDB. It is an open leaderboard hosted on HuggingFace that aggregates ten benchmarks, including novel culture-alignment (Tuguesice-PT) and safeguards (DoNotAnswer-PT) tasks, alongside translated reasoning and NLU benchmarks. All evaluations are performed generatively via the LM Evaluation Harness to ensure broad model compatibility. Initial results indicate that fine-tuning on PT-PT data (e.g., Gervásio 70B) significantly improves cultural alignment relative to its base model (Llama 70B), establishing a standardized evaluation suite for PT-PT LLMs.

## Method Summary
CLARIN-PT-LDB compiles ten benchmarks: two novel PT-PT-specific tasks (Tuguesice-PT for cultural knowledge QA, DoNotAnswer-PT for safety refusal) and eight translated or adapted benchmarks (e.g., MMLU-PT, GPQA-Diamond-PT). The evaluation pipeline uses the LM Evaluation Harness in a purely generative mode—prompts (defined by YAML and Jinja templates) are sent to the model and the generated text is evaluated via exact match, regex, or classification by a judge model (Llama 3.3 70B Instruct or Gemini 2.5 Flash) for open-ended tasks. The leaderboard is a HuggingFace Space with a backend that schedules submissions, orchestrates evaluation across NVIDIA L40S and RTX Pro 6000 GPU nodes, and updates public results. This design maximizes compatibility (supports open-weight, quantized, LoRA, and API models) and ecological validity by mimicking chatbot deployment.

## Key Results
- CLARIN-PT-LDB provides the first comprehensive open leaderboard for European Portuguese LLMs, featuring 10 benchmarks including novel culture (Tuguesice-PT) and safeguards (DoNotAnswer-PT) assessments.
- Initial evaluation shows Gervásio 70B, fine-tuned on PT-PT data, leads in cultural alignment (39.76% on Tuguesice-PT) compared to its base Llama 70B (25.69%) and achieves a safeguards score of 86.9% on DoNotAnswer-PT; performance generally scales with model size on other benchmarks like MMLU and GPQA Diamond.
- The live leaderboard is accessible at https://huggingface.co/spaces/PORTULAN/portuguese-llm-leaderboard and accepts model submissions via HuggingFace IDs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A centralized, open leaderboard aggregating diverse benchmarks drives focused evaluation and progress for European Portuguese LLMs.
- **Mechanism:** By hosting a unified platform (CLARIN-PT-LDB) that scores models on a fixed suite of ten benchmarks—including novel ones for culture and safeguards—it creates a shared, reproducible reference point. This reduces fragmentation, makes gaps in model performance (e.g., cultural alignment) visible, and incentivizes submissions to address them.
- **Core assumption:** Model developers and users prioritize a single, well-maintained, community-accepted benchmark suite over disparate, siloed evaluations.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "This leaderboard comes as a way to address a gap in the evaluation of LLM for European Portuguese..." [section 1] "online open leaderboards have thus become a de facto standard..." [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., ALBA, AMALIA) indicate a concurrent community need for PT-PT evaluation, suggesting the leaderboard addresses a recognized gap.
- **Break condition:** If the research community adopts an alternative leaderboard or benchmark suite as the primary standard for PT-PT, or if benchmark saturation occurs without new, challenging tasks being added.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Incorporating novel, culturally-specific and safety-focused benchmarks reveals performance dimensions missed by translated generic benchmarks.
- **Mechanism:** The creation of *Tuguesice-PT* (implicit cultural knowledge QA) and *DoNotAnswer-PT* (safeguard refusal) provides targeted evaluation where none existed. Their results (e.g., Gervásio 70B's 39.76% vs. Llama 70B's 25.69% on culture) demonstrate that fine-tuning on European Portuguese data impacts these nuanced dimensions differently than on standard NLU tasks.
- **Core assumption:** Cultural alignment and safety behaviors can be operationalized into specific, measurable QA and refusal tasks that are distinct from general knowledge or reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "novel benchmarks, including some that address aspects of performance that so far have not been available... namely model safeguards and alignment to Portuguese culture." [section 3.1] "To the best of our knowledge, there is currently no benchmark that specifically assesses model alignment to Portuguese culture." [section 3.2] "To the best of our knowledge, there is currently no benchmark in Portuguese that tests the safeguards of the model."
- **Break condition:** If the novel benchmarks show near-ceiling performance across models, indicating they are not discriminative, or if their design is criticized for not truly capturing "culture" or "safeguards."

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A purely generative evaluation protocol (no logit-based ranking) ensures maximum model compatibility and ecological validity for chatbot-style deployment.
- **Mechanism:** By requiring all models to generate open-form text answers (even for multiple-choice), the evaluation uses the same interface as real-world LLM deployment. This allows comparison between open-weight, quantized, LoRA-adapted, and potentially closed/API models under identical conditions, as it only consumes text outputs.
- **Core assumption:** The ability to generate the correct textual answer is a sufficient proxy for capability across all evaluated task types, and this mode of evaluation is more relevant than likelihood-based methods for assessing deployed chatbots.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 1] "We opt for this purely generative approach for three main reasons... this approach more closely matches the way LLMs are now deployed when serving as the model underlying chatbots." [section 4.1] "every task in the leaderboard is evaluated as a fully generative process."
- **Break condition:** If generative evaluation is shown to produce highly unstable or uncomparable results compared to logit-based scoring for multiple-choice tasks, or if it introduces significant judge-model bias for the open-ended benchmarks.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Language Variant Specificity (PT-PT vs. pt-BR)**  
  *Why needed here:* The entire leaderboard is built for European Portuguese. Understanding that linguistic, lexical, and cultural differences exist between PT-PT and Brazilian Portuguese is fundamental to grasping the project's motivation and the need for dedicated resources.  
  *Quick check question:* What is a common lexical difference between European and Brazilian Portuguese for the word "ice cream," and why might a model trained only on Brazilian data struggle with a PT-PT cultural benchmark?

- **Concept: Generative vs. Scoring Evaluation Paradigms**  
  *Why needed here:* The paper's core technical choice is generative evaluation. Understanding the trade-off between generating answers and scoring logits (e.g., perplexity, choice probability) is key to understanding the system's design constraints and compatibility claims.  
  *Quick check question:* For a multiple-choice question, what are the two primary ways to evaluate an LLM's performance, and what is one major advantage of the generative method used in this leaderboard?

- **Concept: Judge Model for Open-Ended Assessment**  
  *Why needed here:* Two benchmarks (*DoNotAnswer-PT*, *AA-Omniscience-Public*) rely on a separate LLM (Llama 3.3 70B Instruct, Gemini 2.5 Flash) to classify answers. This introduces a dependency and potential bias. One must understand the role and risk of using a "judge model."  
  *Quick check question:* Why can't an exact match metric be used for the *DoNotAnswer-PT* benchmark, and what role does the judge model play instead?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Frontend (HuggingFace Space) -> Backend Scheduler/Orchestrator -> Evaluation Engine (LM Evaluation Harness fork) -> Task Configurations (YAML + Jinja) -> Judge Models (External) -> Infrastructure (NVIDIA GPUs)

### Critical path (Model Submission to Score Display)
1. User submits a HuggingFace model ID via frontend form.
2. Backend schedules a run, loading the model into the Evaluation Engine.
3. For each benchmark, the Engine iterates through its test set: formats a prompt using the benchmark's Jinja template, generates text from the model, parses the output (exact match, regex, or sends to a Judge Model), records a pass/fail or judge category.
4. After all instances, aggregates per-benchmark accuracy (or other metric).
5. Backend updates the results database/table.
6. Frontend automatically refreshes to show new scores.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Generative vs. Logit Scoring:** Chose generative for maximum compatibility and realism, sacrificing potential efficiency and maybe slight accuracy for multiple-choice.
- **Automatic Translation vs. Native Creation:** For most benchmarks, used Google Cloud Advanced Translation API for scalability. Tradeoff: risk of translational nuance loss vs. manual cost. Only *Tuguesice-PT* was created natively from scratch for cultural validity.
- **Judge Model Reliability:** Using a powerful LLM as a judge enables open-ended evaluation but introduces a new, opaque model as a point of failure and potential bias. The choice of judge (Llama 3.3 70B) is not justified against alternatives.

### Failure signatures
- **Model Load Failure:** Model ID not found, incompatible format, or exceeds GPU memory. Check HuggingFace Hub accessibility and model repo structure.
- **Task Configuration Error:** Prompt template renders incorrectly (Jinja syntax error) or output parsing regex fails to extract answer. Manually inspect a few generated prompts/answers for a given task config.
- **Judge Model Down/Biased:** For *DoNotAnswer-PT* and *Omniscience*, if the judge model API is unavailable or consistently misclassifies (e.g., always outputs "answer"), scores become invalid. Requires monitoring judge model health and potentially a fallback judge.
- **Translation Artifact:** For translated benchmarks, atypical phrasing might confuse models fine-tuned on native PT-PT text. Compare a sample of translated instances against the source English to spot obvious errors.

### First 3 experiments (for a new engineer)
1. **Local Dry Run of One Task:** Clone the LM Evaluation Harness fork, pick one benchmark's YAML config (e.g., `mmlu_pt.yaml`), and run it locally on a small model (e.g., `Gervásio 8B`) with `--limit 5`. Verify prompts look correct and answers parse. This confirms your environment and understanding of the config format.
2. **Submit a Dummy Model:** Create a minimal, valid HuggingFace model repo (e.g., a small 1-parameter model with a `config.json` and `model.safetensors`). Submit it via the frontend. Trace its entire path: submission -> backend queue -> evaluation -> result update. This tests the full pipeline integration.
3. **Judge Model Sanity Check:** For *DoNotAnswer-PT*, manually craft 5 known "safe" (should be refusal) and 5 known "unsafe" (should be answer) prompts. Run them through the judge model using the exact prompt template from Figure 1. Verify the judge's classifications align with your expectations. This isolates the judge component.

## Limitations
- Translation artifacts from Google Cloud API may distort benchmark difficulty for PT-PT-native models.
- Dependency on external judge models (Llama 3.3 70B, Gemini 2.5 Flash) introduces unmeasured bias in open-ended evaluations.
- Long-term discriminative power of *Tuguesice-PT* is unknown; risk of rapid saturation without continuous native item creation.

## Confidence
- **High:** The leaderboard exists, aggregates 10 benchmarks, and consistently reports scores; Gervásio 70B leads on novel culture/safeguard metrics.
- **Medium:** Novel benchmarks (*Tuguesice-PT*, *DoNotAnswer-PT*) measure previously unassessed PT-PT dimensions; fine-tuning impact is inferred from limited model comparisons.
- **Low:** Generative evaluation is equivalently valid and reliable as logit-based scoring for multiple-choice tasks; no empirical comparison provided.

## Next Checks
1. **Translation artifact audit:** Sample 50 translated instances from MMLU-PT/GPQA-PT; have native PT-PT linguists rate naturalness against natively written *Tuguesice-PT* items.
2. **Judge model robustness test:** Re-score *DoNotAnswer-PT* predictions using two alternative judges (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet); measure classification agreement with the current Llama-based judge.
3. **Saturation probe:** Re-run *Tuguesice-PT* on all top-5 models submitted to the leaderboard after Q3 2024; report score distribution and ceiling effect (>90% accuracy on any model).
