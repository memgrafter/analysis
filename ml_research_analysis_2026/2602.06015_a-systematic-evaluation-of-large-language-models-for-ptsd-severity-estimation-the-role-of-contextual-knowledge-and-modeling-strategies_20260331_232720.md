# A Systematic Evaluation of Large Language Models for PTSD Severity Estimation: The Role of Contextual Knowledge and Modeling Strategies

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.06015
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06015
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Systematic evaluation of 11 LLMs on 1,437 clinical transcripts; accuracy improved with detailed symptom definitions and direct score prediction; best ensemble (r=0.565); frontier (GPT-5) and large open (LLaMA-3.1-70B) models exceeded trained human raters (r=0.44).

## Executive Summary
This study assesses the capability of large language models (LLMs) to estimate PTSD severity from clinical interview transcripts, using self-reported PCL-5 scores as ground truth. By systematically varying the contextual knowledge provided to models (e.g., symptom definitions, interview context) and modeling strategies (e.g., zero/few-shot, direct vs. subscale prediction, reasoning steps, redistribution, ensembling), the authors identify factors that enhance predictive accuracy. Key findings indicate that detailed construct definitions and direct scalar prediction yield significant improvements. Frontier models like GPT-5 benefit from increased reasoning effort, while open-weight models plateau beyond 70B parameters. An ensemble combining a supervised RoBERTa model with zero-shot LLM predictions achieves the best performance, surpassing individual LLMs and trained human raters. The work highlights both the promise and current limits of LLMs in clinical severity estimation.

## Method Summary
The evaluation uses 1,437 transcribed clinical interviews from World Trade Center responders, each paired with a PCL-5 score (range 17–85). Eleven LLMs are tested in zero-shot and few-shot conditions via modular prompts: core instructions are constant, while optional “plug-ins” supply varying contextual knowledge (symptom definitions, interview questions, distributional priors). Modeling strategies include direct score prediction versus subscale aggregation, predictive redistribution (Anscombe transform to correct variance shrinkage), and ensemble averaging. A supervised RoBERTa regression model is trained on the same data as a baseline. Performance is measured by Pearson correlation (r) and mean absolute error (MAE) against self-reported PCL scores, with bootstrap resampling (n=1,000) for confidence intervals. No LLM training occurs; all LLM evaluations are inference-only.

## Key Results
- Detailed construct definitions and direct scalar prediction significantly improve accuracy over subscale-based approaches.
- Frontier models (GPT-5, o3-mini) reduce error with increased reasoning effort (Think-Step-By-Step); open-weight models show mixed or negative responses to reasoning prompts.
- Open-weight model performance plateaus beyond 70B parameters; frontier models continue to scale.
- Best performance (r=0.565, MAE=6.81) achieved by ensembling supervised RoBERTa with zero-shot LLM predictions.
- GPT-5 (r=0.59) and LLaMA-3.1-70B (r=0.53) surpass the accuracy of trained human raters (r=0.44).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not provide a mechanistic analysis of how LLMs internally arrive at PTSD severity scores. It attributes accuracy gains to external factors: richer contextual knowledge (e.g., explicit symptom definitions) grounds the model’s estimation in clinical constructs, while direct prediction avoids error propagation from subscale aggregation. Frontier models’ responsiveness to reasoning steps suggests advanced chain-of-thought capabilities, though the specific cognitive pathways remain unexamined. The study focuses on empirical performance under controlled conditions rather than interpretability.

## Foundational Learning
- **PCL-5 as ground truth**: Why needed: Provides standardized, validated PTSD severity metric. Quick check: Verify score distribution (mean, SD) and internal consistency (Cronbach’s α) in the dataset.
- **Zero/Few-shot prompting**: Why needed: Elicit model knowledge without gradient updates. Quick check: Run minimal prompt (instructions only) vs. 3-shot with exemplars; measure Δr.
- **Direct vs. subscale prediction**: Why needed: Direct prediction avoids aggregation bias and simplifies modeling. Quick check: Prompt model for full score vs. sum of subscale scores; compare correlations.
- **Predictive redistribution**: Why needed: Corrects LLM output variance shrinkage toward the mean. Quick check: Apply Anscombe transform (√(y+3/8)) then remap to empirical PCL mean/SD; compare raw vs. redistributed distributions.
- **Ensemble averaging**: Why needed: Combines complementary model strengths (e.g., supervised fine-tuning vs. zero-shot general knowledge). Quick check: Average predictions from RoBERTa and best zero-shot LLM; evaluate against individual models.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Transcript -> Prompt Builder (core + optional knowledge plug-ins) -> LLM Inference (with reasoning setting) -> JSON Output -> Parser -> Raw Score -> [Optional: Predictive Redistributor] -> [Optional: Ensemble Aggregator] -> Final Score
- **Critical path**: Ensure prompt fidelity (exact wording, JSON schema), handle parsing failures (<0.5% of outputs), apply redistribution with correct dataset statistics (µ, σ), and maintain deterministic inference (temperature=0).
- **Design tradeoffs**: Zero-shot simplicity vs. few-shot noise; direct prediction efficiency vs. subscale interpretability; TSBS may introduce irrelevant reasoning or hallucination.
- **Failure signatures**: Malformed JSON outputs (exclude or recover); overly narrow score distributions (high kurtosis) indicating mean shrinkage; inconsistent few-shot gains across models; performance drops when adding conflicting knowledge plug-ins.
- **First experiments**: 1) Replicate zero-shot baseline using exact prompt templates (Figs. S4, S6) and verify initial r/MAE. 2) Isolate each knowledge plug-in (e.g., symptom definitions, interview context) to confirm individual contribution. 3) Validate predictive redistribution by comparing raw LLM outputs to redistributed outputs against ground truth distribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to other populations**: The sample is exclusively WTC responders and uses a single interview format; it is unclear if findings hold for other trauma-exposed groups or conversational settings. Resolution requires multi-site, diverse-population validation.
2. **Multimodal signal incorporation**: The study uses text-only input; acoustic prosody or facial affect may add unique information. Resolution demands controlled experiments augmenting text with automatically extracted multimodal features.
3. **Internal reasoning alignment**: No analysis of how LLMs conceptualize PTSD or whether their “reasoning” aligns with human clinicians. Resolution needs interpretability studies (e.g., chain-of-thought analysis, attention visualization) compared to human think-aloud protocols.
4. **Inconsistent TSBS effects**: Think-Step-By-Step helps some models but harms others; cause (e.g., spurious explanations) is unknown. Resolution requires qualitative/quantitative analysis of generated reasoning chains and systematic variation of reasoning prompt structure.
5. **Open-weight scaling plateau**: Performance of Llama/DeepSeek models plateaus beyond 70B while frontier models scale; reasons (data, architecture, fine-tuning) are uninvestigated. Resolution needs comparative analysis of training regimes and probing at intermediate scales.

## Limitations
- Exact prompt wording, JSON schema, and redistribution parameters (dataset µ, σ) are not fully specified, hindering faithful replication.
- Supervised RoBERTa baseline details (architecture, hyperparameters, trained weights) are unavailable.
- Human rater calibration protocol (feedback mechanism, discussion protocol) is insufficiently described.
- Potential bias from exclusion of malformed outputs (<0.5%) is not analyzed.
- Single-site, homogenous cohort (92.6% male, WTC responders) limits external validity.
