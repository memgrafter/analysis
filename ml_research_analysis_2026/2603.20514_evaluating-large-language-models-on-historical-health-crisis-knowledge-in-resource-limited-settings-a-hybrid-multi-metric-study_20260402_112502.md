# Evaluating Large Language Models on Historical Health Crisis Knowledge in Resource-Limited Settings: A Hybrid Multi-Metric Study

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.20514
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20514
- **Reference Count**: 15
- **Primary Result**: No model achieved high inter-model consistency on any question, with all 18 high-divergence questions originating from the COVID-19 domain; Mistral-7B achieved the highest Hybrid Factual Score (0.5068) and lowest Hallucination Risk Score (0.4786).

## Executive Summary
This study introduces a hybrid multi-metric framework to evaluate large language models (GPT-4, Gemini Pro, Llama 3, Mistral-7B) on their factual recall of historical health crisis information specific to Bangladesh. Using a dataset of 100 questions across four diseases (COVID-19, Dengue, Nipah, Chikungunya), the analysis combines semantic similarity, NLI-based factual grounding, and LLM-as-a-Judge scoring. Key findings reveal significant factual divergence, especially for COVID-19, high contradiction rates in NLI (25–43%), and distinct model failure modes. The work underscores the challenges of relying on vanilla LLMs for precise health knowledge in resource-limited settings and advocates for multi-faceted evaluation and retrieval-augmented approaches.

## Method Summary
The study constructed a dataset of 100 questions (25 per disease) by prompting models with authoritative WHO and journal passages. Each of the four models then answered all questions via API with standardized prompts. A hybrid evaluation pipeline computed three core metrics: (1) pairwise semantic similarity (cosine) using `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` embeddings to measure inter-model consensus, (2) sentence-level NLI classification (`facebook/bart-large-mnli`) against reference passages (Entailment/Contradiction/Neutral), and (3) LLM-as-a-Judge scoring (1–5 scale) for reasoning, factuality, hallucination, and completeness. These were normalized and weighted into composite scores: Hybrid Factual Score (HFS) and Hallucination Risk Score (HRS). Results were stratified by disease domain and analyzed for correlations.

## Key Results
- No model achieved high inter-model consistency (cosine similarity ≥0.85) on any question.
- All 18 high-divergence questions (lowest inter-model similarity) originated exclusively from the COVID-19 domain.
- Mistral-7B achieved the highest Hybrid Factual Score (HFS=0.5068) and lowest Hallucination Risk Score (HRS=0.4786).
- NLI analysis revealed high contradiction rates across all models (25–43%), with only 5.8% of sentences entailing the reference text.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Domain-specific volatility (e.g., rapidly evolving COVID-19 information) drives higher factual divergence and lower inter-model consistency.
- **Mechanism**: LLMs encode temporally inconsistent knowledge from pre-training corpora. For fast-evolving crises, conflicting guidance in source data leads to heterogeneous internal representations, causing divergent outputs.
- **Core assumption**: Model outputs reflect aggregated, often contradictory, textual distributions in training data rather than a coherent factual timeline.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - All 18 high-divergence questions originated exclusively from the COVID-19 domain.
  - The elevated divergence likely reflects inconsistencies in training data composition, differing knowledge cutoffs, and the volume of evolving pandemic guidance in pre-training corpora.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Alignment tuning (e.g., RLHF) creates divergent response strategies: some models become overconfident (low entailment, high contradiction), while others are highly variable.
- **Mechanism**: RLHF rewards confident, helpful responses. In historical recall with partial evidence, this may push models toward assertive statements unsupported by references. Variation in alignment objectives leads to divergent hallucination profiles.
- **Core assumption**: The observed bimodal pattern (e.g., Gemini Pro: high entailment + high contradiction) reflects underlying differences in how models balance fluency, certainty, and conservatism.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - GPT-4 exhibited a zero entailment rate alongside elevated contradiction levels.
  - RLHF may inadvertently encourage overcommitment to definitive answers at the expense of conservative factual grounding.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Multi-metric hybrid evaluation (NLI + similarity + judge) captures complementary failure modes that single metrics miss, improving robustness.
- **Mechanism**: Semantic similarity measures model agreement but not truthfulness; NLI provides reference-grounded verification; LLM-as-Judge assesses reasoning quality. Combining them mitigates individual weaknesses, yielding a more stable composite score (HFS).
- **Core assumption**: Metrics are partially independent; NLI contradiction and judge hallucination scores capture distinct dimensions.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - The near-zero correlation between NLI contradiction rates and judge hallucination scores (Pearson r = 0.015) confirms that the metrics capture distinct dimensions of model behaviour.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Natural Language Inference (NLI) for factual grounding**
  - **Why needed here**: NLI classifies whether a model's sentence is entailed by, contradicts, or is neutral relative to an authoritative source, directly measuring factual alignment at the sentence level, which is critical for health knowledge where precision matters.
  - **Quick check**: If a model response says "Dengue outbreaks peak during monsoon" and the reference states "Dengue transmission increases after heavy rainfall," an NLI model would likely label this as **Neutral** because the timing ("peak during monsoon" vs. "after heavy rainfall") is not directly entailed or contradicted by the reference's causal relationship.

- **Concept: Semantic similarity as a proxy for inter-model consensus**
  - **Why needed here**: Cosine similarity between model embeddings indicates how similarly models interpret a question. Low consistency suggests divergent internal knowledge representations, flagging potential hallucination hotspots.
  - **Quick check**: Two models give different answers to "What was Bangladesh's dengue death toll in 2023?" but both contain the correct number. Their response embeddings would likely show **high cosine similarity** because the correct numerical fact dominates the semantic representation, implying similarity alone cannot detect subtle factual inaccuracies in surrounding context.

- **Concept: Hybrid scoring (HFS/HRS) as a composite reliability metric**
  - **Why needed here**: No single metric captures all aspects of factual reliability. HFS blends NLI, reference similarity, and judge scores to balance groundedness, consistency, and reasoning quality, providing a single comparative number.
  - **Quick check**: If Model A has high reference similarity but low NLI entailment (plausible but unsupported), and Model B has moderate scores on both, Model B would likely have a higher HFS. The weighting matters because high reference similarity without entailment suggests the model's phrasing matches the reference style but not the facts, which the HFS formula penalizes via the NLI component.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Question Generation (from passages) -> Response Collection (API calls) -> Evaluation Pipeline (Embedding similarity, NLI classification, LLM-as-Judge scoring) -> Hybrid Scoring Engine (HFS/HRS calculation) -> Analysis & Stratification (by disease, correlation checks)
- **Critical path**: Question generation (Phase 1) → response collection (Phase 2) → NLI/similarity/judge evaluation (Phase 3) → hybrid scoring → statistical analysis. Phase 1 errors propagate; Phase 3 depends on stable NLI/judge models.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Closed vs. open models: Using proprietary models (GPT-4, Gemini) for judge scoring limits reproducibility; open judges (Llama-3) could substitute but may lower assessment quality.
  - English-only prompts: Excludes Bangladeshi-language contexts, possibly biasing against local knowledge; tradeoff is dataset control.
  - Static references: References are fixed passages, but real health knowledge evolves; tradeoff is simplicity vs. temporal validity.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High divergence in volatile domains (COVID-19) → indicates training data inconsistencies.
  - Bimodal judge scores (Gemini Pro) → model instability; requires variance analysis.
  - Zero entailment rate (GPT-4) → overconfident generation; check NLI contradiction vs. judge hallucination correlation.
  - Low HFS stability (high σ) → inconsistent performance across questions; inspect per-domain HFS.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Reproduce the semantic similarity matrix using `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` on stored responses; verify Table 1 values and confirm all 18 high-divergence questions are from COVID-19.
  2. Validate NLI distribution by processing a random sample of 50 responses with `facebook/bart-large-mnli`; confirm overall contradiction ~32.8% and model-specific patterns (Gemini Pro's bimodal entailment/contradiction, GPT-4's zero entailment).
  3. Test HFS sensitivity by recomputing with equal weights (1/3 each) vs. the paper's (0.5, 0.3, 0.2); observe ranking changes, especially for models with high judge variance (Gemini Pro).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do LLMs perform on historical health crisis queries in multilingual settings, particularly in Bengali for Bangladesh? The current study only used English prompts, limiting applicability to non-English speaking regions.
- Can retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) architectures reduce hallucination and improve factual grounding for LLMs in low-resource health contexts? Observed high contradiction rates indicate poor grounding without external knowledge sources.
- How do LLMs manage factual consistency for rapidly evolving health crises compared to stable endemic diseases? The study identifies the pattern (all high-divergence from COVID-19) but does not isolate causal factors like training data temporal composition.
- What is the reliability of LLMs for health policy decision-support tasks beyond historical recall, such as simulating outbreak scenarios or resource allocation trade-offs? The study assessed factual recall only; policy reasoning requires synthesis and multi-factor trade-offs not evaluated.
- How do longitudinal knowledge timelines affect LLM accuracy and consistency in health crisis domains? The study used static reference passages; real-world health knowledge evolves, and models may conflate outdated information.

## Limitations
- Missing LLM-as-Judge prompts/scores and unspecified hyperparameters (temperature, token limits) block precise replication of the hybrid scoring.
- The evaluation uses proprietary judge models and English-only prompts, limiting reproducibility and generalizability to Bangladeshi-language contexts.
- Static reference passages may not capture the evolving nature of health guidance, potentially underrepresenting real-world volatility.

## Confidence
- **Volatility-driven divergence (Mechanism 1)**: Medium. Strong internal evidence (all high-divergence from COVID-19), but break condition (divergence in other fast-evolving domains) remains untested.
- **Alignment tuning divergence (Mechanism 2)**: Low. Evidence rests on model-specific NLI/judge patterns; no direct corpus backing; requires comparative alignment studies.
- **Multi-metric complementarity (Mechanism 3)**: High. Reported near-zero correlation between NLI contradiction and judge hallucination supports independence; analogous multi-attribute benchmarks exist.
