# In-Context Function Learning in Large Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11863
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11863
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LLMs approach Gaussian Process regression lower bounds as demonstrations increase, with inductive biases shifting with input dimension and steerable via parameter-efficient post-training.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a rigorous framework for quantifying in-context function learning in large language models (LLMs) using Gaussian Process (GP) priors to generate controlled regression tasks. By comparing LLM prediction error to an empirical GP regression lower bound and a 1-nearest-neighbor upper bound, the authors show that LLMs efficiently approximate Bayesian inference as the number of demonstrations grows, with performance scaling with model size. A likelihood-based analysis reveals that LLMs possess an implicit inductive bias toward rougher kernels (e.g., Matérn ½) in low dimensions and smoother kernels (e.g., Squared Exponential) in higher dimensions. Furthermore, parameter-efficient post-training methods like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) can steer these biases toward a target kernel, with GRPO yielding more generalizable improvements. The work establishes LLMs as near-GP learners and demonstrates a pathway to aligning their function-learning priors with desired statistical properties.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates LLMs on a battery of multivariate regression tasks where target functions are sampled from GP priors (Matérn and Squared Exponential kernels) with varying length scales and input dimensions. For each function, 50 noisy (x, y) pairs are generated. At inference, the model is prompted with the first *n* demonstration pairs and a new input *x*, and must predict the corresponding *y*. Model performance is measured by mean absolute error (MAE) as *n* increases, and is compared against two analytical baselines: the error of an empirical GP regressor (lower bound) and the expected error of a 1-nearest-neighbor predictor (upper bound). To characterize inductive bias, the log-likelihood of each model prediction under the posterior predictive distribution of different GP kernels is computed; the kernel that maximizes the average likelihood is taken as the model's implicit prior. Post-training experiments fine-tune a base model (Qwen-3-8B) using QLoRA adapters on data from a target kernel (Squared Exponential, ℓ=8), using either SFT (cross-entropy) or GRPO (reward based on prediction error or GP likelihood). The effects of post-training are evaluated by changes in learning curves and inductive bias profiles on held-out kernels.

## Key Results
- LLM prediction error decreases and converges toward the GP regression lower bound as the number of in-context demonstrations increases, with larger models showing faster convergence.
- LLMs exhibit a dimension-dependent inductive bias: in 1D they favor rougher kernels (Matérn ½), but as input dimension increases, their predictions become more probable under smoother kernels (Squared Exponential).
- Parameter-efficient post-training (QLoRA) shifts the model's inductive bias toward the smoothness of the training kernel, with GRPO producing more generalizable improvements than SFT across both seen and unseen kernels.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LLMs approximate the performance of Bayesian GP regression as the number of in-context demonstrations increases.
- **Mechanism:** The transformer's attention mechanism, when processing demonstration pairs, effectively computes a kernel-weighted average that converges to the GP posterior mean. This implicit algorithm mirrors the statistical optimality of GP regression under the implicit prior.
- **Core assumption:** The forward pass of a frozen LLM implements a deterministic statistical inference procedure analogous to GP regression.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Results show LLMs approach the GP lower bound as demonstrations increase."
  - [section 2.1]: "We evaluate prediction error... compare against... an empirical GP-regression learner that gives a lower bound."
  - [corpus]: Neighbors like "In-Context Learning through the Bayesian Prism" frame ICL as Bayesian inference; this work provides empirical convergence evidence.
- **Break condition:** If LLM error remains persistently above the GP bound with many demonstrations, the architecture fails to approximate Bayes-optimal inference for that kernel.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** LLMs exhibit a systematic inductive bias favoring rougher (Matérn ½) kernels in low dimensions and smoother (Squared Exponential) kernels in higher dimensions.
- **Mechanism:** By computing the log-likelihood of the model’s point prediction under the posterior predictive distribution of different GP kernels, the kernel with the highest aggregated likelihood reveals the model’s implicit prior over function smoothness.
- **Core assumption:** The GP kernel family that maximizes predictive likelihood best characterizes the model’s inductive bias for continuous function learning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "likelihood-based analysis reveals LLMs are inductively biased toward rougher kernels (e.g., Matérn ½) in low dimensions and smoother kernels (e.g., Squared Exponential) in higher dimensions."
  - [section 3.2]: Describes computing log-likelihoods for each prediction under various kernels and aggregating.
  - [corpus]: Methodology adapted from cognitive science (Griffiths et al., 2008) for inferring human inductive biases.
- **Break condition:** If likelihood rankings are inconsistent across random seeds or do not scale monotonically with kernel smoothness, the bias measurement is unreliable.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Parameter-efficient post-training (SFT, GRPO) can steer the model’s inductive bias toward the kernel used in the training curriculum.
- **Mechanism:** Updating low-rank adapters (QLoRA) on a dataset of in-context regression tasks from a target kernel shifts the model’s internal representations such that its predictions become more probable under that kernel’s posterior. GRPO, using a reward signal, yields more generalizable shifts than SFT.
- **Core assumption:** Modifying only a small subset of parameters suffices to alter the model’s effective prior over functions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "parameter-efficient post-training (SFT, GRPO) shifts these biases toward the training kernel’s smoothness, with GRPO showing more generalizable improvements."
  - [section 3.3]: Details SFT (cross-entropy) and GRPO (reward = negative absolute error or GP log-likelihood) with QLoRA adapters.
  - [section 4.3] & [figure 6]: Show learning curves improve and likelihood profiles shift toward the training kernel.
- **Break condition:** If post-training does not change likelihood rankings or degrades performance on held-out kernels, the adaptation fails.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Gaussian Process (GP) Regression
  - **Why needed:** The paper’s entire evaluation framework relies on GP priors to generate functions, compute the empirical lower bound (GP regression), and define posterior predictive distributions for likelihood-based bias analysis.
  - **Quick check:** What two hyperparameters of a GP kernel primarily control the smoothness and correlation scale of sampled functions? (Answer: smoothness parameter ν and length scale ℓ.)
- **Concept:** Inductive Bias
  - **Why needed:** The core inquiry is what implicit assumptions LLMs make about continuous functions—i.e., which function classes they learn more sample-efficiently. Measuring and steering this bias is the paper’s goal.
  - **Quick check:** Why might a bias toward rougher kernels be problematic for real-world regression tasks? (Answer: Many real-world phenomena are smooth; a rough bias leads to overfitting or high-variance predictions.)
- **Concept:** In-Context Learning (ICL)
  - **Why needed:** The paper studies ICL as the mechanism by which LLMs adapt to new function tasks at inference time without weight updates. All experiments condition on demonstration sequences.
  - **Quick check:** How does ICL differ from gradient-based meta-learning? (Answer: ICL occurs within a single forward pass through a frozen model; meta-learning updates model parameters over many tasks.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Generator -> Prompt Builder -> LLM Engine; baselines (GP regression, 1-NN) and analysis modules run in parallel; post-training modules (QLoRA, SFT/GRPO) modify LLM Engine.
- **Critical path:** 1) Choose kernel family, length scale, input dimension. 2) Generate N functions, each with 50 noisy (x,y) pairs. 3) For each n in [0,49]: for each function, take first n pairs as demonstrations, query LLM for next x, record error. 4) Aggregate errors → learning curve; compute GP/1-NN bounds. 5) For inductive bias: for each prediction, compute likelihood under each kernel posterior → average. 6) For post-training: sample training set from target kernel; fine-tune adapters (SFT/GRPO); repeat steps 3–5 on held-out data.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Kernel family:** Matérn ν=½ (rough) vs. SE (smooth) provide clear contrast; more kernels increase coverage but complicate analysis.
  - **Model scale:** Larger models (32B) show better ICL but are costlier; quantization (4-bit) speeds inference at potential precision loss.
  - **Post-training method:** SFT is stable and fast but may overfit; GRPO is sample-efficient and generalizes better but requires careful reward shaping.
  - **Dimensionality:** Higher d tests scalability; LLM performance remains stable but inductive bias shifts toward smoother kernels.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Learning curves:** LLM error > 1-NN bound suggests failure to generalize beyond nearest retrieval; no downward trend with n indicates ICL is not occurring.
  - **Inductive bias:** Negative or near-zero log-likelihood differences across kernels imply predictions are indistinguishable from random; high variance across seeds indicates unreliable bias estimate.
  - **Post-training:** After SFT, likelihood on training kernel improves but drops on other kernels → overfitting; after GRPO, reward does not increase → optimization failure.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Learning curves:** Compare Qwen-3-8B/14B/32B on 1D functions from 4 kernels (Matérn½, SE(ℓ=1), SE(ℓ=8)), plot error vs. n against GP and 1-NN bounds.
  2. **Inductive bias:** For each model and dimension (1D–4D), compute average log-likelihood of predictions under GP posteriors with kernels (Matérn ν∈{½, 3/2, 5/2}, SE ℓ∈{1..8}); identify which kernel maximizes likelihood.
  3. **Post-training:** Fine-tune Qwen-3-8B via SFT and GRPO on SE(ℓ=8) data (2k steps); measure change in learning curves and likelihood profiles on held-out kernels (including Matérn½).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can the variance inflation effect in the likelihood-based inductive bias analysis be properly corrected to accurately infer LLMs' true functional priors?
  - **Basis:** The authors explicitly identify a "variance inflation problem" and propose a heuristic τ²-adjustment, stating future work will explore alternatives.
  - **Why unresolved:** The τ²-adjustment is partial and not fully validated across kernels and dimensions; theoretically grounded correction is lacking.
  - **Resolving evidence:** A correction method that recovers known biases of synthetic GP predictors across diverse kernel families, validated on both LLMs and Bayesian models.
- **Open Question 2:** To what extent do the observed in-context function learning capabilities and inductive biases generalize from controlled synthetic GP tasks to real-world, ecologically valid problems like user interaction or iterative coding?
  - **Basis:** Authors cite as a key limitation that generalization to ecologically valid ICL settings is unclear.
  - **Why unresolved:** The synthetic GP framework isolates specific statistical properties but may not capture complexity, noise structures, or sequential dependencies of real tasks.
  - **Resolving evidence:** Applying the same analytic framework to a diverse battery of real-world ICL tasks (e.g., API usage, debugging, dialogue state tracking) to test persistence of patterns like rough-kernel bias.
- **Open Question 3:** What is the mechanistic origin of the dimension-dependent shift in inductive bias—from rough kernels (Matérn ½) in low dimensions to smooth kernels (Squared Exponential) in higher dimensions?
  - **Basis:** Paper documents the empirical finding but speculates on causes (attention features or pre-training data properties) without testing.
  - **Why unresolved:** Work is correlational; causal factors (architectural interventions, curated pre-training data) are not manipulated.
  - **Resolving evidence:** (a) Controlled pre-training with functions from a fixed kernel family across dimensions, then testing on GP framework. (b) Ablation studies modifying transformer architectures to isolate driving components.
- **Open Question 4:** Can post-training (SFT/GRPO) successfully steer inductive biases for function classes beyond stationary kernels, such as periodic, compositional, or non-Gaussian processes?
  - **Basis:** Authors restrict to Matérn and SE, stating periodic/compositional kernels are beyond scope due to interpretability and parameter complexity concerns.
  - **Why unresolved:** Success on stationary kernels does not guarantee transferability to fundamentally different structures (e.g., oscillatory, hierarchical).
  - **Resolving evidence:** Replicate post-training with functions from periodic or additive compositional kernels; measure if SFT/GRPO can shift predictions toward these target kernels and improve learning curves.
- **Open Question 5:** Do the observed benefits of GRPO over SFT for generalizing inductive biases hold consistently across higher-dimensional function spaces and when the training kernel's smoothness is held constant?
  - **Basis:** Paper suggests GRPO generalizes better based on 1D experiments; all post-training was in 1D except brief supplementary results. Authors note further research needed on scaling and dimensionality.
  - **Why unresolved:** The SFT vs. GRPO trade-off (memorization vs. generalization) might change with task complexity in higher dimensions.
  - **Resolving evidence:** Run SFT and GRPO on models learning 2D, 3D, and 4D functions from a fixed target kernel (e.g., SE). Evaluate learning curves and bias shifts on held-out functions from a different kernel family (e.g., Matérn 3/2) to test cross-kernel generalization across dimensions.

## Limitations
- Missing SFT/GRPO hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, generations N) hinders exact replication of post-training bias-steering.
- Likelihood-based inductive bias inference may be confounded by posterior variance; the proposed τ² adjustment lacks full validation.
- Experiments are restricted to synthetic GP data; real-world generalizability of findings is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **LLMs approach GP lower bound with more demonstrations:** High. Scaling behavior and optimal baseline comparison are robust.
- **Dimension-dependent inductive bias (rough in low-d, smooth in high-d):** Medium. Relies on aggregated likelihood; potential artifacts from variance differences remain.
- **Post-training steering (GRPO > SFT):** Medium. Results are persuasive but hyperparameter opacity and limited dimensionality testing reduce certainty.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate post-training experiments with full disclosure of SFT/GRPO hyperparameters and multi-seed evaluation to confirm the bias shift toward the training kernel.
2. Validate the τ²-adjusted likelihood method on synthetic data where the true kernel is known and posterior variance differs, testing robustness to variance inflation across kernel families.
3. Extend evaluation to non-stationary kernels (e.g., spectral mixture) and standard real regression datasets (UCI) to assess external validity of the GP framework and bias measurements.
