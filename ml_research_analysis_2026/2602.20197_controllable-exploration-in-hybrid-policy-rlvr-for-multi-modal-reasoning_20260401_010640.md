# Controllable Exploration in Hybrid-Policy RLVR for Multi-Modal Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.20197  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20197  
- **Reference count**: 33  
- **Primary result**: CalibRL improves over GRPO by 5.45% on in-domain and 2.61% on out-of-domain tasks across eight multimodal benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Reinforcement learning from verbal rewards (RLVR) for multi-modal reasoning suffers from entropy collapse and inefficient exploration due to sparse rewards and vast state spaces. Existing hybrid-policy approaches struggle with distributional mismatch, often either over-constraining or destabilizing exploration. CalibRL addresses this by introducing controllable exploration via two mechanisms: distribution-aware advantage weighting, which scales policy updates by the rarity of responses within a group to preserve diversity, and a LeakyReLU-based asymmetric activation that uses expert trajectories as a baseline to moderate overconfident updates while preserving corrective direction. Across geometry, math, vision, and science benchmarks, CalibRL demonstrates consistent improvements over prior methods, generalizes across model sizes, and extends to pure-text reasoning tasks.

## Method Summary
CalibRL operates within a hybrid-policy RL framework where two complementary mechanisms regulate exploration. First, distribution-aware advantage weighting groups generated responses by their statistical rarity within a batch and scales the policy gradient updates inversely to group frequency—rarer responses receive larger updates to prevent common outputs from dominating and collapsing the policy distribution. Second, a LeakyReLU-based asymmetric activation function is applied to the advantage estimates, using expert-provided trajectories as a baseline. This allows negative advantages (indicating a need for correction) to propagate but with reduced magnitude, preventing destabilizing over-corrections while maintaining a directed learning signal. Together, these mechanisms calibrate the policy update magnitude and direction, sustaining entropy and enabling efficient exploration in sparse-reward, high-dimensional multimodal spaces.

## Key Results
- CalibRL outperforms GRPO by **5.45%** on in-domain and **2.61%** on out-of-domain tasks across eight multimodal benchmarks.
- Gains generalize across model scales (Qwen2.5-VL-7B/3B, InternVL3-8B) and transfer to pure-text mathematical reasoning.
- Ablations confirm that both distribution-aware weighting and LeakyReLU activation are necessary for stable, entropy-preserving exploration; removing either degrades performance significantly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Distribution-aware advantage weighting counteracts the "rich-get-richer" effect of standard policy gradients, where high-probability (common) responses are reinforced excessively, by proportionally boosting updates for under-explored, rare responses. This actively maintains a diverse proposal distribution. The LeakyReLU asymmetric activation, anchored to an expert trajectory baseline, ensures that the policy does not overreact to negative advantages (poor outputs) while still allowing corrective updates—this avoids the instability of hard clipping or symmetric activations. The combination yields a policy that explores broadly (high entropy) but updates directionally toward expert-like performance, mitigating premature collapse in hybrid-policy settings where both discrete and continuous action spaces coexist.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Hybrid-Policy RLVR**  
   *Why needed*: Multi-modal reasoning requires joint generation of text and coordination of visual/analytic decisions, necessitating policies that output both token distributions and strategic controls.  
   *Quick check*: Verify the policy architecture produces both categorical (token) and continuous (e.g., bounding box, reasoning step) outputs.

2. **Entropy Collapse in RL**  
   *Why needed*: Sparse rewards in large state spaces cause policies to prematurely converge to suboptimal, low-entropy distributions, stifling further exploration.  
   *Quick check*: Track policy entropy during training; a sharp, sustained drop indicates collapse.

3. **Advantage Estimation with Baseline**  
   *Why needed*: Reduces variance of policy gradient updates; using expert trajectories as a baseline provides a stable reference point for judging response quality.  
   *Quick check*: Ablate the expert baseline and measure increase in gradient variance or performance instability.

4. **Distribution-Aware Reweighting**  
   *Why needed*: Standard RL updates preferentially reinforce high-frequency responses, distorting the policy distribution toward a few modes.  
   *Quick check*: Analyze the frequency distribution of generated responses before and after weighting; rarer modes should increase in probability.

5. **LeakyReLU Asymmetry**  
   *Why needed*: Symmetric activations (e.g., ReLU) zero out negative advantages, removing corrective signals; LeakyReLU preserves them at reduced magnitude for stable refinement.  
   *Quick check*: Compare update magnitudes for positive vs. negative advantages; negative should be attenuated but non-zero.

6. **Multi-Modal Benchmarking**  
   *Why needed*: Evaluates whether exploration mechanisms transfer across domains (vision, math, science) and modalities (text vs. image).  
   *Quick check*: Run cross-domain evaluations; consistent gains indicate robust exploration rather than overfitting to a single task type.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Trajectory Collection (env interaction) -> Response Grouping (by rarity) -> Advantage Computation (with expert baseline) -> Distribution-Aware Weighting -> LeakyReLU Activation -> Policy Update

Critical path: The core loop collects hybrid-policy trajectories, groups responses to compute rarity-aware weights, estimates advantages relative to expert baselines, applies asymmetric activation, and performs a constrained policy update. The weighting and activation stages are the key control points for exploration.

Design tradeoffs: Distribution-aware weighting adds computational overhead for grouping and weight computation but is essential for diversity; LeakyReLU slope (leakiness) trades off between correction strength and update stability—too leaky risks instability, too steep risks suppressing corrections.

Failure signatures: (1) Entropy drops rapidly within early training steps, (2) Performance plateaus on tasks requiring rare/creative responses, (3) Gradient norms spike after updates involving negative advantages, indicating destabilization.

First experiments:
1. Train CalibRL on a single in-domain benchmark (e.g., GeoQA) with default settings; plot entropy and accuracy curves to confirm sustained exploration and learning.
2. Ablate the distribution-aware weighting by using uniform weights; measure drop in out-of-domain generalization and diversity of outputs.
3. Vary the LeakyReLU negative slope (e.g., 0.1, 0.01, 0.0) on a mixed-difficulty task; identify the slope that maximizes final accuracy without gradient explosion.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter sensitivity (e.g., grouping thresholds, LeakyReLU slope) and their task-dependent optimal values are not comprehensively studied.
- Computational overhead from distribution-aware grouping and expert baseline maintenance is acknowledged but not quantified relative to GRPO or LUFFY.
- Generalizability is limited to the evaluated model families (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3); applicability to other architectures or unimodal LLMs remains unknown.

## Confidence
- **Benchmark performance improvements (5.45% in-domain, 2.61% out-of-domain)**: Medium confidence. Results are specific to the reported experimental setup; independent reproducibility is required.
- **Necessity of both proposed mechanisms (ablation results)**: Medium confidence. Ablations strongly support contribution but may not capture all nonlinear interactions.
- **Generalization across model sizes and to pure-text math**: Low confidence. Validated only on 2–3 model families; broader transfer is speculative.
- **Stable, directed exploration without entropy collapse**: Medium confidence. Empirical metrics (entropy curves, diversity scores) are provided, but long-term training dynamics beyond the reported epochs are not explored.
