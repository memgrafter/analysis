# Causally Robust Reward Learning from Reason-Augmented Preference Feedback

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.04861  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04861  
- **Reference count**: 14  
- **Primary result**: ReCouPLe improves reward accuracy under distribution shifts by up to 1.5× and achieves up to 2× higher downstream policy success rates on novel tasks compared to multi-task baselines.

## Executive Summary
ReCouPLe is a method for robust reward learning from preference feedback augmented with natural language rationales. It tackles causal confusion—where models latch onto spurious correlations—by using rationales as projection axes in a shared trajectory-language embedding space. The trajectory representation is decomposed into reason-aligned and reason-orthogonal components, and preference learning is constrained to depend only on the aligned part, isolating causal features. This design naturally supports zero-shot transfer to novel tasks that share rationales. Evaluations on ManiSkill and Meta-World benchmarks demonstrate significant gains in reward accuracy and policy success under distribution shifts.

## Method Summary
ReCouPLe extends preference-based reinforcement learning (PbRL) by incorporating natural language rationales that explain human preferences. A frozen language encoder maps tasks and rationales to embeddings θ (task) and ψ (rationale). A trainable trajectory encoder ϕ maps a trajectory τ to an embedding, which is orthogonally decomposed into ϕ∥(τ) (parallel to ψ) and ϕ⊥(τ) (orthogonal). The reward r(τ,ℓtask) = ϕ(τ)^T θ is expressed as r∥ + r⊥, but the preference loss (Bradley‑Terry) is applied only on r∥, forcing the model to explain preferences using features aligned with the rationale. Orthogonal components are regularized via either an equality constraint (EC) or an inequality constraint (IC) to prevent them from encoding preference-relevant confounders. Because the same language model and trajectory encoder are shared across tasks, the reason-aligned subspace generalizes to new tasks that reuse rationales, enabling zero‑shot transfer. The approach is validated on manipulation benchmarks, showing robustness to distribution shifts.

## Key Results
- Up to **1.5×** improvement in reward accuracy under distribution shift.  
- Up to **2×** higher downstream policy success rates on held‑out tasks compared to multi‑task baselines.  
- Both ReCouPLe‑EC and ReCouPLe‑IC consistently outperform baselines like BT‑Multi and RFP on ManiSkill and Meta‑World.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method’s effectiveness rests on three interconnected mechanisms:

1. **Rationale as a causal projection axis**: The rationale embedding ψ defines a direction in the joint embedding space. By decomposing the trajectory embedding into components parallel and orthogonal to ψ, the method isolates features that are reason‑aligned (assumed causal) from those unrelated to the rationale. Preference learning uses only the aligned component, thereby avoiding spurious correlations that reside in the orthogonal part.

2. **Orthogonal consistency constraints**: Two variants enforce that the orthogonal reward components r⊥ do not influence preferences. ReCouPLe‑EC forces r⊥ to be equal across compared trajectories, while ReCouPLe‑IC encourages differences in r∥ to dominate total reward differences. These constraints prevent the orthogonal component from capturing preference‑relevant confounders.

3. **Zero‑shot transfer via shared rationales**: The use of a frozen language encoder and a shared trajectory encoder means that the same rationale ψ (e.g., “avoids collisions”) activates the same aligned subspace across tasks. When a new task reuses a known rationale, the model can directly apply the learned aligned features without additional data, enabling robustness to novel tasks.

The approach assumes that rationales accurately describe the cause of preference and that causal factors are linearly representable as a projection in the embedding space. Violations (e.g., ambiguous rationales, non‑linear causal relations) may limit performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Preference‑Based Reinforcement Learning (PbRL) and Bradley‑Terry Model**  
  PbRL learns rewards from pairwise preferences; the Bradley‑Terry model defines the probability that τA is preferred over τB as σ(r(τA)‑r(τB)), where σ is the sigmoid. This foundational loss is adapted by ReCouPLe.  
  *Quick check*: Write the Bradley‑Terry probability formula.

- **Joint Embedding Spaces with Language Grounding**  
  ReCouPLe uses a shared vector space where trajectories and text (tasks/rationales) are embedded; reward is the inner product ϕ(τ)^T θ. This aligns language goals with trajectory features.  
  *Quick check*: What does ϕ(τ)^T θ represent? Answer: a scalar reward; ϕ and θ are learned to align semantically.

- **Causal Disentanglement in Representation Learning**  
  The goal is to separate causal features (true drivers of outcomes) from spurious correlations. ReCouPLe’s decomposition is a form of linear disentanglement, fitting into broader causal representation learning.  
  *Quick check*: What distinguishes a causal feature from a spurious correlation in reward hacking? Causal features directly cause the desirable outcome; spurious correlations are incidental patterns that do not cause the outcome but correlate with it in training data.

- **Orthogonal Projection and Vector Decomposition**  
  Given a vector v and a direction w, the parallel component is (v·w/||w||²)w and the orthogonal component is v minus that. This operation is central to splitting the trajectory embedding.  
  *Quick check*: How do you compute the component of v parallel to w? Use the dot product projection.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Language encoder (frozen) → θ (task), ψ (rationale); Trajectory encoder ϕ (trainable) → ϕ(τ); Projection module → ϕ∥(τ) = (ϕ(τ)^T ψ/||ψ||²)ψ, ϕ⊥(τ) = ϕ(τ)‑ϕ∥(τ); Reward calculator → r∥ = ϕ∥(τ)^T θ, r⊥ = ϕ⊥(τ)^T θ, total r = r∥+r⊥; Loss modules → BCE on r∥, consistency (EC/IC), λ_ratio L_ratio; Optimizer → updates ϕ only.

**Critical path** (per training step)  
1. Sample batch (τA, τB, preference label y, ℓreason, ℓtask).  
2. Encode ℓtask → θ, ℓreason → ψ via LM.  
3. Encode trajectories → ϕ(τA), ϕ(τB).  
4. Compute projections: ϕ∥(τ), ϕ⊥(τ) for both.  
5. Compute r∥, r⊥ for both.  
6. L_reason = BCE(y, σ(r∥(τA)‑r∥(τB))).  
7. Consistency:  
   – ReCouPLe‑EC: Leq = (r⊥(τA)‑r⊥(τB))².  
   – ReCouPLe‑IC: Lineq = BCE(y, σ(r(τA)‑r(τB))) + λ_ineq·|r⊥(τA)‑r⊥(τB)|² (or similar ratio‑based penalty).  
8. L_ratio = Σ ReLU( |r∥| / (|r∥|+|r⊥|+ϵ) ‑ α ).  
9. Total loss = L_reason + λ_ratio L_ratio + λ_consistency·Leq/Lineq.  
10. Backpropagate, update ϕ.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- EC vs IC: EC is strict and may fail if orthogonal features vary naturally; IC is more flexible but requires careful tuning of λ_ineq and α.  
- Regularization strengths (λ_ratio, λ_eq/λ_ineq): Too high can force collapse or discard useful orthogonal information; too low leaves spurious correlations.  
- Frozen LM vs fine‑tuning: Freezing preserves semantic consistency but limits adaptation; fine‑tuning risks overfitting.  
- Linear projection assumption: May fail if causal factors are non‑linear in the embedding space; extending to nonlinear decomposition increases complexity.

**Failure signatures**  
- No OOD improvement over BT‑Multi: model still uses spurious features; check ψ ≠ 0 and that L_reason dominates.  
- Orthogonal component magnitudes near zero: over‑regularization collapse; adjust λ_ratio and α.  
- Leq does not decrease: EC too strict; switch to IC or reduce λ_eq.  
- High variance with rationale paraphrases: embedding may not capture semantic similarity; consider better LM or fine‑tuning.  
- Transfer fails on novel tasks: reason‑aligned subspace underfit; increase training data and rationale diversity.

**First experiments**  
1. *Projection sanity check*: Generate random vectors v and w; verify v = v∥ + v⊥ and v⊥·w ≈ 0.  
2. *Synthetic linear reason test*: Build dataset with known causal feature (size) and spurious feature (color). Use linear ϕ, provide rationale “size”. Train, then test with flipped correlation; expect >90% accuracy if projection works.  
3. *Reproduce paper results on ManiSkill*: Implement data collection for MS‑Pick‑Larger (or similar). Train ReCouPLe‑EC, ReCouPLe‑IC, BT‑Multi, RFP. Validate OOD reward accuracy ~0.82+ as in Table 1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can active learning strategies be integrated into ReCouPLe to selectively query the most informative rationales, minimizing annotation cost while preserving robustness against causal confusion?  
2. Does ReCouPLe’s ability to mitigate causal confusion generalize to real‑world robotic tasks with natural human feedback and physical domain shifts?  
3. Can ReCouPLe be extended into an end‑to‑end policy learning algorithm that directly consumes preferences and rationales without separate reward modeling, and would this improve efficiency or performance?

## Limitations
- Assumes rationales are accurate and that causal features are linearly representable; if not, the projection axis ψ may be misleading, potentially worsening spurious correlations.  
- Orthogonal constraints may discard essential task‑relevant signals if those depend on features orthogonal to the rationale (e.g., safety shaping).  
- Zero‑shot transfer relies on rationale semantics being invariant across tasks; out‑of‑distribution rationales or lexical variations can break this assumption.

## Next Checks
1. **Synthetic causal robustness test**: Construct trajectories with a known causal feature (e.g., object size) and a spurious correlate (e.g., color). Flip the correlation at test time; ReCouPLe should maintain >90% accuracy if the projection isolates the true cause.  
2. **EC vs IC ablation on diverse trajectories**: Evaluate both variants on pairs with natural orthogonal variation (e.g., different gripper positions). IC should outperform EC when orthogonal features differ meaningfully.  
3. **Zero‑shot transfer with rationale paraphrases**: Test transfer success when novel tasks use semantically equivalent but lexically different rationales (e.g., “faster completion” vs. “minimize duration”). Success indicates embedding invariance.
