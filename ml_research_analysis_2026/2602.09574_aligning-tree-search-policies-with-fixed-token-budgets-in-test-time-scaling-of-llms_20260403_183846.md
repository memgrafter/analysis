# Aligning Tree-Search Policies with Fixed Token Budgets in Test-Time Scaling of LLMs
## Quick Facts
*   arXiv ID: 2602.09574
*   Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09574
*   Reference count: 40
*   Primary result: Budget-Guided MCTS (BG-MCTS) consistently achieves the highest accuracy under fixed token budgets (10k, 20k, 30k) across two models and two math benchmarks, outperforming budget-agnostic baselines in 11 of 12 settings.

## Executive Summary
Standard tree-search decoding for large language models (LLMs) under fixed token budgets is inefficient, as budget-agnostic methods either over-branch late in the search or stop prematurely. This paper introduces Budget-Guided Monte Carlo Tree Search (BG-MCTS), which dynamically aligns the search policy with the remaining token budget. By conditioning node selection, expansion (widening), and value estimation on a continuouslyupdated budget sufficiency ratio (ρ), BG-MCTS anneals exploration, adds a completion bias toward deeper nodes, and suppresses late-stage widening. This promotes a "wide-to-deep" shift, prioritizing the refinement of promising solution paths as the budget depletes. Evaluations on mathematical reasoning benchmarks with sub-10B instruction-tuned LLMs show BG-MCTS significantly improves accuracy and precision of correct answer nodes compared to strong baselines.

## Method Summary
BG-MCTS modifies standard MCTS by introducing a budget sufficiency ratio, ρ = 1 - C_used / B, where C_used is tokens consumed and B is the fixed budget. The selection score (BG-PUCT) multiplies the standard PUCT exploration term by ρ, linearly suppressing exploration as the budget declines. A completion bias, κ(1-ρ)*d(x)/d_ans, is added to node value estimates, where d(x) is node depth and d_ans is a running estimate of typical answer depth, making deeper nodes more attractive near budget exhaustion. For widening (creating a virtual new child from an internal node), the score is the mean reward plus λρ times the reward variance, causing the widening incentive to fade with ρ. The standard MCTS loop (selection, expansion, evaluation, backpropagation) proceeds until the token budget is exhausted. The best-completed answer node (highest Q-value) is returned.

## Key Results
*   BG-MCTS achieves the highest accuracy under fixed budgets of 10k, 20k, and 30k tokens on MATH500 and AIME24/25 using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, outperforming budget-agnostic baselines in 11 of 12 settings.
*   It yields up to a 77% precision in correct answer nodes, demonstrating effective token utilization by shifting from broad exploration to deep refinement.
*   The method induces a clear wide-to-deep transition in tree topology: early high width (exploration) gives way to increased depth (exploitation/completion) as the budget sufficiency ratio ρ decreases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Budget-Annealed Exploration
*   Claim: Multiplying the PUCT exploration term by ρ reduces wasteful branching from shallow nodes in later stages.
*   Mechanism: The BG-PUCT score is ˜W(s,ρ)/m_s + ρ c P(s|p) √(ln(m_p)/m_s). The ρ factor linearly scales down the exploration bonus as tokens are consumed (ρ→0), gradually shifting selection from exploration to exploitation.
*   Core assumption: New branches initiated late lack sufficient tokens to reach a viable solution depth; thus, exploration should be suppressed.
*   Evidence anchors:
    *   [abstract] "anneals exploration and adds a completion bias as the budget depletes."
    *   [section] §3.2: "the multiplicative factor ρ is incorporated in the exploration term (exploration is gradually suppressed as ρ decreases)."
*   Break condition: Too slow a decay (high ρ late) maintains inefficient branching; too fast a decay causes premature convergence on suboptimal paths.

### Mechanism 2: Completion Bias
*   Claim: Adding a depth-dependent bonus scaled by (1-ρ) encourages deepening of promising paths as the budget winds down.
*   Mechanism: Node value ˜W(s,ρ) = Σ_x∈T(s) [Q(x) + κ(1-ρ) d(x)/d_ans]. The term κ(1-ρ) d(x)/d_ans grows as ρ decreases, making nodes with greater depth d(x) more attractive relative to the estimated typical answer depth d_ans.
*   Core assumption: Correct solutions are more likely to be found at greater depths; biasing toward depth near exhaustion increases the chance of completing a valid solution.
*   Evidence anchors:
    *   [abstract] "adds a completion bias as the budget depletes."
    *   [section] §3.2: "the correction is scaled by (1−ρ), so it is negligible early and becomes more influential as the budget runs out."
*   Break condition: Over-biasing (high κ) may trap search in deep but incorrect paths; under-biasing fails to sufficiently shift focus to completion.

### Mechanism 3: Budget-Guided Widening Suppression
*   Claim: The incentive to create a new child (widen) from an internal node is scaled by ρ, preventing the introduction of new branches when tokens are scarce.
*   Mechanism: The score for the virtual generative child s_gen(p) is E_gen(p,ρ) = µ(p) + λ ρ σ²(p). The variance-based uncertainty term σ²(p) is multiplied by ρ, causing the widening reward to diminish as the budget depletes.
*   Core assumption: New branches started late cannot mature to a solution within the remaining budget; suppressing them saves tokens for deepening existing viable paths.
*   Evidence anchors:
    *   [abstract] "while suppressing late-stage widening."
    *   [section] §3.3: "the factor ρ ensures that this incentive is strong early and fades as the budget runs down."
*   Break condition: Excessive suppression (low ρ effect) may remove useful exploratory branches too early; insufficient suppression allows late-stage breadth expansion that wastes tokens.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Budget Tracker -> Selector -> (Expander -> Evaluator -> Backpropagator) -> Tree Update -> Repeat until Budget Exhausted
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Compute current budget sufficiency ratio ρ = 1 - C_used / B.
    2.  Starting from root, at each internal node p, compute Selection Score for each selectable child s ∈ S(p) using BG-PUCT (for standard children) or E_gen (for virtual widening child).
    3.  Choose s = argmax Score. If s is the virtual child, trigger a widening expansion (generate 1 new standard child from p). Else, descend to s.
    4.  Continue selection until a leaf node is reached. At the leaf, perform a standard expansion: generate k=2 new children.
    5.  Evaluate all newly expanded leaf nodes with the process reward model (GenPRM-7B) to obtain Q-values.
    6.  Backpropagate the Q-values up the visited path. Update subtree visit counts m(·) and the running estimate of answer depth d_ans using nodes containing completed answers.
    7.  Add the tokens consumed in this iteration (generation + PRM scoring) to C_used.
    8.  Repeat from step 1 until C_used ≥ B.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Exploration annealing (ρ factor): A linear decay is simple but may not be optimal; alternative schedules (e.g., exponential) could be tested.
    *   Completion bias (κ): Requires a stable estimate of typical answer depth (d_ans); this estimator may be noisy early in the search with few answered nodes.
    *   Widening uncertainty (λ): The scaling factor for the variance term; balance is needed to allow useful early exploration while suppressing late branching.
    *   Token counting: Must precisely account for all generated tokens (LLM outputs) and potentially PRM input tokens; different generation modes (sequential vs. full) affect cost.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   Late-stage over-branching: Tree width continues to grow significantly when ρ is very low → indicates ρ scaling in E_gen is ineffective or λ is too high.
    *   Early convergence: Search stabilizes on a single path while ρ is still high → suggests over-aggressive completion bias (high κ) or exploration suppression.
    *   Low final answer precision: Many nodes marked as "answered" but few correct → reward model (GenPRM-7B) lacks discriminative power; BG-MCTS cannot separate good from bad paths.
    *   High seed variance: Large performance differences across random seeds → randomness in node expansion/selection is not being managed by the budget-guided signals.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Reproduce main results:** Run BG-MCTS and all reported baselines (MCTS, Best-of-N, AB-MCTS-M, LiteSearch) on a small, fixed subset (e.g., 50 problems) of MATH500 for budgets 10k, 20k, 30k. Verify the accuracy ranking and the wide-to-deep depth/width shift matches Figures 4, 10, 11.
    2.  **Ablation check:** On the same 50-problem subset, systematically disable key BG-MCTS components: set ρ=1.0 (no annealing), κ=0 (no completion bias), and λ=0 (no widening suppression). Measure the drop in accuracy compared to full BG-MCTS to quantify each mechanism's contribution.
    3.  **Sensitivity sweep:** Vary the core hyperparameters κ (completion bias strength), λ (widening uncertainty weight), and c (base exploration constant) over plausible ranges (e.g., κ ∈ [0.1, 1.0], λ ∈ [0.5, 2.0], c ∈ [0.5, 2.0]). Plot accuracy and tree statistics (avg. depth, width at different ρ) to identify robust configurations and failure modes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1.  Can BG-MCTS incorporate input and reward token costs into the fixed budget without losing performance?
    *   Basis: Future work: "extending the budget to account for input tokens and reward computation."
    *   Why unresolved: Current method counts only output tokens; ignoring PRM and prompt costs misaligns search with real-world token limits.
    *   What evidence would resolve: Implement total token accounting (LLM output + input prompts + PRM calls). Re-evaluate on benchmarks under equal total budgets; compare accuracy and tree dynamics.
2.  How does reward score granularity and calibration affect BG-MCTS's budget efficiency and final accuracy?
    *   Basis: "GenPRM-7B often outputs near-binary scores... Improving reward calibration is therefore a key lever."
    *   Why unresolved: Sparse, poorly calibrated rewards weaken node discrimination, potentially hindering the budget-guided selection and widening signals.
    *   What evidence would resolve: Replace/calibrate GenPRM-7B (e.g., temperature scaling, regression fine-tuning). Re-run experiments; analyze changes in tree topology (depth/width) and accuracy.
3.  Does BG-MCTS maintain its advantage when applied to larger language models (>50B parameters) and diverse domains beyond mathematical reasoning?
    *   Basis: (Inferred) Study limited to two sub-10B models on math; applicability to other models/tasks is untested.
    *   Why unresolved: Search dynamics (e.g., output length, prior quality) differ with scale and domain, possibly affecting the optimal wide-to-deep schedule.
    *   What evidence would resolve: Replicate with larger models (e.g., Llama-3.1-70B) on diverse benchmarks (HumanEval, BBH). Compare accuracy under fixed budgets against MCTS/Repeated Sampling baselines.

## Limitations
*   **Baseline hyperparameters unspecified:** Exact configurations for AB-MCTS-M and LiteSearch (e.g., early-stopping thresholds, corrective feedback loops) are not detailed, making fair comparisons potentially unreliable.
*   **PRM scoring implementation ambiguous:** The specific architecture/head of GenPRM-7B and its near-binary output thresholding/normalization are unclear, which may affect reward signal quality and search guidance.
*   **Mechanism isolation incomplete:** The paper lacks full ablations that independently disable each BG-MCTS component (annealing, bias, widening suppression) across all budgets, leaving the precise quantitative contribution of each less certain.

## Confidence
*   **Overall BG-MCTS advantage (11/12 settings):** High confidence. Results are consistent across two models and three budgets on two benchmarks.
*   **Mechanism claims (annealing, bias, suppression):** Medium confidence. Theoretical justification and supportive empirical trends (e.g., depth shifts) exist, but direct causal evidence via comprehensive ablations is limited.
*   **Baseline performance characterization:** Low confidence. Without exact hyperparameters, the strength of the budget-agnostic baselines may be misrepresented, affecting the magnitude of the reported gap.
