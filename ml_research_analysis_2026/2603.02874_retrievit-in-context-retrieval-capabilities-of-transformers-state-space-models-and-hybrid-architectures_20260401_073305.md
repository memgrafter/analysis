# Retrievit: In-context Retrieval Capabilities of Transformers, State Space Models, and Hybrid Architectures

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02874
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02874
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Interleaved Mamba2 hybrids achieve superior sample efficiency and generalization on synthetic in-context retrieval tasks compared to pure Transformers and SSMs.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether hybrid architectures combining Transformers and State Space Models (SSMs) can efficiently process long contexts while maintaining strong in-context retrieval capabilities. Using controlled synthetic tasks—n-gram retrieval (suffix/prefix) and position retrieval—the authors compare pure SSMs (Mamba2), pure Transformers (with RoPE), and interleaved hybrids. Hybrids, particularly interleaved Mamba2, significantly outperform pure SSMs on n-gram retrieval, matching or exceeding Transformers with ~10× better sample efficiency, perfect generalization to 4× longer sequences, and robustness to duplicate queries. For position retrieval, Transformers converge fastest; SSMs fail to reach 95% accuracy within 20M training steps, while hybrids narrow but do not close the gap. Representation analysis reveals SSMs develop a 2D spiral embedding structure that preserves locality, explaining their strength in summary-based tasks but weakness in exact two-hop associations.

## Method Summary
The study employs two synthetic tasks to isolate in-context retrieval mechanisms. The n-gram retrieval task requires models to recall a token from a stored sequence given a query, testing associative memory. The position retrieval task demands identifying a token at a specified offset, testing precise positional indexing. Models are trained from scratch on sequences up to 100 tokens, with evaluation on generalization to 400 tokens. Performance is measured by accuracy and sample efficiency (steps to 95% accuracy). For representation analysis, the authors visualize embeddings from trained SSMs via dimensionality reduction, observing a spiral pattern that preserves positional locality.

## Key Results
- On n-gram retrieval, interleaved Mamba2 hybrids converge to ≥95% accuracy with ~10× fewer training examples than Transformers, generalize perfectly to 400-token sequences (4× training length), and maintain low error with up to 10 duplicate queries, avoiding positional biases seen in pure Mamba2.
- On position retrieval, Transformers (with RoPE) converge fastest; SSMs fail to reach 95% accuracy within 20M examples; hybrids narrow the gap but still lag behind Transformers.
- SSMs develop locality-aware 2D spiral embeddings in synthetic tasks, keeping adjacent positions as neighbors—a property absent in Transformers—which explains SSMs' strength in summary tasks but weakness in exact two-hop association.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SSMs' inherent recurrence and state-carrying mechanism create a continuous, locality-preserving embedding space (spiral structure) that efficiently summarizes local context but struggles with non-local, exact associations required for precise position retrieval. Hybrids interleave SSM and Transformer layers, allowing the SSM layers to build compressed local representations and Transformer layers to perform precise in-context lookups, combining efficiency with accuracy. The spiral embeddings enable strong performance on n-gram retrieval where summary is sufficient, but position retrieval demands discrete indexing that Transformers with RoPE provide.

## Foundational Learning
- **In-context retrieval:** Ability to recall stored information from context without parameter updates. *Why needed:* Core capability for long-context tasks like QA and summarization. *Quick check:* Can the model answer questions based on a provided document without fine-tuning?
- **State Space Models (SSMs):** Continuous-time sequence models (e.g., Mamba2) with structured state transitions. *Why needed:* Offer linear complexity for long sequences. *Quick check:* Does the model maintain a hidden state that compresses history?
- **Transformer attention:** Parallelizable mechanism computing token-token interactions. *Why needed:* Provides direct access to all positions, enabling exact retrieval. *Quick check:* Does attention weight distribution show focus on relevant tokens?
- **Hybrid architectures:** Combine SSM and Transformer layers. *Why needed:* Aim to balance efficiency and retrieval accuracy. *Quick check:* Does alternating SSM/Transformer layers improve sample efficiency on retrieval tasks?
- **Synthetic evaluation tasks:** Controlled n-gram/position tasks. *Why needed:* Isolate specific retrieval mechanisms from noise of natural language. *Quick check:* Do results hold when task complexity (e.g., duplicate queries) increases?
- **Locality-preserving embeddings:** Representation where nearby positions remain neighbors. *Why needed:* Explains SSM efficiency on local patterns. *Quick check:* Do position embeddings show smooth, continuous transition when visualized?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Input Tokens -> [SSM Block -> Transformer Block] x N -> Output Logits  
*(Interleaved Mamba2 hybrid; N is number of repeated block pairs)*

**Critical Path:** Token sequences flow through alternating SSM and Transformer layers. SSM layers sequentially update a compressed state per token, creating locality-aware summaries. Transformer layers then perform multi-head attention over the SSM-processed sequence, enabling direct retrieval from any position. The final output layer maps to vocabulary for next-token prediction.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- SSM-only: Efficient (linear complexity) but poor at exact position retrieval due to locality bias.  
- Transformer-only: Accurate retrieval but quadratic cost, limiting long-context scaling.  
- Hybrid: Aims for SSM efficiency + Transformer accuracy; tradeoff is increased architectural complexity and potential positional bias if interleaving is unbalanced.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- SSMs fail position retrieval (accuracy <95% within 20M steps).  
- Pure Mamba2 shows positional bias with duplicate queries.  
- Hybrids may still lag on position retrieval if Transformer layers are insufficiently deep/frequent.

**First 3 Experiments:**  
1. Train pure SSM (Mamba2), pure Transformer (RoPE), and interleaved hybrid on n-gram retrieval; compare accuracy vs. training steps.  
2. Test generalization by evaluating trained models on sequences 4× longer than training.  
3. Probe position retrieval accuracy on Transformers/SSMs/hybrids; measure steps to 95% and inspect failure cases.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to synthetic n-gram and position tasks; real-world in-context retrieval (e.g., natural language queries, noisy contexts) remains unverified.
- Hybrid architecture assessment focuses almost exclusively on interleaved Mamba2; other hybridization schemes (e.g., layer-wise, cross-attention bridges) are not explored.
- Representation analysis (spiral embeddings) is derived from controlled synthetic data; it is unclear whether SSMs develop analogous structures when processing natural language or much longer contexts.

## Confidence
- **High:** Interleaved Mamba2 hybrids substantially outperform pure SSMs and match/exceed Transformers in sample efficiency and generalization on synthetic n-gram retrieval.
- **Medium:** Transformers (with RoPE) converge fastest on synthetic position retrieval; SSMs fail to reach 95% within 20M steps; hybrids narrow but do not close the gap.
- **Medium:** SSMs develop 2D spiral embeddings in synthetic tasks, explaining their summary strength and two-hop weakness; transfer to natural language is uncertain.

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate hybrid vs. pure architectures on **real-world long-context retrieval benchmarks** (e.g., GovReport, QASP) to test generalization beyond synthetic patterns.
2. Systematically compare **additional hybrid designs** (e.g., alternating block depths, cross-attention bridges) on the same synthetic and one real-world retrieval task.
3. Probe **SSM representations in natural language models** (e.g., Mamba2 pretrained on corpora) using dimensionality reduction and positional probing to check for locality-preserving structures analogous to the synthetic spiral.
