# Can Large Language Models Keep Up? Benchmarking Online Adaptation to Continual Knowledge Streams

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.07392
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07392
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** State-of-the-art LLMs average only 39.4% (OAKS-BABI) and 57.5% (OAKS-Novel) accuracy on a streaming knowledge benchmark, with best-in-class Gemini 3 Pro reaching just 66.3% and 75.5%, revealing a fundamental gap in online adaptation.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the OAKS benchmark to evaluate large language models' ability to adapt online to streaming, continually evolving knowledge—a critical capability for real-world dynamic applications. OAKS streams fine-grained context chunks and repeatedly queries the same questions, measuring not just final accuracy but the timeliness and stability of knowledge updates. Evaluating 14 models, including memory-augmented systems, reveals普遍 limitations: average accuracy is low (39.4% and 57.5% on the two datasets), performance degrades under frequent updates, and all methods exhibit flawed state-tracking, such as over-updating or under-updating to fact changes. While inference-time "thinking" improves complex reasoning by up to 15.4%, the results indicate a significant gap in the ability of current LLMs to track and integrate evolving information in a streaming setting.

## Method Summary
The evaluation uses two streaming QA datasets: synthetic OAKS-BABI (1.2k questions, 65 chunks) and novel-based OAKS-Novel (870 questions, ~77 chunks). Context arrives sequentially in chunks; after each new chunk, the model must answer each question based on all accumulated context. Three context strategies are tested: **Base** (concatenate prior chunks, truncating oldest if needed), **RAG** (retrieve top-30 relevant past chunks using Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B), and **Agentic Memory** (HippoRAG2, MemAgent, A-Mem). Primary metrics are interval-averaged accuracy, with secondary behavioral metrics: Acquisition Latency (AL), Distraction Susceptibility (DS), and Phase Miss (PM). Models are queried with fixed prompt templates, optionally using a "thinking" mode for chain-of-thought.

## Key Results
*   On OAKS-BABI and OAKS-Novel, 14 evaluated models averaged only 39.4% and 57.5% accuracy, respectively. Under a frequent-update subset, performance dropped further to 33.3% and 53.0%.
*   Enabling thinking (inference-time scaling) improved performance on complex bridge-type questions by up to 15.4%, but Distraction Susceptibility remained high, indicating persistent distraction.
*   Even the top performer, Gemini 3 Pro, achieved only 66.3% and 75.5% accuracy, underscoring a substantial, fundamental gap in continual knowledge adaptation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Models exhibit over-updating or under-updating due to inadequate temporal state integration.
*   **Mechanism:** Without an explicit mechanism to track fact evolution, models rely on local context cues. This leads to changing answers when the state is stable (over-updating/Volatility) or failing to change when the state transitions (under-updating/Obstinacy).
*   **Core assumption:** Models treat each input token independently without maintaining a persistent state representation across chunks.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "models struggle with timely state-tracking, exhibiting over-updating or under-updating behaviors"; [section 6.1] Table 5 shows Volatility (32.1% under No GT Transition) and Obstinacy (26.1%) as dominant error modes.
*   **Break condition:** If a model implements an explicit state tracker (e.g., memory buffer) that maintains fact versions, over/under-updating should decrease.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Accuracy degrades as context length increases due to distraction from irrelevant information.
*   **Mechanism:** As more chunks are accumulated, models attend to noise and distractor content, causing them to lose track of relevant facts, leading to higher Distraction Susceptibility (DS) and lower accuracy in later intervals.
*   **Core assumption:** Transformer attention is content-based but not robust to long-range interference; relevant evidence becomes diluted.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 6.4] "Accuracy degrades at later intervals... and at later time intervals as context length increased."; [section 6.2] Table 6 shows DS remains high even for thinking models.
*   **Break condition:** If context pruning or explicit retrieval reduces irrelevant content, degradation should be mitigated.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Thinking mode (inference-time scaling) improves performance on complex reasoning questions by enabling explicit multi-step integration.
*   **Mechanism:** The internal reasoning process allows models to synthesize evidence from multiple chunks and maintain coherence, especially for bridge and comparison questions.
*   **Core assumption:** Standard autoregressive generation is myopic; intermediate reasoning steps provide a scaffold for combining dispersed facts.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 5.2] "Enabling thinking mode leads to consistent improvements... up to 15.4% on bridge-type questions."; [abstract] "While thinking modes improved performance on complex reasoning questions by up to 15.4%..."
*   **Break condition:** If the thinking process is ablated or restricted, the gains should disappear, especially on multi-hop questions.

## Foundational Learning

## Architecture Onboarding
Datasets -> Context Builder -> Model Inference -> Metric Engine; Retriever (for RAG) feeds Context Builder.
*   **Critical path:** For each model/strategy: load dataset -> for each interval t: build context St (using strategy) -> for each question: query model -> compare prediction to ground truth -> store results -> after all intervals, compute metrics.
*   **Design tradeoffs:** Base vs RAG: RAG reduces context length but may miss needed evidence; retrieval quality crucial. Context length: Longer context preserves information but risks distraction; rolling window limits history. Agentic memory: More sophisticated but computationally heavier and still underperforms. Thinking mode: Improves reasoning but increases latency and token costs.
*   **Failure signatures:** Low accuracy on Frequent subset: Model cannot handle many state changes. High PM (Phase Miss): Model fails to capture a phase entirely. High AL (Acquisition Latency): Delayed adaptation to new state. High DS (Distraction Susceptibility): Model answers incorrectly even after initial correct identification. Performance drop over time: Indicates error accumulation or context degradation.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1. Replicate baseline results: Evaluate Qwen3-30B with Base context on OAKS-B to verify reported ~35.8% accuracy.
    2. Test RAG effectiveness: Implement RAG with top-30 chunks and compare accuracy to baseline; expect modest improvement on Sparse but degradation on Frequent.
    3. Evaluate thinking mode: Run Gemini 2.5 Flash with and without thinking; expect significant gain on bridge questions (e.g., ~15% improvement) and overall accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Question:** How should the OAKS benchmark be extended—both in terms of model architectures evaluated and in the complexity/frequency of fact transitions—to better assess scalability and generalizability of online adaptation?
*   **Question:** How can future datasets eliminate the confounding effect of models' parametric knowledge to measure pure online adaptation?
*   **Question:** What methods can enable parametric online learning, where models update their internal weights in response to streaming knowledge, as opposed to purely contextual adaptation?

## Limitations
*   **Dataset specifics:** Exact chunk/answer formatting and interval boundary definitions are inferred from the repo, not explicitly detailed in the paper.
*   **Retrieval replication:** The paper uses Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B; using a different embedder may cause RAG results to diverge due to recall differences.
*   **Model version parity:** Cited models (e.g., Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507) may have public equivalents with subtle differences affecting scores.

## Confidence
*   *Over/under-updating due to poor state tracking*: Medium (Supported by error mode breakdown, but mechanism inference is indirect).
*   *Accuracy degradation with longer context*: High (Consistent with reported results and external "lost-in-the-middle" findings).
*   *Thinking mode improves complex reasoning*: High (Clear ablation-style gain reported, up to +15.4% on bridge questions).
