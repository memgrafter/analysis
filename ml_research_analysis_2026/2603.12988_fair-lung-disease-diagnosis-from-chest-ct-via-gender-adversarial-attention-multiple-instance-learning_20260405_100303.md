# Fair Lung Disease Diagnosis from Chest CT via Gender-Adversarial Attention Multiple Instance Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.12988
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12988
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: The model achieves balanced per-gender macro F1 (male 0.679, female 0.688) with a competition score of 0.685, demonstrating effective gender fairness mitigation.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles fair multi-class lung disease diagnosis from chest CT scans, aiming for balanced performance across genders. It proposes an attention-based Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) framework with a ConvNeXt backbone that identifies diagnostically relevant slices without slice-level annotations. To mitigate gender bias, a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) adversarially removes gender-predictive features from the volume embedding. Additional techniques—focal loss, label smoothing, stratified cross-validation, and targeted oversampling—address severe class and intersectional (gender-disease) imbalance. Inference ensembles five folds with horizontal-flip test-time augmentation and out-of-fold threshold optimization. The method yields a mean validation competition score of 0.685 (±0.030) and post-optimization out-of-fold macro F1 scores of 0.679 for males and 0.688 for females, confirming successful fairness mitigation, though Squamous Cell Carcinoma remains a challenging class (F1 0.366).

## Method Summary
The task is 4-class classification (Healthy, COVID-19, Adenocarcinoma, Squamous Cell Carcinoma) of chest CT volumes. Each volume consists of an ordered sequence of 2D axial slices; preprocessing resizes slices to 224×224, replicates to 3 channels, and normalizes with ImageNet statistics. To handle variable slice counts, the method caps M=32 slices: during training, M slices are randomly selected; during inference, uniformly selected; shorter volumes are zero-padded with a binary attention mask.

The model follows an attention-MIL architecture. A ConvNeXt backbone (Tiny for experiments, Base for final) extracts a feature embedding for each slice. An attention network (2-layer MLP) produces attention scores for each slice; softmax over non-padded slices yields weights, and the weighted sum of slice embeddings forms a volume embedding H. Two heads attach to H: a disease head (linear layer) predicts the class logits, and an adversarial gender head (2-layer MLP) predicts gender via a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) that negates gradients flowing from the gender head to the shared embedding, thereby discouraging H from encoding gender information.

Training uses a two-stage schedule: first freeze the backbone for 5 epochs with learning rate 1e-3 for heads; then unfreeze with backbone LR 1e-5 and heads LR 1e-4. The loss combines focal loss (γ=2, α=0.25) with label smoothing (ε=0.1) and an adversarial term λ_adv multiplied by the gender classification loss (cross-entropy). Stratified 5-fold cross-validation on the joint (class, gender) strata ensures each fold preserves subgroup distributions. A WeightedRandomSampler oversamples the rare female Squamous Cell Carcinoma subgroup to guarantee its presence in mini-batches. Gradient accumulation (K=4) yields an effective batch size of 16, and the optimizer is AdamW (β1=0.9, β2=0.999, weight_decay=0.05) with cosine annealing to zero.

Inference ensembles predictions from all five fold checkpoints. For each volume, both the original and horizontally flipped views are processed (test-time augmentation), and logits are averaged across folds and views. Out-of-fold (OOF) predictions are collected to optimize per-class thresholds t*_c on a dense grid [0.05,0.95] to maximize binary F1 (one-vs-rest). The final single-label rule: if any class probability p_c ≥ t*_c, predict the class with the highest p_c among those; otherwise, predict argmax.

## Key Results
- Competition score P = (MacroF1_male + MacroF1_female)/2 reaches 0.685 ±0.030 on validation.
- Out-of-fold macro F1: male 0.679, female 0.688, indicating near-parity.
- Squamous Cell Carcinoma remains the hardest class with F1 0.366 due to small sample size and class difficulty.
- The adversarial approach and oversampling together successfully close the gender performance gap.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The model's fairness stems primarily from adversarial training: the GRL forces the volume embedding H to be uninformative for gender prediction, thereby reducing spurious correlations between gender and disease. Attention MIL focuses on the most diagnostically relevant slices, sparing the model from noise in irrelevant slices and improving generalization. Focal loss down-weights easy examples and focuses learning on hard, misclassified instances, which helps with severe class imbalance. Label smoothing prevents overconfidence and stabilizes training. Stratified cross-validation ensures reliable validation across all subgroups, while targeted oversampling guarantees that rare intersectional groups are seen during training. The ensemble and threshold optimization further boost performance by reducing variance and calibrating decision boundaries per class, particularly for minority classes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multiple Instance Learning (MIL)**: Treats a volume as a bag of slices with only a bag-level label. Needed because slice-level disease annotations are unavailable. Quick check: Understand difference between instance-level and bag-level classification.
- **Attention Mechanism in MIL**: Assigns learnable weights to slices to focus on informative ones. Quick check: Implement a softmax over slice features and verify gradients flow to attention network.
- **Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL)**: Applies a negative λ factor to gradients during backprop, enabling adversarial removal of protected attribute (gender) from shared representation. Quick check: Forward passes identity; backward multiplies gradient by -λ.
- **Focal Loss**: Modifies cross-entropy with (1 - p_t)^γ to emphasize hard examples. Quick check: Replace standard CE with focal and monitor loss on imbalanced data.
- **Label Smoothing**: Replaces one-hot targets with softened labels (e.g., (1-ε) for true class, ε/(K-1) for others). Quick check: Verify that model logits do not become overconfident on training set.
- **Stratified Sampling / Oversampling**: Ensures balanced representation of subgroups in each batch. Quick check: Use torch.utils.data.WeightedRandomSampler and inspect batch class/gender distribution.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input CT Volume -> Slice Extractors (resize, normalize) -> Slice Embedding (ConvNeXt backbone) -> Attention Module (MLP -> softmax) -> Volume Embedding H (weighted sum) -> Disease Head (linear) -> Class logits; also H -> GRL -> Gender Head (2-layer MLP) -> Gender logits.

**Critical path**: During training, for each sampled volume, extract M slices, pass through backbone to get embeddings, compute attention weights (mask applied to exclude padded slices), aggregate to H, compute disease and gender logits, combine losses with λ_adv, backpropagate. During inference, for each fold and both flip augmentations, embed slices, aggregate, apply softmax, average logits across folds/views, then threshold.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Fixed M=32 balances memory/performance but discards many slices; random vs uniform sampling trades off simplicity vs coverage.
- Using Tiny vs Base ConvNeXt: capacity vs compute; authors used Tiny for experiments but Base for final, implying performance gains justify cost.
- GRL λ_adv strength: too weak leaves gender bias, too strong may harm disease classification.
- Oversampling minority subgroups risks overfitting to those few examples.
- Threshold optimization introduces a small number of hyperparameters but should be done on OOF to avoid leakage.

**Failure signatures**:
- Gender head accuracy >60% → GRL ineffective.
- Attention weights uniform or focusing on padding → attention MLP not trained or mask misapplied.
- Female Squamous F1 near zero → oversampling insufficient or model still biased.
- Large variance across folds → unstable training or data split issues.
- OOF macro-F1 lower than with plain argmax → threshold overfitting.

**First experiments**:
1. Gender information probe: after training, freeze the backbone, extract H for all volumes, train a linear logistic regression to predict gender. Expect accuracy ~50% (chance).
2. Attention visualization: for a few validation cases with known disease, plot attention weights across slices; high weights should localize to slices containing pathology.
3. Ablation study: train three models – (a) baseline with no GRL and no oversampling, (b) oversampling only, (c) GRL only – compare per-gender macro-F1 gaps to isolate contribution of each fairness mechanism.

## Limitations
- Critical hyperparameters such as GRL scaling factor λ_adv and exact oversampling weights are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Model variant (ConvNeXt Tiny vs Base) used for the reported scores is ambiguous.
- Subsampling details for slices (random contiguous block vs random indices) and exact CV split generation over 8 strata are not provided.
- Performance numbers are sensitive to threshold optimization and may not generalize.
