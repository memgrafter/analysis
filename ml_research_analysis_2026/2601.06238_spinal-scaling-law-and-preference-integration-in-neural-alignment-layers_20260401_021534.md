# SPINAL -- Scaling-law and Preference Integration in Neural Alignment Layers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06238  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06238  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** SPINAL reveals that DPO alignment induces a coupled signature of increased spectral tail exponent and decreased Fisher-Rao step length in the final ~10 layers across five LLM families, serving as a robust internal diagnostic for alignment strength and training stability.

## Executive Summary
We introduce SPINAL, a geometry-based diagnostic for detecting and quantifying preference alignment in large language models. SPINAL computes two layer-resolved metrics: the spectral tail exponent αℓ, which measures representational concentration (sharpness of the singular value spectrum), and the Fisher-Rao step length eLℓ, which measures the smoothness of the layer-to-layer evolution of next-token distributions. Across five model families (Phi-2, DeepSeek, Gemma, Qwen, Llama3), DPO-aligned checkpoints consistently show a coupled terminal-layer effect: αℓ increases (sharper spectrum) and eLℓ decreases (smoother belief progression) in the last ~10 layers. This localized calibration effect allows SPINAL to compare alignment strength, detect training instability early, and suggests that preference tuning primarily acts on output-critical layers. The method is fully post-hoc, requires no retraining, and yields a composite SPINALScore for rapid triage.

## Method Summary
SPINAL operates on paired base and aligned checkpoints using a fixed prompt pool (512 prompts). A deterministic forward pass (prefilling only) extracts residual activations Hℓ (batch×d) at the final token position for each layer ℓ. For αℓ, SVD of centered Hℓ produces singular values σk; a linear fit to the log–log tail (k from ⌈0.1rℓ⌉ to rℓ) gives slope bβℓ, with αℓ = −1/bβℓ retained only if R² ≥ 0.97. For eLℓ, the next‑token distribution pℓ is obtained by applying the unembedding matrix (logit lens) with a top‑k truncation (k=2048) and renormalization; the Fisher–Rao step between adjacent layers is 2 arccos( BhattacharyyaCoefficient(pℓ, pℓ₊₁) ), averaged over prompts and normalized by π. Terminal block metrics (last 10 layers) are aggregated: Δalign = Σ(α^DPO−α^base − (eL^DPO−eL^base)), terminal coherence Scoh, and gradient footprint Gterm (when available). SPINALScore combines robust z‑scores of these components with fixed weights. The protocol emphasizes reproducibility: identical prompts, token positions, seeds, and numerical settings are mandatory for base/aligned runs.

## Key Results
- Coupled terminal signature: αℓ increases and eLℓ decreases in the final ~10 layers for DPO-aligned models.
- Localization effect holds across five diverse LLM families (Phi-2, DeepSeek, Gemma, Qwen, Llama3).
- SPINALScore correlates with alignment strength and can flag training instability early in the trajectory.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Spectral Sharpening via Tail Exponent αℓ**  
- **Claim:** DPO alignment increases αℓ in terminal layers, indicating stronger representational concentration.  
- **Mechanism:** For each layer ℓ, SVD of the centered activation matrix Hℓ yields singular values σₖ. A linear fit to log–log tail (k ∈ [⌈0.1rℓ⌉, rℓ]) gives slope bβℓ; αℓ = −1/bβℓ. Larger αℓ means faster tail decay, i.e., variance concentrates into fewer principal directions.  
- **Core assumption:** Preference tuning induces anisotropy in late‑layer representations, sharpening the decision surface that directly influences next‑token distribution.  
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 3.1; heavy‑tailed spectral diagnostics are used in prior work for implicit regularization, but DPO‑induced sharpening is novel.  
- **Break condition:** Tail fit fails (R² < 0.97) for many terminal layers; αℓ does not increase monotonically; effect disappears under prompt resampling.

**Belief‑Transport Contraction via Fisher–Rao Length eLℓ**  
- **Claim:** DPO alignment decreases eLℓ between adjacent terminal layers, indicating smoother belief progression.  
- **Mechanism:** For each prompt/position, induce categorical next‑token distribution pℓ via logit lens (top‑k_FR truncation). Fisher–Rao distance d_FR(p,q)=2 arccos(BC(p,q)), where BC is Bhattacharyya coefficient. Aggregate to obtain eLℓ. Smaller eLℓ means less abrupt change per layer.  
- **Core assumption:** Alignment stabilizes the model’s output distribution trajectory near final layers, reducing “belief jolts”.  
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 3.2; Amari & Nagaoka information geometry.  
- **Break condition:** eLℓ does not decrease in terminal window; truncation artifacts dominate; trends inconsistent across families.

**Terminal Localization as Coupled Calibration Zone**  
- **Claim:** Alignment effects are geometrically localized to final ~10 layers, evidenced by coupled αℓ rise and eLℓ fall.  
- **Mechanism:** Summarize terminal shift with Δalign = Σ_{ℓ∈terminal} (α^DPO_ℓ − α^base_ℓ) − (eL^DPO_ℓ − eL^base_ℓ). Positive Δalign indicates simultaneous sharpening and contraction in terminal block.  
- **Core assumption:** Preference gradients concentrate on output‑critical layers, acting as a “scalpel” rather than diffuse rewrite.  
- **Evidence:** Abstract, Section 3.3, Figure 1.  
- **Break condition:** Δalign not consistently positive; terminal randomization ablations fail; effect spreads uniformly.

## Foundational Learning

**Spectral Tail Fitting / Power‑Law Diagnostics**  
*Why needed:* SPINAL uses αℓ derived from fitting a tail of the singular spectrum; understanding the fitting window, R² gating, and potential pitfalls is essential.  
*Quick check:* Why fit only the tail, and why is a goodness‑of‑fit filter mandatory?  
*Answer:* The low‑k singular values are noisy and may not follow the heavy‑tail regime; the tail captures asymptotic decay, and R² ensures a reliable linear relationship in log‑log.

**Information Geometry (Fisher–Rao Metric & Bhattacharyya Coefficient)**  
*Why needed:* eLℓ is a Fisher–Rao step length; the Bhattacharyya coefficient provides a stable angle on the probability simplex.  
*Quick check:* Why is Fisher–Rao preferred over KL divergence for measuring layer‑to‑layer belief change?  
*Answer:* Fisher–Rao is a true Riemannian metric, invariant under reparameterization, and its geodesic distance corresponds to statistical efficiency; KL is asymmetric and lacks metric properties.

**Mechanistic Interpretability (Layer‑wise Localization Hypothesis)**  
*Why needed:* SPINAL posits that alignment effects concentrate in terminal layers, contrasting with global analyses like safety directions.  
*Quick check:* How does SPINAL’s depth‑trajectory view differ from analyzing safety vectors in the residual stream?  
*Answer:* SPINAL compares geometric properties across layers for the same representation, tracing how distributions evolve with depth, whereas residual analyses look for static directions correlated with behavior across prompts.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Prompt Pool → Deterministic Forward Pass (prefill) → Extract per‑layer residual activations Hℓ → Compute αℓ: SVD → tail fitting (R²≥0.97) → Compute pℓ: apply unembedding → top‑k_FR truncation & renormalize → Compute eLℓ: BC(pℓ,pℓ₊₁) → 2 arccos(BC) → aggregate → Terminal metrics (Δalign, Scoh, Gterm) → SPINALScore (weighted robust z‑sum)

**Critical path**  
The pipeline must use identical prompts, token positions, and numerical settings (dtype, seeds, clamping) for base and aligned runs. Any drift (different prompt subsets, top‑k thresholds, or precision) breaks comparability.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Fixed protocol (reproducibility) vs. architectural adaptability (different depths, MoE).  
- Truncation (efficiency) vs. full‑vocab accuracy (captured mass).  
- Scalar score (triage) vs. per‑layer curves (mechanistic insight).

**Failure signatures**  
- High undefined‑layer rate for αℓ due to R² failures → insufficient effective rank or numerical floors.  
- eLℓ changes drastically with k_FR → captured mass too low; increase τ or k_FR.  
- SPINALScore ranking contradicts behavioral trends → check prompt distribution slice or token‑position choice.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Reproduce terminal localization: Plot (ℓ, αℓ, eLℓ) for paired base/aligned checkpoint; verify αℓ rises and eLℓ falls in last 10 layers.  
2. Ablation test: Randomize terminal layer weights (as in paper) and confirm Δalign collapses while earlier layers remain stable.  
3. Robustness sweep: Vary k_FR and token position (prefill vs. short decode); ensure Spearman rank ≥0.9 across sweeps and report captured mass stability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does the SPINAL terminal-layer calibration signature generalize across alignment methods (e.g., RLHF, constitutional AI) and architectures (encoder‑decoder, MoE, long‑context)?  
2. Are terminal layers causally necessary for producing aligned behavior, as the localization hypothesis suggests?  
3. Under what conditions does Fisher‑Rao step length reduction in late layers guarantee more stable or safer outputs?  
4. Does SPINALScore robustly predict alignment failures across diverse models and evaluation scenarios beyond the small studied set?

## Limitations
- Tail‑fit protocol (kmin = ⌈0.1rℓ⌉, R² ≥ 0.97) lacks validation across layers and model families.  
- Fisher‑Rao truncation (top‑k_FR=2048, τ≈0.999) not shown stable; may distort eLℓ.  
- Evaluation restricted to decoder‑only models with DPO; generality to other alignment methods and architectures unknown.  
- SPINALScore’s predictive validity tested on very small sample (n=10).

## Confidence
- Coupled terminal signature (αℓ ↑, eLℓ ↓ in final layers): Medium  
- Localization to final ~10 layers: Medium  
- Diagnostic utility (SPINALScore predicts alignment strength/instability): Low
