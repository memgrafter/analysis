# AeroTherm-GPT: A Verification-Centered LLM Framework for Thermal Protection System Engineering Workflows

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.01738
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01738
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** AeroTherm-GPT achieves 88.7% end-to-end success rate on the HyTPS-Bench thermal protection system design benchmark, with a 4.16× root-cause fix efficiency over flat-checklist repair.

## Executive Summary
Hypersonic thermal protection system (TPS) design imposes strict, sequential engineering constraints that general-purpose large language models (LLMs) cannot satisfy in a single pass due to cascading violations. AeroTherm-GPT introduces the Constraint-Closed-Loop Generation (CCLG) framework, which iteratively generates, validates, and repairs artifacts using a Constraint Dependency Graph (CDG) that prioritizes upstream error resolution based on empirically observed co-resolution patterns. This yields a root-cause fix efficiency of 4.16× compared to a flat checklist, and an end-to-end success rate of 88.7% on the HyTPS-Bench benchmark—a 12.5 percentage point improvement over a non-CDG ablation. The system also preserves general scientific and coding capabilities, demonstrating that constrained iterative repair can effectively bridge LLMs and high-stakes engineering workflows.

## Method Summary
AeroTherm-GPT operationalizes CCLG through a five-stage iterative loop: initial generation of a TPS design memo and simulation code; sequential validation across five engineering gates (unit consistency, physics fidelity, numerical stability, execution, and audit); repair guided by a CDG that ranks failing constraints by estimated downstream impact; execution of the repaired artifact; and audit logging. The CDG is constructed from 4,206 historical repair episodes, encoding empirical conditional probabilities of co-resolution. Constraint-decomposed Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is applied to a base LLM using repair outcomes to form preferences (binary pass/fail, self-evolution, severity-graded), reducing first-pass violations and decreasing the burden on the iterative loop. A process reward model (PRM) scores candidate repair actions during tree search (K=4). The framework uses constraint asset libraries (JSON schemas with validators) and a violation-triggered RAG retriever to supply domain guidance. Training combines outcome-supervised SFT on 8,500 passing traces with constraint-decomposed DPO, and the system is evaluated on the HyTPS-Bench benchmark involving ~2,500 TPS design tasks.

## Key Results
- 88.7% End-to-End Success Rate (EESR) on HyTPS-Bench, +12.5 percentage points over a matched non-CDG baseline.
- Root-Cause Fix Efficiency (RCFE) of 4.16× relative to flat-checklist repair, meaning each repair action on average resolves 4.16 downstream violations.
- The framework retains general scientific reasoning and coding abilities, as verified on out-of-domain benchmarks (e.g., SciBench, HumanEval).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Iterative Closed-Loop Generation (CCLG)
- **Claim:** Iterative closed-loop generation overcomes one-pass LLM failure by systematically verifying and repairing constraint violations.
- **Mechanism:** The workflow cycles through generation, validation, CDG-guided repair, execution, and audit, converting artifact creation into a constrained search with short-circuit termination on upstream failures.
- **Core assumption:** Multi-gate engineering constraints are sequential and causal; upstream violations propagate downstream, making earlier gates higher leverage.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] CCLG organizes TPS artifact generation as an iterative workflow; Flat-VER adds +29.9 pp EESR (Figure 5).
  - [section] Section 3.2 defines the five-stage loop and constraint assets; Table 1 shows RCFE improvement from 1.13 (random) to 4.16 (CDG).
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors are not directly relevant (low FMR, no citations); this is a novel framework with minimal community uptake yet.
- **Break condition:** If the VER loop exhausts the compute budget (8 iterations) without reaching Ready(a)=1, the task fails.

### Mechanism 2: CDG-Guided Upstream-Priority Repair
- **Claim:** CDG-guided upstream-priority repair resolves cascading violations efficiently by targeting root causes with highest estimated downstream gain.
- **Mechanism:** The CDG encodes empirical co-resolution probabilities among constraint categories, respecting a lifecycle ordering prior; during repair, the agent targets the violation node maximizing the sum of edge weights to its descendants, thereby fixing multiple downstream symptoms per action.
- **Core assumption:** Upstream constraint failures are more likely to cause downstream violations; addressing them first reduces total iterations. (Assumption: The empirically calibrated edge weights approximate true propagation probabilities within the TPS domain.)
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] CDG achieves RCFE of 4.16 vs 1.76 for flat checklist; +9.1 pp EESR from CDG ordering (Figure 5).
  - [section] Section 3.3 defines CDG construction from 4,206 repair episodes; edge weights wu→v(c) are empirical conditional probabilities; Eq. (2) shows priority scoring.
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence; the approach is specific to this work.
- **Break condition:** Deep multi-layer cascades where CDG edge weights are miscalibrated (5.2% of tasks fail, Section 5).

### Mechanism 3: Constraint-Decomposed DPO
- **Claim:** Constraint-decomposed DPO trained on CDG-guided repair episodes internalizes constraint-compliant generation, boosting first-pass success and reducing VER load.
- **Mechanism:** Three preference levels (binary pass/fail, self-evolution, severity-graded) guide the model to avoid high-severity violations; the policy learns to produce artifacts that inherently satisfy upstream constraints, lowering violation rates before repair.
- **Core assumption:** Reward modeling on repair outcomes can shape the generation distribution toward constraint satisfaction. (Assumption: The automatically constructed preferences accurately reflect engineering desirability.)
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] DPO contributes +11.5 pp EESR; largest gains in numerical and audit gates (Figure 6).
  - [section] Section 3.4.3 describes three-tier preference construction and DPO objective; Table 1 shows Flat-VER+SFT+DPO reaches 76.2% vs 78.4% CDG-SFT, isolating DPO’s marginal gain.
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence; DPO is a known technique applied in a novel engineering context.
- **Break condition:** If preference data is biased or incomplete, DPO may overfit to narrow compliance patterns and degrade generality.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Natural-language requirement + specs → Generator (initial artifact) → Sequential Validators (Unit→Physics→Numerics→Execution→Audit) → CDG (priority scorer) → RAG Retriever (guidance) → Generator (repair) → Reference Executor (sandboxed run) → Audit Logger → Output (executable package + provenance)

### Critical Path
1. Parse requirement → extract constraints.  
2. Generate initial artifact (Design Memo + code).  
3. Run validators sequentially (U→P→N→E→A).  
4. If any fail, CDG computes priority over failing nodes.  
5. RAG retrieves guidance for top-priority node; sample K candidates; PRM scores; select best.  
6. Apply repair, loop back to 3.  
7. If Ready(a)=1, emit artifact and audit record.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Computation vs. reliability:** More VER iterations improves success but increases latency (avg 4.2 min/task). Mitigate by setting max iterations (8) and early stopping when PRM plateaus.
- **CDG generality:** CDG calibrated on TPS data may not transfer to other domains without recalibration. Mitigate by domain-specific calibration or using lifecycle ordering alone as fallback.
- **DPO vs. SFT:** DPO reduces VER burden but risks overfitting to constraint patterns; LoRA mitigates catastrophic forgetting.

### Failure Signatures
- **CDG weight miscalibration:** Deep cascades where edge probabilities underestimate true propagation (5.2% failure rate). Sign: Repair targets intermediate node but root cause remains upstream.
- **Missing constraint assets:** Unseen material regimes lack validator definitions. Sign: Validator returns "unknown" or fails to parse.
- **Physics bounds too restrictive:** Extreme heat flux exceeds pre-defined parameter ranges. Sign: Physics gate rejects physically plausible values; requires manual bound adjustment.
- **VER budget exhaustion:** Iterations >8 without convergence. Sign: Repeated cycle of partial fixes; audit shows no progress.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Ablation study:** Disable CDG ordering, PRM, or DPO one at a time to quantify incremental EESR gain (Figure 5).
2. **Cross-backbone transfer:** Apply full pipeline to diverse base models (Llama, Qwen, MoE) to test robustness (Section 5.4, Figure 8).
3. **Cross-benchmark validation:** Evaluate on SciBench, HumanEval to confirm transfer of iterative repair and domain reasoning beyond HyTPS-Bench (Track B, Table 7).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can executable constraint validators be automatically generated from engineering standards?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Section 7.1(1) identifies validator engineering as a key limitation and calls for automated generation from standards.
- **Why:** Manual validator creation requires domain expertise, limiting scalability to new domains.
- **Evidence:** Build a system that parses TPS-related standards (e.g., NASA documents) into JSON-schema validators; evaluate correctness against manually created validators on unseen constraints.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can constraint satisfaction be extended to probabilistic validation to handle epistemic uncertainty in complex simulations?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Section 7.1(3) notes current deterministic gates and calls for probabilistic satisfaction.
- **Why:** Real TPS simulations involve uncertainty; deterministic pass/fail may not reflect actual confidence.
- **Evidence:** Define probabilistic validators with tolerance bands; integrate with CDG and VER loop; test on noisy simulation outputs and compare decisions to expert judgment.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can the Constraint Dependency Graph be effectively generalized to other aerospace or multi-physics domains beyond TPS?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Section 7.1(4) states CDG is calibrated only on TPS repair episodes and requires new data for other domains.
- **Why:** Generalization depends on empirical co-resolution patterns that may differ across domains, with unknown transferability.
- **Evidence:** Collect repair episodes in a new domain (e.g., hypersonic propulsion), reconstruct CDG, and measure RCFE improvement vs flat checklist on that domain’s benchmark.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How can the computational latency of the VER loop be reduced for time-sensitive engineering workflows?
- **Basis:** [explicit] Section 7.1(2) reports 4.2 minutes per task average and calls for optimization.
- **Why:** Current iterative repair incurs high wall-clock time, hindering interactive or real-time design use.
- **Evidence:** Implement optimizations (e.g., caching, parallel validation, model distillation); evaluate trade-off between speed and EESR on HyTPS-Bench.

## Limitations
- CDG edge weights and full node mapping are not publicly released, preventing fine-grained verification and exact replication of prioritization.
- Model checkpoints (SFT, DPO, PRM) are unreleased, so reproducing the reported performance requires training on undisclosed data.
- CDG calibration relies on empirical co-resolution probabilities from TPS-specific repair episodes; miscalibration leads to ~5.2% of task failures, and cross-domain generalizability remains unvalidated.
