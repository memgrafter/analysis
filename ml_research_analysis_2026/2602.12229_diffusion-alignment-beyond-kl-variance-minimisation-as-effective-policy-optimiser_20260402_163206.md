# Diffusion Alignment Beyond KL: Variance Minimisation as Effective Policy Optimiser

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.12229
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12229
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** VMPO-Diff fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v1.5 to achieve a higher HPSv2 score (0.2822±0.0040) and substantially higher ImageReward (0.4973±0.0780) compared to GRPO, though with a noted drop in diversity (DreamSim).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Variance Minimisation Policy Optimisation (VMPO) for aligning pretrained diffusion models with reward objectives. It re-frames the standard KL-divergence-based alignment problem as minimizing the variance of log importance weights along the denoising trajectory, a perspective grounded in Sequential Monte Carlo. Theoretically, VMPO's objective is minimized by the reward-tilted target distribution, and under on-policy sampling its gradient coincides with that of KL-based methods, unifying existing approaches. Empirically, VMPO-Diff applied to Stable Diffusion v1.5 with the HPSv2 reward yields superior image quality metrics (HPSv2, ImageReward) over the GRPO baseline, demonstrating its effectiveness as a flexible and computationally efficient alternative for diffusion model alignment.

## Method Summary
VMPO treats the diffusion denoising process as a proposal mechanism and aims to match a reward-tilted target distribution. The core objective minimizes the variance of the log importance weight \( \log w_t = \log \frac{p_{\text{ref}}(x_{t-1}|x_t) \exp(r(x_{t-1})/\beta)}{p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)} \) at each timestep \( t \). To make this tractable, the global variance of the full-trajectory weight is upper-bounded by the sum of per-timestep variances, allowing local optimisation. This is achieved by designing potential functions that telescope to the final reward. Practically, for each training transition \((x_{t-1}, x_t)\) sampled from the old policy \(p_{\theta_{\text{old}}}\), a group of \(K\) samples is drawn. The advantage for a sample is computed as \(A_i = \log R_\theta(x_{t-1}^i|x_t^i) + r(x_{t-1}^i)/\beta - \text{LOO baseline}\), where \(\log R_\theta = \log p_{\text{ref}}/p_\theta\) and the baseline is the group mean excluding the current sample. The loss is the mean squared advantage, combined with a KL penalty \( \lambda \ KL(p_\theta || p_{\theta_{\text{old}}}) \). An amortised neural estimator \(M_\phi(t)\) can be trained to predict the expected log weight, reducing the required group size \(K\).

## Key Results
*   On SD1.5 with HPSv2 reward, VMPO-Diff achieves a higher HPSv2 score (0.2822±0.0040) than GRPO (0.2684±0.0035).
*   The same setup yields a substantially higher ImageReward (0.4973±0.0780 vs. 0.3449±0.0758).
*   For visual text rendering on SD3.5-M, VMPO achieves comparable OCR accuracy (0.91) to Flow-GRPO (0.92) with significantly lower computational resources.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Minimizing the variance of log importance weights along the denoising trajectory aligns the policy \( p_\theta \) with the reward-tilted target distribution \( p_{\text{tilt}} \).
*   **Mechanism:** The objective \( L^{\text{Var}}_h(t;\theta) = \frac{1}{2} \text{Var}_h(\log w_t) \) with \( w_t = \frac{p_{\text{ref}}(x_{t-1}|x_t)\exp(r(x_{t-1})/\beta)}{p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)} \) is minimized when \( p_\theta = p_{\text{tilt}} \). Zero variance implies \( w_t \) is constant almost surely, forcing \( p_\theta \propto p_{\text{ref}} \exp(r/\beta) \), which is \( p_{\text{tilt}} \) up to normalisation.
*   **Core assumption:** The reference distribution \( h \) shares the same support as \( p_{\text{ref}} \) and \( p_\theta \); the denoising steps are conditionally independent given the Markov property.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "We prove that the variance objective is minimised by the reward-tilted target distribution." [section 3, Proposition 1] Formal proof that the optimiser \( \theta^* \) yields \( p_{\theta^*} = p_{\text{tilt}} \).

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** The variance of the full-trajectory importance weight \( w_{0:T} \) is upper-bounded by the sum of per-timestep variances, enabling tractable local optimization.
*   **Mechanism:** Using Jensen’s inequality on the SMC decomposition, \( \text{Var}_h(\log w_{0:T}) \leq \sum_{t=1}^T \text{Var}_h(\log w_t) \). By designing potentials \( U(x_t:T) \) to satisfy \( \prod_t U(x_t:T) = \exp(r(x_0)/\beta) \), the global variance reduction reduces to minimizing local \( \text{Var}_h(\log w_t) \) at each step, with \( w_t \) redefined to incorporate potentials.
*   **Core assumption:** The denoising process is Markovian (\( p_{\text{ref}}(x_t|x_{t+1:T}) = p_{\text{ref}}(x_t|x_{t+1}) \)); the potential functions satisfy the telescoping product constraint.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section C.1] Derivation of the bound \( \text{Var}_h(\log w_{0:T}) \leq \sum_t \text{Var}_h(\log w_t) \). [section C.2] "Option 1: Return-to-Go" and "Option 2: Difference" specify potential choices that satisfy the constraint.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Under on-policy sampling (\( h = p_{\theta_{\text{old}}} \)), the gradient of the variance objective coincides with the gradient of the KL-based alignment objective.
*   **Mechanism:** When \( h = p_\theta \), the gradient \( \nabla_\theta L^{\text{Var}}_h(t;\theta) \) reduces to \( \nabla_\theta \text{KL}(p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t) || p_{\text{tilt}}(x_{t-1}|x_t)) \), despite \( L^{\text{Var}}_h \) being a biased estimator. The Monte Carlo estimator \( \hat{L}_{\text{Var}} \) with a leave-one-out baseline gives an unbiased gradient estimator matching \( J_{\text{KL}} \)'s gradient.
*   **Core assumption:** The rollout samples \( x_{t-1} \) come from the current policy \( p_{\theta_{\text{old}}} \); the estimator uses a leave-one-out baseline (\( \bar{f}_t \)) that cancels bias.
*   **Evidence anchors:** [section 3, Proposition 1] "Moreover, \( \nabla_\theta L^{\text{Var}}_h(t;\theta)|_{h=p_\theta} = \nabla_\theta \text{KL}(p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t)||p_{\text{tilt}}(x_{t-1}|x_t)) \)." [Appendix B.2] Proof that \( \mathbb{E}_{p_\theta}[\nabla_\theta \hat{L}_{\text{Var}}(t;\theta)|_{h=p_\theta}] = \nabla_\theta \text{KL}(\ldots) \).

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC)**
    *   **Why needed here:** VMPO reinterprets diffusion alignment as an SMC problem where denoising is a proposal and reward defines target potentials. This justifies the variance minimisation strategy for aligning the final marginal distribution.
    *   **Quick check question:** How does the telescoping product of potentials ensure the marginal over \( x_0 \) matches the reward-tilted distribution?
*   **Concept: KL Divergence in Policy Optimization**
    *   **Why needed here:** KL regularization (\( \text{KL}(p_\theta || p_{\text{ref}}) \)) stabilizes training by limiting policy deviation. VMPO’s gradient equivalence shows it inherits these regularization benefits while optimising a different primary objective.
    *   **Quick check question:** Why does \( \text{KL}(p_\theta || p_{\text{tilt}}) \) decompose into reward maximization minus a KL penalty to the reference model? (Answer: \( \log p_{\text{tilt}} = \log p_{\text{ref}} + r/\beta - \log Z \), so KL becomes \( -\mathbb{E}_{p_\theta}[r/\beta] + \text{KL}(p_\theta||p_{\text{ref}}) + \text{const} \).)
*   **Concept: Variance Reduction via Baselines**
    *   **Why needed here:** Unlike pure REINFORCE, VMPO uses the group-mean advantage (leave-one-out baseline) and an implicit reference (\( p_{\text{ref}} \)) to reduce gradient variance without bias, which is crucial for high-variance diffusion rollouts.
    *   **Quick check question:** Why does subtracting \( \log p_{\text{ref}}(x_{t-1}|x_t)/p_\theta(x_{t-1}|x_t) \) from the reward act as a KL-regularizing baseline?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Pretrained diffusion model (p_ref) -> Current policy (p_θ, UNet/LoRA) -> Rollout buffer (trajectories x_t) -> Mean estimator (M_ϕ, neural net) -> VMPO loss (L_VMPO(θ,ϕ))
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Initialize \( p_\theta \) from \( p_{\text{ref}} \) via LoRA.
    2.  For N epochs:
        a. Sample K trajectories using \( p_{\theta_{\text{old}}} \) with DDIM (e.g., 50 steps).
        b. Compute reward \( r(x_{t-1}) \) for each \( x_{t-1} \) (clean samples via \( \hat{x}_\theta(x_t,t) \) or direct reward).
        c. Compute \( \log R_\theta = \log p_{\text{ref}}/p_\theta \) for all \( (x_{t-1}, x_t) \) pairs.
        d. Update \( M_\phi \) via MSE loss on \( (\log R_\theta + r/\beta - M_\phi(t))^2 \).
        e. Update \( p_\theta \) via \( -\sum A_i \log p_\theta \) with advantage \( A_i = \log R_\theta(x_{t-1}^i|x_t^i) - \text{LOO baseline} \), optionally clipped.
    3.  Periodically evaluate held-out prompts with reward model and CLIP/DreamSim.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **Potential function choice:** Return-to-go (simpler, no intermediate reward estimation) vs. Difference (local credit assignment, requires estimating \( r(x_t) \)). Corpus evidence for intermediate reward design is limited; the paper adopts \( r(x_t)=r(\hat{x}_\theta(x_t,t)) \).
    *   **Monte Carlo vs. Amortized variance estimation:** Low K (e.g., 8) requires \( M_\phi \) for stability; high K is sample-inefficient. The paper uses \( M_\phi \) with \( K=8 \).
    *   **KL regularization strength:** Coefficient 1 (default) mitigates reward hacking but may cap reward gains. Adjustment per reward model scale is necessary.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Reward hacking:** CLIPScore/DreamSim drop while target reward rises (observed in Table 1). Monitor non-target metrics every epoch.
    *   **Training instability:** Gradient norms explode → reduce learning rate or increase KL coefficient.
    *   **Sample degeneracy:** Low effective sample size in \( \log R_\theta \) values → check if \( p_\theta \) collapses to few modes (histogram \( x_{t-1} \) diversity).
    *   **Incorrect intermediate reward:** For Difference, if \( r(x_t) \) poorly estimated (e.g., blurred predictions), variance minimization may fail. Monitor \( |r(x_t)-r(x_0)| \) during rollout.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Unit test estimator variance:** With frozen \( p_\theta \), train \( M_\phi \) on a small rollout; verify \( M_\phi(t) \approx \mathbb{E}_h[\log w_t] \) by comparing to a large-MC ground truth.
    2.  **Gradient equivalence check:** Compute \( \nabla_\theta L_{\text{VMPO}} \) and \( \nabla_\theta \text{KL}(p_\theta||p_{\text{tilt}}) \) on a minibatch; they should match (within MC error) when \( h = p_\theta \).
    3.  **End-to-end alignment on SD1.5 with HPSv2:** Baseline: GRPO vs. VMPO-Diff. Track HPSv2, CLIPScore, DreamSim over 5 epochs to reproduce Table 1 trends.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** Which VMPO variant (potential/var-minimisation) is optimal across reward tasks?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Appendix F says the design space is vast and calls for systematic benchmarking to identify strengths across scenarios.
*   **Why unresolved:** Only VMPO-R2G and VMPO-Diff tested on limited rewards (HPSv2, ImageReward); full variant space unexplored.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Large-scale evaluation comparing all VMPO variants on multiple rewards (HPSv2, ImageReward, OCR) using reward and diversity metrics, with statistical comparisons.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** Does VMPO generalize to other modalities (video, audio) and advanced models (e.g., Qwen-Image)?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Appendix F states empirical work focused on SD1.5/SD3.5-M, making extension to videos and newer models a "promising direction".
*   **Why unresolved:** No experiments on video/audio diffusion models yet.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply VMPO to video models (e.g., HunyuanVideo) and advanced image models; show improvement over KL-based alignment on modality-specific metrics.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** How can VMPO avoid reward hacking (reward gains at cost of diversity) beyond standard KL penalties?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Appendix F notes VMPO remains "susceptible to reward hacking" and asks for regularisation strategies beyond KL.
*   **Why unresolved:** Current KL regularisation does not prevent diversity drop; new methods needed.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Introduce diversity-preserving regularisers (e.g., feature matching, MMD); demonstrate improved DreamSim scores with minimal reward loss on benchmarks.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** Is VMPO effective for aligning one-step diffusion models and beneficial over existing single-step methods?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Appendix F lists applying VMPO to one-step generative models as a future direction.
*   **Why unresolved:** VMPO only evaluated on multi-step samplers; compatibility with one-step training (e.g., consistency distillation) unknown.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Adapt VMPO objective for one-step models; train and compare against single-step baselines (e.g., Diffusion DPO) on reward alignment and sample quality.

## Limitations
*   Theoretical guarantees rely on strong assumptions: the reference distribution \( h \) shares support with \( p_{\text{ref}} \) and \( p_\theta \), and the denoising process is strictly Markov. Real diffusion models may violate these, potentially destabilizing the objective.
*   Empirical results are restricted to two reward models (HPSv2, ImageReward) and a single base model (SD1.5) for alignment, plus one text-rendering task (SD3.5-M). The observed drop in DreamSim diversity is acknowledged but not mitigated.
*   The claim of significantly lower computational cost for SD3.5-M versus Flow-GRPO is qualitative; exact FLOPs, memory footprints, or wall‑clock times are not provided.

## Confidence
*   **Theoretical recovery of reward‑tilted distribution & gradient equivalence to KL on‑policy:** High – proofs are rigorous under stated assumptions, though practical deviations may degrade behaviour.
*   **Empirical superiority over GRPO on primary metrics (HPSv2, ImageReward):** Medium – results show statistically significant gains, but evaluation uses few seeds and limited reward models; hyperparameter sensitivity is insufficiently explored.
*   **VMPO as a flexible, compute‑efficient alternative for diffusion alignment:** Medium‑Low – evidence is promising but not yet broad; flexibility across reward types and purported resource savings need stronger validation.
