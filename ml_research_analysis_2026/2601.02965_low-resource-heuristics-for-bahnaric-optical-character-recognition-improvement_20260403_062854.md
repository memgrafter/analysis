# Low-Resource Heuristics for Bahnaric Optical Character Recognition Improvement

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.02965
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02965
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** Heuristic post‑processing increased word‑level OCR accuracy from 72.86% to 79.26% (6.4 percentage points) on a sample of 969 words from the Bahnar Dialect Dictionary.

## Executive Summary
This study tackles the digitization of scanned Bahnar language documents, which suffer from degraded image quality and the absence of dedicated OCR support. The authors develop a lightweight pipeline that first segments structured dictionary tables using OpenCV‑based line and contour analysis, then applies Tesseract OCR (with Vietnamese/English models) to each cell, and finally corrects systematic errors—especially in Bahnar‑specific diacritics—using a frequency‑based n‑gram dictionary built from a validated Bahnar vocabulary. Evaluation on 969 words shows an absolute accuracy gain of 6.4 percentage points, demonstrating that carefully designed heuristics can significantly improve OCR in low‑resource settings without requiring large training corpora or deep learning models.

## Method Summary
The pipeline consists of image preprocessing (cropping, Otsu binarization, edge detection, rotation correction), table detection via Hough line transforms and intersection computation, cell segmentation, and OCR using Tesseract with PSM 6 and the `vie+en` language models. A reference dictionary is constructed from a cleaned Bahnar word list; n‑grams of lengths 2–4 are extracted and their frequencies recorded per word length. During post‑processing, a sliding‑window algorithm flags n‑grams with low frequencies (<5) and attempts single‑character substitutions from the Bahnar alphabet to maximize frequency. Additional character mappings address known artifact conversions. The system outputs structured text with tables reformatted and non‑table regions linearized.

## Key Results
- Word accuracy improved from 72.86% to 79.26% (6.4% absolute gain).
- The heuristic corrected 62 additional words, mainly by fixing Bahnar‑specific diacritics.
- The approach achieves gains without large training corpora, using only OpenCV and Tesseract.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Table detection and cell segmentation isolate structured content.** OpenCV detects horizontal/vertical lines, computes their intersections, and identifies rectangular cell boundaries. This segmentation prevents layout confusion during OCR by feeding clean, uniform text blocks to the engine. The method assumes well‑defined orthogonal borders and contained cell content; breakage occurs with nonlinear layouts or missing lines.

2. **Tesseract’s LSTM model yields a usable baseline despite lacking Bahnar training.** Using Vietnamese/English models (PSM 6) on preprocessed, layout‑aware inputs leverages visual similarity between Bahnar diacritics and supported scripts. The core assumption is that Bahnar base characters share glyph features with Vietnamese/English; systematic diacritic errors are then left for post‑hoc correction. If Bahnar glyphs are too distinct, base misrecognitions would be unrecoverable.

3. **Frequency‑based n‑gram dictionary corrects local errors.** The heuristic queries a precomputed map of n‑gram frequencies against word length. Low‑frequency n‑grams trigger a constrained substitution search within the Bahnar alphabet, selecting the variant with highest normalized count. This exploits the regularity of Bahnar character clusters. The dictionary’s coverage and threshold (`thres=5`) are critical; an incomplete vocabulary may over‑correct or miss fixes.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (scanned PDF) → Preprocessing (crop, binarize, line detection, rotation correction) → Table detection (line intersections, cell segmentation) → OCR (Tesseract on each region) → Heuristic post‑processing (n‑gram correction) → Output (structured text)

- **Critical path:** Cropping → Binarization → Table detection → OCR → Heuristics. A failure in table detection cascades, producing merged or fragmented cells that OCR cannot resolve.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - OpenCV (traditional CV) vs. deep learning for tables: OpenCV is lightweight and interpretable but only works for clean, ruled tables.
  - Heuristic post‑processing vs. neural language model: Heuristics are data‑efficient and explainable but brittle; neural models need more data (section IV.C shows prior failure).
  - Threshold (`thres=5`): Empirical; low values risk over‑correction, high values under‑correct.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Table segmentation: Missing lines → merged/fragmented cells → OCR layout errors.
  - OCR stage: Systematic misrecognition of Bahnar‑specific base characters not fixed by diacritics.
  - Heuristic stage: Over‑correction of valid low‑frequency n‑grams or under‑correction due to high threshold/incomplete dictionary.
  - Skew rotation error: Misaligned lines → invalid cell corners → skipped tables.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Validate preprocessing on a sample page: Ensure Otsu preserves diacritics and Hough lines align with visible borders.
  2. Isolate table detection: Run segmentation on one page, manually count detected cells vs. expected layout.
  3. Compare baseline OCR vs. heuristic on 10 table cells: Measure word accuracy change and verify examples of diacritic corrections.

## Limitations
- The validated Bahnar n‑gram dictionary is not provided; its coverage and representativeness remain unknown.
- The complete Bahnar alphabet is unspecified, which is essential for the substitution search in post‑processing.
- The table detection method depends on clear orthogonal borders and is brittle to degraded rulings; the use of `vie+en` Tesseract models may cause systematic base‑character errors not correctable by heuristics.

## Confidence
- **Table detection & segmentation:** Medium (mechanism clear but fragile).
- **Baseline OCR with Tesseract:** Low (weak evidence; high baseline error suggests unrecoverable issues).
- **Heuristic post‑processing:** Medium‑Low (improvement shown on a small single‑source test; dictionary uncertainty and threshold sensitivity reduce robustness).
