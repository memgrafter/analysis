# Which course? Discourse! Teaching Discourse and Generation in the Era of LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02878  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02878  
- **Reference count:** 28  
- **Primary result:** Developed a new cross-listed undergraduate course that integrates discourse theories with NLG/LLMs, yielding 95% student satisfaction and strong impact on academic/career plans.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the gap in undergraduate curricula that separates discourse processing from natural language generation (NLG), especially in the context of large language models (LLMs). The authors designed and delivered a novel upper-level course, “Computational Discourse and Natural Language Generation,” which weaves linguistic discourse theories (RST, QUD, Centering) throughout NLG modeling, evaluation, and LLM analysis. Using open-ended assignments, project-based learning, and collaborative design across disciplines, the course aims to equip students with an integrated understanding of how discourse structure influences coherent generation. An independent survey of 19 students showed overwhelming satisfaction (95%) and reported significant influence on their future work (80% intent to apply; 75% impacted academic/career plans). Students notably improved in their ability to analyze LLM capabilities, limitations, and the role of discourse in coherence.

## Method Summary
The course was built around a “threading” pedagogical strategy: instead of treating discourse theories as a separate module, they were embedded into every major topic related to NLG and LLMs (e.g., decoding strategies, evaluation, RLHF). Content was co-created by instructors from linguistics, computer science, and cognitive science to serve a diverse student body (majors: CS, Linguistics, Mathematics, Statistics). The curriculum combined lectures, in-class mini-workshops for hands-on annotation, three progressive open-ended assignments, and a semester-long group research project. Assignments intentionally lacked a single correct answer, encouraging high-level exploration, scope definition, and critical analysis. Tools included `transformers`, `minicons`, LLM APIs, and Jupyter notebooks. The course ran once as a pilot; outcomes were measured via a post-course survey covering satisfaction, learning gains, and career impact.

## Key Results
- 95% of surveyed students reported satisfaction with the course.
- 80% indicated they intend to apply the knowledge in future work.
- 75% said the course influenced their academic or career plans.
- Overwhelmingly, students agreed the course improved their understanding of LLM capabilities, limitations, and discourse structure’s impact on coherence.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Threaded Integration of Discourse and NLG
- **Claim:** Deeply threading discourse theories (e.g., RST, QUD, Centering) throughout NLG content enables students to form integrated mental models connecting linguistic insights to generative model behavior.
- **Mechanism:** Conceptual scaffolding across lectures and assignments repeatedly surfaces discourse phenomena (coherence, entity tracking, QUD) within NLG contexts (decoding, evaluation, RLHF), creating associative links that prevent compartmentalized knowledge.
- **Core assumption:** Students retain and apply concepts better when encountered in varied, functionally relevant contexts rather than isolated modules.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...deeply integrated linguistic discourse theories... with NLG modeling, evaluation, and LLM analysis..."
  - [section 3] "Threading Discourse within NLG... embed core concepts... into modeling and decoding techniques..."
  - [corpus] Corpus shows related courses treat discourse and NLG separately; this course's novel integration is its primary differentiator.
- **Break condition:** If discourse content remains in distinct, late-semester modules disconnected from hands-on NLG work, integration fails and students perceive them as separate topics.

### Mechanism 2: Open-Ended, Exploration-Focused Assignments
- **Claim:** Open-ended assignments foster a researcher mindset, improving critical analysis of LLM capabilities/limitations beyond surface-level interaction.
- **Mechanism:** Removing prescriptive “solve this problem” constraints forces students to define scope, select datasets, and design analyses, mirroring real research cycles and encouraging ownership of inquiry into discourse phenomena.
- **Core assumption:** Authentic engagement with uncertainty and open questions builds deeper cognitive skills than following predetermined solution paths, especially in a rapidly evolving field.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3] "All assignments are intentionally open-ended... focus... should incorporate high-level exploration and project design..."
  - [section 5] Assignment descriptions emphasize “open-ended” questions, analysis, and freedom in model/dataset choice.
  - [section 6] Survey shows high perceived gains in “critical thinking skills” and “applying theoretical concepts.”
- **Break condition:** If assignments become overly scaffolded with step-by-step instructions or have single-correct answers, the exploratory learning objective is undermined.

### Mechanism 3: Collaborative Multi-Expert Course Design
- **Claim:** Collaborative multi-expert course design (linguistics, CS, cognitive science) ensures content is simultaneously accessible to diverse backgrounds and adequately challenging, sustaining engagement across student populations.
- **Mechanism:** Cross-disciplinary vetting during development surfaces hidden assumptions, balances technical and conceptual depth, and creates multiple entry points (e.g., linguistic examples with computational relevance, code demos for abstract concepts).
- **Core assumption:** Interdisciplinary topics suffer if taught from a single paradigm; inclusive pedagogy requires embedded multiple perspectives.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3] "Collaborative Design of Content... brings together diverse pedagogical traditions... ensuring... reflects the multifaceted nature..."
  - [section 1] Team has “complementary expertise” and spans “multiple institutions and disciplinary backgrounds.”
  - [section 6] Enrollment from “Computer Science, Linguistics, Mathematics, Statistics...” with varied prior knowledge, yet 95% satisfaction.
- **Break condition:** If content is developed primarily by one disciplinary group, it will likely bias toward that group’s norms, increasing cognitive load for students from other backgrounds and reducing perceived relevance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Core Language Modeling Principles** (autoregressive training, decoding strategies)  
  *Why needed here:* Baseline for all subsequent analysis of LLM outputs; students must distinguish model mechanics (next-token prediction) from perceived discourse capabilities.  
  *Quick check:* Why does nucleus sampling produce more diverse text than greedy decoding, and how might this affect discourse coherence metrics?
- **Linguistic Discourse Frameworks** (RST, QUD, Centering)  
  *Why needed here:* Provide formal, theory-grounded lenses to hypothesize about and analyze coherence beyond superficial fluency.  
  *Quick check:* How would an RST `ELABORATION` relation versus a `CONTRAST` relation differently constrain what content should be included in a summary?
- **NLG Evaluation Paradigms** (reference-based vs. free, intrinsic vs. extrinsic, LLM-as-a-judge)  
  *Why needed here:* Critical for assessing whether improved generation is genuine or artifact; discourse-level phenomena are often poorly captured by standard metrics.  
  *Quick check:* Why might BLEU score fail to detect a coherent but factually inconsistent long-form generation, and what alternative evaluation could be used?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Lectures (thematic) -> Mini-workshops (annotation) -> Assignments (3 progressive) -> Project (group research cycle)
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Establish LM/decoding basics (Assignment 1).  
  2. Introduce discourse theories concurrently with prompting/evaluation (Assignments 2 & 3).  
  3. Launch project after core concepts are covered (post-Assignment 2), using Assignment 3 for pilot ideas.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Exploration vs. Scalability: Open-ended assignments yield rich learning but require intensive manual grading; no simple auto-grader.  
  - Interdisciplinary vs. Depth: Covering both linguistics and CS foundations limits time for deep dives into any single sub-area.  
  - Computational Access vs. Feasibility: Reliance on Colab/APIs restricts model size experiments; larger models require external resources.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High volume of coding questions in forums → foundational programming gaps not addressed.  
  - Project proposals overly vague or misaligned with discourse focus → insufficient guidance on scoping.  
  - Students default to “evaluate with BLEU/ROUGE” in projects → failure to internalize discourse-centric evaluation principles.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Decoding & Diversity: Implement greedy, top-p, min-p decoding on a story prompt. Quantify (e.g., distinct n-grams) and qualify output coherence; relate to discourse plan disruption.  
  2. Discourse Connective Minimal Pairs: Use a dataset like Drenhaus et al. (2014). Compute LM probabilities for pairs differing in a connective (e.g., `because` vs. `despite`). Test if LM sensitivity correlates with human coherence judgments.  
  3. QUD-Based Content Selection: Prompt an LLM to generate a question for a news paragraph (potential QUD). Judge salience (human or LLM-judge). Analyze if generated QUDs align with salient entities/actions, probing discourse planning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1: How can computational resource barriers be mitigated for student projects involving large language models in discourse-NLG courses?
- *Basis in paper:* The authors identify compute resources as a main challenge, noting that Colab Pro was insufficient for some assignments and that providing API credits or dedicated resources would be helpful (Section 7).
- *Why unresolved:* Cost and infrastructure constraints limit hands-on access to state-of-the-art models, hindering equitable exploration of discourse phenomena at scale.
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Comparative studies on the effectiveness and feasibility of different resource-provision models (e.g., university HPC integration, subsidized APIs, optimized smaller models) for achieving course learning objectives.

### Open Question 2: What viable alternatives to manual grading exist for open-ended, exploratory assignments in NLP education while preserving the spirit of inquiry?
- *Basis in paper:* The authors note that open-ended assignments, though educationally valuable, entail difficult-to-scale manual grading (Section 7). They call for innovative ways to combine exploration with automatic grading.
- *Why unresolved:* Traditional auto-graders struggle with qualitative analysis, error diagnosis, and creative problem-solving central to such assignments.
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Development and empirical validation of rubrics or AI-assisted tools that can reliably assess code soundness, analysis depth, and reasoning quality in exploratory NLP tasks, benchmarked against expert human grading.

### Open Question 3: How can a discourse-NLG curriculum be effectively expanded beyond English to incorporate multilingual discourse frameworks and LLM evaluation?
- *Basis in paper:* The authors explicitly state the first iteration remains “largely English-centric” and plan to expand to other languages in the future (Section 7).
- *Why unresolved:* Major discourse theories (RST, QUD) and evaluation datasets are heavily English-biased; multilingual discourse phenomena may differ, and LLM performance varies across languages.
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Creation of multilingual discourse corpora annotated with frameworks like RST/QUD; comparative studies on LLM discourse capabilities across diverse languages; pedagogical strategies for integrating these into assignments.

### Open Question 4: What specific pedagogical scaffolding techniques most effectively support students from heterogeneous academic backgrounds (e.g., linguistics vs. CS) in a unified, technically demanding discourse-NLG course?
- *Basis in paper:* The authors acknowledge varying levels of prior experience due to the course’s interdisciplinary nature and suggest additional recitation sessions may be needed as class size increases (Section 7).
- *Why unresolved:* Without tailored support, students may struggle with either the linguistic theory or the computational implementation, undermining the goal of deep integration.
- *What evidence would resolve it:* Controlled experiments comparing different scaffolding interventions (e.g., modular preparatory modules, tiered assignments, collaborative team composition) on learning outcomes and self-efficacy across diverse student populations.

## Limitations
- Small, single-site sample (19 respondents) limits generalizability.
- Outcomes rely on self-reported surveys without objective measures or a control group.
- No causal attribution linking specific design features (threading, open-endedness, collaboration) to observed effects; survey instrument psychometrics unknown.
- Long-term retention and application of knowledge remain untested.

## Confidence
- Threaded integration builds cohesive understanding: **Medium**
- Open-ended assignments foster researcher mindset: **Low**
- Multi-expert design ensures inclusivity: **Medium**
- Overall positive outcomes (satisfaction, impact): **Medium**

## Next Checks
