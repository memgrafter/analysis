# Building a Strong Instruction Language Model for a Less-Resourced Language

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.01691
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01691
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** GaMS3-12B outperforms Gemma 3 12B on all evaluations, achieves >60% win rate against GPT-4o in the Slovene LLM Arena, and ranks 3rd on the Slovenian-LLM-Eval leaderboard among similar-sized models.

## Executive Summary
This work adapts Gemma 3 12B to Slovene, a less-resourced language, through a systematic multi-stage process. It combines three stages of continual pretraining (CPT) on 140B tokens—including parallel alignment, mixed-language base training, and long-context exposure—with two stages of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on over 200k instruction examples. The resulting GaMS3-12B model demonstrably surpasses its base model across all benchmarks, secures a >60% win rate against GPT-4o in the Slovene LLM Arena, and places third on the Slovenian-LLM-Eval leaderboard for similarly sized models. The methodology highlights how strategic data curation and curriculum learning can overcome the scarcity of high-quality native text.

## Method Summary
The adaptation pipeline begins with data augmentation to overcome Slovene digital scarcity: physical archives are processed via OCR, and high-quality English corpora are machine-translated. Continual pretraining follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) *parallel alignment* uses bilingual corpora to map English representations to Slovene, (2) *base CPT* trains on a Slovene-dominant mix that includes related South Slavic languages (for transfer) and English (to prevent forgetting), and (3) *long CPT* extends the context to 131k tokens using only the highest-quality long documents. Finally, two-stage SFT—first on general instructions, then on chat data with safety examples—instills instruction-following and conversational ability. This staged approach leverages the base model’s English knowledge while systematically building Slovene-specific linguistic and cultural competence.

## Key Results
- GaMS3-12B outperforms Gemma 3 12B on all evaluated benchmarks, including knowledge, reasoning, and chat tasks.
- Achieves a >60% win rate against GPT-4o in the Slovene LLM Arena, indicating strong user preference in practical chat settings.
- Ranks 3rd on the Slovenian-LLM-Eval leaderboard among similarly-sized models, confirming competitive performance in standardized evaluations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Parallel Alignment
**Claim:** Parallel alignment with translated/parallel corpora bridges the representational gap between the English-centric base model and Slovene.  
**Mechanism:** The model is exposed to aligned sentence- or document-level pairs (English followed by Slovene) in the first CPT stage. This encourages internal representations to map semantically equivalent content across languages, leveraging existing English knowledge instead of learning Slovene de novo.  
**Core assumption:** The base model’s English representations are sufficiently rich that aligning them to Slovene is more efficient than learning Slovene from scratch.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [abstract] "...three-stage continual pretraining...followed by two-stage supervised fine-tuning...The resulting GaMS3-12B model outperforms Gemma 3 12B on all evaluations."  
- [section 3.1] "The purpose of the parallel alignment stage is to explicitly align the English and Slovene languages inside the model...We aligned English and Slovene texts on three levels."  
**Break condition:** If parallel corpora are low-quality, mismatched in domain, or too small, alignment may fail or introduce noise.  
**Corpus:** DGT, MaCoCu, KAS, Wikipedia.

### Mechanism 2: Mixed-Language Base CPT
**Claim:** Base CPT with a mixed-language corpus (Slovene dominant, plus related languages and English) builds foundational knowledge while preventing catastrophic forgetting.  
**Mechanism:** Training on this diverse mix allows the model to (1) absorb Slovene-specific grammar and facts from monolingual sources, (2) leverage typological similarity with Croatian, Bosnian, and Serbian to improve generalization and handle code-switching, and (3) retain general world knowledge from the English portion.  
**Core assumption:** Linguistic similarity enables positive transfer, and a minority of English data preserves broader capabilities without dominating adaptation.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [section 3.2] "We included English corpora to prevent forgetting, while Croatian, Bosnian, and Serbian corpora were utilized due to their cultural and language similarity to Slovene...previous research shows that adding such languages is beneficial."  
- [table 2] Explicit percentages: FinePDFs-Slv 5.9%, FinePDFs-Hrv 9.5%, etc.  
**Break condition:** If related-language proportion is too high, the model becomes a poor Slovene specialist; if too low, it misses transfer benefits. If English proportion is too high, Slovene adaptation is insufficient.  
**Corpus:** Table 2 composition (FinePDFs-Slv/Hrv/Srp/Bos, Nemotron splits).

### Mechanism 3: Long CPT
**Claim:** Long CPT with curated, high-quality, long-context documents develops the ability to process and reason over extended Slovene texts.  
**Mechanism:** After initial stages with 65k context, training continues at a full 131k context window using only the highest-quality, longest documents. This forces the model to learn long-range dependencies, maintain coherence, and understand document-level structure in Slovene.  
**Core assumption:** The base architecture supports long contexts, and providing relevant long-form Slovene data during CPT is necessary to activate and fine-tune this capability.  
**Evidence anchors:**  
- [section 3.3] "In the final, long CPT stage, the context window was increased to 131k tokens...only the following, highest-quality, corpora were used."  
- [evaluation section 6.2] GaMS3 shows more truncation errors than Gemma 3, indicating partial failure to leverage long context, hypothesized to stem from insufficient long-context SFT data.  
**Break condition:** If long-context data is scarce or of poor quality (e.g., OCR artifacts), this stage may not yield benefits and could destabilize training.  
**Corpus:** Table 3 long corpora (KAS-Extension, long Trendi, math journals).

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Continual Pretraining (CPT)
**Why needed:** The base Gemma 3 model is pretrained overwhelmingly on English. CPT adapts its internal weights to Slovene’s statistical properties, vocabulary, and cultural context without discarding general knowledge. It is the foundation for all subsequent language-specific capabilities.  
**Quick check question:** What is the key difference between pretraining from scratch and continual pretraining for a less-resourced language?

### Concept: Data Augmentation via OCR & Translation
**Why needed:** Authentic Slovene digital text is scarce (~40B tokens vs. 30T for Llama 4). OCR of physical archives and translation of high-quality English corpora are essential to reach the 140B token scale needed for effective CPT.  
**Quick check question:** Why is machine translation used for data augmentation despite potential quality issues?

### Concept: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Instruction Following
**Why needed:** CPT teaches the model *about* Slovene, but not *how* to respond to user prompts instructionally. SFT on prompt-response pairs teaches format adherence, helpfulness, and task performance (QA, writing, translation) required for an assistant.  
**Quick check question:** What specific risk does the paper identify regarding the use of machine-translated SFT data?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Data Ingestion Pipeline (OCR tools + post-processing + cleaning) -> CPT Stages (Stage 1: Parallel Alignment -> Stage 2: Base CPT -> Stage 3: Long CPT) -> SFT Stages (Stage 1: General Instruction -> Stage 2: Chat & Safety) -> Evaluation Suite (Slovenian-LLM-Eval, Translation Benchmark, LLM Arena).

### Critical path
Data Collection & Preparation (OCR/Translation) -> Parallel Alignment CPT -> Base CPT -> Long CPT -> General SFT -> Chat SFT -> Evaluation. Any failure in data quality propagates. The multi-stage CPT sequence is critical; skipping parallel alignment or long CPT would likely reduce final performance.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Data Quality vs. Quantity:** Machine-translated Wikipedia (high quantity) vs. human-translated KAS (high quality); mix balances scale and fidelity.
- **Context Length vs. Compute:** Using 65k context for most CPT to fit LEONARDO hardware vs. full 131k context for final stage; staged approach manages resource limits.
- **Specialization vs. Generality:** Including English/Nemotron data preserves general skills but dilutes Slovene specialization; mix ratios (Table 2) are a key tuned parameter.
- **SFT Data Source:** Translated chat data (GaMS-Nemotron-Chat) provides scale but introduces "machine-Slovene" style, noted in Arena results.

### Failure signatures
1. **Poor benchmark scores on manually corrected Slovene tasks** (Table 7): Indicates inadequate Slovene linguistic/cultural adaptation.
2. **High truncation or markdown errors in translation** (Table 9): Signals failure in long-context handling or format preservation from SFT data issues.
3. **Arena comments noting "machine-translated" Slovene:** Points to over-reliance on synthetic SFT data and lack of native phrasing.
4. **Degradation on few-shot benchmarks (GSM8K, TruthfulQA):** Suggests long-context training didn’t fully prepare the model for in-context learning with longer demonstrations.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Ablation - Parallel Alignment:** Train a baseline without Stage 1 CPT. Expected: Lower scores on knowledge and chat benchmarks, especially on tasks requiring cross-lingual transfer.
2. **Ablation - Related Languages:** Exclude Croatian/Bosnian/Serbian from base CPT mix. Expected: Performance drop on benchmarks involving code-switching or shared cultural concepts, and possibly on similar-language tasks.
3. **OCR Pipeline Validation:** Manually inspect 100 random OCR outputs from each source (National Library, theses, journals) for each tool (Marker, Llama-4, Nanonets). Measure character error rate (CER) against a clean ground truth subset. Expected: Llama-4 best for academic layout, Nanonets for messy scans, Marker fastest but error-prone.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Explicitly Stated by the Authors (Future Work / Drawbacks Section 7)
1. How can we reduce reliance on and improve the quality of machine-translated data?
2. What is the optimal data composition and curriculum for multilingual continual pretraining (CPT) for a target language?
3. How can we best incorporate reasoning and long-context capabilities into models for less-resourced languages?
4. How can we create high-quality, culturally-native supervised fine-tuning (SFT) datasets for less-resourced languages without prohibitive human cost?
5. How should we effectively evaluate language models for less-resourced languages?

### Inferred from Methodological Limitations & Unstated Assumptions
6. To what extent is the inclusion of closely-related languages (Croatian, Bosnian, Serbian) beneficial vs. potentially harmful noise for Slovene specialization?
7. What are the scaling laws and diminishing returns for continual pretraining on a low-resource language?
8. Can the proposed multi-stage CPT (parallel alignment -> base -> long) be simplified or replaced?
9. How robust is the OCR and document processing pipeline to the diverse, often low-quality, document types common in low-resource language archives?
10. Does the synthetic translation of data introduce systematic biases or "translationese" that affect the model’s discourse style and reasoning?

## Limitations
- Higher truncation errors than the base model suggest incomplete long-context mastery despite Stage 3 CPT.
- Arena feedback indicates a "machine-translated" Slovene style from SFT data, lacking natural fluency.
- Heavy reliance on OCR and machine translation for data scale introduces quality risks (artifacts, translationese) that propagate through all training stages.

## Confidence
- **High:** Parallel alignment mechanism (directly implemented and evidenced in pipeline and results).
- **Medium:** Gains from related-language transfer (compositional evidence from data mix, but not explicitly ablated).
- **Low:** Claims of robust long-context handling and natural Slovene generation (self-identified weaknesses in truncation errors and arena comments).

## Next Checks
