# Adapting Critic Match Loss Landscape Visualization to Off-policy Reinforcement Learning
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14589
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14589
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** Adapting critic match loss landscape visualization to off-policy RL (SAC) using fixed replay batches and precomputed targets, revealing smooth basins for convergent cases and directional collapse or fragmentation for divergent ones, with ADHDP showing sharper, more anisotropic landscapes; applied to spacecraft attitude control and cart-pole.

## Executive Summary
This paper adapts the critic match loss landscape visualization method from online to off-policy reinforcement learning, specifically for the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm. By using a fixed replay batch and precomputing critic targets, the authors create a static surrogate loss surface that decouples critic parameter perturbations from moving targets and data distributions. Applied to spacecraft attitude control and cart-pole, the method shows that convergent SAC yields smooth loss basins with moderate anisotropy, while divergent cases exhibit either strong directional collapse (high PCA variance, no basin) or fragmented optimization trajectories. Comparisons with ADHDP reveal sharper, more anisotropic landscapes without basins. The approach provides geometric diagnostics for critic optimization dynamics in replay-based RL.

## Method Summary
The adaptation involves constructing a frozen-target critic match loss landscape. During SAC training, critic weights are recorded periodically. At a chosen snapshot (e.g., final policy), a fixed mini-batch is sampled from the replay buffer. Bellman targets are precomputed using the snapshot's actor and target critics, then held constant. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to all recorded weight vectors to obtain two projection directions. A grid is defined around the final weights in this PCA plane; for each grid point, weights are perturbed along the principal directions and the loss is evaluated on the fixed batch with frozen targets. The resulting 3D loss surface is plotted with the projected weight trajectory overlaid. Quantitative metrics—sharpness (max loss within a radius), basin area (area where normalized loss ≤ threshold), and local anisotropy (log condition number of Hessian)—are computed to characterize the landscape geometry. This surrogate isolates the critic's local sensitivity while accounting for the off-policy data flow.

## Key Results
- **Convergent SAC (spacecraft attitude):** Smooth loss basin with area 1252.26 and anisotropy logκ=3.72; continuous weight trajectory converging to basin.
- **Divergent SAC cases:** Two patterns—directional collapse (96.5% variance on PC1, elongated ridge, no basin, monotonic weight norm growth) or fragmented trajectory clusters (smooth basin but discontinuous parameter jumps).
- **ADHDP comparison:** Sharper, more anisotropic landscapes without a well-defined basin; negative normalized loss indicates final parameters not at a local minimum in the subspace.
- **Cart-pole validation:** Consistent smooth basins for convergent SAC, confirming method generalizability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Fixed Replay Batch for Stationary Surrogate**
- Claim: Adapting to off-policy RL requires a locally stationary loss evaluation.
- Mechanism: A fixed mini-batch from the replay buffer is sampled once and held constant during landscape construction. This decouples loss evaluation from the non-stationary data distribution encountered during actual SAC updates, creating a consistent reference.
- Core assumption: The fixed batch represents the policy's state distribution sufficiently for geometric interpretation.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "using a fixed replay batch..."; [section 2.3A] "Using a fixed batch of replay data instead of continuously collected online samples."
- Break condition: Unrepresentative batch mischaracterizes the critic's true optimization geometry.

**Mechanism 2: Precomputed Targets Isolate Critic Geometry**
- Claim: Freezing Bellman targets focuses analysis on the primary critic's local loss.
- Mechanism: For each perturbed critic, the TD target y_t is computed once using the final policy's actor and target critics, then kept constant. This removes coupling from target dynamics during evaluation.
- Core assumption: Critic parameter sensitivity under a fixed policy snapshot is diagnostic of optimization dynamics.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "precomputed critic targets from the selected policy."; [section 2.3B] "SAC Bellman targets are precomputed and fixed... freezing the actor and temperature parameters."
- Break condition: Poor or unstable final policy makes frozen-target landscape uninformative about earlier training phases.

**Mechanism 3: Quantitative Metrics Correlate with Optimization Patterns**
- Claim: Landscape metrics (sharpness, basin area, anisotropy) differentiate convergent vs divergent critic updates.
- Mechanism: Sharpness measures local sensitivity; basin area indicates robustness; anisotropy (Hessian condition number) reveals directional imbalance. Combined with PCA-projected trajectories, they distinguish smooth convergence (smooth basin, balanced anisotropy) from instability (directional collapse or fragmentation).
- Core assumption: Frozen-target surrogate geometry meaningfully reflects critic optimization despite not being the exact training objective.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "analyzed both qualitatively and quantitatively using sharpness, basin area, and local anisotropy metrics."; [section 4] Metric patterns align with control outcomes (e.g., convergent SAC: Aρ=1252.26, logκ=3.72; divergent with collapse: 96.5% PC1 variance, no basin).
- Break condition: Smooth basin does not guarantee closed-loop success (one divergent SAC case), so metrics require trajectory continuity and control performance for full diagnosis.

## Foundational Learning
- **Critic match loss (Bellman residual under fixed targets)**  
  Why needed: Measures critic approximation error but must be decoupled from moving targets to form a static landscape for visualization.  
  Quick check: How does precomputing targets differ from computing TD error online during SAC updates?

- **Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for parameter projection**  
  Why needed: Reduces high-dimensional critic weights to a 2D plane for visualization, capturing major directions of weight evolution.  
  Quick check: Why not randomly sample directions and instead use PCA on recorded weights?

- **Off-policy vs. online RL data flow**  
  Why needed: The adaptation hinges on replay buffers and batch updates versus stepwise online interaction; misunderstanding leads to incorrect landscape construction.  
  Quick check: In SAC, what provides the data for critic updates, and how often are weights updated relative to environment steps?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Replay buffer -> Fixed probe set; Actor network & target critics -> Precomputed targets; PCA module (on recorded weights) -> Projection directions (δ, η); Loss evaluator (uses fixed batch, fixed targets, δ, η) -> Loss surface and metrics.

**Critical path:**
1. Train SAC to convergence/divergence, recording critic weights at intervals (e.g., every 5000 steps).
2. At chosen snapshot (e.g., final policy), sample a fixed mini-batch from replay buffer.
3. Compute critic targets y_t for batch using final actor and target critics; freeze them.
4. Perform PCA on all recorded critic weight vectors to obtain top two principal components δ and η.
5. Define a grid (α, β) around final weights in PCA plane.
6. For each grid point, reconstruct weights w' = w_final + αδ + βη, evaluate fixed-target loss on batch.
7. Plot 3D loss surface and overlay projected weight trajectory.
8. Compute sharpness, basin area, and log κ from normalized loss around final point.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Fixed batch vs. multiple batches: Single batch is feasible but may not represent full state distribution; multiple batches would average landscapes but increase cost.
- Global PCA vs. local PCA: Global PCA uses all recorded weights for consistent trajectory projection but may distort early snapshots; local PCA per snapshot shows contemporaneous geometry but breaks trajectory continuity.
- Visualizing primary critic only: Twin critics are symmetric; focusing on one simplifies but ignores potential disagreement magnitude.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Type 1 (Directional collapse):** Extreme PCA dominance (e.g., >95% PC1 variance), elongated ridge landscape, monotonic weight norm growth; no basin.
- **Type 2 (Fragmented trajectory):** Smooth basin present but projected optimization path consists of separated clusters with gaps; discontinuous parameter relocations.
- **ADHDP pattern:** Sharp, irregular contours, narrow/no basin, high anisotropy; negative normalized loss indicates final parameters not at a local minimum.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replicate convergent SAC on spacecraft attitude: Verify smooth basin (large Aρ, moderate logκ) and continuous converging trajectory.
2. Run divergent SAC with high learning rate/small buffer: Expect either PC1 dominance (Type 1) or trajectory fragmentation (Type 2); confirm via PCA variance and cluster analysis.
3. Compare with ADHDP on same task: Expect sharper, more anisotropic landscape without clear basin; note negative normalized loss.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can the frozen-target critic match loss landscape be extended to account for dynamic TD target updates and replay distribution shifts, providing a more faithful representation of the actual critic optimization process?  
*Basis:* Section 4.5 states the frozen-target landscape "does not coincide exactly with the instantaneous critic loss" and is a static surrogate.  
*Why unresolved:* The static nature may miss instability from evolving targets and data distributions, limiting diagnostic power.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* A dynamic variant that recomputes targets along the path or analytical corrections for target drift, with validation showing stronger correlation between landscape features and training curves across algorithms.

**Open Question 2:** For complex algorithms like SAC, what additional training components (actor, entropy, replay dynamics) must be incorporated into the critic match loss framework to better predict overall control convergence?  
*Basis:* The paper observes divergent SAC with smooth basin geometry, indicating critic stability does not imply control success.  
*Why unresolved:* Current visualization isolates the critic, ignoring interactions with other modules that can cause divergence.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Combining critic landscape metrics with actor loss, entropy, or replay statistics; demonstrating improved convergence prediction in multiple SAC runs with varied hyperparameters.

**Open Question 3:** Are the proposed quantitative metrics (sharpness, basin area, anisotropy) universally reliable indicators of training stability, or do they require task-specific calibration?  
*Basis:* Section 2.4 defines these metrics; results show basin area exists in some divergent cases, suggesting ambiguity.  
*Why unresolved:* Metrics tested on only two tasks without statistical rigor; thresholds for convergence are unknown.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Large-scale experiments on standard benchmarks (MuJoCo, Atari) with multiple algorithms, establishing statistically significant differences between convergent and divergent runs and early-warning thresholds.

**Open Question 4:** How does the choice of projection (PCA vs. alternatives) impact the interpretability of the loss landscape and insights about optimization trajectories?  
*Basis:* Section 2.3 uses global PCA on all recorded weights; Section 4.5 notes global PCA may misalign with local neighborhoods.  
*Why unresolved:* PCA is global linear; locally important directions might be missed, affecting temporal evolution interpretation.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Comparing PCA with other projections (random, local PCA, manifold learning) on same weight histories, assessing consistency of geometric patterns and correlation with training outcomes.

## Limitations
- Incomplete hyperparameter specification for convergent SAC (network architecture, exact SB3 defaults) hinders exact replication.
- Ambiguity in fixed replay batch sampling (source, size, seed) risks non-representative landscapes.
- Heuristic PCA grid range (±1.5 std dev) lacks validation; metrics may be sensitive to range choice.
- Frozen-target surrogate may not reflect early-training dynamics; temporal snapshots needed.
- Smooth basin does not guarantee closed-loop stability (one divergent SAC case), limiting diagnostic certainty.

## Confidence
- **Adaptation method (fixed batch + precomputed targets):** Medium. Mechanism coherent with internal evidence, but no external validation; break condition highlights batch representativeness dependency.
- **Diagnostic metrics correlation with convergence:** Medium. Experimental contrasts support claim, but imperfect prediction (smooth basin yet failure) and limited task variety reduce confidence.
- **Cross-task generalization:** Low. Only two tasks evaluated; statistical generalizability weak.
