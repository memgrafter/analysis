# Temporal Fact Conflicts in LLMs: Reproducibility Insights from Unifying DYNAMICQA and MULAN

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.15892
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15892
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** Contradictory findings on LLM temporal fact updateability from DYNAMICQA and MULAN are resolved as dataset-dependent; MULAN’s claim (temporal facts easier to update) holds across evaluation frameworks, while DYNAMICQA’s opposite conclusion does not generalize. Extending to Gemma models reveals non-monotonic size effects, contradicting both studies’ size-agnostic assumptions.

## Executive Summary
This study reconciles conflicting conclusions about how well large language models (LLMs) update temporal facts when presented with conflicting external context. By cross-reproducing the DYNAMICQA and MULAN benchmarks on each other’s datasets and standardizing evaluation, the authors find that the disagreement stems primarily from dataset construction and the operational definition of “temporal” facts. MULAN’s finding—that temporal facts are more updateable than static ones—holds robustly across different evaluation protocols. In contrast, DYNAMICQA’s opposite conclusion fails to generalize, largely due to its reliance on Wikipedia edit counts, which captures many non-temporal facts. Furthermore, scaling experiments with Gemma models (1B, 4B, 12B) reveal a non-monotonic relationship between model size and update success, challenging the size-agnostic claims of both original studies. The work underscores that conclusions about temporal knowledge editing are highly contingent on dataset design, choice of metric, and model scale.

## Method Summary
The core method involves reproducing original evaluations for both benchmarks, then cross-applying each benchmark’s evaluation protocol to the other’s dataset after format conversion. This requires converting MULAN’s fact-completion triples into QA pairs and DYNAMICQA’s QA pairs into sentence completions, while preserving template variability. MULAN’s programmatic context statements are replaced with LLM-generated natural passages (using Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct and NLI filtering) to better match DYNAMICQA’s context style. Key metrics are swapped: DYNAMICQA’s Coherent Persuasion Score (distributional shift measure) is applied to MULAN data, and MULAN’s Update Success Rate (exact-match binary) is applied to DYNAMICQA data. The study also extends evaluation to the Gemma family (1B, 4B, 12B) by adapting prompt templates and decoding settings. A critical implementation fix addresses a known bug in the original DYNAMICQA code where without-context accuracy was incorrectly computed.

## Key Results
- **Dataset dependence dominates:** MULAN’s conclusion that temporal (mutable) facts are easier to update holds when evaluated with DYNAMICQA’s metrics, whereas DYNAMICQA’s contradictory finding does not generalize to MULAN’s dataset.
- **Non-monotonic scaling:** Across Gemma models, Update Success Rate for mutable facts peaks at 4B parameters and drops at 1B and 12B, contradicting any simple size–updateability relationship.
- **Metric-driven divergence:** Coherent Persuasion Score (higher for mutable facts in MULAN) and Update Success Rate (showing inconsistent scaling) measure distinct aspects of conflict resolution, leading to potentially divergent interpretations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dataset Operationalization of “Temporal”
- **Claim:** The contradictory findings arise because DYNAMICQA and MULAN define and select “temporal” facts differently, creating non-overlapping subsets with inherently different mutability properties.
- **Mechanism:** DYNAMICQA infers temporality from Wikipedia edit counts, which includes many facts with minimal edits (e.g., count=2) that may reflect corrections rather than genuine temporal change. MULAN uses Wikidata relations explicitly admitting multiple time-bound values, yielding a cleaner set of mutable facts. Thus, the “temporal” class in each bench­mark is not measuring the same underlying property.
- **Core assumption:** The set of facts labeled temporal in DYNAMICQA and MULAN are not equivalent in terms of genuine time-sensitivity.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [section 3.1] DYNAMICQA’s temporal class includes many unchanged facts; 1,369 of 2,495 temporal facts have an edit count of just 2.
  - [section 5.2] Authors note that edit count “may not consistently signal genuine temporal change.”
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers do not directly address this dataset construction issue.

### Mechanism 2: Evaluation Metric Semantics
- **Claim:** The choice between Coherent Persuasion (distributional shift) and Update Success Rate (binary exact match) fundamentally shapes conclusions because they capture different aspects of model behavior under conflict.
- **Mechanism:** Coherent Persuasion is sensitive to subtle probability shifts across multiple generations; Update Success Rate depends solely on the greedy output. A fact can show high distributional shift yet still yield the original answer greedily (failing USR), or vice versa. The metrics are not perfectly correlated and operationalize “update” differently.
- **Core assumption:** There is no single ground truth for successful update; the metric itself defines what counts as a successful resolution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] Explicit contrast: DYNAMICQA “quantifies the extent to which context shifts the model’s output distribution” vs. MULAN “measures the proportion of cases in which context changes a memorised answer.”
  - [section 3.1] Definitions show different operationalizations (Equation 1 vs. exact match).
  - [corpus] Other works propose alternative metrics but do not compare these two directly.

### Mechanism 3: Non-Monotonic Model Size Effects
- **Claim:** The relationship between model size and temporal fact updateability is not monotonic and interacts with dataset characteristics, invalidating size-agnostic claims from both original studies.
- **Mechanism:** In Gemma models, mutable fact update success (per MULAN’s Update Success Rate) is highest at 4B parameters and lower at 1B and 12B. This suggests that parametric knowledge strength (generally increasing with size) and receptiveness to in-context editing are mediated by dataset-specific factors like fact phrasing and relation types.
- **Core assumption:** Model scale influences updateability, but the effect is non-linear and dataset-dependent.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.2] Table 9: Gemma 1B (0.694), 4B (0.474), 12B (0.437) for mutable facts—non-monotonic.
  - [section 5.3] Authors note the pattern is “observed only in Gemma 3 4B.”
  - [corpus] No direct scaling study for temporal facts in neighboring work.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Temporal Fact Operationalization**
  - **Why needed:** The entire conflict hinges on two different definitions of what makes a fact “temporal.” An engineer must recognize that DYNAMICQA’s edit-count heuristic and MULAN’s Wikidata relation-based selection are not interchangeable and will yield different fact sets.
  - **Quick check:** Give an example of a fact that is temporal in MULAN but static in DYNAMICQA, and explain why based on their selection criteria.

- **Concept: Evaluation Metric Semantics**
  - **Why needed:** Choosing between Coherent Persuasion (probabilistic distribution shift) and Update Success Rate (binary exact match) is a theoretical stance on what “update” means. Misalignment causes apples-to-oranges comparisons.
  - **Quick check:** If a model’s top-5 generations include the conflicting context but the greedy output remains the original fact, which metric counts this as an update?

- **Concept: In-Context Editing vs. Parametric Memory**
  - **Why needed:** Observed “updateability” results from the tension between the model’s frozen parametric knowledge and the dynamic prompt context. Understanding this interplay is key to interpreting any in-context editing experiment.
  - **Quick check:** Why might a model with higher confidence (first-token probability) in its original answer be *less* likely to exhibit a high Coherent Persuasion score when conflicting context is added?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Dataset loaders (DYNAMICQA JSON/QA, MULAN triples) -> Format converters (QA↔sentence completion, alias expansion) -> Context generator (LLM for natural passages, programmatic for MULAN-style) -> Model inference backend (Hugging Face `transformers` with Gemma/Llama/Mistral support) -> Metric calculators (Coherent Persuasion: NLI clustering + KL-div; Update Success Rate: exact match; others: F1, confidence, Semantic Entropy)

- **Critical path:** 1. Reproduce original results on native datasets (with bug fix). 2. Convert dataset formats and generate contexts. 3. Run cross-benchmark evaluations (swap metrics). 4. Extend to new model sizes (Gemma). 5. Analyze interactions between dataset, metric, and scale.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Metric computation:* Coherent Persuasion requires multi-sample generation + NLI clustering (slow), while Update Success Rate uses single greedy decode (fast).
  - *Context fidelity:* LLM-generated natural contexts are more realistic but introduce variability; programmatic contexts are controlled but artificial.
  - *Dataset size:* MULAN’s 49k samples necessitate subsampling for expensive metrics, risking statistical power loss.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Inconsistent conclusions when swapping evaluation frameworks (e.g., DYNAMICQA’s method on MULAN data yields mixed rather than clean results).
  - High variance in Coherent Persuasion scores across random seeds for generation/clustering.
  - Template conversion errors (e.g., mishandling MULAN aliases) causing artificial performance drops.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Metric Validation:** On original DYNAMICQA with Llama-2-7B-Chat, verify that the corrected without-context accuracy (comparing to original answers, not substitutes) matches the paper’s reported static fact accuracy.
  2. **Cross-Protocol Swap:** Apply MULAN’s Update Success Rate to DYNAMICQA data converted to fact-completion format. Hypothesis: Should observe lower update success for temporal facts (per Table 7). If not, investigate conversion quality.
  3. **Scale Sensitivity Check:** Run MULAN’s Update Success on Gemma 1B/4B/12B. Expect non-monotonic pattern (4B peak) as in Table 9. If absent, check subsampling seed or context generation method.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** Does external context reliably resolve temporal knowledge conflicts in LLMs?  
  **Basis:** The conclusion states this remains open.  
  **Why unresolved:** Conflicting findings between benchmarks with strong dataset dependence; conclusions vary across metrics and model sizes.  
  **Resolving evidence:** A standardized benchmark with controlled variables (context style, temporality definition, metric) tested across diverse models.

- **Question 2:** Why does temporal fact update success exhibit a non-monotonic relationship with model size within the Gemma family?  
  **Basis:** RQ2 results show Gemma 4B peaks in mutable update success, contrary to size-agnostic claims.  
  **Why unresolved:** Both original studies assumed size invariance; no theoretical explanation for the intermediate optimum.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Systematic scaling across more model sizes/architectures, with probing of internal representations to understand memory-update trade-offs.

- **Question 3:** Which specific dataset construction choices (temporality labeling, template design, context generation) most strongly influence conclusions about temporal fact updateability?  
  **Basis:** RQ3 reveals dataset dependence but does not isolate drivers; differences include edit-count vs. Wikidata temporality, single vs. multiple templates.  
  **Why unresolved:** Confounded differences; the study compares datasets holistically without ablating individual factors.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Controlled ablation studies varying one dataset dimension at a time (e.g., temporality definition, template variability, context authenticity).

- **Question 4:** Do LLM-generated natural contexts accurately mimic real-world Wikipedia passages for evaluating temporal knowledge updates?  
  **Basis:** Synthetic contexts replace MULAN’s programmatic ones for cross-benchmark comparison; quality assessed via NLI and 200 manual checks.  
  **Why unresolved:** Potential distribution shift from real Wikipedia; NLI may not capture all contextual nuances affecting updates.  
  **Resolving evidence:** Human evaluation of context realism and comparative experiments using real Wikipedia passages vs. synthetic on the same fact set.

## Limitations
- **Dataset operationalization opacity:** The exact filtering criteria for DYNAMICQA’s edit-count temporality are undisclosed, limiting verification that its temporal set contains many non-temporal facts.
- **Metric equivalence unmeasured:** While Coherent Persuasion and Update Success Rate are theoretically divergent, their empirical correlation on the same instances is not quantified.
- **Scaling generalization weak:** The non-monotonic Gemma pattern is restricted to one model family; without cross-architectural replication (e.g., Llama, Mistral), it may be dataset-specific.
- **Corpus evidence thin:** Neighboring papers focus on adjacent topics (e.g., knowledge propagation) and do not directly engage with the specific dataset/metric conflicts central to this work.

## Confidence
- **Dataset-Dependent Contradiction:** High – Direct evidence from dataset descriptions and cross-evaluation results strongly supports this as the primary driver.
- **Metric-Driven Divergence:** Medium – Theoretical mechanism is sound and definitions explicit, but lack of empirical agreement data tempers confidence.
- **Non-Monotonic Size Effects:** Low – Observation is robust within Gemma but lacks cross-architectural replication; could be confounded by training data or architecture differences.

Verify that corrected static fact accuracy matches the paper’s reported value before any cross-benchmark comparison.
