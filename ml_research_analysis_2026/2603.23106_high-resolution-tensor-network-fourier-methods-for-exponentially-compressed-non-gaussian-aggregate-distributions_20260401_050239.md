# High-Resolution Tensor-Network Fourier Methods for Exponentially Compressed Non-Gaussian Aggregate Distributions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.23106
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23106
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Exponentially compressed representation of weighted sum distributions via QTT-accelerated Fourier methods, enabling polylogarithmic scaling and high-resolution (N=2^30) computations for risk measures.

## Executive Summary
Evaluating probability distributions of weighted sums of independent random variables is fundamental in quantitative finance and reliability analysis but becomes computationally prohibitive for large numbers of components, especially with non-Gaussian marginals. This paper presents a novel hybrid method that leverages Fourier spectral techniques together with quantized tensor train (QTT) representations to overcome these limitations. By exploiting the low-rank structure inherent in the characteristic functions of such sums—stemming from spectral smoothness in continuous models or energy concentration in discrete models as the component count grows—the approach achieves exponential compression. For discrete Bernoulli sums, a sharp bond-dimension collapse is observed for D ≳ 300 components, yielding polylogarithmic time and memory costs. For continuous lognormal sums, the method attains high-frequency discretizations up to N = 2^30 on standard hardware, far exceeding the N = 2^24 limit of conventional dense FFT implementations. These advances make it feasible to compute tail risk measures such as Value at Risk (VaR) and Expected Shortfall (ES) for large, complex aggregates with high accuracy and efficiency.

## Method Summary
The core algorithm begins by forming the characteristic function of the weighted sum as a product of the individual component characteristic functions, which holds under independence. This product is then discretized on a uniformly spaced frequency grid. Instead of storing the full high-dimensional tensor of values, the data is encoded in a QTT format—a tensor network that decomposes the function into a chain of low-rank core tensors. For many common distributions, the discretized characteristic function exhibits either analyticity (discrete case) or spectral concentration (continuous case), leading to rapidly decaying singular values and thus low bond dimensions in the QTT representation. The compressed representation permits efficient computation of the inverse Fourier transform, yielding either the probability density function or cumulative distribution function directly in the QTT domain. Finally, tail probabilities (VaR, ES) are extracted from the CDF with minimal additional cost. The key innovation is the recognition and exploitation of the low-rank structure that arises naturally, avoiding the curse of dimensionality that plagues traditional FFT or Monte Carlo methods.

## Key Results
- For discrete weighted Bernoulli sums, a sharp bond-dimension collapse occurs at D ≥ 300 components, enabling polylogarithmic time/memory scaling.
- For continuous lognormal sums, the method achieves high-resolution discretizations of N = 2^30 frequency modes on standard hardware, far beyond the N = 2^24 limit of dense implementations.
- The approach facilitates efficient and accurate computation of Value at Risk (VaR) and Expected Shortfall (ES) for large aggregate distributions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The exponential compression stems from the low-rank nature of the discretized characteristic function. For discrete models with finite support, the characteristic function is analytic and periodic, so its Fourier series coefficients decay exponentially. When the function is reshaped into a high-dimensional tensor over a multidimensional grid (via a nested ordering), its matricizations exhibit rapidly decaying singular values, yielding low bond dimensions in the QTT decomposition. For continuous models (e.g., lognormal), while global analyticity is absent, the product of many smoothed characteristic functions concentrates energy in a low-dimensional subspace as the number of components grows; this mirrors the central limit effect but at the level of the spectral representation. The QTT format captures this low-rank structure efficiently, reducing storage from O(N) to O(d log N) where d is the bond dimension, which often remains bounded or grows very slowly. Consequently, operations like inverse FFT or point evaluation become tractable even for very large N, breaking the usual polynomial scaling barrier.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component Map: Input (weights & component distributions) -> Characteristic Function Builder -> QTT Compressor -> Inverse Fourier Transformer -> Output (density, CDF, VaR, ES)

Critical Path: The performance bottleneck lies in the QTT compression and inversion steps. First, the product of characteristic functions must be computed while maintaining low bond dimensions—this often requires careful ordering and adaptive tolerance. Second, the inverse Fourier transform (recover PDF/CDF) must be performed within the QTT representation, which involves operations like element-wise multiplication in the Fourier domain and may require additional compression. Third, extracting tail probabilities from the CDF involves point evaluations that should also be done in compressed form to avoid decompression. Optimizing these three phases determines overall efficiency.

Design Tradeoffs:
- **Compression tolerance**: Smaller singular value truncation thresholds increase accuracy but also increase bond dimensions and computational cost.
- **Grid resolution**: Finer grids reduce aliasing and improve accuracy, particularly for sharp features, but increase the size of the initial tensor and potentially the QTT ranks if the function is not smooth enough.
- **Tree structure**: QTT can use different tree shapes (binary, higher-arity). Binary trees are standard but deeper trees may improve compression for some functions at the cost of more cores.
- **Analytic vs. non-analytic CFs**: For non-analytic CFs (e.g., lognormal), the low-rank property may be weaker; one might need to use a different basis or apply smoothing.

Failure Signatures:
- **Rank explosion**: Bond dimensions grow linearly with component count D or grid size N, indicating the characteristic function does not have the expected low-rank structure (e.g., due to non-smoothness or insufficient resolution).
- **Aliasing artifacts**: Oscillations in the recovered density or inaccurate tail probabilities suggest the frequency grid is too coarse.
- **Memory overflow**: The QTT representation exceeds available memory because bond dimensions are too large, often caused by overly loose compression or poor initial ordering.
- **Slow scaling**: Empirical runtime scales polynomially rather than polylogarithmically as D or N increases, pointing to inefficiencies in the implementation (e.g., not using QTT operations throughout).

First Experiments:
1. **Bond-dimension collapse replication**: For Bernoulli sums with uniform weights, vary D from 100 to 1000 and record the maximum bond dimension after QTT compression. Confirm a sharp drop around D ≈ 300 and subsequent polylogarithmic growth.
2. **Lognormal accuracy benchmark**: Construct a lognormal sum characteristic function with D=200 components; compare the QTT representation at N=2^30 against a dense FFT reference at N=2^24. Compute maximum pointwise error in the PDF and assess compression ratio.
3. **Risk measure validation**: For a large portfolio (D=500 lognormal components), compute VaR at 99% and ES using the QTT method. Compare against Monte Carlo estimates from 10^8 samples to evaluate both accuracy and runtime speedup.

## Limitations
- Exponential compression for Bernoulli sums assumes analytic characteristic functions; may not hold for non-analytic or dependent components.
- Lognormal characteristic function lacks analyticity in a strip; high-resolution claims and VaR/ES results lack error analysis, hardware details, and confidence intervals.
- QTT parameters (tolerance, tree depth) are not systematically studied; optimal tuning could alter scaling predictions and accuracy.

## Confidence
- Discrete Bernoulli exponential compression: High
- Continuous lognormal high-resolution: Medium
- Efficient VaR/ES: Medium
