# SynthAgent: A Multi-Agent LLM Framework for Realistic Patient Simulation -- A Case Study in Obesity with Mental Health Comorbidities
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.08254
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08254
- **Reference Count**: 38
- **Primary Result**: A multi-agent LLM framework generates clinically coherent and diverse synthetic patient profiles for obesity with mental health comorbidities; Claude 4.5 Sonnet achieved the highest cohort diversity (div=0.353), while GPT-5 achieved the highest mean quality (76.27±2.84).

## Executive Summary
SynthAgent addresses the challenge of fragmented, biased, and privacy-restricted real-world data for complex comorbid conditions by introducing a modular multi-agent system. It integrates heterogeneous data sources—including NHANES surveys, medical claims, and PubMed literature—through a pipeline of specialized LLM agents to produce synthetic patient profiles enriched with psychological traits that influence behavioral adherence. Evaluation of over 100 patients demonstrated that modern LLMs, particularly Claude 4.5 Sonnet and GPT-5, can generate outputs with high clinical fidelity and cohort diversity, enabling privacy-preserving research for complex patient populations.

## Method Summary
The framework employs a five-agent pipeline: a **Summarizer** (fixed to Gemini 2.0 Flash) constructs a demographic and clinical blueprint by matching epidemiological targets to one NHANES record and three similar claims trajectories. A **Generator** (variable LLM) creates an initial full patient profile from this blueprint. An **Augmenter** then enriches the profile by retrieving and synthesizing evidence from ten PubMed case reports per disease keyword. An **Evaluator** audits the profile for logical, temporal, and behavioral consistency, flagging issues, which a **Refiner** resolves to finalize the synthetic patient. The system is evaluated using an LLM-as-a-Judge (GPT-4o) scoring rubric across ten dimensions and a cohort diversity metric based on embedding distances.

## Key Results
- GPT-5 (mean quality 76.27±2.84) and Claude 4.5 Sonnet (76.17±3.90) significantly outperformed Gemini 2.5 Pro and DeepSeek-R1 in overall fidelity.
- Claude 4.5 Sonnet achieved the highest cohort diversity (div=0.353), suggesting greater variation in generated profiles.
- The multi-agent design successfully integrates disparate data sources to produce clinically plausible and varied patient simulations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The summarizer agent generates a realistic foundational blueprint by matching probabilistic demographic/comorbidity targets to real NHANES samples and claims trajectories.
- **Mechanism**: It samples a demographic profile and comorbidity from epidemiological distributions, selects the most similar NHANES record and top three matching claims trajectories, and summarizes these into a structured blueprint.
- **Core Assumption**: Nearest-neighbor matching to real patient records yields a clinically plausible starting point.
- **Evidence Anchors**: [abstract] integration of claims, surveys, and literature; [PAGE 3] blueprint construction from NHANES and claims.
- **Break Condition**: If input data lack appropriate matches for rare comorbidities, the blueprint may be unrealistic.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The augmenter agent improves medical authenticity by retrieving and integrating disease-specific evidence from PubMed case reports.
- **Mechanism**: It extracts disease keywords, queries PubMed for 10 relevant case reports (filtered by age/gender), synthesizes common patterns, and revises symptoms, psychological scales, and role-play sections.
- **Core Assumption**: Published case reports capture representative disease manifestations applicable to synthetic patients.
- **Evidence Anchors**: [abstract] patient-centered literature; [PAGE 4] augmentation with evidence-based details from case reports.
- **Break Condition**: Sparse or low-quality case reports may add noise or incorrect details.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The evaluator-refiner loop enforces internal coherence and plausibility across dimensions.
- **Mechanism**: The evaluator audits for logical, temporal, and behavioral consistency, rating issues by severity; the refiner makes precise adjustments to correct flagged problems.
- **Core Assumption**: LLM-based auditing can accurately identify errors that reduce realism.
- **Evidence Anchors**: [abstract] autonomous agent interactions; [PAGE 4] evaluator as automated clinical reviewer, refiner makes adjustments.
- **Break Condition**: If the evaluator overlooks subtle contradictions or the refiner over-corrects, coherence may degrade.

## Architecture Onboarding
Summarizer -> Generator -> Augmenter -> (Evaluator -> Refiner)*
*The Evaluator-Refiner loop may iterate.

- **Component map**: Summarizer Agent (blueprint from NHANES/claims) -> Generator Agent (initial profile) -> Augmenter Agent (literature enrichment) -> [Evaluator Agent (audit) <-> Refiner Agent (correction)] -> Final profile. Data layer: NHANES, claims, epidemiological distributions, PubMed. LLM core: interchangeable engine (GPT-5, Claude, etc.).
- **Critical path**: 1. Summarizer produces blueprint. 2. Generator creates base profile. 3. Augmenter enriches with literature. 4. Evaluator reviews and flags issues. 5. Refiner finalizes profile.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Modular agent design improves specialization but adds orchestration complexity and latency. Using LLMs as reasoning engines offers flexibility but introduces variability and cost; model choice trades off structural coherence (GPT-5) vs. psychological diversity (Claude). Reliance on empirical data ensures realism but may propagate biases and limit rare phenotypes.
- **Failure signatures**: Low overall quality score (<70) from evaluator; low cohort diversity (div <0.275); specific dimension failures (e.g., poor timeline coherence, mismatched habits/labs); temporal errors (treatments before diagnoses).
- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Engine comparison: Run 30 patients per LLM engine (same blueprint set) and compare mean quality and diversity; verify Claude’s diversity advantage and GPT-5’s timeline strength. 2. Data source ablation: Remove NHANES matching or PubMed enrichment and measure impact on quality scores and clinical plausibility. 3. Rare phenotype stress test: Simulate patients with uncommon comorbidity triads and evaluate coherence.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: What specific techniques can mitigate biases inherent in SynthAgent's heterogeneous input data to ensure equitable representation of rare psychiatric phenotypes? *Basis*: framework’s performance depends on quality/representativeness of input data like claims/surveys, which may introduce biases. *Why unresolved*: No mechanisms to detect/correct source-specific biases. *What evidence*: Quantifiable reduction in disparity metrics within synthetic cohorts after bias-adjustment; clinician audits confirming improved plausibility.
- **Open Question 2**: Can SynthAgent's inter-agent reasoning evolve beyond rule-based conflict resolution to model emergent, dynamic psychosocial stressors that realistically alter treatment adherence trajectories? *Basis*: inter-agent reasoning remains partially rule-based, limiting emergent complexity. *Why unresolved*: Rule-based resolutions constrain adaptive interactions. *What evidence*: Longitudinal simulations with RL-based conflict negotiation showing variable adherence; expert evaluation confirming greater authenticity.
- **Open Question 3**: How can SynthAgent move beyond proxy personality variables (e.g., HEXACO) to integrate sociocultural context and improve psychological realism? *Basis*: psychological modeling relies on proxy variables, which cannot capture real-world variability or sociocultural nuance. *Why unresolved*: Proxy traits oversimplify cultural/socioeconomic factors. *What evidence*: Cross-validation against region-specific behavioral surveillance data; mixed-methods studies identifying culturally specific patterns.
- **Open Question 4**: What clinician-in-the-loop validation protocols are most effective for ensuring alignment with real-world clinical reasoning? *Basis*: future work should integrate clinician-in-the-loop assessment. *Why unresolved*: Current LLM-as-a-Judge lacks human clinical expertise for nuanced assessment. *What evidence*: Multi-center clinician review studies measuring concordance in management decisions; adjustments based on clinician feedback loops.

## Limitations
- **Reproducibility Blockers**: Exact agent prompts, NHANES/claims matching algorithms, PubMed query logic, and model parameters are undisclosed.
- **Data & Generalizability**: Reliance on US-specific, obesity-focused data may limit applicability to other conditions/regions; synthetic personas inherit source biases.
- **Evaluation Subjectivity**: LLM-as-a-Judge scoring lacks human clinical validation; diversity embedding metric may not capture clinically meaningful variation.

## Confidence
- **High**: Multi-agent pipeline structure, data sources used, reported evaluation metrics (quality, diversity).
- **Medium**: Performance superiority of GPT-5/Claude is supported but hinges on unreported implementation details.
- **Low**: Claims about clinical realism and adherence influence are indirectly inferred; no prospective validation with real patients/clinicians.
