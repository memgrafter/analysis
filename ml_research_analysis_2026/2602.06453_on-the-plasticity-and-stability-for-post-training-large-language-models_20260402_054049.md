# On the Plasticity and Stability for Post-Training Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06453
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06453
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** PCR improves reasoning accuracy by an average of +3.5% pass@1 over GRPO on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B and up to +4.7% on AIME 2025, while training more smoothly and improving the stability-accuracy Pareto frontier.

## Executive Summary
Post-training large language models with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) often suffers from instability due to conflicting gradients between plasticity (reasoning improvement) and stability (knowledge retention). This paper identifies that this conflict stems from noisy gradient estimates and geometric opposition. They propose Probabilistic Conflict Resolution (PCR), which models each gradient as a Gaussian distribution capturing estimation uncertainty, then applies a Bayesian arbitration to perform a soft projection that dynamically scales the conflicting component. PCR is applied only to MLP layers for efficiency. Evaluations on multiple base models and reasoning benchmarks show consistent state-of-the-art improvements, with smoother training and better trade-offs between reasoning accuracy and linguistic stability.

## Method Summary
The core of GRPO involves two gradient components: a plasticity gradient derived from task-specific rewards and a stability gradient from a KL-divergence penalty that prevents deviation from a reference model. These gradients often conflict, leading to unstable training. PCR addresses this by treating each gradient as a multivariate Gaussian with mean equal to the observed gradient and covariance capturing noise (estimated via intra-group variance). The plasticity gradient is decomposed into components parallel and orthogonal to the stability direction. Only the parallel component is arbitrated: using Bayesian inference with Gaussian priors, PCR computes a retention coefficient based on the precisions (inverse variances) of the two gradients. The final update for MLP layers replaces the raw plasticity gradient with a soft-projected version that reduces the conflicting part proportionally to its uncertainty. Attention and LayerNorm layers are updated via standard GRPO. This hybrid approach yields a more stable optimization and better performance on reasoning tasks like AIME and MATH500, while maintaining linguistic quality as measured by WikiText-2 perplexity.

## Key Results
- PCR achieves an average gain of +3.5% pass@1 over GRPO on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B and up to +4.7% on AIME 2025.
- Training trajectories are significantly smoother, with reduced oscillations in gradient norms.
- PCR improves the Pareto frontier between reasoning accuracy (higher Pass@1) and linguistic stability (lower WikiText-2 perplexity) compared to prior methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
#### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** PCR replaces deterministic gradient vectors with probabilistic Gaussian distributions to capture estimation uncertainty.
- **Mechanism:** Each gradient (plasticity g_pla, stability g_sta) is modeled as a multivariate Gaussian N(μ, Σ), where the mean is the observed gradient and the variance quantifies sampling noise. This enables uncertainty-aware decisions.
- **Core assumption:** The Monte‑Carlo gradient estimates from group sampling follow a Gaussian distribution (by CLT) and errors are conditionally independent.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 4.1 describe probabilistic modeling and CLT justification.
- **Break condition:** Violation of independence or heavy-tailed noise could destabilize the Bayesian update.

#### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The conflict is resolved by geometric decomposition of the plasticity gradient into orthogonal and parallel components relative to the stability direction.
- **Mechanism:** Write μ_pla = μ_⊥ + μ_∥, where μ_∥ is the projection of μ_pla onto μ_sta. Only μ_∥ is subject to arbitration; μ_⊥ is preserved entirely.
- **Core assumption:** The stability direction anchors the update to preserve pretrained knowledge.
- **Evidence:** Abstract mentions geometric conflict; Section 4.2 defines decomposition.
- **Break condition:** If stability gradient is poorly estimated, its direction may be inaccurate, leading to erroneous split.

#### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Bayesian arbitration computes a soft projection coefficient α = λ_sta/(λ_pla+λ_sta) that scales the conflicting component, optimizing the bias-variance trade-off.
- **Mechanism:** Treat true update along conflict axis as a random variable with Gaussian prior (mean 0, variance σ²_sta). Observed magnitude from plasticity gradient is noisy likelihood (variance σ²_pla). Posterior mean yields retention coefficient k = λ_pla/(λ_pla+λ_sta); α = 1 – k.
- **Core assumption:** Precision (inverse variance) reflects confidence; linear estimator is MMSE-optimal.
- **Evidence:** Abstract mentions uncertainty-aware soft projection; Section 4.3 derives arbitration rule; Section 5 proves MMSE optimality.
- **Break condition:** Mismatched variances (due to poor estimation) cause α to be suboptimal, leading to over- or under-constraint.

## Foundational Learning
#### Concept: Gradient Conflict in Multi-Task Optimization
- **Why needed:** PCR’s core problem is destructive interference between plasticity and stability gradients; understanding directional conflict (e.g., cosine similarity) is essential.
- **Quick check:** Q: If two gradient vectors have negative cosine similarity, what does that imply about their combined update? A: They partially cancel each other, reducing effective update magnitude.

#### Concept: Bayesian Inference with Gaussian Priors and Likelihoods
- **Why needed:** PCR’s arbitration combines a Gaussian prior (stability) and likelihood (plasticity) to compute a soft projection coefficient. Familiarity with precision-weighted averaging is required.
- **Quick check:** Q: Given prior N(0, σ_prior²) and likelihood N(x_obs, σ_lik²), what is the posterior mean? A: (σ_prior⁻²·0 + σ_lik⁻²·x_obs) / (σ_prior⁻² + σ_lik⁻²).

#### Concept: GRPO Objective and KL Penalty
- **Why needed:** The stability gradient arises from the KL-divergence term that anchors the policy to the reference model. Understanding the effect of the penalty coefficient β is necessary.
- **Quick check:** Q: In GRPO, what does the KL penalty coefficient β control? A: The strength of regularization preventing the policy from deviating too far from the reference model.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Gradient estimator -> Variance estimator -> Geometric decomposer -> Bayesian arbiter -> Soft projector -> Hybrid selector
- **Critical path:**
  1. For each batch, compute g_pla (from surrogate gain) and g_sta (from KL divergence) per parameter tensor.
  2. Estimate per-tensor variance (e.g., from intra-group gradient samples) to obtain σ² (isotropic Σ).
  3. For MLP tensors only: decompose g_pla into orthogonal and parallel components relative to g_sta, compute α = λ_sta/(λ_pla+λ_sta), and apply soft projection: g_final = g_pla - α * μ_parallel.
  4. For non-MLP tensors (Attention, LayerNorm): compute g_final = g_pla + β * g_sta.
  5. Apply optimizer step (e.g., Adam).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Full-model PCR vs. hybrid MLP-only: Full PCR theoretically optimal but memory-heavy; hybrid aligns with MLPs as primary knowledge stores.
  - Isotropic vs. full covariance: Isotropic Σ reduces overhead but may miss directional noise patterns.
  - Soft vs. hard projection: Soft adapts to signal confidence; hard (α binary) is simpler but brittle under noise.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - α saturates at 1 for all MLP layers → overly rigid stability gradient or misestimated plasticity variance.
  - Large oscillations in training loss/gradient norm → variance estimator outdated or group size too small.
  - Degradation on reasoning benchmarks but stable WikiText-2 perplexity → α may be too high, suppressing plasticity.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Benchmark validation: Compare PCR against GRPO and SOTA baselines (GVPO, MRT, GCPO) on reasoning tasks (AIME, MATH500, HumanEval) to confirm gains.
  2. Ablation of hybrid strategy: Run PCR on all layers, only MLPs, and only attention to verify MLP-only matches all-layers at lower cost.
  3. Projection strength analysis: Track α distribution during training; expect continuous range in MLPs and correlation with gradient variance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Validity of Gaussian gradient model:** Are GRPO gradient estimates truly Gaussian, and how sensitive is PCR to non-Gaussian noise? The paper justifies via CLT but does not empirically verify.
2. **Conditional independence assumption:** PCR assumes errors in plasticity and stability gradients are independent. Correlated errors could invalidate the Bayesian update.
3. **MLP-only application:** Is restricting PCR to MLP layers optimal across all transformer architectures? The paper cites MLPs as knowledge stores but lacks ablation comparing to attention or all layers.
4. **Generalization beyond reasoning:** Does PCR transfer to other post-training scenarios (instruction following, dialogue, safety)? Evaluations are limited to math/code benchmarks.

## Limitations
- Missing algorithmic details: variance estimation method, exact group size, training duration, and unreported baseline hyperparameters hinder faithful reproduction.
- Gaussian noise assumption not empirically validated; heavy-tailed gradients could break PCR’s theoretical guarantees.
- Exclusive MLP focus lacks explicit ablation in the paper; the design choice may not generalize to all model families.
