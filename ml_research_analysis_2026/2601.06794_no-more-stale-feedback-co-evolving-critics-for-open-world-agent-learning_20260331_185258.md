# No More Stale Feedback: Co-Evolving Critics for Open-World Agent Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06794
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06794
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** ECHO improves average performance over GRPO by 7.28 points across four open-world benchmarks, achieving state-of-the-art results on WebShop, ALFWorld, SciWorld, and DeepSearch.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a fundamental limitation in critique-guided reinforcement learning for LLM agents: critic staleness. As the policy evolves, a fixed critic's feedback becomes misaligned with the policy's current failure patterns, reducing learning efficiency. The proposed method, ECHO, solves this by co-evolving the policy and critic in a synchronized dual-track training loop. It introduces a cascaded rollout mechanism that generates group-structured trajectories for robust relative advantage estimation and a saturation-aware gain shaping reward that emphasizes incremental improvements near performance ceilings. Evaluated on Qwen3-4B across four challenging open-world benchmarks, ECHO yields significant gains over standard GRPO and establishes new state-of-the-art results.

## Method Summary
ECHO synchronizes the updates of the policy (actor) and critic via dual-track Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on the same batch of on-policy trajectories. For each query, the policy first generates an initial rollout, which is scored by an external reward model. The critic then produces multiple independent diagnostic critiques conditioned on this initial trajectory and its score. Each critique augments the query to prompt the policy to generate a refined rollout. This cascaded process yields two groups: a critique group and a refinement group. Advantages for the policy are computed by normalizing refinement scores within the group. For the critic, a saturation-aware intrinsic gain—defined as the logarithmic ratio of failure probabilities—replaces linear reward differences to emphasize improvements on already high-performing trajectories. Both models are updated simultaneously using GRPO with KL penalties, ensuring the critic’s feedback remains calibrated to the policy’s evolving weaknesses.

## Key Results
- On WebShop, ECHO achieves 90.03 vs. GRPO’s 82.37.
- On ALFWorld, ECHO achieves 91.25 vs. 87.50.
- On SciWorld, ECHO achieves 82.88 vs. 79.14.
- On DeepSearch, ECHO achieves 47.25 vs. 33.25.
- Average improvement over GRPO: +7.28 points.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Co-evolving Critic-Policy Synchronization
- **Claim:** Synchronized dual-track updates prevent critic staleness by ensuring the critic adapts to the policy's evolving failure patterns.
- **Mechanism:** Both policy and critic are updated on the same batch of on-policy trajectories. The critic is rewarded based on the policy's improvement (gain from critiques), aligning its diagnostic focus with the current policy's weaknesses.
- **Core assumption:** Failure patterns drift over time in on-policy RL; a stationary critic becomes misaligned.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "By employing dual-track GRPO updates, ECHO ensures the critic’s feedback stays synchronized with the evolving policy." Section 3.3 notes "This synchronized optimization ensures the critic’s diagnostic focus is continuously calibrated to the policy’s evolving failure patterns." Corpus: Neighboring work typically freezes critics after offline training.
- **Break condition:** If the external reward model is noisy or biased, the gain signal becomes unreliable, leading both models to co-adapt to incorrect objectives.

### Mechanism 2: Cascaded Rollout with Group-Structured Advantages
- **Claim:** Cascaded multi-view diagnosis and conditional refinement produce diverse trajectory groups for robust relative advantage estimation, improving sample efficiency.
- **Mechanism:** For each query, the policy generates an initial rollout τ₀. The critic produces N independent critiques conditioned on τ₀ and its score. Each critique augments the query to generate a refined rollout τᵣ. This yields groups: critiques Gᶜ and refinements Gᴾ. Advantages are computed by group-relative normalization within each group, allowing the policy to identify the most effective refinements and the critic to identify the most impactful critiques.
- **Core assumption:** Multiple critiques for the same initial trajectory capture diverse diagnostic hypotheses; refinements conditioned on them span a spectrum of corrective actions, enabling meaningful relative comparisons.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "cascaded rollout mechanism where the critic generates multiple diagnoses... followed by policy refinement to enable group-structured advantage estimation." Section 3.1 details the production of baseline score, critique group, and refinement group as empirical signals.
- **Break condition:** If N is too small, group-relative estimation becomes high-variance; if critic lacks diversity (e.g., mode collapse), group advantages collapse to similar values.

### Mechanism 3: Saturation-Aware Gain Shaping
- **Claim:** Nonlinear gain function emphasizes incremental improvements near performance ceilings, encouraging the critic to target subtle, last-mile flaws that are otherwise undervalued by linear rewards.
- **Mechanism:** Replace linear reward Δs = sᵣ - sₒ with intrinsic gain g(sₒ,sᵣ) = ln((1-sₒ+η)/(1-sᵣ+η)). This function grows larger for the same absolute improvement when the initial score is higher, thus rewarding the critic more for improving near-saturation trajectories.
- **Core assumption:** As policy performance approaches the ceiling, marginal improvements require disproportionately more effort; linear scaling fails to incentivize such fine-grained critique.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "We address the challenge of learning plateaus via a saturation-aware gain shaping objective." Section 3.2 describes the choice as yielding "a principled shaping signal... it is saturation-aware."
- **Break condition:** If η is poorly tuned, gain can become numerically unstable near sᵣ≈1; if tasks have discrete/binary rewards, the continuous gain may not correlate with true progress.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Policy Model (Pθ) generates initial trajectories and performs critique-conditioned refinements -> Critic Model (Cψ) takes query, initial trajectory, and baseline score to produce multiple diagnostic critiques -> Reward Model (R) assigns scalar scores to trajectories -> Cascaded Rollout Manager coordinates Stage1 (multi-view diagnosis) and Stage2 (conditional refinement) -> Advantage Calculator computes group-relative advantages for policy (from sᵣ) and critic (from saturation-aware gain) -> Dual-Track GRPO Updater applies GRPO updates to policy and critic separately but on the same batch.

### Critical path
1. Sample query q from dataset.  
2. Policy generates initial rollout τ₀ ∼ Pθ(·|q).  
3. Reward model scores τ₀ → sₒ.  
4. Critic generates N critiques {cₒ} ∼ Cψ(·|q, τₒ, sₒ).  
5. For each critique, form augmented query (q, cₒ) and have policy generate refined rollout τᵣ.  
6. Reward model scores each τᵣ → sᵣ.  
7. Compute saturation-aware critic rewards rᶜ = ln((1-sₒ+η)/(1-sᵣ+η)).  
8. Compute group-relative advantages Aᴾ from {sᵣ} and Aᶜ from {rᶜ}.  
9. Perform GRPO update on policy using {τᵣ} and Aᴾ.  
10. Perform GRPO update on critic using {cₒ} and Aᶜ.

### Design tradeoffs
- Synchronized updates simplify the loop but require careful learning rate coordination; mismatches can cause one model to dominate.
- Cascaded rollout increases computational cost (N× rollouts) but provides group structure for robust advantage estimation; N balances diversity vs efficiency.
- Saturation-aware shaping introduces hyperparameter η; too high flattens gains, too low causes instability near sᵣ=1.
- Using same backbone for both models reduces parameters but may limit capacity; separate backbones increase cost.

### Failure signatures
- Critic loss plateaus while policy improves → critic may have stopped learning; check diversity of critiques (e.g., embedding similarity).
- Policy gradient norms vanish → group advantages may be near zero; increase N or adjust temperature.
- Sudden drop in both losses → reward model inconsistency; verify reward calibration.
- Training instability when sᵣ close to 1 → adjust η downward.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does ECHO handle noisy, biased, or underspecified external reward models, and what techniques can mitigate resulting misalignment?  
  Basis: Limitations section states effectiveness depends on reward quality; no empirical analysis of reward imperfections.
- **Open Question 2:** Can reward evaluation and critique generation be unified into a single model, and would this integration improve training efficiency and feedback consistency?  
  Basis: Future work mentions separate models and leaves integration to future work.
- **Open Question 3:** How can ECHO be applied to environments with purely binary or extremely sparse rewards, where the saturation‑aware gain shaping is not directly applicable?  
  Basis: SA gain assumes meaningful reward magnitudes; evaluation on binary-reward tasks like ALFWorld lacks adaptation.

## Limitations
- Exact GRPO hyperparameters (clip ϵ, KL penalty β, batch size, training steps) and reference model for KL divergence are unreported.
- Reward model interfaces and calibration procedures for each environment are unspecified.
- Prompt templates for task-specific scoring criteria (Appendix D) are missing.

## Confidence
- **Co-evolving critic mechanism:** Medium. Evidence from synchronized updates and ablations supports the claim, but reliance on unreported GRPO details and reward consistency tempers confidence.
- **Cascaded rollout gains:** Medium. The group-relative design is clearly described and ablations imply benefit, but the impact of N and critique diversity is not validated.
- **Saturation-aware shaping:** Low-Medium. The mathematical form is provided and ablation shows gain, but no analysis of sensitivity to η or task reward distributions is given.
