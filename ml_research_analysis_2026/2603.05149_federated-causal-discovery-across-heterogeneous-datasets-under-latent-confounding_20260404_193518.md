# Federated Causal Discovery Across Heterogeneous Datasets under Latent Confounding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.05149
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05149
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Introduces fedCI, a federated conditional independence test for heterogeneous data under latent confounding, and fedCI‑IOD for causal discovery; both achieve performance comparable to centralized pooled analysis and far surpass Fisher’s meta‑analysis.

## Executive Summary
Performing causal discovery on data distributed across sites is challenging due to privacy constraints, heterogeneous variable sets, mixed data types, and latent confounders. This paper presents **fedCI**, a federated conditional independence (CI) test that uses federated Iteratively Reweighted Least Squares (IRLS) to fit Generalized Linear Models (GLMs) for likelihood‑ratio tests without sharing raw data. Privacy is protected via additive masking and careful handling of site‑specific effects. The fedCI test is integrated into the Integration of Overlapping Datasets (IOD) algorithm, yielding **fedCI‑IOD**, a fully federated pipeline that learns Partial Ancestral Graphs (PAGs) from heterogeneous distributed data even with latent confounding.

Simulations on 5‑node causal graphs with up to 12 clients show that fedCI matches centralized pooled accuracy in CI testing (≈1.0) and outperforms Fisher’s meta‑analysis (≈0.6). For causal discovery, fedCI‑IOD achieves normalized SHD scores between 0.166–0.175, nearly identical to pooled analysis (0.166–0.174) and far better than Fisher’s (up to 0.403). Statistical tests confirm negligible deviation from the pooled baseline. The authors release Python/R packages and a web application.

## Method Summary
The core of the approach is **fedCI**, a federated CI test based on GLMs. For a query such as $X \perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid Z$, the method fits two nested GLMs in a distributed fashion: the restricted model $Y \sim Z$ (or $X \sim Z$) and the unrestricted model $Y \sim X + Z$ (or $X \sim Y + Z$). IRLS decomposes the Fisher information matrix and score vector into client‑local contributions; these are aggregated at a central server under additive masking, allowing global maximum‑likelihood estimation without exposing individual data. A likelihood‑ratio test compares the models, and a bidirectional strategy combines p‑values from both directions to reach a final CI decision. Site‑specific effects are addressed either by modeling site as a fixed effect in the global GLM or via a coordinate‑ascent variant (fedCI‑CA) that keeps site coefficients on the client, further enhancing privacy.

To achieve causal discovery across heterogeneous datasets, fedCI is embedded into the IOD algorithm. IOD works with partially overlapping variable sets and uses CI constraints to infer PAGs, which encode Markov equivalence classes under latent confounding. In fedCI‑IOD, the standard CI testing (often local GLMs plus Fisher’s meta‑analysis) is replaced by the federated LRT, leveraging all available data per query without pooling. The algorithm also exploits orientations from triples with order to reduce the candidate PAG list. This yields a privacy‑preserving, federated causal discovery framework robust to latent confounding.

## Key Results
- **CI test accuracy**: fedCI matches centralized pooled performance (accuracy≈1.0) and significantly outperforms Fisher’s meta‑analysis (≈0.6) when data are partitioned into 12 clients.
- **Causal discovery quality**: fedCI‑IOD yields normalized SHD scores between 0.166–0.175, closely tracking pooled analysis (0.166–0.174) and far surpassing Fisher’s (up to 0.403).
- **Statistical equivalence**: Cohen’s d indicates fedCI‑IOD’s deviation from pooled is negligible, validating its effectiveness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Federated CI testing via IRLS‑GLM
- **Claim**: Federated conditional independence testing is feasible for heterogeneous datasets with non‑identical variable sets and mixed data types.
- **Mechanism**: A federated Iteratively Reweighted Least Squares (IRLS) procedure fits nested Generalized Linear Models (GLMs) for likelihood‑ratio tests. Each client computes local contributions to the Fisher information and score vector; these are aggregated under additive masking at a central server, which updates global parameters and computes log‑likelihoods. The LRT compares restricted (independence) and unrestricted (dependence) models. A bidirectional testing approach combines p‑values from testing both directions into a final decision.
- **Core assumption**: The GLM specification is flexible enough to capture true conditional relationships, and the likelihood equations are solvable via IRLS under the distributed partitioning.
- **Evidence**: Sections II‑B and III‑A detail the federated IRLS update and the decomposition of statistics; related work addresses similar challenges but not this exact mechanism.
- **Break condition**: IRLS fails to converge due to perfect separation, severe multicollinearity, or incompatible link functions across heterogeneous local data.

### Mechanism 2: Privacy and site‑effect handling
- **Claim**: Data privacy and client heterogeneity (site‑specific effects) are preserved during federated CI testing.
- **Mechanism**: Two layers: (1) **Additive masking** obfuscates each client’s contributions to the Fisher information and score vectors during every IRLS iteration. (2) **Site effects** are handled either by modeling site as a fixed effect in the global GLM or via a coordinate‑ascent variant (fedCI‑CA) where site effects are subtracted locally before sharing, ensuring site coefficients never leave the client. For Gaussian data, dispersion is estimated globally using aggregated squared residuals.
- **Core assumption**: Additive masking effectively hides individual contributions, and the site‑effect strategy correctly accounts for heterogeneity without sharing raw site‑varying data.
- **Evidence**: The abstract mentions privacy and site effects; Section III‑A describes additive masking, the fixed‑effect approach, fedCI‑CA, and Gaussian dispersion handling.
- **Break condition**: Masking is insufficient against model inversion with very small local datasets; or site effects not independent of predictors violates the informational independence assumption in fedCI‑CA.

### Mechanism 3: Federated causal discovery via IOD
- **Claim**: Federated causal discovery under latent confounding across partially overlapping datasets is achievable via a privacy‑preserving IOD algorithm fed by federated CI tests.
- **Mechanism**: The fedCI‑IOD pipeline integrates fedCI into the Integration of Overlapping Datasets (IOD) algorithm. IOD learns Partial Ancestral Graphs (PAGs) from multiple datasets with different variable sets, using Fisher’s method to combine CI test p‑values. fedCI‑IOD replaces Fisher’s meta‑analysis with the federated LRT, directly fitting global GLMs on all available data across sites. This increases statistical power and avoids errors from local unfaithfulness. IOD then orients edges using FCI rules on the combined CI decisions, enumerating candidate PAGs when some triples are unobserved due to non‑overlapping variables.
- **Core assumption**: Faithfulness holds globally so that all true conditional independencies are detectable by CI tests, and fedCI provides accurate p‑values reflecting the global distribution despite partitioning.
- **Evidence**: The abstract describes fedCI‑IOD as enabling federated causal discovery; Section III‑B explains the replacement and modifications; Section IV presents simulation results.
- **Break condition**: Faithfulness is violated globally or locally; or the number of partitions is so high that many critical variable triples are never jointly observed, rendering some CI relations untestable.

## Foundational Learning

### Generalized Linear Models (GLMs)
**Why needed**: GLMs provide a flexible, likelihood‑based foundation for modeling mixed‑type outcomes (continuous, binary, ordinal, categorical) in CI tests. The choice of link function and distribution family determines the LRT’s validity for a given variable type.
**Quick check**: For a binary outcome Y and a mix of continuous and categorical predictors Z, what GLM family and link function would you specify to test Y ⊥ X|Z?

### Iteratively Reweighted Least Squares (IRLS)
**Why needed**: IRLS is the workhorse for fitting GLMs via maximum likelihood. Its federated version decomposes the Hessian (Fisher information) and gradient into sums of local client statistics, enabling privacy‑preserving distributed optimization.
**Quick check**: In the IRLS update $\beta^{(t+1)} = (X^\top W X)^{-1}X^\top W z$, what do the weight matrix $W$ and working response $z$ represent, and why are they updated at each iteration?

### Partial Ancestral Graphs (PAGs)
**Why needed**: PAGs represent the Markov equivalence class of causal graphs under latent confounding and selection bias. IOD outputs a list of possible PAGs consistent with all CI constraints from the distributed datasets. Understanding PAG semantics (tail, arrowhead, circle) is crucial for interpreting the causal discovery results.
**Quick check**: In a PAG, what does an edge marked $X \rightarrow \circ Y$ indicate about the relationship between $X$ and $Y$ in the true causal model?

## Architecture Onboarding

#### Component Map
- **Client**: Holds the local dataset; computes local GLM statistics; participates in additive masking; can perform local CI tests if required variables are present.
- **Server**: Coordinates the federated IRLS; aggregates masked client contributions; updates global model parameters; computes log‑likelihoods; makes final CI decisions; runs the modified IOD algorithm.
- **fedCI (Python package)**: Implements federated IRLS‑GLM fitting, bidirectional LRT with Tsagris p‑value combination, variable type detection, additive masking, and logic for non‑identical variable sets.
- **rIOD (R package)**: Privacy‑preserving IOD implementation; accepts CI test results either as locally computed p‑values or via calls to the fedCI server; implements enhanced PAG construction using all triples with order.
- **WebApp (Dockerized)**: Integrates Client, Server, fedCI, and rIOD into a UI; users can upload data, configure variable types, start a “room,” and view local/global PAGs.

#### Critical Path
1. **System initialization**: Server broadcasts the union of variable labels and levels to all clients.
2. **CI query execution**: For each query (X,Y|Z), server requests initial β; clients with all required variables run federated IRLS to fit the restricted and unrestricted GLMs (M0 vs. M1 for both directions); masked statistics are aggregated; server computes global log‑likelihoods, LRT statistics $T_Y$, $T_X$, and combines p‑values into a final CI decision.
3. **IOD processing**: All CI p‑values are fed into rIOD to generate a list of global PAGs.
4. **Result distribution**: PAGs are returned to the clients for inspection.

#### Design Tradeoffs
- **Privacy vs. Accuracy**: Additive masking and local site‑effect removal enhance privacy but may reduce efficiency if masking overhead is high. fedCI‑CA assumes informational independence between regression coefficients and site effects; violation can bias estimates.
- **Generality vs. Stability**: GLMs are broadly applicable but rely on correct specification (family, link). Non‑linear relationships require basis expansions, increasing parameter count and potentially destabilizing IRLS convergence in the federated setting.
- **Power vs. Completeness**: Using all available data per CI test maximizes power and handles non‑identical variable sets, yet some edges may remain unresolved if no client jointly observes all three variables in a triple.

#### Failure Signatures
- **IRLS non‑convergence**: Symptoms include excessive iterations or exploding coefficients. Diagnose with ridge regularization or Levenberg‑Marquardt damping; likely cause: perfect separation or severe multicollinearity in a client’s subset.
- **PAG list explosion**: IOD produces an excessively large candidate list, indicating many missing CI constraints due to vertical partitioning. Mitigate by employing the triple‑with‑order IOD variant and ensuring sufficient variable overlap.
- **Synchronization errors**: Clients report mismatched variable sets or levels during setup. Root causes: incorrect manual union specification or inconsistent categorical level ordering. Verify that all clients encode categorical/ordinal variables with identical level ordering.

#### First Three Experiments
1. **Unit test federated GLM**: Run fedCI on two clients with identical variable sets on a known dataset; compare federated β estimate to a centralized pooled estimate; expect near‑identical values (|diff| < 0.01) for i.i.d. data.
2. **Compare CI test power**: For a synthetic dependency $X \not\!\perp\!\!\!\perp Y \mid Z$, evaluate fedCI, Fisher’s meta‑analysis, and pooled tests across increasing client counts (4, 8, 12). Plot power versus sample size; fedCI should match pooled, while Fisher’s power degrades with more partitions.
3. **End‑to‑end causal discovery**: Generate a 5‑node PAG with a latent confounder, partition vertically into two clients with three overlapping variables. Run fedCI‑IOD and IOD‑with‑Fisher; compute normalized SHD between inferred and oracle PAGs. Expect fedCI‑IOD SHD ≤ 0.05 (near‑oracle), Fisher’s > 0.3.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **GLM linearity vs. nonlinearity**: How does the linearity assumption in GLMs affect accuracy when the true data‑generating process involves complex nonlinear relationships? The paper only tests linear SCMs; no evaluation on nonlinear data.
2. **Scalability**: What are the computational and communication limits of fedCI‑IOD with hundreds of clients or thousands of variables? The study only covers up to 12 clients and 5‑node graphs.
3. **Faithfulness violations**: How well does fedCI‑IOD tolerate or detect violations of the faithfulness assumption? Simulations avoid empirical unfaithfulness; no controlled experiments on cancellation effects.
4. **Fixed vs. random effects for sites**: Does modeling site as a random effect (via GLMMs) improve accuracy relative to fixed effects, especially when sites are many or considered a sample from a broader population? The paper’s fixed‑effect approach shows no loss in small‑scale settings but does not explore random effects.
5. **Privacy mechanism choice**: How does additive masking compare to differential privacy in terms of the trade‑off between utility and resistance to model inversion or reconstruction attacks? The paper notes additive masking may leak information on small datasets but avoids differential privacy due to potential noise.

## Limitations
- Real‑world applicability remains unvalidated; evaluation relies solely on synthetic simulations.
- Correct GLM specification is critical; mis‑specification may bias CI tests and subsequent causal discovery.
- Privacy via additive masking lacks formal differential privacy guarantees and may be vulnerable to inference attacks, particularly when local datasets are small.
- The IOD approach depends on the faithfulness assumption; empirical violations could disrupt discovery.
- Vertical heterogeneity may leave some variable triples unobserved in any single client, leading to incomplete PAGs.

## Confidence
- **Federated IRLS‑GLM mechanism**: High
- **Privacy preservation and site‑effects modeling**: Medium
- **FedCI‑IOD for causal discovery under latent confounding**: Medium
- **Scalability to many clients/high dimensions**: Low
- **Robustness to severe model mis‑specification**: Low
