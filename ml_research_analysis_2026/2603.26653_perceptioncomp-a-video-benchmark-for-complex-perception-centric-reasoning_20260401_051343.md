# PerceptionComp: A Video Benchmark for Complex Perception-Centric Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26653
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26653
- **Reference Count:** 30
- **Primary Result:** State-of-the-art multimodal language models achieve at most 45.96% accuracy on this challenging benchmark, far below human performance (85.10% with rewatching, but only 18.97% with single view).

## Executive Summary
PerceptionComp introduces a new benchmark to evaluate multimodal language models on complex, long-horizon video reasoning that requires repeated evidence gathering across temporally dispersed segments. The dataset comprises 279 high-complexity videos (2–10 minutes) and 1,114 multiple-choice questions, each demanding the integration of multiple perceptual subconditions under conjunctive or sequential logic. Human performance drops to near chance (18.97%) when viewing the video only once, highlighting the benchmark's difficulty. In contrast, the best model (Gemini-3-Flash) attains only 45.96% accuracy, with open-source models below 40%, indicating a substantial gap in perception-centric reasoning capabilities. The benchmark demonstrates sensitivity to both input frame count and reasoning compute, underscoring the need for models that can robustly handle long-horizon perception tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed PerceptionComp by manually selecting 279 high-complexity videos from everyday domains (city walks, indoor tours, sports, games). Candidate videos were filtered using automatic proxies: SAM2 instance counts and optical-flow magnitude to ensure visual richness. For each video, one or more five-choice questions were crafted, each combining multiple subconditions (objects, attributes, relations, locations, actions, events) that must be satisfied simultaneously (conjunctive) or in a specific order (sequential). This forces models to locate and integrate evidence scattered across the video timeline. The evaluation protocol: for models without native video support, 64 frames are uniformly sampled; proprietary models use Chain-of-Thought prompting, while open-source instruct models output directly; thinking models employ CoT with a temperature of 0.7 and a large generation budget (up to 16,384 tokens). Final answers are judged by Gemini-2.5-Flash for semantic equivalence to ground truth.

## Key Results
- Human single-view accuracy is only 18.97%, whereas with unrestricted rewatching ordinary participants reach 85.10%.
- The best multimodal model, Gemini-3-Flash, scores 45.96%; open-source models perform below 40%.
- Model accuracy improves with more input frames (16→64) and larger thinking-token budgets (1k→8k), revealing scaling benefits for perception-reasoning compute.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PerceptionComp works by forcing long-horizon evidence integration: questions are designed such that no single short clip contains all necessary information. Each question decomposes into several perceptual subconditions that must be verified in specific spatiotemporal contexts. The conjunctive and sequential logical structures require models to maintain a consistent hypothesis while searching across the video, similar to a detective piecing together clues. This design eliminates shortcuts like reacting to a single salient event, thus truly testing the model's ability to accumulate and reason over distributed perceptual evidence.

## Foundational Learning
- **Temporal Evidence Aggregation:** Models must retrieve relevant moments from a long video and combine them. Needed because real-world perception often spans extended time. Quick check: Can the model answer a question requiring two events separated by a minute?
- **Subcondition Composition:** Questions consist of multiple constraints (object, attribute, relation) that must all hold. Needed to mimic complex natural language queries. Quick check: Does the model correctly handle a conjunctive query requiring both "red car" and "left of tree"?
- **Spatial Reasoning in Video:** Tracking objects and their spatial relations (left/right, near/far) across frames. Needed for accurate scene understanding. Quick check: Can the model determine whether an object moves from left to right across a scene?
- **Logical Sequencing:** Some questions enforce an order (e.g., "first X, then Y"). Needed for tasks involving cause-effect or temporal order. Quick check: Does the model respect sequential constraints when both events occur?
- **Perception-Reasoning Decoupling:** The benchmark isolates perception (identifying facts) from reasoning (combining them). Needed to pinpoint model weaknesses. Quick check: Does providing explicit evidence snippets improve reasoning accuracy?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Raw video -> (optional frame sampling) -> Multimodal encoder -> MLLM with Chain-of-Thought -> Answer choice -> LLM judge (Gemini-2.5-Flash). Critical path: video ingestion -> frame sampling (for non-native models) -> model inference producing reasoning trace and final answer -> judge verifies correctness. Design tradeoffs: more frames increase compute but capture rare events; larger thinking budgets yield more reasoning steps but add latency; frame sampling strategy (uniform vs. adaptive) affects information retention. Failure signatures: (1) Mid-chain spatial collapse – errors in intermediate spatial judgments; (2) Logical hallucination – ignoring required subconditions; (3) Protagonist bias – fixation on a single object; (4) Insufficient perception budget – accuracy drops with few frames. First three experiments to run: (1) Baseline: 64 frames, standard CoT, measure accuracy; (2) Frame scaling: repeat with 16 and 32 frames to assess sensitivity; (3) Thinking scaling: vary token budget (1k, 4k, 8k) with fixed frame count to examine reasoning gains.
