# QuantFL: Sustainable Federated Learning for Edge IoT via Pre-Trained Model Quantisation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.17507
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17507
- **Reference count**: 20
- **Primary result**: Reduces total communication by ~40% (over 80% uplink) while matching/exceeding uncompressed FedAvg accuracy; e.g., 89.00% on MNIST and 66.89% on CIFAR-100 with 40.62% bit reduction.

## Executive Summary
QuantFL introduces a sustainable federated learning approach for edge IoT devices by leveraging pre-trained model initialization to enable simple, memory-efficient bucket quantisation of client updates. This avoids the communication overhead and memory cost of error-feedback mechanisms. The method achieves significant communication reduction (over 80% on uplink, 40% total) while maintaining or improving accuracy compared to uncompressed FedAvg, even under strict bandwidth constraints and non-IID data. Experiments on MNIST and CIFAR-100 demonstrate robust performance, with bucket-uniform quantisation achieving 89.00% and 66.89% test accuracy respectively at a 40.62% bit reduction. By concentrating update statistics through pre-training, QuantFL provides a practical, green solution for scalable IoT federated learning.

## Method Summary
QuantFL modifies the standard FedAvg pipeline by initializing the global model with pre-trained weights (e.g., from ImageNet), which concentrates the distribution of client updates. Clients then perform local SGD and quantize their updates using a per-layer bucket quantiser: each layer’s update values are mapped to a fixed number of intervals (buckets), and only the bucket indices are transmitted. The quantisation boundaries (codebooks) are computed infrequently on the server and broadcast periodically, amortizing their transmission cost. The server decodes indices using interval mid-points and aggregates the updates. This simple scheme avoids storing error-feedback residuals, saving memory and bits. Two quantisers are explored: bucket-uniform (BU) with fixed-width intervals, and bucket-quantile (BQ) with equal-mass intervals. The total communication cost per round is the sum of index bits and amortized codebook bits.

## Key Results
- Communication reduction: ~40% total (over 80% uplink) compared to FedAvg.
- Accuracy: On MNIST, bucket-uniform (BU) achieves 89.00% test accuracy with 40.62% bit reduction; on CIFAR-100, BU achieves 66.89% with same reduction.
- Robustness: BU maintains performance under non-IID data distributions where other quantisation methods degrade.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Pre-training Concentrates Update Distribution
Pre-training the global model initialisation concentrates the distribution of client updates `∆w`, reducing their dynamic range and variance. Starting from a parameter set already in a good optima region means local SGD steps make smaller, less dispersed adjustments. The core assumption is that the pre-trained model provides a sufficiently good feature extractor so that local fine-tuning requires only modest updates across diverse client data, even under non-IID conditions. Evidence includes Section IV.A showing reduced dynamic range `Rℓ` and Section V.B demonstrating that pre-trained initialisation consistently yields higher accuracy than scratch (NP) variants for a fixed bit budget. However, if the pre-trained model is poorly matched to the target data distribution (domain shift), local updates may become large and dispersed again, negating the concentration benefit.

### Mechanism 2: Simple Bucket Quantisation Without Error-Feedback
Simple, memory-efficient per-layer bucketed scalar quantisation (BU/BQ) with mid-point decoding achieves low quantisation distortion on concentrated updates without needing error-feedback (EF) memory. For a layer ℓ, the update vector is scalar-quantised into `Lℓ` intervals defined by boundaries `Bℓ`; each value is replaced by an interval index (`⌈log2 Lℓ⌉` bits). The server decodes using the interval mid-point, which is MSE-optimal for a uniform scalar quantiser. Because pre-training has shrunk the range/variance, the quantisation error (`≈ ∆ℓ²/12`) becomes negligible even for small `Lℓ` (e.g., 64). This avoids the need to store and transmit EF residuals, saving memory and bits. Evidence: Abstract states it avoids energy-intensive EF; Section IV.B notes BU remains stable under non-IID; Section V.B shows BU outperforms QSGD (which has EF-style guarantees) on CIFAR-100 non-IID, suggesting EF is unnecessary due to update concentration. Break condition: Under severe data heterogeneity where updates are highly biased and/or heavy-tailed, the fixed small `Lℓ` uniform buckets (BU) may still work well, but BQ (adaptive buckets) can fail if the empirical CDF from a small sample is inaccurate under skew (as seen in Table III).

### Mechanism 3: Infrequent Codebook Refresh Amortises Overhead
The bucket boundaries `Bℓ` (quantisation codebooks) are transmitted periodically (every `Tℓ` rounds) rather than every round, amortising their transmission cost. Since the optimal range or quantile boundaries change slowly over FL rounds when starting from pre-training, a single `Bℓ` remains a good approximation for many rounds. The amortised per-round cost for codebook transmission is `(b * Lℓ) / Tℓ` bits per layer, which becomes negligible compared to the `dℓ * ⌈log2 Lℓ⌉` bits from indices when `Tℓ` is large. Evidence: Section IV.C derives the cost model; Section V.A sets `T` and reports results; the paper does not ablation `T`, implying chosen values were sufficient. Break condition: If local update distributions drift rapidly (e.g., due to high client heterogeneity or aggressive local training), the fixed `Bℓ` becomes misaligned quickly, increasing distortion; also, if `Tℓ` is forced small, amortisation benefit vanishes.

- **Scalar Quantisation & Distortion**: The process of mapping a continuous range of values to a discrete set of levels, introducing mean-squared error (MSE) proportional to the square of the step size. Reducing bits per round can thus yield significant energy savings, sometimes more than reducing the number of rounds, because computation (quantisation) is relatively cheap. *Quick check*: Why might reducing the number of bits per round be more impactful for IoT device battery life than reducing the number of communication rounds by a similar factor?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Pre-trained Model Initialiser → Server (global model) → Broadcast model → Client (Per-Layer Bucket Quantiser + Codebook Manager) → quantize Δw → send indices → Server (Mid-Point Decoder + Aggregation Module) → aggregate → (Periodic: Codebook Manager refresh Bℓ → broadcast Bℓ) → repeat. Bit-Accounting Logger runs alongside to track bits.

### Critical Path
1. Server initializes global model `w0` from pre-trained weights.
2. Server broadcasts `w0` to selected clients.
3. Each client performs local SGD on its data to compute update `Δw_i^k`.
4. Client quantizes `Δw_i^k` using per-layer bucket boundaries `Bℓ` (from local Codebook Manager) to produce index tensor.
5. Client sends indices to server.
6. Server decodes each index to a mid-point value using `Bℓ`.
7. Server aggregates decoded updates via FedAvg: `w^{k+1} = w^k + (1/|S_k|) * Σ_i∈S_k ˜Δw_i^k`.
8. Every `Tℓ` rounds, server recomputes new boundaries `Bℓ` from recent updates and broadcasts them; clients update their local codebooks.
9. Repeat from step 2.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **BU vs BQ**: Bucket-uniform (BU) uses fixed-width intervals and is simpler and more robust to non-IID/heavy-tailed updates; Bucket-quantile (BQ) adapts intervals to empirical CDF but can be brittle under skew (Table III shows BQ accuracy drops more than BU as Dirichlet α decreases).
- **Levels L vs Refresh T**: Higher `L` reduces quantisation distortion but increases index bits (`log2 L`) and codebook size (`b·L`). Lower `T` (more frequent boundary refresh) keeps boundaries aligned with drifting distributions but increases amortised codebook cost. The paper finds `L=64` with moderate `T` hits a sweet spot.
- **Uplink-only vs Downlink Quantisation**: The paper quantises only uplink; downlink remains full-precision. Quantising downlink would save more bits but requires synchronising codebooks for model broadcast, adding complexity.

### Failure Signatures
1. **Accuracy collapse under non-IID for BQ**: As seen in Table III, BQ's test accuracy drops significantly compared to BU when client data is highly heterogeneous (Dirichlet α=0.1), indicating BQ's equal-mass buckets fail to cover important tail values under skewed updates.
2. **Poor convergence for scratch (NP) variants**: Table II shows all NP (no pre-training) methods have higher train/test loss and lower accuracy at the same bit budget, proving pre-training is essential for aggressive quantisation.
3. **Divergence with very low L**: Though not shown in main results, using too few levels (e.g., L=16) would likely cause convergence instability or final accuracy far below baseline, indicating a lower bound on `L` for given pre-training and dataset.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline Reproduction & Quant sanity check**: Run uncompressed FedAvg (NQ) from pre-trained and from random init (scratch) on MNIST/CNN. Confirm pre-training yields faster/accurate convergence. Then apply a simple scalar quantiser with `L=64` (uniform buckets over full observed range) every round *without* codebook refresh (transmit boundaries every round). Measure accuracy vs. bits to see the value of refresh amortisation and mid-point decoding.
2. **Isolate Pre-training Concentration**: Run BU (`L=64`, large `T`) from **pre-trained** and from **scratch** on CIFAR-100/ResNet-18. Measure final test accuracy and the *dynamic range* `(max(∆w) - min(∆w))` averaged over layers in early rounds. Expect smaller range for pre-trained, correlating with higher accuracy (as in Table II NP vs. Ours).
3. **Non-IID Stress Test of BU vs. BQ**: On CIFAR-100 with Dirichlet α=0.1, run BU and BQ at `L=64`. Also run QSGD as a stochastic comparator. Expect BU to significantly outperform BQ and QSGD in final test accuracy, confirming BU's robustness claim. Monitor per-round loss to see if BQ/QSGD show more volatility.

## Limitations
- Pre-training may fail to concentrate updates under domain shift (not evaluated).
- Codebook refresh period `T` sensitivity is unreported; assumption of slow drift is plausible but unvalidated.
- Experiments limited to MNIST/CIFAR-100; generalizability to real edge IoT data (e.g., sensors, video) is unclear.

## Confidence
- Pre-training concentrates updates: Medium
- Bucket quantisation without error-feedback works: Medium-High
- Amortised codebook refresh reduces overhead: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Domain-shift test: Apply QuantFL to IoT-native datasets (e.g., sensor readings, video) with mismatched pre-training (e.g., ImageNet) to probe concentration collapse.
2. Refresh period ablation: Vary `T` across {1, 10, 50, 100} rounds under high non-IID (Dirichlet α=0.05) and aggressive local steps; measure accuracy vs. amortised cost.
3. Update distribution tracking: Compute per-layer dynamic range and kurtosis of `∆w` across rounds for pre-trained vs. scratch on CIFAR-100 non-IID; directly validate concentration claim and its temporal stability.
