# Why Deep Jacobian Spectra Separate: Depth-Induced Scaling and Singular-Vector Alignment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.12384
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12384
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Deep Jacobians exhibit depth-induced exponential scaling of singular values and strong spectral separation, forcing alignment of dominant singular vectors and enabling decoupled singular-value dynamics.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates the implicit bias of gradient-based training in deep neural networks, focusing on the structure of the Jacobian spectrum. It proposes that deep Jacobians naturally develop two distinctive properties: an exponential scaling of ordered singular values with depth and a strong spectral separation. These properties emerge from the multiplicative structure of the Jacobian as a product of random linear maps (modeled via fixed Bernoulli gates and Gaussian weights). The authors prove that for such models the singular values grow exponentially with depth, characterized by Lyapunov exponents, and derive finite-depth corrections. They show that strong spectral separation forces the dominant left singular vectors of intermediate Jacobians to align with those of the full Jacobian, establishing an approximately shared singular basis across layers. This alignment decouples the evolution of individual singular values, yielding dynamics analogous to those in balanced deep-linear networks, even without explicit balancing. Extensive experiments on synthetic and MNIST data confirm the theoretical predictions, including scaling laws, alignment measurements, and trajectory matching.

## Method Summary
The core of the work is a theoretical analysis of the Fixed-Gates Linear Network (FGLN), where the Jacobian J of a deep network is expressed as a product of masked linear layers: J = W_L D_{L-1} W_{L-1} ... D_1 W_1. The gates D_ℓ are diagonal Bernoulli random variables (simulating the activation pattern) and the weights W_ℓ are i.i.d. Gaussian. The gates are fixed after initialization, isolating the effect of depth on the Jacobian's singular values. The authors treat the product of random matrices and use tools from random matrix theory and Lyapunov exponents to characterize the asymptotic growth of the top singular values with depth. For the Bernoulli-gated Gaussian case, they derive closed-form Lyapunov exponents and provide finite-depth correction terms based on expected log-determinants of submatrices. The second major contribution is to show that when the singular value spectrum exhibits strong separation (i.e., successive ratios s_{i+1}/s_i → 0), the leading left singular vectors of the partial products A_ℓ = W_ℓ D_{ℓ-1} ... D_1 W_1 become aligned with those of the full Jacobian. This alignment yields an approximately common singular basis throughout the network. Consequently, the evolution of each singular value under gradient flow becomes effectively independent and follows a mode-wise dynamics that mirrors the balanced deep-linear case, even though no explicit balancing condition is imposed. The theory is validated through experiments measuring depth scaling, alignment (via diagonal correlation), and dynamics on synthetic low-rank regression and MNIST classification.

## Key Results
- Deep Jacobians exhibit depth-induced exponential scaling of ordered singular values and strong spectral separation.
- Strong spectral separation forces alignment of dominant singular vectors across layers, yielding an approximately shared singular basis and decoupled singular-value dynamics.
- Theoretical predictions (Lyapunov exponents, finite-depth corrections, alignment rates) are experimentally validated on synthetic and MNIST tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Depth-Induced Scaling of Singular Values
- Claim: At initialization, the ordered singular values of deep Jacobians grow exponentially with depth, creating separation.
- Mechanism: For products of masked linear maps (Bernoulli-gated Gaussian weights), Lyapunov exponents γ_i govern the asymptotic linear growth of log(s_i,L) with depth L, causing consecutive singular values to separate.
- Core assumption: Gates have rank concentrated near np (i.e., r ≪ np) and weights are i.i.d. Gaussian; the product is square with dimensions fixed.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] The paper identifies "depth-induced exponential scaling of ordered singular values" as a key signature.
  - [section 5] Theorem 5.3 proves existence of Lyapunov exponents and provides a closed-form expression for γ_i in the Bernoulli-gated Gaussian model (Remark 5.4); finite-depth corrections are derived.
  - [corpus] Weak: neighboring papers (e.g., on MoE Jacobian spectra) study Jacobian geometry but not depth-driven scaling in gated products.
- Break condition: If gates are too sparse (p small) or depth is insufficient, the asymptotic regime (L → ∞) may not be reached; non-Gaussian weights or rectangular layers could alter exponents.

### Mechanism 2: Spectral Separation Forces Singular-Vector Alignment
- Claim: Strong spectral separation (rapid decay of singular value ratios) forces the dominant left singular vectors of intermediate Jacobians to align with those of the full Jacobian.
- Mechanism: In a factorization JL = A_ℓ B_ℓ, if A_ℓ has strong separation, its extreme ellipticity suppresses perturbations from B_ℓ, making the leading left singular subspace of JL close to that of A_ℓ (Theorem 6.1).
- Core assumption: For top r singular values of A_ℓ, s_{i+1}/s_i → 0 as depth increases; also B_ℓ has linearly independent leading columns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "strong spectral separation forces alignment of dominant singular vectors across layers, yielding an approximately shared singular basis."
  - [section 6] Theorem 6.1 gives quantitative alignment rates; alignment is measured experimentally via diagonal correlation (Section 8, Figure 5–6).
  - [corpus] Limited: some papers (e.g., on residual alignment) study vector alignment but not via spectral separation in gated products.
- Break condition: If spectral separation is weak (e.g., clustered singular values), alignment degrades; dependent or poorly conditioned B_ℓ can also disrupt alignment.

### Mechanism 3: Decoupled Singular-Value Dynamics Without Balancing
- Claim: Under depth scaling and alignment, the evolution of each singular value becomes approximately independent, mirroring balanced deep-linear dynamics while requiring no balancing condition.
- Mechanism: An approximately shared singular basis (from alignment) decouples the dynamics of ordered singular values; each evolves according to a mode-wise equation similar to Arora et al. (2019), with a global scaling factor from depth.
- Core assumption: Depth scaling (exponential singular value profiles) holds during training; alignment persists for intermediate products (U_J ≈ U_Aℓ, V_J ≈ V_Bℓ) across all ℓ.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "singular-value dynamics become effectively decoupled, mirroring classical balanced deep-linear analyses without requiring balancing."
  - [section 7] Proposition 7.1 derives approximate dynamics under explicit scaling and alignment assumptions; experiments in Section 8 validate predicted scaling, alignment, and dynamics on MNIST/synthetic tasks.
  - [corpus] Moderate: corpus includes papers on implicit bias in deep linear networks (e.g., Saxe et al.), but this extends to gated products without balancing.
- Break condition: If alignment drifts during training (e.g., due to non-stationary weight evolution) or scaling deviates from exponential form, the decoupling approximation fails.

## Architecture Onboarding

*Component map*: Fixed-Gates Linear Network (FGLN): J = W_L D_{L-1} ... D_1 W_1, with diagonal Bernoulli gates D_ℓ and Gaussian weights W_ℓ. Training via gradient flow on W_ℓ; gates fixed (simulate single activation region).

*Critical path*: 
1. At initialization, verify depth scaling: log(s_i,L)/L → γ_i (Theorem 5.3).
2. During training, monitor alignment: diagonal correlation of U_J^T U_Aℓ should be high for ℓ ≪ L (Theorem 6.1).
3. If both hold, approximate singular-value dynamics using Proposition 7.1; compare trajectories to balanced deep-linear case.

*Design tradeoffs*:
- Depth vs. width: Deeper networks enhance separation but require sufficient width n to maintain gate rank (r ≪ np); too sparse gates (small p) collapse rank.
- Assumption stringency: Spectral separation and exponential scaling are asymptotic; for finite L, corrections (Theorem 5.3) and approximate alignment (Remark 6.3) matter.
- Generality: FGLN approximates Jacobian in a fixed activation pattern; multi-mode extensions (piecewise-linear) add complexity.

*Failure signatures*:
- No scaling: log(s_i,L) not affine in L; indicates non-i.i.d. initialization or gate distributions.
- Low alignment: Diagonal correlation of U_J^T U_Aℓ ≪ 1 even for ℓ ≪ L; suggests separation insufficient or B_ℓ not well-conditioned.
- Dynamics mismatch: Predicted decoupled singular-value trajectories diverge from observed; likely due to time-varying γ_i or alignment breakdown.

*First 3 experiments*:
1. **Depth scaling test**: Randomly initialize FGLN with varying L; compute top singular values; plot log(s_i,L) vs. L; fit slope and compare to γ_i from Remark 5.4.
2. **Alignment validation**: Train on MNIST (augmented); for each ℓ, compute diagonal correlation between U_J and U_Aℓ; expect high correlation for ℓ far from L after training (Figure 5).
3. **Dynamics replication**: On synthetic rank-10 dataset, track s_1(t) during training; overlay with prediction from Proposition 7.1 (using measured g(t)); adjust scaling factor C for quantitative match (Figure 7).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**  
- **Question**: Extend depth-scaling and spectral separation to multi-mode FGLNs with dependencies.  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Authors mention studying multi-modes FGLNs as future work.  
- **Why unresolved**: Current analysis assumes single-mode; dependencies could impact alignment and scaling.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Theoretical analysis of alignment under mode-switching; experiments with correlated activation patterns.

**Open Question 2**  
- **Question**: Do depth-induced scaling and spectral separation hold in nonlinear networks (e.g., with ReLU) under standard training?  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Authors propose extending to simple non-linear models.  
- **Why unresolved**: Fixed-gates linear models may not capture nonlinear activation effects on Jacobian spectra.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Empirical measurement of Lyapunov exponents and spectral separation in trained MLPs/CNNs of varying depth; theoretical extension of product model to include nonlinearities.

**Open Question 3**  
- **Question**: Can singular-value dynamics be refined by incorporating first-order alignment corrections (Remark 6.3) instead of perfect alignment?  
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Authors suggest reworking Proposition 7.1 under more experimentally sound assumptions.  
- **Why unresolved**: The strong spectral separation assumption may be unrealistic at moderate depths; first-order corrections from Cholesky factors could improve accuracy.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Derivation of an updated dynamic with explicit correction terms; validation against simulation data for trained FGLNs; comparison of prediction errors.

**Open Question 4**  
- **Question**: At what effective depth does strong spectral separation emerge in random initializations and how does training affect it?  
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] Paper discusses depth scaling but does not quantify the depth needed for separation to become strong.  
- **Why unresolved**: Knowing the depth scale is necessary to apply the approximation regime in realistic architectures.  
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Measurement of spectral ratios (s_{i+1}/s_i) across depth at initialization and during training; mapping the transition to asymptotic regime.

## Limitations
- The analysis assumes a fixed-gates linear network, which approximates a single activation region and ignores pattern switching in real ReLU networks.
- Theoretical results are asymptotic (L→∞) and require gate rank concentration (r ≪ np); finite-depth corrections rely on Haar sampling approximations.
- Experimental details (optimizer, learning rate, augmentation specifics) are not fully reported, hindering exact replication.
