# Gauss-Newton Unlearning for the LLM Era

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.10568
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10568
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: K-FADE achieves state-of-the-art output suppression on WMDP and TOFU while substantially improving specificity (e.g., Alpaca KL divergence 2.9×10⁻² vs 5.3–6.7×10⁻² for prior methods), with computational cost comparable to first-order methods.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents K-FADE (K-FAC for Distribution Erasure), a novel second-order unlearning algorithm for large language models. K-FADE computes a few uphill Gauss-Newton steps using Kronecker-Factored Approximate Curvature (K-FAC/EK-FAC) to efficiently approximate the ideal retraining process. By preconditioning the forget-loss gradient with the retain-set Hessian, it simultaneously suppresses undesirable outputs and minimizes distributional shift on the retain set. Evaluations on the WMDP and TOFU benchmarks show that K-FADE matches prior art in forget-set suppression but achieves significantly better specificity, as measured by KL divergence on diverse instruction data. The method scales to 14B-parameter models with near-linear runtime and supports reapplying the unlearning update after subsequent fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
K-FADE formulates unlearning as optimizing a forget objective (e.g., negative margin loss) while bounding the KL divergence from the base model on a retain set. The solution involves a preconditioned gradient step using the Gauss-Newton Hessian `G`, approximated via K-FAC/EK-FAC. Each step: (1) fit a Kronecker-factorized approximation `G` on the retain set; (2) compute gradient `g` of the forget loss on the forget set; (3) solve for direction `r = (G + λI)^{-1}g`; (4) scale `r` by `α / ⟨g, r⟩` to control KL step size; (5) update model weights only on MLP up/down projections. The Hessian is refitted each step. The resulting parameter delta `Δθ` can be transferred to any fine-tuned variant by addition.

## Key Results
- On WMDP, K-FADE reduces Bio accuracy from 64.3% to ~30% with Alpaca KL of 2.9×10⁻², outperforming prior methods (5.3–6.7×10⁻²).
- On TOFU’s challenging 10% unlearning, one K-FADE step attains Forget Quality 0.85 and Model Utility 0.57, beating SimNPO and other baselines.
- K-FADE runs as fast as first-order approaches and enables cheap hyperparameter tuning via Hessian caching.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: A single preconditioned update (Gauss-Newton step) can simultaneously achieve output suppression on a forget set while minimally changing outputs on a retain set.
- **Mechanism**: The Gauss-Newton update `θ + η G⁻¹ ∇θ LF` uses the inverse Hessian `G⁻¹` (fitted on the retain set) to precondition the forget-loss gradient. This transforms the constrained multi-objective problem into an unconstrained step where the Hessian metric automatically respects the retain set’s geometry. Under local linearity of outputs and accurate Hessian, the step exactly approximately solves the Lagrangian.
- **Break condition**: Too large η or inaccurate Hessian degrades retain performance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: K-FAC/EK-FAC enables efficient and sufficiently accurate estimation of the Gauss-Newton Hessian for LLMs, making unlearning computationally feasible.
- **Mechanism**: For each linear layer, K-FAC approximates the Hessian block as a Kronecker product `A ⊗ S`, where `A` is the activation covariance and `S` the pseudogradient covariance. This factorization reduces storage and inversion cost to cubic in layer dimensions rather than model size. EK-FAC adds a diagonal `Λ` to correct eigenvalues.
- **Break condition**: Independence assumption fails for layers with strong activation-pseudogradient coupling; eigenvalue correction may be insufficient if eigenspaces differ.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The K-FADE update direction is transferable to fine-tuned models, allowing cheap maintenance of unlearning.
- **Mechanism**: After fine-tuning to `θ_ft`, one reapplies the original delta `Δθ = θ_unlearned - θ_base` as `θ_final = θ_ft + Δθ`. The unlearning direction (derived from retain/forget geometry) remains relevant under mild fine-tuning distribution shifts.
- **Break condition**: Full-rank fine-tuning on the forget set reduces unlearning; transfer is not a full defense.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: LLM unlearning as multi-objective optimization.
  - *Why needed*: Framing unlearning as maximizing forget-loss under a KL constraint clarifies the accuracy-specificity trade-off and motivates second-order methods.
  - *Quick check*: Why is it harder to preserve utility (specificity) than simply suppressing forget-set performance? Because gradient-based forgetting can overfit to the forget loss and damage unrelated capabilities.

- **Concept**: Natural gradient / Gauss-Newton method.
  - *Why needed*: The preconditioned update arises from a second-order expansion of the KL constraint; this explains why a single step can balance both objectives.
  - *Quick check*: How does a Gauss-Newton step differ from vanilla gradient ascent, and why does it better respect a KL-divergence constraint? (Answer: It incorporates the Fisher information metric of the retain distribution, which approximates the Riemannian metric induced by KL.)

- **Concept**: Kronecker-Factored Approximate Curvature (K-FAC).
  - *Why needed*: K-FAC is the computational linchpin that makes Hessian approximation practical for large models.
  - *Quick check*: Why does factorizing the Hessian as `A ⊗ S` reduce cost? Because the Kronecker product allows solving linear systems by inverting the small factors `A` and `S` separately, avoiding full Hessian inversion.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: 
  - Input: Base model θ_base, Forget set D_F, Retain set D_R
  → Hessian Estimator (K-FAC/EK-FAC on D_R) → G
  → Gradient Calculator (∇_θ LF on D_F) → g
  → Preconditioned Solver (r = (G+λI)^{-1} g) → r
  → Step Normalizer (scale r by α / ⟨g, r⟩) → Δθ
  → Update Applier (apply Δθ to targeted MLP layers) → θ_new
  → (Optional) Transfer: after fine-tuning θ_ft, θ_final = θ_ft + Δθ.

- **Critical path**:
  1. Load base model and define D_F and D_R.
  2. Fit K-FAC/EK-FAC on D_R to obtain G.
  3. Compute gradient g of forget loss on D_F.
  4. Solve for r via factorized inversion.
  5. Scale r to get Δθ.
  6. Apply Δθ to model weights.
  7. For multi-step: repeat steps 2–6 with refitted G.
  8. For maintenance after fine-tuning: add Δθ to fine-tuned model.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Hessian estimator: K-FAC (faster, less accurate) vs. EK-FAC (more accurate, slightly costlier); fitting on full retain vs. subset.
  - Loss choice: margin loss (less saturating) vs. cross-entropy (for approximating retraining).
  - Layer targeting: Updating all MLPs vs. fewer layers (controls strength vs. side effects).
  - Step size scaling: Normalizing by ⟨g, r⟩ stabilizes multi-step but requires solving for r each step.
  - Damping λ: Larger λ improves stability but weakens unlearning.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor specificity: excessive KL on Alpaca or MMLU. Likely due to inaccurate Hessian (too small retain set) or oversuppression.
  - Insufficient forgetting: high WMDP/TOFU accuracy. Likely due to too small α, too high λ, or poor gradient (CE saturation).
  - Numerical instability: NaNs or exploded updates. Increase λ, use EK-FAC, ensure float32 factors.
  - Fine-tuning attack vulnerability: unlearning effect decays quickly under full-rank fine-tuning on related data.

- **First three experiments**:
  1. **WMDP benchmark**: Measure suppression (Bio/Cyber accuracy) and specificity (MMLU, MT-Bench, Alpaca KL). K-FADE matches prior suppression with better KL.
  2. **TOFU benchmark**: Compute Forget Quality and Model Utility; single-step K-FADE attains SOTA Pareto front.
  3. **Hessian estimator ablation**: Compare K-FAC, EK-FAC, diagonal, and identity on TOFU 10% forget; plot Forget Quality vs. KL and runtime; K-FAC/EK-FAC dominate.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How should LLM unlearning benchmarks be redesigned to better reflect real-world deployment needs, particularly regarding functionality disruption and robustness to fine-tuning attacks? (The authors argue current benchmarks like WMDP’s random-guessing target are a poor proxy.)
2. To what extent can second-order unlearning methods like K-FADE scale to frontier model scales (>100B parameters) and different architectures (e.g., Mixture-of-Experts), and what are the practical computational bottlenecks? (Empirical evidence currently limited to 14B models.)
3. Can unlearning methods be made robust to post-unlearning fine-tuning attacks (i.e., “relearning”) without sacrificing specificity, or is this a fundamental trade-off? (All tested methods, including K-FADE, fail under full-rank fine-tuning; transfer helps but is not a full defense.)
4. Do distributional divergence metrics like KL divergence on general instruction datasets reliably correlate with preserved utility on downstream, deployment-relevant tasks, or are they insufficient proxies? (The paper uses Alpaca KL but notes may not reflect all utility aspects.)
5. How can unlearning be adapted for the traditional machine unlearning threat model where data providers revoke consent, especially when revoked data’s influence is entangled with the retain set? (The paper’s threat model assumes trainer-initiated forgetting; the revocation setting conflicts with utility preservation when forgotten data was useful.)

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters and layer selection for each benchmark are not fully disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- The Gauss-Newton linearity and Hessian approximation assumptions may break under large distribution shifts or aggressive fine-tuning, as noted in break conditions.
- Transferability of the unlearning direction is only shown for limited fine-tuning; its robustness to full-rank or multi-task updates remains unclear.
