# Evidence-Driven Reasoning for Industrial Maintenance Using Heterogeneous Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.08171
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08171
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Achieved high condition agreement (CAR up to 0.91) and very low unsupported claims (UCR ≤0.008) while reducing per-asset analysis time from 20–30 minutes to 15–30 seconds.

## Executive Summary
Industrial maintenance decisions are often hindered by fragmented evidence from work orders, sensors, and failure knowledge, leading to ungrounded alerts. This paper introduces the Condition Insight Agent, a two-stage pipeline that first constructs deterministic evidence by abstracting raw operational data into uniform behavioral summaries, then uses a constrained LLM guided by failure semantics and a rule-based verification loop to generate auditable insights. Deployed across approximately 1,500 assets, the system drastically reduces analysis time while preserving human oversight, demonstrating high compliance with operational rules and minimal unsupported claims.

## Method Summary
The system processes heterogeneous industrial data through a deterministic evidence construction stage. Raw meter readings are transformed into behavioral events (e.g., trends, resets) via statistical summarization, while work orders are analyzed with NLP to extract patterns. These, combined with Failure Modes and Effects Analysis (FMEA) knowledge, form a structured `asset_facts` packet. An LLM then reasons over this packet under strict prompt constraints that mandate alignment with the provided evidence and FMEA items. Finally, an independent rule-based verifier cross-checks the LLM’s overall condition classification against deterministic criteria, suppressing unsupported outputs. This separation ensures auditability and minimizes hallucination.

## Key Results
- Condition Agreement Rate (CAR) reached 0.91 under constrained prompting with full evidence.
- Unsupported Claim Rate (UCR) was ≤0.008, demonstrating effective suppression of unsupported LLM outputs.
- Analysis time per asset decreased from 20–30 minutes to 15–30 seconds, enabling scalable deployment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Deterministic Evidence Abstraction
The system converts heterogeneous raw data (meters, work orders) into uniform behavioral summaries (e.g., meter events, work-order patterns) via rule-based statistical and NLP methods. This `asset_facts` packet serves as the sole factual basis for LLM reasoning, eliminating schema misalignment and semantic ambiguity. Core assumption: Decision-critical information can be captured by interpretable abstractions. Evidence: Ablation studies show structured evidence reduces UCR from 0.007 to 0.003. Break condition: If essential failure semantics or temporal patterns cannot be expressed in summaries, the abstraction may omit critical evidence, leading to unsupported inferences.

### Mechanism 2: Failure Semantics Guardrails
FMEA-derived failure mechanisms bound the LLM’s hypothesis space. Using Unbalanced Optimal Transport, work-order patterns are semantically matched to FMEA items, and the LLM prompt restricts insights to these items. This prevents plausibly fluent but engineering-invalid explanations. Core assumption: A comprehensive FMEA library exists and semantic matching is reliable. Evidence: Constrained prompts improve CAR to 0.91. Break condition: Novel failure modes absent from FMEA will be systematically excluded, causing misclassification.

### Mechanism 3: Rule-Based Verification Loop
After LLM generation, an independent deterministic module applies hard-coded rules to the `asset_facts` and compares the result with the LLM’s condition classification. Discrepancies trigger revision or suppression, decoupling generative synthesis from governance. Core assumption: Deterministic rules are complete and correct. Evidence: Low UCR (≤0.008) under constrained prompts shows verification effectiveness. Break condition: Underspecified or contradictory rules lead to unstable verification, allowing invalid classifications or suppressing valid insights.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Failure Modes and Effects Analysis (FMEA) Structure**  
  Why needed: The constraint envelope and recommendation grounding rely on an asset-class-specific FMEA library. It provides the component-mechanism-action hierarchy that LLM outputs must adhere to.  
  Quick check: Given a FMEA entry linking "bearing" to "fatigue spall" with "replace bearing", would the LLM be allowed to recommend "increase lubrication" for increased vibration? No, unless also listed as an action for a matched mechanism.

- **Concept: Behavioral Signal Abstraction from Low-Frequency Meters**  
  Why needed: Raw CMMS meters are aggregated and sparse; the deterministic abstraction layer converts them into events (resets, anomalies). Misunderstanding this leads to misinterpreting `meter_facts`.  
  Quick check: For runtime meter values [100, 200, 300, 290] hours, what events are detected? A reset event at the last reading due to negative increment.

- **Concept: Constrained vs. Unconstrained LLM Prompting for Governance**  
  Why needed: Reliability comes from prompt-level constraints forcing adherence to evidence and FMEA items, not the LLM's innate knowledge.  
  Quick check: In the Constrained prompt, what if the LLM generates an insight referencing a failure mode not in `fmea_facts`? It violates prompt instructions; should be caught by verification or refined.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Sources (CMMS, IoT/SCADA, FMEA) -> Analytics & Evidence Construction (work-order NLP, meter abstraction, FMEA matching) -> Domain LLM Agent (reasoning over `asset_facts`) -> Deterministic Verifier (rule-based check) -> CMMS UI (display and feedback).
- **Critical path:** `asset_facts` creation → LLM reasoning → Verifier check → UI display. `asset_facts` quality is the root dependency.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - Governance vs. Expressivity: Strict FMEA constraints and verification ensure auditability but may suppress novel, valid failure hypotheses.  
  - Determinism vs. Adaptability: Fixed abstraction rules and prompts are brittle to new data schemas, requiring manual re-engineering.  
  - Human-in-the-loop placement: The system is advisory, not automated, limiting scalability gains to analysis time.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - High Unsupported Claim Rate (UCR) indicates incomplete `asset_facts` or ineffective prompt constraints.  
  - Low Condition Agreement Rate (CAR) suggests flawed deterministic rules or LLM ignoring constraints.  
  - High Mean Insight Count (MIC) with low High Specificity Rate (HSR) signals unconstrained reasoning.  
  - Practitioner override frequency >15% may indicate FMEA/library misalignment with field realities.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Evidence Scope Ablation: Test pipeline with (a) only work orders, (b) work orders + meters, (c) full evidence. Measure UCR, HSR, CAR. Expect: Adding structured evidence reduces UCR; adding FMEA improves CAR.  
  2. Prompt Constraint Test: Swap Constrained prompt for Naive prompt while keeping full `asset_facts`. Measure CAR and MIC. Expect: Naive prompt drops CAR and increases MIC.  
  3. Verification Loop Sensitivity: Disable the deterministic verifier. Measure UCR and contradiction rate (CR). Expect: UCR and CR increase sharply.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Handling missing FMEA coverage**: The system relies on FMEA to constrain reasoning and uses conservative "Not Enough Data" for sparse evidence. For novel assets or emerging failures absent from the FMEA library, the constrained LLM may systematically exclude plausible hypotheses, leading to false negatives. Resolution requires experiments with held-out failure modes to quantify this limitation.  
2. **Generalizing verification to fine-grained claims**: The verification loop currently only checks overall condition classification; finer-grained insights (e.g., component involvement) are audited by an LLM judge, limiting auditability. A formal reasoning layer (e.g., logic-based constraints) could automatically validate each claim against `asset_facts`.  
3. **Dependence on deterministic evidence quality**: The fixed abstraction heuristics (e.g., meter event thresholds, work-order pattern matching) may not generalize across diverse IoT/CMMS ecosystems with evolving schemas. Robustness to schema drift or novel meter semantics remains an open operational challenge.

## Limitations
- FMEA library completeness is critical; novel failures will be missed, limiting applicability to mature asset classes with well-curated knowledge bases.
- Deterministic rules are brittle to data schema changes, requiring manual re-engineering rather than adaptive learning.
- Human oversight scalability is unquantified; practitioner cognitive load for reviewing ~1,500 assets is unknown.
- Generalization across industries is uncertain due to domain-specific FMEA and rule dependencies.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Deterministic Evidence Abstraction): High – supported by empirical ablation and clear specification.
- Mechanism 2 (Failure Semantics Guardrails): Medium – CAR improvement is strong, but confidence tempered by unverified FMEA completeness assumption.
- Mechanism 3 (Rule-Based Verification): High – low UCR demonstrates effectiveness, though rule correctness remains a theoretical risk.

## Next Checks
1. **FMEA Coverage Stress Test**: Introduce synthetic novel failures into evidence streams. Measure false negative rate and ability to correctly flag unsupported claims for known modes.
2. **Rule Completeness & Contradiction Audit**: Systematically sample cases where verifier and LLM disagree. Audit deterministic rules to identify overlapping, missing, or contradictory criteria causing unstable verification.
3. **Human Override Impact Analysis**: Track practitioner overrides on a held-out asset set. Correlate override reasons (e.g., "missed novel pattern") with evidence packet characteristics to quantify operational cost of guardrails.
