# CRL-VLA: Continual Vision-Language-Action Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03445
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03445
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** CRL-VLA achieves state-of-the-art final average return (FAR) of 0.98 on single-task and 0.74 on multi-task continual learning on LIBERO, with minimal forgetting (BWT -0.02) and positive forward transfer (BWT 0.17).

## Executive Summary
CRL-VLA addresses the challenge of continual reinforcement learning for vision-language-action (VLA) models, where an agent must sequentially learn new robotic manipulation tasks without forgetting previously acquired skills. The core difficulty lies in balancing stability (retaining old knowledge) and plasticity (adapting to new tasks). The method identifies the magnitude of the goal-conditioned advantage as the key factor governing this trade-off and introduces a dual-critic architecture: a frozen goal-conditioned value (GCV) critic that anchors values from prior tasks and a trainable Monte Carlo (MC) critic for learning new tasks. This setup enables asymmetric regularization—constraining advantage on old tasks while permitting controlled growth on new ones—augmented with KL penalties to bound policy divergence. On the LIBERO benchmark, CRL-VLA substantially outperforms prior continual RL methods, achieving higher final average returns and significantly reduced forgetting.

## Method Summary
CRL-VLA performs continual post-training of a base VLA policy (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT) on a sequence of language-conditioned robotic manipulation tasks from LIBERO. For each new task, the agent collects on-policy trajectories and stores them in a replay buffer. The training objective combines standard PPO with three regularization terms: a KL divergence penalty against the old policy on replayed old-task data, a value-consistency loss that keeps the new value function close to the frozen GCV critic on old states, and an MC value loss that trains a fresh critic on new-task returns. The frozen GCV critic is inherited from the converged MC critic of the previous task. After convergence on the current task, the new MC critic is frozen to serve as the GCV for the next stage. This design theoretically bounds forgetting and adaptation via controlled advantage magnitudes.

## Key Results
- **Final Average Return (FAR):** 0.98 on single-task continual learning (vs. 0.96 best baseline) and 0.74 on multi-task (vs. 0.63), showing superior overall performance across tasks.
- **Backward Transfer (BWT):** -0.02 (single-task) and 0.17 (multi-task), indicating minimal catastrophic forgetting and even positive transfer in the multi-task setting.
- **Ablation:** Removing the GCV component significantly degrades performance, confirming its necessity; performance is sensitive to loss weighting, with optimal values identified (e.g., β_V ≈ 0.01).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Advantage Magnitude Governs the Stability-Plasticity Trade-off
The stability-plasticity trade-off in continual VLA learning is governed by the magnitude of the goal-conditioned advantage. Define advantage magnitude as \(M_g(\pi_\kappa) = \sup_{(s,a)\in \supp(d^{\pi_\kappa}_g)} |A^\pi(s,a)|\). Using the goal-conditioned performance difference lemma, the performance change on old tasks is bounded by \(\frac{2\gamma}{(1-\gamma)^2} M_{old} D_{old}\) and on new tasks by \(\frac{1}{1-\gamma} M_{new} D_{new}\), where \(D_{old}\) and \(D_{new}\) are KL-based policy divergences under respective state-occupancy distributions. Controlling \(M_{old}\) and \(M_{new}\) directly regulates forgetting and adaptation.  
**Core assumption:** The performance difference lemma holds for goal-conditioned MDPs; advantage magnitudes computed under the visiting policy accurately reflect potential performance shifts.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "identifies goal‑conditioned advantage magnitude as the key factor"; Section 4.1 defines advantage magnitude and Theorem 4.1 derives the unified bounds.  
**Break condition:** If the critic approximation error on old tasks becomes large, \(M_{old}\) may increase, violating the stability bound and causing forgetting. If \(M_{new}\) is underestimated (e.g., by biased MC returns), the agent may not fully exploit allowed adaptation, reducing plasticity.

### Mechanism 2: Dual-Critic Architecture Enables Asymmetric Regulation
A dual-critic architecture with a frozen goal-conditioned value (GCV) critic and a trainable Monte Carlo (MC) critic enables independent control of \(M_{old}\) and \(M_{new}\). At each task transition, the converged MC critic from the previous stage is frozen as \(\theta_{GCV}\). During new-task training, backbone features are fed to both \(\theta_{GCV}\) (fixed) and a fresh MC critic \(\theta_{MC}\). The frozen critic enforces value consistency on old-task states through a consistency loss, while the MC critic learns accurate returns on the new task. This decoupling allows asymmetric regulation: constraint on old-task advantage via \(\theta_{GCV}\) and adaptation via \(\theta_{MC}\).  
**Core assumption:** The frozen \(\theta_{GCV}\) reliably anchors prior value semantics; backbone updates do not degrade reference values beyond allowable error when regularized. The MC critic fitted on bounded yields accurate \(M_{new}\).  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "dual‑critic architecture … a frozen critic anchors semantic consistency and a trainable estimator drives adaptation"; Section 4.3 details the infeasibility of direct advantage optimization and the dual-critic design.  
**Break condition:** If backbone updates cause \(\theta_{GCV}\) predictions to drift (high \(L_{V}^{GCV}\)), the bound on \(M_{old}\) loosens, causing forgetting. If MC critic is poorly trained, \(M_{new}\) may be inaccurate, leading to overly conservative or unstable updates.

### Mechanism 3: Asymmetric Regularization Implements Theoretical Bounds
The total training objective combines PPO surrogate, KL divergence penalty \(L_{KL}\) (bounding \(D_{old}\)), frozen-critic value-consistency loss \(L_{V}^{GCV}\) (bounding \(\varepsilon_V\) and thus \(M_{old}\)), and MC value loss \(L_{V}^{MC}\) (bounding \(M_{new}\)). Hyperparameters \(\alpha, \beta_V, \eta\) act as Lagrange multipliers balancing constraints. This implements the theoretical bounds from Mechanism 1 in practice, harmonizing stability and plasticity.  
**Core assumption:** The Lagrangian relaxation accurately reflects the constrained optimization; individual loss terms are valid surrogates for the theoretical quantities \(D_{old}\), \(D_{new}\), \(M_{old}\), \(M_{new}\).  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes "augmented with KL regularization to bound policy divergence"; Section 4.4 presents \(L_{total} = L_{PPO} + \alpha L_{KL} + \beta_V L_{V}^{GCV} + \eta L_{V}^{MC}\).  
**Break condition:** Poor weight choices cause imbalance: too small \(\alpha\) weakens \(D_{old}\) bound, increasing forgetting; too large \(\alpha\) narrows trust region, harming plasticity. Similarly, \(\beta_V\) too low fails to control \(M_{old}\), too high over-constrains backbone; extreme \(\eta\) impairs MC critic accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Goal-Conditioned Value Functions:** VLA policies must condition both value and action predictions on language goals to distinguish tasks and apply the dual-critic architecture correctly.  
  *Quick check:* How does conditioning the value head on language embeddings help maintain task-specific value semantics during continual learning?
- **Advantage Magnitude:** It is the key quantity linking policy divergence to performance change; the theoretical bounds and subsequent regularization design revolve around controlling it.  
  *Quick check:* If the advantage magnitude on an old task is zero for all visited states, what does that imply about the effect of policy updates on that task?
- **KL Regularization and Trust Region:** KL divergence measures policy change; bounding it directly limits \(D_{old}\) and \(D_{new}\) in the theoretical bounds, preventing catastrophic forgetting.  
  *Quick check:* Why is it necessary to apply the KL penalty on old-task data rather than only on the new-task distribution?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Shared VLA backbone φ (vision-language encoder) → Action head ω  
  Two critics: frozen GCV θ_GCV (from previous task) and trainable MC θ_MC  
  Replay buffers: B_old (old tasks), B_new (current rollouts)  
  Optimizer: PPO with losses L_PPO, L_KL, L_V^GCV, L_V^MC
- **Critical path:**  
  1. For task T_k: collect trajectories τ using current policy π_θ.  
  2. Sample mini-batches from B_new (from τ) and B_old.  
  3. Compute PPO advantage (e.g., GAE) and L_PPO.  
  4. Compute L_KL on (s,g) ~ B_old w.r.t. old policy π_old.  
  5. Compute L_V^GCV using frozen θ_GCV on B_old.  
  6. Compute L_V^MC on B_new with Monte Carlo returns.  
  7. Sum L_total and perform gradient descent on θ (updates φ, ω, θ_MC only; θ_GCV frozen).  
  8. After convergence on T_k, freeze π_θ and copy θ_MC to θ_GCV for next task.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - V-based vs Q-based GCV: V-based marginalizes actions, more robust to action diversity; Q-based may over-constrain old actions.  
  - Loss weight selection: β_V controls strength of old-value anchoring; α (KL) adjusts trust region width; η adjusts MC critic accuracy.  
  - MC return horizon: full Monte Carlo simplifies theory but may increase variance; n-step alternatives trade bias/variance.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Catastrophic forgetting (low BWT, high forgetting F) indicates M_old not controlled; check L_V^GCV loss magnitude.  
  - Stagnant plasticity (low FAR, negative FT) suggests over-regularization; monitor average KL divergence on B_old.  
  - High MC value loss or exploding returns imply poor critic training, destabilizing updates.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Single-task continual learning (Task-1 split): FAR 0.98, BWT -0.02.  
  2. Multi-task continual learning (Task-2 split): FAR 0.74, BWT 0.17.  
  3. Ablation study (Task-4 splits): Systemic variation of loss weights shows GCV component necessity and optimal weight ranges (e.g., β_V≈0.01, α≈1e-5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can CRL-VLA be extended to handle partial observability and non-stationary language goals?**  
  *Basis:* Conclusion states extension to "partial observability and non-stationary language goals" as future work.  
  *Why unresolved:* Current method assumes fully observable states and fixed instructions; real-world agents face noise, missing info, dynamic goals.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Introduce belief-state or recurrent modules; evaluate on benchmarks with occlusion or delayed goal disclosure; show improvements over baselines.
- **Can dependence on hand‑tuned loss weights be eliminated via adaptive regularization?**  
  *Basis:* Inferred from ablation sensitivity; weights fixed without adaptation.  
  *Why unresolved:* Manual tuning is brittle and may not generalize across task sequences.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Develop meta-optimizer or gradient-based schedule adjusting weights online based on validation metrics; show comparable performance without extensive grid search.
- **How valid are the theoretical bounds when applied to high‑dimensional VLA policies?**  
  *Basis:* Inferred from Theorem 4.1 assumptions (bounded rewards, uniform critic error) not empirically probed.  
  *Why unresolved:* Tightness and practical relevance unclear; assumptions may be violated in practice.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Stress-test with extreme reward scales or large policy updates; compare actual forgetting/plasticity against predicted bounds; derive relaxed, data‑dependent bounds.
- **Does integrating CRL‑VLA’s asymmetric regulation with other continual learning techniques yield further scalability?**  
  *Basis:* Inferred from introduction’s critique of replay/architecture methods; current design uses limited old data but no full replay or network expansion.  
  *Why unresolved:* May not scale to very long sequences or large state spaces; hybrid strategies could help.  
  *What evidence would resolve:* Implement variants with full replay or progressive networks while preserving dual‑critic core; evaluate on extended LIBERO or new benchmarks; compare against pure CRL‑VLA and hybrid baselines.

## Limitations
- Replay buffer configuration (size, sampling strategy, overwriting) is unspecified, potentially affecting stability.
- Hyperparameter ranges for loss weights (α, β_V, η) lack comprehensive sweeps across all task sequences; optimal values may vary.
- Evaluation protocol details (episode count, frequency, deterministic vs stochastic actions) are missing, hindering metric reproducibility.
- Theoretical bounds assume accurate advantage magnitude estimation; approximation errors could violate assumptions.
- Long-term accumulation of value errors in the frozen GCV critic may degrade anchoring over many tasks.

## Confidence
- Advantage magnitude governs stability-plasticity: Medium (theoretically sound but practical approximation uncertain)
- Dual-critic architecture enables asymmetric regulation: Medium (empirical success but sensitivity to freezing/training details unclear)
- Asymmetric regularization implements bounds: Medium (ablation supports necessity but weight tuning critical and not fully characterized)
