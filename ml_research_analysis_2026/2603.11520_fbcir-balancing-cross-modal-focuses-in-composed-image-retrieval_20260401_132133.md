# FBCIR: Balancing Cross-Modal Focuses in Composed Image Retrieval
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.11520
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11520
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: FBCIR introduces a focus interpretation technique and a hard-negative data augmentation workflow that reduces cross-modal focus imbalance and improves robustness in composed image retrieval, yielding up to +9.8% hard-case recall gain and up to 0.55 reduction in focus imbalance metric while maintaining standard benchmark performance.

## Executive Summary
Composed Image Retrieval (CIR) requires joint reasoning over an image and a modifying text to retrieve a target image. However, models often exploit shortcuts by over-relying on one modality, leading to failures on hard negatives aligned with the neglected modality. The paper identifies this as focus imbalance. To address it, they propose FBCIR, which includes (1) a focus interpretation method using iterative token pruning to quantify each modality's contribution to retrieval decisions, and (2) a data augmentation workflow that automatically generates targeted hard negatives—text-augmented (image-similar but text-inconsistent) and image-augmented (text-consistent but image-different)—to force balanced cross-modal integration. Experiments across multiple CIR models (CLIP-based and VLM-based) show that finetuning on the augmented data substantially improves performance on a new hard-case benchmark (FBCIR-Data) and reduces focus imbalance, while maintaining or slightly improving standard CIR benchmarks.

## Method Summary
FBCIR comprises two synergistic components. The focus interpretation module quantifies modality contributions: images are segmented into tokens via SAM-ViT, text split into words. Using iterative token pruning with beam search, it removes non-critical tokens while preserving the original ranking; the surviving minimal token sets define indispensable tokens, from which focus ratios rI (image) and rT (text) are computed and imbalance measured as |rI−rT|. The data augmentation workflow constructs hard negatives that specifically challenge imbalanced focuses. For each training triplet, the Qwen3-VL model produces (a) altered instructions that keep visual similarity but alter text to yield text-augmented negatives (penalizing image over-focus), and (b) a fused description of query and target to drive Qwen-Image-Edit and Qwen-Image, generating image-augmented negatives (penalizing text over-focus). The resulting FBCIR-Data replaces easy negatives. Models are finetuned with a contrastive loss, optionally via LoRA for efficiency, and a small distillation term to retain general knowledge. The overall pipeline: raw CIR data → tokenization → focus diagnosis (optional) → VLM-based hard negative generation → augmented dataset → finetuning → robust CIR model.

## Key Results
- Hard-case recall on FBCIR-Data improves significantly (e.g., +9.8% Rs@1 for GME-7B, +6.5% for MM-Embed-7B) after finetuning with augmented data.
- Focus imbalance metric |rI−rT| decreases by up to 0.55, confirming reduced over-reliance on a single modality.
- Standard CIR benchmarks (CIRR, FashionIQ, GeneCIS) are maintained or slightly improved, demonstrating that robustness gains do not sacrifice general retrieval ability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Shortcut learning due to easy negatives
- **Claim**: CIR models tend to over-rely on one modality because standard training data contain easy negatives that can be distinguished using only one input.
- **Mechanism**: In contrastive training, if a negative candidate is semantically distant from the target along one modality, the model can achieve correct retrieval by focusing predominantly on the other modality. This provides a training signal that does not enforce balanced integration, encouraging shortcut behaviors.
- **Core assumption**: Prevalence of easy negatives permits single-modality reliance without penalty.
- **Evidence**: Paper abstracts and Section 1 describe shortcuts; prior works also note modality bias in CIR.
- **Break condition**: If all negatives require joint reasoning (each negative is consistent with exactly one modality), single-modality reliance fails, forcing balanced integration.

### Mechanism 2: Precise focus interpretation via iterative token pruning
- **Claim**: The FBCIR interpretation method can identify indispensable image regions and text tokens.
- **Mechanism**: Starting with the full query, the method repeatedly removes tokens (by masking image regions or replacing text words) and retains states that preserve the original ranking via beam search. Minimal surviving token sets across beams reveal the model's essential focus.
- **Core assumption**: Model decisions are stable under removal of non-critical tokens.
- **Evidence**: Section 3.1 details the process and its guarantee of indispensability.
- **Break condition**: Highly sensitive models (e.g., adversarial) may yield unstable minimal sets.

### Mechanism 3: Hard-negative augmentation forces balanced cross-modal reasoning
- **Claim**: Training with targeted hard negatives mitigates focus imbalance and improves robustness.
- **Mechanism**: The augmentation creates two types of negatives: text-augmented (visually similar but text-inconsistent) to penalize over-attending to image; image-augmented (text-consistent but visually different) to penalize over-attending to text. Exposure to these forces the model to leverage both modalities.
- **Core assumption**: Model capacity is sufficient to learn from the harder examples.
- **Evidence**: Section 5.3 and Table 4 show improved hard-case recall and reduced |rI−rT| after finetuning.
- **Break condition**: Poor-quality negatives (unrealistic edits) could harm performance or cause distribution shift.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input (CIR dataset) → Tokenization (SAM segmentation, text tokenization) → Focus Interpretation Module (iterative pruning to compute focus ratios) → Data Augmentation Module (VLM-driven hard negative generation) → CIR Model (finetuned with augmented data) → Output (robust retrieval and focus metrics)

**Critical path**:
- For diagnosis: Given a CIR model and validation set, run iterative pruning on each sample → compute focus ratios → aggregate imbalance.
- For augmentation: For each triplet, use VLM to produce text-augmented and image-augmented negatives → construct new candidate pools → output augmented dataset.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Token granularity: Finer segmentation increases precision but quadratically raises computational cost; coarser tokens may miss subtle focuses.
- Hard negative quality: Using powerful VLMs yields more realistic negatives but is slower and costlier; simpler rule-based edits are cheaper but less diverse.
- Augmentation ratio: Higher proportions improve robustness but risks degrading standard benchmarks if the model overfits to the harder distribution.
- Weighting scheme: Area-based weights for image tokens vs. uniform for text is heuristic; more principled importance estimation (e.g., attention) may improve metrics but adds complexity.

**Failure signatures**:
- Interpretation: Extremely high runtime suggests token count too large or beam width insufficient; inconsistent focus ratios across similar samples indicate instability.
- Augmentation: If finetuning yields large drops on standard benchmarks (e.g., CIRR R@1), the hard negatives may be out-of-distribution or too difficult, causing catastrophic forgetting.
- Overall: No reduction in |rI−rT| after augmentation suggests either augmentation ineffective or model capacity insufficient to learn from the harder data.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Baseline diagnosis**: Run FBCIR interpretation on pretrained CIR models using the FBCIR-Data benchmark. Record focus imbalance (|rI−rT|) and hard-case recall (Rs@1). Expect high imbalance and low Rs@1.
2. **Augmentation sensitivity**: Finetune a VLM-based model (e.g., GME-7B) with varying proportions (0%, 50%, 100%) of FBCIR-augmented data. Measure changes in standard benchmark recall, FBCIR-Data Rs@1, and focus imbalance. Expect monotonic improvement in hard-case metrics and imbalance reduction, with possible trade-off at 100% on standard benchmarks.
3. **Generalization test**: Apply the identical augmentation strategy used for training to the CIRR dataset to create FBCIR-CIRR. Evaluate zero-shot performance of the finetuned model from Experiment 2 on this new hard-case benchmark. Expect consistent gains, confirming transferability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can the heuristic token weighting (area-based for images, uniform for text) in FBCIR's focus balance metric be replaced with more principled strategies that better capture cross-modal semantic dependencies?
- **Basis**: Section 6 explicitly states current weights are intuitive approximations that may introduce biases and do not model fine-grained semantic interactions.
- **Why unresolved**: The reliance on heuristics limits metric interpretability and may not generalize to samples with atypical token distributions or complex interplay.
- **Evidence to resolve**: Propose and validate alternative weighting schemes (e.g., using attention maps, gradient-based saliency, or learned importance scores) that correlate more strongly with human annotations of model focus or with performance on targeted hard-case subsets.

### Open Question 2
Can the computational complexity of FBCIR's iterative token pruning be significantly reduced while preserving its ability to identify minimal multimodal focuses?
- **Basis**: Section 6 acknowledges the method's relatively high time complexity due to multiple model inferences and notes it is not optimized for large-scale use.
- **Why unresolved**: Quadratic growth with token count makes it prohibitive for diagnosing large datasets or real-time applications, restricting its utility to small-scale analyses.
- **Evidence to resolve**: Developing efficient approximations (e.g., single-pass attribution, learned surrogate models, or adaptive pruning schedules) and demonstrating strong correlation with original FBCIR outputs and similar diagnostic insights at lower computational cost.

### Open Question 3
Why do CLIP-based CIR models show limited improvement or even degradation on standard benchmarks after FBCIR-Data finetuning, whereas VLM-based models consistently gain robustly?
- **Basis**: Section E.2 reports that CLIP-based models exhibit smaller gains or drops on standard benchmarks compared to consistent improvements by VLM-based models, with hypothesized reasons (benchmark objectives, model capacity, finetuning constraints) not empirically disentangled.
- **Why unresolved**: The root cause—whether due to architectural limitations, pretraining distribution mismatch, or overly aggressive shortcut reliance—remains unclear, hindering targeted improvements for CLIP-based architectures.
- **Evidence to resolve**: Controlled experiments isolating factors like fusion module design, pretraining data diversity, or finetuning schedules to pinpoint why CLIP-based models trade off general performance for hard-case robustness less effectively than VLMs.

### Open Question 4
Are the current FBCIR-Data construction strategies (image-augmented negatives, filtering original positives) sufficient to teach models fine-grained distinctions on hard negatives that are semantically balanced but weakly consistent with the positive?
- **Basis**: Appendix A Question #2 discusses that even models with balanced focuses may fail on such cases, and FBCIR-Data includes mechanisms to target them, but the paper does not quantitatively isolate their effectiveness.
- **Why unresolved**: It is unclear whether additional negative generation techniques (e.g., more subtle edits, multimodal adversarial examples) or training protocols are needed to close this remaining robustness gap.
- **Evidence to resolve**: Constructing a dedicated evaluation set of balanced-but-weak hard negatives and measuring incremental gains from each FBCIR-Data component, potentially correlating improvements with focus balance metric reductions.

## Limitations
- Reproduction depends on proprietary Qwen models (Qwen3-VL, Qwen-Image-Edit, Qwen-Image); using alternative VLMs may yield different hard-negative quality and downstream results.
- Token pruning assumes stable decision boundaries under removal of non-essential tokens; for complex models, minimal sets may be unstable or inconsistent across runs.
- Correlation between reduced focus imbalance (|rI−rT|) and improved hard-case recall does not prove causation; confounding factors like overall representation quality could contribute.

## Confidence
- Focus imbalance exists in CIR models: **Medium**
- FBCIR interpretation accurately captures indispensable tokens: **Medium**
- Augmentation with FBCIR-Data mitigates imbalance and improves robustness: **High**
