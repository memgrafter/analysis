# Predictive Associative Memory: Retrieval Beyond Similarity Through Temporal Co-occurrence

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.11322
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11322
*   **Reference Count:** 25
*   **Primary Result:** PAM achieves 97% precision for top retrieval and successfully retrieves temporally co-occurring states across representational boundaries where cosine similarity is zero.

## Executive Summary
Current neural memory systems rely on embedding similarity for retrieval, which fails to capture associations formed purely by temporal co-occurrence without featural resemblance. This paper introduces Predictive Associative Memory (PAM), which uses an Inward JEPA predictor trained on temporal co-occurrence to navigate associative structure. On a synthetic benchmark, PAM achieves high precision (AP@1=0.970) and significant cross-boundary recall (CBR@20=0.421) where similarity methods fail. Ablations confirm the mechanism's dependence on genuine temporal order and its anchor-specific, episodic character.

## Method Summary
The method trains an Inward predictor on a fixed synthetic world of 20 room clusters in 128D space. Composite states are multimodal embeddings from 500 trajectories of 100 timesteps. Training uses all within-window (τ=5) temporal co-occurrence pairs (242,264 pairs) with a contrastive InfoNCE loss and in-batch negatives. The predictor is a 4-layer MLP that maps a query state to a predicted point ẑ; retrieval finds stored states near ẑ. Evaluation uses macro-averaged recall metrics on a fixed query set, with held-out anchor tests to confirm episodic fidelity.

## Key Results
*   Association Precision@1 (AP@1) = 0.970.
*   Cross-Boundary Recall@20 (CBR@20) = 0.421, retrieving associates from different rooms despite zero cosine similarity.
*   Discrimination AUC = 0.916 overall, 0.849 cross-room.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Temporal Co-occurrence as the Primitive
*   **Claim:** A predictor trained on temporal co-occurrence retrieves states experienced together, even when representationally dissimilar.
*   **Mechanism:** The Inward JEPA maps a query state s(t) to a predicted point ẑ(t) trained (via contrastive loss) to be near embeddings of temporally neighboring states s(t±τ). Retrieval finds stored states within a threshold ϵ of ẑ(t).
*   **Evidence Anchors:** The paper states the predictor learns to map to a region containing temporal neighbors (Section 3.4). A temporal shuffle control collapses cross-boundary recall by ~90%, proving reliance on temporal order (Section 5.2).
*   **Break Condition:** Destroying temporal structure (e.g., shuffling trajectories) causes performance to collapse.

### Mechanism 2: Anchor-Specific Episodic Recall
*   **Claim:** The Inward predictor recalls associations only from query states it experienced as anchors during training.
*   **Mechanism:** The predictor learns a mapping g_ϕ(s_q) specific to the training anchor distribution. A held-out query (never an anchor) yields zero cross-boundary recall because the predictor has not learned to route from that novel input.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** Held-out query evaluation shows near-zero cross-boundary recall (Section 5.3), consistent with faithful memorisation of experienced viewpoints.
*   **Break Condition:** High recall for held-out queries would indicate generalisation/hallucination, violating the recall objective.

### Mechanism 3: Multi-hop Creative Bridging (Theoretical)
*   **Claim:** Iterated application of the Inward predictor can transitively bridge across separate experience trajectories if entities persist.
*   **Mechanism:** If trajectory 1 contains A→B and trajectory 2 contains B→C (persistent entity B), then from A: hop 1 reaches B, hop 2 from B reaches C, linking A to C despite no direct co-occurrence.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** The mechanism is described (Section 3.7), but the empirical test failed because the synthetic world had no cross-trajectory edges (Section 5.5).
*   **Break Condition:** Without overlapping temporal neighborhoods (shared entities), the association graph has no cross-episode edges, preventing bridging.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)**
    *   *Why needed:* PAM’s Inward predictor is a JEPA applied to temporal associates instead of futures. Understanding its stop-gradient/EMA mechanism prevents representational collapse.
    *   *Quick Check:* Why does JEPA use a stop-gradient on the target encoder, and what fails without it?
*   **Concept: Recall vs. Retrieval Evaluation Paradigm**
    *   *Why needed:* PAM evaluates faithful memorisation of *experienced* associations (recall), not generalisation to unseen ones (retrieval). A train/test gap is expected and correct.
    *   *Quick Check:* Why is a large gap between train and held-out *association* performance expected for an associative memory?
*   **Concept: Temporal Co-occurrence as a Primitive Signal**
    *   *Why needed:* This unsupervised signal captures associations with no featural similarity, distinguishing PAM from similarity-based methods.
    *   *Quick Check:* Give an example of a useful association missed by embedding similarity but captured by temporal co-occurrence.

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:** An encoder f_θ maps multimodal inputs to composite state s(t). An Inward predictor g_ϕ (4-layer MLP) maps s(t) to predicted point ẑ(t). A memory bank stores all past embeddings s_i (with stop-gradient ¯s_i). Retrieval finds s_i where distance(ẑ, ¯s_i) < ϵ (or top-k). Training uses a contrastive InfoNCE loss over fixed temporal co-occurrence pairs with in-batch negatives.
*   **Critical Path:** The system depends on the predictor learning to map a query to the centroid of its temporal neighborhood. This requires distinct embeddings for different contexts, a predictor with sufficient capacity, and a retrieval threshold that captures the neighborhood without excessive noise.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   Fixed vs. online pairs: Fixed pairs improve recall fidelity but risk overfitting; online sampling misaligns with the recall objective.
    *   Single-point vs. distributional prediction: A single ẑ is simple but may lack precision; predicting a distribution could improve precision@k.
    *   Threshold ϵ vs. top-k: Fixed ϵ is sensitive to predictor calibration; top-k is more robust for evaluation but requires setting k.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   Collapse to similarity: Top retrievals align with nearest neighbors in embedding space (checked via temporal shuffle control).
    *   Low AP@1: Predictor fails to learn a sharp mapping to the associate neighborhood (capacity/training issue).
    *   Held-out query success: System generalises beyond memorisation, indicating hallucination/shortcut learning.
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  Baseline replication: Train as described (fixed pairs, InfoNCE, cosine LR) and verify AP@1 ≈ 0.97, CBR@20 > 0.4.
    2.  Temporal shuffle ablation: Permute timesteps within trajectories; verify CBR@20 drops by ~90% (to ~0.04).
    3.  Held-out anchor evaluation: Train on a subset of anchors; evaluate on held-out anchors; verify CBR@20 ≈ 0.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **How does combining Outward (similarity) and Inward (association) predictors produce episodic specificity?** The authors hypothesise specificity arises from the intersection of the two channels, but only the Inward predictor has been evaluated. Evidence would require training a dual-channel system and testing episode-specific queries.
*   **Does entity persistence enable creative recombination via transitive bridging?** The test failed in the current synthetic world due to lack of persistent entities across trajectories. Evidence would require a world with overlapping entities and demonstration of multi-hop retrieval to remote associates.
*   **How does joint learning of the embedding space affect performance and scalability?** Current work uses fixed synthetic embeddings; joint training with an encoder on raw data could change the associative structure and is needed for real-world application.

## Limitations
*   Query set construction and seeds are unspecified, risking evaluation mismatch.
*   The mapping between retrieval threshold ϵ (architecture) and top-k (evaluation) is unclear.
*   Stop-gradient/EMA usage in the predictor-only setting is ambiguous; a joint-encoder variant may be necessary for generality.
*   Generalisability beyond the controlled synthetic 20-room environment is unproven.

## Confidence
*   **Primary claim (AP@1=0.970, CBR@20=0.421)**: Medium (strong metrics and controls, but independent reproduction required).
*   **Mechanism 1 (temporal co-occurrence as primitive)**: Medium-High (supported by 90% drop in shuffle control).
*   **Mechanism 2 (anchor-specific recall)**: High (held-out query experiment shows near-zero recall).
*   **Mechanism 3 (multi-hop creative bridging)**: Low (theoretical but empirically unproven in this environment).
