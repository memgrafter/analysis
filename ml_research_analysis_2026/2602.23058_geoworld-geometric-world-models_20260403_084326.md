# GeoWorld: Geometric World Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23058
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23058
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** GeoWorld improves success rate by ~3% on 3-step and ~2% on 4-step goal-conditioned visual planning over V-JEPA 2, with consistent gains across model scales and superior performance on longer horizons (up to T=8).

## Executive Summary
GeoWorld addresses key limitations of existing energy-based predictive world models that rely on Euclidean latent spaces, which fail to capture geometric and hierarchical structure and degrade over long-horizon planning. It introduces two main components: Hyperbolic JEPA, which maps latent representations from Euclidean space onto a hyperbolic manifold using an exponential map, preserving geometry and enabling geodesic-aware dynamics; and Geometric Reinforcement Learning (GRL), which optimizes the predictor via hyperbolic energy minimization and triangle-inequality regularization to ensure geodesic-consistent rollouts. Evaluated on CrossTask and COIN for goal-conditioned visual planning, GeoWorld demonstrates superior performance, particularly on longer planning horizons.

## Method Summary
GeoWorld builds upon V-JEPA 2 by projecting the encoder's Euclidean latent vectors onto a hyperbolic manifold (Poincaré ball) via a differentiable exponential map, with a learnable curvature parameter. The action-conditioned predictor operates entirely in this hyperbolic space, learning transitions that minimize hyperbolic geodesic distances. Training proceeds in two stages: first, a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage minimizes one-step and two-step hyperbolic prediction errors; second, a Geometric RL (GRL) stage reformulates prediction as a reinforcement learning problem, where the negative hyperbolic distance serves as reward. A triangle-inequality regularization term is added to the GRL loss to enforce that multi-step rollouts remain consistent with hyperbolic geodesics. At inference, a Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) planner samples action sequences to minimize the hyperbolic distance between the predicted final state and the encoded goal state.

## Key Results
- Outperforms state-of-the-art V-JEPA 2 by approximately 3% in success rate for 3-step planning and 2% for 4-step planning on CrossTask and COIN.
- Achieves consistent improvements across different model scales.
- Exhibits superior stability and performance on longer horizons (up to T=8), where baseline methods degrade more significantly.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Hyperbolic JEPA preserves geometric and hierarchical structure in latent space by mapping Euclidean encoder outputs onto a hyperbolic manifold and learning dynamics along hyperbolic geodesics.
**Mechanism:** The frozen encoder produces a Euclidean latent, treated as a tangent vector at the origin and projected onto the hyperbolic manifold via the exponential map `exp₀`. The predictor then operates on these hyperbolic states, learning transitions that minimize the hyperbolic geodesic distance between predicted and true next states. This aligns the learned energy landscape with the manifold's intrinsic geometry.
**Core assumption:** The hierarchical, tree-like branching of multi-step futures is more efficiently encoded in hyperbolic space due to its exponential volume growth and tree-like metrics.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.2 describe the mapping and hyperbolic predictor operation. Neighbor literature on hyperbolic RL supports the use of hyperbolic geometry for hierarchical reasoning, though not the specific JEPA integration.
**Break condition:** If the task's transitions are inherently linear or Euclidean (e.g., simple kinematic control), the hyperbolic mapping adds complexity without benefit and may destabilize training.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Geometric Reinforcement Learning (GRL) refines the predictor's energy-based representation to produce geodesic-consistent rollouts, improving long-horizon stability without training a separate policy or reward model.
**Mechanism:** GRL redefines one-step prediction error as a negative reward. The predictor is optimized to maximize the cumulative discounted reward (i.e., minimize total hyperbolic distance) over a horizon. A triangle-inequality regularization term `L∆` enforces that the sum of consecutive step distances is not less than the direct skip-step distance, aligning rollouts with hyperbolic geodesics.
**Core assumption:** The optimal planning trajectory on the hyperbolic manifold corresponds to a geodesic path; enforcing the triangle inequality pushes learned dynamics toward such paths.
**Evidence anchors:** Section 3.4 defines the RL objective and `L∆`. Section 4.5/Table 3 shows less degradation on long horizons with GRL. Neighbor work on hyperbolic RL supports the viability of hyperbolic value optimization.
**Break condition:** If the optimal trajectory in the latent space is not a geodesic (e.g., due to obstacles), the regularization could force suboptimal or infeasible plans.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The combination of hyperbolic latent dynamics (H-JEPA) and GRL enables geometry-aware, stable energy-based planning via CEM, leading to superior long-horizon success rates.
**Mechanism:** During inference, CEM samples action sequences and evaluates them using an energy cost defined as the hyperbolic distance between the predictor's imagined final state and the encoded goal state. Because the predictor has been shaped by supervised and reinforcement losses to output geodesic rollouts, the CEM search efficiently discovers action sequences that follow low-energy paths in the curved latent space.
**Core assumption:** The CEM optimization objective (minimizing hyperbolic distance) is well-aligned with the predictor's representation, which has been trained to produce geodesic-consistent sequences.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 4.5/Table 3 report improved success rates across horizons, particularly longer ones.
**Break condition:** If CEM's sampling or iterations are insufficient to explore the highly non-linear hyperbolic geometry, it may fail to find geodesic minima even with a well-trained predictor.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)**  
  **Why needed:** GeoWorld extends V-JEPA 2; understanding JEPA's principle—predicting future latent representations without pixel generation—is key to seeing why GeoWorld operates in latent space and uses an energy (distance) cost.  
  **Quick check:** What is the key difference between a generative video model and a JEPA-based world model in terms of what they predict and plan over?
- **Concept: Hyperbolic Geometry (Poincaré Ball Model)**  
  **Why needed:** The entire mechanism depends on hyperbolic properties: geodesics, exponential/log maps, triangle inequality. Knowing how distances (`d_H`) and projections (`exp₀`) compute explains the training and planning losses.  
  **Quick check:** In the Poincaré ball, why does distance increase dramatically as point norms approach 1? What hierarchical representation benefit does this provide?
- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning Value Function & Policy Optimization**  
  **Why needed:** GRL reframes multi-step prediction as RL where the predictor's output defines a path value. Understanding the shift from supervised loss to cumulative discounted return (`V(s, s')`) explains the long-horizon consistency gain.  
  **Quick check:** How does defining reward as negative one-step prediction error (`r_t = -c_t`) transform planning into a value maximization problem?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Encoder `Eθ` (frozen) → Exponential map `exp₀` (learnable curvature `c`) → Action-conditioned Predictor `Pφ` (transformer) → [Supervised Loss `L_SFT` (λ·L_TF + (1-λ)·L_rollout) and GRL Loss `L_GRL` (discounted sum + β·L_Δ)] → CEM Planner (minimizes `d_H` to goal)
- **Critical path:** Input video frames → Encoder `Eθ` → Euclidean latent `x̄_t` → `exp₀` → hyperbolic state `sx̄_t,H` → `Pφ` (conditioned on action) → Rollout of predicted states → Compute losses (`L_SFT` then `L_GRL`) → At inference, freeze `Eθ` and `Pφ`, run CEM to minimize final `d_H` between predicted rollout and encoded goal.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Frozen vs. Fine-Tuned Encoder:* Full fine-tuning yields marginal SR gains (~0.5%) but increases cost; paper uses frozen for efficiency.  
  *Learnable curvature `c`:* Allows adaptation (init 1, clamp [0.1,10]); fixed `c=1` may be suboptimal, unconstrained risks instability.  
  *SFT vs. GRL combination:* SFT gives strong short-term accuracy; GRL improves long-horizon consistency; combining them yields best results, indicating complementary roles.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  *Exploding hyperbolic coordinates:* If `∥s∥` exceeds unit ball, check `c` divergence, reduce LR, clip gradients.  
  *GRL no improvement:* Verify `L_Δ` decreases and gradients flow to `φ`; ensure only `φ` is trained in GRL stage.  
  *CEM poor solutions:* Confirm energy decreases across CEM iterations; if not, increase samples `N` or iterations `I`, or adjust initial distribution.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Baseline:** Train with `L_SFT` varying `λ`. Expect `λ`≈0.5 best; increasing rollout weight `(1-λ)` helps longer horizons (T=4 vs T=3). Validate 1-step and 2-step hyperbolic prediction errors decrease.  
  2. **Geometric RL (GRL) Application:** After SFT, optimize `L_GRL` (with `β`, `γ`). Expect `L_Δ` to decrease and rollout consistency (skip-step vs sum-of-steps distance alignment) to improve. Expect T≥4 planning SR to rise over SFT-only.  
  3. **CEM Planning Validation:** Freeze trained (SFT+GRL) model. For fixed (start, goal) pairs, run CEM varying `N` (samples) and `I` (iterations). Plot SR vs `N` and `I`. Expect SR to plateau after sufficient `N` (e.g., 800) and `I` (e.g., 10), indicating searchable energy landscape.

## Limitations
- **Encoder & data dependence:** Performance is tied to the inaccessible V-JEPA 2 encoder (pretrained on non-public VideoMix22M) and unspecified data preprocessing (frame sampling, split details), hindering faithful replication.
- **Mechanism attribution:** The improvement may stem from hyperbolic geometry, GRL regularization, or their interaction; isolating hyperbolic geometry as the sole key factor lacks direct proof (e.g., Euclidean model with identical GRL).
- **Planning coupling:** Long-horizon success is attributed to geodesic rollouts but is coupled with CEM search; it is unclear how much gain is from the learned energy landscape versus the planner's capacity to exploit it.

## Confidence
- Encoder dependence limits replication confidence: **Medium**
- Hyperbolic geometry as the primary enabler of hierarchical modeling: **Low**
- GRL's triangle-inequality regularization improves long-horizon consistency: **Medium**
- Combined H-JEPA + GRL + CEM yields superior T=8 planning: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Replicate Supervised Stage (SFT):** Train with published `L_SFT` (λ=0.5) and report 1-step and 2-step hyperbolic prediction errors (`d_H`) on a held-out set to validate basic hyperbolic dynamics learning independent of GRL.
2. **Quantify Triangle-Ineffectiveness Reduction:** Compute `L_Δ` on rollout trajectories for SFT-only vs. full GeoWorld (SFT+GRL) models; expect significant reduction in the full model, directly evidencing the regularization effect.
3. **CEM Sensitivity Analysis:** For a fixed trained GeoWorld model, vary CEM's sample count `N` (e.g., 200, 800, 1600) and iterations `I` on a fixed test set. Plot success rate vs. `N` and `I`; a plateau indicates a well-formed energy landscape, while a continuous rise suggests planner bottlenecks.
