# HAS-VQ: Hessian-Adaptive Sparse Vector Quantization for High-Fidelity LLM Compression

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.06959
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06959
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: Achieves Pareto dominance over INT4 (14.23 vs 20.03 perplexity) and matches FP16 quality at 2.3× compression on SmolLM2-1.7B.

## Executive Summary
Standard integer quantization (e.g., INT4) often severely degrades the performance of small LLMs due to heavy-tailed weight distributions that are poorly served by uniform grids. HAS-VQ introduces a Hessian-adaptive approach that strictly decouples high-sensitivity outlier parameters from the dense weight body using second-order sensitivity analysis. It then applies robust vector quantization to the body and employs a residual sparse feedback mechanism to exactly reconstruct the outliers. On SmolLM2-1.7B, this yields two key outcomes: (1) Pareto dominance over INT4, achieving 14.23 perplexity at 4.23 bits-per-parameter versus the INT4 baseline's 20.03 PPL at 4.71 BPP; (2) High-fidelity compression, matching FP16 perplexity (10.12 vs. 10.04) while reducing model size by 2.3× at 7.03 BPP.

## Method Summary
HAS-VQ operates in a linear pipeline. First, a calibration dataset is used to estimate the diagonal Hessian (or Fisher Information) of the model, computing a per-parameter importance score Iij = |W_norm,ij| * √H_ii. The top ρ indices (Ω) are masked as outliers, and the remaining weight body B is formed by zeroing these indices. The body B is then quantized via robust K-Means vector quantization, enhanced with stability-bounded sampling (ensuring the number of calibration samples N ≫ K) and a dead unit revival mechanism to prevent centroid collapse. After quantization, a sparse residual S is computed as the difference between the original outlier values and their reconstruction from the VQ body, and stored in high precision. The final compressed model reconstructs the weights as cW = Q(B) + S, where Q(B) is the VQ reconstruction of the body. This design ensures that sensitive parameters are preserved exactly while the dense body is efficiently represented by learned codebooks.

## Key Results
- On SmolLM2-1.7B, HAS-VQ achieves 14.23 perplexity at 4.23 bits-per-parameter (BPP), outperforming the INT4 baseline's 20.03 PPL at 4.71 BPP.
- It matches FP16 perplexity (10.12 vs. 10.04) while reducing model size by 2.3× at 7.03 BPP.
- The method demonstrates a Pareto frontier that strictly dominates uniform integer quantization across evaluated bit-widths.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Hessian-Masked Decoupling
HAS-VQ computes an importance score per parameter using the diagonal Hessian (Iij = |W_norm,ij| * √H_ii) on calibration data. The top ρ indices (Ω) are identified as high-sensitivity outliers and masked to zero in the body matrix B before vector quantization. This prevents the K-Means centroids from drifting toward extreme values, ensuring they accurately capture the dense weight distribution. The approach relies on the diagonal Hessian as a computationally feasible proxy for parameter sensitivity; however, if this approximation is poor (e.g., off-diagonal terms dominate), the masking may misidentify sensitive parameters and harm performance.

### Robust Vector Quantization for Dense Body
The masked body B is quantized using K-Means with two stabilizations: stability-bounded sampling (requiring N ≫ K calibration samples to reduce centroid variance) and a dead unit revival mechanism that periodically relocates inactive centroids to regions of high reconstruction error. These measures yield high-fidelity codebooks that match the heavy-tailed distribution of the weights. The method assumes sufficient calibration data exists; with limited samples, centroids become high-variance and reconstruction degrades.

### Residual Sparse Feedback
After quantizing the body, HAS-VQ computes a sparse residual S = (W_norm - Q(B))[Ω] only at the outlier indices and stores it in high precision. The final reconstruction cW = Q(B) + S ensures that the outlier values are exactly recovered (up to FP16 precision). This correction assumes that the primary quantization error stems from the sensitive dimensions identified by the Hessian mask; if important information lies elsewhere, the residual may be insufficient to restore performance.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Calibration data → Hessian estimation & sensitivity scoring → Weight tensor → Hessian-Masked Decoupling (mask Ω, body B) → Robust K-Means (codebook C, indices IDX) → Reconstruction R → Sparse residual S → Outputs (scales s, codebook C, indices IDX, mask Ω, residual S)

### Critical Path
The logical flow is: Sensitivity → Mask → VQ → Residual. The most computationally heavy and failure-prone step is Robust K-Means on the masked body, which depends critically on correct masking and sufficient calibration data.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Sparsity ratio (ρ)**: Higher ρ protects more outliers but leaves a smaller body for VQ, which may become noisier; lower ρ risks under-masking sensitive parameters.
- **Codebook size (K) / bits per centroid**: Larger K improves body fidelity but increases codebook storage; there is a tradeoff between bits allocated to centroids, indices, and residuals.
- **Block size (b)**: Smaller blocks adapt better to local weight statistics but increase index storage overhead and may fragment the sensitivity pattern.

### Failure Signatures
- **Centroid Collapse**: Many "dead" centroids in K-Means output despite Dead Unit Revival, indicating calibration data insufficiency or overly aggressive masking.
- **Residual Overflow**: Sparse residual values have magnitudes comparable to the original weights, suggesting extremely poor VQ reconstruction of the body.
- **No Pareto Gain**: Perplexity worse than INT4 at lower BPP, implying ineffective Hessian masking or residual compensation.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Sensitivity sanity check**: Visualize a histogram of the importance scores Iij; confirm a clear gap between the outlier tail (Ω) and the dense body. Absence of a gap suggests mis-specified ρ or uninformative Hessian.
2. **VQ convergence check**: On a small masked weight matrix, run Robust K-Means and plot reconstruction MSE versus iteration; expect a monotonic decrease and no dead centroids at termination.
3. **Residual correctness check**: For a test batch, compute R = Q(B); verify that max| (W_norm - R)[Ω] - S | is near machine epsilon (FP16). Also check that the mean absolute error outside Ω is the dominant remaining error source.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does HAS-VQ's advantage over INT4 hold for larger LLMs (≥2B) and other architectures like MoE or CNNs? The paper only evaluates SmolLM2-1.7B and claims generality without evidence.
2. How does varying the sparsity ratio ρ (outlier fraction) impact the compression-accuracy trade-off, and can the stored residuals be quantized to reduce bits further? The paper tests only two fixed ρ settings and keeps residuals in full precision.
3. Are the results robust to the calibration data used for Hessian estimation and to the diagonal Hessian approximation? No experiments vary calibration size/domain or compare to alternative importance metrics.

## Limitations
- Diagonal Hessian approximation may miss off-diagonal interactions, risking misidentification of sensitive parameters.
- The Dead Unit Revival mechanism's stability across diverse weight distributions lacks extensive validation.
- The method assumes sufficient calibration data; performance may degrade under extremely limited calibration scenarios.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The reported Pareto dominance over INT4 (14.23 vs 20.03 PPL) and high-fidelity compression (matching FP16 at 2.3× reduction) on SmolLM2-1.7B are well-supported by the results.
- **Medium Confidence**: The Hessian-Masked Decoupling principle is sound, but the fidelity of the diagonal Hessian as a sensitivity proxy is a key uncertainty that could limit performance on other models.
- **Medium Confidence**: The Robust VQ with Dead Unit Revival is conceptually valid, but its specific implementation details and stability across different weight distributions require further scrutiny.

## Next Checks
1. **Hessian Approximation Fidelity**: Compare performance when using diagonal vs. approximate full Hessian (e.g., via randomized methods) on a subset of weights to quantify the impact of the diagonal approximation.
2. **Calibration Data Sufficiency**: Systematically reduce the amount of calibration data and measure the impact on VQ centroid variance and final perplexity to establish minimum data requirements.
3. **Architecture Generalization**: Apply HAS-VQ to a different LLM family (e.g., Llama or Mistral) and measure whether the same perplexity vs. BPP gains hold, and if optimal ρ and K values differ significantly.
