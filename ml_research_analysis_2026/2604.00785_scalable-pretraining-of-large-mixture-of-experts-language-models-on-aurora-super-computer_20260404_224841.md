# Scalable Pretraining of Large Mixture of Experts Language Models on Aurora Super Computer

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2604.00785
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00785
- **Reference Count**: 13
- **Primary Result**: Developed the Optimus training library to pretrain MoE models up to 220B total parameters (10B active) on 12,288 GPUs, achieving ~90% weak scaling efficiency and up to 1.71x end-to-end speedup via custom kernels and fault tolerance.

## Executive Summary
Pretraining large Mixture of Experts (MoE) language models at scale presents severe efficiency and reliability challenges due to irregular compute patterns, memory overhead from expert parallelism, and high failure rates on supercomputers. This paper introduces the Optimus library, designed for the Aurora exascale system, which combines custom GPU kernels (FastSparseMoE), an EP-aware sharded optimizer (EPSO), and a comprehensive fault tolerance suite. These innovations enabled successful pretraining of models up to 220B parameters across 12,288 GPUs, maintaining ~90% weak scaling efficiency and achieving significant speedups over baseline implementations.

## Method Summary
The Optimus library orchestrates 4D parallelism (Data, Tensor, Pipeline, and Expert) to distribute both model parameters and computation. Its core innovations are: (1) **FastSparseMoE**, which replaces irregular all-to-all communication with an all-gather-based pipeline and fuses small expert matrix operations into grouped GEMMs; (2) **EPSO**, which shards optimizer states differently for expert parameters (DP-only) and non-expert parameters (DP+EP) to eliminate redundant memory and synchronization; and (3) a reliability layer with dual checkpointing, model broadcasting, and soft/hard node failure handling to maintain training continuity at extreme scale.

## Key Results
- Successfully pretrained Mula-220B-A10B (220B total, 10B active) on 12,288 GPUs.
- Achieved ~90% weak scaling efficiency from 384 to 12,288 GPUs.
- FastSparseMoE and EPSO delivered up to 1.71× end-to-end and 1.36× optimizer-only speedups.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: FastSparseMoE Kernel Fusion and Communication
- **Claim**: Custom GPU kernel fusion and replacing all-to-all with all-gather for MoE token communication reduces per-step compute time.
- **Mechanism**: A 5-stage algorithm: (1) all-gather tokens to all EP ranks, (2) count tokens per expert, (3) generate index for grouped computation, (4) execute fused expert GEMMs, (5) reduce and re-scatter outputs. This addresses MoE's irregular compute and communication overhead.
- **Core Assumption**: The volume increase from all-gather is offset by OneCCL's efficiency versus irregular all-to-all, and fusing small GEMMs improves GPU utilization.
- **Evidence**: Abstract reports up to 1.71× end-to-end speedup; Section 3.1 details the 5-stage design.
- **Break Condition**: Forced Uniform Routing (extremely uniform token distribution) may shift compute/communication balance, reducing relative gains.

### Mechanism 2: EP-Aware Sharded Optimizer (EPSO)
- **Claim**: EPSO reduces memory replication and communication for optimizer states under combined Data and Expert Parallelism.
- **Mechanism**: Standard sharded optimizers duplicate non-expert parameter states across EP ranks. EPSO applies fine-grained sharding: expert states are sharded only over DP, while non-expert states are sharded over DP × EP.
- **Core Assumption**: Non-expert parameters (attention, norms) are inherently replicated across EP ranks; sharding these states across EP eliminates redundancy.
- **Evidence**: Abstract reports 1.36× optimizer-only speedup; Section 3.2 and Figure 6 detail parameter splitting (P_E vs. P_NE) and sharding strategies.
- **Break Condition**: Models without EP (all parameters sharded by DP/TP/PP) gain no benefit and add complexity.

### Mechanism 3: Fault Tolerance Suite for Exascale Stability
- **Claim**: A robust set of reliability features maintains training continuity despite frequent failures at extreme scale.
- **Mechanism**: Addresses multiple failure points: (1) contiguous data sharding to avoid filesystem pressure, (2) model broadcasting to prevent parallel load hangs, (3) dual checkpointing to survive write failures, (4) persistent model-only checkpoints for divergence recovery, (5) DP-scattered checkpointing to distribute I/O, (6) hard/soft node failure handling with buffer nodes for automatic restart.
- **Core Assumption**: Failures (I/O errors, node hangs, NaNs) are inevitable and frequent; the system must anticipate and recover without manual intervention.
- **Evidence**: Abstract mentions robustness; Section 4 details each feature's implementation.
- **Break Condition**: If MTBF is lower than checkpoint interval plus restart time, or if many soft failures occur simultaneously, recovery may be overwhelmed.

## Foundational Learning
- **Mixture of Experts (MoE) Routing & Expert Parallelism (EP)**
  - *Why needed*: FastSparseMoE and EPSO rely on understanding that experts are sharded across ranks, causing non-uniform compute and parameter replication.
  - *Quick check*: With EP=4 and 64 total experts, how many experts per rank? Which parameters (router, experts) are replicated across all EP ranks?
- **3D Parallelism (DP, TP, PP, EP)**
  - *Why needed*: Performance modeling (scaling efficiency) and optimizer design (EPSO) depend on how DP/TP/PP/EP interact and replicate parameters.
  - *Quick check*: For DP=8, TP=2, PP=4, EP=2, what is the total number of GPU ranks? Where are the router and a single expert's parameters placed?
- **Sharded Data Parallelism (ZeRO/FSDP) & AdamW State Sharding**
  - *Why needed*: The memory model and design of standard SO and EPSO rely on partitioning optimizer states (weights, grads, moments) across data-parallel groups.
  - *Quick check*: In ZeRO stage 1, what four components are partitioned across DP ranks, and what communication is required after backward pass before the optimizer step?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Optimus Core (orchestrates DP/TP/PP, microbatches, activation checkpointing) -> FastSparseMoE (5-stage EP-aware kernel pipeline) -> EPSO (expert vs. non-expert parameter sharding) -> Reliability Layer (data preprocessing, broadcasting, checkpointing, failure handling).
- **Critical Path**: Data loading (mmap) -> Forward (TP/PP/EP with FastSparseMoE) -> Backward -> Gradient reduce-scatter (DP) -> Optimizer step (EPSO) -> Parameter all-gather (DP) -> Asynchronous scattered checkpoint.
- **Design Tradeoffs**:
  - *Memory vs. Communication*: FSMOE's all-gather increases volume but uses efficient collectives; EPSO reduces memory but adds parameter grouping complexity.
  - *Checkpoint Frequency vs. Recovery*: Frequent checkpoints reduce lost work but increase I/O pressure; model-only saves space but risks divergence.
  - *Uniformity vs. Efficiency*: Forced Uniform Routing (FUR) showed similar scaling, suggesting non-uniform MoE compute is not the primary scaling limiter, but sacrifices accuracy.
- **Failure Signatures**:
  - *Hang at startup*: Likely concurrent model load; use model broadcasting.
  - *Vanishing/exploding loss*: Check for soft node failures (NaNs); monitor local loss/grads per rank.
  - *Checkpoint restore failure*: Ensure dual checkpointing and file integrity.
  - *Poor scaling (<85% at 1k+ nodes)*: Profile communication (all-gather in FSMOE, reduce-scatter in DP); consider TP/PP balance.
- **First 3 Experiments**:
  1. Validate EP sharding: Run a small MoE (e.g., Mula-7B-A1B) on 2 nodes with EP=2. Use memory profiler to confirm expert parameters only on assigned EP ranks and non-expert params replicated. Check optimizer state memory matches EPSO prediction.
  2. Benchmark FSMOE communication pattern: Isolate SparseMoEBlock. Compare step time with standard all-to-all vs. all-gather under varying expert selectivity K. Expect speedup when K is high and token distribution is irregular.
  3. Test soft node failure recovery: Inject NaN in one rank's forward output. Verify detection, clean exit, and restart from latest valid checkpoint using buffer nodes without manual intervention.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: What systematic factors cause the ~10% drop in weak scaling efficiency beyond 1,000 GPUs, even with Forced Uniform Routing (FUR) to eliminate routing imbalance?
- **Open Question 2**: To what extent does EPSO generalize beyond the tested MoE configurations (EP=12, PP=4/8) to other EP degrees, TP setups, or non-MoE models with similar replication patterns?
- **Open Question 3**: Do FastSparseMoE kernel optimizations introduce hidden trade-offs in training stability (gradient explosion, numerical precision) or downstream model quality compared to standard implementations?
- **Open Question 4**: What is the quantitative performance overhead of the fault tolerance suite (dual checkpointing, model broadcasting, etc.) during normal training, and how does it scale with system size?

## Limitations
- Dataset preprocessing specifics (tokenization, shuffling, sharding) for OLMoE-mix-0924 are not disclosed.
- Full hyperparameter suite for the largest model (Mula-220B-A10B) is missing (global/micro batch sizes, LR schedule, checkpoint frequency).
- Aurora hardware details (GPU tiles per node, interconnect topology, memory per tile) are omitted, affecting communication modeling.
- Fault tolerance implementation nuances (checkpoint sync, NaN detection thresholds, buffer node swap logic) are not elaborated.
- Exact parallel configuration breakdowns (DP/TP/PP/EP degrees) for each model size are incomplete.

## Confidence
- **FastSparseMoE speedup (1.71×)**: Medium (internal benchmarks, no external corroboration; corpus shows low citation impact).
- **EPSO optimizer speedup (1.36×)**: Medium (paper-specific design, limited independent validation of memory/compute savings).
- **Weak scaling efficiency (90%)**: Medium (strong internal metrics but unique Aurora stack limits generalizability).
- **Fault tolerance robustness**: Medium (described mechanistically, not externally stress-tested).
