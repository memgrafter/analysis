# ReLE: A Scalable System and Structured Benchmark for Diagnosing Capability Anisotropy in Chinese LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.17399
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17399
- **Reference count:** 36
- **Primary result:** ReLE reduces evaluation costs by 70% while preserving ranking fidelity (ρ=0.96) and reveals significant capability anisotropy (RSA=11.4, Ianiso=0.74) across 304 Chinese LLMs.

## Executive Summary
ReLE addresses benchmark saturation and prohibitive evaluation costs in Chinese LLM assessment through a scalable system combining a Symbolic-Grounded Hybrid Scoring Mechanism and a Dynamic Variance-Aware Scheduler. Evaluating 304 models on 207,843 samples, it achieves ~70% compute reduction without fidelity loss (Spearman ρ=0.96). Crucially, it uncovers pervasive capability anisotropy: model rankings are highly unstable under weighting changes (Rank Stability Amplitude = 11.4 vs ~5.0 in traditional benchmarks), indicating that strengths are specialized rather than general, and rankings are sensitive to evaluation design.

## Method Summary
ReLE constructs an orthogonal Domain×Capability taxonomy (7 domains × 22 capabilities) to decouple knowledge from reasoning. Evaluation uses a hybrid scoring pipeline: objective tasks use exact match; semi-objective tasks cascade a semantic filter (BGE-M3 embeddings) with calibrated LLM judging (GPT-4o) for ambiguous cases (similarity 0.60–0.92), with adversarial bias mitigation. Cost efficiency stems from a two-stage Neyman allocation scheduler: a pilot phase estimates per-stratum variance, then allocates samples proportional to (Wh * Sh)/√ch, stopping when confidence interval widths meet a Hoeffding-Serfling threshold. Full-set control experiments confirm the scheduler's noise is minimal (RSA drop only 5.2%).

## Key Results
- **Cost & Fidelity:** Dynamic scheduler reduces compute by 70% while preserving ranking fidelity (Spearman ρ=0.96 vs full-set evaluation).
- **Anisotropy Magnitude:** Models exhibit a Rank Stability Amplitude (RSA) of 11.4, far exceeding the ~5.0 baseline from traditional benchmarks (p<0.001).
- **Specialization Index:** Anisotropy index (Ianiso) reaches 0.74, confirming that model capabilities are highly specialized and rankings depend critically on weighting schemes rather than reflecting broad superiority.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Symbolic-Grounded Hybrid Scoring
- **Claim:** Eliminates embedding-based false positives in reasoning tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Cascaded filter: (1) high-confidence exact/symbolic matches auto-scored; (2) embedding similarity (BGE-M3) separates clear matches/mismatches; (3) only ambiguous similarity bands (0.60–0.92) routed to LLM judge; (4) judge prompts calibrated against adversarial samples to reduce self-preference bias.
- **Core assumption:** Embedding similarity alone is insufficient for judging reasoning correctness due to semantic equivalence risks (e.g., plausible but incorrect reasoning).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "eliminates embedding-based false positives"; [section 3.2] three-tier pipeline with Cohen’s κ=0.81.
- **Break condition:** If symbolic ground truth unavailable (e.g., open-ended generation), defaults to judge-only scoring, re-introducing bias.

### Mechanism 2: Dynamic Variance-Aware Scheduler
- **Claim:** Reduces compute costs by ~70% while preserving ranking fidelity (ρ=0.96).
- **Mechanism:** Treats evaluation as stratified sequential estimation. Stage 1 probes per-stratum variance (Sh,m). Stage 2 allocates samples proportional to Wh * Sh / sqrt(ch), prioritizing high-variance strata. Uses Hoeffding-Serfling bound for confidence intervals without normality assumptions, stopping when interval width δ_h is achieved.
- **Core assumption:** Variance across capability dimensions (strata) is non-uniform and model-specific; adaptive allocation can prune stable strata aggressively.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "reduces compute costs by 70%... ranking correlation ρ=0.96"; [section 3.3] formal Neyman allocation; [section 6.1.1] control shows full-set RSA drop only 5.2%.
- **Break condition:** If pilot variance estimates (n0) highly unstable (e.g., very small strata), initial allocation suboptimal, requiring fallback to uniform sampling.

### Mechanism 3: Capability Anisotropy as Intrinsic Property
- **Claim:** High RSA (11.4) reflects intrinsic model specialization, not sampling noise or contamination.
- **Mechanism:** ReLE’s orthogonal Domain×Capability matrix decouples knowledge from reasoning. RSA measures max rank displacement across weighting schemes. Control experiments (full-set vs. dynamic) isolate anisotropy from sampling variance.
- **Core assumption:** Anisotropy exists if rankings remain unstable under weighting perturbations even with full data; traditional benchmarks mask this via coarse, correlated tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Rank Stability Amplitude of 11.4... strengths highly specialized"; [section 6.1] RSA comparison to baselines (p<0.001); [section 6.1.1] full-set control shows 94.8% of instability persists.
- **Break condition:** If capability dimensions highly correlated (low anisotropy), RSA would converge to baseline (~5.0) regardless of orthogonality, suggesting taxonomy conflation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Stratified Sequential Sampling**
  - Why needed here: ReLE’s cost reduction depends on non-uniform variance across capability strata; uniform sampling would negate savings.
  - Quick check: If stratum A has double the variance of stratum B, how should sample allocation differ under Neyman allocation?
- **Concept: Capability Decomposition Orthogonality**
  - Why needed here: Separating Domain (e.g., Finance) from Capability (e.g., Reasoning) isolates whether failures stem from knowledge or reasoning deficits.
  - Quick check: Why is a "Gaokao Math" question classified as D_Edu ∩ C_Reasoning rather than a monolithic "Math" domain?
- **Concept: False Positive Mitigation in LLM Judging**
  - Why needed here: Embedding similarity can rate factually wrong but semantically plausible answers correct, inflating reasoning scores.
  - Quick check: What specific step in ReLE’s scoring pipeline directly targets "reasoning hallucinations" in judge models?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Unified Prompt Schema -> Model Adapter Layer -> Task Scheduler -> Scoring Pipeline -> Leaderboard Module.
- **Critical path:** Prompt Schema → Adapter → Scheduler (decides sample count per stratum) → Inference → Scoring (hybrid verification) → Metric Aggregation (RSA/CI) → Leaderboard update.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Cost vs. Fidelity: Dynamic scheduler trades exhaustive coverage for statistical efficiency; may miss rare failures in tiny strata.
  - Generality vs. Specialization: Orthogonal matrix reveals anisotropy but may underrepresent cross-domain reasoning (e.g., medical+logic).
  - Automation vs. Safety: Judge-model bias calibration requires adversarial human validation; automation risks propagating judge preferences.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Scheduler: RSA from dynamic runs deviates >15% from full-set (indicates variance estimation failure).
  - Scoring: High agreement (κ>0.8) on objective tasks but sharp drop on ambiguous reasoning (judge brittleness).
  - Anisotropy: Low RSA (<6) despite fine-grained decomposition suggests taxonomy still conflated (e.g., "Reasoning" too broad).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Run 50 models on fixed pilot set; verify per-stratum sample counts correlate with observed variance (Sh,m) and total cost reduction ≈70%.
  2. On 500 adversarial reasoning samples, measure: (a) false positive rate of embedding-only vs. hybrid; (b) judge calibration effect (raw vs. calibrated prompts).
  3. Compute RSA on ReLE vs. C-Eval for same model set; confirm ReLE RSA >10 and statistically higher (bootstrap p<0.01).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
Assumption: The paper does not explicitly list open questions. However, the authors' discussion of limitations and next steps implies several open questions for future research:
- How does ReLE's hybrid scoring mechanism generalize to other judge models (e.g., Qwen-Max, Claude) and languages beyond Chinese?
- What is the theoretical lower bound on anisotropy (Ianiso) given a fixed orthogonal taxonomy, and how does it relate to model architecture?
- Can the dynamic scheduler's pilot phase be made adaptive to model-specific variance patterns without inflating overhead?

## Limitations
- Scoring hinges on undisclosed BGE-M3 variant/thresholds and GPT-4o judge calibration details; unknown if bias mitigation generalizes beyond adversarial set.
- Dynamic scheduler’s pilot size (5% of which baseline?) and cost coefficients per stratum are unspecified, risking suboptimal allocation for low-variance strata.
- Anisotropy depends on orthogonal taxonomy validity; unmeasured cross-domain capability correlations could inflate RSA. Generalization gap Δg on Private Anchor Set not reported.

## Confidence
- **Cost-Reduction & Fidelity (ρ=0.96):** High (robust control experiment shows only 5.2% RSA drop).
- **Anisotropy Findings (RSA=11.4, Ianiso=0.74):** Medium (relies on taxonomy orthogonality; no ablation on correlated dimensions).
- **Scoring Mechanism False Positive Reduction:** Medium (Cohen’s κ=0.81 strong, but GPT-4o selection bias remains threat).
