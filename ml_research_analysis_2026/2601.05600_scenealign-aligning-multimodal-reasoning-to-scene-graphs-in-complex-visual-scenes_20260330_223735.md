# SceneAlign: Aligning Multimodal Reasoning to Scene Graphs in Complex Visual Scenes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.05600
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05600
- **Reference count:** 39
- **Primary result:** Improves accuracy and reasoning faithfulness across seven benchmarks, with 3%–5% average gains on reasoning-intensive tasks.

## Executive Summary
SceneAlign addresses reasoning unfaithfulness in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) when handling complex visual scenes. Existing preference-based methods often allow models to rely on language priors instead of visual grounding. To counter this, SceneAlign uses scene graphs as structured visual representations to generate contrastive reasoning pairs via four perturbation strategies (swap, replace, shorten, overthink) that simulate realistic grounding failures. These pairs are used in Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to steer models toward structure-faithful reasoning. Evaluations on seven benchmarks show consistent improvements in both answer accuracy and reasoning fidelity.

## Method Summary
The framework begins by extracting a scene graph (entities, attributes, relations) from the image-question pair using GPT-4o. A positive chain-of-thought (CoT) is generated that is explicitly grounded in this graph. The CoT-grounded subgraph is then perturbed through four targeted interventions to create negative scene graphs, which are used to generate negative CoTs that remain linguistically plausible but grounded in inaccurate visual facts. After filtering by Jaccard overlap and diversity-based sampling to ensure medium-difficulty negatives, the resulting preference pairs are used in DPO to align the MLLM. This process exposes fine-grained structural errors and encourages the model to produce reasoning that faithfully reflects the visual scene.

## Key Results
- Average improvements of 3%–5% on reasoning-intensive tasks.
- Gains observed on vision-centric benchmarks as well (e.g., MME-RealWorld, EMMA-mini, ScienceQA).
- Outperforms strong baselines and prior CoT-based preference construction methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
SceneAlign improves grounding faithfulness by exposing fine-grained structural errors through scene-graph perturbations. By perturbing the CoT-grounded subgraph (swap, replace, shorten, overthink) and generating contrastive CoTs, the model learns to distinguish visually grounded reasoning from plausible but incorrect alternatives. The core assumption is that scene graphs capture all relevant elements for reasoning, and perturbations simulate realistic grounding failures. Break condition: If scene graph extraction misses critical entities or relations, perturbations cannot simulate realistic failures.

### Mechanism 2
Diversity-based sampling improves negative quality by ensuring medium-difficulty samples. After filtering negatives by Jaccard overlap to exclude trivial or irrelevant samples, a max-min criterion selects negatives that are semantically coherent yet structurally inconsistent with the positive. The core assumption is that hard negatives neither too similar nor too dissimilar provide the strongest supervision for preference optimization. Break condition: If sampling fails to capture structurally relevant negatives, the contrastive signal becomes weak.

### Mechanism 3
Preference optimization with scene-graph grounded positives and negatives steers the model toward structure-faithful reasoning. DPO optimizes the MLLM to assign higher likelihood to reasoning chains consistent with the true scene graph while penalizing those with grounding errors. The core assumption is that the reference model and preference pairs are sufficiently informative to guide the MLLM. Break condition: If preference pairs are poorly constructed or the optimization coefficient β is inappropriate, alignment may not converge.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Scene graph generation (GPT-4o) → Scene graph extraction → CoT generation (MLLM) → Perturbation operators → Negative CoT generation → Diversity sampling → DPO training; Reference model πref → Preference pairs P → Optimized MLLM πθ

**Critical path:**  
1. Generate scene graph from image+question  
2. Generate positive CoT grounded in scene graph  
3. Extract CoT-grounded subgraph  
4. Apply perturbations to create negatives  
5. Filter and sample diverse negatives  
6. Apply DPO with preference pairs  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Perturbation complexity vs. computational cost (more operators increase negatives but raise cost)  
- Overlap thresholds (γℓ, γu) vs. negative quality (too strict yields few samples, too loose yields weak supervision)  
- Number of negatives per instance vs. training efficiency (diminishing returns beyond 3–4)

**Failure signatures:**  
- No improvement on reasoning benchmarks: potential issues with scene graph quality or perturbation operators  
- Degraded performance on vision-centric tasks: possible over-alignment to reasoning structure at expense of perception  
- Training instability: likely issues with preference pair construction or DPO hyperparameters

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Validate scene graph extraction quality on 100 random samples from A-OKVQA  
2. Test each perturbation operator independently on a small subset to verify semantic coherence and individual impact  
3. Run DPO with only one negative per instance to establish baseline before scaling up

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Multi-image/video generalization:** How would SceneAlign perform on inputs requiring temporal or cross-view consistency? The paper focuses on single-image reasoning and does not address dynamic relationships.
2. **Robustness to scene graph quality:** How sensitive is performance to GPT-4o-generated scene graphs when visual elements are ambiguous or occluded? The paper acknowledges occasional redundant nodes but does not test robustness to errors.
3. **Human vs. automatic evaluation:** Would human-centered evaluation provide different assessments of grounding fidelity compared to the LLM-based automatic evaluation used? The paper notes automatic evaluation may miss nuanced judgments.
4. **Scaling negative samples:** What is the impact of using more than 3–4 negatives per instance? The paper observes diminishing returns but does not explore higher numbers or finer-grained trade-offs.

## Limitations
- Scene graph extraction quality is critical; failures to identify entities, attributes, or relations break the perturbation mechanism.
- Key DPO hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, β) are not fully specified, introducing sensitivity risks.
- Potential over-alignment to structured reasoning could degrade performance on vision-centric tasks.

## Confidence
- **High:** Core mechanism of scene-graph perturbations (supported by ablations and benchmark results).
- **Medium:** Diversity-based sampling approach (effective but may require tuning for datasets/models).
- **Low:** Long-term stability and out-of-distribution generalization (untested).
