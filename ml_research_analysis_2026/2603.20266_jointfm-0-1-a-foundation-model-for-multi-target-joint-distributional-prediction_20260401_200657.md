# JointFM-0.1: A Foundation Model for Multi-Target Joint Distributional Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.20266
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20266
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** In zero-shot evaluation on 200 unseen SDE systems with 10 coupled targets (history=504, forecast=63), JointFM reduces energy loss by 14.2% on average and 10.5% at the longest horizon vs. strongest baseline.

## Executive Summary
JointFM addresses the practical limitations of Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) modeling—high risk, brittle calibration, and expensive simulations—by introducing a foundation model that directly predicts future joint probability distributions from historical context without task-specific tuning. It employs a Transformer architecture with distributional output heads (multivariate Gaussian mixture or skewed Student-t mixture) trained on an infinite, procedurally generated curriculum of synthetic SDEs, incorporating correlated diffusions, jumps, and regime switching. In zero-shot evaluation on 200 unseen SDE systems, JointFM consistently outperforms baselines across all horizons on energy loss, marginal energy, and CRPS-sum, demonstrating accurate recovery of joint distributions and dependencies.

## Method Summary
JointFM tackles zero-shot multi-target joint distributional forecasting. Given a context window of 504 historical steps for M exogenous and N=10 target series, it predicts the full joint distribution over H=63 future steps in one forward pass. Training uses an infinite stream of synthetic SDE paths from a staged curriculum (levels 0–7) that progressively introduces complexity: correlated diffusions, jumps, and regime switching. The architecture is a Transformer encoder with Variable-Factored Attention and two distributional heads—one for a mixture of multivariate Gaussians, another for a mixture of multivariate skewed Student-t distributions. The model is trained by minimizing a proper scoring rule (negative log-likelihood) between its output distribution and Monte Carlo ground-truth samples from the same SDE. Inference generates forecast samples by drawing from the predicted mixture.

## Key Results
- JointFM reduces average energy loss by 14.2% and longest-horizon (63-step) energy loss by 10.5% compared to the strongest baseline (DCC-GARCH or Historical Simulation) on 200 unseen SDE systems.
- It consistently outperforms baselines across all horizons on energy loss, marginal energy, and CRPS-sum, showing accurate joint and marginal calibration without per-task calibration.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** JointFM replaces the traditional select‑calibrate‑simulate pipeline by learning a direct mapping from historical context to future joint distributions, bypassing explicit SDE specification and parameter fitting.  
**Mechanism:** An infinite, procedurally generated curriculum of synthetic SDE systems (covering correlated diffusions, jumps, regime switching) is used for pretraining. The Transformer internalizes the conditional density \( p(X_{T+1:T+H} \mid X_{1:T}) \) across this vast space of dynamics. At inference, a single forward pass produces distributional parameters without task‑specific optimization.  
**Core assumption:** Real‑world stochastic processes are special cases or approximations of the synthetic SDE universe; the model’s learned priors transfer zero‑shot to unseen systems.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract (“sample an infinite stream of synthetic SDEs”), Section 3 (curriculum levels), corpus (neighbors on neural SDEs).

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Predicting a full parametric joint distribution (via mixture models) rather than independent marginals or point paths enables accurate recovery of cross‑variable dependencies.  
**Mechanism:** The output head predicts parameters for a multivariate mixture (Gaussian or skewed Student‑t). Training loss (e.g., negative log‑likelihood) is computed on samples from the true joint distribution, forcing the network to capture covariance structures, tail dependencies, and asymmetries across all targets simultaneously.  
**Core assumption:** A finite mixture of flexible parametric families can approximate the true conditional joint distribution for the curriculum’s SDE classes; the number of components is fixed and adequate.  
**Evidence anchors:** Section 3.3 (first foundation model to predict full joint distribution), Section 4 (Energy Loss improvement).

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Unified inference acts as a “simulation shortcut”: the model implicitly performs model selection and calibration within its forward pass.  
**Mechanism:** The Transformer’s attention processes the historical window \( X_{1:T} \) to infer latent state variables (e.g., regime, volatility, correlation). The learned mapping from activations to distributional parameters substitutes for explicit SDE coefficient estimation and Monte Carlo simulation.  
**Core assumption:** The historical context contains sufficient evidence to infer the generating SDE’s state; the architecture can encode this inference.  
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract (“implicitly performing model selection and calibration”), Section 1 (inferring “market regime”), Section 4 (constant‑time inference).

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Synthetic Data Curriculum for Physical Dynamics**  
  **Why needed:** The model must generalize across an infinite space of SDEs without seeing real data during training. A staged curriculum (levels 0–7) introduces complexity progressively, preventing catastrophic forgetting and ensuring learned features are composable.  
  **Quick check:** If a new SDE type (e.g., stochastic volatility) is added at curriculum level 8, what risks does this pose for a model trained only up to level 7?

- **Concept: Foundation Model Transfer via Task Distribution**  
  **Why needed:** JointFM is a foundation model because it is pretrained on a *distribution of tasks* (synthetic SDE systems) to solve a *distribution of tasks* (predicting any unseen SDE’s future distribution). Zero‑shot transfer occurs because the test SDE is a new sample from the same meta‑distribution.  
  **Quick check:** Why does zero‑shot performance on a *new* synthetic SDE system constitute evidence of foundation model capabilities, whereas fitting a DCC‑GARCH does not?

- **Concept: Proper Scoring Rules for Joint Distributions**  
  **Why needed:** Evaluating multi‑variable forecasts requires metrics that assess the full joint distribution, not just marginals. Energy Loss is a proper multivariate scoring rule sensitive to all dependence structures; using only Marginal Energy or CRPS‑sum would miss correlation errors.  
  **Quick check:** Why would a model that minimizes CRPS‑sum still produce poor portfolio risk assessments?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Input Encoder → Transformer Backbone → Distributional Heads → Sampling Layer (inference)

**Critical path:** Synthetic Data Generator → Transformer Training → Zero‑shot Inference. The curriculum generator must produce diverse, well‑sampled SDE paths; training must converge on negative log‑likelihood of ground‑truth future samples; inference is a single forward pass through the trained model with a new history window.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Curriculum Breadth vs. Depth:* Broader SDE families improve generalizability but risk unstable training if parameter ranges are not normalized; deeper focus on fewer families may hurt universal transfer.  
- *Mixture Complexity:* More components \( K \) increase expressive power but cause training instability and higher inference cost; too few may underfit complex joint structures.  
- *Context Length \( T \):* Longer histories improve regime inference but increase memory and may dilute signal with irrelevant lags.

**Failure signatures:**  
- *Poor Marginal Calibration (high Marginal Energy):* Mixture variances or means are misestimated; check normalization and mixture capacity.  
- *Joint Mismatch (high Energy Loss despite good marginals):* Correlation structure is wrong; check if \( K \) is sufficient, visualize scatter plots.  
- *Breakdown on Unseen Dynamics:* Sudden metric deterioration on systems with dynamics from curriculum levels > 7; identify missing dynamic component (e.g., rough volatility) and add to curriculum.  
- *High‑Dimensional Collapse:* Performance degrades as \( N \) exceeds training maximum; check Variable‑Factored Attention scaling.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Curriculum Ablation:** Train on curriculum subsets (e.g., levels 0–3 vs. 0–7). Expected: Full curriculum significantly reduces Energy Loss on level‑7 systems.  
2. **Output Head Comparison:** Train separate models with MV‑GMM vs. skewed Student‑t heads; evaluate on jump‑heavy SDEs. Expected: Student‑t head wins, validating parametric flexibility.  
3. **Context Sensitivity Test:** For a fixed level‑7 SDE, vary history length \( T \) (100, 504, 1000). Expected: Energy Loss improves with \( T \) up to a point then plateaus/degrades, testing regime‑inference mechanism.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question:** How can JointFM’s synthetic physics curriculum be extended to include stochastic volatility (e.g., Heston, SABR), local‑stochastic volatility, rough volatility, and non‑Markovian memory dynamics beyond the current Markovian levels?  
**Basis:** The authors state they “plan to extend the curriculum to richer volatility specifications—including stochastic volatility (e.g., Heston, SABR), local‑stochastic volatility, and rough volatility models” (Limitations & Future Work).  
**Why unresolved:** These dynamics are prevalent in finance; without them, JointFM may not generalize to processes with stochastic volatility or long memory.  
**What evidence would resolve:** Successful integration of these dynamics into the curriculum, training a new JointFM variant, and demonstrating zero‑shot recovery of unseen SDE systems that include such dynamics, with performance at least comparable to existing levels.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** What architectural and training modifications are necessary to scale JointFM to longer context windows (e.g., >1000 steps), higher‑dimensional target spaces (e.g., >100 targets), and larger exogenous feature sets (e.g., >100 features) while maintaining inference speed and accuracy?  
**Basis:** The authors state they “intend to scale both the data pipeline and the model, and are investigating architectural changes that will make scaling to longer contexts, higher‑dimensional target spaces, and richer feature sets practical. Early results indicate that scaling beyond 100 features and 100 targets is feasible but in combination with an increased context length and number of output horizons, hardware constraints become a bottleneck.”  
**Why unresolved:** Real‑world applications often involve many variables and long histories; without scaling, JointFM’s applicability is limited.  
**What evidence would resolve:** A scaled version of JointFM that processes contexts of length >1000 and dimensions >100 with similar zero‑shot performance on synthetic SDEs, and efficient inference (e.g., sub‑second latency) on standard hardware.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** How can hybrid inference be implemented in JointFM to mitigate the observed dynamics fallacy, where the model may fail to assign probability to events not present in the context window? Can explicit time‑series labeling during training enable practitioners to incorporate domain knowledge (e.g., “jump risk”, “regime switch”) as categorical tokens?  
**Basis:** The authors write that “future iterations will incorporate explicit time‑series labeling during training. Because we control the data‑generating process, we can supply categorical tokens (e.g., ‘Jump‑Diffusion’, ‘Mean‑Reverting’) alongside the time‑series data, enabling a ‘hybrid inference’ mode where practitioners optionally guide the model’s priors with domain knowledge while retaining purely data‑driven inference for novel situations.”  
**Why unresolved:** Purely data‑driven approach may miss rare but important events; hybrid inference could incorporate structural risks and improve robustness.  
**What evidence would resolve:** Implementation of labeling and hybrid inference, with experiments showing that when provided with correct labels, JointFM assigns appropriate probability to events absent from the recent history, and that this improves decision‑making in risk‑sensitive tasks.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** To what extent does the generally trained JointFM (without domain specialization) generalize to real‑world multivariate time series data from various industries (e.g., finance, energy, commodities), and how does its distributional predictive performance compare to traditional SDE calibration methods and other forecasting baselines?  
**Basis:** The paper only evaluates JointFM on unseen synthetic SDE systems; real‑world generalization is hypothesized but not tested. Although the authors mention a financially‑focused variant applied to real market data in a blog post, the current study provides no empirical evidence for the generally trained model on real datasets.  
**Why unresolved:** Real‑world data may contain noise, missing values, structural breaks, and dynamics not captured by the synthetic curriculum; performance on synthetic data does not guarantee real‑world efficacy.  
**What evidence would resolve:** Rigorous evaluation of the generally trained JointFM on multiple real‑world multivariate time series datasets from different domains, using metrics like energy loss, CRPS, and decision‑oriented utility (e.g., portfolio return, risk measures), with comparisons to traditional calibrated SDE models and other forecasting methods.

## Limitations
- Exact model hyperparameters (layers, heads, embedding size, mixture components K) and training details are unreported, blocking faithful reproduction.
- SDE curriculum parameter distributions (e.g., volatility ranges, correlation priors, jump intensities) are unspecified, limiting assessment of diversity and generalizability.
- Implementation of “Variable‑Factored Attention” for dynamic asset count N is not elaborated.

## Confidence
- Zero‑shot transfer via synthetic pretraining: **Medium**. Evidence rests on synthetic evaluations; no real‑world validation.
- Joint distribution modeling with mixture heads: **Medium**. Energy Loss improvements indicate better joint calibration, but tail dependency capture is not thoroughly probed.
- Unified inference as simulation shortcut: **Medium**. Constant‑time inference is shown, but the implicit mechanism lacks direct empirical dissection.

## Next Checks
1. **Curriculum ablation:** Train on subsets (e.g., levels 0–3) and evaluate on level‑7 systems; full curriculum should yield significantly lower Energy Loss, confirming staged learning.
2. **Output head comparison:** On jump‑heavy SDEs, compare MV‑GMM vs. skewed Student‑t; Student‑t is expected to outperform, validating parametric flexibility.
3. **Context sensitivity:** Vary history length T (100, 504, 1000) and measure Energy Loss; performance should improve then plateau, testing the regime‑inference mechanism.
