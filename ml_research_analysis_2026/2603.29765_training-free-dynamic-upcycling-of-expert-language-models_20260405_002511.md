# Training-Free Dynamic Upcycling of Expert Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.29765
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29765
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: DUME merges domain-specialized LLMs into a single multi-domain MoE without additional training, retaining up to 97.6% of single-expert performance on CLM and up to 102.1% on reasoning tasks.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Dynamic Upcycling MoE (DUME), a training-free method to combine multiple domain-specialized language models into a single Mixture of Experts model. DUME first merges the experts' non-MLP parameters to create a shared backbone and then initializes MoE routers using closed-form ridge regression on the domain-specific training data. This approach avoids catastrophic forgetting and overspecialization, achieving state-of-the-art results on both causal language modeling and reasoning benchmarks. The method is embarrassingly parallel, scales to multiple domains, and even allows dynamic expert addition.

## Method Summary
DUME operates in three stages: (1) Train dense expert models on separate domain datasets. (2) Merge experts by averaging all parameters except MLP weights; the MLPs become the distinct experts for each transformer block, forming an MoE architecture. (3) Initialize routers via closed-form ridge regression: forward each domain's training data through the merged backbone (using its own MLP for MoE blocks) to collect activation statistics; compute router weights as the ridge regression solution that maps activations to one-hot domain labels. No further training is required. The ridge regression formulation is schedule-invariant, enabling parallel statistics collection across domains.

## Key Results
- On causal language modeling across five domains, DUME achieves a normalized average perplexity of 92.8%, retaining up to 97.6% of a single-domain expert’s performance and outperforming baselines like BTX.
- On reasoning tasks (HumanEval, GSM8k, M_ARC, IFEval), DUME scores up to 102.1% of the best expert and a normalized average of 95.2%, even surpassing the Oracle in some domains.
- DUME is training-free, scalable, and supports dynamic expert addition without degradation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Ridge regression on pre-activation features from domain-specific experts can initialize MoE routers to route tokens to the correct expert without further training.
- **Mechanism:** For each MoE block, DUME sequentially forwards the entire training datasets (each token labeled with its domain) through the *already merged* model up to that block. Using the pre-MoE activation vectors \( F^{<l}(x_i) \) and one-hot domain labels \( y_i \), it accumulates matrices \( A^l = \sum F^{\top}F \) and \( b^l = \sum F^{\top}y \) per layer. The router weights \( W^l_* = (A^l + \lambda I)^{-1}b^l \) are then a closed-form solution that learns a linear classifier mapping activations to domain experts.
- **Core assumption:** The latent feature space (\( F^{<l}(x) \)) produced by the averaged self-attention and earlier layers is sufficiently discriminative such that a linear router (ridge regression) can reliably predict the originating domain of a token.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "routers are initialized via closed-form ridge regression using the experts' training data, eliminating the need for further optimization."
  - Section 2.3 details the sequential forwarding and matrix accumulation process, calling it "exactly the same optimal parameters as if we computed the ridge regression solution using Equation 2."
  - The neighbor papers focus on upcycling via parameter merging or replication, not on ridge regression for routing; validation is primarily empirical within this work.
- **Break condition:** If the pre-MoE activation distributions for different domains are not linearly separable, the ridge regression router would misroute tokens, leading to performance collapse toward random routing.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** "MoErging" (averaging all non-MLP parameters while retaining MLPs as separate experts) preserves the general, shared knowledge (e.g., syntax, relational patterns from attention) that is common across domains, while keeping domain-specific computation isolated in the experts.
- **Mechanism:** The method assumes that self-attention and embedding layers learn largely domain-agnostic representations, whereas the MLP blocks (feed-forward networks) specialize for domain-specific transformations. By averaging the non-MLP weights, a single, coherent "backbone" is created. The MLPs from each dense expert are kept separate as the conditional experts within the MoE blocks.
- **Core assumption:** The specialization of LLM fine-tuning is concentrated primarily in the MLP layers, not in the attention layers.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 2.2: "The rationale for excluding MLP layers from averaging is that they are less specialized than the MLP layers, as noted by (Sukhbaatar et al., 2024)." *(Note: The text appears to contain a typo; it likely means attention layers are less specialized than MLP layers.)*
  - Section 1 references BTX which also excludes MLP layers from averaging, supporting this design choice as a community practice.
  - Neighbors like "Upcycling Large Language Models into Mixture of Experts" and "BAM! Just Like That" also use similar "upcycling" where FFN layers are replicated as experts and other layers are merged.
- **Break condition:** If significant domain-specific knowledge is encoded in the attention layers’ parameters (e.g., domain-specific positional biases or attention patterns), then averaging them will destroy that expertise, harming all downstream routing.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The ridge regression solution for router initialization is "schedule-invariant," meaning the order and batching of processing the domain datasets do not affect the final \( A^l \) and \( b^l \) matrices, enabling truly one-pass, embarrassingly parallel statistics collection.
- **Mechanism:** The update rules \( A^l \leftarrow A^l + F^{\top}F \) and \( b^l \leftarrow b^l + F^{\top}y \) are simple matrix additions. Because matrix addition is commutative and associative, the final accumulated values are identical regardless of the sequence in which data batches from different domains are processed. This allows each domain's data to be processed independently in parallel with only the final small matrices needing to be shared.
- **Core assumption:** The sufficient statistics for ridge regression (\( X^{\top}X \) and \( X^{\top}y \)) are additive and order-independent, a mathematical property of the least-squares objective.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 2.3: "the datasets can be accessed sequentially, as the ridge regression solution is schedule-invariant (Fanì et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2022)."
  - Equation 7 presents the update rule that ensures this property.
  - The cited works (Fanì et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2022) are on federated/online learning and support schedule-invariance; the application to MoE router initialization is novel.
- **Break condition:** The mechanism is mathematically guaranteed for the exact ridge regression objective; it would break only if the procedure were altered (e.g., using a running average or stochastic optimizer) or if numerical instability caused floating-point divergence.

## Foundational Learning
### Ridge Regression (Closed-Form Solution)
- **Why needed:** It is the core mathematical engine that turns domain labels into router weights without gradient-based optimization. Understanding the Tikhonov regularization parameter \( \lambda \) and the matrix inversion \( (X^{\top}X + \lambda I)^{-1}X^{\top}y \) is key to grasping DUME’s initialization.
- **Quick check:** What is the effect of increasing \( \lambda \) on the computed router weights \( W_* \), and why might this be important when domains have very different data sizes?

### Mixture of Experts (MoE) Sparse Routing
- **Why needed:** The target architecture is an MoE. One must understand that a router computes a weighted selection (e.g., Top-k) over experts per token, and that DUME uses top-1 routing for cost parity with dense models.
- **Quick check:** If the router is initialized perfectly (100% accuracy), what would be the computational cost (in FLOPs relative to a dense model) of a 4-expert MoE with top-1 routing versus a dense model?

### Upcycling / Model Merging
- **Why needed:** DUME is an "upcycling" technique. The foundational problem is that averaging fine-tuned models causes catastrophic interference; DUME’s novelty is how it merges (MoErging) and adds the routing layer (ridge regression).
- **Quick check:** Why might simply averaging the weights of two models fine-tuned on contradictory tasks fail, and how does creating an MoE instead of a single dense model address this?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Seed model -> MoErging (average non-MLP parameters, keep MLPs as experts) -> Ridge Regression Statistics Collector (forward domain data, accumulate A^l, b^l) -> Router Initializer (compute W^l_*) -> Final DUME Model (MoE with fixed routers).

### Critical Path
1. Train D dense experts on separate domain datasets.
2. MoErging: Create merged backbone by averaging all parameters except MLPs; store MLPs as experts.
3. Router Init: For each layer l, initialize A^l=0, b^l=0. For each domain d in parallel: forward all batches of domain d through the backbone (using its MLP expert for MoE blocks), update A^l += F^T F, b^l += F^T y (one-hot domain label).
4. Compute W^l_* = (A^l + λI)^-1 b^l and normalize columns. Set routers to these weights.
5. Deploy the MoE model; no further training.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Compute vs. Performance: Larger context for statistics collection improves router quality but costs more; diminishing returns after 2-8 tokens for CLM.
- Router Capacity (k): Top-1 routing matches dense compute cost; higher k offers little gain.
- Regularization (λ): Prevents overfitting to small domains; λ=0.01 robust for reasoning, CLM less sensitive.
- Data Requirement: Needs access to representative data from each domain to compute statistics; privacy constraints motivate training-free claim but data is still required for initialization.

### Failure Signatures
- Random routing performance: If DUME’s performance approaches the random routing baseline, ridge regression failed; check condition number of A^l or λ.
- Catastrophic forgetting of a domain: If a domain’s performance degrades to near model averaging, the router misroutes that domain; check distribution shift.
- Overall performance < single expert: Indicates merged backbone degradation or high router errors.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can DUME be applied when domain labels are unavailable during the ridge regression statistics collection phase?
- **Basis**: The conclusion identifies that DUME requires domain labels in the training dataset, which may be absent in federated settings with domain-uniform partitions.
- **Why unresolved**: Real-world decentralized training often lacks explicit domain annotations.
- **Resolution evidence**: Evaluate DUME using pseudo-labels from unsupervised domain clustering or classifier-free routing heuristics, comparing performance against the supervised variant.

### Open Question 2
Does the approximate ridge regression solution for deeper MoE routers (layers l > 1) degrade with increased model depth or domain heterogeneity?
- **Basis**: Section 2.3 admits that for l > 1 the input space varies across domains, making the solution non-optimal, though effects are deemed negligible based on 3B model results.
- **Why unresolved**: The claim is not theoretically guaranteed and may not hold for deeper architectures or more divergent domains.
- **Resolution evidence**: Analyze feature space alignment (e.g., CCA similarity) across domains in deeper layers for larger models (10B+) and more domains (10+), correlating with performance drops.

### Open Question 3
What is the scalability limit of DUME's dynamic expert addition before performance degrades due to router saturation or interference?
- **Basis**: The abstract and method claim scalability and dynamic addition without degradation, but experiments only use up to 5 domains.
- **Why unresolved**: Incremental addition of many experts could overfit the fixed ridge regression statistics or cause routing conflicts.
- **Resolution evidence**: Incrementally add experts (e.g., 20 steps) while measuring per-domain and average performance, tracking forgetting and routing entropy.

### Open Question 4
Can DUME's training-free router initialization generalize to non-language tasks or those with highly non-linear decision boundaries?
- **Basis**: BTX underperforms in reasoning tasks (Section 3.3), possibly due to task mismatch, and DUME is only evaluated on CLM and reasoning.
- **Why unresolved**: Ridge regression assumes linear separability in the feature space, which may fail for vision, speech, or complex reasoning.
- **Resolution evidence**: Apply DUME to multimodal or structured prediction tasks (e.g., VQA, code execution) and compare against learned routers, assessing when linear initialization suffices.

## Limitations
- Relies on the assumption that domain specialization resides primarily in MLP layers; if attention layers encode domain-specific knowledge, averaging them harms performance.
- Requires access to domain-labeled training data for router initialization, limiting applicability in privacy-sensitive or unlabeled settings.
- Scalability beyond a modest number of domains (tested up to 5) is unproven; many experts could cause routing interference or overfitting.

## Confidence
- Training-free router initialization: **High** (closed-form ridge regression is mathematically well-defined).
- Reported performance retention (92.8% CLM, 95.2% reasoning): **Medium** (empirical on specific benchmarks; variance across domains noted).
- Generalization to arbitrary new domains: **Low** (only OOD test with related datasets; no evidence for dissimilar tasks).
