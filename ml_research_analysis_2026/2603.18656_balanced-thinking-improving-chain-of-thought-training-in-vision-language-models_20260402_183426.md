# Balanced Thinking: Improving Chain of Thought Training in Vision Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arxiv-id:** 2603.18656
- **source-url:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18656
- **reference-count:** 36
- **primary result:** SCALe (Scheduled Curriculum Adaptive Loss) improves accuracy over vanilla SFT by up to 3% and over GRPO by up to 5.4%, while drastically reducing invalid generations.

## Executive Summary
Vision-language models (VLMs) fine‑tuned with standard supervised learning suffer from token imbalance: long ` traces dominate the loss, short but critical `<answer>` segments are under‑supervised, leading to verbose and often inaccurate outputs. We introduce SCALe, which decouples cross‑entropy loss over reasoning and answer segments and applies a cosine schedule that gradually shifts focus from reasoning to answer. Evaluated on ScienceQA and IconQA, SCALe‑SFT boosts accuracy by up to 3% over vanilla SFT; SCALe‑GRPO outperforms standard GRPO by up to 5.4%. Remarkably, SCALe‑SFT alone matches the performance of full SFT+GRPO while requiring only ~1/7 of the training time. The method also reduces generation failures (missing tags) by 8.62%–87.4% relative.

## Method Summary
SCALe modifies the SFT loss by splitting tokens into two segments: `<think>` and a combined `<answer>` (including the closing `</answer>` tag). For each segment, it computes mean cross‑entropy (per‑token loss averaged over the segment) to neutralize length bias. A cosine schedule changes a weight `w_t` applied to the reasoning segment from 1.0 to 0.5 over training, while the answer weight stays at 1.0. This curriculum starts with high reasoning emphasis to teach structured CoT, then shifts to answer correctness. Optionally, SCALe can be followed by GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization), where the reward combines tag placement accuracy and exact match with equal importance. Training uses LoRA adapters, bfloat16, DeepSpeed ZeRO2 on 8×H100; only one epoch of SFT is needed.

## Key Results
- SCALe‑SFT improves exact‑match accuracy over vanilla SFT by **up to 3%** on ScienceQA and IconQA.
- SCALe‑GRPO outperforms standard GRPO by **up to 5.4%**.
- SCALe‑SFT matches the performance of full SFT+GRPO while using **only ~1/7** of the training time.
- The method reduces generation failures (missing `<answer>` tags) by **8.62%–87.4%** relative to baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Uniform cross‑entropy loss in standard SFT causes the model to overemphasize lengthy `<think>` tokens, leading to verbose but inaccurate reasoning because the loss is dominated by the numerous reasoning tokens.
- **Mechanism:** Total loss is summed over all tokens; `` sections are much longer than `<answer>` sections (∼150× on average), so gradients are primarily determined by reasoning errors. This biases the model towards fluency in reasoning at the expense of answer correctness.
- **Core assumption:** Evaluation depends primarily on the final answer; reasoning is a means to that end.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "In standard SFT, all tokens contribute equally to the loss, even though reasoning data are inherently token‑imbalanced. Long `<think>` traces overshadow short but task-critical `<answer>` segments..."
  - [section] Section 3.1: "This uniform weighting is suboptimal for reasoning‑oriented vision‑language tasks... evaluation metrics depend primarily on the final short answer segment."
  - [corpus] Weak – neighboring papers do not directly address token imbalance.
- **Break condition:** If reasoning and answer lengths were similar, or if evaluation rewarded reasoning length, the imbalance would not be problematic.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A cosine schedule that gradually shifts weight from reasoning to answer stabilizes training and improves answer accuracy by first teaching structured reasoning and then aligning it with correct answers.
- **Mechanism:** Early training uses a higher weight on reasoning tokens (`w_t` starts at 1.0) to encourage coherent CoT generation when the model is unstable. Later, `w_t` decays (to 0.5) while answer weight remains 1.0, increasing emphasis on factual correctness once a stable reasoning structure is established.
- **Core assumption:** Reasoning structure should be learned first; then answer precision can be refined.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "SCALe‑SFT gradually shifts the focus from `<think>` to `<answer>` throughout training via a cosine scheduling policy..."
  - [section] Section 3.1: "Early in training, higher `w_t` encourages robust reasoning supervision, while later iterations shift emphasis toward answer accuracy... This curriculum‑like schedule improves both convergence stability and reasoning–answer alignment."
  - [corpus] Weak – neighboring RL‑focused works use reward shaping rather than scheduled SFT loss.
- **Break condition:** If the schedule were reversed (high answer weight early), reasoning might never develop; if the decay were too abrupt, training could become unstable.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Computing mean cross‑entropy within each segment (length‑independent) prevents longer reasoning traces from dominating the loss, ensuring equal supervisory influence per segment.
- **Mechanism:** The loss for a segment is averaged over its tokens (`L_seg = (1/N_seg) * sum CE`). This normalizes the contribution of each segment, so a long `<think>` does not contribute more total loss than a short `<answer>` even if the sum of CE over the segment is larger.
- **Core assumption:** Reasoning and answer segments should be treated as equally important supervisory units, regardless of token count.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] Section 3.1: "To prevent longer reasoning sequences from dominating the loss, each segment loss is computed in a length‑independent manner... This formulation computes mean cross‑entropy ensuring that longer reasoning traces do not reduce the influence of shorter examples."
  - [abstract] Implicit: "decouples the cross‑entropy loss over `` and `<answer>` segments with dynamic, length‑independent weighting."
  - [corpus] Weak – none of the neighboring papers discuss length‑independent loss normalization.
- **Break condition:** If segment lengths were naturally balanced, the mean vs. sum distinction would be negligible, but given the extreme imbalance, this normalization is critical.

## Foundational Learning
- **Chain‑of‑Thought (CoT)**
  - *Why needed:* SCALe operates on outputs structured with `<think>` and `<answer>` tags, assuming the model generates intermediate reasoning before answering.
  - *Quick check:* What is the purpose of CoT in VLMs? → To produce interpretable reasoning that leads to the final answer.
- **Supervised Fine‑Tuning (SFT)**
  - *Why needed:* SCALe modifies the standard SFT loss; understanding baseline SFT is essential to see the improvement.
  - *Quick check:* How does standard SFT train a model on demonstration data? → By minimizing token‑level cross‑entropy between predictions and targets.
- **Length Bias in Loss Functions**
  - *Why needed:* The core problem is that summing cross‑entropy over tokens gives longer sequences disproportionate influence.
  - *Quick check:* Why might summing CE loss over tokens bias training toward longer segments? → Because longer segments contribute more total loss, leading to larger gradient updates.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Data (Vision‑R1) → Model (VLM + LoRA adapters) → SCALe loss (segmented mean CE + cosine weights) → AdamW (DeepSpeed ZeRO2) → Checkpoint → (optional GRPO phase with reward `r = λ_tag·r_tag + λ_ans·r_ans`) → Evaluation (lmms‑eval).
- **Critical path:**  
  1. Tag parsing: identify `<think>` and `<answer>` spans.  
  2. Compute L_think and L_answer as mean CE over tokens in each segment.  
  3. Apply scheduled weights (cosine decay of `w_t`, `w_a` fixed).  
  4. Backpropagate total loss.  
  5. Optionally, initialize GRPO from SCALe checkpoint and train with combined rewards.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - SCALe‑SFT vs. full SFT+GRPO: SCALe offers ~1/7 training time and comparable accuracy, but GRPO may still yield marginal gains.  
  - Schedule hyperparameters: fixed start/end weights (1.0→0.5) work across models; tuning could improve further but adds complexity.  
  - Length‑independent normalization: using mean avoids length bias but could underweight short segments with high per‑token loss; keeping answer weight fixed at 1.0 mitigates this.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Persistent verbosity: reasoning weight decays too slowly.  
  - Collapsed reasoning: answer weight too high early.  
  - Invalid generations: missing tags if closing token not grouped with answer or if answer loss weight is too low.  
  - Generalization gap: schedule derived from typical CoT may not suit datasets with different token ratios.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline comparison:** Train Qwen2.5‑VL‑3B with vanilla SFT vs. SCALe‑SFT on ScienceQA; expect ∼2‑3% absolute accuracy gain.  
  2. **Ablation (schedule):** Replace cosine schedule with fixed weights (reasoning=answer=1.0) and compare to SCALe; expect reduced gains (∼0.5% less).  
  3. **Validity check:** Measure % of generations missing `<answer>` tags; SCALe‑SFT should reduce this by 8.62%–87.4% relative to vanilla SFT.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does SCALe generalize to multi‑image vision‑language tasks and other modalities beyond single‑image VQA?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Conclusion (page 7): "Future work will extend the proposed framework to a broader range of and modalities and multi‑image cases."
- **Why unresolved:** Current evaluation is limited to single‑image benchmarks. Multi‑image reasoning involves different attention patterns and context integration, which may affect token imbalance and SCALe’s scheduled weighting.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate SCALe on multi‑image datasets (e.g., CLEVR‑Multi, NLVR2) and other modalities (e.g., video reasoning) using the same pipeline; compare accuracy gains and invalid generation rates against single‑image results.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can an automatic online weight tuning mechanism, as opposed to the predefined cosine schedule, further optimize SCALe’s performance or reduce hyperparameter sensitivity?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Conclusion (page 7): "Another research direction will be to explore an automatic online weight tuning."
- **Why unresolved:** The fixed cosine decay (1.0→0.5) may not be optimal for all datasets or models and requires manual tuning. An online method could adapt based on training dynamics (e.g., validation loss, reasoning quality).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement adaptive scheduling strategies (e.g., driven by validation answer accuracy or reasoning trace length) and compare them to the fixed cosine schedule across multiple models and benchmarks; measure final accuracy, convergence speed, and stability.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the SCALe method improve chain‑of‑thought reasoning in pure language‑only tasks without visual inputs?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Conclusion (page 7): "We also plan to apply our method to language‑only reasoning datasets."
- **Why unresolved:** SCALe was designed for VLMs; token imbalance may be similar in language tasks, but visual grounding could introduce unique challenges. It is unclear whether the same schedule (cosine from 1.0 to 0.5) transfers to language tasks where reasoning traces have different statistics.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply SCALe to language reasoning benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, MATH) using language models of comparable scale. Compare accuracy and reasoning quality against standard SFT and other CoT training methods, and analyze whether scheduled weighting yields similar benefits in concise reasoning and reduced invalid generations.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** What is the primary mechanism by which SCALe reduces invalid generations (e.g., unclosed `<answer>` tags)? Is it due to the length‑independent loss normalization, the scheduling of weights, or both?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper shows SCALe reduces invalid outputs (Table 2) but does not isolate contributing factors. The method includes two components: segment‑wise mean loss (length‑independent) and scheduled weighting. The ablation in Table 3 compares scheduled vs fixed weights but both use length‑independent normalization, so the individual effect of normalization is not tested against a length‑dependent baseline.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding the mechanism could lead to simpler variants or better designs. If normalization alone suffices, scheduling might be unnecessary; if scheduling is key, we might optimize it further.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct an ablation with four conditions: (1) vanilla SFT (no segment separation), (2) segment‑separated loss with length‑dependent weighting (sum instead of mean), (3) segment‑separated with length‑independent but fixed weights (as in SFT‑FW), and (4) full SCALe with scheduling. Compare invalid generation rates and other metrics to disentangle the effects.

## Limitations
- Generalization to other VLM tasks/datasets (e.g., MME, natural image QA) remains unproven.
- Hyperparameter choices (loss weights, schedule curve) may be dataset‑specific and require tuning.
- Training details (LoRA rank, batch size, optimizer) are omitted, hindering exact replication.
- SCALe requires explicit `<think>`/`<answer>` tag formatting; other formats need adaptation.
- Neighboring literature does not directly address token imbalance, so supporting evidence is limited.
