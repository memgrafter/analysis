# Chart Deep Research in LVLMs via Parallel Relative Policy Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.06677
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06677
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: PRPO fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-VL-7B yields up to 13.26% absolute improvement on MCDR-Bench and 6.36% on ChartQAPro, nearly matching Claude-3.7.

## Executive Summary
Chart deep research—complex reasoning from visual data—remains hindered by training conflicts (multi-dimensional reward interference, heterogeneous data gradient clashes) and evaluation focused on shallow QA. This paper introduces PRPO (Parallel Relative Policy Optimization), which parallelizes optimization across reward dimensions and partitions data by capability, and MCDR-Bench, a benchmark that injects controllable errors to convert subjective report generation into objective error identification. Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B with PRPO yields substantial improvements: on MCDR-Bench, mean scores increase from 61.71% to 69.62% (+7.91) and from 63.00% to 76.26% (+13.26) under chain-of-thought prompting, with consistent gains across all five analysis dimensions (background, facts, relationships, report, forecast). On the external ChartQAPro benchmark, PRPO achieves a +6.36% gain (41.33% → 47.69%). The approach narrows the gap with state-of-the-art commercial models (e.g., 76.89% vs Claude-3.7’s 77.08%). Overall, the unified PRPO + MCDR-Bench framework systematically advances chart deep research by resolving training bottlenecks and enabling objective evaluation.

## Method Summary
PRPO unifies two components: Reward-PRPO computes advantages separately for each reward dimension, normalizing rewards within each dimension to avoid interference; Data-PRPO groups training samples by cognitive capability (e.g., fact extraction, relationship construction) and normalizes advantages within each group, with iterative outlier relegation to maintain robust statistics. The overall objective sums per-dimension, per-partition clipped policy losses with optional weights, combined with a KL penalty. Training uses a mix of chart reasoning datasets (SophiaVL-R1-130k, R1-Onevision) annotated with capability labels, fine-tuning a base LVLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B). Evaluation is performed on MCDR-Bench, which injects five error types (background, facts, relationships, report, forecast) into gold-standard reports to enable objective error detection.

## Key Results
- PRPO boosts MCDR-Bench mean accuracy by up to 13.26% (standard 61.71%→69.62%, CoT 63.00%→76.26%) with uniform gains across all five dimensions.
- On ChartQAPro, PRPO improves accuracy by 6.36% (41.33%→47.69%), narrowing the gap with Claude-3.7 to 0.19% (76.89% vs 77.08%).
- PRPO consistently outperforms GRPO under both direct and chain-of-thought prompting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Decomposing multi-dimensional rewards into parallel optimization streams prevents signal interference and preserves distinct optimization gradients.
- **Mechanism**: For a question *q* with *G* sampled outputs, rewards *R*<sub>i</sub><sup>(k)</sup> across *K* dimensions are normalized separately per dimension (mean *µ<sup>(k)</sup>*, std *σ<sup>(k)</sup>*) to compute advantage *Â<sub>i</sub>*<sup>(k)</sup>. - **Core assumption**: Different reward dimensions (e.g., factual accuracy, logical consistency, format) have non-overlapping optimization signals that cancel when aggregated.

- **Break condition**: If reward dimensions are perfectly correlated (e.g., factual accuracy always implies logical consistency), parallelization provides no benefit and increases computational cost.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Partitioning training data by cognitive capability (e.g., "fact extraction" vs. "relationship construction") resolves gradient dominance conflicts from heterogeneous data types.
- **Mechanism**: Samples are assigned coarse-grained `capability_uid` groups. Within each group *m*, advantages *Â<sub>i</sub>*<sup>(m)</sup> use group-specific statistics (*µ<sup>(m)</sup>*, *σ<sup>(m)</sup>*). Outlier samples (|*Â*| > *τ*) are relegated to individual rollout-level optimization to prevent skewing group statistics.
- **Core assumption**: Charts requiring different cognitive skills generate qualitatively distinct gradient patterns; mixing them causes simpler-task gradients to dominate.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "partitions data by capability... effectively disentangling conflicts between heterogeneous data"
  - [section 4.3] Data-PRPO algorithm with iterative validation and outlier relegation (Eq. 6–8).
  - [corpus] Corpus includes works on multi-task learning but not capability-based partitioning for LVLM chart reasoning; assumption is paper-specific.
- **Break condition**: If all chart tasks share a common underlying reasoning structure (e.g., all reduce to data extraction), capability partitioning is artificial and harms generalization.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Transforming subjective report generation into objective error identification via controlled error injection enables scalable, fine-grained evaluation of deep research capabilities.
- **Mechanism**: Five error types (background, facts, relationships, report, forecast) are injected into gold-standard reports. Each error is uniquely detectable against the original correct statement, converting generation evaluation into binary classification.
- **Core assumption**: Deep research quality can be approximated by the absence of specific, injectable errors; error types map cleanly to capability dimensions.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "injects controllable errors to convert subjective report generation into objective error identification"
  - [section 3] Multi-agent annotation pipeline and "error uniqueness principle"; Appendix E details error injection.
  - [corpus] Corpus benchmarks (ChartQA, InfoChartQA) use QA; none use error-injection for report evaluation—this is a novel paradigm.
- **Break condition**: If chart analysis admits multiple equally valid reports, injected errors may not be uniquely "wrong," or errors could introduce artifacts that change the task's nature.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Multi-dimensional reward interference
  - *Why needed here*: Understanding why scalar reward aggregation fails for complex chart reasoning is prerequisite to grasping PRPO's reward parallelization.
  - *Quick check question*: If a model receives separate rewards for "factual accuracy" (+1) and "conciseness" (+1), why might summing them yield a misleading advantage of +2 for a verbose but accurate response?
- **Concept**: Heterogeneous gradient conflict
  - *Why needed here*: Recognizing that gradients from different chart-task types (e.g., simple extraction vs. causal reasoning) have mismatched scales and directions explains the need for data partitioning.
  - *Quick check question*: In a batch mixing "read this number" and "explain this trend" samples, which gradient is likely to dominate without partitioning, and why?
- **Concept**: Evaluation via discriminative tasks
  - *Why needed here*: The shift from generation to error detection underpins MCDR-Bench's design; understanding its rationale is key to interpreting the benchmark's scores.
  - *Quick check question*: How does injecting a "false background" error allow objective scoring, whereas asking a model to "write a background" does not?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Data sampler → Rollout generator → Reward scaler → Advantage calculator → Policy updater.

**Critical path**: Sample → Partition by `capability_uid` → Generate rollouts → Compute per-dimension rewards → Normalize within `(m,k)` groups → Compute advantages → Sum weighted losses → Update *π<sub>θ</sub>*.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- *Partition granularity*: Fewer capability groups → simpler stats but risk intra-group heterogeneity; more groups → purer stats but fewer samples per group and higher variance.
- *Outlier relegation threshold τ*: Low τ → many samples revert to individual optimization (loses grouping benefits); high τ → outliers corrupt group stats.
- *Weighting scheme*: Fixed *λ* (paper uses mean) vs. learned; fixed is simpler but may not reflect task difficulty.

**Failure signatures**:
- *Stagnant per-dimension rewards*: Indicates reward parallelization is not helping; dimensions may be redundant or weights mis-set.
- *High outlier relegation rate*: Suggests capability partitions are poorly defined or reward distributions are heavy-tailed.
- *Degradation on simple tasks*: PRPO may over-optimize for complex dimensions if *λ* weights are unbalanced.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Ablation – Reward-PRPO only**: Train with parallel rewards but no data partitioning; expect gains on GRPO but residual conflicts from heterogeneous data.
2. **Ablation – Data-PRPO only**: Train with capability partitioning but scalarized rewards; expect gains from better gradient alignment but persistent reward interference.
3. **MCDR-Bench dimension breakdown**: Evaluate PRPO vs. GRPO on each error type (BG, FE, RL, DR, F/P); hypothesis: PRPO improves uniformly, especially on high-dimensional tasks (RL, DR).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Does MCDR-Bench's controlled error injection adequately represent the diversity of real chart deep research errors?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] from Section 3 and Appendix E, where error types are manually designed for five dimensions, with filtering of imperfectly categorized samples.
- **Why unresolved**: The set of injected errors is finite and heuristic; real-world analytical errors may include nuanced cognitive biases or domain-specific pitfalls not captured.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Construct an error taxonomy from large-scale human analyst logs; correlate model scores on MCDR-Bench with expert evaluations on authentic chart analysis tasks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How robust is PRPO's parallel optimization when scaling to many reward dimensions or highly heterogeneous data distributions?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] in Section 4. PRPO decomposes optimization across K reward dimensions and M data groups; experiments use fixed configurations.
- **Why unresolved**: Interactions among many dimensions could reintroduce interference; data distribution shifts might destabilize partition-based advantages and gradient updates.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Ablation studies varying K and M on diverse datasets; monitor gradient conflict metrics, partition stability, and performance relative to GRPO.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Does capability-based data partitioning in PRPO generalize to unseen chart domains or modalities beyond the training distribution?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] from Section 4.3, where partitions are derived from GPT-4o annotations on specific chart collections.
- **Why unresolved**: Partitioning criteria may be tied to the source datasets; novel chart types (e.g., scientific plots) could exhibit different capability structures, leading to suboptimal grouping.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Cross-domain experiments: train on business dashboards, test on scientific or infographic charts; measure performance drop versus GRPO and analyze partition consistency.

## Limitations
- Inaccessibility of MCDR-Bench and RL datasets hinders independent verification.
- Insufficient reporting of hyperparameters (λ weights, τ threshold, KL coefficient) and reward definitions.
- Unspecified evaluation protocols (prompts, few-shot examples, generation settings) may affect score comparability.

## Next Checks
1. **Benchmark & Data Release**: Demand public release of MCDR-Bench (with injection code) and the RL capability-annotated datasets to enable replication and audit of annotation quality.
2. **Ablation Isolation**: Re-train with separate ablations—(a) only Reward-PRPO (no data partitioning), (b) only Data-PRPO (scalarized rewards)—to quantify each mechanism’s individual impact on the reported gains.
3. **Reward Correlation Analysis**: Compute pairwise correlations (Spearman’s ρ) between the five reward dimensions on a held-out set. High correlations (>0.8) would invalidate the core assumption of non-overlapping signals for parallelization.
