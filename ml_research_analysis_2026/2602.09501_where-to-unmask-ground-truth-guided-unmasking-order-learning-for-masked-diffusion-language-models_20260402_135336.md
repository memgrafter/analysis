# Where-to-Unmask: Ground-Truth-Guided Unmasking Order Learning for Masked Diffusion Language Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.09501
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09501
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Supervised unmasking planner (trained via Gt-Margin oracle) improves reasoning accuracy by up to +24pt (GSM8K) over heuristic Margin without modifying token model.

## Executive Summary
Masked diffusion language models (MDLMs) generate text by iteratively unmasking positions, but lack supervised guidance for the critical "where-to-unmask" decision. This paper introduces Gt-Margin, an oracle score that uses ground-truth tokens to rank masked positions by the confidence margin between the correct token and its strongest competitor. The authors then train a supervised unmasking planner via learning-to-rank to imitate this oracle ordering from partially masked states, using a reconstruct‑then‑plan input that includes the token model’s predictions. The oracle Gt‑Margin order dramatically boosts accuracy on reasoning benchmarks (e.g., +24.4 points on GSM8K with LLaDA‑8B). The learned planner, applied only in the early decoding steps (partial‑plan), further improves performance by +10.0 points on GSM8K and +10.5 points on MATH over the heuristic Margin baseline, without altering the token model.

## Method Summary
The core approach is two‑fold. First, they define Gt‑Margin: for each masked position, the score is the predicted probability of the true token minus the maximum probability of any other token. This score induces an oracle unmasking order that prioritizes easier positions, reducing early error propagation. Second, they train a planner network to mimic this oracle. Planner training uses trajectories generated by controlled decoding with Gt‑Margin. For each training step, a partial state \(x_t\) is sampled via stochastic rank‑conditioned masking based on the oracle rank vector. The planner’s input combines the original prompt, the token model’s argmax reconstruction of masked positions (\(x'_0\)), and the current masked state \(x_t\). A listwise ranking loss (PiRank, a relaxed NDCG@k) teaches the planner to assign higher scores to positions that should be unmasked earlier according to the oracle. During inference, the planner is used only for the first half of unmasking steps (partial‑plan), after which the simpler heuristic Margin takes over. This design targets the high‑impact early phase while keeping computational overhead modest.

## Key Results
- Gt‑Margin oracle order yields large accuracy gains: e.g., +24.4 points on GSM8K with LLaDA‑8B compared to the heuristic Margin baseline.
- The learned partial‑plan unmasking planner improves GSM8K by +10.0 points and MATH by +10.5 points over Margin, demonstrating effective imitation of the oracle.
- The planner underperforms the heuristic on Sudoku (0.085 vs 0.110 accuracy), indicating challenges in distilling oracle ordering for highly structured tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Gt‑Margin induces an oracle unmasking order that prioritizes positions where the correct token is confidently separated from alternatives, reducing early error accumulation.
- **Mechanism:** For each masked position, compute the probability margin between the ground‑truth token and the top competing candidate. Positions with large positive margins are “easy” (greedy decode will be correct if unmasked now); small/negative margins are “hard.” Unmasking high‑margin positions first fixes correct tokens early, providing reliable context for subsequent harder positions.
- **Core assumption:** Early token errors are disproportionately harmful because later predictions condition on earlier ones; a safe early unmasking trajectory constrains the generation path toward correctness.
- **Evidence anchors:** Controlled decoding experiments show Gt‑Margin dramatically outperforms Margin (e.g., +24 pt on GSM8K). Perturbations to early steps cause large accuracy drops, supporting the harm of early errors.
- **Break condition:** If the token model’s confidence margins are poorly calibrated (high margin for wrong tokens), Gt‑Margin’s ordering could be misleading.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A supervised planner trained via learning‑to‑rank to imitate Gt‑Margin’s ordering distills a deployable where‑to‑unmask policy that generalizes to inference.
- **Mechanism:** Generate oracle trajectories by running controlled decoding with Gt‑Margin on training data, producing rank vectors. Train a planner network to score masked positions so its ranking matches the oracle ranks, using a listwise ranking loss (PiRank/relaxed NDCG@k).
- **Core assumption:** The oracle ordering induced by Gt‑Margin on the training distribution is a sufficiently good target that a planner, with an appropriate input representation, can learn to approximate it from partially observed states.
- **Evidence anchors:** Planner training uses tuples \((c, \hat{x}^0, r)\) from Gt‑Margin decoding. The learned planner improves over Margin on GSM8K and MATH, confirming it can imitate the oracle in practice.
- **Break condition:** If token model predictions (used to construct \(x'_0\) in planner input) are misaligned with the oracle’s view, the planner may learn a distorted mapping. Ablation shows performance drops without token predictions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Applying the learned planner only during early decoding steps (partial‑plan) maximizes its benefit by targeting the high‑impact phase where oracle guidance matters most.
- **Mechanism:** Based on evidence that oracle ordering matters primarily in early steps, use the planner for the first \(\rho\) fraction (e.g., 50%) of unmasking steps, then switch to the heuristic Margin. This focuses computational effort on the critical phase.
- **Core assumption:** The marginal benefit of oracle‑like ordering decays after a certain fraction of tokens are fixed; heuristics become adequate once sufficient context is established.
- **Evidence anchors:** Figure 3 shows using Gt‑Margin for only the first ~50% of steps recovers near‑full performance. Table 2’s “Ours” uses partial‑plan while “Ours w/o partial plan” degrades, confirming the benefit.
- **Break condition:** If a task’s difficulty is concentrated in late‑stage resolution, early planning might be insufficient (e.g., Sudoku where planner underperforms).

## Foundational Learning
- **Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLMs):** Generative framework that iteratively unmask tokens according to a noise schedule. Understanding the forward/reverse process, masking schedule, and decoding strategies (greedy vs. sampling) is essential. - **Learning‑to‑Rank (LTR):** A family of methods for training models to order items. The paper formulates where‑to‑unmask as an LTR problem, using a listwise objective (PiRank) to optimize top‑k ranking accuracy. Quick check: Why is a listwise ranking loss (like PiRank) more suitable than pairwise or pointwise loss for this task?
- **Reconstruct‑then‑Plan Input Design:** The planner’s input is \(\text{prompt} + x'_0 + x_t\), where \(x'_0\) is the token model’s argmax fill of masked positions. This hybrid representation provides both a hypothesis of missing content and the current mask pattern. Quick check: What two pieces of information does this input provide, and why is each necessary?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Base Token Model (\(\mu_\theta\)) -> provides token predictions and argmax reconstruction -> input to Planner Model (\(f_\phi\)). Oracle Score (Gt‑Margin) generates rank vectors \(r\) for training trajectories. Training State Sampler uses \(r\) to produce partial states \(x_t\). Ranking Loss (\(L_{\text{PiRank}}\)) compares planner scores \(s\) to target ranks (derived from \(r\)) over masked positions. The trained Planner is then used during early steps of inference.

### Critical Path
1. Generate oracle trajectory \(r\) by controlled decoding with Gt‑Margin.
2. Sample diffusion time \(t\) and create \(x_t\) via stochastic rank‑conditioned masking based on \(r\).
3. Compute \(x'_0\) by filling \(x_t\) with token model’s argmax predictions.
4. Run planner on \(\text{prompt} + x'_0 + x_t\) to obtain scores \(s_i\) for masked positions.
5. Construct target relevance \(y\) from \(r\) restricted to the masked set \(M_t\).
6. Minimize PiRank loss \(L_{\text{PiRank}}(s, y)\); repeat across examples.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Planner input richness:** Including \(x'_0\) improves performance (ablation “w/o token pred” hurts), but adds noise if token model is poorly calibrated.
- **Planner application duration:** Partial‑plan (first 50% of steps) yields better results than using planner throughout; full‑plan degrades performance, suggesting early steps are highest ROI.
- **Computational cost:** Planner adds one forward pass per step during its active phase; partial‑plan mitigates this overhead. Planner is lightweight (MLP head) relative to the base model.

### Failure Signatures
- Planner significantly underperforms heuristic Margin (as on Sudoku), possibly due to Gt‑Margin being a poor teacher for structured tasks, rank‑conditioned masking distribution mismatch, or insufficient planner capacity.
- Training instability with PiRank loss (e.g., temperature \(\tau\) or gradient norms) may occur; paper uses \(\tau=5\) and max grad norm 1.0.
- Distribution shift if token model is retuned (e.g., LoRA changes predictions) without updating planner or oracle trajectories.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Replicate controlled decoding oracle gap:** Using Algorithm 1, vary only the order score (Random, AR, Margin, Gt‑Margin) on a small validation set. Verify Gt‑Margin substantially outperforms Margin on a reasoning sample (e.g., +24pt on GSM8K).
2. **Validate rank‑conditioned masking:** Given an oracle rank \(r\) and a sampled \(x_t\), check that unmasked positions tend to have lower (earlier) ranks than masked ones. Compute average rank of unmasked vs. masked across many samples.
3. **Smoke‑test planner training:** Train the planner for a few steps on a tiny subset. Monitor PiRank loss and a simple ranking metric (Spearman correlation between planner scores \(s\) and oracle ranks \(r\) on \(M_t\)). Expect loss to decrease and correlation to rise. Also check that planner’s top‑ranked positions often lie within the oracle’s top‑k.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why does the learned unmasking planner fail to match Gt‑Margin’s near‑perfect Sudoku accuracy, while the heuristic Margin already performs reasonably? Understanding this gap is key for structured reasoning tasks.
2. Is the fixed 50% partial‑plan schedule optimal, or could a dynamic switching criterion between planner and heuristic improve performance? An adaptive rule might better balance cost and accuracy across tasks.
3. How does the token model’s quality (calibration, confidence distribution) affect planner effectiveness, and could joint training of token model and planner yield mutual gains? Token model errors may propagate to planner decisions.
4. Does Gt‑Margin constitute the optimal oracle ordering under greedy token updates, or are there alternative ground‑truth‑guided scores that could induce even better orders? Identifying a theoretically optimal oracle could refine the training signal.

## Limitations
- Exact LoRA hyperparameters (rank, alpha) and some training details are unspecified, requiring reasonable defaults for reproduction.
- Dataset splits, precise completion‑length thresholds, and tokenization/prompt templates are not detailed; these must be adopted from related work.
- Planner underperformance on Sudoku suggests limited generalization to tasks with strong structural constraints.
- The planner relies on the token model’s argmax reconstructions; distribution shift may occur if the token model’s predictions change after fine‑tuning.

## Confidence
- Gt‑Margin ordering yields large accuracy gains: High
- Learned planner imitates oracle and outperforms heuristics: Medium
- Partial‑plan (early‑phase only) maximizes benefit: High
