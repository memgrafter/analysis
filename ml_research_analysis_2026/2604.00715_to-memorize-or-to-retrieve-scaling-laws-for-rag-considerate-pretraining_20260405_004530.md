# To Memorize or to Retrieve: Scaling Laws for RAG-Considerate Pretraining
## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2604.00715
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00715
*   **Reference count:** 27
*   **Primary result:** A 3D scaling law (model size, pretraining tokens, retrieval tokens) fits RAG performance well and identifies a crossover at D/N≈4.14 where retrieval efficiently substitutes for pretraining.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how to optimally allocate a fixed data budget between pretraining and retrieval for RAG systems. Training OLMo-2 models from 30M to 3B parameters, the authors find retrieval consistently improves performance over parametric-only baselines. They introduce a 3D scaling law that models loss as a function of model size (N), pretraining tokens (D), and retrieval store tokens (R). The analysis reveals retrieval's marginal benefit decreases with model scale and is task-dependent, with a key crossover point (D/N ≈ 4.14) beyond which each retrieval token effectively replaces multiple pretraining tokens. These findings provide quantitative guidance for designing compute- and data-efficient RAG systems.

## Method Summary
The study conducts a factorial experiment across model scales (30M–3B), pretraining data amounts (D, 1–150× parameters), and retrieval store sizes (R, 1–20× parameters), with total tokens D+R ≤ 100B. Models are trained from scratch on the DCLM corpus. A fixed FAISS index (using Qwen3-Embedding-8B) is built from a held-out corpus slice, with nested indices ensuring fair retrieval scaling comparisons. Performance is evaluated via RAG-Evaluation-Harness using top-5 retrieval and perplexity as the primary metric. A 3D scaling law, L(N,D,R) = A·N^(-α) + B·D^(-β) - C·log(1+ηR/10^9) + L0, is fitted to the results. Substitutability (σ) and marginal benefit (κ) are derived by projecting RAG loss onto the parametric scaling surface.

## Key Results
*   Retrieval yields consistent perplexity improvements across all model scales; smaller models (e.g., 30M) gain the most per retrieval token.
*   A crossover at D/N ≈ 4.14 marks where retrieval becomes an efficient substitute for pretraining; beyond this, each retrieval token replaces multiple pretraining tokens in reducing loss.
*   The proposed 3D scaling law fits well (cross-validation error 2–13%), but extrapolation reliability is low for reasoning-heavy tasks (e.g., PIQA LOMO ARE ~40%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Retrieval can substitute for pretraining tokens in saturated regimes, quantified by a crossover threshold.
*   **Mechanism:** When pretraining data per parameter (D/N) exceeds ~4.14 tokens/parameter, the loss reduction from one retrieval token is equivalent to that from multiple pretraining tokens. This is computed by projecting the RAG loss onto the fitted parametric-only scaling surface and solving for the saved D.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Crossover explicitly stated in abstract and section 4.3; derived from scaling law projections.
*   **Break condition:** Mis-specification of the retrieval gain functional form or insufficient data in high-D/N regimes could invalidate the threshold.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** The marginal benefit of retrieval decreases with model size and is strongly task-dependent.
*   **Mechanism:** Larger models internalize more knowledge during pretraining, reducing reliance on external retrieval. Task dependence arises because retrieval primarily aids factual recall, not computational reasoning; hence gains are smaller on reasoning-heavy tasks.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.3 shows decreasing gains with model scale; section 5 and Table 1 (higher ARE on reasoning tasks) support task dependence.
*   **Break condition:** A significantly stronger retriever could flatten the scale trend; mis-categorization of task type could obscure the pattern.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** A 3D scaling law (L = A·N^(-α) + B·D^(-β) - C·log(1+ηR/10^9) + L0) predicts joint optimization under a fixed budget.
*   **Mechanism:** The law combines classic power-law terms for model and pretraining scale with a logarithmic term for retrieval gains. Fitting parameters to perplexity data allows estimating optimal (D,R) splits for given N and total tokens T.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and section 4.2 report good fit quality (CV ARE 2–13%); appendix shows robustness to alternative retrieval-term forms.
*   **Break condition:** Log-form may fail if retrieval gains are non-diminishing or if N-R interactions are more complex; extrapolation beyond observed R is risky (saturated η).

## Foundational Learning
*   **Scaling Laws for Language Models**
    *   **Why needed:** The paper extends 2D laws (L(N,D)) to 3D by adding retrieval. Understanding the power-law baseline (L ∝ N^(-α) + D^(-β)) is prerequisite.
    *   **Quick check:** What does a larger β exponent imply about sensitivity to pretraining data?
*   **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**
    *   **Why needed:** The study treats the retrieval store as an alternative data sink; core trade-offs depend on how RAG shifts knowledge from weights to an external index.
    *   **Quick check:** How does the computational cost profile of RAG differ from standard pretraining?
*   **Resource Allocation Under Constraint**
    *   **Why needed:** The central question is a constrained optimization: given total tokens T = D + R, find (D,R) minimizing L(N,D,R). Iso-loss contours and trade-off frontiers are key.
    *   **Quick check:** Along D+R=constant, what loss surface shape would indicate retrieval is a perfect substitute for pretraining?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    OLMo-2 Model (30M–3B) -> Pretraining on DCLM (D tokens) -> Build FAISS Index from Held-out DCLM (R tokens) -> RAG Evaluation (top-5) -> Perplexity
*   **Critical path:** Corpus permutation -> Index construction (fixed embedding, chunking) -> Training runs across (N,D,R) grid -> Evaluation -> Scaling law fitting.
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    - *Retriever isolation vs. realism*: Single frozen retriever isolates R-scale effects but likely understates gains from stronger pipelines.
    - *Metric choice*: Perplexity provides smooth scaling but may not capture generative quality or factuality.
    - *Corpus uniformity*: All data from DCLM; results may not generalize to heterogeneous mixtures.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    - High LOMO ARE (>15%) on reasoning tasks (e.g., PIQA 39.82%) → scaling law extrapolation unreliable.
    - Negative marginal benefit κ at high D/N (e.g., OpenBookQA σ@100× = -10.49) → over-allocation of retrieval harms.
    - Retrieval term saturation (η=10.0) → observed R range insufficient to bound true scaling exponent.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Parametric baseline replication**: Train models at varying (N,D) with R=0. Fit L(N,D) power-law (Eq. 1). Confirm low CV ARE (<5%) and consistent α,β.
    2.  **3D law validation**: Train models across (N,D,R) grid. Fit Eq. 2. Check CV ARE ≤13%; note which tasks have bounded vs. saturated η.
    3.  **Trade-off curve extraction**: For each N and total T, sweep D from 1×N to T-1×N (R=T-D). Compute σ (tokens saved per retrieval token) and κ (marginal benefit). Verify crossover at D/N≈4.14 and decreasing κ with N.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** How do stronger retrieval systems (e.g., reranking, adaptive chunking, LLM-based relevance) shift the optimal pretraining-retrieval allocation under a fixed data budget?
*   **Basis:** Explicitly proposed in Future Work.
*   **Why unresolved:** Current study uses a simple, fixed retriever; gains from more sophisticated pipelines are unknown.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments incrementally improving retrieval quality (varying rerankers, chunking) while re-fitting 3D laws to observe changes in η and crossover.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** Can we derive a unified scaling law that predicts retrieval benefits across diverse tasks based on interpretable task characteristics (e.g., knowledge dependence, reasoning burden)?
*   **Basis:** Future Work urges "a more principled way to unify scaling behavior" and identify latent structure.
*   **Why unresolved:** Separate laws are fitted per benchmark; no general theory explains task differences (e.g., CommonsenseQA vs. PIQA).
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Collect task metadata or intrinsic properties; perform meta-regression to predict task-specific scaling exponents (α, β, η) from those features, validating on held-out tasks.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** Does the identified crossover point (D/N ≈ 4.14) generalize to other model architectures, pretraining objectives, and corpora?
*   **Basis:** Inferred from Discussion noting conclusions are "limited to the scales, architectures, and corpora explored here."
*   **Why unresolved:** Crossover may depend on architectural inductive biases, training losses, or data distribution; no broader testing.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate factorial design with diverse architectures (decoder-only, encoder-decoder), objectives (MLM, UL2), and corpora (web, code, scientific); compare estimated crossovers.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** How does the optimal pretraining-retrieval allocation differ when evaluated on downstream metrics like accuracy or reasoning scores rather than perplexity?
*   **Basis:** Inferred from Limitations stating evaluation "focuses primarily on perplexity... does not fully capture all downstream behaviors."
*   **Why unresolved:** Perplexity is a proxy; allocation trade-off could be metric-dependent.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate selected frontier checkpoints on multiple downstream metrics (accuracy, factuality); compare optimal allocation frontiers derived from perplexity vs. accuracy.

## Limitations
*   The D/N≈4.14 substitution threshold is derived from scaling-law projections and may be context-specific to the retriever and corpus used.
*   The 3D scaling law often exhibits retrieval-term saturation (η=10.0) and high out-of-sample errors for reasoning tasks (LOMO ARE up to 39.82%), limiting reliable extrapolation.
*   Experiments use a single pretraining corpus (DCLM) and a frozen retriever; findings may not generalize to heterogeneous data or improved retrieval pipelines.

## Confidence
*   **Retrieval substitution crossover:** Medium (empirically observed but relies on scaling law assumptions).
*   **Decreasing marginal benefit with model scale:** High (consistent across multiple scales and tasks).
*   **3D scaling law for joint optimization:** Medium (good in-sample fit but questionable extrapolation reliability).

## Next Checks
1.  **Retriever robustness test:** Repeat (D,R) trade-off experiments with alternative retrievers (e.g., sparse retriever, different embedding model) to verify stability of crossover threshold and marginal benefit trends.
2.  **Held-out scaling law validation:** Train models at interpolated (N,D,R) points not used in fitting (e.g., N=1B, various D/R splits along D+R=constant); compute prediction error, focusing on tasks with high LOMO ARE.
3.  **Cross-corpus and metric generalization:** Evaluate selected (N,D,R) configurations on a different pretraining corpus (e.g., The Pile) and with task-specific metrics (accuracy, factuality) to test allocation guideline portability.
