# "I May Not Have Articulated Myself Clearly": Diagnosing Dynamic Instability in LLM Reasoning at Inference Time

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.02863
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02863
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** A training‑free instability signal based on Jensen‑Shannon divergence and token entropy predicts reasoning failures (AUC 0.66‑0.74) and the timing of peak instability distinguishes corrective vs. destructive regimes.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a simple, training‑free diagnostic for detecting reasoning failures in large language models during autoregressive decoding. The instability signal combines Jensen‑Shannon divergence between consecutive token distributions and the entropy of the current distribution to capture dynamic shifts and uncertainty. Evaluated on GSM8K and HotpotQA with models up to 8B parameters, the signal predicts incorrect final answers with AUCs ranging from 0.66 to 0.74. Moreover, the timing of the peak instability differentiates corrective (early peaks) from destructive (late peaks) regimes, revealing that not all instability is harmful. The method requires only top‑k token probabilities accessible during standard inference, making it applicable to black‑box APIs. Its effectiveness is demonstrated across multiple model families and decoding strategies, and it persists even with limited top‑k logging. The work frames decoding as a dynamical system and provides theoretical intuition linking the observed signal to internal state changes.

## Method Summary
The approach operates solely on the token log probabilities available during standard autoregressive decoding. At each generation step, the model outputs logits for the next token; the authors take the top‑k (default k=50) logits, apply a softmax, and renormalize to obtain a distribution ˜p_t over the k most likely tokens. Two quantities are computed per step: entropy H_t = −∑ ˜p_t log ˜p_t (capturing uncertainty) and Jensen‑Shannon divergence D_t = JSD(˜p_t, ˜p_{t−1}) (capturing distributional shift from the previous step). The per‑step instability signal is I_t = D_t + λ H_t with λ=1. For a complete generation trace, the strength S is defined as the maximum I_t across all steps; an early‑window variant S_w considers only the first w steps (e.g., w=50). A higher S indicates a greater likelihood that the final answer will be incorrect. No training or parameter updates are required; the approach is applicable to any model that can output top‑k probabilities. Key implementation details include zero‑padding both distributions to their union support before computing JSD and renormalizing, as well as handling short traces by using the actual length for S_w. The signal’s robustness is shown across different top‑k sizes (≥20), decoding strategies (greedy, stochastic), and model scales (1B to 8B).

## Key Results
- Instability strength S predicts incorrect answers with ROC‑AUC between 0.66 and 0.74 on GSM8K and HotpotQA across Llama and Qwen models.
- Test accuracy decreases monotonically as S increases across quintile buckets, confirming a robust negative correlation.
- Early instability peaks (within the first 25% of generation steps) are associated with higher final accuracy (e.g., ~46%) compared to late peaks (after 50% of steps, ~14%), demonstrating that the temporal pattern of instability determines whether it is corrective or destructive.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The instability signal `I_t = D_t + λ H_t` (with `λ=1`) effectively predicts reasoning failure because it combines two complementary indicators of dynamic instability: distributional shift and uncertainty.
- **Mechanism:** `D_t` (Jensen–Shannon divergence between consecutive token distributions) captures abrupt route switching, while `H_t` (entropy of the current distribution) captures decision fragility (near ties among top candidates). Their sum is robust under top‑k truncation: entropy compensates when support overlap is low.
- **Core assumption:** Large `D_t` lower‑bounds internal state change (Theorem 1), and high `H_t` implies the model’s next‑token choice is easily disrupted (Lemma 4). Together they proxy for regime shifts in the hidden state.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “We define a simple instability signal that combines consecutive‑step distributional shift (JSD) and uncertainty (entropy)… Instability strength predicts wrong answers with above‑chance AUC.”
  - [section] Section 3.2 defines `I_t`; Section 5.1 shows monotonic bucket declines in accuracy as `S` increases.
  - [corpus] Weak: corpus neighbors focus on robotic articulation, cloud instability, and phishing detection, not on token‑level instability diagnostics.
- **Break condition:** The signal collapses if the logged top‑k is too small (e.g., `k<10`), as JSD estimates compress (Appendix E). It also fails to capture stable‑but‑wrong failures (Section 7.1).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The timing of the peak instability relative to the remaining decoding horizon determines whether the episode is corrective (recoverable) or destructive (failure‑prone).
- **Mechanism:** A corrective transition (entering a “good” internal basin `A_G`) requires a minimum number of steps `τ_mix` to stabilize into a correct answer. If the instability peak occurs late (`ρ = t*/T > 0.5`), the remaining budget is insufficient, leading to failure; early peaks (`ρ < 0.25`) allow recovery.
- **Core assumption:** There exist internal basins `A_G` (converging to correct answers) and `A_B` (converging to wrong answers) and a finite stabilization time `τ_mix` such that (S1) sufficient budget yields high success and (S2) fresh entry with insufficient budget yields low success (Appendix B.1, Theorem 2).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “Crucially, we show that instability is not uniformly harmful: early instability can reflect subsequent stabilization… whereas late instability is more often followed by failure.”
  - [section] Section 6.2 defines `ρ`; Table 2 shows early‑peak accuracy 46% vs late‑peak 14% on held‑out run; Figure 4 visualizes the gap.
  - [corpus] Weak: neighboring papers do not study temporal patterns in LLM reasoning dynamics.
- **Break condition:** If models lack intrinsic self‑correction ability, early peaks may not correlate with recovery. The threshold `ρ=0.25` is coarse; alternative thresholds still show ordering but may alter absolute gaps (Appendix I).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The diagnostic is model‑agnostic and training‑free, requiring only top‑k token probabilities from standard inference APIs.
- **Mechanism:** All observables (`H_t`, `D_t`) are computed from renormalized top‑k distributions `˜p_t`, which are available in black‑box settings. Proposition 1 extends the theoretical JSD lower‑bound to the restricted simplex of top‑k supports, justifying that even truncated distributions reflect meaningful state changes.
- **Core assumption:** Top‑k logging retains sufficient information; the renormalized distributions approximate the full distribution well enough for entropy and JSD to be meaningful.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “The method requires only top‑k token probabilities, is training‑free, and works across model families and scales.”
  - [section] Section 3.1 details the top‑k implementation; Section 4.1 evaluates Llama and Qwen models; Appendix E demonstrates stability across `k∈{20,50,100}`.
  - [corpus] Weak: one neighbor (“Diagnosing and Resolving Cloud Platform Instability”) deals with instability but in a completely different domain.
- **Break condition:** If only very small `k` (e.g., 5) is provided, entropy and JSD become noisy. Poor probability calibration could distort absolute values, though relative trends may persist.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Jensen‑Shannon Divergence  
  - *Why needed:* Quantifies the difference between consecutive token distributions, which is the core indicator of abrupt regime shifts during decoding.  
  - *Quick check:* Why is JSD preferred over KL divergence for comparing token distributions, and what does its bounded nature help with?
- **Concept:** Shannon Entropy  
  - *Why needed:* Measures the uncertainty of the next‑token distribution; high entropy indicates a fragile decision point where many tokens are plausible, complementing JSD.  
  - *Quick check:* If a step has high entropy but low JSD, what might that imply about the model’s reasoning state?
- **Concept:** Autoregressive Decoding as a Dynamical System  
  - *Why needed:* The paper frames generation as a closed‑loop system `h_{t+1}=f(h_t,x_t)`. Instability corresponds to large internal state changes manifesting in `p_t`.  
  - *Quick check:* In the system view, what does a large `D_t` imply about `||h_t - h_{t-1}||` under the assumptions?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  1. **Logging** – during decoding, capture per‑step top‑k token log probabilities.  
  2. **Signal computation** – for each step `t`, compute `H_t` on `˜p_t` and `D_t = JSD(˜p_t, ˜p_{t-1})` on the union support.  
  3. **Summarization** – per trace, compute `S = max_t (D_t + H_t)` and early‑window variants `S_w = max_{t≤w} (D_t + H_t)`.  
  4. **Analysis** – correlate `S` with correctness via AUC, bucketed accuracy, and peak position `ρ = t*/T`.
- **Critical path:** Decoding with per‑step logging is foundational; without it, no signal can be computed. All downstream steps depend on complete logs.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Top‑k size:** Larger `k` improves entropy/JSD accuracy but increases storage; `k≥20` is sufficient (Appendix E).  
  - **λ weighting:** Fixed `λ=1` works across tasks; JSD‑only (`λ=0`) fails on some models (Appendix G).  
  - **Early‑window:** `S_w` (e.g., `w=50`) controls for trace length and enables early warning but may miss late instability that still matters.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - `S` nearly constant across examples → signal non‑discriminative.  
  - No monotonic bucket trend → diagnostic may not generalize.  
  - Inverted AUC (e.g., on ReClor) → errors are mostly stable‑but‑wrong, not dynamic.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Monotonic buckets:** Run Llama‑3.2‑1B‑Instruct greedy on GSM8K‑300 with `k=50`; bucket by `S` quintiles; plot accuracy. Expect descending line.  
  2. **Separability:** Compute `AUC_wrong(S)` and `AUC_wrong(S50)`. Expect >0.6 and `S50` still predictive (Table 6).  
  3. **Timing effect:** On a full‑vocabulary run (or large `k`), compute `ρ = t*/T`; split into early (`ρ<0.25`) and late (`ρ>0.5`); compare accuracy. Expect early >> late (Table 2).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does the instability signal generalize to larger models (>8B parameters) and a broader range of reasoning tasks beyond math and multi‑hop QA?
2. Do the theoretical assumptions about local smoothness, observable gain, and stabilization time (τmix) hold in actual transformer trajectories during reasoning?
3. What causes the instability signal to weaken or invert (AUC < 0.5) in settings like ReClor, and how can it be adapted for stable‑but‑wrong failure modes?
4. Can the abstract "good" and "bad" basins (AG, AB) from the theoretical framework be identified and characterized in the model's activation space?
5. How does the calibration quality of model probabilities affect the reliability of the instability diagnostic?

## Limitations
- JSD computation details (smoothing, zero‑padding, renormalization) are not fully specified, risking implementation variance.
- Answer extraction for GSM8K and HotpotQA is ambiguous; different parsing strategies could alter correctness labels and AUC estimates.
- The early/late peak threshold `ρ=0.25` is arbitrary and may not generalize; the underlying assumption of recoverable internal basins lacks direct verification.

## Confidence
- *Instability signal predicts wrong answers (AUC 0.66–0.74)*: Medium.
- *Timing differentiates corrective vs. destructive regimes*: Medium‑Low.
- *Method is model‑agnostic and training‑free*: High.
