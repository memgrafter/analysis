# Recursive Concept Evolution for Compositional Reasoning in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.15725  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15725  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: RCE integrated with Mistral-7B achieves absolute gains of 12–18 points on ARC-AGI-2, 8–14 points on GPQA and BBH, and reduces depth‑induced error on MATH and HLE with negligible computational overhead (~1.04× base model cost).

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) often fail at compositional reasoning because their internal representations are static after pretraining and cannot dynamically form new abstractions. This paper introduces Recursive Concept Evolution (RCE), a method that equips frozen LLMs with the ability to modify their latent geometry during inference. RCE spawns low‑rank concept subspaces when a failure signal indicates representational inadequacy, selects them via a Minimum Description Length (MDL) criterion, merges synergistic concepts, and consolidates via constrained optimization. Integrated with Mistral‑7B, RCE yields substantial improvements on challenging benchmarks: 12–18 absolute points on ARC‑AGI‑2, 8–14 on GPQA and BBH, and reduced error on MATH and HLE, all while increasing computational cost by only ~4%. Ablations confirm that MDL‑based selection and invariance augmentation are critical for robust, generalizable concept formation.

## Method Summary
RCE is implemented by injecting a concept module into decoder layer 17 (0‑indexed) of a frozen Mistral‑7B. The module maintains a library of up to 128 concept slots; each slot holds an orthonormal basis of rank 16. During training on a mixed reasoning curriculum, every batch triggers the following: The forward pass captures the hidden state at the injection layer. At the last prompt token, a failure score \(F = \text{entropy} / (\text{margin} + \varepsilon)\) is computed from the next‑token logits; if \(F > 5.0\) and free slots exist, a new concept is spawned. Spawning uses a 3‑layer generator MLP to produce 5 candidate bases, adds Gaussian noise (\(\sigma=0.03\)), orthogonalizes via QR, and selects the candidate minimizing reconstruction error of a pooled hidden state. An MDL decision compares the expected reduction in description length against a complexity term \(\Omega = \alpha\cdot\|B\|_* + \beta\cdot KL(g\|\pi)\); acceptance occurs if \(\Delta L > 0.5\cdot\Omega\). Active concepts are chosen by a gate MLP (2‑layer) that scores all slots; the top‑2 are selected and their contributions \(\Delta h = g_i B_i B_i^\top h_{\text{layer}}\) are summed and injected as a residual. Additional losses enforce orthogonality (\(\lambda_{\text{orth}}=0.05\)), limit overlap with existing concepts (\(\lambda_{\text{ov}}=0.02\)), regularize gate entropy (\(\lambda_{\text{gate}}=0.01\)), and penalize KL divergence from the base model (\(\lambda_{\text{KL}}\), monitored to keep perplexity rise <5%). Periodically (every 800 steps), pairs of concepts with high co‑activation are evaluated for synergy; if merging synergy is sufficiently negative, their bases are concatenated and re‑orthogonalized. Concepts with low usage are pruned when the active library exceeds 96 slots. Training uses AdamW (lr=2e‑4, weight decay=0.01, grad clip=1.0), cosine LR schedule, 200 warmup steps, and runs for 10k steps on sequences of length 512 with batch size 1. Invariance augmentation (color permutation, rotation, distractors) and optional discriminative scoring augment candidate selection where labels are available.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RCE overcomes the static representation limitation by dynamically expanding the model’s latent space when a failure score indicates uncertainty and low decision margin. New concepts are introduced as low‑rank subspaces that are orthogonal to existing ones, preserving diversity. The MDL criterion balances utility (reconstruction of the pooled hidden state) against complexity (nuclear norm of the basis and KL from a uniform prior), preventing uncontrolled library growth. A learned gate MLP selects a small set of relevant concepts based on the global context, enabling task‑specific assembly of a subspace. Periodic merging of synergistic concepts (those that co‑activate and reduce total complexity) builds higher‑level abstractions while controlling library size. Together, these mechanisms allow a frozen LLM to adapt its internal geometry on‑the‑fly, yielding significant gains on compositional reasoning tasks with minimal extra compute.

## Foundational Learning
- **Compositional Reasoning**: The ability to combine multiple reasoning steps; why needed because target benchmarks explicitly test composition. Quick check: Evaluate on ARC‑AGI‑2 before/after adaptation.  
- **Low‑Rank Concept Subspaces**: Compact representation of new abstractions; why needed to keep overhead low while introducing expressive directions. Quick check: Verify concept basis matrices have rank ≤16 and remain approximately orthonormal.  
- **Minimum Description Length (MDL) Selection**: Balances concept utility and complexity; why needed to avoid library bloat and ensure generalisation. Quick check: Monitor acceptance ratio and confirm \(\Delta L > 0.5\cdot\Omega\) for accepted concepts.  
- **Invariance Augmentation**: Applies transformations (color perm, rotation) to inputs; why needed to force robustness to task‑irrelevant variations. Quick check: Test on transformed inputs; aim for <10% accuracy drop.  
- **Synergistic Concept Merging**: Combines frequently co‑activating concepts; why needed to form higher‑level abstractions and manage library size. Quick check: Track merge events; library size should stabilise after initial growth.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Input → Mistral‑7B (frozen) layers 0‑17 → [Hook at layer 17] → pooled hidden state → Gate MLP → top‑k concept selection → Δh via concept bases (Bᵀh) → residual add → layers 18‑end → output.  
Training loop additionally: failure score \(F\) → Generator MLP (spawn) → MDL check → library update; periodically: synergy evaluation → merge → prune.

**Critical path** (inference):  
1. Forward pass to layer 17.  
2. Compute \(F\) from next‑token distribution.  
3. If \(F > 5.0\) and spare capacity, spawn/concept selection.  
4. Gate selects active concepts; compute Δh and inject.  
5. Continue forward to output.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Low rank (r=16) vs. concept expressiveness.  
- Fixed library size (128 slots) vs. adaptability/memory overhead.  
- MDL hyperparameters (α,β,π): strictness controls library growth.  
- Gate MLP capacity: more parameters improve selection but add cost.  
- Merge threshold: aggressive merging loses distinctions; conservative causes bloat.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Library overgrowth (spawn rate >50% → lower τ).  
- Poor OOD robustness without augmentation.  
- No merges (synergy threshold unmet → adjust λ_m).  
- Degenerated bases (orthogonality penalty >0.1 → increase λ_ov).  
- Base model drift (val perplexity ↑ >5% → increase λ_KL).  
- Low concept reuse (average usage <4 → library may overfit).

**First three experiments**:  
1. **Baseline verification**: Confirm base Mistral‑7B numbers on benchmarks (e.g., ARC‑AGI‑2 ~12.4%).  
2. **Spawning sanity**: Use a small synthetic set that triggers high \(F\); verify new concepts are spawned, bases stay orthogonal, and gate activates them.  
3. **MDL ablation**: Set α=β=0 (no complexity penalty); observe library growth and performance drop relative to full RCE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Canonical concept decomposition?**  
   - Basis: Explicitly stated; identifiability remains open.  
   - Why unresolved: Different seeds produce distinct libraries with similar aggregate performance; orthogonality and MDL reduce but do not eliminate variability.  
   - Resolving evidence: Formal analysis proving convergence to a unique fixed point under certain conditions, or systematic empirical studies measuring decomposition similarity across runs and correlating with OOD generalisation.

2. **Scaling the merge mechanism** (to thousands of concepts).  
   - Basis: Inferred from Limitations; merge is computationally quadratic.  
   - Why unresolved: Current pairwise synergy evaluation is only tractable for modest libraries; scaling requires approximate selection.  
   - Resolving evidence: Development and evaluation of approximate merge algorithms (e.g., clustering, learned predictors) that reduce complexity while maintaining or improving performance.

3. **Integration with external memory** to provide persistent storage.  
   - Basis: Inferred from Failure Analysis; RCE lacks storage for long‑range tracking.  
   - Why unresolved: RCE only amplifies residual directions; tasks requiring explicit memory (e.g., tracking many objects) may exceed its capacity.  
   - Resolving evidence: A hybrid architecture combining RCE with a memory module, trained and evaluated on state‑tracking benchmarks, with analysis of design choices.

## Limitations
- Unspecified composition of the "mixed reasoning curriculum" and exact prompt format may lead to divergent concept learning.  
- MDL hyperparameters (α, β, π) and discriminative scoring integration are missing, forcing reproduction to rely on heuristics.  
- Merge candidate selection (how 12 pairs are chosen) is vague; our heuristic (co‑activation frequency) may not capture the original behavior.
