# Motion-aware Event Suppression for Event Cameras
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23204
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23204
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: Introduces a motion-aware event suppression framework that jointly segments independently moving objects and predicts their future motion, achieving 67% higher segmentation accuracy on EVIMO and real-time operation at 173 Hz.

## Executive Summary
Event cameras generate asynchronous events from both camera ego-motion and independently moving objects (IMOs). The mixture of these events overwhelms downstream perception systems. This paper introduces the first motion-aware event suppression framework that learns to filter events in real time. The method jointly segments IMOs in the current event stream and predicts their future dense optical flow, then warps the segmentation mask forward to anticipate where future IMO events will occur. This anticipatory suppression reduces event volume while preserving important dynamic objects. A lightweight multi-task architecture with an Attention-based Time Conditioning module enables forecasting for arbitrary future horizons. The system runs at 173 Hz on consumer GPUs (<1 GB memory). On the EVIMO benchmark, it improves segmentation accuracy by 67% and is 53% faster than prior work. Downstream, it accelerates Vision Transformer inference by 83% via motion-guided token pruning and improves event-based visual odometry, reducing Absolute Trajectory Error by 13%.

## Method Summary
The framework processes a temporal window of events (Δt=50 ms) represented as a two-bin voxel grid. A conv-GRU encoder extracts features, which branch into two decoders: one predicts a binary mask of independently moving objects at the current time; the other predicts dense forward optical flow for a future horizon Δt_p. The current mask is warped forward using the predicted flow to obtain a future mask, which suppresses events unlikely to belong to IMOs. A key component is the Attention-based Time Conditioning (ATC) module, which conditions the flow decoder on the desired Δt_p via cross-attention between a temporal query (sinusoidal encoding of Δt_p) and the spatial encoder features. The model is trained with a hybrid loss: supervised terms for mask (BCE+Dice) and flow (L1+Charbonnier) where ground truth exists, plus an unsupervised contrast maximization loss that maximizes sharpness of warped events, providing a dense signal from raw events alone.

## Key Results
- On EVIMO, achieves 67% higher segmentation mIoU than prior SOTA while running 53% faster.
- Real-time performance: 173 Hz inference on consumer GPUs with <1 GB memory.
- Downstream benefits: accelerates Vision Transformer inference by 83% via motion-guided token pruning, and reduces Absolute Trajectory Error in event-based visual odometry by 13%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Jointly segmenting current independently moving objects (IMOs) and predicting their future dense optical flow enables anticipatory suppression by aligning the predicted mask with future events.
- **Mechanism:** The model processes a temporal window of events to produce a binary IMO mask \(M_t\) and a forward flow field \(\psi_{t \rightarrow t+\Delta t_p}\). The mask is warped forward using the flow (Eq. 4), yielding a future mask \(\hat{M}_{t+\Delta t_p}\) that spatially anticipates where IMO events will occur. Events in the future window that fall under this warped mask are retained; others are suppressed. This breaks the chicken-and-egg dilemma by using future prediction to preemptively filter events before they arrive.
- **Core assumption:** The motion of IMOs over short horizons (\(\Delta t_p \leq 100\) ms) is spatially smooth and can be extrapolated via dense flow. Ego-motion events are assumed to form a static background pattern warped by the camera's own motion.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "jointly segments IMOs in the current event stream while predicting their future motion, enabling anticipatory suppression of dynamic events before they occur."
  - [Section III-A] Formalizes anticipatory suppression as composing segmentation (\(\hat{M}_t\)) and motion prediction (\(\psi\)) to compute future mask \(f\hat{M}_t\) (Eq. 4).
  - [corpus] Corpus shows related works (EV-IMO, OMS) lack this anticipatory component; their masks lag. Weak direct corpus support beyond confirming novelty of suppression task.
- **Break condition:** If IMO motion is highly non-linear (e.g., abrupt direction changes, rotations) within \(\Delta t_p\), the linear flow assumption fails, causing mask misalignment and poor suppression. Evidenced by steep mIoU drop for fast sequences in Fig. 6.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Attention-based Time Conditioning (ATC) module allows a single model to generate flow fields for arbitrary future timestamps \(\Delta t_p\) without retraining, by modulating spatial features with a temporal query.
- **Mechanism:** A cross-attention operation uses a Query derived from a sinusoidal positional encoding of the target \(\Delta t_p\) and Key/Value from the current spatial feature map \(E\). This produces a time-conditioned embedding \(E_{t+\Delta t_p}\) that the flow decoder uses to predict flow for that specific horizon. The model learns a continuous mapping from time delta to flow dynamics.
- **Core assumption:** The relationship between temporal horizon and required feature transformation can be captured by attending to spatial features based on a learned function of \(\Delta t_p\).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section III-C1] Details ATC architecture: "Query \(Q\) is derived from the temporal positional encoding \(t_{enc} = PE(\Delta t)\)... Key and Value are obtained by flattening the spatial dimensions of \(E\)."
  - [Section IV-F] Shows model trained with randomized \(\Delta t_p\) generalizes across horizons, confirming ATC's role in continuous time conditioning.
  - [corpus] Weak; corpus lacks direct ATC analogs. Nearest is transformer time-conditioning in video models, but not event-based forecasting.
- **Break condition:** For \(\Delta t_p\) far outside training distribution (e.g., >200 ms), the positional encoding may not extrapolate well, leading to degenerate flow predictions. Ablations in Fig. 6 show monotonic mIoU decay with horizon.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A hybrid supervised + unsupervised training loss (contrast maximization) enables learning from sparse event data without requiring dense ground-truth flow or depth, especially for IMOs where standard flow GT is invalid.
- **Mechanism:** Supervised losses (BCE-Dice on masks, Charbonnier on flow where GT exists) provide a strong prior. The unsupervised contrast maximization loss (maximizing sharpness of Image of Warped Events) provides a dense, data-driven signal from raw events alone. This combination lets the model learn motion coherence for both ego-motion and IMIOs even when GT flow is incomplete (e.g., DSEC GT ignores IMOs).
- **Core assumption:** Maximizing contrast (sharpness) of warped events is a valid proxy for correct optical flow, as errors cause event smearing. This holds under constant illumination and brightness change assumptions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [Section III-D] "L_unsup employs a contrast maximization objective... encourages flow fields that 'deblur' the event stream."
  - [Section IV-A] Notes on DSEC: "supervised part... is masked and only the optical flow outside of the IMOs is supervised," relying on unsupervised loss for IMO regions.
  - [corpus] Strong support from prior work (EV-FlowNet, E-RAFT) using contrast maximization; this paper combines it with segmentation in a multi-task setting.
- **Break condition:** Under changing illumination (e.g., flickering lights), contrast maximization fails as brightness changes not due to motion create misleading events. Also, in low-texture regions, events may be too sparse for meaningful contrast.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Event Representation (Voxel Grids / Stack)**
  - Why needed here: The network consumes dense tensors, not raw asynchronous events. The 2-bin stack over \(\Delta t\) converts sparse events into a spatial-temporal histogram, providing a continuous input for convolutions.
  - Quick check question: How does the choice of temporal discretization (2 bins vs. more) affect the trade-off between motion blur and noise in the input tensor?
- **Concept: Dense Optical Flow Estimation**
  - Why needed here: Flow is the explicit mechanism for warping masks forward. The model must learn per-pixel motion vectors to anticipatively relocate IMO pixels.
  - Quick check question: Why is Charbonnier loss preferred over plain L1 for flow smoothness, especially near object boundaries?
- **Concept: Multi-Task Learning**
  - Why needed here: Joint segmentation and flow prediction forces the encoder to learn representations useful for both static scene structure (mask) and dynamics (flow). The tasks regularize each other, improving generalization.
  - Quick check question: What failure mode could occur if the two tasks had completely disjoint decoders with no shared encoder?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. Event Encoder: conv-GRU stack processing \(E_{[t-\Delta t, t)}\) into features \(E\).
  2. ATC Module: Cross-attention injecting \(\Delta t_p\) into \(E\) to get \(E_{t+\Delta t_p}\).
  3. Mask Decoder: Transposed convs from \(E\) → binary mask \(M_t\).
  4. Flow Decoder: Transposed convs from \(E_{t+\Delta t_p}\) → flow \(\psi_{t \rightarrow t+\Delta t_p}\).
  5. Flow Warping: Backward warping of \(M_t\) logits by \(\psi\) to get future mask \(\hat{M}_{t+\Delta t_p}\).
  6. Suppression Gate: Thresholds \(\hat{M}_{t+\Delta t_p}\) to filter future events.
- **Critical path:** Events → Encoder → (ATC → Flow Decoder) AND (Mask Decoder) → Warping → Suppression. The ATC branch must condition on target \(\Delta t_p\) correctly to align flow and mask.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Prediction horizon vs. accuracy: Longer \(\Delta t_p\) gives more latency compensation but decays mIoU (Fig. 6). Choose \(\Delta t_p\) based on system latency budget.
  - Model scale vs. speed: Larger models (L) give +~1-2% mIoU but increase GFLOPS (Tab. V). For 173 Hz, S/M models may suffice.
  - Supervised vs. unsupervised loss weight: On datasets with incomplete flow GT (DSEC), rely more on \(L_{unsup}\). Paper uses fixed weights, but tuning \(w_{unsup}\) could help in new domains.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High mIoU on static scenes but failure on fast IMOs: Check if ATC is learning non-linear warping (ablate ATC vs. linear in Tab. IV).
  - Suppression latency still high: Verify runtime on target hardware (Tab. III shows 5.8 ms on RTX 2080 Ti). Consumer GPUs may be slower.
  - Downstream VO degrades: If suppressed events remove too much background, VO may lose scale. Monitor ATE on sequences with static features (Table VI: Boxes/Wall improved, static scenes stable).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate EVIMO segmentation: Train on EVIMO train, evaluate mIoU and R@0.5 on test (Tab. I, II). Expect ~76-80% mIoU on Boxes/Floor, ~68% on Fast.
  2. Measure prediction age: Compute \(T_{age} = \Delta t_p - T_{runtime}\). With \(\Delta t_p=100\) ms and \(T_{runtime} \approx 5.8\) ms, expect ~94 ms positive age (Sec. IV-D).
  3. Downstream token pruning: Integrate masks into SViT [32] on DSEC. Prune tokens using dilated masks (Fig. 5). Expect ~10 FPS speedup at <7% SegAP drop (Fig. 7).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**
  - Question: What is the maximum prediction horizon for which motion-aware suppression remains accurate, and how does it correlate with object velocity and scene texture?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper reports a monotonic decline in mIoU with increasing prediction horizon (Fig. 6), especially for fast-moving scenes, but does not establish a theoretical model or limit.
  - Why unresolved: Knowing this limit would help system designers trade off latency vs. accuracy for specific applications and guide architectural improvements for longer-term forecasting.
  - What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study across multiple datasets with varying dynamics, modeling the accuracy decay as a function of object speed, occlusion rates, and temporal horizon.
- **Open Question 2**
  - Question: How does the method handle events triggered by non-motion factors such as illumination changes or sensor noise?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The framework assumes constant illumination, stating that events arise only from motion. No mechanism is proposed to filter events from lighting variations.
  - Why unresolved: Illumination changes are common in real-world deployments and could generate大量 events that overwhelm the suppression or cause false positives/negatives.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Testing on datasets with synthetic or real illumination changes, measuring segmentation accuracy and downstream task performance under varying lighting conditions.
- **Open Question 3**
  - Question: Can the motion segmentation network be trained without ground-truth masks, using only self-supervised signals from event data?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The training loss includes a supervised mask loss that requires ground-truth segmentation masks (Eq. 9). The unsupervised component only addresses optical flow.
  - Why unresolved: Pixel-wise mask annotations for event data are scarce and expensive to obtain. An unsupervised approach would enable scaling to large, diverse datasets and new environments.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Designing a fully self-supervised loss, e.g., by extending contrast maximization to segmentation, and evaluating on benchmarks without mask annotations, comparing to the supervised baseline.
- **Open Question 4**
  - Question: How well does the framework scale to scenes with numerous independently moving objects and complex interactions like mutual occlusions?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] Evaluations on EVIMO (up to 3 IMOs) and DSEC (mostly vehicles) do not cover highly crowded scenarios with dozens of interacting objects.
  - Why unresolved: Crowded environments pose challenges such as frequent occlusions, similar motion patterns, and fine-grained boundaries, which may degrade segmentation and prediction accuracy.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Applying the method to event-based crowd datasets or synthesizing dense multi-object scenes, and analyzing performance metrics as a function of object count and occlusion density.

## Limitations
- Exact architecture details (e.g., number of conv-GRU stages, channel sizes) are not specified, hindering exact replication.
- The framework's performance is sensitive to event density; using a fixed Δt=50 ms may be suboptimal for varying motion conditions.
- Generalization assumptions (constant illumination, sufficient texture) may not hold in all real-world scenarios, potentially causing failures.

## Confidence
- Anticipatory suppression via joint segmentation and flow warping: Medium
- ATC for continuous time conditioning: High
- Hybrid supervised+unsupervised training: High
