# LLMberjack: Guided Trimming of Debate Trees for Multi-Party Conversation Creation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.04135
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04135
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Human-AI platform transforms structured debate reply trees into linearized multi-party dialogues; tree visualization increased naturalness preference by 37 percentage points and selection speed by 25%, while LLM assistance improved turn quality preference by 36 points and refinement speed by 83%.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LLMberjack, a human‑AI platform for creating high‑quality multi‑party conversation (MPC) datasets from structured debate reply trees. It tackles the scarcity of such resources by enabling annotators to interactively trim hierarchical reply structures into coherent linear dialogues, preserving speaker identities and discourse relations. The system couples an interactive tree visualization (guiding message selection) with LLM‑assisted speaker profiling and message refinement.

Human evaluations demonstrate that the tree visualization significantly improves naturalness (65.62% preference over 28.13%) and speeds up turn selection by 25%. LLM‑based refinement boosts turn quality (64.06% preference) and accelerates editing by 83%. The combined workflow offers a scalable, controllable alternative to pure LLM generation or passive data scraping.

## Method Summary
The platform integrates a frontend with an interactive D3.js tree view and a Vue.js‑based chat construction pane, and a Python backend managing data (JSON discussion/draft files) and LLM orchestration via Groq’s Llama 4 Maverick model. Annotators select and sequence messages from the reply tree, optionally editing addressee links. For refinement, the system recontextualizes a chosen message using the speaker’s profile and prior turns, generating suggestions that the annotator can accept or modify. LLM tasks use distinct temperatures: 1.2 for speaker profiling, 0.7 for message refinement, and 0.0 for tree normalization. The human‑in‑the‑loop design ensures final control while leveraging AI speed and consistency.

## Key Results
- Tree visualization yielded 65.62% preference for naturalness (vs. 28.13% without) and a 25% increase in turn‑selection speed (1.82 vs. 1.46 turns/min).
- LLM‑assisted refinement achieved 64.06% preference for general turn quality and an 83% speedup in the refinement process (0.86 vs. 0.47 tokens/sec).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Interactive tree visualization enables annotators to select and sequence messages into coherent multi‑party conversations with greater naturalness and speed.
- Mechanism: The visualization displays parent‑child relationships and author color‑coding, reducing cognitive load for understanding discourse structure and addressee links. This context‑aware selection prevents fragmented or implausible turn‑taking.
- Core assumption: Human decision‑making for linearizing non‑linear structures benefits from seeing hierarchical relationships.
- Evidence anchors: [Section 4.2] Quantitative results show 65.62% preference for naturalness with tree vs. 28.13% without, and 25% faster turn selection (1.82 vs. 1.46 turns/min). [Abstract] “tree visualization facilitates the creation of coherent, meaningful conversation threads”.
- Break condition: If the underlying reply tree is too large or deep, visualization may become cluttered and counterproductive; annotator feedback suggests it is especially beneficial for larger annotation rounds.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: LLM‑assisted message refinement improves turn quality and drastically accelerates the editing process.
- Mechanism: The LLM recontextualizes a message using the speaker profile and prior conversation history, generating stylistic and length adjustments. The human‑in‑the‑loop acceptance/modification step ensures quality control.
- Core assumption: LLMs can effectively adapt message style/temperament/length based on constrained instructions and contextual evidence.
- Evidence anchors: [Section 4.3] 64.06% preference for general turn quality with LLM support, 83% faster refinement (0.86 vs. 0.47 tokens/sec). [Section 3.3] Specific implementation: LLM receives message, local context, speaker profile, and constraints (style, length, temperament).
- Break condition: LLM outputs may introduce repetitive interjections or stylistic drift; effectiveness depends on annotator expertise to detect and correct such issues.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The combined Human‑AI workflow (guided tree trimming + LLM refinement) addresses the scarcity of high‑quality, controlled multi‑party conversation datasets.
- Mechanism: Starting from structured reply trees (abundant on debate platforms), the platform guides creation of linearized dialogues with explicit addressee relations and speaker consistency, while LLM assistance polishes linguistic attributes. This leverages existing data structures and automates tedious editing.
- Core assumption: Synthetic, human‑refined conversations can achieve sufficient quality and variability for NLP research, with better control than pure LLM generation or passive data scraping.
- Evidence anchors: [Abstract] “addressing a lack of resources of this type” by transforming reply trees into linearized dialogues. [Introduction] Argues that pure LLM generation struggles with complex multi‑party structures and that online platforms have privacy/structural limitations.
- Break condition: If the source reply trees are stylistically too far from the target genre (e.g., Telegram chat), even with refinement the output may remain unnatural; annotator feedback noted style gaps sometimes requiring substantial rework.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: JSON data files (discussion/draft) → Backend (Python REST API, tree data model, LLM orchestration) → Frontend (Vue.js/D3.js UI); LLM Service (Llama 4 Maverick via Groq) is invoked by the backend.

**Critical path**:
1. Upload discussion file → Backend parses JSON into internal tree.
2. Frontend renders interactive tree (D3) and chat construction pane.
3. Annotator selects nodes → system adds to linear sequence, allows reordering/addressee editing.
4. For refinement: annotator selects message → Frontend sends message+context+profile to Backend → Backend calls LLM → suggestion returned → annotator accepts/modifies.
5. Export final conversation as JSON.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Visualization: Showing the full tree aids global navigation but can be slow for huge trees; the focused‑node view mitigates this.
- LLM autonomy: High creativity (temp=1.2) for speaker profiles vs. low (temp=0.0) for tree normalization; message refinement uses mid‑range (temp=0.7) for balance.
- Human control: LLM suggestions are optional; annotator must explicitly accept, reducing automation risk but requiring vigilance.

**Failure signatures**:
- Tree parsing fails if input JSON doesn’t match expected schema; the LLM normalization step (temp=0.0) attempts repair but may produce invalid structures.
- LLM hallucination: message refinement may add content not grounded in the original message or profile; watch for factually inconsistent or repetitive generations.
- UI desync: Frontend/Backend state mismatch if draft file updates aren’t propagated correctly; watch for “stale” visualization.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. **Basic tree load & navigate**: Upload a small synthetic tree (3‑4 nodes). Verify D3 renders correctly, color‑coding works, and clicking nodes updates focused view/chat pane. Measure load time.
2. **Toggle LLM refinement**: For a selected message, run refinement with LLM on and off. Compare speed and note any out‑of‑scope edits (e.g., changing argument rather than style). Test with different temperature settings.
3. **Export & validate**: Create a 10‑turn conversation with custom addressee edits. Export JSON and validate schema (node order, author consistency, parent‑child links). Check that exported structure matches UI preview.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**  
- Question: How does the benefit of tree visualization scale with the depth and branching factor of reply trees in multi‑party conversation creation?  
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Annotators reported the visualization would be “even more advantageous in larger annotation rounds (more than 3)” as it reduces cognitive effort and aids in identifying multiple plausible conversation paths.  
- Why unresolved: The evaluation used a fixed depth of 4 and limited rounds; scalability to larger, more complex trees remains untested.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying tree size (depth, branching) and annotation volume, measuring quality and speed metrics.

**Open Question 2**  
- Question: What mitigation strategies can reduce LLM‑induced stylistic issues (e.g., repetitive interjections) during message refinement while preserving acceleration benefits?  
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Evaluators noted the LLM “occasionally introduces repetitive interjections” that require minimal editing, indicating a trade‑off between efficiency and output quality.  
- Why unresolved: No analysis of error patterns or adaptive prompting strategies to minimize such artifacts without human intervention.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies on prompt design, decoding parameters, or post‑processing filters targeting repetitive patterns, evaluated against quality and speed.

**Open Question 3**  
- Question: To what extent can the platform’s linearization approach capture the complex addressee structures and parallel sub‑dialogues typical of asynchronous multi‑party conversations?  
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The introduction contrasts tree‑based debates (explicit single addressee) with messaging platforms (implicit multiple addressees, overlapping sub‑dialogues). The system allows redefining addressee relations but its effectiveness for complex, non‑tree‑like flows is unassessed.  
- Why unresolved: Evaluation focused on synthetic trees with fixed reply‑to links; real‑world asynchronous MPCs with multiple implicit addressees were not tested.  
- What evidence would resolve it: User studies with conversations derived from real asynchronous platforms (e.g., group chats), measuring fidelity to original interactional complexity.

**Open Question 4**  
- Question: How effective is LLMberjack when applied to real‑world, noisy debate trees compared to the controlled synthetic ones used in evaluation?  
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The evaluation used GPT‑4 generated trees with perfect structure and balanced stances. The system includes an LLM‑assisted normalization step for imperfect structures, but its impact on final quality was not evaluated on authentic data.  
- Why unresolved: Potential performance gaps due to noise, inconsistency, or missing metadata in real trees remain unknown.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies using both synthetic and real‑world debate trees (e.g., from Reddit, Kialo), assessing quality metrics and normalization efficacy.

**Open Question 5**  
- Question: Can the human‑AI workflow be adapted to generate privacy‑preserving paraphrases of authentic multi‑party conversations without compromising conversational naturalness?  
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The ethical statement notes the platform “can help to paraphrase also real conversations for pseudo‑anonymization purposes.”  
- Why unresolved: No experiments on anonymization tasks; trade‑offs between privacy (e.g., entity removal) and conversational coherence are unexplored.  
- What evidence would resolve it: User evaluations of paraphrased real conversations, measuring both anonymization strength (e.g., entity recognition failure) and human‑rated naturalness/coherence.

## Limitations
- Tree visualization may become cluttered or slow for very large trees, limiting scalability.
- LLM‑driven refinement requires annotator vigilance to catch drift or hallucinations; effectiveness depends on unseen prompt/guideline details.
- Combined workflow assumes synthetic trees mimic real debate structures; performance on noisy, real‑world data is uncertain.

## Confidence
- Tree visualization benefits: High
- LLM‑assisted refinement: Medium
- Combined workflow scalability: Medium‑Low
