# Two-Stream Interactive Joint Learning of Scene Parsing and Geometric Vision Tasks

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.13588
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13588
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** TwInS achieves state-of-the-art on Cityscapes and KITTI 2015, improving segmentation mIoU up to 2.05% and reducing stereo matching end-point error up to 47.82% over prior methods.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces TwInS, a bidirectional two-stream framework for joint learning of scene parsing and geometric vision tasks (stereo matching/optical flow). It addresses limitations of prior joint learning methods, such as unidirectional interaction and reliance on expensive ground-truth correspondences. TwInS features two interactive streams: the scene parsing stream provides multi-level contextual features to guide iterative refinement in the geometric stream, while the geometric stream’s refined hidden states are fused back into the parsing stream via a Cross-Task Adapter (CTA). Additionally, a semi‑supervised training strategy with uncertainty‑aware pseudo‑labels enables learning from large‑scale unlabeled multi‑view data. Experiments on Cityscapes and KITTI 2015 show significant improvements over prior art and demonstrate the approach’s versatility in boosting other networks with fewer parameters.

## Method Summary
TwInS employs a shared ConvNeXt encoder that extracts multi‑scale contextual features for both tasks. The geometric stream builds a correlation pyramid from early features and uses a GRU‑based iterative refinement process, where contextual features from the parsing stream are infused at each step to guide correspondence estimation. The parsing stream uses a query‑based decoder (Mask2Former‑style). The Cross‑Task Adapter (CTA) projects the final GRU hidden states into the contextual feature space using linear attention and selectively fuses them into the parsing decoder. For training, the model is first supervised on synthetic vKITTI2 data. For real‑world data without correspondence ground truth, a semi‑supervised approach is used: a teacher model (trained on synthetic data) predicts correspondences and per‑pixel aleatoric uncertainty; low‑uncertainty predictions serve as pseudo‑labels to supervise a student model, with the teacher updated via exponential moving average. This strategy eliminates the need for costly ground‑truth correspondences while leveraging large unlabeled datasets.

## Key Results
- On Cityscapes, TwInS improves semantic segmentation mIoU by up to **2.05%** over prior feature‑fusion joint learning methods.
- On KITTI 2015, it reduces stereo matching end‑point error by up to **47.82%** compared to unsupervised approaches.
- The framework is versatile, boosting performance of other networks while reducing parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Contextual Features Guide Geometric Refinement
- **Claim:** Contextual features from the scene parsing stream guide the iterative refinement in the geometric vision stream, improving correspondence matching in ambiguous regions (e.g., texture‑less surfaces).
- **Mechanism:** Multi‑level contextual features (early for local details, late for global semantics) from the parsing encoder are aligned and infused into the GRU‑based iterative refinement process at each update step, providing high‑level semantic constraints that regularize the correspondence search.
- **Core assumption:** Correspondences within the same semantic object exhibit smooth variations; thus, contextual semantics can resolve matching ambiguities where photometric cues fail.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] infusion mentioned; [section III.B] details extraction of `F e,i` and `F l,i` and their infusion; [section IV.E.1] ablation shows progressive EPE/D1 reductions when adding context features.

### Mechanism 2: Cross‑Task Adapter Selective Fusion
- **Claim:** The Cross‑Task Adapter (CTA) selectively fuses iteratively refined geometric features into the scene parsing stream, enhancing segmentation with cross‑view 3D cues.
- **Mechanism:** Geometric hidden states are projected into the contextual feature space via a linear attention mechanism; the projected features act as queries to selectively aggregate contextual features (key/value), producing fused features that combine both modalities.
- **Core assumption:** GRU hidden states, refined over iterations, accumulate richer geometric information and can be meaningfully aligned with contextual features.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] projection and selective fusion; [section III.C] describes projection and fusion; [section IV.E.2] CTA outperforms alternatives; Figure 7 t‑SNE visualizations support.

### Mechanism 3: Uncertainty‑Aware Semi‑Supervised Learning
- **Claim:** The semi‑supervised training strategy with uncertainty‑aware pseudo‑label generation enables effective learning from large‑scale unlabeled multi‑view data, eliminating need for costly correspondence ground truth.
- **Mechanism:** A teacher model (pre‑trained on synthetic data) predicts correspondences and aleatoric uncertainty (via variance of iterative residuals). Pixels with uncertainty below an adaptive, quantile‑based threshold are selected as pseudo‑labels to supervise a student model. Teacher parameters are updated via EMA.
- **Core assumption:** Uncertainty estimates derived from iterative prediction fluctuations correlate with confidence; high‑quality pseudo‑labels can be automatically filtered from unlabeled data.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] unleashes potential of large‑scale multi‑view data; [section III.D] uncertainty modeling and thresholding; [section IV.E.3] semi‑supervised yields best joint performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Iterative Refinement (RAFT‑style)**
  - *Why needed here:* The geometric stream uses GRUs to iteratively update correspondence estimates; understanding this coarse‑to‑fine process is key to grasping how contextual features are infused and how hidden states are generated for CTA.
  - *Quick check question:* How do multi‑level GRU hidden states evolve from capturing large motions to fine details across iterations?
- **Concept: Query‑based Mask Prediction (Mask2Former)**
  - *Why needed here:* The scene parsing stream uses a query‑decoder architecture; this determines how contextual features are processed and where CTA fused features are injected.
  - *Quick check question:* In a query‑based decoder, how do learnable queries interact with multi‑scale features to produce masks?
- **Concept: Aleatoric Uncertainty Estimation**
  - *Why needed here:* The semi‑supervised strategy hinges on estimating per‑pixel prediction confidence from the teacher model to filter pseudo‑labels.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is modeling uncertainty from iterative residuals (Equation 6) more suitable for refinement‑based networks than multi‑scale decoding?
- **Concept: Feature Space Alignment & Linear Attention**
  - *Why needed here:* CTA relies on projecting geometric features into the contextual space and using linear attention for efficient, selective fusion.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the purpose of using a linear attention mechanism (Equation 4) instead of standard softmax attention in CTA?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**
Input (stereo pairs or video frames) -> Shared ConvNeXt Encoder -> Multi‑scale features F c = {F1,F2,F3,F4}.
F1 -> Correlation Pyramid.
F e (early features, e.g., from stage1‑3 first block) -> Initializes GRU hidden states.
GRU Loop: at each step, receives context from F l (late features, e.g., stage1‑3 last block) and correlation features, updates hidden state -> final hidden states H.
H + F c -> Cross‑Task Adapter (linear attention) -> fused features F f.
F c + F f -> Mask2Former Parsing Decoder -> semantic/instance segmentation outputs.
H -> Geometric Decoder -> disparity/flow outputs.

**Critical path**  
The shared encoder extracts F c. Features split: F1 used for correlation pyramid; early features (F e) initialize GRU; late features (F l) guide GRU updates. The geometric stream iterates GRUs to produce final hidden states H. H is fed to the Cross‑Task Adapter, which fuses it with F c to produce F f and inject into the parsing decoder. Both streams then produce outputs; losses are computed. For semi‑supervised learning, a teacher model generates H and uncertainty to create pseudo‑labels that supervise the student model on unlabeled data.

**Design tradeoffs**
- *Bidirectional interaction vs. stability:* Infusing parsing features stabilizes geometric matching but may propagate parsing errors. CTA’s selective fusion mitigates but does not eliminate this risk.
- *Semi‑supervised efficiency vs. ceiling:* Pseudo‑labels enable use of unlabeled data but quality is limited by teacher’s performance on synthetic data; distribution shifts can cap gains.
- *Parameter efficiency vs. overhead:* TwInS uses a single encoder and lightweight GRU/CTA, reducing parameters compared to duplex encoders, but adds recurrent and attention overhead.

**Failure signatures**
- **Geometric stream collapse:** In low‑texture or overexposed regions, geometric predictions become unreliable, leading to high uncertainty and degraded segmentation (see Figure 8).
- **CTA misalignment:** If projected geometric features are poorly aligned with contextual feature space, fusion may add noise, harming parsing performance. HFSB baseline underperformance in Table IV/Figure 7 illustrates this.
- **Semi‑supervised drift:** If uncertainty estimation is miscalibrated (e.g., overconfident in ambiguous regions), pseudo‑labels may be erroneous, propagating errors to the student and harming both tasks.

**First 3 experiments**
1. **Ablation of Context‑Enriched Refinement:** Train with/without infusing late features (F l) into GRU updates (Table III row 2 vs. 3). Expect EPE/D1 improvement if mechanism works.
2. **CTA vs. Alternative Fusion:** Replace CTA with Feature Fusion Module (FFM, addition) or Hierarchical Feature Selection Block (HFSB) and measure mIoU on Cityscapes/KITTI (Table IV). Expect CTA to outperform.
3. **Semi‑supervised Strategy Validation:** Compare fully supervised (vKITTI2 only), unsupervised continuation on Cityscapes, and proposed semi‑supervised (vKITTI2 → Cityscapes with pseudo‑labels) (Table V). Expect semi‑supervised to yield best joint performance, demonstrating utility of unlabeled data.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The authors identify three open questions for future research:

1. **Cross‑task mutual supervision for self‑evolving joint learning:** How can mechanisms be designed to enable a fully self‑evolving joint learning framework that minimizes reliance on human annotations for both scene parsing and geometric vision tasks? (Future work direction stated in the paper.)
2. **Robust task interaction against failure propagation:** What robust task interaction mechanisms can be developed to mitigate the adverse influence of failure in one task (e.g., geometric vision under poor illumination) on the other in bidirectional joint learning? (Identified in Limitations.)
3. **Extension to open‑vocabulary segmentation:** How can TwInS be extended to support open‑vocabulary segmentation, handling novel semantic classes without additional annotations, thereby further reducing annotation dependency? (Also in Limitations.)

## Limitations
- Precise architecture details (e.g., ConvNeXt variant, channel dimensions, CTA’s linear attention implementation) are not fully specified, hindering reproducibility.
- Semi‑supervised pipeline relies on uncertainty estimates from a model trained on synthetic data; calibration on real‑world scenes is untested and may produce erroneous pseudo‑labels.
- Evaluation is limited to two datasets; generalization to other environments or tasks (e.g., panoptic segmentation, depth estimation) is not demonstrated.
