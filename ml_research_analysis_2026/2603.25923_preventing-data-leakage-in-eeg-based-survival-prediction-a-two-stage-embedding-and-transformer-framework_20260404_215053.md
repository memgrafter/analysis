# Preventing Data Leakage in EEG-Based Survival Prediction: A Two-Stage Embedding and Transformer Framework

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.25923  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25923  
- **Reference count:** 10  
- **Primary result:** A two-stage framework with strict patient-level isolation prevents data leakage, achieving test AUC 0.909 and Sensitivity@99% Specificity 0.618 on post-cardiac-arrest EEG data.

## Executive Summary
This study identifies and addresses a critical data leakage issue in EEG-based survival prediction for comatose patients after cardiac arrest. The leakage arises from reusing windowed EEG segments across multi-stage training, inflating validation performance while harming generalization. The proposed solution is a two-stage framework: first, a CNN with ArcFace loss extracts embeddings from 5‑minute EEG windows; second, a Transformer aggregates these embeddings for patient‑level prediction. Crucially, training stages are isolated at the patient level to ensure no overlap of patient data between stages. Control experiments demonstrate that violating this isolation causes a stark divergence between validation (AUC >0.99) and test performance (AUC 0.900, Sensitivity@99% 0.324). With leakage prevention, the model achieves robust test performance (AUC 0.909, Sensitivity@99% 0.618) and close alignment between validation and test metrics, confirming effective generalization under stringent specificity constraints.

## Method Summary
The framework operates in two distinct stages. Stage 1 processes raw EEG recordings segmented into non‑overlapping 5‑minute windows. A 1D convolutional neural network (CNN) maps each window to a fixed‑dimensional embedding, trained with the ArcFace loss to enhance class separability between survivors and non‑survivors. Stage 2 treats the set of embeddings from a single patient as a sequence and feeds them to a Transformer encoder. The Transformer aggregates contextual information across windows, and a final linear layer outputs a probability of survival to hospital discharge. The key design principle is **patient‑level isolation**: all windows from a given patient are assigned exclusively to either training stage 1 or stage 2 (or validation/test), preventing any patient’s data from influencing both stages. This ensures that the model learns truly generalizable features rather than memorizing patient‑specific patterns.

## Key Results
- **Leakage dramatically inflates validation but harms test performance:** With patient overlap between stages, validation AUC exceeds 0.99 and Sensitivity@99% nears 0.90, yet independent test AUC drops to 0.900 and Sensitivity@99% collapses to 0.324.  
- **Isolation yields robust generalization:** The non‑leaked model achieves test AUC 0.909 (95% CI: 0.883–0.933) and Sensitivity@99% Specificity 0.618 (0.407–0.687).  
- **Validation and test metrics align:** Without leakage, validation AUC (0.955) and test AUC (0.909) are comparably high, indicating effective control of overfitting.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The root cause of leakage is the reuse of windows from the same patient across training stages, which allows the model to indirectly “see” the same patient during both feature extraction and aggregation. This creates a shortcut: the second stage learns to recognize the patient identity rather than to combine clinical information. By enforcing patient‑level splits, the second stage is forced to generalize from embeddings produced by the first stage on entirely unseen patients. The two‑stage design thus ensures that the embedding space learned by the CNN (with ArcFace) is discriminative and transferable, and that the Transformer learns meaningful aggregation strategies independent of patient identity.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Raw EEG → Windowing → CNN+ArcFace (embedding per window) → Sequence of embeddings → Transformer (aggregation) → Patient‑level prediction  

**Critical path:**  
Patient EEG recordings → split into 5‑min windows → stage 1 CNN extracts embedding for each window → all embeddings from a patient form a sequence → stage 2 Transformer processes sequence → final layer outputs survival probability.  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Window length: shorter windows increase noise, longer windows may dilute informative content; 5 min chosen empirically.  
- CNN vs. other temporal models: CNN efficient for local features; may miss long‑range patterns within a window.  
- ArcFace vs. softmax: ArcFace improves separability but adds hyperparameter (margin).  
- Transformer depth: deeper models capture complex interactions but risk overfitting on limited patient data.  

**Failure signatures:**  
- Overly optimistic validation performance coupled with poor test performance indicates patient overlap between stages.  
- Large gap between validation and test AUC (when isolation is enforced) suggests model instability or data shift.  

**First three experiments:**  
1. Compare leaked (patient overlap) vs. non‑leaked (patient isolation) training, measuring validation and test AUC/Sensitivity@99%.  
2. Ablate ArcFace loss (replace with standard softmax) to quantify its contribution to embedding quality and final performance.  
3. Vary window duration (e.g., 1 min, 3 min, 5 min, 10 min) and report impact on AUC and Sensitivity@99% to identify optimal segmentation.

## Limitations
- **Single‑center cohort:** All data from one medical center may limit generalizability to other populations and EEG protocols.  
- **Fixed clinical threshold:** Performance evaluated only at 99% specificity; utility at other operating points (e.g., 95% specificity) not reported.  
- **Incomplete ablation:** Isolating the contribution of each framework component (CNN vs. Transformer, window length) remains an open question.

## Confidence
- **Leakage Impact Claim:** High – Stark, reproducible divergence between leaked and non‑leaked regimes on the same dataset.  
- **Framework Generalization Claim:** Medium – Robustness shown on held‑out test set from same center, but lacks external validation.  
- **Clinical Utility Claim:** Medium – Meets stringent specificity bar, but prospective validation and net benefit analysis are needed for deployment.

## Next Checks
1. **Multi‑Center Replication:** Train and evaluate the exact framework on at least two independent, geographically distinct post‑cardiac‑arrest EEG datasets to quantify performance retention or decay.  
2. **External Model Audit:** Apply the patient‑level isolation protocol to re‑evaluate 2–3 existing EEG‑prognostication models from the literature to empirically gauge prevalence of the leakage artifact.  
3. **Threshold‑Robustness Analysis:** Generate full ROC curves and report sensitivity at multiple specificities (95%, 99%, 99.5%) for the non‑leaked model to characterize stability across clinically relevant trade‑offs.
