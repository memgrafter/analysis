# When Is Collective Intelligence a Lottery? Multi-Agent Scaling Laws for Memetic Drift in LLMs
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.24676
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24676
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Neutral multi-agent LLM consensus is often driven by memetic drift (sampling noise) rather than bias, with drift rate scaling as \(1/N^2\) and \(1/m\), consensus time scaling as \(mN^2/\alpha^2\) steps, and a drift–selection crossover when \(\Gamma_h = mN/(\alpha|h|) \sim 1\).

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates when agreement in multi-agent LLM systems arises from collective reasoning versus random sampling noise—termed *memetic drift*. The authors propose that mutual in-context learning, where agents learn from each other’s sampled outputs, creates a feedback loop that can amplify stochastic noise into population-wide consensus even without external bias. They introduce the Quantized Simplex Gossip (QSG) model, a tractable framework where agents hold continuous beliefs on a simplex and exchange quantized messages (hard single labels, top-\(m\) lists, or soft distributions). Analysis yields precise scaling laws: the polarization (drift) rate scales as \((1-\|x\|^2)/(mN^2)\), mean-field consensus time scales as \(mN^2/\alpha^2\) interaction steps, and for weak biases \(h\), fixation probability follows a logistic curve with \(\Gamma_h = mN/(\alpha|h|)\), indicating a drift–selection crossover. These predictions are validated in QSG simulations and LLM-based naming-game experiments across population sizes \(N=2\)–\(32\) and bandwidths \(m\), confirming the \(1/N^2\) and \(1/m\) scalings. The work provides a null model for multi-agent coordination, helping distinguish when consensus is a “lottery” from when it reflects amplified systematic bias.

## Method Summary
The study combines theoretical analysis of the QSG model with empirical LLM experiments under the Neutral Naming Drift (NND) protocol. In QSG, \(N\) agents each maintain a probability vector \(x_i \in \Delta^{K-1}\) over \(K\) labels. Each interaction samples an ordered speaker–listener pair uniformly. The speaker emits a quantized message \(y\) from its belief \(x_S\): Hard (\(m=1\): one-hot sample), Top-\(m\) (empirical distribution from \(m\) samples), or Soft (\(m=\infty\): full distribution). The listener updates \(x_L \leftarrow (1-\alpha)x_L + \alpha y\). Metrics include polarization \(U = \|\bar{x}\|^2\), early drift \(\mathbb{E}[\Delta U]\), consensus time (first step with \(U \geq 0.9\)), and fixation probability.

LLM experiments use GPT-4o and Claude Haiku with synthetic 5-character labels. Agents interact via structured prompts enforcing JSON output; speaker labels are revealed only after the listener generates its response. The listener updates an internal memory (last \(H=10\) observed labels), approximating the QSG update with an effective \(\alpha\). Parameters varied: \(N \in \{2,\dots,32\}\), \(m \in \{1,2,3,4,10\}\) (number of labels emitted), and introduced biases \(h\) via prompt tilts. Results are aggregated over 5–10 random seeds per condition.

## Key Results
- Early memetic drift rate scales as \(\alpha^2 (1-\|x\|^2)/(mN^2)\); experiments confirm \(1/N^2\) and \(1/m\) dependence.
- Mean-field consensus time scales as \(mN^2/\alpha^2\) interaction steps (\(\propto mN/\alpha^2\) rounds); LLM data follows \(N^2\) scaling in steps and \(N\) scaling in rounds.
- For two labels with weak bias \(h\), fixation probability matches \(\text{logistic}(\Gamma_h)\) with \(\Gamma_h = mN/(\alpha|h|)\), defining a drift–selection crossover at \(|\Gamma_h| \sim 1\).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Symmetry breaking and consensus in neutral multi-agent LLM naming games arise from **mutual in-context learning**, where agents learn from one another's sampled outputs rather than a fixed external distribution.  
**Mechanism:** Each agent's arbitrary initial sample becomes evidence for another agent in subsequent interactions. This feedback loop allows stochastic sampling noise to compound across the population, driving consensus even when no individual agent has a prior preference (neutral setting).  
**Break condition:** External rewards or strong priors (non-neutral setting) introduce selection forces that may dominate drift.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Quantized communication (sampling discrete labels from continuous belief distributions) injects **sampling variance** that destabilizes symmetric states and accelerates drift toward polarization.  
**Mechanism:** When an agent with an uncertain belief (high entropy) sends a discrete sample (Hard) or short list (Top-\(m\)), the conditional variance of the message is nonzero. This variance appears as an additive positive drift in the polarization potential \(U = \|\bar{x}\|^2\), pushing the population away from symmetry. The variance scales as \(1/m\).  
**Break condition:** Infinite bandwidth (\(m \to \infty\), Soft) eliminates sampling variance, making the symmetric state noise-stable under neutrality.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** A **drift–selection crossover** occurs, controlled by \(\Gamma_h = mN/(\alpha |h|)\). For \(|\Gamma_h| \ll 1\), memetic drift dominates and the winner is nearly random (“lottery”). For \(|\Gamma_h| \gg 1\), weak systematic biases \(h\) are amplified to determine consensus.  
**Mechanism:** Sampling-driven drift scales as \(1/(mN^2)\) per step, while bias-driven selection scales linearly in \(h/\alpha\). Their competition is captured by a diffusion approximation for \(K=2\), yielding the logistic fixation probability.  
**Break condition:** Very small \(N\) or large \(\alpha\) always favors drift; very large \(|h|\) always favors selection, making the crossover less sharp.

Quick check: If doubling \(N\) in neutral Hard QSG, what happens to (a) early drift rate and (b) mean-field consensus time? Why?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Agents (belief \(x_i\)) -> Interaction scheduler (uniform random speaker–listener pairs) -> Quantized emitter (Hard/Top-m/Soft message \(y\)) -> In-context updater (\(x_L \leftarrow (1-\alpha)x_L + \alpha y\)) -> Measurement probe (estimate \(\bar{x}\), \(U\)).

**Critical path:** Initialize all \(x_i(0)=1/K\) -> repeat: pair selection -> speaker samples \(y\) from \(x_S\) -> listener updates \(x_L\) -> (optional) probe \(\bar{x}\) -> check consensus threshold \(U \geq 0.9\) -> terminate.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Bandwidth \(m\): Higher \(m\) reduces drift (\(\propto 1/m\)) and makes system more responsive to biases, but increases token cost.  
- Adaptation rate \(\alpha\): Larger \(\alpha\) speeds dynamics but weakens bias impact relative to drift (\(\Gamma_h \propto 1/\alpha\)).  
- Population size \(N\): Larger \(N\) suppresses drift (\(\propto 1/N^2\)) and increases consensus time (\(\propto N^2\)), making outcomes more deterministic but slower.

**Failure signatures:**  
1. Drift-dominated consensus: High run-to-run variability, slow \(U\) growth, fixation \(\approx 1/K\).  
2. Selection suppression: Small bias \(h\) still leads to wrong consensus; \(\Gamma_h \ll 1\).  
3. Mean-field breakdown: Discrete jumps for \(\alpha=1\) cause faster polarization; finite-size effects dominate at small \(N\).

**First experiments:**  
1. Validate \(1/N^2\) drift scaling: Sweep \(N\) in neutral Hard QSG/LLM naming game, measure early \(\Delta U\), expect slope \(-2\) on log-log.  
2. Validate \(1/m\) bandwidth scaling: Fix \(N,\alpha\), sweep Top-\(m\), measure early drift; confirm inverse proportionality.  
3. Probe drift–selection crossover: Inject tiny bias \(h\), sweep \(N\), plot fixation probability vs. \(N\); expect logistic S-curve crossing 0.5 at \(N_c \approx \alpha/(m|h|)\).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Structured networks:** How do small-world or scale-free topologies alter the drift–selection crossover and scaling laws?  
   *Basis:* Discussion calls for extending analysis to structured networks.  
   *Why unresolved:* QSG assumes well-mixed; real systems have constrained communication.  
   *Evidence needed:* Simulations/LLM experiments on various network graphs.

2. **Heterogeneous agents:** How do varying internal uncertainties, adaptation rates, or prompt designs affect collective dynamics?  
   *Basis:* Discussion identifies heterogeneous agents as a necessary extension.  
   *Why unresolved:* QSG assumes identical agents; real LLM populations vary.  
   *Evidence needed:* QSG with agent-specific parameters; LLM experiments mixing model families/prompts.

3. **Pretraining priors:** To what extent do pretraining data biases compete with or overwhelm memetic drift?  
   *Basis:* Discussion calls for incorporating training-data priors as selection forces.  
   *Why unresolved:* QSG isolates neutrality; pretraining adds asymmetries that may interact nonlinearly.  
   *Evidence needed:* Compare QSG with added fixed biases to LLMs with known dataset skews.

4. **Agent alignment:** Can individually aligned agents still produce misaligned collective outcomes?  
   *Basis:* Discussion raises whether “a society of individually aligned agents can still produce misaligned collective outcomes.”  
   *Why unresolved:* QSG treats agents as neutral; alignment might introduce hidden asymmetries or new failure modes.  
   *Evidence needed:* Run NND with aligned vs. misaligned LLM populations, tracking convention alignment.

## Limitations
- **LLM replication unknowns:** Exact decoding parameters (temperature, top_p), label generation method, and Top-\(m\) response format are unspecified.
- **Effective \(\alpha\) mapping:** In LLM experiments, \(\alpha\) is implicit via memory updates and treated as a fitted parameter; direct control is unclear.
- **Protocol timing:** Memory update timing (delayed reveal synchronization) and probe measurement details (sample count, decoding) are not fully specified.

## Confidence
- **Theoretical derivations (QSG scaling laws):** High (rigorous analysis)
- **Empirical scaling in QSG simulations:** High (direct implementation matches theory)
- **LLM experimental scalings (fitted \(\alpha\)):** Medium (rely on post-hoc fitting and stochastic outputs)
- **Drift–selection crossover validation:** Medium (validated via fitted \(\Gamma_h\), not direct \(h\) control)
