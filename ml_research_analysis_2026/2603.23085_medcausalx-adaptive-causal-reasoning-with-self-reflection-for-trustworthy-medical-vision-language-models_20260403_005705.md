# MedCausalX: Adaptive Causal Reasoning with Self-Reflection for Trustworthy Medical Vision-Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.23085
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23085
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MedCausalX improves diagnostic consistency by 5.4 points, reduces hallucination by over 10 points, achieves state-of-the-art spatial grounding with 81.2% average accuracy and 78.7% diagnostic consistency on five medical benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Medical vision-language models often suffer from spurious correlations and unreliable diagnoses due to lacking explicit causal reasoning. This paper introduces MedCausalX, an end-to-end framework that integrates three key innovations: (1) the CRMed dataset with fine-grained anatomical annotations, structured causal chains (A→P→Y), and counterfactual variants; (2) a two-stage adaptive reflection mechanism using special tokens <causal> and <verify> to enable self-correction; and (3) trajectory-level optimization via error-attributed reinforcement learning that combines off-policy DPO and on-policy GRPO. Evaluated on five medical benchmarks, MedCausalX significantly improves diagnostic consistency (+5.4), reduces hallucination (>10 points), and sets new records on spatial grounding tasks, demonstrating the viability of explicit causal reasoning for trustworthy medical AI.

## Method Summary
MedCausalX employs a causal factorization of diagnostic reasoning, modeling the joint distribution p(Y,P,A|I,q) as p(Y|A,P,I,q)·p(P|A,I,q)·p(A|I,q). This factorization is supervised by the CRMed dataset, which provides aligned anatomical areas, pathologies, diagnoses, and counterfactual perturbations that selectively disrupt spurious shortcuts. The model (a base VLM like Qwen2.5-VL) is first fine-tuned via supervised learning to generate a two-stage reasoning trace: after seeing <causal>, it produces a preliminary chain; encountering <verify> triggers a self-correction phase. To further refine causal reliability, a dual-policy reinforcement learning pipeline is introduced. Off-policy Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) constructs preference pairs from error trajectories by localizing the first divergence point (tfail) via a semantic similarity threshold τ; the policy is then trained to prefer corrected continuations. Followed by on-policy Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which samples multiple trajectories per input, computes a composite reward R = R_acc + R_format + R_causal, and updates the policy with group-relative advantages. This combination steers the model toward causally consistent reasoning while maintaining format adherence and accuracy.

## Key Results
- Diagnostic consistency increased by 5.4 points, reaching 78.7% on average across five benchmarks.
- Hallucination rate reduced by over 10 points (to 36.4%), significantly lowering false positives.
- Spatial grounding improved, setting a new record of 29.83% Region-F1 on multi-region detection; average accuracy 81.2% surpasses prior state-of-the-art.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** MedCausalX explicitly encodes causal structure between anatomy, pathology, and diagnosis via a factorization and supervised data, which helps the model avoid spurious correlations.
- **Mechanism:** The framework factorizes the joint distribution p(Y,P,A|I,q) into p(Y|A,P,I,q)·p(P|A,I,q)·p(A|I,q). CRMed provides triples (A,P,Y) with fine-grained anatomical bounding boxes and textual chains, plus counterfactual variants that selectively perturb A or P to break shortcuts. This supervision forces the model to learn genuine A→P→Y dependencies.
- **Core assumption:** Clinical diagnosis follows a compositional causal hierarchy where anatomical localization precedes pathological characterization, which together determine the diagnosis.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We first introduce the CRMed dataset providing fine-grained anatomical annotations, structured causal reasoning chains, and counterfactual variants..."
  - [section 3.1] "We model diagnostic reasoning as a structured conditional generation process over three semantically grounded variables: anatomical annotation A, pathological characterization P, and diagnostic conclusion Y."
  - [corpus] Neighboring work "CF-VLM: CounterFactual Vision-Language Fine-tuning" also uses counterfactuals, but MedCausalX integrates them within an SCM and RL pipeline.
- **Break condition:** If anatomical annotations are inaccurate or counterfactual perturbations fail to disrupt spurious features, the model may still rely on shortcuts. The rigid A→P→Y ordering might also not capture all diagnostic patterns.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The two-stage adaptive reflection with <causal> and <verify> tokens enables the model to detect and correct its own causal disruptions during generation, reducing errors without external intervention.
- **Mechanism:** During training, every sample is formatted as (I,q,<causal>,R_biased,<verify>,R_gold,y). The model first generates a preliminary reasoning chain after <causal>; then <verify> signals a self-correction phase where it revises the chain. At inference, the model learns to emit <verify> when internal consistency checks fail.
- **Core assumption:** Clinicians iteratively refine diagnoses by revisiting hypotheses; this process can be mimicked by a token-triggered two-stage generation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "a two-stage adaptive reflection architecture equipped with <causal> and <verify> tokens, enabling the model to autonomously determine when and how to perform causal analysis and verification."
  - [section 3.1] "Reflective token augmentation... This construction enforces a two-stage reasoning protocol: the model first generates a preliminary causal analysis... then performs verification and correction triggered by <verify>."
  - [corpus] "Self-Refine: Iterative Refinement with Self-Feedback" (Madaan et al.) demonstrates self-iteration, but MedCausalX embeds it into the token stream for tighter control.
- **Break condition:** If the model fails to learn proper token emission (e.g., omitting <verify> or producing invalid transitions), the correction mechanism breaks down, leading to unrefined outputs.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Error-attributed reinforcement learning with off-policy DPO and on-policy GRPO, guided by localized failure points and a composite reward, shapes the reasoning trajectory to prioritize causal consistency over superficial accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** After SFT, error trajectories are gathered. For each failure, a divergence threshold τ identifies the first step tfail where the generated chain deviates from ground truth. Preference pairs are constructed by contrasting the erroneous continuation from tfail onward with the correct one, while keeping the prefix fixed. DPO optimizes the policy off-policy on these pairs. Then GRPO samples on-policy trajectories, computes a composite reward R = R_acc + R_format + R_causal, and updates using group-relative advantages.
- **Core assumption:** Causal errors can be pinpointed to specific reasoning steps; learning from corrected continuations from the point of failure is more efficient than trajectory-level losses. Group normalization stabilizes on-policy exploration.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "trajectory-level optimization via error-attributed reinforcement learning combining off-policy DPO and on-policy GRPO."
  - [section 3.2.2] "Error Localization Mechanism... we precisely identify the failure point tfail via step-wise semantic divergence detection" and "Stage II: On-policy Training... compute a composite reward R(g) = R_acc + R_format + R_causal."
  - [corpus] DeepSeek-R1 uses RL for reasoning; MedCausalX adapts RL for causal correction with explicit error attribution.
- **Break condition:** Inaccurate tfail (e.g., due to poor semantic similarity metric) yields misleading preference pairs, harming learning. Composite reward weights (λ_acc, λ_causal) need tuning; improper weighting may neglect causality or over-prioritize format.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** CRMed dataset (images, A, P, Y, counterfactuals) -> Base VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) with LoRA -> Vocabulary augmentation (<causal>, <verify>) -> Training pipeline: Causal SFT -> Rollout -> Error collection (tfail detection) -> Off-policy DPO -> On-policy GRPO -> Inference (generates <causal> then optional <verify> and answer)

- **Critical path:**
  1. Annotate/curate CRMed: obtain anatomical boxes, write causal chains, generate counterfactual perturbations.
  2. Initialize model: load base VLM, add special tokens, set up LoRA.
  3. Run SFT for 3 epochs on CRMed with unified format.
  4. Generate rollouts on training split; collect error trajectories.
  5. Run DPO for 2 epochs using error pairs.
  6. Continue with GRPO for 2000 steps, sampling on-policy.
  7. Evaluate on benchmarks; adjust hyperparameters (τ, G, reward weights) based on validation.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - SCM factorization ensures causal grounding but may be too restrictive; some diagnoses might not follow strict A→P→Y. Simpler than full causal graph discovery but loses flexibility.
  - Reflective tokens require the model to learn token semantics; if training data is insufficient, the model may ignore them. Also increases sequence length, affecting memory.
  - Dual-policy RL: DPO provides stable off-policy learning from static errors but may overfit to collected errors; GRPO allows on-policy exploration but introduces variance. Combining them increases training time and hyperparameter sensitivity.
  - Counterfactual generation: Perturbations (spatial shifts, pathology misalignment) are cheap approximations of interventions; they may not perfectly capture true causal mechanisms and could introduce artifacts.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low diagnostic consistency: Check if <verify> token appears in outputs; analyze whether correction steps actually improve predictions. Possibly τ is too high (missing errors) or too low (false positives).
  - High hallucination: Reward component R_causal may be underweighted; inspect causal consistency scores. Also CRMed may lack diversity in pathological descriptions.
  - Poor spatial grounding (low IoU): Anatomical localization might be weak; examine if bounding box predictions are accurate during SFT. Could be due to insufficient anatomical annotation quality or model capacity.
  - Training instability: Loss spikes during GRPO might indicate group size G too small or reward scaling issues. Reduce group size or adjust ε.
  - Model produces long, verbose chains: Might be reward for format or causal consistency encouraging verbosity; check response length trends.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablation study (Table 4): Removing CRMed dataset drops BLEU-1 by ~10 points, showing causal supervision is critical. Removing reflective tokens or RL also harms performance, confirming each component's contribution.
  2. Benchmark comparison (Table 2): MedCausalX achieves state-of-the-art accuracy (81.2%) and lowest hallucination (36.4%) on average across four medical VQA datasets, beating MedVLM-R1 and Med-R1.
  3. Case analysis (Figure 3): Visual examples illustrate how MedCausalX corrects spurious cardiac silhouette localization via adaptive verification, whereas baselines either mislocalize or produce inconsistent descriptions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Does MedCausalX's fixed A→P→Y factorization work for medical VQA tasks with different causal structures?  
  *Basis in paper:* The paper assumes this order (Sec 3.1) without testing alternatives.  
  *Why unresolved:* All benchmarks likely follow it; unknown if model fails when causal dependencies differ.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Evaluate on tasks with reversed causality or learn causal graph from data.

- **Open Question 2:** Can CRMed's counterfactual generation be automated to reduce manual annotation?  
  *Basis in paper:* CRMed uses expert annotations and handcrafted perturbations (Sec 3.1).  
  *Why unresolved:* The process is labor-intensive and may not scale.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Develop generative/self-supervised methods to produce high-quality counterfactuals.

- **Open Question 3:** How robust is MedCausalX to distribution shifts across diverse medical modalities and rare conditions?  
  *Basis in paper:* Out-of-domain tests only on three datasets (Table 11), limited scope.  
  *Why unresolved:* Medical imaging is vast; model may degrade on unseen types.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Test on many modalities and diseases, measure causal consistency.

## Limitations
- CRMed construction details and counterfactual generation are underspecified, risking replication variance; strict A→P→Y hierarchy may not capture all diagnostic patterns.
- R_causal reward relies on an external LLM judge (GPT-4o) with no specified local alternative; error-localization threshold τ and reward weights are sensitive and require careful tuning.

## Confidence
- **Performance gains (accuracy, consistency, grounding):** High – robust across five benchmarks with ablations.
- **Causal factorization via CRMed:** Medium – hinges on dataset quality and counterfactual validity.
- **Adaptive reflection mechanism:** Medium – effectiveness contingent on proper token usage.
- **Error-attributed RL optimization:** Medium – sensitive to τ, reward design, and may introduce instability.
