# Towards Realistic Personalization: Evaluating Long-Horizon Preference Following in Personalized User-LLM Interactions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.04191  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04191  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** LLM performance in following user preferences degrades significantly with longer contexts (up to 247K tokens) and more implicit preference expressions; RAG most effectively mitigates long-context decay.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RealPref, a benchmark designed to evaluate large language models (LLMs) on their ability to follow nuanced, long-term user preferences across extended, multi-session dialogues. The benchmark constructs 100 detailed user profiles with 1,300 preferences spanning four levels of explicitness, embedded within realistic conversational histories of varying lengths. Evaluations across state-of-the-art LLMs reveal consistent performance drops as context grows and preferences become more implicit. The work identifies retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) as the most effective mitigation strategy and highlights a critical gap: current models lack proactivity in generalizing preferences to new scenarios. RealPref provides a rigorous, multi-dimensional evaluation framework for personalized LLM research.

## Method Summary
RealPref is a synthetic benchmark built via a multi-stage pipeline. Starting from PersonaHub personas, a "Profile Generator" creates user biographies and life-event timelines. A "Preference Tagger" then derives 10 original and 3 generalized preferences per user, categorized by topic and type (e.g., Relative, Negative). For each original preference, a "Conversation Builder" generates four dialogue variants expressing the preference at increasing levels of implicitness (Explicit Direct, Explicit Contextual, Implicit Stylistic, Implicit Experience). These are mixed with "life-event" and "random" conversations by a "Context Mixer" to reach target token lengths (2K to 247K) via interval or tail insertion. A "Query Designer" crafts three non-leaking question types per preference (MCQ, True-or-False, Open-ended). Finally, an LLM judge (Claude Sonnet 4) scores open-ended responses on three axes: Preference Awareness, Preference Alignment, and Answer Quality.

## Key Results
- **Long-context decay:** LLM performance drops significantly as context length increases, with the steepest declines in Preference Awareness and Alignment.
- **Implicit preference gap:** Performance declines noticeably from explicit to implicit expression types, especially for experiential and stylistic cues.
- **Generalization failure:** Models struggle to apply learned preferences to novel, related scenarios unless provided with a "Reminder" prompt.
- **RAG efficacy:** Retrieval-Augmented Generation most effectively mitigates performance loss in very long contexts (e.g., 247K tokens).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** LLM performance degrades as conversational context length and complexity increase.
- **Mechanism:** Relevant user preference cues become sparse and diluted by irrelevant conversational noise, reducing the signal-to-noise ratio within the model's active context window. The model's pattern-matching and localized attention struggle to retrieve and reason over separated preference signals.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs rely on proximity-based attention; when preference-indicative turns are distant from the query, retrieval fails.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 5.3.3; supported by long-context benchmark literature.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** LLMs struggle more with implicit than explicit preference expressions.
- **Mechanism:** Explicit statements provide direct textual patterns for matching. Implicit expressions (stylistic cues, experiential feedback) require deeper semantic inference, cross-session narrative integration, and abstraction of latent principles.
- **Core assumption:** Current personalization is surface-level information extraction; inferring latent traits from behavioral cues is a separate, harder capability gap.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 5.3.2, Section 3.3.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Current LLMs lack proactivity in generalizing preferences to unseen scenarios.
- **Mechanism:** Generalization demands extracting a core reason/value behind a preference and applying it to a novel domain—an inferential step beyond associative retrieval. Models optimized for direct QA within context fail at this abstraction.
- **Core assumption:** Preference learning is associative, not inferential; building and applying a user model is not standard.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract, Section 5.3.5, Section 6.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Long-horizon, multi-session dialogue construction.
  - **Why needed:** RealPref simulates extended interactions with interspersed irrelevant content to test temporal preference retention.
  - **Quick check:** What is the functional difference between "Interval Token" and "Tail Token" context configurations, and what specific challenge does each test?

- **Concept:** Granular, multi-dimensional evaluation rubrics for open-ended generation.
  - **Why needed:** Generation tasks better measure proactive preference following than classification; the three-axis rubric (Awareness, Alignment, Quality) ensures reliable, actionable scoring.
  - **Quick check:** For a "Relative Preference" (Prefers A over B), what is the difference between a score of 3 and a score of 4 on "Preference Alignment"?

- **Concept:** Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) as a mitigation for long-context decay.
  - **Why needed:** The key practical finding is that RAG is most effective for very long contexts; understanding how retrieval surfaces sparse preference signals is crucial for implementation.
  - **Quick check:** In the RAG implementation, what sentence embedding model is used, and what specific content is retrieved (user msg, assistant msg, or both)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Profile Generator -> Preference Tagger -> Conversation Builder -> Context Mixer -> Query Designer -> LLM Judge. Generalized preferences diverge after the Preference Tagger.
- **Critical path:** User Profile -> Original Preferences -> Preference-Expression Conversations -> Context Assembly (with Random Interpolation) -> Query Generation -> Model Inference -> LLM-as-Judge Scoring.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Synthetic vs. real data (scale vs. ecological validity); explicit vs. implicit expression (reliability vs. realism); classification vs. generation tasks (cheap evaluation vs. diagnostic realism).
- **Failure signatures:** High MCQ but low True-False accuracy (format heuristics); minimal explicit-implicit drop (surface-level matching); poor generalization even with Reminder (no abstraction); RAG helps only for Extended contexts (confirms native window bottleneck).
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  Baseline Degradation Curve: Test a strong model (e.g., GPT-5) zero-shot across 6 context configurations with Experience Feedback expression; expect monotonic drop in open-ended scores.
  2.  Expression Type Sensitivity: Test same model in Normal context across 4 expression types; expect significant drop from Type 2 -> Type 3.
  3.  Mitigation Method Comparison: For a weaker model (e.g., Llama 3.3 70B) in Extended context, compare Zero-shot, Reminder, Few-shot CoT, and RAG; expect RAG to outperform.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **OQ1:** How can LLMs effectively model and adapt to temporally evolving or conditionally dependent user preferences in long-horizon interactions? (Basis: Section 6 identifies "Complex Preference" as a limitation.)
- **OQ2:** How do multimodal interaction contexts (e.g., images, video) impact LLMs' ability to infer and apply implicit user preferences over extended dialogues? (Basis: Section 6 notes "Richer Interaction Context.")
- **OQ3:** Can incorporating explicit user feedback signals (e.g., thumbs-up/down) improve LLMs' proactive recall and application of scattered user preferences? (Basis: Section 6 suggests "Complex User Feedback.")
- **OQ4:** How should evaluation rubrics for personalized LLMs be extended to include ethical dimensions such as fairness, privacy, and transparency? (Basis: Section 6 proposes "Evaluation Extension.")

## Limitations
- Synthetic data may not capture real conversational noise and complexity, threatening ecological validity.
- LLM-judge scoring is subjective; the choice of Claude Sonnet 4 and exact rubric formatting risk reproducibility.
- Unavailable models (GPT-5, GPT-5 mini) preclude direct verification of primary results.
- Tokenization specifics are unclear, so constructed context lengths may mismatch intended values.
- Generalization queries may not be truly out-of-distribution, possibly overstating the abstraction challenge.

## Next Checks
1.  **Cross-generator robustness:** Regenerate RealPref using two alternative LLM generators (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5) and verify that the same performance trends (length decay, implicit-explicit gap) hold with statistical significance.
2.  **Human-LLM correlation:** Collect a small set of genuine multi-session human-LLM dialogues, annotate ground-truth preferences, and evaluate the same models. Compute correlation between RealPref scores and real-data scores.
3.  **Judge consistency audit:** Sample 500 open-ended responses and score them with three distinct judge models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, Llama 3.1 405B). Report inter-rater reliability (e.g., ICC) and analyze systematic biases.
