# Make It Hard to Hear, Easy to Learn: Long-Form Bengali ASR and Speaker Diarization via Extreme Augmentation and Perfect Alignment
## Quick Facts
* arXiv ID: 2602.23070
* Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23070
* Reference count: 8
* Primary result: Achieved WER ~0.308 and DER ~0.245 on a 22-hour Bengali test set using a noise-augmented Whisper fine-tune and heuristic post-processing, with inference at ~0.019 RTF.

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the scarcity of Bengali long-form ASR and diarization resources by constructing the 882-hour Lipi-Ghor-882 dataset. For ASR, the authors discovered that scaling raw fine-tuning data or ensembling was ineffective; instead, fine-tuning Whisper-Medium on a small, perfectly aligned subset with 20% synthetic noise/reverberation yielded the best robustness. For diarization, retraining state-of-the-art models failed to improve performance; the primary gains came from applying strict heuristic post-processing (gap enforcement, segment merging) to outputs from the Pyannote Community-1 model. The resulting dual pipeline is highly efficient (~0.019 RTF) and establishes a new benchmark for Bengali long-form speech processing.

## Method Summary
The core methodology is data-centric and heuristic-driven. The authors first built Lipi-Ghor-882 by automatically aligning YouTube audio with subtitles using the Pyannote API. For ASR, they identified a small subset of this corpus with perfectly aligned transcriptions and fine-tuned a Whisper-Medium model, corrupting 20% of the training audio with on-the-fly noise and reverberation to force robust feature learning. For diarization, they used the off-the-shelf Pyannote Community-1 model and developed a deterministic post-processing algorithm (Algorithm 1) that enforces minimum gaps between speakers, merges close segments, and prunes short-duration speakers. The two pipelines are run separately and combined only at the evaluation stage.

## Key Results
* Created Lipi-Ghor-882, a 882-hour multi-speaker Bengali long-form dataset with SSTT annotations.
* ASR: Fine-tuned Whisper-Medium on a noisy, perfectly aligned subset achieved a public WER of ~0.308 on the 22-hour test set.
* Diarization: Pyannote Community-1 with custom heuristic post-processing achieved a public DER of ~0.245.
* Optimized inference pipeline (using faster-whisper and tuned VAD) achieved a real-time factor of ~0.019.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
* **Claim:** For low-resource Bengali long-form ASR, fine-tuning on a small subset of **perfectly aligned** data augmented with **synthetic noise/reverberation** outperforms raw data scaling, ensembling, and other architectural modifications.
* **Mechanism:** Synthetic degradation acts as a regularizer, preventing the model from memorizing acoustic artifacts from poorly aligned large-scale data. Perfect alignment ensures a clean supervision signal, allowing the model to learn invariant acoustic-phonetic mappings from distorted inputs to correct text.
* **Core assumption:** The pretrained Whisper model already encodes Bengali phonetics; the bottleneck is overfitting to dataset-specific noise and alignment errors. Noise augmentation通用izes within the language.
* **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states synthetic degradation is the "singular most effective approach." Section III.C confirms that training on the perfectly aligned, corrupted subset forced robust feature learning and led to the best score.

### Mechanism 2
* **Claim:** For Bengali long-form diarization, retraining SOTA models fails; performance is primarily lifted by **heuristic post-processing** of a robust base model's output.
* **Mechanism:** Pyannote Community-1 produces raw outputs with micro-segments and label inconsistencies. The post-processor (Algorithm 1) imposes conversational priors (minimum pauses, segment duration) via deterministic rules, correcting systematic structural errors that the model cannot reliably learn from the available data.
* **Core assumption:** The acoustic and speaker variability in this Bengali corpus exceeds what current open-source diarization architectures can model without massive labeled data. Structured conversational rules are a more accessible signal.
* **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes Diarizen performed poorly and retraining yielded negligible gains, while heuristics were the primary driver. Section IV.C details the algorithm and empirically derived thresholds.

### Mechanism 3
* **Claim:** The creation of **Lipi-Ghor-882** is the enabling mechanism; it provided the aligned, multi-speaker long-form testbed where failure of standard methods was observed, forcing the investigation into alternative data-centric and heuristic strategies.
* **Mechanism:** The scarcity of Bengali long-form resources prevented systematic evaluation. Auto-aligning YouTube audio with subtitles using Pyannote produced a large-scale corpus, revealing that naive scaling (ASR) and model retraining (diarization) were ineffective, which directed effort toward noise augmentation and post-processing.
* **Core assumption:** The initial Pyannote-based diarization used for alignment is a sufficiently good prior to create a useful, albeit noisy, aligned dataset.
* **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Introduction introduce Lipi-Ghor-882 as the benchmark addressing scarcity. Section II describes the collection and alignment pipeline.

## Foundational Learning
* **Concept:** Contrastive Learning via Synthetic Degradation
  * **Why needed:** Understanding why adding noise improves generalizability requires viewing it as creating a harder problem that prevents reliance on superficial acoustic cues.
  * **Quick check:** How might training with 20% noise/reverberation encourage invariant representations, and why could this be more effective than training on larger amounts of clean data?

* **Concept:** VAD-Driven Chunking for Long-Form ASR
  * **Why needed:** The pipeline's low RTF depends on efficient chunking. The trade-off between context loss and boundary accuracy (Silero vs. Pyannote) is critical for reproducing inference gains.
  * **Quick check:** What are two primary failure modes of VAD-based chunking, and how did manual parameter tuning for `faster-whisper` address one of them?

* **Concept:** Diarization Heuristic Post-Processing as Structured Prediction Correction
  * **Why needed:** The insight is that diarization errors have structured, rule-governed components (minimum turns, plausible overlaps). The post-processor deterministically corrects these.
  * **Quick check:** In Algorithm 1, what is the purpose of the "Strict Rename" step, and how does "Overlap Resolution & Gap Enforcement" differ from a simple merge?

## Architecture Onboarding
* **Component map:** Input Audio -> Silero VAD (chunking) -> faster-whisper (CT2-optimized Whisper-Medium) -> Stitched Transcript (ASR). Input Audio -> Pyannote Community-1 -> Custom Post-Processor (Algorithm 1) -> Final RTTM (Diarization). Lipi-Ghor-882 creation: YouTube Audios -> yt-dlp (subtitles/audio) -> Pyannote API (initial diarization) -> SSTT alignment.
* **Critical path:**
  1.  Create Lipi-Ghor-882 or download the dataset.
  2.  Extract the perfectly aligned ASR training subset from Lipi-Ghor-882.
  3.  Convert Whisper-Medium to CTranslate2; fine-tune on the subset with 20% on-the-fly noise/reverb.
  4.  Configure `faster-whisper` with manually tuned Silero VAD parameters.
  5.  For diarization, load Pyannote Community-1; run inference on audio.
  6.  Apply Algorithm 1 with tuned thresholds (θ_merge=3.79s, θ_gap=0.17s, etc.) to Pyannote output.
* **Design tradeoffs:**
  * **ASR:** Chose a single, lightweight, noise-regularized model over raw data scaling or ensembling, sacrificing potential ceiling for robustness and speed.
  * **Diarization:** Accepted inability to modify the core model; used a deterministic, tunable post-processor instead, trading model flexibility for predictable behavior.
  * **Overall:** Prioritized pipeline simplicity and speed (RTF ~0.019) over end-to-end joint optimization, deemed infeasible.
* **Failure signatures:**
  * **ASR:** No improvement when using the full noisy dataset instead of the perfectly aligned subset; worse performance with ROVER ensembling; word truncation at chunk boundaries with default VAD.
  * **Diarization:** <0.5% DER improvement after 20 epochs of retraining; degraded performance if input audio is pre-processed with Demucs noise removal.
  * **Inference:** Explosive RTF if batch processing or Demucs is reintroduced; poor diarization if post-processing thresholds are not tuned.
* **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  Reproduce ASR noise paradigm: Fine-tune Whisper-Medium on 1h perfectly aligned clip with 20% OTF noise/reverb vs. clean. Expect ~5-10% relative WER reduction on a held-out noisy validation set.
  2.  Validate diarization post-processing: Run Pyannote on a 30min test file, apply Algorithm 1. Manually inspect RTTM; expect elimination of sub-0.5s micro-segments and >170ms enforced gaps.
  3.  Benchmark VAD choice: Chunk a 10min test file with Silero vs. Pyannote VAD before `faster-whisper`. Compare WER and boundary errors; expect Silero to cause fewer premature cuts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
* **Question:** Why do global SOTA speaker diarization models (e.g., Diarizen) fail on long-form Bengali audio while simple heuristic post-processing yields gains?
* **Basis:** The paper states Diarizen performed poorly and retraining helped little; heuristics were the primary driver. The root cause—linguistic properties, recording artifacts, or architectural limits—is not diagnosed.
* **Why unresolved:** No failure analysis or ablation isolating Bengali-specific factors.
* **What evidence would resolve:** Error breakdowns by overlap, speech rate, noise; comparison of model embeddings on Bengali vs. other languages.

### Open Question 2
* **Question:** What specific properties of "perfectly aligned" annotations and synthetic corruption enable robust ASR fine-tuning, whereas raw data scaling and ensembling do not?
* **Basis:** The paper claims the combination is singularly effective but does not explain whether the benefit is from noise-induced invariance, clean labels, or matched corruption.
* **Why unresolved:** No controlled experiments varying alignment quality or noise profiles.
* **What evidence would resolve:** Ablations with synthetic misalignments and varied/no noise types; probing of learned representations.

### Open Question 3
* **Question:** Why does synthetic audio corruption improve ASR robustness but harm (or not help) diarization performance?
* **Question basis:** ASR improved with 20% noise/reverb; diarization saw no gain from muffled training data.
* **Why unresolved:** No comparison of how corruption affects acoustic features for CTC-based ASR vs. speaker embedding extraction.
* **What evidence would resolve:** Measure speaker embedding discriminability (EER) on corrupted vs. clean audio; test different corruption types separately for diarization.

### Open Question 4
* **Question:** To what extent do annotation inconsistencies in Lipi-Ghor-882 cap performance, and can semi-supervised learning mitigate this?
* **Basis:** Authors cite a ceiling from poor annotation quality (missing transcripts, diarization errors) but did not explore using the noisier full dataset.
* **Why unresolved:** No experiment adding varying fractions of noisy data or pseudo-labeling.
* **What evidence would resolve:** Controlled study adding noisy data to the perfect-subset training; pseudo-labeling with confidence filtering on the full 882h.

### Open Question 5
* **Question:** How do the heuristic diarization thresholds (θ_merge, θ_gap, etc.) generalize to other Bengali domains or speaker densities?
* **Basis:** The pipeline relies on fixed thresholds (e.g., θ_gap=0.17s) from empirical tuning; no cross-dataset validation.
* **Why unresolved:** No sensitivity analysis or tests on other Bengali corpora.
* **What evidence would resolve:** Ablation over threshold ranges on multiple Bengali diarization datasets or synthetic gap/overlap perturbations.

## Limitations
* Unspecified criteria and size of the "perfectly aligned" ASR training subset hinder exact replication.
* Heuristic diarization thresholds may be dataset-specific; no cross-validation or robustness analysis provided.
* Dataset construction via Pyannote API and YouTube subtitles risks propagating alignment noise into Lipi-Ghor-882.

## Confidence
* **Mechanism 1** (Noisy fine-tuning on perfect alignment): *Medium* – Strong internal evidence, but lacks external validation and subset details.
* **Mechanism 2** (Heuristic post-processing): *Medium* – Empirical results solid, but thresholds may not generalize; retraining failure unexplained.
* **Mechanism 3** (Lipi-Ghor-882 as key resource): *Medium* – Dataset is public and large, but auto-alignment quality could limit benchmark utility.
