# Probing Cultural Signals in Large Language Models through Author Profiling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16749  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16749  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** LLMs show non-trivial zero-shot author profiling accuracy on lyrics but exhibit systematic ethnic biases (defaulting to North American or Asian) and rely on thematic shortcuts; new fairness metrics (MAD, RD) quantify disparities.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates cultural biases in large language models (LLMs) by testing their ability to infer singer gender and ethnicity from song lyrics without fine-tuning. Using a balanced dataset of over 10,000 English-translated lyrics, the authors find that models achieve above-chance accuracy but systematically default to certain ethnic regions (e.g., North America). Analysis of model rationales reveals a reliance on broad thematic and emotional shortcuts rather than precise cultural-linguistic cues. The paper introduces fairness metrics—Modality Accuracy Divergence (MAD) and Recall Divergence (RD)—to quantify these biases and shows that prompt engineering can modestly improve fairness, though disparities persist across models.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed a dataset of 10,808 song lyrics from Deezer and Spotify, filtering for solo artists and translating non-English songs to English to remove grammatical gender confounds. Balanced subsets were created for ethnicity (six macro-regions) and gender. Multiple open-source LLMs (1.5B–24B parameters) were evaluated in a zero-shot author profiling task using five prompt variants (from basic to corrected instructions that forbid thematic reasoning). Predictions and rationales were extracted, and performance was measured via accuracy, statistical tests on prediction distributions, and the newly proposed MAD and RD fairness metrics. Error analysis involved word cloud comparisons of rationale terms and case studies of misclassified artists.

## Key Results
- LLMs achieve non-trivial zero-shot profiling accuracy but show strong systematic ethnic biases; most models default to North American ethnicity, while DeepSeek-1.5B aligns with Asian ethnicity.
- New fairness metrics (MAD, RD) reveal significant disparities across modalities; Ministral-8B exhibits the strongest ethnicity bias, while Gemma-12B is most balanced.
- Model rationales frequently cite high-level themes and emotions as evidence, and a "Corrected" prompt that suppresses this overreliance improves fairness for some models (e.g., Llama-8B) with a modest accuracy trade-off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Thematic Shortcut Reliance
- **Claim:** LLMs infer sociodemographic attributes by relying on high-level thematic and emotional shortcuts rather than precise cultural-linguistic cues.
- **Mechanism:** Rationales often mention broad themes (e.g., "emotions," "struggle") as primary evidence; this heuristic substitutes for analyzing specific lexical or cultural markers.
- **Core assumption:** Thematic content correlates with author identity in training data, making it a usable proxy even when culturally ambiguous.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "analysis of model rationales reveals reliance on thematic shortcuts... over precise cultural cues"
  - [section 6.3] Word clouds show "theme" and "emotional" as top words in false reasoning; "Corrected informed" prompt improves fairness by forbidding thematic reasoning.
  - [corpus] Balanced dataset decouples thematic content from cultural markers (e.g., Miriam Makeba's song misclassified as Asian due to thematic shortcuts about gold/slavery).

### Mechanism 2: Default Prediction Bias
- **Claim:** LLMs exhibit a default prediction toward the majority ethno-cultural norm (North American) in their training distribution, leading to under-prediction of minority regions.
- **Mechanism:** The model's internal probability distribution over ethnicity labels is skewed, making North American a high-probability fallback for ambiguous signals.
- **Core assumption:** Training data contains disproportionate North American content, calibrating the model's prior toward that modality.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "most models default toward North American ethnicity"
  - [section 6.1] Prediction distributions heavily weighted toward North America; ROC curves show high TPR/FPR for North America.
  - [section 6.4] Statistical tests reject uniform prediction distribution; Ministral-8B and Mistral-24B show particularly strong skew.
  - [corpus] Balanced evaluation dataset reveals non-North American English lyrics often misclassified as North American.

### Mechanism 3: Prompt Sensitivity
- **Claim:** Prompt design directly influences activation of cultural reasoning pathways; instruction complexity and explicit guidance modulate accuracy and bias.
- **Mechanism:** Incremental prompt engineering alters the model's internal focus during inference; complex prompts (Well-informed) can overload models, while targeted corrections (Corrected) suppress unhelpful heuristics.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs are instruction-sensitive enough that latent cultural knowledge can be steered, but with performance trade-offs and model-specific responses.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "prompt engineering... can modestly improve fairness"
  - [section 6.2] Prompt impact summary (Table 7): Well-informed lowers accuracy; Corrected improves ethnicity accuracy and reduces MAD/RD for some models.
  - [section 4.2] Prompt variants demonstrate gradient of instruction specificity.
  - [corpus] Same lyric instances under different prompts yield divergent rationales and predictions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Zero-shot author profiling  
  - **Why needed:** Evaluates whether pre-trained LLMs generalise cultural/linguistic knowledge to infer sociodemographic attributes without task-specific fine-tuning, distinguishing memorisation from abstract representation.  
  - **Quick check:** If a model predicts singer ethnicity from unseen lyrics with no examples, what does that imply about its training data's cultural coverage?

- **Concept:** Fairness metrics for profiling tasks (MAD, RD)  
  - **Why needed:** Traditional fairness metrics assume the prediction target is separate from the sensitive attribute; in author profiling the target *is* the sensitive attribute, requiring new metrics to measure parity in accuracy/recall across modalities.  
  - **Quick check:** Why might a model have high overall accuracy but still be unfair in profiling tasks?

- **Concept:** Sociological vs. biological constructs of ethnicity/gender  
  - **Why needed:** The paper adopts a sociological view (ethnicity as culturally constructed, expressed through language/style) to justify textual analysis, framing the task as detecting *perceived* cultural identity rather than immutable traits.  
  - **Quick check:** How does defining ethnicity as a "socially constructed cultural identity" change interpretation of model errors?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Lyric collection (Deezer/Spotify) → Metadata harmonization (artist gender/region) → Filtering (solo artists, balanced subsets) → Translation (non-English to English via Mistral Small 3.2) → Prompt module (5 variants) → Inference engine (multiple LLMs via vLLM) → Analysis suite (accuracy, MAD/RD, rationale mining, statistical tests)

- **Critical path:** Balanced dataset construction → prompt selection → model inference on all lyrics → prediction/rationale extraction → fairness metric calculation → qualitative error analysis → prompt iteration (Corrected).

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Translation vs. original cues:* Translation removes grammatical gender confounds but may distort cultural expressions.  
  - *Dataset balance vs. size:* Balancing reduces statistical power but isolates bias from majority-class effects.  
  - *Prompt complexity vs. performance:* Complex prompts yield richer data but degrade accuracy; simpler prompts are more reliable.  
  - *Model size vs. memorisation:* Smaller models less likely to memorise specific lyrics but have lower capacity for subtle cues.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. Output format non-compliance (models omit required fields under complex prompts).  
  2. Modality collapse (models predict zero instances for certain ethnicities, e.g., Oceania, South America).  
  3. Thematic overgeneralisation (rationales dominated by "emotions"/"themes" correlate with misclassification).  
  4. Translation artifacts (accuracy swings between translation models).

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline profiling:** Run `Informed` prompt on all models with ethnicity-balanced dataset; record per-model accuracy and prediction distributions. Expect non-trivial accuracy (> random) but skew toward North America.  
  2. **Bias quantification:** Compute MAD and RD per model/prompt. Expect Ministral-8B highest bias, Gemma-12B lowest; DeepSeek-1.5B non-North default.  
  3. **Prompt correction test:** Apply `Corrected informed` prompt; compare MAD/RD and accuracy against `Informed and expressive`. Expect modest fairness improvement for some models (e.g., Llama-8B) with slight accuracy trade-off; validate thematic shortcut suppression via rationale word-frequency shift.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Which specific linguistic/cultural cues do LLMs rely on for demographic inference, and how do these align with sociolinguistic theory vs. superficial thematic shortcuts?**  
  *Basis:* Section 6.3 finds overreliance on themes/emotions; Appendix A shows surface lexical cues (e.g., "white man"). *Why unresolved:* Analysis limited to word frequencies; no systematic mapping to sociolinguistic features or controlled cue ablation. * Needed evidence:* Controlled study manipulating specific cultural/linguistic signals while controlling confounds, plus probing model representations.

- **What aspects of training data or architecture cause distinct default ethnic biases (e.g., North American vs. Asian default), and can these be linked to developer region or data curation?**  
  *Basis:* Most models default to North America; DeepSeek-1.5B to Asian. *Why unresolved:* Correlation lacks mechanistic explanation due to opaque training corpora. *Needed evidence:* Audits of training data distributions (regional/linguistic/genre balances) or controlled pre-training experiments varying data composition.

- **Can bias mitigation beyond prompt engineering (e.g., decoding constraints, fine-tuning with balanced data, post-processing calibration) achieve equity without sacrificing accuracy?**  
  *Basis:* "Corrected informed" prompt improves fairness modestly but disparities persist (e.g., Ministral-8B). *Why unresolved:* Only prompt variations tested. *Needed evidence:* Systematic evaluation of other mitigation methods (adversarial debiasing, balanced fine-tuning) on the proposed evaluation framework.

- **How do ghostwriters or co-writers affect validity of artist-profiling tasks, and do models implicitly track "performer" vs. "lyricist"?**  
  *Basis:* Section 3.2 acknowledges assumption that lyrics reflect singer's identity is contested; dataset does not control for authorship. *Why unresolved:* No analysis of co-authorship impact. *Needed evidence:* Compare LLM profiling accuracy/bias on singer-songwriter tracks (verified single author) vs. collaborative tracks.

## Limitations
- **Translation artifacts:** Removing grammatical gender via translation may distort culturally specific expressions; impact unknown without non-translated baselines.
- **Prompt sensitivity:** Results depend on exact prompt wording and output parsing; minor variations could alter fairness metrics.
- **Generalizability:** Findings tied to English song lyrics and six coarse ethnicity regions; applicability to other genres/languages unclear.

## Confidence
- **Thematic shortcut reliance:** Medium. Supported by rationale word clouds and prompt correction effects, but causal inference is indirect.
- **Default North American bias:** High. Robust statistical evidence (prediction distributions, ROC skew) across multiple models.
- **Prompt engineering improves fairness:** Medium. Modest gains observed for some models, but effects are inconsistent and model-specific.

## Next Checks
1. **Replication with fixed inference settings:** Run the exact pipeline using the paper's reported parameters (temperature, max_tokens) and compare prediction distributions to detect sensitivity.
2. **Non-translated multilingual test:** Evaluate top models on a held-out set of original non-English lyrics to see if thematic shortcuts and default biases persist without translation.
3. **Prompt ablation study:** Systematically vary prompt components (e.g., remove "cultural references," add few-shot examples) across additional models (e.g., GPT, Claude) to test portability of the "Corrected prompt" effect.
