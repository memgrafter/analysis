# AECV-Bench: Benchmarking Multimodal Models on Architectural and Engineering Drawings Understanding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.04819
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04819
- **Reference Count**: 6
- **Primary Result**: Multimodal models excel at OCR and text-centric QA on AEC drawings (>0.95 accuracy) but fail at robust symbol counting (doors/windows: 0.34–0.39 exact-match accuracy, 15–20% MAPE), revealing limited drawing literacy.

## Executive Summary
AECV-Bench introduces a benchmark to evaluate multimodal models on real-world architectural and engineering drawings, addressing a gap in measuring the ability to interpret graphical symbols versus text. It assesses two tasks: counting objects (doors, windows, etc.) across 120 floor plans and answering 192 drawing-grounded questions that test OCR, spatial, counting, and comparison skills. Results reveal a consistent capability gradient: models perform strongly on text-based tasks but struggle significantly with symbol-centric understanding, particularly precise counting. The paper concludes that current systems function effectively as document assistants but lack the robust "drawing literacy" required for safety-critical AEC automation.

## Method Summary
The benchmark construction involved curating a dataset of 120 real architectural floor plans and engineering drawings, designing two evaluation tasks: (1) counting objects (doors, windows, etc.) across all floor plans, and (2) answering 192 drawing-grounded questions that test OCR, spatial reasoning, counting, and comparison skills. State-of-the-art multimodal models (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Pro) were tested on these tasks. Metrics include exact-match accuracy for counting and accuracy for QA, with additional error analysis via Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) for counting tasks. No explicit reproduction notes are provided in the paper.

## Key Results
- Models achieve near-perfect accuracy (>0.95) on OCR-based and text-centric QA tasks, demonstrating strong document understanding capabilities.
- Spatial reasoning performance is moderate, showing intermediate capability in interpreting layout and relative positions.
- Symbol-centric object counting, especially for doors and windows, remains largely unsolved; top models attain only 0.34–0.39 exact-match accuracy with high MAPE (15–20%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The observed capability gradient likely stems from two factors: (1) Pretraining data bias: Multimodal models are pretrained on natural images and text-heavy documents, making them adept at OCR and text-centric QA but unfamiliar with domain-specific AEC symbols. (2) Architectural limitations: Current models struggle with precise symbol localization and aggregation required for counting, as their multimodal fusion may not adequately bridge low-level visual features (symbol edges, shapes) with high-level counting logic. The text decoder's autoregressive nature is suitable for free-form QA but brittle for exact numeric outputs.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept 1: Multimodal Document Understanding**  
- *Why needed*: Models must integrate visual patterns (symbols, lines) with textual elements in AEC drawings.  
- *Quick check*: Evaluate model ability to answer questions that require correlating a label in a drawing with its corresponding graphical symbol.

**Concept 2: Symbol Recognition & Taxonomy**  
- *Why needed*: AEC drawings use standardized but visually diverse symbols (e.g., different door types) that differ from natural images.  
- *Quick check*: Test model accuracy on identifying specific symbol categories (e.g., sliding door vs. hinged door) in isolated instances.

**Concept 3: Spatial Layout Reasoning**  
- *Why needed*: Counting and QA require understanding room boundaries, adjacency, and symbol placement within a scaled plan.  
- *Quick check*: Pose questions about "rooms adjacent to the kitchen" or "toilets on the top floor" to measure spatial inference.

**Concept 4: Precise Numeracy from Graphics**  
- *Why needed*: Counting demands aggregation of multiple, possibly occluded or stylized, instances of a symbol.  
- *Quick check*: Retask models to count all occurrences of a specific symbol in a plan and compute absolute count error.

**Concept 5: Drawing-Scale & Projection Literacy**  
- *Why needed*: Engineering drawings include scale bars, dimensions, and projection views that affect interpretation.  
- *Quick check*: Include QA pairs that require converting measured distances using the provided scale.

**Concept 6: Symbol-Text Grounding**  
- *Why needed*: Legends and callouts link textual identifiers to graphical symbols; mis-grounding leads to counting errors.  
- *Quick check*: Provide a legend and ask for the count of a symbol referenced only by its legend key (e.g., "Type-A door").

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Input Drawing -> Visual Encoder (e.g., Vision Transformer) -> Multimodal Fusion -> Text Decoder/LLM -> Output Generation

**Critical Path**  
The core challenge lies in the `Multimodal Fusion -> Text Decoder` segment, where the model must transform a dense, structured visual representation of a technical drawing into a discrete, logical output (a count or an answer). Failures in symbol localization, feature aggregation, or instruction following along this path cause the observed counting errors.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *Resolution vs. Context*: High-resolution inputs capture fine symbol details but increase computational cost and may dilute global layout understanding.  
- *Specialist vs. Generalist*: A model pretrained on natural images may lack prior knowledge of AEC symbol semantics, while a domain-specialized model may sacrifice broader language capability.  
- *Autoregressive vs. Structured Output*: Free-form generation (for QA) works for text-heavy tasks but is brittle for precise numeric output (counting).

**Failure Signatures**  
- **Symbol Hallucination**: Reporting counts for symbols not present or missing obvious ones.  
- **Aggregation Error**: Correctly identifying individual instances but failing to sum them (e.g., counting one per room but not totaling across floors).  
- **Legend Misapplication**: Confusing similar symbols due to misreading the legend or scale.
- **Spatial Boundary Ignorance**: Counting symbols outside the relevant area (e.g., furniture vs. fixed elements).

**First 3 Experiments**  
1. **Isolate Symbol Recognition**: Present cropped, high-quality instances of each target symbol (door, window, etc.) and query "What is this?" to measure pure visual classification accuracy divorced from layout.
2. **Controlled Counting Complexity**: Generate synthetic floor plans with varying symbol densities, occlusion levels, and symbol styles to test scalability of counting performance.
3. **Legend Ablation**: Run the full benchmark with legends removed from context to quantify reliance on textual symbol mappings versus pure visual pattern matching.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can we quantify confidence and calibrate outputs in multimodal AEC models?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "Research is needed on: Quantitative methods for estimating confidence and calibrating outputs in multimodal settings."
- **Why unresolved**: Current models provide no uncertainty estimates, limiting safe deployment in safety-critical AEC workflows.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Evaluate calibration metrics (e.g., expected calibration error) on AECV-Bench and develop confidence scores that predict correctness.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can probabilistic model outputs be integrated with rule-based systems to ensure safety in AEC automation?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "Principled ways to combine probabilistic model predictions with conservative rule-based systems" are needed.
- **Why unresolved**: Models make errors in symbol counting; rule-based checks could catch them but integration is unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Build hybrid systems where model predictions trigger rule validation; assess error reduction on AECV-Bench counting tasks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What user interfaces and processes best keep engineers "in-the-loop" for AI-assisted drawing interpretation?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] "User interfaces and processes that enable engineers to remain in the loop" require research.
- **Why unresolved**: Existing interfaces lack human-centered design for AEC workflows, risking inefficiency or over-reliance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Prototype interfaces with uncertainty visualization and correction tools; conduct user studies with AEC professionals.

## Limitations
- **Dataset Scope**: Evaluation relies on 120 floor plans and 192 QA pairs; generalizability to broader AEC drawing types (structural, MEP) and drawing styles is unknown.
- **Benchmark Adoption**: The paper shows low average citations (0.0) and moderate FMR (0.516), suggesting AECV-Bench may not yet be widely validated or integrated into multimodal research.
- **Error Attribution**: High MAPE (15–20%) on counting indicates poor performance but does not clarify whether failures stem from symbol detection, counting logic, or both—no error decomposition is provided.
- **Conceptual Undefinition**: The core concept of "drawing literacy" is invoked but not formally defined or systematically tested beyond the benchmark's specific tasks, limiting interpretability of the conclusion.

## Confidence
- **Capability gradient claim (OCR strong → spatial moderate → symbol counting weak)**: Medium – Supported by internal results but lacks cross-benchmark confirmation.
- **Specific low scores for door/window counting (0.34–0.39 exact-match)**: High – Directly reported metrics with clear model comparisons.
- **Conclusion that systems lack “robust drawing literacy”**: Medium – Reasonable inference from tasks, but “drawing literacy” is not formally defined or tested beyond the benchmark’s scope.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-benchmark replication**: Run top models (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Pro) on related AEC benchmarks (e.g., DesignQA, ViRED) to verify if symbol-counting weakness persists across different evaluation setups.
2. **Error-type analysis**: Manually annotate model failures on AECV-Bench to quantify proportions due to (a) symbol misrecognition, (b) spatial mislocalization, (c) counting aggregation errors.
3. **Dataset expansion test**: Augment AECV-Bench with diverse drawing styles (hand-drawn sketches, international standards) and additional domains (structural details, schematics) to assess robustness beyond the current corpus.
