# JEPA-DNA: Grounding Genomic Foundation Models through Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17162
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17162
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** JEPA-DNA improves genomic representation learning by predicting latent embeddings of masked segments, yielding AUROC gains of +3–6% in linear probing and +6–7% in zero-shot variant effect prediction over generative-only baselines.

## Executive Summary
Genomic foundation models (GFMs) pretrained via masked language modeling (MLM) or next token prediction (NTP) suffer from a "granularity trap": they excel at recognizing local nucleotide motifs but fail to capture the broad functional context necessary for high-level biological reasoning. JEPA-DNA addresses this limitation by introducing a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture. Instead of reconstructing individual nucleotides, the method trains a [CLS] token to predict the latent representation of a masked genomic segment, as computed by a target encoder. This predictive objective, combined with the original generative loss and VICReg-style variance/covariance regularization, forces the model to learn global functional semantics while retaining nucleotide-level precision. Using DNABERT-2 as a backbone, JEPA-DNA achieves consistent improvements: linear probing AUROC gains of +3.19% (TF binding), +0.98% (promoter), +4.82% (splice site), and +5.98% (coding pathogenicity); zero-shot variant effect prediction gains of +6.94% for expression effects and +7.30% for Mendelian traits. These results demonstrate more biologically grounded and functionally informative representations.

## Method Summary
JEPA-DNA is a self-supervised pretraining framework that augments standard generative objectives (MLM/NTP) with a joint-embedding predictive loss to overcome the granularity trap. The architecture includes a backbone encoder (`E_θ`), a target encoder (`Ē_θ`) maintained by exponential moving average, and a predictor network (`P_ϕ`). During training, contiguous spans (covering 20–40% of the sequence) are masked to create a context view. The context encoder processes this masked input and produces token embeddings plus a [CLS] token. Simultaneously, the target encoder processes the unmasked full sequence to produce a stable latent target `z_target` from its [CLS] token. The predictor consumes the context encoder's full sequence (with the masked spans re-masked) and generates a predicted representation `h_pred` for the context [CLS] token. The total loss is a weighted sum: `L_total = λ₁L_llm + λ₂L_jepa + λ₃L_var + λ₄L_cov`. Here, `L_llm` is the standard token reconstruction loss, `L_jepa` is a cosine similarity loss between `h_pred` and `z_target`, and `L_var`/`L_cov` are VICReg regularizers that prevent representational collapse. By forcing the [CLS] token to forecast the target's latent embedding, the model learns to encode global functional information into that token. The design is model-agnostic: for encoder architectures a prepended [CLS] is used; for decoders or SSMs an appended [EOS] token is supervised in the same way.

## Key Results
- Linear probing on diverse downstream tasks yields AUROC improvements: +3.19% (TF binding), +0.98% (promoter), +4.82% (splice site), and +5.98% (coding pathogenicity) compared to generative-only baselines.
- Zero-shot variant effect prediction shows gains of +6.94% for expression effects and +7.30% for Mendelian traits.
- The consistent improvements indicate that JEPA-DNA learns representations that are more biologically grounded and better at capturing functional semantics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Overcoming the Granularity Trap
- **Claim:** JEPA-DNA mitigates the "granularity trap" by forcing the model to learn high-level functional semantics via a latent prediction objective, rather than solely reconstructing local nucleotide tokens.
- **Mechanism:** A [CLS] token (or [EOS] depending on backbone) is supervised to predict the latent representation (embedding) of a masked sequence segment, as generated by a separately maintained target encoder. This shifts the learning signal from exact token reconstruction to predicting an abstract, functional summary of the missing region.
- **Evidence:** The abstract states it "forces the model to predict the high-level functional embeddings of masked genomic segments rather than focusing solely on individual nucleotides." Section 3.4 explains that the JEPA objective grounds the model by forcing the [CLS] token to capture functional semantics from partial observations. Section 2.2 contrasts with GeneJEPA, highlighting the focus on primary genomic sequence.

### Mechanism 2: Composite Loss Prevents Collapse and Preserves Precision
- **Claim:** The composite loss function (`L_total = λ₁L_llm + λ₂L_jepa + λ₃L_var + λ₄L_cov`) prevents representational collapse while preserving nucleotide-level precision, creating a "latent grounding" effect.
- **Mechanism:** The LLM loss (`L_llm`) retains sensitivity to local sequence syntax. The JEPA loss (`L_jepa`) incentivizes the [CLS] token to develop embeddings predictive of global function. VICReg-style variance (`L_var`) and covariance (`L_cov`) regularization explicitly prevents the predictor and context embeddings from collapsing to constant vectors or redundant dimensions, ensuring a rich latent space.
- **Evidence:** Section 3.4.1 notes the use of variance and covariance constraints to prevent collapse, following VICReg. Section 4.1 details the implementation with weights 25.0 for variance loss and 0.5 for covariance loss. However, direct ablation evidence for the necessity of these regularization terms is lacking.

### Mechanism 3: Model-Agnostic Functional Grounding
- **Claim:** The architecture's design, using a special token ([CLS] or [EOS]) as the latent aggregation point, allows JEPA-DNA to impose functional grounding on diverse genomic backbone architectures (encoders, decoders, SSMs).
- **Mechanism:** By supervising a single, globally pooled token, the JEPA loss provides a universal training signal for global sequence understanding, regardless of the backbone's native aggregation mechanism. For transformer encoders (MLM), a prepended [CLS] is used; for decoders (NTP) or SSMs, an appended [EOS] token is supervised similarly.
- **Evidence:** The abstract states JEPA-DNA extends both NTP and MLM paradigms and can be deployed across architectures. Section 3.5 describes the extension to transformer decoders and state space models, noting the use of [EOS] for global pooling. Section 4.1 confirms compatibility, though experiments only use DNABERT-2 (encoder) as backbone.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
- **Backbone (E_θ):** Pre-trained DNABERT-2 (Transformer Encoder). Processes masked input, outputs token embeddings and a [CLS] token.
- **Target Encoder (Ē_θ):** Exponential moving average (EMA) copy of the backbone. Processes the *unmasked* full sequence and provides a stable latent target `z_target` from its [CLS] token.
- **Predictor (P_ϕ):** A lightweight Transformer that takes the context encoder’s full sequence (with the masked spans re-masked) and predicts a representation for the context [CLS] token. Its output is aligned to `z_target`.
- **Loss Aggregator:** Computes the weighted sum `L_total = λ₁L_llm + λ₂L_jepa + λ₃L_var + λ₄L_cov` using the three loss components and the VICReg regularizers.

### Critical Path
1. **Input & Dual-Masking:** Sample contiguous spans covering 20–40% of the sequence. Create a masked version `x_masked` for the context encoder; keep an unmasked copy `x_target` for the target encoder.
2. **Forward (Context):** Pass `x_masked` through backbone `E_θ` to obtain token states and the [CLS] embedding `h_cls`. Re-mask the token states at the span positions (i.e., zero out or replace with mask tokens).
3. **Forward (Target):** Pass `x_target` through the EMA target encoder `Ē_θ` (without gradients) to obtain `z_target` from its [CLS] token.
4. **Prediction:** Feed the context’s full sequence (with re-masked spans) into the predictor `P_ϕ` to produce a predicted representation `h_pred` for the context [CLS] token.
5. **Loss Computation:** Compute:
   - `L_llm` on the masked token predictions (cross-entropy).
   - `L_jepa = 1 - cosine_similarity(h_pred, z_target)`.
   - `L_var` (variance) and `L_cov` (covariance) on the batch of predicted and target embeddings to prevent collapse.
6. **Backward & Update:** Update backbone parameters `θ` and predictor parameters `ϕ` via backpropagation. Update target encoder parameters `θ̄` by EMA: `θ̄ ← m·θ̄ + (1−m)·θ` with momentum `m`.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Span masking vs. random token masking:** Longer contiguous spans force the model to reason globally but can make `L_llm` artificially hard and destabilize training. The paper uses 1–3 spans totaling >20% of the sequence.
- **EMA momentum:** Higher momentum (e.g., 0.996–1.0) yields more stable targets but slows adaptation; too low a momentum makes the target encoder change quickly, reducing the predictive challenge.
- **Loss weighting (`λ`s):** The weight `λ₂` for `L_jepa` must balance with `λ₁` for `L_llm`. Too high `λ₂` risks neglecting local syntax; too low fails to induce latent grounding. Regularization weights (`λ₃=25` for variance, `λ₄=0.5` for covariance) are critical to prevent collapse but are sensitive to tuning.

### Failure Signatures
- **Predictor collapse:** `L_jepa` plateaus at a high value, and `h_pred` vectors show near-zero variance (`L_var` → 0) or perfect correlation (`L_cov` → 0). Remedy: increase `λ₃`/`λ₄`, check predictor capacity.
- **Degraded `L_llm`:** MLM loss rises significantly without a corresponding decrease in `L_jepa`, indicating the model is failing to preserve local syntax under the new objective. Remedy: reduce `λ₂`, increase `λ₁`, or shorten masking spans.
- **No downstream gain:** Linear probing or zero-shot AUROC improvements are negligible or negative. Diagnose: visualize [CLS] embeddings (e.g., t-SNE); check gradient flow from `L_jepa` into backbone.

### First 3 Experiments (Validation Checklist)
1. **Baseline Replication with Span-Masking Only:** Pre-train DNABERT-2 using the same span-masking strategy but *without* JEPA components (only `L_llm`). Compare linear probing AUROC to both the original DNABERT-2 and JEPA-DNA. This isolates whether gains result from the span strategy itself or from the JEPA latent objective.
2. **JEPA-Only Ablation (`L_jepa + VICReg` sans `L_llm`):** Train with only the JEPA loss plus variance/covariance regularization. Expect a drop in tasks requiring nucleotide precision (e.g., splice site detection) but possible maintenance or gain in zero-shot functional tasks. This tests the necessity of `L_llm` for preserving local syntactic knowledge.
3. **Linear Probing on Target Encoder Embeddings:** After training, freeze the EMA target network and extract `z_target` embeddings for downstream tasks. Perform linear probing on these embeddings. If the gains observed with JEPA-DNA disappear, it suggests the improvements stem from the context encoder’s representations rather than from the predicted target latent space, challenging the “grounding via target prediction” premise.

## Limitations
- The necessity of the VICReg-style variance and covariance regularization is not directly validated; no ablation shows performance without these terms.
- The assumption that the target encoder's [CLS] embeddings encode "functional biological state" lacks explicit validation (e.g., clustering visualizations by functional class).
- Claims of architectural generalizability to decoders and state-space models are not empirically tested; the experiments only use an encoder backbone (DNABERT-2).
