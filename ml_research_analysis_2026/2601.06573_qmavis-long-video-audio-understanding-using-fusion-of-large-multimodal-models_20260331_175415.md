# QMAVIS: Long Video-Audio Understanding using Fusion of Large Multimodal Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.06573
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06573
- **Reference count**: 15
- **Primary result**: QMAVIS achieves 66.46% accuracy on VideoMME (52% long videos), a 38.75% improvement over VideoLLaMA2 (47.9%). It also gains +1.51% on PerceptionTest and +1.72% on EgoSchema.

## Executive Summary
QMAVIS tackles the challenge of understanding videos that span minutes to hours, a regime where current large multimodal models (LMMs) struggle due to context window limitations. The proposed solution is a modular pipeline that processes the video and audio in chunks, each handled by specialized models—a video LMM for visual content and an ASR system for speech. The chunk-level outputs are then aggregated by a powerful text-only LLM, which synthesizes a coherent final response. This late-fusion approach avoids end-to-end training and can leverage state-of-the-art components. On several benchmarks featuring long videos, QMAVIS significantly outperforms prior methods, demonstrating the effectiveness of chunking and aggregation for long-form video-audio understanding.

## Method Summary
Given an input video and a question/prompt, QMAVIS first splits the video into short, contiguous chunks (e.g., 30 seconds). For each chunk, it extracts frames at a fixed rate (e.g., 1 FPS) and feeds them to a video LMM (such as Qwen2-VL-72B) to generate a caption or answer. Simultaneously, the audio track of the chunk is transcribed using Whisper Large v3. The video caption and audio transcript for a chunk are interleaved into a single text block; all blocks together form a set S. If S exceeds the context window of the aggregation LLM, a recursive summarization strategy is used to compress the information hierarchically. Finally, a text-only LLM (e.g., Llama 3) takes S and the original prompt to produce the final answer. This pipeline preserves fine-grained details within chunks while enabling cross-chunk reasoning via the aggregation LLM.

## Key Results
- QMAVIS achieves 66.46% accuracy on VideoMME (where 52% of videos are long), representing a 38.75% relative improvement over VideoLLaMA2 (47.9%).
- It also outperforms baselines by +1.51% on PerceptionTest and +1.72% on EgoSchema.
- Ablations reveal that removing the aggregation LLM (using naive concatenation) lowers accuracy by 3.72% on VideoMME, and removing audio transcription lowers it by 18.28% on VideoMME (while slightly improving PerceptionTest by 0.65%), underscoring the importance of both components for speech-dependent tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Chunking to preserve local detail.** Splitting long videos into short segments (e.g., 30 seconds) allows each to be processed by the video LMM within its optimal context window. This avoids the information loss that would occur from aggressive uniform frame sampling (e.g., 1 frame per second over hours) and prevents context overflow. Fine-grained spatiotemporal details are retained in each chunk’s caption, which is crucial for answering detailed questions.

**Mechanism 2: Aggregation LLM for cross-chunk reasoning.** The interleaved set of chunk-level captions and transcripts is fed to a capable aggregation LLM. This model reasons across the distributed descriptions, linking events, identifying themes, and resolving references to produce a coherent final response. Ablation studies show that without the aggregation LLM, performance drops significantly (3.72% on VideoMME), proving its necessity for synthesizing long video narratives.

**Mechanism 3: Audio transcription integrates spoken content.** Whisper converts each chunk’s audio into text, which the aggregation LLM can seamlessly combine with visual captions. On speech-heavy benchmarks like VideoMME, removing audio transcription causes an 18.28% accuracy drop, confirming the value of spoken information. However, on PerceptionTest, which includes non-speech audio, audio removal slightly improved performance, indicating that ASR may introduce noise when the task relies on sound events rather than speech.

## Foundational Learning
- **Late Fusion**: Combines outputs of specialized models without end-to-end training, providing modularity and ease of component upgrades. *Why needed*: QMAVIS integrates existing video LMMs, ASR, and LLMs without retraining. *Quick check*: What are the advantages and disadvantages of late fusion compared to early or intermediate fusion in multimodal systems?
- **Sequence Chunking**: Splits long inputs into shorter segments to fit model context windows. *Why needed*: Enables processing videos of arbitrary length by using models optimized for short clips. *Quick check*: How would you determine the optimal chunk size for a given video LMM and target video length?
- **Aggregation with LLMs**: Uses a large language model to synthesize information from multiple chunk-level descriptions. *Why needed*: Provides reasoning across temporally dispersed events, producing coherent final responses. *Quick check*: What techniques can be used when the aggregated text from chunks exceeds the LLM's context window? (Answer: recursive aggregation.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Video Chunker → (Video LMM, Audio Extractor + Whisper) → Interleaver → Aggregation LLM (with recursive summarization if needed) → Output Formatter

**Critical path**:  
1. Input video + prompt.  
2. Chunk video/audio into N segments (duration based on total length).  
3. For each chunk: run video LMM → X_V,i; run ASR → X_A,i.  
4. Interleave X_V,i and X_A,i per chunk to form text blocks; collect all blocks into set S.  
5. If S exceeds aggregation LLM context, recursively summarize subsets of blocks to reduce length.  
6. Feed S (or summaries) and original prompt to aggregation LLM to generate final answer.  
7. Return answer.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Chunk duration*: Shorter chunks (e.g., 10s) preserve more local detail but increase the number of chunks and aggregation load; longer chunks (e.g., 60s) reduce count but may exceed the video LMM’s context. The paper adapts chunk length based on video duration.  
- *Frame sampling rate*: Higher FPS captures more motion but increases tokens; lower FPS reduces cost but risks missing fast actions. The paper uses 1 FPS for short/medium videos and 0.5 FPS for long ones.  
- *Aggregation LLM size*: Larger models (e.g., 70B) produce better synthesis but require more compute; smaller models are faster but less accurate. Using the video LMM itself for aggregation (instead of a separate LLM) yields a 3.72% drop on VideoMME.  
- *Recursive aggregation*: Necessary for very long videos but repeated summarization may lose detail.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Inconsistent or contradictory information across chunks: suggests poor aggregation or video LMM errors.  
- Missing audio-related details: likely ASR failure or integration issue; check Whisper output quality.  
- Repetitive or overly verbose output: aggregation LLM not summarizing effectively.  
- Very slow processing: too many chunks or oversized models; consider parallelism or quantization.  
- Out-of-order event mentions: aggregation may mix up temporal sequence; verify chunk ordering.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. *End-to-end sanity test*: Run on a short video (1 minute) with a simple factual question; verify correct answer and ensure no degradation compared to a baseline video LMM.  
2. *Component ablation*: Disable the aggregation LLM (use naive concatenation) and disable audio transcription; measure performance drop on a small validation set to replicate ablation results.  
3. *Long video stress test*: Use a 30+ minute video with multiple scenes and dialogues; ask a narrative question; check that the output coherently mentions events from different chunks. Also vary chunk sizes (e.g., 30s vs 120s) to observe impact on quality and speed.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does extending audio analysis beyond speech transcription to include non-speech cues (tone, background sounds) affect performance on audio-reliant benchmarks? The current pipeline uses Whisper for speech-only transcription, leaving the impact of richer audio semantic features unexplored.
- Under what dataset characteristics (audio type, video length) does including audio transcription via Whisper degrade overall performance, as hinted by the PerceptionTest ablation? No analysis identifies which specific questions or audio types cause the observed 0.65% drop when audio is removed.
- How does the choice of aggregation LLM (model size, architecture) affect QMAVIS’s ability to synthesize chunk-level information for long-video narratives? The paper uses a single LLM without comparison, leaving optimal characteristics unknown.
- What is the optimal chunking strategy (duration, frame sampling rate) for balancing computational cost and temporal understanding? The paper uses fixed thresholds but does not systematically study their interaction.

## Limitations
- Key implementation details (aggregation LLM identity and prompt, chunking thresholds, per-dataset prompts) are unreported, hindering exact replication.
- The reliance on ASR for audio discards non-speech sounds, which may harm tasks involving sound events; the PerceptionTest ablation suggests ASR can introduce noise for such tasks.
- Recursive aggregation details (hierarchy depth, summary prompts) are not provided, leaving the scalability to very long videos unvalidated.

## Confidence
- **Chunking preserves local detail**: High (directly justified by context constraints and strong empirical gains).
- **Aggregation LLM enables cross-chunk reasoning**: High (ablation shows significant drop without it).
- **ASR improves speech-based QA**: Medium (strong evidence on VideoMME but contradictory on PerceptionTest, indicating speech-dependence).

## Next Checks
1. Test QMAVIS with alternative aggregation LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Claude) and prompt variations to assess robustness to the unspecified aggregation design.
2. Conduct a chunking threshold sweep: vary chunk duration (10–300s) and frame rate (0.5–5 fps) on long videos to find Pareto-optimal accuracy/compute trade-offs and validate the paper’s adaptive choices.
3. Augment audio analysis with non-speech event tags (e.g., from PANNs) and evaluate on subsets of VideoMME/PerceptionTest that require sound event understanding, to see if this mitigates the performance drop observed when using ASR alone.
