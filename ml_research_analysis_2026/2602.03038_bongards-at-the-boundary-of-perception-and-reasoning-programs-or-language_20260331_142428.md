# Bongards at the Boundary of Perception and Reasoning: Programs or Language?

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.03038
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03038
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** A neuro-symbolic system combining LLM-generated rules, VLM-synthesized parameterized programs, Bayesian optimization, and CoT reasoning solves 51/100 Bongard problems, exceeding average human performance (47) and the prior best (43).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a neuro-symbolic system for solving Bongard problems (BPs), visual reasoning puzzles requiring the discovery of abstract separating rules. The core approach uses a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to generate candidate natural-language rules and to synthesize parameterized Python programs that implement each rule. Bayesian optimization efficiently tunes program parameters to perfectly classify provided training images. A chain-of-thought (CoT) verifier serves as a fallback when no program achieves a high training score. The combined system achieves state-of-the-art performance on a standard BP dataset, with analysis revealing that program-based and language-based reasoning are complementary: programs excel on spatial and similarity-based rules, while CoT handles conceptual ones.

## Method Summary
The solution pipeline operates in two stages. First, a **Hypothesis Generator** (VLM) proposes candidate rules for a given BP, conditioned on the rules from the three preceding BPs (exploiting a curriculum) and random BPs for diversity. Second, each candidate rule is scored by a **Verifier** with two paths. Path A (**Program**) induces a parameterized Python `classify_image` function via a VLM, then uses Bayesian optimization (15 steps) to fit its parameters on 5 training image pairs. If any program achieves a training score ≥0.9, its ensemble classifies a held-out test image. Path B (**CoT**) is a fallback where a VLM classifies the test image directly using chain-of-thought reasoning with the training examples as in-context shots. In the **solution task**, the rule with the highest verifier score is output. The **verification task** (provided the true rule) isolates the verifier's capability.

## Key Results
*   On the verification task (given the correct rule), Claude 3.7 Sonnet using both programs and CoT attains 86.5% accuracy, surpassing a GPT-4o variant (79%).
*   For solving from scratch, the Claude-based full system solves 51 of 100 problems, exceeding the average human solver (47) and the previous best automated system (43).
*   Analysis shows program verification is stronger on "Spatial" and "Same Size" problem categories, while CoT is stronger on "Concept" and "Number" categories; their combination yields consistent gains across most types, with limited evidence of memorization from an inversion test.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Parameterized Program Synthesis with Bayesian Optimization
*   **Claim:** This process converts vague visual concepts (e.g., "elongated") into precise, executable functions by tuning hyperparameters.
*   **Mechanism:** The VLM generates a Python program skeleton with placeholder parameters (e.g., a `circularity_threshold`). Bayesian optimization (15 iterations) efficiently searches the bounded parameter space to maximize classification accuracy on 5 training pairs. The core assumption is that the VLM can generate the correct *program structure* while black-box optimization finds the optimal *parameter values*.
*   **Evidence:** [section 3] describes using VLM-specified ranges and 15 BO steps. [section 4] Figure 4 exemplifies optimizing a circularity threshold for BP #11 ("elongated vs. compact").
*   **Break condition:** If no program achieves a training score ≥0.9 after one repair attempt, the system falls back to CoT.

### Mechanism 2: Complementarity of Programmatic and Language Reasoning
*   **Claim:** Programs and CoT excel at different abstraction types; their combination covers more BPs than either alone.
*   **Mechanism:** The verifier first attempts program synthesis. If successful (set A non-empty), the majority vote of optimized programs classifies the test image. If no qualifying program exists (A = ∅), the CoT verifier makes the prediction. The final system can also use both paths' votes.
*   **Core assumption:** Spatial/geometric and similarity-based rules align with formalizable calculations (programs), while high-level conceptual/compositional rules are more naturally reasoned about in language (CoT).
*   **Evidence:** [abstract] and [section 4] Table 2 show category-wise improvements: programs boost "Same Size" and "Spatial" over the Claude baseline, while the combination improves nearly all categories. [section 4] notes the complementary solving patterns.
*   **Break condition:** The synergy fails if the VLM is poor at program synthesis (e.g., GPT-4o variant) or for problems where both modalities struggle (e.g., BP #44).

### Mechanism 3: Curriculum Exploitation in Hypothesis Generation
*   **Claim:** Conditioning the hypothesis generator on the rules from the three preceding BPs exploits the original BP curriculum to increase the likelihood of generating the correct rule.
*   **Mechanism:** The VLM samples candidate rules given in-context examples from the three *preceding* BPs (as per the problem sequence) plus random BPs. The assumed design of BPs as a pedagogical sequence means earlier problems introduce concepts reused later.
*   **Core assumption:** The fixed BP ordering (#1–100) constitutes a learning trajectory; providing predecessor rules activates this latent structure in the VLM.
*   **Evidence:** [section 3] states this sampling strategy. The full system's 51/100 score exceeds human averages (47) and prior best (43), partly attributed to this generator.
*   **Break condition:** If a BP's correct rule is conceptually unrelated to its predecessors, this conditioning could introduce harmful bias.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Bongard Problem Structure:** Each BP has 12 hand-drawn images (6 positive, 6 negative). The task is to induce a natural-language rule that perfectly separates them. Distinguishes verification (given rule) from solution (induce rule).
    *   **Quick check:** What is key difference between the verification and solution tasks in this paper's evaluation? *(Answer: Verification uses the provided ground-truth rule; solution requires generating the correct rule from scratch.)*
*   **Program Synthesis with Parameter Optimization:** Core loop: VLM generates parameterized Python `classify_image` program → Bayesian optimizer fits parameters on 5 training pairs → accept if max score ≥0.9. Converts qualitative concepts to tunable executable functions.
    *   **Quick check:** In BP #11 ("elongated vs. compact"), what parameter is optimized and using what technique? *(Answer: A `threshold` for circularity; Bayesian optimization.)*
*   **Neuro-Symbolic Hybrid Architecture:** Combines neural components (VLM/LLM for rule proposal and CoT) with symbolic ones (executable programs, exact verification). Necessary because neural models propose high-level concepts, while programs provide precise, verifiable application.
    *   **Quick check:** Why might a pure CoT VLM fail on some BPs solvable by programs? *(Answer: Programs excel at exact spatial calculations and geometric thresholds that are error-prone for language-based reasoning.)*

## Architecture Onboarding
Hypothesis Generator -> Verifier (Path A: Programs OR Path B: CoT) -> Rule Selector
*   **Component map:** BP Images -> Hypothesis Generator (VLM: images + in-context rules) -> Candidate Rules -> For each rule: Verifier (Path A: VLM generates programs -> BO fits parameters -> if score≥0.9, majority vote; else Path B: CoT VLM) -> Rule Scores -> Rule Selector (highest score) -> Solution Rule. For verification, the true rule bypasses the generator and enters the verifier directly.
*   **Critical path (Solution Task):** `BP Images → Hypothesis Generator → Candidate Rules → Verifier (scoring each) → Select top rule → Output.`
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Program expressivity vs. optimization difficulty: Complex programs with many parameters are harder to optimize within 15 BO steps.
    *   Verification sample count: 10 programs/rule for verification vs. 5 for solution; fewer samples increase failure risk.
    *   Curriculum reliance: Generator performance depends on the validity of the assumed BP curriculum ordering.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Program path fails:** No program reaches ≥0.9 training score → CoT fallback. Diagnose by inspecting generated code stubs and parameter ranges.
    *   **CoT fails on spatial tasks:** Evident in lower accuracy on "Spatial" categories in Table 2; if a BP is spatial and program path also fails, error is likely.
    *   **Hypothesis generator misses:** Correct rule not in candidate set (evident from 51/100 solved count). Common for compositional concepts (e.g., BP #75).
    *   **Memorization suspicion:** Inversion task shows similar accuracy, suggesting reasoning, but correlation with problem popularity remains unknown.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Verification replication:** Run Claude 3.7 + both paths on the 6 train/test splits for all BPs. Expect ~86.5% average accuracy. Deviation >2% indicates prompt/BO issues.
    2.  **BO ablation:** For a spatial BP (e.g., BP #11), run program synthesis *without* Bayesian optimization (use default parameters). Expect near-zero training accuracy, proving optimization's necessity.
    3.  **Category stress test:** Select one BP from each category (Concept: #100, Number: #90, Spatial: #19, Same Size: #59). Run full solution task and record which verifier path (Program/CoT/both) succeeded. Pattern should match Table 2/Figure 5.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **How essential is the original Bongard problem ordering (curriculum) to the hypothesis generator's performance?** The paper conditions on preceding BPs but does not test shuffled orderings.
*   **What problem-specific features determine whether a Bongard problem is better solved by program verification, chain-of-thought reasoning, or their combination?** Categories are broad; precise predictive features are unidentified.
*   **To what degree does the system's high performance rely on memorization of Bongard problem solutions from pretraining data rather than on-the-fly reasoning?** The inversion test is indirect; memorization of specific problem-rule pairs is possible.

## Limitations
*   **Implementation gaps:** Exact Bayesian optimization configuration (kernel, acquisition function) and RAG construction (embedding model, source of 59 programs) are unspecified, risking reproduction drift.
*   **Ablation incompleteness:** No direct comparison of hypothesis generation with vs. without curriculum conditioning (preceding vs. random in-context rules only).
*   **Generalization scope:** Results are restricted to one BP dataset; performance on other visual reasoning benchmarks (e.g., Bongard-OpenWorld) is unknown.

## Confidence
*   **Combined system solves 51/100 BPs:** **High** (direct report, aligned with human benchmark).
*   **Programs vs. CoT complementarity:** **Medium** (category-wise Table 2 supports, but Figure 5 shows problem-specific failures).
*   **Curriculum exploitation hypothesis:** **Low** (inferred from design; no experimental validation of pedagogical sequence assumption).

## Next Checks
1.  **Verification replication:** Re-run Claude 3.7 + both paths on all 6 splits; expect ≥86% accuracy. Significant deviation indicates implementation error.
2.  **BO parameter sensitivity:** For BP #11, vary BO iterations (5→30) and parameter ranges (±20%). Track training score ≥0.9 rate. Collapse below 80% suggests optimization fragility.
3.  **Curriculum ablation:** In solution task, compare rules conditioned on preceding BPs vs. only random BPs. A drop ≥5 solved problems confirms curriculum utility.
