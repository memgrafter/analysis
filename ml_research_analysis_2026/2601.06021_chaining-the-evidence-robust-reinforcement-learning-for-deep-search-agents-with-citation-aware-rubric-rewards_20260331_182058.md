# Chaining the Evidence: Robust Reinforcement Learning for Deep Search Agents with Citation-Aware Rubric Rewards

## Quick Facts

- **arXiv ID**: 2601.06021  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06021  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: C-GRPO, which combines outcome rewards with citation-aware rubric rewards (CaRR), consistently outperforms GRPO and E-GRPO baselines across four deep-search benchmarks, with average improvements of 5.1% (64k) and 8.0% (128k) for 4B models, and 2.6% (64k) and 6.0% (128k) for 30B models.

## Executive Summary

This paper tackles the limitations of outcome-only reinforcement learning (RL) for deep search agents, which often learn shortcuts or hallucinate to maximize sparse correctness rewards. The authors propose CaRR (Citation-aware Rubric Rewards), a framework that decomposes multi-hop questions into verifiable single-hop rubrics requiring explicit entity identification, citation support, and evidence chain connectivity. CaRR is integrated with group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) to form C-GRPO, which combines outcome rewards with normalized rubric rewards. Evaluated on four benchmarks with 4B and 30B models, C-GRPO shows consistent and substantial gains over baselines, particularly with longer contexts, and generalizes to open-ended deep-research tasks, achieving state-of-the-art among open-source agents. The work demonstrates that fine-grained, citation-aware process supervision significantly improves the robustness and reliability of RL-trained search agents.

## Method Summary

The method centers on CaRR, a three-step rubric verification pipeline applied after each agent rollout. First, a judge LLM checks whether the final response explicitly names all hidden entities (e.g., <E1>, <E2>) precomputed from the question decomposition. Second, for each fully identified rubric, the system verifies that the factual statement is supported by the content of the cited webpages extracted from the trajectory. Third, a bipartite graph is built connecting identified entities and supported rubrics; only rubrics reachable from the final answer entity via BFS are counted. The raw rubric reward is the fraction of reachable rubrics out of the total. During RL training (C-GRPO), this rubric reward is normalized within each group of rollouts and combined with the outcome reward (only for correct rollouts) using a weighted sum with hyperparameter α (set to 0.3). The mixed reward then feeds into a GRPO-style group-relative advantage computation and policy update. The approach uses precomputed rubrics for training questions, a ReAct-style agent with web search tools (search, open, find), and a separate judge LLM (DeepSeek-v3.2) for automated verification.

## Key Results

- C-GRPO outperforms GRPO and E-GRPO on all four benchmarks (BrowseComp, BrowseComp-ZH, xbench-DS, GAIA) for both 4B and 30B models, with larger gains at 128k context (up to +8.0% for 4B).
- While GRPO’s performance degrades with longer contexts, C-GRPO improves, indicating better robustness to extended information.
- C-GRPO increases the number of cited webpages (4.3 vs 3.8 from SFT) and satisfied rubrics (5.2 vs 4.5 from SFT), showing more thorough evidence gathering.
- On the open-ended DeepResearch Bench, C-GRPO achieves state-of-the-art among open-source agents and surpasses some proprietary ones.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Hidden Entity Identification for Reasoning Completeness
Explicitly requiring the agent to name every hidden entity (<E1>, <E2>, …) in its final response forces it to address all intermediate constraints, discouraging shortcuts that ignore parts of the question. A judge LLM matches the set of hidden entities (precomputed from the rubric) against the entities mentioned; only rubrics with all entities identified are eligible for reward. If the answer is correct but an entity is omitted, those rubrics are unsatisfied and the reward is reduced.

### Mechanism 2: Citation-Based Rubric Judgment for Factual Grounding
Each identified rubric is verified against the actual content of the cited webpages (capped at 20 URLs) to ensure the factual statement is fully supported. This prevents hallucinations by grounding statements in retrieved documents. If a cited page lacks sufficient evidence, the rubric is unsatisfied regardless of answer correctness.

### Mechanism 3: Evidence Connectivity Check for Logical Chain Consistency
A bipartite graph is constructed with nodes being identified entities and supported rubrics; an edge exists if an entity appears in a rubric. A BFS from the final answer entity <E0> determines which rubrics are reachable. Only reachable rubrics count in the reward. This ensures a coherent evidence chain linking every supporting fact back to the answer, preventing the agent from satisfying isolated, irrelevant facts.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Reinforcement Learning with Sparse Outcome Rewards**  
Why needed: Outcome-only RL (e.g., GRPO) yields agents that achieve correct answers via shortcuts or hallucinations, failing to generalize to harder or longer-context tasks. Understanding this limitation motivates process-based rewards.  
Quick check: What are typical symptoms when an RL agent receives only a binary outcome reward for a long-horizon task? (Answer: It may learn to achieve correct answers with minimal or unfaithful reasoning, failing to generalize to harder contexts.)

**Concept: Rubric-Based Fine-Grained Evaluation**  
Why needed: CaRR transforms a complex question into multiple verifiable single-hop rubrics. Knowing how to construct and score rubrics is essential to appreciate the reward structure.  
Quick check: How would you break a multi-hop question into atomic rubrics? (Answer: Identify all hidden intermediate entities and create factual statements that each place these entities in context; each rubric corresponds to one hop.)

**Concept: Citation Grounding and Evidence Chains**  
Why needed: The agent must produce transparent, verifiable reasoning. Simply having many citations is insufficient; they must collectively support a logical path to the answer.  
Quick check: Why does CaRR include an evidence connectivity check in addition to citation support? (Answer: Without connectivity, an agent could satisfy rubrics by citing documents that happen to contain unrelated true facts; connectivity ensures the chain of evidence leads to the final answer.)

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Agent policy (LLM with ReAct loop) -> web search tools (search, open, find) -> trajectory with observations; precomputed rubric generator; judge LLM; reward calculator (outcome + normalized CaRR); GRPO optimizer.

**Critical path**  
1. Load precomputed hidden entities and rubrics for a question.  
2. Agent executes trajectory (think → action → observation) and produces final response with citations and answer.  
3. Compute outcome reward (exact match). If outcome=1, run CaRR: extract cited URLs; collect snippets from observations; judge entity identification, citation support, and connectivity to compute R_r.  
4. Normalize R_r within group of rollouts; mixed reward R = (1−α)*1 + α*(R_r_norm).  
5. For each group of G rollouts, compute group-relative advantages, apply GRPO loss (Eq 15), update policy.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- α (rubric weight): balances final accuracy vs process quality; tuned to 0.3 for 4B, higher values degrade performance.  
- Citation limit (20 URLs): prevents reward hacking via excessive irrelevant citations.  
- Precomputed rubrics: efficient but assumes synthetic multi-hop structure; may not adapt to open-ended queries.  
- Judge LLM dependency: automated verification is cost-effective but not infallible; human eval shows ~95% accuracy, leaving residual risk.

**Failure signatures**  
- Shortcut exploitation: decreasing tool calls while outcome reward plateaus; evident when GRPO performance drops with longer contexts.  
- Hallucination: fabricated facts; CaRR penalizes via citation mismatch, but judge errors could mask some.  
- Overlength rollouts: token/tool limits cause automatic zero reward; encourages conciseness but may truncate valid search.  
- Connectivity manipulation: agent might add spurious entities to create artificial connections; BFS limits but does not eliminate gaming.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Benchmark evaluation**: Compare C-GRPO with GRPO/E-GRPO on four deep-search benchmarks at 64k and 128k contexts, and on DeepResearch Bench.  
2. **Ablation studies**: Remove hidden entity identification, evidence connectivity check, and the “rubric only for correct rollouts” design to validate contributions.  
3. **Training dynamics & case studies**: Track tool calls, outcome and rubric rewards during RL; analyze cited webpage counts and rubric satisfaction; compare trajectories qualitatively.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**  
How can the CaRR framework be adapted to open-ended question answering tasks where the question does not explicitly state all constraints?  
Basis: Section 6 (Limitations) notes that rubric generation relies on the compositional structure of synthetic multi-hop questions and may not directly adapt to open-ended QA with implicit requirements.  
Why unresolved: Implicit or ambiguous constraints make rubric decomposition non‑trivial.  
What evidence would resolve it: A rubric generation approach for implicit requirements (e.g., iterative clarification or learning from diverse demonstrations) that improves performance on truly open-ended benchmarks.

**Open Question 2**  
How can fine‑grained rubric rewards be applied to incorrect rollouts to provide denser learning signals without destabilizing policy optimization?  
Basis: Ablation studies show that adding weighted rubric rewards for all rollouts (not only correct ones) led to poor performance because incorrect rollouts could receive positive advantages when few correct rollouts existed in the group.  
Why unresolved: C‑GRPO currently gives zero reward to incorrect rollouts, missing partial feedback. A safe integration method remains unknown.  
What evidence would resolve it: A modified reward scheme (e.g., learned baseline, conditioning on rollout quality, adaptive weighting) that successfully uses rubric scores for all rollouts while maintaining stable training.

**Open Question 3**  
How does the optimal weighting α between outcome and rubric rewards vary across model scale, task difficulty, and context length?  
Basis: α was tuned only for the 4B model (tested 0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5) and then fixed at 0.3 for the 30B model without further investigation.  
Why unresolved: A single α may be suboptimal; the trade‑off likely depends on model capacity, task difficulty, and context budget.  
What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments over α for multiple model sizes (4B, 30B, 70B), across benchmarks of varying difficulty, and with different context limits to reveal scaling laws or guidelines.

**Open Question 4**  
What explains the performance degradation of outcome‑only RL (GRPO) with increased context length, and how can we formally analyze this phenomenon?  
Basis: Figure 3 shows GRPO’s accuracy declines at 128k tokens while C‑GRPO improves; the authors attribute this to shortcut exploitation but provide no detailed analysis.  
Why unresolved: Understanding the root cause could uncover fundamental limitations of outcome‑only rewards in long‑horizon web search.  
What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of policy gradients, advantage distributions, or search trajectories across context lengths; possibly a theoretical model linking outcome sparsity to shortcut emergence, supported by diagnostic experiments (e.g., measuring intermediate entity coverage, citation diversity).

## Limitations

- Tool observation specifics (search result count, snippet length) are unspecified, affecting reproducibility.
- Judge LLM (DeepSeek-v3.2) is not publicly available; substitution may alter reward quality and training dynamics.
- Precomputed rubrics assume explicit multi-hop structure and may not cover open‑ended queries.
- GRPO grouping details are ambiguous, risking incorrect advantage computation.
- Connectivity check may be circumvented by spurious entity insertion.
