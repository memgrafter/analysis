# End-to-end data-driven prediction of urban airflow and pollutant dispersion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17606
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17606
- **Reference count:** 10
- **Primary result:** A modular reduced-order model combining SPOD, autoencoder, LSTM, and CNN achieves accurate and stable prediction of turbulent airflow and pollutant dispersion in an urban street canyon at a fraction of the computational cost of LES.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an end-to-end data-driven framework to predict turbulent airflow and pollutant dispersion in an urban street canyon, offering a computationally efficient alternative to large eddy simulation (LES). The method employs spectral proper orthogonal decomposition (SPOD) to extract a reduced spatial basis, an autoencoder to compress the modal coefficients nonlinearly, a long short-term memory (LSTM) network to forecast their temporal evolution, and a convolutional neural network (CNN) to map velocity fields to concentration fields. Applied to LES data of a skimming flow regime, the SPOD achieved a 97% dimensionality reduction while preserving key spectral features. The autoencoder further compressed the basis by a factor of 66. The LSTM produced stable long-term velocity forecasts, and the CNN successfully reconstructed mean concentration fields and vertical mass flux profiles. The end-to-end pipeline provides an accurate and stable surrogate model at a small fraction of the computational cost of high-fidelity CFD.

## Method Summary
The framework builds a reduced-order model (ROM) from a high-fidelity LES dataset of a street canyon (H/W=1, Re_H≈9600) with a continuous line pollutant source. The process consists of four main modules: (1) SPOD decomposes velocity snapshots into a set of frequency-resolved modes and their time coefficients, achieving significant dimensionality reduction (97%) via spectral energy and spatial similarity criteria. (2) An autoencoder compresses the temporal coefficients (real and imaginary parts separately) into a low-dimensional latent space (N_z=30), representing a nonlinear manifold of the dynamics. (3) An LSTM network learns the temporal evolution of the latent vectors, enabling multi-step prediction. (4) A fully convolutional CNN maps the predicted velocity fields to concentration fields, leveraging spatial correlations. The modules are trained sequentially on the LES data and then combined for end-to-end forecasting, requiring only an initial set of velocity fields to bootstrap the prediction.

## Key Results
- SPOD reduces dimensionality by 97% (from 7200 to 2003 modes) while preserving spectral features such as the Kelvin-Helmholtz peak at f*=1.0.
- The autoencoder compresses the basis by a factor of 66 (2003 → 30 latent dimensions) and reconstructs dominant coefficient trajectories with bounded error.
- The LSTM-ROM yields stable, bounded long-term velocity forecasts, with error growth saturating and attractor topology preserved.
- The CNN reconstructs mean concentration fields and vertical mass flux profiles from predicted velocity, though with some smoothing of fine-scale structures.
- Overall, the end-to-end pipeline provides a computationally efficient surrogate that matches LES statistics at a fraction of the cost.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Spectral Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (SPOD)
**Claim:** SPOD provides a spectrally coherent, reduced spatial basis that captures the most energetic multi-scale structures in urban canyon turbulence while enabling significant dimensionality reduction.  
**Mechanism:** SPOD performs a frequency-domain decomposition via Welch's method, solving an eigenvalue problem on the cross-spectral density tensor at each discrete frequency. This isolates spatiotemporal coherent structures (e.g., Kelvin-Helmholtz vortices) tied to specific frequency bands, unlike standard time-domain POD. Truncation using spectral energy (RIC) and spatial similarity (γ) criteria removes redundant and low-energy modes.  
**Core assumption:** Dominant dynamics (shear layer instabilities, cavity recirculation) are captured by a small subset of spectrally coherent structures whose evolution can be modeled in a reduced subspace.  
**Evidence:** SPOD dimensionality reduction achieved 97% while preserving key spectral features (abstract). The study uses thresholds ε_RIC=0.99 and ε_γ=0.2 (section 3.1.2). Reconstruction with ε_γ=0.2 preserves large-scale structures and TKE, while ε_γ=0.1 degrades features (section 4.1.3).  
**Break condition:** If pruned basis fails to reconstruct leading eigenvalue spectrum within tolerance (e.g., NMSE in Fig. 11 exceeds threshold for low frequencies), essential coherent structures are lost, destabilizing downstream forecasting.

### Mechanism 2: Autoencoder for Nonlinear Compression
**Claim:** A dense autoencoder performs nonlinear compression of the time-domain SPOD coefficients into a low-dimensional latent space, capturing the intrinsic manifold of the dynamics more efficiently than linear truncation.  
**Mechanism:** The autoencoder (encoder-decoder) is trained to minimize reconstruction error (MSE) of the SPOD coefficient trajectories. The bottleneck layer forces a compact representation (N_z ≪ N_m) that retains salient temporal correlations. It acts as a nonlinear filter, discarding high-frequency stochastic fluctuations while preserving dominant deterministic evolution.  
**Core assumption:** The dynamics of the SPOD coefficients lie on a low-dimensional, nonlinear manifold that can be efficiently encoded by a shallow neural network.  
**Evidence:** Autoencoder compressed the basis by a factor of 66 (abstract). Architecture: dense layers, tanh/linear activations, trained via SGD/Adam (section 3.2). With N_z=30, dominant coefficient trajectories are reconstructed with bounded error; N_z=5 loses high-frequency content (section 4.2).  
**Break condition:** Latent dimension N_z too small causes underfitting, losing phase information and low-frequency trends, leading to poor LSTM training signals.

### Mechanism 3: Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) Forecast
**Claim:** An LSTM network learns and forecasts the temporal evolution of the compressed latent state, maintaining bounded, statistically stationary predictions over long horizons.  
**Mechanism:** The LSTM processes sequences of latent vectors through gated cells (input, forget, output) that regulate memory. Trained via teacher forcing on the AE's latent trajectories, it learns a mapping from recent history to the next latent state. The gating mechanism mitigates vanishing gradients, enabling learning of multi-scale temporal patterns.  
**Core assumption:** The latent space dynamics are governed by a deterministic evolution law that is approximately Markovian of order N_t,in and can be approximated by a recurrent network.  
**Evidence:** LSTM-ROM produced stable, bounded long-term velocity forecasts (abstract). Details in section 3.3. Validation via Poincaré sections (Fig. 17) and bounded error growth (Fig. 19) indicate learned statistics (section 4.3).  
**Break condition:** If predicted attractor diverges from reference (Poincaré map mismatch) or error saturates at unphysically high levels, the latent dynamics are not sufficiently low-dimensional or training is inadequate.

### Mechanism 4: CNN for Velocity-to-Scalar Mapping
**Claim:** A fully convolutional neural network establishes a direct, nonlinear mapping from the predicted velocity field to the pollutant concentration field, leveraging spatial coherence and the dominance of advective transport.  
**Mechanism:** The CNN is trained in a supervised manner to minimize MSE between predicted and LES concentration fields. Its filters learn to associate local velocity patterns with scalar plume topologies. ReLU in the final layer enforces physical non-negativity of concentration.  
**Core assumption:** In the skimming flow regime, the instantaneous concentration field is a sufficiently deterministic function of the instantaneous velocity field; turbulent mixing effects are implicitly captured by learned filters.  
**Evidence:** CNN successfully reconstructed mean concentration fields and vertical mass flux profiles from predicted velocity (abstract). Rationale explained in section 3.4. Results show replication of mean concentration (Fig. 21) and mass flux (Fig. 22a), though with smoothing of fine scales (section 4.4).  
**Break condition:** If CNN consistently underestimates/overestimates concentrations in specific regions beyond tolerance, the velocity-to-scalar relationship is not sufficiently local or velocity prediction error is too high.

## Foundational Learning
- **Spectral Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (SPOD)**  
  *Why needed:* It extracts a spectrally resolved basis from turbulent data, critical for urban canyon flows with distinct frequency mechanisms (e.g., Kelvin-Helmholtz instability).  
  *Quick check:* Why does SPOD use Welch's method and solve an eigenvalue problem at each frequency instead of a single global POD?

- **Autoencoder (AE) for Nonlinear Manifold Learning**  
  *Why needed:* Linear truncation of SPOD coefficients requires many modes; the AE compresses further by learning a nonlinear manifold, making LSTM training feasible (2003 → 30).  
  *Quick check:* What is the risk of setting the AE's latent dimension too small, and how is this diagnosed in training loss curves?

- **Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) Networks**  
  *Why needed:* The latent dynamics are time-dependent and potentially chaotic; LSTMs handle long sequences and mitigate vanishing gradients, enabling stable long-term forecasting.  
  *Quick check:* What do the three gates (input, forget, output) in an LSTM cell control, and why is this architecture suitable for time-series prediction?

- **Fully Convolutional Networks for Physics-Based Mapping**  
  *Why needed:* CNNs preserve spatial correlations when mapping velocity to concentration, crucial because pollutant dispersion is driven by local advection and coherent structures.  
  *Quick check:* Why is a ReLU activation used in the final CNN layer for concentration prediction, and what would happen if a linear activation were used?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
LES velocity snapshots → SPOD → Autoencoder → LSTM → CNN → Concentration field.

**Critical path:** Autoencoder compression → LSTM training → CNN training. The AE's latent dimension N_z is the key bottleneck.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- SPOD truncation: Higher energy thresholds preserve more TKE but increase N_m, burdening AE/LSTM.
- AE latent size N_z: Larger N_z improves accuracy but slows LSTM; paper tested N_z=5,15,30 and chose 30.
- LSTM sequence length N_t,in: Longer look-back may capture more history but increases training complexity; paper fixed N_t,in=10.
- CNN architecture: Depth vs. overfitting; paper used 7 layers with increasing then decreasing channels; ReLU final layer for positivity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- SPOD: Reconstruction error spikes at specific frequencies (check Fig. 11) indicate lost coherent structures.  
- AE: Validation loss increases sharply with N_z reduction; reconstructed coefficient trajectories lose high-frequency fluctuations (Fig. 12a).  
- LSTM: Poincaré sections show distorted attractor topology; predicted latent trajectories diverge unboundedly (Fig. 19 error growth >~0.1).  
- CNN: Systematic bias in concentration fields (e.g., underprediction in recirculation zone) or excessive smoothing.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. SPOD parameter sweep: Vary N_fft and overlap; compute leading eigenvalue spectra; select N_fft=4096, 50% overlap.  
2. AE compression ratio study: Train AEs with N_z∈{5,15,30}; compare reconstruction of dominant coefficient (Fig. 12) and full-field NMSE (Fig. 13b).  
3. LSTM single-step validation: Perform one-step prediction on validation set; compare latent trajectories (Fig. 16) and compute Poincaré sections (Fig. 17).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can the ROM's robustness and accuracy be systematically improved for a wide variety of urban geometries and wind conditions beyond the single skimming-flow configuration?  
*Basis:* The authors state the framework relies on SPOD modes spanning the parameter range; more configurations must be included in training.  
*Why unresolved:* Training database only covers one aspect ratio (H/W=1) and skimming flow; transferability untested.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Evaluate on a multi-case database with various aspect ratios, wind directions, and boundary layers; report performance metrics for unseen configurations.

**Open Question 2:** To what extent does the smoothing effect introduced by the CNN's MSE loss training (and the AE's low-pass filtering) impact the ROM's utility for predicting high-concentration, health-relevant micro-scale pollutant exposure peaks?  
*Basis:* The paper notes the CNN exhibits smoothing, particularly near the point source, typical of MSE loss; implications for peaks not discussed.  
*Why unresolved:* Validation focuses on mean fields and fluxes; impact on peak magnitude, duration, and intermittency not quantified.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Compare predicted and reference distributions of concentration gradients, peak values, and exceedance frequencies of thresholds.

**Open Question 3:** Does the presence of a dominant, non-physical "box mode" (caused by streamwise periodicity in the LES) in the SPOD basis adversely affect the ROM's predictive capability for long-term statistics when applied to a non-periodic real-domain scenario?  
*Basis:* A leading SPOD mode at f*=0.5 is identified as a domain-scale periodicity artifact but is retained because it also captures low-frequency pumping.  
*Why unresolved:* The ROM is validated on the same periodic LES; behavior when this mode is absent (e.g., with physical inflow) is unknown.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Re-train the ROM on a non-periodic LES dataset (or filter out the box mode) and compare performance on instantaneous and time-averaged statistics.

**Open Question 4:** Can the temporal prediction horizon of the LSTM-ROM be extended beyond the current "long-term" (≈100 H/U_ref) stable forecast without significant error saturation, and what architectural or training modifications are most effective?  
*Basis:* LSTM forecast is described as stable and bounded, with error saturating in the test window (Fig. 19). The paper does not explore much longer horizons.  
*Why unresolved:* Applications like real-time forecasting may require simulations over many thousands of convective time units; error saturation mechanisms not investigated.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Run recursive multi-step forecasts for significantly longer horizons (e.g., 10× current) and track error; test modifications like residual losses, physics-informed constraints, or sequence-to-sequence training.

**Open Question 5:** Is the proposed end-to-end data-driven framework generalizable to pollutant dispersion scenarios involving chemically reactive species, buoyancy-driven flows, or source configurations other than a continuous line source?  
*Basis:* The framework is validated for a passive scalar with a continuous line source in a neutrally stratified, forced-drag-dominated skimming flow; the CNN mapping is a black box without advection-diffusion constraints.  
*Why unresolved:* The strong velocity-concentration correlation may break down for reactive species, buoyant plumes, or intermittent sources; the CNN may fail under these conditions.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Test on modified LES datasets: (a) reactive scalar (e.g., with chemical decay), (b) buoyant scalar, (c) intermittent point source. Success would require CNN generalization or re-architecting; failures would indicate need for physics-informed designs.

## Limitations
- The LES dataset is not publicly available, preventing external verification and replication.
- The model is only validated for a single skimming flow regime; its generalizability to other urban configurations and wind conditions remains untested.
- Critical hyperparameter details (e.g., LSTM layer count, CNN regularization) are missing from the paper, hindering full reproduction.

## Confidence
- **SPOD dimensionality reduction & spectral preservation:** Medium (based on eigenvalue spectra and mode visuals).  
- **Autoencoder compression factor & manifold learning:** Medium (compression factor clear, dynamics retention inferred from limited tests).  
- **LSTM long-term forecast stability:** Medium (Poincaré sections suggest bounded attractor, but quantitative Lyapunov analysis absent).  
- **CNN concentration reconstruction accuracy:** Medium (mean fields match, but smoothing noted; lacks SSIM/PSNR metrics).  
- **End-to-end computational efficiency:** Low (no benchmarking data provided).
