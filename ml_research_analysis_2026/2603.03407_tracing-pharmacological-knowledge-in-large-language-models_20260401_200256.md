# Tracing Pharmacological Knowledge In Large Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03407
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03407
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** Drug-group semantics in Llama-based biomedical LLMs are encoded in early layers (0-10), with strongest causal effects from intermediate tokens within the drug-group span; linear probing reveals distributed representation requiring sum-pooling for perfect separability.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how pharmacological group knowledge is represented within Llama-based biomedical large language models. Using activation patching and linear probing, the authors localize the encoding of drug-group semantics and test their linear decodability. They find that early layers (0-10) store drug-group knowledge, with the most critical information residing in intermediate tokens of the drug-group mention rather than the final token. Furthermore, token-level representations are not linearly separable, but sum-pooled activations achieve perfect separability even before the first transformer layer, indicating a distributed encoding across tokens. These results suggest that drug-group knowledge is not localized to single tokens but emerges from aggregated early-layer features.

## Method Summary
The authors employ two main methodological approaches: activation patching to causally identify where drug-group knowledge is stored and used, and linear probing to assess the decodability of pharmacological semantics. Activation patching involves replacing activations from a counterfactual (incorrect answer) prompt with those from a clean (correct answer) prompt across layers and token positions, measuring the normalized logit difference to quantify causal impact. For probing, they train logistic regression classifiers on activations to predict drug-group classes, comparing performance on individual token representations versus sum-pooled representations across layers, including pre-layer embeddings. The study uses a corpus of drug-group mentions and evaluates across multiple Llama-based biomedical LLMs.

## Key Results
- Early layers (0-10) exhibit the strongest causal effects on drug-group predictions via activation patching.
- The most significant causal contributions come from intermediate tokens within the drug-group span, not the final token.
- Linear probes on single-token activations perform near chance (~0.52-0.64 accuracy), while sum-pooled representations achieve perfect accuracy (1.00) even at the embedding layer.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Early layers (0–10) in Llama-based biomedical LLMs causally encode drug-group knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** Activation patching replaces activations from a counterfactual (incorrect answer) prompt with those from a clean (correct answer) prompt. A strong positive change in the logit difference metric after patching a specific layer indicates that the clean activations in that layer contain information necessary for the correct prediction.
- **Core assumption:** Intervening on causally relevant activations will alter the output logits in a predictable direction. The metric isolates the effect of the patch by normalizing with clean and counterfactual baselines.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "early layers play a key role in encoding drug-group knowledge"
  - [section 3.2] "We observed a causal effect when patching drug group tokens at first ten layers."
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors (e.g., "CircuitProbe", "Quantifying Semantic Emergence") validate the use of circuit tracing and probing for semantic analysis but are not specific to pharmacology. Evidence for this *specific* mechanism is **weak or missing** in the provided corpus.
- **Break condition:** Patching early layers (0–10) fails to produce a positive normalized logit difference; patching later layers produces consistently stronger causal effects.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The strongest causal effects for drug-group knowledge arise from intermediate tokens within the drug-group span, not the final token.
- **Mechanism:** Activation patching is performed at each token position within the drug-group mention. The position yielding the maximum patching effect reveals where the critical representation resides for this task.
- **Core assumption:** Token position within a mention influences how pharmacological semantics are stored and retrieved. This challenges a simplification that factual knowledge always localizes to the final subject token.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "the strongest causal effects arising from intermediate tokens within the drug-group span, not the final drug-group token."
  - [section 3.2/3.3] "Similar to residual-stream patching, interventions on intermediate drug-group tokens exhibited strong causal impacts... the average maximum effect for drug-group tokens was 0.76, compared to 0.80 for the final token of the prompt"
  - [corpus] The corpus does not contain direct evidence for token-position effects in drug-group semantics.
- **Break condition:** Patching the final token of the drug-group span produces a strictly and substantially higher effect size than all intermediate tokens across the evaluated prompts.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Drug-group semantics are distributed across token representations and are linearly decodable only after aggregation (sum-pooling), even from pre-layer embeddings.
- **Mechanism:** Linear probes (logistic regression) are trained to predict drug-group class from activations. Near-chance performance on single-token activations versus perfect performance on sum-pooled activations indicates the semantic signal is distributed and requires combination.
- **Core assumption:** High accuracy from a linear probe indicates the target concept is encoded in a linearly separable manner within the activation space. Pooling aggregates distributed features.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "token-level probes perform near chance (e.g., ~0.52-0.64 accuracy), while sum-pooled representations achieve perfect linear separability (1.00 accuracy) even before the first transformer layer."
  - [section 3.4] "probes trained on individual tokens exhibited substantially lower performance... semantic information is not aligned with single-token activations... already present in the embedding space."
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "IERL: Interpretable Ensemble Representation Learning" discuss distributed semantics, supporting the distributed representation premise, but not the pooling finding specifically.
- **Break condition:** A linear probe trained on single-token activations achieves accuracy significantly above chance (~0.55+), or sum-pooled activations are not perfectly separable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Activation Patching**  
  *Why needed:* To move beyond correlation and establish *causal* locations where drug-group knowledge is stored and used.  
  *Quick check:* If patching layer 5's residual stream changes the model's answer, what does that imply about the information in that layer?
- **Linear Probing**  
  *Why needed:* To test whether pharmacological semantics are explicitly encoded in a decodable (linearly separable) format within token or pooled representations.  
  *Quick check:* What does it mean if a probe on single tokens performs at chance, but on their sum performs perfectly?
- **Tokenization & Span Structure**  
  *Why needed:* Drug names are multi-token; the analysis must define and aggregate over the correct token span representing the drug-group term. The finding about intermediate tokens depends on correctly identifying this span.  
  *Quick check:* Why might the final token of a multi-token drug name *not* hold the strongest signal for its pharmacological class?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input → Token Embeddings → Early Layers (0-10) [Residual Stream + MLP blocks] → Later Layers (11-31) → Output Logits. Key study components: Residual stream patching at layer start; MLP output patching over a layer window; extraction of activations for the drug-group token span.
- **Critical path:** Knowledge is injected/accessible in the early residual stream (0-10), particularly at intermediate tokens of the drug-group span. This information is distributed across those tokens. MLP blocks in early layers are implicated as key transformation points. Final aggregation (sum-pooling) of these distributed token features is necessary for linear decoding.
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Patching scope:* Single layer vs. windowed (10-layer) for MLPs. Window chosen because single MLP patches had minimal effect.  
  - *Intervention type:* Symmetric token replacement (clean activation substituted) vs. adding noise. Symmetric chosen to avoid out-of-distribution artifacts.  
  - *Probe input:* Individual token vs. sum-pooled span. Pooling chosen to test distributed hypothesis.
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Activation patching yields uniformly low or noisy effects across all layers/positions.  
  - Linear probes on sum-pooled activations fail to reach high accuracy (<0.9).  
  - Token-level probes show high accuracy, contradicting the distributed claim.  
  - Patching the final token of the drug-group span is *always* the strongest effect.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Replicate core activation patching: On a held-out prompt set, patch the residual stream at layer 5 for all tokens in a drug-group span. Verify the normalized logit difference is positive and peaks at an intermediate token.  
  2. Replicate linear probing contrast: Train probes on activations from layer 0 (pre-transformer) and layer 10. Confirm: (a) token-level probes ~0.55 accuracy, (b) sum-pooled probes ~1.00 accuracy.  
  3. Test embedding space separability: Extract *only* token embeddings (no transformer layers) for drug-group spans. Train a sum-pooled linear probe. Confirm perfect separability (1.00 accuracy) at this earliest stage.

## Limitations
- Generalizability limited to Llama-based biomedical LLMs; other architectures may differ.
- Token-span definitions rely on a specific biomedical tokenizer; intermediate-token effects may be tokenization artifacts.
- Perfect linear separability in pre-layer embeddings might reflect dataset curation rather than universal semantic structure; out-of-distribution validation absent.

## Confidence
- **Early layers encode drug-group knowledge:** Medium  
- **Intermediate tokens hold strongest causal effect:** Medium  
- **Distributed semantics requiring sum-pooling:** Medium-High  

## Next Checks
1. Cross-architecture replication: Run activation patching and linear probing on non-Llama biomedical LLMs (e.g., BioBERT, PubMedBERT) to verify early-layer and intermediate-token effects generalize.  
2. Tokenizer invariance test: Re-tokenize drug names using alternative tokenizers (e.g., WordPiece, SentencePiece) and re-evaluate patching effects; confirm intermediate-token dominance persists.  
3. Aggregation method challenge: Compare sum-pooling against mean, max, and attention-weighted pooling in linear probes; test on a held-out set of drug groups to rule out dataset overfitting.
