# FactGuard: Agentic Video Misinformation Detection via Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.22963
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22963
- **Reference count**: 29
- **Primary result**: Achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on FakeSV (79.3%), FakeTT, and FakeVV benchmarks with evidence-grounded reasoning.

## Executive Summary
FactGuard tackles video misinformation detection by overcoming the fixed-depth inference limitations of multimodal LLMs. It introduces an agentic framework where the model iteratively reasons, assesses its uncertainty, and selectively invokes external tools (FactProbe for web knowledge retrieval and ClipScout for targeted video inspection) to gather evidence. Training combines domain-specific supervised fine-tuning on synthetic agentic chains of thought with decision-aware reinforcement learning (GRPO) that optimizes tool usage and calibrates risk-sensitive decisions. This approach yields substantial gains over discriminative models, zero‑shot MLLMs, and prior reasoning‑enhanced systems, demonstrating the value of uncertainty‑driven evidence acquisition and tailored reward shaping.

## Method Summary
FactGuard employs a two-stage agentic reasoning process. Given multimodal inputs (video, audio transcript, text metadata), the model first generates an initial chain-of-thought and decides whether external evidence is needed. If uncertain, it calls FactProbe (web search RAG) or ClipScout (video clip analysis by timestamp). The tool outputs are integrated into a second reasoning stage, which produces the final FAKE/REAL verdict. Training begins with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on agentic trajectories built using a 72B teacher model, teaching multi‑step reasoning and tool invocation. This is followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a reinforcement learning phase that samples multiple candidate trajectories per input, computes a reward combining accuracy, format compliance, risk‑sensitive penalties, and a tool usage bonus, and updates the policy via a KL‑regularized objective.

## Key Results
- FactGuard sets new state‑of‑the‑art results: 79.3% accuracy and 81.4% F1 on FakeSV, with comparable gains on FakeTT and FakeVV.
- It significantly outperforms discriminative models (BERT, SV‑FEND), zero‑shot MLLMs (Gemini 2.0, GPT‑4o, o1‑mini), and the reasoning‑enhanced Fact‑R1.
- Ablations confirm both training stages are essential; removing RL reduces accuracy dramatically (e.g., to 62% on FakeSV). Explainability audits show more coherent, evidence‑grounded reasoning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Iterative reasoning with uncertainty‑driven tool invocation improves verification accuracy in evidence‑scarce scenarios.
- **Mechanism**: The model assesses ambiguity after initial reasoning; if uncertain, it selects appropriate tools (FactProbe or ClipScout). Tool outputs are incorporated into a second reasoning stage to refine the final judgment.
- **Core assumption**: The uncertainty estimator reliably distinguishes between sufficient and insufficient internal evidence.
- **Evidence**: The abstract describes the agentic process; Section 3.2 formalizes the two‑stage equations; prior work (VideoZoomer, CMIE) explores tool use but not the uncertainty loop specific to misinformation.
- **Break condition**: Miscalibrated uncertainty leads to either excessive tool calls (inefficiency) or missed evidence (retained hallucinations).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Two‑stage training combining domain‑specific SFT with decision‑aware RL yields structured, risk‑calibrated verification behavior superior to either alone.
- **Mechanism**: SFT on high‑quality agentic CoT data instills multi‑step reasoning and tool‑use patterns; GRPO then optimizes a reward that includes accuracy, format, risk, and tool incentives, shaping the policy toward calibrated decisions.
- **Core assumption**: The SFT dataset provides effective demonstrations of uncertainty‑driven evidence acquisition.
- **Evidence**: The abstract highlights the two‑stage regime; Sections 3.4 and 3.5 detail SFT and GRPO; Fact‑R1 uses RL but lacks explicit tool and risk rewards.
- **Break condition**: Poor SFT data quality leads RL to reinforce flawed patterns; overly aggressive RL may override SFT inductive biases.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Asymmetric false positive/negative penalties in the RL reward enable adjustable precision‑recall trade‑offs for different deployment needs.
- **Mechanism**: The risk component of the reward is `Rrisk(τ) = -α·I_FP - γ·I_FN`; tuning the α:γ ratio shifts the model’s risk preference, encouraging calibrated tool use.
- **Core assumption**: The relative costs of false positives vs false negatives are stable enough to encode as fixed coefficients.
- **Evidence**: The abstract mentions risk‑sensitive decision making; Section 3.5 defines `Rrisk`; Table 2 shows empirical precision‑recall shifts under different cost ratios.
- **Break condition**: Mis‑specified cost ratios yield suboptimal operating points; if costs are context‑dependent, a static ratio is inadequate.

- **Why needed**: FactGuard’s tool‑use policy depends on accurately recognizing when internal belief is insufficient; miscalibration leads to unnecessary tool calls or missed verification.

- **Why needed**: FactGuard uses GRPO for RL fine‑tuning; understanding its groupwise advantage estimation and KL regularization is key to interpreting training dynamics.
- **Quick check**: How does GRPO’s groupwise advantage computation differ from PPO’s, and why might this be beneficial for verification tasks with multiple valid reasoning paths?

- **Why needed**: The system’s evidence acquisition is entirely tool‑mediated; grasping the design of FactProbe (RAG) and ClipScout (temporal sampling) is essential for debugging and extension.
- **Quick check**: What are the failure modes of each tool, and how does the agent’s query formulation affect retrieval quality?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component Map**: Qwen2.5‑VL‑7B backbone serves as the multimodal reasoning engine. It is first fine‑tuned via an SFT module on agentic CoT data. The GRPO trainer then performs decision‑aware RL. During inference, the Inference controller orchestrates two turns: initial reasoning and tool decision, followed by tool execution (FactProbe for web knowledge, ClipScout for video clip inspection) and refined reasoning. Tools are stateless; ClipScout is limited to one call per instance.

- **Critical Path**:
  1. Input: video frames, audio transcript, text metadata.
  2. First turn: model generates chain‑of‑thought and decides on tool invocations (outputs `<tool>` and `<query>`).
  3. If tools are called, execute them: FactProbe performs web search and returns a textual summary; ClipScout extracts 4 frames from the specified timestamp and returns a 2×2 image grid.
  4. Second turn: model incorporates tool outputs into a new reasoning pass and produces a final `<answer>` (FAKE/REAL).
  5. Post‑processing: validate output format and extract verdict.

- **Design Tradeoffs**:
  - SFT data quality vs. scale: High‑quality synthetic trajectories from a 72B teacher are expensive but avoid noise; larger lower‑quality datasets may misguide the policy.
  - RL rollouts vs. compute: More rollouts (e.g., 12 vs 8) improve advantage estimates but increase memory/time; diminishing returns beyond 8.
  - KL coefficient (β): Balances policy stability vs. adaptation; too low (0.02) allows collapse to tool‑free, too high (0.06) stifles learning.
  - Tool latency vs. accuracy: Each tool call adds ~1–2 seconds; aggressive tool use improves accuracy but hurts inference speed.
  - α:γ ratio: Directly trades precision vs. recall; must be set per deployment scenario (moderation vs. monitoring).

- **Failure Signatures**:
  - Cross‑modal hallucination: Model generates specific evidence not present in inputs and never calls tools.
  - Tool misuse: Calls FactProbe with vague queries or ClipScout with invalid timestamps; violates ClipScout’s single‑call constraint.
  - Reward hacking: Always calls tools to gain `R_tool` even when unnecessary, or overfits `Rformat` by correct tag placement without meaningful content.
  - Calibration drift: After RL, tool call rate remains high even on trivially verifiable claims, indicating uncertainty estimator not improved.
  - SFT collapse: Without RL, model rarely calls tools or produces shallow reasoning.

- **First 3 Experiments**:
  1. Uncertainty calibration test: Run on a held set with clear evidence levels (high vs low). Measure tool call rate and correlation with human‑judged difficulty. Expected: higher tool calls on low‑evidence cases.
  2. Ablate `R_tool`: Train with λ=0 for `R_tool`. Expect: Tool usage becomes indiscriminate or absent; precision may drop due to false positives or negatives.
  3. Risk ratio sweep: Vary α:γ (e.g., 1:3 to 3:1) and measure precision‑recall curves. Verify the system shifts operating point as intended.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How does FactGuard scale with video length and complexity?** The system imposes a 16k token context limit and restricts ClipScout to a single call, which may hinder evidence acquisition from longer or temporally distributed videos. No evaluation clarifies these constraints.
- **What principle should guide the selection of the asymmetric error cost ratio (α:γ)?** The reward function allows tuning precision‑recall trade‑offs, but the paper offers no method to determine α:γ automatically from operational requirements; deployment decisions remain heuristic.
- **How robust is FactGuard to failures or low‑quality outputs from external tools?** Tools like FactProbe (web search) and ClipScout (frame sampling) are assumed reliable; there is no discussion of error handling or fallback strategies when tools return irrelevant or misleading evidence.
- **Can FactGuard generalize to multilingual or cross‑cultural misinformation without language‑specific retraining?** Training datasets are likely English‑dominated, and no multilingual evaluation is reported. Cultural and linguistic nuances may affect what constitutes credible evidence and tool query effectiveness.

## Limitations
- Critical implementation details are underspecified: the λ weight for `R_risk` in GRPO reward, FactProbe’s retrieval configuration (API, result count, summarization), and ClipScout’s frame resolution threshold.
- The paper does not directly validate the uncertainty estimator’s calibration on held evidence‑level data, leaving a core mechanism assumption untested.
- Generalization beyond the evaluated datasets (FakeSV, FakeTT, FakeVV) remains uncertain, particularly for longer videos, multilingual content, or noisy tool environments.

## Confidence
- Claim 1 (Iterative reasoning with uncertainty‑driven tool invocation): **Medium** – strong benchmark gains but no direct calibration evidence.
- Claim 2 (Two‑stage SFT+RL training): **Medium** – ablations supportive, but SFT data quality and GRPO hyperparameters are not fully explored.
- Claim 3 (Adjustable risk trade‑off via α:γ): **High** – systematic experiments provided.

## Next Checks
1. Calibration test: Evaluate tool call rate on a held set with annotated evidence levels; correlate with human difficulty judgments.
2. R_tool ablation: Remove the tool usage reward (λ=0) and analyze impact on tool usage patterns and accuracy.
3. Risk sensitivity sweep: Systematically vary α:γ (e.g., 1:3, 1:1, 3:1) and plot precision‑recall curves to confirm tunable operating points.
