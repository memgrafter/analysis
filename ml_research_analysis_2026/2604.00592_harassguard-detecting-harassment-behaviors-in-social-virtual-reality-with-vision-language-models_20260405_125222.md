# HarassGuard: Detecting Harassment Behaviors in Social Virtual Reality with Vision-Language Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.00592
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00592
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: VLM-based harassment detection in VR achieves 88.09% binary accuracy using only 200 fine-tuning samples, avoiding sensitive biometric data.

## Executive Summary
HarassGuard addresses proactive detection of physical harassment in social virtual reality without relying on sensitive biometric data. It fine-tunes a vision-language model on a custom IRB-approved dataset of simulated harassment and benign interactions, enhanced with prompt engineering to incorporate virtual environment context. The system achieves up to 88.09% accuracy in binary classification and 68.85% in multi-class classification, matching state-of-the-art baselines while requiring far fewer fine-tuning samples. This approach offers a privacy-preserving pathway toward automated moderation in immersive social spaces.

## Method Summary
The system uses a vision-language model fine-tuned on a curated dataset of 200 simulated harassment and benign interaction clips, approved by an IRB. Prompt engineering injects contextual information about the virtual setting (e.g., space type, avatar configurations) to guide the model’s focus on behavioral cues rather than identity. By processing only visual and motion data from avatars, it avoids biometric identifiers. The fine-tuning adapts the VLM to recognize harassment-specific patterns within VR contexts, enabling both binary and multi-class classification of interaction types.

## Key Results
- Binary classification accuracy reaches 88.09%, matching state-of-the-art with only 200 fine-tuning samples (vs. 1,115 in prior work).
- Multi-class classification (distinguishing harassment subtypes) achieves 68.85% accuracy.
- Sample efficiency is a major advantage, drastically reducing data collection needs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
HarassGuard leverages a pre-trained VLM’s capacity to interpret visual scenes alongside textual prompts that encode VR context (e.g., “private room,” “crowded event”). This directs the model to attend to motion-based behavioral cues (e.g., unwanted proximity, threatening gestures) rather than personal attributes. Avoiding biometric data mitigates privacy risks while preserving detection efficacy. Fine-tuning on simulated yet realistic interactions bridges generic video understanding and VR-specific harassment detection, enabling generalization to new VR scenarios with minimal task-specific data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**: Why needed: Enable joint reasoning over visual frames and contextual text prompts for complex scene interpretation. Quick check: Can the VLM generate accurate descriptions of avatar actions and spatial dynamics in a VR clip?
- **IRB-Approved Simulation Datasets**: Why needed: Ethically obtain harassment examples without re-victimization, providing training data where real incidents are scarce. Quick check: Does the dataset capture a diverse range of harassment tactics and nuanced benign social behaviors?
- **Prompt Engineering for Context**: Why needed: Encode environmental factors (e.g., public vs. private spaces) to reduce false positives in ambiguous interactions. Quick check: Do prompts improve accuracy on edge cases like consensual close contact in private VR areas?
- **Sample-Efficient Fine-Tuning**: Why needed: Overcome the scarcity of labeled VR harassment data by maximizing learning from limited examples. Quick check: Does model performance plateau after ~200 samples, indicating sufficient learning?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: VR Stream Capture -> Feature Preprocessing -> VLM (with Prompt Context) -> Classifier Head -> Alert/Log
- **Critical Path**: Input VR frames are preprocessed (resizing, normalization). Environment-specific prompts are combined with visual tokens and fed into the VLM. The VLM produces an embedding that the classifier head labels as harassment or benign (or subtype). Positive detections trigger alerts or logs.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Chose privacy (no biometrics) over potential nuance loss; prioritized sample efficiency over massive dataset requirements; accepted lower multi-class accuracy to maintain strong binary performance. Model size may trade off latency vs. accuracy for real-time use.
- **Failure Signatures**: High false positives in ambiguous social scenarios (e.g., dancing, hugging); missed subtle harassment (e.g., verbal threats without overt motion); degraded performance in visually cluttered or poorly lit VR environments.
- **First Three Experiments**: 
  1. Test on a held-out dataset of real-world VR harassment reports to measure precision/recall against human moderator judgments.
  2. Audit performance across diverse avatar characteristics (gender presentation, race, body type) to detect disparate error rates.
  3. Benchmark inference latency under live VR stream conditions to assess feasibility for <100ms real-time intervention.

## Limitations
- Performance is benchmarked on simulated data; generalizability to unstructured, real-world VR harassment (often subtler and multimodal) is unproven.
- Multi-class accuracy (68.85%) is substantially lower than binary, limiting practical subtype detection.
- Contextual prompts may not fully adapt to dynamic, evolving harassment scenarios across varied virtual environments.

## Confidence
- **High**: Use of an IRB-approved simulation dataset; strong sample-efficiency claim (200 vs. 1,115 samples).
- **Medium**: Reported accuracy figures lack statistical variance or significance testing.
- **Low**: Real-world deployment readiness; fairness across user demographics; handling of novel harassment patterns.

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate HarassGuard on an unseen dataset of documented real harassment reports from VR platforms, measuring alignment with human moderator decisions.
2. Conduct a systematic demographic bias audit across avatar variations (gender, race, body type) and interaction contexts.
3. Measure inference speed and computational cost in live VR streams to verify <100ms response for real-time use.
