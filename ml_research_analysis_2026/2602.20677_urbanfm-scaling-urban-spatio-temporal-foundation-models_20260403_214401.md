# UrbanFM: Scaling Urban Spatio-Temporal Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20677
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20677
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** UrbanFM achieves 39.0%–70.2% lower MAPE than existing foundation models in zero-shot generalization and matches full-shot expert performance on unseen cities and tasks.

## Executive Summary
Urban spatio-temporal forecasting models are typically scenario-specific, limiting their applicability to new cities or data modalities. This paper introduces UrbanFM, a scaling framework to build generalizable urban foundation models. It combines three scaling axes: (1) **data scaling** via WorldST, a billion-point standardized corpus from over 100 cities; (2) **computation scaling** via MiniST, a tokenization that unifies grid and sensor data; and (3) **architecture scaling** via a minimalist self-attention model. The approach enables strong zero-shot and few-shot transfer, significantly outperforming prior foundation models and matching full-shot experts on unseen cities and tasks, while being efficient and robust.

## Method Summary
The method hinges on a three-pronged scaling strategy. First, **WorldST** aggregates and standardizes massive heterogeneous urban data (traffic, air quality, taxi demand, etc.) from 100+ cities into a uniform tensor format, addressing data heterogeneity. Second, **MiniST** tokenizes any spatial structure (sensors or grid cells) by clustering nodes into fixed-size patches that preserve local proximity, creating a unified computational unit. Third, **UrbanFM** is a self-attention model with factorized spatio-temporal attention, spatio-temporal Rotary Position Embeddings (S-RoPE, T-RoPE), and reversible instance normalization (RevIN). It is pre-trained with a generative objective (masked future prediction) on the WorldST corpus, enabling direct zero-shot application to new cities and tasks.

## Key Results
- UrbanFM reduces MAPE by **39.0%–70.2%** in zero-shot transfer compared to existing foundation models across multiple cities and tasks.
- In few-shot adaptation (10% target data), it improves by **28.2%–65.2%** over full-shot expert models.
- It is **4× faster** at inference than Chronos while achieving lower prediction error, demonstrating efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Data Scaling via WorldST
- **Claim:** Aggregating massive, diverse urban signals into a unified corpus (WorldST) addresses spatio-temporal heterogeneity, allowing the model to learn generalizable distributions rather than overfit to local specifics.
- **Mechanism:** A curation pipeline aggregates 1B+ data points from 100+ cities across 8 domains, standardizes temporal resolution to 5-minute intervals, and applies quality control (dead node removal, outlier clipping, pre-completion). This produces dense, distributionally compatible tensors.
- **Core assumption:** Heterogeneity (spatial variances and temporal fluctuations) is the primary barrier; only massive, high-quality multi-city data can cover the true underlying distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] WorldST is constructed "from over 100 global cities into a unified data format" to address heterogeneity.
  - [section 4.1] Describes a three-step pipeline (aggregation, alignment, quality control) designed for "extreme heterogeneity in file formats and metadata standards."
  - Neighboring works like OpenCity have limited scale; WorldST's billion-point corpus is a key differentiator for zero-shot transfer.
- **Break condition:** If pre-training data lacks sufficient geographic or domain diversity, the model cannot learn invariant patterns, and zero-shot performance degrades on out-of-distribution cities.

### Mechanism 2: Computation Scaling via MiniST Tokenization
- **Claim:** MiniST tokenization enables unified modeling of heterogeneous spatial structures (grid vs. sensor) by discretizing continuous fields into capacity-constrained, locality-preserving tokens, making correlation learning computationally tractable.
- **Mechanism:** A greedy capacity-constrained clustering algorithm (KD-Tree inspired) groups spatial nodes into fixed-size patches (Sp × Tp), preserving local proximity while linearizing irregular topologies. These patches become the standard units for attention.
- **Core assumption:** Spatio-temporal autocorrelation (Tobler's First Law) implies "near things are more related"; modeling requires units that embed local proximity without fixed, city-specific adjacency matrices.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] MiniST "unifies representations of grid-based and sensor-based observations."
  - [section 4.2] Contrasts MiniST with convolution/graph operators that require city-specific adjacency, arguing tokenization "renders tokens structurally independent, enabling massive parallel training."
  - Graph-based methods like Graph Wavenet rely on predefined graphs; MiniST's data-driven clustering is framed as a scalability breakthrough.
- **Break condition:** If spatial window Sp is too small, local correlations are lost; if too large, weak/noisy relationships dilute signals, causing diminishing or negative returns (as shown in scaling experiments).

### Mechanism 3: Architecture Scaling via UrbanFM
- **Claim:** UrbanFM's minimalist self-attention architecture with limited inductive biases allows autonomous learning of dynamic spatio-temporal dependencies from scaled data, avoiding bottlenecks from handcrafted priors (e.g., static graphs).
- **Mechanism:** The model uses factorized spatio-temporal attention, S-RoPE/T-RoPE for positional encoding, and RevIN to capture evolving patterns without fixed topological or temporal structures. The generative pre-training objective (masked future prediction) unifies forecasting and imputation.
- **Core assumption:** Urban systems are non-stationary; static inductive biases (e.g., constant road graph) inhibit modeling shifting dynamics (e.g., festival vs. commute crowds). Scale + minimal bias enables emergent dynamic modeling.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] UrbanFM addresses dynamics via "a minimalist self-attention architecture... to autonomously learn dynamic spatio-temporal dependencies."
  - [section 4.3] States: "we eschew superfluous structural priors, relying solely on self-attention to capture dynamics," linking to scaling laws where depth and data reduce loss.
  - [section 5.2] Zero-shot results surpass scenario-specific experts and other foundation models that inject priors (e.g., FactoST's spatial adapters), implying minimalist design confers advantage.
- **Break condition:** If data scaling is insufficient, self-attention cannot learn complex dynamics from limited examples, leading to poor generalization; over-scaling model depth without commensurate data risks overfitting.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Spatio-Temporal Heterogeneity**
  - **Why needed:** Urban data varies across space (e.g., dense downtown vs. suburban) and time (e.g., rush hour vs. night). Recognizing this explains why a unified corpus (WorldST) is necessary—isolated city data captures only local statistics.
  - **Quick check:** If you train a model only on New York taxi data, will it accurately predict traffic in São Paulo? Why or why not?
- **Concept: Autocorrelation and Locality**
  - **Why needed:** Tobler's First Law underpins MiniST token design. Spatial autocorrelation (near locations are similar) and temporal order (past influences future) must be encoded without rigid graphs. This explains why clustering neighbors into tokens works.
  - **Quick check:** How would you represent two adjacent sensors’ data so a model knows they’re spatially close without an adjacency matrix?
- **Concept: Foundation Model Scaling Laws**
  - **Why needed:** The paper leverages the "bitter lesson"—general methods (self-attention) scale with data/compute better than specialized heuristics. This justifies UrbanFM's minimalist architecture and focus on scaling data/computation.
  - **Quick check:** If you double the pre-training data size, what does scaling law theory predict for zero-shot error on a new city?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** WorldST -> MiniST -> UrbanFM -> EvalST

**Critical path:**
1. **Data ingestion:** Apply WorldST pipeline to raw city logs → clean, aligned tensors.
2. **Tokenization:** For each city’s tensor, run MiniST clustering (using lat/long or grid indices) → token sequences.
3. **Pre-training:** Feed tokens into UrbanFM with generative objective (mask future steps) across all WorldST data.
4. **Zero-shot inference:** For new city, repeat steps 1–2, then run UrbanFM without fine-tuning.
5. **Few-shot adaptation:** Fine-tune UrbanFM on small target dataset (10% train set).

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Minimal inductive bias vs. data hunger:** UrbanFM avoids graph priors but requires massive data to learn dynamics from scratch. With scarce data, models with stronger priors (e.g., GWNet) may initially perform better.
- **Token size (Sp, Tp):** Larger Sp captures broader spatial context but may include irrelevant far nodes; larger Tp gives longer temporal context but risks noise. Empirically, Sp≈16, Tp≈48 optimal for PEMS.
- **Factorized attention vs. full attention:** Reduces quadratic complexity but may miss fine-grained spatio-temporal interactions; acceptable given scale.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Poor zero-shot on new city:** Likely due to inadequate WorldST diversity (e.g., missing similar climate/land-use cities) or poor MiniST clustering (e.g., Sp too small/large).
- **High noise sensitivity:** Without sufficient pre-training data diversity, model cannot learn robust representations; check if pre-training included varied sensor failure scenarios.
- **Slow inference:** UrbanFM uses FlashAttention; if slow, verify hardware/utilization or if batch size is too small.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Zero-city generalization:** Train UrbanFM on WorldST (excluding City X), then evaluate on City X across all tasks without fine-tuning. Expect ≥39% MAPE reduction vs. baselines.
2. **Few-shot adaptation:** Fine-tune pre-trained UrbanFM on 10% of target city’s training data. Expect ≥28% improvement over full-shot experts.
3. **Robustness stress test:** Add 30% Gaussian noise or 30% zero-masking to input data; measure MAPE degradation. UrbanFM should degrade less than expert models due to generative pre-training.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What are the true limits of scaling for UrbanFM in terms of data volume and model capacity before encountering saturation or degradation in zero-shot generalization?
- **Basis:** The paper investigates scaling by varying pre-training data ratios (up to 10×) and model depth (up to 10 layers), observing no saturation but not testing extremely large scales (e.g., trillion-point datasets or 100-layer architectures).
- **Why unresolved:** Absence of saturation within tested ranges does not guarantee indefinite scaling benefits; diminishing returns or instability may emerge at orders-of-magnitude larger scales.
- **Resolving evidence:** Conduct experiments with two to three orders of magnitude more pre-training data and proportionally larger models, measuring performance curves to identify inflection points.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can UrbanFM’s zero-shot capabilities be made robust and equitable for cities with sparse, low-quality, or non-existent sensor infrastructure, addressing the inherent bias toward data-rich metropolises?
- **Basis:** The authors acknowledge WorldST’s data coverage is biased toward developed cities ("data silos"), and limitations note the "rich-get-richer" risk in fairness, but no solution is proposed for under-sampled regions.
- **Why unresolved:** The model’s generalization hinges on WorldST’s distribution, which may not capture dynamics in low-infrastructure areas, potentially exacerbating urban AI divides.
- **Resolving evidence:** Perform zero-shot evaluations on cities with minimal or no sensor deployment (e.g., using proxy data from similar regions) and compare performance; develop data augmentation or transfer techniques to mitigate the gap.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can UrbanFM’s architecture and pre-training objective be extended to fuse multimodal urban data streams (e.g., text event reports, video feeds, social media) while preserving its zero-shot generalization promise?
- **Basis:** The paper explicitly limits UrbanFM to structured Euclidean grid and sensor data, and the ethics section identifies integration of unstructured modalities (text, visual) as a key future need but provides no mechanism.
- **Why unresolved:** Real-world urban systems generate heterogeneous data; ignoring multimodal signals limits the model’s comprehensiveness and applicability to tasks requiring semantic understanding.
- **Resolving evidence:** Design a multimodal extension of MiniST and UrbanFM that ingests non-numerical inputs, pre-train on aligned multimodal corpora, and evaluate on tasks like incident-driven forecasting or human activity recognition.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Does UrbanFM’s generative pre-training objective inherently equip it to serve as a world model for closed-loop decision-making (e.g., traffic signal control, resource dispatch), or does it require architectural modifications for stable simulation and policy learning?
- **Basis:** Future directions propose evolving UrbanFM from forecasting to decision-making via reinforcement learning, but the current model is designed for open-loop prediction without explicit simulation robustness.
- **Why unresolved:** Forecasting models often fail as simulators due to compounding errors and lack of action conditioning; UrbanFM’s suitability for interactive environments remains untested.
- **Resolving evidence:** Integrate UrbanFM as a differentiable dynamics model within an RL or model-predictive control framework, evaluate sample efficiency and stability on control benchmarks, and compare against specialized simulators.

## Limitations
- **Undisclosed corpus details:** WorldST's exact city list, domain distribution, and aggregation pipelines are not revealed, hindering reproducibility and assessment of diversity claims.
- **Implementation ambiguities:** MiniST's handling of undersized clusters, masking integration, and spatial ordering for S-RoPE are unspecified. UrbanFM's hyperparameters (embedding dimension, attention heads, FFN size, RevIN placement, generative head design) are omitted.
- **Missing training specifics:** Optimizer settings, learning-rate schedule, batch composition, and regularization strategies are not provided.

## Confidence
- **Data scaling claim (WorldST enables generalization):** Medium. Evidence is coherent, but undisclosed corpus composition prevents verifying coverage of heterogeneity.
- **Computation scaling claim (MiniST unifies representations):** Medium. Mechanism described but implementation details and ablation on token size missing.
- **Architecture scaling claim (minimalist self-attention learns dynamics):** Medium. Logical scaling argument supported by results, but hyperparameter sensitivity unknown.
