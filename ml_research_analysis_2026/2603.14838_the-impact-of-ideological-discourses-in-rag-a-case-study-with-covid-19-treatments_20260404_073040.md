# The Impact of Ideological Discourses in RAG: A Case Study with COVID-19 Treatments

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14838
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14838
- **Reference count:** 11
- **Primary result:** RAG-based LLM outputs are significantly more aligned with the ideology of retrieved documents than LLM-only outputs; explicitly disclosing the ideological framework in the prompt further amplifies this alignment.

## Executive Summary
This study examines how ideologically charged external knowledge influences LLM outputs within RAG systems, using COVID-19 treatment discussions as a high-stakes case study. By applying Lexical Multidimensional Analysis (LMDA) to a curated corpus of 1,117 academic articles, the authors identify three key ideological dimensions and their poles. Experiments with four LLMs show that retrieving texts from a specific ideological pole increases semantic and lexical alignment of generated responses with that pole, compared to LLM-only generation. Adding LMDA-derived descriptions to the prompt (enhanced prompt) amplifies this effect. The findings reveal a critical vulnerability: RAG systems can inadvertently or deliberately amplify ideological bias from their knowledge source.

## Method Summary
The authors first curated a corpus of academic articles on COVID-19 treatments, distinguishing between controversial and medically endorsed viewpoints. LMDA was applied: keywords were extracted via log-likelihood, collocations via Log-Dice, and factor analysis identified three latent ideological dimensions. Each document was scored on these dimensions and assigned to a pole (positive/negative) based on highest scores. For 18 topics, the RAG pipeline retrieved the top-3 text chunks from a metadata-filtered subset (only texts belonging to the target pole). Two prompt types were used: *regular* (question + retrieved texts) and *enhanced* (adds the dimension label, description, and typical vocabulary). Four LLMs generated five answers per condition (LLM-only, RAG-regular, RAG-enhanced). Ideological alignment was measured by cosine similarity between concatenated model outputs and concatenated reference texts of the target pole, using semantic (BERT embeddings) and lexical (TF-IDF) representations. ANOVA tested for significant effects.

## Key Results
- RAG with a regular prompt significantly increased alignment over LLM-only: average semantic similarity rose from 0.80 to 0.87; lexical similarity from 0.66 to 0.72.
- The enhanced prompt further amplified alignment, reaching average semantic similarity of 0.86 and lexical similarity of 0.72.
- ANOVA confirmed the enhanced prompt’s effect on semantic similarity was statistically significant, demonstrating that explicitly framing the ideological context strengthens the RAG-induced alignment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1:** Retrieved ideological texts steer RAG-generated outputs toward alignment with the source ideology. The retriever selects documents from a pre-filtered ideological subset using LMDA metadata; the generator LLM conditions its response on this context, shifting its output distribution toward the lexical and semantic patterns of the retrieved texts. Evidence: Tables 2–3 show consistent similarity increases across models and poles; the corpus is explicitly labeled via LMDA. Break condition: If retrieved texts are ideologically neutral or the model exceptionally robust to context, alignment may not increase.

**Mechanism 2:** Explicitly disclosing the ideological framework (via LMDA descriptions) in the prompt amplifies alignment. The enhanced prompt provides both example texts and meta-descriptors (label, description, vocabulary), creating dual priming: direct lexical/semantic priming from texts and conceptual priming from descriptors, which may activate related latent concepts. Evidence: Enhanced prompts generally yield higher similarity; ANOVA significance for semantic; decay in cases where RAG underperforms LLM-only. Break condition: Poorly defined or abstract descriptors may confuse the model, reducing alignment.

**Mechanism 3:** The alignment effect is primarily a retrieval-context phenomenon, not a pure expression of the LLM’s latent training bias. Comparing LLM-only (no retrieval) to RAG isolates external knowledge influence. LLM-only baselines (~0.80 similarity) suggest some latent alignment, but the consistent increase only with ideological retrieval shows the model adapts to immediate context. Evidence: RAG vs. LLM-only differences; ANOVA interaction showing significant difference only with enhanced prompt. Break condition: Perfectly balanced training data or strong safety overrides in high-stakes domains might mitigate the effect.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Corpus & LMDA Module -> Metadata Filter -> Vector Store -> Retriever -> Prompt Assembler -> LLM Generator -> Alignment Scorer

**Critical path:**  
User Query -> Map to Ideological Dimension/Pole -> Metadata Filter (subset corpus) -> Similarity Search (top-3 passages) -> Prompt Assembly (Regular/Enhanced) -> LLM Generation -> Alignment Scoring vs. Pole Reference.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Retrieval Precision vs. Ideological Purity:* Metadata filtering ensures ideological purity but may exclude relevant documents not labeled for that dimension. A broader search risks mixing poles.
- *Prompt Explicitness vs. Naturalness:* Enhanced prompts are more effective but unnatural and reveal manipulation; regular prompts are covert but less powerful.
- *Evaluation Granularity:* Using concatenated pole references measures alignment with an "archetypal" discourse, not specific source texts, losing per-document variation.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low RAG similarity despite ideological texts → retrieval failed or generator ignored context.
- Enhanced prompt worse than regular → LMDA descriptors are confusing/contradictory.
- Lexical high, semantic low → model parrots surface vocabulary without grasping discourse meaning.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline RAG vs. LLM-only:** For each model, run 18 topics with regular prompt in both RAG (retrieval on) and LLM-only (no retrieval) modes. Hypothesis: RAG shows higher alignment. Confirms Mechanism 1.
2. **Prompt Type Impact:** For RAG runs only, compare Regular vs. Enhanced prompts for all dimensions/poles. Hypothesis: Enhanced shows higher alignment. Confirms Mechanism 2.
3. **Dimension-Specific Alignment:** Analyze which LMDA dimensions (1, 2, 3) and poles (+/-) show the strongest RAG effect. Hypothesis: Dimension 1 (highest factor loading) shows largest delta. Tests granularity of ideological signal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** What specific technical or architectural interventions can effectively mitigate the amplification of ideological bias in RAG outputs while preserving factual accuracy?  
*Basis:* Conclusion calls for strategies to mitigate bias.  
*Why unresolved:* No concrete mitigation techniques are evaluated or proposed.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Systematic study comparing multiple bias-mitigation strategies (e.g., adversarial retrieval, diversity-aware re-ranking) on the same setup, measuring both bias reduction and factual correctness.

**Open Question 2:** To what extent are the observed ideological alignment effects generalizable beyond the COVID-19 treatment domain and the specific LMDA-derived dimensions?  
*Basis:* Single-domain case study with 1,117 articles on COVID-19 treatments; no test of other contested domains.  
*Why unresolved:* Mechanisms may be domain- or dimension-specific; effects could vary with different ideological conflicts or knowledge sources.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Replications in other high-stakes, ideologically contested domains (e.g., climate change, economics) using comparable pipelines.

**Open Question 3:** Can the LMDA-based identification of ideological dimensions be automated or scaled without requiring costly expert linguistic analysis?  
*Basis:* Methodology involves manual, expert-driven interpretation of factor loadings and pole labeling.  
*Why unresolved:* Human expert bottleneck for real-time, large-scale bias monitoring.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Development and validation of an automated/semi-automated pipeline (e.g., LLM-assisted labeling) that reproduces expert-derived labels with high reliability.

**Open Question 4:** What is the causal mechanism by which "enhanced prompts" increase ideological alignment—does it stem from clearer instruction following, increased priming, or a combination?  
*Basis:* Results show amplification but prompt design conflates multiple components (label, description, vocabulary, texts).  
*Why unresolved:* Which element drives the effect is unclear, hindering safeguard design.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Ablation study systematically removing components of the enhanced prompt to isolate each contribution.

**Open Question 5:** Do the chosen evaluation metrics (cosine similarity on BERT and TF-IDF vectors) fully capture ideological alignment nuances, or do they primarily measure lexical/semantic overlap without assessing rhetorical or argumentative structure?  
*Basis:* Metrics based on vector similarity; paper acknowledges they reward conceptual alignment or vocabulary reuse but does not correlate with human judgments of ideological fidelity or argument coherence.  
*Why unresolved:* High similarity may reflect superficial jargon adoption rather than coherent stance.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Human evaluations rating ideological consistency, strength, and coherence; correlating these with automatic metrics.

## Limitations
- **LMDA replication fidelity:** Exact factor analysis parameters (rotation, loading thresholds) and pole assignment logic are unspecified; incorrect replication yields different dimensions.
- **Context-response alignment metric:** Using concatenated pole references overgeneralizes alignment; measures affinity for an "archetypal" discourse, not direct source tracing.
- **Prompt naturalness & realism:** Prompts explicitly instruct ideological reflection; real-world RAG queries rarely include such directives, limiting ecological validity.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Retrieval-driven alignment): High** – Robust statistical improvement across 4 models and multiple dimensions (Tables 2–3).
- **Mechanism 2 (Enhanced prompt amplification): Medium** – ANOVA shows significance for semantic similarity, but lexical gains are inconsistent; depends on descriptor quality.
- **Mechanism 3 (Context over latent bias): Medium** – LLM-only baselines confirm latent influences, but isolation of RAG effect relies on LLM-only representing "no retrieval."
