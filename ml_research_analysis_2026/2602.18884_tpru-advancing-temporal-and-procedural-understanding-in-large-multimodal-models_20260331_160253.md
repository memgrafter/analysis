# TPRU: Advancing Temporal and Procedural Understanding in Large Multimodal Models

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.18884
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18884
*   **Reference Count:** 18
*   **Primary Result:** Fine-tuning a 7B model with TPRU data and GRPO RL raises test accuracy from ~50% to 75.7%, exceeding GPT-4o and improving ordering on MuirBench by 20.32 points.

## Executive Summary
Small multimodal language models (MLLMs) struggle with understanding temporal sequences and procedural logic in visual data, a critical gap for embodied AI. This paper introduces TPRU, a large-scale dataset and training framework designed to close this gap. TPRU provides 24,750 curated samples from robotics, GUI, LEGO, and navigation scenarios, each framed within one of three complementary tasks: Temporal Reordering, Next-Frame Prediction, and Previous-Frame Review. A key innovation is the inclusion of challenging negative samples that force models to perform active cross-modal verification. Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL models using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) reinforcement learning leads to dramatic, state-of-the-art improvements on the TPRU test set and positive generalization to other procedural benchmarks like MuirBench and LEGO-Puzzles.

## Method Summary
The core method combines a novel dataset (TPRU-25k) with a reinforcement learning fine-tuning procedure. The dataset is automatically constructed from source videos across four domains. Frames are extracted, filtered for quality and logical coherence using a large model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B), and paired with textual descriptions. Each instance is formulated into one of three tasks that target bidirectional procedural reasoning, and negative samples (deliberately mismatched text-image pairs) are crafted to require cross-modal validation. For training, a 7B or 32B parameter Qwen2.5-VL model is fine-tuned using the GRPO algorithm, which optimizes a reward based on task accuracy while preventing deviation from the base model via a KL penalty. This RL approach is posited to cultivate genuine reasoning rather than superficial pattern imitation.

## Key Results
*   TPRU-7B accuracy on TPRU-Test jumps from a 50.33% baseline to 75.7% after GRPO fine-tuning, outperforming GPT-4o.
*   On MuirBench, overall accuracy increases from 58.35% to 65.04%, with the Ordering subset improving by 20.32 points.
*   On LEGO-Puzzles, TPRU-7B achieves 42.82%, representing a 24-point gain in ordering performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** Negative samples force cross-modal verification rather than pattern matching.
*   **Mechanism:** Deliberately mismatched text-image pairs require the model to validate consistency across modalities, preventing reliance on textual priors or superficial heuristics.
*   **Core assumption:** Small models have latent temporal reasoning capacity but lack training signals that force active verification.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.1 describe the negative sampling strategy as compelling "models to transition from passive observation to active, cross-modal validation." The corpus confirms this specific application is novel.
*   **Break condition:** Negative samples are either trivially easy (model ignores them) or impossibly hard (model cannot learn from them).

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** Three complementary tasks build bidirectional procedural reasoning.
*   **Mechanism:** Temporal Reordering (global sequence), Next-Frame Prediction (forward reasoning), and Previous-Frame Review (backward reasoning) jointly train the model to understand temporal flow in both directions and grasp causal dependencies.
*   **Core assumption:** Genuine procedural understanding requires more than forward prediction—it needs backward reasoning and full sequence comprehension.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Section 3.1 and the abstract state the tasks are engineered to advance MLLMs "beyond static image analysis" by jointly addressing these directions. The corpus finds no systematic combination of these three tasks.
*   **Break condition:** Tasks are redundant or fail to cover complementary reasoning directions.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** GRPO reinforcement learning optimizes for reasoning rather than imitation.
*   **Mechanism:** RL with group-wise preference optimization encourages exploration of reasoning paths and reward shaping for correct temporal inferences, whereas SFT encourages superficial pattern matching.
*   **Core assumption:** SFT leads to imitation of training examples; RL promotes development of internal reasoning capabilities.
*   **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.4 shows GRPO (75.70%) outperforms SFT (72.88%) on TPRU-Test. The text states GRPO "moves beyond simple pattern imitation to better cultivate advanced reasoning capabilities." The corpus notes the specific application of GRPO to temporal procedural tasks is novel.
*   **Break condition:** Reward design misaligns with true reasoning or exploration is insufficient.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept:** Multi-image temporal coherence
    *   Why needed: Models must treat sequences as processes with state changes, not unordered collections.
    *   Quick check: Can the model correctly order shuffled frames based on visual state transitions?
*   **Concept:** Cross-modal consistency verification
    *   Why needed: Negative samples require rejecting text descriptions that don't match the visual sequence.
    *   Quick check: Does the model output "None of the choices provided" for mismatched text-image pairs?
*   **Concept:** Procedural decomposition
    *   Why needed: Tasks require identifying missing steps, dependencies, and causal relationships.
    *   Quick check: Can the model predict the correct intermediate frame given start and end states?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:**
    Source videos (robotic/GUI/LEGO/navigation) -> frame extraction -> filtering with Qwen2.5-VL-72B -> text description generation -> task formulation (3 tasks + negatives) -> GRPO RL fine-tuning on Qwen2.5-VL backbone -> evaluation via VLMEvalKit
*   **Critical path:**
    1.  Quality filtering: Remove blurry/illogical sequences (uses large model as judge)
    2.  Negative sample crafting: Create plausible but procedurally incorrect distractors
    3.  Task formulation: Preserve temporal structure while creating question formats
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   Dataset size: 24,750 samples sufficient (diminishing returns beyond 20k per Figure 4)
    *   Negative ratio: ~30% negatives balance learning without overwhelming
    *   Task mix: Three tasks increase training complexity but cover all reasoning directions
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   Training instability: Reward curves not converging or oscillating wildly
    *   Catastrophic forgetting: Performance drops on general multi-image benchmarks
    *   Format overfitting: Correct answer format but wrong reasoning (high TPRU-Test but low ablation performance)
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  Baseline: Evaluate Qwen2.5-VL-7B on TPRU-Test (~50%) to establish gap
    2.  Positive-only: Train with only positive samples; expect moderate improvement (~60%)
    3.  Full TPRU: Add negatives and all tasks; expect large jump (~75%) demonstrating cross-modal validation effect

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   Does fine-tuning TPRU improve large models (>70B) or saturate? The authors only fine-tune up to 32B.
*   How does the choice of reinforcement learning algorithm (e.g., GRPO vs. PPO/DPO) affect the effectiveness of TPRU training? Only GRPO is used and compared to SFT.
*   Does the procedure learned on TPRU's source domains generalize to truly out-of-distribution procedural tasks, such as medical surgery or cooking? Evaluation is limited to MuirBench/LEGO/EPIC-KITCHENS.

## Limitations
*   GRPO reward design and dataset access details missing from provided text, blocking faithful reproduction.
*   Generalization tested only on a limited set of benchmarks (MuirBench, LEGO-Puzzles, EPIC-KITCHENS); transfer to other temporal domains is unproven.
*   Dataset curation relies on Qwen2.5-VL-72B, risking propagated model biases; no independent human verification of all samples.

## Confidence
*   Large TPRU-Test gains: **Medium** (self-reported, unreplicated)
*   Negatives force cross-modal verification: **Medium** (plausible mechanism, unmeasured)
*   Triplet tasks enable bidirectional reasoning: **Medium** (no direct ablation evidence)
*   GRPO RL > SFT for reasoning: **Medium-High** (direct comparison provided, but RL instability possible)
*   Generalization to other benchmarks: **Medium** (limited benchmark suite)
