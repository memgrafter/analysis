# SimuAgent: An LLM-Based Simulink Modeling Assistant Enhanced with Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.05187
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05187
- Reference count: 28
- Primary result: Fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B with SimuAgent achieves 51.89% average accuracy on SimuBench, surpassing GPT-4o and other baselines.

## Executive Summary
SimuAgent is an LLM-based agent for Simulink modeling that replaces verbose XML with a concise Python dictionary representation, drastically reducing token usage and enabling fast in-process simulation. It employs a two-stage plan-execute training curriculum and Reflection-GRPO (ReGRPO) to tackle sparse rewards in long-horizon tasks. On the new SimuBench benchmark (5300 tasks), a Qwen2.5-7B model fine-tuned with SimuAgent outperforms GPT-4o and other baselines. The system runs entirely on-premise on modest hardware, offering a privacy-preserving, cost-effective solution for industrial model-driven engineering. Cross-platform transfer to Modelica and PSCAD shows promising generalization with minimal fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
SimuAgent uses a plan-execute architecture where the LLM generates or edits a Python dictionary (`sys_dict`) that encodes Simulink blocks and connections. A local Python testbed validates signal types and wiring before simulation. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 focuses on execution skills using small systems (≤12 blocks) with dense feedback; Stage 2 introduces planning for larger models and incorporates abstract-reconstruct data augmentation (200 summary-reconstruction pairs). The core optimization is Reflection-GRPO, which extends Group Relative Policy Optimization by adding self-reflection traces from failed rollouts to provide denser learning signals. The agent employs tools such as `search_blocks` (RAG) and symbolic utilities. Evaluation on SimuBench shows state-of-the-art accuracy.

## Key Results
- SimuAgent (Qwen2.5-7B) achieves 51.89% average accuracy on SimuBench, beating GPT-4o (50.45%) and other baselines (CoT, RAG, SFT).
- The dictionary representation reduces token count from ~43k to ~2.7k and enables fast in-process simulation without MATLAB Engine.
- Reflection-GRPO converges faster than standard GRPO and improves robustness; the two-stage curriculum yields a 7.51% gain over ablations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Replacing verbose XML with a concise Python dictionary representation drastically reduces token consumption, improves interpretability, and enables fast in-process simulation.
- **Mechanism:** The dictionary discards visual styling and coordinates, retaining only block names, key parameters, and connectivity in a JSON-compatible format. LLMs generate or edit this directly; a local Python testbed validates signal types and wiring without MATLAB Engine calls.
- **Core assumption:** Essential modeling information is captured without XML overhead, and LLMs handle Python structures more naturally.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "SimuAgent replaces verbose XML with a concise, dictionary-style Python representation, dramatically cutting token counts, improving interpretability, and enabling fast, in-process simulation."
  - Section 2: "Compared with screenshots or XML files, the dictionary offers three clear benefits: Compact, semantically focused... Naturally LLM-friendly... Fast simulation and debugging."
- **Break condition:** If the dictionary omits critical constraints (e.g., Simscape port domains, solver settings), validation may miss errors, producing non-executable models. Extremely large systems might still exceed context windows.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A two-stage training curriculum (execution then planning) builds foundational tool-use skills first, then high-level design reasoning, yielding better generalization on complex models.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 trains on small systems (≤12 blocks) with dense feedback; Stage 2 introduces larger models, encouraging decomposition, abstraction, and subsystem encapsulation. Abstract‑reconstruct augmentation is added in Stage 2.
- **Core assumption:** Hierarchical skill acquisition stabilizes learning; execution mastery scaffolds planning.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "A lightweight plan–execute architecture, trained in two stages, equips the agent with both low-level tool skills and high-level design reasoning."
  - Section 3.1: "Stage 1: Execution focus... Stage 2: Planning integration... All tokens generated in the main dialogue are used for gradient updates."
- **Break condition:** If Stage 1 tasks are too narrow or Stage 2 lacks sufficient complexity, the agent may fail to generalize. Ablation shows skipping Stage 1 drops average accuracy by 7.51% (Table 2, G).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Reflection‑GRPO (ReGRPO) integrates self‑reflection traces into Group Relative Policy Optimization, providing rich intermediate feedback that accelerates convergence and improves robustness in sparse‑reward, long‑horizon tasks.
- **Mechanism:** For each query, a first subgroup generates rollouts; failed rollouts trigger a reflection step that produces textual critique. A second subgroup re‑attempts with the reflection prepended, guiding exploration. Advantages are computed group‑wise as in GRPO.
- **Core assumption:** Self‑reflection can distill actionable insights from environmental feedback, offering denser learning signals than scalar rewards alone.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: "To tackle sparse rewards in long‑horizon tasks, we propose Reflection‑GRPO (ReGRPO), which augments Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with self‑reflection traces that supply rich intermediate feedback, accelerating convergence and boosting robustness."
  - Section 3.3: Detailed algorithm (Algorithm 1) and equation (2) with reflection text `e_i`.
  - Section 4.2: Figure 3 shows ReGRPO converges faster than GRPO on GSM8K; Figure 4 shows similar gains on SimuBench. Table 3 shows consistent improvements across domains (e.g., +0.90% on GSM8K, +5.9% on HumanEval).
- **Break condition:** Poor‑quality reflections (vague, incorrect) may misguide the second subgroup. Over‑reliance on reflection early on could slow inference if not decayed; the adaptive schedule is critical.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)
- **Why needed here:** ReGRPO extends GRPO; understanding its group‑based advantage estimation (no value network) is key to seeing how reflection augments it.
- **Quick check:** How does GRPO compute the advantage `ˆAi,t` without a separate critic?  
  *Answer:* By using the average reward of the sampled group as a baseline, making the advantage relative to peers.

### Concept: Structured Representation Engineering for LLM Code Generation
- **Why needed here:** The shift from XML to Python dictionary is central to SimuAgent’s efficiency; one must grasp trade‑offs between semantic fidelity and LLM‑friendliness.
- **Quick check:** What are two specific benefits of using a JSON‑compatible dictionary over XML for Simulink modeling with LLMs?  
  *Answer:* (1) Token count reduction (∼43k → ∼2.7k); (2) Direct generation and editing without format conversion, enabling fast Python‑based validation.

### Concept: Curriculum Learning in Sequential Decision Making
- **Why needed here:** The two‑stage training is a form of curriculum that progressively increases task complexity; this principle underlies the reported performance gains.
- **Quick check:** Why might training on smaller systems first improve performance on larger, more complex ones?  
  *Answer:* It allows the agent to acquire basic tool‑use and construction skills in a stable, dense‑reward setting before facing the combinatorial complexity of large designs, reducing catastrophic forgetting and guiding exploration.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
User → Plan‑Execute module → LLM core (Qwen2.5-7B) → Python dictionary (sys_dict) → Validation testbed (Python) → Simulink simulation (if needed). Tools: search_blocks (RAG), symbolic‑math, parameter‑tuner. Training data: SimuBench + abstract‑reconstruct pairs. ReGRPO algorithm updates policy.

### Critical path
1. Receive user query.
2. (Optional) Plan generation: LLM outputs a plan or directly starts execution.
3. Iterative execution: LLM emits `<tool>` or `<python>` actions; tools modify `sys_dict`; Python validator checks signal types and wiring.
4. If validation fails, replan; else continue until `Finish`.
5. Convert `sys_dict` to Simulink commands; run simulation if required.
6. During training: collect rollouts, generate reflections for failures, update policy via ReGRPO loss.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Representation conciseness vs. fidelity:** Dropping visual/coordinate data saves tokens but may omit layout‑dependent constraints.
- **Curriculum simplicity vs. scalability:** Fixed block‑count thresholds are easy but may not capture all complexity dimensions.
- **Reflection overhead vs. benefit:** Reflection adds sampling cost; however, gains in sample efficiency outweigh compute, and overhead shrinks with task length.
- **On‑premise deployment vs. cloud scale:** Modest hardware requirements enable privacy and cost control but limit model size; 7B chosen as a sweet spot.

### Failure signatures
From failure analysis:
- **Topology/connection errors** (33.9%): mismatched port names, domain mismatches.
- **Block selection errors** (28.5%): wrong library paths, missing required blocks.
- **Parameter omissions** (17.7%): missing fields, misspellings.
- **Premature termination** (12.5%): halting after tool errors or mis‑judging completion.
- **Context length exceeded** (7.3%): long reasoning traces or large systems.

### First 3 experiments
1. **ReGRPO vs. GRPO convergence:** Train Qwen2.5‑7B on a fixed SimuBench subset (e.g., 1000 tasks), group size 8, plot training reward vs. steps. Expect faster ascent for ReGRPO.
2. **Two‑stage curriculum ablation:** Train three models: (a) only Stage 1, (b) only Stage 2, (c) full curriculum. Evaluate on full SimuBench test set. Full curriculum should outperform (51.89% vs. 46.55% and 44.38%).
3. **Abstract‑Reconstruct augmentation impact:** In Stage 2, train with and without the 200 augmented examples. Compare convergence curves and final accuracy; augmentation should raise plateau and improve robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can multimodal information (e.g., visual diagrams) be integrated into SimuAgent's workflow to improve model generation and verification?  
*Basis:* Future work states "Future work will explore richer multimodal grounding (e.g., incorporating visual information from Simulink diagrams)".  
*Why unresolved:* SimuAgent currently processes only textual/dictionary representations; visual layout and diagram features are ignored, which could resolve ambiguities in block placement and wiring.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Develop a multimodal encoder for Simulink diagrams, incorporate it into the agent, and measure improvements on layout‑sensitive tasks.

### Open Question 2
How can human feedback be incorporated into the reward model to better align SimuAgent's outputs with engineer preferences and domain‑specific quality criteria?  
*Basis:* Future work states "human-in-the-loop reward modeling".  
*Why unresolved:* Automated rewards miss subtle design aspects (readability, maintainability, style); human preferences could capture these but are not currently integrated.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Conduct user studies to collect preference rankings of model outputs; train a reward model from this data; evaluate improvements in perceived quality and task success.

### Open Question 3
To what extent can the SimuAgent framework (plan‑execute, ReGRPO, dictionary representation) be transferred to other graph‑based engineering environments (e.g., Modelica, PSCAD) without domain‑specific retraining, and what are the barriers to cross‑domain generalization?  
*Basis:* Future work: "extensions to broader graph‑based design environments beyond Simulink" and cross‑platform experiments showing improved accuracy after minimal fine‑tuning on Modelica/PSCAD.  
*Why unresolved:* Transfer was not zero‑shot; reasons for partial success and necessary adaptations are unclear. Generalization limits are not well understood.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic evaluation across multiple graph‑based environments, ablation of framework components, and analysis of representational commonalities to determine transferability factors.

### Open Question 4
What modifications to planning, representation, or training are needed to mitigate topology/connection errors and block selection errors, which together cause over 60% of SimuAgent failures?  
*Basis:* Failure analysis identifies these as top failure modes (33.9% and 28.5% respectively), but the paper does not propose targeted solutions.  
*Why unresolved:* Current training (two‑stage curriculum, ReGRPO) does not specifically address these error types; understanding root causes is necessary to design effective interventions.  
*What evidence would resolve it:* Detailed error categorization, design of specialized tools or losses (e.g., port consistency checker), and experiments measuring reduction in failure rates.

## Limitations
- The paper lacks crucial details on SimuBench train/test splits, the source of the 200 abstract‑reconstruct pairs, exact reward weights, and the adaptive reflection decay schedule, blocking faithful replication.
- The Python dictionary representation may omit critical Simulink constraints (e.g., port domains, solver settings), risking undetected errors.
- Cross‑platform evaluation is limited to two domains, and scalability to larger models or more complex systems remains unclear.

## Confidence
- **Token reduction & simulation speed:** High (quantified reduction ∼43k→2.7k, direct validation).
- **Two‑stage curriculum:** Medium (ablation shows gain, but stage thresholds and task distribution not fully described).
- **Reflection‑GRPO:** Medium (convergence advantage shown, but reflection quality and decay sensitivity unexamined).
- **Cross‑platform transfer:** Low (results reported with minimal fine‑tuning details; only Modelica and PSCAD tested).
