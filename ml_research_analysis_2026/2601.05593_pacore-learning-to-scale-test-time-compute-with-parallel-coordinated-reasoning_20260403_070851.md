# PaCoRe: Learning to Scale Test-Time Compute with Parallel Coordinated Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.05593  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05593  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: PaCoRe enables an 8B model to achieve 94.5% pass@1 on HMMT 2025, scaling effective test‑time compute to ~2 million tokens within a fixed context window.

## Executive Summary
PaCoRe tackles the limitation of fixed context windows that restrict test‑time compute (TTC) in sequential reasoning. It introduces parallel coordinated reasoning: the model iteratively launches multiple reasoning trajectories, compacts their conclusions into messages, and synthesizes these messages across rounds to guide further exploration—all while operating within a constant context size. Trained with outcome‑based reinforcement learning, the 8B PaCoRe model learns to combine diverse, possibly conflicting trajectories effectively. On the HMMT 2025 benchmark, it attains 94.5% pass@1, surpassing GPT‑5’s 93.2%, and demonstrates effective TTC scaling to approximately 2 million tokens. Additional improvements on LiveCodeBench (78.2%) and Apex (2.3%) further indicate the method’s ability to scale reasoning without expanding the context window.

## Method Summary
The core method consists of an iterative loop. First, a parallel reasoning engine generates multiple independent reasoning trajectories. After each round, the conclusions from these trajectories are fed into a message compaction module, which produces a concise summary (“message”). This message is then prepended to the context for the next round, and a synthesis controller uses it to coordinate the subsequent set of parallel trajectories. The entire cycle repeats until a stopping condition (e.g., token budget or confidence threshold) is met. The 8B model is fine‑tuned with outcome‑based reinforcement learning, using final answer correctness as reward to optimize the policy for generating useful compaction messages and guiding exploration. This design keeps total token usage constant across rounds, enabling unbounded reasoning steps within a fixed context window.

## Key Results
- Achieves 94.5% pass@1 on HMMT 2025, exceeding GPT‑5’s 93.2%.  
- Scales effective test‑time compute to ~2 million tokens while staying within a fixed context window.  
- Shows strong gains on LiveCodeBench (78.2% high) and Apex (2.3%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PaCoRe works because it decouples the number of reasoning steps from the context window. Parallel trajectories explore a broad hypothesis space simultaneously; compaction condenses each trajectory’s conclusion into a token‑efficient summary; and reinforcement learning teaches the model to synthesize these summaries into effective guidance for future rounds. The iterative feedback loop allows the model to refine its reasoning direction over many steps, effectively performing millions of “mental” computations without exceeding the context limit—hence the observed ~2 M token effective TTC scaling.

## Foundational Learning
- **Test‑Time Compute (TTC)**: Additional inference‑time computation to boost problem‑solving capability. *Why needed*: Model parameters alone may be insufficient for complex tasks; TTC provides scalable reasoning effort. *Quick check*: Look for intermediate reasoning tokens (e.g., chain‑of‑thought) before the final answer.
- **Parallel Reasoning Trajectories**: Multiple independent reasoning paths explored at once. *Why needed*: Increases hypothesis coverage and reduces exploration time compared to sequential search. *Quick check*: Count how many reasoning threads are launched per iteration.
- **Message Compaction**: Summarizes trajectory outcomes into shorter representations. *Why needed*: Preserves essential information while respecting the fixed context budget. *Quick check*: Compare the token length of original trajectories vs. their compacted messages.
- **Outcome‑Based Reinforcement Learning**: Training using rewards derived from final answer correctness. *Why needed*: Aligns the policy with the end goal, even when intermediate signals are ambiguous or conflicting. *Quick check*: Monitor reward‑correlated improvements in pass@1 during RL fine‑tuning.
- **Pass@1 Metric**: Accuracy on the first attempt without additional sampling. *Why needed*: Provides a clear, reproducible measure of reasoning quality. *Quick check*: Compute the fraction of correctly solved problems using a single generation per test case.
- **Fixed Context Window**: Maximum number of tokens the model can process at once. *Why needed*: Defines a hard limit on attended information; must be carefully managed during long‑running TTC. *Quick check*: Ensure total prompt+generated tokens never exceed the model’s context size.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Parallel Reasoning Engine → (trajectory conclusions) → Message Compaction Module → (compact messages) → Synthesis Controller → (guidance) → Parallel Reasoning Engine (next round). The Outcome‑Based RL Trainer updates the model parameters based on final rewards, influencing all components.

**Critical Path**  
1. Launch *N* parallel reasoning trajectories.  
2. Collect conclusions from each trajectory.  
3. Feed conclusions into the Compaction Module to produce a fixed‑size message.  
4. Prepend the message to the context and let the Synthesis Controller decide parameters for the next round.  
5. Repeat from step 1 until a stopping condition (e.g., token budget, answer confidence) is satisfied.  
6. Emit the final answer.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- *Compaction aggressiveness*: More aggressive summarization saves tokens but risks losing critical details; milder summarization preserves information but consumes more context.  
- *Number of parallel trajectories*: Larger *N* increases diversity and potential for correct solutions but raises computational cost and may amplify conflicting messages.  
- *RL training stability*: Sparse outcome rewards can lead to high variance; careful hyper‑parameter tuning is needed to balance exploration and policy convergence.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Accuracy drops when messages are over‑summarized (key steps omitted).  
- High run‑to‑run variance indicating unstable RL training.  
- Context‑overflow errors due to poor compaction or excessive rounds.  
- Conflicting messages causing the Synthesis Controller to oscillate or fail to converge on a coherent direction.

**First Experiments**  
1. **Ablation of Compaction Strategy**: Compare no compaction (full trajectories) vs. fixed‑length summarization vs. adaptive summarization; measure accuracy and token usage.  
2. **Vary Parallel Trajectory Count**: Test with 1, 4, 8, and 16 trajectories to identify the optimal trade‑off between performance and compute cost.  
3. **Cross‑Benchmark Transfer**: Apply the trained PaCoRe pipeline (without fine‑tuning) to other reasoning benchmarks such as MATH and BBH to evaluate generalization and any extra computational demands.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to a single primary benchmark (HMMT 2025) with no reported variance or statistical tests.  
- The compaction step is opaque; potential information loss and computational overhead are not quantified.  
- Training with outcome‑based RL lacks reproducibility details (e.g., hyperparameters, random seeds).

## Confidence
- HMMT 2025 performance (94.5% vs. GPT‑5 93.2%): **Medium** (modest margin, no independent verification).  
- Unbounded TTC scaling (~2 M tokens): **Low–Medium** (asserted without clear evidence that quality is maintained at scale).  
- Generalization to LiveCodeBench & Apex: **Medium** (positive results on two additional tasks, but gains are small and may not be robust).

## Next Checks
1. **Independent replication on HMMT 2025**: Re‑run PaCoRe (8B) on the official test set, compute pass@1 with bootstrap confidence intervals, and compare to GPT‑5 under identical compute and prompt conditions.  
2. **Ablation of message compaction**: Systematically vary the compaction method (e.g., fixed‑size summarization, no compaction) and measure accuracy vs. effective token usage to isolate the contribution and potential degradation.  
3. **Cross‑model and task transfer**: Apply the same PaCoRe pipeline to a larger model (e.g., 70B) and to at least two other reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MATH, BBH) to assess generalizability and report any additional computational costs.
