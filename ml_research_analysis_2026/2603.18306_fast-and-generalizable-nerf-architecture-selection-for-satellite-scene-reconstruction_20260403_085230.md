# Fast and Generalizable NeRF Architecture Selection for Satellite Scene Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.18306
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18306
- **Reference count:** 7
- **Primary result:** PreSCAN predicts NeRF architecture performance in under 30 seconds with <1 dB PSNR error, achieving a 1000× speedup over per-scene NAS, and enables hardware-aware selection that reduces edge deployment power by 26% and latency by 43% with only 0.79 dB quality loss.

## Executive Summary
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) are powerful for 3D scene reconstruction but require expensive per-scene architecture search when deployed on satellite imagery. This paper introduces PreSCAN, a framework that predicts the final reconstruction quality of a given NeRF architecture before training, using lightweight scene descriptors. Through SHAP analysis, the authors discover that multi-view consistency—not architecture—is the primary determinant of quality. PreSCAN’s linear predictor, trained on a large NAS dataset, achieves sub-1 dB PSNR prediction error in seconds. When combined with offline hardware cost profiling, it selects architectures that dramatically reduce edge device power and latency with minimal quality loss. The approach generalizes across diverse satellite scenes without retraining, offering a practical solution for scalable satellite NeRF deployment.

## Method Summary
PreSCAN operates in two phases. First, a large-scale NAS over 144 architectures across 64 satellite scenes yields a dataset of scene descriptors (geometric and photometric consistency metrics) and architecture parameters mapped to final PSNR. SHAP analysis on this dataset reveals that multi-view consistency features dominate performance prediction, leading the authors to train a simple linear regression model that predicts PSNR from concatenated scene and architecture features. At deployment time, for a new scene, its descriptors are extracted in seconds. For each candidate architecture, the predictor estimates PSNR. A hardware-aware selection then discards architectures below a quality threshold and picks the lowest-cost option from a pre-profiled table of inference latency and power on the target edge device. This decouples quality prediction from static cost profiling, avoiding costly hardware-in-the-loop training.

## Key Results
- PreSCAN predicts NeRF PSNR with error less than 1 dB in under 30 seconds, achieving a 1000× speedup over full NAS.
- The framework generalizes to unseen satellite scenes without retraining.
- In a Jetson Orin case study, hardware-aware selection reduces inference power by 26% and latency by 43% with only a 0.79 dB PSNR drop.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Multi-view consistency (geometric and photometric alignment across input views) is the primary determinant of final NeRF reconstruction quality for satellite scenes, outweighing the specific neural architecture chosen.
- **Mechanism:** SHAP analysis on a large dataset of scene-architecture pairs quantifies the contribution of each input feature to the predicted PSNR. Features derived from inter-view consistency (inverse PSNR, photometric variance, cosine similarity) consistently exhibit the highest SHAP values, indicating they are the strongest predictors of performance. The model implicitly learns that scenes with high initial view consistency require less architectural capacity to achieve good results, while inconsistent scenes are fundamentally limited regardless of architecture.
- **Core assumption:** The relationship between measurable, pre-training scene-level descriptors and final reconstruction quality is stable and generalizable across different architectural search spaces and geographic regions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Through SHAP analysis, the authors find that multi-view consistency, not model architecture, is the primary determinant of reconstruction quality."
  - [section 3.2] Figures 2a, 2b, 2c and accompanying text show SHAP value analysis across JAX, OMA, and combined datasets, where inverse PSNR (w'), photometric variance (Var(Ii)), and cosine similarity (cos sim) are consistently top-ranked.
  - [corpus] Neighboring papers (e.g., G3R, SAT-NGP) focus on architectural/algorithmic NeRF improvements but do not challenge the primacy of architecture; this work provides a specific, quantified counter-finding for the satellite domain.
- **Break condition:** If a new satellite scene type exhibits fundamentally different degradation modes (e.g., severe atmospheric turbulence not captured by the defined descriptors) where architecture *does* become the primary bottleneck, the model's predictions would fail. The SHAP finding is conditional on the dataset and feature set used.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A lightweight, linear regression model trained on a combination of architectural hyperparameters and the identified scene descriptors can predict the final PSNR of a fully-trained NeRF with error < 1 dB, before any training begins.
- **Mechanism:** The prediction model (Eq. 10) is a simple parametric function (`PSNR_pred = β^T X`). Its efficacy hinges on the hypothesis that the complex, non-linear interaction between scene properties and architecture on final quality manifests as an approximately linear relationship in the carefully constructed feature space (scene descriptors + architecture params). The model learns weights (β) that approximate this mapping from a NAS-generated dataset of 3,650 scene-architecture pairs.
- **Core assumption:** The error surface between pre-training descriptors and final PSNR is smooth enough to be captured by a linear model in the chosen feature representation, avoiding overfitting despite the simplicity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] "We deliberately choose a parametric shallow performance estimator... for three reasons: (1) our SHAP analysis reveals approximately linear relationships... (2) linear models provide direct interpretability... (3) they generalize well under limited training data without overfitting."
  - [section 4.3 & Table 3] Empirical results show MAE (∆PSNR) between 0.07 dB and 0.89 dB across 9 test scenes, with most <0.5 dB, validating the claim.
  - [section 4.5 & Table 4] Sparse training (45 scenes) results still maintain < 1 dB error for most unseen scenes, supporting the generalization assumption.
- **Break condition:** A significant distribution shift in scene characteristics (e.g., moving from optical satellite to different sensor modalities like SAR) where the defined scene descriptors are no longer predictive of NeRF optimization difficulty would cause the linear model to fail. Retraining or new descriptors would be needed.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** PreSCAN's quality predictions can be fused with static, offline hardware cost profiles to perform hardware-aware architecture selection, yielding significant reductions in inference power (26%) and latency (43%) on edge platforms with only a minor, acceptable drop in quality (0.79 dB PSNR).
- **Mechanism:** The mechanism decouples two orthogonal optimization problems: (1) *What is the best possible quality for this scene given an architecture?* (answered by PreSCAN's scene-aware prediction), and (2) *What are the fixed deployment costs (latency, power) for a given architecture on a given device?* (answered by offline profiling). The selection heuristic is a simple filter-and-optimize: discard architectures below a quality threshold (`PSNR_min`), then pick the lowest-cost architecture from the remainder. This bypasses the need for costly hardware-in-the-loop training.
- **Core assumption:** Deployment cost is a deterministic function of architecture and hardware (not scene), and the quality threshold can be relaxed without catastrophic loss of utility for the downstream application.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.1] "Deployment costs... are primarily driven by architectural parameters... This independence allows us to decouple cost estimation from scene-specific quality prediction."
  - [section 5.3 & Table 6] Empirical case study on Jetson Orin quantifies the trade-off: 26% power reduction (render), 43% time reduction (render), for a 0.79 dB PSNR difference.
  - [abstract] "...combining PreSCAN’s predictions with offline cost profiling reduces inference power by 26% and latency by 43% with only a 0.79 dB quality loss."
- **Break condition:** If deployment costs become scene-dependent (e.g., due to dynamic memory allocation tied to scene complexity in an unexpected way) or if the application has a hard, non-negotiable quality floor that the 0.79 dB drop violates, this selection strategy would be invalid.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Scene Descriptor Extractor -> Architecture Parameter Encoder -> PreSCAN Predictor -> Selection Logic (with Hardware Cost Profiler lookup)

**Critical path for a new scene:**
1. Extract scene descriptors from the raw satellite image set and camera metadata.
2. For each candidate architecture in the search space, invoke the PreSCAN predictor to get `PSNR_pred`.
3. Apply the hardware-aware selection logic using the offline cost table.
4. Output the selected architecture; proceed to full training.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Descriptor Complexity vs. Prediction Speed:** The chosen descriptors (pair-based calculations) are lightweight but may miss higher-order scene statistics. Using deep semantic features (like from a CNN) could improve accuracy but violate the "<30 sec" and interpretability goals.
- **Linear Model vs. Non-linear:** The linear model (Eq. 10) is fast, interpretable, and works well with limited data. A more complex model (e.g., GBDT) *might* squeeze out marginally better accuracy but risks overfitting and loses the direct causal link from SHAP.
- **Search Space Coverage:** PreSCAN's training data came from 144 S-NeRF/SatNeRF configs. Applying it to a vastly different architecture family (e.g., 3DGS-based models) or very different hyperparameter ranges requires retraining or risks extrapolation error.

**Failure signatures:**
- **High prediction error (>1.5 dB) for a scene:** The scene likely has characteristics outside the training distribution (e.g., extreme shadowing, poor initial view coverage). The `MAE` metric from Table 3 is the key monitor.
- **Inconsistent ranking across architectures:** If for two scenes, A and B, the predicted PSNR ranking of architectures differs wildly from the *actual* ranking obtained from NAS, PreSCAN's model of the arch-scene interaction is flawed.
- **Hardware-aware selection yields >1.5 dB drop:** The `PSNR_min` threshold is too aggressive for the application, or the cost-quality trade-off curve (Figure 5e) is steeper than observed in the validation set.

**First 3 experiments for validation:**
1. **Baseline Replication:** Run the full NAS pipeline (TPE search over 144 architectures) on 2-3 held-out satellite scenes. Compare the architecture selected by PreSCAN + cost-profiling against the "Best Accuracy" architecture chosen by NAS. Measure the actual PSNR, training time, and final hardware cost difference to verify the predicted ~0.8 dB loss and cost savings.
2. **Sparse Data Generalization Test:** Retrain the PreSCAN predictor using only 25-30 scenes from the original 64. Evaluate prediction MAE on the remaining 9 test scenes. This tests robustness to limited training data, simulating deployment to a new geographic region with few pre-probe scenes.
3. **Cross-Model Invariance Test:** As in section 4.6, include a second NeRF variant (e.g., SatNeRF) in the NAS training database. Train a single PreSCAN model on the mixed data (S-NeRF + SatNeRF results). Evaluate whether it can accurately predict PSNR for *both* model families on unseen scenes with similar error (<1 dB), confirming the scene-driven rather than model-driven selection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization to other NeRF architectures:** Does PreSCAN generalize to architectures beyond S-NeRF and SatNeRF (e.g., MVSNeRF, Transformer-based NeRFs)? The paper only tested two families; evaluation on at least three other major families is needed.
2. **Robustness to diverse satellite sensors and conditions:** How does PreSCAN perform on imagery from different sensors (e.g., SpaceNet, NAIP) and under extreme conditions (seasonal changes, cloud cover)? The current study used only DFC2019; testing on additional datasets is required.
3. **Causal mechanism of multi-view consistency dominance:** Why do consistency metrics dominate? Is it due to better geometric initialization, photometric supervision, or both? Controlled ablations or gradient analysis could isolate the cause.
4. **Non-linear predictor for extreme scenes:** The linear model shows higher errors on hard scenes (e.g., OMA-244). Could a shallow non-linear predictor improve worst-case accuracy while remaining fast? Comparisons on hard scenes are needed.
5. **Sensitivity of cost profiles to dynamic runtime conditions:** The hardware cost profiles are static; real edge devices face thermal throttling and variable power. Profiling under varying conditions and developing runtime adaptation would validate robustness.

## Limitations
- SHAP-based conclusion is limited to the studied datasets; may not hold for scenes with unusual degradation (e.g., SAR, heavy haze).
- The linear predictor’s ≈1 dB error assumption might fail under distribution shifts or for architectures outside the S-NeRF/SatNeRF family.
- Hardware cost independence from scene content is assumed; scene-dependent resource usage could break selection in practice.

## Confidence
- *Multi-view consistency primacy:* Medium (dataset-specific SHAP support).
- *PreSCAN prediction accuracy (<1 dB):* Medium (empirical but relies on linear model).
- *Hardware-aware selection benefits:* Medium (validated on a single edge device).
- *Cross-scene generalization without retraining:* Medium (tested on nine scenes only).
- *1000× speedup over NAS:* High (direct computational comparison).
