# KEPo: Knowledge Evolution Poison on Graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11501  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11501  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** KEPo achieves state-of-the-art attack success rates (up to 73.2% ASR on HippoRAG 2) by forging temporally coherent knowledge evolution paths that integrate seamlessly into GraphRAG knowledge graphs.

## Executive Summary
Graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GraphRAG) enhances question answering by constructing knowledge graphs from textual corpora. However, these systems are vulnerable to poisoning attacks where malicious texts are injected to manipulate outputs. Existing RAG poisoning methods fail in GraphRAG because injected facts often conflict with the graph structure and exhibit high perplexity, making them detectable and poorly integrated. KEPo addresses this by generating poisoned texts that form temporally coherent evolution paths from original facts to a toxic target, thereby reducing conditional perplexity and ensuring seamless integration into the knowledge graph. For multi-target attacks, KEPo links poisoned subgraphs via fabricated relations between critical nodes, amplifying toxic communities. Extensive experiments across multiple GraphRAG frameworks and datasets show KEPo significantly outperforms prior methods, achieving up to 73.2% attack success rate, and remains effective under basic defenses, revealing critical security gaps in GraphRAG deployments.

## Method Summary
The KEPo attack begins when the attacker probes the target GraphRAG system to obtain a query, its correct answer, and an inferred temporal anchor. Using a Fabricator LLM, the attacker generates a poisoned corpus that weaves a narrative: a precursor fact, an evolution path linking it to the original fact, the original fact, a second evolution path to the poisoned fact, and finally the poisoned fact containing the target answer. This structure presents the poisoned information as a logical, updated conclusion, lowering its conditional perplexity relative to the original knowledge. For multi-target attacks, the attacker computes semantic similarity between target answers, selects highly similar pairs, and fabricates relations between the highest-degree nodes of their respective poisoned subgraphs, creating a larger, interconnected toxic community. The poisoned corpus is injected into the source database, which the target GraphRAG crawls to rebuild its knowledge graph. During retrieval, the poisoned subgraph is extracted and fed to the generator LLM, which produces the target answer.

## Key Results
- KEPo achieves state-of-the-art attack success rates across GraphRAG frameworks, with up to 73.2% ASR on HippoRAG 2.
- The attack significantly outperforms prior RAG poisoning methods by integrating poisoned facts as temporally coherent continuations rather than conflicting statements.
- KEPo remains effective under basic defenses (e.g., query paraphrasing, instruction ignoring), demonstrating robustness and highlighting severe vulnerabilities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Conditional Perplexity Reduction
KEPo reduces the conditional perplexity of poisoned injections by constructing temporally coherent evolution paths that bridge verified original facts to adversarial targets. By framing the poisoned fact as a logical update (e.g., "Earlier research suggested X; new study shows Z"), the intermediate tokens become more predictable when conditioned on the original context, lowering the surprise metric defined in Equation (7). This reduction allows the text to blend naturally with the existing corpus, avoiding detection as anomalous and increasing its retention and retrieval ranking. The core assumption is that GraphRAG's knowledge extraction and LLM reasoning are sensitive to statistical coherence; lower perplexity leads to higher trust. Evidence includes the paper's theoretical derivation and experimental results showing higher retrieval ranks for KEPo-injected facts.

### Mechanism 2: Seamless Knowledge Graph Integration
KEPo ensures poisoned facts merge seamlessly into the knowledge graph by aligning them semantically and temporally with existing nodes. The forged evolution path mimics real-world knowledge progression, placing the poisoned fact at a later temporal anchor so it appears as the latest state. This structure allows the KG extraction process—which parses entities and relations—to treat the injected triples as valid, connecting them to original entities and forming a coherent subgraph. The core assumption is that GraphRAG's extractor relies on surface-level coherence and temporal cues without verifying factual consistency. Evidence is found in the paper's description of KG fusion and the abstract's claim that poisoned knowledge merges seamlessly.

### Mechanism 3: Multi-Target Community Amplification
For multi-target attacks, KEPo links individual poisoned subgraphs by fabricating relations between their highest-degree centrality nodes, creating a larger, mutually reinforcing toxic community. Since GraphRAG retrieval (especially global search) prioritizes larger, better-connected communities, this artificial linkage boosts the retrieval weight of poisoned facts. The attacker selects target answers with high semantic similarity to ensure coherent fabricated links. The core assumption is that community size and connectivity directly influence retrieval ranking. Evidence includes the abstract's mention of expanding poisoned communities and experiments showing KEPo-Multi outperforms KEPo-Single.

## Foundational Learning

**Conditional Perplexity (C-PPL)**  
Why needed here: KEPo's core lever is reducing the predictability gap between new poisoned facts and the existing knowledge base. Understanding C-PPL as \( p(y|x)^{-1/|y|} \) shows why a logically evolving narrative is less surprising than a standalone contradiction.  
Quick check question: How does adding an intermediate "evolution path" sentence between a known fact and a poisoned fact lower the conditional perplexity of the poisoned fact?

**Knowledge Graph Community Detection & Retrieval**  
Why needed here: GraphRAG's vulnerability stems from partitioning the KG into semantic communities. Attack success depends on poisoned triples being assigned to a retrieved community. Understanding community size, connectivity, and summary-based ranking is essential.  
Quick check question: Why might a poisoned triple that forms an isolated node in the KG have lower attack success than one that connects to a large, pre-existing community?

**Information Fusion vs. Conflict**  
Why needed here: KEPo shifts from conflicting with existing knowledge to fusing with it via evolution narratives. Fusion reduces model uncertainty, while conflict triggers internal knowledge or verification.  
Quick check question: In an LLM-based generator, what is the likely behavioral difference between retrieving "X causes Y" followed by "New study: X actually prevents Y" (conflict) versus "X was thought to cause Y, but new evidence suggests X prevents Y" (evolution)?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Attacker -> Fabricator LLM -> Poisoned Database -> Target GraphRAG System (KG Builder -> Community Partitioner -> Retriever -> Generator LLM).  
Note: Attacker initially probes Target GraphRAG to obtain (query, correct answer).

**Critical path:**  
1. Attacker obtains (q, a) from target GraphRAG via query interface.  
2. Extract anchor fact f_t and time t from a.  
3. Fabricator LLM generates poisoned corpus d: f*_t-Δt, L(f*_t-Δt, f_t), f_t, L(f_t, f*_t+Δt), f*_t+Δt.  
4. Inject d into the source database crawler's path.  
5. Target GraphRAG rebuilds KG from poisoned database; d's triples are extracted, forming/joining a community.  
6. On query q, retriever fetches a community/subgraph containing d.  
7. Generator LLM, conditioned on retrieved context, outputs target answer a*.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Text length vs. Perplexity:** Longer evolution paths lower C-PPL but increase token cost and incoherence risk; optimal ~100 words.  
- **Multi-target linkage (k):** Higher k expands toxic community but risks including semantically dissimilar pairs, raising perplexity; peak at n=5-6.  
- **Fabricator strength:** More capable LLMs (GPT-4o) produce more coherent paths, but even smaller models (Qwen3-14B) work.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **High C-PPL of injected text:** indicates poor integration; compute perplexity against clean corpus.  
- **Isolated subgraph in KG:** poisoned triples disconnected from original nodes, low retrieval probability.  
- **Low community connectivity:** in multi-target, fabricated relations sparse or critical nodes low degree.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Single vs. Multi-target comparison:** Run KEPo-Single and KEPo-Multi on a fixed dataset (e.g., Graph-Story with LightRAG local search); expect higher ASR for Multi.  
2. **Text length sensitivity test:** For fixed query, generate poisoned corpora with lengths 20–160 words in increments; plot ASR vs. length; expect rise then plateau.  
3. **Fabricator LLM ablation:** Use GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Qwen3-14B as Fabricator with LightRAG global search; measure ASR difference; expect performance drop with weaker models.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **What novel defense mechanisms can effectively detect or mitigate KEPo attacks?** The paper only evaluates basic defenses and calls for graph-aware or temporally aware strategies that analyze temporal coherence or source reliability.
2. **How do intrinsic knowledge graph properties influence KEPo's success?** The attack's dependency on graph topology is discussed but not quantified; controlled experiments with varied topological metrics are needed.
3. **In multi-target attacks with dissimilar targets, how effective is KEPo's similarity-based linking?** The method assumes semantic coherence among targets; effectiveness for opposing answers is unexplored.
4. **What is the minimum required capability of the Fabricator LLM?** The paper compares off-the-shelf LLMs but does not isolate critical skills like temporal reasoning; a wider range of models and error analysis is needed.
5. **How well does KEPo generalize to domains with different knowledge structures?** Experiments are limited to narrative and clinical datasets; applicability to legal or procedural domains is unknown.

## Limitations
- **Generalizability across GraphRAG implementations:** Results are tied to specific frameworks (Nano-GraphRAG, LightRAG, HippoRAG 2); vulnerability may not hold for all KG builders with built-in fact consistency checks.
- **Conditional Perplexity as a proxy:** C-PPL is computed with the Fabricator's LLM, which may not match the Target GraphRAG's Generator distribution, making it an imperfect integration predictor.
- **Black-box assumption realism:** Requires precise extraction of f_t and t from the target's answer; extraction errors could break the attack pipeline.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (C-PPL reduction):** Medium – Strong theoretical derivation and indirect retrieval evidence, but lacks direct validation that lowered perplexity *caused* integration.
- **Mechanism 2 (Seamless KG integration):** Low-Medium – Relies on assumption that extractors are fooled by temporal narratives; no controlled experiment isolating temporal coherence as the sole factor.
- **Mechanism 3 (Multi-target community growth):** Medium – Experimental tables show ASR improvement, but claimed critical-node linkage mechanism isn't directly measured (e.g., community size metrics pre/post linking absent).

## Next Checks
1. **Direct C-PPL Causation Test:** Vary only evolution path coherence while keeping target fact constant; measure KG integration metrics (node degree, retrieval rank) to confirm correlation with low C-PPL.
2. **KG Extractors Sanity Check:** Feed canonical evolution narratives to the actual KG builder used in the target GraphRAG framework; audit if expected triples are extracted without rejection.
3. **Multi-Target Linkage Isolation:** Compare ASR for KEPo-Multi with linkage step removed versus added for fixed targets; explicitly measure community size (nodes/edges) before and after linkage to isolate amplification effect.
