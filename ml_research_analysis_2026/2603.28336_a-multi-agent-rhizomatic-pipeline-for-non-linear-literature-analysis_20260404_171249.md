# A Multi-Agent Rhizomatic Pipeline for Non-Linear Literature Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.28336
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28336
- **Reference count:** 3
- **Primary result:** The system surfaces cross-disciplinary convergences and structural gaps missed by traditional systematic reviews, with Rupture Protocol activating in 30–40% of analyses, revealing frequent paradigm centralization, and semantic topography identifying orthogonal isolations and voids.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a computational pipeline that operationalizes Deleuzian rhizome principles for non-linear literature analysis. Conventional systematic reviews are argued to suppress lateral connections and emergent patterns; the proposed system uses 12 LLM agents across seven phases to enact connection, heterogeneity, multiplicity, asignifying rupture, and cartography. Dual-source ingestion from OpenAlex and arXiv feeds into parallel processing via orthogonal theoretical lenses generated by an Epistemology Agent. A Resonance Detector and Rupture Protocol actively counter paradigm centralization by re-entering heterodox literature when edge density exceeds 40%. The final phase produces a semantic topography using SciBERT embeddings, revealing voids and orthogonal isolations. Preliminary results indicate the system uncovers cross-disciplinary convergences and gaps missed by traditional methods, with the Rupture Protocol activating in 30–40% of cases.

## Method Summary
The task is to conduct non-linear literature analysis on a user-provided “phenomenon zone.” Inputs are academic metadata and abstracts from OpenAlex and arXiv APIs. The method is a seven-phase pipeline with 12 specialized agents orchestrated via server-sent events (SSE). Phase 1: Epistemology Agent generates 3–5 orthogonal theoretical lenses. Phase 2: Concurrent ingestion, DOI deduplication (trigram Dice ≥0.85), ABS journal ranking, citation shadow mapping. Phase 3: Parallel lens agents asynchronously scan the corpus. Phase 4: Resonance Detector flags cross-lens convergent anomalies; Rupture Protocol triggers heterodox re-entry if edge concentration >40%. Phase 5: Assemblage Builder synthesizes present‑continuous narrative; Rhizome Builder classifies edges. Phase 6: Unified cartography with metadata. Phase 7: SciBERT embeddings → UMAP → HDBSCAN to identify semantic clusters, voids, and orthogonal isolations. LLMs (Mistral via Ollama) serve as the agent brains; layout details (prompts, exact parameters) remain unreported.

## Key Results
- The system surfaces cross‑disciplinary convergences and structural gaps missed by traditional systematic reviews.
- The Rupture Protocol activates in 30–40% of analyses, revealing frequent paradigm centralization.
- Semantic topography identifies orthogonal isolations and voids in the literature landscape.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Orthogonal Lens Generation
- **Claim:** The system prevents paradigm capture and ensures multi‑paradigmatic analysis from the outset by generating orthogonal theoretical lenses.
- **Mechanism:** The Epistemology Agent (Phase 1) processes a “phenomenon zone” input to produce 3–5 deliberately incommensurable theoretical frameworks (e.g., “Algorithmic Governmentality,” “Post‑Human Affect”), which then operate as independent parallel agents in Phase 3.
- **Core assumption:** A single researcher‑introduced conceptual lens structurally limits discovery; pre‑emptively installing heterogeneity via algorithmic lens generation surfaces connections a unified perspective would miss.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: “operationalizes the six principles of the rhizome—connection, heterogeneity, multiplicity… Epistemology Agent generates 3–5 orthogonal theoretical lenses…”
  - Section (Page 5): “Phase 1: Ontological Destabilization… The Epistemology Agent receives a ‘phenomenon zone’… and generates 3–5 orthogonal theoretical lenses… ensuring that the corpus is never read through a single dominant paradigm.”
  - Corpus: Neighboring papers describe multi‑agent SLR systems but do not address how initial frames are generated or diversified; this mechanism is a novel contribution.
- **Break condition:** If the Epistemology Agent’s generated lenses are semantically proximal (e.g., all from adjacent sub‑fields of economics) rather than orthogonal, the core assumption fails and the system reverts to a form of multi‑perspective arborescence.

### Mechanism 2: Dynamic Rupture Protocol
- **Claim:** The system computationally enforces the rhizomatic principle of “asignifying rupture” to actively counter emergent paradigm centralization during analysis.
- **Mechanism:** The Resonance Detector and Rupture Protocol (Phase 4) continuously monitor the density distribution of the emerging knowledge graph. When edge concentration exceeds a heuristic threshold (>40% on a few nodes), the system triggers a forced “re‑entry from the outside,” automatically fetching literature from pre‑defined heterodox, non‑mainstream sources to re‑diversify the network.
- **Core assumption:** Left to their own convergence logic, multi‑agent LLM analyses will exhibit a “rich‑get‑richer” effect, amplifying canonical literature and suppressing marginal but generative connections; a formal, external intervention is required to simulate the researcher’s deliberate pursuit of heterodoxy.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: “dynamic rupture detection protocols… The Rupture Protocol activates in 30–40% of analyses, revealing frequent paradigm centralization…”
  - Section (Page 6): “Rupture Protocol monitors the emerging knowledge graph for centralization risk: if a small number of nodes accumulate disproportionate edge density (>40%), the system triggers a ‘re‑entry from the outside,’ automatically fetching and integrating literature from heterodox traditions…”
  - Corpus: Related work on multi‑agent SLR automation does not mention a dynamic, threshold‑based intervention to force heterodox re‑entry during synthesis.
- **Break condition:** The 40% threshold is a heuristic. If centralization risk is systematically misidentified (false negatives), paradigm capture proceeds unchecked. If false positives are frequent, the system introduces irrelevant noise, disrupting coherent assemblage building.

### Mechanism 3: Semantic Topography of Voids and Isolations
- **Claim:** The system operationalizes “cartography” by generating a material map of semantic voids and orthogonal isolations, revealing structural gaps invisible to citation or keyword‑based analysis.
- **Mechanism:** In the final phase, SciBERT embeddings of the corpus are reduced via UMAP and clustered with HDBSCAN. The algorithm identifies (a) semantic voids (low‑density regions between clusters) and (b) orthogonal isolations (clusters with shared vocabulary but high inter‑cluster distance in the embedding space). A marginalization index flags peripheral papers.
- **Core assumption:** The high‑dimensional semantic space of scientific text, when topographically mapped, reveals “missing” research territories (voids) and disciplinary silos (orthogonal isolations) that constitute the most generative sites for rhizomatic inquiry.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: “Semantic topography identifies orthogonal isolations and voids.”
  - Section (Page 7): “Phase 7: Semantic Topography… identifies: (a) semantic clusters… (b) semantic voids—strategic gaps between clusters where literature is absent; and (c) orthogonal isolations—clusters sharing vocabulary but inhabiting separate semantic spaces…”
  - Corpus: Nearby papers focus on review automation (screening, writing); none describe a final phase explicitly designed to compute topographical absences (voids) in the semantic landscape.
- **Break condition:** If the embedding model (SciBERT) is biased toward certain disciplines or publication types, the resulting topography misrepresents voids and isolations. Voids could also be artifacts of limited corpus size or source domain (OpenAlex/arXiv) rather than true research gaps.

## Foundational Learning

### Deleuzian Rhizome Principles
- **Why needed:** These are the ontological axioms the system claims to operationalize (connection, heterogeneity, multiplicity, asignifying rupture, cartography, decalcomania). Understanding the distinction between an arborescent (tree) model and a rhizomatic (non‑hierarchical, acentric) model is essential to grasp why the system’s architecture (e.g., parallel lenses, rupture protocol) is designed contrary to traditional systematic review logic.
- **Quick check:** How does generating multiple, orthogonal theoretical lenses at the start (Phase 1) differ methodologically from conducting a sensitivity analysis with different keyword sets in a traditional systematic review?

### Process‑Relational Ontology
- **Why needed:** This is the philosophical foundation that makes the rhizomatic method “productive” rather than merely abstract. It underpins the system’s focus on relations and becoming (e.g., “assemblages,” “present‑continuous tense” synthesis) over static entities and classifications.
- **Quick check:** Why is writing synthesis in the “present‑continuous tense” (Phase 5) a specific implementation of a process‑relational worldview, and how does this differ from the typical past‑tense summary of a systematic review?

### Multi‑Agent System Design with LLM Orchestration
- **Why needed:** The system’s novelty lies in its translation of philosophical principles into a 12‑agent, seven‑phase computational pipeline. Key technical concepts include agent specialization, asynchronous concurrent processing (`asyncio`), server‑sent events (SSE) for orchestration, and the role of LLMs as “sense‑making” agents rather than just text generators.
- **Quick check:** What is the functional role of the “Resonance Detector” agent, and how does its output differ from simply aggregating the keyword hits from all parallel lens‑agents?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** The pipeline consists of seven sequential phases orchestrated via SSE streaming. Agents: Epistemology (P1), Deduplication/ABS Evaluator/Citation Mapper (P2), Parallel Lens Agents (P3), Resonance Detector & Rupture Protocol (P4), Assemblage/Rhizome Builder (P5), Cartography Output (P6), SciBERT/UMAP/HDBSCAN pipeline (P7). Dataflows: central trajectory graph updated across phases; corpus metadata persists; lens‑specific signals feed resonance calculation.
- **Critical path:** 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7. Sequential with massive intra‑phase parallelism (Phase 3). The Rupture Protocol (P4) can trigger a loop back to ingestion/fetching, creating the only non‑linear path.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *LLM Agent Consistency vs. Innovation:* Using autonomous LLM agents per lens increases discovery potential but risks inconsistent depth and hallucinated “signals.” Mitigated by prompt engineering and metadata logging (P6).
  - *Source Domain Specificity:* Ingestion is limited to OpenAlex (academic metadata) and arXiv (preprints). This provides structured data but excludes non‑academic sources (blogs, policy) used in manual methods, trading breadth for API reliability.
  - *Heuristic Thresholds:* The >40% centralization threshold (P4) and the Dice coefficient ≥0.85 for deduplication (P2) are not derived from theoretical first principles but from empirical tuning, creating a “settings” burden for the user.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Rupture Protocol Inertia:** The >40% threshold is never exceeded across multiple runs on a phenomenon zone, suggesting either a highly decentralized literature or, more likely, that the edge‑density metric is not sensitive to the type of “paradigm capture” the lens‑agents produce.
  - **Semantic Voids as Corpus Artifacts:** Phase 7 identifies voids that, upon manual inspection, are simply due to the absence of a key journal or conference in the OpenAlex query results, not a genuine research gap.
  - **Cartographic Output Incoherence:** The unified trajectory (P6) shows low confidence scores across most agents and high latency, indicating the LLM agents are failing to extract meaningful, lens‑specific signals from abstracts, defaulting to generic summaries.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Replication:** Run the pipeline on a phenomenon zone from a published systematic review. Compare the final cartography (edges, voids) and the Rupture Protocol activation against the review’s explicit inclusion/exclusion logic and identified gaps. Does the system surface connections/gaps the review missed?
  2. **Threshold Sensitivity Analysis:** Systematically vary the centralization threshold (P4) from 20% to 60% across multiple runs on the same corpus. Quantify the change in (a) number of “heterodox re‑entry” events, (b) shift in the semantic topography (P7), and (c) human evaluation of the synthesis’s “rhizomatic” quality.
  3. **Lens‑Input Stress Test:** Manually corrupt the Epistemology Agent’s output (P1) by forcing 3–5 lenses that are explicitly from the same theoretical tradition (e.g., three different strands of institutional economics). Run the full pipeline. The hypothesis: Resonance detection (P4) will be poor (few convergent anomalies), and the Rupture Protocol will fire more frequently to inject heterogeneity, demonstrating its compensatory function.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **How does the transition from metadata/abstract‑only ingestion to full‑text analysis alter the depth and accuracy of rhizomatic pattern detection, particularly for semantic voids and edge classification?**  
   The pipeline currently ingests metadata and abstracts rather than full texts, constraining the depth of analysis. Comparative deployments using both abstract‑only and full‑text ingestion are needed to measure changes in identified clusters, voids, connective edge density, and alignment with expert‑identified key insights.

2. **What is the empirically optimal centralization threshold (the 40% heuristic) for the Rupture Protocol to balance sensitivity to paradigm capture with computational efficiency across different research landscapes?**  
   The 40% trigger is a fixed heuristic; its effectiveness likely varies with domain size, heterogeneity, and citation network structure. A sensitivity analysis correlating different threshold settings (e.g., 30%, 40%, 50%) with qualitative assessments of whether “heterodox re‑entry” yielded genuinely novel, relevant literature versus noise would resolve it.

3. **What is the level of agreement between the pipeline’s automated classification of inter‑paper relationships (constructive, critical, rhizomatic) and human expert coders, and what are the primary sources of disagreement?**  
   The classification scheme is central to the cartographic output, but its validity rests on LLM interpretation. A multi‑rater study where a stratified sample of paper pairs from multiple phenomenon zones is coded independently by the pipeline and by at least two domain experts; analysis would compute inter‑rater reliability (e.g., Krippendorff’s alpha) and qualitatively analyze discordant cases.

4. **To what extent is the pipeline’s architecture (e.g., Epistemology Agent lens generation, Rupture Protocol effectiveness) generalizable across profoundly different academic domains, such as moving from socio‑technical fields (energy‑information) to pure mathematics or literary studies?**  
   The system has been tested primarily in the energy‑information nexus domain; transferability to other fields remains to be demonstrated. Replication studies in at least three cognitively distant fields (e.g., a formal science, a humanities discipline, an applied field) would provide evidence: (1) Epistemology Agent output rated by field experts for orthogonality and relevance, (2) Rupture Protocol activation rates and nature of heterodox re‑entry material, (3) comparative qualitative assessment by experts of whether the final cartography captures domain‑specific latent structures.

5. **How do inherent biases and hallucination tendencies in the underlying LLMs (e.g., Mistral) specifically distort the rhizomatic analysis, particularly in the generation of theoretical lenses, the detection of cross‑paradigm convergences, and the identification of marginalized/heterodox work?**  
   The LLM‑based agents inherit the biases and hallucination risks of the underlying language models. A multi‑model audit—running the full pipeline on the same corpus using different LLMs (open‑weight vs. proprietary, different sizes)—and comparing outputs would resolve it. Specific tests: audit of lens generation for stereotypical frames, manual verification of a sample of “rhizomatic” edges for factual grounding, analysis of citation shadow and heterodox re‑entry sources for systematic over/under‑representation.

## Limitations
- Exact LLM prompts and settings are unreported, risking inconsistent operationalization of “orthogonal lenses” and “rhizomatic edge classification.”
- Key algorithms (e.g., citation shadow mapping, marginalization index) are undefined, hindering replication of Phase 2 and Phase 7 outputs.
- Source domain bias (OpenAlex/arXiv only) may produce “voids” that are corpus artifacts, not true research gaps.
- Heuristic thresholds (>40% centralization, Dice ≥0.85) lack theoretical derivation; their generality across domains is unknown.
