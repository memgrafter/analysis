# S-PRESSO: Ultra Low Bitrate Sound Effect Compression With Diffusion Autoencoders And Offline Quantization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.15082
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15082
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Achieves 0.096 kbps at 1 Hz frame rate (750x compression) with superior objective and subjective quality compared to baselines.

## Executive Summary
S-PRESSO tackles the challenge of maintaining audio quality in neural sound effect compression at ultra-low bitrates, specifically for high-resolution (48 kHz) inputs. It combines a diffusion autoencoder architecture with offline neural quantization, leveraging a pretrained latent diffusion model (Stable Audio) as the decoder. The method involves a three-stage training process: first training a continuous diffusion autoencoder, then performing offline k-means quantization of the latent vectors, and finally fine-tuning the diffusion decoder on these quantized representations. This approach yields an extremely low bitrate of 0.096 kbps at a 1 Hz frame rate, representing a 750x compression ratio. Evaluations show that S-PRESSO outperforms both continuous (e.g., Stable Audio, Music2Latent) and discrete (e.g., SemantiCodec) baselines across metrics such as lower FAD and KAD, higher CLAP audio similarity, and better Si-SDR. Subjective MUSHRA tests further confirm its perceptual superiority and acoustic similarity at comparable bitrates.

## Method Summary
S-PRESSO employs a diffusion autoencoder where an encoder maps input audio to continuous latent vectors, and a decoder based on a pretrained latent diffusion model (Stable Audio) reconstructs audio from these latents. The training proceeds in three steps: (i) Train the continuous diffusion autoencoder end-to-end to optimize reconstruction quality. (ii) Apply offline k-means quantization to the encoder's latent vectors, creating a discrete codebook without gradient-based learning. (iii) Fine-tune the diffusion decoder exclusively on the quantized latent representations to adapt it to the discrete codes while preserving reconstruction fidelity. This decoupling allows aggressive compression (low bitrate) by transmitting only codebook indices at a low frame rate (1 Hz).

## Key Results
- Ultra-low bitrate of 0.096 kbps at 1 Hz frame rate (750x compression) without severe quality loss.
- Superior objective metrics: lower Frechet Audio Distance (FAD) and Kernel Audio Distance (KAD), higher CLAP audio-text similarity, and improved Scale-invariant Signal-to-Distortion Ratio (Si-SDR) compared to continuous and discrete baselines.
- Subjective MUSHRA tests indicate significantly better perceptual quality and acoustic similarity than competing methods at similar bitrates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The diffusion autoencoder leverages the strong generative priors of a pretrained latent diffusion model, which captures rich audio structure. Offline quantization simplifies the latent space into a discrete codebook, enabling ultra-low bitrate transmission via indices. Fine-tuning the decoder on quantized latents bridges the gap between continuous training and discrete inference, reducing domain shift and maintaining reconstruction quality despite severe compression. This separation of concerns allows each component to specialize: the encoder learns efficient continuous representations, quantization provides bitrate efficiency, and the decoder adapts to handle quantized inputs without retraining the entire system.

## Foundational Learning
- **Latent Diffusion Models**: Generative models that operate in a learned latent space, providing high-quality reconstruction and synthesis. *Why needed*: S-PRESSO uses a pretrained latent diffusion model as decoder to exploit its strong audio prior. *Quick check*: Understand how diffusion models work in latent space vs pixel/voxel space.
- **Autoencoders**: Neural networks that encode inputs to a compressed latent representation and decode back. *Why needed*: Core architecture for compression, mapping audio to lower-dimensional latents. *Quick check*: Train a simple autoencoder on MNIST to grasp encoding/decoding.
- **Neural Quantization**: Discretizing continuous latent vectors into a finite set of codes, often via k-means. *Why needed*: Enables transmission of indices instead of full vectors, drastically reducing bitrate. *Quick check*: Apply k-means clustering to feature vectors and reconstruct to see distortion.
- **Rate-Distortion Tradeoff**: Fundamental compression principle balancing bitrate (rate) and reconstruction quality (distortion). *Why needed*: S-PRESSO optimizes for extreme low rate with acceptable distortion. *Quick check*: Experiment with varying quantization bits on image compression and plot rate-distortion curve.
- **Sound Effect Compression**: Specialized audio compression for non-musical, transient-rich sounds. *Why needed*: Different from speech/music, requiring models that handle diverse timbres and impacts. *Quick check*: Listen to examples from FSD50K to understand sound effect characteristics.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Input Audio -> Encoder (CNN/Transformer) -> Continuous Latent -> Offline k-means Quantization -> Quantized Latent (codebook indices) -> Diffusion Decoder (pretrained Stable Audio) -> Reconstructed Audio.

**Critical Path**: 1. Train encoder-decoder pair with diffusion decoder on continuous latents. 2. Generate latents for training set, cluster to create codebook. 3. Freeze encoder, finetune decoder on quantized latents only.

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- Offline quantization vs online: Offline simplifies training but may not adapt codebook to decoder; online (e.g., VQ-VAE) is end-to-end but harder to train.
- Pretrained decoder vs from-scratch: Pretrained provides strong prior but may be domain-biased; from-scratch is flexible but requires more data/compute.
- Frame rate: Lower frame rate (1 Hz) reduces bitrate but loses temporal resolution; higher rates improve timing but increase bits.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Decoder collapse: When fine-tuning on quantized latents, decoder may ignore codes and output prior mean, causing bland reconstructions.
- Codebook mismatch: If encoder outputs distributions far from codebook centroids, quantization distortion high, leading to poor reconstruction.
- Domain gap: Stable Audio trained on music may fail on percussive sound effects, causing artifacts.

**First Experiments**:
1. **Replication pilot**: Train on a small subset (e.g., 1000 FSD50K clips) for one epoch to verify pipeline runs and basic reconstruction.
2. **Frame rate sweep**: Evaluate at 1 Hz, 5 Hz, 10 Hz to measure quality degradation as bitrate increases; check if 1 Hz is uniquely good or if higher rates maintain quality.
3. **Codebook size ablation**: Vary codebook size (e.g., 256, 512, 1024) to see impact on bitrate vs. quality; find sweet spot for 0.096 kbps.

## Limitations
- Bitrate of 0.096 kbps only achieved at 1 Hz frame rate; performance at higher frame rates (e.g., 5–10 Hz) is unexplored, limiting applicability to temporally rich sounds.
- Decoder relies on pretrained Stable Audio, which may introduce domain bias from music training and hinder generalization to diverse sound effects.
- Offline quantization is not end-to-end; potential suboptimality from decoupled training is unquantified, possibly leaving room for improvement with joint training.
