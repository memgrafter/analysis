# Adaptive Regime-Aware Stock Price Prediction Using Autoencoder-Gated Dual Node Transformers with Reinforcement Learning Control

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.19136
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19136
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves 0.59% one-day MAPE and 72% directional accuracy on 20 S&P 500 stocks (1982‑2025), outperforming the baseline integrated node transformer (0.80% MAPE, 65% DA).

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses a critical weakness in stock price prediction: performance degradation during volatile market periods due to uniform treatment of all market states or reliance on manually labeled regimes. It proposes an adaptive framework comprising three core components: an autoencoder trained on normal market data that detects anomalies via reconstruction error, a dual node transformer architecture with separate processing pathways for stable and event‑driven conditions, and a Soft Actor‑Critic (SAC) reinforcement learning controller that dynamically adjusts the anomaly threshold and blending weights based on prediction feedback. Evaluated on 20 S&P 500 stocks spanning 1982‑2025, the full system achieves 0.59% one‑day mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) and 72% directional accuracy, significantly surpassing the baseline integrated node transformer (0.80% MAPE, 65% DA). During high volatility, the model maintains MAPE below 0.85% compared to >1.5% for the baseline, demonstrating robust regime‑aware adaptation.

## Method Summary
The proposed method integrates anomaly detection, dual‑path representation learning, and reinforcement learning control. An autoencoder is trained exclusively on normal market data to learn a compact representation; reconstruction error serves as an anomaly score indicating departures from typical conditions. A dual node transformer processes input features in parallel: one pathway focuses on stable market patterns while the other targets event‑driven dynamics. A Soft Actor‑Critic controller observes the current anomaly score and recent prediction errors, then outputs an adaptive threshold for flagging volatile regimes and blending weights to combine the transformer pathways’ outputs. The system is trained end‑to‑end using a trading‑inspired loss that balances prediction accuracy and directional correctness. Ablation studies attribute 36% relative MAPE improvement to the autoencoder, 15% to the SAC controller, and 7% to the dual‑path design.

## Key Results
- Full system: 0.59% one‑day MAPE, 72% directional accuracy on 20 S&P 500 stocks (1982‑2025).
- Baseline integrated node transformer: 0.80% MAPE, 65% DA.
- During high volatility, model MAPE <0.85% vs. baseline >1.5%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works because it explicitly separates the modeling of normal and anomalous market regimes and dynamically allocates computational resources accordingly. The autoencoder identifies regime shifts by measuring how poorly current data fits the learned normal manifold; high reconstruction error signals a volatile or event‑driven state. The dual transformer allows each pathway to specialize: the stable pathway captures smooth trends, while the event‑driven pathway learns patterns during outliers. The SAC controller continuously adjusts the anomaly threshold and the blending weights based on real‑time prediction feedback, thus adapting to non‑stationary market dynamics without manual intervention. This closed‑loop, data‑driven adaptation prevents the model from overfitting to a single regime and maintains performance across varying conditions.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Raw Market Data → Autoencoder → Reconstruction Error → SAC Controller → Anomaly Threshold & Blend Weights → Dual Node Transformer (Stable Path & Event‑Driven Path) → Weighted Fusion → Price Prediction

**Critical Path**  
1. Input features are fed to the autoencoder; reconstruction error computed.  
2. Error and recent performance metrics are passed to the SAC controller.  
3. Controller outputs updated threshold and blending coefficients.  
4. Dual transformer processes input in two parallel nodes; outputs combined using weights.  
5. Combined representation yields the final price prediction.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Dual‑path increases computational overhead (~2× forward pass) but gains regime‑specificity.  
- Autoencoder relies on clean normal data; contamination can reduce anomaly detection accuracy.  
- SAC controller introduces RL training complexity and may converge to suboptimal policies if reward shaping is poor.  
- End‑to‑end training may require careful regularization to avoid overfitting across components.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Reconstruction error fails to rise during known crisis periods → autoencoder not capturing anomalies.  
- Blend weights become unstable or oscillate wildly → SAC policy divergence.  
- Event‑driven path overfits to noise → performance degrades on normal data.  
- Model predictions become biased during prolonged high volatility → threshold not adapting properly.

**3 First Experiments**  
1. Validate autoencoder anomaly detection: plot reconstruction error vs. VIX over time; expect high correlation.  
2. Inspect SAC controller behavior: track threshold and blending weights across market regimes; verify smooth adjustments.  
3. Ablation test: disable autoencoder or SAC and measure MAPE/DA drop; confirm contribution magnitudes.

## Limitations
- Data timeline inconsistency: Results claim data through 2025; actual cutoff date unclear, raising leakage concerns.
- Regime definition: Autoencoder reconstruction error is used as proxy for volatile regimes without economic justification or comparison to standard volatility labels.
- Controller transparency: SAC policy decisions are not interpretable, making failure diagnosis difficult.

## Confidence
- Overall performance metrics (0.59% MAPE, 72% DA) → Medium (specific but lacking statistical validation and data timeline clarity).
- Isolated contribution attribution (36%/15%/7%) → Low (ablation may not account for interactions; additive claims unsupported).
- Regime‑awareness validity (anomaly detection regime proxy) → Low (untested assumption).
