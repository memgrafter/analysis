# Free Lunch for Stabilizing Rectified Flow Inversion
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11850
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11850
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** Proximal-Mean Inversion (PMI) and mimic-CFG significantly improve reconstruction and editing stability for Rectified Flow models with minimal computational overhead.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the critical instability problem in inverting Rectified Flow (RF) models, where small errors accumulate during the reverse diffusion process, degrading image reconstruction and editing quality. The authors propose Proximal-Mean Inversion (PMI), a training-free gradient correction method that stabilizes inversion by continuously steering the estimated velocity field toward a running average of past velocities. This is combined with a projection step constrained within a theoretically derived spherical Gaussian to avoid low-density regions. For editing, mimic-CFG interpolates between the current corrected velocity and its projection onto the running average, balancing editability with structural consistency. Evaluated on PIE-Bench, PMI yields consistent gains in reconstruction metrics (e.g., +1.55 PSNR) while mimic-CFG improves editing CLIP similarity (+0.21) with fewer neural function evaluations, achieving state-of-the-art performance.

## Method Summary
The core contribution is Proximal-Mean Inversion (PMI), which modifies the standard RF inversion ODE solver. At each step, the computed velocity is corrected by moving it toward an exponential moving average of historical velocities from the same inversion trajectory. To prevent divergence into regions with poor model coverage, this corrected velocity is then projected onto the surface of a sphere centered at the average velocity, with radius derived from the model's assumed Gaussian noise distribution. For editing tasks, mimic-CFG replaces the unconditional velocity used in Classifier-Free Guidance with the projected average velocity from PMI. This creates a smoother interpolation between the original content (using the average) and the desired edit (using the current corrected velocity), controlled by a guidance scale. The entire process is training-free and integrates seamlessly into existing RF solvers.

## Key Results
- PMI improves reconstruction metrics on PIE-Bench: +1.55 PSNR and -2.98 LPIPS for RF-Solver.
- mimic-CFG enhances editing performance: +0.21 CLIP similarity for FireFlow editor while preserving background structure.
- Both methods reduce required neural function evaluations (NFEs) compared to prior SOTA, improving computational efficiency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The instability in RF inversion stems from error accumulation when following the learned velocity field backward in time. PMI mitigates this by stabilizing the trajectory in two ways: first, the running average acts as a low-pass filter, smoothing out high-frequency corrections that lead to divergence. Second, the spherical Gaussian projection explicitly constrains updates to regions where the model's density is theoretically non-negligible, avoiding the "out-of-distribution" zones that cause catastrophic failure. mimic-CFG leverages this stabilized average as a more reliable proxy for the unconditional distribution, allowing guidance to apply edits without destabilizing the underlying structure. The spherical constraint is key—it provides a theoretically sound boundary derived from the RF training objective, replacing heuristic clipping or damping used in prior work.

## Foundational Learning
- **Rectified Flow (RF):** A diffusion modeling framework that learns a deterministic velocity field to transport noise to data. *Why needed:* Forms the base model whose inversion is being stabilized. *Quick check:* Understand the RF training objective and its straight-line probability path assumption.
- **Inversion Instability:** The propensity of reverse-time ODE solvers to drift from the true data manifold due to errors in the learned velocity. *Why needed:* Core problem addressed by the paper. *Quick check:* Analyze error propagation in deterministic samplers for diffusion models.
- **Running Average / Exponential Moving Average (EMA):** A statistical technique to smooth a time series by combining current and past values. *Why needed:* Provides the stable reference velocity in PMI. *Quick check:* Derive the EMA update formula and its effect on variance reduction.
- **Spherical Gaussian Projection:** Projecting a point onto a sphere defined by a Gaussian distribution's iso-density surface. *Why needed:* Theoretically justified constraint to keep velocity updates in high-density regions. *Quick check:* Calculate the projection of a vector onto a sphere centered at the mean.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** A method to steer generation by interpolating between conditional and unconditional model outputs. *Why needed:* mimic-CFG adapts this for editing by using the PMI average as the "unconditional" anchor. *Quick check:* Recall the CFG formula and its effect on sample diversity/quality.
- **Neural Function Evaluation (NFE):** The number of times the neural network (velocity field) is evaluated during sampling/inversion. *Why needed:* Key efficiency metric; the paper claims reductions. *Quick check:* Count NFEs in a standard ODE solver versus PMI.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Input Image -> RF Encoder (if any) -> Initial Noise Estimate -> RF Inversion ODE Solver (with PMI Correction) -> Running Average Buffer (EMA) -> Spherical Projection Module -> Final Latent/Reconstruction. For Editing: Current Velocity + Projected Average -> mimic-CFG Interpolator -> Guided Velocity -> Solver.

**Critical Path:**
1. Start with an input image and initialize a noise sample.
2. Run the standard RF inversion ODE solver forward (to noise) or backward (to data) for N steps.
3. At each solver step, compute the raw velocity.
4. Compute the EMA of all past velocities for this trajectory.
5. Correct the raw velocity by moving it toward the EMA.
6. Project this corrected velocity onto the sphere defined by the EMA and the radius sqrt(d)*σ (where d is dimension, σ is noise level).
7. Use this projected velocity for the next ODE step.
8. For editing, at each step, interpolate between the projected EMA velocity and the current (post-projection) velocity using a guidance scale to produce the final guided velocity.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- **Stability vs. Fidelity:** The EMA smoothing and projection improve stability but may slightly deviate from the exact inversion path, trading off perfect reconstruction for robustness.
- **Edit Strength vs. Structure Preservation:** mimic-CFG's interpolation parameter balances how much the edit influences the output versus preserving the structure encoded in the EMA. A higher scale yields stronger edits but risks degradation.
- **Computational Cost:** PMI adds minimal overhead (maintaining an EMA, one projection per step) but reduces the need for many NFEs by enabling fewer, more stable steps.

**Failure Signatures:**
- **Over-smoothing:** If the EMA momentum is too high or the spherical radius too large, edits lose detail and the output becomes blurry or oversaturated toward the average.
- **Projection Failure:** If the raw velocity correction is too extreme, projection might create a sharp discontinuity, causing artifacts at object boundaries.
- **Edit Bleeding:** With high mimic-CFG guidance, the edit might not localize properly, altering background regions that should remain unchanged.

**First Three Experiments to Run:**
1. **Replication of Reconstruction Metrics:** Run PMI on the PIE-Bench dataset using the same RF-Solver baseline, measuring PSNR and LPIPS against the reported values.
2. **Ablation of Projection Constraint:** Disable the spherical projection (use only EMA correction) and quantify the change in failure rate (e.g., images diverging or grossly distorting) and average reconstruction quality.
3. **Sensitivity Analysis of mimic-CFG Scale:** For a fixed editing prompt, vary the interpolation parameter across a range (e.g., 0.0 to 20.0) and plot CLIP similarity vs. background preservation (e.g., LPIPS between original and edited background) to find the Pareto frontier.

## Limitations
- **Dataset Generalizability:** Results are demonstrated only on PIE-Bench; performance on broader datasets (e.g., ImageNet, FFHQ) or higher resolutions is not shown.
- **Theoretical Assumption Robustness:** The spherical Gaussian constraint relies on the assumption that the data distribution is close to Gaussian in the velocity space; its effectiveness on complex, multi-modal data is not stress-tested.
- **Heuristic Editing Parameter:** The mimic-CFG interpolation parameter appears to be a heuristic without a theoretical optimum, likely requiring task-specific tuning.

## Confidence
- **High:** The algorithmic definitions of PMI and mimic-CFG are precise and implementable; the mechanism of EMA smoothing plus projection is clearly explained.
- **Medium:** Reported metric improvements (e.g., +1.55 PSNR, +0.21 CLIP) are specific but lack statistical variance (standard deviations, confidence intervals) and full ablations to separate the impact of EMA vs. projection.
- **Low:** Claims of "state-of-the-art" and "theoretical robustness" are not fully substantiated due to missing comparisons against all cited SOTA methods under identical settings and a deeper stability analysis beyond the Gaussian case.
