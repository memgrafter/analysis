# Visualizing Coalition Formation: From Hedonic Games to Image Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07890
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07890
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Using γ normalized by graph density, mean F_union¹=0.828 and F_single¹=0.488, showing frequent fragmentation but recoverability.

## Executive Summary
This work frames image segmentation as a coalition formation problem in hedonic games, where pixels are agents forming stable coalitions on a graph via a Constant Potts Model (CPM) potential. The resolution parameter γ controls fragmentation, yielding three distinct regimes: cohesive (high F_single¹), fragmented but recoverable (low F_single¹ but high F_union¹), and intrinsic failure (both low). Crucially, evaluating only the best single coalition (F_single¹) underestimates performance; the union of multiple coalitions (F_union¹) reveals that the foreground object is often fragmented yet recoverable. Experiments on 100 Weizmann images with normalized γ achieve a mean F_union¹ of 0.828 and F_single¹ of 0.488, with a gap of 0.340.

## Method Summary
The pipeline converts an image into a weighted pixel graph (8-neighborhood, color similarity, and Canny edge penalty). A decentralized hedonic optimization uses the Leiden algorithm with the CPM objective to find an equilibrium partition where no pixel benefits from moving. The partition is evaluated against ground truth via two metrics: F_single¹ (best single community’s F1) and F_union¹ (greedy union of communities that improves F1). The resolution parameter γ is normalized by graph density (γ = density(G)/900) to place results in a consistent regime across images.

## Key Results
- Three fragmentation regimes emerge as γ increases: cohesive (both metrics high), fragmented but recoverable (F_union¹ high, F_single¹ low), and intrinsic failure (both low).
- With normalized γ, mean F_union¹ = 0.828, mean F_single¹ = 0.488, average gap = 0.340.
- The substantial gap indicates the foreground object is frequently split across multiple coalitions but remains recoverable via union, highlighting the need for multi-coalition evaluation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Claim:** Pixel coalitions form via decentralized, utility-maximizing moves aligned with a global potential function (Constant Potts Model).  
**Mechanism:** Each pixel evaluates the potential of joining any community based on intra-connectivity and a size penalty governed by γ. Pixels unilaterally move to increase personal potential, inducing stable partitions (no beneficial deviations). The potential is bounded and monotonic, guaranteeing convergence.  
**Break condition:** Cyclical improvements or infinite loops are prevented by potential monotonicity.

**Claim:** The resolution parameter γ directly controls coalition granularity, creating distinct regimes of cohesion and fragmentation.  
**Mechanism:** γ trades off neighbor attraction against a size penalty. Low γ favors large cohesive regions (single dominant coalition). High γ penalizes size, promoting many small coalitions. The transition between regimes is continuous but qualitatively different.  
**Break condition:** Poorly chosen γ relative to graph density yields partitions where the object is either too fragmented (low F_single¹) or merged with background (low F_union¹). Normalization (γ = density/900) mitigates this.

**Claim:** Evaluating only the single best-matching coalition (F_single¹) is insufficient; the union of multiple coalitions (F_union¹) reveals recoverability and distinguishes fragmentation from intrinsic failure.  
**Mechanism:** From a partition, two binary masks are constructed: (a) the single community with highest F1, (b) the union of a greedily selected subset of communities that maximizes F1. The gap F_union¹ − F_single¹ diagnoses whether the foreground is fragmented (large gap) or intrinsically missing (small gap, both low).  
**Break condition:** The greedy union heuristic may be suboptimal if two weak communities are complementary but neither individually passes the inclusion threshold.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Hedonic Games & Potential Games**  
  *Why needed here:* The mechanism models pixels as agents in a hedonic game (preferences depend only on coalition members) that is also a potential game (local moves align with global potential maximization). Understanding stability (no beneficial deviation) is key.  
  *Quick check question:* What is the difference between internal and external stability in a coalition partition, and how does the potential function guarantee both?

- **Concept: Graph-based Image Segmentation**  
  *Why needed here:* The image is converted to a weighted graph (pixels as nodes, affinities as edges). Coalition formation operates on this graph. Understanding how edge weights (color similarity, boundary cues) influence community formation is essential.  
  *Quick check question:* How do the affinity weights in Equation 3 (color similarity and edge map) encourage coalitions to respect object boundaries?

- **Concept: F1 Score for Binary Masks**  
  *Why needed here:* Both metrics are F1 scores comparing derived binary masks against ground truth. Interpreting the gap requires understanding trade-offs between false positives and false negatives.  
  *Quick check question:* If a coalition mask has high recall but low precision, what does that say about its fragmentation/cohesion relative to the object?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input & Graph Builder -> Hedonic Optimizer -> Evaluation Projector -> Regime Classifier

**Critical path:** Image → Graph → Partition → Projections → Metrics. γ flows from user into optimizer.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Graph construction: The affinity function (color + Canny edge) is a heuristic; alternative graphs could drastically change coalition structures.  
- γ normalization: Choosing constant c=900 is empirical; may not generalize to all datasets or graph constructions.  
- Union heuristic: Greedy forward selection is efficient but not optimal for F_union¹; exact subset search is combinatorial.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low F_union¹: Intrinsic failure (partition does not represent object well).  
- High F_union¹, low F_single¹: Fragmented but recoverable (expected regime).  
- Both high: Cohesive success (object emerges as single coalition).  
- Non-monotonic F_single¹: Indicates complex γ-graph interactions, possibly over-fragmentation.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Regime sweep: For a fixed image/graph, sweep γ (e.g., 1e-7 to 1e-1) and verify the three-phase pattern in F_single¹ and F_union¹ curves.  
2. Initialization robustness: Run optimizer from singleton and grand-coalition initializations for same γ; compare partitions via Adjusted Rand Index. Expect near-identical equilibria.  
3. Projection sanity check: For high-γ fragmented cases, visualize communities selected by F_single¹ and greedy union; manually confirm union mask captures the object and gap reflects fragmentation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How do alternative graph construction methods affect equilibrium fragmentation and metrics?**  
  *Basis:* Appendix A states mechanism is agnostic to graph construction; evaluating alternatives is future work.

- **How does the mechanism generalize to multi-object segmentation (Weizmann two-object subset)?**  
  *Basis:* Authors restricted to single-object subset; two-object case is left for future work.

- **Can we design post-processing or modifications to merge fragmented coalitions and reduce the F_union¹ − F_single¹ gap?**  
  *Basis:* Conclusion aims to investigate merging fragmented but recoverable coalitions.

- **Is the density-based normalization γ = density(G)/900 optimal across datasets, or should c be adapted?**  
  *Basis:* c=900 chosen empirically on Weizmann; rationale for normalization given but constant not theoretically derived; may be dataset-specific.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameters (σ_c, σ_e, Canny thresholds, edge discard ε) are unreported; must be sourced from implementation or tuned.
- Graph construction details (border handling, edge density effects) may alter equilibrium structures.
- γ normalization (density/900) is heuristic; constant 900 may not generalize to other datasets or graph types.
- Greedy union heuristic is suboptimal; exact F_union¹ optimization is intractable for many communities.
- Evaluation limited to Weizmann single-object dataset; performance on multi-object or complex scenes unknown.
