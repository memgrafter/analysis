# VIRAASAT: Traversing Novel Paths for Indian Cultural Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.18429  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18429  
- Reference count: 30  
- Primary result: SCoM fine-tuning yields large improvements (e.g., +20.84% Full Match for Qwen2.5-7B over CoT) on VIRAASAT, a new multi-hop QA benchmark for Indian cultural reasoning.

## Executive Summary
LLMs currently perform poorly on Indian cultural reasoning due to a lack of challenging multi-hop benchmarks. This work introduces VIRAASAT, a semi-automated QA dataset with 3,200+ questions grounded in a knowledge graph of 700+ curated Indian cultural artifacts across 13 attributes and all states/UTs. The authors also propose Symbolic Chain-of-Manipulation (SCoM), a neuro-symbolic approach where models learn atomic graph manipulations guided by a symbolic verifier. Zero-shot results are low; CoT fine-tuning improves state prediction but not final answer. SCoM fine-tuning, however, produces substantial gains—on Qwen2.5-7B, Full Match increases from 36.70% to 57.54%, Answer Match from 37.33% to 58.01%, and State Match from 89.11% to 91.45%, up to +20.84% over CoT. Ablations highlight the critical role of the verifier and explicit manipulations. VIRAASAT and SCoM together offer a scalable benchmark and an effective training paradigm for culturally aware reasoning.

## Method Summary
VIRAASAT is a multi-hop question-answering dataset built on a knowledge graph (KG) of Indian cultural artifacts. Each artifact is associated with a state and an attribute (e.g., dance, cuisine). Questions follow a 2-hop template: given an anchor artifact A and a clue about the target artifact B's attribute, the model must identify the intermediate state S that connects A and B. The KG is queried via tool-like atomic manipulations: `lookup_entity_by_name`, `get_artifacts_in_state`, and `resolve_entity_by_description`. SCoM training uses supervised fine-tuning on trajectories that interleave thoughts, actions, and observations, with a symbolic verifier injecting corrective interventions when actions deviate from the ground-truth graph path. Two training regimes are compared: standard chain-of-thought (CoT) and SCoM. The training pipeline employs parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) on base LLMs, and evaluation uses exact-match metrics for state, answer, and full match.

## Key Results
- SCoM fine-tuning yields large gains over CoT: on Qwen2.5-7B, Full Match rises from 36.70% to 57.54%, Answer Match from 37.33% to 58.01%, and State Match from 89.11% to 91.45%—up to +20.84% absolute improvement.
- CoSFT improves State Match but leaves Answer Match low, showing that standard reasoning does not effectively resolve the final answer for long-tail entities.
- Ablations demonstrate that removing the verifier drops Full Match by 16 percentage points and removing explicit manipulations (keeping only thoughts) reduces Answer Match from 52% to 12%, confirming both components are essential.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SCoM improves performance by decomposing multi-hop reasoning into explicit, atomic graph manipulations that ground each step in the symbolic knowledge graph. This decomposition is especially learnable for long-tail cultural entities that are poorly represented in pretraining data. A symbolic verifier provides step-level feedback during trace synthesis, monitoring the actor's proposed actions against ground-truth bridge constraints and injecting corrective interjections to prevent reasoning drift. Ablations show that removing either the manipulations or the verifier causes severe performance drops (e.g., Answer Match from 52% to 12% without manipulations). Additionally, SCoM's state-constrained retrieval prunes the search space after the first hop, making low-probability tail entities more retrievable; this is evidenced by the narrowing gap between State Match and Answer Match in SCoM-trained models compared to CoT-trained ones.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Data Pipeline → KG Construction → Question Generation → Manual Verification → SCoM Trace Synthesis (actor-verifier loop) → Training Pipeline (SFT on CoT/SCoM traces with LoRA) → Evaluation.

**Critical path**:  
Dataset creation: curated artifact list → build KG → sample 2-hop paths → template-based question generation → manual verification → generate SCoM traces using Gemini-2.0-flash with verifier.  
Model training: prepare SFT data → apply LoRA (r=16, alpha=32, lr=5e-5) → train for 5 epochs → evaluate on test split using State/Answer/Full Match.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Semi-automation vs quality: template-based generation scales but requires manual verification; full automation risks noise.  
- 2-hop template simplifies evaluation but limits complexity; deeper hops remain unexplored.  
- Verifier cost: using a strong LLM as verifier adds overhead during trace synthesis but yields high-fidelity supervision.  
- PEFT (LoRA) is efficient but may underfit compared to full fine-tuning.

**Failure signatures**:  
- High State Match but low Answer Match: model retrieves correct bridge state but fails to select the correct target artifact (common in CoT-SFT).  
- Drastic drop when manipulations are removed: indicates reliance on state-constrained retrieval.  
- Errors on state-spanning artifacts: when an artifact belongs to multiple states, state constraint may exclude the correct answer.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. Zero-shot evaluation of various LLMs (closed and open) on VIRAASAT to establish baseline poor performance, especially on Answer Match.  
2. Supervised Fine-Tuning on CoT traces: training models on standard chain-of-thought data; results show improved State Match but limited gains in Answer/Full Match.  
3. Supervised Fine-Tuning on SCoM traces: training on verifier-corrected, manipulation-based trajectories; yields significant improvements across all metrics, including +20.84% Full Match for Qwen2.5-7B over CoT.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can VIRAASAT be extended to support >2-hop reasoning and non-state bridge entities to evaluate more complex cultural reasoning? The current fixed 2-hop template does not cover richer graph patterns (e.g., 3-hop chains or alternate bridge entity types).  
2. How do multilingual and code-mixed variants of VIRAASAT affect LLM performance on Indian cultural reasoning? The benchmark is currently English-only; it does not evaluate state-specific multilingual queries or code-mixed usage.  
3. What underlying reasons cause CoT fine-tuning to inadequately ground and synthesize low-probability cultural facts, leading to poor final answer resolution? The paper does not analyze this failure mechanism, which could inform future approaches.

## Limitations
- KG API/simulator details (tool call signatures, response formats) are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction of the SCoM environment.  
- SCoM trace generation hyperparameters (e.g., maximum steps, verifier intervention prompt phrasing, termination conditions) are missing, risking variance in data quality.  
- Evaluation parsing logic for extracting the final answer is not described, potentially leading to inconsistent scoring across reproductions.

## Confidence
- Benchmark necessity (VIRAASAT fills a gap): Medium  
- SCoM effectiveness (large gains over CoT): High  
- Verifier importance (step-level supervision): Medium  
- State-constrained retrieval (key for tail-entity handling): Medium
