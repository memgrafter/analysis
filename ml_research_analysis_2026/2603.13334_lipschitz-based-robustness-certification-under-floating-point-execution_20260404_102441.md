# Lipschitz-Based Robustness Certification Under Floating-Point Execution
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13334
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13334
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Real-arithmetic Lipschitz-based robustness certification is unsound under floating-point execution; a floating-point-aware certification condition with a margin degradation term restores soundness at small empirical overhead on MNIST/Fashion MNIST but larger on CIFAR-10, while a hybrid high-precision variant significantly improves coverage.

## Executive Summary
Neural network robustness certification often assumes exact real arithmetic, but deployed models use floating-point. This mismatch can invalidate guarantees: rounding errors can cause misclassifications within the certified perturbation ball. This paper provides a formal, compositional theory to bound the ℓ₂ deviation between real and floating-point outputs for ReLU networks, integrating sound overflow checks. The resulting certification condition adds a term accounting for accumulated rounding error. Empirically, the float-aware certifier correctly rejects all counterexamples where real-arithmetic certifiers fail. On standard benchmarks, certification incurs a modest drop in certified robustness for MNIST and Fashion MNIST but a substantial one for CIFAR-10. A hybrid method, using high-precision executions to measure per-input deviation, dramatically reduces this degradation and outperforms a leading verifier on two datasets despite verifying a stronger ℓ₂ guarantee.

## Method Summary
The method extends classical Lipschitz-based certification by incorporating floating-point error analysis. For each layer, it propagates two inductive bounds: (1) a deterministic radius `r_ℓ` bounding the real activation norms over the ε-ball, and (2) a deviation bound `D_ℓ` upper-bounding the ℓ₂ distance between real and floating-point activations at the worst-case point in the ball. Overflow is certified by checking scalar inequalities involving `r_ℓ` and `D_ℓ`. The deviation bound follows a linear recurrence `D_ℓ = α_ℓ·D_ℓ₋₁ + β_ℓ(r_ℓ₋₁)`, where `α_ℓ` amplifies prior error and `β_ℓ` captures fresh rounding error. At the output, the margin degradation terms `E_ctr` (center) and `E_ball` (ball) are subtracted from the classical robustness condition. A hybrid variant tightens `E_ctr` by measuring actual deviation between the target (float32) and a high-precision (float64) copy at the specific input, while `E_ball` uses the worst-case bound.

## Key Results
- Real-arithmetic Lipschitz certifiers are unsound: concrete counterexamples exist for MNIST, Fashion MNIST, and CIFAR-10, including adversarially crafted float32 models with semantically meaningful ε (e.g., ε=0.47).
- Float32-aware certification reduces certified robustness by only 0.24 pp (MNIST) and 1.49 pp (Fashion MNIST) but by 27.22 pp (CIFAR-10) relative to real-arithmetic certification.
- A hybrid pre-deployment method using float64 measurements improves certified robustness, achieving higher Verified Robust Accuracy than ERAN (DeepPoly) on MNIST and Fashion MNIST despite verifying a stronger ℓ₂ guarantee.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Real-arithmetic Lipschitz-based robustness certification can be unsound under floating-point execution because rounding errors can create adversarial examples within the certified ε-ball.
- **Mechanism:** The certification assumes exact real arithmetic, but deployed models use floating-point. Rounding errors accumulate during forward passes, potentially flipping predictions for points that are certified robust under real arithmetic. The paper constructs counterexamples by searching for a point x₀ certified robust at ε and a nearby x₁ (within ε) that is misclassified due to floating-point effects.
- **Core assumption:** The neural network forward pass uses floating-point arithmetic with non-zero rounding error; the Lipschitz-based certifier assumes real arithmetic.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "As motivating evidence, we exhibit concrete counterexamples..." ; [Section 2.3] describes counterexample search and presents instances; related work (Jia and Rinard 2021, Jin et al. 2024) also demonstrates unsoundness in verifiers.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A compositional theory bounds the ℓ₂ deviation between real and floating-point activations uniformly over an ε-ball, enabling sound overflow-free certification.
- **Mechanism:** For each layer ℓ, the deviation bound Dₗ satisfies a linear recurrence Dₗ = αₗ·Dₗ₋₁ + βₗ(rₗ₋₁), where αₗ ≈ ∥Wₗ∥₂ (with floating-point correction) and βₗ(r) captures fresh rounding error proportional to r (the radius of real activations). Radii rₗ are deterministic bounds on real activation norms over the ε-ball. Overflow is certified by bounding preactivation magnitudes using rₗ₋₁ and Dₗ₋₁.
- **Core assumption:** Feed-forward network with ReLU (or FP-exact) activations, identity output; floating-point follows the standard model (round-to-nearest, gradual underflow); no overflow occurs (checked separately).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "We develop a formal, compositional theory..." ; [Section 5] Lemma 5.1 and Corollary 5.2 derive the recurrence; Section 4 interleaves overflow checks.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** In pre-deployment certification over a test distribution, using high-precision (e.g., float64) model executions to measure per-input deviation significantly tightens the certification condition, improving certified robustness coverage.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of using the worst-case deviation bound D_ball for the center point x, measure the actual deviation between the target model (float32) and a high-precision model at x: D_hybrid = ||z_float32(x) - z_float64(x)||₂ + D_float64(x,0). This replaces the center error term E_ctr, while the ball error E_ball still uses worst-case bounds.
- **Core assumption:** The high-precision model (float64) is extremely close to real arithmetic; the target and high-precision models are identical except for precision.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "We also show how... we can significantly reduce this degradation by employing a hybrid certification method..." ; [Section 7] formalizes the hybrid deviation bound and Theorem 7.2 proves soundness; [Section 8.3.2] shows empirical improvement over ERAN.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Floating-point error model (unit roundoff, relative/absolute errors)**
  - Why needed: The paper’s deviation bounds and overflow checks rely on the standard floating-point error model (e.g., fl(x ◦ y) = (x ◦ y)(1+δ) + η) with bounded δ, η.
  - Quick check: "In the model fl(x ◦ y) = (x ◦ y)(1+δ) + η, what do δ and η represent and what are their bounds?"

- **Concept: Lipschitz-based robustness certification (margin Lipschitz constants)**
  - Why needed: The paper extends the classical certification method that uses a margin Lipschitz constant L_{j,i*} to bound how much the margin between correct and competing classes can change per unit input perturbation.
  - Quick check: "Given a neural network N, input x with true class i*, and competing class j, what is the margin m_{j,i*}(x) and how is its Lipschitz constant used to certify ε-robustness?"

- **Concept: Spectral norm of a matrix**
  - Why needed: Spectral norms ∥W∥₂ appear in the Lipschitz constants (for linear layers) and in the amplification factors αₗ; they measure the maximum singular value and induce a matrix operator norm.
  - Quick check: "How is the spectral norm ∥W∥₂ related to the Lipschitz constant of the linear transformation z ↦ Wz?"

## Architecture Onboarding
Overflow Checker -> Deviation Bound Calculator -> Radius Propagator -> Final-Layer Margin Error -> Certification Condition -> Hybrid Variant

**Critical path:** For a given input x and ε:
1. Initialize r₀ = ∥x∥₂ + ε, D₀ = 0.
2. For each hidden layer ℓ = 1..L-1:
   - Compute Sₗ, Mₗ from rₗ₋₁, Dₗ₋₁, weights; run overflow check.
   - Compute αₗ, βₗ(rₗ₋₁); update Dₗ = αₗ·Dₗ₋₁ + βₗ(rₗ₋₁).
   - Update rₗ = L(φₗ)(∥Wₗ∥₂·rₗ₋₁ + ∥bₗ∥₂).
3. For final layer L, for each competing class j:
   - Compute α_L^{(j,i*)}, β_L^{(j,i*)}(r_ball) with r_ball from step 2 (ε>0) and r_ctr (ε=0).
   - Form E_ball = α·D_ball + β(r_ball); form E_ctr using either worst-case D_ball (non-hybrid) or hybrid measurement D_hybrid.
   - Check ˆm_{j,i*}(x) - L_{j,i*}·ε - (E_ctr + E_ball) > 0.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Precision vs. performance: Tighter bounds (more Gram iterations) improve coverage but increase time. Hybrid trades high-precision forward passes for tighter center error.
- Worst-case vs. input-specific: Theory uses worst-case over ε-ball; hybrid tightens only the center term.
- Precision format applicability: Analysis requires nₗ·u ≪ 1; for float16 on wide layers it becomes vacuous.

**Failure signatures:**
- Vacuous certification: 0% certified robust (e.g., float16 on CIFAR-10) indicates deviation bounds too loose.
- Overflow check failure: Sₗ or Mₗ ≥ F_max; overflow cannot be ruled out for given precision.
- Excessive certification time: Dominated by matrix norm (Gram iteration) computation.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Counterexample rejection test: Use search procedure to generate (x₀, x₁, ε) against a float32 model; verify real certifier accepts x₀ and float-aware rejects all.
2. Standard benchmark replication: Run certification on all 10,000 test points for MNIST, Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10 (float32); confirm certified robustness percentages match Table 1.
3. Hybrid vs. non-hybrid comparison: Compare certified robustness using hybrid (float64) against standard float-aware; expect coverage increase, especially on CIFAR-10.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can the floating-point certification theory be extended to modern neural network architectures such as convolutional layers, batch normalization, and residual connections?
- How can activation-function rounding be soundly incorporated for non-ReLU activations (e.g., sigmoid, tanh) in floating-point certification?
- Can floating-point-aware certification be made effective at very low precisions (e.g., float16) where the current analysis becomes vacuous?
- How can the theory be adapted to handle non-IEEE floating-point semantics, such as fused operators, flush-to-zero, or hardware-specific accelerators?

## Limitations
- No public release of pre-trained models and exact spectral-norm values → direct replication of Table 1 and 2 is impossible without authors' data.
- Overflow checks often fail for float16 on wide layers; the feasible precision regime (nₗ·u ≪ 1) is not characterized for common architectures.
- Hybrid method assumes float64 ≈ real arithmetic; this may not hold for very deep nets or large ε, and it only tightens the center error term.

## Confidence
- **Unsoundness of real-arithmetic certifiers:** High (concrete counterexamples provided).
- **Soundness of floating-point-aware theory:** Medium (formal proof under standard FP model; may be vacuous when nₗ·u not ≪ 1).
- **Empirical results (overhead, hybrid gains):** Medium (reported numbers depend on missing assets; could differ if norms recomputed).
