# MTRAG-UN: A Benchmark for Open Challenges in Multi-Turn RAG Conversations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23184  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23184  
- **Reference count:** 12  
- **Primary result:** Frontier LLMs struggle on multi-turn RAG tasks involving unanswerable, underspecified, non-standalone questions and unclear responses.

## Executive Summary
MTRAG-UN is a benchmark targeting four persistent challenges in multi-turn RAG: UNanswerable, UNderspecified, NONstandalone questions, and UNclear responses requiring clarifications. The dataset contains 666 human‑generated tasks (2,800+ turns) across six domains, including two new enterprise corpora (Banking, Telco). Tasks are crafted to force models to handle ambiguity, topic shifts, and clarification requests. Evaluation of frontier LLMs (e.g., GPT‑OSS‑120B, DeepSeek‑V3) shows widespread failures: models often guess instead of asking for clarification on underspecified questions, retrieval performance degrades in enterprise domains, and generation scores lag behind target answers.

## Method Summary
The benchmark is constructed through a human annotation process that designs multi-turn conversations to exhibit one of the four challenge types. Each task consists of a series of turns where the user query may be unanswerable (no answer in the corpus), underspecified (lacking necessary details), non‑standalone (dependent on previous turns), or unclear (requiring clarification). The dataset covers six domains, two of which are enterprise-specific (Banking, Telco), to test generalization. Evaluation involves two sub‑tasks: retrieval (finding relevant documents) and generation (producing an adequate response). Automatic metrics are used for scoring, with human evaluation for a subset.

## Key Results
- Frontier LLMs exhibit significant failures on the four challenge categories, notably guessing on underspecified questions instead of asking for clarification.
- Retrieval performance degrades in new enterprise domains (Banking/Telco R@5: 0.32–0.39) compared to the average across domains (0.52).
- Generation scores are substantially lower than target answers, highlighting deficiencies in multi‑turn reasoning and response adequacy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The benchmark’s effectiveness lies in its targeted design: tasks force models to engage with specific ambiguity types that require interactive clarification and context tracking. The observed failures suggest that current LLMs lack robust mechanisms for uncertainty estimation and proactive dialogue management. Specifically, models tend to generate speculative answers rather than recognizing underspecification and seeking clarification, indicating a training bias toward direct response generation over interactive reasoning. The benchmark isolates these failure modes by controlling for other factors, thereby providing a clear signal of model limitations in multi‑turn RAG scenarios.

## Foundational Learning
The work builds upon the Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG) paradigm, extending it to multi‑turn conversations where context accumulates and queries become interdependent. It highlights foundational learning challenges such as: abstaining from unanswerable questions, detecting underspecification, leveraging conversational history for non‑standalone queries, and interpreting unclear responses. These challenges require models to learn interactive behaviors beyond single‑turn QA, encompassing discourse understanding, uncertainty calibration, and clarification strategies. The benchmark serves as a testbed for evaluating how well models acquire these skills through training.

## Architecture Onboarding
The evaluation framework is model‑agnostic and comprises two main components: (1) the dataset of human‑generated multi‑turn tasks, each labeled with challenge categories, and (2) the protocol for measuring retrieval and generation performance. The critical path processes conversation history and current query through the model’s retrieval component (if any) and generation component, then scores outputs against references. Design tradeoffs include balancing dataset size (666 tasks) with domain diversity and choosing automatic metrics (ROUGE, BLEU) for scalability versus human evaluation for nuance. Failure signatures identified include responses that are off‑topic, speculative, or fail to reference retrieved evidence. Initial experiments with smaller models showed severe performance drops, motivating the focus on frontier LLMs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper raises several open questions: What architectural modifications can enable models to better detect underspecification and generate appropriate clarification requests? To what extent do domain‑specific characteristics (e.g., jargon, structure) versus general domain shift explain performance drops in enterprise corpora? How can the benchmark be scaled to more tasks and domains to ensure robust generalizability? Additionally, what evaluation metrics best capture multi‑turn response adequacy beyond lexical overlap? These questions point to directions for future research in multi‑turn RAG.

## Limitations
- **Scale:** The benchmark’s size (666 tasks) may be insufficient to capture the full diversity of multi‑turn conversation patterns.
- **Domain Shift Ambiguity:** Performance drops in Banking/Telco could stem from domain adaptation issues or inherent corpus properties (e.g., jargon, structure), requiring isolation.
- **Error Analysis Gap:** The claim that models “guess” instead of asking for clarifications lacks detailed output analysis to distinguish between guessing and poor clarification generation.

## Confidence
- Benchmark design & dataset creation: **High** confidence  
- Reported performance gaps (retrieval/generation scores): **Medium‑High** confidence  
- Causal attribution of struggle (e.g., “models guess”): **Low‑Medium** confidence  

## Next Checks
1. Scale evaluation to >2,000 tasks across additional enterprise domains to test generalizability of performance gaps.  
2. Perform fine‑grained output analysis on underspecified questions to quantify guessing versus clarification requests, and correlate with model confidence.  
3. Isolate domain‑shift effects by evaluating retrieval on Banking/Telco corpora using identical queries and retrieval models from other domains.
