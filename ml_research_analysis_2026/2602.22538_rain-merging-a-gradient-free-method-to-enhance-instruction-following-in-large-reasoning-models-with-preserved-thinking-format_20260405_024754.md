# RAIN-Merging: A Gradient-Free Method to Enhance Instruction Following in Large Reasoning Models with Preserved Thinking Format

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.22538
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22538
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: RAIN-Merging improves instruction following by ~4 percentage points on average while preserving or improving reasoning, outperforming all merging baselines.

## Executive Summary
Large reasoning models (LRMs) excel at chain-of-thought but often fail to follow strict output format constraints. RAIN-Merging is a gradient-free model merging approach that enhances instruction adherence in LRMs without altering their structured thinking format (e.g., `<think>`/`). It first projects the instruction-tuned model’s task vector onto the null space of forward features at thinking tokens to preserve reasoning representations, then weights the projected components with per-module coefficients derived from attention alignment scores. Evaluations on four instruction-following and nine reasoning benchmarks show substantial gains and generalization across model sizes and architectures.

## Method Summary
RAIN-Merging combines a large reasoning model (LRM) θ_R and an instruction-tuned model (ITM) θ_I using a shared base θ_B. The process: (1) compute task vector Δ_I = θ_I − θ_B; (2) for each core submodule (Q, K, V, O, FFN), construct a forward feature operator Φ from activations at thinking token positions (e.g., `<think>`/``) on a small reasoning calibration set, and project Δ_I onto the null space of Φ to obtain Δ_⊥; (3) initial merged model θ' = θ_R + Δ_⊥; (4) on an instruction calibration set with spans I(x), R(x), U(x), compute attention alignment a_̃ and leakage u_̃ for each attention head; (5) derive per-head coefficients α_k = clip(g_̃ / H̃) with g_̃ = a_̃ − ρ u_̃ and H̃ = 1 + u_̃ (ρ=10); average for FFN; (6) final merge θ* = θ_R + λ Σ α_k Δ_⊥^k with global scaling λ tuned on validation.

## Key Results
- Instruction adherence: average +3.99 percentage points on Qwen2.5-7B across IFEval, CELLO, etc.
- Reasoning/general performance: average +4.56 points, matching or exceeding the LRM.
- Beats all merging baselines (Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE, AIM) on all suites.
- Generalizes to model sizes 1.5B–32B and to agentic tasks (AlfWorld, WebShop).
- Null-space projection effectively preserves format: missing ` termination rate near zero.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Projecting the ITM task vector onto the null space of forward features at thinking tokens preserves the LRM’s reasoning representations and output format.
- **Mechanism:** For each core submodule, build a measurement matrix Φ from hidden states at `<think>`/`` positions on a reasoning calibration set. The orthogonal projector onto N(Φ) ensures that any update in that null space yields zero linearized change in activations at those positions (Eq.5). Applying this to Δ_I yields Δ_⊥ that does not affect thinking tokens when added to θ_R.
- **Core assumption:** The thinking format (termination, distribution) is primarily determined by activations at those tokens; preserving them suffices.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] “Project the instruction‑tuned model’s task vector onto the null space of forward features at thinking special tokens to preserve the LRM’s reasoning representations.”; [section 3] “Stage 1 keeps the merged model’s intermediate representations and even the final logits at the thinking special tokens close to those of the anchor model.”; [section E.2] “Proposition 1: null‑space projection yields L_think ≈ 0.”
- **Break condition:** Mis‑specified null space if calibration set is unrepresentative; linearization may fail for large updates; cross‑turn dependencies not captured.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Per‑module coefficients from instruction‑attention alignment (response→instruction) and leakage (unrelated→instruction) boost instruction following while suppressing spurious activations.
- **Mechanism:** Compute alignment a_̃ (average attention from response tokens to instruction tokens) and leakage u_̃ (average to unrelated tokens) for each attention head on an instruction calibration set using the intermediate merged model θ'. Approximate gradient g_̃ = a_̃ − ρ u_̃ and diagonal Hessian H̃ = 1 + u_̃. Then α = clip(g_̃ / H̃) within [0,1] gives head‑wise scaling; FFN layers take the head average.
- **Core assumption:** Attention distributions are predictive of instruction compliance; scaling by α steers behavior.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] “Weight the projected components using per‑module coefficients derived from small‑calibration attention alignment scores that amplify instruction‑relevant activations while suppressing leakage.”; [section 3] “Stage 2 … an ideal merge should yield stronger attention responses on instruction‑relevant spans (high alignment) while maintaining low attention activation on instruction‑irrelevant content (low leakage).”; [corpus] Related works on instruction‑following evaluation (e.g., “Instruction‑Following Evaluation of Large Vision‑Language Models”) support the attention‑instruction link, but direct validation comes from the paper’s own ablation (Fig.6, Tab.4).
- **Break condition:** Calibration set may not span diverse instructions; coarse treatment of non‑attention modules; quadratic approximation may fail for large α.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Near‑orthogonality of the LRM and ITM task vectors in principal subspaces enables clean linear merging with minimal interference.
- **Mechanism:** For each layer/submodule, perform SVD on Δ_R = θ_R − θ_B and Δ_I = θ_I − θ_B. The cosine similarity between the top‑S left singular subspaces is consistently low (<0.1) (Fig.2), indicating that the two task vectors lie in nearly disjoint directions. This suggests that arithmetic combination does not cause significant cancellation.
- **Core assumption:** Parameter orthogonality translates to functional non‑interference.
- **Evidence:** [abstract] “principal subspaces are nearly orthogonal …”; [section 2] “Fig.2: Principal subspace cosine similarity … consistently low (<0.1).”; [section E.1] “This phenomenon reflects a low degree of coupling …”.
- **Break condition:** Orthogonality may vary across model families/sizes; orthogonality alone does not guarantee output invariance (hence Stage 1 needed).

## Foundational Learning

**Task Vectors and Principal Subspace Orthogonality**
- *Why needed*: RAIN-Merging’s premise is that fine‑tuning differences are representable as task vectors that can be combined without interference, supported by observed orthogonality.
- *Quick check*: Given two task matrices Δ_R and Δ_I, compute SVD of each, extract top‑S left singular vectors U_R and U_I, and compute cosine similarity = ‖diag(U_R^T U_I)‖_2. A value near 0 indicates near‑orthogonal subspaces.

**Null‑Space Projection for Activation Invariance**
- *Why needed*: Stage 1 uses a forward feature operator Φ to construct a null space that guarantees unchanged activations at thinking tokens, preserving reasoning format.
- *Quick check*: For a measurement matrix Φ, the projector onto N(Φ) is P_⊥ = I − Φ^T (ΦΦ^T + εI)^{−1} Φ. Verify that P_⊥ Φ^T = 0 and that for any δ, Φ P_⊥ δ = 0.

**Attention‑Alignment as Proxy for Instruction Following**
- *Why needed*: Stage 2 relies on attention weights to derive coefficients; understanding the metrics (alignment, leakage) is key.
- *Quick check*: In a transformer head, denote attention weight matrix A ∈ [0,1]^{L×L}. Given token sets I (instruction), R (relevant response), U (unrelated response), define alignment a = (1/(|I||R|)) Σ_{t∈R} Σ_{τ∈I} A[t,τ]; leakage u = (1/(|I||U|)) Σ_{t∈U} Σ_{τ∈I} A[t,τ]. Larger a and smaller u correlate with instruction following.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Base model θ_B → LRM θ_R & ITM θ_I → compute Δ_I → for each core submodule (Q,K,V,O,FFN): use D_R to build Φ_Ωthink → compute P_⊥ → apply to Δ_I^k → Δ_⊥^k → form provisional θ' = θ_R + Σ Δ_⊥^k → use D_I to compute per‑head attention metrics a_̃, u_̃ → derive α_k (FFN: average over heads) → final θ* = θ_R + λ Σ α_k Δ_⊥^k.

### Critical path
1. Compute Δ_I = θ_I − θ_B.  
2. For each core submodule, construct Φ from hidden states at `<think>`/`` positions using D_R.  
3. Compute orthogonal projector P_⊥ and project Δ_I to obtain Δ_⊥.  
4. Form intermediate merged model θ' = θ_R + Σ Δ_⊥.  
5. Run θ' on D_I to obtain per‑head attention weights.  
6. Compute alignment a_̃ and leakage u_̃ for each head, then α_k via closed‑form (Eq.15).  
7. Aggregate coefficients for non‑attention modules.  
8. Apply global scaling λ and produce final merged model.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Calibration size**: Larger D_R/D_I improve stability but increase compute; paper uses 150/365.
- **Granularity**: Per‑head coefficients enable fine‑tuning but require more samples; per‑layer is cheaper but coarser.
- **λ scaling**: Global scalar trades reasoning preservation vs. instruction gain; needs validation tuning.
- **Numerical stability**: FP64 projection avoids rounding errors; storage in BF16 saves memory.

### Failure signatures
- High missing `` rate → Stage 1 null‑space mis‑specification.
- Reasoning benchmarks drop → λ too large or α mis‑scaled.
- No instruction improvement on IFEval → faulty span extraction or calibration set mismatch.
- High variance across runs → small calibration sets or unstable coefficient derivation.
- OOM during Φ construction → reduce samples or use subset of thinking tokens.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Ablate Stage 1**: Merge without null‑space projection; measure ` `` missing rate and L_think.
2. **Ablate Stage 2**: Keep projection but set all α_k=1; evaluate instruction‑attention score and IFEval accuracy.
3. **λ sweep**: Run merges with λ ∈ [0.0,1.5]; plot instruction vs. reasoning metrics to locate Pareto optimum.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multimodal and tool use**: The method has only been evaluated on text‑only tasks; performance in multimodal or tool‑using agents remains unknown.
- **Safety risks**: The ethics statement notes potential drift in safety properties; no empirical safety evaluation is provided.
- **Editing beyond parameter arithmetic**: Potential to extend RAIN‑Merging paradigms to other model editing or fine‑tuning regimes is unexplored.

## Limitations
- Calibration sets (150 reasoning, 365 instruction) are not publicly released; exact samples may affect reproducibility.
- Submodule granularity (per‑layer vs. per‑head) is ambiguous without code; results might depend on this choice.
- Method assumes explicit `<think>`/` delimiters; models with implicit CoT (e.g., GPT) are not evaluated.
- No safety or bias benchmarks; instruction following improvements may have unintended ethical consequences.

## Confidence
- **Thinking format preservation**: *Medium* – First‑order invariance proven, but higher‑order effects not fully characterized.
- **Instruction‑following boost**: *Medium* – Strong empirical gains, but reliance on small calibration sets and heuristic proxy.
- **Orthogonality claim**: *Low–Medium* – Observed on Qwen/DeepSeek families only; generalizability untested.

## Next Checks
1. Conduct null‑space ablation: compare full RAIN‑Merging against removal of Stage 1; verify missing `` rate and L_think on a held‑out reasoning set.
2. Test coefficient necessity: run Stage 1 merge with uniform α_k=1; measure drop in IFEval and instruction‑attention score; optionally try random α.
3. Apply to a different architecture (e.g., Llama‑3.1‑8B): construct appropriate base, reasoning, and instruction models; repeat the pipeline to assess transferability.
