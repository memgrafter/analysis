# Not Just How Much, But Where: Decomposing Epistemic Uncertainty into Per-Class Contributions
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.21160
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21160
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Decomposes mutual information into per-class contributions \(C_k = \sigma_k^2/(2\mu_k)\), enabling targeted uncertainty reduction for critical classes and improving selective prediction and out-of-distribution detection.

## Executive Summary
Standard mutual information (MI) quantifies overall epistemic uncertainty in Bayesian deep learning but fails to indicate which class(es) are responsible. This paper addresses this by deriving a per-class decomposition \(C_k(x) = \sigma_k^2/(2\mu_k)\) from a second-order Taylor expansion of entropy, where \(\mu_k\) and \(\sigma_k^2\) are the mean and variance of class probabilities across posterior samples. The \(1/\mu_k\) weighting corrects boundary suppression, making contributions comparable across rare and common classes, and by design \(\sum_k C_k \approx \text{MI}\). A skewness diagnostic flags when the approximation degrades. The method is applied to selective prediction, out-of-distribution detection, and uncertainty disentanglement, showing significant improvements by targeting critical classes. The findings highlight that posterior approximation quality strongly influences uncertainty behavior.

## Method Summary
Given an input \(x\), the method draws \(S\) stochastic forward passes from a Bayesian posterior approximation (e.g., MC dropout, variational inference, ensembles) to obtain class probability vectors \(p^{(s)}(x)\). For each class \(k\), it computes the mean \(\mu_k\) and variance \(\sigma_k^2\) across samples. The per-class epistemic contribution is \(C_k = \sigma_k^2/(2\mu_k)\), with a small \(\varepsilon\) added to the denominator for numerical stability. The sum \(\sum_k C_k\) approximates mutual information (MI), providing a scalar uncertainty measure while the vector \(C(x)\) attributes uncertainty to specific classes. A skewness diagnostic \(\rho_k = |m_{3,k}|/(3\mu_k \sigma_k^2)\) uses third moments to detect unreliability (\(\rho_k > 0.3\)), triggering fallback strategies like CBEC. This approach enables input-specific, per-class uncertainty analysis without additional training.

## Key Results
- Selective prediction for diabetic retinopathy: Using \(C_{\text{crit\_max}}\) (max over critical classes) reduced area under the selective risk curve (AUSC) by 34.7% over MI and 56.2% over variance baselines.
- Out-of-distribution detection: \(\sum_k C_k\) achieved the highest AUROC on clinical and image benchmarks, and revealed asymmetric distributional shifts invisible to scalar MI.
- Disentanglement under label noise: \(\sum_k C_k\) showed lower sensitivity to aleatoric noise than MI in end-to-end Bayesian training, while both degraded under transfer learning, highlighting posterior quality importance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** The per-class epistemic vector \(C(x)\) approximates each class's contribution to mutual information.
**Mechanism:** Starting from \(\text{MI} = H(\mu) - \mathbb{E}[H(p)]\), expand \(H(p)\) in a second-order Taylor series around the mean prediction \(\mu\). The Hessian of \(H\) is diagonal with \(\partial^2 H/\partial p_k^2 = -1/\mu_k\), so the quadratic term yields \(\frac12 \text{Var}[p_k]/\mu_k\) for each class. Summing over \(k\) gives \(\text{MI} \approx \sum_k C_k\) with \(C_k = \frac12 \text{Var}[p_k]/\mu_k\).
**Core assumption:** The posterior distribution of \(p\) is sufficiently concentrated that third-order and higher terms are negligible.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “derived from a second‑order Taylor expansion of the entropy”; Section 2.1 Theorem 2.2 and Appendix B.1; neighboring work focuses on scalar measures, novelty is per-class vector.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** The \(1/\mu_k\) factor in \(C_k\) corrects boundary suppression, making contributions comparable across rare and common classes.
**Mechanism:** On the probability simplex, \(\text{Var}[p_k] \le \mu_k(1-\mu_k)\), forcing raw variance to vanish as \(\mu_k \to 0\) or \(1\) even if logit-space disagreement persists. Dividing by \(\mu_k\) counteracts this compression, yielding \(C_k \le \frac12(1-\mu_k)\) which stays finite as \(\mu_k \to 0\).
**Core assumption:** Variance alone is an unfair proxy for epistemic uncertainty when class base rates differ; entropy curvature provides the correct re-weighting.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “the \(1/\mu_k\) weighting corrects boundary suppression”; Section 2.3 Lemmas 2.4 and 2.5; related work on variance limitations.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** The vector \(C(x)\) provides input-specific, per-class attribution that sums to approximately MI.
**Mechanism:** By construction from the same second-order expansion, the diagonal Hessian ensures no cross-class terms, so \(\sum_k C_k\) matches the quadratic approximation to MI. The per-class variance estimates aggregate additively.
**Core assumption:** Off-diagonal covariance contributes negligibly to MI at second order; the trace of Hessian times covariance captures the dominant term.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract: “by construction \(\sum_k C_k \approx \text{MI}\)”; Section 2.2 Definition 2.3; Section 2.5 skewness diagnostic; related work on pairwise decompositions.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**
Posterior sampling module -> Statistics estimator -> Cₖ calculator -> Aggregation & fallback -> Skewness diagnostic -> Decision engine

**Critical path**
1. Input \(x\) → \(S\) forward passes → \(p^{(s)}\) vectors.
2. Compute \(\mu_k\), \(\sigma_k^2\), \(m_{3,k}\) for all \(k\).
3. Calculate \(C_k\).
4. Compute \(\rho_k\); if any critical \(\rho_k > 0.3\) compute CBEC using empirical covariance and correlations; else use \(C_{\text{crit\_max}}\) or \(\sum_k C_k\).
5. Compare final score to deferral threshold to output decision.

**Design tradeoffs**
- Computational cost: \(S\) forward passes; \(O(KS)\) time. For many classes, CBEC uses only \(|S|\times|C|\) pairwise terms.
- Choice of \(S\): More samples reduce Monte Carlo error; paper uses \(S=30–512\).
- Rare classes: \(1/\mu_k\) amplifies contributions when \(\mu_k\) tiny, desirable but may cause numerical instability; \(\varepsilon\)-stabilisation used.
- Reliability vs robustness: Under well-calibrated posteriors (low-rank VI from scratch), \(C_{\text{crit\_max}}\) optimal; under approximate posteriors (MC dropout), CBEC more robust due to correlation gate.

**Failure signatures**
- High skewness: \(\rho_k > 0.3\) for a critical class indicates approximation may be inaccurate; consider CBEC or increasing \(S\).
- Collapse of \(\mu_k\): If \(\mu_k \approx 0\) for a relevant class, \(C_k\) becomes large; monitor for outliers.
- Discrepancy between \(\sum_k C_k\) and MI: Large positive residual suggests third-order effects dominate; trigger fallback.
- Regime reversal: Under MC dropout, \(C_{\text{crit\_max}}\) may underperform while CBEC strong; indicates inflated \(\rho_k\).

**First 3 experiments**
1. **Selective prediction for diabetic retinopathy:** Evaluate \(C_{\text{crit\_max}}\) against baselines on reducing critical false negative rate at fixed coverage. Use Bayesian EfficientNet-B4 on pooled DR datasets; metric AUSC.
2. **Out-of-distribution detection:** Test \(\sum_k C_k\) as OoD score against MI, EU_var, MSP on FashionMNIST→KMNIST and MIMIC-III→Newborn; measure AUROC.
3. **Disentanglement under label noise:** Inject symmetric label noise at rates \(\alpha\in[0.1,0.5]\) and compute relative disentanglement ratio \(R_{\text{rel}}(\alpha)\) comparing epistemic vs. aleatoric increase. Compare end-to-end Bayesian training vs. transfer learning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question:** How can the per-class epistemic decomposition \(C(x)\) be extended to structured prediction tasks (e.g., segmentation, sequence labeling) where class identity is not cleanly defined per pixel or token?
**Basis in paper:** Explicitly stated in Discussion: "Future work includes extending \(C(x)\) to structured prediction tasks where class identity is less cleanly defined."
**Why unresolved:** Current derivation assumes discrete, independent class probability vector per input; structured outputs involve dependencies, making per-class attribution non-trivial. Additive decomposition relies on diagonal Hessian, which may not hold.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical extension to structured settings (e.g., via CRF potentials or token-level variance) validated on segmentation/NLP tasks, showing per-module/per-label uncertainty attribution.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** What architectural and algorithmic advances are needed to make end-to-end Bayesian training practically scalable to modern deep networks without sacrificing uncertainty quality?
**Basis in paper:** Explicit conclusion: "making end-to-end Bayesian training more practical and scalable strikes us as a more promising path than refining post-hoc corrections." Also, transfer learning degrades disentanglement.
**Why unresolved:** End-to-end Bayesian methods (e.g., low-rank VI) limited to smaller architectures due to memory/compute costs. Posterior quality—strongest under full Bayesian training—governs metric reliability, but full training infeasible for large models.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Scalable Bayesian method (e.g., smarter factorization, distributed inference) matching full Bayesian uncertainty quality on large benchmarks, computationally competitive with deterministic training.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** How can the second-order Taylor approximation underlying \(C_k\) be robustly corrected or replaced in high-skewness regimes (\(\rho_k > 0.3\)), especially for rare critical classes?
**Basis in paper:** Inferred: Authors introduce skewness diagnostic \(\rho_k\) to flag degradation but state it "does not correct this." They propose CBEC as fallback but requires safe/critical partitions and correlation gating.
**Why unresolved:** Third-order correction \(C_k^{(3)}\) can become negative near simplex boundary, violating non-negativity. Diagnostic only warns, no principled class-specific correction retaining additivity/interpretability. Rare safety-critical classes most vulnerable.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Mathematically sound higher-order approximation or alternative (e.g., convex duality, variational bounds) that remains non-negative, additive to MI, reliable when \(\mu_k \to 0\), validated on rare-class selective prediction.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Do mitigation strategies (e.g., truncated summation, probability-weighted aggregation) effectively control \(O(K^2)\) scaling of \(\sum_k C_k\) in high-cardinality classification (\(K > 50\)) without sacrificing per-class interpretability?
**Basis in paper:** Inferred: Authors note in Appendix F.3 that \(1/\mu_k\) causes \(\sum_k C_k\) to scale as \(O(K^2)\) as class probabilities shrink, suggest truncation/reweighting but not evaluated.
**Why unresolved:** Without mitigation, \(\sum_k C_k\) may become numerically unstable or inflated by negligible classes, reducing utility as scalar summary. Truncation loses info, reweighting changes additive link to MI. Trade-offs unexplored.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical evaluation on high-cardinality datasets (e.g., ImageNet-1k) comparing mitigations against MI and baselines, assessing scalar correlation with exact MI and per-class utility in downstream tasks.

### Open Question 5
**Question:** How does \(C(x)\) behave under alternative posterior approximations (e.g., Laplace, SWA, deeper ensembles), and can skewness diagnostic predict their reliability?
**Basis in paper:** Inferred: Authors evaluate only MFVI, low-rank VI, MC dropout, ensembles; state: "Characterising how \(C_k\) behaves under other approximate inference schemes [...] is a natural next step." Posterior quality drastically changes \(C_k\) reliability.
**Why unresolved:** Taylor approximation assumes posterior samples approximately Gaussian and concentrated. Laplace gives Gaussian around modes, SWA yields averaged weights, deeper ensembles capture multi-modality. Interplay between posterior shape, skewness, \(C_k\) validity not generalised beyond tested methods.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic study of \(C_k\) across diverse posterior approximations on controlled tasks (synthetic 2D Gaussians, Bayesian linear models) where true MI computable, correlating \(\rho_k\) with approximation error to validate diagnostic's predictive power across methods.

## Limitations
- **Approximation reliance:** Second-order Taylor expansion assumes concentrated posterior; high skewness (\(\rho_k > 0.3\)) breaks down, requiring fallback.
- **Rare-class instability:** \(1/\mu_k\) amplifies noise when \(\mu_k \approx 0\); \(\varepsilon\)-regularization helps but may distort estimates for extremely rare classes.
- **Posterior-quality dependence:** Gains shown with well-calibrated approximations (low-rank VI, ensembles); under MC dropout performance often degrades, limiting practical adoption.
