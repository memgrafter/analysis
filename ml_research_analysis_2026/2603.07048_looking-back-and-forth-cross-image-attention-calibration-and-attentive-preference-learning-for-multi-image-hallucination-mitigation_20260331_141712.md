# Looking Back and Forth: Cross-Image Attention Calibration and Attentive Preference Learning for Multi-Image Hallucination Mitigation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07048
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07048
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Key outcome:** Gains of 1-3 points on BLINK and up to 3.5 points on MUIRBench across diverse LVLM architectures, without sacrificing single-image performance.

## Executive Summary
This work addresses multi-image hallucinations in large vision-language models (LVLMs) caused by asymmetric causal attention and insufficient cross-image modeling. The authors propose CAPL, a framework that combines selective cross-image attention calibration with attentive preference learning via DPO. By enabling bidirectional token interactions at key image positions and constructing challenging negatives through attention truncation, CAPL systematically reduces hallucination errors while preserving or slightly improving performance on general and single-image tasks. Empirical results show consistent gains on hallucination benchmarks and robust cross-task generalization.

## Method Summary
CAPL modifies the autoregressive attention mechanism in LVLMs to enable symmetric cross-image information flow at selected key tokens (based on embedding energy). Positive responses are generated with this calibrated attention; negative responses are elicited by truncating all cross-image attention, forcing reliance on priors. These pairs are used in a hybrid DPO+NLL loss training objective, implemented via LoRA fine-tuning. The approach is evaluated on multiple hallucination benchmarks (BLINK, MUIRBench) and general/multi-image tasks, showing consistent hallucination reduction without harming other capabilities.

## Key Results
- **BLINK improvement:** 1-3 points accuracy gain across diverse LVLM architectures.
- **MUIRBench improvement:** Up to 3.5 points accuracy gain, demonstrating strong hallucination mitigation.
- **Task preservation:** No significant degradation on general multi-image (NLVR2, QBench2) or single-image benchmarks (POPE, CHAIR); some tasks show slight improvement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Asymmetric causal attention in standard LVLMs creates unidirectional inter-image information flow, hindering symmetric relational modeling and increasing susceptibility to text-prior hallucinations.
- **Mechanism:** Introduces a selective cross-image attention mask that reactivates bidirectional attention between key tokens from different images while preserving causal structure within each image. Key tokens are selected via embedding energy (∥h∥²) with ratio ρ, and alternating mask application across decoder layers balances stability and cross-image interaction.
- **Core assumption:** Multi-image hallucinations stem partly from weak or asymmetric visual information exchange across images; enabling structured bidirectional flow at key token positions mitigates this.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...limitations in existing attention mechanisms and insufficient cross-image modeling... enable bidirectional token interactions"
  - [section 3.2] "The cross-image mask Mcross... allows bidirectional attention only between tokens from different images... selective cross-image mask Mcross_sel... between key tokens from different images"
  - [corpus] Weak direct corpus evidence; neighboring papers (e.g., "Mitigating Cross-Image Information Leakage") also identify cross-image entanglement but propose different mitigation.
- **Break condition:** If symmetric cross-image attention degrades single-image or temporal-dependent multi-image performance, indicating over-correction.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Hallucination-prone responses can be systematically elicited by truncating all cross-image attention, forcing reliance on per-image and language priors, yielding more challenging negative samples for preference learning.
- **Mechanism:** Constructs negative samples by applying a mask (Mtrunc) that blocks all inter-image attention while preserving intra-image causality. Contrasts outputs from this "truncated" setup (y⁻) with those from the enhanced cross-image setup (y⁺) under DPO.
- **Core assumption:** The model’s inherent hallucination patterns are exposed when cross-image evidence is explicitly denied; these samples provide stronger training signals than unelicited negatives.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Negative samples are constructed by truncating inter-image attention, forcing hallucinations from text priors."
  - [section 3.3] "To generate more hallucinated negative responses, we exploit this limitation by completely truncating all cross-image attention connections... Mtrunc... Text generated under this mask serves as the negative sample."
  - [section 4.3, Fig. 3] "Truncated negatives exhibit consistently lower accuracy... decreases by about 20%"
- **Break condition:** If truncated negatives do not produce significantly lower accuracy (or factual error rate) compared to model-native negatives, the elicited hallucination signal is weak.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Combining DPO with token-level NLL loss on positive samples yields more stable training and better internalization of cross-image reasoning paths than DPO alone.
- **Mechanism:** Uses hybrid loss Ltotal = L_DPO(πθ; π_ref) + λ·L_NLL(πθ). DPO aligns preferences between (y⁺, y⁻) pairs; NLL forces imitation of the positive sample’s token sequence, preventing degradation of language modeling capability.
- **Core assumption:** Pure preference optimization may improve relative ordering but not fully instill the structured reasoning process; auxiliary imitation of high-quality trajectories stabilizes learning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.4] "The DPO term enforces relative preference alignment... while the NLL term reinforces token-level imitation... This hybrid objective improves optimization stability"
  - [section 4.3, Table 3] Combined training shows larger gains than attention-only or DPO-only ablations, particularly on MUIRBench.
- **Break condition:** If λ optimization consistently harms either preference alignment (DPO signal weakened) or fluency (high NLL loss hurts held-out tasks), the balance is brittle.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Autoregressive causal attention in LVLMs.
  - **Why needed here:** Understanding the default unidirectional token visibility (j ≤ i) is prerequisite to seeing how CAPL’s mask modifications alter information flow.
  - **Quick check question:** In a standard causal mask, can an early image token attend to a later image token? What does this imply for cross-image relational symmetry?

- **Concept:** Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).
  - **Why needed here:** CAPL’s training core contrasts positive/negative sample likelihoods; grasping the implicit reward modeling and β temperature is essential.
  - **Quick check question:** How does DPO avoid explicit reward modeling? What role does the reference policy π_ref play?

- **Concept:** Multi-image hallucination taxonomy.
  - **Why needed here:** Distinguishing errors from cross-image confusion vs. single-image grounding is key to understanding CAPL’s targeted design (cross-image attention + truncation).
  - **Quick check question:** Give an example of a multi-image hallucination that would not occur if cross-image attention were perfect.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Base LVLM -> Attention Modifier (Mcross_sel, Mtrunc masks) -> Key Token Scorer -> Sample Generator (y⁺, y⁻) -> DPO Trainer (hybrid loss) -> Fine-tuned LVLM

- **Critical path:** Modify forward pass mask logic → generate paired dataset (y⁺, y⁻) → train with hybrid DPO+NLL → evaluate on multi-image hallucination benchmarks (BLINK, MUIRBench)

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Selectivity ρ:** Low ρ restricts information flow; high ρ introduces noise. Empirically ρ≈0.9–0.95.
  - **Mask alternation:** Odd vs. even layer switching balances cross-image modeling and autoregressive stability.
  - **Negative construction:** Truncation yields harder negatives but may overforce priors; ensure y⁻ remains plausible yet incorrect.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - No gain or regression on MUIRBench/BLINK.
  - Significant drop on single-image benchmarks (POPE, CHAIR) or NLVR2.
  - Training instability (loss spikes, low NLL increase without DPO improvement).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Sanity check:** Run inference with only Mcross_sel (no training) on a few multi-image samples; check if outputs become more grounded in cross-image details.
  2. **Negative quality check:** Measure accuracy of y⁻ vs. model-native outputs on a held-out set; expect y⁻ to be lower.
  3. **Hyperparameter sweep:** Small-scale grid search over ρ ∈ {0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 1.0} and λ ∈ {0, 1, 2.5, 5} on validation subset of MUIRBench.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Question 1:** How does CAPL generalize to multi-image tasks with inherent temporal dependencies where image order is critical?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The alternating mask strategy preserves sequential structure but evaluation is on static image benchmarks (BLINK, MUIRBench) without temporal tasks.
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper doesn't test on temporally sensitive scenarios, so it's unclear if cross-image attention interferes with temporal reasoning.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluate CAPL on video-based or sequential image tasks to measure performance impact.

- **Question 2:** Can the optimal key token selection ratio (ρ) be adaptively determined per input rather than fixed per model?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] ρ is manually set for each model (0.95/0.9) based on empirical results, and performance varies with ρ in ablations.
  - **Why unresolved:** Manual tuning is required; no adaptive mechanism proposed in CAPL.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop a dynamic ρ selection based on image content or task and show improved or stable performance without hand-tuning.

- **Question 3:** Beyond attention asymmetry, what role do other factors (e.g., pretraining data biases) play in multi-image hallucinations?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Negative samples are constructed solely by truncating attention, assuming that is the primary cause, but other hallucination sources aren't disentangled.
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper doesn't isolate attention from data or architectural factors, so the scope of CAPL's effectiveness is unclear.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled studies varying data quality or model pretraining while keeping attention fixed to measure residual hallucinations.

## Limitations

- Gains may not transfer to non-autoregressive architectures or significantly larger models beyond the tested 7B-9B scales.
- The optimal key token selection ratio (ρ) requires manual tuning per model, lacking an adaptive mechanism.
- The role of pretraining data biases in multi-image hallucinations is not isolated, so CAPL’s effectiveness may be limited to attention-related causes.

## Confidence

- **High confidence** in the observed empirical gains on BLINK/MUIRBench across diverse models—these are direct, measurable outcomes.
- **Medium confidence** in the causal explanation linking asymmetric attention to hallucinations, given supporting ablation studies but limited architectural generalization evidence.
- **Low confidence** in the necessity and replicability of the Qwen3 feedback step for positive sample refinement, due to lack of procedural detail.

## Next Checks

1. **Architecture stress test:** Apply CAPL to a different LVLM family (e.g., LLaVA, BLIP-2) and verify hallucination reduction on BLINK/MUIRBench.
2. **Negative sample sensitivity:** Replace truncation-based negatives with an alternative (e.g., random corruption) and measure if performance drops, validating the elicitation hypothesis.
3. **Single-image regression check:** Run CAPL on a large single-image benchmark (e.g., POPE) to confirm no degradation, ensuring cross-task robustness.
