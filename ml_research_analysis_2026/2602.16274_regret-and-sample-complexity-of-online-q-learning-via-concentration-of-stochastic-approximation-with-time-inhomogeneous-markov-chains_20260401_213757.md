# Regret and Sample Complexity of Online Q-Learning via Concentration of Stochastic Approximation with Time-Inhomogeneous Markov Chains
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.16274
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16274
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Establishes the first high-probability regret guarantees for classical online Q-learning in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs without optimism or bonus terms, via a novel concentration bound for contractive stochastic approximation with time-inhomogeneous Markov chains.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies online Q-learning in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs, addressing the challenge of proving high-probability regret bounds without relying on optimism or explicit bonus terms. The core contribution is a new concentration inequality for contractive stochastic approximation where the Markovian noise stems from time-inhomogeneous chains whose mixing time can diverge as exploration diminishes. This technical tool enables the analysis of two model-free exploration schemes: Boltzmann Q-learning, whose regret critically depends on the MDP’s suboptimality gap (sublinear only for sufficiently large gaps), and Smoothed εₙ‑Greedy (SεG), which achieves gap‑robust near‑Õ(N^{9/10}) regret. The work also provides sample‑complexity results, recovering best known offline rates for SεG.

## Method Summary
The paper frames Q‑learning updates within a stochastic approximation (SA) recursion Q_{n+1} = Q_n + β_n( F(Q_n, y_n) − Q_n + M_{n+1} ), where y_n is a controlled Markov chain whose transition kernel depends on the current iterate Q_n and an exploration control ζ_n (temperature λ_n or (ε_n, λ_n)). Two algorithms are analyzed:
1. **Boltzmann Q‑learning** (Alg. 1): Actions are drawn from a softmax distribution σ(Q_n(·)/λ_n). Temperature decays as λ_n = 1/(b log(n+n_0)). The stepsize β_n = β/(n+n_0)^{1−a}.
2. **Smoothed εₙ‑Greedy (SεG)** (Alg. 2): Mixes uniform ε_n with Boltzmann λ_n, i.e., P(a_n = a) = ε_n/|A| + (1−ε_n)σ(Q_n(s_n,a)/λ_n). Schedules are ε_n = 1/(n+n_0)^d, λ_n = 1/(n+n_0)^e with d > e > 0, and β_n as above.
The key technical result (Theorem 2.1) provides a high‑probability bound on ∥x_n − x*∥ for such SA with time‑varying contraction factor α(ζ_n) → 1 (mixing time diverges). This bound is then combined with probabilistic couplings to translate Q‑value errors into regret.

## Key Results
- Novel high‑probability concentration bound for contractive SA with time‑inhomogeneous Markovian noise, allowing the contraction factor to converge to 1 asymptotically (mixing time may diverge).
- For Boltzmann Q‑learning, regret is sublinear only if the MDP’s suboptimality gap is sufficiently large relative to the temperature decay rate b; otherwise, it can become near‑linear.
- Smoothed εₙ‑Greedy achieves a gap‑robust high‑probability regret of near‑Õ(N^{9/10}) and a sample complexity of near‑Õ(1/√n) convergence, with overall Õ(1/(1−γ)^5 ε^2) – recovering best known offline rates.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Novel high‑probability concentration bound for contractive stochastic approximation with time‑inhomogeneous Markov chains where mixing time can diverge.
- **Mechanism**: Combines a time‑varying contraction factor α(ζ_n) → 1 with iterate‑ and time‑dependent transition kernels by solving Poisson equations for each (ζ, x) and averaging Markovian noise via an auxiliary process {z_n}. The bound controls ∥x_n − x*∥ by separately bounding ∥x_n − z_n∥ (martingale decomposition) and ∥z_n − x*∥ (recursion with drift).
- **Core assumption**: Assumptions 1–5 (controlled Markov chain, irreducibility with uniform stationary lower bound, contractive stationary average with common fixed point, contraction factor scaling µ_min(ζ), time‑smoothness and iterate‑sensitivity bounds on transition kernel).
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states the bound allows the contraction factor to converge to one; Section 2 develops the bound; prior work (Chandak et al., 2022) required uniform mixing, while this relaxes that.
- **Break condition**: Condition 1 must hold: mixing time growth rate (κ₁) and kernel sensitivity rates (κ₂, κ₃) must satisfy 2a+6κ₁+max{a+κ₃, κ₂} < 1. For Boltzmann, small MDP gaps can cause λ_n to decay slowly or L₂(ζ_n) to grow too fast, violating Assumption 5(c).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Boltzmann Q‑learning with decaying temperature achieves sublinear regret only if the MDP’s suboptimality gap is sufficiently large; otherwise regret can be near‑linear.
- **Mechanism**: Temperature λ_n = 1/(b log(n+n_0)) reduces exploration. Regret scales as Õ(N^{0.5+4a})/(1−γ)^{3.5} + Õ(N^{1−b·gap(M)/2}). The first term is estimation error from SA concentration; the second arises from non‑vanishing Boltzmann probabilities on suboptimal actions until Q‑values separate by ~λ_n.
- **Core assumption**: MDP has uniform positive lower bound on stationary probabilities under any policy (Assumption 10); temperature decay rate b must scale with gap(M).
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 4.1 characterizes gap dependence; Theorem 4.2 gives explicit trade‑off.
- **Break condition**: For tiny gap(M), b must be reduced to increase exploration, which slows SA convergence (first term dominates) and may fail to make 1−b·gap(M)/2 < 1. No universal b yields sublinear regret for all MDPs without knowing gap(M).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Smoothed εₙ‑Greedy (SεG) Q‑learning achieves gap‑robust high‑probability regret near Õ(N^{9/10}).
- **Mechanism**: Policy f(ε_n, λ_n, Q_n) mixes uniform ε_n with Boltzmann λ_n. The uniform component ensures sufficient exploration for all state‑actions regardless of gap size, while the Boltzmann component ensures smooth transition kernel dependence on Q_n (needed for Assumption 5). Schedules ε_n = 1/(n+n_0)^d, λ_n = 1/(n+n_0)^e with d > e > 0 and tuned hyperparameters yield the rate.
- **Core assumption**: Assumptions 1–9 hold for combined policy; uniform exploration guarantees µ_min > 0 (Assumption 2) and decaying λ_n keeps L₂(ζ_n) growing slowly (Assumption 5(c)).
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 4.2 states gap‑robust bound; Theorem 4.4 gives RN ≤ Õ(N^{9/10})/(1−γ)^{3.5} for a = d = 0.1, e → 0.
- **Break condition**: Condition 9 imposes 2a+6d+4e < 1, d ≤ a, e < d. Optimal d=a=0.1, e→0 requires e very small, increasing sample complexity. Factor (1−γ)^{−3.5} suggests poor scaling as γ → 1.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Contractive Stochastic Approximation (SA)
- **Why needed**: Q‑learning is an SA update. The paper’s core result is a concentration bound for contractive SA with time‑inhomogeneous Markovian noise. Understanding SA convergence (e.g., ODE method, Robbins‑Siegmund) is prerequisite.
- **Quick check**: How does the proof handle the fact that the contraction factor (1−α(ζ_n)) decays to zero as exploration vanishes? What is the role of the auxiliary process {z_n}?

### Concept: Mixing Time of Markov Chains
- **Why needed**: Contraction factor α(ζ) ties to µ_min(ζ), which controls mixing time under policy σ(Q/λ). As λ → 0, µ_min(ζ) → 0 and mixing time → ∞. The paper’s relaxation allows this divergence.
- **Quick check**: Why does the hitting‑time bound in Assumption 2 (E[T_*] ≤ c₁/µ_min(ζ)) require µ_min(ζ) > 0 uniformly over Q? How does this fail for very small gaps in Boltzmann exploration?

### Concept: Exploration‑Exploitation Trade‑off in Policy‑Iteration‑like Updates
- **Why needed**: Q‑learning is policy‑iteration in value space. Exploration policy (Boltzmann or SεG) influences Markov transition kernel {y_n}. Analysis must track how exploration parameters affect SA noise properties (Assumptions 5 and 6).
- **Quick check**: In Boltzmann Q‑learning, how does temperature λ_n appear in the Lipschitz constant L₂(ζ) for the transition kernel? What does Figure 1 illustrate about this sensitivity?

### Concept: High‑Probability Concentration for Adaptive Data
- **Why needed**: The main technical result gives a high‑probability bound (not just in expectation) for SA with dependent, adaptively chosen data. This is essential for regret guarantees.
- **Quick check**: Why do standard martingale concentration inequalities (e.g., Freedman’s) fail here? How does the time‑varying contraction α(ζ_n) complicate the argument?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: SA Core (Eq. 1) -> Controlled Markov Chain {y_n} (depends on ζ_n, Q_n) -> Assumptions Bundle (1‑6) -> Concentration Engine (Theorem 2.1) -> Algorithms (Boltzmann, SεG) -> Regret Analyzer (Section 4)
- **Critical path**:
  1. Verify MDP Assumptions 10/11 hold → ensure µ_min > 0 under exploration policies.
  2. Choose hyperparameters (a, b or d, e) satisfying Conditions 3/7 → keep Assumptions 5 (L₂ growth) and 4 (α decay) tame.
  3. Apply Theorem 2.1 to get ∥Q_n − Q*∥ bound on good set G.
  4. Use probabilistic coupling to convert ∥Q_n − Q*∥ to ∥p − p*∥.
  5. Sum per‑time‑step regret bounds over horizon.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Boltzmann vs. SεG**: Boltzmann is smoother but gap‑dependent; SεG uses uniform εₙ for gap‑robustness at cost of higher polynomial rate (9/10 vs. √N).
  - **Stepsize exponent a**: a = 0 (β_n ∝ 1/n) gives best SA rate O(1/√n) but requires larger n₀; a > 0 easier for conditions but worse rate.
  - **Temperature decay b / ε decay e**: Larger b/e slows SA rate (first term in regret) but reduces suboptimal selection (second term); optimal balance requires gap knowledge for Boltzmann.
  - **γ scaling**: κ₁ = bR_max/(1−γ) blows up as γ→1 unless b scales with (1−γ), forcing b small → slower exploration decay.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Violation of Assumption 2: µ_min(ζ_n) decays faster than (n+n₀)^{-κ₁}, causing mixing‑time blow‑up → concentration bound fails.
  - Violation of Assumption 5(c): Policy too sensitive (e.g., Boltzmann with tiny λₙ) makes L₂(ζ_n) grow too fast → dominates SA rate.
  - Gap unknown: Boltzmann may incur linear regret if b not chosen appropriately; SεG avoids this but needs tuned d, e with a.
  - γ → 1: Constants (1−γ)^{-3.5, -5} become huge; practical performance degrades unless hyperparameters scale with 1−γ.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Concentration validation**: Small MDP (e.g., 5 states, 2 actions), run both algorithms with known Q*. Plot ∥Q_n − Q*∥∞ vs n on log‑log. Check slope ≈ –0.5 when κ₁ small. Compare to Theorem 2.1’s bound.
  2. **Gap sensitivity test**: Two MDPs same size, gaps 0.1 vs. 0.001. Run Boltzmann with fixed b. Expect near‑linear regret for tiny gap, sublinear for large gap. Run SεG with optimized d,e; expect gap‑robust sublinear regret.
  3. **Hyperparameter sweep**: For SεG, vary d and e (d > e). Measure regret at large N. Optimal region near d = a = 0.1, e ≈ 0. Verify regret exponent ~0.9.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Can the Õ(N^{9/10}) regret guarantee for Smoothed ε‑Greedy Q‑learning be improved to match the Õ(√N) rates of optimism‑based methods, or is there a fundamental separation? The authors note it remains open whether the Õ(N^{9/10}) can be improved to the Õ(N^{2/3}) rates obtained for ε‑greedy in bandits, or whether a fundamental separation exists.
- **Open Question 2**: Can the concentration bound for contractive stochastic approximation with time‑inhomogeneous Markov chains be extended to average‑reward reinforcement learning? The authors suggest extending the framework to average‑reward RL algorithms as an interesting direction.
- **Open Question 3**: What are the minimax optimal regret rates for online Q‑learning without optimism, and how do they depend on MDP parameters? Is the Õ(N^{9/10}) rate for Smoothed ε‑Greedy tight, or can it be improved to Õ(N^{2/3}) as in multi‑armed bandits? Lower bounds for this setting are largely unknown.
- **Open Question 4**: Are the smoothness conditions on the transition kernel (Assumption 5) necessary for the high‑probability concentration bound, or can they be relaxed? Specifically, can the bound handle pure ε‑greedy exploration, which introduces a discontinuous dependence of the transition probabilities on the Q‑values? The introduction of SεG suggests smoothness is used to handle sensitivity; necessity is unclear.
- **Open Question 5**: Can the concentration bound be extended to settings with function approximation (e.g., linear or neural network Q‑approximators)? What additional assumptions (e.g., realizability, feature bounds, Bellman completeness) would be needed to maintain a similar high‑probability guarantee? The paper focuses on tabular Q‑learning; extension to function approximation is open.

## Limitations
- **Assumption strength**: Assumptions 1‑9 (irreducibility, time‑smoothness, iterate‑sensitivity) may be violated in practice; e.g., µ_min → 0 as exploration decays, causing mixing‑time blow‑up.
- **Implicit constants**: The Õ‑bounds hide factors with (1−γ)⁻⁵ and unknown mix‑time exponents; no explicit formula to set n₀ or predict sample size.
- **Boltzmann gap dependence**: Regret includes term N^{1−b·gap/2}; sublinearity requires b·gap ≳ O(1), which is unknown a priori. For small gap, the bound becomes near‑linear.
- **Suboptimal rates**: SεG achieves Õ(N^{9/10}), which is polynomial but far from √N; constants grow as γ → 1.

## Confidence
- **Concentration bound (Theorem 2.1)**: Medium – proof is rigorous but relies on strong assumptions and non‑explicit constants.
- **Boltzmann regret (Theorem 4.2)**: High – directly derived from the bound; gap‑dependence is transparent.
- **SεG regret (Theorem 4.4)**: Medium – exponent 9/10 likely loose; analysis requires delicate hyperparameter balancing.
- **Sample‑complexity results**: Medium – follow from the same bound; constant factors remain opaque.
