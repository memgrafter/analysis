# RaCo: Ranking and Covariance for Practical Learned Keypoints

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15755
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15755
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RaCo achieves state-of-the-art repeatability on HPatches (33.9% at 3px) and ETH3D (58.5%), superior rotation equivariance (AUC up to 79.5% @ 1px), and its covariance estimates improve 3D triangulation accuracy and completeness.

## Executive Summary
RaCo introduces a practical learned keypoint system combining a lightweight detector, a differentiable ranker, and an anisotropic covariance estimator. Trained solely on synthetic homography pairs without pose labels, it achieves state-of-the-art repeatability and matching performance under rotational changes and keypoint budget constraints. The method's core innovation lies in using policy gradients to train the detector for repeatability, a ranking module optimized to maximize matches across all budgets, and a covariance estimator that predicts pixel-scale uncertainty via reprojection error modeling. This design yields robust performance, high rotation equivariance, and well-calibrated uncertainties that benefit 3D reconstruction tasks.

## Method Summary
RaCo consists of three modules: (1) a detector based on a modified ALIKED-N16 backbone that outputs a globally softmax-normalized scoremap; keypoints are sampled via NMS and top‑K selection. The detector is trained with a policy‑gradient loss where each sampled point receives a reward based on its reprojection consistency under random homographies, encouraging spatially repeatable detections. (2) A ranker (separate ResNet) predicts a ranking scoremap; trained with Spearman and pull losses, it optimizes the ordering of keypoints to maximize the number of matches when truncated at any budget. (3) A covariance head attached to the shared backbone outputs a Cholesky decomposition per pixel, modeling anisotropic uncertainty. This head is trained by minimizing the negative log‑likelihood of reprojection errors under a Gaussian model that propagates covariances via the homography Jacobian. The training pipeline first trains the detector, then freezes it and separately trains the ranker and covariance head using the detector’s inference‑time sampling.

## Key Results
- Highest repeatability at 3 px on HPatches (33.9%) and ETH3D (58.5%).
- Superior rotation equivariance: repeatability AUC up to 79.5% at 1 px tolerance.
- Learned covariance is well‑calibrated (β=0.94) and improves 3D triangulation accuracy and completeness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Repeatability via Policy Gradient on Synthetic Homographies
- **Claim:** A lightweight detector can be trained to produce spatially repeatable keypoints without 3D ground truth, using only synthetic homography pairs and a reinforcement learning‑inspired reward.
- **Mechanism:** The detector outputs a globally softmax‑normalized scoremap `P`. Keypoints are sampled via NMS and top‑K selection. The reward `ρ(x)` is +1 for a reprojected keypoint within `d_max` (1.2 px) of a neighbor in the other view, and a small negative otherwise. The loss `L_detector` is the negative log‑likelihood of the sampled keypoints, weighted by normalized reward. This encourages the scoremap to assign high probability to pixels that reproject consistently under random homographies.
- **Core assumption:** Maximizing match success on numerous synthetic homography pairs translates to repeatability on real‑world viewpoint and illumination changes.
- **Break condition:** If the synthetic homography distribution does not cover the target domain’s variability (e.g., extreme lens distortion, motion blur), repeatability gains may not transfer.

### Mechanism 2: Budget‑Aware Ranking via Differentiable Sorting
- **Claim:** Standard detection scores are suboptimal for sub‑sampling; a separate ranker trained to maximize matches across all keypoint budgets yields higher effective repeatability when fewer keypoints are used.
- **Mechanism:** A separate network ingests the image and outputs a ranking scoremap `R`. For a matched pair of keypoints, two losses are used: (i) **Spearman loss** minimizes squared Euclidean distance between their soft ranks, encouraging matched points to have similar ranks. (ii) **Pull loss** forces matched points toward rank 1 and unmatched toward rank N. This jointly optimizes for correct pairing and top‑of‑list placement.
- **Core assumption:** Maximizing the sum of matches over truncation thresholds `n=1..N` is a valid proxy for downstream task performance under memory/compute constraints.
- **Break condition:** The ranker’s training signal is derived from match sets on synthetic pairs. If the detector’s initial keypoint set has very low matchability (e.g., on low‑texture images), the ranker may have insufficient signal to learn a meaningful ordering.

### Mechanism 3: Metric Covariance via Reprojection Error Likelihood
- **Claim:** The per‑keypoint spatial uncertainty (anisotropic covariance Σ in pixel units) can be learned in a self‑supervised manner by modeling the reprojection error distribution between matches.
- **Mechanism:** For a matched pair `(x_A, x_B)`, the reprojection error `e_{B→A} = x_A - H_{B→A}(x_B)` is modeled as `N(0, Σ_A + J Σ_B J^T)`, where `J` is the homography Jacobian at `x_B`. The network predicts a Cholesky decomposition `L` (for Σ) from multi‑scale features. The loss is the negative log‑likelihood (NLL) of `e` under this combined covariance. Minimizing NLL forces the predicted Σ to match the empirical spread of reprojection errors.
- **Core assumption:** Reprojection error across a distribution of homographies is predominantly caused by detector localization uncertainty and is well‑modeled as a zero‑mean Gaussian with a covariance that transforms correctly under the homography Jacobian.
- **Break condition:** The Gaussian assumption may break if reprojection errors are heavy‑tailed (e.g., from outlier matches or non‑Lambertian surfaces). The method also requires a sufficient number of matches for a keypoint during training to estimate its covariance reliably.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Policy Gradient for Detection**
  - **Why needed:** To train a non‑differentiable sampling process (NMS + top‑K) with a reward (match success) that only evaluates the final set. Reinforces pixels that lead to good matches.
  - **Quick check:** How does the reward for an individual sampled keypoint depend on the other keypoints sampled in the same view? (It doesn’t directly; normalization by view‑average reward decouples them, approximating a REINFORCE‑style policy gradient.)

- **Concept: Differentiable Sorting for Ranking**
  - **Why needed:** The ranking objective (maximize matches over all truncation thresholds) is a discrete, non‑differentiable function of the rank order. Soft ranking allows gradient flow to learn scores that induce a desired order.
  - **Quick check:** Why is minimizing the Euclidean distance between soft ranks of matched points equivalent to maximizing their Spearman correlation? (Spearman’s ρ is the Pearson correlation of the ranks; if soft ranks are close, their rank correlation is high.)

- **Concept: Uncertainty Propagation via Jacobian**
  - **Why needed:** To predict a keypoint’s covariance in metric pixels that correctly transforms under a homography. The Jacobian propagates uncertainty from view B to the coordinate frame of view A.
  - **Quick check:** If a keypoint has high uncertainty in view B and the homography has a high Jacobian norm at that location, what happens to the combined covariance? (The contribution from `J Σ_B J^T` becomes large, increasing total uncertainty.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Backbone (ALIKED‑N16 modified) → Detector Head (scoremap `S` → global softmax `P`) and Covariance Head (Cholesky `L` from multi‑scale features). Separate ResNet backbone → Ranker (ranking scoremap `R`).
- **Critical path:**
  1. Train detector backbone + head on synthetic homography pairs with `L_detector` (policy gradient) for 400k steps.
  2. Freeze detector backbone + head. Train covariance head with `L_covariance` (reprojection NLL) using inference‑time detector settings.
  3. Freeze backbone (or all except ranker). Train ranker with `L_ranker` (Spearman + Pull) on matches from frozen detector’s inference distribution.
  4. Ranker and covariance can be trained in any order after step 1.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Lightweight backbone and separate ranker keep compute low but may limit capacity. Adding equivariant convolutions improves rotation AUC by ~3% but increases inference time 10×.
  - Exhaustive rotation augmentation obviates complex equivariant architectures, trading training‑time compute for inference efficiency.
  - Training ranker/covariance separately simplifies losses and stabilizes training but risks distribution shift if detector’s keypoint distribution changes between stages; using inference‑time detector settings mitigates this.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Detector: Low repeatability on simple homographies; insufficient rotation augmentation or overly punitive `ρ_neg`.
  - Ranker: No improvement in low‑budget repeatability vs. detector scores; check if Spearman loss is decreasing or Pull loss saturated.
  - Covariance: Low calibration β (<0.8); likely NLL not converging, Jacobian mis‑implemented, or Cholesky scaling issue.
  - Pipeline break: Mismatch between frozen detector’s sampling (NMS radius, top‑K, subpixel) and final inference settings.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Smoke test on synthetic pair:** Generate a random homography pair, visualize scoremap `P`, NMS keypoints, and match reprojection errors; verify >90% of matches have error <1.2 px.
  2. **Ranker sanity check:** After training ranker, extract two sets of 256 keypoints sorted by detector score `p` and ranker score `r`. Inspect if `r`‑sorted list elevates corner/blob‑like points and demotes textureless/edge‑midpoints; measure repeatability of top‑256 from each ordering on a small homography set.
  3. **Covariance calibration probe:** On a validation set, for each matched keypoint compute reprojection error `e` and predicted Σ. Plot `√(trace(Σ))` vs. `∥e∥`; expect positive correlation with slope ≈1. Compute β calibration metric; target β∈[0.92,0.96].

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **OQ1:** Can the ranking module, trained to maximize matches at varying budgets, generalize to downstream tasks beyond matching (e.g., pose estimation, SfM) without retraining? The paper evaluates ranking only on repeatability metrics, not end‑task performance.
- **OQ2:** Is simple rotation augmentation during training sufficient for true rotation equivariance, or does it merely improve invariance without learning equivariant internal representations? The paper measures repeatability under rotation but not feature transformation consistency.
- **OQ3:** How does covariance performance depend on the diversity of homographic adaptations used during training? Training on only synthetic homographies may limit realism and generalization of predicted uncertainties for scenes with depth variations.
- **OQ4:** Does the two‑stage training pipeline (detector first, then freezing for ranker/covariance) create suboptimal solutions compared to end‑to‑end joint training of all three components?
- **OQ5:** Are the predicted anisotropic covariances truly metric‑scaled and physically meaningful, or merely relative weights that work well in the NLL loss? Absolute correctness across scales and resolutions remains unverified.

## Limitations
- Synthetic training may not cover real‑world distortions (lens distortion, motion blur), risking domain gap.
- Ranker relies on detector producing sufficient matches; on low‑texture scenes, ranking signal may be weak.
- Covariance assumes Gaussian reprojection errors; outliers or non‑Lambertian surfaces may violate this.
- Reproduction uncertainty: λ_ranker, exact evaluation parameters (NMS radius, top‑K, matching thresholds) not fully specified, risking implementation drift.

## Confidence
- **Detector repeatability via policy gradient:** High (SOTA results on HPatches/ETH3D, but mechanism not independently replicated).
- **Budget‑aware ranking:** Medium (ablation shows gains, but dependency on detector and lack of external validation).
- **Metric covariance utility:** Medium (strong calibration β=0.94 and triangulation improvements, but Gaussian assumption may not generalize).
