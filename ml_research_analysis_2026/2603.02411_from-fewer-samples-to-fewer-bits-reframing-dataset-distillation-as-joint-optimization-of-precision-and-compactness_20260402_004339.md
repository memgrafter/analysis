# From Fewer Samples to Fewer Bits: Reframing Dataset Distillation as Joint Optimization of Precision and Compactness

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.02411
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02411
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** QuADD matches full-precision distilled dataset accuracy while reducing storage by over 10× on images and 180× on tabular data under fixed bit budgets.

## Executive Summary
Dataset distillation (DD) compresses datasets into small synthetic sets but traditionally neglects storage precision. This paper introduces Quantization-aware Dataset Distillation (QuADD), which jointly optimizes the number of synthetic samples and their bit precision under a fixed storage budget. By integrating a differentiable quantization module into the DD loop, QuADD enables end-to-end co-optimization of synthetic data and quantization parameters. Experiments on image classification and 3GPP beam management show that QuADD achieves accuracy comparable to full-precision distilled datasets while drastically reducing storage. Under fixed bit budgets, performance consistently favors more lower-precision samples over fewer high-precision ones, demonstrating a novel trade-off in DD.

## Method Summary
QuADD modifies the standard dataset distillation framework by inserting a differentiable quantization layer between the synthetic data store and the distillation objective. Synthetic samples are represented as full-precision parameters and then quantized before being used to train a student model. The distillation loss (e.g., trajectory matching from DATM) is backpropagated through the quantizer using a Straight-Through Estimator (STE) or smooth relaxations, updating both the synthetic data and the quantizer's learnable parameters (e.g., clipping threshold α). Synthetic data initialization is guided by quantization-aware selection from the real dataset, computing gradient similarities. The approach supports uniform and adaptive non-uniform quantization (APoT) and is evaluated under a fixed bit budget constraint (M × D × b), where M is sample count, D is feature dimension, and b is bits per element.

## Key Results
- QuADD matches the accuracy of full-precision distilled datasets while reducing storage by over 10× on image data (CIFAR-10/100) and over 180× on tabular data (3GPP beam management).
- Under fixed bit budgets, performance consistently improves with lower precision and more samples rather than fewer high-precision samples.
- The framework supports both uniform and adaptive non-uniform quantization and generalizes across visual and non-visual domains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A differentiable quantization layer enables gradient-based adaptation of synthetic data to low-precision storage constraints.
- **Mechanism:** The quantizer uses Straight-Through Estimator (STE) or smooth relaxations during backpropagation. This allows the distillation loss gradient to flow from the quantized synthetic data back to the full-precision synthetic parameters, tuning them to compensate for information loss from quantization.
- **Core assumption:** STE or smooth surrogate gradients provide a reasonably accurate gradient signal for optimization through discrete rounding operations.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "QuADD integrates a differentiable quantization module within the distillation loop, enabling end-to-end co-optimization of synthetic samples and quantization parameters." [section 4.2]: Details forward/backward passes for hard/soft rounding and STE. "During backpropagation, gradients are estimated as: Straight-through estimator (STE): for hard rounding, treat Q as identity within the clipping range." [corpus]: Corpus papers (e.g., SignRoundV2, LLM-QAT) validate STE and differentiable quantization for model weights but do not address dataset-level application, implying the mechanism's novelty lies in its integration point (distillation loop) rather than the gradient estimator itself.
- **Break condition:** If the quantization-induced distortion is too high relative to the gradient signal, optimization may stall or converge to poor local minima, especially with aggressive bit budgets.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Joint optimization of synthetic data and quantization parameters (e.g., clipping threshold α) aligns the quantizer's discrete levels with the evolving distribution of distilled data, minimizing task-relevant distortion under a bit budget.
- **Mechanism:** The quantizer's learnable parameters (e.g., α in APoT) are updated alongside the synthetic samples via the same distillation loss. This adapts the quantization grid to the synthetic data's dynamic range, allocating more precision to informative regions.
- **Core assumption:** The data distribution during distillation is non-stationary, and a fixed quantizer would misalign with it, causing unnecessary information loss.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "enabling end-to-end co-optimization of synthetic samples and quantization parameters." "adaptive non-uniform quantization, where the latter learns quantization levels from data to represent information-dense regions better." [section 4.2.2]: "The clipping threshold α is learnable and updated automatically through back-propagation, allowing the quantizer to adapt to the evolving data distribution." [corpus]: Corpus works on adaptive quantization for models (e.g., Layer-Wise Quantization) support the principle of parameterized bit allocation but are applied to weights, not datasets.
- **Break condition:** If α becomes unstable (e.g., collapses to extreme values) or the quantization grid fails to track rapid distribution shifts during early distillation iterations, performance may degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Under a fixed bit budget, allocating bits to more lower-precision samples yields higher learning performance than fewer high-precision samples, because sample diversity outweighs per-sample fidelity for task accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** Rate-distortion theory suggests that for a given total bit rate (budget), distributing bits across more independent samples (reducing b, increasing M) preserves more task-relevant information than concentrating bits on fewer samples. QuADD's end-to-end training empirically discovers this optimal trade-off.
- **Core assumption:** The learning task (e.g., classification) benefits more from seeing a diverse set of learned synthetic examples than from high-fidelity reconstruction of a limited set.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "Under fixed bit budgets, performance consistently improves with lower precision and more samples rather than fewer high-precision samples." [section 5.2.1] & Figure 3: "For a fixed bit budget, lower-bit quantization combined with more samples often achieves higher accuracy than using fewer high-precision samples." [corpus]: Corpus evidence is weak; neighboring papers focus on model quantization scaling laws, not dataset distillation's sample-precision trade-off. This mechanism is empirically discovered in this work.
- **Break condition:** If the task is extremely sensitive to fine-grained details (e.g., pixel-level reconstruction), the trend may reverse, and higher precision per sample could be favored.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Dataset Distillation (DD)**
  - *Why needed here:* QuADD modifies the DD loop; understanding the standard bi-level optimization (inner-loop training on synthetic data, outer-loop evaluation on real data) and matching-based objectives (gradient, distribution, trajectory) is prerequisite.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the core objective of DD, and how does a method like DATM approximate the bi-level loss?
- **Concept: Quantization for Neural Networks**
  - *Why needed here:* QuADD applies quantization to data, not models. One must understand uniform/non-uniform quantization, bit depth, and the clipping-projection process to grasp the introduced loss and its optimization.
  - *Quick check question:* What are the two main operations in scalar quantization, and why are they non-differentiable?
- **Concept: Gradient Estimators for Discrete Operations**
  - *Why needed here:* Backpropagation through the quantizer requires surrogate gradients. Knowledge of STE and smooth relaxations is essential to understand how QuADD trains.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) approximate gradients for a rounding operation?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Synthetic Data Store (S) -> Differentiable Quantizer (Q) -> Distillation Framework (ϕ(·), ℒ) -> Optimizer
- **Critical path:** Initialize S → Quantize S via Q → Compute distillation loss between real data and Q(S) → Backpropagate loss through Q to update both S and Q's parameters → Repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Uniform vs. Non-uniform Quantization:* Uniform (simple, stable) vs. APoT (adaptive, better low-bit performance but requires careful initialization/normalization).
  - *Gradient Estimator:* STE (simple, high bias) vs. soft rounding (smoother, potentially more stable).
  - *Per-channel vs. Per-tensor α:* More α parameters increase adaptivity but also hyperparameter tuning cost.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - *Gradient vanishing/explosion:* STE may provide useless gradients (1 or 0) away from clipping boundary.
  - *α instability:* Learned clipping threshold diverges or collapses without proper initialization (e.g., not using P99-P1 percentiles).
  - *Mode collapse:* Synthetic data overfits to a quantized manifold, losing diversity.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Benchmark comparison of quantization methods:** Compare uniform (STE, FSQ) vs. non-uniform (APoT) on a standard vision dataset (CIFAR-10) across bit depths to validate the differentiable quantizer's efficacy.
  2. **Rate-distortion analysis:** Fix total bit budget (M × b × D), vary (M, b) pairs, and plot accuracy vs. total bits. Verify the "more samples, lower precision" hypothesis.
  3. **Cross-domain generalization:** Apply QuADD to a non-visual, tabular dataset (3GPP beam management) under fixed bit budget to confirm modality-agnosticism and substantial compression (e.g., 180×).

## Limitations
- Reproduction blockers due to ambiguous hyperparameters and DATM details (trajectory length, expert refresh schedule, initialization specifics).
- Weak theoretical foundation for the sample-precision trade-off; may not hold for tasks highly sensitive to fine-grained details.
- Limited evaluation scope: only small vision datasets and one tabular task; no large-scale (e.g., ImageNet) or modern transformer architecture validation.

## Confidence
- **Joint optimization feasibility:** High – method is well-specified, differentiable quantizer integrates cleanly, results consistently show storage reduction without accuracy loss.
- **Sample-precision trade-off:** Medium – empirical evidence supports trend but limited task diversity and weak theoretical backing.
- **Adaptive quantization (APoT):** Medium – APoT yields gains over uniform quantization but stability relies on proper α initialization.
- **Cross-domain generalization:** Medium – demonstrated on images and one tabular dataset, broader domain tests needed.
