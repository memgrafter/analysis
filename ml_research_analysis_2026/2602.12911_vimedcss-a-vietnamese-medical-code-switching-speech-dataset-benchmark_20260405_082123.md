# ViMedCSS: A Vietnamese Medical Code-Switching Speech Dataset & Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.12911
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12911
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** Fine-tuning PhoWhisper-Small with Attention Guide (AG) yields the best trade-off, achieving 23.67% WER and 19.50% CS-WER on the test set.

## Executive Summary
ViMedCSS introduces a 34.6-hour dataset of Vietnamese medical speech with embedded English medical terms, specifically designed to benchmark code-switching ASR. Evaluations reveal a key trade-off: Vietnamese-optimized models (PhoWhisper) achieve lower overall word error rates but perform poorly on the embedded English terms, while multilingual models (Whisper) handle English insertions better but lag on general Vietnamese. The most effective solution combines a Vietnamese-optimized backbone with parameter-efficient, language-identity-guided adaptation via the Attention Guide (AG) method, significantly improving both overall and code-switched accuracy without catastrophic forgetting.

## Method Summary
The study compares zero-shot baselines and fine-tuning strategies on the ViMedCSS dataset. Fine-tuning approaches include decoder-side contextual biasing (Dynamic Vocabulary, Rank & Selection, AdaCS), parameter-efficient adaptation (LoRA), and the proposed Attention Guide (AG). AG identifies attention heads in a pre-trained model that are sensitive to language identity (Vietnamese vs. English) using a probing set, then steers these heads during fine-tuning with a guidance loss to enhance code-switching performance while preserving matrix language competence. The dataset’s design—every utterance includes at least one English medical term and a Hard split of rare/unseen terms—enables separate evaluation of overall WER, CS-WER (on the embedded language), and N-WER (on non-switched Vietnamese), along with a test of generalization.

## Key Results
- AG fine-tuning on PhoWhisper-Small achieves the lowest test WER (23.67%) and CS-WER (19.50%), outperforming other fine-tuning methods.
- Vietnamese-optimized PhoWhisper models have lower overall WER than multilingual Whisper in zero-shot settings, but higher CS-WER, confirming a trade-off between matrix and embedded language accuracy.
- All models experience a performance drop on the Hard split (rare/unseen terms), highlighting the challenge of generalizing to unseen lexical items.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Vietnamese-optimized vs. Multilingual Trade-off
- **Claim:** Vietnamese-optimized models (e.g., PhoWhisper) achieve lower overall WER on Vietnamese matrix language but struggle more with embedded English medical terms compared to multilingual models (e.g., Whisper).
- **Mechanism:** Vietnamese-optimized models develop strong acoustic and linguistic representations tailored to Vietnamese phonotactics and vocabulary, reducing errors on the dominant language. Multilingual models train on diverse language pairs, leading to more generalized representations that better handle phonetically distinct embedded languages.
- **Core assumption:** The acoustic and linguistic patterns of Vietnamese and English differ sufficiently that optimizing exclusively for one degrades recognition of the other during code-switching.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...Vietnamese-optimized models achieve lower overall WER but struggle with code-switched terms, while multilingual models handle English insertions better."
  - [section 4.2, Table 4] PhoWhisper-Small: WER 36.31% vs. Whisper-Small 50.03%, but CS-WER 62.55% vs. 61.25%. Larger models invert the overall gap but widen the CS-WER gap (PhoWhisper-Large CS-WER 55.05% vs. Whisper-Large-v3 46.69%).
  - The dataset’s construction ensures every utterance contains English terms, measurably exposing this trade-off.

### Mechanism 2: Attention Guide Adaptation Effectiveness
- **Claim:** Parameter-efficient, language-identity-guided adaptation (AG) on a Vietnamese-optimized backbone yields the best balance between overall and code-switched accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** AG uses a bilingual probing set to identify attention heads most correlated with language identity. During fine-tuning, a guidance loss is applied to these heads to enhance their sensitivity to language switches, thereby improving recognition of English insertions while preserving Vietnamese competence without full-parameter updates.
- **Core assumption:** The pre-trained model already contains subspaces (attention heads) that encode language identity information, which can be selectively targeted for minimal adaptation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...fine-tuning a Vietnamese-optimized backbone (PhoWhisper-Small) with the Attention Guide (AG) adaptation method, which yields the best balance."
  - [section 4.3, Table 5] AG on PhoWhisper-Small: test WER 23.67%, CS-WER 19.50%, significantly better than LoRA (27.13% WER, 30.26% CS-WER) and frozen (36.31% WER, 62.55% CS-WER).
  - The dataset’s guaranteed code-switched terms provide the training signal for AG to learn guidance.

### Mechanism 3: Dataset Design for Diagnosis
- **Claim:** ViMedCSS’s design—every utterance contains at least one code-switched medical term from a curated lexicon and a Hard split of rare/unseen terms—enables precise diagnosis of CS behavior and evaluation of generalization.
- **Mechanism:** By construction, the dataset forces evaluation on CS spans. The curated lexicon provides a known set of target terms. The Hard split, containing only terms removed from training, creates a controlled setting to test generalization beyond exact match (BEM), critical for real-world deployment.
- **Core assumption:** Diagnostic evaluation requires explicit separation of metrics for matrix language (N-WER) and embedded language (CS-WER), and a held-out lexical set to test generalization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The dataset... each containing at least one English medical term... evaluate zero-shot baselines... Results show a clear trade-off..."
  - [section 3.3, 4.1.1] "Each utterance includes at least one code-switched medical term... The Hard split contains only code-switched medical terms that occur once or twice... all occurrences... removed from Train/Valid/Test to prevent leakage."
  - [section 4.2, Tables 4 & 5] Metrics are separated: WER, CS-WER, N-WER. Performance on the Hard set is worse for all models (e.g., PhoWhisper-Small CS-WER: Test 62.55%, Hard 66.01%), confirming the generalization challenge.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Code-Switching (CS) in ASR**
  - **Why needed here:** The core problem is ASR for Vietnamese speech with embedded English medical terms. Understanding that ASR typically performs worst on embedded language spans frames the problem and explains the use of separate metrics (CS-WER vs. N-WER).
  - **Quick check:** If an ASR model has low overall WER but very high CS-WER, what does that indicate about its performance on the critical medical terms?  
    It indicates the model reliably transcribes Vietnamese portions but fails on the English medical terms embedded within.

- **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper shows full fine-tuning is less effective than targeted methods (LoRA, AG). Understanding PEFT explains why AG works: it adapts a small, strategic subset of parameters (language-sensitive attention heads) to avoid catastrophic forgetting of Vietnamese while learning CS.
  - **Quick check:** Why might full fine-tuning of a Vietnamese-optimized model on a 34-hour CS dataset degrade its performance on general Vietnamese, and how does AG aim to prevent this?  
    Full fine-tuning risks overwriting Vietnamese-specific representations (catastrophic forgetting). AG adapts only a subset of language-identity-sensitive heads, preserving existing Vietnamese knowledge while enhancing CS capability.

- **Concept: Contextual Biasing vs. Model Adaptation**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper contrasts decoder-side contextual biasing (DV, RS, AdaCS) at inference with parameter-efficient model adaptation (AG, LoRA) during training. This distinction explains why hybrid approaches (AG+AdaCS) work best: adaptation improves general CS recognition, while biasing rescues rare terms.
  - **Quick check:** If you have a perfect, complete list of all possible English medical terms, which approach (pure contextual biasing or AG adaptation) would be more robust to slight pronunciation variations or unseen compound terms, and why?  
    AG adaptation, because it learns generalizable patterns for recognizing English speech within Vietnamese context, whereas biasing relies on exact lexical matching and may fail with unseen pronunciations or compounds not in the bias list.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Pre-trained PhoWhisper-Small (encoder-decoder Whisper architecture fine-tuned on Vietnamese) -> Attention Guide module (identifies language-sensitive attention heads via probing) -> Guidance loss added to standard CTC/seq2seq loss during fine-tuning -> Adapted model.  
Alternative pipelines: Decoder-side contextual biasing (Dynamic Vocabulary, Rank & Selection) or post-processing (AdaCS) applied at inference without weight changes.  
Evaluation: Hypothesis split into CS spans (using reference boundaries) and non-CS spans -> compute WER, CS-WER, N-WER separately.

**Critical path:**  
1. Load ViMedCSS train/valid/test/Hard splits and extract code-switched term spans.  
2. Run zero-shot inference with frozen PhoWhisper-Small to establish baseline WER/CS-WER.  
3. For AG: run forward passes on a small bilingual probing set to record attention head activations; compute correlation between head patterns and language IDs to select "guide" heads.  
4. Fine-tune PhoWhisper-Small with AG guidance loss on training data.  
5. Evaluate on test and Hard splits, comparing WER, CS-WER, N-WER with other methods (LoRA, AdaCS, hybrids).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- AG vs. LoRA: AG is more targeted (specific heads) but requires a reliable probing set and is architecture-specific; LoRA is general (applies to weight matrices) but may be less sample-efficient for CS phenomenon.  
- Contextual biasing (DV/RS/AdaCS) vs. Adaptation: Biasing is zero-shot for a given bias list but fails on unseen terms/pronunciations; adaptation learns generalizable patterns but needs labeled training data and risks overfitting.  
- Hybrid (e.g., AG+AdaCS): Combines benefits (AG improves general CS, AdaCS rescues rare terms) at the cost of increased pipeline complexity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- AG fails to lower CS-WER: selected heads may not be truly CS-sensitive; check probing set quality and head selection statistics.  
- Overall WER increases after AG fine-tuning: catastrophic forgetting of Vietnamese; reduce AG loss weight or learning rate.  
- Hard set performance remains poor: model overfits to seen CS terms; augment training with lexical variations or increase model capacity.  
- Contextual biasing (AdaCS) degrades N-WER: bias module too aggressively normalizes non-CS Vietnamese; tune confidence threshold.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Reproduce Zero-Shot Baselines:** Run all models (PhoWhisper, Whisper) on ViMedCSS test set to confirm the Vietnamese-optimized vs. multilingual trade-off.  
2. **Implement & Validate AG:** On PhoWhisper-Small, implement AG as described; verify that selected attention heads show different patterns for Vietnamese vs. English tokens in the probing set; fine-tune and confirm published results (Table 5, AG row).  
3. **Ablation: AG on Multilingual Backbone:** Fine-tune Whisper-Small with AG; test if AG improves CS-WER on Whisper and whether the gap between PhoWhisper+AG and Whisper+AG narrows or widens.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Which other contextual biasing techniques, beyond dynamic vocabulary, ranking/selection, and AdaCS, could most effectively improve code-switched medical term recognition on ViMedCSS?
2. What specific linguistic or phonetic properties of the code-switched medical terms in ViMedCSS cause the highest error rates, particularly for rare/unseen terms in the Hard split?
3. How does the Attention Guide (AG) adaptation method specifically alter the model's internal representations to better handle Vietnamese-English code-switching, and does its mechanism generalize to other language pairs or domains?
4. To what extent does increasing the size and diversity of the ViMedCSS training data reduce the performance gap between Vietnamese-optimized and multilingual models on code-switched terms?
5. Can the findings on model adaptation (e.g., AG's superiority) and the Vietnamese-English medical CS trade-off be extended to other code-switching scenarios involving low-resource languages and specialized domains (e.g., legal or technical CS)?

## Limitations
- **AG reproducibility low:** The Attention Guide method relies on an external paper (Aditya et al., 2024) without detailed algorithmic description or code, hindering exact replication.
- **Hard split validity uncertain:** The Hard split’s diagnostic value assumes the rare terms are truly unseen at sub-word/phonetic level; no evidence is provided that they are phonetically disjoint from training terms.
- **Generalizability unknown:** The observed Vietnamese-optimized vs. multilingual trade-off is shown only on one dataset (Vietnamese medical); it may not hold for other language pairs or domains.

## Confidence
- AG fine-tuning improves CS-WER on PhoWhisper-Small: **High**
- Vietnamese-optimized models have lower overall WER but higher CS-WER than multilingual in zero-shot: **Medium**
- ViMedCSS enables separated CS/N-WER evaluation and generalization test: **High**
- Exact reproduction of AG without source code: **Low**
- Hard split truly tests generalization to unseen sub-word patterns: **Medium**
- The observed trade-off holds for other CS scenarios: **Low**
