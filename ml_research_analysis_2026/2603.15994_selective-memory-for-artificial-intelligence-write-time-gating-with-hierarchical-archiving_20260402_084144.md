# Selective Memory for Artificial Intelligence: Write-Time Gating with Hierarchical Archiving

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.15994  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15994  
- **Reference count**: 18  
- **Primary result**: Write-time gating achieved 100% accuracy in LLM evaluations, maintaining performance at high distractor ratios where read-time filtering (Self-RAG) collapsed to 0%.

## Executive Summary
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) degrades in accuracy by storing all incoming knowledge, accumulating noise over time. This paper introduces write-time gating, a method that filters knowledge objects at ingestion using composite salience scores—source reputation, novelty, and reliability—without requiring ground-truth quality labels. Superseded information is preserved in hierarchical version chains. In evaluations, write gating achieved perfect accuracy (100%) versus 13% for ungated stores and 0% for Self-RAG under an 8:1 distractor ratio. The approach scaled effectively, improving accuracy by up to 65 percentage points on challenging datasets, while reducing query-time costs to one-ninth of Self-RAG's.

## Method Summary
The method implements a gating mechanism during write operations that evaluates each incoming knowledge object against three signals: source reputation (trustworthiness of origin), novelty (difference from existing store), and reliability (intrinsic consistency). Objects pass the gate if their composite salience score exceeds a threshold; otherwise, they are rejected. Accepted objects are stored in a hierarchical archive where superseded versions are retained in chain-linked entries, enabling reconstruction of prior states. This prevents noise accumulation by blocking low-quality or redundant content before it enters the store, unlike read-time methods that filter during retrieval.

## Key Results
- Write gating achieved 100% accuracy vs 13% for ungated stores and 0% for Self-RAG at 8:1 distractor ratio.
- Scalability gains: +25pp on Wikipedia, +48pp on post-cutoff arXiv, +65pp on zero-knowledge procedural data.
- Query-time cost reduced to 1/9 of Self-RAG while matching accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Write-time gating addresses the root cause of RAG degradation—noise accumulation—by preventing low-salience content from entering the knowledge store. Composite salience scores combine multiple signals that are cheap to compute and do not require labeled quality data. Hierarchical version chains maintain temporal integrity, allowing the system to revert or trace changes without losing historical context. This design shifts the burden of quality control from retrieval (expensive and error-prone under high noise) to ingestion (where decisions are based on structural signals), yielding robustness in distractor-heavy environments.

## Foundational Learning
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**: Augments LLMs with external knowledge retrieval. *Why needed*: Pure LLMs hallucinate on out-of-training data; RAG provides factual grounding. *Quick check*: Explain how RAG integrates retrieved documents into generation.
- **Composite Salience Scoring**: Combines multiple signals (reputation, novelty, reliability) into a single quality metric. *Why needed*: Single signals are insufficient; composite scores capture diverse aspects of value without ground truth. *Quick check*: List the three signals and propose a simple weighted sum formula.
- **Hierarchical Version Chains**: Stores superseded entries in linked chains within a hierarchical structure. *Why needed*: Preserves historical states for auditability and recovery from erroneous writes. *Quick check*: Sketch how a version chain for a fact update would be stored.
- **Write-Time vs Read-Time Filtering**: Contrasts filtering during ingestion (write) versus during query (read). *Why needed*: Write-time prevents noise entry; read-time must sift through accumulated noise, which becomes intractable at scale. *Quick check*: Compare computational profiles of both approaches under high distractor ratios.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Knowledge Object -> Salience Evaluator -> Gating Decision -> (Accepted -> Hierarchical Archive; Rejected -> Discard). Critical path: During write, an object is scored, thresholded, and either archived (with versioning) or dropped. Design tradeoffs: Higher gating thresholds improve purity but risk false rejections; version chains add storage overhead but enable temporal queries. Failure signatures: Sudden accuracy drops indicate gating threshold misconfiguration; version chain bloat signals excessive supersession without cleanup. First experiments: 1) Sweep gating thresholds across low/medium/high reputation sources to find optimal tradeoff. 2) Inject superseding writes at varying frequencies to measure version chain growth and retrieval latency. 3) Replicate the 8:1 distractor experiment comparing write gating against Self-RAG on a held-out dataset.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the O(n) per-write computational cost of novelty scoring scale to large knowledge stores (millions of objects), and can approximate methods maintain gating effectiveness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Scalability evaluation with approximate novelty computation and large-scale stores is needed" (Section 5.3).
- Why unresolved: Current experiments use stores up to 2,500 objects; real-world systems require scaling to millions, where naive novelty computation becomes prohibitively expensive.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implement approximate nearest neighbor techniques (e.g., locality-sensitive hashing) for novelty estimation, then measure gating precision/recall and overall accuracy as store size scales from 10⁴ to 10⁶ objects.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does write gating maintain its robustness advantage over read-time filtering on web-scale corpora with distractor ratios exceeding 8:1 (e.g., 16:1, 32:1)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Production web-scale retrieval may face even higher ratios... this claim needs validation at 16:1, 32:1, and beyond" (Section 5.3).
- Why unresolved: Experiments only tested up to 8:1 distractor ratios; real web data likely has much higher noise due to AI-generated content and SEO manipulation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Construct large-scale benchmarks with distractor ratios from 16:1 to 1024:1 using real web corpus samples, then compare write gating and Self-RAG accuracy under these conditions.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can a hybrid memory architecture combining discrete knowledge objects (with write gating) and continuous neural memory improve performance on tasks requiring both factual precision and semantic interpolation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "A hybrid architecture might use neural memory for soft statistical patterns... We have not explored such hybrid architectures experimentally; this remains an open direction" (Section 5.4).
- Why unresolved: The paper contrasts discrete vs. neural memory but does not experimentally test a system that uses both for different content types.
- What evidence would resolve it: Build a prototype that routes incoming content to either discrete storage (if it has verifiable source metadata) or neural memory (if it is pattern-like), then evaluate on a mixed benchmark requiring both precise fact retrieval and analogical reasoning.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Does write gating remain effective without source reputation metadata in domains where source identity is sparse or unreliably correlated with correctness (e.g., social media, emerging research)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The signal ablation on Wikipedia showed only a 1.4pp drop without source labels, but the authors note "in the real world, verified sources occasionally contain errors and unverified sources occasionally contain correct information" and test corruption only on synthetic data (Section 5.3).
- Why unresolved: Wikipedia and synthetic benchmarks may not capture metadata noise in domains with weaker source tracking; effectiveness without reliable source signals remains uncertain.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replicate the Wikipedia ablation on datasets from domains with minimal source metadata (e.g., social media posts, preprint servers), measuring accuracy when source reputation signals are absent or noisy.

## Limitations
- Computational overhead of the gating mechanism itself, especially novelty scoring, is unreported; approximate methods may be needed for large-scale deployment.
- Sensitivity of composite salience scores to biased or manipulated source reputations is not fully evaluated; effectiveness in domains with sparse or noisy metadata (e.g., social media) is uncertain.
- Long-term integrity of hierarchical version chains under continuous writes, including bloat and retrieval decay, lacks empirical validation.
