# When AI output tips to bad but nobody notices: Legal implications of AI's mistakes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.23857
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23857
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: AI fabrication of legal citations follows a deterministic tipping mechanism predictable by a formula based on prompt structure and attention parameters, establishing it as a foreseeable engineering risk.

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that AI "hallucinations" in legal contexts, such as fabricating case law, are not random glitches but stem from a deterministic tipping point in the model's internal context vector. By mapping Transformer self-attention to a statistical physics model, the authors derive a formula that predicts exactly when a correctly generating AI will switch to producing false information. In a simulation based on the *Mata v. Avianca* case, the system produced six accurate paragraphs before a fabricated citation appeared at token 13. This transforms fabrication from an unpredictable anomaly into a quantifiable failure mode, directly implicating the legal profession's duty of technological competence and necessitating verification protocols that account for this predictable transition.

## Method Summary
The core method reduces Transformer self-attention to a mean-field statistical physics model. The AI's context vector **N**(t), a weighted average of all prior token embeddings, evolves during greedy decoding (temperature → 0). The next token is selected by maximizing the dot product between a candidate token's embedding **S**<sub>x</sub> and **N**(t). The model defines representative vectors: **S**<sub>A</sub> (background law), **S**<sub>B</sub> (correct legal application), **S**<sub>C</sub> (anomalous novel query), and **S**<sub>D</sub> (harmful falsehood). The "tipping" occurs when **S**<sub>D</sub>·**N**(t) surpasses **S**<sub>B</sub>·**N**(t). An analytical formula for the tipping step *n*<sup>*</sup> is derived under simplifying assumptions (single head, linear accumulation, orthogonality). A simulated legal brief with an "ACCA" prompt containing A, C, and target B/D tokens validates the dynamics, showing the crossover after six B tokens.

## Key Results
- In the simulated brief-drafting scenario, the AI generated six correct legal paragraphs before producing a fabricated citation at token 13.
- The primary result is a closed-form formula *n*<sup>*</sup> that predicts the deterministic tipping step based on the prompt's token count (*m*) and the effective attention temperature (*T*<sub>eff</sub>).
- The mechanism establishes fabrication as a foreseeable engineering risk inherent to Transformer operation under certain prompt structures, thereby mandating proactive verification protocols for legal AI use.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Deterministic tipping via context vector evolution
The AI's internal context vector **N**(t) evolves as a weighted average of all previous token embeddings via self-attention. Under greedy decoding, the next token maximizes **S**<sub>x</sub>·**N**(t). As generation proceeds, **N**(t) drifts; when the dot product with the harmful falsehood vector **S**<sub>D</sub> overtakes that with the correct application vector **S**<sub>B</sub>, the output flips to fabrication. This condition is calculable and deterministic within the model.

### Destabilizing role of the anomalous legal query
An anomalous query (unsettled, novel issue) represented by **S**<sub>C</sub> in the prompt persistently biases **N**(t) toward a region where **S**<sub>D</sub> eventually aligns more strongly. This **S**<sub>C</sub> acts as the primary destabilizing element, pulling the context away from the stable subspace dominated by **S**<sub>A</sub> and **S**<sub>B</sub>.

### Predictable fabrication timing via analytical formula
Under model assumptions (single head, greedy decoding, linear accumulation, **S**<sub>C</sub> perpendicular to A-B-D plane), the tipping step *n*<sup>*</sup> is given by a formula involving the number of A tokens (*m*), dot products between content vectors, and effective temperature *T*<sub>eff</sub>. This allows prediction of when the **S**<sub>B</sub>·**N** and **S**<sub>D</sub>·**N** curves cross.

## Foundational Learning
- **Self-attention mechanism**: Computes the context vector **N**(t) as a weighted sum of previous token embeddings; weights determine influence. *Why needed*: It is the core operation driving context evolution and the tipping dynamics. *Quick check*: In self-attention, what does the attention weight between two tokens represent?
- **Embedding space and vector representations**: Tokens are represented as vectors in a high-dimensional space; dot products measure similarity or relevance. *Why needed*: The positions of **S**<sub>A</sub>, **S**<sub>B</sub>, **S**<sub>C</sub>, **S**<sub>D</sub> relative to each other determine stability and tipping. *Quick check*: How are words/tokens represented in a Transformer, and what does the dot product between two token embeddings indicate?
- **Greedy decoding and energy minimization**: At each step, the token with the highest dot product **S**<sub>x</sub>·**N**(t) is selected, equivalent to minimizing an energy function in the physics analogy. *Why needed*: This deterministic selection rule makes the tipping point predictable and sharply defined. *Quick check*: In greedy decoding, how does the model choose the next token from the vocabulary?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input prompt (tokens with embeddings) -> Self-attention head -> Context vector **N**(t) -> Greedy decoder (argmax **S**<sub>x</sub>·**N**(t)) -> Output token, with predefined vectors **S**<sub>A</sub>, **S**<sub>B</sub>, **S**<sub>C</sub>, **S**<sub>D</sub> and parameter *T*<sub>eff</sub>.
Critical path: 1) Initialize **N**(0) from prompt tokens via self-attention. 2) For t=1,2,...: compute attention weights over all previous tokens, form **N**(t), compute dot products **S**<sub>x</sub>·**N**(t) for all x, select token with maximum, append to sequence. Monitor difference (**S**<sub>B</sub>·**N**(t) - **S**<sub>D</sub>·**N**(t)).
Design tradeoffs: Model uses single-head, single-layer for analytical tractability vs. real multi-head/multi-layer complexity. Assumes greedy decoding vs. common stochastic sampling (temperature >0) which may mask tipping. Fixed content vectors (**S**<sub>A</sub> etc.) are abstractions vs. dynamic, context-dependent embeddings.
Failure signatures: The difference (**S**<sub>B</sub>·**N** - **S**<sub>D</sub>·**N**) shrinks and turns negative. Attention weights may increasingly focus on anomalous query (**S**<sub>C</sub>) tokens. Generated text may show subtle linguistic drift (e.g., hedging) before outright fabrication.
First 3 experiments: 1) Reproduce Appendix B arithmetic with provided vectors, confirming **S**<sub>D</sub>·**N** overtakes **S**<sub>B</sub>·**N** after six B tokens. 2) Vary number of A tokens (*m*) and *T*<sub>eff</sub> in the *n*<sup>*</sup> formula; map how predicted tipping steps shift. 3) Modify embedding vector geometries (e.g., reduce gap between **S**<sub>B</sub> and **S**<sub>D</sub>) and recompute *n*<sup>*</sup>; test sensitivity.

## Limitations
- Model relies on significant simplifications: single-head, single-layer architecture and greedy decoding abstract away complexities of modern multi-head, multi-layer LLMs and stochastic sampling.
- The predefined, fixed content vectors (**S**<sub>A</sub>, **S**<sub>B</sub>, **S**<sub>C</sub>, **S**<sub>D</sub>) are theoretical abstractions; real token embeddings are dynamic and context-sensitive, potentially weakening deterministic predictions.
- Evidence is based on a single simulated legal brief; generalizability across diverse legal prompts, jurisdictions, and model architectures remains unproven, with no external peer replication identified in the corpus.

## Confidence
- Deterministic tipping mechanism (context vector shift): Medium. The theoretical derivation is coherent and internally simulated, but real LLM dynamics may obscure or alter the predicted tipping.
- Analytical formula for tipping step (*n*<sup>*</sup>): Medium. Mathematically sound under assumptions, but sensitivity to embedding geometry and architectural deviations is untested empirically.
- Anomalous query as primary destabilizer: Medium. Logically consistent within the model, but depends on simplifications like vector orthogonality and persistent attention; real prompts may destabilize via other pathways.
