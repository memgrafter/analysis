# Evolving Medical Imaging Agents via Experience-driven Self-skill Discovery

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05860  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05860  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** MACRO achieves SOTA balanced accuracy/F1 on REFUGE2 (92.7% BACC, 80.3% F1), MITEA (77.2% BACC, 74.9% F1), and RAM-W600 (61.75% BACC, 30.00% F1) via autonomous composite tool discovery.

## Executive Summary
Medical imaging agents typically rely on static tool sets, making them brittle under domain shifts. This paper introduces MACRO, a self-evolving agent that overcomes this limitation by autonomously identifying and reusing effective multi-step tool sequences from successful execution histories. By employing an image-feature memory for context-aware retrieval and a GRPO-inspired reinforcement loop to promote the use of discovered composite tools, MACRO learns to orchestrate medical image analysis more robustly. Evaluated on three diverse benchmarks— REFUGE2 (glaucoma), MITEA (heart disease), and RAM-W600 (bone erosion)—MACRO consistently surpasses prior state-of-the-art methods, with ablations confirming the contribution of each component. The framework demonstrates the potential of experience-driven skill discovery for building adaptive medical AI systems.

## Method Summary
MACRO formalizes medical image tool orchestration as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP) with a dynamically expanding action space as new composite tools are synthesized from successful trajectories. The agent employs an LLM-based policy (Qwen2.5-VL-3B with LoRA) that selects actions from atomic tools, a growing registry of composites, or a final answer action. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning on teacher (DeepSeek) demonstrations using behavior cloning with student-forcing to mitigate exposure bias; (2) reinforcement learning via Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), where a sparse binary reward (triggered when a composite tool is invoked) is group-normalized to encourage higher-level abstractions. A frozen visual encoder generates image features for memory retrieval; successful rollouts augment an image-feature memory store and trigger frequent subsequence mining to register new composite tools. The training objective combines clipped PPO updates with a KL penalty to a reference policy, ensuring stable learning while growing the skill repertoire.

## Key Results
- On **REFUGE2** (glaucoma): MACRO reaches 92.7% balanced accuracy and 80.3% F1, exceeding MedAgent-Pro (90.4%/76.4%).
- On **MITEA** (heart disease): Achieves 77.2% BACC and 74.9% F1, improving F1 by 2.6% over the previous best.
- On **RAM-W600** (bone erosion): Obtains 61.75% BACC and 30.00% F1, dramatically outperforming specialized models like MobileViT (52.64%/11.85%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Memory-guided tool selection
The system maintains a store of successful episodes indexed by image features. At inference, it retrieves the most visually similar past successes using cosine similarity and injects their tool sequences as in-context demonstrations, grounding the policy’s decisions in clinically relevant context. This approach assumes that similar images share effective workflows; ablations confirm the memory’s contribution. If visual similarity does not correlate with clinical strategy, however, retrieval may mislead and degrade performance.

### Mechanism 2: Composite tool discovery
From verified successful trajectories, MACRO mines frequent contiguous subsequences of tool calls and promotes those above a frequency threshold τ to composite tools. These composites act as reusable macros, enabling the agent to execute complex, multi-step procedures with a single action. The method presumes that commonly repeated sequences are generally useful; indeed, higher composite utilization correlates with improved outcomes. Nevertheless, if such patterns are artifacts of specific cases rather than robust strategies, newly created composites could harm generalization.

### Mechanism 3: GRPO reinforcement loop
To encourage policy use of composites, MACRO employs a reinforcement loop akin to Group-Relative Policy Optimization. Each step receives a sparse binary reward if a composite tool is invoked; advantages are normalized within groups of trajectories. Policy gradient updates with clipping and KL penalty bias the agent toward using composites. This design assumes that composite usage correlates with successful outcomes, supported by performance gains when the reward is active. However, if the reward becomes decoupled from final accuracy, the agent may overuse composites without real benefit, undermining the intended effect.

## Architecture Onboarding
#### Component map
Memory Store (M) ↔ Policy (π_θ); Composite Registry (C) ↔ Policy (π_θ); Trainer → Policy (π_θ); Trainer → Composite Registry (C).

#### Critical path
For a new image x: (1) extract frozen visual features; (2) retrieve top‑k similar entries from M; (3) construct prompt with retrieved examples and task instruction; (4) policy selects an action (atomic tool, composite, or answer); (5) execute tool (if invoked) and update context; (6) repeat from step 4 until answer or step limit. After a correct final answer, the full trajectory updates M and composite mining updates C.

#### Design tradeoffs
- **Memory size vs. retrieval latency:** Larger M improves coverage but slows cosine‑similarity search.
- **Composite frequency threshold τ:** Low τ yields many noisy composites; high τ yields few, possibly missing useful patterns.
- **GRPO group size G:** Larger G stabilizes advantage estimates but increases sample complexity.

#### Failure signatures
- Memory returns irrelevant examples → policy degrades on similar cases.
- Composite registry grows but never invoked → reward signal ineffective, policy ignores composites.
- GRPO loss diverges → learning rate too high, or reward too sparse/unstable.

#### First 3 experiments
1. **Memory sanity check:** Force retrieval of a known successful trajectory; verify the policy can reproduce its tool sequence when given that context.
2. **Composite registration rate:** Track |C_t| over training; expect rapid early growth then saturation.
3. **GRPO reward correlation:** Plot rolling average composite invocation rate vs. BACC during Stage 2; expect positive correlation if reward is meaningful.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1: Composite quality assurance**  
  How can MACRO ensure that automatically discovered composite tools are high‑quality and not suboptimal, given that noisy trajectories risk introducing flawed skills? The paper explicitly states that quality is bounded by trajectory verification fidelity, but the current system gates updates solely by final answer correctness, allowing intermediate errors to propagate. Resolving this would require manual validation of step‑level correctness, cross‑validation on held‑out data, or lightweight oversight (e.g., confidence scoring).

- **Open Question 2: Generalization to unseen imaging modalities**  
  To what extent does MACRO generalize to entirely unseen imaging modalities with different visual semantics, and what specific failure modes arise? Evaluations were confined to fundus, echocardiogram, and radiograph; transfer to novel modalities (e.g., MRI, CT, histopathology) remains unexplored. Evidence from systematic out‑of‑distribution evaluations and error analysis is needed.

- **Open Question 3: Clinical utility and human factors**  
  In real clinical environments, does MACRO improve radiologists’ workflow efficiency and diagnostic accuracy when used as an assistive tool, and how does it impact clinician trust and decision‑making? The study reports only offline predictive performance; no human‑in‑the‑loop studies exist. Prospective trials measuring time, accuracy, workload, and qualitative feedback are required.

## Limitations
- The paper lacks complete specifications for the atomic tool implementations and their individual accuracy, which are crucial for reproducibility.
- Critical hyperparameters (e.g., composite discovery threshold τ, GRPO group size G, KL coefficient, learning rates) are not reported, hindering exact replication.
- The teacher model (DeepSeek variant) and prompting template are unspecified, introducing uncertainty about demonstration quality.
- Evaluation is restricted to three datasets; broader clinical generalizability remains unproven.
- No analysis of practical deployment metrics such as inference latency or memory overhead is provided.

## Next Checks
- **Memory relevance audit:** For a held‑out clinical subset, retrieve top‑k entries and have experts judge clinical similarity; correlate scores with downstream policy success rates.
- **Cross‑dataset composite transfer:** Train MACRO on REFUGE2 only, then directly evaluate on an external glaucoma dataset without updating composites; quantify performance drop versus in‑dataset test.
- **Reward signal ablation:** Replace the binary composite‑usage reward with a dense, step‑wise accuracy signal and retrain; compare final BACC/F1 to determine whether GRPO specifically incentivizes effective composites or merely any composite call.
