# Bridging the Domain Divide: Supervised vs. Zero-Shot Clinical Section Segmentation from MIMIC-III to Obstetrics
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.17513
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17513
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Zero-shot LLMs, after a simple hallucination correction step, significantly outperform supervised models trained on MIMIC-III when applied to a novel obstetrics dataset.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates domain generalization in clinical section segmentation. Models supervised on the broad MedSecId dataset (derived from MIMIC-III) show strong in-domain performance but degrade substantially on out-of-domain obstetrics notes (ONC). In contrast, large zero-shot language models, despite initially producing invalid section headers, achieve robust performance on ONC after a post-processing step that maps hallucinations to valid labels using a stronger LLM (GPT-4o). The best zero-shot model (Llama 3.3-70B-Instruct) reaches a weighted F1 of 0.86 on ONC, surpassing all supervised baselines. This demonstrates that zero-shot segmentation with hallucination mitigation is a promising low-resource alternative for specialized clinical domains.

## Method Summary
The study compares supervised transformer models (BERT, BioBERT, BiomedBERT, GatorTron) trained on MedSecId (2,002 MIMIC-III notes, 51 labels) against zero-shot instruction-tuned LLMs (Mistral-7B to Llama 3.3-70B) on a held-out obstetrics dataset (ONC, 100 notes, 28 labels). Supervised models were trained in two configurations: line-level independent classification and note-level with a CRF layer. Zero-shot models were prompted with enumerated lines and a label list; their raw outputs were parsed and then corrected via GPT-4o to map any hallucinated (non-standard) section headers to the closest valid label. Performance was evaluated using macro and weighted F1 scores, and hallucination rates were measured.

## Key Results
- Supervised models maintain strong performance on MedSecId (macro F1 up to 0.69) but experience a large performance drop on the out-of-domain ONC dataset (macro F1 down to 0.50).
- Zero-shot LLMs initially hallucinate invalid headers, but after GPT-4o correction, the best model (Llama 3.3-70B-Instruct) achieves a weighted F1 of 0.86 on ONC, outperforming all supervised approaches.
- The correction step increases macro F1 by 9–33% for zero-shot models by reducing label noise from hallucinations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Supervised Overfitting to Source Domain
Supervised models overfit to the linguistic patterns, section header distributions, and note-type specifics of the MIMIC-III-derived MedSecId dataset. The ONC dataset introduces obstetrics-specific headers (e.g., "pregnancy-history") and abbreviations (e.g., "a/p") that are absent or rare in training, causing misclassification. Evidence: Abstract states supervised models suffer a "substantial drop" from 0.69 to 0.50 macro F1; Section 5.4 confirms they "struggle to generalize." Break condition: Greater overlap in headers/phrasing between datasets would narrow the gap.

### Zero-Shot LLMs Leverage Pretrained Generalization
Instruction-tuned LLMs generalize robustly because their massive pretraining on diverse text corpora implicitly encodes general clinical documentation structures and semantic relationships. They use contextual reasoning to map unfamiliar obstetrics content to plausible headers via the prompt’s label set and example, without needing fine-tuning. Evidence: Abstract notes "robust out-of-domain adaptability" after correction; Section 5.4 shows Llama 3.3-70B bests all supervised models on ONC. Break condition: Highly specialized jargon or novel section structures absent from pretraining could cause sharp degradation.

### Hallucination Correction Recovers Performance
A simple postprocessing step using GPT-4o to map hallucinated headers (e.g., "substance-abuse") to semantically closest valid labels (e.g., "social-history") substantially improves performance. This aligns predictions with the evaluation schema, converting precision-damaging false positives into true positives. Evidence: Section 5.2 reports macro F1 increases of 9–33%; error analysis shows reduced "Label confusion." Break condition: If hallucinations are completely unrelated to any valid label or if GPT-4o mapping is inaccurate, correction fails.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Input Data -> Formatter (enumerate lines, append labels) -> LLM Inference -> Output Parser (regex) -> Correction Module (GPT-4o) -> Label Mapper -> Metrics
Supervised path: Data Loader -> Transformer Backend -> [Optional CRF] -> Classification Head -> Loss

### Critical Path
1. Preprocess MedSecId and ONC: split notes into lines, assign section labels.
2. Train supervised models on MedSecID train (80% of lines); evaluate on MedSecId test and full ONC.
3. For zero-shot: construct prompts for each ONC note, run LLM, parse outputs.
4. Apply GPT-4o correction to hallucinated headers.
5. Compute precision, recall, F1 (macro/weighted) and hallucination rate.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Supervised: Line-level independence (simpler, faster) vs. note-level with CRF (models transitions but slower, batch size 1). Affects boundary consistency.
- Zero-shot: Model size (70B vs. 7B) vs. resource constraints; larger models hallucinate less but need more memory.
- Correction: GPT-4o (high accuracy, external API) vs. embedding-based mapping (private, faster but less precise).

### Failure Signatures
- Supervised: Macro F1 drop >20% absolute on ONC vs. MedSecId indicates poor transfer.
- Zero-shot raw: Hallucination rate >10%; many lines as "<none>" or invalid headers.
- Correction: Post-correction F1 does not improve, suggesting mapping errors or divergent hallucinations.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Baseline supervised test:** Train BiomedBERT on MedSecId train (80%), evaluate on MedSecId test and ONC. Confirm in-domain strength and out-of-domain drop.

3. **Correction efficacy test:** Apply GPT-4o mapping to raw outputs from Exp 2. Compare macro F1 before/after. Validate corrected outputs exceed supervised baselines on ONC.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can the Obstetrics Notes Collection (ONC) be expanded and standardized to better capture the full variability of obstetrics documentation and enable cross-study comparability?
2. Can hallucination mitigation strategies for zero-shot LLMs be made fully privacy-preserving and robust enough for clinical deployment without relying on external APIs like GPT-4o?
3. What is the precise reason for the inconsistent performance gain from adding a CRF layer to supervised models (e.g., gains for BERT-base but not GatorTron-base) in out-of-domain generalization?
4. To what extent do the remaining errors after hallucination correction (omissions, label confusion) stem from the zero-shot LLM's lack of deep clinical domain knowledge versus prompt formulation limitations?
5. Does the superior out-of-domain performance of large zero-shot LLMs (like Llama 3.3-70B) primarily result from their scale, their instruction-tuning, or their inherent ability to perform implicit sequence modeling across long contexts?

## Limitations
- Dataset access: ONC is not publicly available via the provided placeholder URL, blocking direct reproduction.
- Reproducibility gaps: Critical supervised training hyperparameters (learning rate, epochs) and the exact GPT-4o correction prompt are omitted.
- Evaluation context: Supervised models truncate lines to 100 tokens while LLMs process full notes, a contextual discrepancy not analyzed as a confounder.

## Confidence
- Supervised models suffer a significant out-of-domain performance drop (MedSecId → ONC): **High** (clear comparative results).
- Zero-shot LLMs (post-correction) outperform supervised models on ONC: **Medium** (supported by tables but tempered by unreported correction details).
- Simple hallucination correction is the primary driver of zero-shot success: **Medium** (improvement shown but without ablation of correction errors).

## Next Checks
1. **Dataset & label alignment audit:** Upon ONC release, verify header distribution and exact mapping between ONC's 28 labels and the 30-header prompt set. Re-evaluate supervised models on the overlapping label subset to isolate pure domain shift.
2. **Correction mapping accuracy test:** Sample raw LLM hallucinations and their GPT-4o mappings; have clinical annotators judge semantic correctness. Report human-GPT agreement and error cases.
3. **Context-length control experiment:** Re-run zero-shot LLMs with a line-level input format (matching supervised truncation) to control for note-level context. Compare performance to full-note prompting to isolate the effect of document context.
