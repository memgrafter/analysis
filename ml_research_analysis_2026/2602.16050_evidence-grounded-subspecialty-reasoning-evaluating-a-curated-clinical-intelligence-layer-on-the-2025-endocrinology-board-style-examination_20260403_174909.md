# Evidence-Grounded Subspecialty Reasoning: Evaluating a Curated Clinical Intelligence Layer on the 2025 Endocrinology Board-Style Examination

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16050
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16050
- **Reference count:** 6
- **Primary result:** A curated, evidence-grounded system (Mirror) achieved 87.5% accuracy on a 120-question endocrinology board exam, exceeding frontier LLMs with web retrieval and a human reference.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates "Mirror," an evidence-grounded clinical reasoning system that operates without web access by using a curated endocrinology corpus, against state-of-the-art LLMs with unrestricted web retrieval on the ESAP 2025 endocrinology examination. Mirror significantly outperformed all compared models, including GPT-5.2 (74.6%) and a human mean (62.3%), while providing 100% citation accuracy on its guideline-tier outputs. The results suggest that for subspecialty reasoning, a controlled, high-quality evidence base with a structured reasoning architecture can surpass the benefits of broad web information access.

## Method Summary
The study conducted a zero-shot evaluation on a proprietary 120-question endocrinology board examination (ESAP 2025). Mirror used a multi-tiered, pre-vetted corpus of society guidelines, peer-reviewed literature, and pivotal trials, organized by evidence authority. An ensemble-style architecture routed questions to specialized reasoning components (e.g., diagnosis, treatment) which generated evidence-linked candidate answers; an arbitration layer selected the final output based on evidence quality and internal agreement. Frontier LLMs (GPT-5.2, GPT-5, Gemini-3-Pro) were evaluated with unrestricted real-time web access and prompted to prioritize authoritative sources. Performance was measured via single-answer accuracy, top-2 accuracy, accuracy by question difficulty/type, and evidence traceability metrics. Statistical comparisons used McNemar’s test.

## Key Results
- Mirror achieved 87.5% accuracy (105/120), exceeding all frontier LLM baselines (GPT-5.2: 74.6%, GPT-5: 74.0%, Gemini-3-Pro: 69.8%) and a human reference mean (62.3%).
- On the hardest questions (human accuracy <50%), Mirror scored 76.7%.
- Top-2 accuracy was 92.5%.
- Evidence traceability: 74.2% of outputs cited at least one guideline-tier source, with 100% citation accuracy on manual verification.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** A curated, domain-specific evidence corpus with explicit hierarchy outperforms general web retrieval for subspecialty clinical reasoning by ensuring quality, relevance, and recency control.
- **Mechanism:** The system consults a pre-vetted, multi-tiered corpus where sources are graded by authority (e.g., society guidelines > high-impact literature > pivotal trials). Retrieval is tuned for precise clinical recommendations rather than general semantic similarity.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states curated evidence outperforms unconstrained retrieval; section 2.4 details corpus organization.
- **Break condition:** If a newly released, high-impact guideline or trial becomes standard of care but is absent from the curated corpus, performance on related questions will degrade relative to systems with fresh web access.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A structured, ensemble reasoning architecture that routes questions to specialized components and uses evidence-based arbitration yields more reliable outputs than a monolithic LLM.
- **Mechanism:** Questions are decomposed by clinical archetype (diagnosis, treatment, etc.); parallel specialized modules generate evidence-linked candidate answers. An arbitration layer selects the final output using signals of evidence tier and inter-module agreement.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions structured reasoning stack; section 2.4 describes ensemble architecture; consistent performance across question types (section 3.4) supports archetype-specific handling.
- **Break condition:** Questions that unpredictably span multiple archetypes (e.g., a diagnostic workup requiring immediate therapy choice) may cause routing or arbitration failures.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Mandatory, verifiable citation traceability transforms the system into an auditable decision-support tool, building clinician trust and enabling safety oversight.
- **Mechanism:** Every output must cite specific, retrievable passages from the curated corpus. A verification step confirms the cited passage exists, is accurately represented, and logically supports the answer.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract reports 74.2% guideline-tier citation and 100% accuracy; section 3.6 details manual verification; section 4.2 links traceability to addressing the "black box" problem.
- **Break condition:** If citation verification fails (misattributed or non-supportive passages), clinician trust collapses regardless of answer accuracy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Evidence Hierarchy in Medicine**
  - **Why needed:** Mirror prioritizes society guidelines (e.g., ADA, Endocrine Society) over lower-tier sources. Understanding why a KDIGO guideline citation carries more weight than a case report is essential to grasp the corpus design and arbitration logic.
  - **Quick check:** For first-line hypertension treatment in diabetic kidney disease, why must the system retrieve a KDIGO guideline rather than a recent observational study suggesting an alternative?
- **Concept: Clinical Reasoning Archetypes**
  - **Why needed:** The architecture splits reasoning into diagnosis, treatment, testing, etc. Distinguishing these is necessary to understand component routing and error patterns (e.g., treatment-related errors).
  - **Quick check:** For a vignette with lab abnormalities and a pituitary mass, is the primary archetype "diagnosis" (identifying acromegaly) or "treatment" (choosing surgery vs. medication)? How might the system route this?
- **Concept: Retrieval vs. Recall in Subspecialty QA**
  - **Why needed:** The paper contrasts "semantic similarity" retrieval with "clinical relevance" retrieval. An engineer must understand why a query like "A1c target for elderly patients" must retrieve a specific ADA guideline table, not a general article.
  - **Quick check:** Why might a web search for "SGLT2 inhibitor in heart failure" return a news article about market pricing, and how does the curated corpus avoid this?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Curated Evidence Corpus -> Archetype Router -> Specialized Reasoning Modules (Diagnosis, Treatment, etc.) -> Arbitration Layer -> Output Formatter
**Critical path:** Question -> Archetype classification -> Parallel evidence retrieval & reasoning -> Arbitration (evidence-based consensus) -> Output with citations. Failure at any point (misrouting, missing evidence) breaks the chain.
**Design tradeoffs:**
- Closed corpus vs. freshness: Sacrifices immediate new evidence for guaranteed quality and traceability; mitigated by scheduled updates.
- Ensemble complexity vs. speed: Parallel modules increase latency but improve robustness across archetypes.
- Rigid structure vs. flexibility: Archetype routing may fail on hybrid questions but provides predictable behavior and easier auditing.
**Failure signatures:**
1. Citation missing or low-tier for a guideline-dependent question (e.g., treatment threshold).
2. Arbitration deadlock: cannot resolve conflicting high-evidence candidates from different modules.
3. Corpus gap: system returns "evidence not found" for questions testing very recent guideline changes.
**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Retrieval precision test:** Feed 50 questions with known guideline-dependent answers; measure % where top retrieved passage is from the correct guideline and contains the exact recommendation.
2. **Archetype routing sanity check:** Manually label 100 questions by primary archetype; compare system assignments to human labels; misrouting rate >15% indicates router failure.
3. **End-to-end citation audit:** Randomly sample 30 outputs. For each, verify: (a) cited passage exists in corpus, (b) passage text matches the claim, (c) no higher-tier relevant evidence omitted. Target: 100% pass on (a) and (b), <5% fail on (c).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Generalization:** Does the evidence-grounded approach generalize to other medical subspecialties (cardiology, nephrology) beyond endocrinology?
2. **Source of advantage:** To what extent does performance stem from curated corpus quality vs. structured reasoning architecture, independent of web retrieval?
3. **Geographic transferability:** How does the U.S.-centric, society-guideline-focused corpus perform in healthcare systems with different standards (e.g., NICE, WHO) or resource constraints?
4. **Contributing factors:** What specific properties of curated evidence (grading, recency control, noise removal) most contribute to the improvement over generic web retrieval?
5. **Clinical utility:** Does high board exam accuracy translate to improved safety and utility in real-world, ambiguous decision-support workflows as assessed by clinicians?

## Limitations
- Undisclosed curated corpus content and update mechanism; coverage gaps unknown.
- Proprietary reasoning architecture (router, arbitration) prevents algorithmic replication.
- Frontier LLM baseline evaluation lacks detail (search protocol, prompts), risking biased comparison.
- Proprietary ESAP 2025 exam blocks independent verification; human reference is aggregate.
- Generalizability outside endocrinology and to live clinical settings untested.

## Confidence
- **Mirror outperforms frontier LLMs:** Medium (strong internal results but unverifiable benchmark).
- **Curated corpus > web retrieval:** Low (confounded by model/architecture differences; no ablation).
- **Ensemble architecture improves reliability:** Low (no monolithic baseline; indirect evidence only).
- **Traceability ensures auditability & trust:** Medium (citation accuracy validated; trust unmeasured).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablate corpus hierarchy:** Randomize or remove tier ordering while keeping documents constant; measure accuracy drop.
2. **Compare to monolithic baseline:** Implement a non-ensemble Mirror (raw LLM + same corpus) on a public endocrinology question bank; measure performance gap.
3. **Open replication with trust assessment:** Use public guidelines (e.g., ADA 2024) and an available question set to reproduce accuracy; run a clinician study on perceived trust with/without citations.
