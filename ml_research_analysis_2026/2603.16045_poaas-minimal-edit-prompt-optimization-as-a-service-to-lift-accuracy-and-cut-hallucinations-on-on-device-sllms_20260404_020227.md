# POaaS: Minimal-Edit Prompt Optimization as a Service to Lift Accuracy and Cut Hallucinations on On-Device sLLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.16045
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16045
- **Reference count:** 31
- **Primary result:** POaaS improves accuracy by +1.4% to +2.3% and factuality by +1.4% to +5.4% over no optimization on sLLMs, while APO baselines degrade both; under token corruption it recovers up to +7.4% accuracy with minimal overhead (~48 tokens, ~0.95s latency).

## Executive Summary
On-device small language models (sLLMs) suffer from the overhead and brittleness of existing Automatic Prompt Optimization (APO) methods, which are designed for large cloud LLMs. POaaS introduces a minimal-edit optimization layer that applies targeted, conservative refinements to user prompts before they reach the sLLM. It uses lightweight CPU-only heuristics to skip refinement for already well-formed prompts, and routes lower-quality prompts to three specialist adapters (Cleaner, Paraphraser, Fact-Adder) that perform minimal edits under strict drift and length controls. Evaluated on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct across six benchmarks, POaaS consistently improves task accuracy (by +1.4% to +2.3%) and factuality (by +1.4% to +5.4%) over no optimization, while three APO baselines degrade performance. Under simulated input corruption (5–15% token deletion/mixup), POaaS recovers up to +7.4% accuracy relative to no optimization. The system adds only ~48 tokens and ~0.95s latency per query, making it suitable for on-device deployment.

## Method Summary
POaaS is a service that intercepts user prompts before they are processed by a frozen on-device sLLM. An orchestrator running on the CPU first computes four heuristic scores: typo density, completeness, fluency, and clarity. If the overall quality score exceeds 0.75 and typo score is below 0.20, the prompt is deemed high-quality and passed through unchanged (conservative skip). Otherwise, the orchestrator selectively activates up to three specialist LoRA adapters: Cleaner (corrects typos and token errors), Paraphraser (improves fluency without altering meaning), and Fact-Adder (prepends concise factual bullets to address missing context). Each specialist’s output is evaluated by a drift guard that computes an ensemble similarity (sequence matching, n-gram Jaccard, token overlap) against the original prompt and checks content preservation (retention of numbers, quotes, entities). Only outputs within agent-specific drift caps (Cleaner ≤0.15, Paraphraser ≤0.08, overall ≤0.18) are accepted. Accepted edits are merged with a fixed precedence: Cleaner first, then Paraphraser, then Fact-Adder bullets, while enforcing a maximum length expansion factor of 2.4×. The optimized prompt is then fed to the sLLM. The entire pipeline adds approximately 48 tokens on average and incurs ~0.95s of latency per query, significantly less than APO methods that require minutes of offline search and add thousands of tokens.

## Key Results
- POaaS yields average task accuracy gains of +1.4% to +2.3% over no optimization on Llama-3.2-3B and Llama-3.1-8B across BBH, GSM8K, and CommonsenseQA.
- Factuality improves by +1.4% to +5.4% over no optimization on HaluEval, HalluLens, and FActScore.
- Under token deletion/mixup (5–15%), POaaS recovers up to +7.4% accuracy relative to no optimization, while EvoPrompt, OPRO, and PromptWizard degrade further.
- Overhead is minimal: adds ~48 prompt tokens and ~0.95s latency per query, contrasting with APO baselines that add thousands of tokens and require minutes of offline search.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
POaaS’s effectiveness arises from three synergistic mechanisms:

1. **Conservative skip logic prevents over-editing.** The orchestrator’s heuristic scores identify high-quality prompts (quality >0.75, typo <0.20) and bypass all specialists. This avoids the risk of unnecessary rewrites that could harm intent preservation, which is critical for sLLMs with limited context capacity and reasoning robustness. The skip gate prefers false negatives over false positives, ensuring only prompts likely to benefit are modified.

2. **Strict drift and content preservation guards maintain intent.** Each candidate edit is scored against the original using an ensemble similarity metric and a content preservation ratio that checks retention of key items (numbers, quotes, entities). Only edits within agent-specific drift caps (Cleaner ≤0.15, Paraphraser ≤0.08) are merged. These cheap lexical guards effectively prevent semantic drift and hallucinations that would otherwise arise from aggressive rewrites.

3. **Targeted repair under degradation via the Cleaner.** When input prompts suffer token-level noise (deletion, mixup), the typo heuristic increases, routing the prompt to the Cleaner specialist. The Cleaner, fine-tuned on error-correction data with synthetic noise augmentation, directly repairs corrupted tokens. Ablation studies show that removing the Cleaner eliminates most of the robustness gains, confirming its primary role in recovering from corruption.

Together, these mechanisms ensure that edits are applied only when they address concrete deficits (typos, missing context) and never at the cost of intent preservation, all within tight latency and token budgets.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Orchestrator (CPU) computes heuristics, applies skip logic, selects specialists, runs drift and content checks, and merges outputs. Entry point: `/infer`. Specialists are three GPU-accelerated LoRA adapters (Cleaner, Paraphraser, Fact-Adder) served via vLLM with endpoints `/clean`, `/paraphrase`, `/fact`. Guards include per-agent guard models that filter unfaithful edits and merger-level sanitizers that strip meta-commentary and preserve few-shot exemplars. The target sLLM (`fθ`) is a frozen model that receives the final optimized prompt.

**Critical path:**
1. User prompt `x` → Orchestrator.
2. Compute heuristic scores → Skip gate? If `quality(x)>0.75` and `typo(x)<0.20`, return `x`.
3. Route to activated specialists: `A ← SELECTAGENTS(ϕ)` based on thresholds.
4. For each activated agent `a ∈ A`: generate candidate `x' ← a(x)` → check `WITHINDRIFT(x,x')`? If yes, keep `x'`.
5. If no candidates passed drift, return `x`.
6. `MERGE(x, C)` where `C` is set of accepted candidates, applying precedence (Cleaner → Paraphraser → prepend Fact-Adder bullets) and length caps (`ρ_max=2.4`).
7. Output optimized prompt `˜x` → frozen `fθ`.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Conservative skip vs. coverage:** Skip threshold (`τ_skip=0.25`) avoids harmful edits but may miss marginal gains on ambiguous prompts.
- **Drift cap tightness:** Tight caps (`δ_para=0.08`) preserve intent but may reject beneficial paraphrases; relaxation only occurs under low clarity.
- **Specialist budget vs. utility:** Fixed budget of ~48 added tokens and ~0.95s latency; adding more specialists could help but risks exceeding budget or increasing drift.
- **Fixed routing thresholds vs. adaptive:** Thresholds are empirically tuned; automatic calibration per deployment could improve portability across domains/languages.

**Failure signatures:**
- **High skip rate on noisy prompts:** If `typo(x)` is low despite semantic corruption (e.g., word substitution), the Cleaner is not invoked → no repair.
- **All specialists rejected:** Drift checks fail for all candidates → fallback to original `x`, but underlying prompt issue remains.
- **Fact-Adder leakage:** Guard fails to filter reasoning-like bullets → increased hallucination risk.
- **Few-shot corruption:** If few-shot exemplars are present but editing logic fails to isolate the final query, exemplars may be altered.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Main accuracy/factuality comparison (Table 1):** Run POaaS and APO baselines (EvoPrompt, OPRO, PromptWizard) on clean BBH, GSM8K, CommonsenseQA (accuracy) and HaluEval, HalluLens, FActScore (factuality) with Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Expect POaaS to show gains (+1.4% to +2.3% acc, +1.4% to +5.4% factuality) while APO degrades.
2. **Degradation robustness (Table 2):** Apply 5%, 10%, 15% token deletion and mixup perturbations to inputs. Expect POaaS to recover accuracy (e.g., +7.4% at 15% deletion vs. No Optimization) while APO baselines degrade further.
3. **Ablation (Table 4):** Disable individual specialists (`w/o Cleaner`, `w/o Paraphraser`, `w/o Fact-Adder`). Expect full POaaS to yield highest gains; Cleaner-only to recover robustness but not clean accuracy; Fact-Adder removal to hurt clean gains most.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Multilingual adaptation:** What specific adaptations are required to port POaaS to multilingual contexts given its English-tuned heuristics and specialists? The paper acknowledges the need but provides no investigation.
- **Automatic threshold calibration:** Can the empirically determined thresholds (e.g., `τ_typo`, `δ_max`) be automated through learning or per-deployment calibration? This would improve portability but is unexplored.
- **Expanding the specialist set:** How can the specialist palette be expanded (e.g., domain-specific agents) without violating POaaS’s minimal-edit and efficiency constraints? The paper suggests this direction but offers no safe integration criteria.

## Limitations
- **Evaluation dependencies:** Factuality scores rely on GPT-5 (unavailable), making exact replication impossible; alternative judges may yield different results. Few-shot exemplars for key benchmarks are undisclosed, hindering exact replication of accuracy numbers.
- **Generalization of fixed thresholds:** Heuristic and drift thresholds are empirically tuned on a limited set of models and datasets; they may not generalize to all domains, languages, or newer models.
- **Scope of evaluation:** Experiments cover only two sLLMs (Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B) and six datasets; broader validation is needed to assert wide applicability.
