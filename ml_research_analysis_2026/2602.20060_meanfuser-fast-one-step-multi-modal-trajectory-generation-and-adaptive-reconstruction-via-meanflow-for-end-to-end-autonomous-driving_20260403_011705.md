# MeanFuser: Fast One-Step Multi-Modal Trajectory Generation and Adaptive Reconstruction via MeanFlow for End-to-End Autonomous Driving

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.20060  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20060  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Achieves 89.0 PDMS (NAVSIM v1), 89.5 EPDMS (v2), and 70.08 Driving Score (CARLA Longest6) at 59 FPS, with up to 5.78× speedup over diffusion-based planners.

## Executive Summary
MeanFuser resolves the robustness–efficiency trade-off in anchor-guided autonomous driving planners by introducing three innovations: a continuous Gaussian Mixture Noise (GMN) prior to replace discrete anchors, a one-step MeanFlow decoder that predicts the mean velocity field to avoid iterative ODE solvers, and an Adaptive Reconstruction Module (ARM) that selects or regenerates trajectories when proposals are suboptimal. Evaluated on NAVSIM and CARLA, it sets new state-of-the-art planning performance while achieving real-time inference (59 FPS), demonstrating that continuous generative modeling can be both highly effective and efficient.

## Method Summary
The method centers on three components. First, GMN constructs a continuous prior from k-means clusters of normalized trajectory deltas, enabling multi-modal generation without fixed anchors. Second, MeanFlow models trajectory generation by predicting the mean velocity field; training uses a loss based on Jacobian-vector products to ensure one-step sampling accuracy. Third, ARM fuses multiple proposals with contextual features via cross-attention and outputs a final trajectory through a projector, supervised by L₁ loss. Training on NAVSIM uses AdamW with an auxiliary mapping loss and combines flow and trajectory losses. During inference, eight noise samples from GMN are transformed in one step by MeanFlow, then refined by ARM to produce the final plan.

## Key Results
- **SOTA planning metrics:** 89.0 PDMS (NAVSIM v1), 89.5 EPDMS (v2), 70.08 Driving Score (CARLA Longest6).
- **Unmatched efficiency:** 59 FPS inference, up to 5.2× faster than GoalFlow and 5.78× faster than DiffusionDrive in planning.
- **Ablation benefits:** GMN enhances trajectory multimodality; ARM improves robustness on edge cases.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
GMN replaces discrete anchors with a continuous mixture, capturing diverse driving behaviors without vocabulary limits. MeanFlow’s one-step prediction leverages the linearity of velocity fields in latent space, avoiding iterative diffusion while preserving multimodality through diverse noise samples. ARM adaptively selects or regenerates trajectories based on contextual cues, ensuring robustness when initial proposals fail. Together, these innovations deliver both high performance and efficiency by eliminating anchor constraints, removing ODE overhead, and adding a safety layer for suboptimal proposals.

## Foundational Learning
- **Anchor-based trajectory generation:** Uses a fixed discrete set of trajectory prototypes; *why needed:* historically simplified multi-modal modeling but limits diversity and generalization. **Quick check:** Verify anchor vocabulary covers all maneuver types in the training dataset.
- **Continuous generative modeling:** Represents trajectories as samples from a continuous distribution (e.g., Gaussian mixture); *why needed:* avoids artificial discretization and supports unbounded variations. **Quick check:** Sample from the prior and ensure coverage of ground-truth trajectory space.
- **One-step diffusion/MeanFlow:** Predicts the mean flow for direct sampling instead of iterative denoising; *why needed:* eliminates ODE solver cost while maintaining quality. **Quick check:** Compare one-step outputs against multi-step diffusion on a held-out set for fidelity.
- **Adaptive trajectory selection:** Uses context-aware mechanisms to choose or regenerate proposals; *why needed:* handles cases where initial proposals are unsafe or infeasible. **Quick check:** Ablate the selection module and measure performance drop on challenging scenarios.
- **Jacobian-vector products (JVP):** Computes derivatives along specific directions for MeanFlow target; *why needed:* enables efficient, stable training of the flow field. **Quick check:** Validate JVP outputs against finite-difference approximations on small perturbations.
- **Cross-attention for fusion:** Allows ARM to relate proposals to contextual features; *why needed:* dynamically weights proposals based on scene context. **Quick check:** Visualize attention maps to confirm focus on relevant regions (e.g., traffic agents).

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Multi-view images + ego state -> Backbone (ResNet-34) -> GMN (sample 8 noise points) -> MeanFlow decoder (predict velocity) -> Proposed trajectories -> ARM (cross-attention fusion + projector) -> Final trajectory.
Critical path: End-to-end training with three losses (trajectory L₁, flow L₁, mapping auxiliary), optimized via AdamW; inference parallelizes 8 proposals in one forward pass.
Design tradeoffs: GMN uses fixed k-means clusters (simple but not learnable); MeanFlow assumes linear latent paths (efficient but may limit long-horizon accuracy); ARM adds complexity for robustness.
Failure signatures: Low trajectory diversity (D metric) indicates GMN variances too small; NaN loss suggests JVP instability; slow inference implies non-parallel proposal generation.
First three experiments:
1. Visualize GMN samples against ground-truth trajectories to confirm continuous prior coverage.
2. Monitor gradient norms during early training to detect MeanFlow target instability.
3. Ablate ARM and evaluate on NAVSIM rare maneuvers to quantify robustness degradation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can we model multi-modal driving behaviors without fixed anchor vocabularies?** MeanFuser’s GMN is a step, but broader continuous representation trade-offs (e.g., VAEs, continuous diffusion) remain unclear.
- **How to optimally parameterize the Gaussian Mixture Noise for trajectory generation?** Current k-means heuristic may not fully capture the distribution; learnable parameters integrated into training could improve generalization.
- **Does the one-step MeanFlow sampling limit performance for longer planning horizons or highly dynamic environments?** Real driving often requires non-linear, multi-step refinement beyond 4 seconds; extending experiments to longer horizons is needed.

## Limitations
- **Missing hyperparameters:** Loss weights (λ₁, λ₂, λ₃), training epochs, and batch size are unspecified, hindering exact replication.
- **GMN clustering vagueness:** k-means details (seed, iterations) are not reported, risking prior inconsistencies across reproductions.
- **CARLA evaluation opacity:** Integration procedure and route selection logic are not detailed, making independent verification difficult.

## Confidence
- **Performance claims (PDMS/EPDMS, Driving Score):** Medium – benchmarks are standard but exact evaluation code and metric weighting are unclear.
- **Efficiency claims (59 FPS):** High – speed is directly measurable and likely reproducible with matching hardware and model size.
- **Methodological claims (GMN/MeanFlow/ARM benefits):** Medium – ablations support gains, but implementation nuances (e.g., JVP stability, ARM projector) may cause deviations.
