# FINEST: Improving LLM Responses to Sensitive Topics Through Fine-Grained Evaluation
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.04123  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04123  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Score-based fine-grained feedback reduces error sentence ratios by up to 33.09% and achieves 88% human preference in improved LLM responses on sensitive topics.

## Executive Summary
Large language models often respond overly cautiously on sensitive topics, sacrificing helpfulness for safety. FINEST addresses this by introducing a fine-grained evaluation taxonomy that decomposes response quality into Content (potential harm), Logic (reasoning coherence), and Appropriateness (contextual responsiveness). Using a Korean-sensitive question dataset, the authors build an automated pipeline: responses are first evaluated via GPT-4o using either error-based or score-based schemes, then refined based on that feedback. The score-based approach—providing category scores with justifications—yields the strongest improvements, significantly lowering error ratios (especially Appropriateness) and earning an 88% win rate in human pairwise evaluations. This demonstrates that structured, targeted feedback can simultaneously enhance both safety and utility.

## Method Summary
The FINEST pipeline consists of two stages: evaluation and improvement. In evaluation, GPT-4o judges each response against the taxonomy, either producing sentence-level error annotations with explanations (error-based) or assigning 1–7 scores per category with justifications (score-based). For improvement, the original question–response pair is combined with the feedback and reprompted to GPT-4o to generate a revised answer. Four improvement conditions are compared: ImprovedFINEST-Score, ImprovedFINEST-Error, ImprovedFINEST-TaxoOnly (taxonomy description only), and ImprovedSelf (no guidance). Experiments use a 3k-sample drawn from a larger Korean-sensitive dataset comprising 19k questions and 175k responses from multiple models and prompt styles. Success is measured by reductions in error sentence ratio and category score gains, alongside human preference.

## Key Results
- Score-based feedback reduced the Appropriateness error sentence ratio by up to 33.09%.
- Human evaluators preferred improved responses over originals in 88% of pairwise comparisons.
- The score-based scheme outperformed error-based, taxonomy-only, and self-improvement methods across most categories.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The score-based feedback provides explicit, structured guidance at the category level, enabling the model to target specific weaknesses without the noise and ambiguity of fine-grained error annotations. By quantifying performance on Content, Logic, and Appropriateness separately, the refinement step can balance improvements across dimensions, avoiding overcorrection in one area at the expense of others. This precision yields more effective and consistent enhancements in both safety and helpfulness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Fine-grained evaluation taxonomy**  
  *Why needed*: Systematically decompose failures in sensitive responses to enable targeted fixes.  
  *Quick check*: Do the three categories comprehensively cover common error types (harmful content, incoherence, contextual mismatch)?

- **Error sentence ratio**  
  *Why needed*: Quantifies the proportion of a response flagged as erroneous, offering a direct measure of problematic content.  
  *Quick check*: Can multiple annotators consistently identify erroneous spans in responses?

- **Sensitive topic handling**  
  *Why needed*: Balancing harmlessness and helpfulness is critical for real-world LLM deployment.  
  *Quick check*: Does the dataset capture diverse sensitive contexts and cultural nuances?

- **Feedback-driven refinement**  
  *Why needed*: External feedback guides the model to correct specific weaknesses.  
  *Quick check*: Does structured feedback lead to measurable improvements without degrading other quality aspects?

- **Cultural specificity**  
  *Why needed*: Sensitivity norms vary across cultures; findings may not generalize.  
  *Quick check*: Would the same taxonomy and approach work for non-Korean sensitive topics?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Korean-sensitive QA dataset → GPT-4o evaluator (FINEST prompts) → feedback (error list or category scores) → GPT-4o refiner (improvement prompts) → refined responses.

**Critical path**  
Evaluation step produces feedback; improvement step consumes feedback plus original Q&A to generate revised answer. The choice of feedback format (error vs. score) is a major branching point.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Error-based: More granular but noisier; may over-identify local logic flaws.  
- Score-based: More stable, provides aggregated view but less actionable detail.  
- Taxonomy-only: No feedback, relies solely on prompt instruction; serves as baseline.

**Failure signatures**  
- Excessive error identification, especially in Logic category.  
- Degradation of originally high-quality responses.  
- High API costs and rate limits when scaling to full dataset.

## Limitations
- GPT-4o’s own biases and annotation inaccuracies (∼19.8% error) affect feedback quality.
- The Korean-only dataset limits cross-cultural generalizability.
- Improvement can sometimes degrade originally strong responses.

## Confidence
- Taxonomy validity: High  
- Superiority of score-based feedback: Medium  
- Quantitative gains (33% error reduction, 88% preference): Low-to-Medium
