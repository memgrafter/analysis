# FedZMG: Efficient Client-Side Optimization in Federated Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.18384
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18384
- **Reference Count:** 31
- **Primary Result:** FedZMG mitigates client-drift in non-IID federated learning via a parameter-free gradient projection, achieving 33% validation accuracy on CIFAR100 in 250 rounds (vs. 805 for FedAvg) with statistically significant improvements (p<0.05) across all tested datasets.

## Executive Summary
FedZMG introduces a lightweight, client-side optimizer that addresses client-drift in cross-device federated learning with non-IID data. By projecting local gradients onto a zero-mean hyperplane before each update, it structurally regularizes the optimization space without requiring additional communication, server-side state, or hyperparameter tuning beyond standard learning rate and weight decay. The method demonstrates substantially faster convergence and higher final accuracy compared to FedAvg and FedAdam on image classification (EMNIST, CIFAR100) and next-character prediction (Shakespeare) tasks.

## Method Summary
FedZMG modifies the local client update rule in standard Federated Averaging. During local SGD, each computed gradient *g* is transformed by the Zero Mean Gradient (ZMG) operator: Φ<sub>ZMG</sub>(*g*) = *g* − mean(*g*), where the mean is computed per layer (column-wise for dense layers, over spatial and channel dimensions for convolutional layers). Clients then perform a decoupled weight decay step (*w* ← *w* · (1 − *ηγ*)) followed by the ZMG-projected update (*w* ← *w* − *η* · Φ<sub>ZMG</sub>(*g*)). The server aggregates client models via weighted averaging without any changes. No optimizer state (e.g., momentum) is transmitted, making the method communication-free beyond model weights. Client learning rate (*η*) and weight decay (*λ*) are the only hyperparameters, tuned via grid search.

## Key Results
- On highly non-IID CIFAR100 (2 classes per client), FedZMG reached 33% validation accuracy in **250 rounds**, compared to **805 rounds** for FedAvg and **420 rounds** for FedAdam.
-FedZMG showed **statistically significant improvements (p<0.05)** over FedAvg in final accuracy across all datasets: EMNIST, CIFAR100, and Shakespeare.
- The method achieved faster convergence and higher final accuracy while requiring **no extra communication** or hyperparameter tuning relative to FedAvg.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** FedZMG reduces client-drift by structurally regularizing local gradients, forcing updates to focus on variance rather than mean-based biases from heterogeneous data.
- **Mechanism:** For each local gradient *g*, the ZMG operator Φ<sub>ZMG</sub>(*g*) = *g* − mean(*g*) projects it onto a zero-mean hyperplane by subtracting the per-layer mean (for weight matrices, column-wise). This removes the component orthogonal to the all-ones vector, which encodes global intensity/bias shifts.
- **Core assumption:** In non-IID FL, the mean of local gradients carries client-specific bias, while the directional variance aligns with shared global features.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "projects local gradients onto a zero-mean hyperplane, effectively neutralizing the 'intensity' or 'bias' shifts inherent in heterogeneous data distributions."
  - Section III.B: Pseudo-code and equations (4)-(7) showing the projection applied before weight updates.
- **Break condition:** If the mean gradient component carries critical, globally consistent learning signal (e.g., certain regression tasks or architectures where magnitude matters equally across features), projection could discard useful information and slow convergence.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** FedZMG achieves communication and hyperparameter efficiency by being a *parameter-free* client-side optimizer—requiring no additional state (like momentum) to be transmitted.
- **Mechanism:** The ZMG operation is stateless and computed solely from the current gradient. Only the updated model weights are sent to the server, identical to FedAvg's payload. No server-side adaptive state (e.g., Adam's moments) or client-side tunable parameters beyond the standard learning rate (*η*) and weight decay (*λ*).
- **Core assumption:** The primary overhead in FL comes from communicating auxiliary optimizer variables; removing this overhead is valuable for resource-constrained IoT.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "parameter-free, client-side optimizer... without requiring additional communication or hyperparameter tuning."
  - Section II: Contrasts with FedCAda and SCAFFOLD which "increase the communication overhead" by transmitting optimizer states.
- **Break condition:** In scenarios where adaptive per-parameter tuning is critical for convergence (e.g., highly sparse gradients), the lack of state might limit performance compared to a tuned adaptive method, despite the communication cost.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The ZMG projection mathematically reduces the *effective gradient variance* term in the convergence bound, leading to tighter theoretical guarantees under non-IID conditions.
- **Mechanism:** The projection matrix Φ = *I* − (1/*d*)11<sup>⊤</sup> is idempotent and non-expansive. Lemma 2 shows the projected gradient variance E[∥*q*<sub>*t*</sub> − *q̃*<sub>*k,t*</sub>∥²] is bounded by the original stochastic variance minus a term (1/*d*)1<sup>⊤</sup>Σ<sub>*g*</sub>1, which is non-negative. This reduces the constant *C* in the convergence recursion Δ<sub>*t*+1</sub> = (1 − *ηγ*)Δ<sub>*t*</sub> + *η*²*C*.
- **Core assumption:** Standard FL assumptions (L-smoothness, µ-strong convexity, bounded stochastic gradient variance) hold, and initialization satisfies 1<sup>⊤</sup>(*ŵ*⁰ − *w*⁎) = 0 (orthogonal error).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section IV.B, Lemma 2: Explicit derivation E[∥*q*<sub>*k,t*</sub> − *q̃*<sub>*k,t*</sub>∥²] = *σ*<sub>*k*</sub>² − (1/*d*)1<sup>⊤</sup>Σ<sub>*g*</sub>¹, proving variance reduction.
  - Theorem 1: Convergence proof showing *C* is strictly smaller than FedAvg's constant *B*.
- **Break condition:** The variance reduction term (1/*d*)1<sup>⊤</sup>Σ<sub>*g*</sub>1 can be small if gradient covariance Σ<sub>*g*</sub> is nearly isotropic (no dominant mean shifts). The benefit is proportional to the mean component's magnitude in the original gradients.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Gradient Centralization (GC)**
  - *Why needed here:* FedZMG's core operator is an adaptation of GC from centralized CNNs to FL. Understanding GC's role in improving generalization and stability in centralized training provides the basis for expecting similar benefits in distributed, non-IID settings.
  - *Quick check question:* In centralized training, what optimization effect does projecting weight gradients to zero-mean have on the loss landscape?
- **Concept: Client-drift in Non-IID FL**
  - *Why needed here:* This is the fundamental problem FedZMG targets. Client-drift describes local models diverging from the global optimum due to heterogeneous data, causing poor aggregation.
  - *Quick check question:* Why do multiple local SGD steps on non-IID data cause a model to move toward a local optimum that hurts global performance?
- **Concept: Projection onto a Subspace (Linear Algebra)**
  - *Why needed here:* The ZMG operation is a linear projection onto the hyperplane {*x* | 1<sup>⊤</sup>*x* = 0}. Understanding projections, orthogonal complements, and idempotent matrices is key to grasping the geometric mechanism.
  - *Quick check question:* Given a vector *v* and the all-ones vector 1, how do you decompose *v* into components parallel and perpendicular to 1?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Central Server → Broadcasts global model → Client Device → (Compute gradient → Apply ZMG → Decoupled weight decay → Update) → Upload model → Server → Weighted average → New global model
- **Critical path:** Server broadcast → client local loop (compute gradient → ZMG projection → decay & update) → client upload → server aggregation → repeat. **No** communication of optimizer moments or control variates.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Pro:* Zero communication overhead, no hyperparameter tuning beyond standard LR/weight decay, simple implementation, theoretically grounded variance reduction.
  - *Con:* Rigidly discards gradient mean; may harm tasks where mean signal is globally consistent. Interaction with normalization layers (BatchNorm) could be redundant or destabilizing in very deep networks.
- **Failure signatures:**
  1. **Convergence plateau at suboptimal accuracy:** Particularly on simple datasets (e.g., EMNIST) or regression tasks; may indicate mean gradient carried useful signal.
  2. **High instability in early rounds:** If batch statistics are extremely noisy, aggressive projection might over-regularize before useful variance emerges.
  3. **No improvement over FedAvg:** On near-IID data splits, the variance reduction term (1/*d*)1<sup>⊤</sup>Σ<sub>*g*</sub>1 becomes negligible, so benefit disappears.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Hyperparameter Grid Search:** As in Section V.B1, perform 9×9 grid over client LR *η*<sub>*c*</sub> and server LR *η*<sub>*s*</sub> (log-spaced 10<sup>−3</sup> to 10<sup>1</sup>) for 50 rounds, select best pair based on last 10-round avg validation accuracy.
  2. **Non-IID Sensitivity Test:** Train on artificially controlled non-IID splits (e.g., 1, 5, 20 classes per client for CIFAR-100) to measure how "Rounds to Threshold" scales with induced heterogeneity.
  3. **Ablation - ZMG vs. No ZMG:** Run FedAvg with identical LR but replace update *w* ← *w* − *ηg* with *w* ← *w* − *η* · Φ<sub>ZMG</sub>(*g*). Isolate the effect of the projection itself from any other FedZMG design choices.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** For regression tasks or model architectures where the mean component of the gradient encodes meaningful signal rather than mere bias, does the FedZMG operator's zero-mean projection impair learning performance compared to standard SGD?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors identify this as a key limitation: "the mean gradient can be considered a bias or noise and not necessarily a learning signal. Although the claim that global features such as variance have high priority for many classification and recognition tasks, there can be other regression problems or specific model architectures where the global intensity or the mean gradient value hold valuable information as well. In these corner cases, projecting the gradient onto a zero-mean hyperplane could potentially slow down the learning process."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper's empirical evaluation is confined to classification tasks (image and next-character prediction). No regression benchmarks or explicit tests on architectures where gradient mean is theoretically informative (e.g., certain generative models) are conducted.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation of FedZMG against FedAvg on standard regression datasets (e.g., UCI regression tasks, federated housing price prediction) and model families (e.g., normalizing flows, energy-based models). Analysis of gradient mean statistics during training to correlate projection impact with task-specific signal content.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the repeated centering operation of FedZMG (on gradients) interact with other normalization layers (e.g., Batch Normalization, LayerNorm) within deep networks, and does this lead to diminishing returns or instability in very deep architectures?
- **Basis in paper:** The limitations section states: "the interaction between the ZMG operator and other normalization layers, such as BN presents a complex dynamic. As activations are also centered by BN, application of ZMG translates to essentially repeating a similar operation to the gradients. Therefore, extremely deep networks could see diminishing returns because the structural constraints can become too redundant."
- **Why unresolved:** All experimental models use moderate depth (max ~5 layers). No ablation study isolates the combined effect of ZMG with modern normalization techniques (BN, LN) across varying network depths (e.g., ResNet-50, Transformers). The theoretical analysis does not model this interaction.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments training deep CNNs (ResNets) and Transformers with/without BN/LN, applying FedZMG only to gradient updates. Measure convergence speed, final accuracy, and gradient norm distributions across layers to detect redundancy or instability. Depth-wise analysis of gradient centering effects.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does FedZMG's empirical communication-computation trade-off compare to state-of-the-art client-side adaptive optimizers like SCAFFOLD or FedCAda, particularly under realistic device heterogeneity (CPU, memory constraints)?
- **Basis in paper:** The future work section explicitly calls for investigation: "it would be beneficial to investigate FedZMG’s performance against other client-side optimization algorithms... comparing FedZMG with other client-side algorithms like SCAFFOLD or FedCAda." The paper contrasts FedZMG primarily with FedAvg and FedAdam (server-side adaptive).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper benchmarks only FedAvg (baseline) and FedAdam (server-side adaptive). No direct comparison with prominent client-side methods (SCAFFOLD, FedCAda, FedAdap) is provided. Theoretical and empirical analysis of per-round computation overhead (momentum/variate updates) and memory footprint versus communication savings is absent.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Re-implementation and head-to-head comparison on the same non-IID datasets, measuring: (1) convergence speed vs. communication rounds, (2) per-client FLOPs and memory usage per round, (3) sensitivity to local epoch count. Analysis should include resource-constrained hardware emulation (e.g., Raspberry Pi benchmarks).

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Can a hybrid optimization scheme that applies FedZMG on the client side *and* an adaptive optimizer (e.g., Adam, Yogi) on the server side yield synergistic benefits, improving stability and convergence beyond either method alone?
- **Basis in paper:** The future work section proposes: "it would be interesting to investigate the performance of hybrid optimization algorithms combining FedZMG’s client-side benefits with an adaptive optimization technique on the server-side. A ‘FedZMG-Adam’ algorithm might be able benefit from the variance reduction in ZMG to make Adam more stable."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper studies FedZMG only with simple FedAvg-style server aggregation. The theoretical convergence analysis (Theorem 1) assumes standard weighted averaging, not adaptive server updates. No experiments test hybrid combinations.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Design and test a "FedZMG-Adam" variant: clients use ZMG-projected SGD; server uses Adam on aggregated model deltas. Compare against pure FedZMG, pure FedAdam, and FedAvg on highly non-IID data (CIFAR100). Metrics: rounds to threshold, final accuracy, variance of global model updates, and sensitivity to server-side hyperparameters (*β*<sub>1</sub>, *β*<sub>2</sub>). Theoretical analysis would need to extend convergence bounds to hybrid setting.

## Limitations
- **Ambiguity in ZMG application to convolutional layers:** The exact axis order (e.g., `[H, W, C_in]` vs. `[C_in, H, W]`) and whether normalization is per-output-channel or global are unspecified, which critically affects the projection and could alter results.
- **Theoretical initialization condition:** Theorem 1 requires 1<sup>⊤</sup>(*ŵ*⁰ − *w*⁎) = 0. The paper does not state how this was approximated or if it was enforced during experiments, leaving a gap between theory and practice.
