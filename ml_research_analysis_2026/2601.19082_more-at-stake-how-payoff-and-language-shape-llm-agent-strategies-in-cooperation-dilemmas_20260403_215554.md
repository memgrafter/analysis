# More at Stake: How Payoff and Language Shape LLM Agent Strategies in Cooperation Dilemmas

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.19082
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19082
*   **Reference Count:** 20
*   **Primary Result:** LLM agents systematically shift cooperative strategies in response to payoff magnitude (stakes) and linguistic framing, with GPT-4o showing the strongest sensitivity to stakes and Arabic/Chinese prompts favoring unconditional strategies.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how the absolute magnitude of payoffs (stakes) and the language used in prompts shape the strategic behavior of three large language models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Haiku, Mistral Large) in repeated 10-round Prisoner's Dilemma games. Using a payoff-scaled experimental design across five languages and three incentive levels, the authors apply a high-confidence LSTM classifier to infer canonical strategies (ALLC, ALLD, TFT, WSLS) from action sequences. Results demonstrate that higher payoffs shift behavior from unconditional defection toward conditional and unconditional cooperation. Cross-linguistic differences are substantial, sometimes matching or exceeding model-specific effects. The findings suggest LLMs possess internal sensitivities to quantitative incentives and cultural-linguistic priors that shape their cooperative decision-making.

## Method Summary
The experimental framework, FAIRGAME, was extended to implement payoff scaling (λ ∈ {0.1, 1.0, 10.0}) while preserving the Prisoner's Dilemma's incentive structure. LLM agents played 1,800 games (3 models × 5 languages × 3 λ × 4 personality pairings × 10 repetitions) with standardized prompt templates translated across languages. To interpret the resulting 36,000 decisions, a supervised LSTM classifier was trained on 10,000 synthetic 10-round trajectories of four canonical strategies with 5% execution noise, achieving 98.4% F1-score. This classifier, combined with a rule-based fallback, assigned a high-confidence strategy label (τ≥0.9) to each agent's action sequence. The primary analysis aggregated these labels by payoff scale, language, and model architecture, with statistical validation via chi-square and ANOVA.

## Key Results
*   **Stake Sensitivity:** Increasing payoff magnitude (λ) shifts strategy distributions from unconditional defection (ALLD) toward conditional cooperation (WSLS) and unconditional cooperation (ALLC), with changes of up to 15 percentage points.
*   **Linguistic Priming:** Strong cross-linguistic divergence exists; Arabic and Chinese prompts favor unconditional strategies (>60%), while French favors conditional ones (>55%). Language effects can rival architectural differences.
*   **Architectural Variation:** GPT-4o exhibits the strongest responsiveness to payoff changes; Mistral Large demonstrates "strategic inertia" with minimal strategy shift across λ, while Claude 3.5 Haiku maintains a generally cooperative profile.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
*   **Claim:** LLM agents systematically adjust cooperative strategies in response to absolute payoff magnitude, shifting from ALLD toward WSLS and ALLC as stakes increase.
*   **Mechanism:** Higher stakes amplify the perceived cost of mutual defection and the benefit of mutual cooperation, altering the agent's internal risk-reward calculus to favor stable, reciprocal strategies over opportunistic exploitation.
*   **Core Assumption:** LLMs possess a sensitivity to numeric value magnitudes that translates into adjustments in strategic behavior, even when the underlying strategic incentives (payoff rankings) are unchanged.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] Systematic incentive sensitivity shown; [section 3.1] Higher defection rates under attenuated (λ=0.1) payoffs.

### Mechanism 2
*   **Claim:** The linguistic framing of the interaction primes LLMs toward distinct strategic archetypes, with effects sometimes rivaling architectural differences.
*   **Mechanism:** Language-specific training data encodes cultural and normative associations. Prompting in a given language activates these latent priors, biasing generation toward strategies congruent with those embedded norms (e.g., Arabic/Chinese → unconditional; French → conditional).
*   **Core Assumption:** LLMs' internal representations retain and can be triggered by linguistic-cultural signals from pre-training, influencing decision-making in game-theoretic contexts.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [abstract] Cross-linguistic divergence noted; [section 3.2] Arabic/Chinese bias toward unconditional, French toward conditional; [section 3.2] Differential stake-sensitivity across languages.

### Mechanism 3
*   **Claim:** Different LLM architectures exhibit distinct "strategic priors" (baseline tendencies) and heterogeneous sensitivities to payoff stakes.
*   **Mechanism:** Varied training objectives, data curation, and alignment techniques instill model-specific behavioral inclinations, resulting in different baseline propensities for cooperation/defection and different adaptability to payoff magnitude changes.
*   **Core Assumption:** Architectural and training differences lead to stable, model-level differences in latent strategic dispositions that persist across languages and payoff conditions.
*   **Evidence Anchors:** [section 3.2] Model-specific profiles (Claude cooperative, Mistral ALLC-biased, GPT-4o "hawk"); [section 3.2] GPT-4o highly responsive, Mistral shows strategic inertia.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Canonical Repeated-Game Strategies (ALLC, ALLD, TFT, WSLS)**
    *   **Why needed:** The entire analysis relies on mapping observed LLM action sequences onto these interpretable, theory-driven behavioral templates.
    *   **Quick Check:** What distinguishes WSLS from TFT in a 10-round game with one execution error?
*   **Concept: Supervised Strategy Classification with Sequence Models (LSTM)**
    *   **Why needed:** Core methodological innovation is using an LSTM to infer latent strategies from noisy LLM trajectories; engineers must grasp its suitability for sequential pattern-matching under noise.
    *   **Quick Check:** Why does an LSTM outperform an HMM for distinguishing TFT from WSLS in trajectories with 5% execution noise?
*   **Concept: Payoff Scaling in Game Theory (λ parameter)**
    *   **Why needed:** λ is the independent variable testing "stake sensitivity." Understanding that it scales all payoffs equally while preserving strategic incentives (T<R<P<S) is crucial for interpreting the causal claim.
    *   **Quick Check:** If λ=10, does the incentive to defect in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma become stronger or weaker compared to λ=1?

## Architecture Onboarding
FAIRGAME Framework -> Payoff Scaler -> Multilingual Prompt Manager -> LLM Backend Interface -> Strategy Classifier Pipeline -> Aggregation & Stats

*   **Critical Path:** Experiment Configuration -> Game Initialization (λ, language) -> Multi-round Gameplay (history -> LLM action) -> Trajectory Logging -> Preprocessing (R/P/T/S encoding) -> Classifier Inference -> Strategy Label (high-conf) -> Aggregation & Stats.
*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    *   **Horizon (N=10):** Enables clean factor isolation but precludes detection of long-horizon strategies (e.g., Grim Trigger).
    *   **High-confidence threshold (τ=0.9):** Ensures reliability (F1=0.984) but discards ~40% of trajectories as ambiguous, potentially missing mixed/emergent strategies.
    *   **4-strategy space:** Maximizes classifier performance and interpretability but cannot capture Zero-Determinant or extortionate strategies.
*   **Failure Signatures:**
    *   **Classifier:** Low confidence (τ<0.9) on >40% of trajectories; high confusion between TFT/WSLS in noise.
    *   **Experimental:** End-game defection spike not observed; language templates fail semantic equivalence.
    *   **Systemic:** No significant main effects for λ or language in chi-square/ANOVA (p>0.05).
*   **First 3 Experiments:**
    1.  Replicate baseline LSTM classification on provided synthetic data (4 strategies, ϵ=0.05) to verify >98% F1-score.
    2.  Single-factor smoke test: GPT-4o, English, all λ, 2 reps. Verify λ=0.1 yields higher ALLD than λ=10; plot round-by-round cooperation for end-game spike.
    3.  Language effect sanity check: GPT-4o in English vs Arabic at λ=1.0, 10 reps. Expect Arabic to show higher unconditional (ALLC+ALLD) percentage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** How do LLM strategy repertoires expand beyond ALLC, ALLD, TFT, and WSLS to include mixed, stochastic, or complex conditional strategies?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Classifier limited to four canonical strategies; trajectories with mixed policies or extortionate play may be misattributed.
*   **Why unresolved:** Methodology cannot reliably detect strategies requiring longer histories or nuanced logic, leaving the question of a richer strategic vocabulary open.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Train/extend classifier on broader synthetic strategy set (e.g., Generous TFT, Grim Trigger) under noise, then apply to LLM gameplay to see if high-confidence labels persist or ambiguous cases reveal new patterns.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** To what extent are observed cross-model differences in payoff-sensitivity (e.g., GPT-4o's high sensitivity vs. Mistral's inertia) an artifact of differing temperature settings versus inherent architectural differences?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Acknowledges use of provider-default temperatures (GPT/Claude: 1.0; Mistral: 0.3) as a potential confound; calls for controlled temperature ablations.
*   **Why unresolved:** Cannot disentangle whether strategic inertia is due to lower temperature (reduced exploration) or deep model properties, limiting causal claims about architectural effects.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiment running all models at identical temperature values (e.g., 0.5 and 1.0) across payoff-scaled games, then comparing magnitude of strategy shifts across λ.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** How do LLM strategies and payoff-sensitivity evolve when the interaction horizon is extended beyond 10 rounds?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] 10-round horizon chosen for interpretability but limits detection of complex conditional strategies; longer-horizon experiments suggested as future work.
*   **Why unresolved:** Finite-horizon backward induction predicts end-game defection, but only rounds 9-10 were observed. Longer games could reveal sustained cooperation via strategies like Grim Trigger.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Repeat experiments with 50-100 rounds, using same or extended classifier, to analyze how strategy distributions and sensitivity change with horizon length.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** What specific linguistic or cultural features in the prompt language drive the observed cross-linguistic divergence?
*   **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Documents strong language effects but only speculates on "cultural values" or training corpora; does not test mechanistic explanations.
*   **Why unresolved:** Without analyzing prompt linguistic content or controlling for semantic differences beyond translation, the root cause of priming (surface wording vs. deep cultural schemas) remains unclear.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies on prompt templates (e.g., swapping key verbs/normative terms within a language) and/or correlating linguistic feature statistics in training corpora with strategy outputs.

### Open Question 5
*   **Question:** How do LLM strategies and payoff-sensitivity compare to those of human participants under identical conditions?
*   **Basis in paper:** [explicit] States that systematic human baselines under matched conditions are absent; findings "echo" known human patterns, positioning work as a foundation for comparative analysis.
*   **Why unresolved:** Without direct human data, impossible to determine if LLM strategies are converging toward, diverging from, or operating in a different regime than human reasoning.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Run identical experiments (same λ, languages, 10 rounds) with human subjects from culturally matched populations, then compare strategy distributions (using same classifier or human coding) to LLM results.

## Limitations
*   **Prompt Template Opacity:** Exact multilingual prompt templates and their back-translation verification process are unreleased, risking linguistic confounds.
*   **Classifier Specificity:** LSTM architecture/hyperparameters and synthetic noise injection details are undocumented, limiting precise replication of the high-performance classifier.
*   **Confounded Sampling:** Architectural comparisons are confounded by differing provider-default temperatures (GPT/Claude temp=1.0 vs. Mistral temp=0.3), making it unclear if observed "inertia" is architectural or a sampling artifact.

## Confidence
*   **Payoff Sensitivity (λ effect):** High. Strong within-model, cross-language evidence with controlled ordering (T<R<P<S) and consistent directional shifts.
*   **Linguistic Framing:** Medium. Robust cross-linguistic patterns reported, but prompt-content verification is impossible; effects could reflect translation artifacts.
*   **Architectural Priors:** Medium. Clear model-specific trends observed, but confounded by temperature differences; architectural vs. sampling effects not disentangled.

## Next Checks
1.  **Payoff-magnitude isolation:** Replicate the λ effect using *identical* prompt templates across all stake levels, holding temperature fixed (e.g., all temp=0.7). Confirm ALLD→WSLS/ALLC shift persists.
2.  **Linguistic priming verification:** Run a minimal 2-language arm (e.g., English vs. Arabic) with prompts rigorously back-translated and certified semantically equivalent by bilingual annotators. Test if strategic divergence remains.
3.  **Architecture vs. temperature disentanglement:** Benchmark all three models at a common low temperature (temp=0.1) and a common high temperature (temp=1.0). Determine if model differences vanish at low temperature.
