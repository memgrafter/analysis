# BLUEPRINT Rebuilding a Legacy: Multimodal Retrieval for Complex Engineering Drawings and Documents

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.13345  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13345  
- **Reference Count:** 40  
- **Primary Result:** On a 5k-file benchmark with 375 expert queries, Blueprint achieves a 10.1% absolute gain in Success@3 and an 18.9% relative improvement in nDCG@3 over the strongest vision-language baseline, while indexing ~770k files at ~9.7 seconds per file.

## Executive Summary
Legacy engineering archives present a retrieval challenge due to hundreds of thousands of unlabeled, format-heterogeneous documents and drawings. BLUEPRINT addresses this with a layout-aware multimodal retrieval system. It detects canonical regions (e.g., title blocks), applies region-restricted VLM-based OCR, normalizes identifiers, and fuses lexical and dense retrieval with a lightweight reranker. Deployed at scale, it automatically generates structured metadata and significantly outperforms dense vision-language baselines on a curated expert query set.

## Method Summary
BLUEPRINT processes mixed-format archives through a routed pipeline. A logistic regression classifier (using CLIP embeddings and heuristic features) routes files to a vision or NLP path. The vision path uses a YOLOv8-S detector to localize four canonical regions (title block, parts list, drawing number, revisions). Each crop is processed by a VLM with field-specific prompts to extract and normalize metadata (e.g., drawing numbers). The NLP path classifies document type (GPT-4o-mini) and extracts text (EasyOCR/PyMuPDF). Both paths produce embeddings—textual and a fusion of textual plus layout features—which are indexed via BM25 and FAISS ANN. At query time, scores are fused (per-query z-score) and a lightweight reranker promotes hits consistent with extracted region fields and revision logic.

## Key Results
- **Scale & Speed:** Indexed ~558,926 filtered files at ~9.7 seconds per file.
- **Effectiveness:** On a 5k-file, 375-query benchmark, achieved +10.1% absolute Success@3 and +18.9% relative nDCG@3 over the best VLM baseline.
- **Potential Headroom:** Oracle ablations (perfect region detection and OCR) suggest further gains are possible.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Layout-Aware Region Detection
- **Claim:** Isolating semantically critical regions (title blocks, parts lists) from noisy full-page renders improves retrieval precision by providing cleaner text for indexing.
- **Mechanism:** A YOLOv8-S detector localizes predefined metadata regions; OCR/IE is restricted to these crops, reducing false positives from irrelevant visual clutter.
- **Break Condition:** Scan degradation or non-standard title block placement causing detector failure; OCR errors within isolated regions still propagate.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 4.2 describe the detector and region-restricted VLM-OCR; mAP results support detection quality.

### Mechanism 2: Hybrid Sparse+Dense Retrieval with Reranking
- **Claim:** Fusing lexical exact-match signals (BM25 for identifiers) with semantic similarity (ANN for conceptual queries) outperforms single-modality baselines.
- **Mechanism:** BM25 matches precise identifiers/drawing numbers; ANN matches semantic intent. A reranker boosts documents where extracted region fields (e.g., facility code) align with query constraints.
- **Break Condition:** Purely conceptual queries may not benefit from BM25; reranker heuristics may over-penalize legitimate variations.
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 4.4 describe fusion and reranker; Section 5.2 ablations show hybrid superiority.

### Mechanism 3: Region-Restricted VLM-OCR
- **Claim:** A VLM processing small, semantically defined region crops with field-aware prompts surpasses traditional full-page OCR for symbol-heavy, stylized engineering text.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of full-page OCR, a vision-language model (e.g., LLaMA-4) processes crops guided by prompts (e.g., "extract drawing number"), improving recognition of abbreviations and stylized fonts.
- **Break Condition:** VLM hallucination on highly degraded text; prompt engineering may not cover all facility-specific conventions.
- **Evidence:** Abstract mentions VLM-OCR; Section 5.4 (Table 6) shows VLM-OCR strength and potential with oracle boxes.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Region-based Document Understanding**  
  *Why needed:* BLUEPRINT assumes engineering drawings have distinct, predictable regions (title block, parts list). This structure informs detector training, prompt design, and reranker logic.  
  *Quick check:* If a drawing lacks a standard title block, which component fails first, and why? (Answer: Region detector fails, cascading to OCR and indexing.)

- **Concept: Hybrid Sparse-Dense Indexing**  
  *Why needed:* Fusion of BM25 (exact matches) and ANN (semantic matches) is central to handling both identifier and descriptive queries. Misunderstanding fusion balance breaks performance.  
  *Quick check:* What query would cause the hybrid score to over-weight dense retrieval, and what risk does that pose? (Answer: A vague conceptual query with no identifiers; risk is drowning precise matches.)

- **Concept: Graded Relevance Evaluation for Technical Corpora**  
  *Why needed:* Engineering queries often have partial correctness (e.g., right part, wrong revision). Binary relevance is insufficient; graded judgments (0/1/2) and nDCG capture nuance.  
  *Quick check:* When would a document receive relevance 1 instead of 2? (Answer: It contains some requested information but is incomplete or not the primary target.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Router (CLIP + heuristics) -> [Vision Path: YOLOv8-S -> VLM-OCR (region crops) -> Normalizer] OR [NLP Path: GPT-4o-mini classifier -> EasyOCR/PyMuPDF -> Text] -> Embedding (NEMOTRON-7B + layout features) -> Unified Index (BM25 + FAISS) -> Reranker (constraint match, revision logic) -> Results.

- **Critical path:** Routing error sends file to wrong pipeline, degrading recall. Region detection failure propagates to OCR and indexing, causing misses even with good queries.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  *Speed vs. accuracy:* YOLOv8-S chosen for low latency (~5 ms/image) vs. slower, higher-AP detectors.  
  *Generality vs. precision:* Fixed 4-region schema scales but ignores site-specific cues.  
  *Automation vs. effort:* LLM-as-judge enables large evaluation but has low expert agreement (κ=0.31).

- **Failure signatures:**  
  *High loss on document-only queries* indicates routing or NLP weakness.  
  *Oracle vs. predicted box ablation gap* shows detector error cascade.  
  *Latency spikes* on complex drawings with large parts lists.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Routing validation:** Manually label 500 random files; compute accuracy/F1 for CLIP+heuristics vs. CLIP alone (target ~94.5% accuracy).  
  2. **Region detection mAP:** Run YOLOv8-S on 250 held-out drawings; target >89% mAP@0.5:0.95.  
  3. **OCR ablation:** Compare VLM-OCR vs. EasyOCR on predicted boxes using a small judged set; expect >5% nDCG@3 gain for VLM-OCR.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
How can relation graphs between components (e.g., part hierarchies, electrical connections) be automatically extracted from engineering drawings to support richer cross-document queries?  
*Basis:* Future Work explicitly states extension to relation graphs.  
*Why unresolved:* Current system extracts flat, region-level metadata only; no modeling of semantic relationships between components.  
*Resolution evidence:* A pipeline linking extracted entities into a graph, evaluated on queries like "show all components downstream of valve X."

### Open Question 2
Can the region-level reranker be enhanced to handle more complex, multi-constraint queries (e.g., combining facility, revision, material, part count) and model inter-document relationships (e.g., drawing-to-procedure links)?  
*Basis:* Reranker currently handles a small set of constraints and does not model cross-document relations.  
*Why unresolved:* Reranker uses simple, hard-coded rules; real queries involve multiple interacting constraints and require linking related documents.  
*Resolution evidence:* A learned reranker (e.g., cross-encoder) trained on annotated query-result pairs with multi-constraint labels, showing improved nDCG on complex queries.

### Open Question 3
What architectural or data augmentation improvements are needed to make BLUEPRINT robust to severely degraded scans (e.g., low resolution, heavy skew, bleed-through) and non-standard or missing title blocks?  
*Basis:* System assumes readable scans and identifiable regions; severe degradation or nonstandard title blocks reduce fidelity. OCR ablations show gaps between predicted/oracle boxes.  
*Why unresolved:* Detector and VLM-OCR trained on relatively clean, annotated drawings; real archives contain poorly digitized materials and atypical layouts.  
*Resolution evidence:* Robustification study applying synthetic degradation (blur, noise, rotation) and style-transfer augmentation to training data, measuring mAP and retrieval Success@3 on genuinely degraded scans.

### Open Question 4
How effective is the proposed unified benchmark and shared task in driving community progress on multimodal retrieval for engineering archives, and what are its limitations?  
*Basis:* Paper plans to release a cross-domain benchmark and organize a shared task.  
*Why unresolved:* Utility depends on adoption, diversity of participating methods, and whether the 5k-file subset captures full complexity (e.g., multilingual text, extreme heterogeneity). Generalization to other domains (aerospace, BIM) is untested.  
*Resolution evidence:* Post-release analysis of shared task submissions, comparing performance ladders and analyzing failure modes to identify remaining gaps.

## Limitations
- Proprietary VLM details (LLaMA-4, GPT-4o-mini prompts) hinder exact replication.
- Reranker weights and BM25/FAISS hyperparameters are partially reported; generalization unclear.
- LLM-as-judge agreement with experts is low (κ=0.31), questioning evaluation robustness.
- System assumes canonical drawing layouts; atypical scans (missing title block, rotation) cause detector failures and cascade errors.

## Confidence
- **Region-aware pipeline (Mechanism 1):** Medium. Evidence includes YOLOv8-S mAP and routing accuracy, but break conditions (non-standard layouts) are under-explored.
- **Hybrid sparse-dense retrieval (Mechanism 2):** High. Ablations show clear gains over single-modality baselines; fusion method is standard and well-supported.
- **VLM-OCR on crops (Mechanism 3):** Low. Direct comparison limited; oracle box ablation suggests potential, but VLM hallucination and prompt dependence are not fully quantified.

## Next Checks
1. **Reproduce region detection on OOD data:** Gather 200 drawings with atypical layouts (skewed, missing borders); measure YOLOv8-S mAP; target >80% to confirm robustness.
2. **OCR accuracy vs. degradation:** Compute Character Error Rate (CER) for VLM-OCR vs. EasyOCR on cropped title blocks under simulated noise (blur, stains); identify degradation thresholds where VLM fails.
3. **Reranker sensitivity analysis:** Grid-search α, β, γ on validation queries to assess stability of Success@3; report variance and locate robust parameter regions.
