# Auditing Sybil: Explaining Deep Lung Cancer Risk Prediction Through Generative Interventional Attributions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.02560
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02560
- **Reference Count**: 40
- **Primary Result**: Sybil's lung cancer risk predictions are well-approximated by a linear model with pairwise nodule interactions, but the model exhibits critical failure modes: sensitivity to irrelevant artifacts (e.g., ECG electrodes) and a radial bias with significantly reduced sensitivity to peripheral pleural nodules ($p<0.001$).

## Executive Summary
The paper presents an audit of Sybil, a deep learning system for lung cancer risk prediction from low-dose CT scans, which lacked causal verification. To address this, the authors develop S(H)NAP, a model-agnostic auditing framework that leverages 3D diffusion bridge modeling to generate realistic counterfactual CTs with nodules removed or inserted. By measuring changes in Sybil's risk scores across thousands of interventions, they reveal that Sybil operates effectively as a linear model that aggregates nodule features and their pairwise interactions (high R²). However, the audit uncovers two major failure modes: (1) the model reacts strongly to clinically irrelevant artifacts such as ECG electrodes, contributing up to 50% of the predicted risk, and (2) it displays a radial bias, with markedly lower sensitivity to peripheral pleural nodules—a clinically important blind spot for adenocarcinoma detection. These findings call into question the reliability of Sybil in real-world deployment and demonstrate the value of generative interventional attribution for deep learning safety.

## Method Summary
S(H)NAP combines a 3D diffusion bridge model with a targeted intervention engine to enable causal attribution for any black-box medical image classifier. Given an input CT scan, the framework first obtains a nodule segmentation (via an external segmenter or the target model's attention). It then modifies the segmentation mask: SHNAP removes selected nodules, while SNAP adds nodules at specified locations. The diffusion bridge model, conditioned on the edited mask, synthesizes a realistic CT volume that reflects the intervention while preserving natural tissue appearance. The synthetic CT is fed to the target model (Sybil), producing a new risk score. The difference between original and counterfactual outputs is interpreted as the causal contribution of the intervened nodule(s). For spatial sensitivity mapping, SNAP systematically inserts nodules across the lung field and records the induced risk changes. The realism of the generated CTs was validated by a panel of radiologists who rated them as indistinguishable from real scans in a blinded assessment.

## Key Results
- Sybil's risk predictions are well fit by a linear model that incorporates individual nodules and their pairwise interactions (high R² across held-out CTs).
- The model exhibits dangerous sensitivity to clinically irrelevant artifacts; for example, ECG electrodes outside the body account for up to 50% of the predicted risk in some cases.
- A statistically significant radial bias is present: peripheral pleural nodules elicit a much lower risk increase than centrally located nodules ($p<0.001$), representing a blind spot for detecting peripheral adenocarcinomas.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The 3D diffusion bridge model learned to map between nodule masks and CT textures, enabling it to inpaint removed nodules or seamlessly add new ones while maintaining global anatomical consistency. This generative capacity provides valid counterfactuals, a prerequisite for causal inference. The high R² of a linear approximation suggests that Sybil has internalized a predictive rule that is dominated by nodule presence and pairwise relationships, possibly because the training data and objective incentivized simple aggregation of nodule evidence. As a result, interventional differences in risk scores accurately reflect the marginal contributions of individual nodules. However, the same linearity also means that spurious correlations—such as the presence of ECG electrodes that co-occur with cancer in the training set—are directly incorporated into the decision, explaining the observed artifact sensitivity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Causal Attribution in Deep Learning**: Needed to explain model decisions in terms of true effect rather than correlation. Quick check: Does changing a feature (e.g., removing a nodule) produce a consistent change in output?
- **Diffusion Models**: Generative models that can synthesize high-fidelity images and perform conditional edits. Quick check: Can the model generate samples that match the training distribution on a held-out set?
- **3D Medical Imaging**: CT scans are volumetric; algorithms must respect 3D context. Quick check: Are 3D convolutional networks used, and do they achieve state-of-the-art on volumetric benchmarks?
- **Lung Cancer Risk Prediction**: The clinical application driving the work. Quick check: Is the model's discrimination performance (e.g., AUC) comparable to radiologists on a validation cohort?
- **Generative Interventional Attribution**: Method for causal effect estimation by simulating interventions with a generative model. Quick check: Are counterfactual samples realistic enough to fool domain experts?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Original CT -> Nodule Segmentation -> Nodule Mask -> Mask Modification (remove/insert) -> Diffusion Bridge Model -> Synthetic CT -> Target Model (Sybil) -> Risk Score

**Critical path**: 1) Score original CT with Sybil. 2) Generate counterfactual CT with desired nodule intervention via diffusion bridge. 3) Obtain counterfactual risk score. 4) Compute difference as causal attribution. For spatial sensitivity, repeat insertion across many positions to build a sensitivity map.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Model-agnostic approach incurs extra computational cost (running diffusion model for each intervention). 
- Diffusion fidelity vs speed: higher fidelity requires more inference steps. 
- Intervention precision vs realism: Inpainting may blur boundaries; added nodules must blend with surrounding tissue. 
- Dependence on accurate nodule segmentation; errors propagate.

**Failure signatures**: 
- Synthetic CTs exhibit unnatural textures or artifacts that could influence Sybil differently than real changes.
- Incomplete nodule removal leaves residual voxels that bias attribution.
- High variance in attributions for the same intervention due to stochastic diffusion sampling.
- Discrepancies between linear model fit and actual predictions for certain subpopulations hint at nonlinear behavior not captured.

**First three experiments**: 
1. Human evaluation of synthetic CT realism (e.g., radiologist blinded rating).  
2. Quantify linearity by training a generalized linear model on nodule features extracted from original CTs and compare its predictions with Sybil's on a held-out set.  
3. Perform a spatial insertion study: place synthetic nodules at increasing distances from the pleura and measure the corresponding risk scores to confirm the radial bias.

## Limitations
- The audit is limited to a single model (Sybil) and dataset, restricting generalizability.
- Realism of synthetic interventions was assessed by a small panel of radiologists; subtle artifacts may remain undetected.
- Causal attributions depend on the accuracy of the diffusion bridge model; biases in the generator could lead to incorrect conclusions.
- The study lacks prospective clinical validation, so the real-world impact of the identified failure modes on patient outcomes is unknown.

## Confidence
- S(H)NAP framework validity: Medium
- Linear behavior of Sybil: High
- Existence of failure modes (artifact sensitivity and radial bias): Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-model/dataset replication**: Apply S(H)NAP to multiple lung cancer risk models and independent multi-center CT datasets to assess consistency of linear approximation and bias patterns.
2. **Rigorous intervention realism**: Conduct a double-blind study with ≥20 radiologists rating realism of synthetic vs. real interventions; also compare with actual pre/post-intervention scans when available.
3. **Prospective clinical evaluation**: Screen real patient CTs with known outcomes to measure the effect of peripheral pleural nodules on Sybil's risk scores and correlate with adenocarcinomas missed by the model.
