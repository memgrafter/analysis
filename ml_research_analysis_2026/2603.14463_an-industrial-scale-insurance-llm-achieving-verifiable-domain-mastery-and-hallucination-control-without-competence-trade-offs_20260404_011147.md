# An Industrial-Scale Insurance LLM Achieving Verifiable Domain Mastery and Hallucination Control without Competence Trade-offs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14463
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14463
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** INS-S1-235B reaches 90.14 on INSEva, a record-low 0.6% hallucination on HHEM, and maintains strong general capability (AIME25: 80.21, Codeforces: 85.17).

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the high‑stakes adaptation of large language models to the insurance domain, where regulatory compliance and zero tolerance for hallucinations are imperative, yet domain expertise must not come at the cost of general intelligence. The authors introduce INS‑S1, an insurance‑specific LLM, trained via a novel end‑to‑end alignment paradigm comprising a Verifiable Data Synthesis System and a Progressive SFT‑RL Curriculum. The data system builds hierarchical, logic‑constrained datasets for actuarial reasoning, compliance, and RAG tasks, while curbing hallucinations through iterative refinement and atomic verifiable tasks. The curriculum dynamically anneals data mixtures and combines Verified Reasoning (RLVR) with AI Feedback (RLAIF) to progressively align the model both on domain rigor and factuality. On the new INSEva benchmark, INS‑S1‑235B surpasses strong baselines like DeepSeek‑R1 and Gemini‑2.5‑Pro, achieves a state‑of‑the‑art 90.14 score, and records a 0.6% hallucination rate, all while preserving performance on general reasoning tasks such as AIME25 and Codeforces.

## Method Summary
The method revolves around two core innovations. First, the Verifiable Data Synthesis System automatically constructs a multi‑tier dataset: a “No‑Query” pipeline injects structured knowledge using empty think tags; a “With‑Query” pipeline creates complex reasoning examples with business‑trap distractors, standardized CoT derivation, and iterative answer/prompt correction loops for actuarial, underwriting, and claims scenarios; an atomic RAG suite (CAIL2019, DRCD, CNN/DailyMail+CASSum, CHEF) trains hallucination control; and a self‑distilled general corpus (110k Chinese STEM/exam samples) preserves broad capabilities. Second, Progressive SFT‑RL training first fine‑tunes with a constant mixture of general and domain data, then applies dynamic data annealing to gradually shrink the general portion, up‑sample reasoning and hallucination‑focused tasks, and boost long‑tail categories. Reinforcement learning follows a hierarchical curriculum, mixing RLVR (rule‑based and semantic checks) for verifiable tasks (e.g., actuarial formulas) and RLAIF (LLM ratings of factuality, professionalism, expression) for open‑ended scenarios, further punished by anti‑duplication, language consistency, and diversity penalties. This design aims to achieve verifiable domain mastery while fully suppressing hallucinations.

## Key Results
- **INSEva SOTA** – INS‑S1‑235B scores 90.14, exceeding DeepSeek‑R1 (82.47) and Gemini‑2.5‑Pro (84.23).
- **Extremely low hallucination** – HHEM hallucination rate of 0.6%, a new record.
- **Retained general intelligence** – Strong results on AIME25 (80.21) and Codeforces (85.17) with minimal trade‑off.
- **Human validation** – Side‑by‑side expert evaluation shows high professional and factual quality, with most outputs preferred over baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Verifiable Data Synthesis System instills domain logic without corrupting general reasoning by using hierarchical, logic‑constrained data pipelines.
- **Mechanism:** Dual pipelines (“No‑Query” for knowledge injection, “With‑Query” for complex reasoning) with sub‑task decomposition. Core techniques include Instruction Isolation (empty think tags) to avoid reasoning bias; Business‑Trap distractors and SOP CoT with logic explicitation for cognitive alignment; Standardized Reasoning (linear derivations) and iterative Answer/Prompt Correction Loops for actuarial/underwriting tasks; Atomic RAG and insurance‑specific RAG adaptation for hallucination control. The core assumption is that verifiable constraints and iterative refinement during data creation produce examples encoding domain logic while preserving the model’s general reasoning space.
- **Evidence:** Empty Think Tags +3.11% on CUFEInse; Standardized Reasoning +10.8%; Prompt Correction 67%→93% (Section 2.2.1). Corpus support for constraint‑based data synthesis is limited, but related work indicates benefits of structured generation.
- **Break condition:** If template‑based constraints fail to capture nuanced regulatory intent, the data becomes brittle and may not generalize to real‑world, context‑dependent cases.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Progressive SFT‑RL Curriculum with dynamic data annealing prevents catastrophic forgetting and balances domain mastery with general capability.
- **Mechanism:** SFT uses a two‑stage dynamic orchestration: Stage1 mixes self‑distilled general data with domain data at constant LR; Stage2 applies Domain‑Adaptive Annealing—general data reduced to 25%, complex reasoning and hallucination tasks up‑sampled to 50% of simple, and long‑tail boosted. RL employs hierarchical curriculum (Knowledge → Complex Reasoning → Alignment) and synergistic RLVR (rule‑based + semantic verification) plus RLAIF (faithfulness/professionalism/expression rewards). The assumption is that strategic, difficulty‑aware mixing and progressive task sequencing enable incremental knowledge acquisition without overwriting foundational skills.
- **Evidence:** Table 1 shows curriculum outperforms naive mixing; Annealing improves INSEva sub‑dimensions (Section 3.1.2); RL curriculum and reward details in Section 3.2.
- **Break condition:** Misestimated task difficulty or overly aggressive annealing could cause over‑fitting to domain patterns or erode general reasoning if general data is too scarce.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Iterative constraint‑based reward shaping suppresses hallucinations to 0.6% on HHEM while maintaining natural, professional responses.
- **Mechanism:** Start with HHEM factuality reward; sequentially add penalties: V1 base, V2 anti‑duplication, V3 language consistency, V4 diversity/naturalness. Combine RLVR (for verifiable tasks using regex/semantic equivalence) and RLAIF (for open tasks evaluating factuality/professionalism/expression). Length reward enforces parsimony only when correct. The assumption is that hallucinations can be incrementally curbed by targeting observable failure modes with explicit penalties, and that hybrid RLVR/RLAIF covers both deterministic and subjective aspects.
- **Evidence:** Table 15 shows HHEM improvement 2.8%→0.6% with negligible drop on general benchmarks (Section A.8). Corpus work like Alipay search corroborates constraint‑based mitigation.
- **Break condition:** Excessive penalization may lead to overly conservative outputs (false refusals) or unnatural phrasing; reliance on automated verifiers risks false positives if valid paraphrases are rejected.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Data Layer (Verifiable Data Synthesis System: No‑Query/With‑Query pipelines, hallucination mitigation data, self‑distilled general corpora) → Training Layer (Progressive SFT‑RL: dynamic mixing, Stage2 annealing, RLVR+RLAIF with rewards and penalties) → Evaluation Layer (INSEva, HHEM, general benchmarks) → Deployment Layer (INS‑S1 as a scenario model for downstream insurance fine‑tuning).

**Critical path:**
1. Collect and preprocess raw insurance corpora (regulations, exams, FAQs).  
2. Run Verifiable Data Synthesis to generate QA pairs, correction loops, and atomic hallucination‑control datasets.  
3. Prepare self‑distilled general data by querying the base model and filtering.  
4. SFT Stage1: train with a constant mixture of general+domain data.  
5. SFT Stage2: apply strategic data annealing (reduce general, up‑sample complex/hallucination, boost long‑tail).  
6. RL: hierarchical curriculum – start with knowledge tasks, interleave complex reasoning, then align with faithfulness using RLVR (verifiable) and RLAIF (open).  
7. Evaluate on INSEva/HHEM/general; iterate on data ratio or rewards.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Data diversity vs. noise:* Larger synthetic datasets increase coverage but risk logical inconsistencies; mitigated by verification loops (regex, semantic equivalence).  
- *General vs. domain ratio:* Over‑aggressive annealing causes catastrophic forgetting; solution: keep self‑distilled general data as stabilizer (≥25%).  
- *RLVR vs. RLAIF:* RLVR is precise but limited to verifiable tasks; RLAIF handles subjectivity; hybrid needed for full spectrum.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Sudden drop on general benchmarks (AIME25, Codeforces) → over‑annealing or insufficient general data.  
- Low INSEva sub‑score in actuarial reasoning → inadequate Standardized Reasoning or RLVR reward mis‑specification.  
- High HHEM despite RL → reward hacking (verbatim copying); check diversity/naturalness penalties.  
- Low ISC (safety/compliance) → missing SOP CoT or insufficient business‑trap distractors.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Ablate Knowledge Injection Strategies* – compare Empty Think Tags vs. Distilled CoT vs. Template‑Constrained CoT on CUFEInse theory benchmark; expect Empty Think Tags +3.11%.  
2. *Test Data Annealing Schedules* – compare constant mixing, uniform downsampling, strategic annealing on INSEva sub‑dimensions; strategic annealing should yield highest gains.  
3. *Validate RLVR vs. RLAIF on Task Types* – measure actuarial improvement with RLVR only, compliance improvement with RLAIF only, and combined synergy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can we effectively mitigate the “Lost‑in‑the‑Middle” problem in ultra‑long contexts (>128k tokens) for insurance document processing to support “Light Training, Heavy Prompting”?  
  *Basis:* Future work identifies “Lost‑in‑the‑Middle” as a key challenge (Conclusion, p.8).  
  *Why unresolved:* Insurance contracts and claim histories often exceed 128k tokens; models struggle to retrieve mid‑context facts.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Build synthetic documents with critical facts at varying positions; test hierarchical attention or RAG; measure accuracy degradation vs. context length/position.

- **Open Question 2:** How can we develop scalable RLAIF‑based “Human‑Feeling” reward models that capture nuanced human preferences (e.g., empathy, risk communication) with minimal human annotation?  
  *Basis:* Proposed future direction: “Scalable Subjective Rewards: Developing RLAIF‑based ‘Human‑Feeling’ reward models” (Conclusion, p.8).  
  *Why unresolved:* Current rewards focus on factual correctness and safety; preferences like tone, clarity, and risk framing are expensive to annotate.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Gather human pairwise preferences on insurance responses; train RLAIF models with varying seed data; correlate rewards with human evaluation and customer satisfaction.

- **Open Question 3:** What architectural and training changes are needed to enable insurance LLMs to perform closed‑loop business transactions (e.g., end‑to‑end claim processing) via reliable function calling?  
  *Basis:* Future work aims to “Enhance Function Calling to bridge the gap from consultation to closed‑loop business transactions via API integration” (Conclusion, p.8).  
  *Why unresolved:* Current tool‑use evaluation (e.g., ITU) covers single‑step extraction; closed‑loop requires multi‑step API orchestration, state tracking, error recovery, and strict compliance.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Create a simulated environment of insurance APIs (submit, assess, approve, pay); require models to complete full workflows; measure success rate, step count, compliance violations, and robustness.

- **Open Question 4:** How can we design training data and objectives to improve models’ reasoning about non‑standardized, ambiguous actuarial problems that require expert judgment?  
  *Basis:* Authors note “a gap persists in processing non‑standardized actuarial reasoning, highlighting the distinction between general math and domain rigor” (Conclusion, p.8).  
  *Why unresolved:* The synthesis system emphasizes standardized derivations and SOP‑based CoT; real actuarial work deals with incomplete data, exceptions, and trade‑offs not captured by rigid templates.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Collect real actuarial case studies with expert traces; build a benchmark of “messy” actuarial problems; evaluate failure modes; augment data or RL rewards for robustness and justification quality.

## Limitations
- Detailed data synthesis pipelines (e.g., exact prompts for answer correction) and SFT annealing schedules are underspecified, hindering exact replication.  
- Evaluation relies heavily on automatic metrics (INSEva, HHEM) with limited human validation; risk of benchmark contamination or overfitting to metric idiosyncrasies.  
- Key architecture parameters (MoE expert count, layer dimensions) are omitted, preventing precise resource planning and scaling analysis.

## Confidence
- *INSEva SOTA*: Medium – strong scores but reproducibility concerns due to missing details.  
- *Preserved general capability*: Medium – general benchmarks hold, but subtle regressions may exist.  
- *Extremely low hallucination*: Medium – HHEM automated; sparse human verification.  
- *Overall framework*: Medium – theoretically sound, yet empirical isolation of components is lacking.
