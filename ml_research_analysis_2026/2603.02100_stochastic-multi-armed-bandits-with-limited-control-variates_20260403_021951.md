# Stochastic Multi-Armed Bandits with Limited Control Variates

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02100
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02100
- **Reference count:** 37
- **Primary result:** UCB-LCV combines reward-only and reward+CV samples using online variance-minimizing weights, achieving lower regret than standard UCB algorithms in Gaussian settings; regret decreases as CV availability increases.

## Executive Summary
We consider stochastic multi-armed bandits where control variates (CVs) are available only intermittently. The paper introduces UCB-LCV, an algorithm that maintains separate estimators for reward-only and reward+CV samples and optimally combines them with weights derived from online variance estimates. For Gaussian rewards and CVs, UCB-LCV employs t-distribution confidence bounds; for general distributions it adapts via jackknifing or splitting. Empirical evaluations on synthetic Gaussian data show that UCB-LCV achieves lower cumulative regret compared to standard algorithms (e.g., UCB1, UCB-V) and that performance improves with higher CV availability. The no-CV variant, UCB-NORMAL, also outperforms UCB1-NORMAL, highlighting the benefits of variance-aware estimation even without CVs.

## Method Summary
**Task:** Implement and evaluate UCB-LCV against baseline bandit algorithms.
**Inputs:** Synthetic Gaussian data where rewards are \(X = V + Y\) and CVs are \(W = Y\). The arm-specific means of \(V\) and \(Y\) are \(0.1 \times i\) with variance \(0.01\). The CV mean \(\omega_i\) is known. CV availability probability \(\varepsilon\) is configurable.
**Objective:** Minimize cumulative expected regret \(R_T = T \mu^* - \mathbb{E}\big[\sum_t X_{t,I_t}\big]\) over \(T=20\,000\) rounds with \(K=10\) arms.
**Procedure:** UCB-LCV stores per-arm sets \(H_i(t)\) (reward-only samples) and \(H^c_i(t)\) (samples with CV). It computes the reward-only mean \(\hat{\mu}^{(nc)}\) and the CV-adjusted mean \(\hat{\mu}^{(c)}\) (using \(\omega_i\)). These are combined as \(\hat{\mu}_s = \hat{\lambda} \hat{\mu}^{(nc)} + (1-\hat{\lambda}) \hat{\mu}^{(c)}\), where \(\hat{\lambda} = B_i/(A_i+B_i)\) is an online weight estimate based on unbiased variance estimators \(A_i\) (from \(H_i\)) and \(B_i\) (from \(H^c_i\), Lemma 1). The UCB index is \(\hat{\mu}_s + V(\alpha)\sqrt{\hat{\nu}_s}\), with \(V(\alpha)\) from the t-distribution (Gaussian case) or via resampling (general). Arms are selected by maximizing this index. Initialization uses \(Q = M+2\) pulls per arm (\(M = q+2\); for \(q=1\), \(Q=5\)). If fewer than \(M\) CV samples are available, \(\hat{\lambda}\) is set to 1.

## Key Results
- UCB-LCV achieves lower cumulative regret than UCB1, UCB1-NORMAL, kl-UCB, UCB-V, and Thompson Sampling on Gaussian test instances.
- Regret decreases monotonically as the probability of CV availability (\(\varepsilon\)) increases; at \(\varepsilon=0\), UCB-LCV reduces to UCB-NORMAL and still outperforms UCB1-NORMAL.
- The no-CV variant UCB-NORMAL outperforms UCB1-NORMAL for normally distributed rewards, demonstrating the benefit of variance-aware confidence bounds even without CVs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Variance Reduction via Optimal Weighting
- **Claim:** Combining reward samples from rounds with and without control variates (CVs) using a variance-minimizing weight reduces estimator variance compared to using only reward-only samples.
- **Mechanism:** Maintain two sets per arm: reward-only \(H_i(t)\) and reward+CV \(H^c_i(t)\). Compute mean estimators \(\hat{\mu}^{(nc)}\) (Eq. 1) and \(\hat{\mu}^{(c)}\) (Eq. 2, which uses known CV mean \(\omega_i\) and an estimated coefficient \(\beta^*\)). Combine as \(\hat{\mu}_s = \hat{\lambda}\hat{\mu}^{(nc)} + (1-\hat{\lambda})\hat{\mu}^{(c)}\). Under joint Gaussianity, the optimal \(\lambda\) minimizing variance is given in Proposition 1, yielding a variance reduction factor \(1-\rho_i^2\) when CVs are used (Eq. 9). The weight \(\hat{\lambda}\) is estimated online as \(B_i/(A_i+B_i)\), where \(A_i\) and \(B_i\) are unbiased variance estimators (Lemma 1).
- **Core assumption:** Reward and CV are jointly Gaussian, ensuring independence of sample mean and variance estimators; this is crucial for the unbiasedness of the combined estimator with estimated \(\hat{\lambda}\) (Lemma 2).
- **Evidence anchors:** Derivation of optimal \(\lambda\) and variance formula (Section III.A, Proposition 1, Eq. 9). Unbiasedness of \(A_i, B_i\) (Lemma 1). Algorithm 1 implements the online \(\hat{\lambda}\) update.
- **Break condition:** Non-Gaussian distributions violate the independence assumption, potentially biasing the estimator; the paper addresses this via jackknifing/splitting (Section V). Noisy \(\hat{\lambda}\) with few samples also degrades performance.

### Mechanism 2: Online Weight Estimation
- **Claim:** Estimating the optimal weight \(\lambda\) from data using unbiased variance estimators enables adaptive combination without prior knowledge of distribution parameters.
- **Mechanism:** For each arm, compute \(A_i\) (variance estimator from \(H_i\)) and \(B_i\) (from \(H^c_i\)) as defined after Proposition 1. Set \(\hat{\lambda} = B_i/(A_i+B_i)\) and use it in the combined estimator.
- **Core assumption:** Under Gaussianity, \(A_i\) and \(B_i\) are unbiased for the true variances of \(\hat{\mu}^{(nc)}\) and \(\hat{\mu}^{(c)}\) (Lemma 1), making \(\hat{\lambda}\) a reasonable estimate.
- **Evidence anchors:** Lemma 1 provides the estimators. Algorithm 1 line “Re‑estimate \(\hat{\lambda}_{t,It}\)…’’.
- **Break condition:** With small sample sizes, \(A_i\) and \(B_i\) become noisy, leading to unstable \(\hat{\lambda}\). The algorithm requires at least \(M = q+2\) CV samples to compute \(B_i\); if \(M_i(t) < M\), it forces \(\hat{\lambda}=1\).

### Mechanism 3: Adaptation to Non‑Gaussian Rewards via Resampling
- **Claim:** For non‑Gaussian reward/CV distributions, resampling methods (jackknifing, splitting) can reduce bias in the CV‑based estimator and provide valid confidence intervals for exploration.
- **Mechanism:** Replace the simple CV‑adjusted mean (Eq. 2) with a jackknife or splitting estimator. Jackknifing computes multiple leave‑one‑out estimators from \(H^c_i(t)\) and averages them; splitting transforms samples using leave‑one‑out \(\beta\) estimates. The sample variance of the resulting combined estimator is then used in the UCB index, removing the Gaussian assumption.
- **Core assumption:** Resampling approximates the sampling distribution under weak regularity; however, no formal unbiasedness or confidence guarantees are given for the resulting UCB index.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions “adapts via jackknifing/splitting”. Section V details the procedures. Section VI.B shows jackknife and splitting outperform batching for non‑Gaussian data (Fig. 4).
- **Break condition:** Theoretical regret bounds are unavailable for these variants (Remark 2). Batching, a simpler alternative, performs poorly in experiments (Fig. 4c), indicating sensitivity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Control Variates (from Monte Carlo variance reduction)
  - *Why needed here:* CVs are correlated with rewards, allowing construction of lower‑variance estimators (Eq. 2, Proposition 1). The core innovation is using them even when sporadically available.
  - *Quick check question:* Given a CV with known mean \(\omega\) and correlation \(\rho\), how does including it change the variance of the sample mean estimator compared to not using it?
- **Concept:** Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) algorithms
  - *Why needed here:* UCB frames the exploration‑exploitation trade‑off by adding a confidence bonus to reward estimates. UCB‑LCV adapts the bonus using the variance of the combined estimator (Eq. 11).
  - *Quick check question:* In standard UCB1, the bonus is \(\sqrt{2\log t / N_i(t)}\). How does UCB‑LCV’s bonus differ when CVs are available?
- **Concept:** Unbiased variance estimation for means
  - *Why needed here:* The weight \(\lambda\) and UCB bonus require accurate variance estimates. Lemma 1 provides estimators for the two sample sets under Gaussianity.
  - *Quick check question:* Why does the denominator in \(A_{t,i}\) use \(N_i(t)(N_i(t)-1)\) instead of \(N_i(t)-1\)? What property does this estimate?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Per‑arm data structures: \(H_i(t)\) (reward‑only samples), \(H^c_i(t)\) (samples with CV), counters \(N_i(t)=|H_i|\), \(M_i(t)=|H^c_i|\), \(S_i(t)=N_i+M_i\). Estimators: \(\hat{\mu}^{(nc)}\) (mean of \(H_i\)), \(\hat{\mu}^{(c)}\) (CV‑adjusted mean from \(H^c_i\) using Eq. 2), combined \(\hat{\mu}_s = \hat{\lambda}\hat{\mu}^{(nc)}+(1-\hat{\lambda})\hat{\mu}^{(c)}\). Variance: \(A_i\) (from \(H_i\)), \(B_i\) (from \(H^c_i\), Lemma 1), \(\hat{\lambda}=B_i/(A_i+B_i)\), and \(\hat{\nu}_s\) (Eq. 10). Decision: compute UCB \(= \hat{\mu}_s + V(\alpha)\sqrt{\hat{\nu}_s}\) (Eq. 11) and select arm with maximum.
- **Critical path:**
  1. **Initialization:** Pull each arm \(Q = M+2\) times (for \(q=1\), \(M=3\), \(Q=5\)). After initialization, set \(\hat{\lambda}=1\) for any arm with \(M_i(t) < M\) (Algorithm line 3).
  2. **Round t:** For every arm, recompute its UCB index using current estimates (line 5). Pull arm \(I_t = \arg\max_i \mathrm{UCB}_{t,i}\) (line 6).
  3. **Feedback:** Observe reward \(X_{t,I_t}\) and possibly CV \(W_{t,I_t}\). If a CV is observed **and** \(M_{I_t}(t) \ge M\), update \(\hat{\mu}^{(c)}\) and \(B_{t,I_t}\) using \(H^c_{I_t}\); otherwise update \(\hat{\mu}^{(nc)}\) and \(A_{t,I_t}\) (lines 8‑11).
  4. **Recompute:** For the selected arm, update \(\hat{\lambda}\), \(\hat{\mu}_s\), and \(\hat{\nu}_s\) (line 13).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Gaussian vs. General:* Gaussian case leverages normality for unbiasedness and t‑distribution bounds; general case uses resampling (jackknife/split), which is computationally heavier and lacks theoretical regret bounds.
  - *Initialization \(Q\):* Chosen as \(M+2\) to ensure enough CV samples to compute \(B_i\) (requires \(M_i \ge M\)). Larger \(Q\) delays CV usage but may stabilize early \(\hat{\lambda}\) estimates.
  - *Weight estimation:* The random \(\hat{\lambda}\) introduces additional randomness; Lemma 2’s unbiasedness proof depends on independence of mean and variance under Gaussianity; this may not hold outside that setting.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Misspecified CV mean \(\omega_i\):** Regret increases significantly with error in \(\omega_i\) (Fig. 3d), even for small errors.
  - **Non‑Gaussian data without resampling:** Using the Gaussian‑assumption UCB‑LCV on log‑normal data degrades performance; batching variant also performs poorly (Fig. 4c).
  - **Numerical instability in \(\hat{\lambda}\):** If \(A_i\) or \(B_i\) become very small, \(\hat{\lambda}\) can become extreme, inflating variance of \(\hat{\mu}_s\).
- **First 3 experiments (reproduction):**
  1. **Gaussian baseline:** Run UCB‑LCV (\(\varepsilon=0.5\)) against UCB1, UCB1‑NORMAL, kl‑UCB, UCB‑V, and Thompson Sampling on Instance 1. Expected: UCB‑LCV attains the lowest cumulative regret.
  2. **CV availability sweep:** Vary \(\varepsilon \in \{0.0, 0.15, 0.45, 0.75, 1.0\}\) on Instance 3. Expected: Regret decreases monotonically with \(\varepsilon\) (Fig. 3c).
  3. **Non‑Gaussian robustness:** Test jackknife, splitting, and batching variants of UCB‑LCV on multi‑modal and log‑normal distributions (Instance adapted from VI.B). Expected: Jackknife and splitting match or beat the Gaussian‑assumption UCB‑LCV; batching fails.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Sub‑linear regret proof:** Can a formal regret upper bound (e.g., \(O(\mathrm{polylog}\,T)\)) be proven for UCB‑LCV? The randomness of \(\hat{\lambda}\) complicates concentration analysis.
- **Unbiased variance estimator for combined mean:** Is there an exact unbiased sample variance estimator for \(\hat{\mu}_s\) when \(\hat{\lambda}\) is random? Lemma 2 covers only fixed \(\lambda\); the random‑\(\lambda\) case remains open.
- **Robustness to inaccurate \(\omega_i\):** How does performance degrade when the known CV means \(\omega_i\) are erroneous or estimated? Experiments show increased regret, but a theoretical characterization is lacking.

## Limitations
- The theoretical guarantees (unbiasedness, confidence intervals) rely on the joint Gaussian assumption; for non‑Gaussian distributions the method is heuristic and lacks regret bounds.
- The algorithm requires accurate knowledge of the CV means \(\omega_i\); errors lead to significant regret increases.
- The specification of CV availability (Bernoulli per arm‑round vs. per‑round) is ambiguous, potentially affecting implementation consistency.

## Confidence
- **Variance reduction (Gaussian):** High — standard control variate theory and Proposition 1 are mathematically sound.
- **Online \(\lambda\) estimation (Gaussian):** Medium — unbiased variance estimators exist, but the ratio estimate can be unstable with few samples; no concentration analysis.
- **Non‑Gaussian adaptation:** Low — empirical results are limited; jackknife/split lack theoretical justification for UCB validity.
