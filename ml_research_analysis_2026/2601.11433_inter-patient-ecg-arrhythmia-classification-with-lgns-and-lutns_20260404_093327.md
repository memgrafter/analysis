# Inter-patient ECG Arrhythmia Classification with LGNs and LUTNs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.11433
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11433
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: Up to 94.28% accuracy and jκ-index 0.683 on four-class MIT-BIH arrhythmia classification with ultra-low inference cost (2.89k-6.17k FLOPs, 2000-2990 LUTs, 5-7 mW power on Artix 7 FPGA).

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles low-power ECG arrhythmia detection for implantable/wearable devices in the challenging inter-patient paradigm, where models must generalize to unseen patients. It introduces Deep Differentiable Logic Gate Networks (LGNs) and Lookup Table Networks (LUTNs) augmented with a novel binary feature preprocessing pipeline, a new LUT training method based on the Boolean equation of a multiplexer (MUX), and the first application of rate coding to these networks. On the MIT-BIH dataset, the best LGN achieves 94.28% accuracy and a jκ-index of 0.683, while consuming only 2.89k–6.17k FLOPs per inference—3–6 orders of magnitude less than prior state-of-the-art. Implemented on an Artix 7 FPGA, the design uses 2000–2990 LUTs and 5–7 mW (50–70 pJ/inference), achieving accuracy comparable to much more complex SVM ensembles but with vastly superior energy efficiency.

## Method Summary
The solution comprises three stages: preprocessing, a logic core (LGN or LUTN), and a readout. Preprocessing extracts a 138-bit binary feature vector per heartbeat, encoding RR-interval dynamics, local variability, tachycardia flags, morphological peak comparisons, crest factors in two windows, and delta-encoded raw signal segments. The logic core processes this vector through multiple layers: an LGN uses randomly connected 2-input differentiable logic gates (16 possible operations), while a LUTN employs N-input LUTs trained via a differentiable MUX formulation (Eq. 4). Training employs backpropagation; for LGNs, rate coding (full-precision probabilities) is used to improve gradient flow. After training, LUTN outputs are binarized layer-wise to obtain the final truth tables. The readout groups outputs per class, sums activations (population count), and applies a temperature-scaled softmax. The MUX-based LUT training ensures a 1-to-1 mapping to FPGA LUTs, enabling highly efficient hardware deployment.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy**: Up to 94.28% accuracy and jκ-index 0.683 on the four-class inter-patient MIT-BIH task, significantly exceeding prior LGN results and comparable to SVM ensembles.
- **Computational efficiency**: 2.89k–6.17k FLOPs per inference (3–6 orders of magnitude less than SOTA).
- **Hardware implementation**: On Artix 7 FPGA, uses 2000–2990 LUTs, power 5–7 mW (50–70 pJ/inference), demonstrating suitability for ultra-low-power implantables/wearables.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Binary feature engineering
**Claim**: A novel binary feature preprocessing pipeline significantly improves classification accuracy in both mixed-patient and inter-patient ECG paradigms compared to prior threshold-only methods.  
**Mechanism**: The pipeline extracts 138 binary features that explicitly encode temporal patterns (RR intervals), beat morphology (peak comparisons), signal statistics (crest factor), and fine-grained shape changes (delta encoding). This engineered representation is directly consumable by discrete logic networks and captures the discriminative cues in MIT-BIH arrhythmias.  
**Core assumption**: The key distinctions between arrhythmia classes in MIT-BIH rely heavily on heartbeat timing, morphology, and amplitude statistics; representing these as stable binary thresholds yields a robust, low-dimensional input for logic-based models.  
**Evidence**: Abstract and Section III-A detail the feature set and report superior performance over existing preprocessing for both paradigms.  
**Break condition**: Omitting critical features (e.g., crest factors) or altering threshold values substantially would likely degrade accuracy, regressing toward the baseline ~90% observed in prior work [30].

### Mechanism 2: MUX-based LUT training
**Claim**: Training LUTs via the differentiable Boolean equation of a multiplexer enables efficient, hardware-native mapping to FPGA LUTs while maintaining high accuracy, outperforming converted ANNs.  
**Mechanism**: Each N-input LUT is modeled as a 2^N:1 MUX (Eq. 4). During training, continuous-valued outputs (W_i) are learned via backpropagation, while the selector matrix encodes the truth table. Inputs are treated as probabilities; the forward pass computes a weighted sum of MUX output probabilities. After training, W_i are binarized layer-wise, yielding the final truth table that directly programs the FPGA LUT.  
**Core assumption**: The MUX formulation is a mathematically exact, differentiable surrogate for the hardware Boolean function, preserving the FPGA primitive during training and avoiding conversion losses.  
**Evidence**: Abstract and Section III-C present the method, algorithm, and hardware results showing fewer LUTs for equivalent accuracy versus prior differentiable LUT training.  
**Break condition**: Modifying the binarization schedule or MUX logic would increase hardware resource usage or reduce accuracy (e.g., from 94.26% for 4-LUTN).

### Mechanism 3: Rate coding
**Claim**: Rate coding (propagating full-precision probabilities as infinite-length bit-stream proxies) during training significantly improves LGN accuracy on complex datasets compared to single-threshold binary inputs, though at a cost in inference hardware efficiency.  
**Mechanism**: During training, inputs and gate outputs are represented as continuous values in [0,1] (probabilities) rather than binary 0/1. This soft relaxation of Boolean logic enables effective gradient flow through differentiable gate operations. At inference, finite-length stochastic bit streams (e.g., 128–1024 timesteps) are generated, where the average bit activity approximates the trained probability; longer streams reduce stochastic noise.  
**Core assumption**: The continuous relaxations are valid probability models; the stochastic binarization noise decreases with bit-stream length, restoring accuracy.  
**Evidence**: Abstract and Section III-B describe rate coding; Figures 5 and 6 show accuracy gains for LGNs on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST and the degradation of jκ-index with short bit streams on MIT-BIH.  
**Break condition**: Using very short bit streams (<64 timesteps) causes a steep drop in accuracy/jκ-index. Additionally, hardware cost (counters, power for bit toggling) increases linearly with bit-stream length, making rate-coded LGNs less efficient than binary LGNs for ultra-low-power implantables.

## Foundational Learning

- **Binary Feature Engineering for Physiological Signals**  
  Why needed: Logic networks operate on pure binary logic; raw ECG is continuous and high-dimensional. The engineered 138-bit vector distills domain knowledge (RR intervals, morphology, crest factors, delta changes) into a stable, low-dimensional binary representation that logic gates can directly process.  
  Quick check: Why was the M3 morphological feature excluded, and how do the crest factor windows target ventricular ectopic beats?

- **Differentiable Relaxations of Boolean Logic**  
  Why needed: LGNs require gradients to learn gate types (16 operations) from data. Real-valued approximations (e.g., XOR as `x0 + x1 - 2*x0*x1`) provide smooth gradients for backpropagation.  
  Quick check: For XOR (`x0 ⊕ x1 = x0 + x1 - 2*x0*x1`), what is the gradient w.r.t. `x0`? How does this differ from the non-differentiable binary XOR truth table?

- **Hardware-Neural Network Co-Design via LUTs**  
  Why needed: The goal is FPGA implementation. A single 6-input LUT can implement any 6-input Boolean function; training networks directly as collections of LUTs ensures a 1-to-1 mapping from software parameters to hardware configuration bits, avoiding conversion losses.  
  Quick check: How many distinct Boolean functions can a single 4-input LUT represent? Why does this exponential capacity (`2^(2^n)`) potentially reduce network depth compared to 2-input logic gates?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Preprocessor → Logic Core (LGN or LUTN) → Readout

### Critical path
Precomputed binary features → Binary vector → [LGN/LUT Layers] → Class-specific population sums → Temperature-scaled softmax → Prediction

### Design tradeoffs
- **LGN vs. LUTN**: LGNs use simpler 2-input gates but require more layers/connections; LUTs (4/6-input) have richer functions per unit, reducing depth/width, but deep LUTNs risk numerical instability from multiplying many small probabilities.
- **Rate coding**: Improves LGN accuracy by providing richer gradients but increases inference hardware (bit-stream length × counter size) and power linearly; LUTNs do not benefit and may degrade.
- **Depth**: Deeper networks capture more complex interactions but may cause performance drops for 6-LUTNs due to vanishing gradients.

### Failure signatures
- **Preprocessing**: Accuracy near 89.21% (normal class prior) indicates feature extraction failure or poor threshold selection.
- **LUTN training**: Sudden accuracy collapse for deep 6-LUTNs (e.g., 4-layer: 88.67%) suggests training instability from probability multiplication.
- **Hardware mismatch**: Post-synthesis LUT count >> software-specified counts or power estimate >> 7 mW indicates inefficient mapping (e.g., routing) or overly long bit-streams.

### First 3 experiments
1. Replicate preprocessing on MIT-BIH: Extract features for one patient recording, verify binary vector length (138) and sanity-check extreme values (e.g., crest factor >1 for VEBs). Compare against [3]'s features.
2. Train a 2-layer rate-coded LGN: Use full-precision 89 features, 8000 gates/layer, 200 epochs, lr=0.01. Validate accuracy >93% on mixed-patient split (Table IV) before inter-patient test.
3. Implement a 4-LUTN and count LUTs: Train a 1-layer 4-LUTN (3000 LUTs). Synthesize in Vivado for Artix 7. Confirm post-synthesis LUT count ≈ 3000 and power ~5–7 mW (Fig 7).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: Why do deep 6-LUTNs exhibit significantly degraded performance compared to shallower or smaller-LUT networks in this ECG task, and is this issue specific to training dynamics or the model architecture? The paper suspects numerical instability from multiplying many small probabilities but does not validate or explore mitigations.
- **Open Question 2**: Why does rate coding, which improves LGN performance, degrade LUTN performance in the inter-patient ECG task? The conjecture is that pre-binarization outputs may exceed [0,1], causing issues during training, but this remains unverified.
- **Open Question 3**: Can LGN/LUTN wake-up systems be effectively integrated with a more complex secondary classifier (e.g., an ANN or SVM) in a hybrid architecture to improve overall system accuracy while maintaining ultra-low average power? The paper suggests this but provides no experiments.

## Limitations
- Preprocessing details (R-peak detection method, segmentation windows, threshold selection) are unspecified, risking feature extraction variance and reproducibility.
- Training instability for deep LUTNs (especially 6-input) and opaque hyperparameter selection (softmax temperature) lack justification, potentially affecting reproducibility and performance claims.
- The inter-patient split could be compromised if global statistics (e.g., mean RR) inadvertently incorporate data from the test set (DS2), invalidating the evaluation.

## Confidence
- Binary feature preprocessing improves accuracy: **Medium** (strong evidence but depends on unknown R-peak and thresholds).
- MUX-based LUT training maps cleanly to FPGA: **High** (method rigorously defined, hardware results provided).
- Rate coding boosts LGN performance: **High** (empirical curves consistent, with clear hardware trade-off).
