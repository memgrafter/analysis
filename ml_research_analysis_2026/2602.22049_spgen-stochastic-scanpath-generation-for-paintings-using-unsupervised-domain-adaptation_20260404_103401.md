# SPGen: Stochastic scanpath generation for paintings using unsupervised domain adaptation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.22049  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22049  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** SPGen achieves state-of-the-art scanpath prediction on natural scene benchmarks and, via unsupervised domain adaptation, significantly improves performance on painting datasets by transferring knowledge without labeled painting data.

## Executive Summary
Predicting human scanpaths (sequences of fixations) for paintings is challenging due to the domain gap between natural scenes (with abundant gaze data) and artworks. SPGen addresses this with a fully convolutional network that generates variable-length scanpaths through differentiable fixation selection and stochastic sampling. It introduces learnable Gaussian prior maps to model viewing biases and uses a gradient reversal layer for unsupervised domain adaptation, aligning feature distributions between natural scenes and paintings. Evaluations show state-of-the-art results on standard natural image datasets and substantial gains on painting-specific benchmarks, demonstrating its utility for cultural heritage analysis.

## Method Summary
SPGen is a deterministic fully convolutional neural network (based on MobileNet) that generates scanpaths iteratively. For each step, it produces a coarse fixation probability map. A parallel fixation selector network generates a dynamic binary mask (via learned mean-based binarization) to control which spatial locations contribute, enabling variable-length paths. Stochasticity is injected by adding scaled random noise to the feature map before masking. A Soft-ArgMax operation converts the masked map into continuous (x,y) coordinates. To handle the lack of labeled painting data, a domain adaptation branch with a gradient reversal layer adversarially aligns feature distributions between natural scenes (source) and paintings (target), forcing the encoder to learn domain-invariant representations.

## Key Results
- SPGen achieves state-of-the-art performance on natural scene scanpath benchmarks (Salicon, MIT1003) in metrics like MM Score and NSS.
- After unsupervised domain adaptation, SPGen shows significant improvements on painting datasets (Le Meur, AVAtt), e.g., increasing MM Score on AVAtt from 0.8245 to 0.8312 and Congruency from 0.1212 to 0.1852.
- Ablations confirm the contribution of the learnable Gaussian priors and the stochastic fixation selector for modeling viewing biases and scanpath length variability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** SPGenerates scanpaths by selecting fixation points in a differentiable, probabilistic manner using Soft-ArgMax over a weighted feature map, combined with a learned binary mask to control scanpath length.
- **Mechanism:** The model outputs a coarse fixation probability map via convolutional layers. Soft-ArgMax converts this map into continuous coordinates. A parallel fixation selector (MLP with mean-based binarization) generates a dynamic mask multiplied with the feature map before Soft-ArgMax, allowing gradient flow while stochastically determining contributing points and thus sequence length.
- **Core assumption:** Human scanpath length and point selection can be modeled by a learned, dynamic gating mechanism over a spatial probability distribution, rather than a fixed-length deterministic output.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract]: "...Fully Convolutional Neural Network (FCNN) with differentiable fixation selection..."  
  [section 3.1.3]: Detailed description of fixation selector, merging layers, and multiplication trick for non-differentiable binarization.
- **Break condition:** If the fixation selector fails to produce mask distributions matching empirical scanpath length distributions (e.g., consistently too short/long), outputs become unrealistic regardless of point accuracy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Learnable Gaussian prior maps are concatenated to the feature extractor's output to model domain- and task-specific viewing biases (e.g., center bias), allowing the main CNN to focus on stimulus-specific saliency patterns.
- **Mechanism:** Multiple 2D Gaussian functions (Eq. 1) with learnable means (µ) and standard deviations (σ) are generated and channel-wise concatenated with MobileNet features. During training, the network learns these parameters, capturing biases present in the training data (e.g., central tendency for natural scenes vs. paintings).
- **Core assumption:** Systematic viewing biases are spatially smooth, approximately Gaussian, and separable from stimulus-driven saliency by learning a small set of parametric bias maps.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract]: "...learnable Gaussian prior maps to model viewing biases..."  
  [section 3.1.2]: Definition of Gaussian function (Eq. 1) and rationale for learning multiple bias maps to disregard general center bias.
- **Break condition:** If the true viewing bias in the target domain is not well-approximated by a mixture of Gaussians, or if the number of learned Gaussians is insufficient, the model under-corrects for bias, harming stimulus-specific feature learning.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Unsupervised domain adaptation via a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) aligns the feature distributions of natural scenes (source) and paintings (target), enabling knowledge transfer without labeled painting scanpaths.
- **Mechanism:** A domain classifier branch is added to the feature extractor's output. The GRL (Eq. 6-7) acts as identity in forward pass but multiplies the domain classification gradient by -1 during backpropagation. This adversarial setup trains the feature extractor to produce features that support good scanpath prediction (primary loss) and confuse the domain classifier, minimizing domain discrepancy (Eq. 8).
- **Core assumption:** The decision boundaries for scanpath prediction are largely shared between natural scenes and paintings, and performance loss on paintings is primarily due to low-level feature distribution shifts rather than fundamentally different high-level gaze rules.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  [abstract]: "unsupervised domain adaptation via a gradient reversal layer, allowing the model to transfer knowledge from natural scenes to paintings."  
  [section 3.3]: Detailed explanation of GRL operation and unified representation space (Eq. 8).  
  [section 4.7]: Quantitative improvement on AVAtt after domain adaptation (e.g., MM Score 0.8312 vs 0.8245; Congruency 0.1854 vs 0.1212).
- **Break condition:** If source and target domains exhibit *label shift* (i.e., the relationship between image features and gaze behavior differs fundamentally), adversarial alignment forces a compromised, non-causal representation, degrading performance.

- **Concept: Fully Convolutional Networks (FCNs) & Differentiable Operations**  
  *Why needed here:* SPGen's core (Soft-ArgMax, fixation mask multiplication) relies on differentiable spatial operations over feature maps to backpropagate scanpath prediction error. FCNs preserve spatial dimensionality, enabling this.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input -> MobileNet Feature Extractor -> [Concat Learnable Gaussian Priors] -> Merging Convolutions (8x conv+ReLU) -> [Add Scaled Random Noise] -> Parallel Branches: (a) Channel Selection (MLP -> Sigmoid) (b) Fixation Selector (MLP -> Binarized Mask) -> Masked Feature Map (noise-added features * mask) -> Soft-ArgMax -> (x,y) fixation. Repeat for sequence. [Domain Adaptation Branch: MobileNet features -> Gradient Reversal Layer -> Domain Classifier].

**Critical path:** MobileNet features → Concatenate Gaussian priors → Merging convolutions → Add noise → Fixation Selector (Mask) → Multiply masked features with noise-added map → Soft-ArgMax → (x,y) fixation. Iterate to build sequence. Primary task loss (BCE + length regularization) supervises this path; domain loss supervises the GRL branch.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Lightweight Backbone (MobileNet) vs. Capacity:** Chosen for efficiency and limited data, but may limit capturing complex painting textures versus heavier backbones (VGG, ResNet) used in baselines.
- **Deterministic Core vs. Stochastic Output:** The core network is deterministic; stochasticity is injected in latent space via noise and learned mask. Simpler than RNN/Transformer sequential models but may not capture strong temporal dependencies.
- **Adversarial UDA vs. Negative Transfer:** GRL encourages domain confusion but provides no guarantee of learning *task-relevant* invariant features. If painting gaze rules differ fundamentally, this can degrade performance.

**Failure signatures:**
- **On Paintings (w/o DA):** Scanpaths collapse to short, central fixations, mimicking natural scene center bias but ignoring painting composition.
- **After DA:** Scanpaths become overly diffuse or noisy if noise temperature is too high, or if domain alignment forces features to be too generic, losing saliency information (drop in NSS on natural scenes).
- **Training Instability:** Balancing primary scanpath loss and domain classification loss requires careful weighting; GRL can cause gradient magnitude issues.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline Establishment (Sec 4.4):** Train the *deterministic* model on Salicon (9k images). Evaluate on Salicon test and MIT1003 (cross-dataset). Compare MM Score, NSS, Congruency against SOTA (PathGAN, Le Meur, SALYPATH). *Goal:* Validate architectural components on natural scenes.
2. **Component Ablation (Sec 4.5):** Train ablations on MIT1003: (a) Remove prior maps. (b) Remove fixation selector. Measure drop in MM Score and changes in length distribution (Fig 4). *Goal:* Isolate contribution of bias modeling and variable-length selection.
3. **Domain Adaptation Efficacy (Sec 4.7):** Take trained deterministic model from Exp 1. Apply UDA training using 2k Salicon + 2k unlabeled paintings. Test on Le Meur and AVAtt. Compare metrics (NSS, Congruency, MM Score) before and after DA. *Goal:* Quantify gap closure for painting datasets.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can SPGen be extended to predict not only spatial scanpaths but also the temporal dynamics of fixations (i.e., fixation durations)?  
  *Basis:* Conclusion explicitly states lack of duration prediction as a limitation for future work.
- **Question 2:** What specific visual features or representations are aligned or misaligned by the unsupervised domain adaptation when transferring from natural scenes to paintings?  
  *Basis:* Inferred; paper describes feature alignment via GRL but does not analyze which features become invariant or how painting-specific characteristics are handled.
- **Question 3:** Do current evaluation metrics (MM Score, NSS, Congruency) adequately capture the stochastic and subjective nature of human scanpaths, or do they inadvertently bias evaluation toward deterministic, average-like predictions?  
  *Basis:* Inferred; ablation results show lower noise temperatures yield higher scores, suggesting metrics may penalize human-like variability.
- **Question 4:** How does SPGen's performance generalize across vastly different artistic styles, cultural contexts, or non-Western art movements not represented in the AVAtt dataset?  
  *Basis:* Inferred; AVAtt covers primarily Western art movements; paper claims generalizability but lacks systematic testing on diverse global art.
- **Question 5:** Can the unsupervised domain adaptation approach be extended to handle multiple target domains (e.g., paintings, cartoons, sketches) simultaneously, or does it require separate adaptation for each?  
  *Basis:* Explicit; method described for binary domain classifier (natural vs. painting); conclusion mentions future work on monuments, architectures, statues, implying multi-domain need.

## Limitations
- Learnable Gaussian priors may oversimplify viewing biases; a limited number might not capture multi-modal patterns in diverse artworks.
- Gradient Reversal Layer assumes shared saliency boundaries; if paintings exhibit different gaze rules (label shift), forced invariance could harm performance.
- MobileNet backbone may limit capacity for high-resolution paintings with intricate textures.

## Confidence
- **Differentiable fixation selection:** High (well-defined equations, stable training).
- **Learnable Gaussian prior maps:** Medium (ablation improvements, but no visualization of learned Gaussians).
- **Unsupervised domain adaptation:** Medium (quantitative gains on painting datasets, but limited cross-dataset generalization checks).
- **Overall SOTA on paintings:** Medium (modest improvements; statistical significance not discussed).
