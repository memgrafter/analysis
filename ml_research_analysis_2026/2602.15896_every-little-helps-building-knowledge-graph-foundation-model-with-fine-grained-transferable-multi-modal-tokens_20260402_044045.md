# Every Little Helps: Building Knowledge Graph Foundation Model with Fine-grained Transferable Multi-modal Tokens

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.15896  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15896  
- **Reference count:** 20  
- **Primary result:** TOFU achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot (MRR 45.93) and fine-tuned (MRR 47.41) performance on 17 multi-modal knowledge graphs, outperforming baselines by 2–3 MRR points with robust cross-graph generalization.

## Executive Summary
Knowledge graph foundation models (KGFMs) that generalize across graphs and modalities remain an open challenge. This paper introduces TOFU, a token-based framework that unifies structural, visual, and textual information into discrete token sequences. By employing a hierarchical fusion architecture and a mixture-of-messages mechanism, TOFU learns transferable representations without entity-specific embeddings. Evaluated on 17 multi-modal knowledge graphs under transductive, inductive, and fully-inductive settings, TOFU attains state-of-the-art zero-shot and fine-tuned performance, demonstrating robust cross-graph generalization.

## Method Summary
TOFU processes multi-modal knowledge graphs by first tokenizing each modality into a sequence of discrete tokens. Structural information from local subgraphs is encoded via relative position tokens learned through a codebook. Visual and textual data are discretized using pre-trained BEiT and BERT tokenizers, respectively. These tokens share a unified embedding space. The framework then processes structural tokens with a graph neural network (Structural Encoder) and visual/textual tokens with a Transformer (Multi-modal Encoder). Their outputs are adaptively fused via a gated mechanism that balances modality contributions. The fused entity representations are further refined by a global propagation module that iteratively messages over the graph. Crucially, TOFU employs a mixture-of-messages (MiM) approach, which dynamically combines TransE, DistMult, and RotatE message functions using query-aware attention and relation-guided temperatures. This eliminates the need for entity-specific embeddings while capturing diverse relation patterns. Training uses a negative sampling ranking loss, and the model can be pre-trained on source graphs and directly applied to unseen target graphs (zero-shot) or fine-tuned.

## Key Results
- **Zero-shot cross-MMKG transfer:** Average MRR 45.93 across 17 datasets, surpassing KG-ICL and ULTRA by ~2–3 MRR points.  
- **Fine-tuned transfer:** MRR 47.41, showing consistent gains with target-specific adaptation.  
- **Robust generalization:** Strong performance under transductive, inductive, and fully-inductive splits.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Modality-Agnostic Tokenization
- **Claim:** Discretizing structural, visual, and textual modalities into token sequences enables transferable representations across knowledge graphs.  
- **Mechanism:** Visual/textual modalities use pre-trained codebooks (BEiT/BERT) to map raw data to fixed vocabulary tokens. Structural modality maps relative graph positions (shortest-path distances to query entities) to a learned codebook. All tokens share a unified embedding space.  
- **Core assumption:** Transferability arises from (a) fixed pre-trained tokenizers for vision/text providing semantic stability, and (b) structural tokens representing relational roles rather than entity identities.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "discretizes structural, visual, and textual information into unified tokens"  
  - [section 4.1] Details tokenization for each modality, including structural tokens based on relative positions.  
- **Break condition:** If pre-trained codebooks are mismatched to target domain (e.g., medical images), or if structural token codebook fails to capture meaningful relational patterns in new graphs.

### Mechanism 2: Hierarchical Modality Fusion
- **Claim:** Separating structural encoding (GNN) from multimodal encoding (Transformer) with an adaptive gate yields modality-invariant features.  
- **Mechanism:** A structural encoder (SE) processes subgraph structural tokens via GNN layers. A multimodal encoder (ME) processes visual/textual tokens via Transformer. A gated fusion (GF) module weighs SE and ME outputs per-entity using a sigmoid gate derived from concatenated features.  
- **Core assumption:** Structure and content exhibit complementary patterns requiring separate processing before fusion; adaptive weighting prevents dominant modalities from overwriting others.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "hierarchical fusion architecture"  
  - [section 4.2] Equations 2-7 define SE, ME, and GF; ablation in Fig 5(1) shows GF contributes positive average gain.  
- **Break condition:** If modality signals are highly correlated (e.g., visual features directly encode structure), the GF weights may become unstable or degenerate to a single modality.

### Mechanism 3: Relation-Guided Mixture-of-Messages (MiM)
- **Claim:** Dynamically weighting heterogeneous message functions (TransE/DistMult/RotatE) per relation enables universal triple modeling without entity-specific embeddings.  
- **Mechanism:** In global propagation (GP), each edge's message is a weighted sum of three base message functions. Weights are computed via query-aware attention (based on head/relation) and a relation-guided temperature (τ) that softens the weight distribution.  
- **Core assumption:** No single scoring function is optimal across all relation types; a data-dependent mixture can approximate relation-specific operators.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "mixture-of-messages mechanism"  
  - [section 4.3] Equations 10-12 define MiM; Figure 7 visualizes distinct weight distributions per relation, and OOD generalization in YAGO15K.  
- **Break condition:** If a new graph contains relation types with patterns outside the convex hull of TransE/DistMult/RotatE (e.g., highly non-linear), MiM may fail to approximate required transformations.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Tokenization for Graph Structures**  
- **Why needed here:** TOFU replaces entity IDs with position-based discrete tokens; engineers must understand how graph neighborhoods map to finite token codes.  
- **Quick check question:** How does the structural token codebook differ from traditional entity embeddings in inductive settings?

**Concept: GNN-Based Subgraph Encoding**  
- **Why needed here:** The structural encoder (SE) is a GNN that operates on tokenized subgraphs; familiarity with message-passing frameworks (e.g., PyTorch Geometric) is required.  
- **Quick check question:** What role does the query vector play in SE's attention-based aggregation (Eq 2-3)?

**Concept: Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Routing**  
- **Why needed here:** MiM is an MoE variant where routing weights are relation-aware; understanding gating mechanisms is key to analyzing weight distributions.  
- **Quick check question:** Why is the temperature τ parameter relation-guided (Eq 11), and how does it affect exploration vs. exploitation?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Tokenizers (Text, Image, Structure) → SE (GNN) & ME (Transformer) → GF (gated sum) → GP (GNN with MiM) → Scoring

**Critical path**  
Tokenization → SE/ME parallel processing → GF fusion → GP iterative propagation → triple scoring. The query (h,r,?) threads through all modules.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Token count:** More tokens improve capacity but increase cost; paper finds 8–16 optimal (Fig 6).  
- **Fixed vs. learnable tokens:** Pre-trained vision/text tokens are frozen (transferability), but structural tokens are learned (lack of pre-training).  
- **Modality balance:** Ablation (Fig 5(2)) shows text strongest; however, removing SE (structure) hurts more than removing vision, implying structure is dominant but multi-modality adds complementary signal.

**Failure signatures**  
- OOD datasets with unseen visual/textual distributions cause tokenizer mismatch → poor zero-shot MRR.  
- Relation types with patterns dissimilar to TransE/DistMult/RotatE → MiM weights collapse to a single function.  
- Small subgraph contexts → SE receives insufficient structural cues → degenerate structural tokens.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **Transductive MMKGR** (Table 2): Train/evaluate on same MMKG; validates token-based modeling beats per-entity embedding methods in-domain.  
2. **Zero-shot cross-MMKG** (Table 1, Fig 3): Pre-train on source MMKGs, test on all 17 MMKGs without fine-tuning; tests generalizability.  
3. **Ablation of MiM** (Fig 5(3)): Replace MiM with single message functions (TransE/DistMult/RotatE) on 5 MMKGs; demonstrates mixture stabilizes performance across diverse graphs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1:** Can TOFU's token-based paradigm be successfully extended to other multi-modal knowledge graph tasks such as entity alignment and entity linking while maintaining robust cross-graph transferability?  
*Basis:* The conclusion explicitly states future integration of more MMKG tasks.  
*Why unresolved:* TOFU is engineered for link prediction; entity alignment requires cross-graph matching and entity linking connects textual mentions to KG entities—each has distinct input-output structures and objectives.  
*What would resolve:* Reimplementation of TOFU’s core components for these tasks, showing zero-shot/few-shot performance on unseen MMKGs comparable to task-specific SOTA models.

**Open Question 2:** What specific graph-theoretic or semantic properties of relations predict the learned weighting among TransE, DistMult, and RotatE message functions in TOFU’s mixture-of-messages mechanism?  
*Basis:* The paper visualizes distinct weight distributions per relation but does not identify which relation characteristics drive these weights.  
*Why unresolved:* Without a mapping from relation features to optimal message types, the mixture remains a black-box ensemble that may not generalize to novel relations.  
*What would resolve:* Large-scale correlation study across multiple MMKGs, measuring consistency between relation-level features (e.g., symmetry, training facts, textual label similarity) and the learned message weights, revealing statistically significant and reproducible patterns.

**Open Question 3:** Is the implicit token allocation—one structural token versus 8–16 visual/textual tokens—optimal for multi-modal transfer, or could performance be improved by rebalancing token budgets based on modality-specific information content?  
*Basis:* The authors note structural tokens have higher information density and select token numbers based on efficiency/performance trade-offs without an information-theoretic justification.  
*Why unresolved:* The current allocation may underrepresent rich visual/textual cues or overcompress structural patterns; optimal allocation should consider actual information gain per token across modalities.  
*What would resolve:* Systematic experiments varying token counts per modality while normalizing total parameters/FLOPs, measuring MRR/Hit@10 across diverse MMKGs; ideally complemented by estimating token-wise mutual information with the link prediction task.

**Open Question 4:** Why does the relation-guided temperature mechanism in the mixture-of-messages remain effective on out-of-distribution (OOD) datasets despite exhibiting temperature distributions distinct from pre-training data?  
*Basis:* The case study shows temperature distributions on OOD YAGO15K differ from pre-training datasets yet still lead to performance gains.  
*Why unresolved:* It is unclear whether robustness stems from temperature adaptation itself, the diversity of message functions, or incidental dataset similarities.  
*What would resolve:* Ablation studies on OOD datasets comparing fixed vs. learned temperature; analysis of temperature values correlated with relation novelty metrics; monitoring temperature adaptation during fine-tuning on OOD data.

## Limitations
- Dependency on pre-trained tokenizers (BEiT/BERT) may hinder generalization to domain-specific modalities (e.g., medical images).  
- Structural token codebook is learned from scratch, risking poor transferability.  
- Mixture-of-messages may fail for relations outside the convex hull of base functions; critical hyperparameters (subgraph size, token count) lack sensitivity analysis.
