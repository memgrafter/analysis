# MM-SCALE: Grounded Multimodal Moral Reasoning via Scalar Judgment and Listwise Alignment

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.03665
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03665
- **Reference count:** 32
- **Primary result:** Scalar listwise supervision improves VLM moral ranking (NDCG@5 up to 0.89, MRR up to 0.60) and safety calibration (AUC-Safety up to 0.79) over binary methods.

## Executive Summary
Vision-language models (VLMs) often fail at nuanced moral reasoning in ambiguous multimodal contexts. This paper introduces MM-SCALE, a framework that combines a large-scale dataset of 32,212 image-scenario pairs with 5-point scalar moral ratings and explicit modality grounding (text, image, both). Annotators used the MORALE interface to provide both scalar judgments and modality labels. VLMs are fine-tuned via listwise preference optimization (ListMLE) to rank scenarios within shared image contexts, encouraging global consistency. Results demonstrate that scalar listwise supervision significantly outperforms binary approaches in ranking fidelity and safety calibration. Notably, 68% of human judgments shift when the visual context is introduced, and modality grounding leads to predictable rating shifts (e.g., +0.90 when visuals reinforce text). MM-SCALE thus advances multimodal moral alignment by capturing continuous moral gradations and comparative reasoning within context.

## Method Summary
MM-SCALE comprises three main stages. First, the MORALE interactive interface collects human annotations: for each image-scenario pair, annotators assign a 5-point Likert scale moral rating and indicate whether the judgment is primarily based on text, image, or both. This yields 32,212 labeled pairs. Second, pairs are grouped by their shared image into lists of scenarios, creating a set of per-image rankings. Third, a pre-trained VLM (e.g., LLaVA, Qwen-VL) is fine-tuned: each scenario (text) and its image are processed to predict a scalar score. A ListMLE loss optimizes the predicted scores to match the human-provided ranking of the list. Optionally, auxiliary losses (MSE for score fidelity, cross-entropy for modality prediction) can be added. The training uses LoRA for efficient adaptation. This pipeline replaces binary safe/unsafe labels with continuous scalars and leverages listwise comparisons to teach relative moral acceptability within a shared visual context.

## Key Results
- **Improved ranking fidelity:** Listwise scalar training achieves NDCG@5 up to 0.89 and MRR up to 0.60, surpassing binary methods (BPO, BCE) across architectures.
- **Better safety calibration:** AUC-Safety reaches 0.79, indicating more reliable separation of safe and unsafe content.
- **Impact of visual context:** 68% of human judgments diverge from text-only norms when images are shown; modality grounding correlates with systematic rating shifts (+0.90 when visuals reinforce text, -0.48 when they contradict).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Scalar supervision captures moral nuance.** Traditional binary labels collapse the spectrum of moral acceptability. The 5-point scale allows the model to learn fine-grained differences between "somewhat acceptable" and "mostly acceptable" actions. Evidence: Listwise scalar training yields substantially higher NDCG@5 and MRR than binary baselines (Table 3). The assumption that moral judgment is scalar is supported by the high rate (68.1%) of visual context shifting judgments, indicating that context influences perceived acceptability on a continuum. A risk is non-uniform intervals on the Likert scale, mitigated by annotator quality control (Krippendorff’s α=0.74).

**Listwise ranking promotes global consistency.** By optimizing the permutation of a whole list of scenarios within one image, ListMLE forces the model to compare all actions relatively, rather than making isolated decisions. This mirrors human comparative judgments and leverages the full ranking signal. Evidence: Kendall’s τ peaks at 0.68 and NDCG@5 at 0.84 for listwise supervision (Table 4). Performance saturates around list size m=4, suggesting an optimal comparative set size. A potential failure is forcing rankings on morally incommensurable items, which is addressed by grouping scenarios under a single coherent image context.

**Modality grounding disentangles evidence sources.** Labels indicating whether a judgment is text-based, image-based, or both teach the model to weight cues appropriately. For instance, an image may downgrade an otherwise neutral text action. Systematic shifts are documented: +0.90 average score increase when visuals reinforce text, -0.48 when they contradict (Table 2). A modality classifier can learn these labels with 72.8% accuracy, confirming coherence. However, annotator agreement on grounding is moderate (61%), introducing noise.

## Foundational Learning
- **Scalar Moral Judgment & Continuous Preference**  
  *Why needed:* MM-SCALE replaces binary safety labels with a 5-point Likert scale, requiring the model to output a continuous acceptability score.  
  *Quick check:* Would you rate "helping a stranger cross the street" as 4 or 5 if the image shows a dark, deserted alley versus a bright, crowded sidewalk? Why might the same text action get different scores?

- **Modality-Attributed Evidence**  
  *Why needed:* The dataset labels which modality (text, image, both) primarily drove the moral judgment. The model must learn to integrate evidence correctly, not just fuse features.  
  *Quick check:* An action text says "drinking from a stranger's cup." Image shows a friendly picnic. Is the judgment more text-grounded (risk) or image-grounded (friendly context)? What if the image shows a dirty cup?

- **Listwise Ranking Loss (ListMLE)**  
  *Why needed:* The training objective optimizes the order of a list of scenario scores per image, not individual scores. The model must produce a permutation matching the human ranking.  
  *Quick check:* Given three scenarios with human scores {5, 3, 1} for one image, what does the model's predicted score vector {4.8, 2.1, 0.9} need to achieve to minimize ListMLE loss?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Data Collection (MORALE interface) → scalar scores & modality labels → grouping by image into lists → VLM forward pass (image+text per scenario) → scalar score predictions → ListMLE loss (optimizes ranking) → LoRA weight update. Optional auxiliary heads: MSE loss for score fidelity, modality prediction loss.

**Critical path:** Raw image + scenario text → human annotation (scalar + modality) → per-image list formation → model produces score vector → ListMLE loss computed against human ranking → gradient update via LoRA. The per-image list grouping is the distinctive structural element that enables listwise supervision.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *List length (m):* Larger lists provide more comparative signal but may include incommensurable items; performance saturates around m=4 (Figure 8a).  
- *Synthetic vs. real images:* Synthetic images approximate real ones (Δ<0.02), enabling scalable data collection, but may miss real-world edge cases.  
- *Modality label usage:* Used for analysis and auxiliary multitask loss; forcing modality as a hard constraint could over-constrain the model.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Low inter-annotator agreement (Krippendorff’s α < 0.7) indicates noisy labels.  
- Poor calibration (high Expected Calibration Error, especially in binary-trained models).  
- Context collapse: model fails to rank scenarios differently within the same image (low NDCG@5, Kendall’s τ).  
- Modality confusion: modality classifier accuracy near random (33%) suggests the model isn't learning grounding.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Scalar vs. Binary Validation:** Train a model with ListMLE (scalar) and BCE (binary) on the same split. Expect ListMLE to achieve higher NDCG@5 and AUC-Safety; binary may have lower Unsafe Rate but worse separability.  
2. **List Size Ablation:** Fix model and data, vary number of scenarios per image (m=2,3,4,5). Expect NDCG@5 to improve and plateau around m=4; larger lists may degrade due to noise.  
3. **Modality Grounding Check:** On a held-out set, stratify scenarios by modality label (text/image/both). Compute mean absolute prediction error against human scores per stratum. Expect lower error for text-grounded cases, but listwise model should handle all better than binary baselines. Also verify that predictions for the same text diverge across different image contexts.

## Limitations
- The 5-point Likert scale intervals may not align with actual psychological moral gradations, risking a distorted utility function.
- Modality grounding labels show only 61% annotator agreement, implying significant noise in the "why" signal.
- Synthetic image validation may not capture real-world visual complexity and ambiguity.

## Confidence
- **Scalar supervision improves nuance:** Medium (internal metrics strong; weak external psychological evidence).
- **Listwise ranking yields better global ordering:** Medium-High (robust ranking gains across architectures).
- **Modality grounding enables context-sensitive integration:** Medium (systematic shifts documented but label noise and moderate classifier accuracy limit certainty).

## Next Checks
1. **Psychological scaling validation:** Conduct a paired-comparison study (n>100) to derive a Thurstone scale for the 5-point labels. If intervals are non-uniform, re-score the dataset and evaluate impact on NDCG@5.
2. **Cross-dataset transfer:** Fine-tune MM-SCALE on the MORALISE benchmark (different moral foundations, no listwise structure). Test whether scalar listwise pretraining yields better few-shot ranking fidelity than binary-pretrained baselines.
3. **Modality noise stress test:** Artificially reduce modality label agreement to 50% during training. Compare per-image NDCG@5 drop against models trained with clean labels to quantify reliance on the grounding signal.
