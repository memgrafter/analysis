# Surgical Repair of Collapsed Attention Heads in ALiBi Transformers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.09616
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09616
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** Systematic BOS-sink collapse affects 31–44% of attention heads in ALiBi BLOOM models; targeted Q/K/V reinitialization with zeroed output projections recovers 98.7% of operational head capacity in BLOOM-1b7.

## Executive Summary
Pretrained ALiBi-based transformers exhibit a predictable failure mode where a subset of attention heads collapses to attending almost exclusively to the start (BOS) token. This “BOS-sink” collapse concentrates in head indices subject to the steepest ALiBi distance penalties, rendering those heads non-functional. The authors introduce a surgical repair technique: selectively reinitializing the query, key, and value projections of collapsed heads while zeroing their output projections and freezing all other parameters. Applied to BLOOM-1b7, the two-pass protocol recovers 242 of 384 initially healthy heads, bringing total operational heads to 379 (98.7% capacity) on a single consumer GPU in two training passes. Intriguingly, applying the same procedure to already-healthy heads yields a model that transiently outperforms the original by 25% on training perplexity, suggesting pretrained attention configurations often reside in suboptimal local minima.

## Method Summary
The repair protocol begins with a diagnostic forward pass using a fixed 512-token sequence to compute two metrics per attention head: BOS mass (average attention weight on position 0) and entropy. Heads are classified as healthy (BOS mass ≤ 0.50), BOS-sink (0.50–0.95), or dead (>0.95). Surgical training then proceeds in two passes. In Pass 1, all collapsed heads within head indices 9–15 and layers 5–22 are targeted. For each, their Q, K, V weight columns are reinitialized with Xavier uniform, their corresponding output projection columns are zeroed, and all other model parameters are frozen via gradient masks. The model is trained for ~3 epochs on a curated corpus using AdamW (LR 5e-5), bfloat16, gradient checkpointing, and gradient clipping (max norm 1.0). Pass 2 targets the remaining collapsed heads (head indices outside 9–15) for one additional epoch. Recovery is confirmed when all targeted heads achieve BOS mass below 0.50.

## Key Results
- 31–44% of attention heads in ALiBi BLOOM models collapse to BOS-sink states, following a predictable pattern aligned with steepest ALiBi slope penalties.
- Surgical reinitialization recovers 98.7% of operational head capacity in BLOOM-1b7 (from 242 to 379 of 384 heads) after two passes.
- Applying surgery to healthy heads produces a transient 25% training perplexity improvement (12.70 vs. 16.99), indicating pretrained configurations are suboptimal local minima.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Slope-Induced Attractor
ALiBi’s head-specific slope schedule `m_h = 2^{-8(h+1)/H}` imposes exponentially steeper distance penalties for higher head indices. During pretraining, these heads converge to a sharp local minimum where attending entirely to the BOS token (position 0) yields lower loss than content-dependent patterns, because penalties for distant positions become energetically prohibitive. Evidence: The collapse band (e.g., H9–H15 in 16-head models) exactly matches the steepest slopes; for H15, attending to position 100 incurs a penalty of ~‑0.39 nats versus 0 for position 0.

### Mechanism 2: Reset Escapes Minimum
Gradient-based fine-tuning alone fails to revive collapsed heads; a full weight reset is necessary. Reinitializing Q/K/V places the head at a random point outside the BOS-sink attractor. Zeroing the output projection ensures the head contributes nothing during the transition, preventing destabilization of the residual stream. Gradient masking isolates training to surgical parameters, allowing stable escape. Evidence: Single-head proof-of-concept shows BOS mass dropping from >0.9 to <0.2 only with reinitialization; two-pass protocol achieves 108/108 recovery in Pass 1 by epoch 3.

### Mechanism 3: Residual Stream Redistribution
Revived heads inject new value vectors into the shared residual stream, altering the input distribution for all downstream layers. This triggers global attention topology reorganization, not just local changes in targeted heads. The character of this redistribution—beneficial or pathological—depends on training corpus properties. Evidence: Untouched heads (H0–H8) exhibit significant drift after surgery; with a curated corpus, 76 such heads shift in a functional direction, while a noisy corpus induces iatrogenic degradation (Phenomenon 2).

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: ALiBi Positional Encoding**  
  *Why needed:* The collapse mechanism is directly caused by ALiBi’s slope schedule; understanding `m_h = 2^{-8(h+1)/H}` is essential to predict which heads are vulnerable.  
  *Quick check:* In a 32-head model, which head indices would show most severe BOS-sink collapse? (Answer: Highest indices, e.g., 24–31, because they have the smallest slopes and thus steepest penalties.)

- **Concept: Bimodal BOS Mass Distribution**  
  *Why needed:* This empirical observation justifies the binary healthy/collapsed classification and indicates collapse is a discrete state, not a continuum.  
  *Quick check:* What does a strongly bimodal BOS mass distribution imply about attention head states? (Answer: The loss landscape contains distinct attractors; heads settle into either functional or collapsed modes.)

- **Concept: Residual Stream as Shared Infrastructure**  
  *Why needed:* It explains why modifying a subset of heads causes global redistribution (Mechanism 3). The residual stream connects all layers ecologically.  
  *Quick check:* If you reinitialize only heads in layer 5, why might layer 20 heads change? (Answer: New value vectors from layer 5 alter the residual stream, which serves as input to all subsequent layers.)

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:  
Input -> Token Embedding -> Layer1 (Attention+MLP) -> Residual Stream -> Layer2 ... -> LayerN -> Output Logits  
Within each Attention block: Q/K/V projections (combined `query_key_value` matrix) -> ALiBi bias addition -> Softmax -> Output projection (`dense` matrix) -> Residual add.

Critical path:  
1. Diagnose: Forward pass with `output_attentions=True` → compute per-head BOS mass and entropy → classify heads.  
2. Prepare surgery: For each target head, reinitialize Q/K/V columns in `query_key_value.weight` (Xavier uniform) and zero corresponding columns in `dense.weight`. Freeze all other parameters via `requires_grad=False`.  
3. Train: Use curated corpus (512-token chunks), AdamW (lr 5e-5), bf16, gradient accumulation (8), clipping (1.0). Monitor target head BOS mass; stop when all ≤ 0.50 or after 3 epochs.  
4. Repeat for Pass 2 with remaining collapsed heads.

Design tradeoffs:  
- Precision: bf16 required; fp16 underflows, fp32 OOM.  
- Surgical scope: Targeting too broad a head band risks iatrogenic drift; too narrow may miss collapsed heads.  
- Training data: Curated corpus yields faster convergence and beneficial redistribution but may imprint domain bias; C4 generalizes better but risks degradation with prolonged training.  
- Zeroed output: Prevents initial destabilization but delays head contribution; could be warmed up gradually.

Failure signatures:  
- Persistent BOS‑sink: Target head BOS mass remains > 0.95 after training → failed escape.  
- Iatrogenic drift: Previously healthy heads show |δ BOS mass| > 0.1 → Phenomenon 2 degradation, often from noisy corpus or over‑training.  
- Overfitting spike: Training perplexity drops then rises sharply within one epoch → transient improvement exhausted; need more diverse data.

First 3 experiments:  
1. **Reproduce diagnosis on stock BLOOM-1b7:** Run 100 prompts, compute BOS mass/entropy, verify bimodal distribution and sick band (H9–H15).  
2. **Single-head reinitialization proof-of-concept:** Select one collapsed head (e.g., L10-H12), perform surgical steps, train 500 steps on small subset, confirm BOS mass drops below 0.2.  
3. **Corpus control replication:** Run Pass 1 surgery on both curated corpus and C4 for exactly 3 epochs; compare head recovery count, training PPL, and outside-zone drift (H0–H8) to confirm corpus drives Phenomena 1/2.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** Does surgical reinitialization effectively repair collapsed heads in larger BLOOM models (3B, 7.1B, 176B)? The technique is only demonstrated on BLOOM-1b7; scalability and potential architectural differences remain untested.
- **Open Question 2:** Can the surgical technique repair attention collapse in models with non-ALiBi positional encodings (e.g., RoPE, absolute embeddings)? The band‑pattern collapse is tied to ALiBi’s slope schedule; other architectures may exhibit different collapse mechanisms.
- **Open Question 3:** Which specific corpus properties (e.g., domain diversity, repetitiveness, formatting) determine whether post-surgical redistribution improves or degrades model quality? The comparison between curated and C4 corpora confounds multiple variables.
- **Open Question 4:** How many distinct stable attention configurations exist within a transformer’s loss landscape, and can we navigate between them without reinitialization? Reinitialization escapes local minima, but alternative gradient‑based navigation methods are unknown.

## Limitations

- The exact token count and preprocessing of the curated corpus are unspecified, hindering faithful replication and potentially biasing results toward its domain.
- Learning‑rate schedule details for Pass 2 (whether it resets or continues from Pass 1 decay) are ambiguous, possibly affecting recovery speed.
- Generalizability beyond BLOOM‑1b7 is unproven; other ALiBi scales or architectures might show different collapse patterns or surgical sensitivities.
- The transient training‑perplexity improvement may reflect overfitting to the curated corpus rather than escaping a poor local minimum; validation on downstream tasks is lacking.
- Iatrogenic drift (Phenomenon 2) indicates the technique is sensitive to noisy training data, limiting robustness.

## Confidence

- **BOS‑sink collapse exists in ALiBi models:** High (empirical across four scales, bimodal distribution robust).
- **Collapse pattern predictable from slope schedule:** Medium (strong correlation but not causal proof; training dynamics may contribute).
- **Surgical reinitialization recovers collapsed heads:** High (98.7% recovery in BLOOM‑1b7, two‑pass validated).
- **Global redistribution via residual stream:** Medium (head drift observed, but mechanisms and stability depend on corpus).
- **Transient outperformance on training PPL:** Medium (short‑lived, overfitting risk; no downstream validation).

## Next Checks

1. **Cross‑model replication:** Run the diagnostic on BLOOM‑3b and BLOOM‑7b1 to verify the sick band (steep‑slope head indices) and collapse prevalence, testing generality of the slope‑based prediction.
2. **Escape‑strategy ablation:** Compare full Q/K/V reinitialization against alternatives (Gaussian noise injection, partial reinit, SAM) to confirm a complete reset is necessary and sufficient to escape the BOS‑sink attractor.
3. **Generalization test:** Perform two‑pass surgery on a held‑out, diverse corpus (e.g., The Pile) and evaluate both head recovery and downstream zero‑shot accuracy (HellaSwag, MMLU) to determine whether benefits persist beyond training perplexity and to detect iatrogenic effects.
