# StaTS: Spectral Trajectory Schedule Learning for Adaptive Time Series Forecasting with Frequency Guided Denoiser
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00037
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00037
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** StaTS reduces CRPS by 10.67–17.43% versus the prior best diffusion method (NsDiff) on eight multivariate benchmarks, while also improving MAE and demonstrating robustness with fewer sampling steps.

## Executive Summary
StaTS is a diffusion-based time series forecasting model that addresses a key limitation: fixed noise schedules in prior work do not align with the model's denoising capability across steps and variables. It introduces two novel components—a Spectral Trajectory Scheduler (STS) that learns a data-adaptive noise trajectory with spectral regularization, and a Frequency Guided Denoiser (FGD) that estimates schedule-induced spectral distortion to modulate the denoising process. A two-stage alternating training procedure stabilizes the coupling between these components. Extensive evaluation on multivariate benchmarks shows consistent probabilistic and point forecasting improvements, validating the necessity of both adaptive scheduling and frequency-aware denoising.

## Method Summary
StaTS operates within a conditional diffusion framework. First, instance normalization aligns the history and target window distributions. The Spectral Trajectory Scheduler (STS) parameterizes the noise schedule β(t) via a lightweight MLP and optimizes it with spectral regularization objectives (terminal spectral flatness, smooth progression, and barrier penalties) alongside a forecasting loss using a frozen denoiser. The Frequency Guided Denoiser (FGD) processes the noisy target; it computes spectral distortion from a schedule-corrupted history, generates a gating signal, and uses it to modulate the noisy input before denoising via a backbone network. The final forecast fuses a deterministic frequency-filtered estimate and the diffusion output. Training proceeds in two stages: alternating updates between STS and FGD followed by a convergence phase with a fixed schedule.

## Key Results
- Reduces CRPS by 10.67–17.43% compared to NsDiff across eight multivariate datasets.
- Improves point accuracy (MAE) and maintains robustness even with fewer diffusion sampling steps (e.g., T=10).
- Ablations confirm that removing either spectral regularization (STS) or frequency guidance (FGD) significantly degrades performance, demonstrating the necessity of both components.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1  
- **Claim:** The Spectral Trajectory Scheduler (STS) learns a data-adaptive noise schedule that produces intermediate noisy states with improved stepwise invertibility and a terminal state with controlled spectral properties, addressing the mismatch between fixed schedules and denoising capability.  
- **Mechanism:** STS parameterizes the variance schedule β(t) via a lightweight MLP and optimizes it with spectral regularization objectives: a terminal spectral flatness loss (Lend), a smooth spectral progression loss (Lprog), barrier and smoothness penalties (Lbar, Lsmooth), and a forecasting objective (Lobj) using a frozen denoiser.  
- **Core assumption:** Spectral flatness (geometric/arithmetic mean power ratio) correlates with the denoisability of a noisy state; a spectrally flatter terminal state aligns better with the diffusion assumption of near-pure noise, and a smooth spectral evolution across steps enhances stepwise separability.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]: "Spectral Trajectory Scheduler (STS) that learns a data adaptive noise schedule with spectral regularization to improve structural preservation and stepwise invertibility."  
  - [section 3.1.1]: Details the spectral flatness objective (Lprog) and endpoint objectives (Lend, Linit) to shape the forward corruption trajectory.  
  - [corpus]: Corpus shows related work on adaptive schedules (e.g., ANT) and spectral considerations (e.g., FALDA), but StaTS uniquely combines schedule learning with explicit spectral regularization for trajectory shaping.  
- **Break condition:** If spectral regularization weights (λs) are misconfigured or the schedule collapses (e.g., β(t) near 0 or 1), the forward process may still produce degenerate intermediate states, failing to improve invertibility or terminal properties.

### Mechanism 2  
- **Claim:** The Frequency Guided Denoiser (FGD) estimates schedule-induced spectral distortion to modulate denoising strength per diffusion step and variable, enabling heterogeneous restoration of frequency components corrupted by the learned schedule.  
- **Mechanism:** FGD generates a corrupted history ct under the same schedule as the noisy target xt, computes the spectral distortion rt as the clipped magnitude difference between rFFT(ct) and rFFT(c0), maps it to a gating signal gt via an MLP and sigmoid, and uses gt to element-wise modulate the noisy target before denoising.  
- **Core assumption:** The spectral distortion between clean and schedule-corrupted history windows reveals how much frequency-specific information has been degraded at step t; this distortion can guide the denoiser to allocate recovery effort non-uniformly across frequency bands and variables.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]: "Frequency Guided Denoiser (FGD) that estimates schedule induced spectral distortion and uses it to modulate denoising strength for heterogeneous restoration across diffusion steps and variables."  
  - [section 3.1.2]: Equations (22)–(24) define the spectral distortion estimation and gating mechanism.  
  - [corpus]: Related papers like FALDA use Fourier-adaptive mechanisms, but StaTS explicitly ties distortion to the *learned schedule* and uses it for step/variable-specific modulation, which is not present in corpus neighbors.  
- **Break condition:** If the spectral distortion estimation is inaccurate (e.g., due to poor schedule alignment or noise in rt computation), the gating signal gt may misrepresent actual corruption, leading to under- or over-correction in specific frequency bands and degraded denoising performance.

### Mechanism 3  
- **Claim:** The two-stage alternating training strategy stabilizes the coupling between schedule learning and denoiser optimization, preventing optimization pathologies from jointly updating both components.  
- **Mechanism:** Stage One performs k epochs of alternating updates: with β(t) fixed, FGD is trained; with FGD frozen, STS is optimized to refine β(t). Stage Two freezes the learned β(t) and trains FGD to convergence under the stationary forward process.  
- **Core assumption:** The schedule β(t) and denoiser parameters θ are highly interdependent; naive joint optimization risks oscillations or divergence because updates to β(t) change the forward distribution q(xt|x0), which in turn changes the denoising target distribution for θ. Alternating updates allow each module to adapt to a relatively stable counterpart.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract]: "A two stage training procedure stabilizes the coupling between schedule learning and denoiser optimization."  
  - [section 3.2]: Describes the alternating k-epoch updates and the subsequent convergence phase, with Theorem 3.1 (PGD convergence for schedule updates) and Theorem 3.2 (bounded forward drift under schedule perturbations).  
  - [corpus]: Corpus contains no direct analog of this two-stage decoupled training for diffusion schedule learning in time series; most related work either fixes the schedule or jointly optimizes without explicit alternation.  
- **Break condition:** If the alternation frequency (k) is too low, the schedule may not sufficiently adapt to the current denoiser; if too high, the denoiser may overfit to a drifting forward process. Stage Two may also fail if the learned schedule from Stage One is poor, leading to a suboptimal stationary distribution for FGD.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) for Time Series**  
  - *Why needed here:* StaTS builds on the conditional diffusion framework where a forward noising process corrupts the target series and a learned denoiser reverses it. Understanding the Markovian forward process, reverse parameterization, and the x0-prediction objective is essential to grasp how STS modifies the schedule and how FGD conditions on the noisy state.  
  - *Quick check question:* What is the role of the cumulative product ¯αt in determining the signal-to-noise ratio at diffusion step t?  

- **Concept: Spectral Analysis of Time Series (FFT, Spectral Flatness)**  
  - *Why needed here:* Both STS and FGD operate explicitly in the frequency domain. STS regularizes the spectral flatness of forward states; FGD estimates spectral distortion via rFFT. Understanding one-sided FFT, power spectrum, and spectral flatness statistic is key to following the spectral regularization and gating mechanisms.  
  - *Quick check question:* How does spectral flatness quantify the "flatness" of a power spectrum, and why might a flatter spectrum be less informative for denoising?  

- **Concept: Instance Normalization for Time Series Distribution Shift**  
  - *Why needed here:* StaTS applies instance normalization using history window statistics to align the distributions of history and target windows, mitigating amplitude shifts. This is a core preprocessing step before diffusion and affects the spectral computations in both STS and FGD.  
  - *Quick check question:* Why is it necessary to invert the instance normalization after denoising, and what would happen if the history statistics were inaccurate?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input (c0, x0) -> Instance Normalization -> STS (computes β(t) and forward corruption to xt) -> FGD: [Corrupt history ct] -> [Estimate spectral distortion rt] -> [Gate xt to ˜xt] -> Denoiser backbone (FiLM-conditioned) -> ˆxdiff_0; Parallel path: rFFT(c0) -> Frequency gating & multi-band reweighting -> ˆxfreq_0 -> Fusion (ω) -> Final forecast ˆx0 -> Loss (Lobj).
- **Critical path:**  
  1. **Stage One (Alternating):** For k epochs:  
     a. Freeze STS, optimize FGD (including fusion weight ω) with Lobj using current β(t).  
     b. Freeze FGD, optimize STS parameters (MLP f) with L_STS (Eq. 13), which includes Lobj evaluated on frozen FGD.  
  2. **Stage Two (Convergence):** Freeze STS, optimize FGD to convergence with fixed β(t).
- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - **Spectral regularization vs. forecasting objective:** Heavy λ_end/λ_prog may force spectral properties that conflict with minimizing Lobj; tuning λs (Table 6) is needed.  
  - **Fusion weight ω:** Balances deterministic (frequency-filtered) and stochastic (diffusion) pathways; too high ω may reduce diversity, too low may lose structural fidelity.  
  - **Computational cost vs. step count:** More diffusion steps T improve quality but increase inference time; STS mitigates this by making states more invertible, allowing fewer steps (Table 3).
- **Failure signatures:**  
  - **High CRPS but reasonable MAE:** Poor uncertainty calibration; likely FGD's spectral gating is misaligned (e.g., rt not reflecting actual corruption).  
  - **Unstable training loss oscillations:** Indicates poor coupling between STS and FGD; may need more Stage One alternation or smaller learning rates.  
  - **Spectral flatness not decreasing along forward trajectory:** STS regularization failed; check λ_prog and β(t) initialization.  
  - **Instance normalization inversion artifacts:** History statistics mean/std are outlier-sensitive; check for extreme values in c0.
- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Ablation study (Table 4):** Remove EPO (Linit/Lend), SDE (spectral distortion estimation), STS (fixed linear schedule), or IN. Observe CRPS/MAE drops, confirming necessity.  
  2. **Schedule comparison (Fig. 6):** Test Linear, Cosine, Quadratic templates with/without STS optimization. STS improves all, reducing sensitivity to initialization.  
  3. **Diffusion step sensitivity (Table 3):** Evaluate CRPS for T ∈ {10,20,50,100} with fixed linear vs. STS. STS shows largest gains at small T, validating improved invertibility.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question:** How can diffusion models for time series inference be made both efficient and adaptive without sacrificing accuracy?  
  *Basis in paper:* Conclusion: "Future work will develop more efficient inference and step adaptive strategies while preserving forecasting quality."  
  *Why unresolved:* Existing methods require many fixed sampling steps, causing high computational overhead and inflexibility to data-specific dynamics.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Benchmark results across multiple datasets with varying diffusion steps T and adaptive timestep policies, reporting CRPS/MAE relative to compute cost (e.g., FLOPs or wall‑clock time).

- **Question:** Can spectral trajectory scheduling and frequency‑guided denoising generalize to irregularly sampled or variable‑length time series?  
  *Basis in paper:* The current pipeline assumes regular intervals, uses rFFT on fixed‑length windows, and applies instance normalization aligned with the history window (Section 3.1.2).  
  *Why unresolved:* Irregular sampling breaks Fourier transform assumptions and may corrupt spectral distortion estimation; variable lengths prevent consistent feature extraction.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Extend FGD to handle missing timestamps (e.g., via Lomb‑Scargle periodograms) and test on irregular‑interval datasets (e.g., medical records), comparing CRPS and calibration against baselines.

- **Question:** What theoretical relationship links spectral flatness regularization to the invertibility and denoising performance of diffusion trajectories?  
  *Basis in paper:* STS includes spectral objectives (Lend, Lprog) to shape the forward process, but no formal analysis connects these metrics to reverse‑process error or stepwise separability.  
  *Why unresolved:* Without a provable link, hyperparameters (λs) are chosen by validation only; the approach may not transfer to datasets with different spectral characteristics.  
  *What evidence would resolve it:* Derive bounds between spectral flatness (e.g., KL to uniform) and the KL divergence between forward distributions under different schedules; corroborate with controlled experiments varying λs and measuring CRPS/MSE correlation with spectral metrics.

## Limitations
- **Spectral flatness as denoisability proxy:** Core assumption lacks theoretical derivation or external validation; may not generalize beyond evaluated datasets.  
- **Spectral distortion estimation (rt):** Relies on schedule-corrupted history ct; sensitivity to noise in rFFT or misaligned schedules could corrupt gating signal gt.  
- **Two-stage training hyperparameters:** Alternation frequency k and λ regularization weights require manual tuning (Table 6); optimal values may vary across domains.

## Confidence
- **STS spectral regularization efficacy:** Medium (strong empirical gains but theoretical grounding is paper-specific).  
- **FGD frequency-guided modulation:** Medium (novel mechanism; ablation supports necessity but alternative frequency metrics untested).  
- **Training stability via alternation:** Medium-High (theoretical convergence hints + empirical stability, but no ablation of alternation frequency k).
