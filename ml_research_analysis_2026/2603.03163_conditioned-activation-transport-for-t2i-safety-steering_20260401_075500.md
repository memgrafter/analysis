# Conditioned Activation Transport for T2I Safety Steering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.03163  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03163  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: CAT reduces attack success rate from ~34% to ~7% on two T2I models while preserving CLIP image similarity scores (~0.33).

## Executive Summary
Text-to-image models can generate unsafe content, and existing activation steering methods often degrade image quality for benign prompts. This work introduces the SafeSteerDataset, a contrastive set of 2,300 safe-unsafe prompt pairs with high semantic similarity, and Conditioned Activation Transport (CAT). CAT employs a nonlinear MLP transport map and geometry-aware conditioning via Mahalanobis distance to apply steering only when activations lie within unsafe regions. Evaluated on Z-Image and Infinity models, CAT lowers the Attack Success Rate from 33.91% to 6.96% and from 31.74% to 4.78% respectively, while maintaining CLIP scores around 0.33. Linear baselines either fail to suppress toxicity or cause severe quality loss, highlighting the necessity of nonlinear, conditioned interventions for safe generation.

## Method Summary
The method comprises two main phases. First, the SafeSteerDataset is constructed to provide paired prompts that are semantically similar but differ in safety, enabling precise learning of activation differences. Second, CAT learns a transport map—a nonlinear MLP—that transforms activations from an unsafe distribution to a safer region. Critically, steering is conditioned on the Mahalanobis distance of a prompt’s activations to the unsafe manifold; only activations that lie close to this manifold are altered. This conditional approach prevents unnecessary modifications for benign prompts, thereby preserving image fidelity.

## Key Results
- On Z-Image: ASR reduced from 33.91% to 6.96% with CLIP score ~0.33.  
- On Infinity: ASR reduced from 31.74% to 4.78% with CLIP score ~0.32.  
- Linear steering baselines either fail to suppress toxicity or severely degrade image quality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CAT succeeds because it combines a nonlinear transport map with geometry-aware conditioning. The MLP captures complex transformations between unsafe and safe activation distributions, while Mahalanobis distance accurately identifies when a prompt’s activations are near the unsafe region. Steering is applied only in those cases, so benign prompts remain unaffected. This targeted intervention maintains output quality and effectively reduces generation of unsafe content.

## Foundational Learning
- **Activation Steering**: Directly modifying model activations to influence outputs; needed to avoid costly retraining.  
  *Quick check*: Does a targeted activation change produce the expected output shift?  
- **Contrastive Dataset**: Pairs of safe/unsafe prompts with high semantic similarity; needed to isolate safety-related activation differences from content variations.  
  *Quick check*: Do human annotators agree the pairs differ mainly in safety?  
- **Mahalanobis Distance**: Distribution-aware metric accounting for covariance; needed to measure proximity to the unsafe manifold robustly.  
  *Quick check*: Does the distance metric reflect the underlying covariance structure?  
- **Nonlinear Transport Map**: MLP that maps activation distributions; needed to model complex, nonlinear shifts that linear methods cannot.  
  *Quick check*: Does the MLP outperform a linear baseline on validation ASR?  
- **Conditional Intervention**: Applying changes only under specific conditions; needed to limit steering to unsafe regions and preserve benign generation.  
  *Quick check*: Are false positives (unnecessary steering) minimized on a held-out set of safe prompts?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Dataset → Transport Training → Activation Extractor → Mahalanobis Condition → Nonlinear Transport → Image Generation

Critical path:  
1. Collect activation differences from SafeSteerDataset.  
2. Train MLP transport map to map unsafe activations toward safe ones.  
3. For a new prompt: extract activations, compute Mahalanobis distance to unsafe distribution.  
4. If distance below threshold, apply transport map to modify activations.  
5. Generate image from (possibly modified) activations.

Design tradeoffs:  
- Nonlinearity vs. computational overhead: MLP increases expressivity but adds latency.  
- Conditioning sensitivity: Threshold choice trades false positives (quality loss) against false negatives (unsafe outputs).  
- Dataset quality: High semantic similarity in pairs is crucial; noisy or dissimilar pairs hurt generalization.

Failure signatures:  
- Significant CLIP score drop on safe prompts (over-steering).  
- ASR remains high on unsafe prompts (insufficient transport).  
- Training loss decreases but validation performance diverges (overfitting).

First experiments:  
1. Compare CAT against linear steering baselines (e.g., activation addition) on ASR and CLIP score.  
2. Ablate Mahalanobis conditioning by using a fixed threshold to measure its impact on safety-quality tradeoff.  
3. Vary MLP architecture (depth, width) to assess the role of nonlinear capacity.

## Limitations
- Model scope: Only Z-Image and Infinity evaluated; generalizability to other T2I models unknown.  
- Evaluation metrics: ASR relies on an undescribed safety classifier; CLIP score may not fully capture image quality; no human evaluation.  
- Dataset transparency: SafeSteerDataset construction and semantic similarity measurement lack detail; diversity of unsafe concepts unclear.

## Confidence
- [Medium‑High] CAT significantly reduces ASR (~34% → ~7%) while preserving image fidelity (CLIP ~0.33) on two models.  
- [Medium] Linear steering baselines fail to balance safety and quality.  
- [Medium] SafeSteerDataset provides high‑quality contrastive prompt pairs.

## Next Checks
