# STAIRS-Former: Spatio-Temporal Attention with Interleaved Recursive Structure Transformer for Offline Multi-task Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.11691
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11691
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: STAIRS-Former achieves state-of-the-art performance on SMAC and SMAC-v2 benchmarks, with substantial gains in unseen tasks (e.g., 24% relative improvement on SMAC-v2).

## Executive Summary
Offline multi-task multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) faces challenges in modeling inter-agent interactions and long-horizon dependencies under partial observability and dataset bias. STAIRS-Former introduces a novel transformer-based architecture that combines three key innovations: a spatial recursive transformer that iteratively refines entity relations with shared parameters, a dual-timescale temporal module that separately captures immediate and long-term history, and token dropout to enhance generalization to varying agent populations. Evaluated on standard MARL benchmarks (SMAC, SMAC-v2, MPE, MaMuJoCo), STAIRS-Former significantly outperforms prior state-of-the-art methods, particularly in unseen tasks, demonstrating robust generalization and effective spatio-temporal attention.

## Method Summary
STAIRS-Former processes each agent's local observation into entity tokens (own, allied, enemy) and two history tokens: a low-level history updated every step and a high-level history updated every \(T_H=3\) steps via a GRU. The spatial backbone is a two-layer recursive transformer; each layer applies a shared transformer \(\nu_l\) times to refine token representations, using separate feed-forward networks for entity and history tokens. These history tokens are integrated into the spatial attention to capture temporal context. During training, token dropout randomly removes entity tokens (except own and history) to simulate varying agent counts, reducing overfitting. The transformer outputs per-agent Q-values, which are aggregated by Qatten into a global Q-value. This design enables adaptive coordination and robustness in offline, multi-task settings.

## Key Results
- On SMAC, STAIRS-Former achieves a total mean win rate of 67.4% (seen: 77.9%, unseen: 64.0%) versus HiSSD's 57.2% (+10.2 pp).
- On SMAC-v2, it attains 30.3% mean, a 24% relative improvement over HiSSD (24.4%).
- It also outperforms on MPE Cooperative Navigation (51.3 vs 49.1) and MaMuJoCo HalfCheetah (129% relative improvement over ODIS).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Spatial recursion through shared-parameter iterative refinement captures richer inter-entity correlations than a single transformer layer.
- **Mechanism:** Each layer `l` applies a shared transformer `f(·;θ_l)` recursively `ν_l` times. The state `z_l^{j+1} = f(z_l^j + z^{l-1}; θ_l)` injects previous-layer context at each recursion step, allowing the same layer weights to iteratively refine relational reasoning. This effectively increases model depth for entity interactions without linearly increasing parameters.
- **Core assumption:** Iterative application of the same transformer layer improves feature separability for diverse agent relationships more efficiently than stacking independent layers, especially under varying population sizes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1] Describes recursive state update: `z_{l}^{j+1} = f(z_{l}^{j} + z^{l-1}; θ_{l})`.
  - [section 3] Shows prior methods (HiSSD/ODIS) use UPDeT with only `depth=1`, leading to uniform attention (Fig. 2). STAIRS uses `M=2` layers with recursion (`ν_1=1`, `ν_2=1` as default).
- **Break condition:** If recursive steps (`ν_l`) exceed an optimal threshold, performance degrades due to over-smoothing or gradient instability, or if shared recursion fails to capture heterogenous relations seen in unseen tasks.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Dual-timescale temporal hierarchy (step-wise low-level + periodic high-level GRU) jointly captures immediate dependencies and long-horizon history under partial observability.
- **Mechanism:** At each step, the transformer input includes two history vectors: `h_i^{L,t-1}` (updated every step from output) and `h_i^{H,t-1}` (updated every `T_H` steps via GRU `g(·;ψ)`). The GRU compresses `h_i^{L}` into `h_i^{H}` at intervals, enabling long-term summarization while maintaining short-term reactiveness.
- **Core assumption:** A single history token (as in UPDeT) is insufficient for MARL’s long horizons; separate buffers for fine and coarse temporal scales prevent interference between immediate tactical and strategic information.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2] Details update rules: `hi,L_t = z_sp[-2,:]` and `hi,H_t = g(hi,H_{t-1}, hi,L_t; ψ)` only when `t mod T_H == 0`.
  - [abstract] States prior methods rely on a single history token, limiting long-horizon modeling.
- **Break condition:** If `T_H` is too large, the high-level history becomes stale; if too small, the hierarchy collapses to a single timescale and loses long-range compression benefits.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Token dropout (dropping embeddings of some entities except own/history) mitigates overfitting to fixed agent counts, improving generalization to unseen population sizes.
- **Mechanism:** During training, entity tokens (`e_i^{own}`, `e_i^{oa}`, `e_i^{en}`) are randomly zeroed with probability `p_drop`, except for `e_i^{own}` and both history tokens. This exposes the model to variable-length inputs, forcing it to rely on robust features rather than spurious correlations with specific entity counts.
- **Core assumption:** Offline multi-task datasets cover only a subset of possible agent populations; stochastic removal during training simulates unseen configurations, enhancing out-of-distribution robustness.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.3] Specifies dropped tokens: "each entity embedding... randomly dropped with probability p_drop, except for: (1) the agent’s own entity, (2) both history tokens."
  - [table 5] Ablation "w/o Dropout" shows degraded unseen-task performance (64.0% → 58.7% total mean), indicating dropout’s role in generalization.
- **Break condition:** If `p_drop` is too high, critical spatial cues are lost; if too low, overfitting persists. The exception for `e_i^{own}` is critical; dropping it destabilizes learning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Partially Observable Decentralized POMDPs (Dec-POMDP)**
  - **Why needed here:** STAIRS-Former operates in cooperative MARL where each agent only sees local observations and must infer global state. Temporal modules address partial observability.
  - **Quick check question:** Why can’t a single history token suffice for long-horizon dependencies in Dec-POMDPs?
- **Concept: Transformer attention as dynamic routing**
  - **Why needed here:** STAIRS repurposes attention to selectively aggregate information from entities (spatial) and history (temporal). Understanding softmax-based weighting is key to interpreting attention maps.
  - **Quick check question:** How does recursive application of the same transformer layer differ from stacking independent layers in expressiveness?
- **Concept: Offline RL distribution shift**
  - **Why needed here:** The method trains on fixed offline datasets. Token dropout and conservative TD3+BC loss (eq. 7) address out-of-distribution actions and unseen agent counts.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is token dropout particularly helpful for varying agent populations, whereas standard dropout in vision models targets pixel correlations?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. Spatial-Former: Recursive transformer layers (M=2) with separate FFNs for entity vs. history tokens (Eq. 5).
  2. Temporal Module: Low-level history (h^L) updated per step; high-level history (h^H) via GRU every T_H=3 steps.
  3. Token Dropout: Randomly zero entity tokens (except self/history) during training.
  4. Output head: Reads z_sp and produces per-agent Q-values; Qatten mixes them into global Q.
- **Critical path:** Agent observation o_i → entity tokens e_i + [h_i^L, h_i^H] → Spatial-Former (recursive layers + dual FFNs) → new h_i^L (from output) & GRU update for h_i^H → per-agent Q → Qatten → global Q.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Recursion depth (ν_l) vs. parameter efficiency: more recursion improves relational modeling but risks overfitting.
  - T_H interval: larger values compress longer history but risk staleness; smaller values increase GRU updates.
  - Dropout rate p_drop: higher improves generalization to unseen agent counts but may drop critical ally/enemy tokens.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Uniform attention across tokens (as in Fig. 2) → spatial recursion insufficient or depth too shallow.
  - Poor unseen-task performance despite strong seen-task results → dropout not applied or too low; or temporal hierarchy missing.
  - GRU history updates not reflected in attention → temporal module disconnected or dual FFNs interfering.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablation on SMAC-Marine-Hard: Remove spatial recursion → -7.5% seen mean (77.9% → 72.4%); remove temporal → -4.1% unseen mean (64.0% → 60.6%); remove dropout → -5.3% unseen mean (64.0% → 58.7%). Validates each component’s role.
  2. Temporal attention visualization (Fig. 5): Compare attention maps across timesteps; expect dynamic shifts to tokens (enemies, allies, history) corresponding to tactical behaviors (focus-fire, kiting). HiSSD shows uniform attention.
  3. Generalization to SMAC-v2: Test on more stochastic, diverse unit compositions. STAIRS should show relative improvement over HiSSD >20%, confirming robustness to environment stochasticity beyond SMAC-v1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the mechanism by which token dropout improves generalization to unseen agent populations?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Token dropout is introduced as a heuristic but not analyzed.
- **Why unresolved:** It could be due to regularization or data augmentation; no controlled study isolates its effect.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablate dropout rates, compare with other augmentations, and vary training agent counts.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Could a deeper non-recursive transformer with comparable parameters match STAIRS performance?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] STAIRS uses recursive updates for depth; baselines are shallow; no deeper non-recursive baseline tested.
- **Why unresolved:** Without such a comparison, it's unclear whether recursion or simply increased capacity yields the gains.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Train a non-recursive transformer with matching parameter count; compare on seen and unseen tasks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the hierarchical temporal module influence spatial attention patterns?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The temporal module aids long-horizon dependencies but its interaction with spatial attention is unstudied.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not examine whether history tokens modulate attention over entities or how T_H affects spatial focus.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Visualize attention conditioned on high versus low-level history; ablate each timescale and analyze spatial token weighting.

## Limitations
- Domain specificity: Results are concentrated on SMAC/SMAC-v2 and two other MARL benchmarks; transferability to vastly different dynamics (e.g., physical robotics, competitive games) is unknown.
- Task scope: Evaluation targets cooperative tasks; efficacy in competitive or mixed-sum multi-agent settings is unexplored.
- Optimal hyperparameters: The chosen defaults (e.g., ν_l=1, T_H=3, p_drop) are not extensively swept across all tasks; their robustness to environment changes is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Performance on reported benchmarks:** High (clear SOTA results with extensive ablations).
- **Mechanisms driving generalization:** Medium (ablations support each component's role, but causal links to unseen-task gains rely on correlated benchmark performance; alternative explanations not fully ruled out).
- **Generalization to unseen agent counts:** Medium (token dropout ablation shows benefit, but only on SMAC tasks with moderate count variations; generalization to orders-of-magnitude differences or new unit types unvalidated).

2. Attention Analysis in Unseen Tasks: For an unseen SMAC-v2 map, visualize spatial and temporal attention maps at critical decision points.
