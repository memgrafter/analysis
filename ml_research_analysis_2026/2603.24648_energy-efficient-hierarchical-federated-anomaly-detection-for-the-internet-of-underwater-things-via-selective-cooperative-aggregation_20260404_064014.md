# Energy-Efficient Hierarchical Federated Anomaly Detection for the Internet of Underwater Things via Selective Cooperative Aggregation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.24648
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24648
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Hierarchical federated learning with selective cooperation and gradient compression enables full network participation and 31–33% energy reduction over always-on cooperation while matching detection accuracy.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of training accurate anomaly detection models in underwater IoT networks where acoustic communication is low-bandwidth and energy-intensive, often preventing direct sensor-to-surface links. Standard flat federated learning fails due to expensive long-range transmissions and reduced sensor participation. The authors propose an energy-efficient hierarchical federated learning framework that uses feasibility-aware sensor-to-fog association, compressed model-update transmission, and selective cooperative aggregation among fog nodes. Results show that hierarchical methods preserve full participation through feasible fog paths, while selective cooperation reduces fog-to-fog energy by 31–33% with negligible accuracy loss. Gradient compression further cuts total energy by 71–95%, making the approach practical for resource-constrained underwater deployments.

## Method Summary
The framework employs a three-tier hierarchy: sensors, mid-water fog nodes, and a surface gateway. Each round, sensors are associated with the nearest fog node that meets a feasibility criterion based on acoustic SNR and a maximum source level constraint. Sensors locally train an autoencoder for anomaly detection and compress their model updates using Top‑K sparsification (5% retention) and 8‑bit quantisation, with error feedback to mitigate bias. Fog nodes aggregate updates from their associated sensors and optionally cooperate with neighboring fog nodes if selective conditions are met (small clusters borrow from larger nearby ones). The gateway then performs a global aggregation and broadcasts the updated model. Formal optimisation jointly minimises energy consumption and maximises detection quality under participation and latency constraints.

## Key Results
- Hierarchical federated learning achieves nearly 100% sensor participation compared to ~48% in flat FL (FedProx) at scale (N=200) by leveraging feasible multi‑hop paths.
- Selective cooperation between fog nodes yields detection accuracy (PA‑F1) statistically indistinguishable from always‑on cooperation while reducing fog‑to‑fog energy by 31–33%.
- Gradient compression (Top‑K + 8‑bit) reduces total communication energy by 71–95% across all federated learning variants with minimal impact on anomaly detection performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Feasibility‑aware sensor‑to‑fog association
- **Claim:** Hierarchical association based on acoustic link feasibility preserves network participation by enabling sensors without direct gateway reachability to contribute via intermediate fog nodes.
- **Mechanism:** Sensors are statically associated to the nearest fog node for which the achievable SNR under a capped source level meets a target threshold. This forms a feasible communication graph where multi‑hop paths (sensor→fog→gateway) replace long, expensive direct sensor→gateway links.
- **Core assumption:** A feasible fog path exists for nearly all sensors within their transmission range, which holds under the synthetic deployment parameters (deep sensors, mid‑water fogs).
- **Break condition:** If a sensor has no feasible fog link in a given round (possible with dynamic fog mobility or extreme interference), it becomes inactive, participation drops.

### Mechanism 2: Gradient compression via Top‑K sparsification and quantisation
- **Claim:** Gradient compression drastically reduces sensor‑to‑fog upload energy by shrinking payload size, making frequent acoustic transmissions feasible.
- **Mechanism:** Local model updates are sparsified (retaining top‑K% magnitudes) and quantised to 8‑bit. Error feedback accumulates discarded gradients to reduce bias over rounds. The effective payload is a small fraction of full‑precision size.
- **Core assumption:** The autoencoder model's gradient sparsity pattern is stable enough that sparsification does not catastrophically degrade convergence, and error feedback mitigates long‑term bias.
- **Break condition:** If the model requires very high precision or data is extremely non‑IID causing volatile gradients, the compression ratio must be increased (less compression), reducing energy savings.

### Mechanism 3: Selective fog‑to‑fog cooperation
- **Claim:** Activating exchange only for smaller clusters borrowing from nearby larger ones matches the detection accuracy of always‑on fog exchange while reducing its substantial energy cost.
- **Mechanism:** A heuristic determines if a fog node is "small" (size ≤ max{2, 0.75×mean_cluster_size}). If small and a larger neighbor exists within close proximity (within the first quartile of fog‑fog distances), it mixes models (80%‑20% weighting). Otherwise, no cooperation.
- **Core assumption:** Smaller clusters suffer more from statistical heterogeneity (non‑IID data), and borrowing from a larger, more representative nearby cluster mitigates this without needing widespread peer‑to‑peer gossip.
- **Break condition:** If clusters are homogeneous in size/data distribution, the selective rule may trigger cooperation unnecessarily or miss beneficial pairings if the "nearest larger neighbor" heuristic fails in a complex topology.

## Foundational Learning
- **Hierarchical Federated Learning (HFL):** Needed because standard FL assumes all clients can reach a central coordinator, which is acoustically expensive and sometimes impossible underwater. HFL inserts a fog tier to localise communication.  
  *Quick check:* Can your deployment's physical stratification (deep sensors, mid‑water relays) naturally support a three‑tier hierarchy, or would multi‑hop routing create unacceptable delay?
- **Communication Compression for FL:** Needed because acoustic bandwidth is extremely limited; full‑precision model updates would be prohibitively costly. Compression is non‑optional.  
  *Quick check:* Have you profiled your model's gradient sparsity to choose a feasible ρs? Does your error feedback implementation handle stragglers correctly?
- **Unsupervised Anomaly Detection with Autoencoders:** Needed because anomalies are rare and unlabelled; training must use only "normal" operational data. Autoencoders learn to reconstruct normal patterns, with high reconstruction error indicating anomaly.  
  *Quick check:* Is your data predominantly normal during federated training? Have you validated that reconstruction error distributions are separable on a held‑out validation set?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Acoustic Channel Module → Association & Cooperation Engine → FL Training Core (Sensors) → Fog Intra‑cluster Aggregation → (Optional Fog Cooperation) → Gateway Global Aggregation → Metrics Logger.

**Critical path:** Each FL round: (1) Association/cooperation decisions, (2) Sensor local training & compressed upload, (3) Fog intra‑cluster aggregation & optional mixing, (4) Surface global aggregation & broadcast. Energy dominated by step 2; accuracy sensitive to step 3 decisions.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Energy vs. Participation: Flat FL (FedProx) minimizes energy but trains on ≤48% of sensors at scale. HFL increases per‑sensor energy (fog infrastructure) but achieves ~100% participation.
- Cooperation Cost vs. Accuracy: Always‑on fog exchange (HFL‑Nearest) has high energy (~2× HFL‑Selective) with negligible F1 gain. Selective cooperation is the cost‑effective default.
- Compression Ratio (ρs) vs. Convergence: Lower ρs (more compression) saves energy but may slow convergence or require more rounds. The paper uses ρs=0.05; tuning is needed for different models/datasets.

**Failure signatures:**
- Low participation (<80%): Check fog deployment density and SL_max. Sensors may lack any feasible fog link.
- High energy, low F1: If HFL‑Selective underperforms HFL‑Nearest, the selective rule may be too conservative. Inspect cluster size distribution.
- No convergence: Verify compression error feedback is implemented correctly; check for straggler‑induced staleness.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Reachability sanity check:** With N=200, M=10, confirm direct gateway reachability ~48% and feasible fog reachability ~100% (replicating Fig. 5a). Adjust fog depth/density if not.
2. **Compression ablation:** Run one round with/without compression on a small subset (N=20) to measure per‑sensor upload energy reduction. Expect ~95% saving for FedProx.
3. **Cooperation rule validation:** At N=150, compare HFL‑NoCoop, HFL‑Selective, HFL‑Nearest F1 and total energy. Expect: HFL‑Selective F1 ≈ HFL‑Nearest, energy ≈ HFL‑NoCoop + small overhead (~25 J vs 101 J for NoCoop).

## Limitations
- Dynamic topology handling: The fog mobility model parameters (Gauss–Markov speed variance, update frequency) are unreported. The main scalability results likely use static positions, so performance under realistic, time‑varying acoustic link feasibility is unvalidated.
- Real‑data partitioning: The exact method for partitioning SMD/SMAP/MSL benchmarks across sensors and train/val/test ratios are unspecified, impeding exact replication of non‑IID effects.
- Anomaly threshold sensitivity: Post‑training thresholding at the 99th percentile of reconstruction error on normal validation data is mentioned but sensitivity to this choice under acoustic compression artifacts is not explored.

## Confidence
- Hierarchical association preserves participation: Medium
- Compression reduces energy with minimal accuracy loss: High
- Selective cooperation matches always‑on accuracy at lower energy: High
