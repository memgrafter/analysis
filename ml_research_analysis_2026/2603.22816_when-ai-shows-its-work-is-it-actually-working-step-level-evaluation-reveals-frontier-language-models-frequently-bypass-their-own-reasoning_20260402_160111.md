# When AI Shows Its Work, Is It Actually Working? Step-Level Evaluation Reveals Frontier Language Models Frequently Bypass Their Own Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22816
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22816
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** Across 10 frontier models and four tasks, the majority show decorative reasoning (step necessity <17%, sufficiency >60%), meaning explanations are largely confabulated and not causally used.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a step-level intervention method to test whether chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning from large language models is genuinely used or merely decorative. By systematically removing or isolating individual reasoning steps, the authors find that for most frontier models, no single step is necessary for the final answer, yet any single step alone is sufficient. This pattern indicates that the CoT text is a post-hoc confabulation rather than a faithful trace of the model's internal computation. Only a couple of models on simpler tasks exhibit genuinely dependent reasoning. The work highlights a critical gap between model accuracy and explanation faithfulness.

## Method Summary
The evaluation uses a causal probing protocol on four standard datasets (SST-2, GSM8K, AG News, MedQA). For each model, the procedure is: 1) Generate a CoT response via API. 2) Segment the response into sentence-level steps. 3) For each step in examples with ≥2 steps, run three probe types:
- **Necessity:** Remove one step and re-prompt with the rest; check if the final answer changes.
- **Sufficiency:** Re-prompt with only that single step; check if the answer matches the original.
- **Shuffle:** Randomly reorder all steps and re-prompt; check if the answer changes.
Aggregate these across ~500 examples per model-task pair to compute step necessity rate (%), sufficiency rate (%), and shuffle sensitivity (%). The method relies solely on API access, costing ~$1–2 per model per task.

## Key Results
- Most frontier models exhibit **decorative reasoning**: step necessity is very low (mean <17%), while step sufficiency is very high (mean >60%).
- This pattern—low necessity, high sufficiency—indicates that reasoning steps are independent cues, not causally involved in producing the answer.
- Exceptions: MiniMax-M2.5 (on SST-2) and Kimi-K2.5 (on AG News) show higher necessity and lower sufficiency, suggesting more genuine step dependence.
- **Output rigidity** varies widely: some models (e.g., GPT-OSS) frequently omit multi-step CoT entirely, creating an evaluation blind spot.
- **Shuffle sensitivity** is near zero for decorative models but higher for context-dependent ones, offering a third diagnostic dimension.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Step Necessity & Sufficiency as Causal Probes
- **Claim:** Removing a step (necessity) and isolating a step (sufficiency) test whether that textual step causally mediates the final answer.
- **Mechanism:** If reasoning is faithful, a necessary step's removal should change the answer, and a single step should rarely suffice. The observed inverse pattern (low necessity, high sufficiency) implies steps are decorative confabulations.
- **Core assumption:** The final answer is determined at text-generation time, not by a prior hidden state; thus, perturbing the generated CoT text can influence the output.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract definition of metrics; Section 2 formalizes expected patterns; neighbor work ("Stop Rewarding Hallucinated Steps") validates step-level intervention paradigm.

### Mechanism 2: Shuffle Sensitivity for Sequential Dependencies
- **Claim:** Shuffling step order detects whether the model processes steps in a genuinely sequential, dependent manner.
- **Mechanism:** Faithful sequential reasoning (e.g., "add then multiply") should break if steps are reordered. Decorative reasoning treats steps as independent keyword matches; order is irrelevant.
- **Core assumption:** Valid causal chains have a dependency structure where early steps constrain later ones.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 2 introduces shuffle test; Table 1 shows near-zero shuffle for decorative models, higher for context-dependent cases. This is an original contribution with weak neighbor support.

### Mechanism 3: Output Rigidity as Implicit Signal
- **Claim:** A model's tendency to produce multi-step CoT versus single-token answers correlates with whether it engages in step-wise processing.
- **Mechanism:** Models that habitually skip externalized reasoning (e.g., GPT-OSS on MedQA: 62% single-token) likely bypass reasoning internally; the absence of steps is itself evidence of shortcutting.
- **Core assumption:** A model that can answer without CoT has internalized direct question→answer mappings, reducing need for iterative reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4 and Table 3 document vast differences in multi-step rates (Claude ~100% vs Qwen-397B ~9%). No neighbor addresses this as a faithfulness signal.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Faithfulness vs. accuracy
  - **Why needed:** The core finding decouples a model's correctness from whether its stated reasoning is used. High accuracy coexists with unfaithful explanations, undermining trustworthiness.
  - **Quick check:** If removing a key step doesn't change the answer, does that make the model more or less trustworthy? (Less—the step was decorative, so errors in the explanation won't be caught by inspecting it.)
- **Concept:** Causal mediation in natural language generation
  - **Why needed:** The step-probe method is a causal intervention: perturbing the CoT text tests whether that text mediates the outcome.
  - **Quick check:** How is step-removal different from just asking the model to answer without CoT? (It tests whether *that specific step* matters, not whether CoT as a whole helps.)
- **Concept:** Parametric knowledge vs. process reliance
  - **Why needed:** Frontier models may have memorized answer patterns (parametric knowledge) while smaller models must compute step-by-step (process reliance). This explains the scale inversion on math.
  - **Quick check:** Why do small models show higher step necessity on GSM8K than frontier models? (Small models lack parametric shortcuts; they must use the CoT steps.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  CoT Generator -> Step Segmenter -> Step Perturber -> Answer Comparator -> Aggregator
  - **CoT Generator:** Calls model API with fixed CoT prompt; parses response.
  - **Step Segmenter:** Splits response into sentences; filters short ones.
  - **Step Perturber:** For each step, constructs three probe variants: (1) removal (all other steps kept), (2) isolation (only that step), (3) shuffled order (multiple trials).
  - **Answer Comparator:** Re-prompts model with each perturbed prompt; extracts final answer via task-specific parser; compares to original.
  - **Aggregator:** Computes per-example binary outcomes; aggregates into necessity, sufficiency, shuffle sensitivity rates.
- **Critical path:** Generate CoT -> Segment steps -> For n steps, run 2n+3 probe calls -> Extract answers -> Compute rates. Bottleneck: API rate limits and cost; requires sampling N=300–500 examples per task.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Sentence vs. token granularity: Sentence-level is 30× cheaper and captures dominant patterns (83% token-level agreement) but may miss fine-grained faithfulness.
  - Static vs. dynamic thresholds: 30% necessity / 50% sufficiency cutoffs calibrated on open-weight models; may not generalize.
  - Cost vs. coverage: Full run (10 models × 4 tasks × 500 examples × ~15 probes) costs ~$80–$320; prioritize high-stakes tasks.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Model outputs极少multi-step CoT → unevaluable (output rigidity blind spot).
  - Answer extraction errors (e.g., AG News parsing "world" from reasoning) → biased metrics.
  - Temperature >0 → non-deterministic answers → high probe variance.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Sanity check:** Run on Qwen3-0.6B (open-weight) for GSM8K; expect necessity ~55%, sufficiency ~14% (per Table 1).
  2. **Cost scaling:** Measure API calls per example (~2n+3) and compute cost for 100 examples; verify $1–2 per model per task.
  3. **Threshold robustness:** Vary necessity threshold (20–40%) on one model-task pair; ensure taxonomy stability (paper’s Appendix C shows 83%+ agreement at 30%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can we evaluate reasoning faithfulness for models that habitually output only a final answer without step-by-step reasoning?
  - **Basis:** The authors identify an "output rigidity blind spot" and state that probing internal reasoning without external steps remains an open problem.
  - **Why unresolved:** Current step probes require visible CoT; models like GPT-OSS produce single tokens often, making evaluation inapplicable.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** New methods inferring faithfulness from answer-only outputs via internal activations, forced CoT prompts, or proxies trained on models with both internal and external reasoning.
- **Question 2:** Is there an inherent trade‑off between reasoning faithfulness and task accuracy when model capability increases?
  - **Basis:** MiniMax‑M2.5 is relatively faithful but not state‑of‑the‑art; authors propose competing explanations ("faithful reasoning as a tax" vs. "different optimisation target").
  - **Why unresolved:** Observed correlation may stem from uncontrolled training differences; no controlled experiment isolates faithfulness as causal.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Train matched model variants with/without faithfulness‑enhancing losses (e.g., step‑intervention consistency) and compare accuracy and faithfulness.
- **Question 3:** Could extremely low step necessity and sufficiency be explained by genuine multi‑path redundant reasoning rather than confabulation?
  - **Basis:** Authors acknowledge frontier models might have multiple valid pathways; sentence‑level interventions may not detect token‑level path independence.
  - **Why unresolved:** Parallel computation could combine information within sentences or via hidden states, evading sentence‑level probes.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Fine‑grained token‑level causal interventions (ablation, mediation) on open‑weight models, or search for independent sub‑circuits.
- **Question 4:** Do these findings generalize beyond the four English‑language tasks tested?
  - **Basis:** Coverage limited to SST‑2, GSM8K, AG News, MedQA; authors note extending to commonsense, legal, code, multilingual would strengthen generality.
  - **Why unresolved:** Task structure (open‑ended, dynamic) or language differences might force genuine step‑wise reasoning; current data cannot speak to these.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Replicate protocol on diverse tasks (coding, logical deduction, multilingual QA) and analyze whether decorative pattern persists or varies with task properties.

## Limitations
- Threshold generalizability: The 30% necessity / 50% sufficiency cutoffs are calibrated on limited models; may not hold across architectures or tasks.
- Answer extraction robustness: Parser errors (e.g., AG News) can bias metrics; manual validation is needed.
- Output rigidity blind spot: Models that rarely produce multi-step CoT (e.g., GPT-OSS) cannot be evaluated via step probes, potentially missing key shortcuts.

## Confidence
- **Core decorative reasoning finding:** Medium. Consistent across 10 models and 4 tasks, but relies on sentence-level segmentation and threshold generalizability.
- **Method validity (step probes as faithfulness test):** High. Causal intervention design is theoretically sound and aligns with neighbor work.
- **Task-specific exceptions (MiniMax, Kimi):** Medium. Observed only on sentiment/topic classification; unverified on other benchmarks.
- **Output rigidity as a signal:** Low. Correlational; no causal link to internal reasoning established.

## Next Checks
1. **Generalize thresholds to new models/tasks:** Evaluate 5+ additional frontier models (e.g., Llama 3.1, Mistral Large) on 2 new tasks (e.g., logical deduction, commonsense QA). Re-calibrate necessity/sufficiency thresholds per architecture/task if agreement with original taxonomy drops below 80%.
2. **Blind audit of answer extraction:** For each model-task pair, manually verify final answers in 100 random samples. Compute parser error rates; if >5%, re-run step-probe analysis with corrected extractions and report metric changes.
3. **Forced CoT evaluation for high-rigidity models:** For models with <20% multi-step outputs (e.g., GPT-OSS on MedQA), use a strong CoT-inducing prompt (“You must reason step by step in at least 3 sentences”) and recompute necessity/sufficiency. Compare to original to assess whether output rigidity biases the evaluation.
