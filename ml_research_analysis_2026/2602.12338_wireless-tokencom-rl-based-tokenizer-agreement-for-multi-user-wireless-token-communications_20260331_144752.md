# Wireless TokenCom: RL-Based Tokenizer Agreement for Multi-User Wireless Token Communications

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.12338  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12338  
- **Reference count**: 22  
- **Primary result**: A hybrid DQN‑DDPG reinforcement learning framework reduces video freezing by 68% versus H.265 at 1080p with 16 users and achieves ~10 dB higher PSNR than conventional methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the joint optimization of tokenizer agreement, sub‑channel assignment, and beamforming in multi‑user wireless semantic communication (TokenCom). The problem is formulated as a mixed‑integer non‑convex optimization. The proposed solution is a hybrid reinforcement learning framework where a Deep Q‑Network (DQN) handles discrete tokenizer selection and sub‑channel allocation, while a Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) actor manages continuous beamforming. The system learns to match tokenizer compression rates to instantaneous channel conditions, balancing semantic quality against power consumption. Extensive simulations show significant reductions in video freezing and consistent improvements in perceptual quality (PSNR) over baselines across varying user counts and transmit powers.

## Method Summary
The method defines a Markov Decision Process where an agent interacts with a simulated TokenCom environment. At the start of each episode, the DQN selects a tokenizer (compression rate η) for each user. In every time slot, the DQN assigns orthogonal resource blocks (RBs) to users via binary indicators, and the DDPG actor generates beamforming vectors based on the current state (channel estimates, rates, and the chosen η). Two separate replay buffers store transitions for the discrete and continuous parts. The reward combines normalized visual quality and power efficiency, with penalties for violating power, rate, and fairness constraints. Training uses ε‑greedy exploration for DQN and Gaussian noise for DDPG, with soft target‑network updates.

## Key Results
- Video freezing reduced by 68% compared to H.265 baseline at 1080p with 16 users.
- Approximately 10 dB higher PSNR than conventional methods in certain configurations.
- Consistent semantic quality improvements and stable adaptation across varying user counts and transmit powers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The hybrid DQN‑DDPG framework efficiently handles the mixed discrete‑continuous action space by separating tokenizer/sub‑channel decisions (discrete) from beamforming (continuous) while sharing a common state representation.
- **Mechanism**: The DQN selects tokenizer indices once per episode and sub‑channel assignments per slot; the DDPG actor generates beamforming vectors using the current state, which includes the chosen tokenizer’s compression rate η. Both networks train on separate replay buffers but experience the same environment transitions, allowing each to specialize without explicit coordination.
- **Core assumption**: Decoupling is admissible because tokenizer choice is slow‑varying relative to beamforming, and the state’s inclusion of η provides sufficient context for beamforming adaptation.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and Sections III.A, III.D define the action space and separate learning updates.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Adaptive tokenizer agreement, guided by RL, matches the tokenizer’s compression rate to instantaneous channel capacity, minimizing video freezing and maximizing perceptual quality.
- **Mechanism**: The DQN’s tokenizer selection balances compression (low η) against decoded quality (high η). The reward includes a normalized quality term and penalties for constraint violations, driving the policy to choose low‑η tokenizers when rates are insufficient and high‑η when resources allow.
- **Core assumption**: The distortion/perception function q(η) is known and monotonic; the state sufficiently predicts feasibility for a given η.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract reports 68% reduction in freezing; Section II.A explains TA process; constraints (2c) enforce rate ≥ ρηHW; Fig. 1a,b show freezing rates.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Joint optimization of tokenizer agreement, sub‑channel assignment, and beamforming via a unified RL policy uncovers beneficial resource‑quality trade‑offs that isolated approaches miss.
- **Mechanism**: The agent learns a mapping from state to combined actions. The per‑slot utility couples quality (via η) and power, encouraging coordination: more resources (RBs, beamforming gain) go to users with high‑η tokenizers, while low‑η users conserve resources.
- **Core assumption**: The MDP formulation is Markov; the discount factor balances immediate vs. future utility; penalties enforce constraints.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states outperformance; Section I motivates joint optimization; Section II.B defines problem (2a); Fig. 1c,d show PSNR gains.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept 1: Tokenization and Tokenizer Agreement
- **Why needed**: TokenCom uses tokens for semantic communication; transmitter and receiver must share identical tokenizer/de‑tokenizer pairs. Different tokenizers impose different compression rates η, directly affecting wireless resource needs and user experience.
- **Quick check**: How does η influence required transmission rate and achievable video quality? (Higher η requires higher rate to avoid freezing but yields higher decoded quality if delivered.)

### Concept 2: Hybrid Reinforcement Learning for Mixed‑Action Spaces
- **Why needed**: The optimization involves discrete decisions (tokenizer index, RB assignment) and continuous decisions (beamforming vectors). DQN cannot handle continuous actions; DDPG cannot directly handle high‑dimensional discrete actions. The hybrid architecture leverages each method’s strength.
- **Quick check**: Why is a separate DDPG actor not sufficient to output both discrete and continuous actions? (Discretizing continuous beamforming would be prohibitively high‑dimensional; actor‑critic methods are sample‑efficient for continuous high‑dimensional spaces.)

### Concept 3: Wireless Resource Allocation and Rate Constraints
- **Why needed**: Beamforming and sub‑channel assignment determine SINR and achievable rate Rt_i. The selected tokenizer’s η imposes a minimum rate ρηHW; violation causes video freezing. Understanding Shannon capacity, power budgets, and interference is essential.
- **Quick check**: What is the consequence of violating constraint (2c)? (The user’s video stream freezes because token data cannot be delivered in time.)

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Environment -> DQN (tokenizer & RB) & DDPG (beamforming) -> Action execution -> Reward & next state -> Separate replay buffers (B1 for discrete, B2 for continuous) -> Network updates -> DQN & DDPG target networks

### Critical path
1. Episode start: Environment reset; DQN selects tokenizers {mi} (ε‑greedy); environment stores η_mi.
2. Per‑slot loop (t = 1 … T):
   a. Observe state st (channels, power, rates, η_mi).
   b. DQN selects sub‑channel assignment κt (ε‑greedy on st).
   c. DDPG actor generates beamforming wt = πψ(st) + exploration noise.
   d. Environment steps: compute SINR, rates, utility, penalties; return rt and st+1.
   e. Store (st, (mi,κt), rt, st+1) in B1 and (st, wt, rt, st+1) in B2.
   f. If buffers large enough, sample mini‑batches and update DQN (Eq. 5), DDPG critic (Eq. 6), and DDPG actor (Eq. 7); soft‑update target networks.
3. Episode end: Decay ε; repeat.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Separate vs. shared replay buffers**: Separate buffers allow independent training dynamics but risk inconsistency because full actions are never stored jointly. A shared buffer would store full transitions but complicate loss computation.
- **Tokenizer update frequency**: Fixed per‑episode simplifies learning but may not react to fast channel variations; per‑slot tokenizer selection would explode the discrete action space.
- **Reward penalty weighting**: λ_pen controls constraint enforcement; too low leads to violations, too high may dominate utility and impede learning of quality maximization.
- **State design**: Including η_mi is crucial to inform beamforming of tokenizer rate demand; omitting it breaks coupling.
- **Exploration strategies**: DQN uses ε‑greedy; DDPG uses additive noise. Balancing exploration across both networks is non‑trivial.

### Failure signatures
- **Persistently high freezing rate**: Tokenizer selection too aggressive (high η) for channel conditions or RB/beamforming cannot deliver required rates.
- **PSNR lower than Fixed‑TA**: Policy overly conservative, always selecting low‑quality tokenizers, possibly due to over‑penalization or poor exploration.
- **Training instability**: Large oscillations in DQN or DDPG losses, or divergence, often due to high correlation in replay samples, inappropriate learning rates, or mismatched network capacities.
- **Scalability collapse**: Performance degrades sharply as user count U increases, pointing to capacity limits in network architectures or insufficient state representation for multi‑user coordination.
- **Constraint violations after training**: Frequent Rt_i < ρηmiHW at test time implies insufficient penalty λ_pen or failed feasibility learning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1**: Could dynamic tokenizer re‑agreement within a communication episode improve performance under fast fading or time‑varying channel conditions?  
*Basis*: The paper fixes the tokenizer for the entire episode (Algorithm 1, Step 4).  
*Why unresolved*: Fast channel variations may render the initial tokenizer suboptimal for later slots.  
*Resolution evidence*: Compare fixed‑per‑episode vs. periodic tokenizer re‑selection under different channel coherence times.

**Open Question 2**: How does the hybrid DQN‑DDPG algorithm scale with an increasing number of available tokenizers and users?  
*Basis*: Experiments use only 4 tokenizers per user (Table III); DQN action space grows combinatorially.  
*Why unresolved*: No scalability analysis; DQN may suffer from curse of dimensionality.  
*Resolution evidence*: Evaluate training convergence, sample efficiency, and final performance for increasing tokenizer counts (e.g., 4, 16, 64) and users, possibly comparing with hierarchical methods.

**Open Question 3**: What is the impact of imperfect channel state information (CSI) on the proposed framework’s performance?  
*Basis*: State representation includes exact channel vectors (Section III.B), assuming perfect CSI.  
*Why unresolved*: Real‑world CSI is noisy and outdated, which can degrade decisions.  
*Resolution evidence*: Introduce CSI error models (noise, Doppler‑induced outdatedness), quantify performance drops in utility, PSNR, and freezing.

**Open Question 4**: Could constrained reinforcement learning (e.g., CMDPs) improve constraint satisfaction compared to the penalty‑based reward approach?  
*Basis*: Reward includes penalty terms for constraint violations (Section III.C); constrained MDP methods are not explored.  
*Why unresolved*: Tuning penalty coefficients is non‑trivial; penalties may not strictly enforce constraints.  
*Resolution evidence*: Replace penalty reward with a CMDP formulation (e.g., Lagrange multipliers), compare constraint violations and utility, analyze hyperparameter sensitivity.

## Limitations
- **Action‑space implementation unclear**: The paper does not specify how DQN factorizes tokenizer selection or enforces per‑user RB bounds [Kmin, Kmax] when using a top‑κ per RB heuristic.
- **Architectural details omitted**: Network layer sizes, activation functions, learning rates, and exploration schedules for DQN and DDPG are missing, hindering exact replication.
- **State representation limitations**: Excluding video motion statistics and long‑term channel correlations may restrict adaptability; the assumption of a monotonic q(η) could be oversimplified.

## Confidence
- **Hybrid DQN‑DDPG efficiently handles mixed discrete‑continuous actions**: Medium (evidence is primarily conceptual; exact mechanism unspecified).
- **Adaptive tokenizer agreement reduces video freezing by 68% vs. H.265**: High (supported by abstract and consistent Figure 1 results).
- **Joint optimization uncovers beneficial resource‑quality trade‑offs**: Medium (PSNR gains reported, but architectural opacity and simplified state representation temper confidence).
