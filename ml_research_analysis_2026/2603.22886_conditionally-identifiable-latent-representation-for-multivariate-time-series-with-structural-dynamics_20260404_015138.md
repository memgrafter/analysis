# Conditionally Identifiable Latent Representation for Multivariate Time Series with Structural Dynamics
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.22886  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22886  
- Reference count: 40  
- Primary result: iVDFM achieves identifiable latent factors up to permutation and component‑wise affine transformations by conditioning the innovation prior on observed variables and using linear diagonal dynamics, leading to improved factor recovery, stable causal interventions, and competitive probabilistic forecasting.

## Executive Summary
Multivariate time series often contain latent factors that drive observed dynamics. Classical dynamic factor models suffer from rotational ambiguity, making factors unidentifiable and difficult to interpret. This paper proposes the Identifiable Variational Dynamic Factor Model (iVDFM), which overcomes this by applying an iVAE‑style conditioning to the innovation process rather than the latent states. The innovation prior is a conditional exponential family depending on observed exogenous variables and a deterministic regime embedding, which ensures identifiability up to permutation and component‑wise affine transformations. Linear diagonal dynamics preserve this property while enabling scalable computation. Experiments on synthetic data show superior factor recovery compared to baselines; causal intervention tests demonstrate stable and accurate impulse responses; and on real‑world benchmarks (ETT, Weather) iVDFM attains competitive predictive performance while providing an interpretable, interveneable latent representation.

## Method Summary
iVDFM factorizes a multivariate time series into latent factors governed by linear dynamics. The key idea is to model the innovation process ηₜ with a conditional exponential‑family prior p(ηₜ | uₜ, eₜ) where uₜ are observed auxiliary variables (e.g., time or exogenous covariates) and eₜ is a deterministic embedding derived from uₜ via a regime network. The prior is non‑Gaussian (e.g., Laplace) and its natural parameters vary sufficiently across distinct contexts, which pins each innovation component. Factors evolve as fₜ₊₁ = Aₜ fₜ + Bₜ ηₜ with diagonal (or block‑diagonal companion) matrices Aₜ, Bₜ that preserve the component‑wise structure, preventing rotational ambiguity. Observations are generated via an injective decoder g: yₜ = g(fₜ) + εₜ with fixed observation noise variance. Variational inference is used: an encoder q_ϕ(ηₜ | y₁:T, uₜ, eₜ) approximates the posterior; the ELBO is optimized with reparameterization. The overall pipeline: (y₁:T, uₜ) → encoder → sample ηₜ → linear dynamics → fₜ → decoder → yₜ. The generative model’s identifiability guarantees are independent of the variational posterior, allowing flexible encoder design.

## Key Results
- **Synthetic factor recovery**: iVDFM significantly outperforms DFM, DDFM, iVAE, and VAE on Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) and subspace distance, indicating accurate recovery of true latent factors.
- **Causal interventions**: On synthetic structural causal models, iVDFM achieves high sign accuracy and bounded impulse response errors across diverse structures, demonstrating stable intervenability.
- **Probabilistic forecasting**: iVDFM matches or exceeds specialized forecasters on ETT and Weather datasets in terms of CRPS and MSE, while providing an interpretable latent space.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Conditional Innovation Prior**  
Identifiability is achieved by conditioning the innovation prior on observed exogenous variables uₜ and a deterministic regime embedding eₜ, rather than on latent states. The innovation ηₜ follows a conditional exponential‑family distribution p(ηₜ | uₜ, eₜ) with component‑wise sufficient statistics. Variation of the natural parameters λ(uₜ, eₜ) across different contexts ensures that the statistics span a full‑rank subspace, enabling recovery of ηₜ up to permutation and component‑wise affine (or monotone invertible) transformations (iVAE principle). *Evidence*: Abstract; Section 3.1; Appendix A.3.

**Linear Diagonal Dynamics**  
Diagonal (or block‑diagonal companion) dynamics preserve the identifiability inherited from the innovations. Because fₜ₊₁ = Aₜ fₜ + Bₜ ηₜ operates component‑wise without cross‑factor mixing, the equivalence class (permutation + component‑wise affine) from ηₜ directly transfers to {fₜ}. Classical DFMs with full A allow orthogonal rotations, but the diagonal structure eliminates this ambiguity. *Evidence*: Abstract; Section 3.2; Appendix A.3.

**Variational Inference with Fixed Decoder**  
The use of a factorized encoder and an injective decoder with fixed observation noise variance does not undermine identifiability. The encoder may leverage the full observation sequence without affecting the theoretical guarantee because identifiability is a property of the generative prior and dynamics. The injective decoder and fixed σ² prevent additional ambiguities (e.g., rescaling). *Evidence*: Section 3.3; Appendix A.3; Appendix B.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Inputs: Multivariate series y₁:T, auxiliary u₁:T, initial factor state f₀.  
Encoder ϕ: (y₁:T, uₜ, eₜ) → Gaussian q_ϕ(ηₜ) with mean μₜ and variance σₜ².  
Prior net: RegimeNet(uₜ) → πₜ (softmax); regime embeddings {eₖ}; conditional prior p(ηₜ | uₜ, eₜ) = ∏ᵢ p(ηₜᵢ | uₜ, eₜ) (exponential family, e.g., Laplace).  
Dynamics: Linear diagonal or companion‑form matrices (A, B) possibly regime‑dependent; compute averaged \bar{A}ₜ, \bar{B}ₜ via πₜ; propagate fₜ₊₁ = \bar{A}ₜ fₜ + \bar{B}ₜ ηₜ.  
Decoder g: MLP mapping fₜ to observation mean; fixed variance σ².  
Output: reconstructed yₜ.

**Critical Path**  
uₜ → eₜ (deterministic via RegimeNet and regime embeddings) → shapes p(ηₜ | uₜ, eₜ) → encoder produces q(ηₜ) → sample ηₜ → apply \bar{A}ₜ, \bar{B}ₜ to obtain fₜ → decode to yₜ. Identifiability relies on sufficient variation in λ(uₜ, eₜ) and diagonal dynamics.

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Prior family: Non‑Gaussian (Laplace/Student‑t) required for identifiability; Gaussian collapses to linear invariance. Computational cost negligible.
- Dynamics expressivity: Diagonal/companion forms preserve identifiability but restrict factor interactions; full state‑space models lose guarantees.
- Context granularity: Fine‑grained uₜ gives rich variation but may overfit; coarse regimes reduce variance but risk insufficient distinct contexts.
- Decoder capacity: Must be injective; too small may violate injectivity, too large may overfit and reduce interpretability.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Low MCC (<0.3) and high subspace distance indicate failed identifiability, often due to insufficient variation in λ(uₜ, eₜ) or Gaussian innovations.
- Intervention metrics near random (sign accuracy ~0.5, unbounded IRF errors) suggest dynamics mix factors or decoder injectivity violated.
- Forecast accuracy much worse than dedicated models may indicate overly restrictive linear dynamics, though iVDFM prioritizes representation over raw prediction.

**First Three Experiments**  
1. Synthetic factor recovery: Evaluate MCC, subspace distance, TraceR² against known ground truth.  
2. Causal intervention: Train on synthetic SCM, compute impulse responses after intervening on a single innovation component; measure sign accuracy and IRF error.  
3. Probabilistic forecasting: Train on real‑world benchmarks (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Weather); report CRPS and MSE.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What other conditional contexts (e.g., learned or task‑specific embeddings) can be used in iVDFM while retaining identifiability?
- Can the dynamical or prior structure be relaxed (e.g., nonlinear or non‑diagonal dynamics) while preserving identifiability guarantees?
- Does iVDFM extend to other data modalities such as language or image sequences?
- How does iVDFM scale to very high‑dimensional observations (large N) and long histories?

## Limitations
- Missing architectural and training details (e.g., exact layer sizes, dropout, weight decay, KL annealing) hinder faithful reproduction.
- Observation noise variance σ² may require dataset‑specific tuning; its optimal value is unclear.
- Regime mechanism parameters (number of regimes K, embedding dimension, temperature) are unspecified, affecting conditioning strength.
- Theoretical assumptions (sufficient variation in λ, non‑Gaussian innovations, diagonal dynamics) may be sensitive in practice; empirical robustness is not fully explored.

## Next Checks
1. **Prior variation sensitivity**: Vary RegimeNet capacity and richness of uₜ on synthetic data; expect improved MCC up to a point, then saturation.
2. **Non‑Gaussianity necessity**: Replace Laplace innovations with Gaussian; identifiability should collapse (MCC ≈ random, high subspace distance).
3. **Decoder injectivity test**: Use a linear decoder; rotational ambiguity should reappear (increased subspace distance). Also make σ² learnable; check for degradation.
