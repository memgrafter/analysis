# A Typologically Grounded Evaluation Framework for Word Order and Morphology Sensitivity in Multilingual Masked LMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00432
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00432
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Multilingual masked LMs (mBERT, XLM-R) heavily rely on word order and fail to compensate with morphology, as shown by near-zero accuracy under full scrambling and sub-additive interactions between order and morphology perturbations.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a typologically grounded diagnostic framework to quantify how multilingual masked language models (mBERT, XLM-R) depend on word order versus inflectional morphology. Using Universal Dependencies annotations, the method applies inference-time perturbations to sentences: full token scrambling, partial scrambling (function words fixed), dependency-based head-dependent swaps, and sentence-level lemma substitution (+L). The same masked content word is evaluated across all conditions. Across English, Chinese, German, Spanish, and Russian, full scrambling collapses word-level reconstruction accuracy (relative drop ~0.91–0.98). Lemma substitution harms accuracy in morphologically rich languages (German, Spanish, Russian) but not Chinese. Critically, combined perturbations (e.g., Full+L) yield sub-additive harm (positive interaction I_full), indicating overlapping cues rather than complementarity. The findings demonstrate a strong order sensitivity and a lack of robust morphology utilization.

## Method Summary
The diagnostic evaluates pretrained mBERT and XLM-R on masked content-word prediction tasks. For each sentence from Universal Dependencies treebanks, one content word (excluding punctuation) is selected and masked. Perturbations are applied to the token sequence after masking: (i) Full scrambling: random permutation of all non-punctuation tokens; (ii) Part scrambling: permutation of content words while fixing function words; (iii) Head swapping: each head token is swapped with one of its dependents; (iv) Lemma substitution (+L): every token replaced by its UD lemma. The model predicts the masked word from the perturbed context; accuracy is measured as exact match against the gold surface form (or lemma for +L conditions). Sensitivity \(S_{\text{cond}} = (A_{\text{Orig}} - A_{\text{cond}})/A_{\text{Orig}}\) quantifies the relative impact of each perturbation, while interaction \(I_{\text{cond}} = A_{\text{cond+L}} - (A_{\text{cond}} + A_{\text{Orig+L}} - A_{\text{Orig}})\) tests for non-additive effects. The study uses balanced per-condition sampling and excludes items requiring more than six subword pieces for reconstruction.

## Key Results
- Full token scrambling reduces word-level reconstruction accuracy to near zero across all five languages (relative drop ~0.91–0.98), indicating extreme sensitivity to global word order disruption.
- Sentence-level lemma substitution (+L) causes substantial accuracy drops in German, Spanish, and Russian but negligible effect in Chinese, aligning with morphological richness.
- The interaction measure \(I_{\text{full}}\) is positive in morphologically rich languages, showing that the combined effect of full scrambling and lemmatization is less damaging than the sum of separate effects; this suggests overlapping rather than complementary cues from order and morphology.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Full token scrambling collapses word-level reconstruction accuracy because models rely heavily on absolute token position to predict masked content words.
- **Mechanism:** The perturbation randomizes all non-punctuation token positions, destroying linear order cues. The model’s masked token prediction fails without a consistent positional scaffold.
- **Core assumption:** Multilingual MLMs internalize a strong prior that specific semantic/grammatical roles occupy predictable positions in the surface string.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "Full scrambling drives word-level reconstruction accuracy near zero in all languages."
  - Section 6: Tables 4–5 show near-zero accuracy (e.g., mBERT DE: 0.143 → 0.013) under Full.
  - Corpus: Weak direct corpus evidence; this paper is the primary source. Neighbors discuss word-order universals but not this specific perturbation.
- **Break condition:** If Full scrambling produced minimal accuracy loss across typologically diverse languages, it would refute the claim of dominant positional reliance.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Sentence-level lemma substitution (+L) harms accuracy in morphologically rich languages (DE, ES, RU) because models use surface inflectional morphology as a cue, but has negligible effect in Chinese where lemmas ≈ surface forms.
- **Mechanism:** +L removes inflectional endings from the context, depriving the model of morphology-based disambiguation signals. The effect magnitude correlates with a language’s morphological richness.
- **Core assumption:** Morphology-sensitive predictions depend on encountering inflectional forms in context; lemmatization strips this information.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "+L has little effect in Chinese but substantially lowers accuracy in German/Spanish/Russian."
  - Section 2: "We include a coarse inflection-stripping diagnostic via a sentence-level lemma substitution (+L)... probes behavior in lemmatized contexts."
  - Corpus: Neighbors like "Enhancing Korean Dependency Parsing with Morphosyntactic Features" highlight morphology's role, but do not directly evidence this mechanism.
- **Break condition:** If +L consistently improved or had no effect across all languages, it would indicate models do not leverage inflectional morphology in the manner probed.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The interaction measure (\(I_{\text{full}}\)) is positive in morph-rich languages, indicating that the cues from word order and morphological form are partially overlapping rather than complementary; disrupting both does not cause more than additive harm.
- **Mechanism:** Positive \(I_{\text{full}}\) means the combined perturbation (Full+L) causes less damage than the sum of individual perturbations. Since Full already causes near-collapse, the positive interaction is partly due to floor effects, but the pattern suggests models use both cues in a redundant manner—the same underlying evidence (e.g., predicate-argument structure) is signaled by both order and morphology.
- **Core assumption:** If cues were complementary (e.g., order for syntax, morphology for agreement), disrupting both would yield supra-additive harm (\(I < 0\)). Sub-additivity (\(I > 0\)) implies overlap in the information each cue provides.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 6: "\(I_{\text{full}}\) is positive (Tables 6–7), i.e., accuracy under Full+L is higher than the additive baseline... consistent with overlapping/partially redundant signals."
  - Section 7: "Non-additivity: in DE/ES/RU, we observe \(I_{\text{full}} > 0\)... meaning the loss under Full+L is smaller than the sum of the separate losses."
  - Corpus: No direct corpus evidence; this is an original analysis metric from the paper.
- **Break condition:** If interactions were uniformly negative (supra-additive) or zero across languages, it would indicate synergy or independence between cues, contradicting the overlap claim.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Universal Dependencies (UD) treebanks
  - **Why needed here:** Provides cross-lingual, standardized syntactic annotations (POS tags, lemmas, dependency trees) to define perturbations (e.g., which tokens are "function words", what is a "head").
  - **Quick check question:** Given a UD sentence, how would you identify "content words" vs. "function words" for the Part perturbation?
- **Concept:** Masked Language Model (MLM) inference
  - **Why needed here:** The diagnostic evaluates pretrained MLMs by masking a single content word and reconstructing it from context. Understanding the MLM forward pass (mask token handling, subword prediction) is essential to replicate the evaluation.
  - **Quick check question:** Why must masking be done *before* perturbation, and how does the mask token move during scrambling?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** 
  1. Models: mBERT (WordPiece) and XLM-R (SentencePiece/BPE) – frozen pretrained encoders.
  2. Data source: UD treebanks (CoNLL-U format) providing tokens, lemmas, UPOS, and dependencies.
  3. Perturbation module: Scripts that apply Full, Part, Head swaps, and +L lemma substitution deterministically given a seed.
  4. Scoring module: Reconstructs full words from subword predictions, applies normalization (Unicode, case), and computes top-1/top-5 accuracy.
  5. Sampling controller: Manages balanced vs. unbalanced sets, span-length caps, and cross-condition target consistency.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Load UD sentences for a language.
  2. Select one eligible content word per sentence (mask span ≤ 6 subwords).
  3. Apply perturbation to the *masked* sentence (mask token moves with its token).
  4. Run model forward pass, get top-k predictions for mask span.
  5. Detokenize predictions, normalize, compare to gold (surface form or lemma based on condition).
  6. Aggregate accuracy; repeat for all conditions and seeds.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Balanced vs. unbalanced: Balancing ensures fair comparison but reduces 𝑁 and can introduce floor effects. Unbalanced uses all valid items but may confound condition differences with sample size disparities.
  - Six-piece reconstruction cap: Prevents degenerate long spans but disproportionately filters morphologically complex languages (e.g., Turkish), creating floor effects.
  - Gold UD trees for Head: Uses human-annotated trees, not parsed at test time; controls for parsing errors but introduces a non-end-to-end artifact.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Floor effects:** Accuracy near 0 in Orig (e.g., Turkish) makes sensitivities and interactions numerically unstable and uninterpretable.
  - **Tokenization mismatch:** Different subword segmenters cause different valid/invalid item sets across models for the same sentence.
  - **Evaluation quirk:** Tiny +L improvements in Chinese are artifacts of normalization/tokenization, not genuine morphology use.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Replicate baseline (Orig):** Run the diagnostic with no perturbation on EN and DE. Confirm baseline accuracy gap matches Tables 4–5.
  2. **Test one perturbation (Full):** Apply only Full scrambling. Verify relative drop S_full ≈ 0.91–0.98 across languages as in Tables 6–7.
  3. **Compare Head vs. Part:** On EN, check Δ accuracy (Part − Head) is negative (Head more harmful). On DE, check if the gap is smaller (mBERT) or reversed (XLM-R) per Figure 4.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Head+L interaction:** How do multilingual MLMs behave under head-dependent swaps combined with lemma substitution (Head+L), and does this interaction reveal different cue dependencies compared to Full+L? The authors explicitly state they did not evaluate Head+L.
2. **Training objectives for order robustness:** Can training objectives that explicitly reduce reliance on absolute word order (e.g., de-scrambling or order-invariant auxiliary losses) improve model robustness under scrambling without degrading performance on original order? This is proposed as future work.
3. **Turkish floor effects:** Why do agglutinative languages like Turkish exhibit such severe floor effects in strict word-level reconstruction, and how should evaluation protocols be adapted for such typologies? The current protocol may be biased toward languages with simpler morphology.
4. **Architectural vs. data biases:** To what extent do the observed order sensitivities reflect a fundamental architectural bias in transformers versus data-related biases (e.g., pretraining corpus word order statistics, tokenization artifacts)? Cross-language comparisons are confounded by pretraining data and tokenization.

## Limitations
- **Undocumented specifics:** Exact UD treebank version, language-specific stoplists, deterministic RNG logic for Head swaps, model checkpoint hashes, and downsampling seeds are not provided, hindering exact replication.
- **Floor effects from balanced sampling:** Balancing per-condition counts can force near-zero baselines (e.g., Turkish), distorting sensitivity (S) and interaction (I) metrics.
- **Cue separability assumption:** The perturbations assume word order and morphology cues are fully separable; overlapping effects might instead reflect deeper syntactic-semantic confounds.

## Confidence
- **Word-order dominance (Full scrambling collapse):** High confidence. Consistent near-zero accuracy across five typologically diverse languages supports strong positional reliance.
- **Morphology cue via +L:** High confidence. Substantial drops in morph-rich languages but not Chinese directly map to morphological richness.
- **Overlapping cues (I_full > 0):** Medium-High confidence. Positive interaction reported in morph-rich languages, but the metric may be inflated by floor effects from Full’s near-zero accuracy; sign stability under higher baselines needs confirmation.
