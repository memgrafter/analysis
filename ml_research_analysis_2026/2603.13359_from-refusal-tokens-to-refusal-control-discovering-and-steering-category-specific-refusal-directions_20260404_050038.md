# From Refusal Tokens to Refusal Control: Discovering and Steering Category-Specific Refusal Directions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13359
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13359
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Extracted categorical steering directions and a learned low-rank combination consistently reduce over-refusals on benign prompts (8.93–13.70%) and increase refusals on harmful prompts (12.86–14.17%), with the low-rank vector transferring to other models of the same architecture.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how language models fine-tuned with categorical refusal tokens (e.g., `[Requests with safety concerns]`) internally represent different refusal types. It finds that these types correspond to separable, category-aligned directions in the residual stream. By extracting these directions, the authors construct both categorical steering vectors and a single, learned low-rank combination that mixes them in a whitened orthonormal basis. Both interventions effectively steer model behavior at inference time: they reduce unnecessary refusals on benign requests while strengthening refusals on harmful prompts. The low-rank combination notably transfers to other models sharing the same architecture without additional training, though with slightly reduced efficacy compared to model-specific categorical steering.

## Method Summary
The method first identifies an optimal intervention layer (layer 18) by evaluating the separability of refusal category activations. For each harmful category, a steering direction is computed as the mean activation difference between that category and benign prompts, followed by thresholding and sparsification. A lightweight linear probe is trained on the optimal layer’s activations to classify prompts as harmful or benign, with its decision threshold calibrated via ROC analysis. At inference, the probe determines the steering sign (toward/away from refusal) and selects the relevant category direction. Alternatively, the categorical vectors are transformed: using a benign covariance estimate, they are whitened and orthonormalized into a basis. A low-rank operator is then learned within this basis to produce a single steering vector that optimizes the trade-off between increasing harmful refusals and decreasing over-refusals on benign prompts.

## Key Results
- Categorical steering and the low-rank combination reduce over-refusal rates on benign prompts by 8.93–13.70% across multiple benchmarks.
- The same interventions increase refusal rates on harmful prompts by 12.86–14.17%.
- The learned low-rank combination transfers zero-shot to other models of the same architecture (e.g., Llama 3 8B Instruct, DeepSeek R1 Distill Llama), yielding nonzero safety improvements while preserving general model performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Refusal token fine-tuning induces separable, category-aligned directions in the residual stream that can be extracted via mean-difference.
- **Mechanism:** Fine-tuning with categorical refusal tokens causes the model's internal representations for prompts of different refusal types to cluster along distinct linear directions in activation space. A category-specific steering direction is isolated by computing the mean activation for each harmful category and subtracting the mean for benign prompts.
- **Core assumption:** The mean activation across a category represents a stable, category-specific "feature" in the residual stream, and the difference from benign captures refusal-related variance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...we show that refusal token fine-tuning induces separable, category-aligned directions in the residual stream, which we extract..."
  - [section 3.1] "By subtracting the benign mean activations ˜νl from each harmful category’s mean activation ˜µl(c), we isolate a unique refusal steering direction..."
  - [corpus] Indirect support: neighboring papers (e.g., "There Is More to Refusal in Large Language Models than a Single Direction") explore multi-category refusal structure.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A lightweight linear probe trained on layer-18 activations can reliably classify prompts as harmful or benign at inference time, enabling dynamic sign selection for steering.
- **Mechanism:** The probe learns a linear decision boundary. At inference, the probe's output probability (vs. a calibrated threshold θ) determines whether to apply steering with α>0 (toward refusal for harmful) or α<0 (away from refusal for benign).
- **Core assumption:** The optimal layer's activations contain linearly separable information about the high-level refusal category, and a simple linear classifier suffices for robust binary harmful/benign discrimination.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2] "We train a lightweight probe to distinguish between harmful and benign prompts given residual-stream activations from the optimal layer l∗."
  - [section 3.2] "We select a probability decision threshold θ... which maximizes Youden’s J statistic."
  - [section A.3] ROC curve shows high TPR (0.969) at low FPR (0.021).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Whitening and orthonormalizing the categorical steering vectors in a benign covariance-estimated space, then learning a low-rank combination, produces a single steering vector that corrects for activation anisotropy and transfers across same-architecture models.
- **Mechanism:** Activation spaces are anisotropic. ZCA whitening (using benign covariance Σ) renders the space isotropic. QR decomposition on whitened vectors creates an orthonormal basis. A learned low-rank operator (s = U(V⊤z)) in this basis finds an optimal linear combination that increases harmful refusals while minimizing benign KL drift. The low-rank constraint prevents overfitting to source model idiosyncrasies, enabling transfer.
- **Core assumption:** Anisotropy causes additive steering to be dominated by a few high-variance directions; correcting the geometry allows a clean, composable combination. Low-rank constraints aid generalization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.4] "Transformer activation spaces are anisotropic... a few principal directions account for the most variance."
  - [section 3.4] "We apply ZCA Whitening... to categorical steering vectors using a benign covariance estimate... orthonormalize... resulting in a single controllable intervention."
  - [section 5.1] Transfer results show nonzero improvements on other models (e.g., −1.45% over-refusal, +0.66% refusal on Instruct).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Activation Steering**
  - *Why needed here:* Core technique—modifying residual stream activations at specific layers to influence model behavior without retraining.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the key difference between steering at the residual stream vs. applying a logit bias to specific tokens?
- **Concept: Residual Stream & Layer Selection**
  - *Why needed here:* Steering vectors are extracted from the "resid_post" of a specific layer (layer 18). Understanding the residual stream's role is essential to know *where* and *why* to intervene.
  - *Quick check question:* Why might steering vectors computed at different layers (e.g., early vs. late) have different effectiveness?
- **Concept: Activation Space Anisotropy**
  - *Why needed here:* Justifies the whitening and orthonormalization steps. Anisotropy means naive vector addition disproportionately affects high-variance directions, harming general capabilities.
  - *Quick check question:* If activations are anisotropic, what risks exist when simply summing two steering vectors?
- **Concept: Linear Probing & Threshold Calibration**
  - *Why needed here:* The probe and its threshold θ are the decision engine that determines steering sign (toward/away from refusal) at inference time.
  - *Quick check question:* Why use Youden’s J statistic for threshold selection instead of default 0.5? What does a high J indicate?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Data Pipeline -> Activation Extraction -> Steering Vector Computation -> Optimal Layer Selection -> Linear Probe Training -> Low-Rank Combination -> Inference-Time Steering
- **Critical path:** Activation extraction (steps 2-4) → Probe training (5) → Low-rank optimization (6) → Inference application (7). The probe and optimal layer selection must be completed before low-rank combination.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Sparsification (K)*: Lower K reduces off-target effects but risks losing category-specific signal. Higher K preserves more variance but may cause over-steering.
  - *Transferability vs. Specificity:* Low-rank combination generalizes across models but yields smaller gains than model-specific categorical steering. Whitening uses benign covariance—if benign distribution shifts, W may misrepresent geometry.
  - *Probe complexity:* Linear probe is lightweight but may fail on complex, ambiguous prompts where refusal is context-dependent beyond linear separability.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor layer selection: If Silhouette score at candidate layers is low (<0.2), vectors will not separate categories → no steering effect.
  - Probe miscalibration: ROC curve shows low Youden’s J (<0.8) → high misclassification rate → wrong steering sign applied.
  - Orthonormalization instability: QR decomposition yields near-singular Q if whitening failed (e.g., Σ ill-conditioned) → unstable low-rank combination.
  - Transfer collapse: Applying low-rank s to non-fine-tuned models (e.g., base Llama) yields negligible or negative changes, indicating overfitting to REFUSE-LLAMA’s weight shifts.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Activation Separability Check:** Compute PCA/t-SNE of layer-18 activations for D_b and D_c. Calculate Silhouette & Davies–Bouldin scores. Target: Silhouette >0.3, DB <1.5.
  2. **Probe Validation:** Train linear probe, plot ROC on validation set. Target: AUC >0.98, Youden’s J >0.94, FPR at optimal θ <0.05.
  3. **Low-Rank Transfer Test:** Apply learned s from REFUSE-LLAMA to a same-architecture model (e.g., Llama 3 8B Instruct). Measure Δ over-refusal and Δ refusal on a small held-out set. Target: nonzero reduction in over-refusal and increase in harmful refusal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** To what extent does the categorical steering framework, developed for refusal control, generalize to other multi-category behavioral control tasks (e.g., sentiment shift, topic adherence) in language models?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The authors state in the conclusion: "The scope of our contributions extends beyond refusals, as it can be used as a general framework for extracting and applying multi-category steering directions. Any setting with several categorical behaviors can, in principle, benefit from the same categorical steering and low-rank combination methodologies."
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper only demonstrates the method on refusal categories. Generalizability to other attribute spaces requires empirical validation on diverse tasks.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic experiments applying the identical methodology (extraction of category-specific directions, whitening, learned low-rank combination) to at least two new multi-category tasks (e.g., controlling output formality levels or emotional tone) with reported success metrics comparable to the refusal case.
- **Open Question 2:** What is the theoretical explanation for the decreased transfer effectiveness of the learned low-rank combination when moving from REFUSE-LLAMA to other same-architecture models (e.g., LLAMA3 8B INSTRUCT), and how can transferability be improved?
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] Results in Table 1 show smaller safety improvements when transferring the low-rank combination to LLAMA3 8B INSTRUCT and DEEPSEEK R1 DISTILL LLAMA versus applying it on REFUSE-LLAMA. The authors note: "the intervention is not exactly model-invariant: changes in weights from fine-tuning can shift the geometry of the learned direction."
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper identifies the phenomenon but does not analyze the geometric or training-induced causes of the shift, nor proposes methods to mitigate it for zero-shot transfer.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* An analysis (e.g., comparing covariance matrices Σ of benign activations, or geometric properties of category directions) between REFUSE-LLAMA and the base model to pinpoint specific distributional shifts. Follow-up experiments with a fine-tuning-based adaptation step (e.g., few-shot tuning of U, V, z on a small set of target model prompts) to quantify potential recovery.
- **Open Question 3:** How sensitive are the performance gains from categorical steering and the low-rank combination to the hyperparameters of the extraction process (τ, K) and the learned low-rank operator optimization (regularization in L_benign, rank constraints)?
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The methodology involves several hyperparameters: threshold τ, top-K sparsification, whitening regularization ε, and the loss weights in L_harmful + L_benign. The paper selects specific values (τ=0.001, K=200) but does not provide an ablation on their impact.
  - *Why unresolved:* The robustness of the approach to these choices is critical for practical deployment but is not established. Performance could degrade significantly with different settings.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Comprehensive ablation studies varying τ, K, ε, and loss weighting across a validation set, reporting sensitivity of key metrics (average over-refusal reduction, harmful refusal increase). This would define acceptable parameter ranges.
- **Open Question 4:** Does the linear probe for binary harmful/benign classification remain reliable and calibrated under distribution shift (e.g., novel prompt styles, out-of-domain benign requests), and how does its failure mode affect downstream steering?
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The probe is trained and thresholded on the CoCoNot dataset's validation split. Its decision (p(x) ≥ θ) determines steering sign and category selection. The paper does not evaluate probe robustness on unseen distribution shifts.
  - *Why unresolved:* If the probe misclassifies a prompt, inappropriate steering (α sign or category) could increase over-refusals on harmful prompts or reduce refusals on benign ones. Probe reliability is fundamental to the system's safety.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Evaluate probe accuracy and Youden's J on multiple held-out, diverse benign/harmful benchmarks not used in training (some are already used for final eval, but separate probeval sets are needed). Correlate probe confidence/score distribution with steering outcome failures.
- **Open Question 5:** Are the extracted categorical steering directions stable over time (i.e., across different training seeds for REFUSE-LLAMA) and what does their stability imply for the underlying mechanism of refusal token fine-tuning?
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The paper computes directions from a single instance of REFUSE-LLAMA. The model diffing (Table 3) compares to a different base model, but intra-model stability across fine-tuning runs is unexplored.
  - *Why unresolved:* If directions are highly unstable across seeds, it suggests the fine-tuning process does not consistently induce the same geometric changes, challenging the claim of "separable, category-aligned directions" as a robust phenomenon. Stability is needed for reproducible steering.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Train multiple (e.g., 5) instances of REFUSE-LLAMA with different random seeds but identical refusal token fine-tuning. Extract categorical steering vectors at the same optimal layer for each. Compute pairwise cosine similarities and clustering metrics (Silhouette, Davies-Bouldin) across the sets. High similarity would indicate stable emergence.

## Limitations
- The method's hyperparameters (threshold τ, top-K sparsification K, whitening ε, loss weights) are selected without published ablation, leaving their sensitivity unquantified.
- Transferability of the low-rank combination to other models yields smaller gains than model-specific steering, indicating some model-specificity remains.
- The linear probe's robustness to distribution shift is not evaluated; misclassification could lead to harmful steering errors.

## Confidence
- **Categorical separability (Mechanism 1):** High. PCA/t-SNE clusters and quantitative scores support distinct directions.
- **Linear probe for sign selection (Mechanism 2):** High. ROC shows AUC 0.969, Youden’s J > 0.94 after calibration.
- **Low‑rank transferable vector (Mechanism 3):** Medium. Transfer yields smaller gains than model‑specific steering and depends on accurate whitening.
