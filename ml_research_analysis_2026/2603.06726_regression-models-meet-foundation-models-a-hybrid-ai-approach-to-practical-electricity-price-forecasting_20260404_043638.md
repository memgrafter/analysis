# Regression Models Meet Foundation Models: A Hybrid-AI Approach to Practical Electricity Price Forecasting

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.06726
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06726
*   **Reference Count:** 40
*   **Primary Result:** FutureBoosting reduces MAE by over 30% relative to zero-shot TSFMs and by 1.8–6.2% relative to regression baselines on Shanxi data, and by 8–18% over TSFMs and 58–63% over regression on the RealE benchmark.

## Executive Summary
Electricity price forecasting is hampered by extreme volatility, nonlinearity, and non-stationarity. Time-series foundation models (TSFMs) excel at capturing temporal patterns but often fail to model critical cross-variate interactions and cannot access future-unavailable drivers like actual renewable generation at forecast time. This paper introduces FutureBoosting, a hybrid framework that uses a frozen, zero-shot TSFM to generate point forecasts for these future-unavailable, price-correlated variables (e.g., system load, renewables). These TSFM-generated forecasts are then fed as enriched features into a downstream regression model (LightGBM). This decouples the tasks: the TSFM provides forward-looking temporal expectations, while the regression model learns task-specific, nonlinear interactions between these expectations, future-available exogenous data, and domain-constructed factors. Validated on real-world markets (Shanxi, France/Germany), FutureBoosting achieves significant and consistent accuracy improvements over both pure TSFM and pure regression baselines while remaining lightweight and interpretable via SHAP.

## Method Summary
FutureBoosting is a two-stage hybrid pipeline. First, a pre-trained, frozen TSFM (e.g., Chronos2, TimerXL) is applied in zero-shot mode to historical context windows (price and other available time series) to produce point forecasts for a selected set of future-unavailable, price-correlated driver variables (e.g., system load, wind/solar generation) for the target forecasting horizon. Second, these TSFM-generated driver forecasts are concatenated with genuinely future-available exogenous variables (e.g., calendar data, pre-committed generation) and hand-crafted domain factors (e.g., thermal auction space) to form an enriched feature vector for each future timestep. A gradient-boosted tree regressor (LightGBM) is then trained to map this enriched feature space to the electricity price. The TSFM is never fine-tuned; its inferences can be cached, making the system computationally efficient and suitable for CPU-only deployment. SHAP analysis is used to interpret the regression model's reliance on the TSFM-augmented features.

## Key Results
*   On the volatile Shanxi market, FutureBoosting reduces MAE by **>30%** against zero-shot TSFMs and by **1.8–6.2%** against LightGBM regression using only standard features.
*   On the RealE benchmark (France/Germany), it improves MAE by **8–18%** over TSFMs and a substantial **58–63%** over regression baselines.
*   The framework is highly efficient; cached inference runs in ~59 seconds on CPU, far outperforming fine-tuning approaches that require multiple GPUs and hours.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Forward-Looking Feature Augmentation.** A frozen TSFM is applied to historical context to generate forecasts for critical driver variables (e.g., system load, renewable output) that are unavailable at the time of day-ahead planning. These forecasts (ˆ𝑋forecast) are treated as expectations of the future supply-demand balance. They are concatenated with genuinely future-available data (Z) and domain factors (C) to form an enriched feature set (F). The core assumption is that even a domain-mismatched TSFM can produce sufficiently accurate driver forecasts to be useful as features, despite its direct price forecasts being poor. Evidence comes from ablation studies showing that removing these TSFM-generated features significantly degrades performance. A break condition occurs if the TSFM's driver forecasts are systematically inaccurate for the target domain (e.g., due to regime shifts in renewables), injecting noise that harms the regression.

**Mechanism 2: Nonlinear Cross-Variate Interaction Capture.** The downstream tree-based regressor (LightGBM) partitions the enriched feature space (F) to learn complex, non-additive relationships between the TSFM's forward-looking expectations (ˆ𝑋forecast), standard exogeneous variables (Z), and domain factors (C). This allows the model to correct TSFM errors by relating the price directly to the underlying supply-demand structure implied by the forecasts, a capability the auto-regressive, univariate-focused TSFM lacks. The assumption is that these cross-variate relationships are learnable and more stable than raw temporal dynamics. Evidence includes FutureBoosting's consistent outperformance of zero-shot TSFMs and ablation showing LightGBM's superiority over linear regression on the nonlinear Shanxi dataset. A break condition is if the relationships are predominantly linear or features are highly multicollinear, where a simpler linear model may suffice, as seen on the RealE dataset.

**Mechanism 3: Decoupled, Efficient, and Interpretable Design.** Freezing the TSFM and using its outputs as static features decouples temporal pattern learning (done once, cacheable) from domain-specific interaction learning (done by the regressor). This avoids costly fine-tuning, enables extremely fast CPU inference via caching, and allows SHAP explanations to directly attribute predictions to specific enriched features, including those from the TSFM. The assumption is that the TSFM's pretrained temporal knowledge is general enough for driver forecasting, and the flexible regressor can compensate for domain mismatch without modifying the TSFM. Evidence includes dramatic speedups vs. fine-tuning and SHAP analyses (e.g., Fig. 7, 10) showing TSFM-derived features are top contributors. A break condition is if the TSFM's driver forecasts are highly inaccurate, where a fine-tuned TSFM might perform better, as suggested by LoRA comparisons.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs)**
    *   **Why needed here:** FutureBoosting relies on TSFMs' ability to zero-shot forecast auxiliary variables (load, renewables) from historical context. Understanding their strength in long-horizon temporal patterns and weakness in cross-variate reasoning/domain mismatch is key.
    *   **Quick check:** Why might a TSFM pretrained on diverse data fail at direct electricity price forecasting but still produce useful forecasts for system load?
*   **Concept: Cross-Variate Regression for Tabular Data**
    *   **Why needed here:** The downstream model must capture interactions between TSFM forecasts, standard exogeneous variables, and engineered features. Knowledge of tree-based (LightGBM) vs. linear models' inductive biases informs the design choice.
    *   **Quick check:** Based on SHAP importance, what does the dominance of features like "renewable ratio" suggest about electricity price formation?
*   **Concept: Electricity Market Mechanics & Data Availability**
    *   **Why needed here:** The problem hinges on what data is available *when* (day-ahead vs. real-time). Knowing which drivers are "future-unavailable" at planning time defines the feature set gap the TSFM must fill.
    *   **Quick check:** Why are actual system load and renewable generation considered "future-unavailable" for day-ahead price forecasting, even though they are critical price drivers?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
TSFM Inference Module -> Feature Construction Module -> Regression Trainer -> Inference Pipeline
(Optional: Explainability Module for SHAP)

**Critical Path:** The two-stage decoupling is critical. TSFM inference must complete before feature construction to produce ˆ𝑋forecast𝐷+1. Enriched features (𝐹𝐷+1) must be correctly time-aligned with the target price 𝑦𝐷+1. Any temporal leakage from future data into the TSFM's context window or misalignment in indices will invalidate training.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
*   **TSFM choice:** Univariate (TimerXL) vs. multivariate (Chronos2). Multivariate models may accept future-known inputs during inference, altering the definition of "future-unavailable."
*   **Regressor choice:** LightGBM (nonlinear, high capacity) vs. linear (interpretable). Choice depends on dataset complexity and risk of overfitting with many TSFM-augmented features.
*   **Feature set 𝑉:** Selecting which future-unavailable variables to forecast with the TSFM (e.g., load, wind, solar). Too many add noise; too few omit key drivers. Guided by domain knowledge.
*   **Caching strategy:** Cache TSFM outputs per (day, model, horizon) to avoid repeated GPU inference. Risk of invalidation if TSFM version or context window changes.

**Failure Signatures:**
*   **TSFM degradation:** Sharp drop in zero-shot TSFM performance on driver variables (e.g., load forecast errors spike). If ˆ𝑋forecast is garbage, FutureBoosting will underperform.
*   **Feature leakage:** 𝑍𝐷+1 or 𝐶𝐷+1 contains post-facto information (e.g, actual load instead of forecast) that would not be known at planning time.
*   **Regressor overfitting:** Very low training error but high test error. Check via validation; SHAP may show noisy, unintuitive attributions.
*   **Regime mismatch:** Performance collapses in months with large distribution shift (e.g., extreme price spikes). Monitor rolling metrics; large swings suggest need for feature adaptation.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1.  **Baseline replication:** Implement zero-shot TSFM (e.g., Chronos2) and direct LightGBM regression (on standard features) on a held-out month. Confirm MAE numbers roughly match paper's tables for the chosen dataset split.
2.  **Ablation - TSFM augmentation:** Run full FutureBoosting but remove the ˆ𝑋forecast block (use only Z and C). Measure MAE increase; expect significant drop (>5% on Shanxi) proving TSFM features' contribution.
3.  **Ablation - Regressor choice:** With fixed TSFM (e.g., Chronos2), compare LightGBM vs. linear regression on the same enriched features (𝐹). Expect LightGBM to win on nonlinear Shanxi; linear to win on simpler RealE, validating regressor selection.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*   **Automated Feature Selection:** How can methods like LASSO or Shapley optimally select from the enriched feature set? The current manual curation may be suboptimal.
*   **Dynamic Feature Selection:** How to design a feature selection mechanism that adapts to non-stationary electricity markets and regime changes? A static set may degrade over time.
*   **Performance Drivers:** Under what specific data characteristics (e.g., volatility, tail risk, renewable share) does FutureBoosting provide the largest gains over baselines? The paper shows varying results across markets but lacks a systematic analysis.
*   **TSFM Fine-tuning Trade-off:** What is the optimal balance between using a frozen TSFM (efficiency) and fine-tuning it (accuracy)? The paper shows LoRA fine-tuning can beat FutureBoosting on MAE but at high computational cost, leaving the Pareto frontier unexplored.

## Limitations
*   TSFM zero-shot driver forecasts may suffer from domain shift (e.g., evolving renewable penetration), reducing feature quality and limiting generalizability.
*   The feature engineering pipeline risks temporal leakage if future-available variables (Z) are misaligned or improperly defined.
*   Generalizability is limited to evaluated markets (Shanxi, France/Germany); performance in regions with distinct market designs or volatility patterns is unknown.
*   Freezing the TSFM assumes its pretrained knowledge is sufficient; fine-tuning could yield better accuracy, creating an unresolved efficiency-accuracy trade-off.

## Confidence
*   **Overall Hybrid Efficacy (MAE Reduction):** High – Consistent gains on two distinct benchmarks.
*   **TSFM Driver Forecast Utility (Mechanism 1):** Medium – Ablation supports contribution, but hinges on TSFM accuracy which is domain-dependent.
*   **Regression Interaction Capture (Mechanism 2):** Medium – LightGBM wins in nonlinear settings (Shanxi) but linear rivals it in simpler ones (RealE), indicating conditional validity.
*   **Freezing Strategy Efficiency (Mechanism 3):** Medium – Caching and speed benefits are proven, but accuracy cost versus fine-tuning is unclear.
