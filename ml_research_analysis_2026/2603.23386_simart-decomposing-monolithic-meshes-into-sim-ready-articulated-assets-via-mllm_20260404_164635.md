# SIMART: Decomposing Monolithic Meshes into Sim-ready Articulated Assets via MLLM

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23386
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23386
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** A unified MLLM framework using a Sparse 3D VQ-VAE reduces geometric token count by ~70% to jointly perform part-level mesh decomposition and kinematic prediction, generating simulation-ready URDF assets with state-of-the-art accuracy.

## Executive Summary
SIMART bridges the gap between static 3D asset generation and the need for articulated, simulation-ready objects. It introduces a Sparse 3D VQ-VAE to compress geometric representation, enabling a single Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) to process complex assemblies and simultaneously output part segmentations and kinematic parameters. Evaluated on PartNet-Mobility and AI-generated datasets, it achieves superior performance in part grounding and joint prediction, producing physically valid URDF files suitable for robotics and VR/AR applications.

## Method Summary
The method uses a two-stage pipeline. First, a Sparse 3D VQ-VAE (based on a 3D-UNet) compresses a 64³ voxelized mesh into a discrete latent grid, representing only occupied voxels as tokens with explicit coordinates. Second, the Qwen3-VL MLLM ingests concatenated tokens from a rendered image, a text instruction, and the sparse geometry sequence. It is fine-tuned to generate a hybrid output: part-specific sparse voxel tokens for segmentation and a structured JSON-like URDF specification containing joint types, axes, origins, and limits. The geometric tokens are decoded back to occupancy grids, which guide mesh segmentation, and the URDF metadata is assembled with the segmented parts.

## Key Results
- **Type Accuracy:** 0.928 on in-domain (PartNet-Mobility) items.
- **Axis Error:** 0.080 (angular deviation) on in-domain items.
- **Part IoU:** 0.777 on out-of-distribution AI-generated objects.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Sparse 3D VQ-VAE reduces geometric token count by ~70%, enabling MLLM processing of complex assemblies.
- **Mechanism:** Encoder outputs a latent 16³ grid, downsampled to 8³. A reserved "zero token" (`e_zero`) represents unoccupied voxels; only occupied voxels are vector-quantized and serialized as `<voxel> [xyz] [K]`, where `[xyz]` is a linearized coordinate (64x+8y+z) and `[K]` is the geometry index.
- **Core Assumption:** Real-world meshes are highly sparse; discarding empty-space tokens preserves topology while cutting sequence length.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract states token reduction; Section 3.3 defines zero-token assignment and coordinate serialization.
- **Break Condition:** Geometries with very high occupancy fraction (e.g., porous lattices >40%) see diminished token savings and risk memory pressure.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A unified MLLM framework jointly performs decomposition and kinematic prediction, avoiding error accumulation from decoupled pipelines.
- **Mechanism:** Qwen3-VL ingests fused multimodal tokens (image, text, sparse geometry) and generates hybrid output: per-part sparse tokens and a JSON URDF. The model learns correlations between part boundaries and motion affordances in one forward pass.
- **Core Assumption:** The MLLM's world knowledge and spatial reasoning can infer physically valid articulation directly from 3D geometry.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.2 describe joint prediction; contrast with prior decoupled methods (Urdformer, Articulate-Anything) that produce invalid articulations.
- **Break Condition:** Ambiguous or non-standard articulations (e.g., ball joints) may default to common priors (e.g., revolute), yielding plausible but incorrect structures; segmentation errors directly corrupt kinematics.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Coordinate-aware tokenization (`[xyz]`) preserves spatial topology within variable-length sequences, enabling fine-grained reasoning.
- **Mechanism:** Each occupied token includes absolute 3D coordinates, decoupling location from token order. The MLLM interprets these to reconstruct a sparse volume for tasks like axis localization.
- **Core Assumption:** The MLLM can learn to treat coordinate tokens as spatial indices.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Section 3.3 explains explicit localization; Section 4.2 shows part grounding IoU improvement (0.807 vs. 0.507) over baselines lacking explicit coordinates.
- **Break Condition:** Coarse discretization (8³) may merge distinct regions for thin/small parts (e.g., shelves), blurring boundaries.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE)**
  - **Why needed:** Compresses continuous 3D voxels into discrete "geometry tokens" (`[K]`) compatible with language model vocabularies. Sparse extension efficiently handles empty space.
  - **Quick check:** How does commitment loss stabilize VQ-VAE training, and what happens without it? (Answer: Prevents encoder outputs from drifting away from codebook entries; without it, the codebook may be underused or the latent space collapses.)

- **Concept: Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) Architecture**
  - **Why needed:** Integrates heterogeneous inputs (image, text, sparse 3D tokens) into one reasoning engine. Must output both free-form spatial tokens and structured symbolic data.
  - **Quick check:** Why use a vision-language backbone (Qwen3-VL) instead of a text-only LLM? (Answer: Its pre-training provides emergent physical reasoning and spatial understanding crucial for linking 2D visual context to 3D structure.)

- **Concept: URDF (Unified Robot Description Format) Parameterization**
  - **Why needed:** Simulation-ready output requires precise kinematic metadata (joint types, axes, limits, parent-child links) and physical properties (density, friction).
  - **Quick check:** What distinguishes a `revolute` from a `prismatic` joint, and how does the `axis` vector define motion? (Answer: Revolute rotates around an axis; prismatic translates along an axis. The axis vector is the unit direction of motion in the parent link frame.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Sparse 3D VQ-VAE Encoder -> MLLM Backbone (Qwen3-VL) -> Output Parser -> VQ-VAE Decoder -> Graph-based Segmentation -> URDF Assembly

- **Critical path:** Input Mesh -> Voxelization (64³) -> Sparse Encoding (8³ tokens) -> MLLM Reasoning (joint output) -> Token Split -> Per-Part Decoding -> Surface Segmentation -> URDF Generation.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Sparse vs. Dense Tokens:* Sparse saves memory (~70% reduction) but requires explicit coordinates; dense is simpler but OOM for complex objects.
  - *Codebook Size:* 4096 entries balance fidelity and overhead; 8192 gives marginal gains.
  - *Coordinate Resolution:* 8³ grid is memory-efficient; 16×8×8 improves fidelity but doubles tokens.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **OOM during training:** Sequence > context length; caused by dense tokens or many parts.
  - **Over-segmentation:** Initial segmentation (P3SAM) produces >6 parts; manual merging needed for ground truth.
  - **Axis Error > 0.2:** Kinematic prediction fails, often for complex joints not in training.
  - **Reconstruction artifacts:** Forcing sparsity without zero token yields high CD (56.10 vs. 4.19).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Compare memory usage and Type accuracy across Dense, Force Sparse (no zero token), and Zero Sparse tokenization schemes.
  2. Ablate the zero token (`e_zero`) by training Force Sparse; expect drastic CD increase and poorer segmentation.
  3. Remove image input tokens from the MLLM; expect drop in Type/IoU on AI-generated items, showing vision resolves ambiguities.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What scalable methods exist to create large, diverse, and accurately annotated articulated 3D datasets?
- **Basis:** Conclusion identifies dataset scarcity and inconsistency as a primary limitation for open-world generalization.
- **Why unresolved:** Existing datasets are small, homogeneous, and manually created; this bottleneck hinders training and evaluation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Demonstrated use of SIMART to auto-generate and validate (via simulation or human review) a large-scale articulated dataset covering many categories.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can we reduce geometric token count further than 70% (e.g., via adaptive sparsity or non-voxel tokens) without sacrificing reconstruction and kinematic accuracy?
- **Basis:** SIMART uses a uniform 8×8×8 sparse grid; resolution increase improves metrics but doubles tokens, indicating a trade-off.
- **Why unresolved:** Lower token count would enable processing more complex assemblies and larger MLLMs; current sparsity might be suboptimal.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare alternative tokenization schemes (octrees, point-based) on token efficiency and performance for objects with many parts (>10).

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How accurate and realistic are the predicted physical properties (e.g., density, friction) in the generated URDFs, and do they lead to plausible simulation dynamics?
- **Basis:** URDF includes material metadata, but the paper does not validate these predictions; it assumes MLLM knowledge is sufficient.
- **Why unresolved:** Inaccurate properties could cause unrealistic dynamics even with correct kinematics; no ground truth exists for evaluation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Collect a dataset with measured real-world material properties; evaluate prediction accuracy; perform physics simulations to compare trajectories with real-world data.

## Limitations
- Sparse representation may fail on high-occupancy geometries (e.g., porous meshes), reducing token savings and risking OOM.
- Joint prediction relies on MLLM priors; non-standard articulations (ball joints, soft bodies) yield plausible but incorrect outputs.
- Coordinate resolution (8³ grid) may blur fine parts, harming segmentation and axis estimation.
- Training details (VQ-VAE hyperparams, fine-tuning schedule, post-processing) are underspecified, hindering exact replication.
- Evaluation is limited to PartNet-Mobility and AI-generated data; real-world scan robustness is unclear.

## Confidence
- **Sparse VQ-VAE efficiency (~70% token reduction):** High (supported by ablations, though extreme geometries could degrade gains).
- **Unified MLLM avoids staged error accumulation:** Medium (SOTA results suggest benefit, but break conditions show vulnerability to ambiguous kinematics).
- **Coordinate-aware tokenization preserves topology:** High (part IoU improvements vs. baselines validate design; coarse resolution limits thin structures—Medium).
- **Reported in-domain & OOD metrics (Type 0.928, Axis 0.080, IoU 0.777):** Medium (strong numbers, but per-category variance and real-world transfer not shown).

## Next Checks
1. Stress-test sparsity limit: Run pipeline on dense/porous meshes (e.g., lattice structures) to record token count, memory use, and accuracy; identify occupied fraction where reduction drops below 70% or OOM occurs.
2. Ablate coordinate resolution: Train models with finer latent grids (e.g., 16³) and evaluate on objects with small/thin parts; compare segmentation IoU and Axis error to confirm resolution sensitivity.
3. Benchmark non-standard joints: Assemble a test set with ball joints, continuous joints, or soft deformations from OOD sources; measure Type accuracy and Axis error to quantify reliance on common priors and propose targeted data augmentation.
