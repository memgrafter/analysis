# LLM Benchmark-User Need Misalignment for Climate Change

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.26106
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26106
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Existing climate change LLM benchmarks show substantial misalignment with real user knowledge needs (63% over‑representation in atmospheric science, 40% higher fact‑lookup intents, 37% gap in structured answers), while IPCC AR6 exhibits high topic alignment (similarity up to 0.88).

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates whether current LLM benchmarks for climate change reflect actual user knowledge needs. The authors introduce a Proactive Knowledge Behaviors Framework and a Topic‑Intent‑Form taxonomy to analyze knowledge‑seeking interactions across multiple data sources: human‑AI chats (WildChat, LMSYS, ClimateQ&A), human‑human Q&A (Reddit), climate knowledge corpora (IPCC AR6, SciDCC), and an existing benchmark (ClimaQA). The analysis reveals a pronounced misalignment: ClimaQA over‑represents atmospheric science topics and fact‑lookup intents while under‑delivering on structured answer forms. In contrast, real user queries prioritize policy, energy transition, and advice/writing, favoring paragraphs and lists. Human‑human queries closely resemble human‑AI queries, validating the former as a proxy for real needs. IPCC AR6 aligns exceptionally well with user topic distributions, making it an ideal source for RAG and benchmark construction.

## Method Summary
The study constructs a taxonomy of 25 climate topics, 29 user intents, and 29 answer forms. Using an LLM (GPT‑5‑mini), each query is annotated with up to three prioritized labels per dimension. Samples are converted into weighted vectors (weights proportional to rank) and aggregated per dataset; vectors are L2‑normalized. Cosine similarity then quantifies alignment of topic, intent, and form distributions across data sources. Data includes real user queries to LLMs (WildChat, LMSYS‑Chat‑1M, ClimateQ&A), human‑human questions (Reddit), benchmark questions (ClimaQA‑Gold/Silver), and knowledge corpora (IPCC AR6, SciDCC). This framework enables fine‑grained comparison of knowledge‑seeking patterns.

## Key Results
- ClimaQA exhibits **63% over‑representation** of atmospheric science topics, **40% higher reliance** on fact‑lookup intents, and a **37% shortfall** in structured answer forms compared to real user queries.
- Real user queries prioritize **policy, energy transition, and advice/writing** intents and favor answer forms such as **paragraphs** and **itemized lists**.
- **Cosine similarities**: Real‑world vs. benchmark topic distributions are low (0.30–0.39), while real‑world vs. human‑human questions are high (0.85–0.86). IPCC AR6 shows strong topic alignment with user needs (similarity up to 0.88).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Existing climate change LLM benchmarks systematically over‑represent atmospheric science topics and fact‑lookup intents, causing misalignment with real‑world user needs.  
- **Mechanism:** Benchmarks like ClimaQA are built primarily from expert‑generated or synthetic questions derived from textbook material, which naturally emphasizes scientific fundamentals and factual recall. This biases the topic‑intent‑form distribution toward atmospheric science and simple answer forms.  
- **Core assumption:** The source material used to create benchmark questions favors academic Climate Science over applied, user‑centric domains.  
- **Evidence:** Abstract and Section 5.1 show 63% over‑representation, 40% higher fact‑lookup, and 37% gap in structured answers.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Knowledge‑seeking behaviors (topic‑intent‑form) are highly consistent between human‑human and human‑LLM interactions.  
- **Mechanism:** A user’s information need is intrinsic; the responder (human or AI) does not alter the fundamental dimensions of the query. Thus, distributions align across interaction types.  
- **Core assumption:** LLMs do not induce users to ask qualitatively different questions.  
- **Evidence:** Abstract reports cosine similarities of 0.85–0.86 between real queries and Reddit.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** IPCC AR6 reports have a topic distribution that closely matches real user knowledge demands, making them ideal for RAG and benchmarking.  
- **Mechanism:** IPCC synthesizes authoritative climate knowledge across physical science, impacts, adaptation, and mitigation, covering a broad yet balanced set of topics that mirror the diversity of user queries.  
- **Core assumption:** The IPCC editorial process yields a topic balance representative of public information needs.  
- **Evidence:** Abstract notes topic similarity up to 0.88 between IPCC and real queries.

  *Why needed:* Provides the analytical lens to quantify misalignment across three orthogonal dimensions (topic, intent, form).  
  *Quick check:* What are the eight major categories of user intents and the eight major categories of expected answer forms?

- **Proactive Knowledge Behaviors Framework**  
  *Why needed:* Structures the comparison of different knowledge‑seeking/provision scenarios and guides data source selection.  
  *Quick check:* Name the three actor types and the two behavior categories within the framework.

- **Cosine Similarity for Distribution Comparison**  
  *Why needed:* Serves as the core quantitative measure to compare topic, intent, and form distributions across datasets.  
  *Quick check:* If two datasets have topic weight vectors [0.5, 0.5] and [1.0, 0.0], what is their cosine similarity?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**  
  Data Ingestion -> LLM-based Annotation Pipeline -> Vector Construction -> Similarity & Analysis  
- **Critical path**  
  Raw query → climate filter (LLM) → initial topic generation → iterative merging → manual taxonomy refinement → topic reassignment → intent/form classification → vectorization → similarity analysis. The longest pole is iterative topic merging.  
- **Design tradeoffs**  
  - LLM vs. human annotation: LLM scales but needs careful prompts; human validation adds overhead.  
  - Single vs. multiple merging configurations: Six configurations improve coverage but multiply compute.  
  - Weighting schemes: Rank‑based weighting emphasizes top labels; simple count weighting is less expressive.  
- **Failure signatures**  
  - Low inter‑annotator agreement (Cohen’s κ < 0.6) → ambiguous taxonomy or poor prompts.  
  - Unexpected “Others” topic dominance (> 20%) → poor domain filtering or granularity mismatch.  
  - Near‑zero similarity between Real‑World and Benchmark groups → data processing error.  
- **First 3 experiments**  
  1. Replicate taxonomy construction on a held‑out subset; verify coarse categories and measure label stability.  
  2. Confirm misalignment: Annotate fresh real queries and benchmark questions; expect > 0.3 topic gap and > 0.4 intent gap.  
  3. Validate IPCC alignment: Re‑annotate IPCC paragraphs; expect topic similarity to Reddit > 0.8.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How can we obtain high‑quality reference answers for large‑scale, open‑ended user queries?**  
  *Basis:* Queries are open‑ended, diverse, and context‑dependent, making large‑scale accurate annotation difficult.  
  *Why unresolved:* Current benchmarks rely on synthetic or expert‑generated questions; scaling to real user queries with reliable answers remains a challenge.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Development and validation of methods (e.g., human‑AI collaboration, active learning) to annotate real queries accurately at scale.

- **Are there other readily accessible, high‑quality knowledge sources besides IPCC AR6 that closely align with real‑world user knowledge needs?**  
  *Basis:* The paper evaluated IPCC AR6 and SciDCC, but other corpora (e.g., national climate reports, educational materials) were not considered.  
  *Why unresolved:* Potential sources may offer better alignment or broader coverage.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Systematic comparison of additional authoritative corpora against real user query distributions using the Topic‑Intent‑Form taxonomy.

- **How can benchmark datasets mitigate biases arising from self‑selected, predominantly English‑speaking users to improve global representativeness?**  
  *Basis:* Users who share interactions may be self‑selected; English dominance risks under‑representing certain populations.  
  *Why unresolved:* Biases may lead to inequitable AI performance across demographic groups.  
  *Resolving evidence:* Collection and analysis of queries from diverse geographic and linguistic regions; measurement of model performance across groups; evaluation of debiasing techniques (e.g., reweighting, data augmentation).

## Limitations
- **Annotation dependency:** The pipeline relies on GPT‑5‑mini, a proprietary model not publicly available; substitutions may shift label distributions and similarity scores.  
- **Limited representativeness:** Real‑world queries come from a few English‑centric platforms (WildChat, LMSYS, ClimateQ&A, Reddit) and may not capture global or demographic diversity.  
- **Taxonomy completeness:** The fixed set of 25 topics, 29 intents, and 29 forms may omit emergent user needs (e.g., climate anxiety, local adaptation).

## Confidence
- Benchmark misalignment: **Medium**
- Human‑human as proxy: **Medium**
- IPCC alignment: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Annotation reproducibility:** Run the full pipeline with an open‑source LLM (e.g., Llama‑3‑70B) on a fixed 500‑sample subset; compute Cohen’s κ against the paper’s labels; target κ > 0.7.  
2. **Generalizability of misalignment:** Augment the Real‑World group with queries from additional platforms (Twitter, Quora, Google Copilot); recalculate Real‑World vs. Benchmark cosine similarities; expect values below 0.4.  
3. **Human‑validated reliability:** Recruit climate‑domain experts to manually annotate 200 samples across all datasets; measure inter‑annotator agreement and expert‑LLM κ; aim for κ ≥ 0.6.
