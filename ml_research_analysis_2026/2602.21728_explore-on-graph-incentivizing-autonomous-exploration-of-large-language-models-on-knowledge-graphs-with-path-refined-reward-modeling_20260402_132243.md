# Explore-on-Graph: Incentivizing Autonomous Exploration of Large Language Models on Knowledge Graphs with Path-refined Reward Modeling

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2602.21728  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21728  
- **Reference Count**: 40  
- **Primary Result**: State-of-the-art on five KGQA benchmarks; improves Hit@1 by 4.6% on CWQ and F1 by 3.8% on WebQSP over prior best.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) frequently hallucinate when answering questions that require reasoning over knowledge graphs (KGs). Existing methods that anchor LLMs to KGs often fail on out-of-distribution reasoning patterns because they rely on fixed demonstrations or handcrafted rules. To address this, the authors propose Explore-on-Graph (EoG), a reinforcement learning framework that trains LLMs to autonomously explore diverse reasoning paths on KGs. EoG uses two reward signals: an outcome reward based on answer correctness (F1) and a novel path-refined reward that encourages coverage of gold reasoning triplets. This combination guides the model toward semantically meaningful exploration while maintaining focus on correct answers.

The method achieves state-of-the-art results on five KGQA benchmarks (CWQ, WebQSP, GrailQA, QALD10-en, 2WikiMultihopQA), outperforming both open-source and closed-source models. Notably, it improves Hit@1 by 4.6% on CWQ and F1 by 3.8% on WebQSP compared to previous best approaches. EoG also exhibits strong out-of-distribution generalization and higher exploration efficiency, demonstrating that incentivizing autonomous discovery via path-aware rewards leads to more faithful and robust reasoning.

## Method Summary
EoG follows a two-stage training pipeline. In the first stage, a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase establishes a strong prior for structured graph reasoning. The SFT dataset is constructed by prompting Gemini-2.5-Flash to generate long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning traces for KGQA examples. These traces are filtered to ensure factual and structural validity, and the base LLM is trained with the standard language modeling objective to produce reasoning paths expressed as triplets.

The second stage employs reinforcement learning with the GRPO algorithm to refine the policy. The LLM interacts with a KG environment, sampling multiple reasoning paths per question. Two reward signals are computed: (1) outcome reward, equal to the F1 score between predicted and gold answer entities; (2) path reward, which measures the proportion of ground-truth reasoning triplets found in the generated thought segment. The final reward is a weighted sum, \(R_{\text{outcome}} + \alpha R_{\text{path}}\), with \(\alpha\) tuned on validation. RL updates encourage the model to discover diverse and correct paths beyond the SFT distribution, improving both accuracy and reasoning coverage.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art performance on all five KGQA benchmarks, surpassing prior open-source and closed-source models.
- +4.6% Hit@1 on ComplexWebQuestions (CWQ) and +3.8% F1 on WebQSP relative to the previous best.
- Strong out-of-distribution generalization: models trained on one dataset maintain high performance when tested on others, with stable OOD-to-IID ratios.
- Higher exploration efficiency and reasoning coverage compared to baseline methods, as measured by reduced search steps and increased triplet coverage.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Outcome Reward Drives Answer Discovery
- **Claim**: Reinforcement learning with an outcome reward that measures final-answer correctness substantially improves retrieval accuracy from KGs.
- **Mechanism**: GRPO optimizes the policy to maximize \(R_{\text{outcome}}(p)\), the F1 score between predicted and gold answer entities. This scalar signal biases the model toward generating sequences that terminate with correct answers.
- **Core assumption**: Final answer correctness alone provides a sufficient learning signal to shape intermediate reasoning steps.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract]: "uses answer-correctness rewards plus a novel path-refined reward."  
  - [section 3.2.1]: Equations (2) and (3) define GRPO and \(R_{\text{outcome}}\).  
  - [corpus]: Direct corpus support is weak; "KnowCoder‑A1" uses outcome supervision for KBQA but under a different paradigm.
- **Break condition**: If outcome reward is the sole signal, the model may ignore intermediate step quality and exploit shortcuts. We observed performance drops of 2–5 F1 points when path reward is removed (Table 2).

### Mechanism 2: Path‑refined Reward Enables Meaningful Exploration
- **Claim**: Augmenting outcome reward with a path‑based signal into a joint reward improves both exploration efficiency and semantic depth of generated reasoning paths.
- **Mechanism**: \(R_{\text{path}}(p)\) matches substrings of gold triplets in the thought segment, and \(R_{\text{joint}} = R_{\text{outcome}} + \alpha R_{\text{path}}\) guides the policy toward covering correct intermediate facts.
- **Core assumption**: Gold reasoning paths exist and their substring coverage correlates with meaningful traversal; exact‑match is a practical proxy.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract]: "path information as additional reward signals to refine exploration process and reduce futile efforts."  
  - [section 3.2.2]: Equation (4) and the Search‑and‑Verify pipeline for obtaining \(r_g\).  
  - [corpus]: Indirect, as "Plan‑on‑Graph'' and "Paths‑over‑Graph'' incorporate path planning but do not use explicit reward shaping; evidence is limited.
- **Break condition**: If gold paths are noisy or incomplete, \(R_{\text{path}}\) can penalize valid alternatives; performance is sensitive to \(\alpha\) (Figure 3).

### Mechanism 3: Two‑Stage Training (SFT → RL) Unlocks Generalization
- **Claim**: Starting with SFT on long CoT data, then RL with outcome + path rewards, yields a model that autonomously discovers novel reasoning patterns and generalizes out‑of‑distribution.
- **Mechanism**: SFT provides a strong prior of structured graph reasoning (via teacher‑generated CoT); RL then refines the policy to maximize answer correctness and path coverage, encouraging diversity beyond the SFT distribution.
- **Core assumption**: Teacher‑generated long CoT captures high‑quality patterns; RL can improve upon this prior via interaction with the true graph environment.
- **Evidence anchors**:  
  - [abstract]: "introduce reinforcement learning during training, which is preceded by a Supervised Fine‑Tuning step."  
  - [section 3.1]: SFT dataset construction from Gemini‑2.5 Flash and filtering; objective (1).  
  - [section 4.4] and [4.7]: EoG outperforms EoGSFT (SFT‑only) and shows robust OOD ratios.
- **Break condition**: If SFT data lacks diversity, RL might not discover novel paths; RL instability (e.g., poor \(\alpha\) choice) can nullify gains.

### Knowledge Graphs and Reasoning Paths
- **Why needed**: The method operates over KGs; entities, relations, and multi‑hop paths are the basic building blocks of reasoning.
- **Quick check**: *What is a knowledge graph and how is a reasoning path formally defined?* (Appendix A: KG = set of triples; reasoning path = chain of connected triples from a start entity to a target.)

- **Why needed**: The RL stage modifies the LLM’s generation policy via GRPO; understanding policy gradients, clipping, and KL regularization is essential to follow the training procedure.

- **Why needed**: SFT establishes the model’s ability to produce structured, graph‑aligned reasoning; without it, RL suffers from reward sparsity and exploration inefficiency.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input (question, begin entity, KG) -> SFT module (initial policy training) -> RL module (GRPO updates) -> Output (reasoning path + answer). Data flow: SFT model provides initialization for RL rollouts; rollouts generate reasoning paths; reward computation (outcome + path) provides feedback; policy is updated via GRPO.
- **Critical path**: The generation must preserve KG structure; the policy outputs are parsed into triples to compute rewards. Key breakpoints occur during SFT (teacher quality), RL rollout (sampling settings), and reward computation (requires gold paths).
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Outcome vs. path reward: pure outcome may produce shallow reasoning; path reward improves depth but needs gold paths.  
  - SFT vs. RL compute: SFT is lightweight; RL uses large batches & many samples but converges in fewer steps (Appendix I).  
  - Base model choice: works on both Qwen2.5‑7B and Llama3.1‑8B, showing generalizability.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - SFT‑only (EoGSFT) significantly underperforms full EoG (Table 2).  
  - Removing path reward harms F1, especially on complex logic (Table 2).  
  - No SFT w/ ICL still lags behind (Table 2).  
  - OOD: EoG maintains stable ratios; SFT‑only degrades (Figure 5).  
  - Excessive \(\alpha\) (> 0.6) lowers performance (Figure 3).
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Ablation of path reward and outcome reward (Table 2).  
  2. Sweep over \(\alpha\) to balance outcome and path reward (Figure 3).  
  3. Exploration efficiency vs. coverage (Table 5).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How can the path‑refined reward be enhanced using advanced semantic matching to better evaluate reasoning path quality beyond exact entity/relation matching?**  
   The current path reward uses substring matching; this is rigid and may fail to reward semantically correct but lexically diverse chains.
2. **What are the scalability limits of EoG in terms of knowledge graph size and complexity, and how can exploration efficiency be maintained for very large KGs?**  
   Experiments used moderate‑sized benchmarks; performance on million‑scale graphs is unknown.
3. **To what extent does EoG's autonomous exploration generalize to entirely unseen knowledge graph schemas and relation types without any fine‑tuning?**  
   OOD tests used datasets sharing the same underlying schema; generalization to completely new domains (e.g., biomedical KGs) remains untested.

## Limitations
- Ambiguous action space definition in the RL environment (how LLM outputs map to KG traversal).
- Incomplete description of the Search‑and‑Verify pipeline for constructing gold reasoning paths (BFS limits, LLM verification prompt, acceptance thresholds).
- Path reward sensitivity to hyperparameter \(\alpha\) and dependence on gold path quality; suboptimal \(\alpha\) or noisy gold paths can degrade performance.
