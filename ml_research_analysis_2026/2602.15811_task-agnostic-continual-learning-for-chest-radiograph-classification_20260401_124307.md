# Task-Agnostic Continual Learning for Chest Radiograph Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.15811
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15811
- **Reference count**: 37
- **Primary result**: CARL-XRay achieves 75.0% task-unknown routing accuracy and AUROC 0.75 on chest X-ray classification with only 0.08% additional parameters, outperforming joint training (62.5% routing, 0.74 AUROC) while forgetting only 0.012.

## Executive Summary
CARL-XRay introduces a framework for task-incremental continual learning in chest radiograph classification, where datasets arrive sequentially and task identity is unknown during inference. The method freezes a large pretrained Swin Transformer backbone and incrementally adds lightweight, dataset-specific adapters and classifier heads. A shared latent task selector, trained with feature-level experience replay and prototype consistency, routes inputs to the correct adapter/head at test time. On two large chest X-ray datasets (MIMIC-CXR and CheXpert), the approach attains high routing accuracy (75.0%) and diagnostic AUROC (0.75) while adding only 0.08% trainable parameters and exhibiting minimal catastrophic forgetting (0.012). This demonstrates that stable task-agnostic inference is feasible in medical imaging with parameter-efficient continual learning.

## Method Summary
CARL-XRay employs a frozen Swin-Large backbone pretrained on ImageNet. Upon arrival of each new dataset (task), a new adapter (a small bottleneck MLP) and a linear classification head are initialized and trained exclusively on that task’s data, then immediately frozen. This isolate‑then‑freeze strategy prevents interference between tasks. A shared latent task selector (an MLP) operates on adapter‑transformed features. During training, the selector is updated using a mixture of current‑task features and a fixed‑size replay buffer of adapted features from previous tasks, ensuring it does not forget earlier task distributions. Task prototypes (running averages of adapted features) provide additional regularization. At inference, for each image, the selector scores all tasks based on their adapter outputs; the highest‑scoring task’s head is used for the final prediction. The training objective for adapters/heads combines masked binary cross‑entropy (handling uncertain/missing labels) with an orthogonality regularizer to diversify features. The selector loss mixes cross‑entropy on the current+replayed batch with a prototype consistency term.

## Key Results
- Routing accuracy under task‑unknown inference: 75.0% (vs. 62.5% for joint training).
- Diagnostic AUROC: 0.75 (task‑unknown) vs. 0.74 (oracle with known task IDs), macro‑averaged across tasks.
- Parameter efficiency: additional trainable parameters only 0.08% of the frozen backbone.
- Minimal forgetting: AUROC drop on first task after learning second is 0.012.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Frozen backbone for stable general features
- **Claim**: A frozen high‑capacity backbone (Swin Transformer) provides stable, generalizable features across sequential datasets, minimizing representational interference.
- **Mechanism**: The backbone weights are held constant during all continual updates. New tasks only modify lightweight, task‑specific adapters and heads. This isolates changes to small, disjoint parameter sets, preventing catastrophic forgetting of the shared representation.
- **Core assumption**: A sufficiently pretrained backbone learns features broadly useful across different chest X‑ray sources; only minor adaptation is needed.
- **Evidence**: Paper states backbone remains frozen throughout; no ablation but rationale is standard in parameter‑efficient transfer learning.
- **Break condition**: If domain shift exceeds adapter capacity or backbone was insufficiently pretrained on medical data, performance degrades.

### Mechanism 2: Isolate‑then‑freeze adapters preserve task‑specific representations
- **Claim**: Training each adapter in isolation and freezing it afterward preserves clear, non‑overlapping representations for each dataset, enabling effective later routing.
- **Mechanism**: Upon arrival of a new dataset, a new adapter/head pair is initialized and trained only on that dataset’s data. After training, these parameters are immediately frozen. This ensures each adapter learns features tailored to its dataset’s distribution without subsequent interference.
- **Core assumption**: Dataset‑specific variations (e.g., acquisition protocols) can be captured by a small bottleneck module applied to shared backbone features, and these transformed spaces remain distinct.
- **Evidence**: The paper’s algorithm explicitly freezes previously learned adapters; routing relies on distinct adapter outputs.
- **Break condition**: If two successive datasets are highly similar, their adapter outputs may not be separable, leading to poor routing.

### Mechanism 3: Selector with replay and prototypes identifies tasks
- **Claim**: A shared latent task selector, trained with feature‑level experience replay and prototype consistency, stably identifies the correct task at inference despite sequential updates and unknown task IDs.
- **Mechanism**: The selector (MLP) takes adapter‑adapted features as input. It is trained on a mixture of current‑task features and a bounded replay buffer of adapted features from previous tasks. Task prototypes (running averages of each task’s adapted features) serve as additional regularization. Replay prevents the selector from forgetting decision boundaries for earlier tasks.
- **Core assumption**: The adapter‑adapted feature space for a given task is consistent enough that stored feature vectors and a centroid suffice to maintain selector performance.
- **Evidence**: Ablation (Table 2): routing accuracy drops to ~14% without replay but reaches 75% with replay, directly proving replay’s role. Prototypes alone give poor performance.
- **Break condition**: If the replay buffer is too small or feature distributions drift severely, the selector’s boundaries erode.

## Foundational Learning
### Catastrophic Forgetting in Neural Networks
- **Why needed**: Continual learning must avoid overwriting weights important for old tasks when learning new ones. Understanding forgetting informs strategies like frozen components and replay.
- **Quick check**: When a network is sequentially trained on two different classification tasks, why does its accuracy on the first task typically drop? Because weight updates for the second task overwrite or interfere with weights that were important for the first task, indicating parameter overlap in the shared representation.

### Parameter‑Efficient Transfer Learning (Adapters/LoRA)
- **Why needed**: CARL‑XRay’s efficiency and stability rely on adapting a large pretrained model via small, structured modifications rather than full fine‑tuning, which would cause forgetting.
- **Quick check**: If you freeze a pretrained transformer and add a small bottleneck MLP after its final layer, what property of the original model’s representations are you assuming? That the pretrained features are already linearly separable for many downstream tasks, so only a small adaptation is needed.

### Multi‑Label Classification with Uncertain/Missing Labels
- **Why needed**: Medical datasets have partially annotated and uncertain labels (e.g., CheXpert’s -1, NaN). Proper loss handling is critical for stable, clinically meaningful learning.
- **Quick check**: How should a loss function handle a target label marked as “uncertain” (-1) versus “missing/not annotated” (NaN)? Uncertain labels should be replaced with soft targets (e.g., uniform distribution over {0,1}) to reflect label ambiguity, while missing labels should be ignored (masked out) because they carry no information.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Frozen Swin‑Large backbone (Φ) → Task‑specific adapter (A_k) → Task‑specific head (H_k)
Adapter output (˜z_k) also → Latent task selector (s) for routing during inference.
Replay buffer (B) stores (˜z, task_id). Prototype memory (M) stores running averages per task.

### Critical path
**Training (for new task k)**:
1. For each image x: extract frozen backbone feature z = Φ(x).
2. Compute adapted feature ˜z_k = A_k(z).
3. Update only A_k and H_k using classification loss (masked BCE) + orthogonality regularizer.
4. In parallel, train selector s using a mixed batch: current‑task (x, k) and replayed features from B. Loss: cross‑entropy on task IDs + prototype consistency on current‑task features.
5. Update prototype M_k via EMA of current‑task adapted features.
6. Add current‑task adapted features (˜z_k) to replay buffer (FIFO).

**Inference (task‑unknown)**:
1. Compute z = Φ(x).
2. For every learned task j, compute adapted feature ˜z_j = A_j(z).
3. Feed each ˜z_j to selector s to get task probability p(t=j|˜z_j).
4. Choose task j* = argmax_j p(t=j|˜z_j).
5. Use head H_j* on ˜z_j* to produce final multi‑label predictions.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Adapter capacity vs. memory**: Larger adapters (e.g., Continuum, 4.61 MB) improve routing but increase memory. Simpler adapters are more efficient but less expressive.
- **Replay buffer size**: Larger buffers better preserve past tasks but consume more memory; 5,000 found optimal (10,000 degraded due to stale features).
- **Frozen backbone vs. adaptability**: Freezing ensures stability but limits learning of dataset‑specific low‑level patterns.

### Failure signatures
- **Routing collapse to most recent task** (e.g., 14% accuracy without replay) indicates insufficient replay.
- **Severe routing imbalance** (e.g., 88% routed to larger initial dataset) indicates prototype similarity alone is insufficient.
- **High routing accuracy but low diagnostic AUC** suggests a mismatch between selector and head quality.
- **Forgetting on early tasks** (AUROC drop >0.05) indicates adapter isolation or replay is inadequate.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Single‑task baseline**: Train only on Task 1 (MIMIC‑CXR) with its adapter/head. Establish upper‑bound AUROC.
2. **Sequential training without replay**: Train Task 1 then Task 2 with no replay buffer; expect severe forgetting and routing accuracy ~14%.
3. **Full CARL‑XRay with replay**: Train sequentially with 5,000‑sample replay buffer; expect minimal forgetting and routing ~75%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does task‑unknown routing accuracy change as the number of sequential tasks increases beyond two?
2. Does CARL‑XRay generalize to other medical imaging modalities (e.g., CT, MRI) and to datasets from diverse institutions with different demographics and imaging equipment?
3. Can adaptive replay strategies (e.g., selecting diverse or important features) reduce the required replay buffer size while maintaining or improving task‑unknown routing accuracy and minimizing forgetting?

## Limitations
- Adapter architecture details (dimensions, branches) for the best Continuum variant are not fully specified, hindering exact replication.
- Replay buffer sampling strategy (batch ratio, per‑task distribution) is ambiguous; uniform sampling may not be optimal.
- Prototype EMA decay rate (η) and other hyperparameters are not provided; optimal values may be dataset‑dependent.
- Evaluation is limited to only two chest X‑ray datasets; order sensitivity observed but not deeply analyzed.
- Reliance on frozen ImageNet‑pretrained backbone may not capture medical‑specific features; performance could improve with medical pretraining.
