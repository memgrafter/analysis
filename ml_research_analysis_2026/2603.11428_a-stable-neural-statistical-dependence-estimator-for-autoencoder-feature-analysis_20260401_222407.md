# A Stable Neural Statistical Dependence Estimator for Autoencoder Feature Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.11428
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11428
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Proposes a stable neural dependence estimator for autoencoders using orthonormal density-ratio decomposition and additive Gaussian noise, achieving consistent substitution patterns and stable estimates (~28.5 vs MINE's ~2.8 on two-moon).

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the ill-posed nature of statistical dependence measurement in deterministic autoencoders and the instability of the MINE estimator due to re-pairing. It introduces a neural dependence estimator based on an orthonormal decomposition of the density ratio, optimized via an efficient NMF-like scalar cost that avoids input concatenation and marginal re-pairing. By assuming additive Gaussian noise on features and reconstructions, the dependence becomes well-defined across inputs, latents, and reconstructions. Experiments on two-moon and MNIST demonstrate consistent substitution patterns (e.g., D(X,Y') ≈ D(Y,Y')) and markedly more stable estimates compared to MINE. The method is computationally efficient, exhibits sequential convergence of singular values, and even enables feature learning without a decoder.

## Method Summary
The approach consists of: (1) training a standard autoencoder (encoder E, decoder D) to minimize reconstruction MSE; (2) freezing the autoencoder and adding independent Gaussian noise to encoder outputs (Y' = Y + √v_p·ε) and decoder outputs (cX' = cX + √v_q·ε), where v_q is set to the final MSE and v_p is chosen empirically (≈10⁻⁴ for 1D features); (3) training two separate multi-output neural networks f(X) and g(Y) with ReLU final layers (output dimension K, e.g., 2000) to maximize the NMF-like cost:
c = [E_{batch}(∑_{k=1}^K f_k(X)g_k(Y))]^2 / ∑_{i,j}(R_F ⊙ R_G)_{i,j}, where R_F and R_G are the within-batch autocorrelation matrices of f and g outputs, respectively. This cost approximates Rényi's order-2 mutual information and avoids re-pairing. For decoder-free feature learning, the autoencoder is omitted and noise is added directly to inputs X and latent Y, with the same objective.

## Key Results
- Consistent substitution patterns: D(X,Y') ≈ D(Y,Y') and D(Y',cX') ≈ D(cX,cX') hold across datasets and noise levels.
- Stable estimates: On two-moon, NMF-DR yields encoder dependence ~28.5 while MINE gives ~2.8.
- Computational efficiency: The NMF-like cost scales linearly with batch size and exhibits sequential convergence of singular values during training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper’s stability and validity stem from three interconnected mechanisms. First, additive Gaussian noise resolves the ill-posedness of dependence in deterministic autoencoders by ensuring proper joint distributions; the noise level v_p is effectively tiny (∼10⁻⁵) due to implicit regularization. Second, the orthonormal density-ratio decomposition with an NMF-like scalar cost eliminates the need for input concatenation and marginal re-pairing, which are sources of instability in MINE; the cost can safely use large K and avoids matrix inverses. Third, under the Gaussian assumptions and a well-trained decoder (approximately inverting the encoder), the singular functions of the density ratio for pairs (X,Y') and (Y,Y') become interchangeable, explaining the observed substitution pattern and indicating an isomorphism between input and latent spaces.

## Foundational Learning
- **Mutual Information and f-divergences:** The method estimates Rényi's order-2 mutual information via an f-divergence (χ²-type). Understanding f-divergences clarifies why the NMF-like cost bounds this quantity and differs from MINE's KL-based variational bound. *Quick check:* How does the choice of f-divergence affect estimator bias-variance tradeoff?
- **Density ratio estimation via singular decomposition:** The core technique approximates the density ratio p(X,Y)/(p(X)p(Y)) as ∑ √λ_k ϕ_k(X)ψ_k(Y) with orthonormal ϕ_k, ψ_k, linking dependence to operator theory and avoiding direct density estimation. *Quick check:* Why does whitening the network outputs before computing the cross-correlation matrix recover the singular functions, and what does the SVD of that matrix represent?
- **Autoencoder variational interpretation:** The Gaussian conditional assumptions p(Y|X)=N(Y;E(X),v_p) and q(X|Y)=N(X;D(Y),v_q) make the autoencoder objective equivalent to MSE plus constants, connecting reconstruction error to v_q. *Quick check:* If the autoencoder minimizes MSE, why is the optimal v_q equal to the MSE, and what does that imply about the "Gaussian ball" around reconstructions?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** 
X -> Encoder (E) -> Y -> [Gaussian noise (v_p)] -> Y' -> Estimator (f,g) 
Y -> Decoder (D) -> cX -> [Gaussian noise (v_q)] -> cX' -> Estimator (f,g)
Estimator networks f and g output K-dimensional nonnegative features used in the NMF-like cost.

**Critical path:** 
1. Train autoencoder (E,D) to convergence on reconstruction MSE.
2. Freeze autoencoder weights; compute v_q = final MSE; choose v_p (empirically ∼10⁻⁴ for 1D latents).
3. Generate noisy pairs (X,Y') and (Y',cX') for dependence analysis.
4. Train estimator networks f and g to maximize the NMF-like cost using mini-batch statistics.
5. Extract singular values λ_k via whitening and SVD of cross-correlation to quantify dependence.
For decoder-free learning, skip autoencoder and add noise to X and Y directly.

**Design tradeoffs:** 
- *NMF vs. trace/logdet costs:* NMF is more scalable (no matrix inverses) and stable for large K, but estimates Rényi-2 MI instead of Shannon MI; trace/logdet may be unbiased but costly.
- *Estimator dimension K:* Larger K increases capacity but risks overfitting when K > sample size; NMF tolerates K larger than true rank better than trace/logdet.
- *Noise variances:* v_p too large degrades reconstruction; too small causes estimation instability. v_q is fixed to MSE. Larger input noise v_X improves generalization in decoder-free learning.
- *Batch size:* Full-batch gives cleaner estimates but is heavy; minibatches reduce discretization bias in high dimensions.

**Failure signatures:** 
- Estimator diverges (>1000) without added noise: indicates ill-posed static problem; fix by adding noise.
- Cost collapses to zero or NaN: likely due to numerical issues; add small ϵ inside log, ensure nonnegative outputs, add input noise.
- Overestimation when K ≫ sample size: bias at initialization; reduce K or increase dataset size.
- No substitution pattern (D(X,Y') ≠ D(Y,Y')): autoencoder poorly trained or noise levels mismatched; check MSE and noise scales.
- Extremely sparse estimator outputs: may indicate overfitting or poor optimization; try larger K or input noise.

**First three experiments:** 
1. **Two-moon comparison:** Train autoencoder (2D→1D). Compute NMF-DR, LOGDET, TRACE, MINE, kernel methods. Verify NMF-DR ≈28.5 for encoder pairs vs MINE ≈2.8; check substitution pattern; observe smooth NMF curves vs MINE dips.
2. **Noise sweep:** Fix latent dim=1, sweep v_p from 10⁻⁷ to 10⁻¹. Confirm MSE increases with v_p, dependence decays, but substitution pattern persists across v_p.
3. **Feature dimension sweep (MNIST):** Increase latent dim from 1 to 5. Monitor MSE (should decrease) and dependence (should increase); verify substitution pattern holds up to dim=3 (decoder output dim capped at 2000).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Intrinsic v_p in deterministic autoencoders:** What is the precise value of the implicit feature variance v_p in noise-free training? Evidence suggests ∼10⁻⁸–10⁻⁹, but empirical sweeps give ∼10⁻⁴–10⁻⁵. A theoretical derivation or controlled measurement is needed.
2. **Concatenated input noise discrepancy:** Why does adding uniform noise to inputs reduce overestimation for two-moon but not for MNIST at later training stages? The paper shows the phenomenon but lacks explanation; systematic experiments across data complexities are required.
3. **Extreme sparsity in static setting:** Why are estimator outputs and density-ratio matrix extremely sparse in the deterministic case? This pattern vanishes with noise; its cause (overfitting, implicit bias, manifold properties) is not analyzed.
4. **Link between singular value convergence and reconstruction:** How do the sequential convergence of singular values during training and the coarse-to-fine evolution of reconstructions relate theoretically? No quantitative model connects spectral growth to MSE reduction.
5. **Equivalence conditions for decoder-free learning:** Under what exact conditions (noise levels, capacity, data dimension) does maximizing the NMF-based dependence objective with noisy inputs recover features equivalent to an end-to-end autoencoder? Empirical evidence exists but no theoretical guarantee.

## Limitations
- The optimal Gaussian noise variance v_p is chosen empirically; no principled rule exists for arbitrary datasets or latent dimensions.
- The estimator’s ReLU nonnegativity constraint may limit expressive power compared to unbounded MINE networks, potentially biasing estimates.
- The method relies on additive Gaussian noise; for highly non-Gaussian latent manifolds (e.g., discrete codes), the measured dependence may not reflect true relationships.

## Confidence
- **Noise makes dependence well-defined:** Medium-High
- **Stability of NMF-like cost:** Medium
- **Substitution pattern as isomorphism:** Low-Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Non-Gaussian stress test:** Train an autoencoder with discrete latent codes (e.g., VAE with Gumbel-Softmax) or heavy-tailed data; verify whether the substitution pattern breaks.
2. **K–sample size scaling:** On MNIST, vary estimator output dimension K (100–10,000) and dataset size N (1k–100k); measure bias (vs ground-truth MI if available) and stability (variance across runs).
3. **Large-scale comparison:** Apply NMF-DR and MINE to a convolutional autoencoder on CIFAR-10 (latent dim 32) using full training; compare convergence speed, estimation variance, and re-pairing artifacts.
