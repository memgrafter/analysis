# SANA I2I: A Text Free Flow Matching Framework for Paired Image to Image Translation with a Case Study in Fetal MRI Artifact Reduction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv: 2604.00298
- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00298
- Citations: 28
- Result: Reduces fetal MRI artifact FID from 60.14 to 51.74 on real data; requires 2-5 inference steps; SSIM 0.724/MAE 0.039 on synthetic.

## Executive Summary
SANA-I2I proposes a text-free flow-matching approach for paired image-to-image translation, eliminating the need for textual conditioning by using the source image latent as the conditioning variable. The method leverages a conditional velocity field in latent space, trained on synthetically corrupted fetal MRI pairs. On real corrupted images, it achieves significant distributional improvement (FID: 60.14→51.74; KID: 0.0310→0.0237) with highly efficient sampling (2-5 steps). The optimal guidance scale is around 1.0-1.1, balancing artifact suppression and anatomical preservation.

## Method Summary
SANA-I2I adapts the SANA text-to-image diffusion transformer by removing all text-based components. The conditioning variable `y` is redefined as the latent representation of the source image, injected via a `PatchEmbed` pathway analogous to ControlNet’s control branch. The model operates in the compressed latent space of a Deep Compression Autoencoder (DC-AE) and is trained with a Flow Matching objective to predict the velocity field that transports noise to the target clean image distribution, conditioned on the source. Training uses synthetic pairs: clean fetal MRI images are corrupted with realistic motion artifacts (via Duffy et al.’s simulator) within an SSIM severity range of 0.6-0.9. Classifier-free guidance is implemented by randomly dropping only the image-based `y` during training while preserving the control signal, enabling tunable guidance at inference without text. Hyperparameters: 100 epochs, batch size 16, CAME optimizer (lr=1e-4), linear flow schedule, FP16 with FP32 attention, gradient clipping 0.1.

## Key Results
- Synthetic evaluation (full-reference): SSIM = 0.724, MAE = 0.039.
- Real fetal MRI evaluation (distribution-based): FID reduced from 60.14 to 51.74; KID reduced from 0.0310 to 0.0237.
- Inference efficiency: High-quality reconstruction in 2-5 steps; optimal guidance scale = 1.0-1.1.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Text-Free Conditioning
**Claim:** Removing textual conditioning allows the model to learn a transformation directly from source-target image pairs, avoiding semantic ambiguity and unnecessary stochasticity inherent in language prompts for paired translation tasks.  
**Mechanism:** The framework redefines the conditioning variable `y` as a latent representation of the source image, injecting it via the same `PatchEmbed` pathway used for the control signal. Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is maintained by randomly dropping only this image-based `y` during training, enabling scalable guidance at inference without text.  
**Core assumption:** The mapping from a corrupted latent distribution to a clean one is sufficiently specified by the paired source image; textual semantics are redundant or detrimental for this deterministic transformation.  
**Evidence:** Abstract states reliance on paired images; Section 3.1 describes redefinition of `y` and CFG; neighboring corpus focuses on text-guided or unpaired settings, making this design novel.  
**Break condition:** If the source image lacks sufficient information to constrain the target distribution (e.g., severe, non-deterministic corruption), the model may fail to produce consistent outputs even with CFG.

### Mechanism 2: Latent-Space Flow Matching
**Claim:** Modeling the image translation as a conditional velocity field in latent space via Flow Matching (FM) enables efficient, high-fidelity generation with minimal inference steps.  
**Mechanism:** FM directly learns the vector field that transports a noise distribution to the target image distribution, conditioned on the source image latent. By operating in the compressed DC-AE latent space and using SANA’s optimized architecture, the model achieves stable training and fast sampling (2–5 steps).  
**Core assumption:** The optimal transport path between corrupted and clean latent distributions can be efficiently approximated by a flow-matching objective, and this latent-space mapping preserves anatomical detail upon decoding.  
**Evidence:** Abstract mentions learning a conditional velocity field; Section 3 describes latent FM and DC-AE compression; Section 6.4 shows image quality plateaus beyond 5 steps.  
**Break condition:** If the latent compression discards critical anatomical features or the flow path deviates significantly from the true transport, decoding may yield blurry or distorted anatomy.

### Mechanism 3: Synthetic Paired Data Training
**Claim:** Training on synthetically corrupted/clean pairs provides a viable supervised signal for real fetal MRI artifact reduction, despite the synthetic-to-real domain gap.  
**Mechanism:** Real clean fetal MRI is artificially degraded with simulated motion artifacts to create synthetic corrupted images. The model learns the mapping from synthetic corrupted to clean. The synthetic artifacts mimic real motion patterns; the model’s distribution-based metrics on real corrupted images improve, suggesting generalization.  
**Core assumption:** The synthetic artifact model captures key statistics of real motion corruption; the gap between synthetic and real corrupted domains is smaller than the gap between corrupted and clean domains.  
**Evidence:** Section 4 describes simulation using Duffy et al.; Section 6.2 shows FID/KID improvement on real data; Section 6 notes training relies on synthetic degradations but evaluation on real data confirms robustness.  
**Break condition:** If synthetic artifacts do not cover the diversity of real motion patterns, the model may overfit to synthetic cues and fail on certain real cases, potentially harming some anatomies.

## Foundational Learning
- **Flow Matching (FM):** A generative modeling objective that directly predicts the velocity field of a continuous-time diffusion process, enabling deterministic sampling and often requiring fewer steps than traditional diffusion. *Why needed:* Understanding FM is essential to grasp SANA-I2I's efficiency and stability. *Quick check:* How does FM's direct velocity prediction differ from diffusion's noise prediction, and why might this lead to faster sampling?
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) in Conditional Flows:** A technique to increase the conditioning strength at inference without retraining, by interpolating between conditional and unconditional model outputs. *Why needed:* Here, CFG allows tuning artifact suppression vs. detail preservation without text, using image-based conditioning. *Quick check:* In this text-free setting, what does the “conditional” vs. “unconditional” branch represent during CFG training, and how does dropping `y` affect output?
- **Synthetic Paired Data Generation for Medical Imaging:** Creating supervised training pairs by artificially corrupting clean images to mimic real artifacts. *Why needed:* Real paired fetal MRI is unobtainable; synthetic pairing is the cornerstone of this work. *Quick check:* Why is the SSIM-based severity range `(0.6, 0.9)` chosen, and how might overly strong or weak synthetic artifacts affect learning?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
The pipeline comprises four main components: (1) DC-AE encoder compresses input images (512×512) into a low-dimensional latent; (2) SANA backbone (DiT) predicts the velocity field, with no text embedding branch; (3) Conditioning injector embeds the source image latent via `PatchEmbed` and injects it via cross-attention/residual blocks similar to ControlNet; (4) CFG logic randomly drops the conditioning latent `y` during training (while keeping control active) and applies a guidance scale >1 at inference.

### Critical Path
Input source image → DC-AE encoding → combine with noise `x` at time `t` to form conditioning `y` → SANA backbone → velocity prediction → integrate ODE (flow matching) → decode target latent → output image.

### Design Tradeoffs
- *Text-free vs. text-conditioned:* Removing text eliminates language dependency and stochasticity but loses semantic steering (e.g., selective artifact removal). For fetal MRI, the goal is fixed, making this acceptable.
- *Synthetic data:* Enables supervised learning but risks synthetic bias; mitigated by using realistic simulation and evaluating on real data.
- *Few-step sampling:* Highly efficient but may trade off fine detail compared to longer schedules (not reported).

### Failure Signatures
- **Over-smoothing:** High guidance scale (>1.1) washes out fine anatomical boundaries.
- **Luminance shift:** Model may globally brighten/darken images, hurting SSIM/MAE but sometimes improving perceptual quality.
- **Residual artifacts:** Insufficient steps (<2) or low guidance (<1.0) leave motion ghosting.
- **Training collapse:** If CFG incorrectly drops both `y` and control, conditioning breaks → outputs degrade to noise or input.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Full-reference on synthetic (C→B):** Compute SSIM and MAE between generated and clean ground truth. Expected: SSIM ~0.72, MAE ~0.039. Note: lower than prior method due to luminance changes—interpret qualitatively.
2. **Distribution-based on real (A→B):** Compute FID and KID between corrected real corrupted images and clean reference set. Expected: FID reduction from ~60 to ~52. This is the key real-world metric.
3. **Ablation: steps and guidance:** Fix guidance=1.0, vary steps [2,5,10,20,40]; then fix steps=5, vary guidance (1.0–1.9). Validate optimal: steps=5, guidance=1.0–1.1 (best FID, detail preservation).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How well do synthetically generated motion artifacts using the Duffy simulator capture the full diversity and statistical distribution of real fetal MRI motion artifacts? The paper assumes sufficient realism but does not quantitatively compare synthetic vs. real artifact distributions.
2. Are full-reference metrics like SSIM and MAE fundamentally inadequate for evaluating medical image translation tasks where clinically meaningful intensity normalization may occur? The paper notes they penalize beneficial luminance adjustments but offers no validated alternative.
3. To what extent does the removal of textual conditioning generalize beyond fetal MRI? Can SANA-I2I handle other paired I2I tasks with different modality shifts, artifact types, or anatomical structures? The claim of broader applicability is not empirically tested.
4. How robust is SANA-I2I to variations in the synthetic training data generation process, particularly the severity parameter range (SSIM bounds) and the specific motion model used? The paper uses a fixed SSIM range (0.6-0.9) and specific simulator without sensitivity analysis.
5. What is the practical utility of the "BIS" variant (dropping both conditioning and control) for medical imaging, and under what conditions might it be preferable to the primary approach? The paper notes it yields inferior artifact suppression for fetal MRI but does not explore its potential uses elsewhere.

## Limitations
- CFG training dropout rate (`p_drop`) is unreported, critically affecting guidance strength and unconditional learning.
- Architectural injection details for the image-based condition `y` lack layer specificity, increasing risk of implementation mistakes.
- Synthetic artifact fidelity is unquantified; the assumption that the Duffy simulator matches real fetal motion patterns remains untested.

## Confidence
- Text-free FM superiority: Medium
- Sampling efficiency (2-5 steps): High
- Synthetic-to-real generalization: Medium
