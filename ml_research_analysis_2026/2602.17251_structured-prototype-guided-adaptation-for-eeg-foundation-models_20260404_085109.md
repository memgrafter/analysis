# Structured Prototype-Guided Adaptation for EEG Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17251
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17251
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** SCOPE improves EEG foundation model adaptation under label scarcity, e.g., +7.6–10.9 Cohen’s Kappa on sleep staging and +6.8–12.3 AUPRC on emotion/workload tasks, with only 2–5% trainable parameters versus full fine-tuning.

## Executive Summary
EEG foundation models (EFMs) suffer significant performance degradation when adapted with limited labeled subjects, not solely due to data scarcity but because of a structural mismatch between noisy, scarce labels and the highly plastic, large parameter space of EFMs. This leads to destructive overwriting of pretrained representations via full fine-tuning and instability in self-training. SCOPE addresses this with a two-stage framework: first, it constructs reliable external supervision by combining a geometry-regularized task-prior network, balanced prototype clustering, and confidence-aware pseudo-label fusion; second, it freezes the EFM backbone and adapts it using a lightweight prototype-conditioned adapter (ProAdapter). Across five EFM backbones and three EEG tasks, SCOPE yields substantial gains over full fine-tuning and parameter-efficient methods, demonstrating that structured, class-level guidance can stabilize adaptation under label constraints.

## Method Summary
SCOPE operates in two stages. **Stage I (Supervision Construction):** A lightweight Task-Prior Network (TPN) is trained on the small labeled set with an Exponential Moving Average (ETF) geometric regularization loss to promote inter-class separation. Unlabeled data embeddings from the TPN are clustered into balanced class prototypes via Sinkhorn-constrained optimization. Pseudo-labels are generated by fusing TPN logits and prototype similarity scores using Dempster-Shafer theory, assigning a confidence γ to each sample; only samples with γ > ρ (threshold) are retained. **Stage II (Adaptation):** The EFM backbone is frozen. A ProAdapter module—inserted into the last L layers—modulates features conditioned on the prototype similarity vector. The adapter and a task-specific classifier are trained with a semi-supervised loss: a supervised loss on labeled data after a warm-up period, plus a pseudo-supervised loss weighted by γ on the high-confidence unlabeled samples.

## Key Results
- On ISRUC sleep staging, SCOPE improves Cohen’s Kappa by +7.6 to +10.9 over full fine-tuning across five EFM backbones.
- On emotion (SEED) and workload (Mental Arithmetic) tasks, SCOPE increases AUPRC by +6.8 to +12.3.
- SCOPE uses only 2–5% trainable parameters compared to full fine-tuning, yet consistently outperforms both full fine-tuning and LoRA under limited supervision.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** EFMs degrade under limited supervision due to a structural mismatch between noisy, scarce labels and the highly plastic parameter space, not merely data scarcity.
- **Mechanism:** Full fine-tuning lets gradients from limited/noisy labeled data overwrite transferable pretrained representations across all layers, causing optimization drift. This is exacerbated by EEG’s high inter-subject variability, promoting shortcut learning.
- **Core assumption:** Pretrained representations contain useful structure that should be preserved; limited supervision is insufficient to reliably retrain all parameters.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] explicit statement of structural mismatch; [page 2, Figure 2] layer-wise CKA degradation under limited supervision; weak direct corpus support from neighboring papers.
- **Break condition:** Abundant supervision mitigates mismatch; fully frozen backbone preserves knowledge but prevents adaptation.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Standard self-training fails for EFMs under limited supervision because model confidence poorly correlates with accuracy and predictions are unstable early in training.
- **Mechanism:** With few subjects, EFMs show early-stage instability and overconfidence, leading to error amplification when pseudo-labels are generated from the model’s own predictions.
- **Core assumption:** Self-training requires a stable model with well-calibrated confidence; EFMs under limited labels violate this.
- **Evidence anchors:** [page 2, Figure 2(A)] early-stage instability/collapse across seeds; [page 2, Figure 2(C)] calibration curve showing overconfidence; [page 2] section on EFMs failing to provide reliable self-guidance.
- **Break condition:** Model becomes stable and calibrated before pseudo-labeling (e.g., after supervised warm-up or with a reliable external teacher).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** SCOPE’s two-stage design stabilizes EFM fine-tuning by providing persistent, structured supervision and constraining updates to align with class-level geometry.
- **Mechanism:** Stage I builds reliable pseudo-labels: (1) TPN with ETF regularization induces inter-class separation; (2) Sinkhorn-constrained clustering yields balanced prototypes capturing intra-class variability; (3) Dempster-Shafer fusion combines evidence and assigns confidence. Stage II freezes the backbone and uses a lightweight ProAdapter that modulates features based on prototype similarity, ensuring updates are conditioned on the external structured signal rather than noisy sample-wise labels.
- **Core assumption:** A structured, class-level representation from a geometrically regularized task-prior provides more stable supervision than raw predictions; conditioning parameter updates on this structure prevents destructive overwriting.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] two-stage description; [page 4, Figure 3] pipeline overview; [page 5-6] methodological details; [page 8, Table 1] consistent improvements over baselines.
- **Break condition:** Poorly learned external supervision (e.g., ETF too weak/strong, prototype collapse) or mis-designed ProAdapter (too deep, improper conditioning) can reintroduce instability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Semi-Supervised Learning with Pseudo-Labeling:** SCOPE leverages abundant unlabeled EEG data. Key to preventing error amplification: confidence thresholding, agreement between multiple evidence sources, and a stable teacher model.
- **Prototype-Based Metric Learning:** Prototypes provide a robust class-level representation capturing intra-class variability. Sinkhorn constraints enforce balanced assignment, preventing collapse onto dominant modes in imbalanced EEG data.
- **Dempster-Shafer Theory for Evidence Fusion:** Combines independent evidence (task-prior logits and prototype similarity) into a confidence score, explicitly handling conflict and providing normalized belief mass. In the singleton-only, no-ignorance case, it degenerates to product of normalized masses, encouraging agreement.

## Architecture Onboarding
Task-Prior Network (TPN) -> Prototype Bank -> Confidence-Aware Fusion -> ProAdapter -> Classifier

**Critical path:**  
Stage I: Train TPN on labeled data (Dl) with ETF loss → Infer TPN embeddings on unlabeled data (Du) → Initialize/refine prototypes via Sinkhorn on Dl ∪ Du(prior labels) → Fuse TPN & prototype predictions on Du to obtain pseudo-labels (ŷj) and confidence (γj).  
Stage II: Freeze EFM backbone → Insert ProAdapter into last 3 layers → Train (ProAdapter + classifier) on Dl (supervised) + Du (pseudo-supervised weighted by γj) with 10-epoch supervised warm-up.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Adapter depth (L):** Deeper increases capacity but risks overfitting; too shallow limits adaptation. (Ablation: U-curve, optimal L=3.)  
- **Confidence threshold (ρ):** Low ρ admits more samples but with noise; high ρ restricts to easy samples. (Optimal ρ≈0.5.)  
- **Prototypes per class (M):** Few limit intra-class capture; many introduce noise. (Optimal M=3.)  
- **ETF loss weight (λETF):** Balances geometric regularization vs. task fitting. (Optimal λETF=0.1.)

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Poor pseudo-label quality:** Agreement between TPN and prototypes drops; coverage or accuracy falls. Check: agreement rate and pseudo-label accuracy on held-out labeled set.  
- **Adapter overfitting/instability:** High seed variance or early collapse. Check: layer-wise CKA to pretrained features; adapter vs. backbone gradient norms.  
- **Prototype collapse to majority classes:** Very low coverage for rare classes. Check: per-class samples with γ>ρ; prototype assignments per class.  
- **Training instability (loss spikes/NaN):** Likely λETF too large or BatchNorm issues. Remedy: reduce λETF to 0.05, ensure no frozen BatchNorm, use gradient clipping.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Ablation on Supervision Construction:** Remove ETF guidance, prototype clustering, or both. Isolates contribution of structured external supervision. (Table 2 shows largest drop when removing supervision construction entirely.)  
2. **Ablation on ProAdapter Design:** Remove ProAdapter (reverts to self-training on frozen backbone), remove prototype conditioning, or remove confidence weighting. Isolates adapter’s role and importance of structured conditioning. (Table 2 shows massive drop without ProAdapter.)  
3. **Sensitivity Analysis:** Sweep adapter depth L and confidence threshold ρ. Finds efficiency-accuracy tradeoff and validates chosen hyperparameters. (Figures 4 & 5; Tables 4-6.)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Why do EEG foundation models exhibit early-stage training instability and overconfident predictions when fine-tuned with limited labeled subjects, and how does this relate to the alleged structural mismatch between supervision and model parameters?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper documents instability and collapse during early training (Figure 2A) and overconfidence in calibration curves (Figure 2C) when using only 30% of subjects for supervision in Section 1, attributing these to a “structural mismatch” but without a mechanistic analysis.
- **Why unresolved:** No investigation into the dynamics of prediction calibration, gradient noise, or representation drift that could explain why limited supervision disproportionately affects EFMs compared to smaller models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Measurements of calibration error and gradient variance over time; ablations that vary label noise levels, backbone size, and representation similarity (e.g., CKA) to establish causal links; testing whether explicit confidence regularization (e.g., temperature scaling, label smoothing) can suppress overconfidence without harming accuracy.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What theoretical or data-driven principle can determine the optimal number of class prototypes per class and the depth of ProAdapter insertion for a given EEG foundation backbone and downstream task, rather than relying on empirical grid search?
- **Basis in paper:** Ablation studies in Appendix F show non‑monotonic effects of the prototype count M (Figure 8) and adapter depth L (Figure 4) on performance, with best values around M=3 and L=3 for two backbones on ISRUC. These are chosen empirically and may not generalize to other architectures or domains.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not relate these hyperparameters to intrinsic properties of the data (e.g., intra‑class variance, cluster separability) or model capacity, leaving no guidance for new settings.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Derive a relationship based on clustering quality metrics (e.g., silhouette score) or manifold complexity; conduct extensive cross‑task and cross‑backbone experiments to validate if a simple rule (e.g., based on dataset size or number of classes) can predict optimal M and L.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the two‑stage training strategy—first constructing external supervision, then updating a frozen backbone with ProAdapter—offer tangible benefits over an end‑to‑end joint training of all components (TPN, prototype bank, adapter, and classifier), and if so, why?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 3.4 and Table 2 describe the two‑stage pipeline with warm‑up and interleaved batches. Ablations replace it with “w/o Warm‑up”, “sequential”, or “Two‑Stage” (supervised only then unsupervised) and show degraded performance, yet no comparison to fully joint training is provided.
- **Why unresolved:** It remains unclear whether decoupling is strictly necessary for stability or whether a properly regularized joint approach (e.g., with multi‑task losses or gradient constraints) could match or exceed the proposed method while being simpler and faster.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implement joint training where the TPN, prototypes, and ProAdapter are updated simultaneously, possibly with auxiliary losses to prevent catastrophic forgetting; compare convergence speed, training stability (e.g., loss variance), and final generalization across multiple backbones and tasks.

## Limitations
- Incomplete hyperparameter specifications (e.g., λ_ϖ, λ_tanh, TPN conv details) and missing subject splits for SEED/Mental Arithmetic hinder exact replication.
- Generalizability beyond three small-scale, binary/5-class EEG tasks is unproven; scalability to many classes or pathological EEG (e.g., seizures) unknown.
- Potential overfitting to specific preprocessing choices (200 Hz resampling, 0.5–45 Hz filter) not evaluated against alternative pipelines.

## Confidence
- **Structural mismatch theory:** High (cross-backbone CKA degradation, consistent full fine-tuning failure under limited labels).
- **Self-training failure:** Medium (calibration evidence but limited seed analysis; needs larger-scale verification).
- **SCOPE design efficacy:** High (substantial gains across 5 backbones, thorough ablations confirming each component’s necessity).

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate SCOPE on additional EEG benchmarks (e.g., TUSZ, BCI Competition IV) with >5 classes and diverse montages to test generality.
2. Systematically vary external supervision quality: adjust TPN capacity, prototype count M, and ETF strength; measure pseudo-label accuracy on held-out labeled data and correlate with final performance.
