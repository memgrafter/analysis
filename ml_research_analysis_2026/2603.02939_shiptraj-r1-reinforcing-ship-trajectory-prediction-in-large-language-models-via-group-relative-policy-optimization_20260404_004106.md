# ShipTraj-R1: Reinforcing Ship Trajectory Prediction in Large Language Models via Group Relative Policy Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02939
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02939
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** Achieves state-of-the-art ship trajectory prediction with the lowest Final Displacement Error (FDE) and Average Displacement Error (ADE) on real-world maritime datasets CSJP and CFDP.

## Executive Summary
ShipTraj-R1 reformulates the ship trajectory prediction problem as a text-to-text generation task using large language models (LLMs). It introduces dynamic prompts that include historical trajectories of nearby conflicting ships, detected via a quaternion ship domain model, to elicit adaptive chain-of-thought reasoning about collision risk and social interactions. A rule-based reward mechanism combines format compliance with geodesic-distance-based accuracy metrics to guide optimization. The model is fine-tuned using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on a Qwen3 backbone, which stabilizes reinforcement learning by normalizing advantages within groups of sampled completions and applying a KL penalty to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Evaluated on the CSJP and CFDP datasets, ShipTraj-R1 significantly outperforms prior LLM baselines and deep learning methods, achieving notably lower FDE and ADE.

## Method Summary
The method centers on three key innovations. First, it constructs dynamic input prompts that incorporate the target ship's observed trajectory alongside those of neighboring ships identified as collision conflicts. This contextual information is designed to trigger explicit reasoning about spatial relationships and safety constraints before outputting a predicted trajectory. Second, a dual-component rule-based reward evaluates both the syntactic structure of the output (ensuring `think` and `answer` tags) and its numerical accuracy against ground truth, using Vincenty geodesic distance with thresholded tolerances. Third, reinforcement fine-tuning via GRPO updates the LLM policy by sampling multiple completions per prompt, computing group-relative advantages from the rewards, and applying a clipped objective with KL regularization to maintain proximity to the reference model. This combination allows the model to learn precise, context-aware trajectory forecasts without requiring manually annotated reasoning traces.

## Key Results
- On the CFDP dataset, ShipTraj-R1-8B achieves an FDE of 0.000311 and an ADE of 3.8912e-07, reducing error by over 50% compared to the Traj-LLM baseline (FDE 0.000674, ADE 9.6587e-06) and the base Qwen3-8B model (FDE 0.000474, ADE 6.7403e-07).
- The approach also sets new state-of-the-art on the CSJP dataset, outperforming prior deep learning methods.
- Ablation studies confirm the importance of conflicting ship context and both reward components (format and accuracy) for achieving high performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Dynamic prompts containing conflicting ship trajectories induce adaptive chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning that improves collision-aware prediction.
- **Mechanism:** The prompt provides spatio-temporal context of nearby ships with collision risk (detected via a quaternion ship domain model), forcing the LLM to reason about social interactions and safety constraints before generating coordinates. This context acts as a cognitive scaffold, prompting the model to perform explicit spatial and social reasoning rather than merely imitating historical patterns.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs can be steered to perform domain-specific, safety-critical reasoning through carefully constructed contextual prompts without requiring manually annotated reasoning traces.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "design a dynamic prompt containing trajectory information about conflicting ships to guide the model to achieve adaptive chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning." [section 3.2] Detailed description of the prompt structure and the QSD conflict detection algorithm. [corpus] Related work on reward design for reasoning supports the generalizability of contextual prompting.
- **Break condition:** If conflict detection fails or the prompt becomes too noisy with irrelevant ships, the model may generate reasoning that is distracted or overly generalized, reducing prediction accuracy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** A rule-based reward function with separate format and accuracy components effectively aligns the LLM's output with the requirements of structured, precise trajectory prediction.
- **Mechanism:** The reward is a weighted sum of a binary `think/answer` format compliance reward and a geodesic-distance-based accuracy reward. This dual objective ensures outputs are both parsable and numerically precise. The accuracy reward uses a two-tier threshold (120m for centroid, 90m per point) to provide sparse but meaningful feedback.
- **Core assumption:** Discrete, rule-based rewards can sufficiently capture the complex, continuous objective of trajectory accuracy and steer policy gradient updates for a text-generation model.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "introduce a comprehensive rule-based reward mechanism to incentivize the reasoning format and prediction accuracy." [section 3.3] Explicit mathematical definitions of the thinking format reward and prediction accuracy reward. [section 4.3, Table 4] Ablation on KL loss coefficient shows sensitivity to regularization, indirectly supporting that the reward signal itself is the primary driver of learning.
- **Break condition:** If the geographic thresholds (120m, 90m) are mismatched to the dataset's typical error distributions or spatial resolution, the reward signal could become either too sparse or too noisy, destabilizing training.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) stabilizes reinforcement fine-tuning by using group-relative advantages, preventing mode collapse and catastrophic forgetting in the small-to-medium (4B/8B) Qwen3 backbone.
- **Mechanism:** For each prompt, multiple candidate completions are sampled from the current policy. Their rewards are normalized within that group (subtracting group mean, dividing by group std) to compute advantages. This relative normalization makes the learning signal less sensitive to absolute reward scale and encourages the policy to improve relative to its own recent performance. The KL penalty (`β D_KL`) prevents excessive deviation from the reference model.
- **Core assumption:** Relative advantage calculation within a per-prompt group is more robust for this sparse-reward, text-generation task than using a fixed learned value baseline (as in PPO), especially with limited training data.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "optimization via Group Relative Policy Optimization on the Qwen3 backbone." [section 3.4] Formal GRPO objective equation showing group-relative advantage and KL regularization term. [corpus] Neighboring papers investigate GRPO's theoretical properties and instabilities, providing contextual evidence that GRPO is an active research area; ShipTraj-R1's success implies they managed these challenges (e.g., via careful β tuning).
- **Break condition:** If the group size M is too small, the relative advantage estimate becomes high-variance. If the KL coefficient β is too high, learning is stifled; if too low, the model collapses to reward hacking. Table 4 shows performance degradation as β increases.

## Foundational Learning
### Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT) / GRPO
- **Why needed here:** The task requires precise numerical output from a generative model, which is poorly suited to standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT). SFT would only teach the model to mimic training trajectories, not optimize for a continuous accuracy metric. RFT with a custom reward directly optimizes for the final displacement error.
- **Quick check:** How does the group-relative advantage in GRPO differ from the advantage computed with a learned critic in PPO, and why might the former be more suitable for a sparse, rule-based reward?

### Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting
- **Why needed here:** The model must reason about spatial relationships and collision risk before outputting a multi-step coordinate sequence. Explicit reasoning (within `think` tags) is incentivized by the format reward and likely helps the model structure its internal computation for this complex, multi-variable prediction.
- **Quick check:** Why is explicitly rewarding *format compliance* (via the `think`/`answer` tags) important for a CoT-based approach, even if the final accuracy is also rewarded?

### Maritime Collision Risk (Ship Domain)
- **Why needed here:** The prompt's value comes from including *conflicting* ship trajectories. Understanding what constitutes a conflict (via the Quaternion Ship Domain model) is essential to design the input that triggers relevant reasoning. Without this, the LLM receives irrelevant context.
- **Quick check:** What are the four key safety distances (`R_bow`, `R_stern`, `R_starb`, `R_port`) in the QSD model, and how does their violation indicate collision risk?

### Geodesic Distance for Coordinate Error
- **Why needed here:** Trajectory coordinates are longitude/latitude on Earth. Using Euclidean distance on raw (lon, lat) values is inaccurate. The Vincenty formula computes true surface distance, making the reward physically meaningful and comparable across global datasets.
- **Quick check:** Why is calculating the distance between the *centroids* of the predicted and ground-truth trajectories an insufficient reward on its own, necessitating the per-point distance check?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Input Processor -> Prompt Generator -> Inference/Generation Engine -> Output Parser & Validator -> Reward Calculator -> GRPO Trainer

**Critical path:**
Data Loading -> Conflict Detection -> Prompt Creation -> Model Generation (multiple samples) -> Output Parsing -> Reward Calculation (for all samples) -> Advantage Normalization -> Policy Gradient Update -> Repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Reward granularity vs. training stability:** The binary/step-threshold reward is simple but potentially sparse. More granular rewards (e.g., continuous distance loss) might provide richer signal but complicate RL optimization and risk overfitting.
- **Conflict detection sensitivity:** A strict QSD threshold yields fewer, higher-risk conflicts (cleaner context) but may miss subtle cues. A lenient threshold adds more noise. This is a hyperparameter not explored.
- **Group size (M):** Larger M gives a more stable advantage estimate but increases memory/compute per batch. The paper doesn't report M.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Format non-compliance:** High rate of outputs missing `think` or `answer` tags, or trajectory not in expected JSON format.
- **Degenerate reasoning:** `think` blocks are repetitive, nonsensical, or ignore conflicting ship context.
- **Prediction collapse:** All predictions are nearly identical across contexts (e.g., a straight line).
- **Inconsistent performance across datasets:** Works on CFDP but not CSJP (or vice versa), indicating potential mismatched hyperparameters or conflict detection.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline sanity check:** Run the base Qwen3-8B (zero-shot) and a supervised fine-tuned version (SFT) on a small validation set. Verify the SFT model learns the output format but shows limited accuracy improvement over zero-shot.
2. **Reward component isolation:** Train with GRPO but **ablate the accuracy reward** (only format reward). Expected: perfect format compliance but useless coordinates. Confirms accuracy reward is necessary for learning.
3. **Prompt context ablation:** Train two GRPO models: one with full conflicting ship context, one with *only* the target ship's history (no neighbors). Expected: significant performance gap. Validates the core hypothesis that conflicting context drives adaptive reasoning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Long-term prediction:** How does ShipTraj-R1 perform on prediction horizons beyond 20 seconds? The paper only evaluates up to 20s; longer forecasts are needed for practical collision avoidance systems.
2. **QSD parameter sensitivity:** How sensitive is accuracy to the quaternion ship domain parameters (safety distances and shape)? No ablation or sensitivity analysis is provided.
3. **Learned reward model:** Would replacing the rule-based reward with a learned reward (e.g., from expert demonstrations) improve prediction accuracy? The framework uses a simple fixed reward; a more adaptive reward could better capture nuanced safety considerations.
4. **CoT reasoning alignment:** Do the generated chain-of-thought outputs align with human expert decision-making for collision avoidance? No expert validation of semantic correctness is performed.

## Limitations
- Error metrics (FDE ∼0.0003, ADE ∼3e-07) are unusually low; units and normalization are unclear, raising concerns about scaling and interpretability.
- Rule-based reward thresholds (120m centroid, 90m per point) are arbitrary without maritime safety grounding; their sensitivity to different traffic patterns is unexplored.
- GRPO hyperparameters (group size M, KL coefficient β) are unreported, making it difficult to assess training stability, collapse risks, and reproducibility.
- The paper does not evaluate the semantic quality or safety alignment of the generated chain-of-thought reasoning, limiting trust and practical deployment prospects.

## Next Checks
1. **Reward component ablation:** Train separate models using only format reward and only accuracy reward to quantify each component's contribution to final performance.
2. **Reasoning quality analysis:** Conduct a qualitative study where maritime experts evaluate a sample of `think` outputs for correctness, completeness, and safety alignment; correlate scores with prediction errors.
