# Grokking as a Phase Transition between Competing Basins: a Singular Learning Theory Approach

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.01192  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01192  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Grokking is identified as a Bayesian phase transition from a higher‑LLC memorization basin to a lower‑LLC generalization basin; the local learning coefficient predicts generalization error, and increasing learning rate lowers peak LLC while exponentially reducing grokking severity.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the phenomenon of grokking—sudden emergence of generalization after prolonged memorization—using Singular Learning Learning Theory (SLT). Focusing on quadratic neural networks trained on modular arithmetic tasks, the authors derive closed‑form expressions for the local learning coefficient (LLC) and show how it captures basin geometry. By tracking LLC during SGD, they observe a sharp decrease coinciding with the drop in validation loss, indicating a phase transition to a lower‑LLC basin. Additionally, they find that larger learning rates reduce the maximum LLC and exponentially decrease a grokking severity measure, suggesting that step size influences basin selection. The LLC thus serves as a robust, geometry‑based metric for understanding and predicting generalization dynamics, extending even to transformers.

## Method Summary
The study employs two‑layer quadratic neural networks with input dimension \(d = 2p\) (where \(p\) is a prime) and hidden width \(K\). The model \(f_\theta(x) = \sum_{j=1}^K v_j (w_j^\top x)^2\) (no biases) is trained on one‑hot encoded modular addition data (operands \(a,b \in \mathbb{Z}_p\), target \(c = a+b \bmod p\)) using centered MSE loss. Theoretical analysis yields closed‑form LLC values: for over‑parameterized networks (\(K \ge d(d+1)/2\)), \(\lambda = p \cdot d(d+1)/4\); for under‑parameterized (\(K < d(d+1)/2\)), \(\lambda = K(d+p-1)/2\). To verify, networks are trained to convergence, and the final LLC is estimated via a stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD) sampler targeting a tempered posterior \(p(\theta|\text{data}, \beta, \gamma)\); the estimator is \(n\beta \big( \mathbb{E}[L_n] - L_n(\theta_{\text{checkpoint}}) \big)\). For dynamics, training is periodically checkpointed, each checkpoint is fed to the SGLD pipeline, and LLC is plotted alongside training/validation losses. A grokking severity measure (GSM) quantifies the delay and magnitude of generalization. The SGLD setup (inverse temperature \(\beta\), step size \(\eta\), localization \(\gamma\), multiple chains, burn‑in) requires careful tuning due to estimator sensitivity.

## Key Results
- Closed‑form LLC expressions for quadratic neural networks on modular arithmetic, validated experimentally across a range of widths.
- Tracking LLC during SGD reveals a sharp drop that coincides with the validation loss decrease, confirming grokking as a phase transition between basins.
- Increasing the learning rate lowers the maximum LLC attained and exponentially reduces GSM, indicating that larger steps help escape sharper memorization basins earlier.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Grokking as a Bayesian phase transition between competing basins
- **Claim**: Grokking is a phase transition from a memorization basin with higher LLC to a generalizing basin with lower LLC, driven by the Bayesian marginal likelihood as training time (or effective sample size) increases.
- **Mechanism**: In SLT, the free energy (negative log marginal likelihood) of a basin \(U\) is asymptotically \(F_n(U) = nL_n(w) + \lambda \log n - (m-1) \log \log n + O(1)\). For two basins with similar training error, the difference is dominated by \((\lambda_a - \lambda_b) \log n\). The basin with smaller \(\lambda\) eventually has lower free energy and dominates the posterior. SGD, via noise‑induced tempering, can follow this Bayesian preference, leading to an abrupt switch.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and Section 1 note the sharp LLC decrease concurrent with validation loss drop; prior work (Chen et al., 2023) describes such first‑order transitions.
- **Break condition**: If the memorization basin’s LLC is not strictly larger than the generalizing basin’s, the free energy may not favor the latter. Also, SLT regularity conditions (i.i.d. data, analytic loss, positive prior) must hold.

### Mechanism 2: LLC as a robust predictor of generalization error
- **Claim**: The local learning coefficient \(\lambda\) of a basin is inversely related to its generalizability; lower \(\lambda\) corresponds to lower expected Bayes generalization error and higher posterior concentration.
- **Mechanism**: SLT states that the expected Bayes generalization error \(E[B_g] = \lambda/n + o(1/n)\) asymptotically, and the marginal likelihood includes \(\lambda \log n\) as the leading complexity penalty. Thus \(\lambda\) measures effective flatness/degeneracy of the local loss landscape.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract links lower‑LLC basins to higher posterior mass; Section 2.1 explains the \(\lambda/n\) relationship; literature on reparameterization‑invariant flatness measures supports the need for \(\lambda\).
- **Break condition**: Asymptotic Bayesian analysis requires realizability and a proper non‑zero prior near optima. If SGD noise is insufficient, it may not reach the Bayes optimum.

### Mechanism 3: Learning rate modulates grokking severity via LLC dynamics
- **Claim**: Larger learning rates reduce the maximum LLC attained during training and exponentially decrease grokking severity, because high steps help the optimizer escape sharper memorization basins earlier.
- **Mechanism**: SGD with noise approximates a tempered posterior; the temperature scales as \(T \propto \eta/B\). Larger \(\eta\) increases gradient noise, acting like stronger tempering and biasing the sampler toward higher‑volume (lower‑LLC) basins. Empirically, GSM ~ \(\exp(-c \eta)\) and peak LLC decreases with \(\eta\).
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and Section 6.4 report the exponential GSM decay and negative correlation with peak LLC; related work shows weight decay influences delays, extending to learning rate.
- **Break condition**: Very high learning rates cause divergence; LLC estimator assumes stationarity, so if mixing is poor the estimates may be biased.

## Foundational Learning
- **Singular Learning Theory (SLT) and the Real Log Canonical Threshold (RLCT)**  
  - *Why needed*: SLT mathematically defines and interprets the local learning coefficient (LLC), linking basin volume to generalization.  
  - *Quick check*: What does the LLC measure about the loss landscape at a local minimum?
- **Bayesian asymptotic expansions (free energy, marginal likelihood)**  
  - *Why needed*: The expansion reveals \(\lambda \log n\) as the leading complexity term, enabling asymptotic basin comparison.  
  - *Quick check*: Write the asymptotic form of the free energy \(F_n\) for a basin with LLC \(\lambda\) and multiplicity \(m\).
- **Regular vs singular models in deep learning**  
  - *Why needed*: Neural networks are typically singular (overparameterized, symmetries), leading to \(\lambda < d/2\) and competing basins with different \(\lambda\).  
  - *Quick check*: Give an example of a property that makes a neural network model singular (i.e., not identifiable, Fisher rank‑deficient).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Dataset (modular arithmetic triples) -> Quadratic Neural Network (2‑layer, \(\sigma(x)=x^2\), no biases) -> SGD training (with weight decay) -> Periodic checkpoints -> SGLD sampler (tempered posterior) -> LLC estimator -> Analysis (LLC curves, GSM)
- **Critical path**:  
  1. Implement forward pass: \(f_\theta(x) = \sum_{j=1}^K v_j (w_j^\top x)^2\).  
  2. Train on the modular dataset until memorization (training loss ≈ 0); save checkpoints.  
  3. At each checkpoint, run SGLD with fixed \(\beta, \eta, \gamma\) to collect local samples.  
  4. Compute LLC estimator: \(n\beta(\mathbb{E}[L] - L_{\text{checkpoint}})\); average over chains.  
  5. Plot train loss, val loss, LLC vs epoch; identify sharp LLC drop (phase transition).  
  6. Vary learning rate, compute GSM and maximum LLC; fit exponential/Gaussian relationships.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - *Closed‑form vs estimator*: Closed‑form provides exact \(\lambda\) but relies on non‑degeneracy; estimator is general but hyperparameter‑sensitive.  
  - *Model simplicity*: Quadratic nets are analytically tractable but may not capture deeper architectures.  
  - *LLC hyperparameters*: Inverse temperature \(n\beta\), step size \(\eta\), and localization \(\gamma\) require careful tuning; absolute values vary, but relative trends within a fixed setup are robust.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - LLC curve does not drop or increases: sampler may drift basins (\(n\beta\) too low) or grokking fails.  
  - GSM vs learning rate not monotonic: LLC estimates unstable; check ESS and chain convergence.  
  - Final LLC deviates from theory: non‑degeneracy conditions (spanning, linear independence) violated; adjust width or initialization.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Closed‑form validation**: Train quadratic nets for various \(p\) and \(K\); plot final LLC vs \(p\) and \(K\) comparing to theoretical lines.  
  2. **LLC dynamics tracking**: With standard hyperparameters, train 100k epochs, checkpoint every 100 epochs; plot LLC alongside train/val losses.  
  3. **Learning rate study**: Vary learning rate (e.g., \(10^{-5}\)–\(10^{-2}\)), compute GSM and max LLC; show exponential GSM decay and negative correlation with peak LLC.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can we rigorously connect Bayesian posterior concentration (as characterized by SLT) to the actual dynamics of stochastic gradient descent in deep learning?  
- Can closed‑form expressions for the LLC be derived for complex architectures beyond quadratic networks, such as deep ReLU networks or transformers with attention?  
- How can LLC estimation be made robust and less sensitive to sampler hyperparameters so that it is reliably applicable to real neural networks?  
- What is the exact mechanism by which SGD hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size) influence the LLC of the final basin and thereby control grokking severity?

## Limitations
- SLT regularity assumptions (realizable model, analytic loss, proper prior) may not hold for practical deep nets.
- LLC estimation via SGLD is hyperparameter‑sensitive; absolute values are not directly comparable across setups.
- Closed‑form LLC formulas depend on non‑degeneracy conditions (spanning, linear independence) that can fail, especially for small widths.
- Empirical validation is largely restricted to quadratic networks and a few transformers; generalizability to common architectures remains uncertain.

## Confidence
- Grokking as an LLC‑driven phase transition: Medium  
- LLC predicts generalization error: Medium
