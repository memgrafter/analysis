# Testimole-Conversational: A 30-Billion-Word Italian Discussion Board Corpus (1996-2024) for Language Modeling and Sociolinguistic Research

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.14819
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14819
- **Reference Count:** 0
- **Primary Result:** Compiled a 30+ billion word-token corpus of Italian discussion board messages (1996–2024) to address the lack of large-scale native corpora for language modeling and sociolinguistic analysis.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Testimole-conversational, a large-scale corpus of Italian discussion board posts collected from Usenet and web forums spanning 1996 to 2024. The corpus was built to fill a gap in resources for training Italian language models and studying digital communication. Using manual web scraping with custom Python scripts, the authors gathered over 30 billion word-tokens—23 billion from forums and 7 billion from Usenet—comprising 560 million posts across 39 million threads. The diachronic coverage enables analysis of language evolution (e.g., lexical shifts like "troll" and "smartphone"). The corpus is released with raw text and frequency lists, aiming to support both computational linguistics and sociolinguistic research on informal written Italian.

## Method Summary
Data collection involved manual web scraping via custom Python scripts targeted at Italian Usenet groups (1996–2024) and web forums (1997–2024). The process aimed to extract raw posts while preserving metadata like timestamps and thread structure. Scraping was constrained by time and resources, preventing a complete enumeration of all historical Italian discussion boards. The raw output was structured into a clean, consistent format, though details on deduplication, language filtering, or spam removal are unspecified. The final corpus aggregates posts from hundreds of sources, with no subsequent linguistic annotation beyond basic tokenization and frequency counting.

## Key Results
- **Scale:** Over 30 billion word-tokens (23B from forums, 7B from Usenet), 560M total posts across 39M threads.
- **Diachronic Coverage:** Continuous data from 1996–2024, enabling trend analysis of vocabulary and topics.
- **Utility:** Provided as raw text with frequency lists, suitable for pretraining Italian LLMs and sociolinguistic studies of online discourse.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The corpus works because raw, unfiltered discussion board data authentically captures informal, contemporary Italian as used by native speakers in real-world digital communication. This contrasts with curated or formal texts (e.g., news, literature), providing the lexical, grammatical, and pragmatic diversity needed for robust language modeling. The large scale (30B tokens) mitigates data sparsity issues for Italian, a lower-resource language compared to English. Diachronic depth allows models to learn stable linguistic patterns amid temporal noise and supports historical sociolinguistic queries. The release of raw text with minimal preprocessing maximizes flexibility for downstream tasks, though it places burden on users for cleaning and annotation.

## Foundational Learning
Three core concepts underpin effective use of this corpus:
- **Diachronic Sociolinguistics**: Needed to separate language change from ephemeral topic trends. Quick check: Verify timestamp distribution is uniform across decades; uneven coverage could bias temporal analysis.
- **Computer-Mediated Communication (CMC) Phenomena**: CMC has distinct features (abbreviations, emoticons, turn-taking) that differ from written standards. Quick check: Sample posts for non-standard orthography and code-switching; quantify prevalence to inform preprocessing.
- **Low-Resource Language Tokenization**: Italian morphology requires subword tokenizers (e.g., SentencePiece) to handle rich inflection. Quick check: Train a tokenizer on a subset; assess vocabulary coverage and out-of-vocabulary rates on held-out samples.

## Architecture Onboarding
The corpus is a static dataset, not a system, so "architecture" refers to its data pipeline and utilization.

- **Component Map**:  
  Source Forums/Usenet -> Web Scraper (Python) -> Raw Post Storage -> Cleaning/Normalization (unspecified) -> Structured Corpus (text + metadata) -> Frequency Lists  
  Critical Path: scraping -> storage -> basic cleaning -> aggregation.
- **Design Tradeoffs**:  
  Completeness vs. Noise: Manual scraping prioritized accessible sources, sacrificing comprehensive historical coverage for manageable noise.  
  Raw vs. Processed: Releasing raw text maximizes reuse but requires users to handle duplicates, non-Italian content, and toxicity.  
  Metadata vs. Privacy: Timestamps and thread hierarchy are kept, but user identifiers are likely omitted or hashed to avoid privacy issues.
- **Failure Signatures**:  
  Missing timestamps for early posts; gaps in coverage for niche or defunct forums; high duplication from cross-posting; non-Italian spam or advertising posts; profanity and misinformation unfiltered.
- **First Three Experiments**:  
  1. Stratified sampling audit: Verify token/post counts, metadata completeness, and source distribution across years.  
  2. Noise quantification: Measure duplication rates, non-Italian content percentage (via langid), and toxicity prevalence (e.g., using HateBERT).  
  3. Diachronic keyword validation: Replicate reported trends ("troll", "smartphone") and test for collocational shifts using distributional semantics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** To what extent does the Testimole-conversational corpus capture the full historical landscape of Italian discussion boards, and what biases are introduced by the unmapped, potentially lost forums?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state: "we have not completed the ambitious project to map every Italian discussion board, as we were constrained by time and resources." They also note that many early 1990s pages are rare "because no longer maintained."
- **Why unresolved:** The corpus collection was necessarily selective due to manual scraping constraints and the fragility of web archives, making an assessment of missing data difficult without a complete enumeration of all historical Italian forums/Usenet groups.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic, crowdsourced or archival audit comparing the corpus's known forum/Usenet hierarchy coverage against historical indexes, directory listings, and academic studies of early Italian online communities to estimate coverage gaps and potential biases (e.g., topic, user demographics).

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the inclusion of noise—such as cross-posted duplicates, misinformation, and unmoderated profanity—affect the utility of the corpus for training factual, safe, and reliable Italian LLMs?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors acknowledge noise: "paragraphs or even entire posts may be duplicated due to cross-posting phenomena; users may have introduced... erroneous or misleading information" and "we cannot guarantee that the corpus is free from profanity, offensive or aggressive language."
- **Why unresolved:** While these risks are noted, the paper provides no quantitative analysis of noise prevalence or experimental evaluation of its impact on downstream LLM performance (e.g., factuality, toxicity).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A study measuring the corpus's noise metrics (duplication rates, misinformation markers, toxic language prevalence) and conducting ablation experiments training LLMs on filtered vs. raw versions to quantify performance differences on safety and factual accuracy benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Beyond tracking keyword frequency (e.g., "troll", "smartphone"), what deeper diachronic linguistic shifts in grammar, pragmatics, and discourse structure within Italian CMC can be systematically analyzed using this corpus?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The abstract and introduction promise analysis of "language evolution and change... at the lexical and grammatical levels" and "informal written Italian, discourse dynamics." However, the results section only presents token counts and keyword trends, with no detailed linguistic annotation or analysis.
- **Why unresolved:** The corpus is released with raw text and frequency lists only. The authors do not perform or provide preprocessed annotations (e.g., part-of-speech, dependency parses, discourse markers) necessary for testing hypotheses about grammatical or pragmatic change.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** The development and application of NLP pipelines for diachronic annotation of the corpus (e.g., tagging for CMC-specific features like abbrevations, emoticons, dialogic acts) and subsequent quantitative studies comparing rates of these features across decades to test specific hypotheses about Italian CMC evolution.

## Limitations
- **Data Quality Unspecified:** No details on deduplication, spam filtering, or language verification; potential high noise from cross-posts, non-Italian content, and misinformation.
- **Representativeness Unknown:** Sources are selectively scraped; likely bias toward popular, enduring forums and specific user demographics (e.g., tech-savvy, male-dominated early internet).
- **Metadata Limitations:** Timestamps may be incomplete or inconsistent for older posts; thread hierarchy sometimes lost; no user identifiers, limiting network or longitudinal user analyses.
- **Reproducibility Gaps:** "Manual web scraping" lacks technical specifics (e.g., list of URLs, rate limits, error handling), hindering replication or extension.
- **No Independent Validation:** No comparison with established Italian corpora (e.g., itTenTen, Wikipedia) to assess lexical or topical overlap and bias.

## Confidence
- **Corpus Size (30B+ tokens)**: Medium — specific counts are reported but unverified by independent audit; sampling could confirm.
- **Data Collection Method**: Low — described vaguely as "manual web scraping"; reproducibility and error rates are uncertain.
- **Diachronic Coverage (1996–2024)**: High — timespan is explicit and plausible given Usenet/forum histories, though timestamp accuracy requires validation.
- **Utility for LLM Pretraining**: Medium — depends entirely on unassessed data quality, noise levels, and metadata robustness.
- **Utility for Sociolinguistics**: Medium — potential is high, but without annotations or representativeness analysis, claims remain speculative.

## Next Checks
1. **Stratified Sample Audit**: Randomly select threads across years and sources (e.g., 1000 per year from Usenet and forums) to verify token counts, post counts, metadata integrity (timestamps, thread IDs), and source authenticity.
2. **Language and Spam Assessment**: Apply language identification (e.g., fastText) and spam detection (e.g., custom heuristic or SpamAssassin rules) to a 0.1% random subset to quantify non-Italian or junk content prevalence.
3. **Representativeness Analysis**: Compare topic distributions (via LDA or BERTopic) of the corpus against historical Italian internet traffic reports or academic surveys of online communities to detect source biases toward specific themes or demographics.
