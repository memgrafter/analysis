# A recipe for scalable attention-based MLIPs: unlocking long-range accuracy with all-to-all node attention

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.06567
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06567
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: AllScAIP achieves state-of-the-art energy/force accuracy on the large OMol25 dataset and enables stable, long-timescale MD simulations that accurately predict experimental observables.

## Executive Summary
The paper presents AllScAIP, a scalable attention-based machine learning interatomic potential (MLIP) designed to capture long-range interactions in large biomolecular and electrolyte systems without relying on explicit physics-based terms. Its two-stage architecture combines local neighborhood self-attention for fine-grained, anisotropic interactions within a fixed radius, followed by all-to-all node self-attention for global information mixing across all atoms. Through extensive ablations across data and model scales, the authors find that while geometric inductive biases (e.g., Legendre Angular Encoding, Euclidean RoPE) improve sample efficiency in low-resource regimes, their marginal benefit diminishes or even reverses as training samples and model capacity increase. Crucially, the all-to-all node attention consistently provides the most durable improvement for long-range accuracy. AllScAIP sets new benchmarks on the OMol25 dataset and demonstrates stable, long-timescale molecular dynamics simulations that accurately predict experimental properties such as density and heat of vaporization, establishing a purely data-driven path for accurate and scalable MLIPs.

## Method Summary
AllScAIP processes atomic systems by first embedding atomic numbers into node features and interatomic distances into radial basis functions. The core architecture consists of repeating layers, each comprising two attention stages: (1) Neighborhood self-attention, where each atom attends to its k-nearest neighbors plus itself using multi-head attention, optionally modulated by Legendre Angular Encoding (LAE) to incorporate angular priors; and (2) All-to-all node self-attention, where every atom token attends to all others, with optional Euclidean Rotary Position Encoding (ERoPE) to bias attention by distance. Both stages include feed-forward networks and residual connections. After the neighborhood stage, an edge feed-forward network updates edge features. The node features from the neighborhood stage are concatenated with those from the node stage to form the input for the next layer. The model predicts per-atom energy, from which forces are derived either via automatic differentiation (conservative) or direct supervision. Training minimizes energy and force mean squared errors, with experiments varying data scale (4M to 102M structures) and model size (35M to 85M parameters). Key design choices include soft k-nearest neighbor graph construction for differentiability and optional geometric encodings.

## Key Results
- AllScAIP achieves state-of-the-art energy and force accuracy on the OMol25 dataset, particularly on biomolecules and electrolytes where long-range interactions dominate.
- Ablations confirm that all-to-all node attention yields consistent, durable gains for long-range accuracy, while geometric encodings (LAE/ERoPE) provide diminishing returns and can even harm performance at large scales.
- The model enables stable, long-timescale MD simulations that accurately predict experimental bulk properties (density, heat of vaporization), demonstrating practical utility for large-scale systems without explicit physics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** All-to-all node attention provides a durable architectural mechanism for capturing long-range interactions by enabling global information mixing across the entire atomic system in a single layer.
**Mechanism:** After local neighborhood self-attention resolves fine, anisotropic interactions within a fixed radius, a second stage applies multi-head self-attention over all node tokens (shape `1 × #nodes × d_model`). This graph-free operation gives every atom a receptive field that spans the entire system, allowing signals to propagate in one step across arbitrary distances, unlike deep stacks of local message-passing which are limited by cutoffs and number of hops.
**Core assumption:** Long-range coupling can be learned from data without explicit physics-based terms if the architecture provides a sufficiently large receptive field and enough model capacity.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract]: "the all-to-all node attention consistently provides the most durable improvement for long-range interactions."
- [section 3.1]: "All-to-all Node Self-attention... allows signals to travel over the whole graph."
- [corpus]: Neighbors discuss long-range challenges (e.g., latent Ewald summation) but do not directly evidence this specific mechanism; support is primarily internal.
**Break condition:** If the O(N²) complexity becomes computationally intractable for systems >10⁵ atoms without aggressive approximations, the long-range gains may be offset by impractical memory/time costs.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Many geometric inductive biases (e.g., rotational equivariance, high-order directional features, radial priors) are learnable under sufficient data and model scale, reducing the need for hard-coded encodings.
**Mechanism:** The model uses soft geometric encodings (Legendre Angular Encoding for angles, Euclidean RoPE for distances) that modulate attention logits. Ablations show these improve sample efficiency in low-resource regimes, but their marginal benefit diminishes or reverses as training samples (4M → 102M) and parameters (35M → 85M) increase, implying the backbone can learn these features end-to-end from raw coordinates.
**Core assumption:** Model capacity and data diversity are the primary bottlenecks for learning certain symmetries and geometric features; architectural scaffolding can be thinned as scale grows.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [abstract]: "in low-data/small-model regimes, inductive biases improve sample efficiency. However, as data and model size scale, these benefits diminish or even reverse."
- [section 4]: Table 2 shows LAE and ERoPE help at 4M/35M but often match or worsen at 102M/85M.
- [corpus]: Weak; neighbors discuss equivariant architectures that *encode* biases, not their learnability under scale.
**Break condition:** If data distribution lacks diversity (e.g., only small molecules) or model capacity is capped, the learned geometric features may remain inaccurate, harming generalization to novel geometries.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** A two-stage attention design (local neighbor attention + global node attention) balances computational efficiency with long-range accuracy, creating a predictable scaling transition from O(Nk) to O(N²) dominated regimes.
**Mechanism:** The neighbor attention operates on fixed k-neighbor lists per atom (O(Nk) cost), efficiently capturing local chemistry. The subsequent all-to-all node attention (O(N²)) provides global mixing. For small systems, runtime is dominated by the local stage; as system size grows, the global stage eventually dominates, creating a clear crossover in throughput vs. size (Figure 4). This allows targeting the biologically relevant 10³–10⁵ atom regime where both accuracy and efficiency are manageable.
**Core assumption:** Local interactions can be resolved with sparse stencils, while long-range coupling requires dense all-to-all communication; the hybrid approach optimizes for the typical system size distribution of interest.
**Evidence anchors:**
- [section 3.1]: "neighborhood self-attention... scales as O(N k), while all-to-all node self-attention mixes all nodes per graph with O(N²) cost."
- [section 5.1]: Figure 4 shows the predicted O(Nk) and O(N²) scaling regimes with a clear transition.
- [corpus]: Neighbors explore efficient long-range methods (e.g., latent Ewald, linear attention) but do not evaluate this hybrid's scaling profile.
**Break condition:** For systems >10⁵ atoms, the O(N²) node attention may become prohibitively expensive even on large GPUs, requiring additional approximations (hierarchical pooling, linear attention) to maintain feasibility.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input embedding → Neighborhood self-attention (with LAE) → Edge FFN + residual → Node self-attention (with ERoPE) → Node FFN + residual → Concatenation (neighbor features with node features) → (repeat per layer) → Final node features → Energy head → Forces (via gradient or direct supervision)
- **Critical path:** Input → [Neighbor Att + Edge FFN] → Node features (self-loop) → [Node Att + Node FFN] → Repeat per layer → Final nodes → Energy → Forces
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Scalability vs. long-range accuracy: O(N²) node attention is costly but necessary for durable LR gains; alternatives (linear attention, hierarchical pooling) trade some expressivity for speed.
  - Inductive bias inclusion: LAE/ERoPE improve low-resource sample efficiency but add complexity; at scale they may be redundant or harmful, suggesting a "bias-light" default with optional toggles.
  - Energy conservation: Using gradient-based forces (conservative) ensures physicality but requires differentiable kNN graph construction (new op, less optimized), slowing inference vs. direct force supervision.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High force/energy MAE on large biomolecules/electrolytes (OMol25 biomolecules split) despite decent small-molecule performance → indicates long-range deficiency, likely missing effective global mixing.
  - Poor rotational equivariance (cosine similarity << 0.999) on rotated test → suggests model has not learned SE(3) invariance, possibly due to insufficient scale or architectural issues.
  - O(N²) memory blowup at moderate system sizes (~10⁴ atoms) → node attention dominates VRAM; check if neighbor list and attention masks are correctly optimized.
  - Distance-scaling test (OMol25) shows rapidly increasing energy error beyond training stretch factors → model lacks robust long-range coupling.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablate node attention:** Train with/without the all-to-all node stage on OMol25 4M; evaluate on biomolecules split and distance-scaling test. Expect significant degradation in long-range accuracy without node attention.
  2. **Scale sweep with/without encodings:** Fix model size (85M), vary data (4M → 102M); also fix data (102M), vary model (35M → 85M). Compare full (LAE+ERoPE) vs. no-encoding variants. Expect encoding benefit to shrink or reverse with scale.
  3. **MD rollout for bulk properties:** Run NPT MD on liquid systems (OMol25 or SPICE) with a pre-trained model; compute density and heat of vaporization. Compare to experiment and to a baseline without global attention (e.g., eSEN). Expect AllScAIP to match or exceed, especially if baseline suffers from compression bias.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What specific long-range physical interactions (e.g., electrostatic, dispersion, induction) does the all-to-all node attention component learn, and how does its implicit representation compare to explicit physics-based formulations?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates that all-to-all attention consistently improves long-range accuracy across systems but does not decompose its contributions into physical components. The authors frame it as a data-driven alternative to explicit terms.
  - Why unresolved: Attention mechanisms lack direct physical interpretability; it is unclear whether the model captures correct functional forms (e.g., 1/r decay) or merely approximates effects statistically.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation on synthetic datasets with isolated long-range forces (e.g., Coulombic point charges, pairwise dispersion) combined with analysis of attention patterns (distance dependence) and ablation against explicit physics baselines.
- **Open Question 2:** Why does the marginal benefit of geometric inductive biases (Legendre Angular Encoding, Euclidean RoPE) diminish and sometimes reverse with increasing data and model scale, and does this indicate a fundamental limit to their utility?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] Ablations in Table 2 show that at larger scales (e.g., OMol 102M with 85M parameters), removing LAE/ERoPE yields equal or better energy/force error, while all-to-all attention remains beneficial.
  - Why unresolved: The authors hypothesize that angular/radial features are "learnable under scale" but do not explain the reversal phenomenon, which may stem from over-parameterization, optimization dynamics, or dataset-specific redundancy.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Deeper scaling studies with representation probing (e.g., measuring angular/radial sensitivity in models with/without encodings) and testing on datasets requiring precise directional features (e.g., hydrogen-bond networks, chiral centers) even at large scale.
- **Open Question 3:** Can the quadratic O(N²) complexity of the all-to-all node attention be mitigated (e.g., via linear or hierarchical attention) without sacrificing the long-range accuracy gains critical for large biomolecular and electrolyte systems?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The discussion (Section 6) identifies O(N²) as a trade-off and proposes "hierarchical node pools, linear-time attention, mixtures-of-experts" as future work to "delay the O(N²) regime."
  - Why unresolved: No efficient attention variant has been implemented or validated within AllScAIP; it is unknown whether sub-quadratic approximations can preserve the durable long-range improvements.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Integration of efficient attention mechanisms (e.g., Performer, Linformer, or cross-pool attention) into AllScAIP, benchmarked on large systems (N > 10⁴ atoms) for both accuracy (energy/force, MD stability) and throughput, compared to the original model.

## Limitations
- Computational scalability: The O(N²) complexity of all-to-all node attention may become prohibitive for systems larger than 10⁵ atoms without approximations, limiting applicability to very large biomolecular assemblies.
- Generalizability: Claims about diminishing inductive biases are based on the OMol25 data distribution; performance on chemically diverse or out-of-distribution systems remains untested.
- Implementation gaps: Critical hyperparameters (e.g., k in kNN, radial basis details, LAE/ERoPE specifics) are not fully specified, hindering exact replication and sensitivity analysis.

## Confidence
- All-to-all node attention enables durable long-range accuracy: Medium (supported by internal ablations but lacks external benchmarks; break condition plausible).
- Inductive biases become redundant at scale: Medium-Low (evidence limited to their scaling setup; may not hold for smaller data diversity or tighter capacity constraints).
- Hybrid scaling (local + global) balances efficiency/accuracy: Medium (scaling trends shown, but real-world MD stability beyond reported cases is not broadly validated).
