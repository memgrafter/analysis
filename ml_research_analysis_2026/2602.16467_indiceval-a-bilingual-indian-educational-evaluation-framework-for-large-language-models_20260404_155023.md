# IndicEval: A Bilingual Indian Educational Evaluation Framework for Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.16467
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16467
- **Reference count:** 15
- **Primary result:** Chain-of-Thought prompting yields substantial accuracy gains (up to +27.05 pp), but severe multilingual degradation persists, with Hindi accuracy drops exceeding 25 pp compared to English under Zero-Shot.

## Executive Summary
IndicEval introduces a bilingual (English/Hindi) benchmark for evaluating large language models on authentic Indian high-stakes examination questions (UPSC, JEE, NEET). The framework assesses model reasoning across three prompting strategies: Zero-Shot, Few-Shot, and Chain-of-Thought (CoT). Key findings reveal that CoT prompting consistently and significantly improves accuracy across domains, with gains of up to 27 percentage points. However, pronounced cross-model disparities are exposed, particularly in Hindi, where performance gaps can exceed 45 points. The study highlights a critical failure mode: severe multilingual degradation in Zero-Shot settings, underscoring weaknesses in non-English reasoning. The work provides an ecologically valid tool for assessing LLM capabilities in complex, real-world educational contexts and calls for improved bilingual reasoning robustness.

## Method Summary
The IndicEval pipeline curates authentic multiple-choice questions from official exam portals for UPSC, JEE, and NEET in both English and Hindi. Questions are human-annotated for gold labels and standardized into a JSON schema with metadata (subject, exam, language, bilingual linkage). Three fixed prompt templates are generated per language: Zero-Shot (question only), Few-Shot (with 2–3 exemplars), and Chain-of-Thought (requesting step-by-step reasoning). Model queries are executed via APIs with constant generation parameters (`temperature=0`, `max_tokens=512`). A regex-based parser extracts answer letters from responses. Accuracy is computed via exact-match and stratified by model, subject, language, and prompting strategy, with three independent runs per configuration.

## Key Results
- Chain-of-Thought prompting produces large, consistent accuracy improvements across models and subjects, e.g., Gemini 2.0 Flash Hindi Chemistry (+27.05 pp from Zero-Shot).
- Significant cross-model gaps are revealed, such as a 45.35-point difference between Gemini 2.0 Flash and LLaMA 3-70B on UPSC Hindi (Zero-Shot).
- Severe multilingual degradation occurs: Hindi accuracy drops sharply versus English under Zero-Shot (e.g., LLaMA UPSC: 67.00% EN vs. 39.53% HI), with CoT only partially mitigating the gap.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Chain-of-Thought Scaffolding
- **Claim:** CoT prompting improves accuracy on complex, multi-step exam questions by externalizing intermediate reasoning steps.
- **Mechanism:** The prompt template instructs the model to generate step-by-step reasoning before finalizing an answer, scaffolding the latent reasoning process and reducing premature conclusion errors.
- **Core assumption:** The model possesses parametric knowledge and basic reasoning capability but fails to consistently activate correct multi-step inference chains without explicit structural guidance.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "CoT prompting consistently improves reasoning accuracy, with substantial gains across subjects and languages." [section 5.1] Reports gains e.g., Gemini 2.0 Flash Hindi Chemistry (+27.05 pp), LLaMA 3-70B UPSC Hindi (+29.07 pp).
- **Break condition:** If a model's internal reasoning pathways are fundamentally misaligned with the domain's logical structure, CoT may not yield gains or could amplify errors.

### Mechanism 2: Cross-Model Reasoning Disparities
- **Claim:** Significant and systematic cross-model performance disparities exist on high-complexity, domain-specific exams, revealing architectural and training differences in applied reasoning robustness.
- **Mechanism:** Differences in model architecture, pretraining data distribution, and optimization lead to variable mastery of disciplinary reasoning patterns. The framework exposes these gaps using ecologically valid, high-stakes exam questions demanding integrated knowledge and multi-step problem-solving.
- **Core assumption:** General-purpose language modeling capability does not uniformly translate to domain-grounded academic reasoning competence; specialized reasoning pathways are unevenly developed.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Significant cross-model performance disparities persist, particularly in high-complexity examinations." [section 5.3] Largest gap: 45.35 pp (Gemini 84.88% vs LLaMA 39.53% on UPSC Hindi, Zero-Shot).
- **Break condition:** If all models were trained on curricula isomorphic to exam domains, disparities would diminish; convergence through targeted post-training would also reduce gaps.

### Mechanism 3: Multilingual Degradation Failure Mode
- **Claim:** A steep drop in Hindi accuracy versus English, especially under Zero-Shot, indicates weaknesses in bilingual cognitive processing and script-specific representation.
- **Mechanism:** Tokenization, training data imbalance, and representational mismatch cause a disconnect between Hindi's linguistic structure (Devanagari script, syntax) and required reasoning patterns. Zero-Shot conditions exacerbate this by lacking cross-lingual grounding; CoT partially mitigates by providing a reasoning template.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs do not process non-English languages with the same depth of conceptual abstraction and logical linkage as English for complex academic domains; this is a representational issue beyond data volume.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Marked Hindi accuracy drops versus English, particularly under Zero-Shot (e.g., LLaMA UPSC: 67.00% English vs. 39.53% Hindi)." [section 5.4] Quantifies gaps; CoT reduces but does not eliminate them.
- **Break condition:** With sufficient high-quality parallel data and balanced multilingual training objectives that enforce cross-lingual semantic consistency at the reasoning layer, degradation could be minimized.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Bilingual Cognitive Processing in LLMs
  - **Why needed here:** The framework evaluates reasoning where language is integrally tied to domain concepts (e.g., legal terms in UPSC Hindi). Understanding that a model's ability to reason in Hindi may involve different internal pathways than in English is key to interpreting large performance gaps.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might a model's Chain-of-Thought in Hindi be less effective at improving math problem accuracy compared to English, even if it knows the relevant Hindi terminology?

- **Concept:** Ecological Validity in Benchmarking
  - **Why needed here:** IndicEval's value stems from using *real, high-stakes exam questions* rather than synthetic tasks. The questions possess inherent complexity, distractors, and domain conventions that simpler benchmarks lack, reflecting real-world educational competence.
  - **Quick check question:** How could a model score highly on a general "logical reasoning" benchmark but fail on an authentic UPSC polity question that requires integrating constitutional articles with current affairs?

- **Concept:** Prompting Strategy as an Independent Variable
  - **Why needed here:** The paper treats Zero-Shot, Few-Shot, and CoT as controlled experimental conditions revealing different aspects of model capability (latent knowledge, example assimilation, reasoning scaffolding). The occasional underperformance of Few-Shot is a critical finding about example sensitivity.
  - **Quick check question:** What might explain a scenario where Few-Shot prompting yields *lower* accuracy than Zero-Shot for a model on a physics problem?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data Ingestion Module -> Annotation & Schema Engine -> Prompt Factory -> Query Orchestrator -> Response Parser -> Evaluation & Dashboard
- **Critical path:** Data Collection → Human Annotation → Metadata Tagging → JSON Structuring → Prompt Generation → Batch Model Querying → Automated Answer Extraction → Accuracy Computation. Breakage at the Parser or Query Orchestrator propagates to all results.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *MCQ-only vs. Subjective:* MCQs enable exact-match automation but limit assessment of partial reasoning or procedural steps.
  - *Fixed API vs. Local:* API access enables testing large proprietary models but introduces version lock-in and reproducibility dependencies.
  - *Bilingual focus (EN/HI) vs. Pan-Indic:* Depth in two languages provides clean comparison but misses broader linguistic diversity patterns.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Parser failure: High "no valid option extracted" rate, especially for Hindi responses with creative formatting.
  - Model instability: High variance across the three runs for a given model-prompt-subject combo.
  - Unexpected Few-Shot dip: Accuracy(CoT) > Accuracy(Zero-Shot) > Accuracy(Few-Shot) for a configuration, indicating exemplar misalignment.
  - Language gap explosion: Zero-Shot Hindi accuracy drops >30 pp below English for the same subject, signaling severe representational bias.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline Establishment:** Run all models on a small, balanced subset (e.g., 20 questions per subject) in English under Zero-Shot to verify pipeline integrity and establish preliminary performance ordering.
  2. **CoT Sanity Check:** On the same subset, switch to CoT prompts. Confirm that accuracy improves for at least one model-subject pair, validating that the CoT template is being correctly applied and parsed.
  3. **Bilingual Disparity Probe:** Select one high-performing subject in English (e.g., NEET Biology). Run Zero-Shot for that subject in Hindi. Expect and quantify the accuracy drop. This confirms the language processing gap is detectable in the pipeline before full-scale runs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How do LLMs perform on long-form, subjective reasoning tasks (e.g., essay answers, derivations) in Indian high-stakes exam contexts, compared to multiple-choice questions?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors note their evaluation is restricted to multiple-choice questions, which "do not capture partial credit or procedural reasoning transparency" (Section 6, Limitations).
  - **Why unresolved:** The current benchmark only uses exact-match scoring on MCQs, preventing assessment of reasoning quality for multi-step problems where partial logic or intermediate steps matter.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Extending IndicEval to include subjective question formats (e.g., JEE/NEET numerical derivations, UPSC essay prompts) with rubric-based, human-annotated scoring of reasoning traces and partial correctness.

- **Open Question 2:** To what extent does multilingual degradation persist across other major Indian languages (e.g., Tamil, Bengali, Marathi) beyond English and Hindi?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The framework currently includes only English and Hindi, limiting "broader generalization across India’s linguistic diversity" (Section 6, Limitations).
  - **Why unresolved:** The observed severe Hindi degradation may be language-specific or reflect broader structural issues in Indic NLP; without data from other languages, the scale and pattern of multilingual gaps remain unclear.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Curating parallel exam-question subsets in additional Indian languages and measuring cross-lingual accuracy gaps, controlling for script family, language resource availability, and tokenization effects.

- **Open Question 3:** What causes the observed underperformance of Few-Shot prompting relative to Zero-Shot in some configurations (e.g., Gemini on JEE Physics)?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Table 4 shows Few-Shot accuracy sometimes lower than Zero-Shot (e.g., Gemini JEE Physics English: 51.85% Few-Shot vs. 67.59% Zero-Shot). The Discussion notes this was "unexpected" and suggests "exemplar selection, formatting sensitivity, and contextual interference" may play a role, but no analysis is provided.
  - **Why unresolved:** The study used fixed exemplars without varying their relevance, diversity, or presentation, leaving the mechanism—whether noise, misalignment, or interference—unexplained.

## Limitations
- Dataset provenance is unspecified: exact sources, years, and bilingual pairing methodology for exam questions are not detailed, hindering faithful replication.
- Prompt engineering details are unreported: precise Few-Shot exemplars and Chain-of-Thought templates must be inferred, introducing reconstruction uncertainty.
- API-based evaluation introduces version lock-in and reproducibility dependencies; fixed generation parameters may not be stable long-term.

## Confidence
- **CoT gains:** Medium. Strong internal evidence but lacks direct neighbor validation; effect may be prompt-sensitive.
- **Cross-model gaps:** High. Robustly supported by reported results across multiple models and subjects.
- **Multilingual degradation:** High. Consistent, large-magnitude gaps observed; corroborated by related Indian-language benchmark studies.
