# SOTAlign: Semi-Supervised Alignment of Unimodal Vision and Language Models via Optimal Transport

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.23353
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23353
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves 26.5% COCO T2I R@1 and 46.1% ImageNet zero-shot top-1 with 10k CC3M pairs + 1M unpaired, surpassing supervised SAIL (21.0% / 35.6%).

## Executive Summary
SOTAlign tackles semi-supervised alignment of frozen unimodal vision and language encoders using minimal paired data (e.g., 10k samples) and abundant unpaired images/text. It employs a two-stage approach: first, a linear teacher (Procrustes, CCA, or contrastive) is fitted on the small paired set to recover a coarse shared geometry; second, a novel KLOT divergence refines the alignment by transferring this geometry to unpaired data while avoiding over-constraint. An explicit gradient for the OT-based loss eliminates memory bottlenecks, enabling large-batch training. Empirical results show SOTAlign significantly outperforms supervised and semi-supervised baselines, reaches SAIL's performance with ~4× less supervision, and remains robust to moderate distribution shifts. The work provides both a practical framework and theoretical insights into geometry-preserving alignment.

## Method Summary
The method comprises two stages. In Stage 1, a linear alignment teacher is learned from the limited paired dataset (A, B). This involves either orthogonal Procrustes, CCA, or a contrastive SigLIP loss to obtain linear projections W_x and W_y that map the unimodal embedding spaces into a shared d-dimensional space. Stage 2 trains trainable alignment layers f and g (e.g., MLPs) on a mixture of the original paired data and large unpaired image and text sets. The training loss combines a supervised SigLIP term on paired samples with an unsupervised KLOT regularization on unpaired samples. The KLOT term uses the teacher's affinity matrix K* (cosine similarities between XW_x^T and YW_y^T) as a target and aligns the student's affinity K (cosine between f(X) and g(Y)) via a KL divergence between entropic optimal transport plans. Crucially, the gradient of KLOT is computed analytically as (OT_ε(K) - OT_ε*(K*)) / ε*, avoiding backpropagation through Sinkhorn iterations. Training is performed with a fixed α weighting, and only f and g are updated; the linear teacher remains frozen. This design leverages the structure captured by the teacher while allowing the flexible alignment layers to adapt to the full data distribution.

## Key Results
- SOTAlign (with 10k CC3M pairs + 1M unpaired) achieves 26.5% COCO text-to-image R@1 and 46.1% ImageNet zero-shot top-1 accuracy, surpassing supervised SAIL (21.0% / 35.6%).
- It attains SAIL's performance with approximately 4× fewer paired samples, demonstrating strong sample efficiency.
- Performance gains generalize across unpaired data sources and correlate strongly (Pearson r = -0.96) with the distributional distance (SSW) between unpaired and paired data, indicating robustness to moderate shift.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Meaningful alignment can be recovered from very few paired samples using a linear teacher, supporting the Platonic Representation Hypothesis.
- **Mechanism:** A simple linear transformation (Procrustes, CCA, or contrastive) is fit on the small paired dataset (A, B) to produce projections Wx and Wy. These projections map the unimodal embedding spaces into a shared space where cross-modal affinities (cosine similarities) are already surprisingly aligned.
- **Core assumption:** Pretrained unimodal encoders encode semantically compatible structures, so a linear projection suffices to align them with minimal paired data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "first recovers a coarse shared geometry from limited paired data using a linear teacher"
  - [Section 4] Details linear methods and Table 1 shows standalone performance (e.g., CCA 21.5% MeanR@1 on COCO with 10k pairs).
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "GeRA: Label-Efficient Geometrically Regularized Alignment" and "Learning Shared Representations from Unpaired Data" also explore linear alignment from few pairs, suggesting this is an active assumption in the field.
- **Break condition:** If the unimodal encoders are pretrained on drastically disjoint data distributions (e.g., medical images vs. legal text) where initial geometries are inherently misaligned, a linear teacher trained on few pairs may fail to capture any shared structure.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** KLOT divergence refines alignment by transferring the linear teacher's geometry to unpaired data without over-constraining the target space.
- **Mechanism:** For each batch of unpaired images X and texts Y, the target affinity matrix K* is computed as cosine similarities between (XWx^T) and (YWy^T). The trainable encoders f and g are regularized to produce affinities K that match K* in terms of the full optimal transport plan (via KL(OT_ε*(K*) || OT_ε(K))), not just nearest neighbors. This preserves relational structure while allowing flexibility.
- **Core assumption:** The geometry induced by the linear teacher on unpaired data (via pseudo-labels K*) is a sufficiently good prior for the true underlying cross-modal alignment.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "refines with a novel KLOT divergence that transfers relational structure without overconstraining the target space"
  - [Section 5] Defines KLOT (Equation 12) and contrasts it with InfoNCE which only enforces nearest-neighbor agreement.
  - [Section 6.2, Figure 5] Shows performance correlates with SSW distance to paired data, implying that when K* is close to the true distribution, KLOT helps.
- **Break condition:** If the unpaired data are from a domain distribution very far from the paired data (high SSW distance), the teacher's geometry K* becomes a misleading prior, and KLOT regularization may force the model toward an incorrect alignment.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Explicit gradient computation for KLOT removes memory bottlenecks, enabling large-batch training and scalability.
- **Mechanism:** Theorem 5.1 derives ∇_K KLOT(K||K*) = (OT_ε(K) - OT_ε*(K*)) / ε*. This avoids backpropagating through Sinkhorn iterations (unrolling) or implicit differentiation, reducing memory from O(n * #iterations) to O(n^2) for the transport plan storage only, and significantly speeding up computation.
- **Core assumption:** The Sinkhorn algorithm's optimal transport plan OT_ε(K) can be treated as a differentiable function of K via this closed-form gradient without needing to store intermediate iterate states.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "explicit gradient for the OT-based loss, eliminating memory bottlenecks"
  - [Section 5, Theorem 5.1] Presents the gradient formula.
  - [Section B.1, Figure 6] Empirically shows 50x faster runtime and 100x less memory than unrolling/implicit methods.
- **Break condition:** If batch size n is extremely large (e.g., millions), even storing the n×n transport plan may exceed GPU memory, though this is a general OT limitation not specific to the gradient formula.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE/SigLIP)
- **Why needed here:** The supervised component of SOTAlign uses SigLIP loss on the small paired set. Understanding contrastive objectives is necessary to grasp how the initial linear teacher is trained and how the supervised loss L(θ; A, B) operates.
- **Quick check question:** Why does SigLIP use a sigmoid loss instead of softmax-based InfoNCE, and how does its learned logit scale affect the affinity matrix K?

### Concept: Optimal Transport and Sinkhorn Algorithm
- **Why needed here:** KLOT divergence is core to SOTAlign's unpaired regularization. One must understand OT plans, entropic regularization, and how Sinkhorn computes OT_ε(K) to appreciate the gradient derivation and the "transport plan vs. distribution" interpretation.
- **Quick check question:** What is the effect of the entropy regularization parameter ε on the sharpness of the transport plan, and why does the paper use different ε for teacher (ε*) and student (ε)?

### Concept: Linear Alignment Methods (Procrustes, CCA)
- **Why needed here:** The linear teacher is built using these methods. Knowing their constraints (orthogonal vs. uncorrelated projections) explains why CCA+KLOT performed best in Table 1 and how they differ from learned contrastive projections.
- **Quick check question:** How does CCA's constraint (shared space directions orthogonal) differ from Procrustes's (transformations orthogonal), and why might CCA be more suitable when dx ≠ dy?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map:
1. **Front-end encoders**: Frozen pretrained image encoder (e.g., DINOv3) and text encoder (e.g., NV-Embed-v2). Output embeddings X (images) and Y (texts) of sizes dx, dy.
2. **Linear teacher**: Trained on small paired set (A, B) via one of: Procrustes, CCA, or contrastive (SigLIP). Produces projections Wx ∈ ℝ^{dx×d}, Wy ∈ ℝ^{dy×d}.
3. **Alignment layers**: Trainable linear fθ1 and gθ2 that map unimodal embeddings to shared ℝ^d. They are optimized end-to-end.
4. **Loss computation**:
   - Supervised term: SigLIP loss on paired batches (f(A), g(B)).
   - Unsupervised term: For unpaired batches (X_b, Y_b), compute target K* = cosine(X_b Wx^T, Y_b Wy^T^), current K = cosine(f(X_b), g(Y_b)), then KLOT(K || K*).
5. **Gradient module**: Uses Theorem 5.1 to compute ∇_K KLOT explicitly, avoiding Sinkhorn unrolling.

### Critical path:
1. Fit (Wx, Wy) on all paired (A, B) once (offline).
2. For each training iteration:
   a. Sample mixed batch: up to 10k paired (A_b, B_b) + unpaired (X_b, Y_b) to fill 32k total.
   b. Compute supervised SigLIP loss on (A_b, B_b).
   c. Compute K* on (X_b, Y_b) using fixed Wx, Wy.
   d. Compute current K on (X_b, Y_b) via f, g.
   e. Compute KLOT(K, K*) via explicit gradient: L_KLOT = KL(OT_ε*(K*) || OT_ε(K)); gradient = (OT_ε(K) - OT_ε*(K*)) / ε*.
   f. Total loss = L_supervised + α * L_KLOT.
   g. Update only f, g (Wx, Wy frozen).
3. Hyperparameter: α controls regularization strength; chosen via validation on CC3M.

### Design tradeoffs:
- **Linear vs. non-linear alignment layers**: Paper uses linear for robustness in low-supervision regime; non-linear MLPs risk overfitting on few pairs.
- **Teacher choice**: CCA + KLOT performed best (Table 1). CCA's orthogonal shared-space constraint may provide a more stable geometry than contrastive teacher's, which could overfit to the few pairs.
- **ε parameters**: Teacher uses smaller ε* (0.01) for sharper target plan; student uses larger ε (0.05) for smoother updates. Tradeoff: sharper teacher gives clearer signal but less tolerance to noise; smoother student prevents over-constraint.
- **Batch composition**: Max 10k pairs + unpaired to 32k total. More unpaired helps up to ~500k samples (Figure 4), but diminishing returns suggest a sweet spot for memory vs. gain.

### Failure signatures:
- **Linear teacher failure**: If standalone linear method performs very poorly (<15% MeanR@1) on the paired set (e.g., with extremely few pairs < 100 or highly mismatched encoders), KLOT will propagate bad geometry and degrade performance.
- **Distribution shift failure**: When unpaired data SSW distance to paired data is high (>0.02 in Figure 10/11), gains vanish or reverse. The model may overfit to the shifted unpaired distribution.
- **Hyperparameter mis-specification**: α too high → model adheres too strictly to teacher, unable to adapt; α too low → unpaired data unused effectively. Poor ε choices lead to unstable OT plans (non-convergent Sinkhorn) or trivial solutions.
- **Gradient instability**: Despite explicit gradient, if OT_ε(K) is near-singular (low entropic regularization), gradient norms may explode; requires careful ε tuning.

### First 3 experiments:
1. **Ablation of teacher and divergence (Table 1)**: Fix 10k CC3M pairs, no unpaired data. Train linear teacher (Procrustes/CCA/Contrastive) and then train f,g with different divergences (CKA/InfoNCE/KLOT). Outcome: CCA + KLOT yields 30.3% MeanR@1, outperforming supervised SAIL (24.2%) with only pairs. This validates that KLOT on top of a good linear teacher is the key.
2. **Paired sample scaling (Figure 4 left)**: Vary paired samples from 100 to 100k, fix 1M unpaired from CC3M. Compare SOTAlign vs. supervised SAIL. Outcome: SOTAlign consistently outperforms, reaching SAIL's 10k-pair performance with ~2.5k pairs. Shows effectiveness in low-supervision regime.
3. **Cross-dataset unpaired data (Table 7)**: Use 10k CC3M pairs, but unpaired images from ImageNet/COCO/CC12M and text from CC3M/CC12M/WikiText. Measure ImageNet classification and COCO retrieval. Outcome: Gains persist even with disjoint sources; ImageNet images improve classification, COCO images improve retrieval. Correlates with SSW distance (Figure 5), confirming robustness to distribution shift within reason.

## Limitations
- Batch composition: Exact sampling strategy for unpaired data (e.g., independent random sampling vs. index-aligned) is unspecified, potentially affecting the affinity computation.
- Implementation gaps: Details such as whether embeddings are L2-normalized before cosine similarity, the exact architecture of alignment layers (depth, nonlinearities), and CCA eigenvalue regularization are not fully described, hindering exact reproduction.
- Distribution shift: While robust to moderate shifts, performance deteriorates when unpaired data are from a distribution far from the paired set (high SSW distance); the boundary of acceptable shift is not quantified.

## Confidence
- Linear teacher sufficiency: Medium
- KLOT divergence effectiveness: Medium-High
- Explicit gradient scalability: High
- Overall semi-supervised gains: High

## Next Checks
1. **Batch construction fidelity**: Replicate the mixed batch sampling (up to 10k paired, fill to 32k with independent unpaired images/text). Evaluate performance sensitivity to batch size, paired ratio, and whether unpaired images/text are sampled independently or in fixed pairs within the batch.
2. **Teacher quality ablation**: Replace the linear teacher with random projections or a poorly trained one (e.g., Procrustes on 100 pairs). Measure impact on final accuracy; expect significant drop if teacher signal is insufficient.
3. **Large-batch memory and convergence**: Train with batch sizes from 1k to 32k, monitor GPU memory and Sinkhorn iteration convergence (marginal constraints). Identify practical limits and whether approximations (e.g., low-rank OT) are needed for >32k.
