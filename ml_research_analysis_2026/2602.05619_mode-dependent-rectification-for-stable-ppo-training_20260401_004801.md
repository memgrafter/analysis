# Mode-Dependent Rectification for Stable PPO Training

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2602.05619
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05619
*   **Reference count:** 24
*   **Primary result:** Proposed Mode-Dependent Rectification (MDR) stabilizes PPO training with BatchNorm by correcting trust-region violations, achieving near-optimal performance in patch-localization and superior returns in Procgen compared to collapsing baselines.

## Executive Summary
Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) relies on a trust region defined by a clipped surrogate objective to ensure stable policy updates. This work identifies that mode-dependent layers like BatchNorm destabilize PPO by creating a mismatch between the policy used for data collection (evaluation mode) and the policy used for gradient updates (training mode). This mismatch, compounded by the non-stationary state distribution in RL, effectively distorts PPO's clipping bound, leading to reward collapse. The authors propose Mode-Dependent Rectification (MDR), a simple two-phase training procedure that alternates standard updates with a deterministic evaluation-mode rectification phase to correct trust-region violations. Experiments across Procgen games and real-world patch-localization tasks show MDR consistently improves stability and performance, and the principle extends to other stochastic layers like dropout.

## Method Summary
MDR is a training schedule applied to an existing PPO implementation that uses mode-dependent layers (e.g., BatchNorm, dropout). In each PPO iteration, after collecting a trajectory dataset *D_k* in evaluation mode, the fixed number of gradient epochs is re-allocated into two sequential phases on the same data: 1) a **Standard Update Phase** where the network is in training mode, and 2) a **Rectification Phase** where the network is switched back to evaluation mode. No additional data collection or gradient steps are introduced; the protocol simply changes the network's mode for a portion of the minibatch updates. This allows the policy to benefit from the inductive biases of training-mode stochasticity while using the rectification phase to undo the trust-region distortion caused by the mode mismatch.

## Key Results
*   MDR prevented catastrophic reward collapse in BatchNorm policies on Procgen and patch-localization, achieving normalized rewards up to 97% of the optimal (oracle) performance in the latter task.
*   On Procgen, BatchNorm+MDR significantly outperformed the unstable BatchNorm-only baseline and matched or exceeded the always-eval baseline in average return across six diverse games.
*   The MDR principle extended to dropout (10%), where it improved both training and test performance relative to a no-normalization baseline and reduced overfitting on held-out test levels.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Policy Mismatch from Mode-Dependent Layers
BatchNorm induces a fundamental policy mismatch in PPO. During interaction, actions are sampled from the policy in **evaluation mode** (using running statistics µ_r, σ_r). During updates, gradients are computed using the policy in **training mode** (using minibatch statistics µ_B, σ_B). For the same parameters θ, this creates two distinct stochastic policies, π_eval and π_train. PPO's trust region assumes the sampling policy (π_old) is identical to the policy implicitly evaluated during the update. Breaking this invariant means the importance ratio r_t = π_eval/π_old, central to the clipped objective, is miscalculated as a corrupted ratio r'_t = π_train/π_old. The divergence between π_train and π_eval (∆π) is the root perturbation.

*Evidence Anchors:* Abstract states "discrepancies between training and evaluation behavior... lead to policy mismatch". Section 3.2 formalizes the dataset collection under π_eval vs. updates under π_train. The analysis of the corrupted ratio r'_t and its effect on the clipping bound is in Section 3.3.

### Mechanism 2: Distributional Drift Amplifies Mismatch
The non-stationary state distribution µ_k in on-policy RL exacerbates the mode mismatch. As the policy improves, the visited state distribution shifts. BatchNorm's running mean/variance (µ_r, σ_r) are exponential averages over *all past* distributions, making them stale relative to the current minibatch statistics from the latest distribution µ_{k+1}. This distributional drift causes the gap between π_train and π_eval to be largest at the start of each training cycle, as the running estimates are most inconsistent with the new data.

*Evidence Anchors:* Abstract and Section 3.2 discuss distributional drift. Figure 6 shows ∆π⁻ rising steadily before collapse in BN-only runs.

### Mechanism 3: Distortion of the Trust Region Clipping Bound
The corrupted ratio r'_t distorts PPO's effective trust region. The intended clipping region for the ratio is [1-ϵ, 1+ϵ]. The perturbation δr = r'_t - r_t shifts these boundaries. If δr is positive, the region expands to [1-ϵ-|δr|, 1+ϵ+|δr|]. This is equivalent to an altered clipping parameter ϵ' = ϵ + ∆ϵ (Section 3.3, Eq. 11). The PPO optimizer then takes a step that would be illegal under the original trust region, leading to instability and potential collapse when ∆ϵ becomes too large.

*Evidence Anchors:* Section 3.3 and Figure 2 illustrate this perturbation view of the trust-region violation.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Concept: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) Clipped Objective**
    *   *Why needed:* The entire mechanism hinges on PPO's stability guarantee from clipping the probability ratio `r_t`. Understanding how `min(r_t * A, clip(r_t, 1-ϵ, 1+ϵ)*A)` enforces a trust region is key to seeing how a corrupted `r'_t` breaks it.
    *   *Quick check:* What does the `clip` term enforce, and why is the accurate calculation of `r_t` (under the same policy used for data collection) critical?
*   **Concept: Batch Normalization Modes**
    *   *Why needed:* The core problem is the dual behavior of BatchNorm: `training=True` uses batch statistics, `training=False` uses running estimates. This difference creates the two policies.
    *   *Quick check:* During RL data collection, should BatchNorm use `train` or `eval` mode for consistent behavior? Why is this choice non-obvious?
*   **Concept: Non-Stationarity in On-Policy RL**
    *   *Why needed:* BatchNorm's design assumes i.i.d. data from a fixed distribution. In RL, the state distribution µ_k changes every iteration, making running statistics inherently stale and worsening the train/eval gap.
    *   *Quick check:* Why are BatchNorm's running averages stable in supervised learning but not in on-policy RL?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component map:** Input -> Shallow ResNet-18 (Blocks 1-3 with BatchNorm/dropout) -> Flattened Features -> Policy Head (MLP) & Value Head (MLP) -> PPO Engine
*   **Critical path (per PPO iteration k):**
    1.  **Interaction:** Set network to `eval` mode. Collect |D_k| steps. Compute GAE advantages.
    2.  **Standard Update Phase:** Set network to `train` mode. Perform α1 * (|D_k|/m) SGD steps on minibatches from D_k using the PPO loss with `train`-mode forward passes.
    3.  **Rectification Phase:** Set network to `eval` mode. Perform α2 * (|D_k|/m) SGD steps on minibatches from D_k using the PPO loss with `eval`-mode forward passes. (α1 + α2 = total epochs).
*   **Design tradeoffs:**
    *   **MDR vs. Baselines:** `Eval-only` avoids mismatch but forgoes training-mode regularization benefits (e.g., dropout). `BN-only` (train-mode updates) is unstable. MDR aims to capture both benefits.
    *   **Phase Ratios (α1, α2):** Tuning controls the balance between learning (α1) and correction (α2). The paper finds (2,1) and (1,2) both work, indicating some robustness.
    *   **No Overhead:** MDR reuses the collected data D_k and total gradient steps; it only re-allocates epochs between two network modes.
*   **Failure signatures:**
    *   **Instability:** A steadily increasing or spiking measured divergence ∆π⁻ (JS divergence between `train`-mode and `eval`-mode action distributions on D_k).
    *   **Collapse:** Sudden drop of episode return to the minimum possible reward, often coinciding with a permanent spike in ∆π⁻.
    *   **MDR behavior:** Under MDR, ∆π⁻ may spike after the standard phase but then decay during the rectification phase. Failure would be if spikes grow across iterations or do not decay.
*   **First 3 experiments:**
    1.  **Ablation on Patch-Localization:** Compare `BN` (train-mode updates), `Eval` (fixed eval), and `BN+MDR` on medical and natural image tasks. Expect BN to collapse, Eval to be stable but lower, BN+MDR to be stable and highest.
    2.  **Procgen Generalization Test:** Train `BN+MDR`, `BN`, `Eval`, and other normalizations (LN, GN, BRN) on 6 Procgen games with 500 levels. Expect BN+MDR to outperform collapsing BN and match/exceed Eval.
    3.  **Extension to Dropout:** Replace BatchNorm with dropout (10%). Compare `Dropout`, `Dropout+MDR`, and `NoNorm` on Procgen (train/test levels) and histopathology. Expect Dropout to be unstable/underperform, Dropout+MDR to exceed NoNorm and reduce overfitting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
*   **Question:** Can MDR be effectively combined with Batch Renormalization (BRN) to further enhance stability and performance beyond what each achieves alone?
*   **Basis in paper:** The paper states: "While a full evaluation of their combination is beyond the scope of this work, preliminary results on histopathology indicate potential gains for BRN when trained under MDR."
*   **Why unresolved:** Only preliminary results on one task are reported; a systematic evaluation across all benchmarks is lacking.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Full experiments integrating MDR with BRN across Procgen and patch-localization benchmarks, measuring stability and performance compared to standalone MDR and BRN.

### Open Question 2
*   **Question:** How do the hyperparameters α1 and α2 (governing standard vs. rectification phase lengths) affect MDR's performance, and can optimal values be derived from task characteristics?
*   **Basis in paper:** The paper notes α1 and α2 are tunable and that both (1,2) and (2,1) configurations worked reliably, but no ablation study is provided.
*   **Why unresolved:** The impact of phase ratios across diverse tasks and architectures is unexplored, limiting practical applicability.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** A comprehensive ablation varying α1/α2 ratios (e.g., 0:1 to 3:1) on multiple tasks, analyzing performance to identify patterns.

### Open Question 3
*   **Question:** Does the rectification phase's benefit stem primarily from entropy regularization or from deterministic evaluation-mode optimization, and can the mechanism be formalized to provide theoretical guarantees?
*   **Basis in paper:** The paper intuitively links rectification to entropy favoring stochastic policies but lacks formal analysis; the perturbation view of ∆ϵ is proposed without derivation or bounds.
*   **Why unresolved:** Without theoretical grounding, it's unclear whether MDR universally prevents trust-region violations or only under specific conditions.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Theoretical analysis deriving conditions under which rectification minimizes KL divergence, coupled with controlled experiments ablating entropy to isolate its role.

### Open Question 4
*   **Question:** Is MDR's stabilizing effect specific to PPO's clipped objective, or does it extend to other on-policy algorithms (e.g., A2C, TRPO)?
*   **Basis in paper:** The paper frames MDR within PPO, explicitly targeting its clipping mechanism.
*   **Why unresolved:** Other algorithms have different update rules (e.g., TRPO's natural gradient), so MDR may require adaptation.
*   **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementing MDR's two-phase structure for A2C and TRPO on the same benchmarks, assessing whether gains persist.

## Limitations
*   **Architectural Generalization:** Experiments used a shallow ResNet-18; applicability to very deep networks, transformers, or RNN-based policies is unverified.
*   **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Phase ratios (α₁, α₂) were tuned on a subset of tasks; their robustness across diverse difficulties or action spaces is unknown.
*   **Benchmark Scope:** Validation is limited to Procgen (discrete control) and patch-localization (CNN-based); missing standard benchmarks like Atari-100k or MuJoCo (continuous control).

## Confidence
*   **High:** BatchNorm-induced policy mismatch and distributional drift cause PPO instability (supported by controlled ablations and ∆π⁻ measurements).
*   **High:** MDR’s alternating rectification phase restores trust-region compliance (consistent collapse avoidance and ∆π⁻ decay across tasks).
*   **Medium:** Extension to dropout and other stochastic layers (evidence limited to dropout; other regularizers untested).
*   **Medium:** Cross-domain performance gains (validated on Procgen and patch-localization; missing broader benchmarks).

## Next Checks
