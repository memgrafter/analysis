# HEEGNet: Hyperbolic Embeddings for EEG

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.03322  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03322  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Achieved 81.7% grand average balanced accuracy across multiple EEG benchmarks with DSMDBN+EA.

## Executive Summary
EEG decoding across subjects is hindered by distribution shifts. This paper shows EEG data exhibits hierarchical structure (low δ_rel) and hyperbolic embeddings improve generalization. HEEGNet is proposed, a hybrid network combining Euclidean feature extraction with hyperbolic encoding, plus a two-stage domain adaptation (DSMDBN) that first aligns moments then distributions in hyperbolic space. It achieves state-of-the-art cross-subject/session balanced accuracy on VEP, emotion, and intracranial EEG datasets, with an 81.7% grand average using DSMDBN+EA. Ablations confirm moment alignment in hyperbolic space is the primary performance driver, and the hybrid design is essential.

## Method Summary
HEEGNet employs a hybrid architecture: Euclidean convolutional layers (from EEGNet) extract spectral-spatial-temporal features from raw EEG. These features are projected onto the Lorentz manifold via a linear projection (ProjX). Hyperbolic operations (pointwise convolutions) then refine hierarchical relationships. For domain adaptation, DSMDBN operates in two stages: first, Riemannian batch normalization (HBN) aligns domain-specific moments (mean, variance) in hyperbolic space; second, a distribution alignment loss (HHSW) matches feature distributions to a standard hyperbolic Gaussian. During training, both stages are used; at test-time, only moment alignment is applied to adapt the target domain.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art cross-subject/session balanced accuracy on VEP, emotion, and intracranial EEG benchmarks.
- Grand average accuracy of 81.7% with DSMDBN+EA, significantly outperforming baselines.
- Ablation studies show moment alignment in hyperbolic space is the primary performance driver, and the hybrid Euclidean-hyperbolic design is essential.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim**: EEG data's hierarchical structure is best captured in hyperbolic geometry, improving cross-domain generalization.  
**Mechanism**: Hyperbolic space's negative curvature offers exponential capacity for tree-like hierarchies, matching cognitive processes. The paper quantifies tree-likeness via δ_rel, showing low values (~0.05–0.15) for EEG embeddings. Replacing Euclidean MLR with hyperbolic MLR (HMLR) boosts performance by better separating classes in hierarchical latent spaces.  
**Core assumption**: Hierarchical cognitive encoding manifests in EEG features, and hyperbolic geometry is the optimal continuous analogue for trees.  
**Evidence anchors**: Pilot study confirming hyperbolicity; HMLR improvements; δ_rel values in tables; supporting work like EEG2GAIT, ViEEG.  
**Break condition**: If EEG from a new domain has high δ_rel (>0.3), indicating weak hierarchy, hyperbolic embeddings may not help or could harm performance.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim**: Hybrid Euclidean-hyperbolic encoder leverages Euclidean efficiency for feature extraction and hyperbolic efficacy for hierarchical encoding.  
**Mechanism**: Euclidean conv layers extract neurophysiologically plausible features (spectral-spatial-temporal). ProjX maps these to hyperbolic space where hyperbolic convolutions refine hierarchical relationships without distorting low-level features, avoiding optimization issues of fully hyperbolic networks.  
**Core assumption**: Euclidean ops are optimal for initial EEG processing; hyperbolic space better for encoding resulting hierarchies.  
**Evidence anchors**: Abstract stating hybrid design; Section 3.2 on hybrid nature; Appendix F/Tab.11 showing fully hyperbolic degradation.  
**Break condition**: If Euclidean backbone fails to extract discriminative features (e.g., high noise), hyperbolic refinement has poor input and harms performance.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim**: Two-stage DSMDBN (moment alignment then distribution matching) yields robust domain-invariant embeddings.  
**Mechanism**: Stage 1 (HBN) explicitly centers and scales each domain's statistics to a common reference in hyperbolic space, crude alignment reducing gap. Stage 2 (HHSW loss) implicitly aligns full distributions to a standard hyperbolic Gaussian, refining representations under the assumption that moments have brought domains close.  
**Core assumption**: Moment alignment is necessary for effective distribution matching under large domain shifts in hyperbolic space.  
**Evidence anchors**: Abstract on coarse-to-fine adaptation; Section 3.1 describing stages; Tab.4 ablation showing moment alignment as primary driver.  
**Break condition**: Under extremely large shifts where moment alignment fails to bring domains near, HHSW may be unstable or misguide, causing negative transfer.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Input (EEG epochs) -> Euclidean Encoder (TempConv, BN, DepthwiseSpatialConv, BN, ELU, AvgPool, DepthwiseTemporalConv) -> Projection Layer (ProjX) -> Hyperbolic Encoder (PointConv, DSMDBN(1), Lorentz ELU, Hyperbolic AvgPool, Flatten) -> Domain Adaptation Module (DSMDBN(1) during train/test, DSMDBN(2) as loss) -> Classifier (Lorentz MLR)

- **Critical path**: The projection layer (ProjX) is most critical. It must create a valid hyperbolic embedding that preserves feature hierarchy without distortion. Poor projection cascades, making DSMDBN ineffective and harming classifier performance.

- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - Hybrid vs fully hyperbolic: Hybrid uses stable Euclidean extraction but may lose finer hierarchy in early layers; fully hyperbolic could capture end-to-end hierarchy but faces instability and cost (Tab.7,11 degradation).  
  - DSMDBN stages: Both stages add overhead but yield best results; moment alignment alone is cheaper but less robust; HHSW alone is theoretically sound but unstable without moment alignment.  
  - Curvature: Fixed at K=-1 for simplicity, but adaptive curvature might better match hierarchy depth across datasets.

- **Failure signatures**:  
  - NaNs/exploding gradients → hyperbolic instability; check Fréchet mean/variance in HBN, use smaller curvature or clamp norms.  
  - No gain from DSMDBN+EA vs EA alone → weak hierarchy (high δ_rel) or misconfigured DSMDBN (e.g., incorrect γ scaling).  
  - Baseline scores diverge from Tab.3 → preprocessing mismatch; replicate filtering, resampling, epoch lengths exactly.  
  - Cross-subject leakage → verify no subject in train/test splits.  
  - Hyperbolic distances saturating → monitor p_t in Lorentz points; adjust initialization or norm normalization.

- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. Replicate pilot study: Compute δ_rel on your EEG dataset embeddings (using intermediate layer output). Expect values <0.2 to justify hyperbolic approach. Swap MLR for HMLR in baseline (e.g., EEGNet) and measure cross-subject accuracy change; should improve if hierarchy exists.  
  2. Ablate moment alignment: Disable DSMDBN(1) and use only DSMDBN(2) (HHSW) or no adaptation. Expect >12% accuracy drop vs full DSMDBN (per Tab.4).  
  3. Test hybrid architecture: Incrementally replace Euclidean convs with hyperbolic (as in Tab.11). Expect hybrid to peak and degrade when replacing deeper Euclidean layers, confirming Euclidean backbone necessity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What algorithmic optimizations can reduce computational overhead of hyperbolic operations for real-time BCI? (Paper acknowledges cost but offers no solutions.)  
2. Can fully hyperbolic architectures match hybrid designs when adequately optimized? (Preliminary fully hyperbolic variant degraded; unclear if due to optimization or fundamental limits.)  
3. Are hyperbolic embeddings universally beneficial for non-hierarchical EEG tasks like motor imagery? (Motor imagery results are competitive but not interpreted; hierarchy hypothesis may not apply.)  
4. Does optimal curvature vary with EEG modality, and can curvature adaptation improve generalization? (Fixed K=-1; δ_rel varies across datasets, suggesting data-dependent curvature might help.)

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter ranges (HHSW weight, learning rates) not systematically reported, hindering tuning.
- DSMDBN(2) mechanics ambiguous: per-batch HHSW computation and standard hyperbolic Gaussian sampling details missing.
- Exact data split definitions for BNCI/Boran datasets unclear, risking invalid splits.
- No random seeds for reproducibility of splits, initialization, or optimizers.
- Computational cost ~3× slower per epoch than EEGNet, limiting scalability.
