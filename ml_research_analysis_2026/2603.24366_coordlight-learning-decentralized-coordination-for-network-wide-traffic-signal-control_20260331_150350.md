# CoordLight: Learning Decentralized Coordination for Network-Wide Traffic Signal Control

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.24366  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24366  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: CoordLight reduces average travel time by 6.39–9.23% on Jinan, 7.87% on Hangzhou high-demand, and achieves 748–1040 seconds vs 772–1107 on New York (196 intersections) using decentralized MARL with Queue Dynamic State Encoding (QDSE) and Neighbor-aware Policy Optimization (NAPO).

## Executive Summary
CoordLight tackles adaptive traffic signal control (ATSC) in large-scale road networks using a decentralized multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) approach. The core challenges are partial observability (each intersection only sees local sensor data) and the need for coordination across intersections to avoid spillbacks and congestion. The method introduces two key innovations: Queue Dynamic State Encoding (QDSE), which extracts detailed queuing dynamics from lane-level sensors (stopped, entering, leaving, and moving vehicles), and Neighbor-aware Policy Optimization (NAPO), which leverages attention to model state-action dependencies among neighboring intersections for more accurate advantage estimation. Evaluated on real-world datasets from Jinan, Hangzhou, and New York, CoordLight significantly outperforms prior state-of-the-art methods in average travel time. Ablation studies confirm the contributions of both QDSE and NAPO, and QDSE shows robustness to sensor noise with less than 2% performance degradation.

## Method Summary
CoordLight operates within the CityFlow simulator using real-world traffic data. Each intersection agent observes its local state through QDSE: for every incoming lane, six features are extracted—stopped queue length (`Q`), entering vehicles (`N_in`), leaving vehicles (`N_out`), moving vehicles (`N_r`), foremost moving vehicle count (`N_fr`), and distance to the foremost (`D_fr`). The entering count `N_in` is predicted using an Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) to anticipate vehicles that will join the queue before the next signal decision. Agents follow homogeneous policies and make phase decisions every 5 seconds, with a 2-second yellow transition.

NAPO consists of an actor-critic architecture with attention. The actor (Spatio‑Temporal Network, STN) embeds the ego state and a positional vector, then applies multi‑head attention over four direct road neighbors (north, south, east, west) to compute weights `α` and aggregate neighbor information. A GRU captures temporal dependencies, and the output phase probabilities are sampled. The critic similarly aggregates neighbor states but also incorporates neighbor actions via an additional attention layer to compute weights `β` over actions before the GRU and value head. Training uses Independent PPO (IPPO) with a clipped surrogate objective, GAE for advantage estimation, entropy bonus, and auxiliary queue‑length prediction losses. Loss weights are set to 0.5 (value), 0.01 (entropy), and 0.005 (prediction), with a discount factor of 0.98, PPO clip ratio 0.2, 6 PPO epochs, and learning rates 0.0003 (actor) / 0.0005 (critic).

## Key Results
- **Jinan (12 intersections)**: Travel time improvement of 6.39–9.23% over the strongest baseline.
- **Hangzhou (16 intersections)**: Under high demand, travel time reduced by 7.87%.
- **New York (196 intersections)**: Two demand scenarios yield average travel times of 748.32±4.60 and 1039.15±10.05 seconds, compared to 771.92±5.04 and 1106.69±15.39 from the best prior method.
- **Ablation**: Removing QDSE or NAPO components degrades performance, confirming their necessity.
- **Robustness**: QDSE experiences less than 2% travel time increase when subjected to Gaussian sensor noise.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CoordLight succeeds because it simultaneously addresses two fundamental issues in MARL for traffic control: partial observability and coordination. QDSE enriches the local observation with fine‑grained queuing dynamics—not just counts but also predictions of entering vehicles via IDM—giving agents a more complete picture of imminent congestion. NAPO then explicitly models the influence of neighboring intersections through attention, allowing each agent to weigh relevant neighbor states and actions when estimating advantages. This neighbor‑aware credit assignment mitigates the non‑stationarity typical in decentralized training and enables emergent coordination without centralized execution. The combination of a expressive state representation and an attention‑based policy leads to more informed phase selections that balance local efficiency with network‑wide flow, resulting in significant travel time reductions across diverse real‑world networks.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Sensors (vehicle counts, positions, speeds) → QDSE extractor → Ego state vector (+position) → Actor (STN: embedding + neighbor attention + GRU) → Phase probabilities.  
Sensors + neighbor actions → Critic (embedding + action‑aware attention + GRU) → State‑value.  
Training loop: collect trajectories → compute GAE advantages (using critic) → update actor & critic via PPO losses + prediction auxiliary losses.

**Critical path**: Real‑time decision flow: raw sensor data → QDSE feature computation → actor network (attention over neighbors) → phase selection → simulator advancement → reward collection → experience buffer → advantage computation → PPO update.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Decentralization*: eliminates central point of failure and scales, but requires careful design to achieve coordination.  
- *Attention*: flexible neighbor weighting vs increased computational cost (O(4·heads) per agent).  
- *QDSE richness*: more accurate state vs reliance on IDM parameters and sensor precision.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Training instability: high variance in episode travel times or plateau at poor values.  
- Neighbor attention ineffective: performance similar to no‑neighbor baseline.  
- QDSE sensitivity: performance drops >5% with moderate sensor noise.

**First experiments**:  
1. Validate QDSE on a single intersection: manually compute features for a fixed trajectory and compare to simulator output.  
2. Ablate neighbor attention on a 4‑intersection grid: compare NAPO against an actor that only uses ego state.  
3. Test QDSE noise robustness: inject Gaussian noise (σ=10m,20m,30m) into `D_fr` and measure travel time change.

## Limitations
- **Unresolved implementation details**: Exact IDM parameters and distance threshold for `N_fr` in QDSE, and neighbor masking scheme for boundary intersections, are undocumented, hindering exact replication.
- **Noise evaluation scope**: Robustness was tested only with Gaussian noise; real‑world sensor anomalies (dropouts, bias) may cause different failure modes.
- **Domain transfer**: Results are confined to simulated cities; performance in real deployments with unpredictable driver behavior remains unproven.

## Confidence
- Scalability to large networks (196 intersections): **High**  
- Reported travel time improvements: **Medium** (due to potential implementation gaps)  
- Ablation attribution of QDSE and NAPO: **Medium**  
- Robustness to sensor noise: **Medium** (limited noise types)  
- Overall reproducibility: **Medium**
