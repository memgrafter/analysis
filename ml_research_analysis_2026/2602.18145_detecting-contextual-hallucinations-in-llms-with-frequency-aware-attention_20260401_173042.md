# Detecting Contextual Hallucinations in LLMs with Frequency-Aware Attention
## Quick Facts
* arXiv ID: 2602.18145
* Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18145
* Reference count: 40
* Primary result: Frequency-aware attention features (especially Fourier-high) consistently outperform verification-based, representation-based, and attention-baseline methods, with AUROC improvements up to ~5% on contextual hallucination detection across LLaMA and Mistral models.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for detecting contextual hallucinations in large language models. The key insight is that hallucinated tokens are associated with unstable attention patterns, which manifest as high-frequency components when attention weights are treated as discrete signals. The method extracts attention weights across all layers and heads, applies spectral operators (DFT, DWT, Laplacian) to isolate high-frequency energy, and uses this as a feature for a linear classifier. Evaluated on RAGTruth and HalluRAG benchmarks with LLaMA and Mistral models, the approach—particularly Fourier-based features—outperforms verification-based, representation-based, and existing attention-based baselines, achieving up to ~5% AUROC improvement.

Unlike methods that rely on external verifiers or fine-tuning, this technique operates intrinsically on the model's own attention dynamics, making it lightweight and model-agnostic. The study demonstrates efficacy for both token-level and span-level hallucination detection and highlights that context-directed attention carries the most predictive signal. The work opens avenues for real-time hallucination monitoring and analysis of attention stability in transformer-based generation.

## Method Summary
The proposed method follows a pipeline: during text generation, the model's attention weights from every layer and head are recorded. For each generated token, the attention distribution over context tokens and over previously generated tokens are split into separate vectors. Each vector is treated as a discrete signal indexed by token position. A high-pass spectral operator—either DFT with a high-frequency mask (cutoff 0.45), discrete wavelet transform (Daubechies 4, level 1), or discrete Laplacian—is applied to isolate the high-frequency component. The ℓ2 norm of this component serves as an energy score. All energy scores (for context and generated attention, across all layers and heads) are concatenated into a feature vector. A logistic regression classifier is trained to predict whether the token is a hallucination. For span-level detection, feature vectors for tokens within a sliding window of size 8 are averaged before classification. The entire process is computationally cheap and does not require any model modifications or external resources.

## Key Results
- Fourier-high features achieve the best overall token-level detection, with average AUROC improvements up to ~5% over baselines like Lookback-Lens across RAGTruth and HalluRAG.
- The method also excels at span-level detection, confirming the signal's robustness at longer sequences.
- Ablations reveal that context-directed attention carries the most discriminative signal, and that frequency-aware features generalize better than other attention-based approaches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Hallucinated tokens exhibit unstable attention patterns over context tokens, manifesting as fragmented and oscillatory distributions.
- **Mechanism:** When an LLM generates a token not grounded in the provided context, it fails to maintain a coherent focus on relevant context positions, causing rapid local shifts in attention weights across the token sequence.
- **Core assumption:** Grounded production corresponds to smooth, concentrated attention, while hallucination induces jagged, high-variation attention.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "hallucinated tokens are associated with high-frequency attention energy, reflecting fragmented and unstable grounding behavior."
  - [section 2.2] "smooth and coherent patterns are naturally represented by low-frequency components, whereas abrupt changes and local irregularities manifest as high-frequency components."
  - [corpus] The corpus includes Lookback Lens (paper ID 156319) that uses attention maps but focuses on allocation; this work extends by quantifying instability through frequency.
- **Break condition:** If hallucinations sometimes produce smooth attention or grounded outputs show high-frequency variation, the correlation fails.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Spectral operators (DFT, DWT, Laplacian) effectively isolate high-frequency attention components that capture fine-grained instability.
- **Mechanism:** Treating attention weights as a discrete signal indexed by token position, high-pass filtering via these operators suppresses low-frequency trends and retains rapid local fluctuations, which are quantified by ℓ2 energy.
- **Core assumption:** High-frequency energy in attention signals is a robust indicator of ungrounded generation across models and tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] "We model attention weights over tokens as discrete signals and extract high-frequency components that reflect rapid local changes in attention."
  - [section 3.4] "We summarize the magnitude of high-frequency variation by using the ℓ2 norm on the high-frequency component ρ=∥z hf ∥2."
  - [section 5] "Fourier-based features achieve the strongest overall performance" (Table 1).
- **Break condition:** If the high-frequency components are dominated by noise or fail to distinguish hallucination, the operator choice or cutoff may be inappropriate.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Aggregating high-frequency energy across all layers and heads—especially from context-directed attention—yields a discriminative feature for token-level hallucination detection.
- **Mechanism:** For each generation step, compute high-frequency energy per head per layer, form separate vectors for context and generated attention, concatenate, and classify with a linear model. Context attention carries stronger signal.
- **Core assumption:** The instability signal is distributed across multiple layers and heads, but context attention is more predictive.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.4] "We aggregate these scores across all layers and heads to form feature vectors v(ctx) and v(gen)... and feed them into the classifier."
  - [section 5.4.3] "frequency-aware signals derived from context-token attention are consistently more informative."
  - [figure 6] Shows context importance dominates.
- **Break condition:** If the instability is localized to few layers/heads not captured, or if generated attention provides complementary signal not utilized, accuracy may drop.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Attention Extractor → Frequency Decomposer → Energy Calculator → Feature Aggregator → Linear Classifier.
- **Critical path**: For each generated token → attention extraction → high-pass filter → energy computation → feature vector → classification.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Operator choice: DFT (global, efficient), DWT (localized, multi-scale), Laplacian (local, parameter-free). Fourier-high often best.
  - Cutoff frequency: For DFT, requires selection; wavelet decomposition level; Laplacian has none.
  - Context vs generated attention: Context-only features most informative; including generated may add noise.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Degraded performance on certain tasks if high-frequency patterns differ (e.g., summarization).
  - Overfitting to specific layers/heads; sparsity analysis shows only top heads matter.
  - Cross-domain transfer weaker for Lookback-Lens but better for frequency methods; if transfer fails, features may be task-specific.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do high-frequency attention patterns mechanistically correlate with hallucination generation in real, multi-layer LLMs beyond the simplified single-layer toy model?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state in Section 3.1 that "a rigorous mechanistic understanding of attention behavior under hallucinated generation remains an open problem" and that their toy model "formalizes a general link" but relies on simplifying assumptions far from real LLM complexity (architectural depth, multi-head specialization, long-range interactions).
- **Why unresolved:** The toy model only considers adjacent token differences and a single layer, while real models exhibit higher-order and multi-scale instabilities that the frequency operators are designed to capture. The exact causal pathway from specific frequency components to hallucinated token generation is not empirically traced.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled interventions (e.g., patching or ablating specific frequency bands in attention) during generation on hallucination-prone tasks, combined with fine-grained analysis of how these manipulations affect output factuality across model depths and head types.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why does the Fourier-based high-frequency operator consistently achieve the strongest overall performance compared to wavelet and Laplacian operators?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Results in Table 1 and Section 5.1 show Fourier-high often ranks best, with the authors speculating that the sparse, uneven nature of attention distributions favors operators that "aggregate high-frequency variation globally." However, this is not directly tested or contrasted with the inductive biases of wavelets (localized) and Laplacian (local second-order differences).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper provides correlative performance differences but does not analyze the specific spectral properties of hallucinated versus grounded attention signals (e.g., power spectral density) to explain why global frequency aggregation (Fourier) is more discriminative than multi-scale (wavelet) or purely local (Laplacian) analysis for this task.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative analysis of the frequency spectra of attention distributions for hallucinated vs. non-hallucinated tokens, measuring the distribution of energy across frequency bands and scales. This could reveal whether hallucination signals are more pronounced in specific spectral regions that align better with Fourier’s global decomposition.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What explains the observed asymmetry in cross-domain transfer performance, where models trained on QA transfer worse to Data-to-Text and Summarization than the reverse?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section D.1 and Table A2 show consistent asymmetry in cross-task transfer. The authors hypothesize that "QA exhibits more constrained and localized attention patterns," which may limit generality, but this is not measured or validated.
- **Why unresolved:** The claim is speculative. The paper does not quantify or compare the attention pattern characteristics (e.g., entropy, sparsity, frequency energy distributions) across the three tasks to confirm that QA patterns are structurally different and less transferable.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Measure and compare intrinsic attention properties (e.g., attention mass distribution, transition smoothness, frequency energy profiles) across the QA, D2T, and Summ tasks in RAGTruth. Correlate these properties with transfer performance deterioration to identify which attention characteristics hinder generalization.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Do frequency-aware attention features generalize to detecting hallucinations beyond context-based generation (e.g., parametric knowledge hallucinations, where the model contradicts its own training data)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The study exclusively uses RAGTruth and HalluRAG, which are context-based benchmarks. The method is framed for "contextual hallucinations," and the authors do not test on datasets where hallucinations stem from model-internal conflicts rather than context mismatch.
- **Why unresolved:** The core hypothesis ties high-frequency attention to "fragmented and unstable grounding behavior" relative to a provided context. It is unclear if the same signal appears when the model hallucinates against its parametric memory without an external context, as the attention dynamics may differ fundamentally.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluation on benchmarks of parametric hallucination (e.g., TruthfulQA, datasets measuring self-consistency) to test if frequency-aware features retain discriminative power. Additionally, analysis of attention patterns when models generate known falsehoods without retrieval context.

## Limitations
- Exact attention extraction protocol (e.g., which layers/heads to include, handling of padding) is unspecified, risking replication errors.
- Span-level detection details (window stride, labeling rule) are unclear from the paper.
- Classifier training (class imbalance handling, regularization strength) is not reported.
