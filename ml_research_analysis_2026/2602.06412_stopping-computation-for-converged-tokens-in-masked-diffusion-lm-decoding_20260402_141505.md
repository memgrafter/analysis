# Stopping Computation for Converged Tokens in Masked Diffusion-LM Decoding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06412
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06412
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SureLock reduces algorithmic FLOPs by 30–50% in masked diffusion LMs while preserving generation quality (MT‑Bench within 0.1 points, WikiText perplexity comparable).

## Executive Summary
Masked diffusion language models (MDLMs) generate text by iteratively unmasking tokens but waste computation by recomputing attention and feed‑forward for all positions every step, even after tokens stabilize. SureLock addresses this by dynamically locking positions when their posterior distribution stabilizes across steps, measured by a local KL divergence below a threshold ε. Once locked, a position’s query projection and feed‑forward sublayers are skipped; its keys and values are cached so other tokens can still attend to it. This reduces the dominant per‑iteration complexity from O(N²d) to O(MNd), where M is the number of still‑unlocked positions, which shrinks monotonically. On LLaDA‑8B, SureLock cuts algorithmic FLOPs by 30–50% across generation lengths while preserving quality. Theoretical analysis bounds the final log‑probability error by the step‑wise KL at lock time, justifying the local stability criterion.

## Method Summary
SureLock modifies standard MDLM decoding by maintaining an active set of unlocked positions. At each step, it computes model forwards only for active positions, using cached key/value tensors from locked positions. After unmasking, it computes per‑position KL divergence between current and previous posteriors; if below ε (and optionally within a confidence percentile), the position is locked. Locked positions cease computing queries and feed‑forward sublayers in subsequent steps, while their cached K/V remain accessible for attention. The active set size M decreases over time, reducing per‑step FLOPs. A theoretical bound links the lock‑time KL to the final log‑probability error, providing a justification for the convergence criterion.

## Key Results
- Reduces algorithmic FLOPs by 30–50% on LLaDA‑8B across various generation lengths.
- Maintains generation quality: MT‑Bench scores within 0.1 points, WikiText perplexity comparable to baseline.
- Theoretical bound shows step‑wise KL at lock time bounds terminal log‑probability error.
- Combined with selection‑based sparsification, achieves up to 1.73× end‑to‑end tokens‑per‑second gains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method relies on three interconnected mechanisms:

1. **Locking via KL‑based convergence detection** – Positions are locked when the KL divergence between consecutive posteriors drops below ε, indicating stabilization. This is supported by theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) linking KL to final error, and empirically validated by stable quality across a range of ε values for longer generations.
2. **Cached K/V for attendability** – Once locked, a token’s key/value pairs are cached so other tokens can still attend to it without recomputing its representation. This preserves inter‑token dependencies while skipping expensive Q and FFN computations for the locked position.
3. **Monotonic reduction in active set size** – As more positions lock, the number of active queries M decreases each step, reducing the dominant attention and FFN costs from O(N²d) to O(MNd). The savings compound over time, as shown by step‑wise FLOP ratio plots.

Potential break conditions include premature locking if KL does not reflect semantic convergence (rare per quality metrics) or stale K/V if context drifts (mitigated by optional unlocking via probing).

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Active/Locked state trackers -> KL calculator -> Lock conditioner -> KV cache manager -> Compute scheduler
- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialize all positions active, empty cache.
  2. For each step: compute forward on active positions only (using cached K/V from locked); obtain posteriors; update mask; compute KL and confidence for active & unmasked positions; lock those meeting thresholds; cache their K/V; freeze their block input.
  3. Repeat until all locked or steps exhausted.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - ε vs. quality: Lower ε yields tighter locks but may freeze too late; higher ε risks premature locking (quality drop in short generations).
  - Confidence gate (m): Prevents locking low‑confidence tokens; introduces hyperparameter (m=20% worked robustly).
  - Irregular compute: Active set changes size and indices each step, causing non‑coalesced memory accesses; limits runtime gains vs. FLOP reduction.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High Gen‑PPL or MT‑Bench drop: Locking too many low‑quality tokens (ε too high) or too early.
  - Low lock ratio (<30% by mid‑sampling): KL threshold too strict or task unsuitable.
  - Unstable KL dynamics: KL increases after locking due to context shift; requires unlocking or threshold adjustment.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Algorithmic FLOPs sweep (Table 1): Vary ε and N_gen; confirm monotonic FLOP reduction.
  2. Temporal dynamics (Fig 2): Plot step‑wise FLOP ratio; shows accelerating savings as M shrinks.
  3. Quality‑efficiency tradeoff (Tables 2‑3): Compare Gen‑PPL/MT‑Bench with baseline; shows minimal degradation for longer generations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Co‑optimization with other acceleration methods** – SureLock is orthogonal to step‑count reduction, reuse, and selection‑based sparsification. Combining them showed additional gains, but full co‑optimization (e.g., joint hyperparameter tuning) is future work.
2. **Extension to continuous diffusion models** – KL‑based locking assumes discrete posteriors; continuous models would need new criteria (e.g., variance‑based) and theoretical treatment.
3. **Optimal threshold setting** – ε and confidence percentile m are fixed, but optimal values likely depend on model, domain, and generation length. Adaptive or learned thresholds could improve the Pareto frontier.
4. **Tightness of theoretical bound** – Theorem 1 bounds terminal log‑probability error by the KL at lock time, but constants are uncalibrated and assumptions may be loose. Evidence is needed to correlate bound with actual quality metrics.
5. **Impact on output diversity** – Locking based on high confidence and small KL might reduce exploration, affecting diversity in creative tasks. This is unmeasured in the paper’s perplexity/MT‑Bench evaluations.

## Limitations
- Unmasking policy details (e.g., block count, adaptive schedule) are not fully specified, affecting active set dynamics.
- Model‑specific architecture constants (layers, heads, dimensions) are needed for precise FLOP counting; approximations may misstate savings.
- Evaluation setup (prompt formatting, temperature defaults) for Instruct models is ambiguous, risking quality comparison drift.
- Confidence gate percentile candidate set is unclear (all active vs. newly unmasked).

## Confidence
- KL‑based locking predicts convergence: Medium (theoretical support, empirical quality holds except short generations).
- Cached K/V preserves attention fidelity: Medium (mechanically sound but stale cache risk if context drifts).
- Per‑iteration complexity reduction O(N²d)→O(MNd): High (straightforward derivation).
- Quality preservation (MT‑Bench, Gen‑PPL): High for long generations, Low for short ones.
