# How2Everything: Mining the Web for How-To Procedures to Evaluate and Improve LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.08808
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08808
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Mining 351K procedures from the web yields a 7K test benchmark and an RL reward that improves procedural validity by >10 points without harming other capabilities.

## Executive Summary
Evaluating and improving large language models’ ability to generate correct, step-by-step procedures remains a challenge due to the lack of scalable, reliable benchmarks. How2Everything addresses this by automatically extracting over 350,000 how-to procedures from nearly one million web pages across 14 topics, creating the How2Bench test set and a large training corpus. The core of the approach is How2Score, which uses a distilled LLM judge (How2Judge) to detect critical failures—omissions, extraneous steps, or deviations—that would prevent a user from achieving the goal. This judge reaches 80.5% agreement with human annotators. When used as a reward in reinforcement learning, models improve by more than 10 points on How2Bench while showing no systematic regressions on 12 standard evaluations, demonstrating a transferable capability gain.

## Method Summary
How2Mine collects tutorial web pages with WebOrganizer, applies heuristic filters (step count, n‑gram repetition), an LLM filter to exclude named‑entity, pure‑math, UI‑interaction, and open‑ended creativity procedures, and post‑processes to standardize goals and resources. This yields 351K structured procedures; a stratified 7K split becomes How2Bench. For evaluation, models are prompted with the goal, resources, and exact number of steps; generations are scored by How2Judge, a Qwen‑8B model distilled from GPT‑5 annotations on 73K examples. The binary score (no critical failure vs. any failure) defines How2Score. Improvement is achieved via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using three rewards: the inverted How2Score, a format verifier ensuring exactly *n* numbered steps, and a length reward that keeps output token count close to the reference. This composite reward avoids verbosity hacking and directly targets procedural validity.

## Key Results
- How2Judge achieves 80.5% agreement with human majority labels (vs. GPT‑5’s 83.0%).
- RL with How2Score improves success rates on How2Bench by >10 points across Qwen‑3 (4B/8B) and OLMo‑3 (7B Think).
- No systematic negative side effects are observed on 12 standard post‑training evaluations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Diverse web‑mined procedures enable scalable benchmarking.**  
The How2Mine pipeline collects 980K web pages across 14 topics, extracts procedures via heuristics and LLM filters, and standardizes them into a consistent format, yielding 351K procedures and a 7K test set. *Core assumption:* Real‑world tutorials provide a broad distribution of valid step‑by‑step instructions amen‑able to automatic extraction. *Evidence:* Sections 3.2 and B.3–B.5 detail the pipeline; neighboring works on procedure evaluation confirm relevance. *Break condition:* Skewed or low‑quality web content could undermine generality.

**Mechanism 2: LLM‑as‑judge detects critical failures with near‑human reliability.**  
How2Score defines a critical failure as any issue that prevents goal achievement. GPT‑5 annotates 73K model generations; its judgments are distilled into the 8B How2Judge (80.5% human agreement). The binary success score is scalable and cheap. *Core assumption:* Frontier LLMs can assess procedural correctness without execution. *Evidence:* Abstract, Sections 4.3–4.4; cross‑judge robustness checks (GPT, Gemini, Claude). *Break condition:* Increasing judge‑human disagreement on new topics would degrade the RL signal.

**Mechanism 3: RL with failure‑avoidance reward improves procedural validity without regressions.**  
GRPO combines three rewards: How2Score (inverted), a step‑format verifier, and a reference‑calibrated length penalty. Across three models, RL yields >10‑point gains on How2Bench while external benchmark scores remain mixed but not systematically worse. *Core assumption:* Optimizing against critical failures transfers to general planning abilities. *Evidence:* Table 2, Section 7 (robustness to memorization/formatting). *Break conditions:* (i) Reward hacking via verbosity (mitigated by length reward); (ii) Negative transfer to other tasks (not observed).

## Foundational Learning

**LLM-as-a-Judge**  
- Why needed: How2Score’s evaluation pipeline relies on an LLM judge; understanding its calibration, biases, and validation against humans is essential to interpret scores correctly.  
- Quick check question: What are two common biases in LLM judges, and how did How2Everything attempt to mitigate them? (Length bias: enforced concision in prompts and added a length reward; self‑preference: cross‑judge robustness tests with GPT, Gemini, Claude.)

**Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF)**  
- Why needed: The improvement loop uses the automatically computed How2Score as an RL reward; this differs from supervised fine‑tuning in objective and stability.  
- Quick check question: Why did pure SFT on How2Train not improve instruction‑tuned models, whereas RL with How2Score did? (SFT imitates reference style and may not reduce critical failures; RL directly optimizes the failure‑avoidance objective.)

**Multi‑Stage Data Mining and Filtering**  
- Why needed: The validity of both the benchmark and training set hinges on the quality of the mined procedures. Understanding why certain categories (named‑entity focused, UI interaction, open‑ended creativity) are filtered clarifies which procedures can be reliably judged without execution.  
- Quick check question: Why are “UI interaction” procedures excluded from How2Mine? (They require specific interface states and executable verification, making critical‑failure judgment brittle.)

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Raw web documents → How2Mine (extraction, heuristic filter, LLM filter, post‑processing) → {How2Bench, How2Train}. Distillation: GPT‑5 → How2Judge (8B). Evaluation: generator model + prompt → procedure → How2Judge → binary score. Training: SFT or GRPO on How2Train with rewards (How2Score + format verifier + length reward) → improved generator.

**Critical path**  
1. Mine diverse procedures from the web.  
2. Distill a reliable, low‑cost judge.  
3. Evaluate models on How2Bench.  
4. Apply RL with How2Score reward.  
5. Re‑evaluate and check out‑of‑domain generalization.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- Judge quality vs. cost: GPT‑5 is accurate but expensive; How2Judge is cheaper but slightly less accurate.  
- Evaluation reliability vs. scalability: Human annotation is gold standard but not scalable; LLM‑as‑judge enables large‑scale evaluation at the risk of biases.  
- Length control: Without a length reward, RL exploits verbosity; the added reward keeps outputs concise and aligned with reference style.

**Failure signatures**  
- Judge disagreement spikes on specific topics (e.g., technical procedures), indicating domain gaps.  
- RL without length control produces verbose outputs that artificially inflate scores.  
- Train‑test leakage if embedding deduplication fails, causing over‑optimistic gains.  
- Formatting errors (wrong step count) that persist despite training, confirming the metric captures more than surface compliance.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. Judge calibration check: Sample 200 examples, run How2Judge and compare with human majority labels; compute agreement breakdowns.  
2. Length bias ablation: Generate with/without length constraints, evaluate with How2Judge to quantify verbosity preference.  
3. Cross‑judge robustness: Rescore same model outputs with GPT‑5, Gemini‑2.5‑Pro, Claude‑4.5‑Opus; verify ranking consistency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can we reduce bias and improve reliability of LLM judges for procedural evaluation beyond current 80.5% human agreement and moderate inter‑annotator agreement?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper introduces How2Judge with 80.5% agreement and Krippendorff’s α=0.593, checks self‑preference but not other biases, and notes the critical failure definition is subjective.
- **Why unresolved:** Remaining biases (topical, verbosity) could distort evaluations; higher consistency is needed for trustworthy benchmarks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Cross‑topic consistency studies, adversarial probing of judge blind spots, and hybrid human‑AI evaluation protocols.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What aspects of pretraining (data composition, training dynamics) explain the observed scaling of RL gains with compute, and why do RL improvements require pretraining maturity?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Figure 6 shows RL gains increase with pretraining FLOPs (e.g., Olmo‑3‑7B gains rise from ~3.6 to ~20.3), but the underlying reasons are not analyzed.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding this could optimize pretraining for procedural reasoning and reduce wasted computation.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled ablations on pretraining data mixtures, representation probing, and RL learning curves across diverse pretrained checkpoints.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the How2Mine pipeline indeed scale to much larger web corpora without degrading quality, and does larger‑scale mining yield a better benchmark?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The abstract states How2Mine “readily scales to larger corpora” but the empirical evaluation used only 980K documents.
- **Why unresolved:** Scaling may introduce noise, topic imbalance, or diminishing returns; this claim remains unverified.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Run the pipeline on tens/hundreds of millions of documents, assess diversity and difficulty metrics, and track downstream model performance on the resulting benchmark.

## Limitations
- Web mining focuses on 14 topics and excludes UI, math, and creative procedures, potentially limiting benchmark diversity.
- How2Judge’s reliability is based on a small human validation set (200 examples); performance on out‑of‑distribution domains is uncertain.
- RL improvements were validated on a limited set of 12 external benchmarks; broader capability impact remains to be tested.

## Confidence
- **Mining & Benchmark:** Medium (large‑scale but domain‑skewed)
- **LLM‑Judge:** High (strong human alignment and cross‑judge checks)
- **RL Transfer:** Medium (solid gains on How2Bench, but external validation narrow)
