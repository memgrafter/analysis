# Generative Shape Reconstruction with Geometry-Guided Langevin Dynamics
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.27016
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27016
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: GG-Langevin achieves state-of-the-art 3D shape reconstruction from sparse/incomplete point clouds, reducing Chamfer Distance by 15-50% and Chamfer Angle by 5-15°.

## Executive Summary
GG-Langevin introduces a novel framework for reconstructing complete 3D shapes from sparse or incomplete point clouds by combining a pre-trained diffusion prior with geometry-guided Langevin dynamics. The method uses a rebalanced VAE decoder and a half-denoising no-denoising (HDND) sampler to integrate measurement consistency while preserving prior structure. It demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on ShapeNet benchmarks, significantly improving Chamfer Distance and Chamfer Angle metrics across categories. Additionally, the rebalanced VAE reduces inference time by half.

## Method Summary
The approach comprises three stages. First, a rebalanced VecSet VAE is trained to predict signed distance functions (SDF) from point clouds, using L1, eikonal, and KL losses with the bottleneck moved to later decoder layers (e.g., 10 decoder layers, 16 encoder layers). Second, a diffusion model (transformer-based) is trained on the resulting latent space via a noise-prediction objective weighted by w_σ = 1+σ². Third, at inference, an input point cloud is encoded to an initial latent z₀. GG-Langevin then iteratively refines z₀ by alternating half-denoising (adding noise and denoising with the diffusion prior) and a no-denoising gradient step derived from an implicit gradient regularization (IGR) loss that aligns the decoded surface with the input points and enforces smooth normals. For incomplete scans, a cosine-annealed noise schedule (σ from 0.2 to 0.02 over 4000 steps, then 1000 steps at 0.02) is used; for sparse scans, constant σ=0.05 for 2000 steps. The final latent is decoded to a mesh via Marching Cubes.

## Key Results
- Reduces Chamfer Distance by 15-50% and Chamfer Angle by 5-15° on ShapeNet benchmarks across all object categories.
- Consistently outperforms optimization-based, learning-based, and prior-based methods for both sparse and incomplete point clouds.
- The rebalanced VAE decoder halves inference time compared to standard VecSet.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of GG-Langevin arises from its decoupled HDND sampler, which isolates the diffusion prior's denoising from the measurement-driven gradient updates. This prevents the gradients from distorting the prior's distributional support. The rebalanced VAE creates a latent space where gradients ∇_z L(z,P) are both computationally efficient and sufficiently smooth to guide sampling without causing instability. The diffusion prior supplies a strong generative model that plausibly fills missing regions, while the IGR loss constrains the output to respect input data and geometric smoothness. Hyperparameters like the noise schedule and guidance strength are tuned to balance prior fidelity and measurement compliance.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Diffusion models on latent spaces**
   - *Why needed*: To learn a data distribution over shape latents that can generate diverse and plausible completions.
   - *Quick check*: Sample unconditionally from the model and decode; verify diversity and quality.

2. **Variational Autoencoders (VAE) with rebalanced bottleneck**
   - *Why needed*: To obtain a latent representation where decoder gradients are informative and stable for optimization, and to accelerate decoding.
   - *Quick check*: During a few gradient steps, monitor the magnitude of ∇_z L; it should be neither vanishing nor exploding.

3. **Signed Distance Functions (SDF)**
   - *Why needed*: Provide a continuous, differentiable implicit representation that supports gradient-based updates and mesh extraction.
   - *Quick check*: Extract a zero-level set via Marching Cubes; the mesh should be watertight with smooth normals.

4. **Implicit Gradient Regularization (IGR)**
   - *Why needed*: Enforces alignment of the decoded surface with input points and smoothness without requiring ground truth SDF.
   - *Quick check*: After optimization, check that the surface passes near the input points and that the eikonal term (|∇f|≈1) is minimized.

5. **Langevin dynamics for sampling**
   - *Why needed*: To sample from the diffusion-prior-modulated posterior by combining stochastic prior steps and deterministic gradient updates.
   - *Quick check*: Track the loss L(z,P) over iterations; it should decrease and stabilize, while the latent remains within the plausible prior region (e.g., low reconstruction error when decoded without guidance).

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Input point cloud → VAE encoder → latent z₀ → GG-Langevin (diffusion prior + gradient guidance + HDND steps) → final latent → VAE decoder → output mesh.

**Critical path**: Training proceeds in two phases: (1) VAE training to learn a latent SDF space; (2) diffusion model training on VAE latents to learn the prior. Inference depends on both models and the GG-Langevin algorithm.

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Bottleneck position: Shallower decoder yields more stable gradients and faster decoding but may reduce expressivity; deeper decoder increases representational capacity but risks noisy gradients.
- Noise schedule vs guidance strength: Larger σ maintains prior diversity but may slow convergence; larger β speeds alignment but can overfit to noisy points.
- Conditioning: Class-conditional priors can improve reconstruction for incomplete scans but require extra annotation and may limit generalization across classes.

**Failure signatures**:
- Incomplete reconstructions (missing large regions) when guidance is too weak, noise too high, or insufficient steps.
- Spurious geometry (artifacts, wiggles) when guidance is too strong, noise too low, or the VAE latent space has poor gradient properties.
- Diverging or vanishing gradients during GG-Langevin steps, indicated by extreme gradient norms.

**First three experiments**:
1. Train the rebalanced VecSet VAE (16 encoder layers, 10 decoder layers) on a subset of ShapeNet cars and evaluate reconstruction quality (CD/CA) on held-out shapes; also compute average gradient norm of ∇_z L during a few optimization steps.
2. Train the diffusion model on the VAE latents and generate unconditional samples; visualize to confirm diversity and shape plausibility.
3. Run GG-Langevin on one incomplete car point cloud using default hyperparameters; log loss L(z,P) and gradient norms over iterations, and inspect intermediate decoded meshes to ensure smooth improvement toward a plausible shape.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: What are the underlying causes of the two identified failure modes (incomplete reconstructions and spurious geometry) in GG-Langevin, and can they be systematically predicted or prevented?
- **Basis in paper**: The failure cases section (Page 18, Fig. 10) categorizes failures into "incomplete reconstructions" and "spurious geometry" and suggests they can sometimes be addressed by adjusting hyperparameters (steps, noise level) or adding loss terms, but at a trade-off.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper identifies *that* failures occur in these forms but does not analyze *why* the Langevin dynamics or guidance get trapped in these specific failure states. The relationship between the loss landscape, prior distribution, and sampling trajectory leading to these distinct artifacts is not investigated.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A detailed ablation study or sensitivity analysis mapping changes in point cloud characteristics (sparsity, noise type, missing region size) to failure probability. Probing the intermediate latent states during failed trajectories to correlate divergence with specific properties of the gradient field or prior score function.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can the noise level schedule σ_t be optimized or learned automatically for different input conditions (e.g., varying sparsity, noise levels, or object categories) to consistently improve reconstruction, especially for incomplete scans?
- **Basis in paper**: Section 4.4 and Appendix G show that reconstruction quality is highly sensitive to σ and that a cosine annealing schedule (σ_max=0.2 to σ_min=0.02 over 4000 steps) is crucial for incomplete scans. However, this schedule is hand-designed.
- **Why unresolved**: The optimal schedule likely depends on the specific geometry and incompleteness of the input point cloud P. The paper uses a fixed, heuristic schedule. There is no theoretical or empirical framework to adapt σ_t based on input metrics (e.g., initial encoder uncertainty, point cloud density).
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An adaptive scheduling algorithm that monitors a convergence metric (e.g., change in L(z,P) or latent norm) during early sampling steps and adjusts σ_t accordingly. Comparing performance across a wide range of synthetic and real scans with different schedules derived from input features.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: To what extent do the design choices for the VAE latent space (specifically the "rebalanced" bottleneck position) impact the general applicability and performance of GG-Langevin for other shape representations or autoencoder architectures?
- **Basis in paper**: Section 3.4 and 4.3 demonstrate that moving the bottleneck to create a smaller decoder (10 layers) improves both speed and reconstruction quality compared to the standard VecSet design (25 decoder layers). They hypothesize this balances expressivity and gradient stability.
- **Why unresolved**: The study is conducted only on the VecSet architecture for SDF representations on ShapeNet chairs. It is unclear if these findings generalize to other neural field representations (e.g., occupancy networks, tri-planes) or different autoencoder training objectives (e.g., different losses, truncation schemes). The core principle—decoder size vs gradient informativeness—needs validation beyond this specific setup.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Repeating the bottleneck position ablation on alternative autoencoder backbones (e.g., using an OccNet or a different latent structure). Measuring not only final reconstruction quality but also the conditioning number of the Jacobian ∇_z D(z,x) during sampling. Testing on datasets with higher shape diversity or different topologies.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: When and why does class conditioning of the diffusion prior provide a measurable benefit for reconstruction, given that the unconditioned model performs similarly on sparse scans but worse on incomplete ones?
- **Basis in paper**: Appendix E shows that class conditioning yields a clear advantage in the incomplete setting (lower CA) but not in the sparse setting. They speculate it's because incomplete shapes rely more heavily on the prior, and a class-specific prior is more accurate.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper does not quantify *how much* more the incomplete setting relies on the prior versus the measurements. It is not clear if the benefit comes from better prior sampling (avoiding implausible class shapes) or from more stable/guided Langevin dynamics. The interaction between conditioning, guidance strength β, and noise level σ is unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An analysis measuring the "prior dependence" of reconstructions, perhaps by comparing the Kullback-Leibler divergence of the final latent z from the class-conditional vs. unconditional prior distributions for different input completeness levels. An experiment varying the strength of class conditioning (e.g., classifier-free guidance scale) and measuring its effect on both metrics and failure modes.

### Open Question 5
- **Question**: Can the computational bottleneck of GG-Langevin—the repeated decoder evaluations for gradient computation—be significantly reduced without sacrificing reconstruction quality, for instance by using coarser spatial gradients or fewer decoder queries?
- **Basis in paper**: The conclusion (Page 14) and Appendix D explicitly state that computational time is a limitation, with most time spent on computing ∇_z L(z,P). It suggests "multiple denoising steps for each guidance step" as a promising direction but does not implement or evaluate it.
- **Why unresolved**: The trade-off between gradient accuracy (which depends on the number and distribution of query points x for D(z,x) and its gradient) and convergence speed/sample quality is not studied. The current implementation uses a fixed, likely dense, set of query points (from the input point cloud and a uniform grid for eikonal). An adaptive or learnable query strategy is not investigated.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An ablation study reducing the number of query points per iteration (e.g., stochastic subset of input points for L_surface, fewer on-grid points for L_eikonal) and measuring the impact on CD/CA and iteration count. Exploring surrogate gradient estimators or using a differentiable rasterizer to obtain approximate surface gradients with fewer queries.

## Limitations
- **VAE architecture**: Exact layer counts/feature dimensions for the "rebalanced" VecSet are inferred from an ablation (Fig. 9), not explicitly defined for all categories. Suboptimal bottleneck placement could degrade latent gradient quality.
- **Guidance optimization**: Adam hyperparameters (`β₁`, `β₂`, `ε`, clipping) for the `O_adam` update in GG-Langevin are unspecified; defaults may not balance prior/measurement consistency.
- **HDND sensitivity**: The interplay between noise schedule (`σ_t`) and guidance strength (`β`) is critical but only partially validated (e.g., sparse vs. incomplete settings). It is unclear if reported schedules generalize across shapes/categories.

## Confidence
- **Performance claims (CD/CA improvements)**: *Medium* – Dependent on correct VAE/diffusion training and precise HDND hyperparameters; architectural assumptions introduce risk.
- **HDND design principle**: *Medium* – Theoretically sound but sensitive to σ/β balance; lack of ablation on optimizer settings weakens reproducibility.
- **Inference speedup (rebalanced VAE)**: *Medium* – Claim hinges on specific encoder/decoder depth; without exact architecture specs, half-speed claim is tentative.
