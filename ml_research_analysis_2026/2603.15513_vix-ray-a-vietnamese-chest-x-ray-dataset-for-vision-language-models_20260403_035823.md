# ViX-Ray: A Vietnamese Chest X-Ray Dataset for Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.15513  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15513  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B fine-tuned on ViX-Ray achieves up to 80.47% ROUGE-L and 29% improvement in factual accuracy via multi-turn training, outperforming GPT-4V and Gemini.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces ViX-Ray, the first Vietnamese chest X-ray dataset for vision-language models, containing 5,400 images with expert-written findings and impressions. To address the scarcity of Vietnamese medical data, the authors fine-tune five open-source VLMs and compare them against proprietary models (GPT-4V, Gemini) using a three-stage evaluation pipeline. Results show Qwen2.5-VL-7B consistently outperforms all models, particularly in multi-turn generation, while proprietary models often refuse to generate outputs. The study demonstrates the potential of tailored fine-tuning for Vietnamese clinical applications and highlights the need for better language and domain alignment in VLMs.

## Method Summary
The authors curate ViX-Ray from Vietnamese hospitals, ensuring expert annotations for both findings and impressions. They split the data into training, development, and test sets. Five open-source VLMs (including Qwen2.5-VL-7B) are fine-tuned in two settings: single-turn (direct generation) and multi-turn (with dialogue history). Evaluation uses lexical metrics (ROUGE, BLEU) and factual accuracy, comparing against zero-shot GPT-4V and Gemini. The pipeline assesses generation quality in three stages: findings, impressions, and multi-turn.

## Key Results
- Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves the best performance, with up to 80.47% ROUGE-L and 71.35% precision on the development set in multi-turn generation.
- Multi-turn fine-tuning yields over 20% improvement in lexical scores and 29% in factual accuracy for larger models.
- All models, especially proprietary ones, struggle with impressions generation, showing lower metrics and frequent output refusals.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not provide a mechanistic explanation beyond empirical results. It attributes improvements to multi-turn fine-tuning that better captures contextual dialogue and to the capacity of larger models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B). However, the underlying reasons for superior performance—such as architectural choices, pretraining data alignment, or linguistic adaptability—are not analyzed.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map:  
Image encoder (e.g., vision transformer) -> Language model decoder (e.g., transformer) -> Output tokens (with cross-attention). For multi-turn: dialogue history tokens are concatenated with image tokens and current query.

Critical path:  
1. Preprocess X-ray images and expert annotations.  
2. Format data into single-turn or multi-turn sequences.  
3. Fine-tune VLM parameters on training set.  
4. Evaluate on development/test sets using lexical and factual metrics.

Design tradeoffs:  
- Model size vs. computational cost: larger models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) perform better but require more resources.  
- Single-turn vs. multi-turn training: multi-turn improves contextual understanding but complicates data formatting and may overfit to dialogue patterns.  
- Open-source vs. proprietary: open-source models can be fine-tuned but may lag in zero-shot capability; proprietary models have refusal policies that hinder evaluation.

Failure signatures:  
- Low scores on impressions generation across all models.  
- Frequent refusals from GPT-4V and Gemini, often due to safety filters or language detection.  
- Inconsistencies in multi-turn responses (e.g., contradicting earlier statements).

First three experiments:  
1. Fine-tune each open-source VLM on the single-turn training data and evaluate on the development set for findings and impressions generation.  
2. Fine-tune the same models on multi-turn data (with history) and measure performance gains over single-turn.  
3. Run the full three-stage evaluation pipeline on the test set for the best-performing model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and compare against zero-shot proprietary models.

## Limitations
- Dataset quality and representativeness: unclear expert qualifications and potential selection bias in the 5,400 X-rays.
- Comparative evaluation: low performance of proprietary models may be influenced by prompt engineering or refusal policies rather than inherent capability.
- Generalizability: all tests are on ViX-Ray splits; no external validation on independent Vietnamese or non-Vietnamese cohorts.

## Confidence
- ViX-Ray dataset exists and is Vietnamese-focused: High  
- Qwen2.5-VL-7B outperforms others on ViX-Ray: Medium  
- Multi-turn fine-tuning improves performance: Medium-High  
- Proprietary models struggle due to refusal/output issues: Low
