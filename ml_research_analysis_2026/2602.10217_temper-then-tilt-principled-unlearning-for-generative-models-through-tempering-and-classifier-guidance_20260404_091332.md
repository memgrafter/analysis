# Temper-Then-Tilt: Principled Unlearning for Generative Models through Tempering and Classifier Guidance
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.10217  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10217  
- **Reference count:** 11  
- **Primary result:** T3-Unlearning achieves state-of-the-art Forget Quality on TOFU with Llama 3.1 8B while training only 0.03% of parameters and running in ~5 seconds per epoch.

## Executive Summary
Machine unlearning removes specific data from generative models without full retraining. Standard classifier guidance fails when the forget data is highly concentrated, as errors scale with the forget set's peak density. This paper introduces Temper-Then-Tilt Unlearning (T3-Unlearning), which first tempers the base model’s distribution to flatten such spikes and then tilts using a lightweight classifier on frozen hidden states. Theoretical analysis proves tempering reduces the Forget Error’s dependence on the forget-set density from linear to sublinear (O(∥p_f∥_∞^{1/T})). Empirically, T3-Unlearning sets new state-of-the-art Forget Quality on the TOFU benchmark with Llama 3.1 8B, maintains strong generative utility (MU-ROUGE), and is highly efficient—training only 2.65M parameters (0.03%) in seconds per epoch.

## Method Summary
T3-Unlearning reformulates unlearning as density ratio estimation: given the original model approximating the mixture distribution p = (1−γ)p_r + γp_f, the goal is to recover p_r. The optimal tilt is the density ratio p_r/p, estimated by a classifier f(z) = P(s=1|z). The method freezes the pretrained generative model, applies a fixed temperature T > 1 to its logits (tempering), and adds guidance from a lightweight classifier trained on mean-pooled hidden states. At inference, the distribution is p̂_r^{(T)}(y|x) ∝ softmax( (1/T) log p_θ*(y|x) + log g_φ(x)_y ). Tempering flattens sharp modes in p, reducing sensitivity to errors in high-density forget regions.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art Forget Quality on TOFU with Llama 3.1 8B.
- Strong generative utility (MU-ROUGE) on retain sets, rivaling fine-tuning baselines.
- Extreme efficiency: trains only 2.65M parameters (0.03%) in ~5 seconds per epoch.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Unlearning as Density Ratio Estimation
**Claim:** Unlearning can be framed as estimating the density ratio between the target retain distribution and the original mixture distribution.  
**Mechanism:** By Bayes’ rule, p_r(z) ∝ p(z) · P(s=1|z). Estimating the class posterior f*(z) = P(s=1|z) suffices to recover p_r via tilting: p_r(z) = p(z) · f*(z) / E_p[f*]. This reduces unlearning to a probabilistic classification subproblem.  
**Core assumption:** The original model approximates p sufficiently well that density ratio estimation via classifier guidance can recover p_r.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] frames task as density ratio estimation; [section 2.1] derives optimal tilt f* = p_r/p; corpus on density ratio estimation and classifier guidance supports formulation.  
**Break condition:** If the classifier cannot accurately estimate the posterior (due to limited samples or severe imbalance), the ratio estimate is poor, causing residual leakage or excessive retain degradation.

### Mechanism 2: Standard Guidance Failure on Concentrated Forgets
**Claim:** Standard classifier guidance fails when the forget distribution p_f is sharply concentrated because Forget Error scales linearly with ∥p_f∥_∞.  
**Mechanism:** For untempered estimator p̂_r^{(1)}(z) ∝ p(z) f̂(z), the Forget Error bound is E_f(p̂_r^{(1)}) ≲ ∥p_f∥_∞ √(δ/(1−γ)), where δ is classifier excess risk. As ∥p_f∥_∞ → ∞ (e.g., memorized rare instances), this bound becomes vacuous.  
**Core assumption:** The failure mode arises when the forget set consists of atypical, high-confidence memorized content relative to retain, common in copyright/private unlearning.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] states failure due to concentrated forget data; [section 3.3] shows bound dependence on sharpness and proves unavoidable (Theorem 3.5); corpus on importance sampling/RL hardness supports intrinsic difficulty.  
**Break condition:** The mechanism fails if p_f is not concentrated (∥p_f∥_∞ bounded), in which case standard guidance may suffice; tempering unnecessary for well-separated distributions.

### Mechanism 3: Tempering Reduces Dependence on ∥p_f∥_∞
**Claim:** Tempering the base distribution before guidance (T3) reduces Forget Error dependence from O(∥p_f∥_∞) to O(∥p_f∥_∞^{1/T}), at the cost of increased Retain Error due to global smoothing.  
**Mechanism:** The T3 estimator is p̂_r^{(T)}(z) ∝ p(z)^{1/T} f̂(z). Raising p to 1/T (T>1) flattens sharp modes, amplifying classifier influence in concentrated regions. Theorem 3.7 shows Forget Error bound: E_f(p̂_r^{(T)}) ≲ ∥p_f∥_∞^{1/T} δ^{1/(2k)} plus a bias term vanishing for T=1 or disjoint supports.  
**Core assumption:** p has sufficient integrability for tempered moments; classifier can achieve low excess risk after tempering simplifies the decision boundary.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] introduces tempering to flatten spikes; [section 3.4] Theorem 3.7 gives improved scaling; [section 2.2] explains tempering amplifies classifier influence.  
**Break condition:** If p_r itself is highly concentrated, tempering bias dominates, severely increasing Retain Error; optimal T depends on ∥p_f∥_∞ and δ.

## Foundational Learning
- **Density Ratio Estimation**  
  *Why needed:* Unlearning target p_r is expressed as a tilt of the original mixture p; optimal tilt = density ratio p_r/p. Estimating this ratio via a classifier is the core inference operation.  
  *Quick check:* Why does P(s=1|z) equal the retain-forget density ratio up to a constant?

- **Surrogate Risk and Excess Risk**  
  *Why needed:* Theoretical guarantees bind unlearning error to classifier excess risk δ = L(f̂) − L(f*). Controlling δ is the training objective.  
  *Quick check:* How does a finite-sample bound on classifier excess risk translate to a distributional error bound for the unlearned model?

- **Tempering as Smoothing under KL Constraint**  
  *Why needed:* Tempering p^{1/T} maximizes entropy subject to a KL constraint to p, explaining the flattening of sharp modes and the bias when p_r is concentrated.  
  *Quick check:* What is KL(p^{1/T}‖p), and how does it control smoothing intensity?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Frozen Base Model (p_θ*) → Feature Extractor (Ψ: mean-pooled final hidden states) → Classifier Head (f_φ: low-rank linear B_φA_φ + sigmoid, output g_φ(x)∈[0,1]^{|V|}) → Tempering Operator (apply 1/T to base logits) → Inference Distribution p̂_r^{(T)}(y|x) = softmax( (1/T) log p_θ*(y|x) + log g_φ(x)_y )

- **Critical path:**  
  1. Preprocessing: Run all retain/forget data through frozen base model to cache pooled hidden states.  
  2. Classifier Training: Train low-rank φ using cross-entropy on cached features and binary labels s∈{0,1}; no gradients into base model.  
  3. Inference: For query x, compute Ψ(x), get g_φ(x), temper base logits by 1/T, add classifier log-odds, sample.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - *Temperature T:* Larger T reduces Forget Error for concentrated p_f but increases Retain Error bias; must be tuned (theory suggests T ∝ log ∥p_f∥_∞).  
  - *Classifier capacity:* Mean-pooled features and low-rank head limit expressivity but ensure efficiency; more expressive heads (e.g., LoRA) may reduce δ but increase compute.  
  - *Caching:* Preprocessing loses flexibility for new data without re-caching but is essential for speedups.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - *Forgotten samples still appear:* Forget Quality ~0, indicating ∥p_f∥_∞ too high for chosen T or classifier too weak (δ too large).  
  - *Retain knowledge degraded:* ROUGE on retain/RA/WF drops, suggesting over-tempering (T too large) or classifier overfitting.  
  - *Training instability:* Optimizer diverges on heavily imbalanced (high γ) or noisy forget sets; needs careful regularization λ.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Synthetic Gaussian Validation:** Replicate Experiment 1 (vary v_f, fix n) to confirm theoretical scaling: as v_f↓ (∥p_f∥_∞↑), optimal T should increase to minimize Forget Error.  
  2. **Ablation over Temperature T:** On a small TOFU split (e.g., 1%), sweep T ∈ [1.0, 4.0] and plot Retain Error, Forget Error, and FQ; expect U-shaped Forget Error curve per Theorem 3.7.  
  3. **Classifier Excess Risk vs. Unlearning Error:** On synthetic data with known ground truth, compute true δ (or empirical estimate) and plot Forget Error against √δ (untempered) vs. ∥p_f∥_∞^{1/T} δ^{1/(2k)} (tempered) to verify scaling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
**Open Question 1:** How can we automatically select the optimal base model temperature T without relying on a hold‑out validation set, given the theoretical trade‑off between forget‑set sharpness and retain‑set distortion?  
*Basis:* The method manually tunes T via grid search; theoretical analysis (Theorems 3.7, 3.8) shows optimal T depends on unknown quantities ∥p_f∥_∞ and classifier excess risk δ.  
*Why unresolved:* Current practice requires expensive validation; an automatic, data‑driven criterion would make the method turnkey, especially with scarce validation data.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Derive an estimator for effective sharpness (e.g., from classifier confidence statistics or tail behavior) and use it to inform T; evaluate on synthetic experiments where ∥p_f∥_∞ is known and on real unlearning benchmarks, comparing automated selection to manual grid search.

**Open Question 2:** Does the reliance on a linear classifier on frozen hidden states limit T3‑Unlearning’s effectiveness for modalities beyond text, where the retain/forget distinction may be non‑linear in the base model’s representation space?  
*Basis:* The authors implement the classifier as a “small linear head on top of the fixed hidden states” and evaluate only on Llama 3.1 8B for the TOFU text benchmark.  
*Why unresolved:* Linear separability of retain and forget distributions in the linearized feature space is an unstated assumption; this may fail for image, audio, or more nuanced text tasks, potentially necessitating more expressive (and costly) classifiers.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Apply T3‑Unlearning to standard image/audio unlearning tasks using both linear and small non‑linear (e.g., MLP) heads; compare forget quality, generative utility, and computational cost; analyze geometry of retain/forget clusters in the frozen representation space.

**Open Question 3:** How robust is T3‑Unlearning to base model misspecification, i.e., when the pretrained model’s distribution deviates from the true data mixture p assumed in the theory?  
*Basis:* The probabilistic formulation (Section 2.1) explicitly assumes “the original model has learned the ground truth distribution p over the full dataset D.” This is an idealization; real models are imperfect approximations.  
*Why unresolved:* Finite‑sample guarantees (Theorems 3.7, 3.8) rely on this assumption; a significant model bias could break the link between the surrogate classifier’s risk and the unlearning errors, leading to unexpected failures.  
*What evidence would resolve:* Train base models on varying data slices or with deliberate biases (e.g., label noise, missing modes) and run T3‑Unlearning; measure degradation in retain/forget metrics; develop an extended theory that incorporates base model approximation error as an additional term in the bounds.

## Limitations
- Real-world base models may not perfectly approximate the data distribution; density ratio errors could undermine unlearning.  
- Tempering introduces bias when the retain distribution is concentrated; optimal T selection remains an open practical question.  
- Claims are validated primarily on the TOFU benchmark; cross-dataset generalizability is unproven.  
- Training the classifier on heavily imbalanced retain/forget data may be unstable and sensitive to hyperparameters.
