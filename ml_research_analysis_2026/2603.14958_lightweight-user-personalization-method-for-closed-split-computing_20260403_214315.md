# Lightweight User-Personalization Method for Closed Split Computing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.14958
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14958
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** SALT achieves up to 93.8% user-specific accuracy on CIFAR-10 with a 443K‑parameter adapter, improving over the baseline 88.1% while cutting training latency by >60% and maintaining >90% accuracy under 75% packet loss and ~88% accuracy under Gaussian noise (σ=1.0).

## Executive Summary
SALT introduces a lightweight client‑side adapter that refines intermediate features in a split computing pipeline, enabling effective model personalization under closed‑model constraints where the head and tail networks are inaccessible. By inserting a small trainable module that adds a residual correction to the latent representation from the frozen head, the method adapts to user‑specific data distributions, communication channel impairments, and privacy‑preserving noise injection—all without altering the original models or increasing the size of transmitted tensors. Evaluated on ResNet‑18 with CIFAR‑10/100, SALT yields substantial accuracy gains, reduces training latency by over 60%, and demonstrates strong robustness to packet loss and noise, all with a modest parameter overhead (~443K). The approach unifies multiple adaptation objectives under a single architecture by varying training conditions.

## Method Summary
SALT places a compact adapter on the client side between the frozen head network \(H\) and the communication channel. Given an input \(x\), the head produces a latent feature \(z = H(x)\). The adapter \(S\) computes a correction \(\Delta z = S(z)\) and outputs \(z' = z + \Delta z\). This adapted feature is transmitted over the channel, possibly degraded to \(\tilde{z} = C(z')\) by simulated packet loss or Gaussian noise. The server applies the frozen tail \(T\) to \(\tilde{z}\) to obtain prediction \(\hat{y}\) and loss. Gradients flow back to the client, which updates only the adapter parameters using Adam. The residual formulation ensures that when no adaptation is needed, \(S(z) \approx 0\). Different goals—user personalization (using user‑specific data), robustness (training with channel degradation), or privacy‑aware inference (training with noise)—are achieved solely by the training conditions; the adapter architecture and loss function remain unchanged.

## Key Results
- User‑specific accuracy improved from 88.1% (no adaptation) to 93.8% on CIFAR‑10.
- Training latency reduced by over 60% compared to full head retraining or fine‑tuning.
- Maintained >90% accuracy under 75% packet loss, demonstrating communication robustness.
- Preserved ~88% accuracy under Gaussian noise (\(σ=1.0\)), supporting privacy‑preserving inference.
- Adapter size approximately 443K parameters, confirming lightweight nature.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Client‑Side Adapter for Feature Refinement
SALT enables adaptation in closed split computing by inserting a lightweight, trainable adapter on the client side that processes the intermediate representation from the frozen head network. The adapter produces a residual correction \(\Delta z = S(z)\) which is added to the original feature \(z\) to yield \(z'\). Only the adapter parameters are updated; the head and tail remain untouched. This satisfies the closed‑model constraint while avoiding any increase in communication payload because the adapter preserves the dimensionality of \(z\). Evidence: Abstract and Sections III.C.1/III.C.3.

### Mechanism 2: Residual Correction for Identity Preservation
The residual design \(z' = z + S(z)\) allows the model to rely on the pre‑trained features when the required adaptation is minimal, facilitating convergence and stable training. When the optimal mapping is near identity, \(S(z)\) converges to zero, so the system behaves like the original split model. This preserves useful pre‑trained representations while allowing targeted adjustments. Evidence: Section III.C.3.

### Mechanism 3: Unified Adaptation via Training Conditions
SALT supports personalization, robustness, and privacy objectives using the same adapter architecture by varying only the training conditions (e.g., data distribution, simulated packet loss, noise injection). The channel operator \(C(\cdot)\) applied during training simulates the degradation the model will encounter at inference. The adapter learns to compensate for these distortions, and different objectives are realized without any change to model structure or loss function. Evidence: Abstract and Section III.D.3.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Frozen Head (H) -> SALT Adapter (S) -> Communication Channel (C) -> Frozen Tail (T)

**Critical path:**  
1. Client computes \(z = H(x)\).  
2. Adapter applies residual correction: \(\Delta z = S(z)\), producing \(z' = z + \Delta z\).  
3. Channel transmits \(z'\) and may degrade it: \(\tilde{z} = C(z')\) (e.g., packet loss mask or additive Gaussian noise).  
4. Server computes prediction \(\hat{y} = T(\tilde{z})\) and loss \(L(\hat{y}, y)\).  
5. Server sends gradient \(\partial L / \partial \tilde{z}\) back to client (optionally degraded by \(C\) on the gradient).  
6. Client updates only adapter \(S\) using the gradient (e.g., Adam).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **Adapter capacity vs. efficiency:** Larger adapters can model complex corrections but increase client compute and training time; SALT chooses a ~443K‑parameter design to balance effectiveness and overhead.  
- **Split point selection:** Earlier splits reduce communication size but yield low‑level features that may be harder to adapt; later splits produce more abstract features but require larger tensor transmission.  
- **Training condition specificity:** Training under one degradation (e.g., fixed packet loss) may not generalize to other conditions; multi‑condition training can broaden robustness but might lower peak performance on any single objective.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Accuracy collapse under high packet loss (e.g., >75%) if the adapter was not trained with sufficient packet loss simulation.  
- Slow or non‑convergent adapter training if the residual corrections are too large or the adapter architecture mismatches the feature dimensions.  
- Increased inference latency if the adapter size exceeds client resources, especially on constrained edge devices.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **User‑specific adaptation:** Train SALT on a user‑specific subset (e.g., 5 classes from CIFAR‑10) and compare accuracy against no adaptation, head retraining, and head fine‑tuning.  
2. **Communication robustness:** Train SALT with a fixed packet loss rate (e.g., p=0.5) and evaluate accuracy across packet loss rates {0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75}.  
3. **Privacy‑aware inference:** Train SALT with Gaussian noise injection (σ=1.0) and measure accuracy across noise levels; also assess resistance to inversion attacks (e.g., using SSIM of reconstructions).

## Limitations
- **Adapter architecture discrepancy:** The textual description (two 3×3 convs + 1×1) does not match the reported ~443K parameter count, creating uncertainty about the actual efficient design.
- **Missing training specifics:** The exact packet loss rates and noise σ used during training for robustness and privacy experiments are not specified, hindering reproducibility and full confidence.
- **Undocumented backward channel behavior:** It is unclear whether the channel degradation operator is applied to gradients during backpropagation, which could affect optimizer dynamics.
