# ReLope: KL-Regularized LoRA Probes for Multimodal LLM Routing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24787
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24787
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** ReLope consistently achieves the highest routing AUC (e.g., 88.21 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct), outperforming standard probes (83.86) and Attention Probe (84.84), and delivers superior accuracy-cost trade-offs in hybrid systems.

## Executive Summary
Probe-based routing in multimodal LLMs suffers degraded performance because visual inputs dilute correctness signals in hidden states. This paper introduces two complementary solutions: Attention Probe, which aggregates token-level hidden states from a preceding layer via learned attention weights, and ReLope, which inserts a lightweight LoRA adapter and applies a KL divergence regularizer to learn compressed, routing-aware representations. Evaluated across five multimodal benchmarks and three backbone models, ReLope achieves state-of-the-art routing AUC and offers a more favorable accuracy-cost trade-off, particularly when only a small fraction of queries are escalated to a large model.

## Method Summary
The proposed methods modify how a router probe consumes intermediate representations from a frozen multimodal LLM. The Attention Probe extracts hidden states from all tokens at layer `l-1`, then computes a weighted sum using a learnable attention query to produce an aggregated vector `ẑ`, which is fed to a fixed 5-layer MLP probe trained with binary cross-entropy. ReLope (KL-Regularized LoRA Probe) injects low-rank adapters (LoRA, rank `r=128`, scaling `α=256`) into transformer layer `l`. The adapted last-token state is passed through two linear heads to produce parameters (`μ`, `logσ²`) of a diagonal Gaussian posterior. During training, a sample `z' = μ + σ * ε` is taken (reparameterization) and fed to the probe; the loss combines cross-entropy with a KL regularizer `β * KL(N(μ, diag(σ²)) || N(0,I))` (`β=1.0`) to encourage compression. Both probe and LoRA parameters are trained jointly with AdamW (learning rate `1e-4`). Hidden states are taken from layer `l=24` for 32-layer models and `l=40` for the 48-layer Gemma3.

## Key Results
- ReLope achieves the highest routing AUC across all five benchmarks and three models, e.g., 88.21 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct vs. 83.86 (standard probe) and 84.84 (Attention Probe).
- In hybrid routing evaluations, ReLope provides a superior accuracy-cost trade-off, especially at low routing ratios where accurate identification of escalations matters most.
- ReLope shows stronger robustness to image perturbations and better OOD generalization than baselines, though performance drops on MathVision indicate some limitations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Standard probe routing degrades in Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) because visual inputs dilute the separability of correctness signals in the final-token hidden state.
- **Mechanism:** The multimodal fusion process mixes visual features with textual reasoning traces, creating hidden states with task-irrelevant variation. A probe trained on the final-token vector must extract a correctness signal from a representation where relevant cues are entangled with noisy or redundant visual features, weakening the signal-to-noise ratio.
- **Core assumption:** Correctness-related information exists in the MLLM’s internal representations but is not optimally concentrated in the single final-token vector; visual modality adds variance uncorrelated with correctness.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "we find that the presence of visual inputs weakens the separability of correctness signals in hidden states"
  - [section 3.3] Empirical results show probe AUC is lower on multimodal inputs vs. text-only versions across multiple datasets.
  - [corpus] Prior work on probe-based hallucination detection focuses on text-only LLMs; this paper identifies the phenomenon empirically in MLLMs.
- **Break condition:** Degradation is minimal if the visual input is semantically trivial or perfectly aligned with the textual task, adding no extraneous noise.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Attention Probe improves routing by aggregating distributed correctness signals from earlier token-level hidden states using a learned, routing-specific attention mechanism.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of the final-token state, it computes a weighted sum of all token hidden states from layer `l-1`. The learned attention weights (`β_i`) allow the probe to re-focus on tokens with stronger correctness cues, bypassing the MLLM’s generation-oriented aggregation that may have diluted those cues.
- **Core assumption:** The necessary information for correctness prediction is distributed across token representations of the preceding layer and can be recovered by a data-driven aggregation not constrained by next-token prediction.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "aggregates token-level hidden states from the preceding layer via learned attention weights to recover distributed correctness signals"
  - [section 4.1] Formal definition `ẑ = Σ β_i z_i^{(l-1)}` with attention-computed weights.
  - [corpus] Related work (e.g., "A BERTology View of LLM Orchestrations") uses token-selective probes, suggesting token-level information is useful for downstream tasks.
- **Break condition:** The mechanism fails if the correctness signal is genuinely concentrated only in the final token after the MLLM’s last self-attention layer, or if all token states are heavily overshadowed by visual noise.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** ReLope learns a compressed, routing-aware representation by adapting the hidden state with a LoRA module and regularizing it with a KL divergence towards a standard Gaussian, filtering out task-irrelevant multimodal variation.
- **Mechanism:** A low-rank adapter (LoRA) is inserted into a transformer layer to produce an adapted hidden state. A variational bottleneck maps this state to a Gaussian distribution (`μ`, `σ²`). The KL regularizer (relative to `N(0,I)`) in the loss encourages a simple, low-variance posterior, while the cross-entropy term ensures the compressed sample `z'` retains correctness-predictive information. This jointly pressures LoRA to reshape the representation to be more informative for routing and less sensitive to irrelevant visual features.
- **Core assumption:** Routing-relevant features can be isolated by learning a nonlinear transformation (via LoRA) that projects the original hidden state into a subspace where correctness is more separable, and an information bottleneck can compress this representation without losing critical signals.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] "inserts a lightweight LoRA adapter and applies a KL regularizer to learn compressed, routing-aware representations"
  - [section 4.2] Detailed pipeline with LoRA adaptation, Gaussian parameterization, and combined loss.
  - [corpus] LoRA-Drop shows LoRA’s efficiency for modifying LLM representations; the information bottleneck principle (Alemi et al.) theoretically justifies the KL regularization.
- **Break condition:** The mechanism fails if the correctness signal is inherently non-compressible or if the LoRA rank is too low to capture the necessary transformation. It also fails if the visual noise is perfectly correlated with correctness in the training distribution, causing the regularizer to suppress signal along with noise.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Information Bottleneck Principle**
  - Why needed here: Provides the theoretical foundation for ReLope’s KL divergence regularizer, explaining why forcing the routing representation to be close to a simple prior (standard Gaussian) improves generalization and robustness by discarding irrelevant variation.
  - Quick check: How does minimizing `KL(q(z'|z) || N(0,I))` encourage a representation to keep only information useful for predicting the target label `Y`?
- **Concept: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)**
  - Why needed here: Enables efficient, targeted adaptation of the MLLM’s hidden representations for the routing task without full fine-tuning; the low-rank constraint acts as an implicit regularizer.
  - Quick check: Why is expressing the weight update `ΔW = BA` with low-rank matrices `B` (in_dim × r) and `A` (r × out_dim) parameter-efficient compared to updating `W` directly?
- **Concept: Attention as a Learnable Aggregation Function**
  - Why needed here: Underlies the Attention Probe; standard model attention is optimized for generation, not routing. A separate, trainable attention module over token states allows the router to perform query-specific aggregation tuned to correctness prediction.
  - Quick check: In the Attention Probe formula `ẑ = Σ β_i z_i`, what does the learnable query vector `q` represent and how is it used to compute the weights `β_i`?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Frozen MLLM backbone (`f_θ`) provides intermediate hidden states. The router probe (`g_ϕ`) is a lightweight MLP classifier. For Attention Probe: a learnable query `q` computes attention weights over token states from layer `l-1`, producing an aggregated vector `ẑ`. For ReLope: LoRA adapters (`θ_LoRA`) are inserted into layer `l`; bottleneck heads produce Gaussian parameters (`μ`, `logσ²`) from the LoRA-adapted last-token state; reparameterization yields sample `z'` (or `μ` at inference). The probe then consumes `ẑ` or `z'` to output a correctness probability.

**Critical path:**  
1. Input `(x_text, x_image)` → MLLM forward pass up to layer `l-1` (or `l` with LoRA).  
2. For Attention Probe: compute `ẑ` as weighted sum of layer `l-1` token states using attention weights from `q`.  
3. For ReLope: obtain adapted last-token `z` from LoRA-modified layer `l`; compute `μ` and `logσ²`; sample `z'` during training (use `μ` at inference).  
4. Probe `g_ϕ` processes `ẑ` or `z'` → `ŷ`; routing decision `r(x)=1` if `ŷ > threshold`.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- **LoRA rank `r`:** Higher rank increases capacity but also parameters and overfitting risk; paper finds `r=128` optimal for 7B/12B models.  
- **Probe layer `l`:** Earlier layers have less semantic abstraction; later layers may have generation-focused transformations; paper uses later intermediate layers (24/40).  
- **KL weight `β`:** Controls compression strength; too low loses bottleneck benefit, too high suppresses useful signal; optimal in `[0.5, 1.0]`.  
- **Attention Probe vs. ReLope:** Attention Probe is simpler (only `q` and probe trained) but only reweights existing features; ReLope actively transforms features but introduces LoRA and bottleneck heads, increasing training complexity.

**Failure signatures:**  
- High variance in routing scores for similar queries, indicating unstable learned representations.  
- Large AUC drop under image perturbations, showing failure to filter image noise.  
- Poor OOD generalization (e.g., on MathVision), suggesting overfitting to dataset-specific visual patterns.  
- AUC collapse at low `β` if KL regularizer is absent, indicating necessity of compression for robustness.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Baseline Probe Degradation Test:** Train a standard last-token probe on text-only vs. multimodal data for the same dataset (e.g., ScienceQA). Expect significantly lower AUC on the multimodal subset, confirming the core problem.  
2. **Attention Probe Ablation:** Replace the standard probe’s input with the Attention Probe aggregation (`ẑ`) while keeping the probe architecture identical. Expect consistent AUC improvement over the standard probe.  
3. **ReLope Component Ablation:** Train ReLope with `β=0` (no KL) and with a frozen LoRA adapter (only mean `μ` used, no stochasticity). Expect both modifications to degrade AUC compared to full ReLope, validating the joint role of LoRA adaptation and KL regularization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What specific mechanisms in multimodal LLMs cause hidden states to exhibit weaker separability of correctness signals compared to text-only LLMs?**  
  *Basis:* The paper acknowledges the underlying reason is still not fully understood (Section 7). *Why unresolved:* The study demonstrates the phenomenon empirically but does not analyze whether visual token interference, altered attention patterns, or fusion processes dilute correctness cues. *Resolving evidence:* Comparative analysis of hidden state distributions (e.g., linear separability, mutual information) between text-only and multimodal inputs; visualization of attention maps to identify if visual regions dominate.
- **Can probe-based routing for MLLMs be designed to operate without supervised correctness labels?**  
  *Basis:* ReLope relies on supervised correctness labels, limiting scalability (Section 7). *Why unresolved:* Labeling outputs for new tasks or proprietary models is costly. *Resolving evidence:* Development of self-supervised objectives (e.g., contrastive learning between correct/incorrect states, consistency-based regularizers) that obviate labeled data; evaluation on new domains using only unlabeled queries.
- **Do multimodal signals beyond static images (e.g., video, audio) further degrade probe routing performance, and can ReLope generalize?**  
  *Basis:* The study exclusively uses image-text benchmarks; the finding that "visual inputs weaken correctness signals" may not extend to other modalities. *Why unresolved:* Temporal dynamics in video or spectral patterns in audio could interact with text differently. *Resolving evidence:* Benchmarking on video-QA or audio-grounded tasks; measuring probe AUC and ReLope’s gains compared to image-text; adapting mechanisms for sequential/temporal data.

## Limitations
- Missing implementation details: exact LoRA insertion sub-layer, probe architecture (hidden sizes, activations), query initialization, and full training hyperparameters (batch size, epochs, β schedule).
- Evaluation scope limited to three MLLMs and five benchmarks; results may not generalize to other architectures (e.g., LLaVA, InternVL) or tasks.
- Metrics focus solely on AUC; no precision/recall, calibration analysis, or efficiency overhead measurement.

## Confidence
- Degradation of standard probes in MLLMs: **High** (consistently lower AUC on multimodal vs. text-only inputs).
- Attention Probe improves routing: **Medium** (gains demonstrated but sensitivity to layer choice and token aggregation not fully explored).
- ReLope’s KL-regularized LoRA: **Medium** (strong results but depend on unknown hyperparameters; information bottleneck assumption may not hold universally).
- ReLope achieves SOTA routing AUC: **High** (statistically significant improvements over all baselines on five benchmarks).
