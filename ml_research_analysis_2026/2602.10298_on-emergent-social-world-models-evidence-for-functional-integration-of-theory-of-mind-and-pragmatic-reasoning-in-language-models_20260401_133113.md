# On Emergent Social World Models -- Evidence for Functional Integration of Theory of Mind and Pragmatic Reasoning in Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.10298
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10298
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Behavioral correlation and causal ablation reveal shared computational mechanisms between Theory of Mind and pragmatic reasoning in language models, suggesting emergent social world models.

## Executive Summary
Large language models (LMs) may develop integrated representations for social cognition, akin to a "social world model." This study tested whether LMs share computational resources between Theory of Mind (ToM) and pragmatic reasoning. Across 48 LMs and 38 datasets, a moderate positive correlation (*r* = 0.68) was observed between ToM and pragmatic task accuracy. More critically, functional localization—inspired by cognitive neuroscience—identified ToM-related subnetworks; ablating these units impaired performance on both ToM and pragmatic tasks, while leaving syntax unaffected. The effects were strongest for localizers involving false beliefs or strategic games. These findings provide causal evidence that LMs repurpose the same latent machinery for diverse social reasoning abilities.

## Method Summary
The study evaluated 48 autoregressive LMs (0.5B–72B parameters) on 38 English datasets: 22 ToM and 16 pragmatics, each annotated with ATOMS categories. Behavioral accuracy was computed via log-prob scoring. For causal tests, 20 models with above-chance performance were selected. Four functional localizer suites (LatentBeliefs, CommunicativeIntent, GameBeliefs, MoralIntent) were constructed using GPT-5-generated synthetic stimuli contrasting target (e.g., false-belief stories) vs. control conditions. For each model, activations at the last context token were recorded, and units were selected based on significant t-tests across contrasts. Two selection metrics (simple vs. conjunctive) were used. For each model-localizer pair, the identified subnetwork (or a matched control subnetwork of least-active units) was ablated (zeroed), and the model was re-evaluated on held-out ToM, pragmatics, and BLiMP syntax datasets. Bayesian beta regression with random effects (by dataset, model) estimated causal effects (differences in accuracy), testing predictions P1.1–P3.2.

## Key Results
- Moderate correlation (*r* = 0.68) between average ToM and pragmatic accuracies across models.
- Ablating ToM subnetworks caused significant drops in both ToM (posterior mean β = 0.24 [0.14,0.35]) and pragmatic performance (β = 0.30 [0.21,0.40]). Effects were larger than control ablations for LatentBeliefs and GameBeliefs localizers.
- Syntax accuracy (BLiMP) was unaffected by ToM-network ablation, supporting domain specificity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Shared Computational Resources for Social Reasoning
The claim that ToM and pragmatic reasoning share causally relevant computational units is supported by the ablation results: zeroing units identified by ToM localizers harms performance not only on ToM tasks but also on distinct pragmatic tasks. This causal overlap suggests that the same latent machinery is repurposed across social reasoning domains. The core assumption is that differential unit activations between carefully matched conditions (false-belief vs. control stories) identify functionally relevant units, and that ablating these units isolates their causal contribution. Evidence anchors include the abstract’s causal statement, section 3.5’s Bayesian regression showing credible decreases for both domains, and the lack of effect on syntax. The hypothesis would be falsified if ablation of ToM-identified units failed to impair pragmatic performance across model families and localizer types.

### Mechanism 2: Functional Localization from Cognitive Neuroscience
Functional localization can identify domain-relevant subnetworks in autoregressive LMs by leveraging stimulus contrasts derived from fMRI research. The mechanism: for a localizer suite contrasting target vs. control stimuli, units are selected if their activations (at a specified token) show a statistically significant difference (Welch’s t-test). The "conjunctive" approach requires significance across all pairwise target-control comparisons. The core assumption is that such differential activations reflect recruitment of computational mechanisms specific to the targeted cognitive function (e.g., ToM). Evidence: localizer suites are built from validated fMRI contrasts and expanded with synthetic stimuli; cross-validation shows generalization for some localizers (e.g., MoralIntent); and Figure 2 shows non-random, layer-specific distributions of localized units. If localization fails to generalize in cross-validation (low significant fold count), its validity is undermined.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Model Zoo -> Evaluation Harness -> Localization Pipeline -> Ablation Engine -> Statistical Inference

### Critical Path
1. Select 20 models with above-chance performance on chosen ToM/pragmatics datasets.
2. For each localizer suite, generate 100 synthetic items per condition (target/control) using provided GPT-5 prompts.
3. For each model, forward-pass all localizer items, record last-token activations for all layers.
4. Compute per-unit selection metric (e.g., t-statistics for simple/conjunctive); apply significance threshold (α=0.05) and/or top‑1% to define critical and control subnetworks.
5. For each model-localizer pair, ablate the critical subnetwork and the matched control subnetwork (least‑active non‑significant units).
6. Evaluate ablated models on the held‑out ToM/pragmatics/syntax datasets (from step 1).
7. Compute per‑dataset accuracy differences vs. intact baseline; aggregate across models/localizers for global analysis.
8. Fit Bayesian beta regression to test predictions P1.1–P3.2, incorporating model family/size and random effects.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Synthetic vs. natural localizer stimuli**: Synthetic stimuli control lexical confounds but may lack ecological validity; natural stimuli risk surface correlations. Trade‑off is scale vs. realism.
- **Ablation method**: Zeroing units is simple but may cause distribution shifts; alternatives (patching, scaling) have different interpretational profiles.
- **Subnetwork size**: Fixed top‑1% vs. all significant units; threshold choice affects effect size and specificity.
- **Localizer suite choice**: Effects differ across suites (LatentBeliefs and GameBeliefs strongest; CommunicativeIntent weak), possibly due to task format or localization strength.

### Failure Signatures
- Localization fails to generalize in 10‑fold CV (low significant fold count).
- No credible difference between ablation conditions (critical vs. control) for ToM/pragmatics.
- Ablation harms syntax performance, indicating non‑specific damage.
- Effects appear only in one model family or size, not generalizable.
- Control subnetwork ablation shows similar or larger effects, indicating poor localization.

### First 3 Experiments
1. Replicate behavioral correlation: On 10 models and 5 ToM/5 pragmatics datasets, compute per‑model average accuracy and Pearson’s *r*; expect ~0.68.
2. Run a single localization (LatentBeliefs simple): Use Llama‑3.1‑8B, generate 100 FalseBelief and 100 FalsePhoto stimuli, record last‑token activations, compute t‑statistics, select top‑1% significant units; plot layer distribution (should concentrate in mid-to-late layers).
3. Perform a minimal ablation test: Ablate the subnetwork from Exp. 2, evaluate on 3 held‑out ToM (e.g., BigToM forward, EPITOME false belief) and 3 pragmatics datasets (e.g., scalar implicature, irony); compute mean accuracy drop vs. baseline and vs. control ablation; expect drop >0 for critical and > control.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How robust are the observed functional integration effects to variations in functional localization methodology, such as different ablation techniques, prompt formats, or localizer suite constructions? The study used a fixed set of design choices; their impact on the key findings is unknown. Resolving this requires systematic experiments varying one parameter at a time while measuring consistency of integration effects.
- **Open Question 2**: To what extent do the synthetic localizer stimuli correspond to human neural activation patterns in Theory of Mind tasks, and what does this imply for claims about the human‑likeness of language models’ functional organization? Without human fMRI validation, we cannot confirm that the same neural substrates are engaged, limiting cross‑species comparisons. Resolving requires human fMRI experiments using the exact synthetic stimuli.
- **Open Question 3**: How does linguistic and cultural diversity of evaluation datasets affect the generalizability of the functional integration hypothesis, given the exclusive focus on English? All data are English; cross‑linguistic and cross‑cultural variation could alter the relationship between ToM and pragmatics. Resolving requires replicating the study with multilingual and culturally diverse datasets.
- **Open Question 4**: What are the causal roles of individual neurons or finer‑grained structures within the localized ToM subnetworks, and how do they contribute to social reasoning capabilities? Ablating whole subnetworks obscures potential heterogeneity. Resolving requires fine‑grained causal intervention methods (e.g., activation patching, targeted neuron editing).
- **Open Question 5**: What is the exact relation between different Theory of Mind aspects (as per ATOMS) and pragmatic reasoning capabilities in language models? The paper shows general integration but does not dissect how specific ATOMS categories (e.g., beliefs, percepts) relate to pragmatics. Resolving requires high‑quality ATOMS annotations across tasks and mediation or structural equation modeling to map latent relationships.

## Limitations
- Synthetic localizer stimuli generated by GPT‑5 are not released; regeneration variability may affect unit selection stability.
- Tokenization differences, especially for chat models, may lead to incorrect activation extraction and flawed localization.
- Functional localization generalizes only for some suites (e.g., LatentBeliefs, GameBeliefs), while others (e.g., CommunicativeIntent) show weak success, questioning broad applicability.

## Confidence
- **Functional integration (ToM‑pragmatics overlap)**: Medium‑High
- **Functional localization validity**: Medium
- **ATOMS category differential prediction**: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Stimulus reproducibility**: Regenerate all synthetic stimuli with multiple random seeds; compute Jaccard overlap of selected units across seeds. Low overlap indicates unstable subnetworks.
2. **Bidirectional ablation**: Ablate pragmatics‑localized subnetworks and evaluate on ToM tasks. Absence of causal effect would challenge symmetric integration.
3. **Token‑position robustness**: For a diverse sample of models, extract activations at several token positions (e.g., last context token, question start) and re‑run localization. High divergence would undermine method reliability.
