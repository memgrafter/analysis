# GATech at AbjadGenEval Shared Task: Multilingual Embeddings for Arabic Machine-Generated Text Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.10007
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10007
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** Simple mean pooling over E5-large encoder embeddings achieved the highest test F1 score (0.75) for Arabic AI-generated text detection, outperforming more complex pooling methods.

## Executive Summary
This submission addresses Arabic AI-generated text detection within the AbjadGenEval shared task. The team fine-tuned the multilingual E5-large encoder and compared four pooling strategies to aggregate token embeddings: mean pooling, weighted layer pooling, multi-head attention pooling, and gated fusion. Simple mean pooling proved most effective, yielding a test F1 score of 0.75. The authors also observed a stark length disparity: human-written texts averaged 632 words versus 303 words for machine-generated texts—a potential strong heuristic signal. The work highlights the value of simplicity and data-aware design when training data is limited.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tuned the multilingual E5-large encoder on the AbjadGenEval Arabic AI-generated text detection dataset. They extracted contextual token embeddings from the encoder and applied four pooling strategies to obtain document-level embeddings: mean pooling (average of all token embeddings), weighted layer pooling (learned weighted combination of embeddings from different layers), multi-head attention pooling (self-attention over tokens), and gated fusion (combining multiple features via learned gates). The document embedding was passed to a binary classifier head (presumably a linear layer) trained with cross-entropy loss. Training details such as learning rate, batch size, and number of epochs are not fully specified in the paper.

## Key Results
- Mean pooling achieved the highest test F1 score of 0.75.
- Mean pooling outperformed weighted layer pooling, multi-head attention pooling, and gated fusion.
- Human-written texts were significantly longer (average 632 words) than machine-generated texts (average 303 words).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The authors attribute the superiority of mean pooling to its robustness under limited training data. More complex pooling methods (weighted layer, attention, gated fusion) introduce additional trainable parameters, which increase the risk of overfitting on the relatively small dataset. Mean pooling, being parameter-free, acts as a strong regularizer by computing a fixed, interpretable average of token embeddings without learning extraneous interactions.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Input Text -> E5-large Encoder -> Pooling Layer -> Classifier Head

**Critical path:** Arabic text is tokenized and fed into E5-large, producing a sequence of contextual token embeddings. The pooling layer aggregates these into a single document vector. The classifier head (architecture unspecified) maps this vector to a binary label (human vs. machine).

**Design tradeoffs:** Mean pooling offers simplicity and regularisation but discards token-level importance weighting. Attention-based pooling can model token significance but adds parameters and compute, increasing overfitting risk on small data. Gated fusion combines multiple signals but is the most parameter-intensive.

**Failure signatures:**
- Training accuracy near 100% while validation accuracy lags → overfitting (especially with complex pooling).
- Model predictions strongly correlated with document length → may ignore linguistic cues.
- Poor performance on dialectal or domain-shifted Arabic text → lack of generalisation from the shared task dataset.

**First experiments:**
1. Replicate the mean pooling baseline: fine-tune E5-large with mean pooling and report validation F1.
2. Swap in other pooling heads (weighted layer, attention, gated) with identical hyperparameters; compare validation curves to diagnose overfitting.
3. Ablate length: truncate/pad all texts to a fixed length (e.g., 300 words) and re-evaluate mean pooling performance to quantify length's contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly list open questions. However, based on the discussion and limitations, several unanswered questions emerge: (1) To what extent does the length difference reflect underlying linguistic differences versus superficial artifacts? (2) Does mean pooling's advantage persist on larger datasets or with different multilingual encoders? (3) How do the models generalize to diverse Arabic dialects or domains outside the shared task data? (4) Can the length bias be mitigated (e.g., via length normalization or stratified sampling) to force models to learn more subtle cues? These questions suggest directions for future research.

## Limitations
- Findings are confined to the AbjadGenEval shared task dataset; generalisability to other Arabic domains or dialects is unknown.
- Conclusions about pooling strategies are specific to E5-large and the given data size; results may differ with larger datasets or other encoders (e.g., mBERT, XLM-R).
- The predictive role of document length is unanalyzed; it may be a proxy for deeper textual differences (e.g., repetition, syntactic simplicity).
