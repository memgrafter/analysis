# A Computer Vision Pipeline for Iterative Bullet Hole Tracking in Rifle Zeroing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.17062
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17062
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Automates rifle zeroing by detecting bullet holes and assigning them to firing iterations with 97.0% mAP50 detection and 88.8% iteration accuracy.

## Executive Summary
Rifle zeroing traditionally requires physical inspection of targets at the range to adjust sights. This paper introduces an end-to-end computer vision pipeline that processes images of targets to automatically detect bullet holes and associate them with the correct shot iteration, enabling remote zeroing without range visits. The system leverages YOLOv8 for robust small-object detection, ORB feature matching for perspective normalization, IoU-based tracking to differentiate new holes between sequential images, and a reverse data augmentation strategy that removes bullet holes from real images to generate synthetic training pairs. Key results demonstrate 97.0% mAP50 for hole detection and 88.8% accuracy in iteration assignment, confirming the feasibility of automated zeroing. The authors note that while full pipeline accuracy is lower due to error compounding, the approach opens the door to real-time deployment and mobile integration.

## Method Summary
The pipeline processes raw target images (JPEG/PNG) from the firing line. First, a preprocessing stage detects the target's four corner fiducials using ORB features and computes a homography to warp the target to a fixed canonical view. If ORB fails to find enough corners—common under occlusion or blur—a YOLOv8 target detector identifies the target region and the ORB step is retried within that crop. Once a normalized target patch is obtained, a fine-tuned YOLOv8 model (trained for 100 epochs with default hyperparameters on a combined dataset of 1,243 single-shot images and 33 synthetic sequential pairs) performs bullet hole detection, outputting bounding boxes. For sequences, the system runs inference on each frame and applies IoU matching between consecutive detections: a hole in the current frame is labeled "new" if its maximum IoU with any previous hole is below 0.5; otherwise it is considered persistent. To train the detector despite scarce real sequential data, the authors generate synthetic before-and-after image pairs by physically or digitally removing bullet holes from a real image to serve as the "before" state. The final output consists of annotated images and a list of holes with iteration labels. The modular design allows independent evaluation of each component, and the authors highlight several tradeoffs such as ORB's speed versus brittleness, the simplicity versus rigidity of IoU tracking, and the limited diversity of reverse-augmented data.

## Key Results
- Bullet hole detection: 97.0% mAP50 on held-out test set.
- Firing iteration classification: 88.8% accuracy on sequential pairs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: YOLOv8 provides accurate small-object detection of bullet holes.
- **Mechanism**: A convolutional neural network (YOLOv8) is fine-tuned on a custom dataset of target images. It learns to localize bullet holes via bounding box regression and classification in a single forward pass.
- **Core assumption**: Bullet holes are visually distinct, small, circular features that can be reliably detected by a supervised learning model given sufficient diverse training data.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] Achieves 97.0% mAP50 on bullet hole detection.
  - [section III.C] Describes fine-tuning YOLOv8 on combined datasets and applying it to segmented target regions.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers (e.g., [3], [4]) also apply YOLO/Mask R-CNN to bullet detection, suggesting this is a validated approach for the domain.
- **Break condition**: Performance degrades on heavily occluded, poorly lit, or ultra-dense shot groups where holes merge or lack clear boundaries.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Intersection over Union (IoU) matching differentiates new bullet holes from existing ones across sequential images.
- **Mechanism**: For each detected hole in the second (current) image, compute its IoU with all holes detected in the first (previous) image. A hole is classified as "new" only if its maximum IoU with any previous hole is below a threshold (0.5).
- **Core assumption**: After perspective normalization, the *same* physical bullet hole will occupy approximately the same pixel coordinates in both images, resulting in high IoU for matching holes and low IoU for newly fired holes.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] Reports 88.8% accuracy in assigning holes to correct firing iteration using this method.
  - [section III.D] Explicitly details the IoU thresholding process and justifies its simplicity over optical flow, assuming fixed target position and successful normalization.
  - [corpus] Weak direct support; IoU is a standard metric, but its novel use for this specific temporal tracking task is claimed by the authors without external validation in the corpus.
- **Break condition**: Target movement between shots, failed perspective normalization causing misalignment, or extremely tight shot groups can cause incorrect IoU matches (e.g., a new hole overlapping spatially with an old one).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: ORB-based perspective correction standardizes target orientation, enabling reliable spatial alignment for IoU matching.
- **Mechanism**: ORB features detect the target's corner fiducials (e.g., bullseye, outer ring). A homography matrix is computed from these points to warp the image into a fixed canonical view. A YOLOv8 fallback provides a bounding box if ORB fails.
- **Core assumption**: The target has a known, rigid geometric shape (a circle) with distinct, stable visual markers (the printed rings) that are detectable across different camera angles and distances.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] States ORB-based correction "standardizes target orientation" and improves accuracy.
  - [section III.B] Details the ORB segmentation process and its 72.9% success rate on fully visible targets. Notes fallback to YOLOv8.
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; ORB is a well-established feature detector, but its specific efficacy for this target type is only evidenced by the paper's own segmentation results.
- **Break condition**: Target is partially occluded, heavily wrinkled, or captured at extreme angles where fiducials are not visible; ORB fails and YOLO fallback may provide an imprecise crop, degrading downstream alignment.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Raw image -> Preprocessing (ORB corner detection & homography; YOLOv8 fallback) -> YOLOv8 bullet hole detection -> IoU matching (threshold 0.5) -> Labeled output.

**Critical path**: Preprocessing success -> Detection accuracy -> IoU match quality. A failure at segmentation (72.9% success rate) or detection cascades.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Segmentation robustness vs. speed: ORB is fast but brittle to occlusion; YOLO fallback is slower but more general.
- Tracking simplicity vs. rigidity: IoU matching is computationally cheap and interpretable but critically depends on perfect spatial normalization; more complex trackers (ByteTrack, DeepSORT) would be more robust to misalignment but add complexity and training overhead.
- Synthetic data generation: Reverse augmentation creates plausible sequences but only from a single viewpoint, limiting diversity compared to multi-view real data.

**Failure signatures**:
- High full pipeline error (40.3% accuracy) vs. high component metrics indicates error compounding: a minor segmentation slip causes detection misses or misaligned IoU matches.
- Low iteration classification accuracy (88.8%) often correlates with shots near target edges or in clusters where hole displacement from recoil/camera shift causes IoU confusion.
- Target detection mAP50 (62.2%) is low; when ORB fails, the YOLO fallback may miss targets, causing total pipeline failure.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Validate Preprocessing Success Rate: Run the ORB segmentation on a held-out set of 100 real range images (fully visible targets). Calculate the exact success rate and categorize failures (occlusion, blur, lighting). This confirms the 72.9% figure on your operational data.
2. Measure Normalization Impact on Detection: Compare YOLOv8 mAP50 on two sets: (a) raw, uncropped images; (b) ORB-warped target patches. Expect a significant drop in (a), quantifying the value of the perspective correction step.
3. Tune IoU Threshold Sensitivity: On the 33 labeled sequential pairs, vary the IoU threshold (0.3 to 0.7) and plot iteration classification accuracy. Identify the optimal operational threshold (paper uses 0.5) and understand the tradeoff between false positives (new hole marked old) and false negatives (old hole marked new).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Can the system be adapted for real-time deployment to provide instantaneous feedback during live firing, and what are the computational bottlenecks? The authors note that the speed of YOLOv8 opens the door for real-time use, but no latency benchmarks have been performed.
- **Open Question 2**: Would a mobile application that allows shooters to correct mislabeled bullet holes and capture new training data in the field improve long-term system accuracy and adaptability? The paper proposes such an app, but no user-in-the-loop study exists.
- **Open Question 3**: Can super-resolution techniques effectively recover detection accuracy on low-resolution or compressed images from cost-constrained cameras? The authors suggest exploring this, but the impact of resolution loss remains unquantified.
- **Open Question 4**: How robust is the ORB-based segmentation pipeline to real-world occlusions (e.g., overlapping targets, partial target ejection), and can fallback mechanisms fully mitigate these failures? The reported 72.9% segmentation accuracy and low fallback target detection (62.2% mAP50) raise concerns but lack detailed analysis.
- **Open Question 5**: Under what conditions does the IoU-based tracking assumption (that holes remain spatially static after perspective normalization) break down, and how frequently do such violations occur in operational settings? The system assumes minimal displacement, but the tolerance to residual misalignments is unknown.

## Limitations
- Unclear model configuration: YOLOv8 size, training hyperparameters, and augmentation details are not specified, impeding exact replication.
- Incomplete specification of ORB segmentation parameters and fallback logic; the reported 72.9% success rate leaves many images without a valid warp, and the fallback's effectiveness under occlusion is unclear.
- Full pipeline accuracy is low (40.3%) due to error compounding, and the evaluation protocol for sequential assignment is insufficiently described; generalization to diverse operational conditions remains untested.
