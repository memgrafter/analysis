# XLinear: Frequency-Enhanced MLP with CrossFilter for Robust Long-Range Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.15645
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15645
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Primary result:** Achieved state-of-the-art performance among MLP-based forecasters on 8 datasets, with significant MSE/MAE reductions for long horizons (e.g., 0.443 vs. 0.491 MSE on ETTm1 for 720-step prediction).

## Executive Summary
XLinear addresses the limited receptive field of standard MLP-based time series forecasters by introducing a frequency-aware architecture. The core innovation is a decomposition strategy that separates the input into trend (low-frequency) and seasonal+noise (high-frequency) components. The trend is processed by an Enhanced Frequency Attention (EFA) block that uses FFT to amplify long-range dependencies efficiently. The seasonal component is handled by a CrossFilter block that fuses a simple static filter (PaiFilter) with an adaptive filter (TexFilter) via a gated multiplicative fusion to denoise robustly. This design allows XLinear to maintain a lightweight MLP backbone while rivaling Transformer-based models on long-horizon tasks, demonstrating clear gains on most benchmarks.

## Method Summary
XLinear first decomposes the input time series using a moving average to extract a smooth trend component `X_trend` and a residual `X_season` containing seasonality and noise. These branches are processed in parallel. For `X_trend`, the Enhanced Frequency Attention (EFA) block projects to Q, K, V, transforms Q and K to the frequency domain via FFT, performs element-wise multiplication and normalization to emphasize low-frequency interactions, applies an inverse FFT, and combines the result with an exponentially-activated V to produce an attention-weighted output. For `X_season`, the CrossFilter block processes the input through two parallel filters: a PaiFilter (static frequency kernel) and a TexFilter (data-dependent neural network on frequency coefficients). Their outputs are fused using `X_out = X_tex ⊙ GELU(X_pai) + X_pai ⊙ GELU(X_tex)` to suppress noise. The trend and seasonal outputs are concatenated and passed through a single linear layer to generate the forecast.

## Key Results
- XLinear set a new state-of-the-art among MLP-based models across 8 diverse datasets.
- For long forecasting horizons (336, 720 steps), it achieved consistent and often substantial reductions in MSE and MAE compared to prior MLP methods like iTransformer (e.g., on ETTm1, 720-step MSE 0.443 vs. 0.491).
- The model maintained a lightweight parameter count and demonstrated strong robustness, with ablations confirming the contribution of both EFA and CrossFilter components.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Specialized Processing via Decomposition
The moving average decomposition splits the series into a low-frequency trend `T_t` and a high-frequency residual `R_t` (seasonal+noise). This allows assigning the computationally intensive, frequency-domain EFA to the trend where long-range structure resides, while a lighter, robust CrossFilter handles the residual. The core assumption is that long-term dependencies are encoded in low frequencies, making this split efficient and effective. Evidence comes from the model's architecture description and spectral analysis literature. A break condition occurs if the decomposition kernel `k` is mis-sized, failing to isolate clean trend/seasonal components.

### Mechanism 2: Enhanced Frequency Attention (EFA) for Long-Range Capture
EFA operates on `X_trend`. After linear projections, FFT transforms Q and K to the frequency domain. Their element-wise multiplication `ϕ = F(Q) ⊙ F(K) / ||...||` inherently emphasizes squared low-frequency terms `f_low^2`, as low-frequency amplitudes typically dominate. An inverse FFT returns this to the time domain, and the result is scaled and combined with `exp(clamp(V))` to non-linearly amplify trend signals before softmax attention. This design captures long-range dependencies more efficiently than spatial attention. Evidence is provided by the theoretical derivation (Eq. 12) and figure showing low-frequency strengthening. Risks include ineffectiveness if the trend is not predominantly low-frequency or numerical instability from the exponential.

### Mechanism 3: CrossFilter for Robust High-Frequency Modeling
The CrossFilter block processes `X_season` with two parallel filters: PaiFilter (simple, fast, `F^{-1}(F(S) ⊙ H)`) and TexFilter (adaptive, `F^{-1}(F(S) ⊙ H_φ(F(S)))`). Their outputs are fused via `X_tex ⊙ GELU(X_pai) + X_pai ⊙ GELU(X_tex)`. The multiplicative GELU-gated fusion is designed to suppress noise that would accumulate with naive concatenation, combining the strengths of both filters. The assumption is that simple and complex filters capture complementary aspects, and their cross-integration denoises more effectively. Evidence is the paper's noise-robustness claim and fusion equation. Break conditions include overlapping noise patterns that both filters amplify or overfitting from TexFilter's adaptivity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Fast Fourier Transform (FFT):** Enables O(N log N) transformation between time and frequency domains. *Why needed:* Core to EFA and CrossFilter; turns convolution into efficient element-wise multiplication. *Quick check:* Why does multiplying two signals in the frequency domain correspond to convolving them in the time domain, and why is this useful for modeling dependencies?
- **Time Series Decomposition (Moving Average):** Splits a series into trend and residual via smoothing. *Why needed:* The entire architecture depends on this separation to route components to specialized modules. *Quick check:* How does the choice of kernel size `k` in the moving average affect the frequency content of the extracted trend component?
- **GELU Activation:** `x * Φ(x)` provides input-dependent, probabilistic gating. *Why needed:* Used in CrossFilter's fusion to non-linearly weight filter outputs for noise suppression, offering smoother behavior than ReLU. *Quick check:* What is the key difference between GELU and ReLU in treating negative inputs, and why might this benefit a noise-filtering gate?
- **Convolution Theorem:** States that convolution in time domain equals multiplication in frequency domain. *Why needed:* Justifies the efficiency of filter-based operations (PaiFilter, TexFilter) via FFT. *Quick check:* If you convolve a signal with a kernel of length `M`, what is the complexity in time vs. frequency domain?
- **Spectral Analysis:** Studying signal frequency content. *Why needed:* Underpins the rationale for separating trend (low-freq) and seasonal/noise (high-freq) and for designing EFA's amplification. *Quick check:* What characteristic of a time series’ power spectral density would indicate a strong long-range trend?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input (multivariate series) -> Decomposition (moving average) -> Parallel branches: Trend -> EFA Block; Seasonal -> CrossFilter Block -> Concatenation (trend + seasonal outputs) -> MLP Head (linear layer) -> Forecast.
- **Critical path:** After decomposition, the two branches operate independently. The trend branch's fidelity is critical for long-horizon accuracy; the seasonal branch's denoising is critical for robustness on noisy data. Their concatenated representation must retain information from both for the final MLP to succeed.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *EFA vs. Standard Attention:* EFA reduces quadratic complexity via FFT and focuses on low frequencies, but adds FFT/IFFT overhead and risks from `exp(clamp(V))`.
  - *CrossFilter Complexity:* TexFilter adds parameters for adaptivity; the fusion formula increases operations. This trades some lightweight nature for improved robustness.
  - *Decomposition Simplicity:* Moving average is fast but may not perfectly separate components for all series (e.g., non-stationary trends), potentially misrouting information.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Poor long-horizon (L≥336):** Likely EFA failure; widening gap vs. SOTA as horizon increases. Diagnose by visualizing `X_trend`.
  - **Poor on noisy/simple datasets:** Likely CrossFilter misconfiguration (incorrect fusion or overfitting). Diagnose by testing on synthetic noisy signals.
  - **Numerical instability:** NaN losses. Check `clamp` limits, epsilon in normalization, and complex tensor handling in FFT.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablate EFA: Replace EFA block in trend branch with a linear layer or standard multi-head attention. Measure MSE/MAE degradation on long horizons (720). Validates EFA's necessity.
  2. Ablate CrossFilter: Use only PaiFilter or only TexFilter on seasonal branch. Compare across datasets (e.g., Weather vs. Traffic). Validates hybrid filter benefit.
  3. Sensitivity to decomposition kernel `k`: Sweep moving average window sizes (e.g., 8,16,32,64) on datasets with different seasonal periods (ETT hourly vs. daily). Plot performance vs. `k` to assess robustness of fixed decomposition.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Why does XLinear still underperform Transformer-based models (e.g., iTransformer) on the Traffic dataset, despite narrowing the gap on other datasets? The paper notes this observation but provides no analysis.
- **Open Question 2:** How does the choice of time series decomposition method affect XLinear's performance, and can more sophisticated decomposition (e.g., STL, wavelets) improve long-range forecasting? The paper uses a simple moving average without exploring alternatives.
- **Open Question 3:** Is the proposed cross-integration of PaiFilter and TexFilter via element-wise multiplication and GELU the most effective way to combine frequency filters, or could other fusion mechanisms yield better denoising and predictive performance? No comparison to alternatives is provided.
- **Open Question 4:** To what extent does the EFA block selectively amplify low-frequency trend components and suppress high-frequency noise, and can this behavior be controlled or interpreted? No spectral analysis of intermediate representations is given.

## Limitations
- Exact model dimensions (hidden size, depth), decomposition kernel size `k`, and TexFilter's `H_φ` architecture are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- The fixed moving average decomposition may not generalize well to series with complex or nonlinear seasonal patterns.
- Practical computational overhead from FFT/IFFT operations might offset claimed efficiency gains on some hardware.
- Numerical instability risks from `exp(clamp(V))` and complex tensor handling require careful implementation.

## Confidence
- **Decomposition premise (trend/seasonal split): High** – Standard technique, supported by literature and ablations.
- **EFA's frequency-amplification for long-range capture: Medium** – Theoretically plausible but sensitive to hyperparameters; limited direct validation.
- **CrossFilter's multiplicative-GELU fusion for noise suppression: Low** – Novel design with no clear precedent; risk of overfitting or ineffective denoising.
- **Overall SOTA performance among MLP models: High** – Strong empirical results reported, but require independent verification.

## Next Checks
1. **EFA ablation:** Replace the EFA block with a simple linear layer on the trend branch and measure MSE/MAE drop on long horizons (≥336). A significant degradation would validate EFA's critical role.
2. **CrossFilter component ablation:** Train variants using only PaiFilter or only TexFilter on noisy datasets (e.g., Traffic, ECL). XLinear should outperform both single-filter versions, confirming the fusion's benefit.
3. **Decomposition kernel sensitivity:** Sweep moving-average kernel size `k` (e.g., 8, 16, 32, 64) across datasets like ETT (hourly/daily) and Traffic. Plot performance vs. `k` to identify optimal ranges and test the robustness of the fixed-`k` approach.
