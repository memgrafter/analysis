# Bridging the Gap in Bangla Healthcare: Machine Learning Based Disease Prediction Using a Symptoms-Disease Dataset

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2601.12068
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12068
- **Reference Count**: 20
- **Primary Result**: An ensemble of top machine learning models achieved 98% accuracy, 97% macro-recall, and 97% macro-F1 on a novel Bangla symptoms-disease dataset.

## Executive Summary
This study tackles the scarcity of Bangla-language resources for automated disease prediction. The authors created and publicly released a new dataset containing 758 symptom-disease relationships spanning 85 diseases and 172 symptoms, curated from medical surveys and reputable Bangla sources. They trained and tuned eight standard classifiers on this binary-encoded data, then combined the top three performers (Random Forest, Logistic Regression, Perceptron) into hard and soft voting ensembles. The resulting models demonstrated high robustness, achieving 98% accuracy and 97% macro-recall, indicating strong capability in predicting diseases from Bangla symptom inputs while avoiding poor performance on rare conditions.

## Method Summary
The research process began with constructing a Bangla symptoms-disease dataset via medical professional surveys and online health sources, yielding 758 samples with 172 binary symptom features and 85 disease labels. Preprocessing involved handling missing values (as absence), standardizing features, and applying PCA to retain 90% variance for dimensionality reduction. Eight classifiers were trained using 5-fold cross-validation to select optimal hyperparameters from a defined search space. The three best-performing models on validation sets were selected to form hard-voting (majority class) and soft-voting (averaged class probabilities) ensembles. Final evaluation on a held-out 20% test set used accuracy, macro-recall, and macro-F1 as primary metrics to ensure balanced performance across all disease classes.

## Key Results
- The curated dataset contains 758 unique relationships across 85 diseases and 172 symptoms.
- Ensemble models (soft/hard voting) achieved 98% accuracy, 97% macro-recall, and 97% macro-F1 on the test set.
- All ensemble metrics outperformed every individual base classifier, demonstrating the benefit of model aggregation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
**Claim**: A linguistically and medically curated Bangla symptoms-disease dataset enables accurate model training for local-language healthcare prediction.
**Mechanism**: The dataset was constructed via medical professional surveys and reputable online sources, then structured as a binary feature matrix (symptoms as columns, diseases as labels). This provides a direct mapping of symptom presence/absence to specific diseases, which classification models can learn.
**Core Assumption**: The symptomatic relationships between diseases and their key indicators are language-agnostic and can be reliably captured in Bangla without loss of diagnostic meaning.
**Evidence Anchors**: [abstract] dataset creation with 758 relationships across 85 diseases and 172 symptoms; [section III] details collection from "medical community" and "medical journals, health-related blogs."
**Break Condition**: Significant translation/semantic drift in Bangla symptom definitions or lack of coverage for common regional diseases would cause model generalization failure.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim**: Ensemble voting (soft/hard) combining top-performing individual models improves robustness and mitigates the error profiles of any single classifier.
**Mechanism**: The authors identified Random Forest, Logistic Regression, and Perceptron as top performers. Soft voting averages their predicted class probabilities, while hard voting uses majority class. This aggregates diverse decision boundaries, reducing variance and bias from any one model's structural limitations.
**Core Assumption**: The error patterns of the top three models are sufficiently non-correlated; their consensus yields a more reliable prediction than any single model.
**Evidence Anchors**: [section IV.F] explicit soft/hard voting formulas using top M=3 classifiers; [section V.C] ensembles achieved 98% accuracy, macro-recall, and macro-F1, outperforming all individual models.
**Break Condition**: Top models share similar failure modes (e.g., all struggle with a specific disease cluster) or one model heavily dominates the vote.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim**: Dimensionality reduction via PCA (preserving 90% variance) from 172 symptoms improves model generalization by filtering noise and reducing computational overhead, without critical loss of predictive signal.
**Mechanism**: High-dimensional binary symptom data likely contains correlated or redundant symptoms. PCA transforms features into orthogonal principal components, keeping those that explain most variance. This denoises the data and prevents models from fitting to spurious correlations in less informative symptom dimensions.
**Core Assumption**: The leading principal components capture the essential symptom-disease distinguishing information; discarded components are primarily noise or redundant.
**Evidence Anchors**: [section IV.A.4] "As the dataset has 172 features (high dimensional data), PCA has proved to be beneficial to reduce noise and computational costs"; PCA is part of the standard preprocessing pipeline.
**Break Condition**: Critical diagnostic symptoms load heavily onto discarded principal components, reducing model accuracy for diseases with subtle or unique symptom profiles.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept**: Binary Feature Encoding for Symptom Presence.
  **Why needed**: Symptoms are categorical (present/absent) and non-ordinal. One-hot encoding creates a clean binary matrix that models like SVM and Logistic Regression expect, avoiding false ordinal relationships.
  **Quick Check**: If a new symptom appears in deployment that wasn't in the training set's 172 symptoms, how would you extend the feature vector without retraining all models from scratch?

- **Concept**: Macro-averaged Evaluation Metrics for Imbalanced Multi-class Prediction.
  **Why needed**: With 85 diseases, some are rarer. Accuracy can be misleading. Macro-recall/F1 computes metrics per disease then averages, treating all diseases equally and highlighting if the model misses entire classes (critical for not overlooking serious illnesses).
  **Quick Check**: Why might micro-averaged recall be a dangerous metric for this disease prediction task, and what specific failure could it hide?

- **Concept**: Online vs. Batch Learning Algorithms (e.g., Passive Aggressive Classifier).
  **Why needed**: PAC is an online algorithm that updates per sample. The paper includes it as a candidate, suggesting potential for incremental learning as new Bangla symptom-disease data arrives without full retraining.
  **Quick Check**: What is a key hyperparameter in PAC that controls the aggressiveness of updates when a prediction is wrong, and how does its value (C=0.01 in Table IV) reflect a cautious update strategy?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Data Ingestion & Curation → Preprocessing Module → Model Zoo → Hyperparameter Tuner → Ensemble Builder → Evaluation & API.
- **Critical path**: Dataset creation → Preprocessing pipeline definition → Single-model baseline training → Top-3 selection → Ensemble training → Final evaluation. The dataset curation is the foundational gating step; without it, no model can learn Bangla-specific patterns.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **One-hot encoding** vs. embeddings: One-hot is simple but creates 172-dim sparse vector; embeddings could capture symptom similarity but require more data and complexity.
  - **PCA** vs. feature selection: PCA is unsupervised and may discard clinically relevant but low-variance symptoms; a supervised method might retain more predictive power but risks overfitting.
  - **Hard vs. Soft voting**: Hard voting is simpler and more interpretable; soft voting uses probability information but can be overconfident if top models are poorly calibrated.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - **Low macro-recall**: Model missing entire disease classes → likely dataset imbalance or insufficient symptom coverage for rare diseases.
  - **Decision tree (78% accuracy) underperforming**: Indicates high model variance or inability to capture complex symptom interactions; suggests reliance on ensembles is necessary.
  - **Gap between train and validation accuracy in learning curves**: Overfitting if train accuracy is high and validation plateaus early; underfitting if both are low and converge.
  - **Soft voting performs worse than best single model**: Poor probability calibration in component models; consider recalibrating (e.g., Platt scaling) before soft voting.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Dataset sanity check**: For a sample of 20 diseases, manually verify that the listed symptoms in the dataset align with standard medical references (e.g., dengue includes "high fever, joint pain" in Bangla). Aim for >95% expert agreement.
  2. **Single-model ablation**: Train all 8 models with default hyperparameters (no tuning) on the preprocessed data. Record accuracy/macro-F1. This establishes a baseline to confirm tuning and ensemble gains are real, not just from lucky splits.
  3. **Ensemble composition test**: Beyond top-3 (RF, LR, Perceptron), try ensembles with different combinations (e.g., include SVM or KNN). Measure if performance consistently degrades when a top-3 model is swapped out, confirming their complementary role.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: What scalable and medically validated methodology can be employed to expand the Bangla symptoms-disease dataset to encompass all diseases in the medical field, while maintaining linguistic accuracy and relational integrity?
- **Open Question 2**: Which deep learning architectures (e.g., neural networks with attention mechanisms) best capture the high-dimensional, binary symptom features of the Bangla dataset, and how do they balance performance with interpretability for clinical use?
- **Open Question 3**: How do regional dialectical variations in Bangla symptom descriptions and demographic factors affect the robustness of the trained models when applied to real-world, noisy user inputs across Bangladesh and other Bangla-speaking regions?

## Limitations
- The claim that the curated dataset accurately captures symptom-disease relationships in Bangla lacks external expert validation or comparison to established Bangla medical lexicons, risking semantic drift or regional bias.
- The reported 98% soft voting ensemble performance relies on averaging `predict_proba` outputs, but key top models like Perceptron and Passive Aggressive Classifier do not natively provide calibrated probabilities; the paper does not describe calibration, risking unreliable probability aggregation.
- Applying PCA to retain 90% variance is assumed to filter noise without losing critical diagnostic signal; there is no analysis showing that diseases with subtle or rare symptom profiles are not harmed by discarding low-variance components.

## Confidence
- **Dataset Validity**: Medium (based on self-described collection methods without external audit)
- **Ensemble Probability Calibration**: Medium (soft voting performance may be unstable without calibrated probabilities)
- **PCA Feature Discard Impact**: Low (no evidence that discarded components don't contain critical diagnostic information)

Compare the top 30 most important symptoms against the loadings of the first 5 principal components. Flag if any clinically critical symptoms (e.g., "hematemesis" for gastric ulcers) are heavily weighted in discarded components.
