# DesignSense: A Human Preference Dataset and Reward Modeling Framework for Graphic Layout Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.23438
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23438
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: DesignSense-10k dataset and its VLM judge, DesignSense, achieve a 54.6% Macro F1 improvement over the strongest baseline and deliver downstream RL and inference gains of ~3% and 3.6%.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces DesignSense-10k, a large-scale human preference dataset for graphic layout evaluation with 10,235 pairs annotated using a 4-class schema (left/right/both good/both bad) to capture nuanced aesthetic judgments. A five-stage curation pipeline transforms raw layouts from Crello into diverse, high-quality pairs. Fine-tuning InternVL3-8B on this dataset yields DesignSense, a VLM-based judge that significantly outperforms existing models (including GPT-4o) on the preference task. When used as a reward model in reinforcement learning or for inference-time candidate selection, DesignSense improves layout generator win rates by approximately 3% and 3.6%, respectively, while generalizing robustly to out-of-distribution layouts.

## Method Summary
The authors construct DesignSense-10k via a five-stage pipeline: (1) semantic grouping of layout elements using GPT-4o; (2) generation of candidate layouts with the AesthetiQ model across multiple aspect ratios; (3) filtering of low-quality outputs with GPT-4o; (4) clustering based on DINOv3 features and IoU to ensure diversity; (5) refinement using OpenAI o3. Human annotators label each pair with one of four classes. The resulting dataset is used to fine-tune InternVL3-8B, with data augmentation via random perturbations. The trained VLM serves as a judge in an RL framework (AAPA) and for ranking multiple generation candidates at inference time.

## Key Results
- DesignSense attains a Macro F1 score that is 54.6% higher than the best proprietary baseline (GPT-4o).
- Using DesignSense as a judge in AAPA RL training improves the generator’s win rate by about 3% (from 14.27% to 17.97% under GPT-4o evaluation).
- Inference-time scaling (selecting the best among generated candidates) yields an additional 3.6% boost in win rate against GPT-5.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Systematic pipeline yields high-quality, diverse pairs.** The five-stage curation (semantic grouping, prediction, filtering, clustering, VLM refinement) transforms raw layouts into pairs with meaningful aesthetic differences while maintaining coherence. Grouping based on GPT-4o achieves ARI ~0.69, prediction with AesthetiQ generates plausible variants, and clustering ensures visual diversity. This rich distribution enables effective reward model training.
2. **4-class annotation captures subjective ambiguity.** Including options for "both good" and "both bad" explicitly models human uncertainty, preventing forced binary choices and improving calibration. Annotators are instructed to use these when preferences are unclear, leading to a dataset that reflects realistic design trade-offs.
3. **Accurate judge directly improves downstream generation.** DesignSense’s strong preference prediction (54.6% Macro F1 gain over GPT-4o) translates to better rewards during RL training and more reliable inference-time selection, yielding win rate improvements of ~3% and 3.6% respectively.

## Foundational Learning
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM) Fine-tuning**: Needed to adapt a general-purpose VLM (InternVL3-8B) to the specialized aesthetic judgment task. The model must reason about spatial arrangement and visual principles beyond object recognition. *Quick check:* How does the model's internal representation of layout quality differ from standard VLM features?
- **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for Generation**: The downstream RL (AAPA) relies on DesignSense as a reward model to align the generator with human preferences. *Quick check:* Why might improvements in Macro F1 not linearly correlate with win rate gains in RL?
- **Dataset Curation via Synthetic Transformation Pipelines**: The dataset is not collected directly but synthesized through multiple automated stages, each introducing potential biases. *Quick check:* What are the risks of using a layout prediction model (AesthetiQ) to generate its own training data for preference learning?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Crello dataset -> Grouping (GPT-4o) -> Layout prediction (AesthetiQ) -> Filtering (GPT-4o) -> Clustering (DINOv3 + IoU) -> Refinement (OpenAI o3) -> Human annotation (4-class) -> Fine-tune InternVL3-8B -> Judge in AAPA RL / inference-time selection

### Critical path
Raw layouts undergo the five-stage pipeline to produce annotated pairs, which train the VLM judge; the judge then guides the layout generator via RL or selects the best candidate at inference. The most resource-intensive step is human annotation (80% via AMT), and the highest-risk failure point is pipeline quality, particularly semantic grouping and prediction, which if poor yield incoherent or uninformative pairs.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Annotation cost vs. scale:** The 10k-pair dataset balances cost and performance; more pairs may not help if pipeline quality degrades.
- **Aspect ratio diversity:** Including stretched/inverse ratios increases challenge and generalizability but may produce unnatural layouts for some generators.
- **Model size:** InternVL3-8B offers a good trade-off between capability and inference cost; a larger backbone could improve accuracy but increase runtime as a judge.

### Failure signatures
- **Dataset-level:** High proportion of "both bad" labels may cause the model to under-recognize "both good" or over-penalize minor flaws.
- **Model-level:** Low Cohen’s κ (<0.3) on the 4-class task indicates unreliable category distinction, often seen in baseline VLMs.
- **Downstream:** Absence of win rate improvement when plugging DesignSense into RL suggests noisy reward signals or reward hacking.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Validate grouping quality:** Compute ARI between GPT-4o groupings and human annotations on a held-out set; target >0.65.
2. **Judge calibration test:** Measure Macro F1 and per-class F1 on a balanced validation set; expect Macro F1 >0.4, with non-negligible scores for "both good/bad."
3. **RL integration sanity check:** Perform one AAPA update with DesignSense vs. ViLA on a small batch (≈50 samples); confirm directional win rate improvement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why do VLMs catastrophically fail the four-class layout preference task? Understanding the failure modes could inform better architectures for spatial reasoning.
2. What is the contribution of each pipeline stage to dataset quality? Ablating stages would identify essential components and streamline future dataset creation.
3. Is the 4-class annotation scheme optimal for training layout preference models? Comparing binary, continuous, and 4-class labeling could reveal the most reliable and efficient scheme.

## Limitations
- The exact prompts and hyperparameters for pipeline stages are undisclosed, hindering exact replication.
- No ablation study quantifies the impact of each curation stage.
- The dataset may overfit to the Crello style distribution, potentially limiting generalization to other design domains.

## Confidence
- Dataset pipeline quality: Medium (ARI ~0.69 supports grouping but full impact is inferred)
- 4-class annotation utility: Medium (intuitive but lacks corpus precedent and risk of annotator misuse)
- Downstream gains: Medium-High (measured improvements, but OOD tests are limited)
