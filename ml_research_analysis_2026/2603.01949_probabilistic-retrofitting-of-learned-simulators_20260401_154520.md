# Probabilistic Retrofitting of Learned Simulators

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.01949
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01949
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Retrofitted deterministic simulators achieve 20–54% lower CRPS and up to 30% better VRMSE with minimal code changes, and support test-time ensemble scaling.

## Executive Summary
Physical systems described by partial differential equations (PDEs) exhibit inherent uncertainty, yet state-of-the-art simulators are typically deterministic. This paper introduces a method to convert existing deterministic neural simulators into probabilistic models by injecting learned noise via conditional layer normalization and training with the Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS). The approach is architecture-agnostic and requires only minimal modifications to the backbone. Evaluated on three model families (Walrus, Lola, Poseidon) across five 2D PDE datasets, retrofitting consistently outperforms compute-matched deterministic fine-tuning, reducing rollout CRPS by 20–54% and improving variance-normalized RMSE by up to 30%. Gains also extend to a PDE foundation model (HalfWalrus), with up to 40% CRPS and 15% VRMSE improvements. The method furthermore enables test-time scaling: increasing ensemble size monotonically enhances accuracy without retraining.

## Method Summary
The core technique is to take a deterministic neural PDE simulator and add a conditional layer normalization module that scales and shifts activations based on a learned noise vector. During training, noise samples are drawn from a simple distribution (e.g., standard Gaussian), and the model is optimized using CRPS, which evaluates the quality of full predictive distributions rather than point estimates. This transformation preserves the original backbone’s architecture and computational graph, requiring only the insertion of the noise injection layer and a change in loss function. The resulting model outputs a distribution (e.g., via finite ensemble or analytic parameterization) that captures epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty. At inference, predictions can be ensembled at test time, with performance improving as more samples are used.

## Key Results
- Rollout CRPS reduced by 20–54% and variance-normalized RMSE improved by up to 30% compared to deterministic fine-tuning on five 2D PDE datasets.
- On the HalfWalrus foundation model, retrofitting yields up to 40% lower CRPS and 15% better VRMSE.
- Test-time ensemble scaling shows monotonic accuracy gains with increasing ensemble size, without additional training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Conditional layer normalization injects structured stochasticity that allows the deterministic backbone to learn a family of outputs conditioned on noise, effectively modeling a predictive distribution. CRPS acts as a proper scoring rule that simultaneously penalizes bias and variance mismatch, guiding the network to produce calibrated uncertainties. The injection is done per-feature and conditioned on global statistics, which maintains the backbone’s feature extraction capabilities while introducing flexibility. This combination yields accurate mean predictions and well-calibrated variances, as evidenced by the improvements on both CRPS and VRMSE.

## Foundational Learning
The method builds on several key concepts:
- **Conditional Layer Normalization**: A variant of layer normalization where scale and shift parameters are dynamically computed from an input conditioning variable (here, noise). *Why needed*: To inject stochasticity in a controlled manner without altering the backbone. *Quick check*: Verify that applying conditional LN to a deterministic model allows output variation across noise samples.
- **Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS)**: A loss function that measures the quality of a probabilistic forecast by comparing its cumulative distribution function to that of the observation. *Why needed*: Proper scoring rule for training full distributions, not just point estimates. *Quick check*: CRPS should be lower than MSE when predictive variance is well-calibrated.
- **Probabilistic Retrofitting**: The process of adapting a deterministic model into a probabilistic one with minimal changes. *Why needed*: Leverages existing pretrained simulators and avoids training from scratch. *Quick check*: Retrofitted model should match or exceed deterministic baseline on point metrics while providing uncertainty.
- **Test-Time Ensemble Scaling**: Using multiple independent noise samples at inference to approximate an ensemble. *Why needed*: Improves accuracy without retraining. *Quick check*: Increasing ensemble size should monotonically reduce CRPS.

## Architecture Onboarding
To integrate probabilistic retrofitting into an existing deterministic PDE simulator:
- **Component map**: Deterministic Backbone → Conditional Layer Normalization (with noise input) → Probabilistic Head (e.g., distribution parameters) → CRPS Loss.
- **Critical path**: During training, for each batch, draw noise, pass through backbone with conditional LN, compute distribution output, evaluate CRPS against target, backpropagate. At inference, sample multiple noise instances to form an ensemble.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Minimal code changes vs. added hyperparameters (noise scale, conditioning dimension); CRPS provides distributional training but may require more compute; ensemble scaling improves accuracy but increases inference cost.
- **Failure signatures**: (1) Predictive variance collapses to near-zero, indicating insufficient noise injection or loss weighting; (2) High training instability, suggesting inappropriate noise magnitude; (3) No improvement over deterministic baseline on CRPS, implying misalignment between CRPS and task or poor conditioning.
- **First three experiments**:
  1. Train a retrofitted model with a small ensemble size (e.g., 5) and compare CRPS and VRMSE against the deterministic fine-tuned baseline on a validation set.
  2. Ablate the conditional LN (remove conditioning) to confirm its necessity for uncertainty learning.
  3. Sweep over noise distribution scale (e.g., std dev) to find the range that yields lowest validation CRPS.

## Limitations
- Scope restricted to 2D PDEs and three model families; generalization to 3D or other physics domains remains uncertain.
- Baselines only compare to compute-matched deterministic fine-tuning; lacking evaluation against other probabilistic methods (e.g., deep ensembles, Bayesian NNs).
- Training stability, hyperparameter sensitivity, and potential biases of the noise injection mechanism are not thoroughly examined.

## Confidence
- 20–54% CRPS reduction on 2D PDEs: **High** (directly measured on multiple datasets)
- Up to 30% VRMSE improvement on 2D PDEs: **High**
- Up to 40% CRPS gain on HalfWalrus: **High**
- Test-time ensemble scaling monotonicity (tested sizes): **High**; extrapolation to larger ensembles: **Medium**
- Architecture agnosticism (across backbones): **Medium** (validated on three families but not exhaustive)
- Broader domain applicability (3D, non-PDE): **Low** (no evidence)

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain generalization**: Apply the retrofit method to 3D PDE datasets (e.g., turbulent Navier-Stokes) and non-PDE physical systems (e.g., robotic control) to evaluate robustness.
2. **Comprehensive probabilistic benchmarking**: Compare retrofitted models against state-of-the-art probabilistic simulators (Deep Ensembles, Bayesian NNs, diffusion-based methods) on the same 2D PDE tasks using CRPS and VRMSE.
3. **Ablation of noise injection mechanism**: Systematically vary noise distribution (Gaussian vs. learned), conditioning strategy, and noise scale; measure impact on probabilistic metrics, training stability, and convergence.
