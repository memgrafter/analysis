# Topology-Preserving Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding for Semantic Communication

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17126
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17126
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** TopoJSCC reduces topological distortion by 4-5× and improves PSNR by up to +2.1 dB at low SNR/bandwidth, while matching or exceeding baseline pixel fidelity.

## Executive Summary
Deep joint source-channel coding (DeepJSCC) enables end-to-end image transmission over wireless channels but focuses primarily on pixel-wise fidelity, neglecting global topological structure such as connectivity or loops. This leads to topological distortions in reconstructed images, particularly under low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and limited bandwidth. TopoJSCC addresses this by integrating two persistent-homology regularizers into DeepJSCC: an image-domain loss that aligns cubical persistence diagrams of input and output, and a latent-space loss that aligns Vietoris–Rips diagrams of encoder features before and after the channel. Experiments on Omniglot and DeepGlobe Road datasets under AWGN and Rayleigh fading demonstrate that TopoJSCC achieves a Pareto improvement—significant reduction in topological error (e.g., ~4-5× lower Wasserstein distance at 0 dB SNR) alongside higher PSNR, with gains of +2.1 dB and 77% lower WD at SNR=0 dB and bandwidth ratio ρ=0.4 compared to MSE-only DeepJSCC.

## Method Summary
TopoJSCC extends the standard DeepJSCC autoencoder (convolutional encoder, differentiable channel, transposed-convolution decoder) with two topological losses. The **image-domain loss** builds a cubical complex from the pixel grid and applies a superlevel-set filtration to compute 0- and 1-dimensional persistent homology diagrams for both input and reconstructed images; the 2-Wasserstein distance between diagrams penalizes topological mismatch. The **latent-space loss** treats a mini-batch of encoder outputs as a point cloud in ℝ²ᵏ, constructs a Vietoris–Rips filtration, and penalizes the 2-Wasserstein distance between the diagrams obtained before and after the channel, encouraging a channel-robust latent manifold. Training combines these with the pixel-wise MSE in a weighted sum (λ_img = 1e-4, λ_lat = 1e-5) and anneals the weights. Random SNR sampling per batch ensures robustness across channel conditions without retraining. This design yields substantial topological preservation and PSNR gains, especially in low-SNR and low-bandwidth regimes.

## Key Results
- TopoJSCC reduces topological distortion (Wasserstein distance) by ~4-5× compared to DeepJSCC at 0 dB SNR.
- At SNR=0 dB and bandwidth ratio ρ=0.4, TopoJSCC achieves +2.1 dB PSNR and 77% lower WD than MSE-only DeepJSCC.
- Improvements are most pronounced in low-SNR and low-bandwidth regimes, while maintaining equal or higher PSNR across conditions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Adding an image-domain persistent homology loss preserves the connectivity and loop structure of the reconstructed image.
- **Mechanism:** The mechanism constructs a cubical complex from the pixel grid and applies a superlevel-set filtration based on image intensities. Persistent homology (PH) extracts 0- and 1-dimensional topological features (connected components and loops) as persistence diagrams. The loss penalizes the 2-Wasserstein distance between the diagrams of the input and reconstructed images, directly encouraging the decoder to maintain birth-death pairs of topological features.
- **Core assumption:** The persistence diagrams computed from cubical complexes on superlevel-set filtrations are sensitive and specific enough to capture the relevant global structure (e.g., road connectivity) that matters for downstream tasks, and that minimizing the diagram distance translates to preserving that structure.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "integrate persistent-homology regularizers... penalizing Wasserstein distances between cubical persistence diagrams of input and reconstructed images."
  - [section III.A] Details the cubical complex construction, superlevel-set filtration, and Wasserstein distance computation for the image-domain loss.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers focus on standard DeepJSCC or perceptual quality; no direct evidence on using PH for topology in JSCC, so this mechanism's novelty is inferred from the paper's claims.
- **Break condition:** If the chosen filtration (e.g., threshold sequence) is poorly matched to the image content, or if the PH computation introduces excessive noise/instability, the gradient signal may mislead training, causing the network to prioritize spurious topological features over meaningful ones.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Adding a latent-space persistent homology loss creates a channel-robust latent representation whose global manifold structure (clusters, branches) is invariant to noise.
- **Mechanism:** For a mini-batch, the encoder outputs are treated as a point cloud in ℝ²ᵏ. A Vietoris–Rips filtration is built using Euclidean distance. PH on this filtration yields diagrams for the "clean" and "noisy" (after channel layer) latent sets. The loss penalizes the Wasserstein distance between these diagrams. This encourages the encoder to map inputs to a latent space where the global shape (homology) of the batch distribution is stable under the channel's stochastic perturbation.
- **Core assumption:** The batch-level latent point cloud's Vietoris–Rips homology at dimensions 0 and 1 is a meaningful proxy for the latent manifold's topology that should be preserved across the channel, and that enforcing consistency at this batch level percolates to individual sample robustness.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "penalizing distance between Vietoris–Rips persistence diagrams of encoder feature sets before and after the channel to encourage a robust latent manifold."
  - [section III.B] Exactly defines the point cloud construction from a batch, the Rips filtration, and the latent loss.
  - [corpus] Weak; corpus neighbors do not address latent-space topological regularization in JSCC.
- **Break condition:** If the batch size is too small, the Rips complex may not capture meaningful topology (e.g., insufficient points to form 1-cycles). The latent loss might then be noisy or degenerate, dominating gradients unhelpfully.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The combined end-to-end training objective (MSE + weighted topological losses with annealing) achieves a Pareto improvement in PSNR and topological fidelity, especially in low-SNR/low-bandwidth regimes.
- **Mechanism:** The total loss is a weighted sum of pixel-wise MSE, image-domain PH loss, and latent-space PH loss. An annealing schedule gradually increases the PH loss weights, allowing the network to first learn a coarse reconstruction (guided by MSE) and then refine topology. The stochastic SNR sampling during training promotes SNR-robustness without retraining.
- **Core assumption:** (1) MSE provides a stable initial optimization landscape; (2) topological improvements can be "fine-tuned" onto a good MSE solution without collapse; (3) the relative weighting (λ_img, λ_lat) can be tuned to balance the competing objectives; (4) annealing prevents early training instability from topological gradients.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section III.D] Describes the two-stage hyperparameter setting and annealing schedule λ(t) = λ(1 - e^(-t/T)).
  - [section IV.A] Details the training protocol: random SNR sampling per batch from {0,5,10,15,20} dB, Adam optimizer, early stopping.
  - [section IV.B, Table I] Ablation shows combining both losses yields the best WD and PSNR, and annealing is implied by the training procedure.
- **Break condition:** If the annealing schedule is too aggressive (λ grows too fast), topological gradients may disrupt MSE convergence early. If the initial λ values are set incorrectly (too high), training may diverge or converge to a poor local optimum with bad PSNR.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept:** Persistent Homology (PH)
  - **Why needed here:** Provides a mathematically rigorous, multi-scale descriptor of connectivity and loops in data. It converts geometric/topological structure into a multi-set of points (persistence diagram) that can be compared with a distance (Wasserstein) and differentiated through.
  - **Quick check:** How does the choice of filtration (superlevel-set vs. Vietoris-Rips) change what topological features are emphasized?
- **Concept:** Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding (DeepJSCC)
  - **Why needed here:** The entire framework is built on an end-to-end trainable encoder/decoder with a differentiable channel layer. This is the base architecture that is modified by adding topological losses. Understanding its standard form (MSE-optimized, power-constrained) is prerequisite.
  - **Quick check:** Why does DeepJSCC avoid the "cliff effect" of separation-based schemes?
- **Concept:** Wasserstein Distance for Persistence Diagrams
  - **Why needed here:** It is the metric used in both topological losses to quantify dissimilarity between diagrams. Its aggregation property (vs. bottleneck) makes it suitable as a smooth regularizer.
  - **Quick check:** Why pair points to the diagonal in the Wasserstein calculation for diagrams?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
- Encoder: 5 Conv layers (5x5, strides 2,2,1,1,1) with PReLU, outputs 2k real vector → power-normalized → k complex symbols.
- Channel Layer: Differentiable; for AWGN adds CN(0, N0I) noise; for Rayleigh multiplies by h~CN(0,1) then adds AWGN. Real/imaginary concatenation for decoder.
- Decoder: 5 Transpose-Conv layers mirroring encoder, PReLU except last layer, final sigmoid.
- PH Loss Modules: 
  - *Image-Domain:* Cubical complex on pixel grid with superlevel-set filtration → persistence diagrams (dims 0,1) → 2-Wasserstein.
  - *Latent-Domain:* Rips complex on batch of encoder outputs (before channel) and channel outputs (after) → diagrams → 2-Wasserstein.
- Loss Aggregator: L = avg(MSE) + λ_img * L_img_top + λ_lat * L_lat_top, with λ annealing.

**Critical path:** Input image → Encoder → Channel → Decoder → Reconstructed image. Meanwhile, (Input, Reconstructed) → Image-PH loss. (Encoder outputs batch, Channel outputs batch) → Latent-PH loss. Gradients flow from all losses back through decoder and encoder.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- λ_img vs λ_lat: paper finds λ_img=1e-4, λ_lat=1e-5; higher λ_lat risks gradient domination due to batch-level loss magnitude.
- Computational cost: PH computations (especially Rips on high-dim latent batch) increase training time significantly but zero inference overhead.
- Bandwidth ratio ρ: separate models trained per ρ; model size does not change, but k (channel symbols) does.

**Failure signatures:**
- Training instability/divergence: likely if λ values too high initially without proper annealing, or if PH gradient computation is buggy.
- Poor PSNR but good WD: λ_img/lat too high, over-regularizing topology at expense of pixel fidelity.
- No topological improvement: λ too low, or filtration parameters (thresholds, ε_max) not suited to dataset scale.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Reproduce Baseline DeepJSCC:** Train the same CNN autoencoder with MSE loss only on one dataset (e.g., Omniglot) at ρ=0.4, SNR=0 dB. Verify PSNR matches paper's baseline (~22.1 dB). This validates the base architecture and channel implementation.
2. **Ablation - Image-Domain Loss Only:** Add only L_img_top with λ_img=1e-4. Expect WD reduction (~132 vs 415) and modest PSNR gain (~22.9 vs 22.1). Confirms primary contribution comes from image-domain regularization.
3. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity Sweep:** For fixed ρ=0.4, SNR=0 dB, train with λ_lat ∈ {1e-6, 1e-5, 1e-4} and λ_img fixed at 1e-4. Observe PSNR/WD tradeoff. Expected: λ_lat=1e-5 gives best balance (Table I: 23.2 dB, WD 94). Identifies correct scale for latent loss weight.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Downstream task performance:** While TopoJSCC shows improved topological fidelity and PSNR, the paper does not evaluate whether these improvements translate to better performance on downstream vision tasks (e.g., object detection, segmentation). This is crucial because semantic communication ultimately aims to preserve task-relevant features, not just geometric structure. Without such evaluation, the practical benefit of topology preservation remains uncertain.
- **Per-sample latent regularization:** The latent-space topological loss operates on mini-batch point clouds, making it unsuitable for single-image inference. Decoupling this regularization from batch aggregation would enable robust per-sample transmission, a necessary step for real-world deployment where images are transmitted individually.
- **Scalability to high-resolution color images:** Experiments are restricted to low-resolution (32x32 or 64x64) single-channel images. The computational complexity of persistent homology, especially Vietoris–Rips on high-dimensional latent vectors, may become prohibitive for larger images. Moreover, it is unclear whether the observed gains generalize to color images and higher resolutions.

## Limitations
- **Persistent homology hyperparameters:** The exact settings for the cubical filtration (e.g., number of threshold levels) and the Vietoris–Rips complex (ε_max, number of steps) are not reported. These hyperparameters strongly influence the sensitivity and stability of the computed persistence diagrams. Their omission hampers reproducibility and may require careful tuning for new datasets, potentially limiting the method's out-of-the-box applicability.
- **Channel layer implementation details:** The paper's description of the differentiable channel layer is incomplete, particularly regarding the handling of real and imaginary components and any power normalization applied to the transmitted symbols. This lack of detail impedes exact replication and may lead to inconsistencies in benchmarking.
- **Annealing schedule dependence:** The training procedure uses an annealing schedule for the topological loss weights (λ_img, λ_lat) with a decay constant T. The paper does not investigate the sensitivity of the results to these hyperparameters. Consequently, the reported Pareto improvement might be specific to the chosen schedule and could degrade with different settings, raising questions about the robustness of the approach.

## Confidence
- **Image-domain topology preservation (Mechanism 1):** Medium
- **Latent-space robust manifold (Mechanism 2):** Medium
- **Combined training Pareto improvement (Mechanism 3):** High

## Next Checks
1. **Filtration resolution sweep:** Vary the number of superlevel-set thresholds (e.g., 10, 50, 100) and Rips ε_max; assess WD/PSNR stability to identify robust ranges.
2. **Batch size effects:** Train with batch sizes {32, 64, 128} while monitoring latent PH loss magnitude and convergence to ensure batch-level topology is adequately captured.
3. **Gradient scaling audit:** Measure and compare gradient norms of MSE vs. PH losses early in training; if PH gradients dominate, adjust λ or annealing to prevent early divergence.
