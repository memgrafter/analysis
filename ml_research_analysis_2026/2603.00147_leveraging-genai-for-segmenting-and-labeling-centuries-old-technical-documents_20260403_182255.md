# Leveraging GenAI for Segmenting and Labeling Centuries-old Technical Documents

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.00147  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00147  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** Demonstrated feasibility of using SAM2 and domain-augmented GenAI for segmenting and labeling 16th‑17th century shipbuilding treatises; SAM2 outperforms Watershed in segmentation detail, while glossary‑guided ChatGPT yields richer but noisier labels.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of automatically segmenting and labeling objects in digitized centuries‑old technical documents, focusing on XVI‑XVII century shipbuilding treatises. The authors propose a modular pipeline that combines Meta’s SAM2 for zero‑shot segmentation with generative AI models (ChatGPT, RAM, BLIP) for labeling, enhanced by a curated nautical ontology (ontoShip) and multilingual glossary (glosShip). The design allows independent optimization of each stage, which is critical given the scarcity of annotated historical data.

Evaluations show that SAM2 produces more detailed and accurate masks than traditional Watershed segmentation. Domain‑augmented labeling—injecting glossary terms into ChatGPT prompts—generates more relevant and detailed labels but also increases false positives. Despite this trade‑off, the approach proves viable, with plans to scale to approximately 4,000 images across 14 treatises. Future work includes comprehensive model comparisons and designing evaluation metrics anchored in the curated domain knowledge.

## Method Summary
The method processes digitized images through a three‑stage modular pipeline. First, segmentation uses SAM2 as a zero‑shot foundation model to produce pixel‑level masks for all objects; Watershed serves as a baseline. Second, labeling applies various vision‑language models to the segmented regions: Recognize Anything Model (RAM), BLIP combined with Tag2Text and NLTK, and ChatGPT. Domain knowledge from ontoShip and glosShip is incorporated by injecting terms into prompts or by constructing specialized embeddings (e.g., via ChatGPT‑generated definitions). Third, Grounding DINO assigns the generated labels to bounding boxes derived from the segmentation masks. The pipeline’s modularity permits swapping component models without re‑training, a key advantage for data‑scarce historical domains.

## Key Results
- SAM2 segmentation yields significantly more detailed and accurate object masks than traditional Watershed, capturing fine components in complex technical illustrations.
- Domain‑augmented labeling (ChatGPT with glossary‑derived terms) produces more detailed and relevant labels but also increases false positives, such as mislabeling background textures as ship parts.
- The modular pipeline demonstrates feasibility for scaling to approximately 4,000 images across 14 treatises, with future work focused on comprehensive model comparisons and evaluation metric design.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** SAM2 generates more detailed and accurate object segments than traditional methods (e.g., Watershed) for these historical images.
- **Mechanism:** SAM2 is a foundation model trained on a massive, diverse dataset, enabling it to recognize a broad range of object boundaries via promptable mask generation. It uses a novel image encoder and a lightweight mask decoder to produce high-quality masks for any object in an image without task-specific training.
- **Core assumption:** Historical technical illustrations, despite their age and style, contain visual features and object structures that are sufficiently represented in SAM2's pre‑training corpus to allow for zero‑shot segmentation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "SAM2 produces more detailed and accurate segments than traditional methods like Watershed."
  - [section IV.A & Fig.5] Direct visual comparison showing SAM2 detects geometric shapes, text, and bleed‑through artifacts that Watershed misses.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers show SAM/SAM2 is actively researched for diverse segmentation tasks (camouflaged, medical, multispectral), indicating its generalizability is a key research focus. The corpus signals confirm the novelty of applying SAM2 to this specific historical domain.
- **Break condition:** Performance degrades if objects are highly stylized, abstract, or damaged in ways not encountered in modern photography datasets, or if components are smaller than SAM2's feature resolution.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Injecting domain-specific terms from a curated ontology/glossary into GenAI prompts (e.g., ChatGPT) improves labeling relevance and detail for nautical components, but increases the risk of false positives.
- **Mechanism:** The ontology (ontoShip) and glossary (glosShip) provide a controlled vocabulary and contextual definitions. When these terms are used to prompt a large language model (LLM) for image labeling or to create a specialized embedding (as in Method 4), the model's output is biased toward the domain's conceptual framework. The LLM's internal knowledge is "grounded" or "anchored" by the expert‑curated terms.
- **Core assumption:** The semantic relationships and definitions within ontoShip/glosShip are accurate, comprehensive enough for the task, and can effectively steer the LLM's open‑vocabulary predictions toward the historical nautical domain.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section V] "We hypothesize that this approach can be useful... leveraging words in definitions from a specialized glossary along with concepts and properties from an ontology."
  - [section VI] "ChatGPT with ontology/glossary‑derived terms yields more detailed labels but introduces false positives."
  - [section IV.D & Fig.7] Shows comparative results: ChatGPT with domain prompting finds more objects but mislabels items (e.g., "axes" for pulleys, "sharpened object" for a book fore‑edge).
  - [corpus] Corpus evidence is weak/absent. Neighbors focus on SAM segmentation or general image labeling, not the specific integration of domain ontologies with LLMs for historical documents.
- **Break condition:** Breaks if the glossary/ontology is incomplete (missing common objects in the images) or if the LLM over‑generalizes from the provided terms to unrelated visual elements.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A modular, decoupled pipeline (segmentation → labeling → boxing) allows for independent optimization of each stage and model swapping, which is crucial given the lack of large, unified training data for the end‑to‑end task.
- **Mechanism:** The pipeline first generates segment masks (SAM2), then generates labels (various models: RAM, BLIP, ChatGPT), and finally assigns labels to bounding boxes (Grounding DINO). The segmentation output (masks) is stored and applied independently, meaning the sequence of model applications can be changed without affecting the core segmentation logic.
- **Core assumption:** The quality of the final labeling is not inherently bottlenecked by the order or integration of the segmentation and labeling steps for this specific data type; the segment masks provide sufficient spatial information for subsequent labeling stages.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section IV.B] Explicitly describes the three‑step pipeline and multiple "Method" variations that swap models in step two (labeling).
  - [section IV.B] "Regardless of when the segmentation is done, the results of this step have no impact on the subsequent steps, because the segment masks are stored and then applied to the image."
  - [section VI] Notes they will test combinations like "ChatGPT caption → Florence‑2 grounding," demonstrating the modular design.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like "MATT for machine‑driven image labeling utilizing SAM" also use SAM as a first‑stage segmenter, supporting the viability of this modular pattern.
- **Break condition:** Would break if a high‑quality mask from SAM2 is required as a *necessary* input for the labeling model to work correctly (i.e., the labeling model is not truly open‑vocabulary/zero‑shot), or if spatial context from the full image lost during masking is critical for accurate labeling.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Foundation Model Zero‑Shot Generalization (e.g., SAM2, CLIP‑based models)**  
  **Why needed here:** Understanding that SAM2 and RAM/BLIP do not require fine‑tuning on the target historical domain is critical. Their power comes from pre‑training on vast modern datasets, and the engineer must understand their limitations (bias toward modern objects, photography styles).  
  **Quick check question:** What are the key architectural components (e.g., image encoder, prompt encoder, mask decoder in SAM2) that enable a single model to segment "anything" without seeing your specific data during training?

- **Concept: Knowledge Grounding in LLMs via Prompt Engineering & Structured Data**  
  **Why needed here:** The core innovation is using ontoShip/glosShip to "ground" ChatGPT's labeling. The engineer must grasp that LLMs are stateless and that providing terms, definitions, or an ontology structure in the prompt fundamentally changes the model's output distribution.  
  **Quick check question:** How does providing a list of 50 domain‑specific terms with definitions in a prompt differ in effect from simply asking the model to "label the nautical parts" in a zero‑shot manner?

- **Concept: Modular Pipeline Design for Data‑Scarce Domains**  
  **Why needed here:** The historical domain has scarce paired data (image+labels). A modular approach lets you use the best available model for each sub‑task independently and swap components as better models emerge, without re‑training an end‑to‑end system from scratch.  
  **Quick check question:** What are the explicit data formats and interface contracts (e.g., mask JSON from SAM2, list of labels from ChatGPT) that must be defined between pipeline components to make this modularity possible?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**  
  Input: Digitized page image (JPG/PNG) → Segmenter: SAM2 (primary) / Watershed (baseline) → Output: JSON with polygon masks for each segment → Label Generator: RAM / BLIP+Tag2Text+NLTK / ChatGPT / Florence‑2 (future) → Grounding: Grounding DINO → Domain Knowledge Base: ontoShip (OWL/RDF) & glosShip (multilingual CSV/JSON) → Final Output: JSON with {bbox, label, mask} for each detected object.

- **Critical path:** Image → SAM2 segmentation → (extract glossary terms) → ChatGPT prompt with terms → list of labels → Grounding DINO → final labeled bounding boxes/masks. *Note: The glossary injection point is the flexible, critical "enhancer" step.*

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  • Detail vs. Precision: More segments (SAM2) and more labels (ChatGPT+glossary) increase recall but cause noise/false positives. Watershed is cleaner but misses fine parts.  
  • Model Cost/Latency: SAM2 is compute‑intensive. ChatGPT (API) has cost/latency and rate limits. RAM/Florence‑2 are local but may have lower domain accuracy.  
  • Knowledge Source Fidelity: Using glosShip directly (simple term list) vs. generating ChatGPT definitions from it. The former is precise but brittle; the latter is flexible but hallucinatory.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  1. **SAM2 "over‑segmentation":** Single logical ship component split into many small polygon masks. Check mask granularity in output JSON.  
  2. **ChatGPT false positives:** Labels appear on margins, text blocks, or background textures. Cross‑reference label positions with segment masks—non‑overlapping or background‑only boxes indicate failure.  
  3. **Glossary mismatch:** Generated labels are common English words (e.g., "box") instead of nautical terms ("floor timber"). Indicates glossary terms were not properly injected or were overwhelmed by the model's prior.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. **Baseline Segmentation Comparison:** Run SAM2 and Watershed on the same 10 treatise images. Quantify: (a) number of segments per image, (b) smallest segment area detected, (c) visual inspection for correctness of complex composite objects. Goal: Confirm SAM2's superior granularity.  
  2. **Labeling Model A/B Test with/without Glossary:** For a fixed set of SAM2 masks, run labeling with: (a) ChatGPT zero‑shot ("label all objects"), (b) ChatGPT with glosShip term list. Compare: number of unique nautical terms, manual false positive rate on 50 sampled masks.  
  3. **Pipeline End‑to‑End Run on Single Treatise:** Execute full pipeline (SAM2 → ChatGPT+glossary → Grounding DINO) on one small treatise (~50 images). Evaluate: (a) processing time per image, (b) consistency of labels for the same component across pages. Goal: Identify bottlenecks and label consistency issues before scaling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Which vision‑language model (Florence‑2, RAM, BLIP, NLTK, Tag2Text) provides the most accurate labeling for XVI‑XVII century shipbuilding treatises?
- **Basis in paper:** We will conduct a comprehensive comparison between Florence‑2 with Recognize Anything Model (RAM), BLIP, NLTK, and Tag2Text.
- **Why unresolved:** No comprehensive comparison has been completed; preliminary results are insufficient.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Run all models on a representative sample of the 4,000 images and compare precision/recall against ontoShip/glosShip ground truth.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does incorporating ontoShip and glosShip improve labeling quality, analogous to query expansion?
- **Basis in paper:** Therefore, we will fully incorporate glosShip, ... ontoShip. We hypothesize that this approach is similar to query expansion...
- **Why unresolved:** The hypothesis remains untested; the magnitude of improvement is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct ablation studies with and without ontology/glossary augmentation, measuring changes in labeling accuracy and false positives.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What evaluation metric best measures label quality against expert‑curated terminology (ontoShip/glosShip)?
- **Basis in paper:** We envision to design and evaluate a metric to better quantify and measure the difference between labels and descriptions generated ... compared with ... glosShip and ontoShip...
- **Why unresolved:** No metric has been designed; standard metrics may not capture domain specifics.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Develop candidate metrics (e.g., semantic similarity, hierarchical matching) and validate them via correlation with expert assessments.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** Which labeling pipeline composition (e.g., ChatGPT caption + Florence‑2 grounding, or Florence‑2 caption + caption‑to‑phrase grounding) yields the best performance?
- **Basis in paper:** Finally, we would like to test different combination of labeling models in our pipeline, for example ...
- **Why unresolved:** Only two preliminary pipelines were explored; optimal combination unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematically evaluate a range of model combinations on a held‑out image set, using ground truth annotations to rank performance.

## Limitations
- Prompt and parameter specifics for Method 4 (RAM + glossary embedding) are unreported, preventing exact replication.
- The composition and integration mechanism of ontoShip and glosShip are unclear (no public URI or format), limiting independent verification and extension.
- No ground truth annotations are provided; the proposed evaluation using the glossary/ontology remains untested and may introduce bias.

## Confidence
- **SAM2 superiority over Watershed:** High — backed by visual comparisons (Fig. 5) and corpus trends on SAM2’s generalizability.
- **Glossary‑augmented labeling improves detail but adds false positives:** Medium — supported by ablation discussion (Section VI) but lacks quantitative error breakdown; corpus evidence weak.
- **Modular pipeline enables independent optimization:** Medium‑Low — design is described, but no empirical test of component‑swapping flexibility or bottleneck analysis is provided.

## Next Checks
