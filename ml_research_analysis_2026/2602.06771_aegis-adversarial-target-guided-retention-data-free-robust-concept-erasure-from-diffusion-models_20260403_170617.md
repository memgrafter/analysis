# AEGIS: Adversarial Target-Guided Retention-Data-Free Robust Concept Erasure from Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.06771  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06771  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** AEGIS breaks the robustness–retention trade-off by reducing attack success rates by 5.31%–24% under strong adversarial prompts while maintaining or improving FID and CLIP scores compared to prior state-of-the-art.

## Executive Summary
AEGIS introduces a retention-data-free framework for robust concept erasure in diffusion models, addressing the trade-off between erasing target concepts and preserving unrelated content. It combines two innovations: an Adversarial Erasure Target (AET) that optimizes a safe semantic center for the erased concept to enhance robustness against adversarial prompts, and Gradient Regularization Projection (GRP) that resolves gradient conflicts between erasure and retention objectives via directional rectification and dynamic weighting. Evaluated on Stable Diffusion v1.4 for nudity, Van Gogh style, and church object concepts, AEGIS achieves superior robustness against P4D, UnlearnDiffAtk, and Ring-A-Bell attacks without sacrificing generative quality.

## Method Summary
AEGIS fine-tunes only the UNet of a frozen diffusion model (e.g., SD v1.4) without any retention data. It introduces two learnable soft tokens: one for the adversarial prompt \(c^*\) and one for the AET \(c'\). Each training step: (1) updates \(c'\) via a single sign-gradient step on an AET loss that pulls \(c'\) toward the semantic center of the erased concept; (2) similarly updates \(c^*\) to maximize erasure loss; (3) constructs a target noise prediction using \(c'\) that represents a “safe” direction; (4) computes erasure loss \(L_e = \|\epsilon_\theta(z_t,c^*) - \text{target}\|^2\) and retention loss \(L_r = \frac{1}{2}\|\theta - \theta_0\|^2\); (5) obtains gradients \(g_e = \nabla_\theta L_e\) and \(g_r = \nabla_\theta L_r\); (6) if \(g_e \cdot g_r < 0\), projects the antagonistic component of \(g_r\) via a dynamically updated weight \(\omega\) (DGR), yielding an effective gradient; (7) updates \(\theta\) with a small learning rate. The dynamic \(\omega\) starts near 0 (prioritizing erasure) and gradually increases to balance retention.

## Key Results
- AEGIS reduces attack success rates (ASR) by 5.31% to 24% under P4D, UnlearnDiffAtk, and Ring-A-Bell attacks compared to prior methods.
- It maintains or improves FID and CLIP scores relative to state-of-the-art, demonstrating effective retention of unrelated concepts.
- Ablations confirm that both AET and DGR are critical: removing AET increases ASR by up to 65.96%, while removing DGR harms retention.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Adversarial Erasure Target (AET) Improves Robustness
- **Claim:** AET improves robustness by optimizing a target embedding near the semantic center of the erased concept class, maximizing predicted-noise distances for a wide range of related prompts.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of a fixed safe prompt (e.g., empty), AET iteratively updates an embedding \(c'\) to maximize \(\|\epsilon_{\theta_0}(z_t|c') - \epsilon_\theta(z_t|c_e)\|\) while staying close to the original concept space. This pushes the erased concept’s representation away from its cluster center.
- **Core assumption:** Adversarial prompts that reactivate erased concepts lie near the semantic center of the original concept cluster in the text–image latent space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: AET “optimizes a safe semantic center for the erased concept to enhance robustness.”
  - Section §3: Figure 2 and Proposition 3.1 show that a single prompt cannot erase the entire concept class and that moving the target near the center enlarges distances for all class members.
  - Corpus: Related work (CRCE, Receler) uses lightweight erasers; AET’s iterative semantic-center targeting is novel.

### Mechanism 2: Gradient Regularization Projection (GRP) Resolves Gradient Conflict
- **Claim:** GRP resolves the gradient conflict between erasure and retention objectives, enabling retention-data-free training without sacrificing robustness.
- **Mechanism:** GRP combines (i) Parameter Regularization (PR): \(L_r = \frac{1}{2}\|\theta - \theta_0\|^2\) to preserve unrelated concepts; (ii) Directional Gradient Rectification (DGR): when \(g_e \cdot g_r < 0\), projects \(g_r\) onto the orthogonal complement of \(g_e\) using a dynamic weight \(\omega\), removing the antagonistic component.
- **Core assumption:** Conflict between erasure and retention gradients causes the robustness–retention trade-off; removing the conflicting component of \(g_r\) maintains erasure direction fidelity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: GRP “selectively projects away the destructive component of the retention update only when it opposes the erasure direction.”
  - Section §4.2: Figures 4, 5, and 10 show negative cosine similarity between \(g_e\) and \(g_r\), confirming conflict.
  - Section §4.3: Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 provide theoretical guarantees under smoothness/convexity assumptions.

### Mechanism 3: Dynamic ω Stabilizes Training
- **Claim:** The dynamic update of the DGR weight \(\omega\) stabilizes training by initially prioritizing erasure (\(\omega \approx 0\)) and gradually increasing \(\omega\) to enforce retention when gradients conflict.
- **Mechanism:** \(\omega\) is updated each epoch via a signed gradient step based on the sensitivity of \(L_e\) to \(\omega\) (via chain rule). This learns *when* to apply more retention pressure.
- **Core assumption:** Early training requires less retention pressure to allow strong erasure updates; later, retention pressure should increase to stabilize.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section §4.2: “During early fine-tuning, the influence of \(g_r\) on \(g_e\) should remain minimal… progressively increasing \(\omega\) helps strike a favorable balance.”
  - Section §5.3: Ablation (Tab. 6) shows fixing \(\omega=1\) degrades robustness (higher ASR) compared to dynamic \(\omega\).

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Predicted-Noise Distance Metrics**  
  *Why needed:* Quantifies intra-class similarity, erasure effectiveness, and adversarial vulnerability by interpreting DMs as classifiers.  
  *Quick check:* Given \(d_1 = \|\epsilon_\theta(z_T|c_e^0) - \epsilon_{\theta_0}(z_T|c_e^0)\|^2\), what does a high \(d_1\) for a related prompt indicate about erasure effectiveness?

- **Concept: Gradient Conflict**  
  *Why needed:* GRP detects when erasure (\(g_e\)) and retention (\(g_r\)) gradients oppose (\(\cos\phi < 0\)), which underlies the trade-off.  
  *Quick check:* If \(\cos\phi > 0\) between \(g_e\) and \(g_r\), what does DGR do and why?

- **Concept: Adversarial Prompt Attacks (APAs)**  
  *Why needed:* APAs like P4D, UnlearnDiffAtk, and Ring-A-Bell search for prompts near the concept center to recover erased concepts, motivating AET.  
  *Quick check:* How does UnlearnDiffAtk attempt to recover an erased concept, and why would a fixed target like ESD’s \(\tilde{c}\) fail?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Inputs (base model \(\theta_0\), erased concept \(c_e\)) → AET Generator & Adversarial Prompt Generator (produce \(c'\), \(c^*\)) → GRP Module (computes \(L_e\) with target from \(c'\), \(L_r\); yields \(g_e\), \(g_r\)) → DGR (conflict check, rectification) → Optimizer (Adam; updates \(\theta\)) → Fine-tuned UNet.
- **Critical path:** For each step: (1) update \(c'\) via sign-gradient on AET loss; (2) update \(c^*\) via sign-gradient on adversarial loss; (3) build target \(\text{tgt} = \epsilon_{\theta_0}(z_t) - (\epsilon_{\theta_0}(z_t|c') - \epsilon_{\theta_0}(z_t))\); (4) compute \(L_e = \|\epsilon_\theta(z_t,c^*) - \text{tgt}\|^2\), \(L_r = \frac{1}{2}\|\theta - \theta_0\|^2\); (5) backprop to get \(g_e\), \(g_r\); (6) if \(g_e \cdot g_r < 0\), compute adjusted \(g_r\) via \(\lambda = -\omega (g_e \cdot g_r)/(\|g_r\|^2+\varepsilon)\); (7) update \(\theta \leftarrow \theta - \alpha (g_e + \lambda g_r)\); (8) update \(\omega\) via signed gradient step.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - AET cost vs. robustness: More AET steps (K) better approximate the center but increase compute; paper uses \(K=1\).
  - PR-only vs. GRP: PR alone protects retention but conflicts with erasure; GRP adds conflict-driven rectification.
  - Static vs. dynamic \(\omega\): Fixed \(\omega=1\) over-regularizes early training, increasing ASR; dynamic \(\omega\) balances.
  - Token budget: AET uses \(m'=1\) learnable token; sufficient for optimization.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High ASR: AET failed to find robust target or \(\omega\) increased too fast.
  - Poor FID/CLIP: Over-pruning or retention failure; check if \(\omega\) reaches 1 prematurely or \(g_e/g_r\) consistently negative without rectification.
  - Training instability: Large \(L_e\) spikes indicate \(\omega\) schedule mismatch or non-smoothness violating assumptions.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation -AET:** Replace AET with fixed \(\tilde{c}\) (like AdvUnlearn). Expect higher ASR (Tab. 6: +65.96%), confirming AET’s robustness role.
  2. **Ablation -DGR:** Use PR only (fixed \(\lambda\)). Expect worse retention (FID ↑) due to gradient conflict; monitor \(\cos\phi\) distribution.
  3. **Vary \(\omega\) schedule:** Compare fixed \(\omega\) (0, 0.5, 1) vs. dynamic. Monitor ASR and FID vs. epoch; dynamic should start with lower FID early and improve robustness later as \(\omega\) rises.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Can AET generation be made more computationally efficient without sacrificing robustness?**  
   *Basis:* Limitations state future work will explore more efficient erasure targets to reduce overhead and retention degradation.  
   *Why unresolved:* AET adds ~2× training time vs. ESD; no alternative proposed.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Compare alternative target generation methods (e.g., one-step approximations, offline caches) measuring ASR, FID, and wall-clock time.

2. **Why does AEGIS' erasure effectiveness degrade on SD v2.1, and how can it be adapted to newer architectures?**  
   *Basis:* §5.2 notes sharp ASR increase on SD v2.1 and hypothesizes “more complex and entangled internal concept representations.”  
   *Why unresolved:* Hypothesis untested; no adaptation offered for OpenCLIP text encoders or different corpora.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Ablate text encoder effect (swap CLIP for OpenCLIP) while keeping UNet fixed; analyze AET/GRP gradients; design and evaluate architectural modifications.

3. **What are the convergence properties of GRP's dynamic ω update under non-convex diffusion fine-tuning?**  
   *Basis:* Theorems 4.1–4.2 assume convexity/smoothness; Appendix E.4 acknowledges non-convex UNets and untested implications for \(\omega\).  
   *Why unresolved:* No extension to non-convex settings; unclear if \(\omega\) schedule prevents harmful interference.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Prove convergence/stability under non-convex assumptions (e.g., bounded gradients, PL inequality) or empirically correlate \(\omega\) with gradient conflict, loss curvature, and robustness.

4. **How does the length of the AET prompt embedding (m′) affect the robustness-retention trade-off?**  
   *Basis:* Implementation fixes \(m'=1\) with no ablation.  
   *Why unresolved:* Single token may not capture complex concept manifolds; longer prompts may introduce extraneous semantics.  
   *Resolving evidence:* Evaluate AEGIS with varying \(m'\) (1, 4, 8) across concepts; report ASR, FID, CLIP, token norms; analyze trade-off surface.

## Limitations
- AET assumes a centralized concept cluster; fragmented or multimodal concepts may leave sub-clusters vulnerable.
- GRP’s theoretical guarantees rely on strict smoothness/convexity assumptions that may not hold in highly non-convex networks, risking over-regularization.
- A single scalar \(\omega\) manages gradient conflicts globally, which may be insufficient for layer-specific variations.
- Key hyperparameters (η, λ, token placement, β) are not fully specified; reproduction relies on heuristics.
- Evaluation limited to SD v1.4 and three concepts; generalizability to other models (e.g., SDXL) and broader concepts is unknown.
- No human evaluation beyond automatic metrics (FID, CLIP).

## Confidence
- **AET robustness:** High (ablation shows dramatic ASR increase) but Medium for generalizability due to centralization assumption.
- **GRP retention:** Medium (strong empirical conflict resolution) but theoretical conditions are strict; \(\omega\) schedule may not capture complex interactions.
- **Breaking robustness–retention trade-off:** Medium (positive results on three concepts) but lacks diverse models, concepts, and statistical significance testing.

## Next Checks
1. **Fragmented concept test:** Apply AEGIS to a concept with known sub-clusters (e.g., “painting styles” including Impressionism, Cubism). Measure ASR across sub-clusters via targeted adversarial prompts to expose AET coverage gaps.
2. **Layer-wise ω ablation:** Replace global \(\omega\) with per-layer \(\omega\) parameters and compare robustness/retention metrics across multiple random seeds to assess stability.
3. **Cross-model generalization:** Evaluate AEGIS on SD v2.1 and SDXL for the same three concepts. Significant performance drops would indicate limited generalizability and require architectural adjustments.
