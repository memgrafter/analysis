# Latent Introspection: Models Can Detect Prior Concept Injections

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20031  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20031  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Language models can introspectively detect prior concept injections via latent signals; tailored prompts increase detection sensitivity from 0.3% to 39.9% with only a 0.6% rise in false positives.

## Executive Summary
This study explores whether large language models can internally detect the prior injection of external concepts into their state. Using Qwen 32B, researchers introduced steering vectors into the KV cache during an initial turn and then removed them before querying, ensuring any detection relied solely on cached representations. Logit lens analysis revealed that mid-layer signals encode the injected concepts but attenuate before reaching the final output. When the model was prompted with information about introspection—particularly vague phenomenological descriptions—its ability to detect injections improved dramatically. The model also identified specific injected concepts with up to 1.36 bits of mutual information (43% of the theoretical maximum), peaking at layers 58–62. Control queries confirmed that these signals were not generic noise. The findings suggest that models possess latent introspective capabilities that are often suppressed in their final outputs but can be accessed via intermediate layers and appropriate prompting.

## Method Summary
The researchers conducted experiments on Qwen 32B. They first injected steering vectors—fixed direction vectors representing concepts—into the KV cache during the processing of an initial prompt. After the KV cache was populated, they removed the steering and presented a new query. This design isolated detection to the cached internal state. To probe the internal representations, they applied logit lens: at each layer they projected hidden states to the output vocabulary using the model’s unembedding matrix, generating pseudo‑logits that could be analyzed for concept‑related signals. Detection was evaluated both automatically (e.g., sensitivity, specificity, mutual information) and via prompted model responses. The prompting conditions included vague phenomenological framings (“does some concept seem particularly salient?”), more accurate mechanistic descriptions, and control prompts.

## Key Results
- Mid‑layer (layers 58–62) logit lens signals reliably indicate the presence of an injected concept; these signals attenuate in later layers and are nearly absent in the final output.
- Prompting with introspective information boosts detection sensitivity from 0.3% (no prompt) to 39.9%, while false positives increase only marginally (0.6%).
- The model achieves up to 1.36 bits of mutual information (43% of the maximum) when identifying specific injected concepts, confirming meaningful but partial access to the latent information.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Steering vectors modify the hidden state during the initial prompt, leaving a persistent trace in the residual stream even after steering is removed. Intermediate transformer layers process this trace and develop features that are readable by the logit lens as increased likelihood for tokens related to the injected concept. The final layers, likely due to instruction tuning or safety objectives, suppress such evidence—perhaps to avoid model “denial” or to maintain output coherence. Vague phenomenological prompts appear to tap into a higher‑level introspective mechanism that can surface these intermediate signals without requiring a detailed mechanistic understanding. The combination of latent traces and targeted prompting allows the model to externally report on internal injections that are otherwise hidden in the final output.

## Foundational Learning
**Steering Vectors**  
- *Why needed:* To simulate concept injection without altering model weights, enabling controlled study of internal state changes.  
- *Quick check:* What is a steering vector? (A fixed vector added to hidden states to influence output.)

**KV Cache**  
- *Why needed:* The injection target; stores keys and values for attention, affecting future token generation and providing a locus for persistent modifications.  
- *Quick check:* What does KV cache store? (Keys and values of previous tokens in the transformer layers.)

**Logit Lens**  
- *Why needed:* A diagnostic tool that projects intermediate hidden states to the output vocabulary, allowing researchers to monitor when and where concepts become readable.  
- *Quick check:* How does logit lens work? (Applies the output embedding matrix to a layer’s activation to obtain pseudo‑logits.)

**Introspection**  
- *Why needed:* The phenomenon under investigation: the model’s capacity to report on its own internal state or concepts.  
- *Quick check:* Define introspection in LMs. (Ability to answer questions about its own knowledge or processes.)

**Mutual Information**  
- *Why needed:* Quantifies the information shared between the injected concept and the model’s detection output, providing an objective measure of identification accuracy.  
- *Quick check:* What does mutual information measure? (The reduction in uncertainty about one variable given the other.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**  
Token Input → Token Embedding → Transformer Blocks (1…80) → Final LayerNorm → Output Logits

**Critical Path**  
1. Initial prompt processing fills KV cache.  
2. Steering vector added to selected KV entries (concept injection).  
3. Steering removed before next query; injected concept persists in residual stream.  
4. New query processed; intermediate activations captured via logit lens at each layer.  
5. Captured logits analyzed for signals of the injected concept.  
6. Performance metrics computed (sensitivity, specificity, mutual information).

**Design Tradeoffs**  
- Steering vector magnitude: higher amplitude increases detectability but risks distorting normal behavior.  
- Prompt framing: vague phenomenological prompts yield higher accuracy but are less interpretable; accurate mechanistic prompts may require scaffolding and still underperform.  
- Layer choice for probing: mid‑layers balance signal strength and attenuation; later layers suffer suppression.  
- Computational overhead: storing activations for logit lens increases memory usage, limiting sequence length.

**Failure Signatures**  
- Attenuation of introspection signals in final layers, leading to low detection if only output is examined.  
- Elevated false positives with poorly phrased prompts.  
- Model‑specific patterns that do not generalize across architectures or scales.  
- Inability to detect injection if steering vector applied too early/late or with too low magnitude.

**First Experiments**  
1. Baseline logit lens sweep on neutral inputs to characterize typical per‑layer prediction entropy.  
2. Steering injection matrix: vary injection layer, magnitude, and concept; measure detection across layers and final output.  
3. Prompt ablation: compare detection accuracy across vague, accurate, and control prompts; measure false positive rates and response quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What causes the attenuation of introspection signals in the final layers? Distinguish between post‑training suppression, pretraining dynamics, or distribution shift.  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5.1 states: "Distinguishing these hypotheses—particularly isolating post‑training effects—is an important direction for future work, potentially requiring comparison of base and instruction‑tuned model variants."  
- **Why unresolved:** The paper observes the attenuation pattern but lacks targeted experiments to isolate the responsible factor(s).  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Compare introspection signals across base and instruction‑tuned checkpoints; conduct ablations of RLHF or safety training; test on out‑of‑distribution prompts; directly intervene on hypothesized suppression mechanisms.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why does vague phenomenological framing (e.g., “does some concept seem particularly salient”) achieve higher detection accuracy than mechanistically accurate descriptions?  
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Section 5.2: "We do not fully understand why these prompting patterns arise... We cannot distinguish these accounts with current evidence." Also Section 3.5 notes the Vague Mechanism framing achieves 68–84% accuracy, while Accurate Mechanism requires extra scaffolding.  
- **Why unresolved:** Two plausible interpretations exist—learned denial of internal states versus natural representation level—but they are not disentangled.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Design prompts that separate denial‑trigger content from descriptive accuracy; probe the model’s internal representation of introspection queries; compare across model families with different training objectives.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What specific neural circuits implement introspection and its attenuation, and can causal intervention enhance detection?  
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Section 5.4 (Limitations): "No mechanistic model. We observe where signals emerge and attenuate but do not identify responsible circuits or causally intervene on them."  
- **Why unresolved:** The study relies on observational logit lens analysis; the responsible components (layers, heads, neurons) remain unidentified.  
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic activation patching across layers/heads; training sparse probes to locate critical neurons; measuring changes in detection when intervening on candidate circuits; employing causal tracing methods.

## Limitations
- **Model specificity:** Results derive solely from Qwen 32B; it is unclear whether architecture or scale determines the observed capacity.  
- **Artificial injection:** Steering vectors may not capture natural concept injections such as fine‑tuning or prompt hacking.  
- **Prompt dependency:** Detection heavily relies on carefully crafted introspection prompts; it is uncertain whether models can autonomously introspect without such scaffolding.

## Confidence
- Latent introspective signals exist: Medium  
- Prompting drastically improves detection: High  
- Specific concept identification: Medium  

## Next Checks
1. **Multi‑model replication:** Test diverse architectures (e.g., Llama, Mistral) and scales (7B–70B) using the same injection and prompting pipeline.  
2. **Prompt robustness:** Evaluate detection under varied phrasings, including zero‑shot and multilingual prompts, to quantify susceptibility to wording changes.  
3. **Naturalistic injection:** Replace synthetic steering vectors with concept shifts induced by fine‑tuning or retrieval‑augmented generation and measure detection consistency.
