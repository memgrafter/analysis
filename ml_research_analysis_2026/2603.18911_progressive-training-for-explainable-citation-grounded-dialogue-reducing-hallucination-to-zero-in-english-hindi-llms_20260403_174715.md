# Progressive Training for Explainable Citation-Grounded Dialogue: Reducing Hallucination to Zero in English-Hindi LLMs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.18911
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18911
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Citation-grounded SFT reduces hallucination to 0.0% for encoder-decoder models from Stage 2 onward, with Citation-F1 up to 0.980.

## Executive Summary
Knowledge-grounded dialogue systems for Indian languages typically lack explicit citations and explainability. This paper introduces XKD-Dial, a progressive four‑stage training pipeline (multilingual adaptation, English citation‑grounded SFT, bilingual SFT, GRPO alignment) applied to six models ranging from 250M to 7B parameters in English‑Hindi. After Stage 2, encoder‑decoder models achieve zero hallucination and high citation accuracy (Citation‑F1 up to 0.980). Smaller models match larger ones on English after SFT. GRPO alignment yields only marginal improvements. The staged approach prevents catastrophic forgetting while boosting Hindi performance. Explainability analyses reveal that citation grounding is learned, but decoder‑only models show a dissociation between producing correct citation format and truly grounding content in the sources.

## Method Summary
The pipeline consists of four sequential stages. Stage 1: one epoch of English‑Hindi translation training using a parallel corpus (IIT Bombay) to build basic bilingual capability. Stage 2: supervised fine‑tuning on English citation‑grounded dialogue data (a machine‑translated mix of DSTC9, FaithDial, and Wizard of Wikipedia), teaching the model to generate responses with inline citations (e.g., “According to [1] …”). Stage 3: bilingual SFT with a mixture of 40% English and 60% Hindi examples to preserve English performance while improving Hindi. Stage 4: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a composite reward (factuality, entity overlap, citation accuracy, hallucination penalty) for alignment; this stage is optional and provides only marginal gains. Models are evaluated on citation accuracy (Citation‑F1), hallucination rate (NLI‑based), lexical overlap (BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore), and factuality (FactScore). Additional explainability analyses (cross‑attention, Integrated Gradients, occlusion) verify genuine grounding.

## Key Results
- Hallucination reduced to 0.0% for encoder‑decoder models after Stage 2.
- Citation‑F1 reached 0.980; smaller models matched larger ones on English after SFT.
- GRPO alignment gave marginal gains (<0.005 metric change).
- Progressive staged training prevented catastrophic forgetting while improving Hindi performance.
- Decoder‑only models exhibited a format‑grounding dissociation: high Citation‑F1 but near‑zero occlusion‑based causal grounding.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Citation‑grounded SFT reduces hallucination by training the model to explicitly associate claims with numbered references, creating an implicit constraint that claims must be supported by provided passages.
- **Mechanism:** During Stage 2 (English Dialogue SFT), every training example contains responses with inline citations. The model learns to generate text only when a specific citation marker can be attached to a supporting passage, reducing unsupported fabrication.
- **Core assumption:** The citation format acts as a structural bottleneck that forces grounding; the model internalizes that claims require a valid [N] reference from the input knowledge.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - Abstract: “citation‑grounded SFT reduces hallucination to 0.0% for encoder‑decoder models from Stage 2 onward.”
  - Section 5.1: “all metrics jump dramatically, with hallucination dropping to exactly zero” after Stage 2 for Flan‑T5 models.
  - Section 5.5: Counterexample—LLaMA‑3.2‑1B achieves 0.0% English hallucination without generating citations, showing hallucination reduction and citation learning are separable.
- **Break condition:** The mechanism fails for small decoder‑only models (LLaMA‑3.2‑1B) where Stage 2 SFT leads to zero citations but still zero hallucination, indicating a conservative, non‑committal generation strategy rather than true grounding.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Progressive staged training prevents catastrophic forgetting of earlier capabilities (e.g., English dialogue) while sequentially adding new skills (Hindi, citations).
- **Mechanism:** Each stage uses a mixture of old and new data (e.g., Stage 3 bilingual SFT uses 40% English, 60% Hindi). English examples act as a “replay buffer” to maintain English performance while Hindi exposure drives new learning. The pipeline builds skills incrementally: multilingual adaptation → English citation‑grounded dialogue → bilingual citation‑grounded dialogue → alignment.
- **Core assumption:** Catastrophic interference in sequential fine‑tuning can be mitigated by interleaving older task examples with new ones, preserving earlier learned representations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: “progressive pipeline prevents catastrophic forgetting while improving Hindi performance.”
  - Section 5.3: “Stage 1 preserves English. All English metrics remain stable (±0.002) after multilingual adaptation” and “Base overtakes Large after Stage 3” due to Hindi gains.
  - Section 5.5: Exception—LLaMA‑3.2‑1B suffers catastrophic forgetting of English citation format after Stage 2, showing the mechanism is not universal.
- **Break condition:** The replay buffer ratio (α=0.4 English in Stage 3) may be insufficient for some model architectures (e.g., small decoder‑only) or if earlier stages overwrite critical pathways irreversibly.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Decoder‑only models can learn citation *format* without *causal grounding*—producing [N] markers without genuinely conditioning content on the cited source.
- **Mechanism:** Decoder‑only architectures process the full prompt (query + knowledge) as a single causal sequence via self‑attention. Without a dedicated cross‑attention sublayer, the model may learn to insert citation markers based on positional heuristics or conversational context rather than through a direct, differentiable pathway to source tokens.
- **Core assumption:** Encoder‑decoder cross‑attention provides a natural, interpretable mechanism for source conditioning (each output token can attend to input tokens). Decoder‑only models must implicitly learn retrieval and grounding within self‑attention, which may dissociate format from substance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Section 5.8: Occlusion analysis shows Gemma‑2‑2B and Mistral‑7B drop to 0.000 causal grounding post‑training despite Citation‑F1 up to 0.903. “This decoder‑only format‑grounding dissociation contrasts sharply with encoder‑decoder models.”
  - Section 5.5: LLaMA‑3.2‑1B's Hindi outputs show correct citation markers but English outputs are generic and citation‑free, showing selective learning.
  - Abstract: “decoder‑only models showing format‑grounding dissociation.”
- **Break condition:** The dissociation may be inherent to causal LM fine‑tuning on structured tasks; alleviating it requires architectural modifications (e.g., explicit retrieval heads) or training objectives that penalize unattributed citations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multilingual Adaptation via Translation**  
  *Why needed here:* Builds basic English‑Hindi bilingual representations, especially critical for models with limited pretraining exposure to Hindi (e.g., Flan‑T5 variants).  
  *Quick check:* Why is a single epoch of translation training used instead of many, and what might happen if we over‑train on translation?

- **Citation‑Grounded Supervised Fine‑Tuning (SFT)**  
  *Why needed here:* Teaches the model the specific output format (inline [N] citations) and the behavior of conditioning claims on provided knowledge passages. This is the core anti‑hallucination training signal.  
  *Quick check:* How does the structured prompt format (“Respond using the knowledge above with citations [1], [2], etc.”) constrain the model’s generation, and why is this format language‑agnostic?

- **Explainability Analysis (Cross‑attention, Integrated Gradients, Occlusion)**  
  *Why needed here:* Verifies that high Citation‑F1 scores correspond to genuine grounding, not just format mimicry. Detects architecture‑specific failure modes like decoder‑only format‑grounding dissociation.  
  *Quick check:* What does a high occlusion causal grounding score (e.g., 0.909 for Flan‑T5‑Large) tell us that a high Citation‑F1 score alone does not?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Stage 1 Adaptor → Stage 2 SFT Engine → Stage 3 Bilingual SFT Engine → Stage 4 GRPO Aligner → Explainability Module  

Critical path: The four stages must be run sequentially. Skipping Stage 1 risks poor Hindi initialization. Skipping Stage 2 means the model never learns the citation format in a high‑resource setting before cross‑lingual transfer. Stage 3 is essential for Hindi competence. Stage 4 is optional but may refine alignment.

Design tradeoffs:
- Learning rate vs. collapse: Aggressive LR (2e‑5) caused generation collapse in Flan‑T5‑XL (3B); smaller models tolerate higher LR (5e‑5 for 250M). Tune LR proportionally to model size/architecture.
- SFT vs. GRPO: GRPO provided marginal gains here (<0.005 metric change). For highly structured tasks, extensive SFT may suffice; RL alignment may only help with subtle preference tuning.
- Encoder‑decoder vs. decoder‑only: Encoder‑decoder models (Flan‑T5) show more reliable grounding via cross‑attention. Decoder‑only models (LLaMA, Gemma, Mistral) can achieve high Citation‑F1 but risk format‑grounding dissociation.

Failure signatures:
- **Generation collapse:** Model outputs empty strings at inference. Detected via eval loss look okay but generation metrics are zero. Flan‑T5‑XL Stage 2. *Recovery:* Continue training with bilingual data (Stage 3).
- **Language‑selective citation failure:** Model learns citations in one language (Hindi) but not another (English) despite shared training. LLaMA‑3.2‑1B Stage 2–4. *Mitigation:* Ensure balanced language mixing; consider separate language‑specific SFT phases.
- **Format‑grounding dissociation (decoder‑only):** High Citation‑F1 but 0.000 occlusion grounding. Gemma‑2‑2B and Mistral‑7B after fine‑tuning. *Detection:* Must run occlusion test; Citation‑F1 alone is insufficient.

First 3 experiments:
1. **Stage ablation:** Train a model with only Stage 2 (English SFT). Expected: High English citation performance, near‑zero Hindi performance, low hallucination (encoder‑decoder). Validates Stage 2 as the core anti‑hallucination stage.
2. **Hindi transfer test:** Take a Stage 2 checkpoint (English‑only SFT) and evaluate on Hindi test set without further training. Expected: Moderate Hindi Citation‑F1 (e.g., Base: 0.718) showing cross‑lingual transfer of citation format.
3. **Explainability sanity check:** For a Stage 3 encoder‑decoder model, run occlusion on 10 test examples. Expected: >0.85 causal grounding. For a decoder‑only model, expect ~0.00 unless architecture‑specific interventions are used. Confirms grounding mechanism.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Does GRPO alignment provide statistically significant improvements over SFT for citation‑grounded dialogue when hyperparameters are systematically optimized? The paper used fixed hyperparameters and found marginal gains; a systematic study is needed.
- Why do decoder‑only models exhibit a dissociation between citation format accuracy and causal grounding, and can this be eliminated through architectural modifications or training objectives? The pattern is observed but not explained.
- What factors cause some decoder‑only models (e.g., LLaMA‑3.2‑1B) to selectively fail at citation learning in English while succeeding in Hindi, and how can such failures be prevented? The authors hypothesize about citation phrase salience but do not test.
- Which automatic evaluation metrics most reliably predict human judgments of quality and grounding for morphologically rich languages like Hindi in knowledge‑grounded dialogue? Hindi BLEU scores were near‑zero despite high ROUGE‑1 and BERTScore, indicating metric limitations.

## Limitations
- Exact dataset construction (machine‑translated 150K EN/HI dialogues, IndicTrans2 version, preprocessing scripts) is not open‑sourced, risking data mismatch in reproduction.
- GRPO implementation details (reward components, group generation, KL reference policy) are underspecified, preventing precise replication of marginal gains.
- Decoder‑only format‑grounding dissociation may be inherent to architecture; Citation‑F1 alone is insufficient to verify true grounding.

## Confidence
- Encoder‑decoder zero hallucination post‑SFT: High
- Progressive training prevents catastrophic forgetting: Medium
- GRPO yields marginal gains: Low
