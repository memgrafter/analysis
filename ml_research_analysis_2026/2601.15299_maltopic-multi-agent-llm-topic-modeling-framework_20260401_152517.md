# MALTopic: Multi-Agent LLM Topic Modeling Framework

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.15299  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15299  
- **Reference count:** 0  
- **Primary result:** MALTopic, a three-agent LLM framework, significantly outperforms LDA and BERTopic on survey data by integrating structured responses, yielding higher coherence/diversity and human-interpretable topics with nuanced distinctions.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MALTopic, a framework that uses three cooperating LLM agents to perform topic modeling on survey data. Unlike traditional methods that ignore structured responses (e.g., job title) and produce abstract topics, MALTopic first enriches free-text answers by merging them with a respondent's structured data. A batched topic modeling agent then identifies latent themes from these enriched texts, and a final deduplication agent refines the topic set. Evaluated on a professional survey (N=202), MALTopic achieves superior quantitative metrics (word coherence, diversity, lower inter-topic similarity) and generates qualitatively richer, more interpretable topics that capture subtle demographic variations missed by baselines.

## Method Summary
MALTopic decomposes topic modeling into a pipeline of three single-shot LLM agents. First, an **Enrichment Agent** processes each survey response, combining the free-text answer with the respondent's structured fields (e.g., job title, experience) into a new, enriched text document. Second, a **Topic Modeling Agent** receives batches of these enriched texts (constrained by the LLM's context window) and is prompted to output a list of `M` exhaustive, non-overlapping topics, each with a name, description, respondent profile, and representative keywords. Third, a **Deduplication Agent** takes the initial topic list and merges semantically similar topics to reduce redundancy. The pipeline uses GPT-4o-mini with low temperature (0.2) for deterministic output.

## Key Results
- **Quantitative:** MALTopic achieves higher word coherence (PMI-based) and word diversity (unique word ratio), and lower average inter-topic similarity (cosine) compared to LDA and BERTopic.
- **Qualitative:** Produces human-readable topic outputs including names, descriptions, and respondent profiles, revealing nuanced distinctions (e.g., student vs. professional AI concerns) that traditional methods conflate.
- **General:** Demonstrates that integrating structured survey metadata via LLM agents yields more contextually relevant and interpretable topics for survey analysis.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Enrichment with Structured Context
- **Claim:** Integrating structured survey data (e.g., job title) into free-text responses via an enrichment agent produces topics with greater contextual relevance and interpretability than using free-text alone.
- **Mechanism:** The Enrichment agent uses an LLM to synthesize each respondent's structured fields and free-text answer into a single enriched document. This explicitly embeds the respondent's profile into the semantic content, allowing downstream analysis to surface themes tied to specific demographic or professional groups.
- **Evidence anchors:** Paper states the agent "leverages structured data to enhance textual responses" and "embeds both linguistic and respondent-specific context" (Sec. III.B).
- **Break condition:** If structured data is sparse, irrelevant, or has too many unique categories, enrichment may introduce noise or create unmanageably long prompts, degrading topic quality.

### Mechanism 2: Batch-Based Global Topic Modeling
- **Claim:** Having a dedicated Topic Modeling agent that processes batches of enriched responses allows the LLM to identify latent, non-overlapping themes across the entire corpus in a single pass, leading to higher topic diversity and coherence.
- **Mechanism:** The agent receives a batch of `N` enriched texts within the model's context limit and is instructed to output `M` exhaustive, distinct topics. By analyzing a batch concurrently, the LLM leverages its broad context to compare and contrast all responses simultaneously, avoiding the myopic topic formation of incremental clustering.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section III.B describes batch processing to "concurrently analyze a batch of N enriched responses" yielding "exhaustive, non-overlapping, and distinct topics." Section V.A shows higher diversity and lower inter-topic similarity vs. baselines.
- **Break condition:** Batch size must balance global context (larger) against token limits (smaller). Too large causes truncation; too small loses corpus-wide context, yielding redundant or fragmented topics.

### Mechanism 3: LLM-Driven Deduplication
- **Claim:** A final Deduplication agent refines the initial topic set by merging semantically similar topics, reducing redundancy and improving clarity, which is necessary when the initial topic modeling agent produces overlapping themes due to batch processing constraints.
- **Mechanism:** The agent receives the initial list of topics (with names, descriptions, keywords) and uses an LLM to identify and consolidate near-duplicate topics based on semantic similarity of their metadata.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "a deduplication agent refines the results." Section III.B notes its necessity for large surveys exceeding Agent 2's capacity. Section V.B shows qualitative examples where MALTopic separates nuanced themes (e.g., "Job Displacement" vs. "Job Market Dynamics") that baselines conflate.
- **Break condition:** An aggressive merging threshold may collapse genuinely distinct but superficially similar topics; a lenient threshold leaves redundant topics.

## Architecture Onboarding
Input (Survey DataFrame) -> Enrichment Agent -> Enriched Texts -> Topic Modeling Agent -> Initial Topic List -> Deduplication Agent -> Final Topics

**Critical path:** Enrichment is foundational; errors propagate. Deduplication is conditional on initial topic count/overlap.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Token Limit vs. Batch Size:** Larger batches improve global context but risk truncation; smaller batches may yield fragmented topics.
- **Prompt Specificity vs. Flexibility:** Overly specific prompts (e.g., "exactly 10 topics") may constrain thematic discovery; vague prompts may yield inconsistent or excessive topics.
- **LLM Cost vs. Performance:** More capable models (GPT-4o vs. mini) may improve quality but at 4-5x higher cost.

**Failure signatures:**
- **Low coherence / high similarity:** Likely failed enrichment or ineffective topic modeling. Check enriched text quality.
- **Excessive topics (>2x expected):** Agent 2 ignored "non-overlapping" instruction, or Agent 3 was not triggered/ineffective.
- **Topics ignore structured data:** Enrichment prompt did not properly incorporate fields.
- **High cost / slow runtime:** Batch size too large causing multiple LLM calls for Agent 2.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Baseline Enrichment Test:** Run Agent 1 on 5 sample rows. Manually inspect if enriched text fluently combines structured fields and free-text without hallucination/omission. Goal: Validate core data fusion.
2. **Batch Size Sweep:** For Agent 2, test batch sizes of 10, 50, 100 (or max allowed). Measure: final topic count, coherence/diversity metrics, token usage/cost. Goal: Find optimal batch for dataset size and context window.
3. **Deduplication Threshold Probe:** If Agent 2 outputs >15 topics, run Agent 3 with prompts for "aggressive," "moderate," and "conservative" merging. Evaluate final topic distinctness and preservation of key nuanced themes. Goal: Calibrate deduplication sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How can agents be expanded to integrate domain-specific context or translate across data modalities?
  - **Basis:** Future Work states agents' functionality should extend beyond augmenting free-text responses to tasks like industry-specific context and modality translations.
  - **Why unresolved:** These are conceptual proposals without implementation details or validation.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Prototypes on non-textual or specialized-domain datasets showing improved topic quality over base MALTopic.
- **Open Question 2:** What specific performance metrics and feedback mechanisms should be incorporated to minimize LLM hallucinations?
  - **Basis:** Authors identify need for "comprehensive performance metrics and robust feedback mechanisms" for each agent to reduce hallucinations.
  - **Why unresolved:** No concrete metrics (e.g., confidence scores, fact-checking) or feedback loop designs are provided.
  - **Resolution evidence:** System defining/implementing such metrics/architectures, quantifying hallucination rates and topic stability vs. single-pass approach.
- **Open Question 3:** How can model bias be mitigated to ensure generalizability across diverse domains and populations?
  - **Basis:** Future Work calls model bias "a significant impediment" and notes a biased LLM will face "substantial challenges."
  - **Why unresolved:** Study uses one general model on one survey; no bias audit or sensitivity to domain/LLM choice.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Cross-domain evaluations (e.g., non-tech industries, different cultures, low-resource languages) using multiple LLMs, with fairness and topic shift analysis.
- **Open Question 4:** What is the precise algorithmic design and thresholding logic of the Topic Deduplication Agent, and how is its optimal configuration determined?
  - **Basis (inferred):** Deduplication is described as necessary for large surveys but its method, parameters, and evaluation criteria are not detailed.
  - **Why unresolved:** Methodology section lacks implementation specifics; no ablation studies or sensitivity analysis.
  - **Resolution evidence:** Detailed algorithm specification coupled with experiments varying thresholds (e.g., similarity cutoffs) and comparing metrics against no-deduplication or alternative strategies.

## Limitations
- **Prompt specifics unknown:** Exact prompts for Agents 2 & 3 are not in the paper; must be retrieved from the linked repository [3].
- **Dataset scale & generalizability:** Validation uses a single small survey (N=202). Performance on larger/more diverse surveys is unproven.
- **Deduplication logic opaque:** Agent 3's merging criteria and thresholds are underspecified, limiting reproducibility and optimization.
- **Cost/benchmark context:** Trade-offs vs. other models (larger LLMs, open-source) are not explored; baselines used concatenated text without their own enrichment.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (Enrichment efficacy):** Medium-High. Clearly described and qualitatively supported, but risk of noise from poor LLM synthesis is noted; not stress-tested on varied structured data quality.
- **Mechanism 2 (Batch topic modeling):** Medium. Quantitative improvements reported, but the assumption that a single-pass batch yields "globally optimal" topics is not validated against iterative/multi-batch alternatives.
- **Mechanism 3 (Deduplication):** Low. Underspecified; qualitative improvements shown, but cannot assess if they stem from the deduplication algorithm or richer prior outputs.
