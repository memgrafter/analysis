# BidirLM: From Text to Omnimodal Bidirectional Encoders by Adapting and Composing Causal LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.02045  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02045  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** BidirLM converts causal LLMs into high-quality bidirectional encoders via biphasic adaptation and weight merging, achieving state-of-the-art performance across text, vision, and audio with fewer parameters.

## Executive Summary
BidirLM introduces a systematic framework to transform decoder-only language models into versatile bidirectional encoders for multiple modalities. The core method employs a biphasic training regimen: first, Masked Next-Token Prediction (MNTP) reconfigures the model’s attention to support full bidirectional context; second, contrastive learning (InfoNCE) refines representations for generic embedding tasks. To address catastrophic forgetting when scaling adaptation on out-of-distribution data, the approach linearly merges the adapted model with the original base checkpoint and introduces a lightweight multi-domain data mixture. Furthermore, specialized capabilities (e.g., safety, vision, audio) are transferred to the encoder by merging with corresponding causal specialists and applying brief fine-tuning. The resulting family of BidirLM encoders establishes new Pareto frontiers on major benchmarks (MTEB, XTREME, MIEB, MAEB) while being more parameter-efficient than prior works.

## Method Summary
The BidirLM pipeline starts from a pre-trained causal LLM (e.g., Gemma3 or Qwen3). It first disables causal masking to enable full bidirectional attention. The model then undergoes MNTP adaptation on a large corpus (10–30B tokens) to learn bidirectional context modeling. If adaptation extends beyond 10B tokens, a 20% mixture of multi-domain data and/or a 50% linear merge with the original base checkpoint mitigates catastrophic forgetting. After adaptation, contrastive training on a multi-domain paired dataset (∼10M samples) with an InfoNCE loss further improves zero-shot embedding quality. To incorporate specialized skills, the resulting encoder is linearly merged (50%) with a specialist causal model (e.g., for safety, vision, or audio). The merged backbone is combined with the specialist’s projection heads and fine-tuned for a few hundred steps, yielding an omnimodal encoder without full retraining.

## Key Results
- BidirLM-270M matches mmBERT’s performance with 10% fewer parameters.
- BidirLM-Omni-2.5B outperforms Nemotron-Omni-3B across text, vision, and audio while using half the parameters.
- The family of five encoders sets new Pareto frontiers on MTEB (text), XTREME (multilingual), MIEB (multimodal image), and MAEB (multimodal audio) benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Biphasic Adaptation (MNTP → Contrastive)
- **Claim:** Sequential training with Masked Next-Token Prediction (MNTP) followed by contrastive learning yields a single model that excels at both full-parameter fine-tuning and zero-shot embedding tasks.
- **Mechanism:** The MNTP phase switches the attention mask to bidirectional and trains the model to reconstruct masked tokens using full context, reconfiguring internal attention patterns. The subsequent contrastive phase then aligns representations via an InfoNCE objective, boosting generic embedding quality. The two phases are complementary: MNTP enables effective bidirectional attention for fine-tuning, while contrastive learning improves out-of-distribution generalization.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 3 (Figure 2) shows that the biphasic combination outperforms variants using only one phase across multiple benchmarks. Contrastive-only sacrifices fine-tuning, while MNTP-only lacks embedding quality.

### Mechanism 2: Weight Merging for Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation
- **Claim:** Linear weight merging between the adapted bidirectional encoder and the original causal base model effectively recovers foundational knowledge lost during out-of-distribution adaptation.
- **Mechanism:** After scaling MNTP adaptation on a corpus diverging from pre-training (e.g., mostly English), the model forgets multilingual, code, and math skills. By linearly merging the adapted weights with the original base weights (e.g., 50% each), the combined model retains both new bidirectional attention and the base model’s broad coverage because the two checkpoints remain close in weight space (cosine similarity >0.78).
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 4.1 (Figure 3) shows performance declines on Arabic, code, and math as adaptation scales. Figure 4 demonstrates that merging at ~50% restores and even exceeds scores. The paper reports cosine similarities of 0.78 (Gemma) and 0.97 (Qwen) between base and adapted weights.

### Mechanism 3: Cross-Model Composition via Weight Merging
- **Claim:** A bidirectional encoder can be seamlessly augmented with specialized capabilities (e.g., safety, vision, audio) by merging its weights with those of a causal specialist, transferring domain or modality knowledge without retraining.
- **Mechanism:** The encoder’s textual backbone is linearly merged with a specialist model sharing the same architecture. The specialist’s projection heads are attached, and a short fine-tuning (∼500 steps) on the target domain aligns representations.
- **Evidence anchors:** Section 6.1 (Figure 7) shows that merging with a safety model yields better out-of-distribution safety performance than either parent. Section 6.2 (Figure 8) demonstrates similar gains for vision and audio. Section 6.3 reports that merging three specialists produces BidirLM-Omni-2.5B, which surpasses a larger specialist model.

## Foundational Learning
### Concept: Causal Language Modeling
- **Why needed here:** All base models are decoder-only LLMs trained with next-token prediction. Understanding causal masking is essential to grasp why adaptation starts from such models and how bidirectional attention must be introduced.
- **Quick check question:** In a causal LM, can token *i* attend to token *i+1* during training? Why or why not?

### Concept: Bidirectional Attention
- **Why needed here:** The goal is to convert a causal model into a bidirectional encoder. The mechanism hinges on switching the attention mask from triangular to full, which requires careful adaptation to avoid instability.
- **Quick check question:** How does full bidirectional attention differ from causal attention in terms of information flow between tokens?

### Concept: Contrastive Learning (InfoNCE)
- **Why needed here:** The second phase of the pipeline uses contrastive objectives to align representations. Knowing how in-batch and hard negatives shape embedding geometry is key to understanding why this phase improves zero-shot performance.
- **Quick check question:** In InfoNCE loss, what is the role of the temperature hyperparameter?

### Concept: Catastrophic Forgetting
- **Why needed here:** The paper explicitly addresses catastrophic forgetting when scaling adaptation on non-pre-training distributions. Without mitigation, the adapted model loses proficiency on diverse tasks.
- **Quick check question:** Why does continual learning on a new domain often lead to forgetting of old domains in neural networks?

### Concept: Weight Merging (Task Arithmetic)
- **Why needed here:** The core scalability and composition strategies rely on linear interpolation of weights (e.g., 50% base + 50% adapted). This technique is central to both forgetting mitigation and specialist composition.
- **Quick check question:** If you have two models fine-tuned from the same base checkpoint, what heuristic suggests that their average might perform well on both tasks?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Base Causal Model → Bidirectional Attention Switcher → MNTP Adaptation Module → [Weight Merging with Base] → Contrastive Training Module → Final Encoder  
Final Encoder → Linear Merge with Specialist Model → Specialist Heads → Short Fine-tuning → Specialist Encoder

### Critical Path
1. Start from a pre-trained causal LLM.
2. Convert attention to bidirectional (no training, just mask change).
3. Run MNTP adaptation on the primary domain corpus (e.g., FineWeb-Edu) until adequate bidirectionality is learned (∼10B tokens).
4. If scaling beyond 10B tokens, mix in 20% multi-domain data and/or merge with base weights (50%) to prevent forgetting.
5. Optionally apply contrastive training on a multi-domain (or multimodal) paired dataset to improve generic embeddings.
6. To specialize: linear merge the encoder with a specialist causal model (e.g., safety, VL, ASR) at 50%, then fine-tune the merged model briefly (500 steps).

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Masking ratio (MNTP):** Paper evaluates 10%–40%; optimal depends on model size (30%–40% often best). Higher rates increase diversity but may slow convergence.
- **Merging ratio:** 0.5 is the robust default; higher ratios favor the specialist, lower favor the encoder. Sensitive for cross-modality merges.
- **Data mixture proportion:** 20% multi-domain data is sufficient to preserve base knowledge; more may dilute domain adaptation.
- **Model size:** Smaller models (∼0.6B) are cheaper but show larger absolute forgetting; larger models (2.5B) benefit more from merging.

### Failure Signatures
- **Fine-tuning collapse:** If contrastive-only training is applied without MNTP, downstream task performance (e.g., XNLI) lags >5 points behind MNTP+Contrastive.
- **Forgetting spike:** When scaling MNTP beyond 10B tokens without merging or multi-domain data, scores on multilingual/code/math drop by >7 points.
- **Merging failure:** Very low cosine similarity (<0.7) between base and adapted weights (e.g., after 50B tokens) leads to merged models that underperform both parents.
- **Cross-modal warm-up:** For vision/audio, the merged model may start with near-random performance and require >100 fine-tuning steps to escape warm-up; insufficient steps yield suboptimal results.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Biphasic Ablation:** On a small model (Gemma3-270M), train four variants: Bi+Base, Bi+MNTP, Bi+Contrastive, Bi+MNTP+Contrastive. Evaluate on MTEB (zero-shot) and XNLI (fine-tuned). Expect Bi+MNTP+Contrastive to win on both; contrastive-only to excel only on MTEB.
2. **Merging Ratio Sweep:** Take a 30B-token MNTP-adapted model, merge with base at ratios [0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%]. Evaluate on a held-out multilingual/code benchmark. Expect a clear peak near 50%.
3. **Specialist Composition:** Merge an encoder (e.g., Qwen3-0.6B Bi+MNTP) with a safety model (Qwen3Guard) at 50%. Fine-tune for 500 steps on Beaver. Compare against Bi+Specialist (specialist with bidirectional attention) and unmerged encoder. Expect merged to converge faster and reach higher final F1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What data composition, hard-negative mining, and scaling regimes maximize contrastive training for omnimodal encoders? The paper calls for systematic study of these factors.
- **Open Question 2:** Does knowledge distillation or cross-architectural distillation provide more effective mitigation of catastrophic forgetting than linear weight merging? Exploring richer regularization strategies is suggested.
- **Open Question 3:** Can the BidirLM adaptation framework be successfully applied to non-transformer causal architectures such as state-space models? Validating on such backbones remains an open question.

## Limitations
- Exact composition of the contrastive training corpus (89 datasets) and multimodal Omni-Contrastive preprocessing are not fully disclosed, hindering exact replication.
- Weight merging assumes linear mode connectivity (cosine similarity >0.78), which may break for highly diverged models or different architectures.
- Cross-modal composition requires modality-specific fine-tuning, introducing extra cost and possibly limiting zero-shot transfer.

## Confidence
- **Biphasic adaptation (MNTP + contrastive) yields high-quality bidirectional encoders:** High.
- **Weight merging mitigates catastrophic forgetting during scaling:** Medium.
- **Cross-model composition transfers specialist capabilities:** Medium.

## Next Checks
1. Measure loss barriers or mode connectivity metrics between base and adapted checkpoints across scaling steps to test the linearity assumption behind merging.
2. Replicate core results using an independently compiled contrastive dataset (e.g., filtered MS MARCO) to evaluate robustness to corpus variations.
3. Attempt composition with mismatched model families (e.g., Gemma encoder + Llama specialist) to delineate the architectural and similarity boundaries for successful merging.
