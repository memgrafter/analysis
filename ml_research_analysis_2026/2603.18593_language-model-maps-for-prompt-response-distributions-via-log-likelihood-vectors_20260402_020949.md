# Language Model Maps for Prompt-Response Distributions via Log-Likelihood Vectors

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.18593
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18593
- **Reference count**: 17
- **Primary result**: Representing models by conditional log-likelihood vectors over a fixed prompt-response set allows efficient comparison; squared Euclidean distance approximates average KL divergence (Spearman ρ=0.94) and captures meaningful global structure, additivity of prompt shifts, and bias reduction via PMI.

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces a scalable method to compare large language models by encoding each model as a vector of log-likelihoods over a fixed collection of prompt-response pairs. The squared Euclidean distance between these vectors serves as an empirical proxy for the average KL divergence between the models' conditional distributions. Applied to 552 public models, this approach yields model maps that cluster by architecture and correlate with benchmark performance. Systematic shifts in the map caused by prompt modifications are found to be approximately additive (median relative error ~0.062), enabling prediction of composite prompt effects. Using PMI vectors (conditional minus unconditional log-likelihood) further reduces unconditional bias and can highlight differences related to training data.

## Method Summary
The method proceeds as follows: a dataset of diverse prompt-response pairs is curated. For each language model, the conditional log-likelihood of each response given its prompt is computed, forming a high-dimensional vector. Pairwise squared Euclidean distances between vectors are used to approximate the average KL divergence between the corresponding conditional distributions. Optionally, vectors are transformed to pointwise mutual information (PMI) by subtracting the unconditional log-likelihood of the response. The resulting distances are then used for dimensionality reduction, clustering, and shift analysis. The approach is highly scalable, as vectors are precomputed and distance computations are cheap, enabling analysis of hundreds of models.

## Key Results
- Squared Euclidean distance between log-likelihood vectors correlates highly with Monte Carlo estimates of average KL divergence (Spearman ρ=0.94) across 552 models.
- Model maps reveal global structure: models cluster by architecture, and vector positions correlate with standard benchmark performances.
- Prompt modifications induce nearly additive shifts in the vector space (median relative error ~0.062), allowing the effect of combined prompts to be predicted from individual effects.
- PMI vectors reduce unconditional bias and sometimes expose training-data related differences more clearly than raw log-likelihoods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The empirical success of the Euclidean distance approximation is supported by a very high correlation (ρ=0.94) with Monte Carlo KL estimates, suggesting that the fixed prompt-response set adequately captures the relevant distributional differences. The additivity of prompt shifts indicates that, for the transformations tested, model responses in log-likelihood space combine linearly, possibly because the modifications act as independent perturbations. However, the paper does not provide a formal theoretical derivation; the approximation's validity is demonstrated through extensive experiments.

## Foundational Learning
- **Log-Likelihood**  
  *Why needed*: Forms the basic coordinates of the model vector; measures model confidence on specific responses.  
  *Quick check*: Given a model, prompt, and response, compute log P(response|prompt) and confirm it is finite and differentiable.
- **KL Divergence**  
  *Why needed*: The gold-standard measure of difference between models' predictive distributions; the target for approximation.  
  *Quick check*: For two discrete distributions P and Q, compute KL(P||Q) and verify it matches an importance-sampling estimate.
- **Conditional Distribution**  
  *Why needed*: Models are compared based on their next-token predictions conditioned on prompts, not marginal over prompts.  
  *Quick check*: For a fixed prompt, sample multiple responses from a model and estimate the empirical distribution.
- **Euclidean Distance**  
  *Why needed*: A computationally efficient metric on log-likelihood vectors that empirically approximates average KL.  
  *Quick check*: Given two vectors, compute sum of squared differences and ensure it is non-negative and symmetric.
- **Prompt-Response Set**  
  *Why needed*: Provides a fixed basis for representing models; diversity influences map quality.  
  *Quick check*: Verify the set covers multiple tasks and that models assign non-negligible probability to many responses.
- **PMI (Pointwise Mutual Information)**  
  *Why needed*: Adjusts for unconditional response frequency, reducing bias from common but irrelevant responses.  
  *Quick check*: Compute PMI = log P(r|p) - log P(r) for a model and check that high values indicate strong association beyond baseline frequency.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**:  
  Fixed Prompt-Response Pairs → Log-Likelihood Extraction (per model) → Vector Assembly → Pairwise Euclidean Distance Computation → Dimensionality Reduction / Clustering → Model Map Visualization.
- **Critical path**:  
  Selection of a representative prompt-response set → accurate computation of log-likelihoods for every model → formation of vectors → computation of distance matrix → validation against KL estimates → exploration of map structure and shift additivity.
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  *Prompt set size*: Larger sets improve coverage but increase computational cost and may introduce redundancy.  
  *Distance metric*: Euclidean is simple and fast but may not capture all divergence nuances; alternatives like cosine or Mahalanobis could be explored.  
  *Log vs probability*: Log scale avoids underflow and emphasizes ratios but can amplify noise for very low probabilities.  
  *PMI transformation*: Reduces unconditional bias but requires reliable estimation of unconditional response probabilities, which may be noisy.
- **Failure signatures**:  
  *High sensitivity*: Distance estimates fluctuate dramatically with small changes in the prompt set, indicating poor coverage.  
  *Numerical instability*: Extreme negative log-likelihoods (due to near-zero probabilities) distort Euclidean distances.  
  *Breakdown of additivity*: For complex or interactive prompt modifications, the sum of individual shift vectors does not approximate the combined shift.  
  *Poor clustering*: Models do not group by architecture or correlate with benchmarks, suggesting the prompt set is not discriminative.
- **First three experiments**:  
  1. **Validation of KL approximation**: For a diverse subset of models, compute pairwise Euclidean distances from vectors and compare to Monte Carlo estimates of average KL; report correlation (Spearman ρ).  
  2. **Map structure analysis**: Apply dimensionality reduction (e.g., t-SNE or UMAP) to the vectors, visualize clusters, and compute correlation between map coordinates and benchmark scores.  
  3. **Additivity test**: Define a set of simple prompt modifications (e.g., adding "briefly" or "in one sentence"); for each, compute the shift vector from a base prompt; then combine modifications and verify that the total shift is approximately the sum of individual shifts (quantify error).

## Limitations
- The fixed prompt-response set may not fully represent model behavior and could introduce selection bias, limiting generalizability.
- The squared Euclidean distance approximation lacks formal theoretical justification and may be unstable for responses with very low probabilities or outliers.
- The observed additivity of prompt shifts is based on a limited set of modifications; its validity for complex or large perturbations remains uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Representation & KL approximation**: High (Spearman ρ=0.94 against Monte Carlo, N=552)
- **Map structure (architecture clustering, benchmark correlation)**: Medium (observational, prompt-dependent)
- **Additivity of prompt shifts**: Medium (median relative error 0.062, narrow test suite)
- **PMI utility**: Medium (consistent pattern but no guarantees)

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt-set ablation**: Randomly subsample prompts and assess stability of map topology and KL approximation metrics; higher variance would signal fragility.
2. **Derive theoretical conditions**: Prove bounds for when Euclidean distance between log-likelihood vectors equals or upper-bounds average KL; validate with temperature-scaled models where KL is analytically known.
3. **Broaden additivity experiments**: Apply hundreds of prompt transformations (semantic paraphrases, instruction tweaks, multi-turn edits) to multiple models; fit additive model and report error distribution to identify non-additive regimes.
