# Value Entanglement: Conflation Between Different Kinds of Good In (Some) Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19101
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19101
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Several LLMs (e.g., GPT-3.5, Qwen2.5-7B) conflate moral, grammatical, and economic value judgments, but directional ablation of morality-related activations can reduce this conflation and improve alignment with human standards.

## Executive Summary
The paper investigates whether large language models distinguish three kinds of value—moral, grammatical, and economic. Using custom stimulus sets (MoralGrammar68, MoralEconomic68) that orthogonalize these dimensions, the authors probed model behavior, embeddings, and residual stream activations. They found pervasive value entanglement in several models: grammatical and economic judgments were excessively influenced by moral content, with cross-domain correlations as high as r = 0.75 in activation projections. Crucially, directional ablation of morality-related activation vectors repaired the conflation, improving alignment with human ratings and ground-truth prices. The effect was present in both pre-trained and instruction-tuned models, suggesting that training contributes to the phenomenon. The study highlights how ambiguous evaluative language in pre-training data and alignment procedures may cause overlapping representations, with implications for model alignment and emergent misalignment risks.

## Method Summary
The authors designed two orthogonal stimulus sets: MoralGrammar68 (varying morality and grammaticality independently) and MoralEconomic68 (varying morality and economic value). Human ratings for morality and grammaticality (41 raters per item) and ground-truth object prices were collected. Models were probed via (a) behavioral prompting (Likert scales), (b) semantic projection of embeddings onto value axes defined by contrastive adjective/object pairs, and (c) residual stream activation projections using difference-of-means vectors from contrast sets. Directional ablation (single and double) of morality-related activation vectors was applied layer-wise during inference to test causality. Cross-domain correlations, ANOVAs, and permutation tests quantified entanglement and ablation effects across multiple models and training stages.

## Key Results
- **Cross-domain correlations:** In GPT-3.5, grammaticality ratings correlated with human morality ratings at *r* = 0.56 (human inter-dimension *r* = 0.05); activation projections showed high morality-grammar correlations (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B *r* = 0.75 at layer 21).
- **Ablation repair:** Directional ablation of morality vectors improved alignment: in Qwen2.5-7B, economic rating correlation with ground truth increased from *r* = 0.20 to *r* = 0.55.
- **Broad presence:** Entanglement appeared in both pre-trained and instruction-tuned models, indicating training contributes; newer closed-source models (Claude 3.7, GPT-4o mini) did not show behavioral entanglement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper proposes three contributing mechanisms:

1. **Ambiguous evaluative language in pre-training data**  
   Words like “good” appear across morally virtuous, grammatically correct, and economically valuable contexts. The next-token prediction objective does not require distinguishing the *kind* of goodness, leading to overlapping representations in a shared positive direction. Evidence: GPT-3.5’s grammaticality ratings correlated with human morality ratings (*r* = 0.56) while human ratings of these dimensions were uncorrelated (*r* = 0.05).

2. **Post-training alignment procedures**  
   RLHF and similar methods use coarse scalar feedback that mixes value dimensions (e.g., “helpful” answers may be both factually correct and morally prudent). This reinforcement can exacerbate pre-training entanglements. Evidence: Both pre-trained and instruction-tuned models exhibited entanglement, with some tuned models showing stronger effects (e.g., Gemma-2-9B, Mistral-Small-24B).

3. **Overlapping subspaces in the residual stream**  
   Value axes occupy adjacent directions; the model’s rating readout linearly mixes the intended value with correlated morality. Ablating the morality component reduces interference. Evidence: High cross-correlations in residual projections (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B morality vs. grammar: *r* = 0.75) and successful repair after directional ablation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Orthogonal Stimulus Design**  
  *Why needed:* To disentangle behavioral confounds, stimuli must vary target dimensions independently. Without orthogonality, observed correlations could be spurious.  
  *Quick check:* If you have sentences that are all morally good but vary in grammaticality, can you test if a model’s grammaticality ratings are influenced by the fixed moral content?

- **Semantic Projection Method**  
  *Why needed:* This method defines a continuous value axis (e.g., “moral good”) as a vector in embedding/activation space derived from contrastive examples, allowing quantification of novel stimuli on that axis without training a new classifier.  
  *Quick check:* How would you construct a “moral goodness” vector from clearly immoral and moral sentences?

- **Directional Ablation (Intervention)**  
  *Why needed:* To establish a causal link between a identified vector and behavior. By selectively removing variance along that direction during inference, we test whether the target task changes, implicating the vector as an interfering source.  
  *Quick check:* What is the difference between ablating a direction (zeroing its component) and flipping it (double ablation), and why might flipping be more diagnostic?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** The system comprises:
1. Stimulus Engine: generates MoralGrammar68 and MoralEconomic68 stimulus sets.
2. Probing Suite: (a) behavioral prompting for Likert ratings; (b) embedding projection using contrastive adjective pairs; (c) residual stream activation projection using contrastive sentence pairs.
3. Intervention Module: implements directional ablation/flipping on residual stream at chosen layers.
4. Analysis Pipeline: computes cross-domain correlations, ANOVAs, and ablation impact statistics.

**Critical path:** Hypothesis (value entanglement) → Stimulus design (orthogonal sets) → Baseline measurement (behavior & representations) → Causal test (directional ablation) → Comparison across models/training stages.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Stimulus naturalism vs. orthogonality: artificial sentences ensure orthogonality but may lack ecological validity.
- Layer selection for ablation: scanning all layers increases computational cost.
- Ablation strength: double ablation (flipping) produces more consistent effects but is a stronger intervention that could introduce non-linear side‑effects.

**Failure signatures:**
- No cross-domain correlation in baseline (model already disentangled).
- Ablation of morality vector does *not* change grammatical/economic ratings (suggests no causal role).
- Ablation of a control vector (e.g., animal size) produces similar changes (indicates non‑specific disruption).
- Inconsistent layer effects (no clear peak in correlation modulation).

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Stimulus validation:** Run human/embedding checks that moral and grammatical/economic ratings are orthogonal in the stimulus set (correlations near zero).
2. **Baseline entanglement check:** For a target model, compute model rating cross‑correlations, embedding projection cross‑correlations, and residual stream projection cross‑correlations; expect significant positive correlations (e.g., *r* > 0.4) in entangled models.
3. **Ablation pilot:** For the layer showing peak cross‑correlation, apply double ablation of the morality vector; re‑measure the target rating (grammatical/economic); expect improvement in correlation with human ground truth (e.g., *r* increase by ~0.2–0.3).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why do some large language models (e.g., newer closed‑source models like GPT‑4o mini, Claude 3.7) not exhibit behavioral value entanglement while others (e.g., open‑weight models) do, despite both showing representational entanglement in embeddings?
2. Can value entanglement be permanently repaired during training, or are interventions like directional ablation only temporary diagnostic tools?
3. What is the precise relationship between representational entanglement (as measured by embedding/activation projections) and behavioral entanglement (as measured by prompted ratings)? Why do some models (e.g., GPT embeddings) show strong representational entanglement without corresponding behavioral effects?
4. Is value entanglement a general phenomenon that extends to other kinds of value (e.g., aesthetic, social, epistemic), and does it predict susceptibility to “emergent misalignment” (e.g., narrow fine‑tuning causing broad harmful behaviors)?

## Limitations
- **Data accessibility:** Full human rating matrices and complete contrast sets for stimuli are not provided, blocking direct replication of core analyses.
- **Implementation ambiguity:** Details of behavioral iteration aggregation and ablation sub‑sampling are underspecified, risking deviation from original methods.
- **Ecological validity & version drift:** Artificial stimulus construction may not capture natural linguistic ambiguity; pre‑trained base models may differ from current releases, affecting reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **High:** The existence of entanglement (cross‑domain correlations) is robustly supported by multiple convergent probes (behavior, embeddings, activations) and causal ablation.
- **Medium:** The mechanism (ambiguous pre‑training data causing overlapping representations) is plausible and consistent with evidence, but not directly tested.
- **Low:** The generality across model families/training regimes is uncertain due to limited model sampling and missing base‑model comparisons for some variants.

## Next Checks
