# Agentic AI-based Coverage Closure for Formal Verification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.03147
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03147
- Reference count: 20
- One-line primary result: Agentic AI workflow achieves 10-20% coverage increase on formal verification benchmarks, with GPT-5 showing highest gains but reduced proven assertion rates.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an agentic AI approach to accelerate coverage closure in IC formal verification. The workflow employs two specialized agents—a Coverage Hole Analyzer and an SVA Property Generator—leveraging LLMs to extract RTL context from coverage reports and generate targeted SystemVerilog assertions. The system iterates with formal tools until coverage goals are met. Benchmarking on open-source and internal designs demonstrates consistent 10-20% coverage improvements across multiple LLMs, with GPT-5 yielding the largest gains on complex designs, albeit with a trade-off in assertion proven rates. The method significantly reduces manual verification effort and speeds up sign-off.

## Method Summary
The proposed method is an iterative, multi-agent AI workflow. Given an RTL design and coverage goals, the Coverage Hole Analyzer processes coverage reports to pinpoint uncovered code regions and extracts relevant RTL context (e.g., signals, modules). The SVA Property Generator uses this context, along with the uncovered locations, to synthesize SystemVerilog assertions targeting those areas. These properties are fed to a formal verification engine; the results update the coverage database, and the loop repeats until coverage targets are satisfied. The agents are implemented with LLMs (various models tested) via carefully designed prompts, and the workflow is designed to be tool-agnostic, integrating with standard formal tools.

## Key Results
- 10-20% consistent coverage increase across open-source and internal designs using multiple LLMs.
- GPT-5 achieved the highest coverage improvements, especially on complex Automotive IP, but with a noticeable drop in assertion proven rates.
- The approach demonstrably reduces manual effort and accelerates verification sign-off, though quantitative time savings are not provided.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The agentic workflow succeeds by automating the most time-consuming aspect of formal verification: writing assertions for uncovered regions. The Coverage Hole Analyzer translates raw coverage data into actionable RTL context, guiding the SVA Property Generator to produce precise, relevant properties. This targeted generation avoids blanket assertions and focuses formal tools on weak spots, efficiently closing coverage gaps. Iteration allows refinement: failed or vacuous properties are excluded in subsequent cycles, and coverage feedback continuously improves the context. LLMs bring flexibility to understand diverse RTL styles and synthesize properties without hand-crafted templates, making the system adaptable to new designs.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**:
RTL Design + Coverage Goals -> Coverage Hole Analyzer -> RTL Context + Uncovered Regions -> SVA Property Generator -> SystemVerilog Assertions -> Formal Tool -> Coverage Database (updated) -> (loop back to Analyzer until coverage goals met)

**Critical Path**: The iterative loop between property generation, formal verification, and coverage update. Each iteration must complete within reasonable time to keep verification on schedule.

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Using LLMs increases flexibility and reduces manual effort but may generate syntactically incorrect or semantically weak properties, leading to lower proven rates (as seen with GPT-5).
- The system prioritizes coverage increase over property quality; some generated assertions may be vacuous or redundant.
- Iteration count and computational overhead depend on formal tool performance; complex designs may require many cycles.

**Failure Signatures**:
- Coverage plateau: no improvement after several iterations, indicating the agents cannot generate effective properties.
- High formal tool timeout or resource exhaustion, often due to overly complex or contradictory assertions.
- Low proven rate (<X%) for generated properties, signaling poor assertion quality.
- LLM output parsing errors or invalid SVA syntax causing tool crashes.

**First Three Experiments**:
1. Smoke test: Run the full workflow on a small, well-understood design (e.g., a FIFO) to validate end-to-end integration and baseline coverage gains.
2. Sensitivity analysis: Compare coverage improvement and proven rates across different LLM models (e.g., GPT-4, Claude 3, Llama) on a medium-complexity design, measuring iteration count and tool runtime.
3. Failure injection: Intentionally provide noisy coverage reports or incomplete RTL context to assess robustness of the property generator and identify degradation patterns.

## Limitations
- Reproducibility gap: The paper lacks reproduction artifacts (RTL, coverage reports, generated SVA) and internal designs are not transparent, hindering independent validation.
- Sparse external validation: Zero citations and reliance on author-curated benchmarks limit confidence in generalizability.
- Trade-off ambiguity: The inverse relationship between coverage gains and assertion proven rates (especially with GPT-5) is noted but not fully explained; possible overfitting to coverage metrics.

## Confidence
- Coverage increase (10–20%): Medium confidence (validated only on author-provided benchmarks; no independent studies).
- Cross-LLM consistency: Medium confidence (multiple LLMs tested but details on models, prompts, and costs are not fully disclosed).
- Manual effort reduction: Low confidence (anecdotal; no quantitative metrics on engineer time or sign-off timeline).

## Next Checks
1. Independent benchmark replication: Re-run the workflow on public formal verification benchmarks (e.g., ASPEED, OpenTitan) with full artifact release.
