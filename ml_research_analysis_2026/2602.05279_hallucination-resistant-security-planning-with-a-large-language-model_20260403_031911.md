# Hallucination-Resistant Security Planning with a Large Language Model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.05279
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05279
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A hallucination-resistant framework reduces recovery times by up to 30% versus frontier LLMs by iteratively checking action consistency and using in-context learning with external feedback.

## Executive Summary
LLM hallucinations pose a significant risk in security planning, where erroneous actions can have severe consequences. This paper introduces a framework that mitigates hallucinations by iteratively generating candidate actions, evaluating their consistency through lookahead predictions, and abstaining from low-consistency actions to gather external feedback for in-context learning. The approach is theoretically justified with bounds on hallucination probability and in-context learning regret. Empirical evaluation on four incident response datasets shows up to 30% faster recovery compared to GPT-4 and Gemini 2.5 Pro, with ablation studies confirming the contribution of each component.

## Method Summary
Given a security incident state (system description, logs, alerts, previous actions), a fine-tuned DeepSeek-R1-14B LLM generates three candidate actions via chain-of-thought, each accompanied by a predicted number of remaining steps to recovery (lookahead). Consistency λ is computed as exp(-0.9 * average squared deviation among the three predictions). If λ exceeds a threshold γ=0.9, the action with the smallest prediction is executed; otherwise, the framework abstains and simulates the top candidate in a digital twin (CSLE testbed), producing a textual explanation of its effectiveness. This feedback is appended to the prompt for in-context learning, and generation repeats. The loop continues until MITRE D3FEND recovery criteria (contained, assessed, preserved, evicted, hardened, restored) are met. Theoretically, Proposition 1 bounds hallucination probability via γ; Proposition 2 bounds ICL regret under Bayesian and bandit feedback.

## Key Results
- Recovery times reduced by up to 30% compared to GPT-4 and Gemini 2.5 Pro across four incident response datasets.
- Significant decrease in ineffective actions and failed recoveries.
- Ablation studies validate the necessity of both consistency checking and feedback-driven ICL.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework combats hallucinations through two coordinated mechanisms. The consistency check filters actions by requiring agreement among multiple lookahead predictions; incoherent predictions (high variance) flag potential hallucinations. Abstention triggered by low consistency invokes a feedback loop: the digital twin simulates the candidate and provides a textual critique, which is used for in-context learning. This allows the LLM to adjust its future reasoning based on concrete, simulated outcomes, gradually aligning its internal model with the true environment dynamics. Theoretical guarantees confirm that tuning γ bounds hallucination probability, while ICL regret remains controlled. Empirically, this synergy yields more reliable and efficient decision-making in incident response.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Consistency-based hallucination detection** – Uses variance in multiple predictions to spot spurious outputs. *Why needed*: Hallucinations often produce contradictory reasoning; consistency offers an unsupervised signal. *Quick check*: Correlate prediction variance with action validity on a validation set.
2. **In-context learning (ICL) with feedback** – Adapts the LLM by appending feedback examples to the prompt. *Why needed*: Enables rapid adaptation without costly fine-tuning. *Quick check*: Measure performance gain after adding a few feedback demonstrations.
3. **Lookahead prediction** – The model estimates steps to recovery before acting. *Why needed*: Enables action evaluation and consistency computation. *Quick check*: Compare predicted vs. actual remaining steps on known trajectories.
4. **Digital twin simulation** – CSLE testbed mimics real incident response dynamics. *Why needed*: Provides safe, automated feedback when real execution is risky. *Quick check*: Validate simulated outcomes against historical incident logs.
5. **Bandit feedback model** – Treats feedback as stochastic rewards for theoretical analysis. *Why needed*: Derives regret bounds for the ICL process. *Quick check*: Verify feedback rewards are unbiased and have bounded variance.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
LLM (generator) -> Action Candidates & Predictions -> Consistency Checker -> Threshold Gate -> Selection / Abstention -> (if abstention) Digital Twin (feedback) -> Prompt Update -> In-Context Learning -> LLM

**Critical path**:  
1. Encode system state into a prompt.  
2. LLM generates three actions and lookahead predictions.  
3. Compute consistency λ from prediction variance.  
4. If λ > γ, choose action with smallest prediction; else, simulate top candidate in digital twin to get textual feedback.  
5. Append feedback to prompt and repeat from step 2 (or proceed if consistent).  
6. Execute selected action, update state, and repeat until recovery.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Threshold γ: Higher reduces hallucinations but may increase abstentions and recovery time; calibrated at 0.9.  
- Candidate count N=3: Balances consistency reliability and computational cost.  
- Lookahead prediction: Noisy; exponential consistency (β=0.9) dampens variance impact.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Low correlation between predicted and actual steps indicates poor lookahead.  
- High abstention rate without performance gain suggests ineffective feedback or low digital twin fidelity.  
- Recovery times worse than baselines may stem from overly conservative γ or flawed prompt design.

**First three experiments**:  
1. Validate lookahead predictions: Collect state-action pairs with known optimal steps; compute correlation between model predictions and ground truth.  
2. Test digital twin fidelity: Compare outcomes of known actions in the simulator vs. real incidents; measure discrepancies.  
3. Ablate consistency threshold: Sweep γ on validation set and assess recovery time and failure rate to gauge sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
*Open Question 1*: Does the framework generalize to other security management tasks beyond incident response?  
*Basis*: Future work includes evaluation on additional security tasks.  
*Why unresolved*: Only incident response was tested; other tasks may differ in decision structure, feedback requirements, or temporal dynamics.  
*Evidence needed*: Experiments on diverse security tasks with suitable datasets and baselines, measuring performance transferability.

*Open Question 2*: How realistic are the Bayesian learning and bandit feedback assumptions in real deployments?  
*Basis*: Theoretical analysis depends on Assumptions 1 and 2, which are not empirically verified.  
*Why unresolved*: Assumption 1 (LLM posterior = true posterior) is strong; Assumption 2 (unbiased immediate reward) may not hold due to delayed/noisy feedback.  
*Evidence needed*: Empirical validation of assumption alignment in operational settings; characterization of feedback mechanisms' properties.

*Open Question 3*: How sensitive is performance to the design of the consistency function λ?  
*Basis*: The paper uses a specific exponential λ and notes task-specific constraints could be added, but no exploration.  
*Why unresolved*: λ directly influences abstention decisions; its parameterization (β, etc.) may critically affect the safety-efficiency trade-off.  
*Evidence needed*: Ablation studies varying λ's formulation (e.g., dispersion metrics, domain rules) across tasks, measuring impact on recovery time and hallucination rate.

*Open Question 4*: What are the practical deployment challenges of the feedback loop (digital twin/expert input)?  
*Basis*: The framework assumes accessible feedback but does not analyze feasibility, cost, or integration overhead.  
*Why unresolved*: Digital twins require accurate system models; expert feedback is costly and slow. The loop's practicality is unclear.  
*Evidence needed*: Field studies in SOCs measuring feedback latency, accuracy, and resource consumption; analysis of alternative feedback sources.

## Limitations
- Lookahead prediction prompting: Unspecified method for eliciting reliable numeric step estimates may yield inconsistent consistency scores.
- Digital twin feedback generation: Undocumented mechanism for automatic textual feedback; may require manual input, limiting automation.
- Generalization beyond testbed: Assumes digital twin accurately mirrors real-world dynamics, which may fail for novel incidents or environments.

## Confidence
- **Experimental claims (recovery time reduction)**: High – rigorous ablations, multiple datasets, reproducible seeds/benchmarks.
- **Theoretical bounds**: Medium – standard bandit/BC assumptions may not capture real LLM policy stochasticity or environment non-stationarity.
- **Reproducibility of prompt/tuning details**: Low – critical components like prompt templates and γ calibration are not explicitly provided.
