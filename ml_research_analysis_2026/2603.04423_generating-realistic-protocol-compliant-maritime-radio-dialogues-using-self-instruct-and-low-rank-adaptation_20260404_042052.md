# Generating Realistic, Protocol-Compliant Maritime Radio Dialogues using Self-Instruct and Low-Rank Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.04423
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04423
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Generated 500 compliant distress dialogues per category from 10 seeds, achieving 87% pass rate with 98.6% format accuracy and 94.7% information accuracy.

## Executive Summary
Maritime VHF radio miscommunication causes over 58% of incidents, but training AI safety systems requires scarce protocol-compliant dialogue data. This study introduces a compliance-aware Self-Instruct method that embeds a 26-filter verification pipeline directly into an iterative LLM generation loop to enforce IMO SMCP rules, entity accuracy, and logical consistency, combined with parameter-efficient LoRA fine-tuning. Using public AIS and geographic datasets, the approach generated 500 synthetic distress dialogues per category from only 10 seed instances. The fine-tuned adapters achieved 87% of dialogues passing all filters, with Format Accuracy of 98.6%, Information Accuracy of 94.7%, Uniqueness above 0.4, and Logical Coherence of 0.83—representing substantial relative improvements over the vanilla Llama 3.1 8B model, which produced 0% valid dialogues. This enables scalable, realistic, protocol-compliant maritime communication data for AI safety systems.

## Method Summary
The method combines iterative Self-Instruct with embedded verification and parameter-efficient fine-tuning. Starting from 10 seed dialogues per distress category, the system repeatedly: (1) generates a realistic context from public AIS and geographic datasets (vessel identities, coordinates, nearby landmarks), (2) prompts Llama 3.1 8B with the context plus 5 few-shot examples (3 seeds, 2 previously accepted synthetic dialogues) to create a new dialogue, and (3) applies 26 fine-grained filters (entity accuracy, IMO SMCP compliance, logical consistency, uniqueness). Only dialogues passing all filters enter the instance pool. After collecting 500 validated dialogues per category, separate LoRA adapters (rank r=256) are fine-tuned for each category. Evaluation uses context-only prompts on the adapters to measure Format Accuracy, Information Accuracy, Uniqueness (Rouge-L < 0.7), and expert-rated Logical Coherence.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuned adapters achieved 87% of dialogues passing all 26 filters, versus 0% for vanilla Llama 3.1 8B.
- High compliance metrics: Format Accuracy 98.6%, Information Accuracy 94.7%, Uniqueness > 0.4, Logical Coherence 0.83.
- Relative improvements over vanilla model: 60% in pass rate, 28% in format accuracy, 698% in logical coherence.
- Scalable generation: 500 compliant dialogues per category produced from only 10 seed instances.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
**Claim:** Embedding a 26-filter verification pipeline directly into the iterative Self-Instruct generation loop ensures that only high-quality, compliant dialogues are used for fine-tuning, thereby internalizing regulatory and realism constraints into the model.
**Mechanism:** Starting from 10 seed instances per distress category, the system repeatedly (a) generates a realistic context from public AIS/geographic data, (b) prompts the LLM with the context plus 5 examples (3 seed, 2 previously accepted synthetic) to create a new dialogue, and (c) applies 26 fine-grained filters (entity accuracy, hallucination detection, SMCP compliance, logical consistency, uniqueness). Only dialogues passing every filter enter the instance pool and ultimately the training set. This closed loop means the model is fine-tuned exclusively on vetted examples, so compliance becomes a learned property rather than a post-hoc correction.
**Core assumption:** That training data filtered for compliance and realism will produce a model that inherently generates outputs adhering to those same criteria.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "compliance-aware Self-Instruct methodology that embeds a 26-filter verification pipeline"; Section 2.3 details algorithm and filter taxonomy; Appendix D lists filters.

### Mechanism 2
**Claim:** Parameter-efficient LoRA fine-tuning allows effective domain adaptation of a large LLM while drastically reducing computational costs, making deployment feasible on resource-constrained maritime systems.
**Mechanism:** LoRA freezes original model weights and learns low-rank decomposition matrices (rank r=256) for attention and feed-forward layers. Only 671M parameters (8.35% of 8B) were trained. The synthetic dialogues from the Self-Instruct pipeline served as training data. The resulting adapters achieve 87% pass rates and high evaluation metrics.
**Core assumption:** That a low-rank update is sufficient to capture the domain-specific knowledge required for generating SMCP-compliant, realistic maritime dialogues.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract mentions "parameter-efficient LoRA fine-tuning"; Section 2.4 describes LoRA config; Section 3.2 reports metrics.

### Mechanism 3
**Claim:** Grounding dialogue contexts in real-world maritime data (AIS, GeoNames, GSHHG) ensures that synthetic dialogues incorporate authentic vessel identifiers, coordinates, and geographic relationships, thereby enhancing factual accuracy and operational realism.
**Mechanism:** The context generator samples from public datasets: vessel names, MMSI, call signs, types, coordinates from AIS; nearby landmarks, ports, harbors, water bodies from GeoNames and GSHHG. These structured contexts are fed to the LLM, which must reproduce the information accurately (enforced by filters). The use of real entities prevents generic or hallucinated details and anchors scenarios in plausible geography.
**Core assumption:** That publicly available maritime datasets are sufficiently comprehensive and up-to-date to serve as reliable context backbones.
**Evidence anchors:** Abstract states "using public AIS and geographic datasets"; Section 2.1 lists datasets; Section 2.3 describes context generation; Appendix D defines context fields and entity-verification filters.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Seed Store -> Context Engine -> Instance Generator -> Filter Suite -> Instance Pool -> LoRA Trainer -> Evaluator -> Output

**Critical path:**
1. Initialize pool with seed instances.
2. Repeat per category until 500 accepted dialogues:
   a. Generate context from real data.
   b. Select 5 pool instances (3 seed, 2 synthetic).
   c. Prompt LLM to generate dialogue with context + examples.
   d. Apply all 26 filters.
   e. If passed, add to pool.
3. Train LoRA adapter on 500 dialogues.
4. Evaluate adapter by generating 100 new dialogues and scoring.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Filter strictness vs. yield: More filters increase compliance but may slow dataset growth.
- LoRA rank (r): Higher rank captures nuance but increases parameters; r=256 chosen as balance.
- Seed diversity: Only 10 seeds risk overfitting; mixing synthetic examples promotes diversity but may propagate errors.
- Context fidelity vs. scenario coverage: Real data ensures realism but limits scenarios to dataset coverage (e.g., no custom port layouts).

**Failure signatures:**
- Low filter pass rate (<50%): Filters too aggressive or LLM cannot meet constraints.
- Low Uniqueness: Mode collapse or insufficient diversity in prompts/contexts.
- Poor Logical Coherence: Dialogues lack coherent turn-taking or operational plausibility.
- High hallucination rates: Incomplete coverage of hallucination checks.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Vanilla model baseline: Prompt Llama 3.1 8B with context-only. Results: 0% valid dialogues.
2. Self-Instruct generation (no fine-tuning): Use pipeline without fine-tuning. Success rates 24–53%.
3. LoRA fine-tuning: Train adapters on 500 filtered dialogues. Achieves 87% valid, high metrics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Adapting to non-distress communications:** How can the framework generate ship-to-ship coordination or port operations dialogues while maintaining SMCP compliance?
   - **Basis:** Section 4 states contextual expansion will include additional communication types.
   - **Why unresolved:** Pipeline tailored to distress; different types may require filter modifications.
   - **Evidence needed:** Comparative experiments on non-distress categories with similar quality metrics.

2. **Acoustic characteristics for TTS-generated dialogues:** What acoustic factors must be simulated to train ASR systems, and how does synthetic speech compare to real recordings?
   - **Basis:** Section 4 proposes converting dialogues to synthetic speech via TTS.
   - **Why unresolved:** Text generation only; acoustic challenges (noise, interference) not addressed.
   - **Evidence needed:** TTS with controlled noise profiles evaluated on real VHF recordings (word error rates).

3. **Optimal real-to-synthetic ratio:** What blend of real and synthetic dialogues yields robust downstream AI models?
   - **Basis:** Section 4 suggests hybrid adaptation strategies.
   - **Why unresolved:** Current work uses only synthetic; mixing effects unexplored.
   - **Evidence needed:** Systematic experiments varying real/synthetic proportions, evaluating on held-out real data.

4. **Impact of larger models or maritime pre-training:** Do larger LLMs or domain-specific pre-training reduce hallucinations and improve compliance beyond LoRA?
   - **Basis:** Section 4 notes only Llama 3.1 8B tested; larger/multimodal variants may help.
   - **Why unresolved:** No comparison across model sizes or domain pre-training.
   - **Evidence needed:** Evaluate larger models or maritime pre-trained models with same pipeline.

## Limitations
- **Filter implementation gap:** Full code/logic for 26 filters not public; exact thresholds/regex unspecified.
- **Seed diversity limitation:** Only 10 seeds per category may constrain variation; error propagation possible.
- **Dataset coverage bias:** AIS data focused on US/Denmark; geographic diversity (e.g., Asian routes) limited.

## Confidence
- **Mechanism 1 (closed-loop filtering):** High internal validity (algorithm details provided), Medium external validity (no independent replication).
- **Mechanism 2 (LoRA efficiency):** Medium (results strong within study, no ablation on rank; neighbor corpus lacks maritime LoRA validation).
- **Mechanism 3 (real-world grounding):** Medium (paper metrics solid, but neighbor paper highlights AIS quality risks not fully mitigated).
