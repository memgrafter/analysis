# HypRAG: Hyperbolic Dense Retrieval for Retrieval Augmented Generation

- Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07739

## Executive Summary
HypRAG addresses a fundamental limitation of standard dense retrieval: Euclidean embeddings struggle to preserve the hierarchical structure of language. This leads to spurious similarities and hallucinations in retrieval‑augmented generation (RAG). The proposed solution uses hyperbolic geometry (Lorentz model) to naturally encode hierarchical relationships, where radial distance from the origin reflects specificity. Two architectures are introduced: HyTE‑FH, a fully hyperbolic transformer built from Lorentzian layers, and HyTE‑H, which projects pretrained Euclidean embeddings into hyperbolic space. A key contribution is the Outward Einstein Midpoint (OEM) pooling, which prevents the radial contraction that standard pooling methods cause in hyperbolic space. The method is trained in three stages—hyperbolic masked language modeling (HyTE‑FH only), large‑scale unsupervised contrastive learning, and supervised contrastive fine‑tuning on BEIR. Experiments show HyTE‑FH beats Euclidean baselines on MTEB, while HyTE‑H achieves up to 29% improvement in context relevance and answer relevance on RAGBench, despite using smaller models. Analysis confirms that hyperbolic embeddings encode specificity through radial norms, with specific concepts exhibiting over 20% larger radii than general ones.

## Method Summary
HypRAG proposes hyperbolic dense retrieval through two model variants. HyTE‑FH is a fully hyperbolic 12‑layer transformer that replaces Euclidean operations with Lorentzian counterparts: linear layers, layer normalization, residual connections, and self‑attention using geodesic distances. Token representations are aggregated with the novel Outward Einstein Midpoint (OEM) pooling, which weights tokens based on their radial norm to avoid the radial collapse that occurs with mean pooling. HyTE‑H, a hybrid approach, starts from a pretrained Euclidean encoder (e.g., ModernBERT) and adds a hyperbolic projection layer to map embeddings into Lorentz space before applying OEM pooling. Both models share a three‑stage training pipeline: (1) hyperbolic masked language modeling on Wikipedia (HyTE‑FH only) with a Lorentz‑softmax head; (2) unsupervised contrastive pretraining on ~235 M text pairs using a contrastive loss based on negative geodesic similarity; (3) supervised contrastive fine‑tuning on BEIR training splits. For retrieval, the similarity between a query and a document is computed as the negative Lorentzian distance (−d<sub>K</sub>), and top‑k documents are fed to a generator (Llama‑3.1‑8B‑Instruct). The approach yields significant gains in retrieval quality and downstream RAG performance.

## Key Results
- HyTE‑FH achieves higher mean scores than Euclidean baselines on the MTEB embedding benchmark.
- HyTE‑H delivers up to 29% improvement in context relevance and answer relevance on RAGBench, while using smaller models.
- Hyperbolic embeddings encode specificity via radial norms: specific concepts show >20% larger radii than general concepts, confirming hierarchical encoding.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Euclidean space cannot preserve hierarchical relationships—items that are semantically distant in a taxonomy may appear close due to the flat geometry, leading to spurious matches and hallucinations in RAG. Hyperbolic space, with its negative curvature, naturally models tree‑like structures: the radial distance from the origin corresponds to specificity (general concepts near the origin, specific ones farther out). This geometric property yields a built‑in specificity signal that Euclidean embeddings lack. However, aggregating tokens (e.g., via mean pooling) in hyperbolic space pulls representations toward the origin, erasing the radial distinction. The Outward Einstein Midpoint (OEM) pooling counteracts this by weighting tokens according to their radial norm, preserving the hierarchical signal. Consequently, query‑document similarity computed via geodesic distance better reflects true semantic relatedness, leading to more precise retrieval and reduced hallucinations in RAG.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Hyperbolic Geometry (Lorentz Model)** – Provides negative curvature to embed hierarchical structures. *Why needed:* Trees require exponential growth, which hyperbolic space accommodates. *Quick check:* In trained embeddings, norms increase along a path from root to leaves.
2. **Lorentzian Distance** – Measures geodesic distance in hyperbolic space, analogous to cosine similarity in Euclidean space. *Why needed:* To compute query‑document similarity that respects hierarchical structure. *Quick check:* For a fixed query, relevant documents should have smaller Lorentzian distances than irrelevant ones.
3. **Outward Einstein Midpoint (OEM) Pooling** – Aggregates token embeddings with radial‑dependent weights to avoid radial contraction. *Why needed:* Mean pooling in hyperbolic space pulls points toward the origin, blurring specificity cues. *Quick check:* After pooling, document norms should reflect average topic specificity rather than shrinking.
4. **Contrastive Learning** – Aligns embeddings of relevant query‑document pairs while pushing apart unrelated pairs. *Why needed:* Direct supervision for retrieval tasks without requiring labeled classification. *Quick check:* Positive pairs exhibit higher (Lorentzian) similarity than negatives after training.
5. **Hybrid Projection (HyTE‑H)** – Maps pretrained Euclidean embeddings into hyperbolic space. *Why needed:* Leverages large‑scale Euclidean pretrained models while gaining hyperbolic benefits. *Quick check:* Projected embeddings maintain neighbor similarity while gaining increased radial separation for specifics.
6. **Three‑Stage Training** – Curriculum from masked language modeling to unsupervised to supervised contrastive learning. *Why needed:* Staged learning stabilizes hyperbolic optimization and transfers knowledge effectively. *Quick check:* Each stage added improves downstream retrieval metrics.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map (HyTE‑FH):  
Input Text → Tokenization → Hyperbolic Transformer (Lorentz Linear + LayerNorm + Self‑Attention + Residual) → Outward Einstein Midpoint Pooling → Hyperbolic Embedding  

Component map (HyTE‑H):  
Input Text → Tokenization → Euclidean Encoder (pretrained) → Hyperbolic Projection → Outward Einstein Midpoint Pooling → Hyperbolic Embedding  

Critical path: Tokenization → hyperbolic encoding → OEM pooling → embedding → similarity via negative geodesic distance → retrieval → generation.  

Design tradeoffs:  
- HyTE‑FH offers end‑to‑end hyperbolic consistency but suffers from training instability and requires custom Lorentz ops.  
- HyTE‑H gains stability by initializing from Euclidean weights and converges faster, but the projection layer may limit expressiveness.  
- OEM pooling preserves radial information at the cost of an extra hyperparameter (power p), whereas simpler pooling methods risk radial collapse.  

Failure signatures:  
1. Radial contraction – norms do not increase with specificity; detectable by probing on a hierarchical test set (e.g., WordNet).  
2. Training instability – NaNs during backpropagation, often from extreme Lorentzian inner products or divisions by near‑zero norms.  
3. Hubness – many queries retrieve the same generic documents; check precision@k diversity.  
4. Overfitting – validation loss rises after initial decrease; monitor early stopping.  

First experiments:  
1. **Radial norm probe** – Encode a known hierarchy (e.g., animal → mammal → dog) and confirm monotonic norm increase.  
2. **OEM ablation** – Replace OEM with mean pooling on a small dataset; compare retrieval metrics and radial norm behavior.  
3. **HyTE‑FH vs HyTE‑H** – Train both for a few steps on identical data; compare loss stability and embedding quality on an MTEB subset.
