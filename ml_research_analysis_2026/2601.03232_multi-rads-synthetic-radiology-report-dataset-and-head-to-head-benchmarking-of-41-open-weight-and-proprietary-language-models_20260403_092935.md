# Multi-RADS Synthetic Radiology Report Dataset and Head-to-Head Benchmarking of 41 Open-Weight and Proprietary Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2601.03232
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03232
- Reference count: 28
- Primary result: GPT-5.2 achieved 99.8% validity and 81.1% accuracy on RADS classification; pooled open models reached 96.8% validity and 61.1% accuracy, with top 20-32B models nearing 99% validity and up to 78% accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RXL-RADSet, a radiologist-verified synthetic dataset of 1,600 radiology reports covering 10 RADS frameworks across 4 imaging modalities. The authors benchmark 41 open-weight language models (0.135–32B parameters) and OpenAI's GPT-5.2 on the task of predicting a single RADS category from complete reports. Using guided prompting—system instructions plus a user query—GPT-5.2 achieved near-perfect validity (99.8%) and 81.1% accuracy. Open models averaged 96.8% validity and 61.1% accuracy, with the largest (20-32B) reaching ~99% validity and up to 78% accuracy. Performance scaled with model size, showing an inflection at ≥10B parameters. Guided prompting significantly improved both validity and accuracy compared to zero-shot prompting.

## Method Summary
The evaluation framework centers on RXL-RADSet, a synthetic collection of radiology reports structured into Clinical Information, Technique, Findings, and Impression sections. The task is single-label classification into one of the predefined RADS categories for each framework. Researchers applied a guided prompting strategy: a RADS-specific system prompt followed by a user prompt asking the model to read the report and output the appropriate category. Inference was run with deterministic decoding (temperature=0, max_tokens=50). Open-weight models were executed locally via Ollama on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 (24GB VRAM), using primarily 4-bit quantized versions. GPT-5.2 was queried through the OpenAI API. Outputs were normalized and evaluated against two metrics: validity (whether the output belongs to the allowed set) and accuracy (collapsed major categories, exact match after normalization). The experimental design allowed direct comparison of model architectures, scales, and prompting styles under identical conditions.

## Key Results
- Validity: GPT-5.2 99.8%; pooled open models 96.8%; top 20-32B open models ~99%.
- Accuracy: GPT-5.2 81.1%; pooled open models 61.1%; best open models (32B) up to 78%.
- Guided prompting improved validity (99.2% vs 96.7%) and accuracy (78.5% vs 69.6%) over zero-shot across models.
- Performance scaled with parameter count, with a noticeable inflection point at models ≥10B.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Guided prompting provides explicit context, constraints, and output formatting instructions, reducing ambiguity and hallucination. System prompts encode the RADS taxonomy and required response structure, steering the model toward valid categories. The predictive power scales with model size: larger models possess richer medical knowledge and better instruction-following capabilities, as reflected by the performance jump at ≥10B parameters. Synthetic reports, while simplified, supply consistent patterns that align with the prompt's expectations, enabling high validity. Together, these factors yield the observed gains in both validity and accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Radiology Report → Prompt Builder → LLM Inference → Output Parsing → Metrics Calculator → Result Storage

Critical path: Load quantized model → Receive report → Construct guided prompt (system + user) → Generate token sequence (temp=0, max_tokens=50) → Extract category string → Normalize → Compare to ground truth → Record validity and accuracy.

Design tradeoffs:
- Quantization vs. accuracy: lower bit reduces VRAM but may degrade nuanced reasoning.
- Model size vs. inference speed: larger models are slower but more accurate; 4-bit helps but still limited by VRAM.
- Guided prompt complexity: more elaborate prompts may improve performance but increase token count and risk of truncation.
- Synthetic vs. real data: synthetic ensures label fidelity but may underrepresent linguistic variation and noise.

Failure signatures:
- Invalid output: model produces text outside the allowed RADS set (e.g., "Other", extra commentary). Often seen in small models (<1B).
- VRAM overflow: OOM errors when loading non-quantized large models (e.g., Llama4 16x17b).
- Slow inference: high latency with 32B models even on RTX 3090, especially without GPU offloading.
- Category collapse: model defaults to a few frequent labels, reducing per-category accuracy.

First experiments:
1. Reproduce guided prompting results: run all 41 models on RXL-RADSet using the exact prompts and compute metrics.
2. Zero-shot vs. guided ablation: remove system prompt and compare performance drop.
3. Prompt format stress test: vary the delimiter (e.g., "Answer with BI-RADS:" vs. freeform) and measure validity changes.

## Limitations
- Prompt sensitivity: exact system prompts were not published; guided results may be irreproducible without them.
- Model tag ambiguity: Ollama model identifiers (quantization variants) are unspecified, leading to potential setup differences.
- Synthetic data generalizability: RXL-RADSet's simplicity may not capture the noise and variability of real-world radiology reports, limiting clinical applicability.

## Confidence
- Validity results: High
- Accuracy scaling with model size: Medium
- Guided prompting effectiveness: Medium
- Real-world generalization: Low
