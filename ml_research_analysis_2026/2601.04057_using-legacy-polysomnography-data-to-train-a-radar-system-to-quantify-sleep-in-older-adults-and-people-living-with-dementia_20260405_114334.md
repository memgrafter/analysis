# Using Legacy Polysomnography Data to Train a Radar System to Quantify Sleep in Older Adults and People living with Dementia

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04057
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04057
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** Achieved 79.5% accuracy and Cohen's Kappa of 0.65 for 4-class sleep staging on radar data using transfer learning with adversarial domain adaptation.

## Executive Summary
Sleep staging with contactless radar is promising but hampered by limited labeled radar data. This study leverages a large polysomnography (PSG) dataset (STAGES, N=1662) to train a deep model that is then adapted to a small radar dataset (47 older adults, mean age 71.2, including 18 with Alzheimer’s disease). The end‑to‑end architecture processes respiration and motion signals, employs a gradient reversal layer to align feature distributions between PSG and radar domains, and fuses modalities to capture complementary physiological cues. On the radar validation set, the model reached 79.5% accuracy and a Cohen's Kappa of 0.65, substantially outperforming a radar‑only baseline (∼71%). The results demonstrate that transfer learning with adversarial domain adaptation can effectively overcome data scarcity and enable robust, non‑invasive sleep monitoring for older adults and people with dementia.

## Method Summary
The task is 4‑class sleep stage classification (wake, REM, light, deep) from radar‑derived respiration and motion signals. The authors use the STAGES PSG database as the source domain (1662 participants) and a proprietary radar dataset collected at the University of Surrey as the target (47 subjects). Both modalities are downsampled to 10 Hz and normalized per subject. Inputs are segmented into 30‑second epochs and grouped into sequences of length L_seq (optimized to 32 epochs).

The model comprises a shared 1‑D CNN (six residual blocks with increasing dilation) that extracts epoch‑level features from each modality; the features are concatenated and fed into a two‑layer bidirectional LSTM with time‑wise attention, producing a 256‑dimensional contextual embedding. Two heads attach to the embedding: a classifier (two‑layer MLP + softmax) for sleep stages, and a domain discriminator (two‑layer MLP + sigmoid) preceded by a gradient reversal layer (GRL). Training minimizes a combined loss: weighted cross‑entropy for classification (L_cls), binary cross‑entropy for domain discrimination (L_dom), and a label‑aware contrastive loss (L_align). The GRL inverts gradients from L_dom when updating the feature extractor, enforcing domain‑invariant representations. An adversarial weight λ_adv is ramped from 0.2 to 1 during training. The model is trained with mixed batches from both domains and evaluated via 5‑fold cross‑validation on the radar data.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy & Kappa:** 79.5% accuracy and Cohen's κ = 0.65 on the radar validation set, exceeding the radar‑only baseline by ~8%.
- **Multimodal benefit:** Incorporating both respiration and motion signals improved accuracy by 6% compared to either modality alone.
- **Domain alignment:** t‑SNE visualizations confirmed that adversarial training mixes PSG and radar samples across sleep stages, reducing covariate shift.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Transfer learning from a large PSG dataset to a small radar dataset provides robust feature extraction capabilities that compensate for limited radar data.
- **Mechanism:** The feature extractor is first exposed to diverse sleep patterns in the STAGES PSG dataset (N=1662), learning generalizable representations of sleep stages. These pretrained features are then adapted to the radar domain via adversarial training, leveraging prior physiological knowledge despite sensor differences.
- **Core assumption:** Underlying physiological dynamics (respiratory patterns, movement) that characterize sleep stages are modality-agnostic; PSG and radar capture different manifestations of the same underlying processes.
- **Evidence anchors:** 
  - [abstract] The study explicitly aims to address limited radar data via transfer learning with adversarial domain adaptation.
  - [section II.A] Source domain size (1662) vastly exceeds target (47), highlighting the data scarcity challenge.
- **Break condition:** If PSG‑radar feature distributions are fundamentally non-overlapping (e.g., due to severe radar corruption or extreme population mismatch), transfer may fail or cause negative transfer.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Adversarial domain adaptation aligns feature distributions between PSG and radar data, reducing domain shift and enabling the sleep classifier to generalize.
- **Mechanism:** A domain discriminator (trained to distinguish PSG vs. radar features) and a gradient reversal layer force the feature extractor to produce domain‑invariant representations. This minimax game ensures features remain discriminative for sleep staging while becoming indistinguishable across domains.
- **Core assumption:** Domain shift between PSG and radar is primarily distributional (covariate shift) rather than due to label mismatch or fundamentally different generating processes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section III.D] "Adversarial alignment using a gradient reversal layer (GRL)... enabling the encoder to preserve sleep-stage discriminability while making the learned feature distribution invariant."
  - [section IV.B] t‑SNE visualizations show feature clustering by sleep stage rather than by domain when adversarial training is enabled.
- **Break condition:** Noisy or inconsistent labels across domains could collapse class separability; an overly strong discriminator may destabilize training.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Multimodal fusion of respiratory and motion signals improves accuracy because these channels provide complementary information about sleep architecture.
- **Mechanism:** Respiratory signals (bandpassed 0.1–0.6 Hz) reflect autonomic regulation changes (e.g., regular breathing in deep sleep) and help distinguish REM/deep sleep. Motion signals (0.25–2.5 Hz) detect gross body movement, excelling at wake detection. The CNN extracts modality‑specific features which are concatenated, allowing the classifier to leverage both.
- **Core assumption:** Sleep stage transitions correlate with coordinated changes in both cardiopulmonary activity and motor output; neither modality alone suffices for full staging.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section IV.C] Models with motion alone had few false positives in wake but struggled with other stages; adding respiration yielded a 6% accuracy gain.
  - [section I] Sleep stages exhibit significant differences in movement, breathing, and heart rate.
- **Break condition:** Severe degradation of one modality (e.g., motion artifacts, respiratory signal loss) could harm performance if fusion is forced.

## Foundational Learning
### Transfer Learning
- **Why needed here:** Data scarcity in the target (radar) domain prevents training a robust model from scratch. Transfer learning allows reusing knowledge from a large source (PSG) dataset to initialize the feature extractor, avoiding overfitting.
- **Quick check question:** What is the key risk when transferring features from PSG to radar, and how does domain adaptation mitigate it?

### Adversarial Domain Adaptation
- **Why needed here:** PSG and radar signals have different distributions; without alignment, the transferred features remain biased toward the source domain. Adversarial training explicitly aligns distributions to make features domain‑invariant.
- **Quick check question:** Explain the role of the gradient reversal layer (GRL) in the minimax game between feature extractor and discriminator.

### Temporal Sequence Modeling for Sleep Staging
- **Why needed here:** Sleep staging is temporally coherent; the label of an epoch depends on neighboring epochs. A bidirectional LSTM with attention captures long‑range dependencies (up to 32 epochs) to improve epoch‑level predictions.
- **Quick check question:** Why is a bidirectional LSTM with attention preferred over a simple sliding‑window classifier for sleep staging?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Raw respiration & motion signals → Normalization (per subject) → 30‑s epoch segmentation → CNN feature extractor (6 residual blocks) → Epoch embeddings → BiLSTM with attention (sequence length L_seq=32) → Contextual embedding → [Classifier C (softmax), Discriminator D (sigmoid via GRL)]

### Critical path
During training, each mini‑batch contains mixed PSG and radar samples. The feature extractor F generates embeddings. The classifier C computes L_cls (weighted cross‑entropy). The discriminator D, preceded by a gradient reversal layer (GRL), computes L_dom (binary cross‑entropy). A contrastive loss L_align enforces label‑aware alignment. The total loss is L = L_cls - λ_adv * L_dom + λ_align * L_align. The GRL inverts gradients from L_dom when backpropagating to F, creating a minimax game. λ_adv is scheduled (sigmoid ramp from 0.2 to 1). Model parameters are updated with Adam (initial lr=0.001, halved every 10 epochs, early stopping after 15 epochs without improvement). Predictions on test sequences are obtained by multiplicative aggregation of overlapping windows.

### Design tradeoffs
- **Sequence length (L_seq):** Longer sequences (up to 32 epochs) improve accuracy by capturing longer temporal context but increase memory usage and latency.
- **Adversarial weight schedule:** λ_adv must ramp gradually; too high too early destabilizes training, too low yields insufficient alignment.
- **Class weighting:** Inverse frequency weighting per training batch mitigates stage imbalance; miscalculation skews loss and degrades rare‑stage recall.

### Failure signatures
- **t‑SNE inspection:** Features remain segregated by domain (PSG vs. radar clusters) → alignment failed.
- **Confidence scores:** Low confidence (high normalized entropy) across many epochs indicates model uncertainty, often during transitions or noisy signals.
- **Stage‑specific F1:** Disproportionately low F1 for REM or deep sleep suggests modality insufficiency or residual domain mismatch.
- **Parameter sensitivity:** Sharp accuracy drop when λ_align is too high indicates contrastive loss forcing harmful class mixing.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Baseline replication:** Train the model on radar data only (no PSG, no domain adaptation). Expect ∼71% accuracy, confirming data scarcity.
2. **Ablation – multimodal:** Train separate models with respiration‑only, motion‑only, and both modalities. Validate that fusion improves accuracy by ∼6%.
3. **Domain alignment check:** Visualize t‑SNNE of feature embeddings from F under (a) no adversarial training and (b) full model. Expect overlapping clusters by sleep stage only with adversarial training.

## Limitations
- **Data access restrictions:** Both the STAGES PSG database and the Surrey radar dataset are not publicly available, preventing independent replication.
- **Incomplete architectural details:** Some hyperparameters (e.g., exact CNN kernel sizes, dropout rates) are inferred but not exhaustively specified, risking implementation drift.
- **Population‑specific generalizability:** The cohort (older adults, some with dementia) may limit applicability to younger or healthier individuals; radar signals may vary with body composition or placement.
