# Optimal Variance-Dependent Regret Bounds for Infinite-Horizon MDPs
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.23926  
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23926  
- Reference count: 40  
- One-line primary result: FOCUS achieves optimal variance-dependent regret Õ(√(SA Var) + lower-order terms) for infinite-horizon MDPs under both average-reward and discounted objectives.

## Executive Summary
The paper studies online reinforcement learning in infinite-horizon Markov decision processes (MDPs) with unknown transitions, targeting average-reward and γ-discounted regret objectives. Existing model-based algorithms suffer from large burn-in costs and do not adapt to instance-specific variance. The authors introduce FOCUS (Fully Optimizing Clipped UCB Solver), a tractable UCB-style algorithm that combines span clipping, Bernstein-style bonuses, and full optimization of Q-estimates via repeated application of the empirical Bellman operator. FOCUS attains the first optimal variance-dependent regret guarantees of the form Õ(√(SA Var) + lower-order terms), where Var is the cumulative transition variance. These results yield minimax-optimal √(SA∥h⋆∥_sp T) worst-case bounds and nearly constant regret for deterministic MDPs. The analysis reveals a fundamental gap between settings with and without prior knowledge of the optimal bias span ∥h⋆∥_sp.

## Method Summary
FOCUS operates in episodes defined by doubling visit counts. It maintains empirical counts N(s,a,s') and N(s,a). At the start of an episode, it computes empirical transitions bP_k and a failure-probability-adjusted bonus  
b(s,a,V) = max(4√(V(bP_k,V)·U/N_k), 32HU/N_k),  
with U = log(9S²AT/δ). Then it runs optimistic clipped value iteration on the empirical Bellman operator  
bT_k(Q) = r + γ bP_k (Clip_H(M Q)) + γ b(s,a,Clip_H(M Q)),  
where Clip_H enforces a span ≤ H and M duplicates values for clipping. The iteration continues until the maximum change is below ε_k(1-γ)/(1+32HU), ensuring near-convergence. The resulting Q-function bQ_k guides greedy action selection until the next episode, triggered when an (s,a) count doubles. This design yields optimism, variance adaptivity, and controlled bias.

## Key Results
- FOCUS achieves variance-dependent regret Õ(√(SA Var) + lower-order terms) for both average-reward and γ-discounted settings, where Var is the cumulative transition variance under the optimal policy.
- The worst-case bound becomes minimax-optimal √(SA∥h⋆∥_sp T), matching known lower bounds when substituting Var ≤ ∥h⋆∥_sp.
- With prior knowledge of ∥h⋆∥_sp, lower-order terms scale as ∥h⋆∥_sp S² A (proven optimal). Without prior, any algorithm needs at least ∥h⋆∥_sp² S A, and FOCUS attains ∥h⋆∥_sp² S³ A, nearly matching—a fundamental gap between the two scenarios.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
FOCUS succeeds through the synergy of three pillars. First, UCB-style optimism ensures the empirical MDP’s optimal value upper‑bounds the true optimum, driving exploration. Second, Bernstein‑style bonuses adapt to transition variance; the √(V/N) term tightens bonuses when outcomes are less stochastic. Third, span clipping imposes a bounded value range, preserving the contraction property of the Bellman operator despite estimation errors. Fully optimizing Q‑estimates by repeated empirical Bellman updates until near convergence minimizes approximation error. Together these yield a regret bound that depends on cumulative variance, while retaining worst‑case optimality.

## Foundational Learning
- **UCB-style optimism** – Ensures coverage of the true optimal value; quick check: bonus decreases as N(s,a) increases.  
- **Bernstein inequality** – Enables variance‑adaptive bonus; quick check: bonus includes √(variance / N), which vanishes faster for deterministic transitions.  
- **Span clipping** – Bounds the span of value functions to maintain contraction; quick check: computed bV_k satisfies max bV_k – min bV_k ≤ H.  
- **Doubling episode scheme** – Limits episodes logarithmically; quick check: episode count ≤ SA·log₂ T.  
- **Empirical Bellman operator convergence** – Guarantees accurate policy evaluation; quick check: iterate until max_change < ε_k.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Counts (N) -> Empirical Transition (bP) -> Bonus Computation (b) -> Clipped Value Iteration (bT) -> Greedy Action Selection

**Critical path**:  
1. Maintain state‑action counts and observe outcomes.  
2. When N(s,a) doubles from its value at the last episode start for that (s,a), begin a new episode.  
3. Compute empirical transitions and Bernstein bonuses.  
4. Run optimistic clipped value iteration until convergence criterion met; obtain bQ_k.  
5. Act greedily w.r.t. bQ_k until next episode trigger.  
6. Repeat until total steps T exhausted.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Bernstein bonus complexity offers variance adaptivity but requires estimating V(bP,V), which can be noisy with limited data.  
- Span clipping parameter H must be at least ∥h⋆∥_sp to preserve optimism; too large weakens contraction, too small may exclude the optimal span.  
- Convergence threshold ε_k balances evaluation accuracy and computational cost; the theoretical choice may be conservative.  
- Doubling episodes yields logarithmic episodes but may delay using newer data after distribution shifts.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Regret scales as √(SAT) on low‑variance MDPs (lack of variance adaptivity).  
- bV_k span exceeds H, indicating clipping violation.  
- Episode count grows super‑logarithmically (e.g., > SA·log T), suggesting incorrect doubling logic.  
- Greedy actions fail to improve performance, hinting at optimism failure or bonus mis‑calibration.

**First experiments**:  
1. **Variance scaling test** – Generate tabular MDPs with fixed S, A but varying transition stochasticity. Compute cumulative transition variance Var under the optimal policy. Plot total regret against √(SA Var) and verify a linear trend with a small constant.  
2. **Deterministic limit** – Run FOCUS on fully deterministic MDPs (Var = 0). Regret should grow polylogarithmically (sub‑√T). Measure regret over increasing T and confirm sub‑√T growth.  
3. **Prior knowledge impact** – Compare FOCUS with an oracle that knows ∥h⋆∥_sp (optimally sets H) versus the prior‑free version. Subtract the √(SA Var) component from regret; the residual should scale as ∥h⋆∥_sp S² A (oracle) versus ∥h⋆∥_sp² S³ A (prior‑free), aligning with theory.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several unresolved questions and future research directions:

- Can the Bernstein bonus constants (e.g., 4, 32) be tightened to reduce the practical overhead without degrading the Õ(√(SA Var)) regret guarantee?
- Is the extra S² factor in the prior-free lower-order term a fundamental information-theoretic limitation, or can it be removed by a sharper analysis or algorithmic innovation?
- Does the identified gap between settings with and without prior knowledge of the optimal bias span ∥h⋆∥_sp reflect an inherent trade‑off, or can future algorithms achieve matching bounds without such prior?
- How robust is FOCUS to the choice of the span clipping parameter H? Can H be estimated adaptively during learning?

## Limitations
- Theoretical constants (e.g., 4, 32 in Bernstein bonus, 9 in union bound) are not proven minimal and may degrade practical high‑probability guarantees.  
- Performance is sensitive to the correct specification of the span clipping parameter H; mis‑specification breaks optimism.  
- The prior‑free lower‑order term contains an extra S² factor compared to the stated lower bound; it is unclear whether this gap is fundamental or an artifact of the analysis.
