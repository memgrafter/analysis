# Decoding AI Authorship: Can LLMs Truly Mimic Human Style Across Literature and Politics?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.23219
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23219
- **Reference count:** 1
- **Primary result:** State-of-the-art LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Claude Sonnet 3.5) cannot convincingly mimic human stylistic signatures; their outputs are detectable with >95% accuracy using a compact set of stylometric features, primarily due to lower stochastic variability (perplexity) and a systemic positivity bias.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates the capacity of leading LLMs to mimic the stylistic signatures of four distinct human authors—Walt Whitman, William Wordsworth, Donald Trump, and Barack Obama—using zero-shot prompting. AI-generated and human-authored texts were compared through a combination of BERT-based neural classifiers and an interpretable XGBoost model trained on eight stylometric features (LIWC psycholinguistics, readability, perplexity). Results show AI mimicry is highly detectable across all model-author pairs. A key finding is that LLMs produce text with significantly lower perplexity (stochastic regularity) and diverge on affective dimensions (Tone, Authenticity), indicating a failure to replicate the nuanced variance inherent to human writing. While LLMs converge with humans on surface readability, they fall short on deeper stylistic markers, suggesting fundamental gaps in modeling authorial "voice."

## Method Summary
The study generated synthetic texts from three LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Claude Sonnet 3.5) using zero-shot prompts ("Write a [poem/tweet] exclusively in the style of [Author] on [Topic]") with temperature 1.0, matching the quantity of human corpora (300 poems per poet, 3,000 tweets per politician). Thematic alignment was enforced via keywords derived from human source metadata. Features extracted included LIWC-22 dimensions (Analytic, Clout, Authentic, Tone), readability scores (Flesch, Flesch-Kincaid, Gunning Fog), and perplexity scored by an external GPT-2 Large model. Classification performance was assessed via fine-tuned BERT (bert-base-uncased, stratified 5-fold CV) and XGBoost with two feature sets: TF-IDF and the eight stylometric features (nested 5-fold CV). Statistical differences were tested using Mann-Whitney U (α = .05).

## Key Results
- AI-generated mimicry is highly detectable, with XGBoost on eight stylometric features achieving accuracy comparable to high-dimensional BERT classifiers (e.g., >95% for Whitman/Sonnet 3.5, >96% for Trump/Sonnet 3.5).
- Perplexity is the primary discriminative metric; all LLM outputs show significantly lower median perplexity than human texts (p < .001), indicating reduced stochastic variability.
- LLMs diverge from humans on affective density (LIWC Tone) and analytic thinking, exhibiting a systemic "positivity bias" and higher clout/authenticity scores, while converging on surface readability metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Stochastic Regularity
- **Claim:** AI text is detectable due to lower stochastic variability (lower perplexity) than human writing.
- **Mechanism:** LLMs optimize for token prediction probability, producing outputs with tighter probability distributions and less "burstiness" than the inherent unpredictability of human cognition.
- **Core assumption:** Human authorial style includes higher lexical/syntactic variance; LLM outputs are regularized toward modal predictions.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and section 4.1 show all human authors have higher median perplexity than LLMs (e.g., Whitman: 82.10 vs. GPT-4o 58.74; Trump: 65.27 vs. Sonnet 3.5 31.92), p < .001. Tables 8–11 provide systematic comparisons.

### Mechanism 2: Affective Density Divergence
- **Claim:** LLMs fail to replicate the affective distribution of human authors, showing a systemic "positivity bias."
- **Mechanism:** RLHF tuning and safety constraints push outputs toward neutral-positive, socially acceptable tones, flattening the emotional range present in source authors (e.g., Trump's negativity, Whitman's ambivalence).
- **Core assumption:** Human writing exhibits measurable affective distributions (LIWC Tone/Authentic) reflecting idiolect, which is constrained by LLM alignment objectives.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and section 4.1 show LLMs produce significantly higher Tone scores (p < .001) across most model-author pairs; Tables 4–7 document systematic shifts.

### Mechanism 3: Latent Feature Covariance Gap
- **Claim:** Stylometric detection relies on a compact feature set because LLMs approximate surface readability but diverge on latent distributional properties.
- **Mechanism:** LLMs capture coarse syntactic complexity but cannot reproduce the fine-grained stochastic and cognitive patterning (analytical thinking, clout, authenticity) that defines an author's fingerprint.
- **Core assumption:** Human style is encoded in higher-order feature covariance, not just word choice or sentence length; LLMs capture the latter but regularize the former.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and section 4.2.2 show the 8-feature XGBoost outperforms TF-IDF (~5-10% accuracy gain). Feature importance highlights perplexity and Tone over readability. BERT accuracy is near ceiling (section 4.2.1), confirming deep embedding divergence.

Critical path: Dataset creation → Prompt/generation → Metric computation → Model training → Feature importance → Interpretation.
Design tradeoffs: Interpretability (XGBoost 8-feature) vs. raw power (BERT embeddings); metric integrity (external LM for perplexity); thematic control (keyword alignment); tokenization consistency (model-native representations).
Failure signatures: Feature collapse (non-significant stylometric differences); TF-IDF superiority (contradicts latent feature claim); model-specific artifacts; perplexity anomaly (human < AI).

1. Replicate stylometric gap for Whitman vs. Sonnet 3.5: compute all 8 features, run Mann-Whitney U; expect significant differences in Perplexity, Tone, Analytic (p < .001), non-significant readability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How generalizable are these findings across diverse languages, literary traditions, and non-Western cultural contexts? The study is limited to English and specific Western authors.
2. Can a hybrid human-AI framework, combining neural detection with expert literary/political analysis, improve accuracy and interpretability beyond current metrics?
3. What specific aspects of LLM training (RLHF, next-token prediction) causally produce the "positivity bias" and reduced stochastic variance, and can these be mitigated without catastrophic capability loss?
4. How robust are current detection methods against adversarially prompted or iteratively refined LLM outputs designed to evade known signals (perplexity, tone)?
5. Is the fundamental limitation a deficiency in capturing an author's *distributional variance* ("burstiness") versus central tendency, pointing to an architectural gap in modeling true authorial "voice"?

## Limitations
- Undisclosed thematic alignment keywords may cause topic drift, confounding style with content.
- Reliance on proprietary LIWC-22 hinders reproducibility; open-source alternatives may yield different scores.
- Evaluation limited to four authors (two poets, two politicians) and three LLMs; generalizability is uncertain.
- Only zero-shot prompting tested; advanced prompting or fine-tuning could yield more human-like outputs.
- Fixed external LM (GPT-2 Large) for perplexity may be outdated; newer models could alter the gap.

## Confidence
- **Stochastic regularity (perplexity) as primary discriminative signal:** High – large, consistent effect sizes (p < .001) across all authors and models.
- **Affective density divergence (positivity bias):** High – systematic, significant shifts in LIWC Tone/Authentic for nearly all model-author pairs.
- **Compact stylometric feature sufficiency:** High – 8-feature XGBoost matches high-dimensional BERT performance, with feature importance highlighting perplexity and Tone over readability.

## Next Checks
1. **Topic-controlled replication:** For each author-model pair, compute topical similarity (TF-IDF cosine) between human and AI texts. If similarity is low (<0.6), refine prompts using human corpus metadata to maximize alignment and re-run classifiers.
2. **Open-source feature cross-check:** Replace LIWC-22 with an open-source alternative (e.g., `liwc` Python package or Empath) to recompute Analytic, Clout, Authentic, Tone. Replicate Mann-Whitney tests to confirm affective divergence persists.
3. **Cross-domain & multi-model extension:** Add at least two authors from distinct domains (e.g., Hemingway, Orwell) and evaluate on current LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, LLaMA 3 70B). Retrain the 8-feature XGBoost; if accuracy remains >90%, the compact feature claim generalizes.
