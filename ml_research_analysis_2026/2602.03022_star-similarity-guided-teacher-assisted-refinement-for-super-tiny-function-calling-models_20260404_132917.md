# STAR: Similarity-guided Teacher-Assisted Refinement for Super-Tiny Function Calling Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.03022
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03022
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: STAR-0.6B achieves SOTA among open models under 1B on BFCLv3 (51.70) and ACEBench Normal (53.00), surpassing Llama3.1-8B and other larger models.

## Executive Summary
The paper tackles the problem of transferring function-calling capabilities from large language models to super-tiny models (under 1B parameters). Existing methods face challenges such as overfitting, training instability, brittle binary rewards, and poor synergy between knowledge distillation and reinforcement learning. STAR addresses these with two key components: Constrained Knowledge Distillation (CKD) to stabilize training and preserve exploration, and Similarity-guided RL (Sim-RL) to provide continuous, fine-grained rewards. These are orchestrated in a curriculum: first refine a teacher model with Sim-RL, then distill into a student with CKD, and finally refine the student with Sim-RL. Experiments demonstrate that STAR-0.6B achieves state-of-the-art performance among open models under 1B, scoring 51.70 on BFCLv3 and 53.00 on ACEBench Normal, outperforming Llama3.1-8B and other larger models. The synergy between CKD and Sim-RL yields robust generalization and large improvements over baselines.

## Method Summary
STAR employs a three-stage curriculum. Initially, a large teacher model (Qwen3-8B) is refined using Sim-RL to generate high-quality rollouts on a merged dataset (ToolACE, xLAM, etc.). These rollouts are filtered by a strict format checker to produce dataset D_T. Next, a super-tiny student model (e.g., Qwen3-0.6B) is trained on D_T via Constrained Knowledge Distillation (CKD). CKD combines top-k forward KL divergence (k=100) with an L1 penalty on the student's top-m probabilities that fall outside the teacher's top-k set, which suppresses confident but incorrect predictions while maintaining exploration. Finally, the student model is further refined with Sim-RL using the GRPO algorithm, where rewards combine format adherence, function-call similarity (ROUGE-L/IoU), and response similarity (ROUGE-L). The resulting model achieves strong performance on function-calling benchmarks.

## Key Results
- STAR-0.6B achieves 51.70 BFCLv3 and 53.00 ACEBench Normal, setting a new SOTA among open models under 1B parameters.
- It outperforms Llama3.1-8B (46.60 BFCLv3) and other larger models, demonstrating the efficacy of the STAR curriculum.
- Ablations confirm that CKD stabilizes training and preserves exploration, while Sim-RL provides a robust, continuous reward signal; together they yield large gains over SFT+RL and recent baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Constrained Knowledge Distillation (CKD) may stabilize training and preserve exploratory capacity for downstream RL by selectively suppressing the student's confident but incorrect tail predictions.
- **Mechanism:** CKD augments standard top-k forward KL divergence with an L1 penalty (`L_tail`) on the student's top-m probabilities that lie outside the teacher's top-k set. This directly penalizes "confident-but-wrong" logits while leaving the rest of the tail (students' low-probability tokens) unsupervised.
- **Core assumption:** The student model's over-confidence in incorrect tokens is a primary driver of training instability and reduced policy entropy, harming subsequent RL.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1.3] Details the `L_tail` formulation and gradient analysis showing it increases gradients for confident-but-wrong tokens and reduces them for other non-top-k tokens.
  - [section 3.1.2] Shows empirically that standard top-k RKL/AKL causes training collapse and yields lower policy entropy than top-k FKL, harming RL performance.
  - [abstract] States CKD "suppress[es] confidently incorrect predictions, ensuring training stability while preserving exploration capacity for downstream RL."
- **Break condition:** If the student model's distribution is already highly peaked (low entropy) or if the teacher's top-k set (`k`) is too small, the tail regularization may over-penalize, excessively flattening the distribution and harming the imitation objective.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Similarity-guided RL (Sim-RL) may provide a richer, more robust learning signal than binary rewards by assigning partial credit for semantically equivalent outputs, improving generalization on multi-solution tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Sim-RL replaces brittle binary success/failure rewards with a continuous score computed via ROUGE-L (for strings) and exact match (for numbers/booleans) at the argument level. This reward is combined with a strict format reward in a composite formula.
- **Core assumption:** Function calling has multiple valid solutions (e.g., optional arguments, phrasing variations), and a binary signal is too sparse and punitive for effective policy learning in small models.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.2.1] Defines the total reward `R` and its components, explicitly using ROUGE-L and IoU-like argument matching.
  - [section 4.3, Table 5] Ablation shows Sim-RL significantly outperforms binary reward (+17.3 points on ACEBench) and specialized rewards like ToolRL on generalization.
  - [abstract] Notes Sim-RL provides a "robust, continuous, and rich signal" for "better policy optimization."
- **Break condition:** If the similarity metric (e.g., ROUGE-L) is poorly aligned with functional correctness for the specific domain, it may reward superficial similarity over actual correct tool usage, leading to reward hacking.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The STAR training curriculum (CKD followed by Sim-RL) may create a synergistic effect where a robustly initialized policy enables more effective RL refinement than sequential SFT+RL.
- **Mechanism:** The curriculum first uses CKD to produce a student model that has learned the teacher's distribution without overfitting, maintaining higher policy entropy. This model is then fine-tuned with Sim-RL to directly optimize for the task-specific reward.
- **Core assumption:** Super-tiny models are capacity-limited; SFT forces hard imitation leading to overfitting and a low-entropy policy that is a poor starting point for RL. A "softer" KD initialization mitigates this.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] Explicitly describes the two-phase "Model Distillation" (using CKD) followed by "Model Refinement" (using Sim-RL).
  - [section 4.3, Figure 4] Shows CKD-initialized models have significantly higher Pass@k and policy entropy than SFT or other KD methods before RL.
  - [section 4.2, Table 1 & 2] The combination (CKD+Sim-RL) substantially outperforms SFT+Sim-RL* (+1.3 BFCL, +14.1 ACEBench) and individual components.
- **Break condition:** If the KD phase is too weak (e.g., from a poor teacher) or the RL phase is under-trained, the synergy might not materialize, and the final performance could be no better than a simpler pipeline.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Knowledge Distillation (KD)**
  - *Why needed here:* Central to transferring capabilities from a large teacher to a super-tiny student without requiring the student to mimic exact token-level predictions on limited data.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the key difference between Forward KL (FKL) and Reverse KL (RKL) in how they shape the student's probability distribution?
- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Human/Environment Feedback (RLHF/RLVR)**
  - *Why needed here:* Used to refine the distilled model's policy on the final task objective (function calling success) beyond what static distillation data can provide.
  - *Quick check question:* Why is a continuous, fine-grained reward (like ROUGE-L) potentially better than a binary reward for a task with multiple valid solutions?
- **Concept: Function Calling as Conditional Generation**
  - *Why needed here:* Frames the problem as generating structured text (e.g., JSON), allowing the use of standard language modeling objectives (KD) and sequence-level rewards (RL).
  - *Quick check question:* In the STAR formulation, what are the two main types of outputs the model must generate (besides the function call itself)?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - **Input:** Prompt with user query + available function schemas.
  - **Teacher Model (`M_T`):** Large LLM (e.g., Qwen3-8B) refined with Sim-RL. Generates soft targets and rollouts.
  - **Student Model (`M_S`):** Super-tiny model (e.g., 0.6B). Primary training subject.
  - **CKD Loss:** `L_CKD = L_FKL-k + λ_tail * L_tail`. Computes top-k FKL + tail L1 penalty.
  - **Sim-RL Reward:** `R = (R_format - 1) + R_format * (R_fc + R_response)`. Uses ROUGE-L/IoU.
  - **RL Algorithm:** GRPO with group advantage calculation and homogeneous group filtering.
  - **Curriculum Controller:** Orchestrates sequence: (1) Refine teacher with Sim-RL, (2) Distill student with CKD, (3) Refine student with Sim-RL.
- **Critical path:** Teacher Refinement (Sim-RL) → Student Distillation (CKD on `D_T`) → Student Refinement (Sim-RL). The output of each stage feeds the next.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **`k` (top-k teacher tokens):** Small `k` risks under-guiding; large `k` increases compute and dilutes tail penalty effect. Paper finds `k=100` effective.
  - **`λ_tail` (tail penalty weight):** Too low: insufficient suppression of confident errors. Too high: over-flattens distribution, harming exploration. Paper finds `λ_tail=10` optimal.
  - **Reward computation:** Sim-RL adds cost (ROUGE-L, AST parsing) versus binary reward, but is offline-computed per rollout.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **KD Phase:** Catastrophic collapse (loss spikes/NaNs) if using unstable top-k RKL/AKL without tail regularization.
  - **RL Phase:** Performance stagnation or degradation if initial policy has low entropy (e.g., from SFT or over-regularized KD), or if reward design leads to reward hacking (e.g., redundant calls).
  - **Curriculum Break:** If teacher model is poorly refined, student after CKD may have low baseline performance, though Sim-RL may still recover.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation on KD strategies:** Compare CE, FKL, RKL, AKL, CKD (with/without RL) to validate CKD's superiority in initial policy quality (Pass@k, entropy) and final performance.
  2. **Ablation on reward designs:** Compare Binary, ToolRL, SwiRL, and Sim-RL on both BFCL and ACEBench to prove Sim-RL's robustness, especially on generalization (ACEBench's format shift).
  3. **Ablation on teacher refinement:** Compare student performance when distilled from a base teacher vs. a teacher refined with Sim-RL, to validate the curriculum's first stage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** How does the STAR framework generalize to tasks beyond function calling, such as SQL generation or mathematical reasoning?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The authors state in the Limitations section: "our current work is validated on function calling, yet the underlying framework shows promising potential for generalization to other tasks (e.g., SQL generation, mathematical reasoning), which presents a promising avenue for future work."
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper only evaluates on function calling benchmarks (BFCL, ACEBench). While the methodology seems general, its effectiveness on other structured output tasks remains untested.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Applying STAR to SQL generation and mathematical reasoning benchmarks, comparing against SOTA baselines, and analyzing whether the synergy between CKD and Sim-RL holds across these domains.

- **Open Question 2:** What alternative similarity measures beyond ROUGE-L and exact match could further improve the fine-grained reward design in Sim-RL?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The authors state: "a more comprehensive investigation into alternative and potentially more sophisticated similarity measures is left for future work. Such an exploration could help in designing more granular feedback, although the potential performance gains remain to be quantified."
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper uses basic similarity metrics (ROUGE-L for strings, exact match for numeric/boolean). More advanced semantic similarity measures (e.g., BERTScore, embedding-based similarity) or task-specific metrics might provide richer signals.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Systematic evaluation of various similarity metrics (embedding-based, syntactic, semantic) on function calling tasks, measuring their impact on model performance, training stability, and generalization.

- **Open Question 3:** Can multi-teacher distillation strategies further enhance the student model's performance by combining complementary strengths from multiple teacher models?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The conclusion mentions: "We hope this work catalyzes further research on compact, reliable agents—exploring multi-teacher strategies, richer reward designs, and deployment-aware constraints..."
  - *Why unresolved:* STAR uses a single teacher model (Qwen3-8B). Different teachers might excel in different aspects (e.g., reasoning, format adherence, API knowledge). Combining them could yield a more robust student.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Experiments where student models are distilled from ensembles or multiple specialized teachers, evaluating whether performance exceeds single-teacher STAR.

- **Open Question 4:** What are the theoretical guarantees (if any) for CKD's ability to preserve policy entropy and prevent premature convergence during RL fine-tuning?
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The paper provides gradient analysis showing CKD reduces suppression on non-confident tokens, and empirically shows higher initial policy entropy leading to better RL outcomes (Fig 4). However, a formal theoretical link between the Ltail regularization and entropy preservation is not established.
  - *Why unresolved:* The current analysis is descriptive and empirical. A formal theoretical framework relating the CKD objective to policy entropy and exploration capability could guide hyperparameter selection and predict performance.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Deriving mathematical bounds relating λ_tail and the student's output entropy, or proving conditions under which CKD guarantees a minimum exploration capacity. Validation through entropy measurements across various λ_tail values.

- **Open Question 5:** How do deployment-specific constraints (e.g., latency, memory footprint, hardware-specific optimizations) interact with the STAR training curriculum, and can the framework be adapted for extreme edge deployment scenarios?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The conclusion mentions "deployment-aware constraints" as a future direction: "We hope this work catalyzes further research on compact, reliable agents—exploring multi-teacher strategies, richer reward designs, and deployment-aware constraints..."
  - *Why unresolved:* The paper focuses on benchmark performance. Real-world deployment on edge devices with strict resource limits may require trade-offs (e.g., shorter context, quantization) that could affect the delicate CKD+Sim-RL balance.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Benchmarking STAR models under various deployment constraints (quantization, pruning, restricted context lengths), measuring the impact on function calling accuracy, and potentially developing training modifications to optimize for these constraints.

## Limitations
- Exact training steps/epochs for teacher refinement, CKD, and RL stages are not specified, hindering reproducibility.
- Rollout generation parameters (temperature, top-p) and detailed `R_format` implementation are omitted, creating ambiguity in reward computation.
- Potential overfitting to benchmark distributions due to data merging without clear train/test separation.

## Confidence
- CKD stabilizes training and preserves entropy: High
- Sim-RL reward improves generalization: High
- Curriculum synergy (CKD→Sim-RL): High
- SOTA results (51.70 BFCLv3, 53.00 ACEBench): Medium

## Next Checks
1. Hyperparameter robustness: Sweep `k` and `λ_tail` in CKD, measure impact on RL convergence and final scores.
2. Reward design sensitivity: Replace ROUGE-L with BERTScore in Sim-RL and evaluate on BFCL/ACE to confirm metric choice doesn't drive gains.
3. Cross-architectural generalization: Apply STAR to a different teacher-student pair (e.g., Mistral-7B → 0.6B) to verify that improvements are not Qwen-specific.
