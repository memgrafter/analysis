# Calibrated Test-Time Guidance for Bayesian Inference

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.22428  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22428  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Proposed Calibrated Bayesian Guidance (CBG) enables consistent Bayesian posterior sampling from diffusion priors, significantly outperforming prior biased guidance methods on distributional fit and imaging tasks.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion models can serve as flexible priors for Bayesian inference, but existing test-time guidance methods (e.g., DPS, LGD) use biased approximations of the diffused likelihood, yielding miscalibrated posteriors. This paper proves these approximations are fundamentally inconsistent and introduces Calibrated Bayesian Guidance (CBG), which directly estimates the required integral via Monte Carlo sampling from the diffusion posterior. CBG provides both gradient-based and gradient-free consistent estimators. Empirical results on a Bayesian inference benchmark and black-hole imaging show CBG achieves superior calibration and performance, with further gains as compute increases, while prior methods plateau.

## Method Summary
CBG addresses the core inconsistency in existing guidance: approximating \( p(y|x_t) = \int p(x|x_t)p(y|x)dx \) with biased shortcuts (e.g., plugging in a point estimate or Gaussian assumption). Instead, CBG uses Monte Carlo to average the true likelihood over samples \( x^{(i)} \sim p(x|x_t) \). Two estimators are derived: a reparameterization-based gradient estimator (requires differentiable likelihood) and a REINFORCE-style gradient-free estimator (works for non-differentiable likelihoods). Both converge to the true guidance signal as the number of inner samples \( K \to \infty \). The method integrates into standard diffusion ODE solvers by modifying the score with the estimated \( \nabla_{x_t} \log p(y|x_t) \).

## Key Results
- CBG achieves lower C2ST scores (better distributional match) than baselines on a 5-task Bayesian benchmark, with average C2ST ↓0.505 vs 0.803 for prior methods.
- CBG’s performance improves with increased compute (larger \( K, M \)), while prior methods plateau.
- On black-hole imaging, CBG reaches PSNR 26.10 dB, matching the prior state-of-the-art.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Inconsistency of Existing Approximations
Existing test-time guidance methods (DPS, LGD, ΠGDM) use biased estimators for the diffused likelihood \( p(y|x_t) \). They employ shortcuts like plugging in the posterior mean (\( \hat{x} \)), assuming a Gaussian form, or applying optimization-based corrections. Theorems 4.1–4.3 prove these approximations converge to incorrect values even with infinite compute, unless the likelihood is trivial or the prior is exactly standard normal. This prevents true Bayesian posterior sampling regardless of tuning.

### Mechanism 2: Consistent Estimation via Monte Carlo
CBG replaces biased approximations with consistent Monte Carlo estimators: gradient-based (Equation 16) and gradient-free (Equation 20). Both sample \( x^{(i)} \sim p(x|x_t) \) and average likelihood evaluations or score contributions. As the number of samples \( K \to \infty \), the estimates converge to the true \( \nabla_{x_t} \log p(y|x_t) \) or \( \nabla_{x_t} \log p(x_t|y) \). Increased compute systematically reduces bias.

### Mechanism 3: Correct Tempering
Standard practice rescales the guidance gradient by a factor \( \gamma \), but this yields a biased tempered posterior \( p(y|x_t)^\gamma \). Theorem 4.3 shows the correct tempered diffused likelihood is \( \int p(x|x_t) p(y|x)^\gamma dx \). Naive rescaling ignores the integral’s curvature and is biased unless the likelihood is constant.

## Foundational Learning
- **Diffusion Models**: Provide the prior \( p(x) \) and the reverse posterior \( p(x|x_t) \). The learned score function \( s_\theta(x_t,t) \) approximates \( \nabla_{x_t} \log p(x_t) \). *Why needed*: CBG modifies the reverse process using a learned prior. *Quick check*: "How does the reverse SDE/ODE use the learned score function to denoise?"
- **Bayesian Inference**: The target is the posterior \( p(x|y) \propto p(y|x)p(x) \). *Why needed*: CBG aims to sample from this posterior using a diffusion prior. *Quick check*: "Why is sampling from \( p(x|y) \) more challenging than MAP estimation, and how does guidance bridge the gap?"
- **Monte Carlo Integration**: Estimates expectations via sampling. *Why needed*: CBG’s estimators rely on averaging over \( x \sim p(x|x_t) \). *Quick check*: "What makes an estimator unbiased and consistent, and why is consistency necessary for Bayesian calibration?"

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Pretrained diffusion model (provides \( s_\theta \), diffusion coefficients) -> Sampling engine (generates \( x^{(i)} \sim p(x|x_t) \) via inner-loop diffusion steps) -> Estimator module (computes likelihood weights and aggregates per Eq. 16 or 20) -> Integrator (updates \( x_t \) using Euler step on modified score)

**Critical path**:  
1. Initialize \( x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I) \).  
2. For \( t = T \) down to 1:  
   a. For \( i = 1..K \): sample \( x^{(i)} \) from \( p(x|x_t) \) using \( M \) inner diffusion steps.  
   b. Compute \( \nabla_{x_t} \log p(x_t|y) \) via gradient-free (Eq. 20) or gradient-based (Eq. 16) estimator.  
   c. Update \( x_{t-1} = x_t + \text{step size} \times (s_\theta(x_t,t) + \text{guidance}) \).

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- Gradient-based: Lower variance for smooth likelihoods but requires differentiating through sampling; memory-intensive.  
- Gradient-free: Memory-efficient; often lower variance for sharp likelihoods but importance weights can peak.  
- \( K \) (samples) and \( M \) (inner steps): Larger values improve calibration (Figure 3) but increase runtime \( O(N \cdot M \cdot K) \). Prior methods are \( O(N) \).

**Failure signatures**:  
- Diverging samples or NaNs: Likely from poor \( p(x|x_t) \) sampling or unstable likelihood evaluation.  
- No improvement with increased \( K \): May indicate near-constant likelihood or misspecified prior.  
- High output variance: Increase \( K \) or switch estimator variant.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. **Bayesian Inference Benchmark**: Run Tasks 1–5 with reference posteriors from Lueckmann et al. (2021). Vary \( K \) and compare C2ST; CBG should improve monotonically while baselines plateau.  
2. **Black Hole Imaging**: Use pretrained diffusion prior from Mizuno 2022 (via InverseBench). Replicate Table 2: \( N=1000, M=50, K=512 \), \( \gamma=0.003 \). Compute PSNR against ground truths; expect ≥26.10 dB.  
3. **Ablation on \( K \) and \( M \)**: On both benchmarks, systematically vary \( K \) and \( M \). Verify C2ST/PSNR increases with compute for CBG but not for baselines; compare variance of gradient-based vs. free estimators.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Computational cost**: How to reduce the \( O(N \cdot M \cdot K) \) cost of sampling \( p(x|x_t) \) for high-resolution images?  
2. **Tempered posteriors**: How to implement calibrated tempering (\( \gamma \neq 1 \)) inside the integral (Eq. 13) in practice?  
3. **Estimator choice**: What problem properties determine whether gradient-based or gradient-free CBG has lower variance?  
4. **Likelihood-free settings**: Can CBG be adapted when \( p(y|x) \) is unknown or only available via simulation?

## Limitations
- **Artifact dependence**: Reproduction requires reference posterior samples and exact diffusion model checkpoints that are not provided.  
- **Scalability**: Runtime scales linearly with inner steps \( M \) and sample count \( K \), making it far slower than approximate guidance methods.  
- **Implementation sensitivity**: Performance depends on accurate sampling from \( p(x|x_t) \); approximate samplers may slow convergence.

## Confidence
- **Existing guidance methods are biased** (Theorems 4.1–4.3): **High**  
- **CBG provides consistent estimators** (Eqs. 16, 20): **High**  
- **CBG empirically outperforms baselines** (C2ST ↓, PSNR ↑): **Medium** (depends on unavailable hyperparameters/artifacts)  
- **Naïve tempering is biased** (Theorem 4.3): **High**
