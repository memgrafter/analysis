# Learning ECG Image Representations via Dual Physiological-Aware Alignments

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2604.01526
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01526
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: ECG-Scan achieves 90.4% average AUC in linear probing (vs. 91.6% for signal-based D-BETA), 75.8% in zero-shot classification, and 94.78% on CODE-test, outperforming human experts.

## Executive Summary
Most ECG data exists only as images, but existing automated analysis methods require raw signals, limiting use in resource-constrained settings. This paper introduces ECG-Scan, a self-supervised framework that learns ECG image representations via dual physiological-aware alignments: (1) multimodal contrastive alignment between image, signal, and text using a Gramian-based loss, and (2) soft-lead consistency regularization during signal reconstruction based on Einthoven’s law and Goldberger’s equations. By synthesizing diverse ECG images on-the-fly from gold-standard signal-text pairs (MIMIC-IV-ECG) and leveraging frozen signal and text encoders as teachers, ECG-Scan narrows the performance gap to signal-based foundation models. It achieves 90.4% average AUC in linear probing (comparable to D-BETA’s 91.6%), 75.8% in zero-shot classification (approaching MELP’s 79.0%), and 94.78% on CODE-test, surpassing medical students (93.61%) and signal-based models (MERL: 85.14%).

## Method Summary
ECG-Scan is a self-supervised framework that learns from ECG images without requiring raw signals at inference. It uses three modalities: synthetic ECG images generated from signals during training, paired 12-lead signals, and clinical text. The architecture comprises a CLIP image encoder fine-tuned via LoRA, a frozen D-BETA signal encoder, and a frozen BioMed-CPT text encoder. Two projection heads on the image encoder output: Proj_ctr for contrastive alignment and Proj_rec for signal reconstruction. A Transformer decoder reconstructs full 12-lead signals from Proj_rec outputs.

Training uses three losses: image-text contrastive loss (L_ctr) aligns visual and semantic content; Gramian-based volume loss (L_gram) enforces three-way geometric consistency among normalized image, signal, and text embeddings; reconstruction loss (L_recon) combines mean squared error with a soft-lead consistency loss (L_rule) that regularizes limb leads according to Einthoven’s law and Goldberger’s equations. Only LoRA parameters in the image encoder and all parameters in the signal decoder are trainable. Synthetic images are produced on-the-fly with realistic augmentations to bridge abundant signal-text data to image representation learning.

## Key Results
- Linear probing across multiple datasets with 100% labeled data: 90.4% average AUC, comparable to signal models (D-BETA: 91.6%).
- Zero-shot classification: 75.8% average AUC, approaching signal-text models (MELP: 79.0%) and surpassing prior image-based pipelines by over 9%.
- On CODE-test: 94.78% AUC, outperforming medical students (93.61%) and signal-based models (MERL: 85.14%).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Gramian-Based Multimodal Contrastive Alignment
A Gramian volume loss computed from normalized image, signal, and text embeddings minimizes the parallelepiped volume spanned by these vectors, forcing them into a low-dimensional coherent subspace that preserves higher-order geometric relationships reflecting cardiac physiology. Combined with standard image-text contrastive learning (CE loss), the image encoder distills physiologically meaningful features from gold-standard signal-text pairs without direct signal access at inference. Evidence: Ablations show ~2% zero-shot gain from Gramian, and CODE-test results demonstrate superior performance to digitization+signal pipelines only with this component. Break condition: Poorly pretrained signal encoder or insufficiently diverse synthetic images may lead to arbitrary geometric alignment that does not transfer meaningful physiology.

### Mechanism 2: Soft-Lead Consistency Regularization in Signal Reconstruction
A Transformer decoder reconstructs full 12-lead signals from the image representation. In addition to MSE, a soft-lead consistency loss (`L_rule`) refines reconstructed limb leads by projecting them onto the constraint manifolds defined by Einthoven’s law and Goldberger’s equations and penalizing deviation from ground truth. This injects domain knowledge about inter-lead electrical relationships, improving physiological plausibility and representation stability without overly restricting the model to handle noise and artifacts. Evidence: Ablation contributes ~2% to linear probing gain, and high SNR values in real signals justify the soft-constraint design. Break condition: If synthetic image generation fails to preserve limb-lead relationships, the decoder may learn to satisfy constraints on synthetic data but fail on real images, destabilizing training.

### Mechanism 3: Synthetic ECG Image Generation as Privileged Pretraining Bridge
Dynamically synthesizing diverse, realistic ECG images from gold-standard paired signal-text data (MIMIC-IV-ECG) provides a scalable, noiseless bridge for the image encoder to learn from abundant signal-derived physiology, circumventing the scarcity of large-scale real paired ECG image-signal-text datasets. The rendering pipeline emulates clinical printouts with stochastic augmentations (noise, geometric changes, grid styles) to simulate real-world variability (e.g., scan quality, printer artifacts). The core assumption is that the mapping from 12-lead signal to standard ECG printout image is invertible in distribution—clinically relevant information is preserved in the spatial layout of waveforms. Evidence: The paper cites a widely-used realistic generation pipeline and shows plausible augmentations. However, the paper acknowledges that systematic tests on large-scale real ECG images are future work, highlighting a potential domain gap.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Self-Supervised Multimodal Contrastive Learning**  
  *Why needed:* Enables alignment of image, signal, and text modalities without explicit labels. The Gramian loss adds a geometric consistency layer on top of standard image-text contrastive learning.  
  *Quick check:* What is the risk of using only image-text contrastive loss without the Gramian signal regularizer? (Answer: The image encoder may align with text semantics but fail to capture fine-grained signal-level morphology, hurting linear probing where signal-like features are needed.)

- **Concept: Physiological Constraints as Soft Regularizers**  
  *Why needed:* ECG leads obey deterministic relationships (Einthoven, Goldberger). Encoding these as soft constraints during reconstruction injects domain knowledge, stabilizing training and ensuring physiological plausibility even with noise or artifacts.  
  *Quick check:* Why use soft constraints instead of hard constraints (e.g., exactly forcing I+III=II)? (Answer: Real signals have noise and individual variations; hard constraints would be too restrictive and could conflict with reconstruction loss, harming optimization.)

- **Concept: Knowledge Distillation from Frozen Foundation Models**  
  *Why needed:* The signal (D-BETA) and text (BioMed-CPT) encoders are large, pretrained foundation models with strong, generalizable representations. Freezing them and using their outputs as teachers provides high-quality, stable targets for the image encoder to align with, avoiding costly joint training or catastrophic forgetting.  
  *Quick check:* What is a potential downside of using a frozen, high-dimensional signal encoder (d=768) while the image encoder (via LoRA) might have limited capacity? (Answer: The image encoder may struggle to fully match the signal embedding space, leading to a representation bottleneck; this is mitigated by the Gramian loss which focuses on relational alignment rather than exact matching.)

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** ECG image -> ImageEncoder (CLIP-ViT + LoRA) -> z_img -> (Proj_ctr for alignment, Proj_rec for reconstruction). Proj_ctr participates in L_ctr with TextEncoder (BioMed-CPT) output and in L_gram with SignalEncoder (D-BETA) output. Proj_rec -> SignalDecoder (Transformer) -> reconstructed signal. Losses: L_ctr (image-text CE), L_gram (3-way Gramian CE), L_recon (MSE + L_rule). Total loss L = α L_ctr + θ L_gram + β L_recon. Trainable parameters: LoRA in ImageEncoder, all in SignalDecoder.

- **Critical path:** (1) Forward: image -> z_img -> z_ctr (via Proj_ctr) and z_rec (via Proj_rec). (2) Align z_ctr with text embedding (L_ctr) and enforce geometric consistency with signal embedding via L_gram. (3) Reconstruct signal from z_rec; compute L_recon = MSE + L_rule. (4) Backpropagate total loss to update only LoRA and decoder.

- **Design tradeoffs:**  
  - LoRA rank: lower preserves CLIP knowledge; higher may adapt better but risks overfitting to synthetic images.  
  - Loss weights (α, β, θ, w_rule): empirical balancing; improper scaling causes instability.  
  - Decoder architecture: Transformer length must match signal length (T=5000); masked query tokens prevent positional shortcuts.  
  - Synthetic image augmentations: must simulate real variability without destroying diagnostic morphology.

- **Failure signatures:**  
  - Poor linear probing → check reconstruction MSE, Gramian loss, L_rule magnitude.  
  - Poor zero-shot → check L_ctr convergence, text encoder compatibility.  
  - High L_rule → decoder may be ignoring z_rec or synthetic distortions; increase w_rule.  
  - Training instability → loss spikes due to imbalanced scaling; monitor gradient norms.

- **First 3 experiments:**  
  1. Visualize synthetic ECG images with augmentations; verify correct lead layout (3x4 grid, lead II rhythm strip) and realistic distortions (noise, blur).  
  2. Train decoder with frozen random vs. pretrained image encoder; ensure reconstruction quality improves (MSE <0.1) and L_rule decreases; plot ground truth vs. reconstructed signals.  
  3. After a few steps, compute pairwise cosine similarities of image-text embeddings; confirm matched pairs are high, mismatched low; monitor Gramian volume decreasing.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **Can ECG image representations achieve parity with or surpass state-of-the-art signal-based foundation models across all downstream tasks?**  
   Basis: Introduction asks if SSL can extend to ECG image-text learning to produce representations approaching signal-based ones. Results show a narrow gap (90.4% vs 91.6% linear probing; 75.8% vs 79.0% zero-shot).  
   Why unresolved: Residual gap suggests possible inherent limitations of the image modality; unclear whether architectural or training improvements can close it.

2. **How does ECG-Scan generalize to authentic ECG images (e.g., photographs of paper printouts, scanned PDFs) compared to synthetically rendered images used in pretraining?**  
   Basis: Conclusion states systematic tests on large-scale real ECG image datasets remain future work. Pretraining relies on a rendering pipeline due to scarcity of real images.  
   Why unresolved: Real-world images exhibit domain shifts (lighting, blur, rotation, handwritten notes) that synthetic augmentations may not fully cover, potentially degrading practical performance.

3. **Can incorporating additional physiological constraints—particularly for precordial leads (V1–V6) or between precordial and limb leads—further improve representation learning and signal reconstruction?**  
   Basis: Inferred; current soft-lead regularizer only uses limb-lead relationships. Precordial leads contain known patterns (e.g., R-wave progression) not modeled.  
   Why unresolved: Such constraints could provide valuable inductive bias but are more complex and dataset-dependent; their benefit and proper formulation remain unexplored.

## Limitations

- Synthetic ECG image generation details (augmentation ranges, grid styles) are not fully specified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- Exact formulation and hyperparameters of the Gramian loss (normalization, temperature) and zero-shot prompt templates are omitted, critical for replicability.
- All evaluations use images rendered from signals; real-world ECG photographs remain untested, leaving the core applicability claim unvalidated.

## Confidence

- Performance on benchmark datasets: High
- Gramian alignment transfers physiological structure: Medium
- Soft-lead regularization improves lead consistency: Medium
- Synthetic pretraining as a scalable bridge: Medium
