# Deep Expert Injection for Anchoring Retinal VLMs with Domain-Specific Knowledge

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.07131
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07131
- **Reference count:** 22
- **Primary result:** EyExIn (7B) achieves SOTA on retinal VQA, with Macro F1 78.07% on TM4K and 80.66% on JSIEC, and leads in text generation metrics, despite using far fewer parameters than proprietary models.

## Executive Summary
General-purpose Vision-Language Models falter on retinal diagnosis due to two critical failures: a Perception Gap, where subtle lesions are missed, and a Reasoning Gap, where linguistic priors override visual evidence. EyExIn is introduced to anchor retinal VLMs with domain-specific knowledge through three innovations: a dual-stream visual encoder that separates anatomical and pathological features; a Semantic-Adaptive Gated Fusion module that dynamically up-weights lesion signals while suppressing background; and an Adaptive Deep Expert Injection mechanism that embeds persistent “Vision Anchors” into intermediate layers of the LLM to prevent visual grounding decay. Evaluated on four retinal benchmarks, EyExIn with only 7B parameters significantly outperforms larger proprietary models, achieving high Macro F1 scores and superior text generation quality, underscoring its clinical fidelity and evidence-based reasoning.

## Method Summary
EyExIn employs a frozen general visual encoder (from Qwen2.5-VL) alongside a trainable retinal foundation model as an expert encoder. Their outputs are projected to a shared space and fused via a Semantic-Adaptive Gated Fusion module, which learns a token-wise weight α to combine the streams, amplifying pathological regions. The fused features are injected into the LLM’s initial embedding and also re-injected adaptively at selected intermediate layers using a router that detects representational decay relative to the pristine fused representation. The LLM is fine‑tuned with LoRA (rank 16) to incorporate these new visual signals while preserving pre‑trained linguistic knowledge. Training uses AdamW (lr 2e‑5, weight decay 0.05), cosine decay with 3% warmup, batch size 48, and BFloat16. This design ensures data efficiency, requiring only ~150K retinal image‑text pairs.

## Key Results
- Macro F1 scores of 78.07% on TM4K and 80.66% on JSIEC for closed VQA, with significantly higher precision than baselines.
- Leading text generation metrics: BLEU‑1 of 0.4579 on TM4K, along with improved ROUGE‑L and METEOR.
- Outperforms larger proprietary models (e.g., GPT‑4V, Claude3) despite having only 7B parameters.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Semantic-Adaptive Gated Fusion module dynamically isolates fine-grained pathological features (lesions) from anatomical background, thereby addressing the *Perception Gap* of general-purpose visual encoders.
- **Mechanism:** The dual-stream encoder produces two feature maps: `F_gen` (global anatomy from a frozen general encoder) and `F_exp` (fine pathologies from a trainable expert encoder projected to shared dimensions). A lightweight MLP router computes a spatial attention map `α` (0 to 1 per token) via a sigmoid. The final fused features `F_fused` are a convex combination: `(1-α)*F_gen + α*F_exp`. This allows the model to up-weight expert-derived lesion signals (`α→1`) and down-weight irrelevant background or general features (`α→0`).
- **Core assumption:** Fine-grained medical pathologies (e.g., microaneurysms) are spatially localized and semantically distinct from broader anatomical structures. A static feature combination (e.g., simple addition) dilutes these weak lesion signals with noise from healthy tissue.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "decouples visual representation into a general stream for anatomical context and a specialized expert stream for pathological semantics... Semantic-Adaptive Gated Fusion module, which dynamically amplifies subtle lesion signals while filtering irrelevant background noise."
  - [section 2.2] "Trivial integration strategies... dilute fragile lesion signals... Our Semantic-Adaptive Gated Fusion... explicitly decouples the feature integration process. By dynamically enforcing α→1 in pathological regions and α→0 in broader structural contexts, this spatial adaptivity purifies the visual features and maximizes the Signal-to-Noise Ratio."
  - [corpus] Weak. Neighbor papers discuss retinal foundation models and multimodal reasoning but do not directly validate this specific gated fusion mechanism for lesion isolation.
- **Break condition:** If the expert encoder's pre-training does not actually capture finer pathological semantics than the general encoder (e.g., if both are trained on similar data distributions), the gating mechanism has no meaningful signal to amplify, rendering fusion ineffective.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Adaptive Deep Expert Injection embeds persistent "Vision Anchors" directly into intermediate LLM layers, preventing the *Reasoning Gap* where visual evidence is progressively overridden by language priors.
- **Mechanism:** The fused visual features `F_fused` are not just added to the initial LLM input (early fusion). Instead, at each intermediate Transformer layer `l`, a token-wise router `g_l` computes a discrepancy signal by concatenating the current visual token hidden state `H_vis_{l-1}` with `F_fused`. `g_l` determines which visual tokens need "refreshing." The injection is a residual bias: `H'_vis_l = H_vis_{l-1} + tanh(γ_l)*(g_l ⊙ F_fused)`. The zero-initialized `γ_l` acts as a global capacity controller, stabilizing early training.
- **Core assumption:** The decay of visual grounding in LLM layers is a representational *discrepancy* that can be detected layer-by-layer by comparing current hidden states to a pristine, high-fidelity visual reference (`F_fused`). Unconditional addition of visual features at every layer disrupts linguistic processing on non-pathological tokens.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "embed fused visual features as persistent 'Vision Anchors' directly into intermediate LLM layers. This mechanism creates a visual shortcut that forces the reasoning stack to remain strictly grounded in visual evidence."
  - [section 2.3] "Rather than blind addition, we first compute a token-wise spatial routing map g_l... By concatenating the current hidden states with the pristine visual evidence, this router explicitly detects representation decay, adaptively evaluating the necessity of visual refreshing."
  - [corpus] Weak. Neighbor papers (e.g., "Better Eyes, Better Thoughts") note that CoT fails in medical VLMs, implying reasoning gaps exist, but do not test deep injection as a solution.
- **Break condition:** If the chosen intermediate layers are not the ones where visual signal degradation occurs (e.g., if degradation happens only in very early or very late layers), the injection may be misaligned. If the router `g_l` is poorly calibrated (e.g., due to insufficient training data), it may fail to distinguish decayed from stable representations.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The overall EyExIn framework achieves state-of-the-art performance in low-data regimes by combining targeted architectural interventions with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA), avoiding catastrophic forgetting while deeply embedding domain knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** The general vision encoder is frozen to preserve broad pre-trained anatomical knowledge. The expert stream and all gating/routing modules (dual-stream projections, fusion router, deep injection routers) are trained from scratch or from specialized pre-training (e.g., the retinal foundation model). Only the LLM backbone is tuned via LoRA (rank 16), updating a small subset of parameters. The deep injection mechanism (`γ_l` zero-initialized) ensures that new visual biases are introduced gradually, allowing the LLM's pre-trained linguistic capabilities to adapt without overwriting.
- **Core assumption:** Medical data scarcity makes full fine-tuning of large LVLMs prone to catastrophic forgetting of general knowledge and unstable convergence. A hybrid strategy—frozen broad modules + trained narrow expert modules + minimal LLM adaptation—optimizes the trade-off between specializing for retinal tasks and maintaining general reasoning fluency.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "data-efficient framework designed to anchor retinal VLMs with expert knowledge... EyExIn with 7B-parameter achieves state-of-the-art... significantly outperforms larger proprietary models."
  - [section 3 Implementation] "To achieve parameter-efficient optimization under limited data, the LLM backbone is fine-tuned using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)... We freeze its general vision encoder while introducing a pre-trained fundus foundation model as the trainable expert stream."
  - [section 3 Ablation] Variant 3 (Direct injection) hurts Precision, showing that naively adding features without adaptive routing and careful initialization disrupts pre-trained linguistic knowledge.
- **Break condition:** If the frozen general encoder's representations are fundamentally incompatible with the expert stream's feature space (e.g., due to severe modality/style shifts in the retinal data), the initial decoupling fails, and `F_fused` remains a poor reference for deep injection. If LoRA rank is too low (e.g., < 8), the LLM may lack capacity to integrate the new vision anchors adaptively.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Domain-Specific Visual Encoding (Dual-Stream Architecture)**
  - *Why needed here:* General vision encoders (pre-trained on ImageNet) lack sensitivity to subtle, fine-grained medical lesions. A separate, specialized encoder pre-trained on a large corpus of retinal images is required to extract these features reliably.
  - *Quick check question:* What specific architectural change forces the model to process anatomical context and pathological semantics through separate pathways before fusion?
- **Concept: Residual Connections & Adaptive Gating in Transformers**
  - *Why needed here:* To combat signal decay in deep LLM layers, features must be re-injected as residuals. Gating (`g_l`, `α`) is crucial to apply these residuals only where/when they are needed, avoiding disruption of fluent language generation on irrelevant tokens.
  - *Quick check question:* How does the `g_l` router mathematically determine *which* visual tokens at layer `l` require a refresh from the expert features?
- **Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) for Vision-Language Models**
  - *Why needed here:* Medical data scarcity makes full fine-tuning of large LVLMs prone to catastrophic forgetting of general knowledge and unstable convergence. A hybrid strategy—frozen broad modules + trained narrow expert modules + minimal LLM adaptation—optimizes the trade-off between specializing for retinal tasks and maintaining general reasoning fluency.
  - *Quick check question:* In the EyExIn training setup, which components are frozen, which are trained from scratch, and which are tuned via LoRA?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - **Input:** Fundus image + text prompt.
  - **Dual-Stream Encoder:** `F_gen` from frozen Qwen2.5-VL vision encoder; `F_exp` from trainable retinal foundation encoder (Guo et al. [9]), linearly projected.
  - **Semantic-Adaptive Gated Fusion:** MLP router computes `α`; fused output `F_fused = (1-α)*F_gen + α*F_exp`.
  - **LLM Backbone (Qwen2.5-VL, 7B):** Standard decoder-only Transformer. Visual tokens are embedded in the initial sequence.
  - **Adaptive Deep Expert Injection (per selected layer `l`):** For visual token positions, `H_vis_{l-1}` (current hidden state) is concatenated with `F_fused`, passed through an MLP to get `g_l`. Update: `H'_vis_l = H_vis_{l-1} + tanh(γ_l)*(g_l ⊙ F_fused)`. `γ_l` is a learnable scalar initialized to zero.
  - **Output:** Generated diagnostic text.
- **Critical path:**
  1. Image → `F_gen` (frozen) + `F_exp` (trainable) → projection.
  2. `F_gen`, `F_exp` → Semantic-Adaptive Gated Fusion → `F_fused` (high-SNR lesion features).
  3. `F_fused` + text prompt → Initial LLM embedding.
  4. During forward pass through LLM layers: at pre-defined injection points, compute `g_l` and apply residual bias to visual token hidden states using `F_fused`.
  5. LLM generates output text conditioned on consistently grounded visual evidence.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Parameter Efficiency vs. Performance:** Freezing the general encoder and using LoRA enables training with 150K samples but limits the model's ability to fully re-tune its visual perception from scratch.
  - **Noise Filtering vs. Signal Loss:** The gating `α` aims to filter background noise but may incorrectly suppress subtle lesions in ambiguous regions if the router is not well-trained.
  - **Deep Injection vs. Linguistic Fluency:** Injecting strong visual biases at many layers can degrade the LLM's syntactic coherence on non-visual tokens. Adaptive routing `g_l` and zero-init `γ_l` are tradeoffs to mitigate this.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **Loss Diversification:** If training loss plateaus at a high value for both VQA and text generation metrics, the injection may be too weak or misaligned.
  - **Routing Failure:** Ablation (Table 3, Variant 3) shows Precision drops if routing (`Adapt`) is removed (`Direct`). Monitor per-token `g_l` values; if they are near 1 for all tokens, the router is not learning to specialize.
  - **Semantic Drift:** Generated outputs become fluent but clinically inaccurate (hallucinations return) → indicates Vision Anchors are being ignored/overwritten in deeper layers. Check activation norms of injected residuals vs. standard residuals.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Validate Dual-Stream Benefit:** Train a baseline with only the general encoder, only the expert encoder, and the dual-stream *without* gated fusion (simple addition). Measure closed VQA Macro F1 on a validation set. Expect dual-stream + addition to improve Recall but not Precision vs. single expert.
  2. **Ablate Gated Fusion:** With dual-stream active, compare "Add" vs. "Gated" fusion (Table 3, Variant 1 vs. 2). Measure Precision. Expect significant jump if gating successfully filters noise.
  3. **Ablate Deep Injection Strategy:** With full model (dual-stream + gated), compare 1) No deep injection (early fusion only), 2) Unconditional deep addition (Direct), and 3) Adaptive injection (Adapt). Measure Precision and Hallucination rate (e.g., false positive diagnoses). Expect Adapt to maximize Precision by preserving fluency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the EyExIn framework generalize to non-retinal medical VLM tasks and imaging modalities?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] Evaluation is exclusively on retinal fundus datasets (TM4K, JSIEC, Retina, ODIR); the dual-stream design's transferability to other medical domains (e.g., radiology, dermatology) is not validated.
- **Why unresolved:** Different medical imaging modalities have distinct anatomical structures and pathological features; the assumption that decoupling general/expert streams is universally effective remains untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Benchmarking EyExIn on diverse medical VQA datasets from other specialties (e.g., chest X-ray, skin lesions) and comparing performance against modality-adapted baselines.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the optimal depth and granularity of Adaptive Deep Expert Injection within the LLM to balance visual grounding with linguistic fluency?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper injects fused features into "intermediate LLM layers" but does not specify layer indices or count; the ablation study only contrasts injection versus no injection, not depth variations.
- **Why unresolved:** The effectiveness of "Vision Anchors" likely depends on injection position—too early may not address high-level reasoning, too late may fail to prevent signal decay; improper depth could also disrupt pretrained syntactic knowledge.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments injecting at varying transformer depths (e.g., every layer, only last few layers) and measuring diagnostic accuracy, hallucination frequency, and text-generation coherence (e.g., BERT-F1).

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How dependent is EyExIn on the availability and quality of a pre-trained domain-specific expert visual encoder for deployment in new medical specialties?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The expert stream requires a "pre-trained fundus foundation encoder"; the framework assumes such encoders are readily available and effective for pathological feature extraction.
- **Why unresolved:** For rare diseases or emerging modalities, high-quality expert encoders may be scarce or nonexistent; the framework's robustness to suboptimal or synthetic expert features is unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Replacing the expert encoder with alternatives of decreasing specialization (e.g., natural-image pretrained, randomly initialized, or weakly supervised models) and evaluating performance degradation on retinal and non-retinal benchmarks.

## Limitations
- **Data inaccessible:** The 150 K training dataset is private; its distribution and annotation quality are unknown, limiting reproducibility.
- **Expert model unreleased:** The retinal foundation model used as the expert encoder (Guo et al. [9]) is not publicly available, hindering exact replication.
- **Clinical realism untested:** Evaluation on VQA/text metrics may not translate to real‑world diagnostic accuracy or safety.

## Next Checks
- **Replicate with public retinal datasets:** Combine JSIEC, Retina, and ODIR into an instruction‑tuned set. Train a variant with additive fusion vs. gated fusion; expect gated fusion to raise closed‑VQA Macro F1 by ≥2 percentage points, confirming noise filtering.
- **Quantify routing selectivity:** On a lesion‑annotated subset, compute average `g_l` values inside lesion regions vs. healthy background. A valid model should show significantly higher `g_l` on lesions (paired t‑test, *p*<0.01). Uniformly low routing would indicate failure.
- **Ablate injection layer placement:** Shift the injection layers earlier (e.g., 4–8) and later (12–16) relative to the reported setup. If the original choice is correct, moving them should degrade Macro F1 by >1 point, validating the assumed visual‑signal decay profile.
