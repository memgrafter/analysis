# Equitable Evaluation via Elicitation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.21327
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21327
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** Multi-accuracy correction reduced fairness loss from 0.048 to 0.0023 while improving test accuracy from 93.7% to 100% on synthetic data.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles bias in skill assessment caused by individuals' varying self-presentation styles (e.g., modesty vs. self-promotion) in self-reported data. It proposes an interactive AI system that adaptively selects questions from a fixed bank, allowing users to respond in their own voice. A question selector (actor) and skill predictor (critic) are trained jointly via reinforcement learning, with the selector optimizing for information gain. To mitigate systematic bias, multi-accuracy fairness constraints are enforced during training, ensuring prediction error has low covariance with Big Five personality traits. The approach demonstrates that active elicitation can reduce endogenous bias while maintaining or improving accuracy, with per-epoch fairness guarantees.

## Method Summary
The method uses a synthetic data pipeline where a fine-tuned LLM (Meta-Llama-3.1-8B) simulates user responses based on public LinkedIn profiles. An actor-critic reinforcement learning framework is employed: the actor (`q_ψ`) selects the next question to maximize expected certainty gain (measured via rollouts), and the critic (`f_ϕ`) predicts the true skill label. Training alternates between standard RL updates and a fairness calibration phase. In the calibration phase, a post-processing step adds a linear combination of Big Five personality scores to the critic's logits, optimizing weights to minimize the covariance between prediction error and these auxiliary functions. This ensures the predictor satisfies multi-accuracy constraints at each epoch.

## Key Results
- Multi-accuracy fairness loss (Equation 2) reduced from 0.048 to 0.0023, falling below the 0.01 target.
- Test accuracy improved from 93.7% to 100% on held-out synthetic profiles.
- Fairness guarantees are provably maintained at each training epoch via periodic calibration.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Adaptive questioning reduces missing information caused by self‑presentation styles.
- **Mechanism:** The actor chooses questions that maximize expected certainty gain (rollout‑based Z), encouraging collection of skill‑relevant evidence that modest or self‑promoting users might omit.
- **Core assumption:** Certainty gain correlates with eventual prediction accuracy and with the presence of missing skill signals.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "interactive AI system that adaptively asks questions..."; Section 3.2: "Certainty‑maximizing policy learning" and rollout‑based Z.
- **Break condition:** If the response simulator fails to generate realistic counterfactual answers, the certainty signal becomes miscalibrated and the selector may ask uninformative questions.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Multi‑accuracy correction eliminates systematic bias in skill scores correlated with personality traits.
- **Mechanism:** After actor‑critic updates, the predictor `f_ϕ` is post‑processed by adding a linear combination of auxiliary functions `c(x)` (Big Five scores) with weights optimized to minimize covariance between prediction error and each `c`.
- **Core assumption:** The set of auxiliary functions `C` is sufficiently expressive to capture relevant dimensions of self‑presentation bias, and the hypothesis class contains an approximately multi‑accurate predictor.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "multi‑accuracy fairness constraints... ensuring the prediction error has low covariance with Big Five personality traits."; Section 3.3: Definition of `(C, ϵ)`‑multi‑accuracy and calibration formula.
- **Break condition:** If calibration optimization fails or `C` is misspecified (e.g., missing important traits), the fairness guarantee degrades.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Interleaved training with periodic fairness calibration guarantees that every epoch’s predictor satisfies the multi‑accuracy constraint.
- **Mechanism:** The training loop alternates between standard actor‑critic steps and a fairness phase that recomputes calibration weights on a held‑out batch and updates `f∗_ϕ`; this ensures the predictor is re‑smoothed to meet the constraint even as data distribution drifts.
- **Core assumption:** The fairness calibration batch is representative and the alternating schedule is frequent enough to prevent drift beyond the `ϵ` threshold.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract: "with fairness guarantees provably holding at each training epoch."; Section 3.4: Algorithm 3 outlines interleaved re‑calibration and revert‑on‑failure.
- **Break condition:** If the actor’s policy changes too quickly between calibrations, the predictor may violate multi‑accuracy before the next calibration phase, causing the algorithm to revert and stall.

## Foundational Learning
- **Reinforcement Learning (Actor‑Critic)**
  - **Why needed:** Jointly learns a question‑asking policy (actor) and a skill scoring function (critic) via policy gradient and supervised updates.
  - **Quick check:** What would happen if the actor and critic were trained separately?
- **Multi‑Accuracy Fairness**
  - **Why needed:** Prevents the skill predictor from systematically over‑ or under‑estimating individuals based on self‑presentation style as captured by Big Five traits.
  - **Quick check:** How does multi‑accuracy differ from multicalibration in terms of guarantees?
- **Synthetic Data Generation with LLMs**
  - **Why needed:** Real profiles with known ground‑truth skills are scarce; the fine‑tuned LLM acts as a simulator to generate counterfactual responses for training.
  - **Quick check:** What are the risks of using an LLM fine‑tuned on public profiles as the response simulator, and how might they affect fairness?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** question_selector.py (Transformer policy qψ) -> skill_predictor.py (Transformer critic fϕ) -> response_simulator.py (fine‑tuned LLM s()) -> multi_accuracy.py (calibration weights lc and post‑processing) -> training.py (orchestrates alternating phases)
- **Critical path:**
  1. Load question bank Q and auxiliary functions C (Big Five scorer).
  2. Initialize qψ and fϕ randomly.
  3. Sample a base profile, generate initial transcript via s().
  4. Use qψ to propose next question, append s() response.
  5. Run rollouts (Algorithm 2) to compute certainty Z.
  6. Update qψ with REINFORCE on Z, update fϕ with MSE on true skill label.
  7. Every B_fair steps: collect fairness batch, solve for lc, apply f∗_ϕ, verify Equation 1; revert if violated.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Fixed question bank: auditability vs. limited coverage.
  - Rollout‑based certainty: faster convergence vs. sensitivity to simulator quality.
  - Post‑hoc fairness: modularity vs. need for large calibration batches.
  - Interleaved calibration: per‑epoch guarantees vs. computational overhead.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Fairness loss (Equation 2) consistently > 0.01.
  - Test accuracy plateauing or decreasing.
  - qψ collapsing to a single/very few questions (low entropy).
  - Simulator generating degenerate responses (empty, repeated).
  Monitor loss curves, question distribution, and calibration batch size.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Calibration sanity check:** Train without fairness, confirm fairness loss > 0.01; enable calibration and verify it drops below threshold.
  2. **Actor necessity:** Replace qψ with random question selection; measure drop in final accuracy and increase in fairness loss.
  3. **Simulator sensitivity:** Swap the fine‑tuned LLM (e.g., Llama‑2) and observe changes in rollouts, accuracy, and fairness; tests robustness to simulator bias.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Can the equitable elicitation framework be extended to simultaneously estimate multiple skills while maintaining fairness guarantees across all skills?**  
  *Basis:* The authors state in Section 5: "While we focus on the interview context with a single skill, equitable elicitation can be applied to estimating multiple skills simultaneously."  
  *Why unresolved:* Extending to multiple skills requires rethinking the question selection policy to optimize information gain across multiple predictors and enforce multi‑accuracy constraints jointly, increasing complexity and risk of fairness‑accuracy trade‑offs.
- **How should equitable elicitation be formalized for predicting future events with inherent uncertainty, and how can fairness be defined when errors partly stem from individual‑environment interactions?**  
  *Basis:* The authors ask: "how should we formalize the goal of equitable elicitation? ... for improving predictors of future events ... the definition ... would have to handle differences in inherent uncertainty that truly are related to the functions in C – even if the uncertainty does not inhere in the individual but rather in the interaction between the individual and the work environment."  
  *Why unresolved:* Current multi‑accuracy assumes predictable ground truth; future events involve aleatoric uncertainty that may correlate with protected attributes, requiring new fairness notions beyond covariance control.
- **What algorithmic mechanisms can detect when the collected features are insufficient for fair prediction, triggering additional elicitation?**  
  *Basis:* The authors explicitly ask: "how can we design algorithms to detect when collected features are insufficient for fairness?" at the end of Section 5.  
  *Why unresolved:* The current system elicits adaptively but lacks a stopping condition based on fairness guarantees; without detection, elicitation may be inadequate or excessive.

## Limitations
- Data source and preprocessing (LinkedIn dataset) unspecified; exact splits and skill extraction unknown.
- Big Five auxiliary functions (`c(x)`) not described; architecture and training details missing.
- All results are on LLM‑simulated data; real human behavior not validated.
- Fixed 30‑question bank may limit generalizability to broader skill sets.

## Confidence
- **Adaptive questioning improves accuracy:** Medium (synthetic RL success; simulator dependent).
- **Multi‑accuracy correction reduces fairness loss:** High (reported drop to 0.0023 on synthetic).
- **Per‑epoch fairness guarantee:** Medium (theoretical; no per‑epoch empirical confirmation).
