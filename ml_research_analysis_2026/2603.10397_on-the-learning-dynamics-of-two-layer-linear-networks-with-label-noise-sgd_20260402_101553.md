# On the Learning Dynamics of Two-layer Linear Networks with Label Noise SGD

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.10397
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10397
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Label noise SGD induces a two-phase learning dynamics in two-layer linear networks: first, weight magnitudes diminish and the model escapes the lazy regime; second, weights align with the ground-truth sparse interpolator, achieving better generalization.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the phenomenon that label noise in stochastic gradient descent (SGD) improves generalization in overparameterized neural networks. Through theoretical analysis of a two-layer linear network, the authors identify a two-phase learning dynamics. In Phase I, label noise induces oscillations in the second-layer weights, which progressively reduce the magnitudes of the first-layer weights, enabling the network to escape the lazy regime and enter the rich regime where feature learning occurs. In Phase II, with sufficiently small first-layer weights, the neuron directions rapidly align with the ground-truth sparse interpolator, leading to convergence and sparse solutions. Experiments on synthetic data and ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 confirm the theory, showing approximately 1.5% higher test accuracy and sparser solutions with label noise SGD. The insights extend to Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), which exhibits similar two-phase behavior due to its implicit noise amplification.

## Method Summary
The study focuses on the implicit bias of label-noise SGD, using a two-layer linear network as a theoretic testbed. The model takes an input $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^d$, with a first-layer weight matrix $\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$ and second-layer weights $\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^m$; the output is $\mathbf{a}^\top\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}$. Training data is either synthetic (Gaussian inputs and a sparse ground-truth $\theta^\star$) or real-world (CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18). The optimization method is SGD with label noise: for regression, Gaussian noise is added to labels; for classification, labels are randomly flipped. A variant using Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) is also examined. The authors initialize weights with NTK scaling ($\mathbf{w}_i(0)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{d}\mathbf{I})$, $a_i(0)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{m})$). Key tracked metrics include first-layer weight norms $\|\mathbf{w}_i\|$ and alignment $\langle\mathbf{w}_i,\theta^\star\rangle$. The theoretical analysis, backed by synthetic experiments, reveals two phases: Phase I shows decreasing weight norms as the model escapes the lazy regime; Phase II shows increasing alignment and convergence. Real-world experiments on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 show that label-noise SGD yields about 1.5% higher test accuracy and sparser solutions compared to standard SGD, corroborating the theoretical insights. The code is publicly available.

## Key Results
- Label noise SGD provably induces two-phase dynamics in overparameterized two-layer linear networks: weight norm decay (Phase I) followed by alignment with sparse ground truth (Phase II).
- Empirical validation on synthetic data confirms the two-phase behavior and the role of label noise in escaping the lazy regime.
- On CIFAR-10, ResNet-18 trained with label noise achieves ~1.5% higher test accuracy and yields sparser solutions compared to standard SGD; SAM shows similar dynamics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Label noise SGD induces oscillations in the second-layer weights that cause a progressive decrease in the magnitudes of first-layer weights, enabling the network to escape the lazy regime and enter the rich regime.
- **Mechanism**: The update of the first-layer weight norm $\|\mathbf{w}_i\|^2$ is coupled to second-layer weight changes via the term $\Delta W_{i(j)} = -\nabla\ell^2\{(\mathbf{x}^\top \mathbf{w}_i)^2 - a_i^2\|\mathbf{x}\|^2\}$. Label noise increases the variance of $\nabla\ell$, leading to larger fluctuations in $a_i$, which makes $\Delta W_{i(j)}$ negative with high probability (Lemma 4.3), thus reducing $\|\mathbf{w}_i\|^2$ over time. This diminishing effect persists until weights become small enough to transition out of the lazy regime (Theorem 4.2).
- **Core assumption**: Overparameterization ($m = \Omega(1/\sqrt{\eta})$), small learning rate ($\eta \le 1/C_{96}$), existence of a sparse ground-truth interpolator ($\|\theta^\star\| \le m^{-1/4}$), and label noise variance $\sigma^2 > 0$.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "In Phase I, the magnitudes of model weights progressively diminish, and the model escapes from the lazy regime; enters the rich regime."
  - [section] Section 4.2 (Theorem 4.2 and Lemma 4.3): formal statements and proofs.
  - [corpus] The neighbor paper "A Theoretical Analysis of Noise Geometry in Stochastic Gradient Descent" studies noise alignment in SGD but focuses on minibatch noise; corpus evidence for label noise specifically is weak. The mechanism is unique to this paper's analysis.
- **Break condition**: If label noise is absent ($\sigma=0$), or if the network is not sufficiently overparameterized ($m$ too small), or if the learning rate is too large ($\eta > 1/C_{96}$), then $\Delta W_{i(j)}$ may not be consistently negative and the lazy regime may persist.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Once first-layer weights become sufficiently small (Phase II), the neurons rapidly align their directions with the ground-truth interpolator $\theta^\star$, leading to convergence to a sparse solution.
- **Mechanism**: In the small-weight regime, the dynamics simplify because the updates are effectively linearized with respect to neuron directions. The combined update matrix shows that each neuron's weight vector rotates toward $\theta^\star$ at a rate proportional to its second-layer weight (Equation 84). This rotation, combined with coupling between neurons, results in increasing alignment (Lemma 4.5). After sufficient steps, the entire network output aligns with $\theta^\star$, achieving convergence (Lemma 4.6).
- **Core assumption**: Phase II begins when $\|\mathbf{w}_i\|$, $|a_i| \le \sqrt{\eta}$ (ensured by Phase I), dataset size $n \ge 1/\eta^2$, and learning rate $\eta$ small.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "In Phase II, the alignment between model weights and the ground-truth interpolator increases, and the model eventually converges."
  - [section] Section 4.4 (Lemmas 4.5 and 4.6): quantitative alignment and convergence guarantees.
  - [corpus] "From Linear to Nonlinear: Provable Weak-to-Strong Generalization through Feature Learning" discusses feature learning (rich regime) and alignment, supporting the idea that small weights lead to alignment. "Escaping mediocrity: how two-layer networks learn hard generalized linear models with SGD" also considers alignment in two-layer networks under SGD.
- **Break condition**: If Phase I fails to reduce weights below the $\sqrt{\eta}$ threshold, alignment may not proceed. Also, if the ground-truth $\theta^\star$ is not sparse (violating A4), the alignment dynamics might be slower or different. Very large learning rates could overshoot alignment.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The two-phase dynamics driven by label noise SGD also occur in Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), indicating a general principle: optimization methods that amplify stochastic noise can escape the lazy regime.
- **Mechanism**: SAM's inner perturbation ($\rho \nabla\ell/\|\nabla\ell\|$) amplifies gradient noise, similar to the effect of label noise. This perturbation induces oscillations in the second-layer weights, which then cause progressive diminishing of first-layer weights (Phase I) and subsequent alignment (Phase II). The paper shows empirically that SAM exhibits the same two-phase pattern in synthetic and real-world experiments (Section 5).
- **Core assumption**: SAM's perturbation radius $\rho$ is positive and not too large; network architecture similar to two-layer linear or wider.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Furthermore, we extend these insights to Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), showing that the principles governing label noise SGD also apply to broader optimization algorithms."
  - [section] Section 5: Figures 4 and 5 demonstrate two-phase dynamics for SAM; text asserts extension.
  - [corpus] "Simplicity Bias via Global Convergence of Sharpness Minimization" analyzes sharpness minimization and simplicity bias, aligning with SAM's implicit bias. However, direct evidence for the two-phase dynamics in SAM is not found in the neighbor corpus; the paper's own experiments are the primary source.
- **Break condition**: If SAM's perturbation radius $\rho$ is too small, the noise amplification may be insufficient to induce oscillations. If $\rho$ is too large, training may become unstable. For deeper networks, the dynamics might diverge from the two-layer linear case.

  - *Why needed here*: The theoretical analysis relies on this simplified model to isolate the coupling between layers.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: The system consists of a two-layer linear network: input $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$, first-layer weight matrix $\mathbf{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d}$, second-layer weight vector $\mathbf{a} \in \mathbb{R}^m$. The output is $\mathbf{a}^\top\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}$. Training uses label noise SGD: at each step, sample a data point, randomly flip its label with probability $\tau$, compute gradient, update parameters. Alternatively, SAM: perform inner adversarial step then outer update.

**Critical path**: Key steps are: (1) Initialize $\mathbf{W}$ and $\mathbf{a}$ with NTK scaling ($1/\sqrt{d}$ and $1/\sqrt{m}$). (2) Run label noise SGD (or SAM). (3) Monitor metrics: training loss, test loss, average neuron norm $\|\mathbf{w}_i\|^2$, average alignment $\langle\mathbf{w}_i, \theta^\star\rangle$. (4) Observe Phase I: weight norms decrease, model escapes lazy regime ($\|\mathbf{w}_i - \mathbf{w}_i(0)\| > 1/\sqrt{m}$). (5) Observe Phase II: alignment increases, loss decreases further. (6) Verify convergence to $\theta^\star$.

**Design tradeoffs**: The theoretical analysis uses a linear model to make coupling analytically tractable, but this limits direct applicability to nonlinear networks. The conditions (A1-A6) are strong: requiring large width, small LR, sparse $\theta^\star$, etc. In practice, one may need to tune label noise level $\tau$ and LR to see the two-phase effect. The extension to SAM suggests the phenomenon is robust to optimization variants that increase noise.

**Failure signatures**:
- No weight norm diminishing: Check overparameterization ($m$ too small), LR too large, or label noise too low.
- No escape from lazy regime: Measure $\|\mathbf{w}_i - \mathbf{w}_i(0)\|$; if stays $\le 1/\sqrt{m}$, lazy persists.
- No alignment increase in Phase II: Possibly Phase I did not reduce weights enough, or ground truth not sparse.
- SAM fails to show two-phase: Perturbation radius $\rho$ may be inappropriate.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Replicate synthetic two-layer linear experiment: Generate data with sparse $\theta^\star$, train with label noise SGD ($\sigma=0.1$), plot loss, avg $\|\mathbf{w}_i\|^2$, avg $|\langle\mathbf{w}_i, \theta^\star\rangle|$ vs iterations. Should see initial norm drop then alignment rise.
2. Compare with linearized model (NTK): Train same architecture with GD on linearized model (without label noise) and with label noise. Plot test loss curves; label noise should deviate from lazy trajectory.
3. Test SAM: Replace SGD with full-batch SAM ($\rho=0.1$) on synthetic data. Check if similar two-phase dynamics appear, especially weight norm reduction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

#### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How do non-linear activation functions (e.g., ReLU, sigmoid) affect the two-phase learning dynamics of label noise SGD in overparameterized neural networks?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors explicitly state in the conclusion that "a future direction is to consider non-linear activation functions and study whether non-linearity influences the training dynamics."
- **Why unresolved**: The current theory is developed for a two-layer linear network, which does not capture non-linearities present in practical deep networks; the coupling between layers and the effect of noise may fundamentally differ with non-linearities.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Extending the theoretical analysis to include non-linear activations, perhaps via mean-field approximations or other analytic tools, and conducting experiments with non-linear networks to verify whether the progressive diminishing and alignment phases persist and how they are altered.

#### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can the two-phase dynamics theory be extended to classification tasks, where the loss function and label noise model differ from regression?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors explicitly mention in the conclusion that "extending our theory to classification tasks remains an open challenge."
- **Why unresolved**: The theoretical analysis assumes a regression setting with squared loss and additive Gaussian-like noise; classification involves discrete labels and typically cross-entropy loss, which may change the dynamics of weight evolution and noise effects.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Developing a theoretical framework for classification (e.g., using cross-entropy loss and label-flipping noise), deriving analogous two-phase conditions, and validating predictions on classification benchmarks like CIFAR-10 or ImageNet.

#### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Does the two-phase learning dynamics induced by label noise SGD generalize to deeper neural networks (more than two layers) or different architectures?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper focuses exclusively on a two-layer linear network; deeper networks are standard in practice and may exhibit different dynamics due to increased layer interactions and non-linearities.
- **Why unresolved**: The analysis for two layers already involves complex coupling between layers; extending to multiple layers introduces additional challenges (e.g., gradient flow across many layers), and it is unknown if the same progressive diminishing and alignment phenomena occur or if they are artifacts of the simplified setup.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Theoretical studies for three-layer networks or empirical measurements of weight norms and alignment in deeper architectures (e.g., ResNets, Transformers) trained with label noise SGD to observe if the two-phase pattern holds; possibly developing new analytic techniques for deep linear networks beyond two layers.

#### Open Question 4
- **Question**: Are the two-phase dynamics and implicit bias of label noise SGD universal across different stochastic optimizers (e.g., Adam, RMSProp) and noise models (e.g., Gaussian label noise, asymmetric noise)?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper extends findings to Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) but does not explore other optimizers or noise types; the underlying mechanism might depend on specific algorithmic properties of SGD and the particular label-flipping noise model.
- **Why unresolved**: The theory relies on properties of SGD with label noise; other optimizers incorporate adaptive steps or momentum that could suppress or alter the oscillations driving Phase I, and different noise distributions may affect the transition differently.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Comprehensive experiments with various optimizers and noise models, measuring the same dynamical quantities (weight norms, alignment) to test for consistency; and developing a unified theoretical perspective that captures how optimizer-specific updates and noise characteristics influence the lazy-to-rich transition.

## Limitations
- Theoretical assumptions (large width, small learning rate, sparse ground truth) may not hold in practical deep networks.
- Extension to SAM lacks formal analysis; empirical evidence only.
- Missing experimental details (batch size, SAM perturbation radius, data subset) hamper reproducibility; the reported ~1.5% accuracy gain is shown only on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, so generality is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Two-phase dynamics under label‑noise SGD**: High
- **Extension to SAM**: Medium
- **Generalization/sparsity benefits in deep nets**: Medium
