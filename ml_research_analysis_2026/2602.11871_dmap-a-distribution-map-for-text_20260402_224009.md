# DMAP: A Distribution Map for Text

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.11871
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11871
- **Reference count:** 39
- **Primary result:** DMAP transforms token probabilities into uniform samples on [0,1] to validate generation parameters (via χ² test), reveal failures in machine-text detectors, and expose a head-bias fingerprint of instruction-tuned models.

## Executive Summary
Traditional language model metrics like perplexity fail to contextualize token probabilities because interpretation depends on the shape of the conditional distribution. DMAP addresses this by mapping each token to an interval on the unit interval, where interval length equals the token’s probability and left endpoint sums probabilities of more likely tokens. Uniform sampling from these intervals yields a set that jointly encodes rank and probability. For pure sampling from the evaluator model, these samples are i.i.d. Uniform[0,1], enabling a standardized, model-agnostic representation. Entropy-weighted DMAP further stabilizes visualizations by down-weighting high-certainty tokens. The resulting histogram and a χ² uniformity test allow validation of reported generation parameters, detection of unreported decoding (e.g., top-k), and forensic analysis of model fine-tuning. Notably, DMAP shows that curvature-based detectors fail for pure-sampled base models (AUROC <0.5), overturning a key assumption, and that instruction-tuned models exhibit a characteristic head-bias reflective of training on synthetic data.

## Method Summary
DMAP processes a text sequence token-by-token using an evaluator language model. For each position *i*, it obtains the conditional distribution *p(·|w_{<i})*, sorts tokens by probability, and constructs an interval *Iᵢ = [aᵢ, aᵢ + p(wᵢ)]* where *aᵢ* is the cumulative probability of tokens more likely than *wᵢ*. For vanilla DMAP, a uniform sample *xᵢ* is drawn from *Iᵢ*. For entropy-weighted DMAP, each token’s contribution is weighted by the clipped entropy *h’ᵢ* of its distribution, yielding a weighted step function ˆ*D*(*w*). The samples (or weighted density) are binned over [0,1] to produce a histogram. Under pure sampling with the evaluator, the samples should be uniform, enabling a χ² goodness-of-fit test: bin frequencies are compared to uniform expectations, and the statistic’s p-value quantifies consistency with the claimed generation process.

## Key Results
- **Parameter validation:** The χ² uniformity test detects unreported top-k sampling (e.g., k=50) in machine-text detection papers, exposing a widespread data error.
- **Detector failure:** For pure-sampled base models, DMAP histograms are approximately uniform, and curvature-based detectors achieve AUROC <0.5, proving the probability curvature principle is invalid for this common case.
- **Fine-tuning signature:** Instruction-tuned models show a pronounced left-bias (head-bias) in DMAP histograms compared to their base counterparts, offering a forensic signature of post-training on synthetic, sampled data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** DMAP transforms next-token probability distributions into i.i.d. Uniform[0,1] samples (for pure sampling with the evaluator), providing a standardized representation of text.
- **Mechanism:** For each token *wᵢ*, an interval *Iᵢ* is defined by cumulative probabilities of more likely tokens (left endpoint) and the token’s own probability (length). Uniformly sampling *xᵢ ∈ Iᵢ* preserves rank (via left endpoint) and probability (via length). If text is generated by pure sampling from *p*, Proposition 3.1 proves the *xᵢ* are i.i.d. uniform.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "maps a text ... to a set of samples in the unit interval that jointly encode rank and probability." [section 3.1]: Formal interval definition and Proposition 3.1. [corpus]: Neighbors focus on embeddings/decoding, not this interval-to-uniform transform.
- **Break condition:** Uniformity fails if the generator used unreported decoding (e.g., top-k) or the evaluator model differs significantly from the generator.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Entropy-weighted DMAP suppresses noise from high-certainty tokens, amplifying signal from uncertain choices and stabilizing visualizations.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of raw sample counts, each sample *xᵢ* is weighted by the clipped entropy *h’ᵢ* of the conditional distribution at step *i*. The plotted density ˆ*D*(*w*) is the weighted average of interval indicators, normalized by total weight.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.2]: Definition and rationale: "places more weight on the times when the choice of next token is more uncertain." [appendix F]: Experiments showing low-entropy tokens produce flat plots regardless of generation method. [section 5.3]: Head-bias in instruction-tuned models is clearer with entropy weighting.
- **Break condition:** If the relevant signal lies in high-certainty choices (e.g., over-reliance on very likely tokens), entropy weighting could obscure it. Clipping threshold *λ* requires validation across model families.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A χ² test on DMAP sample bins quantitatively validates reported generation parameters or detects data integrity flaws.
- **Mechanism:** Under the null hypothesis that text was generated by the claimed process (e.g., pure sampling), Proposition D.1 states the χ² statistic from *k* bin frequencies follows a χ² distribution with *k-1* degrees of freedom for large *T*. The p-value measures consistency.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 5.1]: Test protocol and discovery of unreported top-k=50. [appendix D]: Proposition D.1 and finite-sample discussion. [figure 3]: Visual/quantitative differentiation of generation strategies.
- **Break condition:** Invalid if evaluator model differs from generator in unaccounted ways, or if *T* is too small for asymptotic approximation. Also assumes evaluator probabilities are well-calibrated.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Probability Integral Transform (PIT)**
  - *Why needed here:* DMAP is a dynamic, rank-aware application of PIT to discrete, ordered token probabilities. PIT explains why mapping to Uniform[0,1] is principled for representing random variables.
  - *Quick check:* If *X* is continuous with CDF *F*, what is the distribution of *U = F(X)*? How does DMAP adapt this to discrete, ordered token probabilities?
- **Concept: Language Model Decoding Strategies (top-k, top-p, temperature)**
  - *Why needed here:* DMAP’s histogram shape depends critically on the generation strategy. Different strategies produce distinct, characteristic plots.
  - *Quick check:* Describe how temperature, top-k, and nucleus (top-p) sampling modify *p(v|context)*. What does a "head-biased" DMAP plot suggest about the generation strategy?
- **Concept: χ² Goodness-of-Fit Test**
  - *Why needed here:* It is the primary quantitative tool in DMAP for parameter validation, relying on the asymptotic χ² distribution under the uniformity null.
  - *Quick check:* What are the degrees of freedom for a χ² test with *T* samples binned into *k* bins to test uniformity? Why must the expected count per bin be sufficiently large?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Inputs (text *w*, evaluator LM *p*, decoding params) -> Core Loop (for each *i*: get *p(·|w_{<i})*, adjust logits per decoding, compute interval *Iᵢ*, sample *xᵢ* or weight by entropy) -> Aggregation (bin [0,1] into *k* bins, compute frequencies or weighted density) -> Analysis (plot histogram, compute χ² statistic and p-value).
- **Critical path:** Token probabilities → sorted ranks & cumulative sums → interval construction → (sampling or entropy weighting) → binning → histogram / χ². Interval definition correctness is paramount.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - *Bins (*k*) vs. noise:* Fewer bins reduce variance but lose resolution; more bins increase sensitivity but require larger *T*. Terrell-Scott rule (*k* ∝ *T*^(1/3)) is recommended.
  - *Evaluator Model Choice:* Should be smaller/faster (e.g., OPT-125m) for efficiency, but must be competent for the target domain.
  - *Entropy Weighting:* Improves sensitivity but introduces clipping hyperparameter *λ* (default 2). Requires empirical tuning.
  - *Prompt Handling:* Including prompt reduces noise but may cause bootstrap artifacts; a 30-token cutoff for short texts is heuristic.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Non-reproducible histograms across runs for same text (fixed by entropy-weighted DMAP).
  - Unexpectedly flat DMAP for non-pure sampling indicates mismatched decoding parameters.
  - Missing extreme tail bias in black-box base model evaluation suggests evaluator mismatch or poor vocabulary overlap.
  - χ² p-value near 0 for claimed pure sampling indicates unreported decoding (e.g., top-k) or evaluator-generator mismatch.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Sanity check - Pure sampling:** Generate ~10k tokens by pure sampling from a small model (e.g., OPT-125m). Compute DMAP with same model. Histogram should be flat; χ² p-value > 0.05.
  2. **Parameter detection - Top-k shadow:** Take a dataset claimed pure-sampled. Compute DMAP with claimed evaluator. Left-biased histogram and very low χ² p-value (<10^-10) suggest unreported top-k. Recompute with top-k=50 adjustment; uniformity should recover.
  3. **Instruction-tuning signature:** Generate from a base model (e.g., Pythia) and an instruction-tuned variant. Evaluate both with a fixed small model (OPT-125m). Instruction-tuned should show significant head bias relative to base.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** Should calibration of instruction-tuned models target matching DMAP histograms to human text rather than aligning perplexity via temperature scaling?  
  *Basis:* Appendix M.1 explicitly asks about bringing DMAP plots in line with human text.
- **Question 2:** Can quantitative metrics derived from DMAP histograms disentangle a model’s intrinsic limitations from inherent text domain difficulty?  
  *Basis:* Appendix M.2 questions whether DMAP separates prediction difficulty from model-specific limitations.
- **Question 3:** Are differences in DMAP histograms between generator and evaluator models distinctive enough to enable forensic identification of the generator model?  
  *Basis:* Appendix M.3 and conclusion propose new LM identification approaches based on histogram differences.
- **Question 4:** What statistical principles for zero-shot machine-generated text detection are robust across generation strategies (pure sampling, top-k, temperature) and model types (base vs instruction-tuned)?  
  *Basis:* Section 5.2 shows curvature detectors fail (AUROC < 0.5) for pure-sampled base models, indicating curvature is not universal.

## Limitations
- **Evaluator fidelity:** DMAP requires an evaluator matching the generator; mismatches cause spurious results, limiting black-box applicability.
- **Prompt handling:** Including the prompt reduces noise but may introduce bootstrap artifacts; the 30-token cutoff for short texts is heuristic and not universally validated.
- **Histogram subjectivity:** Visual bias detection lacks quantitative thresholds; χ² test depends on binning (Terrell-Scott rule) and can fail for small *T* (<10k tokens).
- **Model coverage:** Most experiments use small OPT/Pythia models; generalizability to larger, multilingual, or heavily fine-tuned models is uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Core transform (uniformity for pure sampling):** High (theoretical proof in Proposition 3.1), Medium (practical use assumes exact generator-evaluator match and pure sampling).
- **Entropy-weighted DMAP:** Medium (rationale plausible, appendix experiments supportive, but clipping threshold λ=2 is heuristic).
- **χ² uniformity test for parameter validation:** Medium-High (asymptotic justification sound, unreported top-k discovery compelling), Medium (black-box settings sensitive to evaluator mismatch and finite samples).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-model uniformity validation:** Generate 10k tokens by pure sampling from ≥5 diverse open-source models (e.g., Llama-2-7B, Mistral-7B, Falcon-7B, Pythia-1.4B). Compute DMAP with the same evaluator and record χ² p-values. Confirm uniformity holds robustly across architectures and scales.
2. **Entropy-weighting sensitivity analysis:** For fixed texts under varied decoding, sweep clipping threshold λ ∈ [0.5, 3.0] and bin counts {20,40,60}. Quantify stability of a head-bias metric (e.g., mass in leftmost 20% of bins). Compare against unclipped full entropy to assess whether λ=2 is a reliable default.
3. **Generalization of detector failure:** Replicate machine-text detection experiments with a broader set of detectors (DetectGPT, Rank1, GPTScore) and datasets (Human-Written, OIG, etc.). Test whether base-model DMAP uniformity consistently correlates with sub-chance AUROC (<0.5) across detectors, confirming the curvature assumption’s failure.
