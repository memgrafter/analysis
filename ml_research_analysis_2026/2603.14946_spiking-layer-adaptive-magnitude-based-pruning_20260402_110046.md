# Spiking Layer-Adaptive Magnitude-based Pruning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.14946
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14946
- **Reference count**: 21
- **Primary result**: SLAMP prunes SNNs with temporal distortion awareness, achieving up to 40% connectivity reduction and accuracy gains (+1.23% on CIFAR10) while significantly lowering spiking operations.

## Executive Summary
Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) offer energy-efficient inference via sparse, event-driven computation, but pruning them is challenging due to temporal dynamics. SLAMP introduces a temporal distortion-aware pruning framework that allocates sparsity through time-aware layer importance scores, minimizing worst-case output distortion across timesteps. By iteratively pruning connections (e.g., 15% per step) and fine-tuning, SLAMP attains substantial connectivity reductions—40% on CIFAR10, 25% on CIFAR100, and 40% on CIFAR10-DVS—while preserving or even improving classification accuracy: +1.23% Top-1 on CIFAR10 and +0.73% on CIFAR100 versus prior methods. The approach also reduces spiking operations considerably, enabling stable and efficient SNN deployment.

## Method Summary
SLAMP comprises an initial training phase followed by iterative pruning and fine-tuning. First, the SNN—built with Integrate-and-Fire (IF) neurons—is trained to convergence using surrogate gradient descent. Then, for each layer, a time-aware importance score \(R_i\) is computed by summing the squared synaptic contributions across all timesteps \(T\) (Equation 3) and normalizing so that scores per layer sum to 1. Pruning removes a fixed fraction (e.g., 15%) of the lowest-scoring weights, subject to a distortion constraint that bounds worst-case output deviation; in practice this is enforced via a repeated prune‑fine‑tune schedule. After each pruning step, the network is fine‑tuned for 15 epochs with SGD and a learning rate scheduler. These cycles continue until the target connectivity is reached (e.g., 40% remaining connections). The distortion constraint, while theoretically grounded, is implemented through the fixed pruning rate rather than an explicit check.

## Key Results
- **Connectivity reduction**: 40% on CIFAR10, 25% on CIFAR100, 40% on CIFAR10-DVS.
- **Accuracy improvement**: +1.23% Top-1 on CIFAR10, +0.73% on CIFAR100 vs. prior methods and the unpruned baseline.
- **Operational efficiency**: Significantly lowered spiking operations (SOPs) while maintaining stable temporal dynamics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SLAMP’s success stems from its distortion-aware importance scoring and iterative fine-tuning. The importance score \(R_i\) aggregates each weight’s contribution to the network output over all timesteps, capturing temporal effects that static magnitude pruning misses. By pruning the lowest-scoring connections, the method minimizes the worst-case distortion bound derived in the paper. The iterative schedule—pruning a modest fraction followed by fine-tuning—allows the network to adapt gradually, preventing catastrophic collapse and often leading to slight accuracy gains due to implicit regularization. The theoretical bound, though not directly enforced, guides the choice of a fixed per‑step pruning rate, ensuring that cumulative removal respects the distortion limit. Fine‑tuning stabilizes membrane potentials and restores performance lost by connectivity reduction.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text)**  
Input → Spiking Convolutional Layers (with IF neurons) → Residual Blocks (ResNet) or Sequential Convs (VGG) → Spiking Global Average Pool → Spiking Fully Connected Layer → Output (membrane potential or spike count)

**Critical path**  
1. Train SNN to convergence using surrogate gradients.  
2. Compute time‑aware importance scores \(R_i\) for all layers via forward passes over \(T\) timesteps.  
3. Prune lowest‑scoring weights (e.g., 15%) while respecting per‑layer distortion constraint (heuristic).  
4. Fine‑tune for a fixed number of epochs (15).  
5. Repeat steps 2‑4 until target connectivity reached.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- *Pruning fraction per step vs. stability*: Larger fractions reduce cycles but risk accuracy loss; 15% is chosen as a safe default.  
- *Time steps \(T\)*: More timesteps better capture temporal dynamics but increase compute; paper uses \(T=2\) for static datasets and \(T=5\) for DVS.  
- *Fine‑tuning duration*: More epochs improve recovery but add overhead; 15 is used without explicit search.  
- *Distortion constraint implementation*: The theoretical bound is approximated by a fixed schedule; a tighter coupling could improve sparsity but is left for future work.

**Failure signatures**  
- Accuracy collapse immediately after pruning, indicating excessive distortion or importance scoring error.  
- Explosive increase in spike rates or NaN losses, suggesting membrane instability.  
- High variance in membrane potential across timesteps post‑pruning, which should decrease with fine‑tuning (see Table II).  
- No improvement in accuracy after fine‑tuning, possibly due to insufficient fine‑tune epochs or aggressive pruning.

**Three first experiments**  
1. **Baseline training**: Train Spiking ResNet19 on CIFAR10 (T=2, 300 epochs) to establish reference Top‑1 accuracy.  
2. **Importance scoring verification**: After baseline, compute \(R_i\) and prune 15% of weights (lowest) without fine‑tuning; evaluate accuracy drop to confirm scores correctly identify critical connections.  
3. **Single prune‑fine‑tune cycle**: Prune 15% and fine‑tune for 15 epochs; compare accuracy to baseline to validate that the network recovers (or improves) and that the pipeline is functional.

## Limitations
- The theoretical distortion constraint \(R_{\text{max}}\) is not fully operationalized; the practical 15% per‑step schedule is a heuristic that may not always respect the bound.
- Missing details on learning rate scheduling across prune‑fine‑tune cycles and some hyperparameters (batch size, weight decay, DVS preprocessing) hinder exact replication.
- The normalization constant \(\lambda_i\) for importance scores is assumed (likely \(1/\sum_j R_{ij}\)) but not formally derived.
