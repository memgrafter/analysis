# ATHENA: Adaptive Test-Time Steering for Improving Count Fidelity in Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.19676
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19676
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Improves exact-count accuracy by up to 22.3% (31.7% → 54.0% on CoCoCount with SDXL) and reduces MAE by 39–49% across multiple diffusion backbones, while being 2.5× faster and using 4× less memory than prior baselines.

## Executive Summary
ATHENA is a training-free, test-time steering framework that systematically improves object count fidelity in text-to-image diffusion models. It addresses the known failure of these models to generate exact object counts specified in prompts by intervening early in the diffusion trajectory. The method estimates object counts from intermediate latents using an external detector, then applies count-aware noise corrections via dual denoiser evaluations with a control prompt. Three variants—Static (fixed steering), Feedback (single correction based on estimated count), and Adaptive (adjusts steering strength based on error direction)—offer trade-offs between computation and accuracy. Evaluated on FLUX.1-dev, SD 3.5 Large, and SDXL backbones, ATHENA-Adaptive achieves significant gains in exact-match accuracy and mean absolute error while maintaining visual quality and outperforming prior methods in efficiency.

## Method Summary
ATHENA operates during sampling without modifying model weights. At a chosen early diffusion step `t_steer`, it queries the denoiser twice: once with the original prompt `p` and once with a control prompt `p̂` (which either removes or replaces the numeric constraint). The difference in predicted noise is scaled by a strength parameter `γ` and added to the original prediction, with normalization to maintain numerical stability. This steers the sampling trajectory toward a distribution associated with the control prompt. For Feedback and Adaptive variants, an intermediate count estimation at step `t_test` guides whether to apply steering and how to adjust `γ`. Adaptive further analyzes the change in estimated count after an initial steering pass: if the error direction persists, `γ` is doubled; if overshoot occurs, `γ` is halved. The process may re-run the steering phase once with the adjusted `γ`. The method is model-agnostic and relies on an off-the-shelf object detector (e.g., GroundingDINO) for intermediate count feedback.

## Key Results
- Exact-count accuracy improves by up to **22.3%** (e.g., 31.7% → 54.0% on CoCoCount with SDXL).
- Mean Absolute Error (MAE) is reduced by **39–49%** across backbones.
- ATHENA-Adaptive outperforms prior baselines CountGen and Counting Guidance while being **2.5× faster** and using **4× less memory**.
- Visual quality is largely preserved; generated images remain coherent and faithful to prompts beyond count.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

1. **Early structural commitments lock object count**: Diffusion models make irreversible stochastic decisions about object multiplicity and spatial layout in early denoising steps; later steps primarily refine local appearance without altering global structure. Intervening before a critical step (`t_steer`) allows corrective steering before errors become entrenched. Evidence: [section 1] asserts early decisions dominate layout, and the paper's experiments show steering after early steps is ineffective. Break condition: if early steps are too noisy for reliable steering or if the model permits late-stage structural changes contrary to typical diffusion dynamics.

2. **Prompt-based steering via dual denoiser evaluations**: At each steered step, the denoiser is queried with `p` and `p̂`; the noise difference `(ϵ_t - ̂ϵ_t)` scaled by `γ` biases the latent toward the control prompt's distribution. This vector difference locally approximates a direction that influences count-related attributes without architectural changes. Evidence: [section 4.3.1] describes it as a controlled perturbation steering the trajectory away from `p̂`-induced behaviors. Break condition: if `p̂` induces unrelated semantic shifts (e.g., changing object category) or if linear combination destabilizes denoising.

3. **Adaptive γ adjustment improves robustness**: A single adjustment of `γ` based on intermediate count feedback (after one steering pass) handles uncertainty in the steering effect. If the error direction remains (e.g., still undercounting), `γ` is doubled to increase pressure; if the direction flips (overshoot), `γ` is halved. Evidence: [Table 2] shows Adaptive consistently outperforms Static and Feedback across backbones. Break condition: if the relationship between initial steering effect and required adjustment is non-monotonic or highly non-linear, a single adjustment may be insufficient.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map** (Adaptive variant):  
Initial noise ξ at T → Sample without steering to t_test → Count Estimator (c1) → if c1==k: continue to t=0; else: Control Prompt Generator (p̂) → Steering Block (γ) from T to t_steer → sample to t_test → Count Estimator (c2) → if c2==k: continue to t=0; else: Adaptive γ Controller (adjust γ) → Steering Block (adjusted γ) from T to t_steer → continue to t=0.

**Critical path** (Adaptive):  
1. Generate and store initial noise ξ at T.  
2. Sample from T to t_test without steering; decode and estimate count c1.  
3. If c1 == target k, continue from t_test to t=0.  
4. Else, construct control prompt p̂ (e.g., replace target count with c1 or remove it) and apply steering from T to t_steer with initial γ. Then sample to t_test and estimate c2.  
5. If c2 == k, continue from t_test to t=0.  
6. Else, adjust γ: double if error direction unchanged (sign(c2−k) == sign(c1−k)); halve if flipped.  
7. Re-run steering from T to t_steer with adjusted γ (using same ξ), then continue to t=0.

**Design tradeoffs**:  
- *Steering horizon (`t_steer`)*: Longer horizon increases correction potential but adds compute.  
- *Estimation step (`t_test`)*: Earlier `t_test` is cheaper but noisier; later `t_test` is more accurate but delays feedback.  
- *γ magnitude*: Too small yields no correction; too large causes overshoot or semantic distortion.

**Failure signatures**:  
- Inaccurate count estimates from detector (false positives/negatives) misguide steering.  
- Control prompt `p̂` induces semantic drift (e.g., changing object category).  
- Norm-matching during steering destabilizes the sampler.  
- Adaptive overshoots when error direction flip is noisy.

**First 3 experiments**:  
1. Sanity check: Run ATHENA-Static on simple CoCoCount prompts with γ=0 vs. γ>0 to confirm steering effect.  
2. Hyperparameter sweep: Vary `t_test` and `t_steer` on a validation set to identify a stable regime where count estimates are reliable and steering effective.  
3. Break analysis: Use a deliberately wrong `p̂` (e.g., removing the object entirely) to test if steering can completely derail generation, validating prompt sensitivity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What is the upper limit on object counts that diffusion models can reliably generate, and why does accuracy degrade beyond ~10 objects?**  
  *Basis*: The authors cap the maximum target count at 10 due to substantial degradation beyond this range but do not investigate the cause.  
  *Why unresolved*: No analysis of whether degradation is fundamental to diffusion sampling or specific to steering.

- **How sensitive is ATHENA's performance to errors in the external count estimator (GroundingDINO), and can the framework tolerate imperfect intermediate feedback?**  
  *Basis*: The method relies on GroundingDINO but does not evaluate robustness to detector errors.  
  *Why unresolved*: Detector over-/under-counting could misdirect steering; no experiments with varying detector accuracy.

- **Would multiple adaptive steering iterations (beyond the single adjustment) improve accuracy, and what is the accuracy-runtime trade-off curve for iterative adaptation?**  
  *Basis*: The method adjusts γ at most once, claiming "diminishing returns" without empirical validation.  
  *Why unresolved*: Multi-step adaptation could help difficult prompts; no ablation on 2–5 iterations.

## Limitations
- Detector configuration (GroundingDINO version, thresholds) is unspecified; results may vary with detector settings.
- Diffusion sampler and noise schedule details are omitted; optimal `t_test` and `t_steer` likely depend on them.
- Control prompt construction (how numbers are removed/replaced) lacks precise definition, risking unintended semantic shifts.
- Normalization step during steering may have edge cases (e.g., zero norm difference) not covered, risking instability.
- The method hinges on an external detector; detector failures propagate to steering decisions.

## Confidence
- **Early structural commitments lock object count**: *Medium* (common diffusion assumption but limited direct evidence in the paper).
- **Dual-noise difference provides a count-biasing direction**: *Medium* (heuristic without theoretical guarantee; may fail if `p̂` alters semantics).
- **Adaptive γ adjustment improves robustness**: *Medium* (empirically effective but could be brittle for non-monotonic error responses).
- **Overall improvements (accuracy ↑22.3%, MAE ↓39–49%, faster runtime, lower memory)**: *High* (consistent across multiple backbones and datasets).

## Next Checks
1. **Steering horizon ablation**: Systematically vary `t_steer` and `t_test` across diverse prompts to map sensitivity of count correction, quantify trade-offs with visual quality, and identify a robust operating regime.
2. **Control prompt robustness study**: Test alternative `p̂` generation strategies (e.g., replace numbers with "some", "many", or drop numerals) and measure impact on count accuracy and semantic drift to validate the chosen construction.
3. **Detector failure analysis**: Evaluate GroundingDINO on intermediate decoded images to estimate false-positive/negative rates; correlate these errors with final count mistakes to quantify the detector’s contribution to overall performance and the need for potential fine-tuning.
