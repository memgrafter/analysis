# Story2Proposal: A Scaffold for Structured Scientific Paper Writing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27065
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27065
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieved an average expert evaluation score of 6.145, exceeding DirectChat by +2.182 and the structured baseline Fars by +0.508 across multiple LLM backbones.

## Executive Summary
Automated generation of scientific manuscripts often suffers from structural drift, missing visual elements, and cross-sectional inconsistencies due to unconstrained text synthesis. Story2Proposal addresses these issues with a contract-governed multi-agent framework. A persistent shared visual contract tracks section structure and registered figures/tables, enforcing explicit obligations. Specialized agents (architect, writer, refiner, renderer) operate under this evolving contract within a generate–evaluate–adapt loop, where evaluation feedback dynamically updates constraints. Evaluations on the Jericho corpus across GPT, Claude, Gemini, and Qwen show significant improvements in structural consistency and narrative–visual alignment over direct chat and prior structured methods.

## Method Summary
The system centers on a structured "visual contract" that serves as a persistent state object. It registers all figures/tables with canonical labels, maps them to required sections, and enforces validation rules. An architect agent decomposes a research story into a blueprint and initializes the contract. A writer agent drafts each section strictly under current contract constraints. A refiner agent ensures global coherence and may propose contract updates. A renderer performs deterministic LaTeX validation. Intermediate outputs are evaluated by specialized evaluators; their feedback triggers contract updates, closing the generate–evaluate–adapt loop to correct misalignments before propagation.

## Key Results
- Average expert evaluation score: 6.145 (Story2Proposal) vs 3.963 (DirectChat), a +2.182 improvement.
- Score vs structured baseline Fars: 5.705 vs 5.197 (+0.508).
- Gains consistent across GPT, Claude, Gemini, and Qwen backbones.
- Demonstrated reduced structural drift and better narrative–visual alignment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

**Mechanism 1: Persistent Shared Visual Contract Prevents Drift and Missing Visuals**
- **Claim:** The contract acts as a centralized state machine that enforces explicit, trackable obligations for section structure and visual artifacts, preventing drift and omissions.
- **Mechanism:** It registers all figures/tables with labels, maps them to sections, and enforces rules like label uniqueness and valid cross-references. Agents must reference only registered artifacts, blocking unregistered or duplicate figures.
- **Core assumption:** Structural inconsistencies arise when visuals are treated as ephemeral text tokens rather than first-class obligations with persistent state.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.3 describe the contract’s three layers (global registry, section-level obligations, validation rules).
- **Break condition:** If the architect fails to register required visuals or writer bypasses checks, structural failures reappear.

**Mechanism 2: Generate–Evaluate–Adapt Loop Improves Alignment**
- **Claim:** Integrating evaluation within the generation pipeline allows early detection and correction of reasoning flaws and visual misalignments via dynamic contract updates.
- **Mechanism:** Evaluation agents assess intermediate drafts (sections, refined manuscripts) for issues like data fidelity or visual mismatch. Their feedback signals update contract constraints (e.g., new validation rules), which then guide subsequent agent actions.
- **Core assumption:** Post-hoc validation is insufficient for long-form consistency; intra-loop feedback prevents error propagation.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.5 describe feedback-driven contract state updates enabling early inconsistency detection.
- **Break condition:** If evaluators miss subtle flaws, the contract may not update, allowing scientifically weak but structurally valid outputs.

**Mechanism 3: Role-Specialized Agents Reduce Error Propagation**
- **Claim:** Decomposing generation into architect, writer, refiner, and renderer with shared contract state isolates concerns while maintaining global structural awareness.
- **Mechanism:** Architect establishes blueprint and initial registry; writer drafts under current constraints; refiner harmonizes globally and updates contract; renderer enforces final validation. Bounded scopes and shared state prevent local decisions from violating global obligations.
- **Core assumption:** Monolithic generation lacks modular expertise and state persistence needed for document-level consistency.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract and Section 3.4 describe role separation and its error-reduction benefits.
- **Break condition:** If agents ignore constraints or contract updates are not propagated between stages, modular boundaries fail and inconsistencies return.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept: Contract-Based Coordination**
- **Why needed here:** The entire system relies on the contract as a persistent, evolving state that all agents read and write—not a static prompt or template.
- **Quick check question:** If the writer references a figure not in the contract’s registry, what happens and why is that critical?

**Concept: Stateful Multi-Agent Systems**
- **Why needed here:** Agents must maintain awareness of prior stages via the contract; this is not a stateless sequence of independent calls.
- **Quick check question:** How does the refiner’s access to the contract state differ from the writer’s, and what does that enable?

**Concept: Structured Document Generation as Constraint Satisfaction**
- **Why needed here:** The problem is framed as satisfying evolving constraints (structure, visuals, references) rather than free-form text generation.
- **Quick check question:** What is the consequence of treating a figure as a “constraint” versus as “just text” (as in DirectChat)?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Shared Visual Contract (C) -> Architect (initializes B, updates C) -> Writer (drafts D_i under C) -> Evaluators (feedback r_k) -> Update(C) -> Refiner (merges D_i to M', updates C) -> Renderer (validates LaTeX from M' under C) -> Final Manuscript M. Evaluation agents operate in the loop after writer and refiner stages.

- **Critical path:**
  1. Input research story S, empty contract C0.
  2. Architect: produces blueprint B and contract C1 (populates visual registry V, section obligations).
  3. For each section in B: Writer drafts D_i under current C; evaluators run on D_i; contract updates.
  4. Refiner: merges drafts into M', performs global checks, updates contract.
  5. Renderer: converts M' to LaTeX M with full deterministic validation against final C.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Complexity vs robustness: Persistent contract and multiple agents increase latency but reduce structural failures.
  - Evaluation overhead: Running multiple evaluators at each stage adds compute cost; tradeoff between feedback granularity and speed.
  - Contract scope: Too many constraints may stifle wording creativity; too few may not prevent drift. Requires tuning validation strictness.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Missing figures/tables: Contract registry not populated by architect, or writer inserted unregistered labels.
  - Broken cross-references: Renderer validation fails; likely placement obligations ignored.
  - Inconsistent terminology: Refiner failed to harmonize or contract lacks terminology constraints.
  - Stagnant contract: No evaluation feedback, so early flaws persist.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Contract Initialization Test:** Feed a story with known figure requirements; verify architect’s contract contains exactly those figures with correct labels and section mappings; check for registry errors.
  2. **Constraint Enforcement Test:** Have writer draft a section where blueprint requires a figure, but prompt omission; verify writer includes reference (contract-enforced) or evaluator catches omission and updates contract.
  3. **Feedback Loop Test:** Introduce a subtle inconsistency (e.g., method describes experiment X, results refer to Y); run with evaluator that detects mismatches; verify contract updates with new rule and refiner corrects mismatch in next iteration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1:** How does incomplete or low-quality input research stories affect output quality and structural adherence?  
*Basis:* Section 5.3 identifies "incomplete research inputs" as a limitation; system enforces structure but cannot generate missing evidence, potentially leading to narrative gaps. No experiments varying input completeness were conducted.

**Open Question 2:** Can evaluation agents reliably detect subtle reasoning flaws (e.g., unsupported claims, logical fallacies) that satisfy structural constraints but undermine scientific validity?  
*Basis:* Section 5.3 warns evaluation agents may miss "subtle reasoning weaknesses" where content meets contract rules but remains scientifically weak, preventing updates. No reasoning-specific benchmarking was done.

**Open Question 3:** What are the individual contributions of each generation agent and the generate–evaluate–adapt loop to the observed performance gains?  
*Basis:* Section 4.3 explicitly states no ablation study was included. Without ablations, it is unclear which components are essential or whether gains stem from the contract, specialization, or feedback loop.

**Open Question 4:** How generalizable is the shared visual contract schema across scientific domains with differing structural conventions (e.g., biology vs. theoretical computer science)?  
*Basis:* Conclusion calls for "domain-specific validation mechanisms," implying current design may not transfer universally. All experiments used the Jericho corpus (computer science); domain diversity untested.

## Limitations
- Unknown specifics: exact agent prompts, contract update logic, evaluation thresholds, and Jericho corpus preprocessing.
- No ablation study: Cannot isolate contributions of contract mechanism, agent roles, or evaluation loop.
- Limited domain testing: Only evaluated on computer science papers; generalizability to other fields unverified.
- Potential expert scoring bias; sample size of reviewers not reported.

## Confidence
- Contract-governed coordination prevents structural drift: **Medium** (architecture supports, but no causal ablation).
- Generate–evaluate–adapt loop improves alignment: **Medium** (loop integral, impact not isolated).
- Role-specialized agents reduce error propagation: **Medium** (plausible modularity, no independent verification).
- Overall performance improvement vs DirectChat/Fars: **High** (empirical results across multiple backbones with consistent gains).

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation of contract:** Run system without shared contract state (no persistent registry) to quantify drift and missing visuals; expect score drop to DirectChat levels.
2. **Evaluator specificity:** Replace LLM evaluators with rule-based checks; measure change in final scores to assess how much improvement stems from semantic feedback vs structural enforcement.
3. **Cross-domain generality:** Test framework on a different scientific domain (e.g., biomedical) using an out-of-sample corpus; compare structural metrics and expert scores to Jericho results.
