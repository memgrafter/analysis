# Deriving Hyperparameter Scaling Laws via Modern Optimization Theory

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.15958  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15958  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Analytical hyperparameter scaling laws for learning rate, momentum, and batch size under fixed model size using a convergence bound for LMO optimizers.

## Executive Summary
This paper develops a theoretical framework to derive scaling laws for hyperparameters—learning rate, momentum, and batch size—when training deep neural networks with Linear Minimization Oracle (LMO) methods such as normalized SGD and signSGD/Muon. By treating a non-convex convergence bound as a proxy for optimization suboptimality, the authors analytically minimize it across different tuning regimes (fixed momentum, fixed batch, or joint) as a function of token budget \(T = bK\). The analysis yields closed-form power-law schedules, revealing distinct behaviors compared to vanilla SGD, including a non-trivial optimal batch size and a strong interaction between momentum and batch scaling. Transfer rules are provided to extrapolate hyperparameters from small to large token budgets, offering a theoretical complement to empirical scaling laws.

## Method Summary
The core method involves using a recently derived convergence bound for LMO optimizers, which expresses worst-case suboptimality as a sum of optimization, noise, burn-in, and smoothness terms (Equation 4). This bound serves as a surrogate objective \( \text{risk}_T(\eta, \alpha, b) \). Under assumptions of constant problem parameters (initial loss gap \(\Delta_0\), smoothness \(L\), noise scale \(\sigma\), norm constant \(\rho\)), finite-variance gradient noise scaling as \(\sigma^2/b\), and matched initialization, the authors perform analytical minimization of this proxy for a fixed token budget \(T = bK\). Three tuning regimes are considered: (i) fixed momentum \(\alpha\), (ii) fixed batch size \(b\), and (iii) joint tuning of all hyperparameters. The minimization yields asymptotic power-law scalings for the optimal hyperparameters as functions of \(T\). The derived laws are verified through numerical minimization of the proxy and small-scale constant-learning-rate experiments.

## Key Results
- **Fixed momentum:** Optimal learning rate \(\eta^\star \propto b^{1/2} T^{-1/2}\); token-optimal batch size \(b^\star \propto T^{1/2}\) for large \(T\).
- **Fixed batch size:** Optimal momentum \(\alpha^\star \propto b T^{-1/2}\); optimal learning rate \(\eta^\star \propto b T^{-3/4}\).
- **Joint tuning:** Asymptotic scalings \(b^\star \propto T^{1/6}\), \(\eta^\star \propto T^{-7/12}\), \(\alpha^\star \propto T^{-1/3}\), with convergence rate \(\propto T^{-1/4}\). However, any batch growth \(b \propto T^{\phi}\) with \(\phi \leq 1/2\) remains near-optimal if momentum is retuned as \(\alpha \propto b/\sqrt{T}\).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
1. **Minimizing the convergence bound**: The LMO bound (Equation 4) quantifies worst-case suboptimality via distinct terms. By analytically minimizing this proxy under \(T = bK\) and assuming constant problem parameters and matched initialization, closed-form power-law schedules emerge (Theorems 1–3). The bound’s structure—especially the burn-in term—drives the scaling exponents. Evidence: Formal derivations in Section 3; numerical proxy minimization in Figures 1 and 2 confirms trends.
2. **Broken cancellation for batch size**: In vanilla SGD, optimization and variance terms cancel after \(\eta\)-optimization, making batch size irrelevant under fixed \(T\). The LMO bound adds a burn-in term (\(\propto 1/(\alpha\sqrt{bK})\)) and a floor term (\(\propto \sigma\sqrt{\alpha}/b\)) that scale differently with \(b\) and \(\alpha\), preventing cancellation. Hence, a non-trivial \(b^\star \propto T^{1/2}\) arises when \(\alpha\) is fixed (Theorem 1). Evidence: Abstract and Section 3.1 highlight this distinction.
3. **Momentum tuning flexibility**: Joint optimization yields a specific optimum, but sensitivity analysis shows that any \(b(T) \propto T^{\phi}\) with \(\phi \leq 1/2\) achieves the optimal \(T^{-1/4}\) rate if momentum is retuned as \(\alpha \propto b/\sqrt{T}\) (Corollary 2). The burn-in term’s trade-off with \(\alpha\) creates a broad plateau of near-optimal configurations. Evidence: Abstract, Section 3.2, Corollary 2, and Figure 9.

## Foundational Learning
- **Linear Minimization Oracle (LMO)**: Unifies normalized SGD, signSGD, and Muon under a norm-based framework. The choice of norm \(\|\cdot\|\) determines the specific optimizer. *Why needed*: The entire analysis is built on the LMO update. *Quick check*: If the norm is \(\ell_\infty\), which optimizer does LMO recover? (Answer: signSGD.)
- **Token budget \(T = bK\) and tuning regimes**: \(T\) represents total tokens processed. The three regimes (fixed \(\alpha\), fixed \(b\), joint) correspond to common practical constraints. *Why needed*: Scaling laws are derived as \(T\) varies under each regime. *Quick check*: For fixed \(\alpha\), what is \(b^\star\) scaling with \(T\)? (Answer: \(b^\star \propto T^{1/2}\).)
- **Structure of the LMO convergence bound**: The proxy \(\text{risk}_T\) combines an optimization term (\(\propto 1/(\eta K)\)), noise terms (\(\propto \sigma/(\alpha\sqrt{bK})\) and \(\propto \sigma\sqrt{\alpha}/b\)), and a smoothness term (\(\propto \eta(1+1/\alpha)\)). *Why needed*: The interplay of these terms dictates the scaling exponents. *Quick check*: Which term prevents batch-size cancellation like in SGD? (Answer: the burn-in term \(\propto 1/(\alpha\sqrt{bK})\).)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Proxy objective (Equation 4) → Derived scaling laws (Theorems 1–3) → Transfer rules (Tables 2 and 3) → Verification experiments (Numerical proxy minimization and small-scale training in Figure 3).
- **Critical path**: 1. Identify applicable tuning regime (fixed \(b\)? fixed \(\alpha\)? joint?). 2. At a small budget \(T_0\), perform grid search over allowed hyperparameters to find optimum \((\eta_0, \alpha_0, b_0)\). 3. Use the appropriate transfer rule from Table 2 or 3 to extrapolate to target budget \(T_1\). 4. Respect feasibility constraints (integer \(b\), \(\alpha \in (0,1]\), \(\eta\) stability caps). 5. Optionally validate with a modest run at \(T_1\).
- **Design tradeoffs**:  
  - *Simplicity vs. optimality*: Regime A (fixed \(b\), fixed \(\alpha\)) is trivial but may suffer a noise floor if batch cannot grow. Regime D (joint) is theoretically optimal but may be impractical on fixed hardware.  
  - *Momentum tuning effort*: Regime B (fixed \(b\), tuned \(\alpha\)) improves rate with minimal extra effort (adjust \(\beta\)), but gain over a good fixed \(\alpha\) is at most a constant factor ~1.19.  
  - *Stability*: Larger batches (\(\phi > 1/2\)) require \(\eta \propto T^{-(1-\phi)/2}\) decay; milder decay leads to saturation (Figure 10).
- **Failure signatures**:  
  - *Learning-rate mismatch*: For fixed \(\alpha\), if \(\eta\) does not decay at least as \(T^{-1/2}\) when increasing \(T\), divergence or worse bounds occur.  
  - *Noise floor under batch cap*: With capped \(b\) and constant \(\alpha\), the bound’s floor \(\propto \sigma\sqrt{\alpha}/b_{\text{max}}\) persists empirically as an irreducible loss.  
  - *Positive \(\eta\)–\(T\) exponent for fixed \(b\)*: Empirically observing \(\eta^\star\) grow with \(T\) contradicts Theorem 2 (\(\eta \propto T^{-3/4}\)), suggesting noise scaling violation (e.g., \(q \neq 1/2\) in \(\text{Var} \propto b^{-q}\)) or presence of warm-up.
- **First 3 experiments**:  
  1. **Proxy verification**: For a range of \(T\) (e.g., \(10^2\) to \(10^{22}\)), compute \(\text{risk}_T\) on log grids for \(\eta, \alpha, b\). Plot optimal hyperparameters vs. \(T\) to confirm predicted exponents (see Figures 1, 2, 9).  
  2. **Constant-η training sweep**: Train a transformer (~160M params) with constant \(\eta\) across batch sizes (e.g., 32, 128, 512) and token budgets up to several billion. For each \((b, T)\), find optimal \(\eta\) via grid search. Check (i) \(\eta^\star\) decreases with \(T\) at fixed \(b\), and (ii) \(\eta^\star\) increases with \(b\) at fixed \(T\) (as in Figure 3).  
  3. **Transfer rule validation**: Tune at small \(T_0\) (e.g., \(10^6\) tokens) via modest search. Predict hyperparameters for larger \(T_1\) (e.g., \(10^9\)) using Regime B or D. Run the predicted configuration and compare performance to a full grid-search optimum at \(T_1\); quantify the gap.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Performance penalty of fixed schedules**: What is the inefficiency when hyperparameters are not retuned as token budget grows? (Section 5)
- **Dependence on model size and noise**: How do optimal schedules interact with model size \(N\), effective smoothness, gradient noise characteristics, and µP parametrization? (Section 5)
- **Source of theory–practice mismatch**: Which factors cause deviations between theoretical exponents and empirical scaling laws: protocol constraints, assumption violations (e.g., noise tails), or bound looseness? (Section 5)

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis assumes constant step size, matched initialization, and finite-variance gradient noise scaling as \(b^{-1/2}\); real training uses schedules, warm-up, weight decay, and may have heavy-tailed gradients.
- Problem-specific constants (\(\Delta_0, L, \rho, \sigma\)) are not calibrated; the proxy is minimized with unit constants, limiting quantitative predictions.
- Empirical validation is limited to a 160M transformer using Adam (a loose LMO variant) with constant \(\eta\) up to 5B tokens; large-scale LMO training (e.g., Muon) remains untested.
- The token budget \(T = bK\) prescribes free scaling of steps \(K\) with batch \(b\); if dataset size caps \(K\) (no data reuse), the derived laws may not hold.

## Confidence
- **Derived scaling exponents (Theorems 1–3)**: High—follow rigorously from bound minimization under stated assumptions.
- **Batch-size relevance via broken cancellation**: Medium—relies on bound structure; validity depends on bound tightness.
- **Near-optimality for \(\phi \leq 1/2\) (Corollary 2)**: Medium—asymptotic analysis; finite-\(T\) effects may shift optimum.
- **Empirical trends (η* decreases with T at fixed b, increases with b at fixed T)**: Low—based on small-scale Adam experiments; weak evidence for LMO methods.

## Next Checks
1. **Calibrate the proxy**: Estimate \(\Delta_0, L, \sigma, \rho\) from short runs of normalized SGD/Muon on a ~1B model. Re-minimize the proxy with these constants and compare resulting exponents to theory; assess sensitivity to constant choices.
2. **Large-scale constant-η sweep**: Train a transformer (≥1B params) with LMO optimizers across batch sizes and token budgets up to ~100B. Extract optimal \(\eta, \alpha\) under each regime; verify predicted power laws (e.g., \(\eta^\star \propto b^{1/2} T^{-1/2}\)) and test transfer rules by predicting large-\(T\) hyperparameters from small-\(T\) optima.
3. **Noise tail analysis**: Measure gradient norm distributions during training to check variance scaling (\(\propto 1/b\)) and tail heaviness (exponent \(p < 2\)). If heavy tails are present, modify the proxy’s noise model (\(\sigma \propto b^{-1/p}\)), derive new exponents, and compare to empirical optima.
