# NewsScope: Schema-Grounded Cross-Domain News Claim Extraction with Open Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.08852  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08852  
- **Reference count:** 1  
- **Primary result:** Fine-tuned LLaMA 3.1 8B with LoRA achieves 98.8% schema validity and 89.4% factual accuracy, comparable to GPT-4o-mini (93.7%, p=0.07), with superior political claim extraction (94.3%).

## Executive Summary
NewsScope introduces an open-model approach for automated news verification by extracting claims that strictly adhere to a predefined schema across diverse news domains. Using a small fine-tuning dataset of 315 examples from 455 articles, it adapts LLaMA 3.1 8B via LoRA, yielding high schema compliance (98.8%) and factual accuracy (89.4%) that rivals proprietary models like GPT-4o-mini, particularly excelling on political claims. A numeric grounding filter further boosts accuracy, and the system generalizes reasonably to out-of-source articles, enabling cost-effective offline deployment.

## Method Summary
The method centers on defining a structured schema for news claims and fine-tuning LLaMA 3.1 8B using LoRA on 315 manually annotated examples drawn from a corpus of 455 articles. The schema enforces consistency in claim formatting and content, while a post-hoc numeric grounding filter verifies numerical statements against article context to improve factual reliability. Evaluation combines automated schema validity checks and human assessment of 400 extracted claims for factual accuracy, with comparisons to GPT-4o-mini and tests of domain generalization.

## Key Results
- Schema validity on fine-tuning set: 98.8%.
- Factual accuracy on human evaluation: 89.4% (vs. GPT-4o-mini 93.7%, p=0.07).
- Political claim accuracy: 94.3% (vs. GPT-4o-mini 87.8%).
- Numeric grounding filter improves accuracy to 91.6%.
- Out-of-source generalization: 88.5% (vs. 89.8% in-domain).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Schema-grounded extraction imposes strict structural constraints on model outputs, reducing variability and ensuring extracted claims are well-formed and comparable. Fine-tuning via LoRA efficiently adapts a large pretrained model to this specific task with minimal examples, leveraging its general language knowledge while specializing in news-domain patterns. The numeric grounding filter acts as a lightweight verifier for a common error source (incorrect numbers), providing a targeted accuracy boost without full model retraining. Together, these elements balance specificity, efficiency, and reliability for open-model deployment.

## Foundational Learning
- **Schema-Grounded Extraction:** Enforces consistent claim structure to enable downstream verification and comparison. *Why needed:* Prevents malformed or inconsistent outputs that undermine utility. *Quick check:* High schema validity rate (98.8%) indicates effective adherence.
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA):** Updates only low-rank subsets of model weights for task adaptation. *Why needed:* Achieves specialization with limited data (315 examples) and reduces computational cost. *Quick check:* Strong performance despite small fine-tuning set.
- **Cross-Domain Generalization:** Model maintains performance on articles outside the fine-tuning distribution. *Why needed:* Real-world news covers unpredictable topics and sources. *Quick check:* Small accuracy drop (1.3%) from in-domain to out-of-source suggests robustness.
- **Numeric Grounding Filter:** Post-processes claims to verify numerical statements against source article text. *Why needed:* Numbers are a frequent source of factual errors in extracted claims. *Quick check:* Accuracy increase from 89.4% to 91.6% demonstrates its impact.
- **Open-Model Deployment:** Uses publicly available models (LLaMA 3.1) and techniques (LoRA) instead of proprietary APIs. *Why needed:* Enables offline use, cost control, and transparency. *Quick check:* Estimated on-demand compute cost of ~$15 supports economic feasibility.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Article -> Schema Definition -> LLaMA 3.1 8B + LoRA Adapters -> Raw Claim Extraction -> Numeric Grounding Filter -> Validated Claims.
- **Critical path:** Article ingestion → model inference with schema constraints → numeric verification → output.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Schema complexity (coverage vs. precision); fine-tuning data size (robustness vs. overfitting risk); numeric filter (precision vs. recall, potentially discarding valid claims).
- **Failure signatures:** Schema violations (98.8% validity implies ~1.2% failure rate); factual inaccuracies especially in non-political domains; degradation on radically new publishers or topics; filter over- or under-verification.
- **First experiments:** 1. Evaluate on a larger, multi-domain human dataset (n≥1500) with confidence intervals. 2. Ablate numeric filter to measure its isolated effect on precision/recall. 3. Test on articles from completely unseen news outlets and time periods to probe generalization limits.

## Limitations
- Fine-tuning dataset is small (315 examples), risking limited schema coverage and model robustness.
- Domain distribution is unclear; strong political performance may not generalize to all news topics.
- Human evaluation sample (400 claims) is moderate; lack of confidence intervals weakens statistical claims.
- Comparison to GPT-4o-mini yields p=0.07, providing only marginal evidence of parity.
- Schema validity (98.8%) does not perfectly correlate with factual accuracy (89.4%), indicating residual error sources.
- Numeric grounding filter’s contribution lacks precision/recall trade-off analysis.
- “Out-of-source” generalization definition is vague; minimal drop may not hold under true distribution shift.
- Cost estimate ($15) is underspecified regarding hardware, latency, and batch size assumptions.

## Confidence
- **Schema compliance (98.8%):** Medium (small dataset risk of overfitting to schema).
- **Overall factual accuracy (89.4%):** Medium (moderate eval size, no confidence intervals).
- **Political claim superiority (94.3% vs 87.8%):** Medium (subset analysis, not pre-registered).
- **Generalization (88.5% out-of-source):** Low (vague out-of-distribution definition).
- **Cost-effectiveness ($15 on-demand):** Low (unspecified deployment parameters).

## Next Checks
1. Scale human evaluation to ≥1,500 claims across at least 5 distinct news domains, reporting 95% confidence intervals for all metrics.
2. Conduct a paired significance test on political claim extraction between NewsScope and GPT-4o-mini using n≥500 to resolve the p=0.07 ambiguity.
3. Perform rigorous out-of-distribution testing with articles from entirely unseen publishers and time periods, coupled with detailed error analysis to identify failure modes.
