# CRPS-Optimal Binning for Conformal Regression

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.22000  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22000  
- **Reference count:** 27  
- **Primary result:** A non-parametric method that produces contiguous bins by minimizing a closed-form leave-one-out CRPS cost via dynamic programming, yielding substantially narrower prediction intervals than existing conformal methods while maintaining nominal coverage.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a distribution-free method for conditional distribution estimation and conformal prediction. It sorts data by a univariate covariate and partitions it into contiguous bins by minimizing a closed-form leave-one-out Continuous Ranked Probability Score (LOO-CRPS). The bin boundaries are found exactly via dynamic programming. The number of bins is chosen by cross-validated test CRPS to avoid overfitting. For a new observation, the within-bin empirical distribution is used to construct either a Venn prediction band or a conformal prediction interval with CRPS as the nonconformity score, which provides finite-sample marginal coverage guarantees. The approach adapts automatically to heteroscedasticity and multimodality, achieving significantly narrower intervals than benchmarks on real data.

## Method Summary
The procedure begins by sorting the training data \((x_i, y_i)\) by \(x\). For any candidate contiguous bin, the total LOO-CRPS cost has a closed form \(\frac{m}{(m-1)^2} W\), where \(m\) is the bin size and \(W\) is the sum of absolute pairwise differences of the \(y\)-values. This cost is additive across bins. A dynamic programming recurrence computes the globally optimal partition for any fixed number of bins \(K\). To select \(K\), an alternating hold-out split is used: for each candidate \(K\), the optimal partition on the training half is evaluated on the calibration half via test CRPS. The \(K\) minimizing test CRPS is chosen. The final model refits the DP on the full data with this \(K\). For a test point \(x^*\), its bin’s empirical CDF yields a Venn band (fixed-width) or, using CRPS as a nonconformity score, a conformal interval with guaranteed marginal coverage.

## Key Results
- Prediction intervals are 11–40% narrower than Gaussian split conformal, CQR, and CQR-QRF on the Old Faithful dataset, and 2.2× narrower on the motorcycle accident dataset, while maintaining near-nominal coverage (90.3% and 91.0% at the 90% level).
- The method fully adapts to heteroscedasticity and multimodality in the conditional response \(Y|X\).
- The LOO-CRPS cost function enables exact global optimality of bin boundaries via dynamic programming, a novel integration of optimal segmentation with distribution-free prediction.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The closed-form LOO-CRPS cost enables exact global optimal bin boundaries via dynamic programming.
- **Mechanism:** For a bin of size \(m\), the total leave-one-out CRPS simplifies to \(\frac{m}{(m-1)^2} W\), where \(W\) is the sum of absolute pairwise differences of the bin’s \(y\)-values. This cost is additive over bins. The dynamic programming recurrence considers all possible last-bin boundaries and selects the minimum total cost for each \(k\) and \(j\), guaranteeing a globally optimal \(K\)-partition.
- **Core assumption:** The covariate is univariate and observations are sorted, so contiguous bins are meaningful; the cost function depends only on the \(y\)-values within each bin and is additive.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "closed-form cost enables exact global optimality via dynamic programming."  
  - [section 4] Proposition 1 derives \(\text{cost}(S) = \frac{m}{(m-1)^2} W\).  
  - [section 5] DP recurrence and optimal substructure property.
- **Break condition:** For multivariate covariates the contiguity constraint does not naturally extend; a projection or greedy tree would be needed, losing global optimality.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Cross-validated test CRPS selects the number of bins \(K\) that balances bin homogeneity and statistical efficiency, avoiding the in-sample optimism of within-sample LOO-CRPS.
- **Mechanism:** The data are split into alternating training and test halves. For each candidate \(K\), the DP finds the optimal partition on the training half. The test CRPS is computed by evaluating the within-bin empirical CDFs on the test observations. This score penalizes partitions that overfit accidental \(y\)-heterogeneity (high test CRPS) and partitions that are too fine (high test CRPS due to small \(m\)). The U-shaped curve yields a minimum.
- **Core assumption:** The alternating split preserves the \(x\)-ordering and is representative; the test CRPS as a function of \(K\) is U-shaped with a clear minimum. If the DGP lacks a clear optimal granularity, the curve may be flat or multi‑modal.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "Model selection for the number of bins \(K\) uses a cross-validated test CRPS criterion to avoid in‑sample optimism."  
  - [section 7] "Within-sample LOO-CRPS turns out to be inappropriate... we instead select \(K\) by evaluating test CRPS on an alternating held‑out split, which yields a U‑shaped criterion."  
  - [section 7.1] Figure 2 shows within-sample nearly monotone decreasing vs. test U‑shape.
- **Break condition:** Very small sample size yields high variance in test CRPS, making \(K\) selection unstable. If the DGP is such that test CRPS does not have a unique minimum, the selected \(K\) may be suboptimal.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Using CRPS as the nonconformity score yields prediction intervals with finite‑sample marginal coverage guarantees and connected intervals, while adapting to local conditional dispersion.
- **Mechanism:** For a test point falling in a bin, compute nonconformity scores for each training point as the LOO‑CRPS of leaving that point out of the bin’s ECDF. The test score is the CRPS of the full bin ECDF evaluated at the candidate \(y_h\). The conformal \(p\)-value is the fraction of scores as large as the test score. The prediction set \(\Gamma_\varepsilon = \{y_h : p(y_h) > \varepsilon\}\) has coverage at least \(1-\varepsilon\) under exchangeability of the bin’s responses. The convexity of the CRPS score in \(y_h\) ensures \(\Gamma_\varepsilon\) is a connected interval.
- **Core assumption:** The \((y_1,\dots,y_m,y^*)\) are exchangeable within each bin. This holds exactly if the conditional distribution \(P(Y|X=x)\) is constant over the bin’s \(x\)-range; approximately if the bin is narrow.
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - [abstract] "conformal prediction set using CRPS as the nonconformity score, which provides finite‑sample marginal coverage guarantees."  
  - [section 8.2] Proposition 2 and proof.  
  - [section 8.4] convexity argument for connected intervals.
- **Break condition:** Violation of exchangeability leads to coverage below nominal, which occurs when bins are too wide relative to the smoothness of the conditional distribution. If \(m < \lceil 1/\varepsilon \rceil\), the set may be empty or the entire real line.

## Foundational Learning

**Concept:** Proper Scoring Rules (CRPS)  
- *Why needed here:* The binning objective (LOO‑CRPS) and the nonconformity score both rely on CRPS, a strictly proper scoring rule that incentivizes honest distributional forecasts.  
- *Quick check:* "What does it mean for a scoring rule to be strictly proper, and why does that property matter for selecting bin boundaries?"

**Concept:** Exchangeability  
- *Why needed here:* The finite‑sample coverage guarantee of conformal prediction rests on the exchangeability of training and test responses within each bin.  
- *Quick check:* "In the context of this method, what would break exchangeability within a bin, and how does that affect coverage?"

**Concept:** Leave‑One‑Out Cross‑Validation (LOOCV)  
- *Why needed here:* The total LOO‑CRPS for a bin is computed via a closed‑form that sums over leave‑one‑out predictions. This is the cost minimized by the DP. Model selection for \(K\) uses a test CRPS that is essentially a held‑out evaluation.  
- *Quick check:* "Why is the within‑sample LOO‑CRPS not suitable for choosing \(K\), whereas the held‑out test CRPS is?"

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**  
Preprocessing (sort by x) -> Cost matrix computation (compute c[i][j] for all i<j) -> Dynamic programming (optimal partition for fixed K) -> Cross-validation (alternating split, test CRPS evaluation) -> Model selection (choose K*) -> Refitting (final bins on full data) -> Prediction (locate bin, compute Venn band or conformal interval).

**Critical path:** The dominant cost is the \(O(n^2 \log n)\) precomputation of the cost matrix. The DP for a single \(K\) is \(O(n^2 K)\); cross‑validation requires computing DP up to \(K_{\max}\) once on the training half, total \(O(K_{\max} n_{\text{train}}^2)\). With \(K_{\max} = \lfloor n/10 \rfloor\), this is \(O(n^3)\) in the worst case, feasible for moderate \(n\) (e.g., \(n \le 2000\)) but may require approximations for larger \(n\).

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Full‑data vs. split conformal:* Full‑data reuses all data for fitting and calibration, yielding narrower intervals but relying on approximate exchangeability; split ensures exact exchangeability but uses less data.  
- *CRPS vs. \(k\)-NN nonconformity:* CRPS gives connected intervals; \(k\)-NN yields potentially non‑connected sets that better capture multimodal within‑bin distributions.  
- *Exact DP vs. greedy segmentation:* Exact DP guarantees global optimality in \(O(n^2 K)\); greedy segmentation is \(O(n \log n)\) but can be suboptimal.  
- *K selection via CV:* More reliable than within‑sample but computationally heavier; an information‑criterion alternative is not available.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Test CRPS curve monotone decreasing indicates the alternating split failed to break dependency (e.g., strong autocorrelation) or the DGP has no clear optimal \(K\).  
- Coverage below nominal suggests bins are too wide; consider reducing \(K\) or using a narrower alternating split.  
- Excessively wide intervals may indicate \(K\) too large, yielding bins with very few points.  
- Precomputation time \(O(n^2 \log n)\) becomes prohibitive for \(n > 10^4\); consider sampling or approximate DP.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. *Synthetic heteroscedastic example* (Section 9): verify adaptive bin widths, nominal coverage, and narrower intervals than constant‑width methods.  
2. *Old Faithful dataset* (Section 10.1): check that the method captures the bimodal conditional via a single partition boundary and achieves 11–40% width reduction vs. competitors.  
3. *Motorcycle accident dataset* (Section 10.2): validate adaptation to rapidly changing variance and 2.2× width reduction vs. Gaussian split conformal.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does the LOO-CRPS cost function \(c(i,j)\) satisfy the quadrangle inequality, enabling a divide-and-conquer DP speedup from \(O(n^2 K)\) to \(O(n K)\)?
2. Does coherence between using LOO-CRPS for binning and as nonconformity score yield tighter finite-sample coverage bounds or more efficient prediction sets than a mismatched pair?
3. As \(K\) increases (bins narrow), does conditional coverage at a fixed \(x^*\) improve monotonically? Can we bound the exchangeability violation as a function of bin width and local DGP smoothness?
4. Does the cross-validated \(K^* = \arg\min_K \text{TestCRPS}(K)\) converge to a consistent estimator of an optimal bin count as \(n \to \infty\), and under what conditions does the population \(\text{TestCRPS}(K)\) have a unique minimum?

## Limitations
- Multivariate extension: contiguous binning relies on 1D ordering; beyond univariate requires heuristics.
- Computational scaling: \(O(n^2)\) cost limits large \(n\) (\(n > 2000\)).
- Model selection via alternating split may be unstable for small \(n\) or weak \(x\)–\(y\) dependence.
- Coverage guarantee is marginal and assumes exchangeability; wide bins can violate exchangeability.
- Empirical evidence limited to two datasets; lacks broad validation (e.g., high-dimensional, large-scale).

## Confidence
- **Optimal bin boundaries:** High. Closed-form LOO‑CRPS and DP optimality proved (Prop 1); relies on contiguity.
- **K selection via test CRPS:** Medium. U‑shaped test CRPS shown on one dataset; consistency for other DGPs unproven.
- **Conformal coverage:** High. Finite-sample marginal coverage bound derived (Prop 2) under exchangeability.
- **Empirical width reduction:** Medium. Results from two datasets; lacking statistical tests and broader benchmarks.

## Next Checks
1. **Scalability & multivariate extension:** Test DP on \(n > 10^4\) via approximations; evaluate multivariate heuristic (e.g., random projections) and compare interval width and coverage.
2. **Stability of K selection:** Across 100 random alternating splits on 20 synthetic DGPs, compute variance of chosen \(K\) and correlate with held‑out test CRPS to identify unstable regimes.
3. **Coverage under exchangeability violations:** Simulate within‑bin heteroscedastic data (variance increasing with \(x\)) and measure empirical coverage for nominal 90%; compare to theoretical guarantee and bin width.
