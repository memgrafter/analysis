# Align and Filter: Improving Performance in Asynchronous On-Policy RL

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.01365  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01365  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** VACO improves robustness to policy lag in asynchronous on-policy RL, achieving higher normalized returns, IQM, and median scores on MuJoCo and GSM8k tasks compared to PPO, IMPALA, and SPO.

## Executive Summary
Asynchronous on-policy reinforcement learning suffers from performance degradation due to policy lag—the mismatch between the behavior policy collecting data and the learning policy being updated. This lag arises from both backward mismatch (initial distribution shift) and forward divergence (accumulated updates). The proposed VACO method combines Advantage Realignment, which uses a fixed V-trace estimator to compute advantages from off-policy data aligned with the initial policy, and TV-based filtering, which discards minibatch gradients that would exceed a total variation divergence threshold. Experiments on MuJoCo robotic tasks with varying simulated asynchronicity and a large language model reasoning task (GSM8k) show VACO consistently outperforms baselines, maintaining higher returns and accuracy as asynchronicity increases.

## Method Summary
VACO augments standard on-policy algorithms (e.g., PPO) with two mechanisms. First, **Advantage Realignment** computes the advantage function using a V-trace estimator that remains fixed to the initial learning policy, correcting for backward lag when using stale off-policy data. Second, **TV-based filtering** monitors the total variation divergence between the current policy and the initial policy; during minibatch updates, any gradient step that would push this divergence beyond a threshold δ is filtered out, mitigating forward lag from repeated updates. This allows safe policy progression despite asynchronous data collection.

## Key Results
- On MuJoCo tasks with simulated asynchronicity, VACO achieves higher normalized returns, interquartile mean (IQM), and median scores than PPO, IMPALA, and SPO.
- On the GSM8k LLM reasoning task, VACO maintains higher evaluation accuracy as the number of asynchronous minibatches (forward lag) increases, while adaptively filtering fewer updates at low lag and more selectively at high lag.
- VACO demonstrates superior robustness to backward lag compared to all baselines across tested domains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Advantage Realignment addresses backward lag by using a V-trace estimator with fixed importance weights (based on the initial policy), providing stable advantage estimates from off-policy data without requiring frequent re-estimation. This prevents early training instability from stale gradients. TV-based filtering tackles forward lag by bounding the cumulative policy shift: each minibatch gradient is evaluated for its contribution to total variation divergence from the initial policy, and updates exceeding a threshold are discarded. This selective updating ensures the policy does not drift too far from the distribution of the data used to compute advantages, maintaining on-policyness despite asynchronous updates. The combination allows VACO to leverage large amounts of stale data safely while avoiding divergence.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Actors (collect experience) → Replay Buffer (store transitions) → V-trace Module (compute fixed advantages) → TV Filter (evaluate per-minibatch TV contribution) → Policy Update (apply filtered gradients).

**Critical path**: Ingest new data → compute V-trace advantages using initial policy → for each minibatch, estimate TV increase from proposed update → if TV increase < δ, apply gradient; else skip → repeat until batch exhausted.

**Design tradeoffs**: Fixed V-trace (computationally cheap, may be biased) vs. continuous re-estimation (expensive, adaptive); TV threshold δ (higher → more updates but risk drift, lower → safer but slower progress).

**Failure signatures**: Sudden performance collapse when filtering removes nearly all updates (δ too low); persistent suboptimal performance when V-trace truncation underestimates advantages (¯ρ too restrictive); divergence if TV threshold is ignored or set too high.

**First experiments**:

2. Ablate Advantage Realignment: replace V-trace with standard on-policy advantages (using current policy) and measure degradation under lag.
3. Sweep TV threshold δ from 0.01 to 1.0 on Walker2d, plotting final return versus average fraction of filtered updates to identify robust range.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **How does the TV divergence threshold δ in VACO's filtering mechanism interact with varying degrees of asynchronicity across different task domains?**  
   *Basis in paper*: The paper fixes δ=0.2 and performs a limited ablation on MuJoCo tasks (Appendix D.2), showing robustness but not exploring diverse domains or extreme lag conditions.  
   *Why unresolved*: The optimal δ likely depends on task complexity and lag magnitude; a fixed threshold may be suboptimal for new environments or higher asynchronicity levels not tested.  
   *What evidence would resolve it*: Systematic evaluation of VACO with a wide range of δ values on both robotics (e.g., MuJoCo, Panda) and LLM tasks (e.g., GSM8k, MATH), measuring performance metrics and TV divergence profiles.

2. **What is the precise computational-performance trade-off between Advantage Realignment (fixed V-trace) and continuous advantage re-estimation (as in IMPALA) under different policy lag regimes?**  
   *Basis in paper*: The paper claims Advantage Realignment is "significantly more computationally efficient" than IMPALA (Section 4.2.1) but does not quantify the trade-off.  
   *Why unresolved*: While VACO outperforms IMPALA empirically, the contribution of advantage realignment versus TV filtering is not isolated; moreover, the efficiency gain may diminish with frequent policy updates or complex value networks.  
   *What evidence would resolve it*: Controlled experiments varying the frequency of advantage re-calculation (from once per data batch to every gradient step) and measuring wall-clock time, memory usage, and final returns across tasks with controlled lag levels.

3. **Does VACO's TV-based filtering generalize effectively to on-policy algorithms beyond PPO/GRPO, such as TRPO or A2C, without interfering with their specific trust region mechanisms?**  
   *Basis in paper*: VACO is implemented on PPO (and GRPO, a PPO variant) and compared to SPO; TRPO and A2C are not evaluated, despite being standard on-policy methods.  
   *Why unresolved*: TRPO uses KL constraints, while A2C lacks explicit trust regions; the compatibility of TV filtering with these different update rules and its impact on stability remain unknown.  
   *What evidence would resolve it*: Integrate VACO's filtering into TRPO and A2C implementations, then train on asynchronous MuJoCo and LLM tasks, comparing performance, constraint satisfaction, and sample efficiency against their vanilla versions.

4. **How do the V-trace hyperparameters ¯ρ (importance truncation) and ¯c (bias correction) affect VACO's robustness to policy lag, and is the default ¯c=1 optimal across settings?**  
   *Basis in paper*: The paper varies ¯ρ in an ablation (Appendix D.3) but does not vary ¯c, despite both appearing in the V-trace definition (Equation 14).  
   *Why unresolved*: ¯ρ controls the fixed point of V-trace, while ¯c influences convergence; their interaction with TV filtering and advantage realignment is unexplored, especially under high asynchronicity.  
   *What evidence would resolve it*: Conduct a grid search over ¯ρ ∈ {1, 2, ∞} and ¯c ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0} in the simulated asynchronous MuJoCo setup and LLM experiment, analyzing performance and TV divergence to identify optimal combinations per lag level.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to MuJoCo and GSM8k; generalizability to other domains (e.g., Atari, real robotics) unknown.
- No statistical reporting (means, standard errors) across random seeds; results could be noisy or spurious.
- Baselines (PPO, IMPALA, SPO) may not have been tuned for asynchronous settings, risking unfair comparisons.
