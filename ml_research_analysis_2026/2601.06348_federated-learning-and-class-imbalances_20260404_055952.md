# Federated Learning and Class Imbalances

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06348
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06348
- **Reference count:** 40

## Executive Summary
Federated learning with heterogeneous clients faces robustness challenges from label noise and non-IID data distributions. This work reproduces and extends RHFL+, a method combining dynamic label refinement, symmetric cross-entropy loss, and client confidence reweighting to mitigate noisy labels. Implemented in NVIDIA's production-grade NVFlare framework, RHFL+ was evaluated on CIFAR-10 and three medical imaging datasets. On CIFAR-10 with synthetic noise, it achieved superior accuracy and scaled stably to 100 clients. On medical datasets, improvements over local-only training were observed in low-noise settings, but performance on CBIS-DDSM was inconsistent, highlighting the need for dataset-specific tuning. The study demonstrates the feasibility of robust FL in noisy, heterogeneous environments, though real-world deployment and hyperparameter adaptation remain open challenges.

## Method Summary
RHFL+ is a heterogeneous federated learning framework designed for robustness against label noise and non-IID data. It operates through three core components: (1) Dynamic Label Refinement (DLR), which adaptively blends original noisy labels with the model's evolving predictions to generate reliable soft targets; (2) Symmetric Cross-Entropy (SL) loss, which combines standard cross-entropy with its reverse to prevent overconfidence on wrong labels; and (3) Enhanced Client Confidence Reweighting (ECCR), which weights client contributions during collaborative knowledge distillation based on label quality and learning efficiency. Implemented via custom NVFlare controller and executor components, RHFL+ alternates between collaborative training on a public dataset and private retraining with DLR and SL, avoiding central model aggregation.

## Key Results
- On CIFAR-10 with synthetic noise (μ=0.1–0.2), RHFL+ achieved average accuracies of 79.86% (pairflip) and 79.20% (symmetric), consistently outperforming nine benchmark methods.
- Scalability tests showed stable per-client performance up to 100 clients.
- On medical datasets (BreastMNIST, BHI, CBIS-DDSM), RHFL+ improved over local-only training in low-noise settings, but performance on CBIS-DDSM was inconsistent, indicating the need for dataset-specific tuning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Dynamic Label Refinement (DLR)
**Claim:** DLR mitigates label noise by adaptively blending the original (potentially noisy) label with the model's evolving prediction, creating a more reliable soft target for local training.  
**Mechanism:** At each local epoch, the pseudolabel for a sample is computed as a weighted average: `ˆy = (1-s_tc)·˜y + s_tc·ϕ(x)`. The weight `s_tc` increases with communication round `t_c`, shifting trust from the initial noisy label toward the model's current prediction as it becomes more reliable.  
**Core assumption:** The model's prediction on a given sample, after some training, is more likely to be correct than the initial noisy label, especially if the model has seen similar clean examples via collaborative learning.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "combining dynamic label refinement... to mitigate noisy labels"; [section 3.1] Details the DLR equation and the increasing schedule `s_tc = t_c / (ζT_c + t_c)`; [corpus] Corpus positions this paper within research (FedNoRo, FedGSCA) explicitly addressing label noise and class imbalance heterogeneity in FL.  
**Break condition:** If the public dataset used for collaborative learning is itself noisy (as noted in [section 3.4], the authors avoided this), the model's predictions used in DLR may become corrupted early, leading to a negative cycle. DLR also assumes some underlying clean signal exists; under extreme noise rates (e.g., µ>0.5 as seen in BreastMNIST [figure 14]), performance degrades sharply.

### Mechanism 2: Symmetric Cross-Entropy (SL) Loss
**Claim:** SL loss improves robustness to noisy labels by jointly optimizing the standard cross-entropy (CE) and its reverse (RCE), preventing the model from overfitting to simple samples while still learning from hard ones.  
**Mechanism:** `L_SL = λ·L_CE + γ·L_RCE`. `L_CE` minimizes divergence from the (noisy) label distribution. `L_RCE` minimizes divergence from the model's *own* predicted distribution back to the label. This penalizes the model when it becomes either over-confident on wrong labels or under-confident on correct ones.  
**Core assumption:** Noisy labels primarily cause the model to produce incorrect but over-confident predictions. The reverse term (`L_RCE`) regularization counters this by encouraging the model's predictive distribution to remain closer to the (flawed) label distribution, providing a stabilising effect.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "symmetric cross-entropy loss"; [section 3.1] Provides mathematical definitions for `L_CE` and `L_RCE` and the combined loss; [corpus] SL loss originally proposed for robust learning with noisy labels in centralized settings; adoption here is a direct application to the federated, heterogeneous context.  
**Break condition:** If the noise is extremely high and structured (e.g., pairflip between semantically similar classes), the forward and reverse losses may reinforce each other's errors. The ablation study ([table 5]) shows SL alone at high noise (µ=0.2) yields lower accuracy than combinations including DLR, suggesting SL's limitations without complementary refinement.

### Mechanism 3: Enhanced Client Confidence Reweighting (ECCR)
**Claim:** ECCR improves aggregation robustness in heterogeneous FL by weighting each client's contribution to collaborative learning based on a combined score of its label quality and learning efficiency.  
**Mechanism:** Client confidence `F_k = Q_k(eD_k) · P(θ_k)`. `Q` is the inverse of average SL loss on local data (lower loss → higher quality). `P` measures learning progress as `(Drop in SL loss) / (Normalized model update magnitude)`. The intuition is that a good client shows consistent improvement (`∆L`) without excessive, potentially unstable updates (`|∆θ|/|θ|`). Weights `W_k` are normalized from `F_k`.  
**Core assumption:** A client's local SL loss on its *current* model is a reasonable proxy for its underlying label quality. Furthermore, clients with large, erratic parameter updates are likely learning patterns specific to their local (noisy) distribution, not the global signal, and should be down-weighted.  
**Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "client confidence reweighting"; [section 3.2] Defines Equations 7–11 for `Q`, `P`, `F`, and `W`. Explicitly states the rationale for the update normalization term; [section 3.8] Notes the empirical finding: "CCR outperforms ECCR in most scenarios," hypothesizing ECCR may "over-penalize clients when update magnitudes are unstable."  
**Break condition:** The normalization term in `P` may be a poor proxy for "learning efficiency" if update magnitudes are not correlated with noise or heterogeneity. As evidenced by the ablation ([table 6]), CCR (which omits this term) often outperforms ECCR, suggesting the added complexity can be detrimental. The core assumption about SL loss correlating with label quality fails if the model has already overfitted to the local noise.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Federated Averaging (FedAvg)**  
*Why needed here:* RHFL+ is built for *heterogeneous* FL, but understanding FedAvg—the standard homogeneous baseline where a single global model is averaged—is essential to grasp what RHFL+ replaces (parameter aggregation) with its distillation-based approach.

**Concept: Knowledge Distillation (KD) and Logits**  
*Why needed here:* RHFL+ relies on sharing model outputs (logits) on a public dataset to transfer knowledge between heterogeneous clients. Understanding soft label distillation is key to following the collaborative phase.  
*Quick check question:* Why is using soft probabilities (logits) from a public dataset more suitable for heterogeneous FL than sharing model weights?

**Concept: Loss Functions for Noisy Labels (e.g., Symmetric Cross-Entropy)**  
*Why needed here:* The core innovation of RHFL+ is its robustness to label noise. Understanding why standard cross-entropy fails with noisy labels and how SL mitigates that is central.  
*Quick check question:* What specific behaviour of a model trained with CE on noisy labels does the `L_RCE` term in SL aim to correct?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Server (NVFlare Controller) orchestrates rounds; receives client public logits and confidence scores; computes weighted collaborative loss (aggregated logits); distributes loss signal back to clients. Client (NVFlare Learner/Executor) performs: (1) computes local `Q` and `P` on private data; (2) runs public data through local model to get logits; (3) receives collaborative loss and performs KD on public data; (4) performs Private Retraining with DLR and SL loss on private data. Custom components required: Controller for logit aggregation and loss distribution; Executor to chain inference, KD, and private retraining within a round.

**Critical path:**  
1. Data Partitioning & Injection: Apply consistent non-IID split and synthetic label noise (pairflip/symmetric) to private client datasets; keep public dataset clean.  
2. Local Confidence & Logits: Each client evaluates current model on private data (`Q`) and public dataset (`ϕ`), uploads both.  
3. Server Aggregation: Server normalizes client confidences (`W_k`). Aggregates public logits `ϕ_i` into a consensus logit (e.g., weighted average). Computes collaborative loss (e.g., KL divergence between each client's `ϕ_i` and consensus).  
4. Collaborative Training (KD): Each client minimizes collaborative loss on public dataset using local model.  
5. Private Retraining with DLR: Client regenerates soft labels `ˆy` using latest predictions from post-KD model. Trains on private dataset for `E` epochs using `L_SL` with `ˆy`.  
6. Repeat.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Memory vs. Accuracy (FCCL): Computing full cross-correlation matrices between all client logits caused OOM. Resolved via batch-wise approximation, but may weaken collaborative signal, likely contributing to FCCL's underperformance vs. original paper.  
- CCR vs. ECCR Stability: ECCR's normalization term (`|Δθ|/(|θ|+1)`) aims to penalize unstable updates but empirically harms performance ([table 6]). Simpler CCR is more robust, suggesting penalty heuristic is dataset-dependent.  
- Public Dataset Choice: For medical imaging, input dimensions/color space must match (e.g., MNIST for grayscale 32×32 BreastMNIST, CIFAR-100 for 224×224 RGB CBIS-DDSM). Mismatch breaks distillation.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Overfitting on small datasets: Medical datasets cause rapid overfitting. Symptoms: training loss ↓ while validation accuracy plateaus/regresses. Mitigation: stronger augmentation (ColorJitter), smaller models, possibly no pretraining as seen in BHI results ([figure 10]).  
- Dataset Mismatch: RHFL+ fails on CBIS-DDSM with default setup ([figures 15–17]). Likely due to highly imbalanced density classes, subtle imaging features, and small sample size. Sign: FL (RHFL+) performs similarly or worse than LocalOnly, and overall accuracy is very low (~50% for 4-class).  
- Central Controller SPOF: The design relies on a central server for aggregation. If it fails, the entire FL process halts. NVFlare's HA mode ([reference 31]) is a necessary production mitigation.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Ablation Study: Disable DLR, SL, ECCR components sequentially on CIFAR-10 (μ=0.1/0.2). Expect: full RHFL+ (HFL+SL+DLR+CCR) yields highest avg accuracy. Verify Table 4 & 5 results.  
2. Benchmark Comparison: Run all baseline methods (FedMD, FedDF, KT-pFL, etc.) on CIFAR-10 with μ=0.1/0.2 under same model heterogeneity. Expect: RHFL+ columns in Table 7 & 8 show top or near-top avg accuracy across client models.  
3. Scaling Test: Increase client count (10, 20, 50, 100) with homogeneous ResNet12 on CIFAR10. Expect: stable per-client accuracy with minor fluctuations ([figure 5]), confirming codebase and algorithm scalability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How does implementing a fault-tolerant architecture (e.g., NVFlare's High Availability) affect RHFL+'s robustness and scalability in real-world deployments?  
2. To what extent does dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning improve RHFL+'s performance on diverse medical imaging datasets compared to using parameters optimized for CIFAR-10?  
3. Can integrating advanced data augmentation techniques (e.g., Mixup) enhance RHFL+'s generalization and robustness on small-scale medical imaging tasks with limited data?  
4. How does RHFL+ perform in actual production deployments with distributed physical clients compared to NVFlare's simulation mode, considering real-world network conditions and device heterogeneity?

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of ECCR is low confidence; ablations show CCR (simpler) often outperforms ECCR, indicating the added learning efficiency heuristic may be detrimental.
- Medical dataset results are medium confidence due to lack of dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning; inconsistent gains (e.g., CBIS-DDSM underperformance) suggest assumptions may not generalize.
- Unknowns in heterogeneous model assignment and public dataset preprocessing for medical tasks block faithful reproduction and may affect reported performance.

## Confidence
- Core claim reliability: RHFL+ superiority on synthetically noisy CIFAR-10: **High**; on medical datasets: **Medium**.
- Component effectiveness: ECCR: **Low**.
- Implementation assumptions: Unknowns (model assignment, public dataset match) reduce reproducibility confidence.
