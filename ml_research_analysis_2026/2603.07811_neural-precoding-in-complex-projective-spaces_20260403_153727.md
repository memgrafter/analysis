# Neural Precoding in Complex Projective Spaces
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.07811
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07811
- **Reference count**: 20
- **Primary result**: Parameterizing channels and precoders in the complex projective space ℂℙ^(N-1) removes global phase redundancy, leading to significant sum-rate gains and superior generalization over conventional representations with negligible complexity increase.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a fundamental redundancy in deep learning (DL)-based multi-user multiple-input single-output (MU-MISO) precoding. Conventional neural network representations use real/imaginary or amplitude/phase components for complex channel and precoder vectors, but the relevant performance metrics depend only on the magnitudes of inner products, which are invariant to global phase rotations. This invariance creates a mismatch between the representation's degrees of freedom and the true problem dimensionality. The authors propose a DL framework where both channels and precoders are parameterized as points in the complex projective space CP^(N-1), eliminating the redundant phase dimension. Two specific parameterizations are introduced: a real-valued embedding (P_cps) and a complex hyperspherical coordinate system (P_hsc). Benchmarking against standard baselines shows that the proposed complex projective space (CPS) methods substantially improve sum-rate and generalization across signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) ranges. For a 4-antenna, 4-user system at 10 dB SNR, P_cps outperforms the best conventional baseline by 0.4 bit/s/Hz and achieves 94% of the optimal WMMSE rate at 0 dB SNR, compared to 82.5% for the conventional approach.

## Method Summary
The core innovation is to represent both channel vectors **h**ₖ and precoding vectors **w**ₖ as elements of the complex projective space ℂℙ^(N-1), which is the space of unit-norm complex vectors modulo a global phase factor (i.e., **v** ∼ e^(iψ)**v**). This space naturally captures the physical invariance of the inner-product magnitudes that determine the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR). The learning task is decomposed into predicting a unit-norm *direction* vector for each user (2N-2 real degrees of freedom) and a separate *power allocation* scalar (summing to the total power budget). Two CPS parameterizations are explored: **P_cps** uses a simple real/imaginary embedding with the first component's phase fixed to zero, yielding 2N-1 real numbers per user. **P_hsc** uses a chain of amplitude and phase angles (hyperspherical coordinates), yielding exactly 2N-2 numbers but with highly coupled trigonometric structure. For baselines, **P_ncv** normalizes channels and outputs unit vectors on the complex sphere (S^(2N-1)), retaining phase redundancy, and **P_ri** uses raw real/imaginary coefficients without decomposition. All methods use an identical 5-layer MLP backbone (128-256-256-128 neurons with PReLU and BatchNorm). The loss function combines a direction-matching term (cosine similarity or angle-based) with a power allocation term (KL divergence), weighted to approximate sum-rate improvement.

## Key Results
- At 10 dB SNR for N=4, K=4, **P_cps** outperforms **P_ncv** by 0.4 bit/s/Hz and **P_ri** by 0.8 bit/s/Hz.
- **P_cps** achieves 94% of the WMMSE sum-rate at 0 dB SNR, while **P_ri** achieves only 82.5%.
- The CPS parameterizations consistently outperform benchmarks across the entire tested SNR range (0–20 dB), with gains being more pronounced at higher SNRs where precise phase alignment becomes critical.
- Despite having identical parameter counts to **P_ri**, **P_cps** shows superior accuracy, demonstrating that the performance improvement stems from the representational geometry, not model size.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Exploiting Global Phase Invariance
The sum-rate depends on |**h**ₖᴴ**w**ₖ|², which is invariant to a simultaneous global phase rotation of **h**ₖ and **w**ₖ. Conventional representations use 2N real numbers for each complex N-dimensional vector, but the physically meaningful equivalence class (vectors differing only by a global phase) lives in the 2N-2 dimensional manifold ℂℙ^(N-1). By projecting both channels and precoders to ℂℙ^(N-1), the DNN learns a mapping on the correct, lower-dimensional manifold. This removes the need for the network to implicitly discover the phase invariance from data, simplifying the optimization landscape and improving generalization. The empirical gains confirm that for ideal Rayleigh fading and weighted sum-rate maximization, the global phase carries no useful information and acts as noise.

*Evidence*: The abstract explicitly states the redundancy; Section V.A argues that operating on "geometrically consistent representations" eliminates phase-induced ambiguity; the strong performance of **P_cps**/**P_hsc** over **P_ncv** (which still has phase redundancy) validates the mechanism.

*Break condition*: If the environment introduced absolute phase information (e.g., hardware phase noise or a phase-sensitive metric), removing this redundancy would discard signal and harm performance.

### Mechanism 2: P_cps Outperforms P_hsc Due to Optimization Geometry
Both **P_cps** and **P_hsc** eliminate phase redundancy, but **P_cps** uses a direct, nearly-linear real-valued embedding while **P_hsc** uses a recursive trigonometric parameterization (hyperspherical coordinates). The nested product structure of **P_hsc** (e.g., u₂ = e^(iϕ₁) sinθ₁ cosθ₂) creates strong coupling between parameters: a change in an early angle (θ₁) affects the norm of all subsequent components. In a standard deep feedforward network with unbounded activations (PReLU), this coupling leads to ill-conditioned gradients and a harder optimization problem. **P_cps**'s simpler embedding allows for a smoother loss landscape, leading to faster convergence and higher final accuracy.

*Evidence*: The paper notes **P_hsc** underperforms **P_cps** and attributes it to "nested trigonometric couplings" causing ill-conditioned gradients (Section VI.B). The authors also smooth **P_hsc**'s phase encoding (sin/cos) and center amplitude angles, indicating known optimization challenges.

*Break condition*: A network architecture explicitly designed for manifold operations (e.g., using normalized layers, exponential maps, or retractions) could stabilize **P_hsc** training and potentially close the gap.

### Mechanism 3: Explicit Direction/Power Decomposition
The optimal WMMSE precoder has an implicit structure: **w**ₖ = √(pₖ) **v̂**ₖ, with **v̂**ₖ unit-norm and pₖ ≥ 0. Baselines like **P_ri** regress the full scaled complex vector directly, imposing no structure and potentially penalizing valid phase-rotated solutions. Both CPS methods and **P_ncv** explicitly separate the prediction of the unit-norm direction and the power fractions (via a softmax). This architectural decoupling aligns the network's output structure with the optimal solution's structure, making the learning task easier and the loss function better conditioned. The direction loss uses a phase-invariant cosine similarity, and the power loss uses a KL divergence on split ratios.

*Evidence*: Section VI.C shows that decomposition alone (**P_ncv**) improves over **P_ri**, especially at medium/high SNR. The larger gains from CPS parameterizations show that combining decomposition with phase invariance is synergistic.

*Break condition*: If the optimal precoder for a given objective did not factor cleanly into a normalized direction and independent power (e.g., for certain non-linear precoding schemes), this decomposition would lose expressiveness.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Global Phase Invariance in Inner Products**
  - *Why needed*: The performance metric (|**h**ₖᴴ**w**ₖ|²) is unchanged by (**h**ₖ, **w**ₖ) → (e^(iψ)**h**ₖ, e^(-iψ)**w**ₖ). A learner must either discover this symmetry from data (wasteful) or have it built-in. Understanding this principle shows why the redundant phase dimension should be removed.
  - *Quick check*: Given two channel vectors **h**₁, **h**₂ and two precoders **w**₁, **w**₂. If ( **h**₁, **w**₁ ) produce the same SINR as ( e^(iπ/3)**h**₁, e^(-iπ/3)**w**₁ ), how many real degrees of freedom are truly needed to represent the *pair* ( **h**₁, **w**₁ ) for sum-rate prediction?

- **Concept: Complex Projective Space ℂℙⁿ as a Manifold**
  - *Why needed*: ℂℙ^(N-1) is the set of all lines through the origin in ℂᴺ, or equivalently, unit vectors modulo global phase. It is a smooth 2N-2 dimensional manifold. The paper's key move is to treat channels and precoders as points on this manifold.
  - *Quick check*: A vector in ℂᴺ has 2N real degrees of freedom. After fixing unit norm, how many DoF remain? After additionally identifying **v** ∼ e^(iψ)**v**? What space does this describe?

- **Concept: Hyperspherical Coordinates and Their Coupling**
  - *Why needed*: To explicitly parameterize ℂℙ^(N-1). The recursive product structure (e.g., u₂ = e^(iϕ₁) sinθ₁ cosθ₂) means changing an early angle affects the magnitude of all later components. This coupling can hinder gradient-based learning in deep networks.
  - *Quick check*: In a hyperspherical parameterization, if you increase θ₁ slightly while holding all other angles constant, which components of **u** change magnitude? Does the phase of u₂ depend on θ₁?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: 
  Raw Channel Matrix **H** (ℂ^(N×K)) -> Per-UE Channel Preprocessor -> Input Feature Vector (size varies by parameterization) -> 5-Layer MLP (128-256-256-128, PReLU, BatchNorm) -> Raw DNN Outputs (size varies) -> Parameterization-Specific Post-Processor -> Unit-Norm Precoder Directions **û**ₖ & Power Fractions ζₖ -> Feasible Precoder **w**ₖ = √(P ζₖ) **û**ₖ.

- **Critical path**: The system converts raw complex channels into a phase-invariant representation, feeds it to a standard feedforward network, and then uses a parameterization-specific decoder to reconstruct geometrically valid (unit-norm) precoder directions and a simplex-constrained power allocation vector. The global logic is "predict the interference geometry and power split from phase-normalized channel features."

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **P_cps vs. P_hsc**: **P_cps** uses a dense, simple embedding (2N-1 reals/user) that trains well in standard MLPs. **P_hsc** uses a minimal, manifold-native parameterization (2N-2 reals/user) but suffers from coupled trigonometric functions that create ill-conditioned gradients.
  - **Parameterization vs. Benchmark**: **P_ncv** removes channel norm but not global phase (lies on S^(2N-1)). **P_ri** has no decomposition or invariance. The CPS methods combine both advantages.
  - **Loss design**: Using phase-invariant losses (cosine similarity for directions, KL for power) is crucial. **P_ri** uses simple MSE on raw coefficients, which is incompatible with the invariance.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - **P_hsc** stagnates at low accuracy during training. Root: Ill-conditioned gradients from nested trigonometric couplings in a standard MLP.
  - **P_ncv** matches **P_cps** at low SNR but degrades significantly at high SNR. Root: At high SNR, precise phase alignment across users is critical; **P_ncv**'s redundant phase dimension leads to inconsistent learning.
  - All models underfit at very high SNR (accuracy <100%). Root: Fixed-feature network may lack capacity for precise high-SNR emulation; or SNR input feature is insufficient.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **What geometry‑aware neural architectures can further improve precoding by exploiting the CP^n structure?** The authors identify designing DL models that exploit the geometry of ℂℙⁿ (e.g., via equivariant layers or manifold operations) as a key future direction. Current work uses standard fully-connected layers.
- **How robust are CPS‑based precoding methods to channel estimation errors and hardware impairments?** The paper focuses on an ideal scenario. Real-world effects like phase noise, antenna coupling, and CSI error could invalidate the global phase invariance assumption and need investigation.
- **Why does the complex hyperspherical coordinate parameterization (P_hsc) yield inconsistent gains across system dimensions, and how can its optimization be stabilized?** **P_hsc** underperforms **P_cps** despite using the exact degrees of freedom. The authors attribute this to "nested trigonometric couplings" causing ill-conditioned gradients and note the gain varies with model complexity and system size. Understanding and fixing this would make manifold-native parameterizations reliable.

## Limitations
- Results are only for N=4, K=4 with ideal Rayleigh fading and WSR maximization. Generalization to other antenna/user counts, correlated channels, or different objectives (e.g., non-linear precoding) is unproven.
- The WMMSE implementation details (tolerance, iterations) are not fully specified, risking label variability in reproduction.
- The claim that **P_hsc** fails due to gradient ill-conditioning is theoretical; no experiment isolates this factor (e.g., by using a manifold-aware network).
- The global phase invariance assumption may not hold under hardware impairments like phase noise, leaving real-world applicability uncertain.

## Confidence
- **Phase redundancy removal** (High): Strong theoretical foundation and consistent empirical gains over **P_ncv**.
- **P_cps > P_hsc** (Medium): The performance gap is observed but not definitively proven to be caused by optimization geometry; may depend on network architecture.
- **Direction/power split** (Medium): Decomposition helps, but the major additive gain comes from phase invariance, not decomposition alone.
- **Cross-SNR generalization** (Medium): Gains are shown across [0,20] dB, but extrapolation to much higher SNR or other channel models is untested.

## Next Checks
1. **Statistical Validation**: Repeat training with 5 different random seeds and data splits; compute mean and standard deviation of accuracy for each method to confirm the significance of the **P_cps** advantage.
2. **Manifold-Aware Network for P_hsc**: Implement a version of the DNN that uses operations respecting the ℂℙ^(N-1) geometry (e.g., normalized linear layers, exponential maps). If **P_hsc** then matches or exceeds **P_cps**, it supports the gradient conditioning hypothesis.
3. **Robustness to Channel Non-Idealities**: Introduce realistic imperfections (CSI error, phase noise, Rician fading) into the channel model. Measure the relative performance degradation of CPS vs. conventional (**P_ri**, **P_ncv**) methods across SNR to assess practical robustness.
