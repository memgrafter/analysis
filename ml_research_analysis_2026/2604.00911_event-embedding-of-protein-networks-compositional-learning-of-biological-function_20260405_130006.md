# Event Embedding of Protein Networks : Compositional Learning of Biological Function

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2604.00911
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00911
- **Reference Count:** 3
- **Primary Result:** Enforcing additive compositionality in protein network embeddings (Event2Vec) substantially improves relational reasoning tasks like pathway coherence and functional analogy compared to a non-compositional skip-gram baseline (DeepWalk).

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates whether imposing a compositional, additive structure on protein network embeddings enhances the learning of biological function. Using the human STRING interactome, the authors compare Event2Vec—which imposes a linear recurrence constraint on latent states—against a standard DeepWalk skip-gram model trained on identical random walks. The core finding is that the enforced additivity in Event2Vec leads to dramatic improvements on tasks requiring relational reasoning: pathway membership coherence, functional analogy solving, and hierarchical pathway organization. In contrast, simpler geometric properties like the anticorrelation between embedding norm and protein degree are shared with or stronger in the skip-gram baseline, suggesting they emerge from co-occurrence statistics rather than the compositional constraint. The work demonstrates that additive constraints enable meaningful "protein arithmetic" and transfer learning across pathways, while skip-gram effects govern more basic geometric signatures.

## Method Summary
The study uses the human STRING v12.0 interactome (16,201 proteins, 89,234 high-confidence edges) to generate random walks (10 walks/protein, length 15). Two embedding models are trained on these identical sequences: **DeepWalk**, a standard skip-gram model, and **Event2Vec**, which maintains a recurrent state history (`h_t = h_{t-1} + e_{s_t}`) trained with a next-event prediction loss plus a reconstruction penalty (`λ * ||h_t - e_{s_t} - h_{t-1}||²`, λ=0.5). Both models use 64-dimensional embeddings, a context window of 5, and are trained for 100 epochs. Performance is compared on pathway coherence (mean pairwise cosine within pathways), functional analogy (e.g., `EGFR - AKT1 + INSR`), hierarchical clustering of pathway centroids, and geometric metrics like norm–degree correlation.

## Key Results
- **Pathway coherence:** Event2Vec achieves a 30.2× improvement over random baseline (mean similarity 0.870) versus DeepWalk's 2.9× improvement (0.650).
- **Functional analogy:** Event2Vec's mean top-1 cosine similarity on 15 transfer queries is 0.966 vs. DeepWalk's 0.650, with biologically plausible retrievals (e.g., `EGFR:AKT1::INSR:? → KRAS`).
- **Hierarchical organization:** Pathway centroid clustering shows sharp, well-separated super-clusters for Event2Vec versus a diffuse structure for DeepWalk.
- **Geometric properties:** The norm–degree anticorrelation is stronger in DeepWalk (r = −0.801) than Event2Vec (r = −0.627), indicating it arises from skip-gram frequency effects rather than enforced compositionality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Asymmetric Möbius Addition for Directionality
- **Claim:** Asymmetric Möbius addition in hyperbolic space preserves sequence directionality for ordered event streams.
- **Mechanism:** In the Poincaré ball, Möbius addition (⊕) is gyrocommutative but non-associative, meaning the order of operations matters (`a ⊕ b ⊕ c ≠ a ⊕ (b ⊕ c)`). This property ensures that a transformation from relation (A→B) to (C→?) is sensitive to the sequential order of events in a walk.
- **Core assumption:** Biological signaling pathways have inherent directionality (e.g., receptor activation → kinase cascade) that should be reflected in embedding algebra.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] notes hyperbolic Möbius addition is “not strictly commutative.”
  - [section 2.2] states: “Unlike Euclidean vector addition, Möbius addition is not strictly commutative… By contrast, standard addition is commutative; nevertheless, the model is still trained on ordered sequences.”
  - No direct external corpus support; corpus keywords focus on geometric self-supervision and protein graphs.

### Mechanism 2: Hyperbolic Norm Shrinkage for Hub Centrality
- **Claim:** Hyperbolic norm shrinkage under repeated addition naturally models hub protein embeddings having smaller norms.
- **Mechanism:** In hyperbolic geometry, vector addition near the boundary of the ball shrinks norms. Frequent walk visitation (high-degree hubs) corresponds to many additive updates (`h_t = h_{t-1} ⊕ e_{s_t}`), pushing hub embeddings toward the ball’s center with smaller norms.
- **Core assumption:** Network hubs appear often in random walks, and their functional “centrality” should correspond to geometrically central (small-norm) positions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.3] notes both models show norm–degree anticorrelation, but DeepWalk’s is stronger (r = −0.801 vs −0.627). The authors interpret this as a “skip-gram frequency effect,” but note Event2Vec’s additive constraint produces “smaller, more tightly distributed norms.”
  - [section 4] reiterates: “norm–degree structure is primarily a skip-gram frequency effect,” while Event2Vec’s constraint provides “modest additional centrality structure.”
  - No external corpus support.

### Mechanism 3: Enforced Additivity for Functional Analogy Transfer
- **Claim:** The reconstruction penalty (`λ||h_t − e_{s_t} − h_{t-1}||²`) creates linear latent transformations enabling functional analogy transfer.
- **Mechanism:** The loss forces histories to compose as sums (`h_t ≈ ∑_{i=1}^t e_{s_i}`). This yields approximate linear relations: if pathway A involves (protein X activates Y) and pathway B involves (protein M activates N), then vectors may satisfy `e_Y − e_X ≈ e_N − e_M`, enabling queries like `EGFR:AKT1 :: INSR:?` to retrieve `KRAS`.
- **Core assumption:** Protein functional relationships (kinase → substrate, receptor → effector) are approximately additive in the embedding space and transfer across pathway contexts.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.2] defines the reconstruction loss that “encourages the enforced additivity `h_t − e_{s_t} ≈ h_{t−1}`.”
  - [section 3.2] shows Event2Vec achieves mean analogy similarity 0.966 vs DeepWalk’s 0.650, with specific correct retrievals.
  - [section 3.5] demonstrates embedding drift along pathways is smoother (PC1 variance 96–98% vs 89–93%), directly supporting additive composition.
  - No external corpus support.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Skip-gram negative sampling**
  - **Why needed:** Both models use a skip-gram–style next-event prediction objective. Understanding negative sampling is key to interpreting what co-occurrence statistics both models share (e.g., norm–degree anticorrelation).
  - **Quick check:** How does increasing the number of negative samples per positive example affect embedding norm distributions for high-degree vs low-degree proteins?
- **Concept: Centrality vs. peripheral embedding geometry**
  - **Why needed:** The paper distinguishes hub centrality effects (shared with skip-gram) from compositional relational structure. Norm–degree anticorrelation reflects centrality; pathway coherence reflects relational organization.
  - **Quick check:** If you compute the embedding centroid for all proteins, would hubs be closer to the centroid in hyperbolic vs Euclidean space under the same training objective?
- **Concept: Transfer learning via vector arithmetic**
  - **Why needed:** The core claim is that compositional embeddings support analogy-based function transfer. This requires understanding that linear operations in embedding space can encode biological transformations.
  - **Quick check:** Given a trained embedding, how would you systematically test whether `e_Y − e_X` consistently represents “activation of X leads to Y” across multiple (X, Y) pairs in signaling pathways?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Random Walk Generator -> Event2Vec State Update (h_t = h_{t-1} + e_{s_t} or ⊕) / DeepWalk Embedding Lookup -> Skip-gram Loss (negative sampling, window=5) + [Event2Vec: Reconstruction Penalty λ||h_t - e_{s_t} - h_{t-1}||²] -> Trained Embeddings -> Evaluation (Pathway Coherence, Analogy, Clustering, Geometric Metrics)
- **Critical path:** Walk generation → (Event2Vec: recurrent state update + combined loss) OR (DeepWalk: independent skip-gram training) → embedding extraction → downstream evaluation (coherence, arithmetic, clustering, drift).
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Euclidean vs. hyperbolic addition: Euclidean is simpler/faster; hyperbolic may better model hierarchy but introduces non-associativity and slower training.
  - Reconstruction weight λ: Controls compositionality strength; λ=0 reduces to DeepWalk, λ too high may overfit to exact additivity.
  - Walk length and number: Longer/denser walks may increase co-occurrence noise; shorter walks may miss longer-range dependencies.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low pathway coherence (<0.5 mean) despite high training accuracy → model not capturing functional grouping.
  - Analogy queries return nearest neighbors to A or C instead of relational transformation → additive constraint ineffective.
  - Norm–degree correlation weaker in Event2Vec than DeepWalk → reconstruction penalty may be suppressing frequency effects too strongly.
  - PCA on cumulative sums shows <80% PC1 variance → embedding drift not following smooth linear trajectories.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline replication:** Train DeepWalk on provided walks (gensim Word2Vec, 64 dim, window 5, 100 epochs). Verify pathway coherence ∼0.65 and norm–degree r ≈ −0.8 to establish shared skip-gram effects.
  2. **Compositionality check:** Train Event2Vec (Euclidean, λ=0.5). Compute analogy `EGFR:AKT1::INSR:?` and check top-1 similarity >0.95 and biologically plausible target (e.g., RAS family).
  3. **Geometric isolation:** Compare random baselines for pathway coherence. If Event2Vec’s random baseline is not substantially lower than DeepWalk’s (e.g., <0.1 vs >0.2), the sharpness in results may be due to hyperparameter choices, not compositionality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Does the three-theme hierarchical organization of pathway centroids (housekeeping, nuclear/genome, signaling) align with established biological ontologies like KEGG or Reactome?
2. How does Event2Vec's relationship transfer performance generalize when evaluated with standard information retrieval metrics (e.g., Hits@k, MRR) across the full proteome, rather than mean similarity on a curated set of 15 analogies?
3. Does the hyperbolic (Möbius addition) variant of Event2Vec offer any advantage for protein network embedding over the Euclidean variant, particularly for modeling hierarchical or tree-like biological structures?
4. Do the high-similarity geodesic paths generated by linear interpolation between protein embeddings correspond to known, biologically plausible signaling cascades or mechanistic intermediates?

## Limitations
- **Unspecified walk generation details:** The exact random walk algorithm (restart policy, node repetition) is not described, risking corpus mismatch and affecting reproducibility.
- **Reconstruction penalty ambiguity:** Implementation specifics (norm clipping value, whether applied to all steps) are unclear, potentially impacting the strength of the compositional constraint.
- **Lack of hyperbolic validation:** The hyperbolic variant is proposed but not evaluated; its theoretical advantages for directionality and hierarchy remain unsubstantiated by empirical results.

## Next Checks
1. **Replicate walk generation exactly:** Implement the walk sampler as per DeepWalk defaults or infer from the paper. Verify both models are trained on identical sequences; any deviation invalidates the ablation.
2. **Ablate reconstruction penalty (λ):** Train Event2Vec with λ ∈ {0.0, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0}. Expect pathway coherence and analogy accuracy to degrade monotonically as λ → 0, confirming the constraint’s causal role.
3. **Test analogy generalizability:** Beyond the 15 provided queries, sample 100 random (A,B,C) triples from curated kinase-substrate pairs. Compute success rate (known D in top-5) for both models; Event2Vec’s advantage should persist broadly.
