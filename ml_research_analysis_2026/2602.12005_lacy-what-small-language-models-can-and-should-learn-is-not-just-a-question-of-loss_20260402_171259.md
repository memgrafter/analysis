# LaCy: What Small Language Models Can and Should Learn is Not Just a Question of Loss
## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.12005  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12005  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** LaCy-trained SLMs achieve a FactScore of 22.71% in cascade generation, outperforming Rho-1 (20.97%), LLM-judge (21.63%), and baseline (15.89%) while reducing fact leakage to 11.28% with minimal CPU preprocessing overhead.

## Executive Summary
Small Language Models (SLMs) have limited capacity to store factual knowledge, which leads to hallucinations. The key question is which tokens an SLM should learn versus delegate to a larger model via a special `<CALL>` token. Loss-based token selection alone is insufficient because high loss occurs both for factual errors and for acceptable non-factual continuations (e.g., stylistic variations). LaCy addresses this by combining the training loss signal with a spaCy grammar parser to identify factual tokens; it then delegates only factual tokens with high loss, allowing the SLM to safely learn other high-loss tokens. In cascade generation with a larger model, LaCy achieves a FactScore of 22.71%, substantially higher than competing methods, and reduces fact leakage on QA benchmarks to 11.28%, all with negligible preprocessing overhead on CPU.

## Method Summary
LaCy’s training procedure begins by computing a token‑level loss signal (e.g., from a reference model or the model itself) on the training corpus. In parallel, spaCy’s dependency parser annotates each token as belonging to a factual expression (such as a predicate‑argument structure or named entity) or not. A selector then combines these two signals: if a token is part of a factual span *and* its loss exceeds a threshold, it is marked for delegation; otherwise the SLM is trained to predict it. Delegated tokens are replaced in the training data with a special `<CALL>` token, which teaches the SLM to defer generation of that segment to a larger model. During inference, the SLM generates normally until it emits a `<CALL>`, at which point a larger model is invoked to generate the corresponding text, which is then concatenated seamlessly. This selective delegation lets the SLM focus its limited capacity on non‑factual content and acceptable variations, avoiding both memorization of unreliable facts and unnecessary delegation of high‑loss non‑factual tokens.

## Key Results
- **FactScore** reaches 22.71% in cascade generation (baseline 15.89%, Rho‑1 20.97%, LLM‑judge 21.63%).
- **Fact leakage** on QA benchmarks is reduced to 11.28%.
- **Preprocessing overhead** is minimal; the grammar parsing step runs efficiently on CPU.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Loss‑only selection fails because high loss can arise from two distinct sources: (1) factual inaccuracies that the SLM cannot reliably memorize, and (2) acceptable non‑factual variations (e.g., paraphrases, stylistic choices) that the SLM could safely learn. LaCy’s hybrid approach uses spaCy’s parser to isolate the first category: only tokens within syntactic structures that express factual propositions are considered for delegation. Then, among those factual tokens, a high loss indicates the SLM’s uncertainty about the fact, triggering delegation. This prevents both under‑delegation (factual errors that would cause hallucinations) and over‑delegation (wasting capacity on non‑factual high‑loss tokens that the SLM could learn). The SLM thus trains on a curated subset of tokens that match its representational capacity, while factual uncertainties are offloaded to the larger model at inference time.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map (plain text):**  
Training Corpus → spaCy Parser → Factual Span Annotations; Training Corpus → Reference Model → Token‑Level Loss Scores; Factual Annotations + Loss Scores → Selector → Delegation Mask; Delegation Mask + Modified Training Corpus → SLM Trainer → Trained SLM.  
Inference: User Query → SLM Generation → (on `<CALL>`) → Large Model → Combined Output.

**Critical path:**  
1. Compute loss per token on training data (using reference model).  
2. Run spaCy parser to tag factual spans.  
3. Combine signals: factual+high‑loss → delegate; otherwise train.  
4. Train SLM on modified data with `<CALL>` tokens.  
5. At inference, call large model whenever `<CALL>` is generated.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Parser accuracy vs. computational cost – using a fast off‑the‑shelf parser (spaCy) may sacrifice domain‑specific performance.  
- Delegation granularity – token‑level is precise but may increase `<CALL>` frequency; span‑level could reduce calls but risks including non‑factual content.  
- Loss computation overhead – requires a reference model or additional forward pass; adds training time but improves selection.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Over‑delegation: Parser marks non‑factual spans as factual, causing unnecessary `<CALL>` tokens and reduced SLM learning.  
- Under‑delegation: Parser misses factual spans, leaving high‑loss factual tokens in the training set → hallucinations persist.  
- Threshold mis‑calibration: Too low → excessive delegation; too high → insufficient delegation.  
- Parser bottlenecks: CPU preprocessing becomes slow for very large corpora.

**First experiments (3):**  
1. **Baseline comparison:** Evaluate FactScore, fact leakage, and efficiency of LaCy‑trained SLMs against Rho‑1, LLM‑judge, and a non‑selective baseline.  
2. **Ablation of parser:** Remove the grammar‑parsing filter (use loss‑only) to quantify its contribution to FactScore improvement.  
3. **Scalability test:** Measure CPU time, memory usage, and latency of the spaCy parsing step on corpora of 1M, 10M, and 100M tokens.

## Limitations
- **Grammar parser generalization:** LaCy’s effectiveness depends on spaCy’s accuracy; performance may degrade on complex, idiomatic, or domain‑specific factual expressions (e.g., nested clauses, tabular data).  
- **Benchmark scope:** Evaluation uses FactScore and QA benchmarks, which may not capture all hallucination types (e.g., subtle logical inconsistencies) or real‑world deployment scenarios with diverse query distributions.  
- **Minimal overhead verification:** The claim of minimal CPU preprocessing overhead lacks empirical scaling benchmarks across varied corpus sizes and linguistic complexities.

## Confidence
- **High:** Core finding that loss‑only selection is insufficient (conflates factual errors with acceptable non‑factual continuations).  
- **Medium:** Assertion that grammar parsing effectively separates “factual” from “non‑factual” tokens as a proxy for delegation.  
- **Low:** Broad generalizability of the “minimal preprocessing overhead” claim without empirical scaling evidence.

## Next Checks
1. **Parser Accuracy Audit:** Manually annotate a diverse sample of model generations for factual vs. non‑factual spans and compute spaCy’s precision/recall.  
2. **Out‑of‑Domain Evaluation:** Test LaCy on a significantly different domain (e.g., biomedical, legal) using domain‑specific QA/factuality benchmarks to measure performance drop‑off.  
3. **Preprocessing Scalability Test:** Measure CPU time, memory usage, and preprocessing latency as a function of corpus size (1M, 10M, 100M tokens).
