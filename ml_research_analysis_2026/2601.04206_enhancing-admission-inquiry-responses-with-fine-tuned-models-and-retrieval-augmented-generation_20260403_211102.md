# Enhancing Admission Inquiry Responses with Fine-Tuned Models and Retrieval-Augmented Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04206
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04206
- **Reference count:** 11
- **Primary Result:** Hybrid fine-tuned RAG model achieves 92.7% fact recall, 88.3% precise data recall, and 8.9/10 user satisfaction, outperforming RAG-only, fine-tuned-only, and baseline models.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of slow and inaccurate responses to high-volume university admissions inquiries by proposing a hybrid system that combines Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) with domain-specific fine-tuning of a large language model. The system uses a BERT-based retriever to fetch relevant document chunks from a segmented knowledge base, and a Gemma 9B model fine-tuned via LoRA on 8,000 curated question-answer pairs to generate context-aware responses. Evaluation on 210 historical inquiries demonstrates that the hybrid model outperforms RAG-only, fine-tuned-only, and baseline approaches, achieving 92.7% fact recall, 88.3% precise data recall, and 8.9/10 user satisfaction. The results underscore the necessity of integrating factual grounding via retrieval with domain-adapted generation for high-quality automated responses.

The study also provides a detailed analysis of the mechanisms behind the hybrid approach: RAG ensures up-to-date factual accuracy for volatile information such as deadlines, while fine-tuning equips the model with the specialized terminology, tone, and procedural reasoning required for admissions. The synergy between these components allows the generator to better interpret and synthesize retrieved information. The paper further explores architectural considerations, including chunking strategies, retrieval parameters, and fine-tuning trade-offs, and outlines avenues for future work such as multi-domain adaptation and dynamic knowledge updates.

## Method Summary
The proposed system employs a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline combined with supervised fine-tuning of a large language model. The knowledge base consists of raw admissions documents (regulations, FAQs) split into 512-token chunks with 64-token overlap. A BERT-based retriever encodes queries and chunks, performing cosine similarity search to retrieve the top-3 most relevant chunks. The generator is a Gemma 9B model fine-tuned via LoRA for three epochs on a dataset of 8,000 Alpaca-formatted question-answer pairs, which were automatically distilled from the source documents using DeepSeek-R1. At inference, the query and retrieved chunks are concatenated and fed to the fine-tuned model, which generates a response. The system is evaluated on 210 historical admissions inquiries using metrics of fact recall, precise data recall, and user satisfaction.

## Key Results
- Fact recall: 92.7%, Precise data recall: 88.3%, User satisfaction: 8.9/10.
- The hybrid fine-tuned RAG system outperforms RAG-only, fine-tuned-only, and baseline models across all metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hybrid system's success stems from three interrelated mechanisms:

### Mechanism 1: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for Factual Grounding
- **Claim:** RAG grounds responses in factual, up-to-date institutional knowledge, mitigating hallucination and ensuring precision for volatile data like deadlines.
- **Mechanism:** An embedding-based retriever (BERT) fetches the top 3 most relevant document chunks from a segmented knowledge base (512-token chunks, 64-token overlap). These chunks provide the generator with a factual context window, constraining its output to retrieved information.
- **Core assumption:** The institutional knowledge base is curated, current, and segmented in a way that preserves semantic meaning for retrieval.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "integrating a fine-tuned language model with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)... RAG effectively retrieves relevant information from large datasets... enabling the system to generate highly tailored responses."
  - Section 2.1: "RAG’s ability to fetch domain-specific documents, such as admissions rules... enables the system to generate answers that are not generic, but instead directly relevant... This is a significant advantage in environments where precision and correctness are paramount."
  - Corpus: Weak direct evidence for this specific mechanism; neighboring papers (e.g., URAG, MARAUS) apply RAG to admissions, suggesting domain relevance but not the specific chunking/retrieval parameters.
- **Break condition:** Retrieval fails if the knowledge base is outdated, if query phrasing yields low similarity to relevant chunks, or if critical information is fragmented across chunks not retrieved together.

### Mechanism 2: Domain-Specific Fine-Tuning for Contextual Understanding
- **Claim:** Domain-specific fine-tuning adapts the base LLM to interpret retrieved context correctly and generate responses that adhere to admissions-specific terminology, tone, and procedural nuance.
- **Mechanism:** The base model (Gemma 9B) is fine-tuned via LoRA for 3 epochs on a distilled dataset of ~8,000 Alpaca-formatted Q&A pairs. This process instills domain "analytical skills" and communication style, enabling the model to synthesize retrieved fragments into coherent, appropriate answers.
- **Core assumption:** The distilled fine-tuning dataset is a high-quality, representative sample of the domain's question space and desired response patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "fine-tuning on 8,000 curated question-answer pairs enhances the model’s ability to interpret retrieved context and generate admissions-appropriate responses."
  - Section 2.2: "fine-tuning involves... further training it on a smaller, domain-specific dataset... Through fine-tuning, the model can be retrained to focus on this specialized vocabulary, improving its ability to generate precise and contextually appropriate answers."
  - Section 3.1: Describes the distillation process using DeepSeek R1 to create the Q&A pairs from raw source documents, linking the fine-tuning data creation to the source knowledge.
- **Break condition:** Fine-tuning fails if the training data lacks coverage of edge cases, contains inaccuracies, or introduces biases; the model may then generate plausible but incorrect or stylistically inappropriate responses even with good retrieval.

### Mechanism 3: Synergy Between RAG and Fine-Tuning
- **Claim:** The hybrid architecture synergistically combines RAG's factual grounding with fine-tuning's contextual understanding, leading to superior performance over either approach alone.
- **Mechanism:** Fine-tuning prepares the generator to recognize the significance of retrieved information, prioritize facts, and structure responses according to domain conventions. RAG supplies the exact, current data points. The fine-tuned model is thus better at "interpreting and synthesizing" the retrieved chunks than a generic model.
- **Core assumption:** The tasks of factual retrieval and contextual response generation are separable but complementary; optimal performance requires competence in both.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - Abstract: "integrating RAG for factual grounding with fine-tuning for domain understanding is essential for high-quality, context-aware automated responses."
  - Section 2.3: "fine-tuning can help address one of the key limitations of RAG: the need to interpret and synthesize large volumes of information... fine-tuning enables the model to better understand how to prioritize and structure this information."
  - Section 4.3, Table 1: Quantitative results show the hybrid model (Fine-Tuned with RAG) achieves the highest fact recall (92.7%) and user satisfaction (8.9), while the RAG-only model has higher precise data recall (91.4% vs 88.3%) but lower satisfaction, and the fine-tuned-only model has abysmal precise data recall (48.3%).
- **Break condition:** Synergy breaks if the fine-tuned model becomes overly reliant on patterns from its training data and dismisses or misinterprets retrieved facts that contradict its learned priors, or if retrieval consistently returns irrelevant context that the fine-tuned model cannot reject.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**
  - *Why needed here:* It is the primary mechanism for factual accuracy and handling volatile information (deadlines, URLs). Without it, the model must rely on parameters frozen at training time or its fine-tuned memory, which fails for dynamic data.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the core difference between a model's parametric knowledge (from pre-training/fine-tuning) and the knowledge injected at inference time via RAG?
- **Concept: Domain-Specific Fine-Tuning (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT)**
  - *Why needed here:* It provides the model with the specialized "language" and procedural reasoning of admissions. A generic LLM, even with RAG, may structure responses incorrectly or miss nuances (e.g., distinguishing undergraduate vs. graduate requirements).
  - *Quick check question:* Why is a dataset of curated Q&A pairs (like the 8,000 pairs) more effective for fine-tuning than simply feeding the raw regulatory documents to the model?
- **Concept: Hybrid RAG-Fine-Tuning Architecture**
  - *Why needed here:* The paper's central thesis is that neither component alone is sufficient. Fine-tuning without RAG fails on precise, changing facts. RAG without fine-tuning fails on synthesis and style. Understanding their interdependence is key.
  - *Quick check question:* Based on the results, what specific weakness of the "Fine-Tuned (No RAG)" model does the RAG component most directly solve?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1.  Knowledge Base (KB): Structured storage of preprocessed source documents (regulations, web FAQs, past Q&A). Documents are chunked (~512 tokens, 64 overlap) and embedded.
  2.  Retriever: Takes a user query, computes its embedding, performs cosine similarity search against KB chunk embeddings, returns top-K (K=3) chunks.
  3.  Generator: A LoRA fine-tuned Gemma 9B model. Input = [Query] + [Retrieved Chunks]. Output = generated response.
  4.  Distillation Pipeline (Offline): Uses DeepSeek R1 to convert raw source documents into the fine-tuning Q&A dataset.
- **Critical path:** Query → Retriever fetches KB chunks → Generator (fine-tuned) consumes query + chunks → Response.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Chunk Size/Overlap: Larger chunks provide more context but risk noise; smaller chunks risk missing connective information. Overlap helps maintain context across chunk boundaries.
  - Fine-Tuning Compute vs. Breadth: LoRA reduces compute but may limit adaptation depth. A larger, more diverse fine-tuning dataset improves coverage but requires more curation.
  - RAG `top_k`: Higher `k` increases chance of including relevant info but adds noise and context length, straining the generator's context window.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Hallucination despite RAG: Generator ignores retrieved facts and fills gaps with parametric knowledge. Check retrieval relevance scores.
  - Incomplete/Inaccurate precise data: Fine-tuned model misreads or omits numbers/dates from retrieved chunks. Check chunk selection and model's attention to structured data.
  - Poor tone/style: Response is factually correct but sounds robotic or informal. Indicates fine-tuning dataset didn't capture desired communication style.
  - High latency: Excessive chunk size or `top_k` leads to long context, slowing generation. Monitor token counts.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Baseline Establishment: Run the 4 models (Baseline, RAG-only, Fine-tuned-only, Hybrid) on the 210-evaluation set to replicate reported metrics. Confirm hybrid superiority in fact recall and user satisfaction.
  2. Ablation on Retrieval Parameters: Fix the fine-tuned generator. Vary `top_k` (1, 3, 5) and chunk size (256, 512, 1024). Measure impact on precise data recall and response latency. Identify optimal retrieval configuration.
  3. Failure Mode Analysis: Select 20 "hard" inquiries where the hybrid model scored <2 (would not send without edits). Manually inspect: Was retrieval failure (no relevant chunks) or generation failure (ignored/misused chunks) the primary cause? This directs next steps (improve KB indexing vs. more/finer fine-tuning data).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** How can parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (e.g., LoRA, adapters) be optimized to enable a single model to effectively handle multiple, distinct admissions domains (e.g., undergraduate vs. graduate) without requiring separate fine-tuned instances?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The paper states that deploying across different admissions departments might require separate fine-tuned models, raising cost and hardware concerns.
  - *Why unresolved:* The current approach treats domain adaptation as a per-model task, which is computationally expensive. A solution would reduce resource barriers for smaller institutions.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Comparative experiments measuring performance (fact recall, user satisfaction) and resource usage (memory, training time) when using a single multi-domain adapter versus multiple single-domain adapters on a diverse evaluation set spanning different admission programs.
- **Question 2:** What is the optimal strategy for dynamically updating the knowledge base and model parameters in a fine-tuned RAG system to balance information freshness with system stability and cost?
  - *Basis in paper:* [explicit] The paper proposes a dynamic knowledge update mechanism to keep RAG current and reduce fine-tuning frequency, but does not specify implementation or evaluation.
  - *Why unresolved:* The trade-off between frequent, cheap RAG updates and periodic, expensive fine-tuning cycles is unquantified. The ideal split of "stable" (fine-tuned) vs. "volatile" (RAG) knowledge is unknown.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* A longitudinal study simulating policy changes, measuring response accuracy and system overhead under different policies (e.g., RAG updates daily/weekly vs. fine-tuning quarterly) and knowledge partitioning strategies.
- **Question 3:** How can a fine-tuned RAG system be made more robust to truly ambiguous, out-of-distribution, or novel queries that fall outside the scope of its curated knowledge base and training data?
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The paper acknowledges limitations in handling "truly ambiguous or novel questions" and the "residual risk of hallucination," but the proposed system lacks a described fallback or confidence mechanism.
  - *Why unresolved:* The evaluation used historical inquiries likely within the knowledge distribution. Performance on unprecedented edge cases (e.g., a new visa rule, an unprecedented program combination) is an open risk for real-world deployment.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Evaluation on a crafted adversarial or hold-out test set of intentionally ambiguous/novel queries, measuring metrics like correctdeclination rate (appropriately saying "I don't know"), escalation to human agents, and error type analysis.
- **Question 4:** How does the performance and cost-effectiveness of the fine-tuned RAG approach scale with larger, more diverse evaluation datasets and across different institutional contexts (e.g., universities with varying regulatory complexity)?
  - *Basis in paper:* [inferred] The paper does not question the generalizability of results to other domains or larger scales.
  - *Why unresolved:* The system's dependency on curated, high-quality Q&A pairs and a specific regulatory environment may limit transferability. The cost-benefit analysis for lower-volume or less-regulated institutions is unclear.
  - *What evidence would resolve it:* Replication studies at multiple universities with different admission volumes and policy complexities, tracking not only accuracy/satisfaction metrics but also implementation cost (data curation, compute, maintenance) and staff time savings.

## Limitations
- **Implementation gaps:** Critical hyperparameters for LoRA fine-tuning (rank, alpha, target modules) and the exact BERT retriever variant/prompt template are undisclosed, hindering precise replication.
- **Evaluation scope:** Reliance on a small, historical test set (210 inquiries) may not capture evolving query patterns or generalize to other institutions; user satisfaction ratings lack inter-rater reliability reporting.
- **Data provenance:** The quality and diversity of the 8k distilled Q/A dataset are not audited; potential biases or coverage gaps could limit robustness.

## Confidence
- **Hybrid superiority claim:** Medium – Supported by comparative metrics on a single eval set, but confounding from undisclosed parameters and small sample size.
- **RAG factual grounding mechanism:** Medium – Theoretically sound and partially evidenced by higher precision in RAG-only, but retrieval effectiveness depends on unvalidated chunking/indexing.
- **Fine-tuning for domain adaptation:** Medium – Plausible given fine-tuned-only’s poor data recall, but the exact "analytical skills" instilled are inferred, not directly measured.
- **Synergy between components:** Medium – Inferred from ablation results; no explicit experiment isolating why the fine-tuned model better uses retrieval.

## Next Checks
1. Hyperparameter Sensitivity Ablation: Re-run the hybrid model with varied LoRA ranks (e.g., 8, 16, 32) and `top_k` (1, 3, 5) to identify performance cliffs and optimal configurations.
2. Error Mode Diagnosis: Manually classify 50 failure cases from the hybrid model’s eval set into retrieval failure (relevant chunks absent) and generation failure (chunks present but ignored/mis-synthesized) to quantify the primary bottleneck.
3. Cross-Institution Generalization Test: Evaluate the final hybrid model on a held-out set of 100 fresh inquiries from a different university department (e.g., finance vs. admissions) to check overfitting.
