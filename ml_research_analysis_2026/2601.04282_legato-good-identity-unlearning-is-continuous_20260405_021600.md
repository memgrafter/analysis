# LEGATO: Good Identity Unlearning Is Continuous

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.04282
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04282
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** LEGATO achieves state-of-the-art identity unlearning with ID avg reduced to 0.00–0.18 and FID_pre as low as 6.09, improving retention by 14–29% over GUIDE, while reducing fine-tuned parameters by 95% and update time by 67%.

## Executive Summary
LEGATO introduces a novel continuous Neural ODE adapter approach for generative identity unlearning that addresses key challenges of inefficiency, uncontrollable forgetting, and catastrophic collapse. By freezing pre-trained generator weights and inserting lightweight ODE-based adapters, LEGATO enables precise control over forgetting intensity via step size modulation and ensures stable forgetting through trajectory consistency constraints. On FFHQ and CelebAHQ benchmarks, it significantly outperforms prior art (e.g., GUIDE) in both forgetting efficacy and retention quality, while being highly parameter- and time-efficient. The method also supports conflict-free multi-identity unlearning and demonstrates robustness to noise, with theoretical guarantees on smoothness and non-intersection of forgetting trajectories.

## Method Summary
LEGATO augments a frozen pre-trained GAN (EG3D with StyleGAN2 backbone) with lightweight Neural ODE adapters inserted after each synthesis layer. Each adapter consists of two 1×1 convolution layers and defines a continuous dynamical system that transforms latent representations over a fixed number of steps *T* with step size Δt. The ODE solver (Euler) is used for discretization. Unlearning is performed by inverting a source identity image *x_u* to its latent *w_u* using a frozen inversion network, then generating a target latent *w_t* via an “Un-Identifying” operation that shifts *w_u* along a random direction by distance *d*=30. The training loss combines a forgetting loss *L_u* (Euclidean, perceptual, and identity losses between images generated from *w_u* and *w_t*), a retention loss *L_r* (perceptual loss on a retain set sampled from Gaussian noise), and a trajectory consistency loss *L_TC* that penalizes changes in the ODE vector field over time to ensure smoothness. Only the adapter parameters are trained, keeping the backbone frozen, resulting in ~95% fewer fine-tuned parameters. The step size Δt directly controls forgetting intensity, and the consistency constraint prevents catastrophic collapse.

## Key Results
- **State-of-the-art unlearning:** On FFHQ (single image) LEGATO reduces identity similarity (ID avg) to 0.00–0.18 and achieves FID_pre as low as 6.09, improving retention by 14–29% over GUIDE.
- **Efficiency:** Reduces fine-tuned parameters by 95% and update time by 67% compared to full fine-tuning.
- **Stability and scalability:** Enables conflict-free unlearning of multiple identities and shows robustness to noise, supported by theoretical guarantees on trajectory smoothness and non-intersection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Neural ODE Adapters Enable Smooth, Parameter-Efficient Forgetting
- **Claim:** LEGATO uses lightweight Neural ODE adapters inserted into a frozen pre-trained generator to perform identity unlearning, reducing fine-tuned parameters by 95% and avoiding catastrophic collapse.
- **Mechanism:** The Neural ODE adapter defines a continuous dynamical system *h'(t) = f(h(t), t, θ)*. Solving this ODE transforms the latent representation gradually. The ODE solution is smooth (Theorem 1), leading to stable forgetting and minimal accumulated error. The pre-trained weights remain frozen.
- **Core assumption:** Identity information can be removed by a continuous transformation in latent space, and the ODE flow is smooth (C^1).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "introducing continuous Neural ODE adapters that freeze pre-trained weights"
  - [section 3.3]: Theorem 1 (Smooth Neural ODE Trajectories) and discussion on smoothness.
  - [corpus]: The corpus does not directly address Neural ODEs for unlearning, but the paper's empirical results (Table 2, Figure 3) demonstrate the benefits.
- **Break condition:** If the ODE vector field *f* is not expressive or the solver is unstable (e.g., Δt too large), forgetting may fail or cause collapse.

### Mechanism 2: ODE Step Size Controls Forgetting Intensity
- **Claim:** The forgetting intensity is precisely modulated by the ODE step size (Δt), providing fine-grained control and interpretability.
- **Mechanism:** In the discretized ODE (e.g., Forward Euler), *h_{k+1} = h_k + Δt * f(h_k, θ_k)*. Δt scales the update magnitude: larger Δt → stronger forgetting. Theorem 2 shows performance is non-monotonic in Δt, with an optimal interval.
- **Core assumption:** Forgetting and retention losses trade off with Δt due to discretization error and noise.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "forgetting intensity is precisely modulated via ODE step size"
  - [section 3.3]: Theorem 2 (Non-Monotonicity of Step Size).
  - [corpus]: The corpus does not directly address step size control, but the paper's experiments (Table 3) show the effect.
- **Break condition:** Δt too large → instability and collapse; Δt too small → negligible forgetting.

### Mechanism 3: Trajectory Consistency Constraint Prevents Catastrophic Collapse
- **Claim:** The trajectory consistency constraint (L_TC) smooths the ODE's vector field, ensuring stable forgetting and avoiding catastrophic collapse.
- **Mechanism:** *L_TC = Σ_{t=0}^{T-1} ||f(h(t+1), t+1, θ) - f(h(t), t, θ)||^2*. This penalizes changes in the vector field over time, encouraging smooth trajectories and robustness to perturbations.
- **Core assumption:** A smooth vector field leads to gradual, stable changes in latent representations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "trajectory consistency constraints ensure stable forgetting"
  - [section 3.4]: Equation (11) and discussion on smoothing.
  - [corpus]: The corpus includes papers on catastrophic collapse, but not on trajectory consistency. Evidence is from the paper's ablation (Table 6, Figure 3).
- **Break condition:** If λ_TC is too high, forgetting is hindered; too low, stability is lost.

## Foundational Learning
- **Neural ODEs:** Continuous-depth neural networks modeling evolution of hidden states via ODEs. **Why needed:** LEGATO's adapter is a Neural ODE that transforms latents continuously. **Quick check:** How does the adjoint method save memory during backprop? (It avoids storing intermediate activations by solving the adjoint ODE backward.)
- **GAN Inversion:** Mapping an image to the latent space of a GAN. **Why needed:** To obtain *w_u* from *x_u*. **Quick check:** Why use an inversion network instead of optimization? (Faster, more stable, but may sacrifice accuracy.)
- **Catastrophic Forgetting:** When unlearning one identity degrades others. **Why needed:** LEGATO aims to avoid this. **Quick check:** Why does full-model fine-tuning cause catastrophic forgetting? (It updates all weights, overwriting knowledge for other identities.)
- **Identity Unlearning:** Removing a specific person's identity from a generator. **Why needed:** The task of the paper. **Quick check:** How is it different from removing a generic object? (Identity requires preserving fine-grained facial features of others, more stringent.)

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  - Frozen pre-trained generator (StyleGAN2 backbone) and mapping network
  - Synthesis layers with Neural ODE adapters (trainable, two 1x1 convs)
  - ODE solver (e.g., Euler) with step size Δt
  - Frozen inversion network E
  - Losses: L_u (forgetting, between source and target images), L_r (retention, on retain set), L_TC (smoothness of f)
  - Un-Identifying: Computes w_t from w_u

- **Critical path:**
  1. Invert x_u → w_u (using E).
  2. Compute w_t and neighbor latents (source and target).
  3. Sample retain latents w_r,a.
  4. For batch:
     - Generate source images from w_u, neighbors via unlearned generator.
     - Generate target images from w_t, neighbors via pre-trained generator.
     - Generate retain images from w_r,a via both generators.
  5. Compute L_u, L_r, L_TC.
  6. Update only Neural ODE adapter parameters.
  7. Repeat, monitoring ID_avg and FID_pre.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Parameter efficiency (95% less) vs. expressiveness: Adapters are small but continuous flow compensates.
  - Step size: Δt controls forgetting intensity. Optimal Δt in [0.2,0.6] (Table 3).
  - L_TC: Improves stability but requires tuning λ_TC.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor forgetting (high ID_avg) or poor retention (high FID_pre).
  - Retention loss (L_r) spikes during training (without L_TC).
  - Distorted images (ODE instability, Δt too large).

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replicate Table 1 (FFHQ, single image) with Δt=0.4, T=4, Chidden=256, Euler. Expect ID≈0.00, FID_pre≈8.76.
  2. Step size sweep: Fix T·Δt=1.6, vary Δt. Expect non-monotonic FID_pre with minimum at Δt≈0.4.
  3. Ablate L_TC: Train with λ_TC=0 and λ_TC=0.1. Plot retention loss over epochs. Without L_TC, expect higher and more volatile loss.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
**Question:** Can LEGATO's Neural ODE adapter framework be effectively generalized to diffusion-based and transformer-based generative models (e.g., Stable Diffusion, large language models), or are its design principles inherently tied to GAN architectures?
- **Basis in paper:** Section 7 ("Extend to More Architectures") acknowledges that current identity unlearning is GAN-focused and proposes a theoretical comparison with diffusion/flow-matching architectures, but only provides a preliminary experiment on a flow-matching model for MNIST.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper's core design (inserting lightweight adapters after synthesis layers) assumes a GAN-like backbone with discrete, resolution-based synthesis blocks. Diffusion models operate on continuous denoising steps in pixel/latent space, and LLMs have fundamentally different sequential architectures, making direct adapter insertion non-trivial.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic empirical validation on mainstream diffusion (e.g., Stable Diffusion) and LLM benchmarks, demonstrating comparable forgetting efficacy, retention, and parameter efficiency. Theoretical analysis showing how Neural ODEs can be integrated into the denoising/attention pipeline without destabilizing training.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How robust are the theoretical guarantees (e.g., Theorem 3 on conflict-free multi-identity unlearning) when the latent manifolds for different identities are not perfectly separated or when the unlearning targets are not carefully chosen, as might occur in real-world, high-dimensional data?
- **Basis in paper:** The proofs (Supp. Section 3) rely on strong assumptions: disjoint identity manifolds (A2) and localized unlearning (A3) where the vector field is modified only within forgotten identity regions. The paper uses the "Un-Identifying" strategy to ensure target separation, but real-world latent spaces may have overlapping or entangled identity features.
- **Why unresolved:** The assumptions are simplifying idealizations. If identity manifolds have non-trivial intersections or if the unlearning gradient updates affect regions beyond the intended identity clusters, trajectory non-intersection and retention decoupling may fail, leading to conflicts or collateral forgetting not captured by current theory or experiments (which test only 2-3 identities).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical stress tests using diverse identities with known latent space proximity (e.g., similar facial attributes) and failure-case analysis showing when/why conflicts re-emerge. Theoretical relaxation of assumptions to probabilistic manifold separability.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Can the Neural ODE step size Δt be automatically and dynamically scheduled during unlearning to optimally balance forgetting intensity and retention, rather than relying on a fixed hyperparameter tuned per dataset?
- **Basis in paper:** Theorem 2 proves the existence of an optimal interval [Δt_min, Δt_max] and shows performance is non-monotonic in Δt. The paper manually tunes Δt (e.g., 0.4) and states it offers "interpretability and controllability," but does not automate its scheduling.
- **Why unresolved:** The optimal step size likely depends on the specific identity, the model's state during training, and the retention-loss landscape. A fixed Δt may be suboptimal across different forgetting stages (e.g., needing larger steps early for strong forgetting, smaller steps later for fine-tuning). No mechanism is proposed to adapt Δt based on real-time metrics (e.g., gradient norms, retention loss plateaus).
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of an adaptive scheduler (e.g., based on controlling discretization error, retention loss gradients, or a validation set) that maintains or improves the privacy-utility trade-off while reducing manual tuning. Ablation studies comparing fixed vs. adaptive Δt across diverse identities.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** What is the long-term stability of LEGATO's unlearned generator? Does the "smooth" ODE trajectory truly prevent catastrophic collapse over extended training or under distribution shifts, or could subtle, cumulative drifts in the latent space manifold eventually degrade retention capability?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper demonstrates stability up to 1000 epochs (Figure 3) and attributes it to the Trajectory Consistency (TC) constraint and smoothness (Theorem 1). However, catastrophic collapse is framed as an acute failure during unlearning progress. The long-term behavior—whether the unlearned generator maintains retention indefinitely or suffers slow degradation—is not assessed.
- **Why unresolved:** The experiments measure FID_pre and ∆FID_real at a fixed endpoint. There is no analysis of retention metrics as a function of continued training beyond convergence, or under latent space interpolation to out-of-support regions. The smoothness theorem (C¹ continuity) does not guarantee bounded curvature over long horizons, which could lead to gradual manifold distortion.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Longitudinal experiments training for many more epochs (e.g., 5000+) and monitoring retention metrics. Evaluation on extrapolative latent interpolations or novel identity generations to detect slow drifts. Analysis of trajectory properties (e.g., Lyapunov stability) of the learned Neural ODE vector field.

## Limitations
- **Inversion network dependency:** Exact architecture and weights of the inversion network *E* (from Yuan et al. 2023) are unspecified, risking deviations in latent extraction.
- **ODE solver and step size ambiguity:** The choice of solver (Euler vs. adaptive) and its impact on step size interpretation remain unclear.
- **Baseline reproducibility:** Hyperparameter details for baselines (e.g., GUIDE) are not fully provided, possibly affecting comparative fairness.
- **Retain set sampling:** Details on *N_r* and distribution for retain set are insufficiently described, which could influence retention metrics.

## Confidence
- **Forgetting/retention performance (SOTA):** Medium (empirical results strong but baseline reproducibility uncertain)
- **Parameter/time efficiency:** High (reductions directly measurable and consistently reported)
- **Neural ODE adapter mechanism:** Medium (supported by Theorem 1 and ablations, but adapter expressiveness and solver stability could limit generality)
- **Trajectory consistency for stability:** Medium (ablation evidence clear, but theoretical justification heuristic)
