# MoKus: Leveraging Cross-Modal Knowledge Transfer for Knowledge-Aware Concept Customization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.12743
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12743
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** MoKus enables knowledge-aware concept customization via cross-modal transfer, achieving state-of-the-art results on KnowCusBench in concept fidelity, prompt fidelity, human preference, and training efficiency.

## Executive Summary
Prior concept customization methods rely on rare tokens (e.g., `<sks>`), which lead to unstable generations and fail to incorporate the target concept's knowledge. This paper introduces knowledge-aware concept customization, which binds natural language knowledge to visual concepts. The proposed MoKus framework performs this binding through a two-stage process: first, visual concept learning uses LoRA finetuning to bind a concept's appearance to a rare token "anchor" representation; second, textual knowledge updating edits the LLM text encoder to map knowledge queries to this anchor. Evaluated on the new KnowCusBench, MoKus achieves state-of-the-art performance, significantly outperforming baselines in fidelity metrics and being markedly more efficient (~6 minutes vs. ~27 minutes for Naive-DB). Ablations confirm robust performance even as the number of knowledge items increases.

## Method Summary
MoKus operates on a set of reference images for a target concept and a corresponding set of textual knowledge items. In Stage 1 (Visual Concept Learning), the DiT backbone (e.g., SD3-medium) is finetuned with LoRA applied to its self-attention layers. A rare token (e.g., `<sks>dog`) is used as an anchor; its embedding from the LLM encoder is optimized to reconstruct the concept's images via a Rectified Flow loss, implanting the visual identity into that token's representation. The anchor embedding is then fixed. In Stage 2 (Textual Knowledge Updating), for each knowledge item formatted as a query (e.g., "What is my friend’s dog?"), the LLM encoder's parameters in specific MLP layers (layers 18–26, Gate/Up Projections) are edited. A closed-form least-squares solution computes a parameter shift Δθ* that maps the query's hidden representation to the fixed anchor, leveraging a formulation adapted from knowledge editing (UltraEdit). The updated LLM encoder then conditions the same DiT (with frozen LoRA from Stage 1) during generation.

## Key Results
- **State-of-the-art on KnowCusBench:** CLIP-I-Seg 0.764 (reconstruction), 0.718 (generation); CLIP-T 0.305; Pick Score 21.30.
- **High efficiency:** ~6 minutes for 5 knowledge items vs. ~27 minutes for Naive-DB baseline.
- **Scalable performance:** Ablations show minimal degradation as knowledge count increases, with near-linear time cost per additional knowledge.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Updating factual knowledge in the LLM text encoder causally influences the visual generation of the diffusion model.
- **Mechanism:** The framework performs knowledge editing on the LLM encoder's parameters to map a natural language knowledge query to the predefined anchor token. During image generation, this altered textual representation is processed by the same encoder and fed into the DiT. The cross-modal knowledge transfer hypothesis states that the edit propagates through the cross-attention mechanisms within the DiT, steering the visual output toward the edited knowledge.
- **Core assumption:** The latent alignment between the LLM's text feature space and the DiT's text-conditioning space is sufficiently strong and linear that a local, targeted parameter edit in the LLM induces a predictable change in the generated visual output.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract states cross-modal transfer; Section 3 compares pre/post-edit generations showing the edited answer in images; corpus shows neighboring work found no significant evidence, highlighting MoKus's specific architectural choice (editing MLP layers in a specific range) as key.
- **Break condition:** If the DiT's conditioning is not linearly sensitive to the edited latent directions, the transfer fails, degrading prompt fidelity or corrupting the concept.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Using a rare token as an "anchor representation" efficiently binds a target concept's visual appearance to textual knowledge without full fine-tuning of the generation model.
- **Mechanism:** In Visual Concept Learning, LoRA is applied to the DiT's self-attention layers. The rare token's textual embedding is optimized to reconstruct the concept's images, implanting the visual identity into that token's representation within the text encoder's output space. In Textual Knowledge Updating, this anchor acts as a fixed target when editing the LLM encoder; knowledge queries are forced to output the pre-learned anchor representation, decoupling visual learning from knowledge binding.
- **Core assumption:** A single, low-rank LoRA adaptation on the DiT is sufficient to create a distinct, disentangled textual embedding for a new visual concept without catastrophic interference.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract describes the anchor binding via LoRA; Section 4.1 details the LoRA training objective (Eq. 5) and the anchor's role; corpus shows related work on rare tokens but MoKus's use as a fixed target for knowledge editing is novel.
- **Break condition:** If LoRA fails to create a high-fidelity anchor (e.g., collapses to generic or overfits), subsequent knowledge binding targets a poor visual representation, lowering CLIP-I-Seg scores.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The two-stage, separated design enables efficient binding of multiple pieces of knowledge to a single concept with near-linear time cost.
- **Mechanism:** Stage 1 (Visual Learning) is done once per concept. Stage 2 (Knowledge Updating) operates per knowledge item but only on a small subset of the LLM encoder's parameters (16 MLP matrices from layers 18–26). The parameter shift Δθ* is computed per item via a closed-form least-squares solution, which is orders of magnitude faster than full fine-tuning.
- **Core assumption:** The selected "updatable layers" in the LLM encoder are the primary locus for factual/associative knowledge; editing only these layers suffices to alter responses without damaging broader capabilities.
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract notes updating completes in seconds; Section 4.2 specifies layers and efficiency (~7 seconds per knowledge); Table 2 shows near-constant performance and linearly increasing time.
- **Break condition:** If the knowledge resides outside the edited MLP layers (e.g., in attention or earlier layers), the localized edit is ineffective, requiring more extensive edits.

## Foundational Learning
- **Diffusion Transformer (DiT):** The image generation backbone using a transformer architecture. It processes patchified latents, timesteps, and text embeddings, with cross-attention fusing modalities. *Why needed:* Essential to understand where LoRA is applied and how text conditioning steers generation. *Quick check:* What role does the timestep `t` play in the velocity prediction loss (Eq. 2)?
- **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA):** A parameter-efficient finetuning method that injects trainable rank-decomposition matrices into existing layers (e.g., self-attention). *Why needed:* Enables efficient visual concept learning by adapting the DiT without full fine-tuning. *Quick check:* Why apply LoRA to the DiT's self-attention layers for visual learning but not to the LLM encoder during knowledge updating?
- **Knowledge Editing:** Techniques for modifying a model's factual knowledge stored in its parameters (e.g., via gradient-based shifts). *Why needed:* Forms the algorithmic basis for the textual knowledge updating stage, adapted from LLM editing to multimodal generation. *Quick check:* How does the closed-form solution Δθ* = (HᵀH+I)⁻¹HᵀV (Eq. 8) balance reconstruction error and update magnitude? What does the identity matrix `I` regularize?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Pre-processor (VAE) -> Visual Concept Learner (DiT with LoRA) -> Anchor Repository (fixed rare token embedding) -> Knowledge Editor (computes Δθ* on LLM layers 18–26) -> Updated LLM Encoder -> Generation DiT (with same LoRA)
- **Critical path:** Reference Images -> Stage 1 (DiT+LoRA training) -> Anchor Token Embedding -> Stage 2 (Compute Δθ* for LLM layers) -> Updated LLM Encoder -> Inference (Generate from knowledge+prompt)
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Rare token anchor vs. direct knowledge embedding: Decouples expensive visual learning from cheap knowledge binding, but introduces a non-semantic token that must be learned well.
  - Editing specific MLP layers vs. full fine-tuning: Efficient and preserves general capabilities, but assumes knowledge resides in those layers and may lack capacity for complex knowledge.
  - Two-stage sequential vs. joint end-to-end: Clear separation of responsibilities and efficiency, but prevents gradient flow from knowledge update back to visual learning and may cause suboptimal joint optimization.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High CLIP-I but low CLIP-I-Seg: Visual learning failed to localize/conceptually isolate the target concept.
  - Good reconstruction but poor prompt fidelity (low CLIP-T): Knowledge update distorted the LLM's general text-image alignment (over-editing or wrong layers).
  - Progressively worse generations with more knowledge: Parameter shift accumulation causes catastrophic forgetting or conflict in the LLM encoder.
  - Training time close to Naive-DB: Likely error in editing too many layers or inefficient update computation.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Anchor Quality Sanity Check: Train Stage 1 only, generate images using the rare token alone, compute CLIP-I and CLIP-I-Seg against reference set; expect >0.75 CLIP-I-Seg.
  2. Single-Knowledge Transfer Validation: After both stages with one knowledge item, generate using *only* that knowledge as prompt; evaluate if image matches the knowledge's semantic meaning (e.g., via human evaluation or fine-grained classifier).
  3. Efficiency Baseline: Time Stage 2 for 1, 3, and 5 knowledge items; confirm near-linear time increase (~7s per knowledge) and total time substantially less than Naive-DB.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **How to extend MoKus to video generation?** MoKus handles single images; video requires temporal consistency.
- **What evaluation metrics better capture knowledge fidelity?** Current metrics (e.g., CLIP-I-Seg) measure visual similarity, not semantic correctness of the knowledge.
- **Can an end-to-end MoKus improve efficiency and performance?** The two-stage pipeline may cause suboptimal joint optimization and extra training time.

## Limitations
- Query conversion format for knowledge items is unspecified, affecting reproducibility.
- Exact updatable layer indices (Gate/Up Projections in Qwen-Image layers 18–26) require manual code inspection.
- Scalability to large knowledge sets per concept (beyond 5) and potential interference remain unexplored.

## Confidence
- **Cross-modal knowledge transfer** (editing LLM influences visuals): Medium
- **Rare token anchor binding** (visual concept learning): High
- **Two-stage efficiency** (linear time per knowledge): High

## Next Checks
1. **Scale knowledge density:** Test 10–20 knowledge items per concept, measuring CLIP-I-Seg, CLIP-T, and total time to verify linearity and detect interference.
2. **Mechanistic probing:** Compare LLM encoder hidden states and DiT cross-attention maps before/after edits (e.g., via representational similarity analysis) to confirm propagation of the edit.
3. **Cross-model generalization:** Replicate the pipeline on SDXL with its CLIP text encoder to assess whether the approach transfers beyond Qwen-Image and SD3.
