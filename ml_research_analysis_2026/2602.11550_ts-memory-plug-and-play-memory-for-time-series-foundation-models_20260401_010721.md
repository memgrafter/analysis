# TS-Memory: Plug-and-Play Memory for Time Series Foundation Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2602.11550
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11550
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Reduces average MSE by 5.8% (up to 16.0%) and MAE by 2.1% across four TSFMs and eight benchmarks while matching frozen backbone inference speed.

## Executive Summary
Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) exhibit performance drops when applied to domains different from their training data. Existing adaptation methods face a trade-off: parametric fine-tuning risks catastrophic forgetting and high maintenance costs, while non‑parametric retrieval introduces heavy inference latency. TS‑Memory introduces Parametric Memory Distillation, a plug‑and‑play adapter that bridges this gap. A lightweight memory module is trained in two stages: first, an offline kNN teacher synthesizes confidence‑aware quantile forecasts from retrieved similar series; second, this teacher distills distributional corrections into the memory via confidence‑gated supervision, keeping the backbone frozen. At inference, the memory and backbone predictions are fused in constant time, eliminating retrieval overhead. Experiments across four TSFMs and eight benchmarks show consistent improvements in point and probabilistic forecasting (MSE ↓5.8% avg, up to 16.0%; MAE ↓2.1%) while preserving the backbone’s speed, outperforming both retrieval and fine‑tuning baselines.

## Method Summary
TS‑Memory augments a frozen TSFM backbone with a lightweight memory adapter to handle domain shift. The training pipeline comprises two phases. In the first phase, an offline teacher is constructed: for each training series, a k‑nearest neighbor search in the frozen backbone’s embedding space retrieves similar historical contexts; from the futures of these neighbors, the teacher synthesizes quantile forecasts and adjusts for mean shifts, producing confidence‑aware targets. This teacher is leakage‑safe because it only uses past information. In the second phase, the memory module—a small neural network—takes the backbone’s embedding and predicts a distributional correction (e.g., additive or multiplicative). The memory is trained via knowledge distillation: its outputs are compared to the teacher’s quantile targets using a loss that is gated by the teacher’s confidence, so that uncertain teacher predictions down‑weight the gradient. The backbone remains frozen, preventing catastrophic forgetting. At inference, the backbone produces both an embedding and a base forecast; the memory produces a correction from the embedding; the two forecasts are fused with a single scalar weight α (learned or tuned) in O(1) time, bypassing any retrieval.

## Key Results
- **Accuracy**: Average MSE reduction of 5.8% (up to 16.0%) and MAE reduction of 2.1% over the frozen backbone across eight benchmarks.
- **Efficiency**: Inference speed matches the frozen backbone; constant‑time fusion eliminates retrieval latency.
- **Trade‑off**: Outperforms both online retrieval and parameter‑efficient fine‑tuning baselines in the accuracy‑efficiency landscape.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
TS‑Memory succeeds because it efficiently transfers the domain‑specific knowledge captured by retrieval into a compact parametric form. The offline kNN teacher acts as a high‑capacity but slow oracle: by examining similar historical series, it can forecast distributional shifts that the backbone misses. Distilling this knowledge into a memory module via confidence‑gated supervision ensures that only reliable patterns are encoded, avoiding noise from poor retrievals. The frozen backbone retains its general representations, while the memory learns lightweight corrections tailored to the target domain. At inference, the fusion step combines the backbone’s general forecast with the memory’s specialized correction in constant time, thus achieving the best of both worlds: accuracy close to retrieval‑based methods and speed comparable to the backbone. The confidence gating further stabilizes training by reducing the influence of uncertain teacher predictions, leading to robust improvements.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Domain Shift**  
   - *Why needed*: TSFMs trained on source data may fail on target domains with different distributions.  
   - *Quick check*: Evaluate zero‑shot performance on a held‑out domain; large drop indicates shift.
2. **Foundation Models**  
   - *Why needed*: Pre‑trained TSFMs provide strong general representations but lack domain specialization.  
   - *Quick check*: Apply the model to a new dataset without adaptation; assess accuracy.
3. **Knowledge Distillation**  
   - *Why needed*: To compress the teacher’s retrieval‑based knowledge into a small, fast student.  
   - *Quick check*: Measure student’s output similarity to teacher on a validation set.
4. **Parametric vs Non‑parametric Adaptation**  
   - *Why needed*: Balances maintenance cost and inference speed; parametric methods are fixed‑size, non‑parametric scale with data.  
   - *Quick check*: Compare parameter count and inference time across methods.
5. **Confidence‑weighted Supervision**  
   - *Why needed*: Not all teacher predictions are equally trustworthy; weighting improves student learning.  
   - *Quick check*: Correlate teacher confidence (e.g., neighbor consistency) with student loss magnitude.
6. **Constant‑time Fusion**  
   - *Why needed*: To preserve the backbone’s low latency, fusion must not depend on memory size.  
   - *Quick check*: Measure inference latency as memory capacity increases; should remain flat.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**:  
Frozen Backbone → (Embedding, Forecast)  
Memory(Embedding) → Correction  
Fusion(Forecast, Correction) → Final Forecast

**Critical path**:  
*Training*: For each training series, offline teacher retrieves neighbors → synthesizes quantile targets → memory is trained to predict corrections matching targets (loss gated by teacher confidence).  
*Inference*: Backbone computes embedding and base forecast → memory produces correction → fusion with weight α yields final forecast.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- Offline teacher construction enables fast inference but requires retraining for new domains.
- Global fusion weight α is simple and O(1) but cannot adapt to per‑instance reliability.
- Memory size offers a capacity‑accuracy trade‑off; larger memory increases parameters but still constant‑time fusion.
- Confidence gating mitigates noisy teacher signals but introduces an extra hyperparameter.

**Failure signatures**:
- Teacher retrieves irrelevant neighbors → memory learns misleading corrections.
- Confidence gating threshold mis‑set → either under‑fitting (too strict) or over‑fitting to noise (too lenient).
- Fusion weight α poorly tuned → performance degrades (e.g., over‑reliance on weak backbone).
- Memory capacity insufficient → bottleneck prevents capturing complex corrections.

**Exactly 3 first experiments**:
1. Memory size ablation: vary memory parameter count (e.g., hidden dimension) and plot accuracy vs. inference latency.
2. Confidence gating ablation: compare using confidence‑weighted loss vs. uniform weighting on datasets with varying teacher reliability.
3. Fusion weight sensitivity: sweep α across [0,1] and evaluate accuracy and calibration (e.g., CRPS) to find robust ranges.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Continual memory updates**  
   The current memory is static after offline training; it cannot adapt to streaming or non‑stationary data without full retraining. Evidence: The paper states future directions include continual updates under evolving domains. To resolve: evaluate on benchmarks with distribution drift, measuring adaptation latency, stability, and whether privacy‑preserving approximate teacher updates maintain performance.
2. **Robust teacher constructions**  
   The vanilla kNN teacher uses Euclidean distance in the frozen embedding and simple mean shift, which may fail under complex shifts (e.g., variance changes, temporal warping) or noisy retrievals. Evidence: The paper suggests richer teacher constructions with improved reliability estimation. To resolve: compare alternative designs (multi‑metric retrieval, ensembles, learned reliability) across diverse shift patterns.
3. **Input‑adaptive fusion**  
   Fusion currently uses a single global weight α, ignoring that memory reliability may vary across time steps, variables, or horizons. Evidence: The paper proposes input‑adaptive fusion and uncertainty‑aware calibration. To resolve: develop confidence metrics for memory outputs and evaluate context‑aware fusion strategies on probabilistic calibration metrics (CRPS, reliability diagrams).
4. **Scalability and broader tasks**  
   Teacher construction requires O(|D|) nearest‑neighbor search, which is costly for large corpora; the paradigm’s applicability beyond forecasting is unexplored. Evidence: The paper mentions scalable teacher compilation and extension to other tasks. To resolve: use approximate nearest‑neighbor methods to speed up teacher creation; adapt the distillation framework to imputation or anomaly detection and compare to task‑specific baselines.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to eight benchmarks and four TSFMs; generalization to other time series tasks and domains remains unknown.
- No statistical tests or confidence intervals are reported for MSE/MAE improvements, weakening the strength of the claims.
- Efficiency claims (matching backbone speed) lack detailed runtime metrics (hardware, batch size, memory size) and do not quantify the memory module’s parameter overhead.
- The behavior of the memory module under streaming data and its ability to handle concept drift are not studied.
- The leakage‑safe guarantee of the offline kNN teacher requires more elaboration and verification.

## Confidence
- **Performance gains (MSE/MAE)**: Medium – results are consistent but lack statistical rigor and broader evaluation.
- **Inference speed matching backbone**: Low – claims are not backed by detailed timing measurements.
- **Method novelty**: Medium – conceptually sound but needs deeper comparison with concurrent retrieval‑based adaptation methods.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the experiments with multiple random seeds, report variance, and test on standard time series datasets (e.g., M4, Yahoo) to assess robustness and generalization.
2. Conduct an ablation on memory size, measuring both accuracy and latency under controlled hardware settings to verify the constant‑time fusion claim.
3. Evaluate TS‑Memory on cross‑task scenarios (anomaly detection, classification) and extreme domain shifts to probe the limits of its adaptability.
