# Prototype-Based Knowledge Guidance for Fine-Grained Structured Radiology Reporting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2603.11938
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11938
- Reference count: 31
- Primary result: ProtoSR achieves state-of-the-art overall F1 of 34.4 on Rad-ReStruct, with a 72.1% relative improvement on fine-grained L3 attributes (F1 gain 7.4) and 82% coverage of L3 attributes in the mined knowledge base.

## Executive Summary
Structured radiology reporting automation faces a key challenge: sparse supervision for fine-grained, rare attributes in small structured datasets, despite large free-text corpora. This paper introduces ProtoSR, a method that automatically mines a multimodal knowledge base from free-text MIMIC-CXR reports using an instruction-tuned LLM. The knowledge base aligns each structured answer option with a visual prototype derived from sample images. During inference, ProtoSR retrieves relevant prototypes for the current image-question pair and augments the base model's predictions via a prototype-conditioned residual at the logit level. On the Rad-ReStruct benchmark, ProtoSR sets a new state-of-the-art with overall F1 of 34.4. Its most substantial gains are on detailed attribute questions (L3-F1: 7.4), representing a 72.1% relative improvement over the baseline, demonstrating effective knowledge transfer from free-text for fine-grained decision correction. The knowledge base covers 82% of L3 attributes, providing substantial support even for long-tailed findings.

## Method Summary
ProtoSR operates in two phases. First, offline knowledge base construction: An instruction-tuned LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) extracts answers for each structured template question from MIMIC-CXR free-text reports. A terminology expansion step maps clinical paraphrases to canonical labels, and post-processing enforces hierarchical consistency and filters noise. For each label, up to five images are sampled and their image-encoder embeddings are aggregated via element-wise max pooling to form a fixed visual prototype. Second, online knowledge-augmented inference: A base model (image encoder EfficientNet-B5 + text encoder RadBERT + fusion transformer) processes the current image and question (with dialog history) to produce base logits. The current fused representation is projected and compared to all prototypes via cosine similarity; the top-weighted prototypes and their one-hot labels are aggregated into support vectors (visual evidence v and label tendencies u). These are concatenated and passed through an MLP to produce a support bias b_sup. A learned per-answer scaling vector s modulates the bias, which is added to the base logits: z_final = z_base + s ⊙ b_sup. This late-fusion design allows targeted correction of fine-grained errors while preserving coarse decisions.

## Key Results
- Overall F1 score of 34.4 on the Rad-ReStruct benchmark, achieving state-of-the-art performance.
- Fine-grained attribute (L3) F1 improvement of 7.4 points, a 72.1% relative gain over the baseline model.
- The automatically mined knowledge base covers 82% of L3 attributes in Rad-ReStruct, providing prototype support for the majority of fine-grained options.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Mining free-text reports with an LLM creates a large, template-aligned knowledge base that provides visual exemplars for rare, fine-grained structured attributes, mitigating data sparsity.
- **Mechanism:** An instruction-tuned LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) is used to extract answers for each structured template question from free-text MIMIC-CXR reports. Terminology expansion maps clinical paraphrases to canonical labels. Post-processing filters noise and enforces hierarchical consistency. For each label, up to K=5 images are sampled, and their embeddings are max-pooled to create a prototype.
- **Core assumption:** LLM extraction can reliably map unstructured narrative descriptions to the precise, predefined options of a structured template, and max-pooled image embeddings from these extracted studies will form a representative visual prototype for that label.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...automatically mining a multimodal knowledge base from free-text MIMIC-CXR reports using an instruction-tuned LLM, aligning each structured answer option with a visual prototype."
  - [section 2.1] Details the three-step pipeline: terminology expansion, template-constrained extraction, and post-processing.
  - [section 4.1] Table 2 shows 82% coverage of L3 attributes in the mined knowledge base.
  - [corpus] Corpus evidence for this specific LLM-driven extraction pipeline is weak; neighboring papers (e.g., RadEx, Reshaping Free-Text Notes) explore similar LLM extraction but not for prototype creation.
- **Break condition:** If LLM extraction accuracy degrades significantly on unseen report styles or rare findings, leading to noisy or absent prototypes for critical long-tail attributes.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Representing each answer option with a visual prototype (rather than text or a single example) provides a robust, aggregate signal for retrieval that captures intra-class visual variation.
- **Mechanism:** A prototype for label ℓ is a fixed vector Pℓ, computed as the element-wise max over the image encoder embeddings of K sampled, label-positive studies. At inference, the current image-question representation S is compared to all M prototypes via cosine similarity. The top-weighted prototypes and their associated one-hot labels are aggregated into a support vector.
- **Core assumption:** A max-pooled aggregate of multiple positive examples better represents a visual concept (e.g., "diffuse lung opacity") than any single example, making the prototype more generalizable and less sensitive to idiosyncrasies of one study.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.1] "...aggregate their image-encoder embeddings into a single prototype using element-wise max pooling, preserving the strongest signals across the sampled images."
  - [section 2.2] The retrieval step computes similarity to these fixed prototype embeddings.
  - [section 4.2] Table 4 ablation: Randomizing prototypes (destroying their semantic structure) reduces performance to baseline, indicating the prototype *content* is critical.
- **Break condition:** If the K sampled images for a label are visually inconsistent (e.g., due to extraction errors), the max-pooled prototype becomes an invalid or noisy representation, harming retrieval.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Injecting retrieved prototype evidence as a learnable, per-answer residual at the logit level allows for selective correction of the base model's predictions, specifically targeting fine-grained errors while preserving correct coarse decisions.
- **Mechanism:** Retrieved prototype features (v) and label tendencies (u) are concatenated and passed through an MLP to produce a support bias, b_sup. This bias is scaled by a learned per-answer vector s and added to the base model's logits: z_final = z_base + s ⊙ b_sup. The scaling vector s is trained to modulate the influence of knowledge for each answer option.
- **Core assumption:** Errors in fine-grained, long-tail attribute predictions can be corrected by a separate, data-driven "second opinion" signal without destabilizing the backbone model's learned predictions for more frequent patterns. The residual addition is a safer fusion than modifying the backbone's features.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "...augments the base model's predictions via a prototype-conditioned residual at the logit level."
  - [section 2.2] "This late-fusion design preserves the backbone decision pathway and enables targeted corrections where prototype evidence is informative."
  - [section 4.2] Table 4: The late-fusion variant outperforms an "Early Fusion" baseline that prepends knowledge embeddings to the input sequence, suggesting residual addition is more effective.
- **Break condition:** If the learned scaling vector s collapses to near-zero for most attributes, the knowledge branch is ignored. Alternatively, if s is uniformly high, the base model's signal is overridden, potentially degrading performance on reliable in-distribution samples.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Hierarchical Visual Question Answering (VQA) for Structured Reporting**
  - **Why needed here:** The Rad-ReStruct benchmark formulates structured reporting as a multi-turn VQA task over a hierarchical template (L1: existence, L2: findings, L3: attributes). Understanding this sequential, context-dependent QA setup is fundamental to grasping the task formulation and the model's iterative prediction process.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the context of prior Q&A pairs influence the prediction for the current fine-grained attribute question?
- **Concept: Prototype-Based Few-Shot/Retrieval Learning**
  - **Why needed here:** ProtoSR's core innovation is using *prototypes* (aggregated embeddings) instead of individual examples for retrieval. This requires understanding how prototypes summarize a class, how similarity to a prototype relates to class membership, and why max-pooling might be used for aggregation.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the potential trade-off between using a single nearest neighbor and a max-pooled prototype for retrieval?
- **Concept: Residual Learning and Late Fusion in Multi-Modal Architectures**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper's fusion strategy (adding a prototype-derived bias to base logits) is a specific form of residual learning. This concept is key to understanding why this integration might be more stable than early or feature-level fusion, especially for making discrete, fine-grained adjustments.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might adding a correction signal at the *logit* level be less disruptive to a trained model's feature representations than fusing it earlier in the network?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  1. Input: Chest X-ray image (x), Question & dialog history (q).
  2. Base Model (Backbone): Image encoder (EfficientNet-B5) + Text encoder (RadBERT) → Fusion Transformer → Classifier → `z_base` logits.
  3. Knowledge Bank (Static during batch): Dictionary of M prototypes (P ∈ R^{M×d}) and their one-hot labels (A ∈ R^{M×|Y|}).
  4. Knowledge Retrieval Branch: Shared projection layer for S (from fusion) and P → Cosine similarity → weights α → Weighted sum: v = αᵀP (visual evidence), u = αᵀA (label tendencies). Concatenation [v; u] → MLP → support bias b_sup.
  5. Final fusion: Learned per-answer scaling vector s → scale b_sup → z_final = z_base + s ⊙ b_sup.
- **Critical path:** The knowledge retrieval branch operates in parallel with base model inference; its output b_sup is added to base logits after scaling. The knowledge bank is pre-computed offline and does not change during training/inference.
- **Design tradeoffs:** 
  - Max-pooling prototypes vs. averaging: max-pooling preserves strongest features, potentially robust to outliers but may blend distinct visual cues.
  - Late fusion at logits vs. early fusion in features: logit addition minimally perturbs backbone features, reducing catastrophic forgetting; but it may lack fine-grained alignment.
  - Fixed prototypes vs. dynamic example retrieval: prototypes are stable and fast, but may lose diversity that a set of examples could provide.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Missing prototypes for many L3 attributes (18% uncovered) leads to no correction for those questions.
  - Noisy LLM extraction yields visually inconsistent samples, causing ambiguous prototypes that mislead retrieval.
  - Scaling vector s near zero for long-tail attributes indicates the knowledge branch is underutilized, possibly due to insufficient training signal.
- **Exactly 3 first experiments:**
  1. Measure coverage and extraction accuracy: Sample a subset of MIMIC-CXR reports; have radiologists annotate according to Rad-ReStruct template; compute agreement of LLM extraction vs. ground truth. Focus on rare L3 terms.
  2. Prototype consistency analysis: For a stratified set of L3 labels, compute intra-prototype embedding variance (e.g., average pairwise cosine distance among constituent images). Visualize with t-SNE to assess homogeneity.
  3. Scaling vector s analysis: Inspect learned s magnitudes per attribute; correlate with attribute frequency and ΔF1 (ProtoSR minus baseline) to verify targeted correction for rare attributes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can the correctness of LLM-extracted labels from MIMIC-CXR free-text reports be validated in the absence of ground-truth template annotations for that dataset?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors state: "Since the target free-text corpus (MIMIC-CXR) does not provide ground-truth answers in the Rad-ReStruct template space, extraction correctness cannot be measured directly." They evaluate the extractor on Rad-ReStruct instead.
- **Why unresolved:** The knowledge base is built from MIMIC-CXR, but its quality is only inferred via proxy evaluation on a different dataset (Rad-ReStruct). This leaves uncertainty about how well the extraction generalizes to the true target distribution and whether noise in the knowledge base limits or benefits performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conduct a human evaluation or cross-check study where radiologists annotate a sample of MIMIC-CXR reports according to the Rad-ReStruct template to obtain ground-truth labels for MIMIC-CXR itself, then measure extraction agreement.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Why does the knowledge base fail to cover 18% of L3 attributes (1167 total, 966 covered), and are the uncovered attributes systematically rarer, clinically more ambiguous, or associated with specific reporting styles?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] Table 2 reports 82% coverage for L3 attributes. The paper notes this yields "substantial prototype support even for fine-grained attributes, which are typically long-tailed" but does not analyze the missing 18%.
- **Why unresolved:** Understanding coverage gaps is critical for assessing the method's limitations and potential failure modes. Without this, it is unclear whether missing attributes reflect inherent extraction challenges (e.g., complex phrasing, implicit findings) or data sparsity in MIMIC-CXR.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analyze the semantic and statistical properties of uncovered L3 attributes (e.g., frequency in MIMIC-CXR, lexical diversity of associated phrases, hierarchical depth) and compare them to covered attributes to identify systematic barriers.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How sensitive is ProtoSR's performance, particularly on L3 attributes, to the number of images K sampled per prototype (set to 5 in the paper)?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method "uniformly sample[s] up to K=5 images for each prototype" but provides no ablation or justification for this choice. Prototype aggregation via max pooling could be affected by sample size and diversity.
- **Why unresolved:** K controls the trade-off between prototype stability and diversity. Too few images may yield noisy prototypes; too many may dilute attribute-specific signals. The optimal value may differ across attribute types or datasets.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** An ablation study varying K (e.g., 1, 3, 5, 10) measuring L3-F1 and overall performance, along with an analysis of prototype intra-class variance.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** On which specific L3 attribute categories (e.g., location, severity, texture) does ProtoSR provide the largest gains, and what characteristics of these attributes make them most amenable to prototype-based correction?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper highlights "the largest improvements on detailed attribute questions (L3)" and a 72.1% relative improvement over the baseline, but does not break down results by attribute subtype within L3.
- **Why unresolved:** L3 attributes are heterogeneous. Identifying which subtypes benefit most would clarify the mechanism of knowledge transfer (e.g., spatial attributes vs. descriptive ones) and guide future refinement of the knowledge base.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Report performance stratified by L3 attribute type (as defined by the Rad-ReStruct template ontology) for ProtoSR vs. baseline, and correlate gains with attributes' base frequency, extraction coverage, and prototype availability.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Can ProtoSR's knowledge extraction and integration pipeline generalize to structured reporting templates beyond Rad-ReStruct (e.g., different anatomies, modalities) or to institution-specific reporting styles?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The method is designed for the Rad-ReStruct chest X-ray template and evaluated only on it. The extraction pipeline relies on a "template-aligned" approach and terminology expansion tailored to that template.
- **Why unresolved:** The value proposition hinges on transferring knowledge from free-text to structured templates. Without testing on other templates or modalities, it is unclear whether the approach is broadly applicable or specific to the MIMIC-CXR/Rad-ReStruct setting.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply the full pipeline to a different structured reporting benchmark (e.g., brain MRI, mammography) with a new template, and evaluate knowledge base coverage and end-to-end performance gains compared to a baseline without knowledge injection.

## Limitations
- LLM extraction reliability is unvalidated; 18% of L3 attributes lack prototypes, and mapping free-text to strict templates may fail on rare paraphrrases, leading to noisy or missing knowledge.
- Prototype quality depends on noisy image sampling; max-pooling may blend visually inconsistent examples for long-tail attributes, producing unrepresentative prototypes.
- Overall F1 of 34.4 indicates limited absolute performance; the learned scaling vector s might underutilize knowledge for rare attributes, possibly due to insufficient training signal or over-regularization.

## Confidence
- Knowledge base mining (LLM alignment): Medium
- Visual prototype aggregation: High
- Logit-level residual fusion: High
