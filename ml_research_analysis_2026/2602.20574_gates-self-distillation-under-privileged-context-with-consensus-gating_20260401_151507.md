# GATES: Self-Distillation under Privileged Context with Consensus Gating

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.20574
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20574
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Held-out in-domain student accuracy increases from 46.0% to 62.0%; average maj@8 accuracy on four public document-free math benchmarks rises from 20.2% to 35.4%.

## Executive Summary
GATES tackles self-distillation without ground-truth labels in asymmetric document-grounded QA, where the same model acts as tutor (with document) and student (without document). It samples multiple tutor rollouts, uses consensus agreement to gate learning, and distills full reasoning trajectories (token-level) into the student only when consensus is strong. This prevents the student from learning from unreliable tutor outputs while providing dense supervision. On held-out in-domain questions, student accuracy improves by 16 percentage points (46.0% → 62.0%). On four public document-free math benchmarks, average majority@8 accuracy increases by 15.2 points (20.2% → 35.4%), substantially outperforming answer-only SFT, outcome RL, and unfiltered trajectory distillation.

## Method Summary
GATES constructs a training set of math questions paired with documents using a separate model (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) with a consensus filter: a question is retained if at least 5 out of 8 tutor rollouts agree on the final answer and produce a parsable \boxed{} response, and none mention the document. The base model (Qwen3-4B-Base) is then trained under an asymmetric setup: for each batch, the tutor generates 8 rollouts conditioned on the document; a consensus gate (threshold τ=4) determines whether the question is reliable; only rollouts matching the modal answer and passing document-leakage guardrails contribute to the off-policy negative log-likelihood loss. Additionally, the student generates 8 rollouts without the document; their tokens are weighted by an advantage computed from the tutor’s log-probabilities (on-policy component). A small KL penalty toward the initial model stabilizes training. The combined loss is optimized with a low learning rate (1e-6) for one epoch.

## Key Results
- In-domain held-out accuracy: +16.0 pp (46.0% → 62.0%).
- Out-of-domain benchmarks (MATH, AMC, Minerva, OlympiadBench): average maj@8 +15.2 pp (20.2% → 35.4%).
- Answer-only SFT catastrophically degrades student accuracy to 10–12%; unfiltered trajectory distillation underperforms GATES.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
GATES achieves stable self-improvement through three key mechanisms:

**Consensus gating** prevents learning from unreliable tutor rollouts. By sampling multiple tutor rollouts per question and requiring at least τ agreement, the method filters out questions where the tutor is uncertain or inconsistent. Only rollouts that match the modal answer and pass document-leakage guardrails contribute to the loss. The core assumption is that agreement under privileged context correlates with correctness. Ablations confirm that removing the gate significantly degrades performance.

**Dense token-level trajectory distillation** transfers reasoning skills. Instead of training on final answers only, GATES imitates the full tutor-generated reasoning trace (tokens). This dense supervision teaches intermediate steps and problem-solving procedures, avoiding the high variance of sparse reward updates. Ablations show that answer-only SFT fails catastrophically, while full trajectories yield large gains.

**Asymmetric input context** creates a meaningful tutor-student capability gap. The tutor and student are the same model, but the tutor has access to the document while the student does not. This asymmetry provides the tutor with privileged information that improves its reliability (tutor accuracy 70.1% vs student 46.0% on held-out), enabling the student to learn from a stronger teacher. This instantiation of Learning Using Privileged Information (LUPI) does not require external correctness labels.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Token-level distillation vs. answer-only training**
  - Why needed: Answer-only SFT catastrophically degrades student accuracy (10–12%) because it trains only on final targets, discarding intermediate reasoning structure. GATES preserves procedural knowledge via full trajectories.
  - Quick check: If you only have final answers (like \boxed{7}) but lack intermediate steps, what key information is missing for teaching document-free reasoning?

- **Concept: Self-consistency as a reliability signal**
  - Why needed: Without ground truth, agreement among multiple samples serves as a proxy for correctness. GATES extends self-consistency from inference-time selection to training-time gating.
  - Quick check: Why might majority agreement among tutor rollouts be a better training signal than a single tutor answer?

- **Concept: On-policy vs. off-policy distillation**
  - Why needed: Off-policy imitation of tutor rollouts provides the main gains; on-policy weighting adds modest improvement. Mixing both without gating can destabilize.
  - Quick check: What is the risk of off-policy distillation when the tutor’s distribution differs significantly from the student’s (prior to training)?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Tutor sampler (generates k rollouts with document) -> Consensus gate (filters questions and trajectories based on agreement and document leakage) -> Distillation module (computes off-policy and on-policy losses). The student sampler (generates rollouts without document) feeds into the Distillation module for the on-policy component. Guardrails are integrated into the Consensus gate.

### Critical path
1. For a batch of (document, question) pairs, the tutor sampler generates k rollouts per question.
2. Extract final answers from each rollout, compute consensus; set question-level gate g_i = 1 if at least τ rollouts agree.
3. For each rollout, set eligibility e_ij = 1 if its answer matches the consensus and it passes document-leakage guardrails; otherwise 0.
4. Accumulate loss only over eligible completion tokens: off-policy NLL on tutor tokens and on-policy advantage-weighted NLL on student tokens.
5. Backpropagate total loss L(θ) = λ_off L_off + λ_on L_on + λ_KL KL(ref).

### Design tradeoffs
- Consensus threshold τ/k: Higher τ increases reliability but reduces the fraction of training questions used; the paper uses τ=4 with k=8.
- Off-policy vs. on-policy weights (λ_off, λ_on): Off-policy dominates (λ_off=1.0); λ_on=0.1 adds stability without overwhelming the primary signal.
- Document-leakage filtering: Strict keyword-based filtering prevents invalid shortcuts but may discard trajectories with incidental document references; trade-off between safety and coverage.

### Failure signatures
- Very low consensus pass rate (<10% of training questions): indicates tutor is too weak or rollout diversity insufficient → increase temperature or improve base model.
- Student accuracy drops below pretrained baseline: likely document leakage or gating too permissive → tighten guardrails or raise τ.
- Tutor accuracy remains much higher than student after training: suggests ungated training (oracle-only trajectories) taught document-dependent shortcuts → ensure consensus gate is active.
- High frequency of invalid answer formatting (missing \boxed{}): adjust prompts or max completion tokens.

### First experiments
1. **Consensus ablation:** Remove the consensus gate (train on all tutor rollouts). Expected: student accuracy drops to ~54% in-domain and ~31% on benchmarks, confirming gate necessity.
2. **Trajectory ablation:** Replace full trajectory distillation with answer-only SFT on the tutor’s modal answer. Expected: catastrophic failure (~10–12% student accuracy), validating need for dense token-level signal.
3. **Threshold sweep:** Vary τ (e.g., 3/8, 4/8, 5/8) while keeping k=8. Higher τ should improve per-token reliability but reduce effective training tokens; measure student accuracy vs. token count to identify optimal operating point.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How should the consensus threshold and number of tutor rollouts be adjusted as model capability increases to optimize the trade-off between reliability and coverage?
- Under what conditions does tutor consensus fail to be a reliable proxy for correctness, and how can GATES be made robust to such failures?
- What evaluation protocols and reliability mechanisms are needed for GATES when using an adaptive, self-generated question distribution in non-stationary training?

## Limitations
- Exact prompt for generating training questions is unspecified; dataset distribution may differ from what was used.
- Document-leakage filtering rules (keywords, matching) are vague, risking inconsistent guardrails across reproductions.
- The method assumes consensus correlates with correctness; if the tutor is systematically biased or low-diversity, high consensus may still be wrong.

## Confidence
- Consensus gating prevents learning from unreliable rollouts: High
- Dense token-level distillation transfers reasoning skills: High
- Asymmetric context creates a meaningful tutor-student gap: High
- Overall GATES improvement (46.0%→62.0% in-domain, 20.2%→35.4% on benchmarks): Medium
