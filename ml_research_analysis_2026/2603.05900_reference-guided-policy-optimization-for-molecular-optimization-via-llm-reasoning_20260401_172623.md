# Reference-guided Policy Optimization for Molecular Optimization via LLM Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.05900
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05900
- **Reference Count:** 40
- **Primary Result:** RePO achieves up to 17.4% improvement in Success Rate × Similarity over GRPO baselines on molecular optimization benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
Instruction-based molecular optimization suffers from a supervision mismatch: training data provides only a single reference molecule per task without intermediate reasoning trajectories. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) collapses multi-step reasoning, while reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) yields conservative edits under similarity constraints. Reference-guided Policy Optimization (RePO) combines reward-driven RL updates on full sampled trajectories with answer-level supervision on the reference molecule. The RL term encourages property-improving exploration, while the reference guidance term—applied only to the final answer—anchors outputs to valid solutions without suppressing reasoning diversity. On TOMG-Bench and MuMOInstruct, RePO consistently outperforms SFT and GRPO, delivering higher success-rate×similarity, better multi-attribute balance, and stronger generalization.

## Method Summary
RePO is an offline RL algorithm built on GRPO. For each query (instruction, input molecule, reference molecule), it samples multiple completions (reasoning + answer SMILES) from the current policy. A scalar reward is computed for each completion based on property improvement (binary) and structural similarity (Tanimoto). The policy is updated via two terms: (1) a standard GRPO clipped surrogate loss applied to all tokens to upweight high-reward completions, and (2) a reference guidance term β·logπθ(m_ref | q, t_i) applied only to the answer tokens, where t_i is the model’s own reasoning prefix. Gradients from the guidance term are masked for reasoning tokens to preserve reasoning diversity. A KL regularizer prevents deviation from a reference policy. This hybrid approach leverages exploration from RL while anchoring final answers to high-quality references.

## Key Results
- RePO significantly outperforms SFT and GRPO baselines on TOMG-Bench and MuMOInstruct, with up to 17.4% higher Success Rate × Similarity.
- It better balances competing objectives in multi-attribute tasks, achieving higher property improvements without sacrificing similarity.
- RePO generalizes to unseen instruction styles and shows favorable inference-scaling trends, remaining effective across model sizes (3B to 8B parameters) and architectures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Reward-Driven RL on Full Trajectories
- **Claim:** RePO uses RL on full sampled trajectories to encourage property-improving exploration.
- **Mechanism:** For each instruction, the policy generates a complete response (reasoning + molecule). A verifiable reward (property improvement + similarity) is computed. Using group-relative advantages (GRPO-style), a clipped policy gradient update is applied to all tokens, upweighting high-reward completions.
- **Core Assumption:** The policy can generate diverse trajectories, some yielding property-improving molecules, providing a learning signal.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract states "reward-driven RL updates on full sampled trajectories"; Section 4 describes training with verifiable rewards in an RL manner.
- **Break Condition:** If reward distribution is extremely sparse (few high-reward completions), RL updates become uninformative and exploration may stall.

### Mechanism 2: Answer-Level Reference Guidance
- **Claim:** RePO applies reference guidance only to final answer tokens to anchor outputs to valid solutions without suppressing reasoning diversity.
- **Mechanism:** For each sampled trajectory, compute β·logπθ(m_ref | q, t_i). Gradients are masked so only answer tokens are affected; reasoning tokens are frozen.
- **Core Assumption:** The reference molecule is a high-quality solution; conditioning on the model’s own reasoning prefixes preserves diverse reasoning.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract: "applied only to the final answer, not the reasoning"; Section 4: "guidance term increases likelihood of reference molecule conditioned on reasoning prefix"; Fig. 7 ablation shows masking improves performance.
- **Break Condition:** Low-quality references mislead; overly strong β collapses to answer imitation; too weak β yields insufficient guidance.

### Mechanism 3: Gradient Masking for Reasoning Diversity
- **Claim:** Gradient masking isolates reference guidance to answer tokens, preventing interference with reasoning token distribution.
- **Mechanism:** During backpropagation, gradients from the reference guidance term are zeroed for reasoning tokens; only answer tokens receive gradients.
- **Core Assumption:** The model’s self-generated reasoning prefixes are valid intermediates; forcing a specific reasoning pattern is undesirable.
- **Evidence Anchors:** Abstract: "applied only to the final answer, not the reasoning"; Section 4: "RePO does not imitate reasoning tokens"; Fig. 7 ablation.
- **Break Condition:** Without masking, reasoning diversity collapses and outputs become answer-only.

## Foundational Learning
### Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)
- **Why needed:** SFT is a baseline that fails by collapsing reasoning, highlighting the supervision mismatch in molecular optimization.
- **Quick Check:** What symptom appears in model outputs when SFT is applied with only final-answer supervision? (Answer: Outputs become short, answer-only, with missing or formulaic reasoning.)

### Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)
- **Why needed:** RLVR (e.g., GRPO) explores via rewards but yields conservative edits under similarity constraints; understanding its limitations motivates RePO's hybrid design.
- **Quick Check:** Why does GRPO without SFT initialization tend to produce conservative molecular edits (high similarity, low property gain)? (Answer: The reward landscape is sparse; without a good starting policy, the algorithm favors small, safe edits that preserve similarity to avoid penalties.)

### Molecular Similarity and Reward Design
- **Why needed:** The objective balances property improvement and structural similarity; grasping Tanimoto similarity and reward composition is key to interpreting results.
- **Quick Check:** How is structural similarity quantified in RePO, and why is it a hard constraint in molecular optimization? (Answer: Tanimoto coefficient on ECFP4 fingerprints; similarity ensures generated molecules remain chemically similar to the input, maintaining drug-like properties and synthesizability.)

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Tokenizer -> LLM Policy -> Parser -> Reward Calculator -> RL Loss Module + Reference Guidance Module -> KL Regularizer -> Optimizer

### Critical Path
1. For batch of (query q, reference m_ref): sample G completions {o_i} from current policy π_old, each o_i = [t_i; ˆm_i].
2. Parse each ˆm_i and compute reward r_i = r_prop(ˆm_i, m0) + r_struct(ˆm_i, m0).
3. Compute token-level advantages: Â_i,k = (r_i − mean({r_j}))/std({r_j}) for all tokens in o_i.
4. Compute RL loss: apply GRPO clipped surrogate objective to all tokens in all o_i, average.
5. Identify answer token span in each completion; compute guidance loss = −β · logπθ(m_ref_tokens | q, t_i) for those tokens only.
6. Mask gradients from guidance loss for reasoning tokens (set to zero).
7. Compute KL loss: per-token KL divergence between π_θ and reference policy π_ref, scaled by γ.
8. Total loss L = L_RL + (β term) + γ · L_KL; backpropagate and update π_θ with Adam.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **β (guidance weight):** Higher values anchor more strongly but may reduce exploration; paper finds β=10 effective, with saturation beyond.
- **Gradient masking:** Critical; without it, reasoning diversity collapses and performance degrades (Sec. 5.3).
- **Reward design:** Binary property reward more stable than continuous; similarity term balances exploration and constraint.
- **Group size G:** Larger G improves advantage estimates but increases sampling cost.
- **Reference quality:** Some robustness to corruption, but poor references degrade performance.

### Failure Signatures
- **Reasoning collapse:** Outputs become short or answer-only → likely due to missing masking or β too high.
- **Low success rate + high similarity:** Resembles GRPO behavior → insufficient exploration (β too low or reward shaping inadequate).
- **High invalid SMILES rate:** Policy generating chemically invalid strings → consider adding validity penalty to reward.
- **Overfitting to references:** Model replicates reference even when it violates constraints → β too high.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Supervision mismatch replication:** Train SFT, GRPO (base), GRPO (SFT init) on a single task (e.g., QED). Measure output length, SR, Sim. Expect SFT collapse, GRPO(SFT init) short outputs, GRPO conservative.
2. **Main evaluation:** Train RePO on TOMG-Bench/MuMOInstruct; compare SR×Sim with baselines. Should show consistent improvement.
3. **Gradient masking ablation:** Train RePO with no masking vs. full masking; evaluate SR×Sim and training reward curves. Expect masked version superior; no-mask may underperform or diverge.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How to automatically generate high-quality reference molecules for molecular optimization when demonstrations are unavailable?
- **Basis in paper:** Limitations note "relies on the availability of demonstrations, which may be scarce" (Appendix E); Conclusion states future work on "improving automatic reference construction".
- **Why unresolved:** RePO’s answer-level guidance depends on reference molecules; without auto-constructed ones, the method cannot be applied to new properties or targets lacking human-curated examples.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A system that generates chemically valid and objective-aligned references (e.g., via retrieval or generative models), with RePO using such references matching performance of human-provided references across several optimization tasks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Does RePO generalize to other scientific optimization domains beyond molecular design (e.g., materials, proteins)?
- **Basis in paper:** Conclusion proposes "extending to broader scientific optimization domains" as future work; evaluation is limited to molecular benchmarks.
- **Why unresolved:** Different domains feature distinct search spaces, reward functions, and feasibility of reference provision; transferability remains unproven.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Apply RePO to at least two non-molecular optimization problems, demonstrate competitive or superior results compared to domain-specific baselines, and analyze whether hyperparameters (e.g., β) transfer without extensive tuning.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can we improve the interpretability of RePO’s reasoning to build trust with domain experts?
- **Basis in paper:** Limitations state the "black-box nature of LLM still presents challenges for domain experts seeking to understand the precise reasoning behind specific structural modifications" (Appendix E).
- **Why unresolved:** In high-stakes applications like drug discovery, experts need to understand why a model suggests a certain edit to assess risks; lack of transparency hinders adoption.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Integrate mechanisms such as attention visualization, feature attribution, or generated natural language explanations into RePO; conduct user studies with chemists showing these tools increase confidence in or utility of the suggested molecular edits.

## Limitations
- **Reward design ambiguity:** The exact composition of multi-objective rewards (e.g., for MuMOInstruct’s five properties) and the precise thresholding for binary improvement are unspecified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- **Implementation details underspecified:** The KL regularizer’s K3 estimator formula and the SMILES parsing pipeline (handling multiple answers, validation heuristics) lack full description, affecting reported metrics and training stability.
