# How Retrieved Context Shapes Internal Representations in RAG

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.20091
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20091
- **Reference count:** 28
- **Primary result:** Random documents cause large representation drift and high abstention (>60% in instruction-tuned models), while relevant documents minimally shift representations but increase confidence; a single relevant document stabilizes multi-document representations.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how retrieved context influences the internal representations of large language models (LLMs) in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). Through controlled experiments that isolate documents as relevant, distracting, or random across four QA datasets and three LLMs, the authors show that random contexts induce substantial drift in hidden states and trigger abstention, particularly in instruction-tuned models. In contrast, relevant documents align with the model’s existing knowledge, producing minimal representation shifts but higher output confidence. In multi-document scenarios, a single relevant document anchors the representation, rendering the output robust to additional noise. Layer-wise analyses reveal that early layers quickly flag random contexts, while later layers increasingly favor parametric knowledge, diminishing retrieval influence especially on hard queries. Linear probes and PCA visualizations provide mechanistic explanations for phenomena like abstention and confirmation bias in RAG.

## Method Summary
The study uses Trivia QA, Natural Questions, Pop QA, and Strategy QA, together with Gemma3-27B, Llama4-17B, and Qwen3-Next-80B (base and instruction-tuned variants). For each query, the Contriever retriever (indexed on MassiveDS) fetches the top 20 documents, which are then classified by GPT-5 into relevant, distracting, or random (neutral). Controlled prompts are built with either a single document (one of the three types) or four documents (1 relevant plus 3 distracting or 3 random), with document order shuffled. The LLM processes each prompt; the last-prompt-token hidden state is extracted from every layer. Analyses include cosine similarity between representations with and without context, PCA for visualizing representation clusters, and linear probes (trained on PCA-reduced, L2-normalized features) to quantify separability of context types. Output behavior is evaluated via LLM-as-judge for correctness, abstention, and log-likelihood of the generated answer. Exact prompt templates and decoding parameters are not fully disclosed.

## Key Results
- Random documents produce large representation drift (low cosine similarity to the no-document state) and trigger high abstention (>60% in instruction-tuned models vs. <20% in base models).
- Relevant documents minimally shift internal representations but significantly increase response confidence (higher log-likelihood, p<0.001).
- In multi-document settings, a single relevant document anchors the representation, stabilizing it despite distracting or random noise.
- Layer-wise analysis shows random contexts become distinguishable by early layers (L2–3), whereas separation between relevant and distracting emerges only in later layers; later layers increasingly favor parametric knowledge, reducing retrieval impact on hard queries.
- Linear probes confirm separability metrics across layers, providing a mechanistic account of abstention and confirmation bias in RAG.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Random documents induce large representation drift and trigger abstention
Random (semantically dissimilar) documents cause a substantial shift in the model’s internal state, as measured by low cosine similarity to the query-only representation. The model’s early layers (L2–3) detect a coarse semantic mismatch between the query and the input context, signaling a lack of useful information; this pushes the internal state toward uncertainty. In instruction-tuned models, this signal is amplified into explicit abstention behavior, leading to refusal rates above 60%. Base models, lacking built-in abstention tendencies, show much lower refusal (<20%). The effect persists only if the document is truly random; misclassification or use of a base model breaks the pattern.

### Mechanism 2: Relevant documents reinforce existing knowledge without significant representation shift
When a relevant document is provided, the resulting internal representation remains close to the query-only state, indicating minimal drift. However, the response confidence (log-likelihood) increases significantly. The mechanism is that the information in the relevant document aligns with the model’s pre-existing parametric representations for the query, effectively reinforcing the correct answer path rather than introducing a novel signal. This reinforcement is most effective for “easy” queries where the model already possesses the answer; for “hard” queries where parametric knowledge is absent or incorrect, relevant documents fail to alter representations or improve outcomes, highlighting a limitation in evidence integration.

### Mechanism 3: A single relevant document anchors multi-document representations; later layers downweight retrieval
In multi-document inputs containing noise, the presence of even one relevant document stabilizes the combined representation, keeping it close to the representation obtained with a relevant-only context. The attention mechanism selectively weights the high-quality signal. Concurrently, transformer layers progressively blend the retrieved context with the model’s own knowledge: deeper layers show a convergence between no-document and relevant-document representations, indicating that parametric knowledge dominates in later processing. This reduces the influence of retrieval, especially on hard queries. If no relevant document is present or if its signal is too weak, the noise may dominate.

## Foundational Learning
- **Representation Drift / Cosine Similarity in Latent Space:** Measures how hidden states change when context is added. Cosine similarity between the query-only and with-context final-layer token representation quantifies drift; lower similarity indicates the model perceives the context as less useful. *Quick check:* If a random document causes a small cosine similarity, what does that suggest about the model’s internal perception of that document’s usefulness?
- **Layer-wise Processing in Transformer Decoders:** Early layers capture coarse, semantic-level features, while later layers refine and integrate with parametric knowledge. In this paper, early layers distinguish random from other contexts, whereas relevant vs. distracting separation only emerges later. *Quick check:* In which layers would you expect to see the largest separation between representations of prompts with “relevant” vs. “distracting” documents?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
RAG System → Retriever (Contriever + reranker) → Retrieved Document Set (S_q) → LLM Prompt Formatter → LLM (Decoder-only Transformer) → Output Generation.  
Analysis components: Hidden State Extractor (captures h_q,Sq at last prompt token per layer), Linear Probe Classifier, PCA Dimensionality Reduction.

**Critical path:** The pipeline begins with a query, which is sent to the retriever to obtain a set of candidate documents. Documents are categorized as relevant, distracting, or random via an LLM judge. Controlled prompts are constructed (single or multi-document) and fed to the target LLM. During the forward pass, hidden states at the last prompt token are saved for all transformer layers. These representations are then analyzed: PCA visualizes clusters; linear probes train classifiers to separate context types; cosine similarity compares with/without context; and output metrics (correctness, abstention, log-likelihood) are correlated with internal states.

**Design tradeoffs:** The study favors depth of mechanistic insight over breadth of scenarios: it examines only four QA datasets and three specific LLMs, uses a fixed prompt structure, and focuses on the final prompt token rather than the entire generated sequence. Scalability is achieved by employing an LLM-as-judge for document classification and response evaluation, trading potential label noise for human annotation cost.

**Failure signatures:**
- Excessive abstention (>60%) on easy queries when irrelevant context is present, indicating over-suppression of parametric knowledge.
- Hard queries show minimal accuracy improvement even with relevant documents, signaling a failure to effectively integrate external evidence.
- In multi-document settings, if the relevant document’s signal is weak or its position is suboptimal, noisy representations may dominate.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Replicate Single-Document PCA: Generate prompts with query-only and one document (relevant, distracting, random) for a single dataset/model; extract final-layer last-token representations; apply PCA; verify that random documents cluster far from no-doc while relevant documents remain close.
2. Measure Abstention vs. Similarity: Compute cosine similarity between with-context and query-only representations; bin queries by similarity; calculate abstention rate per bin; expect strong positive correlation (higher drift → higher abstain), especially for instruction-tuned models.
3. Layer-wise Linear Probe: For relevant vs. random classification, train a linear probe on representations from early (e.g., layer 2–3) and late (e.g., second-to-last) layers; verify that probe accuracy and decision boundary distance are high for early layers (easy separation) and lower for late layers (representations converge).

## Limitations
- Reproduction relies on unspecified details (exact Contriever/MassiveDS preprocessing, prompt templates, decoding parameters), risking implementation mismatch.
- Findings are limited to four QA datasets and three specific LLMs; their generalizability to other tasks or model architectures remains unknown.
- Use of an LLM-as-judge for document relevance and response evaluation may introduce label noise and circularity.
