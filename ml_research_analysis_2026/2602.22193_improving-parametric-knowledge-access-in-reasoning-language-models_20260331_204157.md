# Improving Parametric Knowledge Access in Reasoning Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.22193
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22193
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Reinforcement learning with answer-correctness reward on TriviaQA substantially improves closed-book QA recall (+27.1% EM) and transfers to other datasets, while "think step-by-step" prompting helps recall but harms math accuracy.

## Executive Summary
Reasoning language models trained via reinforcement learning on math and coding tasks may not optimally access their parametric knowledge for factual question answering. The authors discover that prompting with "think step-by-step" improves closed-book QA recall but degrades math performance, indicating the base policy is under-optimized for knowledge retrieval. To address this, they train models using reinforcement learning with a reward based on answer correctness on TriviaQA. This yields large gains on TriviaQA (+27.1% exact match, +9.9% excerpt recall) and transfers to multiple QA datasets, though reasoning traces become longer with limited qualitative improvement. The work demonstrates that models can be effectively trained to reason better for parametric knowledge access via outcome-only reinforcement learning.

## Method Summary
The study uses GPT-OSS-20B, a 20B parameter model pre-trained on math/coding via RL. First, they append "think step-by-step" to prompts and evaluate on TriviaQA, Natural Questions, and MATH, finding consistent QA recall gains but math degradation. This suggests the base policy lacks a trigger for factual retrieval. They then implement reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards: for each question, the model generates a reasoning trace and answer, receiving a reward of 1.0 for exact match, 0.5 for substring match, plus a small format bonus. Training uses a GRPO-style algorithm with LoRA adapters (rank 32, group size 8, KL penalty 0.01) on TriviaQA for 1240 steps. Baselines include supervised fine-tuning on a static set of correct reasoning traces (Reasoning-SFT) and SFT on answers only. Evaluation on TriviaQA, Natural Questions, HotpotQA, SimpleQA, and StrategyQA uses exact match and excerpt recall, with answer extraction assisted by an auxiliary LLM.

## Key Results
- RL training on TriviaQA yields +27.1% EM and +9.9% Ex-Recall on TriviaQA, with transfer gains to Natural Questions (+4.2% Ex-Recall), HotpotQA (+2.1% Ex-Recall), SimpleQA (+0.6% Ex-Recall), and StrategyQA (+3.0% EM).
- "Think step-by-step" prompting improves closed-book QA recall across models (+0.8% avg Ex-Recall) but degrades math accuracy, indicating base policy under-optimization for factual recall.
- RL outperforms supervised fine-tuning on correct reasoning traces; reasoning traces become longer but show limited qualitative improvement.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
Claim: The explicit "think step-by-step" cue activates latent reasoning pathways for parametric knowledge recall not engaged by default.  
Mechanism: The cue forces generation of intermediate reasoning tokens, which may function like spreading activation—triggering related factual associations in parameters before answer synthesis. The base policy, optimized for math/coding via RL, lacks a learned trigger to spontaneously initiate this process for factual QA.  
Evidence: Cue improves QA recall but hurts math [abstract]; Table 1 shows consistent +Ex-Recall across four models on TriviaQA/NQ while MATH accuracy degrades [section 3.5]; neighbor papers on script barriers support modality-specific knowledge access.  
Break condition: If base policy already generated sufficiently associative traces, the cue would provide no benefit; math degradation indicates the cue's pattern conflicts with specialized deductive reasoning.

### Mechanism 2
Claim: Reinforcement learning with verifiable reward directly optimizes the policy to generate reasoning traces that better elicit parametric knowledge, rather than imitating human-like reasoning.  
Mechanism: The RL objective updates the policy to maximize probability of sequences yielding high reward (answer correctness). This teaches the model to adjust its intermediate trace `c` to whatever internal pattern maximizes correct answer generation, even if `c` is not human-interpretable. On-policy exploration during training is critical.  
Evidence: RL outperforms Reasoning-SFT [abstract]; Table 2 shows large improvements across transfer datasets [section 4.4]; prior work on dynamic knowledge encoding implies internal patterns must filter irrelevant activations.  
Break condition: If initial trace distribution has negligible overlap with optimal retrieval patterns, or reward is too sparse, RL may fail. Modest qualitative trace change suggests optimized patterns are subtle.

### Mechanism 3
Claim: RL primarily improves answer calibration (better guessing/selection from parametric activations) rather than qualitatively richer, multi-step deductive reasoning in the trace.  
Mechanism: Reward only on final answer incentivizes traces that quickly converge on correct answers or pad with generic text; longer traces are a side effect, not the goal. "Good" reasoning is defined instrumentally by answer correctness, unlike mathematics where step validity is independently checkable.  
Evidence: Traces are longer but not qualitatively better [section 4.4]; trained model often presents correct answer earlier in trace rather than deeper reasoning [section 4.4]; prior work on implicit CoT shows intermediate representations optimized for accuracy without natural language fidelity.  
Break condition: Augmenting reward with trace-based constraints (e.g., minimum length) could alter patterns, but current evidence suggests such constraints are unnecessary for gains observed.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**  
  Why needed: Core method uses RL with reward from answer correctness. Understanding policy gradient, on-policy sampling (GRPO), and exploration/exploitation in language generation explains why RL improves over SFT and how training affects trace generation.  
  Quick check: Why might on-policy RL (updating policy while generating new traces) outperform SFT on a static set of correct reasoning traces in this context?

- **Concept: Parametric Knowledge vs. In-Context Knowledge**  
  Why needed: Paper studies accessing knowledge stored in model parameters (closed-book QA), distinct from retrieving it from provided context (open-book). This distinction is fundamental to problem setup and result interpretation (e.g., transfer implies internal knowledge access, not retrieval ability).  
  Quick check: If RL on TriviaQA improves Natural Questions, does this prove better parametric recall, or could it reflect improved general reasoning skill?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Base Model (pθ) generates reasoning trace c and answer y → Reward Function computes r(ˆy, y) based on exact match (1.0), substring recall (0.5), and format bonus → RL Algorithm (GRPO-style with LoRA) updates pθ using group-relative advantages → on-policy sampling continues with new traces.

**Critical path:**  
1. Start with base reasoning model pre-trained on math/coding via RL.  
2. Discovery: Add "think step-by-step" cue → improved QA recall but degraded math accuracy → base policy under-optimized for factual recall.  
3. Intervention: Design RLVR on TriviaQA with answer-correctness reward.  
4. Training: Online RL with LoRA (group size 8, 32 groups/batch, lr 2e-5, KL penalty 0.01, max seq len 1028) for 1240 steps.  
5. Validation: Select checkpoint based on TriviaQA test performance (step 1240).  
6. Transfer: Evaluate on Natural Questions, HotpotQA, SimpleQA, StrategyQA; compare to Reasoning-SFT and SFT baselines.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Reward shaping: Using only answer correctness maximizes target metric but yields traces with minimal qualitative improvement; adding trace-based rewards could introduce gaming or reduce gains.  
- On-policy vs. off-policy: GRPO with KL penalty (0.01) prevents too-rapid departure from initial policy, likely necessary if initial trace distribution supports exploration.  
- Dataset choice: TriviaQA's broad trivia coverage enables transfer; a specialized dataset might yield narrower improvements.

**Failure signatures:**  
- No transfer to other QA datasets → learned pattern is dataset-specific or reward not promoting generalizable strategies.  
- Degradation on MATH → catastrophic interference with existing math reasoning policy.  
- Trace collapse (average length decreases) → model learned to guess directly without reasoning.  
- Reasoning-SFT parity → on-policy adaptation not critical; static traces suffice.

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Cue Impact Baseline: Compare base (no-cue) vs. +Cue on TriviaQA/NQ/MATH; confirm +Ex-Recall on QA and neutral/negative on MATH using same decoding and significance testing.  
2. RL vs. SFT A/B Test: Train RL and Reasoning-SFT from same base (GPT-OSS-20B) with matched compute/LoRA; evaluate on no-cue prompts; RL should show superior EM/Ex-Recall on TriviaQA and transfer sets.  
3. Cross-Dataset Transfer & Ablation: After RL on TriviaQA, evaluate on NQ, HotpotQA, SimpleQA, StrategyQA; ablate reward's recall component (use only EM) to test sensitivity to reward granularity and check if SimpleQA shows smallest gain.

## Limitations
- Model weights for GPT-OSS-20B are not publicly identified, hindering faithful reproduction.
- TriviaQA train/validation split and random seeds are unspecified, introducing variance in results.
- Transfer gains are modest (e.g., +0.6% on SimpleQA), suggesting limited generalization.
- Qualitative analysis of traces is anecdotal; mechanistic understanding of how RL improves recall remains weak.
- Possible interference with math/coding accuracy not thoroughly evaluated; "under-optimization" claim may not generalize across domains.

## Confidence
- RL yields large EM/Ex-Recall gains on TriviaQA: High  
- Transfer improvements to NQ/HotpotQA/StrategyQA: Medium  
- RL optimizes answer calibration over explicit reasoning: Medium  
- Base policy under-optimized for parametric knowledge: Medium  
- Reasoning-SFT underperforms RL: High  

## Next Checks
1. Reimplement GRPO from scratch (without Tinker) and train on TriviaQA; confirm gains (+~27% EM) replicate within ±2% variance.
2. Ablate KL penalty (range 0.001–0.1) and group size (4–16) in RL to identify stable hyperparameters and ensure reported numbers are not cherry-picked.
3. Evaluate RL model on out-of-distribution math/coding benchmarks (MATH, HumanEval) to quantify any trade-off and test whether "under-optimization" extends to other domains.
