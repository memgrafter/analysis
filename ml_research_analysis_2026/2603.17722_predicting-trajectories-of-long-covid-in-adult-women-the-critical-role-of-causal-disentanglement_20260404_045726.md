# Predicting Trajectories of Long COVID in Adult Women: The Critical Role of Causal Disentanglement

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.17722
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17722
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** A causal-disentangled LLM framework achieved 86.7% precision in predicting clinical Long COVID severity in adult women by suppressing confounding signals like menopause.

## Executive Summary
Predicting Long COVID (PASC) severity in women is complicated by significant symptom overlap with confounders such as menopause and diabetes. This study introduced a novel causal-disentangled framework that integrates static clinical profiles with four weeks of wearable cardiac and sleep data. By explicitly separating causal PASC signals from confounding noise within an LLM's latent space, the model achieved high predictive precision (86.7%) on a cohort of 1,155 women. Saliency analysis confirmed the model focused on direct pathological indicators (e.g., breathlessness) while successfully downweighting known confounders.

## Method Summary
The framework encodes structured clinical data and wearable aggregates into a narrative processed by a small LLM (Qwen-2.5-0.5B). A disentanglement attention layer uses learned gates to split the final latent representation into a "causal" vector (`xo`) and a "confounder" vector (`xc`). An environment mixing module synthetically combines `xo` from one patient with `xc` from another to force the causal prediction head to be invariant to confounding background. A contrastive `Lmix` loss ensures mixed representations remain semantically aligned with their original causal source. Three regression heads predict severity from `xo`, `xc`, and the mixed representation, with the primary loss supervising the causal head.

## Key Results
- The model predicted clinical PASC severity with **86.7% precision** on the held-out test set (n=1,155 women).
- **Saliency analysis** showed maximum focus on direct symptoms (e.g., breathlessness, saliency=1.00) and strong suppression of key confounders (menopause, diabetes; saliency<0.27).
- The causal network **outperformed XGBoost** in precision but exhibited higher regression variance, illustrating a trade-off between interpretability and stability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Disentanglement Attention Layer explicitly partitions latent representations into causal features (direct PASC pathology) and confounder features (baseline noise like menopause), allowing the model to focus on invariant disease signals.
- **Mechanism:** A learned MLP projects the final LLM hidden state `h` into two softmax gates `[αc, αo]`. These scalar weights multiply `h` to produce separate vectors: `xo = αo · h` (causal) and `xc = αc · h` (confounding). The regression heads are supervised only on `xo` for the primary prediction.
- **Core assumption:** Pathological signals and confounding signals occupy separable subspaces in the LLM’s latent space, and a linear projection (via MLP) can approximate this separation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "explicitly separating causal PASC signals from confounding noise via a disentanglement attention layer."
  - [section, Page 4]: "We introduce a Disentanglement Attention Layer that learns to bifactorize this representation: [αc, αo] = Softmax(MLP(h))."
  - [corpus]: Neighboring papers use attention for temporal fusion but not for explicit causal/confounder bifurcation; this architectural novelty is not directly mirrored.
- **Break condition:** If causal and confounding features are non-linearly entangled or share dominant dimensions, the linear gating may fail to cleanly separate them, leading to suppressed causal signal or residual confounding in `xo`.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Environment Mixing forces the causal regression head to be invariant to a patient’s confounding background by training on synthetic mixtures of causal and confounder features from different patients.
- **Mechanism:** During training, a mixed representation `xmix` is created by combining the causal features `xo,i` of patient *i* with the confounder features `xc,j` of a different patient *j* from the same batch. The joint prediction head `fco(xmix)` is supervised, but the causal head `fo(xo,i)` must still predict correctly regardless of the attached `xc,j`.
- **Core assumption:** The true PASC severity is a function *only* of the causal features `xo`; confounding features `xc` are independent of the outcome given `xo`. Mixing breaks spurious correlations between `xc` and the label.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: Implied in "causal network" and "suppressing confounders"; explicit in "environment mixing."
  - [section, Page 4]: "we generate a synthetic 'counterfactual' representation xmix by combining the causal features of patient i with the confounding features of a randomly sampled patient j... This forces the regression head to predict the PASC score solely from the causal signal xo,i."
  - [corpus]: Corpus contains active learning and spatiotemporal attention papers but none describe feature-space mixing for confounder invariance; this appears to be a novel regularization strategy.
- **Break condition:** If confounders are not independent of causal features (i.e., they interact causally with PASC), mixing may create unrealistic synthetic examples that degrade learning or if the mixing operation (e.g., addition) fails to preserve semantic coherence of `xo`.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The InfoNCE-based Mix Loss (`Lmix`) mathematically enforces that the mixed representation `hmix` remains semantically aligned with the original patient’s graph, ensuring the causal features extracted are stable and informative even after environment mixing.
- **Mechanism:** `Lmix` is a contrastive loss that treats the original patient representation `hi` as the positive example and other batch representations `hn` as negatives. It maximizes similarity between `hmix,i,j` (mixed sample) and `hi`, encouraging the model to preserve the core causal information in `xo` through the mixing process.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic consistency (as measured by cosine similarity in the LLM’s latent space) between a mixed sample and its original source indicates that causal features were preserved; deviation implies loss of causal signal during mixing.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: Not explicitly named, but implied in "causal attribution analysis" and "clinically 'honest' representation."
  - [section, Page 4-5]: Equation (3) defines `Lmix` using InfoNCE to "ensure that the causal information remains the dominant signal."
  - [corpus]: Several papers use contrastive learning (e.g., for symptom extraction) but not specifically for enforcing invariance in a disentangled causal setup; this application is specialized.
- **Break condition:** If the LLM’s latent space is not semantically smooth (small perturbations cause large jumps), the contrastive objective may fail to correlate `hmix` with `hi`, or if `xo` is already weak, `hmix` may align with `hi` via confounder overlap rather than true causal content.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Causal Disentanglement & Confounding**
  - **Why needed here:** The paper’s core challenge is distinguishing PASC symptoms from mimics (menopause, diabetes). Without understanding how confounders bias prediction, the disentanglement architecture lacks a theoretical foundation.
  - **Quick check question:** If a feature (e.g., sleep disturbance) is correlated with both PASC and menopause, what does it mean for a model to "suppress" it as a confounder, and under what assumptions is that valid?

- **Concept: InfoNCE Contrastive Loss**
  - **Why needed here:** `Lmix` relies on InfoNCE to enforce that mixed representations stay semantically close to their causal source. Understanding noise-contrastive estimation is key to grasping how the model learns stable causal features.
  - **Quick check question:** In the InfoNCE loss used here, what is the role of the batch negatives, and why is cosine similarity chosen as the similarity metric?

- **Concept: LLMs for Structured Clinical Integration**
  - **Why needed here:** The framework encodes structured clinical profiles and wearable aggregates into a "semantically structured narrative" for the LLM. This requires understanding how to represent non-text data (wearable means, clinical codes) in a token space the LLM can process.
  - **Quick check question:** How might the choice of LLM (Qwen-2.5-0.5B) and its tokenizer impact the encoding of numerical wearable features (e.g., mean heart rate variability), and what risks does this introduce?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input Encoder -> LLM Backbone (Qwen-2.5-0.5B) -> Disentanglement Attention Layer -> {Causal Head (on xo), Confounder Head (on xc), Joint Head (on xmix via Environment Mixing)}
- **Critical path:** Raw data → Input Encoder → LLM → Disentanglement Layer → `xo` → Causal Head (`fo`) → Primary Prediction.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - The causal network achieves higher precision (0.836) but worse regression stability (higher MSE/σ) than XGBoost. The disentanglement objective may sacrifice some raw predictive power for clinical interpretability.
  - Using a small LLM (0.5B parameters) limits capacity but reduces overfitting risk and computational cost; however, it may underfit complex interactions between wearables and clinical history.
  - Max Saliency from the attention gate is used as a proxy for causal importance. This assumes the gating mechanism’s weights are well-calibrated and directly reflect feature relevance, which may not hold if the MLP gate is poorly trained.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High variance across random seeds in the causal network’s metrics (Accuracy σ=0.044, Precision σ=0.116) indicates sensitivity to initialization; monitor training loss curves for divergence.
  - Confounder saliency scores not suppressed (e.g., menopause >0.5) suggest the disentanglement layer failed; inspect `αc` distribution—should be low for known confounders.
  - Causal head performance (`fo`) drops significantly relative to joint head (`fco`) after environment mixing is introduced, indicating `xo` may not contain sufficient signal.
  - InfoNCE loss (`Lmix`) converges to a high value (poor alignment) means mixing destroys semantic content; check if `xmix` construction is valid.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Saliency Replication & Ablation:** Train the model with synthetic tokens inserted (e.g., random words as "administrative" fillers). Verify their saliency stays <0.2. Then, ablate the Disentanglement Layer (use only `h` for regression) and confirm confounder tokens (menopause) gain high saliency, degrading precision.
  2. **Confounding Stress Test:** Create a dataset variant where a strong confounder (e.g., age) is artificially correlated with the PASC label (by label permutation). The causal network’s precision should remain stable (if it suppresses age), while XGBoost’s performance should degrade.
  3. **Mixing Robustness:** Withhold a batch of patients during training. For these, compute `fo(xo)` vs. `fco(xmix)` where `xmix` uses a confounder from a *different* age/race strata. If causal invariance holds, predictions should be similar; large discrepancies indicate the model relies on environmental correlations in `xc`.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** Can the precision-regression trade-off observed between XGBoost (stable, lower precision) and the Causal Network (higher precision, unstable regression) be reconciled through adjustments to the disentanglement loss or model hybridization?
- **Open Question 2:** Does the model’s ability to suppress confounders like menopause generalize to predicting PASC severity in men, younger women, or diverse racial/ethnic groups beyond the studied older, predominantly menopausal female cohort?
- **Open Question 3:** Does the model’s saliency-based suppression of menopause and diabetes translate to tangible clinical improvements, such as reduced over-diagnosis of PASC or more accurate differentiation from menopausal symptoms in practice?
- **Open Question 4:** Does the use of mean-aggregated four-week wearable data obscure critical temporal dynamics (e.g., symptom fluctuations, acute relapse patterns) that are essential for accurate long-term PASC trajectory prediction?

## Limitations
- The mixing strategy assumes confounders (e.g., menopause) are conditionally independent of PASC given causal features; if they causally influence PASC (e.g., via sleep disruption), mixing may discard valid signals.
- Using Qwen-2.5-0.5B may limit modeling of nonlinear interactions between clinical history and wearable dynamics, risking underfitting.
- Reliance on MLP gate weights as feature importance proxies assumes well-calibrated training; no direct validation that low saliency truly means "suppressed confounder."

## Confidence
- **Primary precision claim (86.7%):** High – directly reported on internal cohort (n=1,155).
- **Mechanism efficacy (confounder suppression):** Medium – inferred from saliency thresholds; lacks causal ground truth.
- **Generalizability to broader populations:** Low – study limited to adult women; no external or longitudinal validation.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation of Disentanglement Layer:** Retrain without the gating mechanism; confirm confounder saliency increases and precision drops significantly.
2. **Artificial Confounder Stress Test:** Permute labels to artificially correlate a strong confounder (e.g., age) with severity; verify causal network maintains stable precision while XGBoost degrades.
3. **Cross-Strata Mixing Robustness:** For a held-out subset, compute predictions from `fo(xo)` vs. `fco(xmix)` where `xmix` combines `xo` with `xc` from a demographically distinct patient group; large prediction differences would indicate residual confounding in `xo`.
