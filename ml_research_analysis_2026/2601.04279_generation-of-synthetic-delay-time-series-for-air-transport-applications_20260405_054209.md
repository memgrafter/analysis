# Generation of synthetic delay time series for air transport applications

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2601.04279
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04279
*   **Reference Count:** 5
*   **Primary Result:** A simplified Genetic Algorithm method for generating synthetic airport delay time series significantly outperforms state-of-the-art deep learning models (TimeVAE, TimeGAN), producing data nearly indistinguishable from real records while correctly eliminating spurious delay propagation.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of scarce and privacy-sensitive airport delay data by generating realistic synthetic hourly delay time series. It benchmarks two deep learning generative models (TimeVAE, TimeGAN) against a novel, simplified Genetic Algorithm (GA) approach. The GA method conditions sampling on decile distributions of previous delays and iteratively refines outputs with a ResNet. It demonstrably surpasses the deep learning alternatives in statistical fidelity and indistinguishability metrics across 30 European and US airports. Crucially, validation via Granger causality tests confirms the synthetic data preserves true airport-specific patterns but lacks the artificial delay propagation signals present in the real data. The authors publicly release the generated datasets to support reproducible air transport research.

## Method Summary
The core method is a Genetic Algorithm (GA) that synthesizes hourly delay time series. It first models the empirical decile distribution of delays conditioned on the previous hour's value. The GA samples initial sequences from these conditional distributions. A key step is an iterative refinement process using a ResNet model, trained to predict the next hour's delay, which corrects the initially sampled sequences to better match the real data's temporal dynamics. This pipeline is contrasted with two variational autoencoder (TimeVAE) and generative adversarial network (TimeGAN) based deep learning approaches, all trained and evaluated on the same real delay datasets from multiple airports.

## Key Results
*   The Genetic Algorithm method substantially outperforms both TimeVAE and TimeGAN, achieving median classifier accuracy (real vs. synthetic) below 0.6 for European and below 0.7 for US airports, indicating high indistinguishability.
*   Synthetic series maintain a moderate median correlation (~0.7) with real data, preserving individual airport delay variability while correctly removing spurious Granger-causal delay propagation between airports.
*   Publicly released synthetic datasets are provided for 30 major European and US airports.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The GA method's strength lies in its explicit preservation of the core statistical relationship driving the time series: the conditional distribution of the current delay given the previous delay. By sampling directly from this empirically observed conditional decile distribution, it inherently captures the primary autoregressive and marginal characteristics. The subsequent ResNet refinement step acts as a correction mechanism, leveraging a learned predictive model to adjust the sampled sequences, smoothing them and enforcing higher-order temporal patterns without over-complicating the initial generative process. This two-stage approach (distributional sampling + predictive refinement) proves more effective and stable for this specific, relatively low-dimensional univariate time series problem than end-to-end deep generative models, which can struggle with mode collapse or losing fine-grained temporal correlations.

## Foundational Learning
*   **Synthetic Time Series Generation:** The core task of creating artificial data sequences that mimic the statistical properties of real sequential data. *Why needed:* To enable research while avoiding privacy and data scarcity issues. *Quick check:* Can the synthetic data fool basic statistical tests (e.g., distribution, ACF)?
*   **Temporal Correlation Preservation:** Ensuring synthetic sequences maintain the autocorrelation and dependence structure of the original series. *Why needed:* Real delays are highly dependent on recent history; breaking this renders data useless. *Quick check:* Compare autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation functions (ACF/PACF) of real vs. synthetic.
*   **Genetic Algorithms (GA) for Sequence Generation:** A population-based search heuristic. *Why needed:* Provides a flexible framework to iteratively improve candidate sequences towards an objective (here, matching conditional distributions and ResNet loss). *Quick check:* Does the GA fitness score improve monotonically or converge over generations?
*   **ResNet for Temporal Refinement:** Using a deep residual network to predict and correct time series values. *Why needed:* Captures complex non-linear temporal patterns for post-processing sampled sequences. *Quick check:* Does the Refiner ResNet achieve high one-step prediction accuracy on real hold-out data?
*   **Granger Causality for Spurious Signal Detection:** A statistical hypothesis test to determine if one time series is useful in forecasting another. *Why needed:* The real air transport network shows artificial spillover effects; removing these from synthetic data is a key validity check. *Quick check:* Do Granger causality p-values for airport pairs drop to non-significant levels in synthetic vs. real data?
*   **Discriminator-Based Evaluation:** Training a classifier to distinguish real from synthetic data. *Why needed:* Provides an "indistinguishability" metric; lower classifier accuracy indicates higher fidelity. *Quick check:* Is the discriminator's accuracy significantly above random chance (0.5)?

## Architecture Onboarding
*   **Component Map:**
    Raw Delay Data -> Preprocessing -> Conditional Decile Model -> GA Sampler (Initial Population) -> ResNet Refiner (Fitness Evaluation & Iterative Update) -> Final Synthetic Sequences -> Validation (Classifier, Correlation, Granger Test)

*   **Critical Path:** The iterative loop between the GA Sampler and ResNet Refiner is critical. The ResNet defines the fitness landscape (prediction error) that the GA optimizes. This coupling transforms simple conditional samples into sequences that satisfy complex temporal constraints.

*   **Design Tradeoffs:**
    1.  **Simplicity vs. Expressivity:** The GA/ResNet pipeline is conceptually simpler than joint variational-GAN training but requires careful tuning of two separate components.
    2.  **Preservation vs. Innovation:** The strong conditioning on past delays preserves local structure well but could potentially limit the generation of novel but plausible multi-hour patterns (mitigated by ResNet refinement).
    3.  **Computational Cost vs. Performance:** The iterative GA+ResNet refinement is computationally expensive per sample but outperforms one-shot deep generative models, offering a better performance/effort ratio for this specific univariate case.

*   **Failure Signatures:**
    1.  **Collapse to Mean:** Synthetic series show abnormally low variance or revert to global mean delays, indicating GA divergence or ResNet over-smoothing.
    2.  **Broken Autocorrelation:** ACF/PACF of synthetic data shows rapid decay to zero, failure to match the lag-1 or lag-2 correlation of real data.
    3.  **High Discriminator Accuracy:** A trained classifier quickly achieves >90% accuracy, signaling fundamental distributional mismatch.
    4.  **Persistent Granger Causality:** Synthetic data still shows significant Granger-causal links between airports, indicating spurious propagation was not removed.

*   **First Three Experiments:**

Report the primary metrics: median discriminator accuracy (using their classifier architecture), median Pearson correlation, and Granger causality p-value summary.
    2.  **Ablation Study:** Run the pipeline with the ResNet refinement step disabled. Compare all metrics to the full method to quantify the Refiner's contribution to indistinguishability and correlation.
    3.  **Conditional Model Sanity Check:** Isolate and visualize the conditional decile distributions (P(delay_t | delay_{t-1})) for 2-3 airports. Visually inspect if the sampled distributions before refinement match the empirical ones. Quantify with a KS test per conditional bin.

## Limitations
*   **Generalizability:** Performance is validated on 30 specific EU/US airports; applicability to regions with different traffic patterns (e.g., high-density Asian hubs) is uncertain.
*   **Metric Sensitivity:** The "indistinguishability" claim heavily depends on the specific classifier's architecture and training; a more powerful discriminator might yield different results.
*   **Causal Validation Scope:** The elimination of spurious propagation is confirmed only by Granger causality tests not finding signals, which does not prove the correct causal structure is absent—only that this specific test statistic is non-significant.

## Confidence
*   **GA Method Superiority on Tested Set:** **High** (Clear, multi-metric quantitative evidence on the evaluated airport set).
*   **Preservation of Individual Airport Patterns:** **Medium** (Supported by correlation and visuals, but lacks a universal aggregate statistical test across all 30 airports).
*   **Elimination of Spurious Propagation:** **Low-Medium** (Relies entirely on the power and assumptions of the applied Granger causality test).
*   **Public Dataset Utility:** **Medium** (Release is factual; community adoption and impact require time to assess).
