# Self-Supervised Multi-Stage Domain Unlearning for White-Matter Lesion Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.13328
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13328
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** SSMSU integrated into nnU-Net improves white-matter lesion segmentation on unseen MRI scanners, with DSC gains of +0.047 (MSSEG), +0.051 (MSLJ), and +0.065 (ISBI) over baseline nnU-Net.

## Executive Summary
Inter-scanner variability in MRI creates a domain shift that degrades the performance of white-matter lesion (WML) segmentation models on new scanners. This paper proposes Self-Supervised Multi-Stage Unlearning (SSMSU), a method integrated into the nnU-Net framework to address this issue. SSMSU attaches domain classifiers to multiple stages of the nnU-Net encoder and uses a confusion loss to force the encoder to suppress scanner-specific features while preserving lesion-relevant information. A self-supervised schedule controls when unlearning begins for each stage, based on the classifier's ability to reliably distinguish the source domains. Evaluated on four public datasets, SSMSU significantly outperforms several benchmarks on unseen test scanners, demonstrating improved generalization without requiring target-domain labels or extensive preprocessing.

## Method Summary
SSMSU extends the nnU-Net 3D-fullres architecture by adding a parallel domain classifier (C1–C6) to the output of each encoder stage (E1–E6). During a warm-up phase, the network is trained with the standard segmentation loss (Dice + cross-entropy) and a classification loss (cross-entropy) for all domain classifiers. After warm-up, a self-supervised schedule monitors each classifier's accuracy. When a classifier's accuracy exceeds an upper-bound accuracy (random chance + tolerance) for a predefined patience threshold, unlearning is activated for that stage. For activated stages, a confusion loss (KL divergence to a uniform distribution) is computed and backpropagated through the corresponding and all preceding encoder blocks, forcing features toward domain invariance. Training alternates between standard segmentation steps and unlearning steps for activated stages. The method requires only FLAIR input and minimal preprocessing (registration, intensity scaling, cropping).

## Key Results
- SSMSU improved DSC on unseen test sets: MSSEG 0.635 vs. 0.588 (nnU-Net), MSLJ 0.574 vs. 0.523, ISBI 0.674 vs. 0.609.
- Enhanced lesion sensitivity: higher True Positive Rate (TPR) and lesion-wise TPR (LTPR).
- Reduced volume error (RVE) compared to benchmarks.
- Slight increase in lesion-wise False Discovery Rate (LFDR) was observed.
- Ablation showed unlearning only the bottom three encoder stages (E4–E6) yielded the best average rank performance.
- No significant degradation in segmentation performance on the seen (source) scanners.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Multi-Stage Classifier Unlearning
- **Claim:** Multi-stage domain classifier unlearning suppresses scanner-specific information from hierarchical latent features while preserving lesion segmentation capability.
- **Mechanism:** Domain classifiers attached to each encoder stage are trained to predict the scanner source of input features. A confusion loss (KL divergence to uniform distribution) is backpropagated through preceding encoders to force features toward domain-invariance, creating a gradient that pushes the encoder to remove domain-discriminative information.
- **Core assumption:** Domain-specific variations (scanner contrast, noise) are encoded in intermediate feature maps and can be separated from task-relevant lesion features through adversarial confusion.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes the approach; [section 3.4] details classifier architecture and loss; [section 4.2, Table 3] shows classifier accuracy drops to 48–67% in stages 4–6 after unlearning, indicating successful suppression, while stages 1–3 remain high (77–85%) when unlearned.
- **Break condition:** Continuous unlearning without scheduling degrades segmentation performance, likely because excessive suppression removes features useful for lesion detection.

### Mechanism 2: Self-Supervised Learning-Unlearning Schedule
- **Claim:** The self-supervised schedule triggers unlearning only when a stable domain signal is detected, preventing premature disruption of segmentation learning.
- **Mechanism:** For each classifier, an "upper-bound accuracy" (UBA = random chance + tolerance) is defined. A counter tracks consecutive iterations where accuracy exceeds UBA. When the count exceeds a patience threshold, unlearning via confusion loss is activated for that stage. This ensures domain information is first reliably learned before being removed.
- **Core assumption:** Domain-discriminative features emerge gradually during training; waiting for stable above-chance accuracy prevents confusing the network before it has learned meaningful domain representations.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] mentions the schedule; [section 3.5] provides Algorithm 1 with monitoring details; [section 5.1] states it balances trade-offs.
- **Break condition:** If patience is too low, unlearning triggers before domain features are properly learned; if too high, domain information may become entrenched.

### Mechanism 3: Deeper Encoder Stage Unlearning
- **Claim:** Unlearning deeper encoder stages (higher-level semantic features) yields optimal domain generalization because scanner-specific artifacts are more entangled with lesion representations there.
- **Mechanism:** Encoder stages produce hierarchical features; shallow layers capture low-level textures/edges, deep layers capture high-level semantics. Ablation shows unlearning only stages 4–6 (bottom three) gives best performance. Shallow unlearning harms spatial detail; deep unlearning removes domain bias from semantic representations.
- **Core assumption:** Domain-induced variations (intensity non-uniformity, contrast differences) affect high-level feature spaces more than low-level; thus, removing domain info from deep layers minimally impacts spatial precision while maximizing generalization.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.2] states deeper stages are more amenable; Table 2 shows bottom-three unlearning achieves best average rank (RS=4.0); Table 3 shows post-unlearning accuracy for unlearned bottom stages drops to 48–67%, while top stages remain high.
- **Break condition:** Over-unlearning deep stages may remove contextual cues, but results show TPR/LTPR improve, suggesting the trade-off is beneficial.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Domain shift and unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA)**
- **Why needed here:** Inter-scanner variability creates domain shift; a model trained on source scanners fails on unseen target scanners without labeled target data. SSMSU is a UDA technique that removes domain-specific features from representations.
- **Quick check:** How would you detect domain shift in segmentation performance, and why might data augmentation alone be insufficient?

**Concept: Adversarial/confusion loss for feature disentanglement**
- **Why needed:** The core unlearning mechanism uses a domain classifier forced to output uniform predictions via confusion loss (KL to uniform). This creates a gradient that removes domain-discriminative information while keeping task-relevant information.
- **Quick check:** What's the difference between using confusion loss (uniform target) vs. standard adversarial loss (domain classifier loss with gradient reversal)? Why might uniform target be preferable for multi-class domains?

**Concept: Deep supervision and multi-level feature extraction**
- **Why needed:** nnU-Net's encoder produces multi-resolution feature maps. Attaching classifiers at multiple stages allows staged unlearning; the paper shows deeper stages are optimal. This relies on understanding hierarchical feature learning in U-Nets.
- **Quick check:** Why might low-level features (stage 1) be less suitable for domain unlearning than high-level features (stage 6)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**
Base nnU-Net 3D-fullres (Encoder E1–E6 with stride-2 downsampling, Decoder D1–D6 with transpose conv, skip connections) -> Added parallel domain classifiers C1–C6 (each Cx takes Ex output, applies (x-1) stride-2 convs to 4×4×4, then 2 conv blocks + flatten + FC to Nd outputs) -> Self-supervised schedule monitors per-stage classifier accuracy and triggers unlearning -> Losses: Segmentation loss (DSC + CE), Classifier loss (CE), Confusion loss (KL to uniform)

**Critical path:**
1. **Warm-up (ew epochs):** For each batch: forward pass -> segmentation output -> backprop Lseg; also forward Ex->Cx -> backprop LCE for all C1–C6 (only domain classification learning).
2. **Learning-unlearning (post-ew):** Same as warm-up, plus: for each Cx where unlearning is triggered, perform fresh forward Ex->Cx, compute Lconf, backprop through Ex–E1 (all preceding encoders).
3. **Self-supervision loop:** After each iteration, update per-stage accuracy counters; if accuracy > UBA, increment counter; else reset. When counter > Pat, mark stage as "unlearning active".
4. **Note:** Confusion loss backprop requires a separate forward pass because parameters were updated by Lseg and LCE in the same iteration.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- **Fixed vs. adaptive schedule:** Fixed LUR (learning:unlearning steps ratio) is simpler but self-supervised adapts to when domain info becomes detectable. Paper shows self-supervised (bottom-3 unlearning) best.
- **Which stages to unlearn:** Unlearning all stages harms performance (Table 2, RS=6.2). Unlearning only deepest stages (4–6) best. Unlearning top stages (1–3) also poor. Assumption: shallow features need preservation for spatial precision.
- **Modality choice:** FLAIR-only avoids registration/harmonization errors from multi-modality preprocessing. May lose complementary info but simplifies pipeline and improves robustness.
- **Classifier capacity:** Cx architecture uses stride-2 convs to reduce spatial dims to 4×4×4 before FC. Paper doesn't ablate classifier depth; assume sufficient for domain discrimination.

**Failure signatures:**
- **No unlearning triggered:** Check classifier accuracies—if all stay near random, domain features not being captured. May need longer warm-up, higher classifier LR, or stronger augmentation.
- **Segmentation degrades on seen data:** Over-unlearning (too many stages, too early, too strong confusion loss weight). Monitor DSC on seen validation set; seen performance should remain comparable to baseline.
- **Unlearning ineffective (accuracy remains high):** Confusion loss weight too low relative to classification loss; or patience threshold too high. Check per-stage accuracy post-unlearning (should drop toward 50% for binary domain).
- **LFDR increases excessively:** False positives rise; may indicate model becoming too sensitive. Could post-process with small-object filtering (paper notes FPs tend to be small, RVE low).
- **Inconsistent cross-domain gains:** Some scanners show little improvement; might need per-scanner tuning or more domain diversity.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. **Classifier learning sanity check:** After warm-up, evaluate each Cx on validation set. Expected: accuracies >> random chance (e.g., >80% for 3 domains). If not, increase classifier capacity or LR.
2. **Unlearning activation test:** Reduce patience to force early unlearning (e.g., Pat=1). Check if segmentation DSC on seen data drops sharply—confirms that unlearning can harm task if applied prematurely. Then restore default self-supervised schedule.
3. **Stage-specific unlearning validation:** Manually enable unlearning only for stages 4–6 (as per best ablation). After training, verify per-stage classifier accuracies: stages 4–6 should be near random (~50%), while stages 1–3 remain high (>70%). This confirms unlearning localizes to deeper stages.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does the self-supervised multi-stage unlearning (SSMSU) strategy generalize to other medical image segmentation tasks (e.g., organ, tumor) beyond white-matter lesions, and how does its efficacy compare across different anatomical contexts and pathologies?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors state: "One limitation of this study is that the proposed approach was evaluated exclusively on the task of WML segmentation. While clinically significant, this represents only one of many possible applications in neuroimaging and beyond." They also note the method's integration into nnU-Net suggests applicability but requires validation.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper provides no empirical validation outside WML segmentation. The interaction between unlearning stages and diverse feature hierarchies remains unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic benchmarking on multiple public segmentation datasets (e.g., prostate, liver, brain tumors) with varied domain shifts, measuring performance gains and stability of the self-supervised schedule across tasks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the precise contribution of unlearning deeper encoder stages (E4–E6) versus shallower stages (E1–E3) to domain robustness, and is there a universal "optimal" stage configuration or does it depend on the specific domain shift characteristics?
- **Basis in paper:** The ablation study (Table 2, Figure 3) shows bottom-three unlearning (E4–E6) yielded best average ranking (RS=4.0), while unlearning top-three (E1–E3) or all stages performed worse. The authors infer deeper stages capture higher-level features where scanner artifacts entangle with lesions.
- **Why unresolved:** The study tests only one dataset (WMH) for ablation. It is unclear whether the optimal stage selection generalizes to other domain shifts.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled experiments varying the unlearning stage set across multiple multi-scanner datasets with known shift sources (e.g., vendor vs. field strength vs. pulse sequence), correlating stage-wise unlearning effectiveness with quantitative measures of domain feature representation.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** To what extent does the observed increase in lesion false detection rate (LFDR) with SSMSU stem from over-sensitivity to true lesions missing in inconsistent reference standards, versus the introduction of novel spurious detections due to unlearning?
- **Basis in paper:** The authors report: "SSMSU achieved significantly higher DSC, TPR, LTPR, and lower RVE on the unseen test set, while exhibiting a higher LFDR." They suggest "many of these correspond to plausible lesions that were not annotated in the reference standard" but lack quantitative disentanglement.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not perform a detailed error analysis to classify false positives as reference omissions vs. model hallucinations. The causal link between unlearning and LFDR increase remains speculative.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A dedicated study where all false positives from SSMSU and the baseline are independently reviewed by multiple experts to categorize them as "likely true lesion omitted" or "spurious". Correlating LFDR increase with unlearning stage and domain classifier accuracy would clarify the mechanism.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How do the hyperparameters of the self-supervised schedule (patience threshold Pat, tolerance Tol) interact with dataset characteristics (e.g., number of source domains, per-domain sample size), and can they be auto-tuned to avoid manual exploration?
- **Basis in paper:** The schedule uses Pat and Tol (Section 3.5) but the paper states: "We have tested several combinations... but listed... only those that achieved best value per each evaluated performance metric." The "self-supervised" claim is limited; parameters were tuned on WMH seen/validation sets, not fully automated.
- **Why unresolved:** The robustness of the schedule to different numbers of domains (Nd) and class imbalance is unexplored. The authors note equal domain distribution per batch is required, but sensitivity to Pat and Tol across datasets is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A sensitivity analysis varying Pat and Tol across datasets with different numbers of scanners (e.g., Nd=2 vs. Nd=5) and imbalanced sample sizes, measuring impact on segmentation metrics and training stability. Develop a heuristic to set these parameters without validation data.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Why does extensive preprocessing (N4 bias correction, denoising) consistently degrade segmentation performance and UDA efficacy compared to minimal preprocessing, and what specific domain-relevant features are removed that harm generalization?
- **Basis in paper:** Table 4 shows "nnU-Net Preprocessing" performs worse than default nnU-Net and SSMSU on all unseen sets. The authors state: "the harmonization effects due to intensity manipulation do not outweigh the loss of information relevant for segmentation task."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not analyze what information is lost (e.g., texture, subtle contrast). The hypothesis is that preprocessing removes scanner-specific cues that the unlearning method could otherwise exploit, but this is not tested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Feature ablation studies comparing feature distributions (e.g., in early vs. deep encoder layers) between minimally and extensively preprocessed data. Train domain classifiers on preprocessed vs. raw features to quantify discriminability loss. Assess if reintroducing synthetic scanner noise into preprocessed data restores unlearning efficacy.

## Limitations
- Training uses only two source scanners (Philips, Siemens); generalizability to more vendors is untested.
- Self-supervised schedule parameters (patience, tolerance) are not reported, limiting reproducibility.
- Slight increase in lesion false detection rate (LFDR) may impact clinical acceptability.
- The method relies on FLAIR-only input, potentially discarding useful multi-modal cues.
- Requires source domain labels; domain identification in practice may be noisy or unavailable.

## Confidence
- **Unlearning suppresses domain features:** High (classifier accuracy near random, Table 3).
- **Self-supervised schedule effective:** Medium (mechanism sound but key parameters missing).
- **Deeper stages optimal for unlearning:** High (ablation, Table 2).
- **Gains on unseen scanners:** High (consistent DSC improvements, Table 4).
- **No seen-domain degradation:** High (DSC comparable to baseline).
