# Relevance Matters: A Multi-Task and Multi-Stage Large Language Model Approach for E-commerce Query Rewriting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.02555
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02555
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Online A/B testing on JD.com demonstrated significant lifts: +0.106% Unique Visitor Value, +0.181% user conversion rate (UCVR), and +0.082% user click-through rate (UCTR).

## Executive Summary
Existing e-commerce query rewriting methods often neglect explicit product-query relevance, which can degrade retrieval quality despite bridging lexical gaps. This paper introduces a multi-task, multi-stage LLM framework to jointly optimize rewrite quality and relevance. The approach first uses multi-task supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to generate rewrites and predict binary relevance tags. It then applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a composite reward that combines relevance, productivity, increment, rule-based, and tag-based signals. Offline evaluations show improvements in tagging accuracy, recall, and relevance scores. Large-scale online A/B testing on JD.com confirms significant gains in key business metrics, and the system has been deployed since August 2025.

## Method Summary
The method consists of two stages. First, a multi-task SFT fine-tunes an LLM (Qwen3-8B after domain pretraining) to simultaneously generate a query rewrite and predict a binary relevance tag. The loss combines cross-entropy for the rewrite with a weighted binary cross-entropy for the tag. Second, GRPO aligns the model using a composite reward. For each query, the policy samples multiple candidate rewrites via beam search. The reward fuses a "feedback" component (based on an offline search engine’s relevance, increment, and productivity scores) with a rule-based penalty and a tag reward that checks consistency with a thresholded relevance model. Advantages are computed relative to the group of candidates, and the policy is optimized with a KL regularization term. During online serving, the model generates rewrites and tags, filters by tag, and caches the top three rewrites for 14 days.

## Key Results
- Offline: Improved tagging accuracy, higher recall of clicked products, and increased average relevance score.
- Online (JD.com A/B test): +0.106% Unique Visitor Value, +0.181% user conversion rate (UCVR), +0.082% user click-through rate (UCTR).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The multi-task SFT stage forces the model to internalize product-query relevance through the joint objective, producing rewrites that are more likely to retrieve clicked products. The GRPO stage then fine-tunes the model with a reward that explicitly balances relevance (via the offline relevance model and tag consistency), diversity (increment reward), business rules (character/length penalties), and conversion-oriented signals (productivity). The group-relative normalization in GRPO stabilizes training by reducing variance among sampled candidates, preventing overfitting to any single reward component. This combined approach aligns the LLM’s output with both relevance and downstream conversion metrics.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multi-task Learning:** Jointly learns rewrite generation and relevance tagging. *Why needed:* Enforces relevance awareness during rewriting. *Quick check:* Monitor both loss curves; tagging accuracy should improve alongside rewrite quality.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** PPO variant with group-normalized advantages. *Why needed:* Stabilizes RL training by reducing variance and avoiding reward overfitting. *Quick check:* Compare policy loss stability with and without group normalization.
- **Reward Shaping for E-commerce:** Composite reward combining multiple signals. *Why needed:* Balances competing objectives (relevance, diversity, rules). *Quick check:* Ablate each reward component to measure impact on offline metrics.
- **Relevance Tagging:** Binary prediction of whether a rewrite matches clicked products. *Why needed:* Provides a dense, model-based relevance signal for training. *Quick check:* Tagging accuracy against held-out ground-truth tags (derived from relevance model).
- **Query Rewriting with LLMs:** Using LLMs to generate synonymous search terms. *Why needed:* Captures semantic similarity beyond lexical synonym dictionaries. *Quick check:* Evaluate rewrite fluency and semantic similarity to original query.
- **Cached Online Serving:** Storing top-K rewrites for a fixed period. *Why needed:* Reduces latency and computational cost at inference time. *Quick check:* Monitor cache hit rate and freshness (staleness) over time.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Query Logs → Data Filtering → Multi-task SFT Model → Beam Search Candidate Generation → GRPO Reward Computation (offline search engine + tag verification) → Policy Update → Online Serving (with 14‑day cache)

Critical path: (1) Extract query-rewrite pairs from search logs and assign relevance tags via a thresholded JD relevance model. (2) Train multi-task SFT model. (3) Generate N candidate rewrites for each query using beam search. (4) Compute rewards using offline search engine metrics and tag consistency. (5) Apply GRPO update with group-relative advantages. (6) Deploy model; for each query, generate up to three rewrites, filter by tag, and cache results.

Design tradeoffs: 
- Relevance vs. Recall: Overemphasizing relevance (r_R or r_Rule) may reduce recall (coverage of clicked products); the increment reward (r_I) and tag reward (r_Tag) mitigate this.
- Offline vs. Online metrics: Offline relevance scores may not align with conversion; hence online A/B testing is essential.
- Complexity vs. Efficiency: Multi-task LLM and beam search are computationally heavy; caching top‑3 rewrites reduces latency but introduces staleness.

Failure signatures: 
- Low tagging accuracy: SFT model not learning relevance; adjust λ loss weight or clarify tag boundary.
- Recall collapse during GRPO: Over‑optimization for relevance; reduce weight of r_R or r_Rule, or increase beam size.
- Online degradation despite offline gains: Distribution shift or caching issues; run segmented A/B test by query frequency.

First experiments:
1. SFT loss weighting sensitivity: Vary λ and measure tagging accuracy vs. recall on validation set.
2. Reward component ablation in GRPO: Remove/adjust r_R, r_I, r_P, r_Rule weights to understand trade‑offs.
3. Small-scale online A/B test: Deploy on 1% traffic, compare offline relevance/recall improvements against online UCVR/UCTR lifts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
Based on the limitations and proposed next steps, the paper implicitly raises open questions about:
- The impact and design of the proprietary offline search engine's relevance model and reward signals.
- The sensitivity to critical hyperparameters (e.g., λ in SFT, α in rule penalty, reward weights, beam size).
- The generalizability of the multi-task GRPO framework to other e-commerce domains or languages.
- The trade-off between offline relevance metrics and online conversion rates across different query segments.
- The effectiveness of the 14-day caching strategy in maintaining rewrite freshness and performance.

## Limitations
- Unknown specifics of the proprietary offline search engine and relevance model used for reward computation.
- Critical hyperparameters (λ in SFT, α in r_Rule, internal reward weights, beam size) not disclosed.
- Domain-specific pretraining details (data, objective, duration) are missing, affecting baseline performance.

## Confidence
- **High:** Online efficacy of the deployed system (UV value +0.106%, UCVR +0.181%, UCTR +0.082%) due to large-scale A/B testing.
- **Medium:** General validity of the multi-task GRPO framework for improving relevance and conversion.
- **Low:** Ability to replicate identical performance due to unreported hyperparameters and proprietary signals.
