# Federated Continual Learning for Privacy-Preserving Hospital Imaging Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2601.06742  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06742  
- **Reference count:** 25  
- **Primary result:** DP-FedEPC improves average macro-AUROC by 2.8–4.1 percentage points over baselines and reduces a standard forgetting metric by over 40%, while providing formal (ε,δ)-DP guarantees.

## Executive Summary
Federated learning across hospitals faces the twin challenges of data privacy and catastrophic forgetting as new data streams arrive. This paper introduces DP-FedEPC, a method that integrates elastic weight consolidation (EWC) and latent prototype rehearsal into a federated averaging framework with client‑side differential privacy (DP‑SGD). Neither raw images nor their embeddings are shared; instead, models exchange noised gradients and compact prototype statistics. Evaluated on chest radiography datasets (CheXpert, MIMIC‑CXR), DP-FedEPC achieves a 2.8–4.1 pp lift in macro‑AUROC and cuts forgetting by more than 40% compared to baselines, all under meaningful (ε,δ)-DP. Ablations confirm that EWC and prototypes are complementary, and that moderate DP noise incurs only minor utility loss. The approach thus enables legally compliant multi‑hospital collaboration on evolving medical imaging tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors tackle federated continual learning for chest X‑ray classification, where each hospital (client) receives a sequence of tasks with disjoint label sets and must retain performance on all earlier tasks without accessing past raw images. Input data come from CheXpert, split into four sites (A-D) with different patient demographics; tasks T1–T3 incrementally introduce new findings. The objective is to maximize macro‑averaged AUROC across all tasks while minimizing forgetting (F). 

DP‑FedEPC extends FedAvg with three core components: (1) client‑side DP‑SGD, where each client clips gradients to norm C=1.0 and adds Gaussian noise (σ=0.5) to ensure (ε,δ)-DP; (2) Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC), where a global Fisher matrix (decayed with factor α) regularizes updates to preserve important weights for previous tasks; (3) latent prototype rehearsal, where each client maintains up to 20 prototype vectors per class, computed via k‑means on correctly classified samples’ embeddings, and uses prototype distillation as an additional loss term. The local loss combines binary cross‑entropy, an EWC penalty (λ_EWC=500), and prototype distillation (λ_proto=1.0). Training proceeds task‑by‑task: for each task, the server broadcasts the current global model and global Fisher; each client performs one local epoch per communication round (40 rounds total) with DP‑SGD; after local training, clients compute updated Fisher and prototypes, optionally noising them before transmission; the server aggregates model parameters via FedAvg and updates the global Fisher as a weighted, decayed average. The method avoids any raw‑image replay and provides formal privacy guarantees.

## Key Results
- Average macro‑AUROC improves by 2.8–4.1 percentage points over non‑private and privacy‑agnostic continual learning baselines.
- The forgetting metric F is reduced by more than 40% compared to the strongest baseline.
- With σ=0.5 the method achieves meaningful (ε,δ)-DP while suffering only a minor utility drop relative to the non‑private version.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DP‑FedEPC mitigates catastrophic forgetting through two complementary regularization mechanisms. First, EWC uses a Fisher information matrix to identify weights crucial for previously learned tasks; the global Fisher (updated with decay α) provides a shared importance map that guides all clients, preventing destructive overwrites while still allowing adaptation. Second, latent prototypes capture class‑conditional distributions in the model’s embedding space; distilling these prototypes during training replays a compressed version of past knowledge without exposing raw data. Client‑side DP‑SGD injects calibrated noise into gradients and, if applied, into Fisher and prototype statistics, ensuring that no single client’s update can be reverse‑engineered, thereby satisfying differential privacy. The combination of these techniques within FedAvg allows hospitals to collaboratively train a continually improving model while adhering to strict privacy regulations.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map (plain text)**
Client (local data, model) -> DP‑SGD (clip + noise) -> Compute EWC loss (using global Fisher) + Prototype distillation -> Update local Fisher & prototypes -> Send noised updates -> Server (FedAvg aggregation) -> Update global Fisher (decay α) -> Broadcast to clients

**Critical Path**
1. Server initializes global model and Fisher.
2. For each task:
   a. For R=1..40:
      - Server sends global model and Fisher to all clients.
      - Each client trains 1 local epoch on current task data with DP‑SGD, computes EWC penalty and prototype loss, updates local Fisher and prototypes.
      - Client sends noised model delta (and possibly noised Fisher if sharing) to server.
   b. Server aggregates model deltas via FedAvg and updates global Fisher as weighted decayed average of client Fishers.
3. After task completion, evaluate on all test sets.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- DP noise (σ) vs utility: larger σ improves privacy but degrades accuracy; moderate σ (0.5) gives acceptable trade‑off.
- Prototype budget (≤20 per class): more prototypes improve representation but increase communication overhead; 20 is a memory‑utility sweet‑spot.
- Global Fisher decay α: closer to 1 retains old knowledge longer but slows adaptation; α not reported, tradeoff unexplored.
- Local epochs vs rounds: 1 epoch per round limits computational load but may require more communication; fixed at 1.
- Client participation: all 4 clients each round, but with non‑IID data, smaller sites (Site D) may be dominated in Fisher aggregation.

**Failure Signatures**
- **High forgetting (F)**: Early task performance drops sharply after learning new tasks, indicating insufficient EWC or prototype rehearsal.
- **Utility gap with non‑private baseline**: Macro‑AUROC significantly lower than the non‑DP version, suggesting excessive DP noise or mis‑tuned clipping.
- **Inconsistent per‑site performance**: One site (e.g., D) lags far behind others, possibly due to Fisher dilution or data heterogeneity.
- **Privacy budget mismatch**: Reported ε values are unreasonably low/high given σ and composition, pointing to accounting errors.

**First Three Experiments**
1. **Baseline single‑task validation**: Train a non‑private FedAvg model on Task 1 only (no EWC, no prototypes) to confirm data pipeline and achieve macro‑AUROC ≥ 0.85 (as in baseline numbers).
2. **DP‑SGD sanity check**: Run DP‑FedEPC on Task 1 with σ=0.5 and no continual components; verify that the achieved (ε,δ) matches the moments accountant and that utility drop is ≤ 2 pp relative to Experiment 1.
3. **Forgetting mitigation check**: Train on Tasks 1→2 with three variants: (a) no EWC & no prototypes, (b) EWC only, (c) prototypes only. Measure F after Task 2; expect both (b) and (c) to reduce forgetting versus (a), and combining them (full DP‑FedEPC) to yield the largest reduction, confirming complementarity.

## Limitations
- **Missing hyperparameters**: Values for global Fisher decay (α), prototype candidate pool size, and clipping norms for Fisher/prototype updates are not reported, hindering exact replication.
- **Incomplete privacy accounting**: Details of δ and composition over 120 total rounds (40 × 3 tasks) are absent, making the claimed (ε,δ)-DP guarantees difficult to verify.
- **Potential DP–EWC interaction**: Noise added to Fisher diagonals may degrade their reliability as importance weights, possibly affecting forgetting mitigation.

## Confidence
- Utility improvement (macro‑AUROC +2.8–4.1 pp): Medium
- Forgetting reduction (>40%): Medium
- Formal (ε,δ)-DP guarantees: Low
- Complementarity of EWC and prototypes: Medium‑High
- Minimal DP performance loss: Medium
