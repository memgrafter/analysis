# The Multiverse of Time Series Machine Learning: an Archive for Multivariate Time Series Classification

## Quick Facts
*   **arXiv ID:** 2603.20352
*   **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20352
*   **Reference count:** 40
*   **Primary result:** The UEA MTSC archive is expanded from 30 to 133 unique datasets (147 with variants), establishing a new standardized benchmark; baseline evaluations show HIVE-COTE 2.0 achieves the best average rank, though top methods are often statistically indistinguishable and face major scalability hurdles.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the chronic lack of diverse, standardized benchmarks in multivariate time series classification (MTSC) by creating the "Multiverse" archive. It consolidates 133 unique datasets from multiple sources, far surpassing the original 30-dataset UEA archive, and provides cleaned, consistent variants (equal length, no missing values) to eliminate preprocessing bias. Recognizing computational constraints, the authors curate a representative 66-dataset subset (Multiverse-core). Baseline evaluations of 17 classifiers reveal that while HIVE-COTE 2.0 (HC2) attains the best average rank on a feasible evaluation set, performance differences among leading methods are frequently not statistically significant. Critically, only five algorithms completed all 66 core datasets, underscoring severe scalability challenges in the field. The archive and framework are released to standardize future MTSC research.

## Method Summary
The core methodological contribution is the construction and release of the Multiverse archive. The authors aggregated MTSC datasets from the original UEA repository, EEG/MEG archives, MONSTER, Rehab-Pile, discretized regression tasks, and standalone contributions. For datasets with unequal lengths or missing values, they created standardized `_eq` (FFT-based resampling) and `_nmv` (linear interpolation) variants. To improve practical utility, they defined the Multiverse-core (MV-core) subset by filtering out datasets exceeding a 0.5 GB memory budget, overrepresented rehabilitation problems, and "dubious" datasets. The evaluation methodology involved running 17 classifiers from the `aeon` toolkit on these datasets under strict, uniform resource limits (3 days, 200GB RAM) using default train-test splits and a fixed random seed. Performance was primarily measured by classification accuracy, with statistical comparisons via Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (Holm correction) and critical difference diagrams.

## Key Results
*   The Multiverse archive contains 133 unique MTSC datasets (147 with preprocessed variants), dramatically increasing benchmark diversity beyond the original UEA archive.
*   A curated 66-dataset subset (Multiverse-core) is proposed to make comprehensive evaluation computationally feasible.
*   HIVE-COTE 2.0 (HC2) achieved the best average rank across the 74 datasets where all 17 classifiers completed, but differences among the top-performing algorithms were often not statistically significant.
*   Only five algorithms (HC2, RDST, MRHydra, Catch22, 1NN-DTW) succeeded in evaluating all 66 datasets in the MV-core subset, highlighting significant scalability barriers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Expanding Dataset Diversity Reduces Evaluation Bias
**Claim:** Consolidating 133 datasets from heterogeneous sources mitigates the overrepresentation of motion/biosignal domains (e.g., EEG, HAR) that plagued the original UEA archive, leading to more generalizable algorithm comparisons.
**Mechanism:** A larger, more diverse archive samples a wider range of problem characteristics (train/test sizes, series lengths, channel counts, application domains). This exposes algorithms to a broader distribution of challenges, preventing performance assessments from being dominated by a narrow set of similar tasks. The core assumption is that algorithm performance is not uniformly correlated with dataset characteristics; a method strong on short EEG series may fail on long satellite image series. The heterogeneous benchmark reveals these failure modes.
**Evidence anchors:** The paper notes the original archive's bias, with over 75% of datasets being motion- or biosignal-based. Summary statistics in their tables show a wide range of sizes, lengths, and classes. Prior studies are cited for inconsistent dataset selection, which a fixed, large archive resolves.

### Mechanism 2: Standardized Preprocessing Ensures Fair Comparisons
**Claim:** Providing equal-length, no-missing-value (`_eq`, `_nmv`) variants eliminates preprocessing choices (truncation, padding, interpolation) as a confounding variable in algorithm comparisons.
**Mechanism:** Original datasets contained unequal lengths and missing values. Researchers previously handled these inconsistently. The Multiverse archive releases cleaned variants using consistent methods (FFT resampling for equalization, linear interpolation for missing values). Thus, all algorithms evaluated on these variants are compared on an identical data representation, ensuring performance differences reflect algorithmic merit rather than preprocessing luck.
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract and Section 4.7 explicitly describe the provision of these variants and the specific methods used. Section 3.3 documents the inconsistent handling in prior papers that this standardization prevents.

### Mechanism 3: MV-core Subset Lowers Computational Barrier
**Claim:** The Multiverse-core (MV-core) 66-dataset subset enables reproducible, routine benchmarking by filtering out infeasibly large datasets and overrepresented domains while preserving diversity.
**Mechanism:** The full archive includes massive datasets (e.g., S2Agri-17, ~59M cases) that are prohibitive for many algorithms or academic resources. MV-core applies size-based filters (≤0.5 GB) and excludes excess rehabilitation/EEG data and "dubious" datasets. This curated selection aims to maintain the archive's diversity signal while being accessible for standard computational budgets.
**Evidence anchors:** The abstract and Section 5 define MV-core and its exclusion criteria. Figures comparing rankings on MV-core (42 datasets) vs. the larger feasible set (74 datasets) show similar trends, suggesting subset validity.

## Foundational Learning

### Concept: Benchmark Archive Role in ML Progress
*   **Why needed here:** The paper's primary contribution is creating a benchmark resource. Understanding why a well-designed, diverse, and standardized benchmark is crucial for reproducibility, progress measurement, and providing a common ground for comparison is fundamental to using this archive.

### Concept: Preprocessing Consistency
*   **Why needed here:** A major value proposition of the Multiverse archive is providing cleaned data variants. Users must understand why inconsistent handling of unequal lengths or missing values (different padding, interpolation) introduces noise into algorithm comparisons, potentially misattributing performance to the algorithm rather than the preprocessing.
*   **Quick check question:** If two research groups compare algorithms on the same original unequal-length dataset but use different padding strategies, can their results be directly compared? Why?

### Concept: Computational Scalability Trade-offs
*   **Why needed here:** The archive includes datasets of vastly different scales. Choosing which datasets to evaluate requires understanding the memory/time complexity of algorithms and the trade-off between evaluation completeness and practical feasibility.
*   **Quick check question:** Why might an algorithm that achieves 95% accuracy on a small dataset fail to complete training on a dataset with 1M cases, even if the series length is short?

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Data Layer (aeon dataset loader, TRAIN/TEST.ts files, DATAINFO.csv) -> Algorithm Layer (sklearn-like wrappers for 17 classifiers in aeon) -> Evaluation Layer (custom script looping over dataset-algorithm pairs, capturing accuracy, handling timeouts/memory) -> Analysis Layer (critical difference/MCM diagrams from aeon utilities) -> Curation Layer (scripts defining MV-core filtering logic in multiverse-core.csv)

### Critical path
1.  **Ingest:** Select dataset variant (`*_eq.ts` or `*_nmv.ts`). Load into 3D numpy array `(n_cases, n_channels, n_timepoints)`.
2.  **Preprocess:** Archive provides standardized formats; avoid additional transforms that break comparability. Minimal scaling may be needed.
3.  **Train/Test:** `clf.fit(X_train, y_train)` → `preds = clf.predict(X_test)` → `accuracy = (preds == y_test).mean()`.
4.  **Record:** Append `(dataset_name, clf_name, accuracy)` to master results table.
5.  **Analyze:** Generate rank matrix across datasets → apply statistical tests (Wilcoxon, Holm) → produce critical difference plot.

### Design tradeoffs
*   **Completeness vs. Feasibility:** Running all 17 classifiers on all 133 datasets is infeasible. Tradeoff: prioritize deep coverage on the smaller MV-core or broader, shallower coverage across the full archive.
*   **Resource limits vs. faithfulness:** Hard runtime/memory caps (3 days, 200GB) may exclude algorithms that would succeed with more tuning or parameter optimization, favoring "out-of-the-box" performance.
*   **Default splits vs. resampling:** Using provided single train-test splits speeds evaluation but increases variance compared to cross-validation, making comparisons noisier.
*   **Equalized variants vs. original:** Using `_eq`/`_nmv` ensures consistency but discards potentially informative structure (e.g., natural irregularity). Reporting which variant is used is essential.

### Failure signatures
*   **MemoryError/OOM:** Likely on large datasets (e.g., S2Agri-17) or high-channel datasets (e.g., `FaceDetection` with 144 channels) with kernel/ensemble methods (HC2, Arsenal).
*   **Timeout:** Deep learning models (TST, ConvTran) may hit the 3-day limit on large datasets even with sufficient memory.
*   **Shape mismatch:** Algorithm expects univariate input but receives multivariate, or vice-versa. Check `aeon` wrapper compatibility.
*   **Missing value crash:** Using original `_raw` variant with algorithms not handling NaNs will fail. Must use `_nmv` or `_eq`.
*   **Class imbalance collapse:** Accuracy near majority-class proportion indicates the classifier failed to learn (e.g., `Dummy` predictor behavior).

### First 3 experiments
1.  **Smoke test:** Run `HC2` on a small, clean MV-core dataset (e.g., `BasicMotions`). Expect ~100% accuracy. Verifies data loading, classifier API, and basic pipeline integrity.
2.  **Resource probe:** Run `HC2` on a large full-archive dataset (e.g., `S2Agri-17`). Expect failure (OOM/timeout). Confirms resource limits are enforced and identifies scale boundaries.
3.  **Comparative baseline:** Run top 3 algorithms from paper (`HC2`, `RDST`, `MRHydra`) on all 66 MV-core datasets. Generate critical difference diagram. Checks replication of finding that top methods are not statistically different.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question:** Why do top-performing MTSC algorithms (e.g., HC2, RDST, CIF) show no statistically significant differences in accuracy across the majority of the Multiverse datasets, despite apparent rank variations?
**Basis in paper:** The authors note differences among top methods are often not statistically significant and that in the MCM diagram for top performers on 74 datasets, only HC2 is significantly better than a few others. The incomplete evaluation coverage (only 74/133 datasets) is also noted.
**Why unresolved:** It is unclear whether the lack of significance stems from the benchmark's insensitivity, genuine algorithmic convergence, or the evaluation design (single split). The incomplete coverage may also skew the analysis.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Repeated resampling (e.g., 30 stratified CV folds) across all datasets, paired with effect size analysis, and per-dataset win-loss analysis by dataset characteristics (size, domain, dimensionality).

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How does the significant overrepresentation of motion and biosignal domains (~75% of datasets) bias algorithm development and generalization claims in MTSC research?
**Basis in paper:** The paper explicitly states the archive is over 75% motion- or biosignal-based and that MV-core curation partly aimed to mitigate this, though the skew persists (~66%).
**Why unresolved:** Algorithm design may overfit to patterns common in human activity/EEG (sensor noise, specific bands) while neglecting challenges from other domains (finance, climatology). Consequently, claims about "general-purpose" MTSC may not hold for non-human-activity data.
**What evidence would resolve it:** A meta-analysis of algorithm performance by domain category, curating a balanced domain-diverse subset and re-benchmarking, and soliciting new datasets from underrepresented fields to test if top algorithms change.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** Do the default train-test splits and preprocessing (equalization, interpolation) in the Multiverse archive introduce hidden biases that distort algorithm comparisons, particularly for deep learning and methods designed for irregular/missing data?
**Basis in paper:** The archive provides `_eq` and `_nmv` variants and uses them in baselines. The paper acknowledges preprocessing choices (FFT resampling) can affect structure and that original splits are used without CV.
**Why unresolved:** Default splits may leak information (temporal/subject). Preprocessing may create artifacts (smoothing, spectral leakage) that favor some algorithms over others. Deep learning models, which often handle raw uneven data, are evaluated only on cleaned data.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Re-running with original unequal-length/missing-value versions for compatible algorithms, auditing split integrity for subject/temporal leakage, and ablating preprocessing strategies (linear vs. spline interpolation, padding vs. resampling).

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Can the Multiverse-core subset (66 datasets) serve as a stable, representative proxy for the full archive (133 datasets) in routine MTSC algorithm development and bake-offs?
**Basis in paper:** MV-core is proposed to reduce burden while aiming for breadth. Criteria include size filters, domain balancing, and excluding "dubious" datasets. However, motion/biosignal skew remains.
**Why unresolved:** The selection criteria are pragmatic but not formally validated. It is unclear if conclusions from MV-core (e.g., top algorithms) generalize to the full archive or future additions. The filtering may exclude datasets where specific methods excel.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Computing rank correlation between algorithm performance on MV-core vs. the feasible full-archive intersection, "leave-one-category-out" analysis per domain, and periodic revision with objective metrics to track benchmark stability as the archive grows.

### Open Question 5
**Question:** How can the MTSC community establish robust, standardized evaluation protocols (e.g., cross-validation folds, statistical tests) that account for dataset heterogeneity and prevent misleading comparisons?
**Basis in paper:** The paper documents wide variation in prior studies (dataset selection, preprocessing, resampling) and uses a single default split for practicality, calling it "not a comprehensive bake off."
**Why unresolved:** Single splits inflate variance and risk overfitting to specific test partitions. There is no consensus on handling missing values/unequal lengths in evaluations or on appropriate multiple comparison corrections. Lack of canonical CV splits (e.g., subject-wise) hurts reproducibility.
**What evidence would resolve it:** Releasing pre-defined, multiple CV splits (stratified by subject/session where possible), mandating reporting of multiple metrics and effect sizes, developing a community-endorsed statistical framework (e.g., ANOVA on ranks with dataset as random effect), and large-scale experiments comparing single-split vs. CV stability.

## Limitations
*   **Implementation & Coverage Uncertainty:** The exact `aeon` toolkit version and classifier settings are unspecified. Results are based on a partial evaluation (74/133 datasets for full comparison; 5/17 classifiers completing all 66 core datasets), limiting the generality of "best algorithm" claims.
*   **Preprocessing Specifics:** The precise parameters for FFT-based resampling and linear interpolation used to create `_eq`/`_nmv` variants are not fully documented, leaving room for variation that could affect results.
*   **Subset Validation Gap:** The MV-core curation criteria are pragmatic but lack formal validation; the assumed correlation between core and full-archive rankings is an unproven assumption critical for practical use.

## Confidence
*   **Archive creation & diversity expansion (High):** Directly evidenced by dataset counts and source listings.
*   **Preprocessing standardization (High):** Explicitly stated with described methods (FFT, linear interpolation).
*   **MV-core subset definition (Medium):** Criteria are clear, but the claim of representativeness is unvalidated.
*   **HC2 performance & statistical ties (Medium/Low):** Supported by the paper's analysis but limited by incomplete evaluation coverage.
*   **Scalability challenges (High):** Directly reported result (only 5/17 classifiers completed all 66 core datasets).
