# Prototype-Aligned Federated Soft-Prompts for Continual Web Personalization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.27678
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27678
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** ProtoFed-SP improves NDCG@10 by +2.9% and HR@10 by +2.0% over strongest baselines across eight benchmarks while reducing forgetting under practical DP budgets.

## Executive Summary
ProtoFed-SP introduces a privacy-preserving federated framework for continual web personalization that tackles non-stationary user preferences via a dual-timescale prompt-based interface. Fast, sparse short-term prompts capture session-level intent, while slow long-term prompts are anchored to a server-side prototype library updated with differential privacy. Context-aware routing retrieves relevant prototypes to compose personalized prompts. Evaluations on eight benchmarks show consistent gains in accuracy and reduced forgetting, demonstrating an effective balance of stability and plasticity in federated settings.

## Method Summary
The method trains a frozen backbone model (e.g., SASRec) with two additive soft prompts per user: a short-term prompt updated per session with ℓ₁ sparsity, and a long-term prompt aligned to a globally maintained prototype library. Client updates include recommendation loss plus an alignment term pulling the long-term prompt’s embedding toward its nearest prototype and sharpening it against others. Periodically, clients compress and noisily upload their long-term prompt embeddings; the server aggregates these under differential privacy (DP‑FedKMeans) to refresh the prototype library, enforcing separation and pruning. At inference, the query encoder retrieves top‑M similar prototypes and weights them to form a mixture added to the dual prompts.

## Key Results
- Improves NDCG@10 by +2.9% and HR@10 by +2.0% on average over strongest baselines.
- Significant gains on Amazon‑Books (+5.0% NDCG vs. INFER), H&M (+2.5% vs. Dual‑LoRA), Taobao (+2.2% vs. FedRAP).
- Reduces forgetting (AF) and Steps‑to‑95% while maintaining accuracy under DP budgets (e.g., ε≈3.4).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Dual‑timescale soft prompts (fast sparse short‑term + slow long‑term) decouple session‑level plasticity from long‑term stability, allowing per‑user control of adaptation.
- **Mechanism:** The client maintains two prompt states: \(p_{u,t}^{\text{short}}\) is updated quickly with an \(\ell_1\) proximal to promote sparsity, capturing immediate intent; \(p_u^{\text{long}}\) updates slowly and is anchored to a global prototype library, preserving stable preferences.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] describes the two prompts; [Section 3.2] details update rules (Eqs. 2–3) and composite prompt; corpus shows multi‑granular prompts are emerging but ProtoFed‑SP uniquely couples them with federated prototypes.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Prototype alignment (via Bregman/InfoNCE losses) tethers the long‑term prompt to a low‑curvature manifold of population semantics, reducing drift‑induced forgetting.
- **Mechanism:** Each long‑term prompt embedding is pulled toward its nearest prototype with a Bregman divergence and sharpened against distractors. Prototypes are refreshed by differentially private federated aggregation of compressed client embeddings.
- **Evidence anchors:** [Section 3.4] defines alignment term; [Section 3.5] describes DP‑FedKMeans and robustness measures; [abstract] notes continual refresh via DP and Top‑M routing.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Context‑aware routing (Top‑M prototype retrieval) composes a personalized prompt mixture that attends to multiple stable themes, improving recommendation diversity and accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** Given a query, a router computes similarity with prototype embeddings, selects top‑M, and forms a convex combination of their vectors added to the prompts.
- **Evidence anchors:** [Section 3.3] gives routing formula; [Section 4.3] ablation shows removing short‑term or routing mixture harms performance; corpus supports prototype‑level composition.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Parameter‑Efficient Fine‑Tuning (PEFT) via Soft Prompts**  
  - *Why needed:* ProtoFed‑SP injects trainable prompt tokens into a frozen backbone; understanding PEFT explains why only prompts are updated and how privacy/communication are preserved.  
  - **Quick check:** What happens if the backbone is unfrozen? How does prompt‑only tuning change communication and privacy vs. full fine‑tuning?

- **Concept: Federated Learning with Differential Privacy (DP‑FL)**  
  - *Why needed:* Prototypes are updated by aggregating compressed client embeddings under Gaussian noise; this combination is the core privacy guarantee.  
  - **Quick check:** How do clipping radius \(R_{\text{clip}}\) and noise scale \(\sigma\) interact to achieve \((\varepsilon,\delta)\)‑DP? What are trade‑offs when increasing \(M\) vs. the DP budget?

- **Concept: Stability‑Plasticity in Continual Learning**  
  - *Why needed:* The dual‑timescale design directly implements stability‑plasticity: short‑term prompts for plasticity, long‑term alignment for stability.  
  - **Quick check:** In the convex surrogate analysis, how does the anchor term \(\lambda_s \|u-c_t\|^2_2\) contract the optimal trajectory? What if \(\lambda_s\) is too high/low?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**  
Client (local data, \(p^{\text{long}}\), \(p^{\text{short}}\), query encoder \(g\), prompt encoder \(\phi\), router, updaters, compressor) -> Server (prototype library \(C\), aggregator, separation/pruning) -> Broadcast updated prototypes -> Client

**Critical path**  
1. ClientUpdate: encode query, retrieve Top‑M prototypes, compute composed prompt \(\tilde{p}_{u,t}\), update \(p^{\text{short}}\) (sparse) and \(p^{\text{long}}\) (alignment).  
2. Periodically, compress and noisify \(p^{\text{long}}\) -> upload.  
3. ServerAggregate: assign clients to nearest prototypes, clip/noise, momentum update, enforce separation, prune, broadcast new \(C\).  
4. OnlineInference: same as step 1 but without gradient updates; optional quick short‑term update with feedback.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- **Sparsity \(\lambda_p\):** Higher → more forgetting protection, weaker session adaptation.  
- **Alignment weight \(\lambda_s\):** Higher → stronger stability, may hinder tracking genuine long‑term drift.  
- **Prototype count \(K\):** Larger → finer semantics, higher communication/retrieval cost; too few causes collapse.  
- **Top‑\(M\):** Larger → more expressive, higher latency; optimal around 4.  
- **DP noise \(\sigma\):** Higher → tighter privacy (\(\varepsilon\downarrow\)), utility degradation; \(\sigma\approx0.4\) (\(\varepsilon\approx3.4\)) preserves accuracy.  
- **Frozen backbone:** Saves communication/protects privacy but limits expressivity; requires sufficient prompt capacity.

**Failure signatures**  
- **Forgetting surge:** AF increases → alignment too weak (\(\lambda_s\downarrow\)) or prototypes stale.  
- **Slow adaptation:** Steps‑to‑95% escalates → \(\lambda_p\) too high or \(\alpha_{u,t}\) ineffective.  
- **Prototype collapse:** Many prototypes unused → separation margin \(\rho\) too small or pruning threshold \(\tau_{\text{util}}\) mis‑set.  
- **Privacy leak:** DP accountant shows \(\varepsilon\) too large → reduce \(R_{\text{clip}}\) or increase \(\sigma\).  
- **Cold‑start pitfall:** New users poor NDCG → consider longer prompt length or initialisation from similar prototypes.

**First 3 experiments for a new engineer**  
1. **Single‑client sanity check:** Run `ClientUpdate` on a synthetic minibatch; verify \(p^{\text{short}}\) becomes sparser and \(p^{\text{long}}\) moves toward a prototype after alignment.  
2. **Prototype aggregation test:** Simulate 10 clients uploading compressed embeddings; run `ServerAggregate` with DP‑FedKMeans; check prototypes separate (pairwise distance ≥ ρ) and noise scale matches expected \((\varepsilon,\delta)\).  
3. **End‑to‑end mini‑federated run:** Train on one time slice of a small dataset (e.g., MovieLens‑10M) with 2 clients; evaluate NDCG@10 and AF; confirm gains over a no‑alignment baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Online deployment:** How does ProtoFed‑SP perform in real‑world A/B tests, considering latency, user adaptation, and feedback loops not captured in offline benchmarks?
- **Adaptive prototype library:** Can \(K\) and \(\rho\) be automatically tuned to data characteristics rather than manually set?
- **Fairness integration:** How to incorporate fairness‑aware constraints into federated prototype aggregation to prevent amplifying demographic biases?
- **Frozen backbone limitation:** Does the frozen backbone assumption limit effectiveness when the backbone itself requires continual updates (e.g., new items/domains)?

## Limitations
- Optimal per‑dataset hyperparameters (\(\lambda_s\), \(\lambda_p\), \(K\), \(M\)) are not reported; replication requires extensive search.
- Compression pipeline details (PCA basis update frequency, product‑quantization codebook training) are unspecified.
- Backbone initialization and pretraining protocol (whether frozen SASRec is pretrained on all data or from scratch) are unclear.
- Drift‑detection mechanism (threshold and window size for triggering uploads) is missing.
- Alignment loss implementation details (exact Bregman form, InfoNCE negative sampling) need confirmation.

## Confidence
- **Dual‑timescale decoupling:** High – supported by ablations and theory.
- **Prototype alignment:** Medium – novel DP‑FedKMeans but sensitive to hyperparameters; lacks robustness analysis.
- **Context‑aware routing:** Medium – effective in experiments but depends on well‑maintained prototypes and query encoder.
- **Overall effectiveness across datasets:** Medium‑High – consistent gains but reproducibility concerns due to hyperparameter opacity.

## Next Checks
1. **Alignment ablation:** Set \(\lambda_s = 0\) and recompute AF and NDCG to isolate anchoring effect.
2. **DP noise sensitivity:** Sweep \(\sigma \in \{0.2, 0.4, 0.8\}\) and clipping radius \(R\); measure NDCG, AF, and prototype diversity (e.g., silhouette score) to chart privacy‑utility frontier.
3. **Cross‑dataset hyperparameter sweep:** On Amazon‑Books, Taobao, and H&M, grid‑search over \(K \in \{64,128,256\}\), \(M \in \{2,4,8\}\), and \(\lambda_p \in \{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2}\}\). Report optimal settings and performance ranges to assess generalizability.
