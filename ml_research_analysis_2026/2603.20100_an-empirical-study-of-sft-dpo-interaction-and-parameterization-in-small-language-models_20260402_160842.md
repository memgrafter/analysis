# An Empirical Study of SFT-DPO Interaction and Parameterization in Small Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2603.20100
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20100
- **Reference count**: 9
- **Primary result**: Full-parameter fine-tuning (FFT) consistently outperforms LoRA in GPT-2-scale models; DPO yields only marginal gains over strong SFT baselines; data scale matters more than epoch count under fixed compute.

## Executive Summary
This empirical study investigates supervised fine-tuning (SFT), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and parameterization strategies (full fine-tuning vs. LoRA) in GPT-2-scale models (124M). Experiments on paraphrase detection and Shakespearean sonnet generation reveal that full-parameter fine-tuning (FFT) significantly outperforms LoRA, and DPO provides only marginal improvements over a well-tuned SFT baseline. Moreover, training on a larger dataset for fewer epochs yields better generalization than more epochs on a smaller dataset under fixed compute budget. The primary conclusion is that in small-model regimes, full-parameter adaptation is the dominant performance lever, while preference optimization and parameter-efficient methods have limited impact.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct experiments on two tasks: binary paraphrase detection using the Quora Question Pairs dataset (283,011 training examples) and sonnet continuation using Shakespeare's 154 sonnets. The base model is GPT-2 (124M parameters). For paraphrase, a linear classification head is appended to the final-token hidden state; for generation, the standard language modeling head is used. Training stages include supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with cross-entropy loss, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with β=0.2, and a staged pipeline (SFT followed by DPO). Parameterizations compared are full-parameter fine-tuning (FFT) and LoRA (rank 8). Key hyperparameters: SFT learning rates of 1e-5 (FFT) and 2e-4 (LoRA); DPO learning rate 5e-6; batch sizes 128 (paraphrase) and 8 (sonnet); max sequence lengths 64 and 256 respectively. Models are selected based on dev performance: highest accuracy/F1 for paraphrase, highest mean chrF (over 20 seeds) for sonnet. The reproduction plan details the exact training regimes, checkpoint selection, and evaluation procedures.

## Key Results
- FFT SFT achieves 89.87% dev accuracy on paraphrase; adding DPO improves marginally to 90.05%. LoRA SFT peaks at 87.70%, consistently underperforming FFT.
- DPO-only on paraphrase reaches 89.46%, competitive with SFT-only when preference pairs directly encode the classification objective (correct vs. incorrect labels).
- On Shakespearean sonnet generation, DPO yields negligible chrF gains (41.94 vs. 41.70), with some DPO strategies causing performance collapse (e.g., V3 top-K augmentation).
- Under fixed compute, training on the full 283k dataset for 2 epochs (86.20 F1) outperforms training on a 10% subset for 10 epochs (82.34 F1).

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Full-parameter fine-tuning (FFT) dominates LoRA in small models.** FFT updates all parameters, allowing the small backbone to fully rewire its internal representations for the target task, while LoRA's low-rank constraint limits the adaptation subspace. Evidence: FFT achieves 89.24% vs. LoRA's 86.34% in paraphrase detection. Break condition: At larger scales (>1B parameters) or on memory-bound hardware, LoRA's efficiency may outweigh its accuracy deficit.

**Mechanism 2: DPO provides only marginal, task-dependent improvements over well-tuned SFT.** DPO acts as a margin-refinement stage that slightly increases separation between chosen and rejected outputs. Its signal is most effective when it mirrors the cross-entropy loss of SFT (e.g., correct vs. incorrect labels). When preferences are noisier or more complex (e.g., stylistic generation), DPO adds little beyond SFT. Evidence: SFT@9→DPO yields 90.05% vs. SFT-only 89.87%; DPO-only reaches 89.46% on paraphrase; sonnet generation shows negligible chrF gains (≤+0.28). Break condition: With increased model capacity or preference data scale, DPO might unlock capabilities SFT cannot reach.

**Mechanism 3: Data scale matters more than epoch count under fixed compute.** A small model quickly captures core task structure within a few epochs on sufficient data. Additional epochs on a limited dataset lead to overfitting, whereas more distinct examples provide greater input space coverage. Evidence: 283k×2 epochs (86.20 F1) outperforms 28.3k×10 epochs (82.34 F1) under similar training time. Break condition: If the larger dataset is of lower quality or the model is severely underparameterized, more epochs on a small high-quality set may be preferable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Why needed: SFT is the foundational step that teaches the model the basic task mechanics (e.g., mapping question pairs to labels or completing sonnets). All subsequent strategies build upon an SFT-initialized model. Quick check: With a pretrained GPT-2 and labeled data, what is the simplest effective adaptation method? (Train with cross-entropy loss on input-target pairs for a few epochs.)
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**: Why needed: DPO is investigated as a potential refinement after SFT to align outputs with subtle preferences (e.g., stylistic quality) that are hard to specify via token-level supervision. Quick check: How does DPO loss differ from SFT loss? (DPO uses (chosen, rejected) pairs and a margin-based loss, teaching relative preferences rather than absolute targets.)
- **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**: Why needed: LoRA is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method tested as an alternative to FFT, to assess if efficiency benefits compromise performance in small models. Quick check: What is the core idea behind LoRA? (It approximates weight updates with a low-rank product (BA), trading expressivity for fewer trainable parameters and memory savings.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Pretrained GPT-2 (124M, 12 layers, d=768) → Task-specific head (linear classifier for paraphrase or LM head for generation) → Adaptation modules (full-parameter updates or LoRA adapters injected into attention and FFN layers via W = W₀ + α/r * BA)

**Critical path**:
1. Load pretrained GPT-2 weights.
2. Attach task-specific head (if classification).
3. **Primary path**: Perform full-parameter SFT for N epochs (N determined by dev performance).
4. **Optional refinement**: Initialize from best SFT checkpoint, run DPO for M epochs with (prompt, chosen, rejected) triples, β=0.2, LR=5e-6.
5. **Alternative parameterization**: Replace step 3 with LoRA (r=8, α=16) and repeat SFT/DPO.

**Design tradeoffs**:
- FFT vs. LoRA: FFT higher accuracy but updates all parameters; LoRA updates <1% but underperforms at this scale; no speedup on H100 (compute-bound).
- SFT vs. DPO: SFT simpler and strong; DPO adds minimal cost but only small gains unless preferences align perfectly.
- Handoff timing: DPO after near-converged SFT (SFT@9→DPO) is best; DPO-only viable for classification but not generation.

**Failure signatures**:
- LoRA rank paradox: higher rank (r=16) worse than r=8 at epoch 3 → optimization instability.
- DPO V3 collapse: top-K augmentation (325 pairs from 65 prompts) drops chrF to 41.46 vs. SFT 41.70 → over-reinforcement.
- Low-temperature generation (T=0.5) harms chrF → overly deterministic, repetitive outputs.

**First 3 experiments**:
1. Baseline FFT SFT: Train full GPT-2 with classification head on Quora (283k) for 8-10 epochs; track dev accuracy (expected ~89.8%).
2. LoRA rank sweep: Repeat with LoRA (r∈{4,8,16}) and LR=2e-4; verify r=8 best but all < FFT.
3. SFT→DPO handoff: From FFT SFT@9 checkpoint, run DPO (β=0.2, LR=5e-6) for 6 epochs; expect best dev accuracy ~90.05%; also try DPO-only (0 SFT epochs) expecting ~89.5%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **For decoder-only models much smaller than GPT-2 (<100M parameters), does FFT continue to outperform LoRA across tasks?** The paper only examined one scale (124M); smaller models may have different capacity-adaptation trade-offs. Systematic comparisons across multiple small model sizes are needed.
2. **In small-model regimes, under what exact task and preference design conditions does DPO provide significant gains over well-tuned SFT beyond cases where preferences mirror supervised labels?** Only two tasks were tested; the boundary for DPO's usefulness remains unclear. Broad evaluation on a suite of small-model tasks with varied preference quality could resolve this.
3. **On memory-bound hardware, does LoRA reduce wall-clock training time for small models?** The H100 setup was compute-bound; on GPUs with tighter memory budgets, LoRA's memory savings might enable larger batches and faster training. Replication on memory-limited systems is required.
4. **How should preference pairs be constructed for DPO in low-resource generative tasks to avoid collapse?** The sonnet experiment showed that simple augmentation (top-K) caused collapse, but the mechanism is not understood. Ablating preference design factors (unique prompts, rejects per prompt, filtering thresholds) in small datasets could yield robust principles.

## Limitations
- Hyperparameter ambiguities (e.g., SFT LR for paraphrase FFT, DPO sampling settings, LoRA target modules) hinder faithful reproduction.
- Implementation details (e.g., log-prob computation with classification head in DPO) are unspecified.
- Scope limited to GPT-2 (124M) and specific datasets; findings may not generalize to other models, tasks, or domains.

## Confidence
- Full-parameter fine-tuning (FFT) outperforms LoRA: Medium
- DPO provides only marginal improvements over SFT: Medium
- Data scale dominates epoch count under fixed compute: High
- Overall conclusion (full-parameter adaptation dominant): Medium

## Next Checks
1. Cross-architecture replication: Repeat experiments with at least two other small models (e.g., OPT-125M, TinyLlama) to verify FFT vs LoRA gap and DPO effectiveness.
2. Hyperparameter resolution: Conduct systematic sweeps for LoRA (rank 4/8/16, target layers) and DPO (β, LR, epochs) to clarify the rank paradox and optimal settings.
3. Scale test: Train larger models (1B+ parameters) with increased preference data to test break conditions: does LoRA become competitive and do DPO gains increase?
