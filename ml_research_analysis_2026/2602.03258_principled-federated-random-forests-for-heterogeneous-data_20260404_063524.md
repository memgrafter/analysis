# Principled Federated Random Forests for Heterogeneous Data

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2602.03258
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03258
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** FedForest enables exact replication of centralized CART split decisions using only aggregated client statistics, with theoretical guarantees and strong empirical performance on heterogeneous data.

## Executive Summary
FedForest introduces the first principled federated random forest method that exactly replicates the split decisions of a centralized CART algorithm while preserving client data privacy. It achieves this through two key innovations: (1) federated quantile sketching to generate candidate split thresholds with bounded error, and (2) evaluation of splits using additive sufficient statistics that allow exact computation of impurity reduction from aggregated client summaries. The method also supports splitting on client identifiers to enable personalized modeling under outcome shift. Theoretical guarantees prove that FedForest approximates centralized split selection, and experiments on synthetic benchmarks and a real federated heart disease dataset demonstrate its effectiveness, matching centralized performance and outperforming existing federated baselines.

## Method Summary
FedForest trains multiple CART trees in a federated manner without sharing raw data. At each node, the server coordinates split selection in two rounds. First, clients send local quantile sketches for each feature; the server combines these to form a pooled empirical CDF and inverts it to produce candidate thresholds. Second, for each candidate, clients compute local sufficient statistics for the left child, and the server aggregates these to exactly recover the impurity reduction as in centralized CART. This ensures identical split choices. The method also allows splits on the client indicator (H) for personalization under outcome shift. Communication is minimized by limiting the number of quantiles (B) and batching tree growth.

## Key Results
- FedForest exactly matches centralized CART in split selection and final model performance under homogeneous and covariate shift settings.
- Under outcome shift, including client-indicator (H) splits yields significant improvements, validating the personalization mechanism.
- On the FedHeart-Disease dataset, FedForest achieves accuracy on par with a centralized random forest and outperforms federated linear and neural network models trained with FedAvg.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Federated Quantile Sketching
The server generates candidate thresholds by mixing clients' local empirical CDFs approximated via linear interpolation between B quantiles. Theorem 3.2 guarantees that for any centralized threshold, there exists a federated candidate that disagrees on at most 3/(2B) of the pooled samples' assignments. This relies on the assumption that linear interpolation accurately approximates local CDFs and that mixing interpolated CDFs equals the pooled empirical CDF. The bound breaks when client sample sizes are very small or data are extremely skewed/heavy-tailed.

### Mechanism 2: Additive Sufficient Statistics
For standard impurities (variance, Gini), the impurity function Ψ depends on additive statistics (e.g., count, sum, sum of squares). Clients compute these statistics locally at the current node and for each candidate's left child. The server aggregates left-child statistics across clients and derives right-child statistics by subtraction, then computes the impurity reduction exactly as in centralized CART. This works because the impurity function is a function of these additive statistics. If a non-additive impurity (e.g., based on medians) is used, exact recovery fails.

### Mechanism 3: Client-Indicator Splits for Personalization
To handle outcome shift (where P(Y|X,H) varies by client), FedForest allows splitting on the client identifier H. The server evaluates a partition of clients by aggregating the already-collected per-client node summaries (from Mechanism 2) for the left and right client groups. The optimal client split is contiguous when clients are sorted by their within-node outcome means, which can be found efficiently via Fisher's grouping algorithm. This incurs no extra communication beyond the standard summaries but requires that per-client summaries are available (which they are from the node statistics).

## Foundational Learning
- **CART split selection:** The core algorithm for decision trees; candidate thresholds are typically chosen as midpoints between sorted feature values, and splits are selected by maximizing impurity reduction. *Why needed:* FedForest aims to replicate exactly this process in a federated setting.
  **Quick check:** What is the formula for empirical impurity reduction in CART, and how are candidate thresholds generated?
- **Heterogeneity types in FL:** Covariate shift (P(X|H) varies) vs. outcome shift (P(Y|X,H) varies). *Why needed:* The method's design (e.g., pooled quantiles vs. H-splits) depends on the type of heterogeneity present.
  **Quick check:** Why does outcome shift necessitate splitting on the client indicator?
- **Sufficient statistics and additivity:** Statistics that fully capture the information needed for a parameter (e.g., mean and variance for Gaussian). *Why needed:* Exact impurity aggregation requires that the impurity be a function of additive statistics so that client summaries can be summed.
  **Quick check:** What are the minimal sufficient statistics for Gini impurity in binary classification, and why are they additive?
- **Empirical CDF and quantiles:** The empirical CDF jumps at each data point; quantiles are values at given cumulative probabilities. *Why needed:* FedForest approximates the pooled CDF from local quantile sketches to generate candidate thresholds.
  **Quick check:** How does linear interpolation between quantiles approximate a CDF?
- **Fisher's grouping algorithm:** A dynamic programming method to find the optimal contiguous partition of ordered items into two groups to minimize within-group variance. *Why needed:* Used to find the best client split when H is ordered by per-client means.
  **Quick check:** What is the time complexity of Fisher's algorithm for grouping n ordered items into k groups?

## Architecture Onboarding
Server -> (broadcasts active nodes/features) -> Clients
Clients -> (send quantile sketches and node summaries) -> Server
Server -> (constructs candidate thresholds, broadcasts) -> Clients
Clients -> (compute left-child stats for candidates) -> Server
Server -> (aggregates, evaluates gains, selects splits) -> Updates active nodes

**Critical path:** For each tree, level by level: 
1. Server selects a batch of active nodes and a subset of features for each.
2. Clients compute and send B quantiles per feature and additive node summaries.
3. Server builds candidate set: pooled CDF inversion for continuous features and H-splits.
4. Clients compute left-child statistics for each candidate.
5. Server aggregates, evaluates impurity reduction, picks best split per node, and activates child nodes for next level.

**Design tradeoffs:**
- *Quantile budget B:* Larger B improves candidate fidelity but increases communication linearly (O(d·B)).
- *Exact vs. AvgImp:* Exact evaluation (summing stats) is necessary under heterogeneity but requires sending S scalars per candidate (e.g., 3 for regression). AvgImp sends one scalar (local gain) but fails under covariate shift.
- *Client subsampling:* Reduces communication but biases the pooled mixture, harming performance under shift; only safe under homogeneity.
- *Top-L screening:* Reduces evaluated features; safe under homogeneity but may discard globally optimal feature under heterogeneity.

**Failure signatures:**
- High test MSE when using AvgImp under covariate shift.
- Poor accuracy when omitting H-splits under outcome shift.
- Increased variance with too small B (e.g., B<20 for high-dimensional data).
- Local ensembling (training independent trees per client) fails under any shift.

**First 3 experiments:**
1. Homogeneous synthetic data: FedForest (with/without H) matches centralized RF MSE, validating exact replication when data are i.i.d.
2. Covariate shift (disjoint supports): FedForest remains robust while AvgImp fails, showing necessity of pooled quantiles and exact evaluation.
3. Outcome shift: Including H-splits (FedForest with H) significantly reduces MSE compared to without H, proving personalization via client indicator.

## Limitations
- The 3/(2B) bound on quantile fidelity assumes linear interpolation accurately approximates local CDFs, which may degrade with very small client sample sizes or extreme data skewness.
- The contiguity assumption for optimal H-splits (enabling Fisher grouping) is a heuristic; for many clients or non-contiguous optimal partitions, exhaustive search may be needed, which is expensive.
- Empirical validation is limited to specific synthetic shifts and one medical dataset; performance under more complex real-world heterogeneity (e.g., concept drift, high-dimensional sparse data) is unknown.

## Confidence
- **High:** Exact impurity recovery via additive sufficient statistics (Mechanism 2) is mathematically sound for standard impurities.
- **Medium:** Federated quantile sketching fidelity (Mechanism 1) is theoretically bounded but relies on interpolation assumptions that may be brittle under severe data skew. H-split personalization (Mechanism 3) is plausible but depends on the contiguity heuristic.
- **Low:** Robustness to all forms of severe heterogeneity beyond tested scenarios; scalability of H-split search for many clients.
