# Retrieval-Feedback-Driven Distillation and Preference Alignment for Efficient LLM-based Query Expansion

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID**: 2603.13776  
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13776  
- **Reference count**: 40  
- **Primary result**: Distills a 685B teacher into a 4B student that achieves ~97% of teacher nDCG@10 on TREC DL19 with a 45× reduction in latency.

## Executive Summary
Deploying large language models (LLMs) for generative query expansion is computationally expensive. This paper introduces a framework that distills a powerful teacher model (DeepSeek-685B) into a compact student (Qwen3-4B) using retrieval-feedback-driven supervision and preference alignment. The student first undergoes supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) to imitate teacher expansions (both zero‑shot and few‑shot), then is further tuned with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) on pairwise preferences derived from nDCG@10 differences. On TREC DL19, the final student reaches 0.6536 nDCG@10 (97% of the teacher’s 0.6741) while cutting per‑query latency from 4.726 s to 0.103 s (≈45× speedup). Gains of 11.75‑15.65 percentage points over BM25, Rocchio, and non‑aligned expansions are observed across multiple benchmarks and languages, demonstrating a practical solution for low‑cost, high‑quality query expansion.

## Method Summary
The method comprises two training stages applied to a small student model (Qwen3-4B). First, supervised fine‑tuning with LoRA trains the student to mimic teacher expansions generated by DeepSeek‑685B under zero‑shot and few‑shot prompts; a unified zero‑shot prompt is used at both training and inference. Second, DPO aligns the student’s generation with retrieval performance: for each training query, the two teacher expansions are evaluated via BM25 to compute nDCG@10; pairs with a significant difference (|Δ| ≥ 0.01) become chosen/rejected examples. The student (initialized from the SFT checkpoint) is optimized to favor the higher‑scoring expansion. At deployment, the DPO‑tuned student produces a zero‑shot expansion that is concatenated with the original query and retrieved using BM25, yielding high effectiveness at low latency.

## Key Results
- The student model attains nDCG@10 = 0.6536 on TREC DL19, ≈97% of the teacher’s 0.6741, while per‑query latency drops from 4.726 s to 0.103 s (≈45× speedup).
- Across TREC DL19/DL20/DL21 and MIRACL‑zh, SFT+DPO outperforms BM25, Rocchio, and non‑aligned student expansions by absolute nDCG@10 gains of 11.75–15.65 percentage points.
- The approach remains effective under dense retrieval (BGE) and in cross‑lingual (Chinese) settings, confirming its robustness and practicality.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
**Mechanism 1: Imitation of Teacher Expansion Patterns via SFT**  
SFT transfers the teacher’s generative capabilities to the student by minimizing token‑level negative log‑likelihood on teacher outputs. The student learns to produce fluent, retrieval‑friendly expansions without few‑shot prompts at inference. The core assumption is that teacher expansions inherently capture semantic augmentation beneficial for retrieval; Tables 2‑5 confirm SFT alone already beats baseline methods.

**Mechanism 2: Retrieval‑Metric Alignment via DPO**  
DPO directly optimizes the student’s generation policy using pairwise preferences derived from retrieval performance. For each query, the two teacher expansions are ranked by nDCG@10; the higher‑scoring expansion becomes the chosen response. By training the student to prefer these chosen expansions, its outputs become better aligned with the target metric. Consistent improvements of SFT+DPO over SFT‑only validate this alignment.

**Mechanism 3: Complementary Supervision from Dual Prompting Strategies**  
Zero‑shot expansions tend to be concise and general, while few‑shot expansions are more structured and detailed. Training on both provides diverse stylistic signals, improving robustness and coverage. Ablations (Section 4.5.6) show combining them outperforms using either alone.

## Foundational Learning
**Concept: Knowledge Distillation**  
*Why needed*: To compress the capabilities of a 685B teacher into a deployable 4B student while preserving generative quality for query expansion.  
*Quick check*: What is the difference between distilling from teacher outputs vs. internal representations? (Answer: This paper uses output distillation via SFT, not hidden‑state distillation.)

**Concept: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**  
*Why needed*: To align the student’s generation with retrieval objectives without training a separate reward model, keeping the pipeline efficient.  
*Quick check*: How does DPO differ from traditional RLHF? (Answer: DPO directly optimizes the policy using pairwise preferences, avoiding reward model training and sampling.)

**Concept: Generative Query Expansion**  
*Why needed*: To replace term‑based expansion (e.g., Rocchio) with context‑rich paragraph expansions that capture broader semantics, especially for cross‑lingual and complex queries.  
*Quick check*: Why might generative expansion be more effective than Rocchio for cross‑lingual retrieval? (Answer: It generates contextual, explanatory text that captures semantics beyond term co‑occurrence.)

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**  
Teacher (DeepSeek‑685B) → generates zero‑shot & few‑shot expansions → SFT module (LoRA on Qwen3‑4B) → DPO module (preferences from BM25 nDCG) → Student model (DPO‑tuned Qwen3‑4B) → expansion → BM25 retrieval.

**Critical path**  
1. For each training query, generate two teacher expansions (E1 zero‑shot, E2 few‑shot).  
2. Train student with SFT (LoRA) on both expansions under a zero‑shot prompt.  
3. Compute nDCG@10 for E1 and E2 for each query via BM25 retrieval on the training corpus.  
4. Filter pairs with |ΔnDCG| ≥ 0.01 to form chosen/rejected expansion pairs.  
5. Train student with DPO (reference = SFT checkpoint) using these pairs.  
6. Deploy DPO‑tuned student to generate zero‑shot expansions and retrieve with BM25.

**Design tradeoffs**  
- LoRA vs full fine‑tuning: LoRA reduces memory and training cost but may limit capacity.  
- Dual expansion supervision: Improves coverage but doubles teacher generation cost.  
- ΔnDCG threshold: Smaller yields more pairs but risks noise; larger purifies signal but may provide too few pairs.  
- Retrieval metric: nDCG@10 focuses on top‑ranked relevance; other metrics could alter preferences.

**Failure signatures**  
- SFT‑only: Expansions are readable but retrieval gains are modest compared to teacher.  
- DPO‑only: Often unstable and underperforms SFT‑only with noisy/insufficient pairs.  
- Overfitting: Student memorizes teacher expansions and generalizes poorly.  
- Language mismatch: English few‑shots for Chinese queries may confuse the student, though robustness is reported.

**First 3 experiments**  
1. **SFT vs. SFT+DPO Comparison**: Train student with SFT only and with SFT+DPO on a held‑out dataset (e.g., DL19); expect higher nDCG@10 for SFT+DPO.  
2. **Preference Threshold Sensitivity**: Vary Δ (0.005, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1) in DPO; expect Δ=0.01 to be optimal per paper.  
3. **Teacher Model Robustness**: Replace DeepSeek with another teacher (e.g., OLMo‑32B); expect similar gains, indicating robustness to teacher choice.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Architecture Generalization**  
   The study only uses Qwen3‑4B as the student. It is unknown whether the method generalizes to other architectures (e.g., Llama, Mistral, encoder‑decoder models). Without systematic comparison, broader applicability remains uncertain.  
   *Resolution*: Train students of varying sizes and types with the same pipeline; compare retrieval performance and latency.

2. **Extension to Iterative Query Expansion**  
   The work focuses on single‑pass generation. It is unclear if the distilled student can be used in multi‑round setups where expansions are refined with retrieval feedback. Iteration could improve effectiveness but increase cost.  
   *Resolution*: Design an iterative extension where the student refines expansions based on retrieved passages, then evaluate the trade‑off between nDCG gain and latency.

3. **Robustness to Noisy Teacher Signals**  
   The pipeline relies entirely on teacher‑generated expansions, which may contain errors or irrelevant content. The impact of such noise on SFT and DPO stages is not analyzed.  
   *Resolution*: Simulate noisy expansions (e.g., add irrelevant sentences) and measure degradation in student retrieval performance.

4. **Multi‑Metric Preference Alignment**  
   DPO preferences are based solely on nDCG@10. This single‑metric focus might neglect other retrieval objectives (e.g., recall, precision). It is unknown if aligning with multiple metrics would yield more robust expansions.  
   *Resolution*: Construct preference pairs using alternative metrics (MAP, MRR) or combinations, then compare performance across a suite of metrics.

## Limitations
- Teacher expansion prompts and sampling parameters are not fully disclosed, complicating exact replication.  
- Preference pairs use BM25 nDCG on training queries, risking overfitting to BM25 and training‑set bias.  
- Evaluation is restricted to TREC DL19‑21 and MIRACL‑zh; broader domain and language applicability remain unverified.

## Confidence
- Overall effectiveness (SFT+DPO approaches teacher, large speedup): **High**  
- Added value of DPO alignment over SFT: **Medium**  
- Generalization to dense retrieval (BGE) and Chinese: **Medium**

## Next Checks
1. **Teacher robustness**: Substitute DeepSeek‑685B with an alternative strong teacher (e.g., GPT‑4o, OLMo‑32B). Retrain the student and compare nDCG@10 on DL19/20/DL21.  
2. **Preference construction sensitivity**: Vary the ΔnDCG threshold (0.005–0.1) and replace nDCG with MAP or Recall in DPO pair selection. Report performance changes across datasets.  
3. **Human‑in‑the‑loop validation**: Sample 100 queries; collect human preference judgments on expansions from SFT‑only and SFT+DPO models; correlate with nDCG@10 to confirm metric alignment.
