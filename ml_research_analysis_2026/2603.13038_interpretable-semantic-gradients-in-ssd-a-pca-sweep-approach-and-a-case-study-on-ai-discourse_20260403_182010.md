# Interpretable Semantic Gradients in SSD: A PCA Sweep Approach and a Case Study on AI Discourse

## Quick Facts
- **ArXiv ID:** 2603.13038
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13038
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Primary result:** The PCA sweep selects dimensionality by jointly optimizing interpretability and stability, revealing a stable Admiration-related semantic gradient in AI discourse at K=15; no robust Rivalry gradient emerges.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a PCA sweep procedure to address the lack of a systematic method for selecting the number of principal components in Supervised Semantic Differential (SSD). By evaluating a range of K values and jointly optimizing for gradient interpretability and stability, the sweep constrains researcher degrees of freedom. Applied to a corpus of AI-related posts, it identifies a parsimonious and interpretable gradient for Admiration narcissism at K=15, contrasting optimistic, collaborative framing with distrustful, derisive discourse. No robust gradient is found for Rivalry. A counterfactual at high dimensionality (K=120) yields semantically diffuse clusters, demonstrating the sweep's ability to preserve psychologically meaningful structure.

## Method Summary
The method extends SSD by adding a PCA sweep over candidate dimensionalities (e.g., K ∈ {1,3,…,119}). For each K, the pipeline: (1) reduces PCA-transformed post embeddings to K dimensions; (2) regresses outcome scores (Admiration, Rivalry) on these projections; (3) back-projects normalized regression coefficients to the original embedding space to define a semantic gradient; (4) retrieves nearest neighbors along the positive and negative poles; (5) clusters neighbors separately (k chosen via silhouette from 2–5); (6) computes interpretability metrics: cluster coherence and alignment with the gradient, aggregated into a size-weighted score; (7) computes stability as the cosine change between consecutive gradients. Interpretability scores are detrended against log-transformed variance explained (residuals z-scored) to avoid overfitting. Both interpretability and stability curves are smoothed (AUCK for interpretability, median filter for stability). A joint score equals the average of smoothed interpretability and stability; the smallest K achieving the maximum joint score is selected.

## Key Results
- At K=15, the Admiration gradient is statistically significant (R²_adj = .19, F=6.32, p<10⁻¹⁰) and semantically coherent: positive pole features optimistic, collaborative AI framing (e.g., “exciting,” “beneficial,” “together”), while negative pole reflects distrust and derision (e.g., “scary,” “cheat,” “control”).
- No robust gradient emerges for Rivalry across all K (best R²_adj = .03, p=.095).
- A counterfactual run at K=120 yields clusters that are semantically diffuse and weakly structured, confirming that the sweep’s parsimonious choice preserves meaningful interpretive organization.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The PCA sweep reduces researcher degrees of freedom by replacing an arbitrary choice of K with a data-driven optimization. It systematically evaluates each candidate K under identical preprocessing, computes joint scores from interpretability and stability diagnostics, and selects the smallest K achieving the maximum joint score. This explicit criterion ties dimensionality selection to SSD’s interpretive goals, assuming that a parsimonious K yielding stable gradients and coherent clusters reflects psychologically meaningful structure.

Detrending interpretability scores prevents overfitting to high-dimensional representations where coherence metrics inflate mechanically with K. The sweep regresses the aggregate interpretability against log-transformed variance explained, using residuals (z-scores) as the true signal. This ensures selected K reflects qualitative improvement beyond mere representation capacity, correcting the positive bias of uncorrected metrics.

Gradient stability across consecutive K values signals that the semantic direction has converged to a robust structure. The sweep computes Δₖ = 1 − cos(β̂ₖ, β̂ₖ₋₁) and smooths it to identify plateaus where the gradient is insensitive to added dimensions. A low, stable Δₖ suggests further dimensions add noise rather than signal, whereas high volatility indicates the gradient captures idiosyncratic variance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Supervised Semantic Differential (SSD)**: A method that uses text embeddings and outcome scores to derive a semantic gradient via regression and back-projection. *Why needed*: The entire sweep builds on SSD’s pipeline (PCA, regression, gradient interpretation). *Quick check*: What is the purpose of back-projecting the regression coefficients to the original embedding space?  
- **PCA for dimensionality reduction**: Technique to reduce high-dimensional embeddings to a lower-dimensional subspace. *Why needed*: PCA is where K is chosen; the sweep innovates by selecting K based on post-PCA interpretability and stability rather than variance thresholds. *Quick check*: How does including more principal components affect the bias-variance trade-off in the subsequent regression model?  
- **Interpretability diagnostics via cluster coherence**: Uses nearest-neighbor clustering and coherence metrics (e.g., average pairwise cosine) to assess whether a gradient yields semantically meaningful word groups. *Why needed*: The sweep’s primary criterion is whether the resulting gradient yields interpretable clusters; coherence alone is insufficient due to monotonic increase with K. *Quick check*: Why is cluster coherence alone insufficient for choosing K, necessitating the detrending step?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Raw text + scores -> Preprocessor (tokenize, embed, SIF-weight, remove top PC) -> PCV aggregator (average document embeddings per author) -> PCA Sweep Loop (for each K: PCA projection -> regression -> normalize coefficients -> back-project -> retrieve neighbors -> cluster -> compute interpretability & stability) -> Diagnostic smoother (apply AUCK to interpretability residuals, median smooth stability) -> Selector (compute joint_score, pick smallest K with max) -> Reporter (output final β̂, clusters, statistics).

Critical path: The PCA Sweep Loop is the central iterative engine; it must refit PCA and regression for every K. All downstream diagnostics depend on its per-K outputs; thus the loop’s efficiency directly determines overall runtime.

Design tradeoffs: (1) Sweeping many K values (e.g., up to 119) is computationally costly; a coarser grid reduces time but may miss optimal plateaus. (2) Detrending assumes a monotonic relationship between variance explained and interpretability; misspecification could mislead selection. (3) Clustering uses silhouette on k∈[2,5] per pole; changing this range affects interpretability scores and stability consistency.

Failure signatures: (1) No clear plateau in joint_score curve (multiple local maxima) → selection ambiguous. (2) Interpretability drop at all K → outcome may not align with semantic structure. (3) Stability never plateaus (Δₖ remains high) → gradient dominated by noise. (4) Regression non-significant at all K → gradient lacks psychological meaning despite sweep’s selection.

First 3 experiments: (1) Sanity check on synthetic data with known latent gradient to verify sweep recovers true K and coherent clusters. (2) Embedding model sensitivity: replicate sweep with different embeddings (e.g., GloVe vs. BERT) to assess model dependence. (3) Heuristic comparison: select K via common rules (e.g., variance >80%, √N) and evaluate resulting clusters’ interpretability/stability against sweep’s choice.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. **Embedding model selection**: How can SSD systematically choose an appropriate base embedding model to ensure gradient stability and interpretability? The current sweep addresses only dimensionality; embedding choice fundamentally shapes the semantic space.
2. **Extending sweep diagnostics**: What framework can extend PCA sweep diagnostics to other SSD parameters such as context window size or aggregation methods? A broader framework for principled model and parameter selection remains open.
3. **Robustness across contexts**: Are the Admiration-related semantic gradients in AI discourse robust across different datasets, languages, and psychological outcomes? The case study uses a single small, English-language corpus; generalizability is unknown.
4. **Causal inference**: Do the observed correlations between Admiration and AI discourse framing reflect causal influences of narcissism on language use? The analysis is correlational; directionality and confounds are unaddressed.

## Limitations
- The AUCK smoothing implementation and clustering algorithm (likely k-means) are unspecified, risking reproducibility.
- The Dolma GloVe embedding model is a preprint and may not be publicly available; alternative embeddings could yield different gradients.
- The corpus is small (N=349) and the null finding for Rivalry may be underpowered.
