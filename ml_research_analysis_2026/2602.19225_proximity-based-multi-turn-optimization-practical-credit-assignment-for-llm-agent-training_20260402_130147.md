# Proximity-Based Multi-Turn Optimization: Practical Credit Assignment for LLM Agent Training
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.19225  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19225  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** ProxMO achieves 90.6% success on ALFWorld with a 1.5B model (vs. GRPO 70.3%) and 87.2% success on WebShop with a 7B model (vs. GRPO 79.2%), with negligible training overhead (+1.09%).

## Executive Summary
Proximity‑Based Multi‑Turn Optimization (ProxMO) addresses the credit assignment problem in multi‑turn reinforcement learning for LLM agents. Existing multi‑turn methods like GRPO suffer from uniform advantage scaling and step‑level grouping artifacts (e.g., singleton degeneracy). ProxMO introduces two complementary mechanisms: an episode‑level success‑rate‑aware modulation that adapts gradient intensity to task difficulty, and a step‑level proximity‑based soft aggregation that uses TF‑IDF similarity to smoothly estimate baselines. Evaluated on ALFWorld and WebShop with Qwen2.5 models (1.5B and 7B), ProxMO substantially outperforms GRPO and GiGPO, showing independent and synergistic benefits while adding almost no computational cost.

## Method Summary
ProxMO integrates into a group‑relative policy optimization setup. For each group of trajectories sharing the same instruction, the method first computes the empirical success rate \(p\) to modulate the episode‑level advantage via a sigmoid‑based scaling function. At the step level, textual states are encoded as TF‑IDF vectors; within the group, pairwise cosine similarities are transformed into soft weights (temperature \(\tau\)) to compute a baseline as the weighted sum of returns from neighboring trajectories. The final advantage combines the modulated episode advantage and a weighted step advantage (balance \(\omega\)). Policy updates use a clipped PPO objective with a KL penalty against a reference policy. The design avoids auxiliary neural networks and aims for minimal overhead relative to standard GRPO.

## Key Results
- **ALFWorld (1.5B):** ProxMO reaches 90.6% success, a 28.9% relative improvement over GRPO (70.3%).  
- **WebShop (7B):** ProxMO achieves 87.2% success versus GRPO’s 79.2% (+10.1% relative).  
- **Ablations:** Both mechanisms contribute independently; their combination yields synergistic gains beyond the sum of parts. Training time increases by only ~1.09%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
- **Success‑rate‑aware modulation:** The group success rate \(p\) estimates task difficulty. The episode advantage \(A_E\) is scaled by \(w(R,p)=1+\beta\cdot f(R,p)\) where \(f\) applies a sigmoid to \(d^\alpha\) (for successes) or \(-p^\alpha\) (for failures). This amplifies signals from hard tasks and suppresses noise in easy ones. *Core assumption:* \(p\) reliably indicates difficulty. *Break condition:* Very small groups make \(p\) noisy, risking erratic weightings.
- **Proximity‑based soft aggregation:** To avoid hard state grouping and singleton degeneracy, step‑level baselines use continuous semantic weights. TF‑IDF vectors represent states; within‑group cosine similarities are converted to soft weights \(w_{ij}\propto\exp(\text{sim}/\tau)\). The baseline \(B_t\) is the weighted sum of returns; step advantage \(A_S = R_t - B_t\). *Core assumption:* Lexical similarity correlates with task‑relevant proximity. *Break condition:* TF‑IDF may fail to capture paraphrastic/contextual semantics, leading to mis‑grouped states.
- **Unified advantage summation:** Final advantage \(A = \tilde{A}_E + \omega A_S\) merges coarse difficulty correction with fine‑grained action valuation. The signals complement each other: episode advantage sets gradient magnitude, step advantage reduces variance. *Core assumption:* The two signals are non‑redundant. *Break condition:* Inappropriate \(\omega\) causes either high variance (\(\omega\) too large) or loss of refinement (\(\omega\) too small).

## Foundational Learning
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Multi‑turn RL method that normalizes advantages within a group but uses uniform scaling and hard step grouping. *Why needed:* ProxMO extends GRPO by adding context‑aware weights and soft grouping. *Quick check:* What are the two key limitations of GRPO’s within‑group normalization in multi‑turn tasks?
- **Credit Assignment in Multi‑Turn RL:** The challenge of attributing sparse, delayed rewards to individual actions across long horizons. *Why needed:* ProxMO’s two‑level advantage design directly tackles this. *Quick check:* Why is credit assignment particularly difficult when rewards are sparse and delayed?
- **TF‑IDF (Term Frequency‑Inverse Document Frequency):** Text representation that weights words by document frequency and inverse corpus frequency. *Why needed:* ProxMO uses TF‑IDF to compute semantic similarity between textual states for soft aggregation. *Quick check:* How does the IDF component reduce the impact of common but non‑discriminative words?

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Environment ↔ Policy LLM ↔ Rollout Buffer → Episode‑level PSC → Step‑level PSA → Advantage Integrator → PPO Trainer → Updated Policy (with Reference Model for KL regularization).

Critical path:
1. Sample \(N\) trajectories per instruction.  
2. Compute returns \(R(\tau)\) and raw episode advantages \(A_E\).  
3. Compute group success rate \(p\); apply PSC to get \(\tilde{A}_E\).  
4. For each step: compute discounted return \(R_t\); encode state via TF‑IDF; compute within‑group similarities → soft weights → baseline \(B_t\) → step advantage \(A_S\).  
5. Integrate advantages \(A = \tilde{A}_E + \omega A_S\).  
6. Perform clipped PPO update with KL penalty.

Design tradeoffs:
- PSC adds only arithmetic; PSA uses TF‑IDF (\(O(L)\) per text length) which is negligible vs. LLM inference.  
- No extra neural networks keeps memory overhead low, but reliance on lexical similarity may miss deeper semantics.  
- Hyperparameters (\(\alpha,\beta,\tau,\omega\)) appear stable across ALFWorld and WebShop but require per‑domain validation.

Failure signatures:
- Extreme \(p\) (near 0 or 1) combined with large \(\alpha\) produces vanishing/exploding PSC weights.  
- Overly sparse TF‑IDF vectors or overly generic vocabularies yield near‑uniform weights, collapsing PSA to a trivial baseline.  
- High variance in the integrated advantage distribution often signals mis‑specified \(\omega\) or strong correlation between \(\tilde{A}_E\) and \(A_S\).

First 3 experiments:
1. **Baseline replication:** Train GRPO on ALFWorld (1.5B) to achieve ~70% success, verifying environment and code correctness.  
2. **Episode‑only isolation:** Set \(\omega=0\) (disable PSA); expect gains especially on tasks with skewed success rates (e.g., *Look*, *Cool*).  
3. **Step‑only isolation:** Disable PSC (\(\beta=0\)) while keeping PSA; expect reduction in singleton‑induced zero‑advantage steps and moderate improvement over GRPO.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Question 1:** Does ProxMO generalize to significantly larger models (>10B parameters) and diverse architectures? *Basis:* Limitations note experiments focused on 1.5B/7B scales. *Unresolved:* Larger models may exhibit different optimization dynamics. *Evidence needed:* Replication on 13B, 34B, 70B+ models across ALFWorld/WebShop.
- **Question 2:** Would replacing TF‑IDF with contextual embeddings improve step‑level proximity aggregation? *Basis:* ProxMO uses TF‑IDF for efficiency but does not explore richer similarity measures. *Unresolved:* TF‑IDF may mis‑capture semantics. *Evidence needed:* Ablate with sentence‑transformer embeddings; measure success rate, stability, and similarity plausibility.
- **Question 3:** What causes the synergy between episode‑level success‑rate modulation and step‑level proximity aggregation? *Basis:* Ablation shows combined gain exceeds sum of individual gains, but the reason is unclear. *Unresolved:* Understanding could reveal complementary roles. *Evidence needed:* Analyze gradient/advantage distributions; conduct controlled experiments varying task difficulty and horizon.
- **Question 4:** Are ProxMO’s hyperparameters (\(\alpha,\beta,\tau,\omega\)) robust across a wide range of multi‑turn environments? *Basis:* Cross‑task consistency demonstrated only on ALFWorld and WebShop. *Unresolved:* Industrial deployment requires minimal tuning. *Evidence needed:* Evaluate on diverse benchmarks (MiniGrid, Crafter, TextWorld, dialogue) with default hyperparameters; measure sensitivity.

## Limitations
- Missing implementation details: exact reward formulation (binary vs. dense) and TF‑IDF preprocessing (tokenization, n‑grams, vocabulary scope) are not described, hindering faithful reproduction.
- The claimed negligible computational overhead (+1.09%) lacks a clear baseline and measurement methodology, making the assertion unsubstantiated.

## Confidence
