# Small Wins Big: Comparing Large Language Models and Domain Fine-Tuned Models for Sarcasm Detection in Code-Mixed Hinglish Text

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2602.21933
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21933
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: Domain-adaptive fine-tuned DistilBERT achieved 84% accuracy, outperforming all LLMs (best few-shot 62.29%) by over 20 percentage points.

## Executive Summary
The study investigates sarcasm detection in code-mixed Hinglish text, a challenging low-resource scenario due to linguistic mixing and limited annotated data. The authors compare zero-shot and few-shot performance of four large language models (Llama 3.1, Mistral, Gemma 3, Phi-4) against a DistilBERT model that is fine-tuned on a small set of LLM-generated (Gemini 2.5 Pro) Hinglish sarcasm examples. The domain-adaptive fine-tuned DistilBERT reaches 84% accuracy, a more than 20-point improvement over the best LLM (62.29% for Llama 3.1 in few-shot). This demonstrates that for niche, data-scarce tasks, task-specific fine-tuning of a compact model can substantially outperform general-purpose LLMs used via prompting.

## Method Summary
The authors use the English "News Headlines Dataset for Sarcasm Detection" (≈18K instances) and "Stanford Sentiment140" (1,935 tweets). They generate synthetic Hinglish translations of a balanced subset using Gemini 2.5 Pro with a carefully designed prompt; manual validation shows about 15% unsatisfactory translations. The data is split into training, fine-tuning, and test sets. A DistilBERT-base-uncased model is first trained on the full English sarcasm training set (100 epochs). It is then sequentially fine-tuned on the Hinglish sarcasm fine-tuning set (2,342 examples). Performance is evaluated on a held-out Hinglish test set. For comparison, four LLMs are evaluated in zero-shot and few-shot settings using a fixed prompt template, with models run via Ollama. Accuracy and F1-score are reported.

## Key Results
- DistilBERT fine-tuned on code-mixed sarcasm: **84% accuracy**.
- Best LLM (Llama 3.1, few-shot): **62.29% accuracy**.
- Domain-adaptive fine-tuning provides **>20 percentage point** gain over general LLM inference.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Domain-specific fine-tuning adapts to Hinglish sarcasm patterns
- **Claim**: Fine-tuning on code-mixed sarcasm data updates the model’s weights to capture morphological mixing, script blending, and pragmatic markers that are absent in general LLMs’ in-context learning.
- **Mechanism**: Weight updates on a small set of code-mixed examples teach the model task-specific features. LLMs in zero/few-shot rely solely on pre-trained knowledge and a few prompt examples, which are insufficient to adapt to the unique distribution of Hinglish sarcasm.
- **Core assumptions**: The synthetic data is of sufficient quality and representativeness; the fine-tuning set size (2,342 examples) is adequate for DistilBERT’s capacity.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract; Table 5 (code-mixed fine-tuning 83.87% vs. best LLM 62.29%); related work on synthetic native samples for humour/sarcasm detection.
- **Break condition**: Synthetic data quality degrades (e.g., >20% unsatisfactory translations) or fine-tuning set is non-representative, leading to performance dropping to LLM levels.

### Mechanism 2: Sentiment transfer learning provides limited benefit for sarcasm
- **Claim**: Sentiment classification supervision alone is insufficient because sarcasm relies on incongruity detection, not mere polarity.
- **Mechanism**: Fine-tuning on sentiment data optimizes the model for positive/negative/neutral classification, which does not emphasize the contextual cues needed for sarcasm. Consequently, representations learned from sentiment are not easily transferable.
- **Core assumptions**: The sentiment dataset lacks sarcastic examples; the transfer protocol (sequential fine-tuning) does not preserve sarcasm-relevant features.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract; Table 5 (English sentiment fine-tuning 53.88%, code-mixed sentiment fine-tuning 58.87%, both << 83.87%); Section 5 explicitly states minimal gains from sentiment data.
- **Break condition**: If the sentiment dataset were larger or contained many sarcastic instances, transfer might improve; otherwise marginal gains persist.

### Mechanism 3: Parameter efficiency of small models suits low-resource adaptation
- **Claim**: DistilBERT’s limited capacity allows effective learning from minimal data via full fine-tuning, whereas large LLMs lack weight updates and require many in-context examples.
- **Mechanism**: With ~66M parameters, DistilBERT’s capacity matches task complexity and fine-tuning data size (2,342 examples), regularizing against overfitting. Full weight updates thoroughly optimize it for sarcasm detection. LLMs (billions of parameters) used in few-shot mode do not update weights; their pre-training may not cover code-mixed sarcasm, and the few-shot context is too small to adapt such large models.
- **Core assumptions**: Sarcasm detection does not demand massive world knowledge; the fine-tuning data covers key linguistic variations.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract; training accuracy convergence (92.75% average); data efficiency experiments (Figures 5 & 6) show high accuracy even with ~1K samples; related work showing fine-tuning LLMs helps but small models succeed with less data.
- **Break condition**: Increased task complexity (e.g., multimodal sarcasm) might cause the small model to underfit; LLMs could surpass with sufficient fine-tuning or more few-shot examples.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Transfer Learning**
  - Why needed: The method relies on transferring a pre-trained DistilBERT to the target sarcasm detection task via fine-tuning, then further adapting to code-mixed data. This explains how a general model becomes specialized with limited labeled examples.
  - Quick check: What are the key differences between fine-tuning a pre-trained model and training a model from scratch, and why might fine-tuning be particularly beneficial when labeled data is scarce?

- **Concept: Code-Mixing**
  - Why needed: The study tackles Hinglish text, which fluidly mixes Hindi and English, introducing challenges like script switching, non-standard grammar, and lexical borrowing. Understanding code-mixing is essential to appreciate why general LLMs (mostly monolingual English) struggle and why domain adaptation is necessary.
  - Quick check: How does code-mixing differ from simple multilingual text, and what specific NLP challenges does it introduce (e.g., for tokenization, syntactic parsing)?

- **Concept: Synthetic Data Generation for Low-Resource NLP**
  - Why needed: Due to scarcity of real code-mixed sarcasm data, the authors used an LLM (Gemini) to generate synthetic Hinglish translations. This concept covers the methodology, benefits, and risks of bootstrapping training data with LLM outputs in low-resource scenarios.
  - Quick check: What are the potential pitfalls of using LLM-generated synthetic data for training, and how might one assess its quality (e.g., through manual annotation as done in the paper)?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**  
Synthetic Data Generation (Gemini 2.5 Pro) -> Data Preprocessing (split, undersample, tokenize) -> DistilBERT English Training -> DistilBERT Code-Mixed Fine-Tuning -> Evaluation on Hinglish Test Set. Parallel: LLM Baselines (Llama 3.1, Mistral, Gemma 3, Phi-4) -> Prompting (zero/few-shot) -> Evaluation.

**Critical Path (to reproduce best result):**  
1. Generate Hinglish translations of the required sarcasm and sentiment subsets using the provided Gemini prompt; manually verify a sample (target <15% unsatisfactory).  
2. Split datasets as per Table 2 (English sarcasm train: 18,760; fine-tune: 2,342; test: 2,344) and tokenize with DistilBERT tokenizer; undersample to balance classes.  
3. Train DistilBERT on the English sarcasm training set for 100 epochs (AdamW, lr=1e-3, batch 32). Expect training accuracy ~92.75%.  
4. Fine-tune the model from step 3 on the code-mixed sarcasm fine-tuning set (2,342 examples) with the same hyperparameters.  
5. Evaluate the fine-tuned model on the held-out code-mixed test set; target accuracy ~84%.  
6. For LLM baselines, run the exact zero-shot and few-shot prompts (Figure 3) on the same test set via Ollama; compute accuracy. Llama 3.1 few-shot should yield ~62.29%.

**Design Tradeoffs:**  
- DistilBERT vs. larger BERT: DistilBERT offers efficiency and sufficient capacity after fine-tuning; trade-off is less pre-trained knowledge but compensated by specialization.  
- Synthetic vs. real data: Synthetic data enables creation but introduces noise (15% unsatisfactory); trade-off between manual validation cost and data quality.  
- Sequential fine-tuning (English → code-mixed) vs. direct code-mixed training: Sequential leverages abundant English data to learn general sarcasm before adapting, reducing need for code-mixed data; risk if English patterns are not transferable.  
- Full fine-tuning vs. parameter-efficient methods (LoRA): Full fine-tuning thoroughly adapts all parameters but may overfit on small data; PEFT could allow larger models but was not explored.

**Failure Signatures:**  
- High unsatisfactory translation rate (>20%) in manual audit: implies poor synthetic data; need prompt refinement or alternative LLM.  
- Overfitting: Very high training accuracy on fine-tuning set (>95%) but low test accuracy (<70%); mitigate by reducing epochs, increasing dropout, or acquiring more data.  
- LLM baseline accuracy much higher than reported (>70%): check for test data leakage into few-shot prompts or prompt formatting errors.  
- Poor performance after English training (≈57%) and after code-mixed fine-tuning (<70%): indicates fine-tuning data non-representative; analyze error patterns (Appendix A.4) for systematic failures (e.g., cultural references).  
- Resource exhaustion: DistilBERT fine-tuning may exceed GPU memory; reduce batch size or use gradient accumulation.

**First 3 Experiments:**  
1. **Baseline English training**: Train DistilBERT on English sarcasm training set, evaluate on English test set. Confirms pipeline; expected accuracy >90%.  
2. **Code-mixed fine-tuning benefit**: Compare model from exp1 (no fine-tuning) vs. after code-mixed sarcasm fine-tuning on code-mixed test set. Expect significant jump (~57% → 84%).  
3. **LLM few-shot replication**: Run Llama 3.1 few-shot evaluation with the provided prompt and same few-shot examples; verify accuracy ~62%.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. To what extent do the reported performance gains of domain-fine-tuned DistilBERT generalize beyond Hinglish to other code-mixed language pairs (e.g., Tamil-English, Bengali-English) with different linguistic mixing patterns?
2. How does the quality and cultural-linguistic appropriateness of the synthetic code-mixed data generated by an LLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) impact the downstream performance of the fine-tuned model, and can this be systematically improved?
3. Why does transfer learning via sentiment classification (as an auxiliary task) yield minimal performance gains for sarcasm detection in code-mixed text, even when the auxiliary data is code-mixed?
4. Would fine-tuning the large language models (LLMs) themselves on the same minimal code-mixed sarcasm data close the performance gap with the fine-tuned DistilBERT, or is the gap primarily due to architectural or inference constraints?
5. How robust are these findings to the use of non-synthetic, naturally occurring code-mixed Hinglish sarcasm data from platforms like Twitter or Instagram, which contain informal orthography, noise, and diverse dialectal variations?

## Limitations
- Synthetic data quality: ~15% unsatisfactory translations; generation scale and filtering details omitted; quality may affect fine-tuning.
- LLM baseline specifications: Model versions/quantizations and few-shot example selection not detailed; replication may be difficult.
- Scope: Results are limited to Hinglish; generalizability to other code-mixed languages or tasks is unknown.

## Confidence
- Domain fine-tuning beats LLMs: Medium
- Sentiment transfer insufficient: Medium
- Small models are data-efficient: Medium-High
