# From Oracle to Noisy Context: Mitigating Contextual Exposure Bias in Speech-LLMs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.24034  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24034  
- **Reference count:** 40  
- **Primary result:** Unified framework with Teacher Error Knowledge, Context Dropout, and DPO reduces WER by up to 7.5% relative (5.59%→5.17%) on TED-LIUM 3 and improves robustness to irrelevant context and cross-domain generalization.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses contextual exposure bias in Speech-LLMs, where training uses perfect oracle conversation history but inference relies on error-prone ASR hypotheses, causing a train-test mismatch. It proposes a unified framework combining three strategies: (i) Teacher Error Knowledge—using Whisper large-v3 hypotheses as training history to simulate real errors; (ii) Context Dropout—randomly masking history during training to reduce over-reliance; (iii) Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)—refining with preference pairs from hard negatives to suppress error propagation. On TED-LIUM 3 with a two-utterance context, the framework lowers WER from 5.59% (oracle-trained) to 5.17% and shows minimal degradation under irrelevant-context attacks. Cross-domain zero-shot evaluation on LibriSpeech further demonstrates consistent gains, with DPO achieving 6.90% average WER at a 5-utterance context window.

## Method Summary
The approach consists of three sequential training stages. Stage 1 trains a context-free base model by learning a modality adapter (MLP) from a frozen Whisper encoder to a frozen Vicuna-7B LLM using standard ASR loss. Stage 2 performs supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for contextual ASR: the history context is constructed from Whisper large-v3 hypotheses of preceding utterances (Teacher Error Knowledge) and is randomly dropped with probability 0.5 (Context Dropout). The SFT updates the adapter and a LoRA adapter for the LLM. Stage 3 applies Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) on a subset of hard negatives—training examples where the SFT model’s WER exceeds 20% under predicted history. DPO trains a separate LoRA adapter while freezing previous modules, using a pairwise loss to favor ground truth over erroneous outputs. At inference, both LoRA adapters are combined with the DPO adapter scaled by γ=0.25 to balance robustness and accuracy.

## Key Results
- On TED-LIUM 3 with two-utterance context: Whisper-based SFT achieves 5.47% WER (vs. 5.59% oracle-trained), and DPO further reduces to 5.17%.
- Under irrelevant-context attacks, DPO degrades minimally (5.17% → 5.63%), whereas baselines show larger drops.
- Cross-domain zero-shot on LibriSpeech (5-utterance context): DPO yields 6.90% average WER, outperforming no-context baseline (7.31%) and SFT-only models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1: Teacher Error Knowledge
**Claim:** Training with error-prone history reduces the train-test mismatch in the context channel by aligning the training distribution of historical text with inference conditions.  
**Mechanism:** Instead of conditioning on oracle transcripts Y_i, the model is trained on history C_t built from Whisper large-v3 hypotheses Ŷ_i^Whisper. This exposes the model to realistic ASR errors during training, forcing it to learn to rely less on perfect context and more on acoustic cues or to correct common error patterns.  
**Core assumption:** The error distribution of a strong teacher ASR (Whisper) is sufficiently similar to the error distribution the model will produce at inference time.  
**Evidence:** The paper shows that using Whisper context (instead of GT) during SFT yields lower WER under predicted history (e.g., 5.47% vs. 5.59% in Table 1). [section 2.3.1] describes the construction.  
**Break condition:** If the teacher's error profile is highly dissimilar to the model's own errors (e.g., different accents, noise), simulated context may misalign and harm performance.

### Mechanism 2: Context Dropout
**Claim:** Context Dropout regularizes the model to prevent over-reliance on potentially erroneous historical context, improving robustness when context is missing, noisy, or misleading.  
**Mechanism:** With probability p_drop, the textual history C_t is masked (C̃_t = ∅) during training. This forces the model to maintain strong acoustic-only transcription and not treat context as a required shortcut. It learns to dynamically weight the trustworthiness of the provided context.  
**Core assumption:** The model over-fits to the context channel during SFT because oracle context provides a strong, consistent signal unavailable at inference.  
**Evidence:** Table 1 shows that for Whisper-trained models, adding dropout (p=0.5) improves WER under predicted history for context lengths N≥2. [section 4.1] reports that Contrain=Whisper consistently benefits from p=0.5 dropout for N>=2.  
**Break condition:** If p_drop is set too high, the model may never learn to utilize helpful context, underperforming when context is accurate.

### Mechanism 3: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)
**Claim:** DPO on hard negatives explicitly suppresses the model's tendency to propagate errors from the context channel by shaping its generation preferences away from failure modes.  
**Mechanism:** For utterances where the SFT model produces high-WER outputs Y^-, DPO constructs preference pairs (Y^+, Y^-) under identical (X_t, C_t, P). It updates the policy to increase the likelihood gap between ground truth Y^+ and erroneous Y^- relative to a frozen reference model, directly teaching the model to avoid repeating specific contextual errors.  
**Core assumption:** The model's failure modes under noisy context are identifiable and can be reversed by a pairwise comparison objective, which is more targeted than SFT's imitation learning.  
**Evidence:** DPO consistently improves WER across configurations, while a second SFT pass (SFT2) often degrades performance. Under irrelevant context attacks, DPO models show the smallest degradation gap. The inference scaling factor γ=0.25 balances robustness and accuracy, indicating DPO's influence must be tempered. [section 4.3 & Table 2].  
**Break condition:** If hard negative mining selects failures not primarily caused by context misuse, DPO may not address the core bias. Over-indexing on a curated set could harm generalization.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Contextual ASR**  
  - **Why needed here:** The problem is utterance-level contextual ASR, where each utterance's transcription depends on a window of preceding transcripts. Understanding this sequential conditioning is prerequisite.  
  - **Quick check question:** In the paper's formalism, what two elements define the input to the recognizer for utterance t?

- **Concept: Exposure Bias (Train-Test Mismatch)**  
  - **Why needed here:** The core problem is contextual exposure bias—a mismatch where training uses perfect context but inference uses error-prone context. Distinguishing this from standard exposure bias is key.  
  - **Quick check question:** How does the context channel's train-test mismatch differ from the standard exposure bias in auto-regressive generation?

- **Concept: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**  
  - **Why needed here:** DPO is the alignment method used to mitigate bias. Understanding its pairwise objective and how it differs from SFT explains why it suppresses error propagation.  
  - **Quick check question:** What does the DPO objective m=β(Δθ - Δr) measure, and why is a separate reference policy π_r used?

- **Concept: LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**  
  - **Why needed here:** The architecture uses two separate LoRA adapters (SFT and DPO) with independent inference scaling (γ). This is critical for decoupled preference optimization and inference-time control.  
  - **Quick check question:** Why does the paper use a separate LoRA block for DPO instead of continuing SFT on the same adapter?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Speech Encoder (frozen Whisper large-v3) -> Modality Adapter (trainable MLP) -> LLM Backbone (frozen Vicuna-7B-v1.5). Additionally, two LoRA adapters (SFT and DPO) are injected into the LLM. The History Constructor (Whisper large-v3) is used offline to generate context C_t during training.

**Critical path:**  
Three-stage sequential training: (1) Train MLP projector on context-free data. (2) SFT with noisy history (Whisper hypotheses) and Context Dropout, updating SFT LoRA & MLP. (3) DPO on hard negatives using a separate DPO LoRA, freezing SFT LoRA & MLP & LLM.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Teacher choice: Whisper's error profile shapes training noise; other ASR systems could be used.  
- Dropout probability p_drop: 0.5 selected empirically; lower may not regularize enough, higher may damage context learning.  
- DPO inference scaling γ: 0.25 chosen to mitigate reward over-optimization; higher risks degradation, lower loses robustness.  
- Hard negative threshold: WER > 20% heuristic; selection stringency robust but defines DPO training subset.

**Failure signatures:**  
- Catastrophic context reliance: High WER under irrelevant-context attacks and poor performance with GT context + no dropout on longer contexts (e.g., 12.62% at N=3).  
- Under-utilization of context: No improvement or degradation when adding context (e.g., 8.15% > 7.89% no-context baseline).  
- DPO over-optimization: Performance collapse at high γ (e.g., γ=0.625 yields ~50% WER).

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. Train base context-free model (Stage 1) and verify reasonable WER on TED-LIUM 3 without history.  
2. Perform SFT with Whisper context and p_drop=0.5 (Stage 2). Compare WER against no-context baseline and GT-context variant under predicted history.  
3. Apply DPO refinement (Stage 3) to best SFT checkpoint. Compare against additional SFT (SFT2). Tune γ on dev set for robustness-accuracy trade-off.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can Speech-LLMs effectively handle contextual ASR in multi-speaker overlapping scenarios? The paper notes this as an unaddressed limitation; overlapping speech likely requires specialized architectural modifications.  
2. Does diversifying the sources of "Teacher Error Knowledge" beyond Whisper improve robustness? Using only Whisper biases training errors toward that model's failure patterns; multi-teacher or adversarial error generation could yield more robust models.  
3. What is the underlying mechanism by which the DPO LoRA scaling factor γ modulates the trade-off between accuracy and robustness? The paper empirically selects γ=0.25 but does not analyze internal dynamics (e.g., logit distributions, attention).  
4. Why does DPO consistently improve cross-domain generalization while SFT with noisy history often fails? The paper hypothesizes DPO penalizes history error repetition but lacks analysis of learned representations or decision boundaries.

## Limitations
- Teacher Error Knowledge assumes Whisper's error distribution aligns with the Speech-LLM's own iterative decoding errors; mismatch could harm training.  
- Context Dropout uses a fixed p_drop=0.5; optimality may vary with domain, context length, or base model capacity.  
- Hard-negative mining relies on a WER > 20% threshold without clarifying utterance/session-level granularity; selected errors may not predominantly stem from context misuse.

## Next Checks

2. **Dropout Sensitivity Sweep:** Ablate p_drop across [0.0, 1.0] for context windows N=1–5 on TED-LIUM dev. Plot clean WER and irrelevant-context attack WER to locate optimal p_drop regimes and detect under-utilization at high dropout.  
3. **Hard-Error Categorization:** Manually annotate a sample of DPO training instances (WER>20%). Classify errors as “context-induced” vs. “acoustic-only.” Compute the context-induced proportion; if <50%, revise mining heuristics.
