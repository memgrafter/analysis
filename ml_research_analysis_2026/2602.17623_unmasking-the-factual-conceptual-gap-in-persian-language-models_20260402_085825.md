# Unmasking the Factual-Conceptual Gap in Persian Language Models
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2602.17623
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17623
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Primary result:** Persian LLMs exhibit a consistent 21.3% drop from factual retrieval to scenario reasoning, severe acquiescence bias, and continuous pretraining amplifies this bias, indicating a failure to internalize cultural schemas.

## Executive Summary
Persian language models (LLMs) are often assumed to internalize cultural knowledge, but it remains unclear whether they truly understand cultural schemas or merely memorize facts. This paper introduces DivanBench, a diagnostic benchmark of 315 questions covering Persian superstitions and customs, to evaluate seven mid-sized Persian LLMs across factual recall, belief verification, and situational reasoning.

Results reveal a persistent factual-conceptual gap (average 21.3% performance drop) and severe acquiescence bias, where models readily affirm culturally positive statements but fail to reject violations. Strikingly, continuous monolingual pretraining—intended to improve cultural fluency—exacerbates bias, and larger models enhance memorization but not reasoning. These findings indicate that current training paradigms fail to capture context-sensitive cultural schemas, calling for objectives beyond standard language modeling.

## Method Summary
The study constructs DivanBench, a manually curated benchmark covering 81 Persian cultural concepts. It includes 100 factual multiple-choice questions, 162 binary verification statements arranged in 81 positive/negative pairs, and 53 scenario-based multiple-choice questions that test conditional application of norms.

Seven Persian-capable LLMs (7–12B parameters) are evaluated zero-shot with five diverse system prompts. Responses are generated using temperature=0.1 and top-p=0.9, and answer extraction is performed by GPT-4o-mini. The primary metrics are task-specific accuracy, acquiescence bias (acceptance rate of positive statements minus rejection rate of negative statements), and the factual-conceptual gap (accuracy on factual MCQs minus accuracy on scenario MCQs). A key comparison is made between the base Llama3.1-8B model and its Persian-continually-pretrained variant Dorna2-8B to isolate the effects of monolingual adaptation.

## Key Results
- All models show an average 21.3% performance drop from factual retrieval to scenario reasoning, demonstrating a factual-conceptual gap that persists across architectures.
- Severe acquiescence bias is observed: six of seven models accept appropriate behaviors 84–92% of the time but reject violations only 19–48%, yielding bias scores up to +65 percentage points.
- Continuous Persian pretraining (Llama3.1→Dorna2) amplifies bias: acceptance increases by 28pp while rejection accuracy decreases by 43pp, a “Persian pretraining paradox” where monolingual data harms cultural discernment.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Models exhibit severe *acquiescence bias* due to surface-level pattern-matching on culturally salient keywords rather than reasoning about normative rules.
- **Mechanism:** Persian training corpora overwhelmingly associate cultural concepts (e.g., *taarof*, *esfand*) with positive, affirming contexts. Models learn a distributional shortcut: presence of such keywords triggers high probability for "yes/appropriate" responses regardless of scenario details, creating an asymmetry where violations are not recognized.
- **Core assumption:** Models rely on lexical co-occurrence heuristics instead of building context-sensitive schemas for cultural norms.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "severe acquiescence bias where 6 of 7 models show high acceptance of appropriate behaviors (84–92%) but fail to reject violations (19–48%)"
  - [section 4.2] Table 5 shows acceptance vs. rejection gaps up to +65 percentage points; paired design isolates this asymmetry.
  - [corpus] Neighbors like *PBBQ: A Persian Bias Benchmark* (Arxiv:2510.19616) and *PerCul* (Arxiv:2502.07459) indicate this bias pattern is a recognized challenge in Persian LLM evaluation, though DivanBench's paired design specifically isolates it.
- **Break condition:** If models showed symmetric high accuracy on both positive and negative scenarios, the pattern-matching hypothesis would be weakened.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** *Continuous Persian pretraining* amplifies acquiescence bias and degrades rejection accuracy, contradicting the assumption that monolingual adaptation improves cultural understanding.
- **Mechanism:** Additional Persian data reinforces the affirmative-heavy distribution of cultural concepts in the base model's training data. This further entrenches keyword-triggered acceptance while overriding any inherent "skeptical" or discriminative tendencies from the original instruction tuning, leading to higher acceptance rates but lower violation detection.
- **Core assumption:** Monolingual data scaling without curriculum or negative/contrastive examples exacerbates existing distributional biases rather than teaching conditional logic.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "continuous Persian pretraining (comparing Llama3.1-8B to Dorna2-8B) amplifies this bias, reducing rejection accuracy by 43 percentage points"
  - [section 4.2] Table 3: Llama3.1 rejection accuracy 73% → Dorna2 30% (-43pp); acceptance 63% → 91% (+28pp); factual accuracy slightly declined (-7pp).
  - [corpus] Limited direct evidence; neighboring papers focus on bias detection (*PBBQ*) or other benchmarks, but do not study *continuous pretraining effects* on reasoning. This mechanism is primarily inferred from the paper's controlled comparison.
- **Break condition:** If a Persian-tuned model showed improved rejection accuracy or a narrowed factual-conceptual gap relative to its base, the paradox claim would be falsified.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** A consistent *factual-conceptual gap* indicates that memorized cultural *facts* and applied cultural *schemas* engage distinct, non-transferring learning mechanisms.
- **Mechanism:** Factual retrieval can be achieved through associative memory (keyword-to-answer mapping). Scenario reasoning requires conditional, context-sensitive application of norms (e.g., *taarof* depends on interaction turns, social hierarchy). Current training objectives and data (text corpora) provide strong signal for the former but weak or no signal for the latter, which typically requires situated social learning.
- **Core assumption:** Cultural schemas, as defined in cognitive anthropology (Strauss & Quinn), cannot be fully derived from text statistics alone; they encode implicit, context-bound rules.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "all models show a 21% performance gap between retrieving factual knowledge and applying it in scenarios"
  - [section 4.2] Table 2 and Figure 3 show this gap persists across all models, including the largest (Gemma3-12B: 87% factual vs 66% scenario).
  - [section 4.3] "The consistent 21 percent performance drop... operationalizes the distinction between cultural facts (retrievable knowledge) and cultural schemas (situational rules)."
  - [corpus] Related work like *Nunchi-Bench* (Korean superstitions, Arxiv:2507.04014) and *PerCul* (story-driven Persian, Arxiv:2502.07459) also note difficulties in scenario-based cultural reasoning, supporting the generality of the gap.
- **Break condition:** If a model showed no significant gap (e.g., <5%) between factual and scenario performance on culturally bound concepts, it would challenge the claim that these are fundamentally separate mechanisms.

## Foundational Learning
### Cultural Schema Theory (Strauss & Quinn)
- **Why needed here:** Distinguishes between *cultural facts* (e.g., "What is *taarof*?") and *cultural schemas* (e.g., "When is *taarof* appropriate?"). Schema theory explains why factual accuracy does not guarantee reasoning—schemas are implicit, context-dependent rules learned through social interaction, not explicit facts.
- **Quick check question:** Can you give an example from your own culture where knowing a fact ("We celebrate New Year with a special table") is different from knowing when and how to apply the associated schema (e.g., specific rituals, guest interactions, taboo behaviors during the celebration)?

### Acquiescence Bias in LLMs
- **Why needed here:** The primary failure mode is systematic asymmetry: models say "yes" to culturally themed statements too readily. Understanding this known LLM pathology (yes-saying) is crucial for interpreting the paired verification task results and diagnosing pattern-matching.
- **Quick check question:** How would you design a multiple-choice question to detect acquiescence bias for a non-cultural concept (e.g., a common-sense physics statement), and what would a biased pattern look like?

### Continuous Pretraining & Distributional Shift
- **Why needed here:** The "Persian Pretraining Paradox" finding hinges on understanding that further pretraining on domain-specific data can shift a model's internal representations and response priors. This explains why Dorna2 became more "culturally fluent" in acceptance but less discerning.
- **Quick check question:** If you continuously pretrain a model on a corpus that contains 90% affirmative sentences about Topic X, what probable effect would this have on the model's responses to neutral/negative questions about Topic X, assuming the base model had a balanced prior?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
DivanBench → Persian LLMs (7 models) → Metrics (task accuracy, acquiescence bias, factual-conceptual gap) → Evaluation Protocol (zero-shot, temperature=0.1, top-p=0.9, 5 system prompts, GPT-4o-mini answer extraction)

### Critical Path
1. **Data Construction:** Manually author culturally insider scenarios ensuring unambiguity for insiders but plausible distractors for outsiders. Create paired positive/negative statements for binary task.
2. **Controlled Evaluation:** Run all models on all tasks with prompt variations to isolate task-type effects from prompt sensitivity.
3. **Comparative Analysis:** (a) Compute bias (accept-reject) to detect pattern-matching. (b) Compare base vs. adapted models to isolate pretraining effects. (c) Compute factual-scenario gap per model to measure schema transfer failure.
4. **Mechanism Inference:** Link observed patterns (bias amplification, gap persistence) to hypothesized mechanisms (distributional shortcuts, separate learning targets).

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Manual curation vs. scalability:** Hand-written scenarios ensure cultural validity and precise control over confounds but limit coverage and introduce author bias. Automated generation would risk losing nuance and insider validity.
- **Paired binary design vs. absolute accuracy:** The paired task optimally measures *discernment* (bias) but does not yield a standalone "cultural correctness" score. Absolute scenario MCQ accuracy is reported but confounded by question difficulty.
- **Model size control:** Selecting 7B-12B models enables fair comparison of architecture/training effects but limits conclusions about scaling laws (e.g., would >50B models close the gap?).

### Failure Signatures
1. **High Acquiescence Bias (>+40pp):** Model accepts >85% of positive statements but rejects <40% of negative ones. Signature: performance asymmetry in paired binary task.
2. **Factual-Conceptual Gap >15%:** Model excels on factual MCQs (>80%) but scenario MCQs drop by >15pp. Signature: large delta in Table 2.
3. **Pretraining Degradation:** Persian-adapted variant shows *worse* rejection accuracy and/or *larger* bias than its base model. Signature: negative Δ in rejection or positive Δ in bias from Table 3.
4. **Prompt Instability:** High standard deviation (>5pp) across the 5 system prompts suggests the model's responses are fragile to framing, indicating lack of robust internalized knowledge.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Replicate Acquiescence Bias Measurement:** Run all models on the 162 binary verification statements (81 pairs). Compute Acc(pos), Acc(neg), and bias. Verify that 6+ models show bias >+40pp. This confirms the primary failure mode.
2. **Isolate Pretraining Effect:** Run both Llama3.1-8B and Dorna2-8B on *all* DivanBench tasks. Compute Δ metrics. Expect to replicate rejection accuracy drop (~-43pp) and acceptance increase (~+28pp) while factual accuracy changes minimally.
3. **Validate Factual-Conceptual Gap per Model:** For each model, calculate `Gap = Acc(Factual MCQ) - Acc(Scenario MCQ)`. Verify mean gap ~21% and that no model's gap is statistically indistinguishable from zero (given prompt variation). This confirms the gap is systematic, not noise.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Is the persistent 21% factual-conceptual gap fundamentally a limitation of model capacity (e.g., parameter count) or does it indicate that cultural schemas require qualitatively different training objectives and data beyond standard language modeling?
- **Basis in paper:** (inferred) The paper establishes the gap empirically across all models but explicitly states that "cultural facts and schemas behave as independent objectives" and that "different learning mechanisms are needed." The limitations section also notes the study's narrow model size range (7–12B) prevents disentangling capacity limitations from data/objective issues.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper demonstrates the gap's consistency but cannot determine whether scaling model size or changing training paradigms would close it. The distinction between memorization (facts) and reasoning (schemas) remains theoretical without a causal solution.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic evaluation of much larger models (e.g., 30B–70B+) on DivanBench, combined with targeted experiments where models are trained with explicit schema-oriented objectives (e.g., counterfactual scenario training, reinforcement learning on rejection accuracy) versus pure scale.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What specific properties of Persian training corpora cause continuous monolingual pretraining to dramatically amplify acquiescence bias (e.g., Dorna2’s 43pp rejection accuracy drop), and how can we design pretraining mixtures to avoid reinforcing surface-level pattern-matching?
- **Basis in paper:** (explicit) The "Persian Pretraining Paradox" finding shows Dorna2's bias increase. The analysis hypothesizes that "Persian corpora contain overwhelmingly affirmative mentions of traditions," creating a distributional bias toward acceptance.
- **Why unresolved:** The hypothesis is plausible but not verified. The paper lacks analysis of the actual data distribution (e.g., ratio of positive/negative cultural statements in pretraining data) and cannot isolate whether the effect is due to data content, data volume, or the continuous pretraining recipe itself.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Computational analysis of the Persian pretraining data (e.g., analyzing sentiment and entailment around cultural keywords), coupled with controlled pretraining experiments where the proportion of positive/negative cultural examples is varied to measure causal impact on bias.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does the observed inverse relationship between model size and cultural discernment (e.g., Gemma3-12B’s high factual accuracy but extreme +61% bias) hold monotonically at larger scales, or is there a threshold where reasoning improves?
- **Basis in paper:** (explicit) Finding 4 states: "Model size improves memorization but not reasoning, and larger models may amplify pattern-matching." The limitations explicitly state the study's size band (7–12B) limits conclusions about scaling.
- **Why unresolved:** With only one model >10B (Gemma3-12B), the trend is anecdotal. It is unclear if the pattern (factual up, bias up) continues beyond 12B, reverses with advanced alignment (e.g., RLHF), or plateaus. The paper cannot extrapolate scaling laws for cultural schemas.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluating a systematic family of models (e.g., 7B, 13B, 34B, 70B, 100B+) from the same lineage (e.g., Llama/Dorna series) on DivanBench to trace the trajectory of factual accuracy vs. acquiescence bias vs. scenario reasoning as scale increases.

### Open Question 4
- **Question:** How do intra-Persian cultural variations (regional, generational, socioeconomic) affect the validity of a single "canonical" benchmark like DivanBench, and to what extent do the observed failures reflect the model's inability to capture this heterogeneity?
- **Basis in paper:** (inferred) The limitations section acknowledges that the manually curated dataset reflects "authors’ judgments about what counts as the 'canonical' interpretation" and may underrepresent variation. The benchmark assumes unambiguous answers for "cultural insiders," which may not hold across diverse Iranian communities.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper treats Persian cultural concepts as monolithic for diagnostic purposes but does not measure performance variance across concepts that are known to have regional variations (e.g., specific Nowruz customs, taarof interpretations). It’s unclear whether model failures are due to missing the core schema or missing regional exceptions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A follow-up study where each DivanBench item is annotated by a diverse panel of Persian speakers from different regions/backgrounds to quantify agreement. Items with low consensus could be analyzed separately to see if models fail more on contested versus consensus items.

### Open Question 5
- **Question:** Is the severe acquiescence bias primarily caused by imbalanced training data (more positive than negative cultural contexts), or is it an inherent artifact of the autoregressive next-token prediction objective combined with culturally-themed prompt distributions used in instruction tuning?
- **Basis in paper:** (inferred) The analysis attributes bias to "distributional patterns in Persian training data" where cultural keywords co-occur with positive contexts. However, the paper also notes that Llama3.1-8B’s initial skeptical tone (negative bias) was erased by Persian pretraining, suggesting sensitivity to the pretraining distribution. No ablation isolates data distribution from objective/architecture effects.
- **Why unresolved:** The experiment comparing Llama3.1 vs. Dorna2 confounds data distribution with the continuous pretraining process (which also changes the model’s internal representations and possibly its "prior"). It cannot disentangle whether bias arises from (a) the content of Persian text, (b) the volume of Persian text relative to other data, or (c) a fundamental tendency of LLMs to affirm culturally salient patterns.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Controlled pretraining runs where a base model is further trained on (i) a balanced dataset of positive/negative cultural scenarios, (ii) an imbalanced dataset mimicking real Persian web text, and (iii) a synthetic dataset where cultural keywords are decoupled from sentiment. Measuring bias changes across these conditions would isolate the causal factor.

## Limitations
- Exact configuration of the GPT-4o-mini answer extraction agent (model version, extraction prompt, error handling) is unreported, introducing potential noise in metric computation.
- The correctness of binary positive/negative pair alignments in the released dataset cannot be automatically verified; misalignment could bias the acquiescence bias metric.
- Causal inferences about continuous pretraining rely on a single model pair (Llama3.1→Dorna2); lack of replication with other Persian-adapted models limits generalizability.
