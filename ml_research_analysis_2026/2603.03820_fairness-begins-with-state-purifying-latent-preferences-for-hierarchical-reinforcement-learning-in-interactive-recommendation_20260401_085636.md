# Fairness Begins with State: Purifying Latent Preferences for Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning in Interactive Recommendation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.03820
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03820
- **Reference Count:** 32
- **Primary Result:** DSRM-HRL breaks the accuracy–fairness trade-off by purifying corrupted user states via diffusion and using hierarchical RL to decouple long-term fairness from short-term engagement, achieving a superior Pareto frontier on KuaiRec and KuaiRand-Pure.

## Executive Summary
Interactive recommender systems face a "rich-get-richer" feedback loop where observed user states are corrupted by popularity bias, misleading reinforcement learning (RL) agents. This paper proposes DSRM-HRL, a framework that first purifies the user state using a diffusion-based Denoising State Representation Module (DSRM) to recover latent preferences. A hierarchical RL policy then separates objectives: a high-level manager enforces long-term exposure fairness while a low-level worker optimizes short-term engagement on the purified state. Evaluated on simulators KuaiRec and KuaiRand-Pure, DSRM-HRL achieves significant gains in both utility (e.g., +21.1% Interaction Length) and fairness (e.g., Absolute Difference 0.008) over baselines, with ablation studies confirming the necessity of both purification and hierarchical decoupling.

## Method Summary
DSRM-HRL operates in two stages. First, a diffusion model (DSRM) is pre-trained to invert popularity-driven corruption in user states. It treats the noisy state as a forward-diffused sample and iteratively projects it back onto a low-entropy latent preference manifold. Second, a hierarchical RL agent is trained with the purified state as input. A high-level manager policy outputs dynamic fairness constraints (e.g., weighting factors) at a low frequency, while a low-level worker policy uses these constraints to score items, balancing accuracy and fairness on a per-step basis. The DSRM is frozen during RL training to maintain stable denoising.

## Key Results
- On KuaiRec (session cap 30), DSRM-HRL increases average Interaction Length by 21.1% (26.600 vs. 21.967) compared to the best fairness-aware baseline and reduces Absolute Difference (exposure imbalance) to 0.008.
- The method achieves a superior Pareto frontier between utility (Len, Rcum) and fairness (AD) on both KuaiRec and KuaiRand-Pure, outperforming general RL methods (high AD) and other fairness-aware methods (lower Len).
- Ablation studies confirm both DSRM purification and hierarchical decoupling are necessary; removing either component degrades the Pareto performance.
- Training curves show DSRM-HRL converges more smoothly and stably than flat RL or HRL without DSRM.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Observed user states are corrupted by a systematic, popularity-driven noise (`𝜁_pop`) that obscures the true latent preference manifold, misleading RL agents into a "rich-get-richer" loop.
- **Mechanism:** DSRM uses a diffusion model’s reverse process to perform iterative probabilistic projection of the noisy state (`˜s_t`) back onto the low-entropy manifold (`M`), recovering a clean preference signal (`ˆs_t`). It models the corruption as a forward diffusion process and inverts it.
- **Core assumption:** True preferences lie on a low-dimensional manifold, and popularity bias is a structured, non-Gaussian corruption invertible via diffusion.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] state contamination; [section 3.1] spurious correlation `R² > 0.85` between popularity and reward; [section 4.2] formalization of diffusion as manifold projection; [corpus] related works on representation-level bias but not diffusion-based inversion.
- **Break condition:** Over-diffusion (too many steps) smooths away genuine preference nuances, collapsing the manifold (evidenced in RQ3).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The conflict between long-term fairness and short-term engagement is unsolvable by a monolithic policy due to gradient interference, but is resolved by hierarchical decoupling where a high-level manager sets dynamic constraints for a low-level worker.
- **Mechanism:** A high-level policy (`πʰ`) optimizes a long-horizon objective (`rʰ_t = r_t + λ·Fair(L_t)`) to output a control variable `z_t = [ω_acc, ω_fair]`. The low-level policy (`πˡ`) then optimizes `Ψ(i; ˆs_t, z_t) = ω_acc·Sim(ˆs_t, e_i) - ω_fair·log(pop_i)` under this constraint, enabling simultaneous pursuit on different timescales.
- **Core assumption:** Fairness is a macroscopic, trajectory-level goal parameterizable as a time-varying constraint for a micro-level optimizer.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] hierarchical decoupling description; [section 4.3] constrained optimization formulation; [section 5.6] HRL (even without DSRM) shows more stable convergence than flat RL; [corpus] hierarchical/constraint-based multi-objective RL work supports the approach.
- **Break condition:** Manager constraint `z_t` becomes misaligned with worker’s feasible set, causing either fairness violation or perpetual engagement sacrifice.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The synergistic combination of diffusion-based state purification and hierarchical policy decoupling is necessary and sufficient to break the accuracy–fairness trade-off; neither alone suffices.
- **Mechanism:** DSRM provides a decision-relevant state (`ˆs_t`) where long-tail utility is disentangled from popularity noise. HRL then uses this clean signal to dynamically balance objectives without destructive interference. The purified state makes the fairness constraint meaningful, while HRL makes it dynamically adaptable.
- **Core assumption:** The trade-off is an emergent artifact of two interacting failures: a corrupted state and a monolithic policy. Solving only one leaves the other dominant.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] superior Pareto frontier; [section 5.3] ablation table shows FLAT (DSRM+single policy) and HRL (no DSRM) both underperform full `+DSRM` (HRL+DSRM); [section 3.2] state purification alone shifts Pareto but HRL needed for optimal decoupled control; [corpus] other fairness methods use direct list adjustment, not this combination.
- **Break condition:** If DSRM over-denoises (no true preference signal), HRL worker has no utility to optimize. If HRL sets improper constraints, worker reverts to popularity exploitation even on clean states.

## Foundational Learning
- **Concept: Diffusion Models for Denoising**
  - *Why needed here:* To model the non-linear, structured corruption of user states and perform non-parametric manifold projection. Standard autoencoders or linear filters are insufficient for the complex, data-dependent noise.
  - *Quick check question:* What is the key difference between using diffusion for *generation* (e.g., DiffuRec) and for *state purification* (DSRM), and why does that difference matter for sequential decision-making?
- **Concept: Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL)**
  - *Why needed here:* To temporally separate the fast timescale of user engagement (steps/turn) from the slow timescale of ecosystem fairness (sessions/episodes), preventing immediate reward from drowning out the delayed objective.
  - *Quick check question:* In DSRM-HRL, what specific information does the high-level manager (`πʰ`) provide to the low-level worker (`πˡ`), and how does this differ from simply shaping the reward function?
- **Concept: Interactive Recommendation & Exposure Bias**
  - *Why needed here:* To understand the *dynamics* of the "rich-get-richer" loop. Actions (recommendations) affect future states (exposure history), creating a self-reinforcing bias cycle that corrupts the MDP's state definition.
  - *Quick check question:* If you could perfectly observe the *true* latent preference (`s*_t`) at each step, would you still need hierarchical RL? Why or why not?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:**
  Raw History -> State Encoder -> Noisy State `˜s_t` -> DSRM (K reverse steps) -> Purified State `ˆs_t` -> High-Level Manager (every `c` steps) -> `z_t = [ω_acc, ω_fair]` -> Low-Level Worker -> Action (Top-K items) -> Simulator -> Updated History
- **Critical path:** `Raw History → State Encoder → DSRM (Purify) → HRL Manager (get z_t) → HRL Worker (act) → Simulator → update History`. DSRM must be pre-trained and frozen before HRL learning ("Purify-then-Decouple").
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Diffusion steps (K):** Low K (<10) → insufficient denoising; high K (>100) → over-smoothing, loss of preference nuance.
  - **Manager update frequency:** Too frequent → similar to flat RL; too infrequent → `z_t` unresponsive to state changes.
  - **Pre-trained DSRM frozen?** Fine-tuning with RL gradients risks catastrophic forgetting; paper freezes it.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High AD + Low Len:** Classic "rich-get-richer"; likely DSRM failed or is ignored.
  - **Low AD + Very Low Len:** Over-aggressive fairness; worker stuck with high `ω_fair` or `ˆs_t` lacks long-tail signal.
  - **Unstable training curves:** HRL tuning issues (learning rates, update interval) or shifting DSRM output distribution.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1.  **Ablation Study:** Train `FLAT` (DSRM + single policy) and `HRL` (no DSRM). Compare `Len` and `AD` to full `DSRM-HRL`. Expected: Full model wins both metrics.
  2.  **Diffusion Step Sensitivity:** Vary `K` (5,10,30,50,100,500). Plot `Len`, `AD`. Expected: Peak at intermediate `K` (e.g., 30), severe drop at high `K`.
  3.  **State Visualization:** t-SNE of `˜s_t` vs `ˆs_t` colored by popularity/category. Expected: Raw state clusters by popularity; purified state shows disentangled semantic clusters.

## Limitations
- Architecture and hyperparameter details for DSRM (e.g., UNet layers, noise schedule) and HRL (e.g., update intervals, PPO settings) are insufficient for exact replication.
- Evaluation is limited to two simulators (KuaiRec, KuaiRand-Pure); real-world generalizability is untested.
- Core assumptions—popularity bias as a structured diffusion process and a low-dimensional preference manifold—remain unverified.

## Confidence
- **State corruption by popularity:** High (empirical `R² > 0.85` spurious correlation).
- **DSRM purification efficacy:** Medium (ablation shows benefit, but depends on unvalidated manifold assumptions and sensitive hyperparameters).
- **Hierarchical decoupling necessity:** Medium–High (ablation demonstrates synergy, though alternative multi-objective approaches might achieve similar gains).
- **Combined DSRM-HRL Pareto superiority:** High (consistent outperformance of strong baselines across datasets and metrics).

## Next Checks

3.  **Constraint mechanism ablation**—replace dynamic weight output with static penalties or Lagrangian methods—to isolate the unique contribution of hierarchical temporal decoupling.
