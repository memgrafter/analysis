# M3-AD: Reflection-aware Multi-modal, Multi-category, and Multi-dimensional Benchmark and Framework for Industrial Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2603.00055
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00055
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** RA-Monitor with Qwen-3-VL-4B achieves 80.3% anomaly detection accuracy and 0.653 localization hard-F1, surpassing larger open-source and commercial models in zero-shot setting.

## Executive Summary
Industrial anomaly detection using multimodal large language models (MLLMs) often yields unreliable decisions due to lack of self-reflection on uncertain cases. M3-AD introduces a reflection-aware framework that enables MLLMs to inspect and correct their own reasoning. The core method, RA-Monitor, employs two stages: Reflection-Aware Warm Start (RAWS) fine-tunes on M3-AD-FT, a dataset containing both thinking and reflective trajectories; and Reflection-Cognitive Reinforcement Learning (RCRL) optimizes a multi-reward policy combining consistency, accuracy, and reflection incentives. The authors also release M3-AD-Bench, a zero-shot benchmark spanning four industrial scenarios and 67 fine-grained anomaly types.

On M3-AD-Bench, RA-Monitor with a 4B MLLM achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance, reaching 80.3% anomaly detection accuracy and 0.653 hard-F1 for localization, outperforming models up to 72B parameters and commercial offerings. Ablation studies confirm that both reflective trajectory data and the multi-reward design are essential for robust performance, especially in fine-grained type and localization tasks. The work demonstrates that reflection mechanisms can significantly enhance decision reliability in industrial vision tasks without requiring model scale.

## Method Summary
RA-Monitor’s training pipeline consists of two sequential stages. First, Reflection-Aware Warm Start (RAWS) applies supervised fine-tuning to a base MLLM using the M3-AD-FT dataset. This dataset is constructed by labeling samples as easy or hard based on the base model’s anomaly existence prediction. For easy samples (mostly correctly predicted), the dataset provides either a single-thinking trajectory (`<answer>`) or, with 30% probability, a reflective one. For hard samples (misclassified), 70% are assigned reflective trajectories that include an initial `<answer>`, a `<reflection>` analysis, and a final corrected `<answer>`. This mixture teaches the model to engage reflection primarily on uncertain cases while avoiding unnecessary overhead on clear ones.

Second, Reflection-Cognitive Reinforcement Learning (RCRL) further optimizes the RAWS model via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). The RAWS model serves as the reference policy, and multiple outputs are sampled for each input. A composite reward R = λc·Rcons + λa·Racc + λr·Rrefl guides the updates. Rcons checks the presence of required tags (`<answer>`, `<type>`, `<location>`, and optionally `<reflection>`). Racc evaluates correctness of anomaly detection, type classification (using hierarchical matching), and localization (IoU ≥ 0.3). Rrefl compares the initial answer (before `<reflection>`) with the final answer: +1 for turning a wrong into a correct answer, -1 for corrupting a correct answer, and -0.5 for ineffective or still-wrong reflection. A KL divergence penalty (β·D_KL(π∥π_ref)) prevents catastrophic forgetting of the RAWS behaviors. The final policy, RA-Monitor, is evaluated zero-shot on M3-AD-Bench by parsing the structured response to extract existence, type, and bounding box predictions.

## Key Results
- **SOTA zero-shot performance:** RA-Monitor with Qwen-3-VL-4B achieves 80.3% anomaly detection accuracy and 0.653 hard-F1 for localization on M3-AD-Bench, outperforming larger open-source models (e.g., Qwen-3-VL-32B) and commercial models (GPT-5.1-Mini).
- **Ablation insights:** Fine-tuning on reflective trajectories significantly boosts performance over thinking-only fine-tuning, and the full multi-reward design (consistency + accuracy + reflection incentives with penalties) is critical; removing any component, especially the accuracy or reflection reward, degrades detection and localization metrics.
- **Transfer to unseen categories:** The framework demonstrates strong generalization to new anomaly types within the four high-level scenarios (Texture, Workpiece, Electronic, Logical), though performance varies, with Texture scenes achieving >80% accuracy and Workpiece/Electronic scenes around 72-79%, indicating room for improvement in high-level semantic reasoning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Reflection-Aware Warm Start (RAWS)
- **Claim:** RAWS fine-tunes the base MLLM on M3-AD-FT, a dataset containing both direct thinking and reflective trajectories, to initialize the model with dual reasoning pathways—immediate judgment and self-correction—conditioned on sample difficulty.
- **Mechanism:** The dataset is partitioned by difficulty: easy samples (correctly classified by a base model) are mostly assigned thinking mode (single reasoning pass), while hard samples (misclassified) are mostly assigned reflective mode (initial reasoning → reflection → revision). The model is trained via standard supervised fine-tuning to generate either a `<think>` block followed by `<answer>` (thinking) or a `<think>` block, then a `<reflection>` block, and finally the revised `<answer>` (reflective). This explicitly teaches the model when to reflect (on hard samples) and how to articulate both initial and corrected reasoning.
- **Core assumption:** The base model’s correctness on a sample is a reliable proxy for whether reflection is needed; reflective trajectories provide effective demonstrations of error correction.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “RA-Monitor uses a two-stage approach: first, Reflection-Aware Warm Start fine-tunes on M3-AD-FT—a dataset with both direct thinking and reflective trajectories—to learn initial and revised reasoning.”
  - [section 4.1] “The training objective of RAWS is to maximize the conditional likelihood of generating the corresponding structured output sequence… For Thinking Mode samples, the output sequence is defined as: O=(⟨think⟩, A). For Reflective Mode samples: O=(⟨think⟩,⟨reflection⟩, A).”
  - [section 3] “For easy samples, the thinking mode is primarily adopted, while 30% of the samples are assigned to the reflective mode… For hard samples, the reflective mode is dominant, accounting for 70% of the instances.”
- **Break condition:** If the difficulty labeling (based on base model) is misaligned with the target model’s actual needs, the model may learn inappropriate reflection triggers. Additionally, low-quality reflective trajectories (e.g., incorrect revisions) could degrade performance.

### Mechanism 2: Reflection-Cognitive Reinforcement Learning (RCRL)
- **Claim:** RCRL optimizes the model’s reflection policy via reinforcement learning with a multi-component reward that balances output consistency, anomaly detection accuracy, and reflection utility, encouraging self-correction only when it improves decision quality.
- **Mechanism:** The RAWS model serves as a reference policy. For each input, multiple candidate outputs are sampled. The reward R = λc Rcons + λa Racc + λr Rrefl combines: (1) a consistency reward that enforces structural validity (presence of required tags); (2) an accuracy reward that checks anomaly existence, type, and localization (with gating); (3) a reflection reward that gives +1.0 if reflection changed a wrong initial prediction to correct, -1.0 if it broke a correct prediction, and -0.5 otherwise (ineffective or still wrong). The policy is updated via GRPO to maximize expected reward while minimizing KL divergence from the reference, preserving fine-tuned knowledge.
- **Core assumption:** The reflection reward’s ternary design (+1, -1, -0.5) effectively distinguishes beneficial from unnecessary or harmful reflections; the KL constraint prevents catastrophic forgetting of RAWS behaviors.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] “Reflection-Cognitive Reinforcement Learning optimizes using a reward combining consistency, accuracy, and reflection incentives to encourage self-correction of unreliable initial judgments.”
  - [section 4.2] Formal definitions: “R = λc Rcons + λa Racc + λr Rrefl” and “Rrefl(y0, y1, y) = {+1.0 if y0≠y∧y1=y, -1.0 if y0=y∧y1≠y, -0.5 otherwise}.”
  - [section 5.3] Ablation in Table 3 and Figure 4: removing reflection reward or using only positive incentives degrades performance; configuration (d) with all three rewards (including -0.5 cost) yields best results.
- **Break condition:** Poor weight choices (λc, λa, λr) can imbalance objectives—e.g., overemphasizing reflection reward might cause excessive reflection; ignoring accuracy reward harms type/localization. The reflection reward assumes binary anomaly existence correctness; errors in type or localization that do not affect existence may go unpenalized.

### Mechanism 3: Difficulty-Aware Data Curation for Efficient Reflection Learning
- **Claim:** By constructing M3-AD-FT with difficulty-based assignment of thinking vs. reflective modes, the model learns to associate reflection with uncertain or error-prone cases, preventing reflection from becoming a default and promoting computational efficiency.
- **Mechanism:** A strong base model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) generates initial predictions on industrial images. Samples where the anomaly existence prediction matches the ground truth are labeled “easy”; mismatches are “hard”. Then, easy samples are mostly used for thinking mode (30% reflective as a control), while hard samples are mostly used for reflective mode (70%). This creates a curriculum where the model sees reflection primarily in contexts where the base model failed, learning to trust its initial reasoning on clear cases and to engage reflection on ambiguous ones.
- **Core assumption:** The base model’s error on anomaly existence is a valid indicator of sample difficulty for the target model; reflection is most beneficial on hard samples.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3] “To enable models to learn when reflection should be triggered, we design the difficulty-aware data construction based on decision correctness… Samples correctly classified by the base model are treated as easy, while misclassified samples are treated as hard.”
  - [section 3] “For easy samples, the thinking mode is primarily adopted, while 30% of the samples are assigned to the reflective mode… For hard samples, the reflective mode is dominant, accounting for 70% of the instances.”
  - [section 5.3] Table 2: Reflective Mode fine-tuning outperforms Thinking Mode Only and no fine-tuning, demonstrating the value of reflective trajectories.
- **Break condition:** If the base model used for difficulty labeling has systematically different failure modes from the target model, the difficulty partition may not align with actual need for reflection. The 70/30 split is heuristic; different ratios might be optimal for other datasets.

## Foundational Learning

### Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)
- Why needed here: RA-Monitor builds upon a pre-trained MLLM (e.g., Qwen-3-VL) that processes image and text inputs and generates structured outputs. Understanding its vision-language architecture, instruction tuning, and tokenization is essential to grasp how visual features are integrated and how special tags (`<think>`, `<reflection>`, etc.) are produced.
- Quick check question: How does an MLLM typically handle a request to output both reasoning and a final answer in a structured format? (Answer: It is trained to emit special tokens or XML-like tags that demarcate reasoning traces and final answers, enabling parsing of intermediate thoughts and decisions.)

### Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) / Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and GRPO
- Why needed here: RCRL employs reinforcement fine-tuning (specifically GRPO) to optimize the reflection policy based on a learned reward. Without knowledge of policy gradient methods, KL regularization, and advantage estimation, the RCRL objective and training dynamics cannot be understood.
- Quick check question: What is the purpose of the KL divergence term `β·D_KL(π(y|x)∥π_ref(y|x))` in RCRL’s objective? (Answer: To keep the current policy close to the reference policy (RAWS model), preventing destructive updates that would erase the fine-tuned reasoning and reflection behaviors.)

### Industrial Anomaly Detection Taxonomy and Evaluation Metrics
- Why needed here: M3-AD covers fine-grained anomaly types (surface, structural, logical) and requires localization. The reward functions and evaluation metrics (accuracy, balanced accuracy, hard-F1 for type and localization) depend on precise definitions of true positives. Understanding these concepts is necessary to design rewards and interpret results.
- Quick check question: Why does the localization metric use a hard IoU threshold (e.g., 0.3) instead of a continuous IoU score? (Answer: Because localization is evaluated as a discrete classification: a predicted box is a true positive only if its IoU with a ground-truth box exceeds the threshold, making the metric a hard-F1 that reflects practical sufficiency of overlap rather than exact precision.)

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component map
Base MLLM → RAWS module (supervised fine-tuning on M3-AD-FT) → RCRL module (GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning) → RA-Monitor (final policy). M3-AD-FT is created using an annotation tool; evaluation uses M3-AD-Bench.

### Critical path
1. **Data preparation:** Aggregate images from public industrial datasets; unify anomaly types via Qwen2.5-VL-72B open prediction and manual consolidation; annotate bounding boxes; generate base model predictions; assign difficulty; construct thinking/reflection captions using Claude-Sonnet-4.5; verify via annotation tool.
2. **RAWS fine-tuning:** Train the base MLLM on M3-AD-FT with standard language modeling loss, using full parameter or LoRA. This yields a model that can output both thinking and reflection modes.
3. **RCRL fine-tuning:** Initialize with RAWS model as reference policy. For each training batch, sample multiple outputs per input (e.g., 4) with temperature 1.0. Compute rewards (consistency, accuracy, reflection). Update policy via GRPO to maximize reward minus β·KL to reference. This teaches the model to decide when to reflect and how to produce accurate final answers.
4. **Zero-shot evaluation:** On M3-AD-Bench, feed the prompt (system message + image + question) to the final model. Parse the structured response to extract anomaly existence, type, and normalized bounding box(s). Compute accuracy, balanced accuracy, and hard-F1 for type and localization (IoU threshold 0.3).

### Design tradeoffs
- **Model scale vs. cost:** Smaller models (4B) are efficient but may have lower reasoning capacity; larger models (72B) perform better but require more GPU memory and slower inference. The paper shows 4B RA-Monitor outperforms larger baselines, suggesting reflection compensates for size.
- **Reflection reward shaping:** The penalty for ineffective reflection (-0.5) vs. breaking correct (-1.0) is a trade‑off: too harsh may inhibit reflection; too lenient may cause over‑reflection. The ablation (Figure 4) justifies the chosen values.
- **IoU threshold for localization:** Lower thresholds (e.g., 0.1) are more permissive but may reward coarse boxes; higher thresholds (e.g., 0.5) require precise alignment. The paper uses 0.3 based on VisionAD’s analysis and shows relative stability (Table 5), but absolute scores vary.
- **Difficulty split ratios:** 70% reflective for hard, 30% for easy are heuristics. Different splits might yield different reflection frequencies; the paper does not explore sensitivity beyond this.

### Failure signatures
- **Model ignores reflection:** Outputs only thinking traces even after RCRL. Could indicate insufficient reflection reward weight or too high KL penalty that locks in thinking-only behavior.
- **Over‑reflection:** Model frequently includes `<reflection>` blocks on easy samples, leading to unnecessary computations and potential error introduction. This aligns with Figure 4 configurations (a)–(c) where positive-only rewards encouraged redundant reflection.
- **Localization degradation:** Despite overall gains, some samples may have inaccurate bounding boxes after reflection, suggesting the accuracy reward’s location component (Rloc) might be underweighted or that reflection sometimes distracts from spatial accuracy.
- **Reward hacking:** Model learns to produce plausible type strings that match ground truth superficially without true visual grounding, especially if type matching is fuzzy (hierarchical taxonomy). The hard-F1 with threshold mitigates this.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Ablation on warm‑start data** (Table 2): Compared no fine‑tuning, thinking‑only fine‑tuning, and reflective‑mode fine‑tuning on Qwen‑3‑VL‑4B/8B. Reflective mode yielded the highest accuracy, balance, type, and localization scores, demonstrating the added value of reflective trajectories beyond thinking.
2. **Ablation on reward composition** (Table 3): Systematically removed consistency, accuracy, and reflection rewards. Full combination (all three) gave the best anomaly detection (80.3% accuracy) and localization (0.653 hard‑F1). Removing accuracy reward severely hurt type and localization; removing reflection reward reduced accuracy; removing consistency had minor impact but still degraded.
3. **Main results vs. baselines** (Table 1 & Table 4): RA‑Monitor (4B) achieved 80.3% anomaly detection accuracy and 0.527 type hard‑F1 / 0.653 localization hard‑F1, outperforming larger open‑source models (e.g., Qwen‑3‑VL‑32B: 79.9% accuracy, 0.368 type, 0.164 loc) and commercial models (GPT‑5.1‑Mini: 65.7% accuracy). This demonstrates the effectiveness of the reflection‑aware framework in zero‑shot settings.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
**Question:** What specific architectural modifications or training paradigms are needed for MLLMs to achieve consistently reliable decision-making in structurally complex industrial scenarios, given that performance degrades significantly on Workpiece and Electronic scenes despite reflection training?

**Basis in paper:** The authors state: "the core bottleneck ... does not primarily stem from insufficient visual perception, but rather from the absence of mechanisms that can explicitly inspect their own reasoning processes, identify potential decision errors, and correct them accordingly." They also note models "suffer significant performance degradation in scenarios involving high-level semantic anomalies such as structural relationships and component absence."

**Why unresolved:** The proposed RA-Monitor improves overall performance but still shows a substantial gap between Texture scenes (80%+ accuracy) and complex scenes (72-79% accuracy), indicating the reflection mechanism is not fully resolving the underlying issue of high-level semantic understanding.

**What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies isolating the impact of reflection on different anomaly categories (surface vs. structural vs. logical), failure case analyses showing whether reflection fails to trigger or fails to correct even when triggered on complex scenes, and comparisons with models explicitly pretrained on structural reasoning tasks.

### Open Question 2
**Question:** How should the multi-reward function in RCRL be optimally balanced (λ_c, λ_a, λ_r) to maximize both anomaly detection and fine-grained analysis without causing reward interference or overfitting to specific reward components?

**Basis in paper:** The ablation in Table 3 shows non-additive effects: including all three rewards yields best results, but the reflection reward alone (without consistency) hurts type/localization. This suggests complex interactions and potential suboptimal default weighting (all set to 1.0).

**Why unresolved:** The paper uses equal weights without justification or sensitivity analysis. The reflection reward's negative impact when accuracy reward is absent indicates potential tension between learning to self-correct versus learning accurate initial judgments.

**What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic hyperparameter sweep over λ ratios on validation sets, analyzing per-reward gradients during training to detect conflicts, and testing whether different industrial domains (e.g., textures vs. electronics) require different reward balances.

### Open Question 3
**Question:** To what extent does the M3-AD-FT dataset's construction—using Qwen2.5-VL-72B as a base model for difficulty partitioning and trajectory generation—introduce cyclical bias that limits the diversity of failure modes and reflection patterns that RA-Monitor can learn?

**Basis in paper:** The paper states: "We employ Qwen2.5-VL-72B as the base model and use its anomaly detection results as the criterion for difficulty assignment." All thinking/reflection trajectories are generated using this same model.

**Why unresolved:** This creates a closed loop: the base model's specific failure modes define "hard" samples, and its corrected reasoning defines the "reflective" trajectory. Models might learn to correct only the errors typical of Qwen2.5-VL-72B, not a broader distribution of industrial anomaly failures.

**What evidence would resolve it:** Evaluating RA-Monitor's performance on errors made by diverse base models (e.g., GPT, Gemini) not used in training; analyzing whether reflection patterns generalize to novel error types; constructing a held-out set of "hard" samples defined by human or multiple-model disagreement.

### Open Question 4
**Question:** Can the reflection mechanism be triggered more efficiently by learning to predict the utility of reflection upfront (a meta-reflection policy), rather than always executing the full reflective trajectory, and would this improve the trade-off between inference cost and accuracy on borderline cases?

**Basis in paper:** The current approach always generates a full thinking OR reflective trajectory based on training data distribution (30% vs. 70% per difficulty). There is no learned gating; the model must implicitly decide via reinforcement learning whether to reflect, but the RCRL reward encourages reflection only when it improves accuracy, yet no explicit early-stopping or confidence-based mechanism is proposed.

**Why unresolved:** The ablation shows reflective mode data improves performance, but at the cost of longer outputs. In real industrial settings, unnecessary reflection on easy samples wastes compute. The paper doesn't analyze the proportion of "unnecessary reflection" (where y0 = y1 = correct) or attempt to prune it.

**What evidence would resolve it:** Measuring the fraction of reflective mode outputs where the reflection reward is -0.5 (ineffective) across scenarios; training a lightweight classifier to predict need-for-reflection and evaluating accuracy/latency trade-off; comparing against a confidence-threshold gating mechanism.

### Open Question 5
**Question:** How robust is the reflection-aware framework to domain shift when applied to industrial categories or anomaly types completely unseen during M3-AD-FT training, especially given that M3-AD-Bench uses unseen categories but within the same four high-level scenarios?

**Basis in paper:** M3-AD-Bench reserves "unseen categories within each high-level scenario" for evaluation. The paper reports results on these held-out categories but does not evaluate on entirely new industrial domains (e.g., medical imaging, satellite inspection) with different anomaly taxonomies.

**Why unresolved:** The reflection mechanism is trained on a fixed set of 67 anomaly types. It is unclear whether the learned reflection patterns (e.g., "re-examine the highlighted region") generalize when faced with novel defect semantics or entirely different visual contexts. Performance drops on Electronic/Workpiece scenes suggest limited generalization even within covered domains.

**What evidence would resolve it:** Zero-shot evaluation on external industrial anomaly datasets not included in M3-AD (e.g., different texture domains, new PCB layouts); analysis of reflection content quality on novel categories via human evaluation; measuring the accuracy of the model's internal "difficulty assessment" on out-of-distribution samples.

## Limitations
- Exact hyperparameters (training steps, reward weights λc/λa/λr, GRPO specifics) are not disclosed, hindering full reproducibility.
- Difficulty labeling depends on the base model's anomaly existence correctness; misalignment between the base model's failure modes and the target model's needs could lead to inappropriate reflection triggers.
- The reflection reward focuses solely on binary anomaly existence correctness, not directly incentivizing improvements in type or localization accuracy, potentially limiting fine-grained gains.

## Confidence
- RAWS effectively teaches dual reasoning pathways (Thinking vs. Reflective modes): **Medium**
- RCRL’s multi-reward optimization yields beneficial self-correction: **Medium**
- Difficulty-aware data curation associates reflection with hard samples: **Medium**
- RA-Monitor achieves SOTA zero-shot anomaly detection on M3-AD-Bench: **High**
- Ablation studies demonstrate necessity of both reflective trajectories and multi-reward design: **High**

## Next Checks
