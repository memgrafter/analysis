# Controllable Reasoning Models Are Private Thinkers

## Quick Facts
- ArXiv ID: 2602.24210
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24210
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Training on explicit reasoning-trace instructions and using Staged Decoding yields large gains in instruction-following and privacy, with a trade-off in task utility.

## Executive Summary
Large reasoning models (LRMs) in AI agents often leak private information because they fail to follow privacy-related instructions during reasoning. This paper introduces a supervised fine-tuning dataset with explicit reasoning-trace (RT) instructions (format, style, reasoning type) and Staged Decoding, which separates reasoning and final answer generation using distinct LoRA adapters optimized for instruction-following. Evaluated on six models (Qwen3, Phi4) across IFEval, MathIF, PasswordEval, and PEEP, Staged Decoding achieves up to 20.9 points higher average instruction-following and up to 51.9 percentage points better privacy scores (e.g., 93.07 on PasswordEval for Qwen3-14B). However, task utility on math benchmarks can decline, confirming a trade-off between reasoning performance and instruction-following. The work demonstrates that improving instruction-following in reasoning traces enhances privacy, while highlighting the need for balanced training.

## Method Summary
The method begins by constructing a supervised fine-tuning dataset. Starting from GSM8K training problems with DeepSeek-R1 reasoning traces, the authors rewrite each reasoning trace to comply with a sampled RT instruction (format, style, or reasoning type) using GPT-4o-128B. This yields three dataset variants: RT-only (1k), RT-or-FA (2k), and RT-and-FA (3k). Models are fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 8, alpha 16) on these datasets, with hyperparameters tuned on a dev set (MathIF GSM8K split). At inference, Staged Decoding first generates the reasoning trace using the adapter fine-tuned for RT instruction-following (IF-RT). Upon emitting an end-of-thinking token, the IF-RT adapter is unloaded and the adapter fine-tuned for final-answer instruction-following (IF-FA) is loaded to continue generation to the final answer. This decoupling prevents interference between the two objectives. Evaluations use IFEval, MathIF, PasswordEval, and PEEP to measure instruction-following, privacy (leakage rate), and utility (math accuracy, answer correctness, or LLM-as-judge scores).

## Key Results
- Up to 20.9-point gain in average instruction-following across models.
- Up to 51.9 percentage-point improvement in privacy scores; e.g., Qwen3-14B achieves 93.07 on PasswordEval.
- Task utility on math benchmarks can drop significantly (e.g., Qwen3-4B from 40.81 to 23.95), indicating a privacy-utility trade-off.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Training LRMs on a dataset with explicit reasoning-trace (RT) instructions improves their ability to follow privacy directives during reasoning, thereby reducing leakage of sensitive information in RTs.
- **Mechanism**: The dataset (constructed by rewriting DeepSeek-R1 CoTs to comply with sampled RT instructions) provides supervised examples where the input includes an RT instruction and the target RT obeys it. Fine-tuning with LoRA teaches the model to map prompts with RT constraints to compliant RTs. Privacy constraints are treated as a subset of RT instructions; thus, improved general RT instruction-following transfers to privacy contexts.
- **Core assumption**: The model's generation behavior can be shaped via supervised fine-tuning to respect arbitrary RT instructions, and privacy constraints can be expressed as RT instructions.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "We propose training models on a new supervised fine-tuning dataset with explicit reasoning-trace instructions... Evaluated on six models... up to 51.9 percentage points better privacy scores."
  - [section 3.1]: Description of dataset construction: "We introduce three types of RT-specific instructions: Formatting... Style... Reasoning type... We rewrite... using GPT-4o-128B."
  - [corpus]: Neighbor "ReasonIF: Large Reasoning Models Fail to Follow Instructions During Reasoning" (arXiv:2510.15211) highlights the baseline deficiency in RT instruction-following that this work addresses.
- **Break condition**: If RT instructions do not cover the diversity of privacy constraints encountered in deployment, or if the model overfits to the training distribution and fails to generalize to new privacy directives.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Staged Decoding, which uses separate LoRA adapters for reasoning-trace and final-answer generation, simultaneously maximizes instruction-following performance in both components without significant interference.
- **Mechanism**: At inference time, the generation is split into two stages: first, the RT is generated using a LoRA adapter fine-tuned for RT instruction-following (IF-RT). Then, that adapter is unloaded and a different LoRA adapter fine-tuned for final-answer instruction-following (IF-FA) is loaded to generate the final answer. This temporal decoupling prevents optimization conflicts between the two objectives that would arise if a single set of parameters were used.
- **Core assumption**: The optimal parameters for complying with RT instructions differ from those for FA instructions; isolating them during generation yields better performance than a monolithic model.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "We further introduce a generation strategy that decouples reasoning and answer generation using separate LoRA adapters."
  - [section 3.3]: "Staged Decoding separates the generation process into two stages: (1) generating the RT using a LoRA adapter fine-tuned for IF-RT, and (2) generating the final answer using the best LoRA weights for IF-FA."
  - [section 5.1]: Results show Staged Decoding achieves the best average IF in 9/12 cases, with gains up to 20.9 points over baseline.
- **Break condition**: If the RT and FA fine-tuning objectives are not truly separable (e.g., they require shared knowledge), staged decoding may degrade performance relative to a unified model, or the overhead of switching adapters could become problematic in low-latency settings (though overhead is negligible in practice).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Better instruction-following in reasoning traces directly leads to improved contextual privacy because privacy preservation can be framed as a set of instructions to be obeyed during reasoning.
- **Mechanism**: In agent scenarios, privacy policies (e.g., "do not mention the password in your reasoning") are provided as part of the system or user prompt. When the LRM has strong RT instruction-following, it adheres to these constraints throughout its internal CoT. This prevents sensitive contextual data from being regurgitated in the RT, which in turn thwarts extraction attacks (e.g., prompt injections) that rely on RT content. The causal chain is: improved IF-RT → fewer privacy-violating tokens in RT → lower leakage rate in privacy benchmarks.
- **Core assumption**: Privacy leakage in LRMs is primarily due to failure to follow instructions that restrict private data usage in RTs, rather than other vulnerabilities such as memorization or side channels.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "We hypothesize that improving their instruction following abilities in the reasoning traces can improve their privacy-preservation skills... Our method yields substantial improvements... up to 51.9 percentage points on privacy benchmarks."
  - [section 5.2]: "Staged Decoding... achieves the best privacy results in seven of the ten evaluated setups... with average privacy gains of 21.65 in PasswordEval and 22.69 in PEEP... These results indicate that stronger instruction-following capabilities yield improved privacy."
  - [section 6] and Tables 12-13: Qualitative examples show baseline leaks password/name in RT while Staged Decoding complies.
  - [corpus]: Neighbor "Leaky Thoughts: Large Reasoning Models Are Not Private Thinkers" (arXiv:2506.15674) documents the privacy leakage problem, providing the baseline that this mechanism aims to fix.
- **Break condition**: If privacy requirements involve more than instruction-following (e.g., need for dynamic reasoning about sensitivity), or if attackers exploit other pathways (e.g., model inversion), improved IF-RT alone may not suffice.

## Foundational Learning
#### Concept: Instruction-Following in LLMs
- **Why needed here**: The entire premise is that LRMs' lack of instruction-following extends to reasoning traces, causing privacy leaks. Understanding what instruction-following means (adhering to lexical, semantic, and behavioral constraints) is fundamental to grasp the problem and solution.
- **Quick check question**: How would you design an instruction for an LRM to control the language of its reasoning trace, and what makes it hard for the model to comply?

#### Concept: Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (LoRA)
- **Why needed here**: The training and Staged Decoding both rely on LoRA adapters to specialize models for different objectives without full retraining. Knowing LoRA's low-rank adaptation is essential to understand the efficiency and modularity claims.
- **Quick check question**: What are the advantages of using LoRA over full fine-tuning for large models, especially when multiple specialized adapters are needed?

#### Concept: Privacy Benchmarks for LLMs
- **Why needed here**: The paper evaluates privacy using PasswordEval and PEEP. Familiarity with these benchmarks and their metrics (leakage rate, privacy score, utility) is required to interpret results and assess claims.
- **Quick check question**: What does a privacy score of 0.75 on PasswordEval represent, and how is utility measured differently?

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component map
Training data pipeline -> LoRA adapters (IF-RT, IF-FA) -> Staged Decoding controller -> Base LRM -> Evaluation suite

### Critical path
1. Dataset creation: Generate RT instruction set, sample from DeepSeek-R1 GSM8K outputs, rewrite RTs via LLM, combine with FA, create SFT splits.
2. Adapter training: For each model, train three LoRA variants (RT-only, FA-only, combined) using hyperparameter tuning; select best checkpoint per objective based on dev (GSM8K split of MathIF).
3. Staged Decoding setup: Package the selected RT-IF and FA-IF adapters; implement adapter switching at inference (e.g., in vLLM).
4. Evaluation: Run baselines and staged decoding on IF and privacy benchmarks, compute metrics, perform statistical tests.

### Design tradeoffs
- Training set size vs overfitting: Small 1k-3k dataset may cause overfitting, evidenced by utility drop on MathIF (section 5.3). Larger datasets could mitigate.
- Staged Decoding vs monolithic generation: Staged decoding adds negligible overhead (adapter switch) but requires careful checkpoint selection; a single model fine-tuned on both objectives might collapse one performance.
- Privacy vs utility: Explicit RT constraints may harm reasoning performance (correlation -0.65), as seen on MathIF. Trade-off is inherent; balancing requires multi-objective RL (section 7).
- Quantization impact: 4-bit quantization speeds training/inference but increases malformed outputs (Appendix D), affecting reliability.

### Failure signatures
- **Malformed outputs**: Missing final answer or broken ` ` ` tags (Table 11). Often due to quantization; consider full precision or adjust decoding parameters.
- **Utility collapse**: Significant drop in math accuracy (e.g., Table 3: RT-IF models drop from 40.81 to 23.95 on Qwen3-4B). Indicates over-specialization; may need to increase training data diversity or incorporate RL.
- **Adapter interference**: Using a single adapter for both stages yields degraded performance (see Table 1: IF-FA checkpoint has high IF-FA but low IF-RT). Remedy: Use Staged Decoding with distinct adapters.

### First 3 experiments
1. **Validate RT instruction learning**: Train a LoRA on the 1k RT-only dataset. Evaluate IF-RT on IFEval (non-overlapping instructions). Expect IF-RT to improve relative to baseline (e.g., +20 points). If not, dataset construction or training setup is flawed.
2. **Compare Staged Decoding vs single-adapter**: For the same model family, compare Staged Decoding (RT-IF + FA-IF adapters) against (a) baseline, (b) RT-IF-only model, (c) FA-IF-only model on both IF and privacy benchmarks. Confirm that Staged Decoding achieves higher Avg. IF and privacy without sacrificing one.
3. **Measure privacy-utility trade-off**: Plot privacy score vs utility (MathIF or PEEP utility) for all variants. Expect a negative correlation but Staged Decoding should shift the Pareto frontier upward (higher privacy for similar utility compared to baseline). If not, revisit adapter selection or dataset.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How can the trade-off between reasoning performance (task utility) and instruction-following (including privacy) be mitigated in controllable reasoning models?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors state: "Addressing this trade-off remains an important direction for future work." They also note that improved instruction-following can reduce utility on math benchmarks, confirming an inherent trade-off.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper identifies and quantifies the trade-off but does not propose a solution; it leaves the development of methods that balance these objectives for future work.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A training or decoding approach that achieves statistically significant gains in both instruction-following (IF-RT/IF-FA) and task utility (e.g., GSM8K accuracy) compared to strong baselines across multiple model scales, without degrading privacy.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) with a multi‑objective reward model improve both privacy and utility in large reasoning models?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The Limitations section says: "We believe future work could incorporate some form of reinforcement learning (RL). For example, a full RL from human feedback (RLHF) pipeline could be implemented by training a reward model to jointly evaluate task correctness and instruction following, followed by PPO."
- **Why unresolved**: The work relies solely on supervised fine‑tuning (SFT); RLHF is suggested as a promising direction but remains unevaluated.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: An RLHF pipeline applied to LRMs that yields higher privacy scores and equal or better task accuracy on benchmarks like PasswordEval and GSM8K compared to the SFT‑only Staged Decoding approach.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Would scaling the instruction‑following training dataset size and diversity eliminate the utility drop observed in Staged Decoding while preserving privacy gains?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The Limitations state: "our training dataset is relatively small, and this may cause overfitting, which could partially explain the utility drop in some cases." They also note: "We believe the potential utility drops could be reduced in such larger training setups."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper uses a small (1k–3k example) dataset derived from GSM8K; it is unknown whether larger, more diverse data would alleviate the utility–privacy trade‑off.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Training models on a substantially larger and domain‑diverse instruction‑following dataset (e.g., >100k examples) and demonstrating that Staged Decoding’s privacy improvements are maintained without the utility declines seen with the smaller dataset.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: How do controllable reasoning models perform on non‑English prompts in privacy benchmarks like PEEP?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors acknowledge: "Although PEEP includes non‑English prompts (around 50%), we do not investigate the performance of the models by language."
- **Why unresolved**: The evaluation aggregates results across languages; disparities in privacy behavior across languages could exist but are not reported.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A language‑wise breakdown of privacy scores (RT/FA leakage) on PEEP (or a new multilingual privacy benchmark) showing whether Staged Decoding’s benefits are consistent across languages or if certain languages exhibit weaker privacy protection.

## Limitations
- Small supervised fine-tuning datasets (1k–3k examples) lead to overfitting and utility collapse on math benchmarks.
- 4-bit quantization may cause malformed outputs, limiting reproducibility and generalizability.
- Privacy evaluation limited to PasswordEval and PEEP; unknown robustness to other attacks.

## Confidence
Overall, we assign **medium confidence** to the paper's claims. The experimental validation is thorough, evaluating six models across multiple benchmarks, and the proposed mechanisms are well-supported by evidence. However, limitations such as small training datasets, potential quantization artifacts, and the inherent privacy-utility trade-off introduce uncertainty about generalizability and robustness.

- **Mechanism 1** (RT instruction training improves IF & privacy): Medium confidence. The evidence includes significant gains on privacy benchmarks and instruction-following evaluations. However, the small dataset size (1k-3k) may limit generalization, and the break condition (RT instructions not covering all privacy constraints) could reduce effectiveness in deployment.
- **Mechanism 2** (Staged Decoding avoids interference): Medium confidence. Results show Staged Decoding achieves best average IF in 9/12 cases and improved privacy, supporting the claim. Yet, the assumption that RT and FA objectives are separable might not hold for all model architectures, and the negligible overhead claim needs verification in low-latency settings.
- **Mechanism 3** (Better RT IF directly yields better privacy): Medium confidence. Strong empirical correlation between IF-RT scores and privacy metrics, plus qualitative examples showing reduced leakage, supports the causal claim. However, privacy may also depend on factors beyond RT compliance (e.g., memorization, prompt injection defenses), and the break condition (other attack vectors) could limit impact. Further, the correlation does not prove causation without an ablation isolating RT IF from other factors.

## Next Checks
1. Full-precision replication: Repeat key experiments with fp16/bf16 to confirm improvements are not quantization artifacts.
2. Scaled training: Expand SFT data to 10k+ diverse examples; reassess privacy gains and utility collapse.
3. Adapter separation audit: Instrument Staged Decoding to verify RT generation is unaffected by the FA adapter and vice versa.
